## 524288 JAPANESE PUZZLES

⟦figure⟧

*The five readers who are the first to send answers will be awarded an annual subscription to «Kvant», and their names will be published in the magazine.*

⟦column⟧

On the last page of the cover there is one of these puzzles: you need to place all the blue figures on a yellow field.

Let's figure out how this puzzle is built. As you can see (see figure on the cover), the yellow field consists of seven regular hexagons. How are the figures themselves constructed? Let's see what figures can be folded by connecting sides $n$ of regular triangles. From one, two and three triangles, one figure is formed: a triangle, a rhombus and a trapezoid (Fig. 1). Three figures are made from four triangles (Fig. 2). True, it is possible to add up the fourth, but it is a mirror image of the third, and we do not take it into account. Five triangles make up four figures (Fig. 3). Twelve figures are made from six triangles. Draw them yourself. Twenty-four figures are made from seven triangles. These are the pieces of our puzzle (check it out!).

Some of the figures have names: bowl, hook, bear, etc. Among the figures there are 5 symmetrical and 19 asymmetrical. By turning over some of the pieces you will get a new puzzle. In total, you can get $2^{19}$ puzzles by flipping (why?). Is it possible to lay any of them on the same yellow field?

Also try to put together some other interesting field (for example, a rhombus) from 12 different figures, made up of six triangles each.
