APPLICANT PRACTICUM

What questions and tasks can applicants expect during the entrance exams? How are these exams going? What are the typical shortcomings of applicants' answers? What to pay attention to first when preparing and how to most effectively organize repetition!

The materials in our traditional section “Applicant's Workshop” are devoted to the answers to these questions. Here the reader will find articles on individual topics and the most important or difficult questions of the program of entrance exams in physics and mathematics, conditions and analysis of problems of the options offered last year to applicants of various universities in the country, information about books to help applicants, etc.

We emphasize that the materials of the “Applicant’s Workshop” cannot replace school textbooks, and they do not set such a goal. In the published articles, readers will find, if possible, an in-depth, detailed presentation of only a few theoretical issues, accompanied, however, by an analysis of problems taken from the practice of admission exams. When outlining the topics of the articles, the editors used the experience of entrance exams and took into account the considerations and suggestions contained in the extensive editorial mail. It will, of course, be useful for readers to get acquainted with those materials from the “Applicant’s Workshop” that have already been published in the magazine in past years. A detailed thematic list of articles for applicants for 1970-1973 is placed in «Kvant» No. 1, 1974, p. 52.

Many of our readers, especially those who graduated from school several years ago, are interested in the question of how the ongoing transition to new programs in mathematics and physics at school has affected the content of entrance exam programs.

Let us recall that in 1972 the program of entrance examinations in mathematics was revised, but in content it completely corresponds to the old high school course. This program is still in effect. It contains a list of basic mathematical concepts that an applicant must master, theorems that must be able to prove, and formulas that must be able to derive. In addition, the program describes the basic mathematical skills that an applicant must possess. No knowledge beyond the program is required from applicants. In particular, applicants are not required to become familiar with the concepts of derivative, integral, the basics of mathematical analysis, elements of vector calculus, etc.

The program for entrance exams in physics is also fully consistent with the old high school course.

We hope that familiarization with the options for entrance exams will give future applicants the opportunity to concretely imagine what a written exam in physics and mathematics is like at universities, pedagogical and technical universities. These options are a convenient reason to try your hand at solving a certain set of problems in a fixed time.

The key to success in entrance exams is systematic and regular independent work. It is necessary to repeat and actively consolidate theoretical material, get sufficient practice in solving problems. This takes time. Therefore, start preparing for the entrance exams without delay, especially if you are going to enter college in six months!
