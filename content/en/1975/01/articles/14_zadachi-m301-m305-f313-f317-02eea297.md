Quantum problems

This section, which has been published from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine, contains a variety of tasks - both relatively simple ones and those that are not easy to solve even for specialists. The most difficult tasks will be marked with an asterisk. If a task interests you, but you can’t solve it right away, don’t give up, try to return to it again in a day, in a week. Then, after the problem has been solved - if it is a really interesting problem - you can continue working on it: think about whether there is a simpler and more beautiful solution, write down the solution in the most rational and convincing way (sometimes this is not at all easy to do), try to generalize the problem, clarify or clarify its result.

The editors post lists of readers who assign the correct solutions to the problem. Schoolchildren who regularly produce interesting and complete solutions throughout the year receive the right to participate in the tourism field of the All-Optimids in mathematics and physics along with the winners of regional and city optimids. In addition, the editors establish special prizes for the winners of the competition for solving the problem from the Problem Book «Kvant».

Of course, not all problems in the Problem Book are published for the first time, although we strive to choose the most original problems. Obviously after formation we indicate who proposed the task to us. Coming up with a new problem, sorry, is even more difficult than solving someone else’s. If you succeed, send us the problem along with the solution. Write how your problem arose, whether you have encountered similar problems, what mathematical theorems your new result is related to. We will publish the most beautiful problems in the “Problem Book” or in other sections of the magazine. Obviously we publish solutions to problems taking into account useful readers by letters in 6-8 months.

Our address: 117071, Moscow, V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, editorial office of the magazine «Kvant».
On the envelope after the address, write the solutions to which problems you are sending (for example, “Problem book M301, M305” or “... F311”). Send solutions to problems in each subject (if you are sending problems in both mathematics and physics) in separate envelopes. The formulation of new problems must be sent in two copies (on the envelope put: “New problem in mathematics” or “... in physics”).

We receive a lot of letters and try to answer all our correspondents. Please prepare solutions carefully to facilitate the work of the editors and consultants of the “Problem Book «Kvant»” department. At the beginning of the letter, you must write your last name, first name, patronymic, home address, as well as the class and school where you are a teacher. Include an envelope with your address written on it {for response after checking the solution}. We will be able to take into account letters from readers only if they are written in Russian and written no later than a month and a half after the publication of the corresponding issue. For example, please send letters with solutions to problems from this issue no later than March 1, 1975.

**M303.** Rectangle $300 \times 1000$ is cut into squares $1 \times 1$, and identical weights are placed at some 30 vertices of the squares. Prove that it is possible to choose two disjoint groups of weights - no more than 10 in each - so that their centers of gravity coincide.

*A. V. Sherstyuk*

**M304.** We will denote by a circle some (yet unknown) operation applicable to any two non-negative integers $a$ and $b$ and resulting in also a non-negative integer $a \circ b = c$. Let the operation “$\circ$” satisfy the following conditions:

1) $a \circ b = b \circ a$;

2) if $a \circ b = c$, then $b \circ c = a$;

3) if $a \circ b > c$, then $b \circ c < a$ or $a \circ c < b$.

a) Find $0 \circ 0$; $0 \circ 1$; $1 \circ 1$; $0 \circ 2$.

b) Prove that $0 \circ a = a$ and

$$1 \circ a = \begin{cases} a+1, \text{ если } a \text{ четно},\\ a-1, \text{ если } a \text{ нечетно}.\end{cases}$$

c) \* Prove that there is at most one operation that satisfies the conditions of the problem.

d) \* Prove that such an operation exists and indicate a rule that allows you to calculate $a \circ b$ given $a$ and $b$.

*A. A. Grigoryan*

**F313**. The refractive index of the planet's atmosphere $X$ decreases with height $h$ above its surface according to the law $n = n_0 - \alpha h$. The radius of the planet is $R$. Find at what height $h_0$ above the surface of the planet there is an optical channel through which light rays will go around the planet, remaining at a constant height.

*N. N. Sedov*
