New example
to the All-Union correspondence
math school

The All-Union Correspondence Mathematical School (VZMSH) accepts students from rural classes. Schoolchildren living in Moscow, Leningrad and their suburbs are not admitted to VZMSH.

Classes will begin on September 1st. School education is free.

Students admitted to the school will regularly (about once a month) receive assignments that contain theoretical questions and assignments to solve.

The All-Union Correspondence Mathematics School has three courses.

Those wishing to enter the VZMSH must send solutions to the problems no later than March 20, 1975. After a performance review (around July 1975), you will be notified whether you have been accepted into the VZMSH. Schoolchildren living in rural areas and workers' settlements will have priority for admission.

Although some of the introductory problems look different from regular school problems, they do not require any additional knowledge of mathematics to solve.

In order to be accepted at school, it is not necessary to solve every problem without exception. When evaluating work, not only the number of problems solved will be taken into account, but also the quality of the solution. The solution to each problem must be justified. The answer without any training may not be given. If there are several possible answers to a problem, then all of them must be indicated.

The work must be completed in Russian in a student notebook. Entry papers will not be processed.

In the envelope along with your notebook you need to put a piece of paper measuring 14x6 cm with your postal address (we will stick it on the envelope when we send you a response).

## Objectives of the entrance test at VZMSH in 1975

1. Is it possible to place six points on a plane and connect them with segments so that each point is connected by segments with four other points and the segments do not intersect each other?

2. After the end of the play “The Inspector General,” Bobchinsky and Dobchinsky began to bicker on stage over who was the first to say “E!”

BOBCHINSKY: It was you, Pyotr Ivanovich, who first said “Eh!” You yourself said so before.

Dobchinsky: No, Pyotr Ivanovich, I didn’t say that. You were the one who ordered the salmon first. You said “Eh!” And I have a tooth whistling in my mouth.

BOBCHINSKY: That I was the first to order salmon, that’s true. And it’s true that your tooth is whistling. But still, you were the first to say “Eh!”

Find out who was the first to say “Eh!” if it is known that out of the nine phrases uttered in this conversation, an odd number are correct.

3. Find four numbers if you know that the product of any three of them is 120 greater than the remaining number.

4. Prove that if any two of the following three points coincide in a triangle:

1) center of the inscribed circle,

2) the center of the circumscribed circle,

3) median intersection point,

then the triangle is equilateral.

5. Four girls - Katya, Lena, Masha and Nina - took part in the concert. They sang songs. Each song was sung by three girls. Katya sang 8 songs - more than everyone else, and Lena sang 5 songs - less than everyone else. How many songs were sung?

6. Is it true that for any natural number $n$ the number $n^4 + (n+1)^4$ is prime?

7. Find the value of the angle $B$ of the triangle $ABC$ if the length of the height $CH$ is half the length of the side $AB$, and the value of the angle $A$ is equal to $75^\circ$.

8. Add seven more integers to the numbers 10, 12, 15 so that the greatest common divisor of any two of the ten written numbers occurs among these numbers and the least common multiple of any two written numbers also occurs among these numbers.

9. Prove that if $a^2(b-c) + b^2(c-a) + c^2(a-b) = 0$, then some two of the three numbers $a$, $b$, $c$ are equal to each other.

10. Find all values ​​of $p$ and $q$ for which the polynomial
$$x^4 + px^3 + 17x^2 + qx + 16$$
can be represented as the square of some quadratic trinomial in $x$.

11. The elements of the sets $A$, $B$ and $C$ are the numbers 1, 2, ..., 9. The following is known:
$$A \cap B = \{1,\ 2\},$$
$$B \cap C = \{3,\ 7\},$$
$$A \cup B = \{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8\},$$
$$A \cup C = \{1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9\}.$$

Find the sets $A$, $B$ and $C$. (Here $A \cap B$ is the intersection of the sets $A$ and $B$, $A \cup B$ is the union of these sets.)

12. It is known about a certain figure on the plane that when rotated around the point $O$ by an angle $48^\circ$ it turns into itself. Is it possible to say that it will transform into itself when rotated around the point $O$ by an angle

a) $90^\circ$?

b) $72^\circ$?
