The trajectory of the weight will be a part of a circle with radius $r = l\sin\beta$, the plane of which is perpendicular to the rod $AB$ and inclined at an angle $90^\circ - \alpha$ to the horizontal plane (Fig. 21, b).

Let us write down the equation of motion of the weight, assuming that its deviations from the equilibrium position are small. To do this, you need to find the sum of the projections of all the forces acting on the weight onto the tangent to the trajectory of the weight. The elastic forces $Q_1$ and $Q_2$, acting on the weight from the side of the rods, lie in the plane of the rods, perpendicular to the trajectory of the weight. Therefore, their projections onto the tangent to the trajectory are equal to zero.

In order to find the projection of the gravity force $mg$, we decompose this force into components (see Fig. 21, b) $F_1$, lying in the plane of the trajectory, and $F_2$, perpendicular to this plane. The projection of the force $F_2$ onto the tangent to the trajectory of the weight is equal to zero, and the projection of the force $F_1$ is equal to $F_1\sin\gamma$ (Fig. 21, c). Since $F_1 = mg\cos\alpha$, the magnitude of this projection is $mg\cos\alpha\sin\gamma$.

Now we can write down the equation of motion of the weight along the trajectory. In this case, it is necessary to take into account, as with the movement of a mathematical pendulum, that the acceleration of the weight along the trajectory is directed opposite to the deviation of the weight from the equilibrium position. Therefore, the projection of gravity and the acceleration of the bob have opposite signs. Hence,

$$ma_\mathrm{т} = -mg\cos\alpha\sin\gamma.$$

Since the angle $\gamma$ is small, then $\sin\gamma \approx \gamma$ and we can write that

$$a_\mathrm{т} = -\,(g\cos\alpha)\,\gamma.$$

This equation coincides with the equation of motion of a mathematical pendulum, in which the acceleration of gravity $g$ is replaced by $g\cos\alpha$. This means that the period of oscillation of the weight is equal to

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l'}{g'}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{g\cos\alpha}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l\sin\beta}{g\cos\alpha}}.$$
