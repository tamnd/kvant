**5.** Using Thomson's formula for the period of electromagnetic oscillations in a circuit $T = 2\pi\sqrt{LC}$ and the known relation $\lambda = vT$ between wavelength $\lambda$ $v$  $T$, we find

$$\lambda = 2\pi v\sqrt{LC}, \qquad (1)$$

where $v = 3\cdot10^8$ *m/s* (in vacuum), $C$ is the capacitance of the capacitor in the circuit.

This capacitance is equal to the capacitance of two identical flat capacitors connected in parallel, formed by the surfaces of the middle plate and each of the outer plates (Fig. 4). Hence,

$$C = 2\varepsilon_0\varepsilon\,\frac{S}{d}. \qquad (2)$$

$$L = \frac{d}{2\varepsilon_0\varepsilon S}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\pi v}\right)^2 = 25\ \textit{мгн}.$$

**6.** Since the gas pressure under the piston in a moving vessel increases, the acceleration **a** of the vessel and the piston is directed vertically upward. Figures 5, *a* and *b* show the forces acting on the piston in a vessel at rest and in a vessel moving with acceleration: gravity $mg$, external pressure force $p_0S$ and gas pressure forces on the lower surface of the piston - $p_1S$ in a vessel at rest and $p_2S$ in a vessel moving with acceleration $a$.

Let us write down the equilibrium condition for a piston in a vessel at rest:

$$(p_1-p_0)\,S - mg = 0, \qquad (1)$$

equation of motion of a piston moving together with a vessel with acceleration $a$:

$$ma = (p_2-p_0)\,S - mg \qquad (2)$$

and the Boyle-Mariotte law for gas under the piston:

$$p_1Sh = p_2S\,[h\,(1-\eta)]. \qquad (3)$$

From relations (1), (2) and (3) we find the external pressure:

$$p_0 = \frac{m}{S}\left[a\left(\frac{1}{\eta}-1\right)-g\right] = 18\ \textit{кн/м}^2.$$
