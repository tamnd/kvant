⟦rubric⟧
Ask - we answer

The editors of «Kvant» receive many letters from their readers. In some letters we are asked questions, the answers to which are of undoubted interest not only to the author of the letter, but also to other readers. We will publish such questions and answers to them on the pages of the magazine under the heading “Ask - we answer.”

Perhaps some of you are hesitant to write to the editor for fear of asking a “stupid” question or making mistakes or inaccuracies in your letter. Of course, it is better not to make them, but the analysis of other mistakes can turn out to be fruitful, interesting, and instructive. In science, the analysis of errors often even leads to discoveries.

In this issue we publish the answer to the question of our reader A. Sidnev.

Here is what A. Sidnev writes:

Dear editors

The magazine «Kvant» No. 2 for 1974 contains an article “An Unusual Journey”, which outlines the idea of ​​​​the so-called “gravity plane” proposed by V.V. Beletsky and M.E. Giverets.

Doesn't this idea contradict the law of conservation of energy? After all, if a “gravity plane” is launched into a geocentric orbit with a first cosmic speed of $v_1 = 8000$ m/s, and goes into outer space with a second cosmic speed of $v_2 = 11\,000$ m/s, then with a mass of $m = 1000$ kg during the period of its stay in orbit it must acquire energy

$$E = \frac{m}{2}\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) \approx 285 \cdot 10^8\ \textit{дж}.$$

Where will the “gravity jet” get this energy?

Let us recall that the article “An Unusual Journey” talked about a spaceship in the form of a dumbbell, the balls of which can be moved or moved apart. The article showed that such a ship moving around the Earth can be accelerated to the second escape velocity if the balls are quickly moved apart at the perigee points each time, and moved at the apogee points.

Below we publish the answer from the author of the article “An Unusual Journey” I. I. Vorobyov.
