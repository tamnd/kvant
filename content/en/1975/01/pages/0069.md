There would indeed be a contradiction with the law of conservation of energy if the expansion of massive parts of the ship did not require energy expenditure. Let's consider the breeding of balls at perigee. We will assume that the expansion is fast (so that the orbital movement can be neglected) and occurring perpendicular to the orbital plane.

Let $r$ be the distance from the Earth to the center of mass of the ship (see Fig.). When spread by an angle of $\alpha$, the distance from each of the balls to the center of the Earth will become $r' = \dfrac{r}{\cos{(\alpha/2)}}$. Recall that when moving along an orbit, the angle $\alpha$ is kept constant. Therefore, the force acting on the dumbbell (gravity gun with the balls moved apart) differs from the force acting on a material point of the same mass (gravity gun with the balls moved apart) by a constant factor $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.

$$A = -\gamma\,\frac{mM}{r'} - \left(-\gamma\,\frac{mM}{r}\right) = \gamma\,\frac{mM}{r}\left(1 - \cos\,\frac{\alpha}{2}\right).$$

The fact that work must be done is clear, because there is a component of the attractive force directed against the movement of parts of the gravitational plane. This work goes towards increasing the potential energy of the ship in the Earth's gravitational field. Thus, the increase in the potential energy of the ship occurs due to the expenditure of energy from internal sources for the expansion of the balls.

During a shift at the apogee, on the contrary, work is done by gravitational forces (you can even store energy in sources). But since the distance to the ship at apogee is greater, this work is less “perigee”. So, in general, energy has to be expended during the cycle, which ultimately goes into accelerating the ship.

The minimum amount of energy required to leave orbit is the same for any acceleration method. However, it may turn out that a gravity jet is more energy efficient than, for example, a rocket engine. After all, it is obvious that during jet propulsion, part of the energy is wasted - it turns into the kinetic energy of emitted gases.
