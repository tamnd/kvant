**M264.** *The city has one blue square and n green squares, and each green square is connected by streets with a blue one and with two green ones (Fig. 10). On each of the 2n streets, one-way traffic was introduced so that you can come to each square and leave from each. Prove that from any square of this city it is possible, without violating the rules introduced, to get to any of the others.*

Before proceeding with the solution, we note that in the problem statement the reference to figure 10 is *essential* - it is part of the condition; so, for example, for the city shown in figure 11 (with one blue and $n$ green squares), all the conditions of problem I are met, but the statement is incorrect: it is impossible to drive from part I to part II.

Let us now move on to solving our problem. Let's split the proof into two parts: we prove that

a) from any green square you can get to the blue one;

b) from the blue square you can get to any green square.

Let's agree to indicate the direction of movement on the streets with arrows.

a) Suppose that it is impossible to get from the green square $A_1$ to the blue square. Since it is possible to leave square $A_1$, then from it, driving along one street, we will reach the green square $A_2$. From square $A_2$ you also cannot get to the blue square. Having left it, we will reach square $A_3$, then $A_4$, etc. Since there are only $n$ green squares, having driven along $n$ streets, we will return to square $A_1$ and make sure that the arrows in the city are placed as in figure 12. But then you cannot come to the blue square, which is a contradiction conditions of the task.

b) Let's change the directions of all the arrows. The resulting motion scheme satisfies the conditions of the problem. Therefore, as shown in point a), with this arrangement of arrows you can get from any green square to the blue one. But this means that with the old arrangement of arrows, you can get from the blue square to any green one.

The problem of B. Rozenshtein about a city with one-way traffic can be generalized as follows. Let there be no “dead ends” in the city (in this case, you can arrange directions on the streets so that you can come to each square and leave from each). A necessary and sufficient condition is that with any such arrangement of arrows from each square one can get to any other; can be formulated as follows: *any two circular routes* (through a city on the streets of which there are no arrows yet) *must pass through the same square.* Prove this.
