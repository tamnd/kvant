⟦figure⟧

The photo shows two electrostatic machines from the collection of the State Museum of Natural History in Leiden (Netherlands).

On the right is an electrostatic machine made in 1788 in Amsterdam by John Cuthbertson. The diameter of the glass circles reaches 165 cm. The machine is connected to a Leyden jar. Cuthbertson's machine is interesting because it is the first electrostatic machine that was used not only to generate toy lightning. With its help, the electrolysis of water was carried out for the first time in 1799.

⟦column⟧

Modern demonstration electrostatic machines are not much different from Cuthbertson's machine.

On the left is an antique electrostatic machine. In it, the charge accumulated on a glass globe under a glass bell. When the air was pumped out, a charge was created under the bell.

The operation of electrostatic machines is based on the phenomenon of electrostatic induction. How charges are induced during electrification through influence is described in this issue of our magazine on p. **74**.
