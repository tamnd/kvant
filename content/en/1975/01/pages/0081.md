2. In order for the illumination of the central light spot on the wall to remain unchanged, it is necessary that the point source of light be at the focus of the collecting lens, that is, the length of the spring when the cart moves must be equal to $F$ (Fig. 1). Since $F < l_0$, the spring will be compressed, and the elastic force $F_{\mathrm{упр}} = k\,(l_0 - F)$, acting on the frame from the side of the compressed spring, will be directed to the right. Consequently, the acceleration of the frame, and therefore the trolley, is directed towards the wall, and the value of sg is determined from the equation of motion of the frame

$$ma = k\,(l_0 - F).$$

From here

$$a = \frac{k\,(l_0 - F)}{m} = 0{,}1\ \textit{м/с}^2.$$

3. The work $A$ of electrostatic forces is equal to the decrease in the potential energy of interaction of electric charges:

$$A = -\,(W_2 - W_1) = -\left(\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \Delta l} - 0\right) =$$
$$= -\,\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \Delta l}\,,$$

where $\Delta l$ is the movement of the ball on the spring, equal to the change in the length of the deformed spring (Fig. 2).

To determine $\Delta l$, we write down the equilibrium conditions of the ball on the spring at the initial and final moments (see Fig. 2, *a* and *b*):

$$k\,(\Delta l)_1 = mg,$$
$$k\,(\Delta l)_2 + F_{\mathrm{к}} = mg$$

$\left(F_{\mathrm{к}} = \dfrac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\,(\Delta l)^2}\right.$ is the Coulomb force of repulsion of electric charges$\left.\vphantom{\dfrac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}}\right)$. From here

$$\Delta l = (\Delta l)_1 - (\Delta l)_2 = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 k}}\,.$$

Substituting the resulting value $\Delta l$ c. expression for work $A$, we get

$$A = -\sqrt[3]{k\left(\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\right)^2} = -\,0{,}09\ \textit{дж}.$$
