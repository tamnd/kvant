**M261.** *A hoop of radius $R$, hanging on a stationary circle of radius $r<R$, begins to roll along this circle. Prove that a point on the hoop describes the same trajectory that would be described by a point on a wheel of radius $R-r$ rolling outside on the same circle of radius $r$ (Fig. 1). (Rolling occurs without sliding - so that the lengths of the arcs rolling over each other are equal.)*

Let us denote the radius of the wheel $R-r$ by $\rho$. The curves described during the indicated rolling by the points of the wheel boundary are called *epicycloids*, their type is determined by the relation $\dfrac{\rho}{r}$; curves described by the points of the hoop - *pericycloids*. Let us prove that with the relationship between the radii ($R=r+\rho$) specified in the condition, the pericycloids coincide with the epicycloids.

Let's fix one point on the wheel and on the hoop. Let at the initial moment the points observed on the wheel and the hoop coincide with the same point $A$ of the boundary of the fixed circle (Fig. 2). For definiteness, let both the wheel and the hoop roll in a circle counterclockwise. If at some moment the wheel touches a stationary circle at point $B$, then the point observed on its boundary (point of the epicycloid) occupies such a position $C$ that the lengths $\smile AB$ and $\smile BC$ are equal ($\smile BC$ is chosen taking into account the direction of rolling (Fig. 3)): this is the condition for rolling without sliding.

Similarly, the position of the point $C'$, observed on the hoop (pericycloid point), at the moment when it touches the fixed circle at the point $B'$, is found from the condition that the lengths $\smile AB'$ and $\smile B'C'$ are equal (taking into account the direction of rolling) (see figure 4).

Let us prove that for any point $B$ on the boundary of a fixed circle, it is possible to select a point $B'$ (also on the boundary of a fixed circle) in such a way that the corresponding points $C$ (epicycloids) and $C'$ (pericycloids) coincide (Fig. 5). (From our proof it will also be clear how to choose $B'$ by $B$.)

Let us take the point $B'$ so that the ratio of the lengths of the arcs $AB$ and $BB'$ is equal to $\dfrac{\rho}{r}$: then the radian measure $\smile BC$ is equal to the radian measure $\smile BB'$ - let’s denote it by $\varphi$. We have

dl. $\smile AB=$ long. $\smile BC=\rho\varphi$, length. $\smile BB'=r\varphi$.
