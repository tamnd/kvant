## All-Union competition of the “Knowledge” society
for the best works of popular science literature

The All-Union Society “Znanie” annually holds an open competition for the best popular science books and brochures on all areas of modern science and technology. The competition involves central and republican publishing houses that produce popular science literature: “Young Guard” and “Children’s Literature”, “Prosveshchenie” and “Radyanska Shkola”, “Atomizdat”, “Nauka”, “Naukova Dumka”, “Belarus” and dozens of other publishing houses. The purpose of the competition is to intensify the work of publishing houses in creating popular science literature for a wide range of readers, as well as for specialists interested in the development of related branches of science, and to improve the quality of published literature.

In 1974, about 400 books and brochures published in 1973 were submitted to the competition. The competition jury, chaired by Academician A.L. Yanshin, awarded the best of them with prizes and diplomas of encouragement. Among the recipients there are several books and brochures devoted to various problems of mathematics, physics and astronomy. We want to briefly tell our readers about them.

First degree diplomas were awarded to two yearbooks regularly published by the publishing house “Znanie” - “Science and Humanity” and “The Future of Science”. The first yearbook popularly talks about the greatest achievements of modern science. In 1973, it contained articles by academician A. N. Skrinsky “Physics of elementary particles and colliding beams”, academician S. N. Zhurkov “Physical foundations of strength”, Nobel Prize winner French physicist A. Kastler “Non-resonant interactions between atoms and electromagnetic radiation”, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Yu. N. Denisyuk “Holography”, doctor physical and mathematical sciences M. Ya. Marova “Modern ideas about Venus” and a number of others. The second yearbook is described in detail in this issue of our magazine (see p. 71). The same diploma was awarded to the team of authors

Kitaygorodsky, “Renix,” which was published in its second edition by the Molodaya Gvardiya publishing house, received the same diploma. In it, the author exposes the anti-scientific sensations that have penetrated the pages of newspapers and magazines in recent years.

The title of the book is borrowed from A.P. Chekhov. In the play “Three Sisters,” the gymnasium teacher Kulygin talks about this incident: “In some seminary, the teacher wrote “nonsense” on an essay, and the student read “renix” - he thought it was written in Latin...”
