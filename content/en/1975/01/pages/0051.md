**F273.** *To produce gases at ultra-high temperatures and pressures, an installation is sometimes used, consisting of a cylinder-barrel closed at one end and a piston-bullet flying into the cylinder from the open side. With good processing of the bullet and barrel, it is possible to achieve low gas leakage through the gap. Due to the very high temperatures, highly compressed gases can still be considered ideal under these conditions. Estimate the upper limit of the temperature of argon subjected to compression in such a setup if a bullet of mass $m = 100$ g flies into a barrel having a volume of $V_0 = 200$ cm$^3$ with an initial speed of $v = 250$ m/s. The initial temperature and pressure of the gas are $T_0 = 300^\circ$ K and $p_0 = 1$ atm, respectively.*

**F274.** *The diode is connected to the circuit shown in figure 18, a. The idealized current-voltage characteristic of the diode is shown in figure 18, b. The capacitor is not pre-charged. Key K is closed. How much heat will be released at resistance R when charging the capacitor? Capacitance of the capacitor C, e. d.s. source E. The internal resistance of the source is negligible.*

⟦column⟧

When a bullet decelerates, its kinetic energy turns into heat. To estimate the maximum temperature to which the gas in the cylinder can be heated, we will assume that all the released heat goes to change the internal energy of the gas. That is, we will not take into account energy losses due to heating of the bullet and cylinder walls. Then we can write

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \Delta U .$$

Since argon is a monatomic gas, then $U = \frac{3}{2} nRT$ ($n$ is the number of moles of gas) and

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2} nRT_{\max} - \frac{3}{2} nRT_0 = \frac{3}{2} nR (T_{\max} - T_0).$$

The value $n$ can be found from the equation of state of an ideal gas:

$$n = \frac{p_0 V_0}{R T_0} .$$

That's why

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2}\, p_0 V_0\, \frac{T_{\max} - T_0}{T_0} .$$

Hence,

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\, p_0 V_0\, \frac{T_{\max} - T_0}{T_0} ,$$

where

$$T_{\max} = T_0 \left( 1 + \frac{mv^2}{3 p_0 V_0} \right) \approx 30\,000\ ^\circ\mathrm{K} .$$

◆

After the key is closed, current will flow through the circuit. The voltage across the capacitor will increase and the current will correspondingly decrease. In this case, until the current decreases to the value $I_0$, the voltage on the diode will always be equal to $U_0$ \*). Let the current decrease to $I_0$ in time $t_1$. Let's find how much heat was released at resistance $R$ during this time.

According to the law of conservation of energy, the work $A$ performed by the source in the entire circuit is equal to the sum of the work on individual sections of the circuit:

$$A = A_{\mathrm{д}} + A_{\mathrm{c}} + A_R.$$

The voltage across the capacitor at time $t = t_1$ is

$$U_{\mathrm{c}} = E - U_0 - I_0 R,$$

this means that during the time $t_1$ a charge passed through the circuit

$$q_1 = C U_{\mathrm{c}} = C (E - U_0 - I_0 R).$$

The work done by the source during this time is

$$A_1 = q_1 E = CE (E - U_0 - I_0 R).$$

The work on the sections containing the diode and capacitor is equal

$$A_{\mathrm{д}} = q_1 U_0 = C U_0 (E - U_0 - I_0 R),$$

$$A_{\mathrm{c}} = W_{\mathrm{c}} = \frac{q_1^2}{2C} = \frac{C (E - U_0 - I_0 R)^2}{2} .$$
