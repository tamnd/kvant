Among the popular science brochures, a first-degree diploma and a cash prize was awarded to the brochure by Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B.V. Gnedenko “Conversations on the Theory of Queuing.” It contains eight short talks on various problems in this relatively young field of applied mathematics. Telephone communications, maintenance of machinery, operation of sea and river ports, emergency medical care - these are some examples of areas of application of this theory. It requires a significant change in the traditional methods of operation of many service sectors, opening up enormous opportunities for improving their activities.

The same award was awarded to another member of the editorial board of our journal, Professor Ya. A. Smorodinsky, for the popular science brochure “Particles, Quanta, Waves.” This is a popular story about quantum mechanics, as the author himself says in an address to the reader, a kind of walk along the main avenues of the huge city into which this science has turned.

Even though quantum mechanics will soon celebrate its 75th anniversary, it still retains its reputation as a very complex science, far removed from the physics that is written about in school textbooks. The fact is that the laws governing phenomena in the microworld are completely different from the laws with which we deal in the ordinary world. The laws of the microworld are discussed in this brochure. Well, anyone who, according to the author, is at least generally familiar with the school physics course, can read it.

A second degree diploma and a cash prize were awarded to the authors of the “Charles Babbage” brochure, R. S. Guter and Yu. L. Polunov. They wrote a biography of the creator of one of the first working computers, the outstanding English mathematician and inventor of the 19th century, Charles Babbage. Moreover, they wrote it in a very accessible, lively and exciting way, with great love for their hero.

All the brochures mentioned here were published by the Znanie publishing house.

Encouragement diplomas were awarded to Chairman of the Atomic Energy Committee A. M. Petrosyants and Vice-President of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Logunov for the brochure “High Energy Physics and Charged Particle Accelerators” (Znanie Publishing House), L. M. Pismen for the brochure “From Miracle to Number” (Pedagogika Publishing House) and a number of other authors of popular science brochures.

## The future of science

At the beginning of 1966, a small book with the attractive title “The Future of Science” appeared on bookstore shelves and was soon sold out. On the title page there is a subtitle: “Prospects. Hypotheses. Unsolved problems." And in the preface the following was written:

“It’s hard to find a person now who wouldn’t be interested in what science will give people in five, ten, or even a hundred years. The role of science in our lives is growing so rapidly that its future is becoming inseparable from the future of human society itself.

The yearbook “The Future of Science” is the first popular publication in our country entirely devoted to the prospects for development and unsolved problems of science. Leading Soviet and foreign scientists speak on its pages, sharing their thoughts on the ways of knowledge and technological progress, talking about new hypotheses, and the future victories of reason.

The participation of the most authoritative experts guarantees the maximum validity of the forecasts and assumptions made.

The yearbook is designed for a wide range of readers interested in the current state and prospects for the development of science.”

The first issue of the yearbook opened with an article by Academician I.E. Tamm, which popularly talked about the current state and prospects for the development of elementary particle physics.
