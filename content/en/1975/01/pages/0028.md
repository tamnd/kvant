But what is the *form of a sentence* (statement)? If statements or their individual “elementary parts” are designated, as is customary in modern logic, (like ordinary algebra) by letters, then the identity (or difference) of their form is usually not difficult to discern. For example, the sentences “From *A* and *B* follows *A*” and “From *C* and *F* follows *C*,” differing only in the designation of “component,” are naturally considered equal, which cannot be asserted (in any case, without additional clarifications and agreements), for example, about the sentences “From *X* follows *Y*” and “From not-*Y* follows not-*X*.” But when we compare sentences in ordinary language, things are not always so simple.
