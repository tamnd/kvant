# Where is the error

“The direct theorem is equivalent to the opposite theorem,” said student Krestikov. — If $\overline{A}\Rightarrow\overline{B}$ is a direct theorem, then along with it theorem $\overline{B}\Rightarrow\overline{A}$ is true or false.

— What are $\overline{B}$ and $\overline{A}$? - asked student Nulikov.

— This is how the negations of statements $B$ and $A$ are denoted.

- How do you know that they are equivalent?

- I can prove it.

- Let's.

- Please. Let theorem $A\Rightarrow B$ be true and theorem $\overline{B}\Rightarrow\overline{A}$ false. Then ver-

⟦column⟧

to Theorem $\overline{B}\Rightarrow A$. From this correct theorem and the correct direct theorem it follows that $\overline{B}\Rightarrow B$. You understand that this doesn’t happen! So, in fact, Theorem $\overline{B}\Rightarrow\overline{A}$ is true.

“I see,” said Nulikov.

They met the next day.

“I proved that the direct theorem implies the converse,” said Nulikov.

- This can’t be! It happens that the direct theorem is true, but the converse is not.

- And I’ll prove it to you!

- Ha! Try!

— Let the theorem $A\Rightarrow B$ be true. We need to prove that Theorem $B\Rightarrow A$ is also true.

⟦column⟧

Let's assume it's wrong. Then theorem $B\Rightarrow\overline{A}$ is true, you said it yourself last time. But Theorem $A\Rightarrow\overline{B}$ is equivalent to it, which means it is also true. It turned out that two such theorems are true: $A\Rightarrow B$ and $A\Rightarrow\overline{B}$, and this is nonsense. This means that Theorem $B\Rightarrow A$ is actually true.

“I don’t understand anything,” muttered Krestikov.

“I reasoned the same way as you,” said Nulikov.

Now answer

1. Is Krestikov's proof correct?

2. Is Nulikov's proof correct?

3. Is Nulikov's last statement true?
