John Napier's Computing Machine

John Napier, Baron Merchiston (1550-1617), a native of Scotland, inventor of logarithms and one of the inventors of decimal fractions (he introduced the sign separating the whole and fractional parts) is rightly considered the first of the outstanding English mathematicians.

These inventions have become partly shared by man in culture. Both decimal fractions and logarithms should now be mastered by every cultured person.

But John Napier also allows about his contemporaries, who are bad at multiplying and dividing multi-digit numbers. In the year of his death he published a book called "Rabdologia". This book outlines an ingenious method of mechanical multiplication and division using Napier's “bone tablets.” You can also make boards yourself from plywood or thick cardboard (see Fig.)

The well-known Pythagorean multiplication table is written on the ninth tablet. Each of the quadratic tables is divided diagonally into “upper” and “lower” triangles. In the bottom - the number of units of the product, in the top - the number of tens.
