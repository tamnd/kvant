Under this title, the publishing house “Znanie” published a brochure *) in 1972, containing two articles translated from English.

*) Mathematicians about mathematics (collection of articles). Compiled by Prof. V. I. Levin. Translation from English by V. N. Trostikov, Candidate of Philosophical Sciences. M., “Knowledge”, 1972. (New in life, science, technical Series “Mathematics, Knibernetics”, 12.)

These articles form a chapter entitled "The Unreasonableness of Mathematics" in the third volume of the four-volume anthology The World of Mathematics, published in the United States in 1956 and in England in 1960. The authors of the articles are famous scientists.

The first article is called "The Lost and Found Paradox" and is written by Edward Kasner and James R. Newmanham. It begins: “Perhaps the greatest paradox is that there are paradoxes in mathematics.”

The first type of paradoxes are contradictions and absurd statements that are the result of incorrect deception. Then - the paradoxes of the country, which seem incredible, but, as the structure of the evidence shows, are true. “The third and most important type of paradox is related to set theory; This type of paradox leads to a revision of the foundations of mathematics. Logical paradoxes have always meant logicians and mathematicians and raised gaps concerning the very essence of mathematics, gaps that to this day have not received a satisfactory solution.”

The articles first look at examples of paradoxes of the second type. Let's give one of them. Two equal coins lie on a table touching a circle. The first (empty left) coin, remaining on the plane of the table, rolls along the rim of the second (stationary) coin. When the point of contact of the coins passes half the circle and ends up at the diametrically opposite point of the stationary coin on the right, then the movable coin will make not a half turn, but a full turn (the number on it is again in its normal position)!

Speaking about geometric errors, the authors only mention optical errors and give examples of errors not of a psychological, but of a mathematical nature. For example, the following paradox is proposed (we give it in abbreviation): ABCD is a right angle (see figure). CH=HB; HO⊥CB; AE=AB; CK=KE; KO⊥CE.

A simple experiment to determine the energy of the explosion: in a calm impact, pieces of paper would fall at his feet, and when, a few seconds after the explosion, the front of the shock wave arrives, they will fall somewhat cymbantirammidal in the direction of propagation of the wave. The second article, “The Crisis of Intuition,” is by mathematician Hans Hahn; The article is devoted to the properties of intuition, its role in mathematics, its ability to generate paradoxes.
The author examines the role of intuition in geometry and shows that “further in that branch of mathematics, which is considered the main and original one, the role of intuition gradually fades away, so as not to finally disappear completely.”
For example, a curved line has been understood since ancient times as a geometric figure drawn by a moving point.
But the Italian mathematician and logician Giuseppe pe Peano proved that such a curve can include entire sections of the plane (a moving point can, for example, pass through every point of a square). Insert examples of curves that have and do not have “connectedness in the small” are given. An idea is given of the method proposed by the Dutch mathematician L. Brauz for constructing a map in which all boundary points will be ternary, of the idea of ​​determining the dimension of exact sets, of curves consisting entirely of branch points, the existence of which was proved by the Polish mathematician V. Serp and nsky.
The author of the article claims that general geometry is the highest manifestation of our intuition; this geometry alone, like other mathematical and physical concepts, is a logical structure.
