Theorem 5. If the heights of the orthocentric tera hedron ABCD intersect at one point, then the opposite edges of the tera hedron are mutually perpendicular. Such a terahedra is called orthocentric.

Let ABCD be an orthocentric tehedron, DG₁ its median, DH₁ its height (Fig. 8). Then G₁ is the centroid, and H₁ is the orthocenter of triangle ABC (prove this yourself - this is where the perpendicularity of the opposite edges of the terahedra is used), and points O₁ (the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle ABC), G₁ and H₁ lie on the same straight line. Note that the center O of the sphere circumscribed about the tera hedron ABCD lies on the perpendicular to the plane of the triangle ABC, restored at the point O₁.

We will prove the theorem in the same way as Theorem 2 for a triangle: construct a point H in different ways that satisfies (5).

First, add the vectors $\overrightarrow{OA}$, $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC}$:

$$\overrightarrow{OM} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC}.$$

By Theorem 1 $\overrightarrow{OG}_1 = \frac{1}{3}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC})$, therefore $\overrightarrow{OM} = 3\overrightarrow{OG}_1$ or $\overrightarrow{G}_1\overrightarrow{M} = 2\overrightarrow{OG}_1$. Points O₁, G₁, H₁ lie on the Euler line of triangle ABC, and $\overrightarrow{H}_1\overrightarrow{G}_1 = 2\overrightarrow{G}_1\overrightarrow{O}_1$. Hence,

$$\overrightarrow{OH} = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{OM} + \overrightarrow{OD}) = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} + \overrightarrow{OD})$$

It follows from this that straight lines H₁M and OO₁ are parallel, and since straight line OO₁ is perpendicular to plane ABC, then straight line H₁M is perpendicular to this plane. Consequently, point M lies on line DH₁ (if points O and O₁ coincide, then points M and H₁ also coincide).

Let theorel $\overrightarrow{OH} = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{OM} + \overrightarrow{OD}) = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} + \overrightarrow{OD})$ From the left equality it follows that point H is the midpoint of the segment DM, that is, point H lies at the altitude DH₁ of the terahedra.

Similarly, the point N is constructed: $\overrightarrow{ON} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OD}$ and also $\overrightarrow{H}:\overrightarrow{OH} = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{ON} + \overrightarrow{OC})$ and it is proved that the point H lies on the altitude of the terahedra drawn from the vertex C, etc.

Consequently, the heights of the orthocentric terahedra intersect at one point H, determined by the corresponding (5).

Theorem 6. The cenir O of the circumscribed sphere, the cenirod G and the orthocentric terahedra ABCD lie on the same straight line, and the points O and H are symmetrical with respect to the point G.

Proof. According to formulas (4) and (5)

$$\overrightarrow{OH} = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} + \overrightarrow{OD}),$$

$$\overrightarrow{OG} = \frac{1}{4}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} + \overrightarrow{OD}),$$

whence $\overrightarrow{OH} = 2\overrightarrow{OG}$. The resulting equality means that points O, G, H lie on the same line, and points O and H are symmetrical with respect to point G.
