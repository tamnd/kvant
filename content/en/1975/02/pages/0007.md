The second wave, having passed plates 2 and 4, is reflected from mirror 5 mounted on a trolley, returns to plate 2, is reflected from its lower surface and goes to the screen. Plate 4 is needed in order to equalize the optical paths of both waves. Indeed, the first wave passes through the plate 2 times, and the second wave only once.

As a result of the interference of two beams, an interference pattern appears on the screen, which is a series of dark and light stripes. When a cart with a mirror moves along an object whose length needs to be changed, the interference pattern will also seem to move across the screen. For example, when the cart moves a distance equal to half the wavelength of light used in the experiment, all dark bars on the screen will become light and vice versa (Fig. 2). Therefore, which point of the screen will be alternately dark or light as the cart moves, that is, “flashes” will appear one after another. Moreover, the number of “flashes” is equal to the number of waves that fit along the length of the object being changed. It is convenient to count the number of “flashes” at the central point of the interference pattern.
