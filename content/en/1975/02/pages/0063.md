Exercises
1 (Poisson problem). The famous French mathematician Simon Poisson (1781-1810) was proposed a problem in his youth. Having become interested in it, Poisson became interested in mathematics and devoted his entire life to this science. This is the task.

Someone has 12 pints of wine (a pint is a measure of volume) and wants to give half of this amount, but he does not have a vessel of 6 pints. He has two vessels: one of 8 pints, the other of 5 pints.

How do you pour 6 pints of wine into an 8 pint vessel? What is the minimum number of transfusions required?

2. There are 4 barrels. The first includes 24 buckets, the capacity of the second is 13 buckets, the third is 11 buckets, the fourth is 5 buckets. At first, only the first barrel is filled. You need to pour its contents into three equal parts so that the first three barrels contain 8 buckets each, and the fourth remains empty.

3 (Old problem). Three soldiers and three robbers must cross the river. They found a boat that only fits two people. You cannot leave more robbers on the shore than soldiers. How can all six of them cross the river? Find all possible ways.*)

---
*) It is allowed to leave only robbers or only soldiers on the shore.

## To the article “Arithmetic-geometric progression”
(see «Kvant», 1975, No. 1)

1. On figure 1 there are shown the acute-angled triangle $A_nB_nC_n$ and the triangle $A_{n+1}B_{n+1}C_{n+1}$ circumscribed around it. Let us express the angle $A_{n+1}$ through the angle $A_n$. The radii $B_nO_n$ and $C_nO_n$ of the circumcircle of the triangle $A_nB_nC_n$ are perpendicular to the sides $A_{n+1}C_{n+1}$ and $A_{n+1}B_{n+1}$ of the triangle $A_{n+1}B_{n+1}C_{n+1}$ and form an angle $B_nO_nC_n$ equal to $2\hspace{-2pt}\not\preceq A_n$; That's why

$$\not\preceq A_{n+1} = \not\preceq B_nA_{n+1}C_n = \pi - \angle B_nO_nC_n = \pi - 2\not\preceq A_n,$$

that is

$$\not\preceq A_{n+1} = -2\not\preceq A_n + \pi.$$

According to formula (2) (p. 37) we find

$$\not\preceq A_{n+1} = (-2)^n \cdot \left(\not\preceq A_1 - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) + \frac{\pi}{3}.$$

Accordingly we will find

$$\not\preceq B_{n+1} = (-2)^n \cdot \left(\not\preceq B_1 - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) + \frac{\pi}{3},$$

$$\not\preceq C_{n+1} = (-2)^n \cdot \left(\not\preceq C_1 - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) + \frac{\pi}{3}.$$
