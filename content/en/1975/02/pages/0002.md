The top photo shows Napier's bone tablets in a copper box, made in 1617, the same year they were depicted. This is a kind of computing device, the operating principle of which uses the very id properties of the Pythagorean Multiplication Table, presenting it in a “detachable version”. Using John Napier's image, by moving the "tablets" you can multiply, divide integers, and even extract square roots. Multiplication is performed especially simply and practically, thanks to the “tablets”, it is replaced by addition. Therefore, contemporaries highly valued “bone tablets”. The shortcomings of the device, including the fact that its components are easy to see, were addressed later.

This device is part of the collection of the Paris Museum of Applied Arts. How to use Napier's computing device is described in «Kvant» No. 2, p. 42.
