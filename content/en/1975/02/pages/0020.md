⟦folio 18⟧

Diffraction (structural) coloration of birds, butterflies and beetles is very common in nature. A wide variety of shades of diffraction colors is characteristic of peacocks, pheasants, black storks, hummingbirds, butterflies of the tropical zone and many species of beetles (among them stag beetles, weevils, leaf beetles, etc.). The diffraction coloring of animals was studied not only by biologists, but also by physicists, for example Rayleigh, Wood, Michelson.

The outer surface of the plumage of many birds and the upper body cover of butterflies and beetles is characterized by regular repetition of structural elements with a period of one to several microns. In some animals, similar structures can cover the body in several layers, forming a multi-story (volumetric) diffraction grating.

Along with diffraction, animals can also have purely interference coloring, which forms on thin and dense outer covers (wedge-shaped in cross-section along the main axis of symmetry) with a thickness of optical activity of a micron up to several microns.

In Figures 1 and 2 you see photographs of the wing of a butterfly and the back of an Asian leaf beetle (areas with diffraction coloring are selected). Images 1, 2, 3 were taken using a microscope with a hundredfold magnification. Side lighting was used to identify structural elements. The photographs clearly show the repetition of structural elements.

The color of the surface of the surface can change depending on the angle at which the light falls on the surface of the grating, how the light rays are oriented in relation to the elements of the structure, and at what angle the surface is viewed.

Obviously, the color of the surface is associated with the reflection of rays. Let's consider the number of diffractions in reflected light. Let the rays of light fall on the grating at an angle $\theta_0$ and reflect from it at an angle $\theta$ (Fig. 4). The difference in the path of rays 1 and 2 is equal to

$$\Delta = \Delta_2 - \Delta_1 = d \sin \theta_0 - d \sin \theta,$$ (1)

or

$$\Delta = 2d \cos \frac{\theta_0 + \theta}{2} \sin \frac{\theta_0 - \theta}{2}.$$ (2)

Since for diffraction maxima $\Delta = k\lambda$ ($k = 1, 2, \dots$), then

$$k\lambda = 2d \cos \frac{\theta_0 + \theta}{2} \sin \frac{\theta_0 - \theta}{2}.$$ (3)

$$\lambda = (\theta_0 - \theta) d \cos \theta_0.$$ (4)
