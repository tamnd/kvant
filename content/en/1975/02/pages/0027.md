The straight line on which the points $O$, $G$, $H$ lie can be called *the Euler straight line of an orthocentric tetrahedron.*

Exercises

Use the scalar product of vectors to solve problems 1-4.

1. a) Prove that if $O$ is the center of the circle circumscribed by triangle $ABC$ and $\overrightarrow{OH} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC}$, then $H$ is the point of intersection of the altitudes of the triangle.

b) Prove that if $O$ is the center of a sphere circumscribed about the orthocentric tetrahedron $ABCD$, and $\overrightarrow{OH} = {}^{1}\!/_{2}(\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} + \overrightarrow{OD})$, then $H$ is the intersection point of the four altitudes of the tetrahedron.

2. Prove that if $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ are four arbitrary points in space, then

$\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{CD} + \overrightarrow{BC} \cdot \overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{CA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BD} = 0.$

Using this equality, prove that

a) three altitudes of a triangle intersect at one point;

b) if the opposite edges $AB$ and $CD$, $AD$ and $BC$ of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ are perpendicular, then the edges $CA$ and $BD$ are also perpendicular.

3. Prove that if $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ are four arbitrary points in space, then

$$|AB|^2 + |CD|^2 - |AD|^2 - |BC|^2 = 2\overrightarrow{AC} \cdot \overrightarrow{DB}.$$

Using this equality, prove that

a) the sum of the squares of the diagonals of a trapezoid is equal to the sum of the squares of its lateral sides added to twice the product of the bases;

b) the diagonals of a quadrilateral are perpendicular if and only if the sums of the squares of opposite sides are equal;

⟦column⟧

c) for a tetrahedron to be orthocentric, it is necessary and sufficient that the sums of the squares of its opposite edges be equal.

4. Prove that all plane angles at one vertex of an orthocentric tetrahedron are either acute, right, or obtuse and at least one of its faces is an acute triangle.

5. a) Prove that the points symmetrical to the orthocenter $H$ of the triangle $ABC$ relative to the midpoints of its sides, as well as points symmetrical to the orthocenter $H$ relative to the lines $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$, lie on the circumcircle of this triangle.

b) Let $H$ be the orthocenter of the tetrahedron $ABCD$, $G_1$ and $H_1$ the centroid and orthocenter of the face $ABC$, respectively, and $G_2$ and $H_2$ the points such that $\overrightarrow{HG_2} = 3\overrightarrow{HG_1}$ and $\overrightarrow{HH_2} = 3\overrightarrow{HH_1}$. Prove that the points $G_2$ and $H_2$ lie on the sphere circumscribed about this tetrahedron.

6. a) Prove that the midpoints of the three sides, the bases of the three altitudes of the triangle and the midpoints of the segments connecting the orthocenter with the three vertices lie on the same circle, and the center of this circle (called *the circle of ten points*) lies on the Euler line.

b) Prove that the centroids of the four faces, the bases of the four altitudes of the orthocentric tetraerd and the points that divide each of the segments connecting the orthocenter with the vertices, in the ratio 1:2 (counting from the orthocenter), lie on the same sphere, and the center of this sphere (called *sphere of twelve points*) is located on the Euler straight line.

7. Prove that

a) if the base of one of the altitudes of the tetrahedron is the orthocenter of the corresponding face, then the tetrahedron is orthocentric;

b) the base of any of the altitudes of an orthocentric tetrahedron is the orthocenter of the corresponding face (these properties were used in the proof of Theorem 5).
