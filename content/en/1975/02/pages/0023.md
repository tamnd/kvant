Theorem 1. The medicines of the transient $ABC$ intersect at one point $G$ and divide it in a ratio of 2:1, counting from the vertex, and
$$3\overrightarrow{PG} = \overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{PB} + \overrightarrow{PC},$$
(2)
food $P$ - any point of the plane, or space.
Proof. On the median $CD$ of the triangle $ABC$ we take the point $G$, determined by the correspondence $|CG| : |GD| = 2:1$ (Fig. 3). According to formula (1),
$$\overrightarrow{PG} = \frac{\overrightarrow{PC} + 2\overrightarrow{PD}}{3},$$
$$\overrightarrow{PD} = \frac{1}{2} (\overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{PB}),$$
where
$$\overrightarrow{PG} = \frac{1}{3} (\overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{PB} + \overrightarrow{PC}).$$
By calculating the vector $\overrightarrow{PG}$ with its end at the point $G'$ dividing either of the other two medians of the triangle in the ratio 2:1 (counting from the vertex), we obtain the same expression on the right:
$$\overrightarrow{PG'} = \frac{1}{3} (\overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{PB} + \overrightarrow{PC}),$$
pose $\overrightarrow{PG'} = \overrightarrow{PG}$, and the point $G'$ coincides with the point $G$. Consequently, all three medians of the triangle intersect at one point $G$, determined by correspondence (2).
