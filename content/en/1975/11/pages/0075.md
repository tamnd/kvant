Have you ever thought about what a point and a straight line are? At first glance, everything is clear here: a point is a point, a straight line is a straight line, what could be incomprehensible here? Well, but still, how can one explain this to someone who doesn’t know this at all and, moreover, understands everything very literally? Is it really that simple? It turns out not at all! This is a fantastic story that happened in one school.

1. Marcinin studies “terrestrial” geometry

A Marsinin boy, Inko, flew to one of the Moscow schools. When he got to the geometry lesson, he didn’t understand anything. The guys decided to help him, explain what was what. Vasya took charge of this. And he had to start... from a point. They lightly touched the board with chalk, Vasya says:

- This is the point. And this is a dot, and this is also a dot,” he continues, leaving tiny traces of chalk on the blackboard. “Do you understand?” Further…

“Wait, wait,” says the Marsinin. “I need to get these dots smoothed out.” And he takes out a microcosplay. “So, the dot is a bunch of white matter.” Interesting! We call it differently... And if this substance is green, will it be dots? And what size can these piles be?

“A point has no dimensions,” says Vasya.

- Why not? You yourself said that the piles of white matter are dots! - Inko answers.

Other students hand Marsinin sheets of paper with traces of a pencil touch, with pin holes.

- These are placers of graphite! This is an equal answer on paper! - Inko answers, looking into the microcosplay.

Vasya is at a loss. The rest too. “Clear” and “understandable” became unclear not only for an alien from another planet.
