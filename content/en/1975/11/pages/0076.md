Then Vasya takes a ruler and uses it to draw a line.

- It's straight! - he says. “It consists of dots.”

Having carefully examined the “straight line”, the Martian says:

- A straight line is a ridge made of placers of chalk or graphite. - The class is upset. What to do?

- Maybe this Inko is a loser? - some of you are thinking. Don't rush to conclusions! A Martian saves the day. “When here on Mars they want to talk about something new, they name its properties.” What properties do your straight lines and points have?

— These properties are indicated in our textbook! - the sixth grader will answer this. - And they call them axioms!

— At the end of the 8th grade textbook, all these axioms are listed! - the eighth grader will add. - Let's use them!

What's left to do? We must agree. Let's start reading the axioms.

**2. Axioms of belonging**

A k s i o m a $I_1$. *Each straight line is a set of points.*

A k s i o m a $I_2$. *For any two points that are distinct from each other, there is one and only one line containing them.*

A k s i o m a $I_3$. *There is at least one line, and each line has at least one point.*

**3. Points and lines, which are described by the axioms of belonging**

After reading the axiom $I_1$, Inko perked up.

“Wait,” he said. “What could we mean by a point and a straight line if there were no other properties?” This axiom does not say what a point is. This means that by points you can mean anything! For example, the numbers 1, 2, 3. What are the straight lines?

what will we have then? Let's find them together.

Together we found straight lines $\{1\}$, $\{2\}$, $\{3\}$, $\{1,\ 2\}$, $\{1,\ 3\}$, $\{2,\ 3\}$, $\{1,\ 2,\ 3\}$.

- Then there is another straight line! - one of you exclaims. This is the empty set—$\varnothing$! After all, axiom $I_1$ does not say that a set of points that is a line cannot be empty.

There is noise in the class. What are these straight lines? There are infinitely many points on a straight line, but here there are one, two, three, and even none!

The unperturbed Martian listens to the argument. Then he says:

— Are you confused that I called the numbers 1, 2, 3 points, and the listed sets, including the empty one, are straight lines? And this doesn’t surprise me at all. Apparently, the fact is that you also know other properties of points and lines, but I don’t know them. Therefore, I will call those “points” and “straight lines” that are described by a single axiom $I_1$: “point one-one”, “straight line one-one” and write: point $(I_1)$, straight line $(I_1)$. Whether other axioms hold for the point $(I_1)$ and line $(I_1)$ does not interest me. Let’s come up with more examples of points $(I_1)$ and lines $(I_1)$,” suggests Inko. There was silence.
