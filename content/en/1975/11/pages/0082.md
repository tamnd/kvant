**2.** Let's compile Table 2 for odd numbers, in which: the first line is the possible sum of two odd digits, the second line is the remainder of dividing this sum by nine, the next five lines are numbers that, when added to the first digit of the line (thousands digit), give the above amount, and the last line is the number of possible pairs of digits that give this sum. A similar table is compiled for even numbers.

Then, combining and ordering the results of these tables, we complete the summary table 3.

When compiling this table, the remainders from dividing the sums of even digits by 9 are entered in such columns that, when added to the remainders from the sums of odd digits, they give nine.

The last row of the table is the product of the number of pairs in a given column, since any pair of odd digits can be associated with any pair of even digits.

The sum of the products gives the desired answer: 64 - the number of different sets of digits of the four-digit number LFNP, the sum of which is divisible by nine.
