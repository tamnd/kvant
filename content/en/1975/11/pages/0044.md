F329 *). Many of you have probably noticed that the moment you step on wet sand, it becomes lighter. This is due to the fact that the sand becomes dry. But as soon as you remove the nose, the trail left by the nose immediately fills with water. Explain this phenomenon.

To explain what happens to sand on the river bank, let's start with... balls. Identical balls can be placed on a plane so that each of them touches six other balls. In the holes between the balls of the first layer you can put the balls of the second layer. Each of them will touch three balls of the lower layer and six neighbors of its layer, etc. The arrangement of balls obtained in this way is called a close packing of balls. If you break the tight packing by moving the balls of one of the holes between the balls of the lower layer, the gaps between the balls will increase. The volume of the entire system will also increase. This means that if a system of densely packed balls is subject to forces that break the tight packing, the volume of the system increases due to an increase in the spaces between the balls.

Any granular medium behaves similarly. Take, for example, millet (or coffee), fill a glass with it, shaking the glass slightly so that the grains are arranged, forming the densest possible package. Then press down on the millet. Pressure will lead to an increase in the volume occupied by the grains, that is, to a violation of the tight packing. Some of the grains will spill out. If you now lightly move around the glass so that the grains are again “packed” as tightly as possible, the glass turns out to be not filled to the top.

Now let's return to the sand on the shore. It is also tightly packed. When pressure is applied to the sand, the dense packing is destroyed, and the volume of the sand increases due to the increase in the space between the sands. Water from the upper layers of sand goes deeper, filling these enlarged gaps. The sand seems to be drying up. When the foot is removed, the dense packing is restored, and the water displaced from the newly reduced spaces is filled with the trace left by the foot.

N. Sh. Slovodechkiy
