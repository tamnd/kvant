⟦folio 36⟧

This country lost no more than half of its gold.

A country that is $k > 0$ from a rich country must border at least one country that is $k-1$ from a rich country, and therefore cannot border more than six countries that are $k+1$ from a rich country. This means that the number of countries located at a distance of $k+1$ from a “rich” country exceeds the number of countries located at a distance of $k$ by no more than 6 times. The countries located at a distance of 1 from the “rich” country are no more than 7. From the above it follows that the number of countries located at a distance of $k$ from the “rich” country does not exceed the number $7 \cdot 6^{k-1}$. Having distributed to each country located at a distance of $k$ no more than $7 \cdot 6^{k-1}$ gold (recall that the “rich” country after the distribution “received” a gold reserve of $M$), we will spend no more gold than

$$M + 7 \cdot \frac{M}{13} + 7 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{M}{13^2} + \dots = M + \frac{7}{6} M \left[ \frac{6}{13} + \left( \frac{6}{13} \right)^2 + \dots \right] = 2M.$$

This means that initially the “rich” country had no more than 2$M$ gold, and thus, after the described redistribution, it lost no more than half of its gold. The fact that the described redistribution is required follows from the fact that two countries bordering each other $B$ and $C$ lie either at the same distance from the third country $A$, or these distances differ by one. This is a special case of the “triangle inequality”:

$$\rho (A, B) + \rho (B, C) \geq \rho (A, C)$$

at $\rho (B, C) = 1$ ($\rho (A, B)$ is the distance from $A$ to $B$); prove this "triangle inequality" for our distance in general.

Let us also note that if at a distance of $k$ from the “rich” country there are exactly $7 \cdot 6^{k-1}$ countries (see Fig. 1), then the gold reserve that needs to be redistributed is exactly equal to 2$M$, and therefore $M$ is equal to half of the original name of the “rich” country’s gold. $$\sum_{k} M_k \cdot 13 - \rho (A_i, A_k)$$ gold, we get the required distribution. In fact, firstly, each country $A_i$ receives at least $M_i$ gold, and, therefore, loses no more than half of its stock by virtue of the choice $M_i$. Secondly, if $A_i$ and $A_j$ are bordering countries, then $\rho (A_i, A_j) = 1$.
