Ampere's new ideas were not understood by all scientists. Some of his named colleagues, including Bio, did not agree with him. By speaking out against the existence of magnetism as an independent phenomenon, Ampere thereby crossed out Biot’s theory, according to which a magnet consists of a set of elementary microsoptic magnets, each of which is similar in its properties to a large magnet. This theory essentially did not explain anything; it only transferred the question of the nature of magnetism from a large piece of magnetized metal to its tiny parts. Biot and his supporters sought to prove that there was nothing fundamentally new in Ampere’s experiments, that all of them, done every day, were just variations of Oersted’s experiment.

Contemporaries say that after Ampere’s first report on the interaction of conductors with current, the next episode will occur. “What, exactly, is new in what you told us? - one of his opponents asked Ampere. “It goes without saying that if two currents have an effect on a magnetic needle, then they also have an effect on each other.” Ampera did not immediately find an answer to this return. But then Arago came to the rescue. He took two keys out of his pocket and said: “Each of them also has an effect on the arrow, but they have no effect on each other, and therefore your conclusion is wrong. Ampere discovered essentially a new phenomenon, much more significant than the discovery of Professor Oersted, whom I respect.”

Despite the attacks of his scientific opponents, Ampera continued his experiment. He decided to find the law of interaction of currents in the form of a strict mathematical formula and found this law, which theoretically bears his name. In the most general form, the expression for the magnitude of the force acting on the side of a small segment of conductor $\Delta l_1$, through which current flows with force $I_1$ (the first current element), on an arbitrarily located segment $\Delta l_2$ of another conductor with current $I_2$ (second current element), has the following form:

$$F_{12} = k \frac{I_1 \Delta l_1 \sin \alpha_1 I_2 \Delta l_2 \sin \alpha_2}{r_{12}^2}.$$

Here $r_{12}$ is the absolute value of the vector $r_{12}$ directed from the first segment to the second (see Fig. 4). The direction of vectors $\Delta l_1$ and $\Delta l_2$ coincides with the directions of currents flowing through them; $\alpha_1$ is the angle between vectors $\Delta l_1$ and $r_{12}$, $\alpha_2$ is the angle between vectors $\Delta l_2$ and $n$ - perpendicular to the plane containing $\Delta l_1$ and $r_{12}$. The direction $n$ coincides with the forward movement of the gimlet as he rotates his arm from $\Delta l_1$ to $r_{12}$. $k$ - coefficient, depends on the choice of unit system. The force $F_{12}$ is perpendicular to the vector $\Delta l_2$ and lies in the plane containing $\Delta l_1$ and $r_{12}$.
