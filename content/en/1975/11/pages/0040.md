Let's combine the last four controls in the system. Solving it and taking into account that $r = r_1 + r_2$, we find

$$r = \frac{T}{2\pi} (v_1 + v_2);$$

$$m_1 = \frac{(v_1 + v_2)^2 v_2 T}{2\pi\gamma};$$

$$m_2 = \frac{(v_1 + v_2)^2 v_1 T}{2\pi\gamma}.$$

The pressure under the curved spherical surface of the liquid differs from the gas pressure above it by $\Delta p = \frac{2\sigma}{r}$, where $r$ is the radius of the sphere.

In the case of complete wetting of the tube walls with water, the menses in the narrow and wide elbows can be taken as hemispheres of radii $r_1 = d_1/2$ and $r_2 = d_2/2$. Consequently, if the menscus is not located at one end of the corresponding capillary, the air pressure above the liquid in a narrow bend is greater than the water pressure below the surface by $\Delta p_1 = 4\sigma/d_1 \approx 28,6$ cm of water. cm, and in the wide knee - by $\Delta p_2 = 4\sigma/d_2 \approx 14,3$ cm of water. see. Therefore, the difference in levels of such menasks (see Fig. 3) can be equal to

$$\Delta h = \frac{\Delta p_1}{\rho g} - \frac{\Delta p_2}{\rho g} \approx 14,3 \text{ cm}.$$

In our case $l < \Delta h$. This means that at least one of the meniscus is located at the end of the knee.

When the ends of the U-shaped tube are simultaneously in contact with the surface of the water, the rise of the liquid in the narrow capillary will lead to such an increase in the air pressure in the tube that it will come out (bubble) through the end of the wide capillary.

Thus, the water in a narrow capillary must reach the very top, where a change of radius $R_1 > r_1$ is formed. Therefore, the tube must be immersed in liquid for the entire length $l$.

At the lower end of a wide tube, the menask may have a radius of $R_2 \geq r_2$. At $R_2 = r_2$ the air pressure in the immersed tube is maximum and equal to

$$\rho_{\text{max}} = \rho_{\text{atm}} = \rho g l + \frac{2\sigma}{r_2}.$$

The bubbling will continue until the pressure mass is reduced enough that its pressure becomes $\rho_{\text{max}}$ or slightly less. The meniscus of radius $R_2 \geq r_2$ in a wide elbow will be located at the lower end of the capillary, that is, at a depth $l$.

If the ends of the tube do not touch the surfaces of the water at the same time, then bubbling will not occur completely or partially. In this case, the answer to the problem will be different.
