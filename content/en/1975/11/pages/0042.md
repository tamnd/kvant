The spoke and spring fastened together can be considered as two springs connected in series with stiffnesses $k_1$ and $k_2$. Let's notice them as one spring and express its stiffness $k$ through the values ​​$k_1$ and $k_2$. Since the total deformation of two series-connected springs is equal to the sum of their deformations, and the elastic forces generated in the springs coincide in absolute value, then $x_0$ total = $x_1 + x_2$, or $\frac{F_{\text{упр}}}{k} = \frac{F_{\text{упр}}}{k_1} + \frac{F_{\text{упр}}}{k_2}$. Therefore, $k = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$, and $v = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k_1 k_2}{(k_1 + k_2) m}}$.

Expressing $k_1$ and $k_2$ from equalities (1) and (2), respectively, we obtain the final

$$v = \frac{v_1 v_2}{\sqrt{v_1^2 + v_2^2}}.$$

F326. The connection, closed with a lid, is filled to the top with rigidity, in which there are two springs. How will the pressure in the liquid change if the springs merge? The initial pressure in the liquid is $p_0$, the tension surface coefficient is $\sigma$, the radius of each spring is $r_0$. Consider the process isometric.

Let us assume that the system under consideration is in a state of weightlessness. This is equivalent to the fact that there is no hydrostatic pressure, and therefore the pressure in the liquid is always the same. Let us write down the equilibrium condition for each spring before merging:

$$p_0 + \frac{2\sigma}{r_0} = p_r,$$

where $\frac{2\sigma}{r_0}$ is the additional pressure caused by the merging of the surface tension of the liquid, $p_r$ is the gas pressure in the spring.

After merging two springs into one, the gas pressure will not change, since the volume occupied by the gas remains constant, and the additional pressure will change, since the radius of the spring becomes different. Radnus $r$ of the new spring will be found from the condition for constructing the volume:

$$2 \frac{4}{3} \pi r_0^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3.$$

From here

$$r = r_0 \sqrt[3]{2}.$$

The equilibrium condition for this spring can be represented as

$$p + \frac{2\sigma}{r} = p_r$$

(p is the new pressure in the liquid). Then from (1) - (3) the change in pressure in the liquid is equal to

$$\Delta p = p - p_0 = \frac{2\sigma}{r_0} \left(1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2}}\right).$$

Try to solve this problem yourself for the case of springs of different radii.
