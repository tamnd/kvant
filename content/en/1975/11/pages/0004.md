This year marks the 200th anniversary of the birth of the great French scientist André-Marie Ampère. In the history of science, he is known mainly as the founder of electrodynamics. Meanwhile, he was a universal scientist, with merits in the fields of mathematics, chemistry, biology, and even linguistics and physiology. He was a lecturer's mind, amazing with his encyclopedic knowledge all the people who were close to him. But the name of Ampere is most firmly entrenched in the history of physics, which is reflected in such terms as “ampere” - for units of current, “Ampere’s law”, “Ampere’s force”, etc.

Ampere himself was a great master at inventing new scientific terms. It was he who introduced such words as “electrostatics”, “electrodynamics”, “solenide” into the everyday life of scientists. It is also interesting to note that, while dealing with the important problem of learning - the issue of classification of sciences, Ampere expressed the idea that in the future, it is believed, a new science will emerge about the general laws of control processes. He proposed precisely this as “cybernetics”.

Ampere's prediction came true. Currently, such a science exists and bears the name he proposed.

Andre-Marie Ampère traces her ancestry back to linguistic craftsmen. His father Jean-Jacques Ampere, together with his brothers, traded linguistic silks. He received a good education, had a good command of ancient languages ​​and tried to keep up with everything that the great French educators of the 18th century taught. He had an excellent library composed of works by famous philosophers, writers and scientists.
