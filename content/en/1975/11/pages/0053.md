⟦folio 51⟧

Let us give another example of a physical analogy between mechanical and electrical systems. The processes occurring in the electrical oscillatory circuit (Fig. 1) are similar to external oscillations of a weight.

This analogy is discussed in detail in the school textbook (see the textbook for the 10th grade of high school). Systems are similar because the phenomena occurring in them are described by the same mathematical relationships. Newton's second law for an external load and Ohm's law for an oscillatory circuit are written in the same way:

$$m \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = -kx, \quad L \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} = -\frac{1}{C} q,$$

where $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ is the speed of the load, $i = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t}$ is the current in the circuit.

From this it can be seen that the mass of the load $m$ can be compared with the inductance of the coil $L$, the stiffness of the spring $k$ - with the inverse value of the capacitance $\frac{1}{C}$. The analogy of these two systems is also preserved in the presence of energy dissipation: the friction coefficient $\beta$ at low speeds of body motion in a liquid or gas is similar to the electrical resistance $R$.

Let us now consider several specific problems, the solutions of which are simplified if we use the analogue method.

Task 1. The clearing is partially filled with liquid. The cutting section is the same everywhere; the total length of the filled part of the cabin is equal to 1. Determine the period of oscillation of the liquid level in this system.

Let's try to check analogues with the well-known problem of oscillations of a load suspended from the outside, and thus find the period of oscillations of this oscillatory system.

Let the liquid level in one of the elbows decrease by $x$, and in the other, correspondingly, increase by $x$ (see Fig. 2), then the difference in levels will be equal to $2x$, and the liquid in the left elbow will be affected by the uncompensated gravity force of a liquid column with a height of $2x$:

$$F = -2S\rho gx.$$

Here $S$ is the cross-sectional area of ​​the cutting, $\rho$ is the density of the liquid. The minus sign in this formula means that the force $F$ tends to return the system to the equilibrium position (as in the case of an external load). The value $2S\rho g$, constant for this system, corresponds to the spring stiffness $k$. Thus, the movement of the fluid occurs under the action of quasi-elastic *) motion. Task analogy:

*) This is the customary name for forces of inelastic origin that change according to the same law as an elastic spring: $F = -kx$.
