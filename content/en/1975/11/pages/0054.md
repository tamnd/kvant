$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{Sl\rho}{2S\rho g}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{2g}}.$$

Now we will formulate several problems from various branches of physics and try to understand the analogy between them.

Problem 2. Determine the work expended on the deformation of the stiffness spring $k$, if its elongation (counting from the equilibrium position of the unstretched spring) changed from $x_1$ to $x_2$.

Problem 3. How much work must be done to change the contaminant of the capacitor embone $C$ from the value $q_1$ to the value $q_2$?

Problem 4. One mole expands ideally when heated, mak that the volume is related to temperature according to the law $T = \alpha V^2$, where $\alpha$ is a non-variable constant. What work will be done by the gas in this process when its temperature changes from $T_1$ to $T_2$?

The systems discussed in these problems are depicted in figure 3.

The tasks are similar, since in all three cases we are faced with the same patterns. Let's look at this in more detail.

In problem 2 you need to find the work done on the deformation of the spring. This work can be determined graphically. Work on a small section of the path $\Delta x$ (i.e. on such a section that the force $F = kx$ can be considered constant) is:

$$\Delta A = F\Delta x = kx\Delta x.$$

This means that the total work $A$ when the coordinates change from $x_1$ to $x_2$ is equal to the shaded area on figure 4, i.e.

$$A = \frac{k}{2} \left( x_2^2 - x_1^2 \right).$$

Let us recall that the quantities $\frac{kx^2}{2}$ are the potential energy $\Pi$ of an elastically deformed spring. Knowing the expression for this energy, we could immediately write the formula for work ($A = \Delta \Pi$). But here we want to make sure that a similar situation will arise in other physical analogous problems.

When solving problem 3, the waste process in small portions $\Delta q$ (such that the potential difference $u$ on the capacitor can be considered constant) is transferred from one plate to another. The work of transferring the cost $\Delta q$ will be written as:

$$\Delta A = u\Delta q = \frac{1}{C} q\Delta q.$$
