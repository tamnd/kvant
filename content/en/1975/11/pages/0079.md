The equations of motion for such systems are the same.

Ohm's law for an electrical circuit:
$$R \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} + \frac{q}{C} = 0,$$ where $\frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = i$ is the current in the circuit.

Newton's second law for a mechanical system:
$$\beta \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} + kx = 0,$$ where $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = v$ is the speed of the body.

The systems are dynamic similar: $q \leftrightarrow x$; $\frac{1}{RC} \leftrightarrow \frac{k}{\beta}$.

It is interesting to note that other analogues can be thought of. Let us consider, for example, the horizontal motion of a ball of mass $m$, which has a certain initial speed, in a viscosity liquid with a viscosity coefficient $\beta$. Newton's second law for a ball will be written as:

$$ma = -\beta v,$$ or $m \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} + \beta v = 0$.

We have arrived at an equation of the same type as the previous ones. This system is also a dynamic electrical circuit, consisting of $R$ and $C$, only now $q \leftrightarrow v$;

$$\frac{1}{RC} \leftrightarrow \frac{\beta}{m}$$.
