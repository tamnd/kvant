All this improved his financial situation and brought some comfort, but not for long. The second marriage was very unsuccessful; his new wife, Zhenini Poto, turned out to be a weighty and limited person. Amperaril made a lot of efforts to somehow reconcile with her in the name of the daughter born from this marriage. However, these efforts were in vain. New ones were added to the experiences on this basis - in 1809, Amperara’s mother died, whose boundless devotion and kindness he felt all the years. These sad events could not but affect his scientific activity. Nevertheless, between 1809 and 1814 Amperara published several valuable works on series theory.

Only one pleasant event of this time consoled Amperar. In 1813, the famous English chemist Davy came to France for scientific purposes, accompanied by his assistant Faraday. A meeting took place between Davy and Amperar at which mainly chemical problems were discussed. Amperar outlined some of his new ideas to the famous scientist, which he perceived with interest and understanding.

The heyday of Amperard's scientific activity dates from 1814-1824 and is associated mainly with the Academy of Sciences, among whose members he was elected on November 28, 1814 for his services in the field of mathematics.

What scientific problems worried Amperar at this time? Almost until 1820, his main research interests were problems of mathematics, mechanics and chemistry. At this time he dealt very little with issues of physics: only two works of this period are known, those studied in optics and the molecular-kinetic theory of gases. As for mathematics, it was in this area that he achieved results that gave rise to his candidacy for the Academy in the mathematics department.

Amperard always considered mathematicians as a powerful apparatus for solving various applied problems in physics and technology. Already his first published mathematical work, devoted to the theory of probability, was essentially of an applied nature and was called “Messages on the Mathematical Theory of Games” (1802). Questions of probability theory interested him in the future.

In the study of many problems in physics and mechanics, the so-called differential equations in arbitrary parts are of great importance. The solution of such equations is associated with significant mathematical difficulties, which the greatest mathematicians worked to overcome. Amperard also made his contribution to mathematical physics, as this branch of science is called. In 1814 alone, he completed several scientists. His achievements in the field of chemistry include the discovery, independently of Avogadro, of the law of equality of molar volumes of various gases, which should rightly be called the Avogadro-Amperard law, as well as the first attempt to classify chemical elements based on a comparison of their properties. But it was not these studies, interesting in themselves, and not his mathematical works that made Amperard’s name famous. A classic of science, a world-famous scientist, he became grateful for his research in the field of electronic science.
