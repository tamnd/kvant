we need to find the boundaries of the overlap area of ​​the beams - the distance $x$ by figure 4.

First of all, we determine the positions of the source images - the distance $f$ from the image to the corresponding line and the distance $H$ from the axis of the system. Using the line formula and taking into account that the focal lengths of the new lines are equal to $F$, we find $f$:

$$f = \frac{Fl}{l - F} = 3F.$$

The optical axes of the new lines are at a distance $h/2$ from the system axis and parallel to this axis. This means that the source is at a distance of $h/2$ from the optical axis of each line, and its images are at a distance of $H - h/2$.

Let's write down the formula for linear increase in line:

$$\frac{H - h/2}{h/2} = \frac{f}{l},$$

where

$$H = \frac{h(l + f)}{2l} = \frac{h(l + 3F)}{2l} = 1,5h.$$

Now it's not hard to find $x$. From the similarity of triangles $AOB$ and $BS_2K$ it follows that

$$\frac{AO}{S_2K} = \frac{OB}{BK},$$ or $$\frac{(D - h)/2}{H} = \frac{x}{f - x}.$$

From here

$$x = \frac{f(D - h)}{D - h + 2H} = \frac{3F(D - h)}{D + 2h} = 112,5\text{ см.}$$

Since the oscillation of the ball on the spoke is an accordion, the abscilotic magnitude of the elastic force acting on the ball when it is mixed from the equilibrium position is proportional to this mixing:

$$F_{\text{упр}} \sim x,$$ or $$F_{\text{упр}} = k_1x,$$ where $k_1$ is the proportionality coefficient. Therefore, the spoke is equivalent to a general spring with a stiffness of $k_1$.

Then for the vibration frequency $v_1$ we can write:

$$v_1 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k_1}{m}}$$

(m is the mass of the ball).

In the second case, when the ball is attached to a spring (we denote its stiffness by $k_2$), the oscillation frequency is equal to

$$v_2 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k_2}{m}}.$$
