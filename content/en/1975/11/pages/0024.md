⟦folio 22⟧

Watching sunbeams reflected from glass or from the surface of water, you may have never thought about what reflected light is. At first glance, it seems that the reflected ray is no different from the incident ray in anything except its direction. In fact, this is not so. The reason for this is the transverse nature of light waves.

Back in 1808, the French scientist Etienne-Louis Malus discovered that light reflected from glass is partially polarized. This means that the intensity of the waves that make up reflected light is no longer the same in different directions, as with natural light. The direction image appears. In the reflected beam, the intensity of waves with this direction of oscillation is maximum, and the intensity of waves with the direction of oscillation perpendicular to the image is minimal. Partial polarization of light occurs during specular reflection from the boundaries of any two dielectrics.

How is light polarized when reflected, and what does the degree of polarization depend on? To understand this phenomenon, let's take a closer look at it.
