⟦folio 50⟧

Averages of various phenomena of different physical natures can often be found in positive phenomena that exhibit the same signs and patterns. In such cases, we talk about physical analogies, or analogous (that is, positive) systems. Physical analogies that exist between electrical, mechanical, acoustic and other systems have long been successfully used in research and calculations. Methods based on the use of analogy, in a number of cases, turn out to be very fruitful in solving problems. They allow one to reduce the solution of some problems to the solution of other (already known) problems (often from other branches of physics).

For example, one cannot misunderstand the analogy between the law of universal gravitation and Coulomb's law.

Expressions for the gravitational force between two material points with masses $M$ and $m$ and for the force of electrostatic interaction between two exact charges $Q$ and $q$ have the form

$$F_T = \gamma \frac{Mm}{r^2}; \quad F_{3.1} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Qq}{r^2}.$$

Here $\gamma$ is the graviton constant, $\varepsilon_0$ is the electric constant, $r$ is the distance between the interacting bodies.

Since forces are expressed by similar formulas (in general cases, inversely proportional to the square of the distance), the movement of point materials in a graviton field (in the gravitational field) and the movement of a charged body in the field of a gravitational charge are described by the same equations. True, there is a difference. Gravitational forces always tend to lick bodies (body attraction), and electrical forces, depending on the signs of interacting charges, can be both attraction and repulsion.
