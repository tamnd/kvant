Here is an example of an electrical problem, from which the solution to Problem 5 immediately follows.

Problem 6. Let us name the capacitance of a spherical capacitor formed by two concentric conducting spheres $R_1$ and $R_2$.

The capacitance of the capacitor $C$ is equal to the relative charge of the capacitor $q$ to the potential difference $\Delta \varphi$ between its plates. The potential difference (by definition) is equal to the revolution made by electrical forces when moving a unit positive charge from one plate to another.

Let the inner sphere of the capacitor have a charge $q$, and the outer sphere have a charge $-q$. Then the electric field in the space between the spheres coincides with the field of the exact charge $q$, located in their common center $*$), and the direction of the field

$$E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}; \quad R_1 \leqslant r \leqslant R_2.$$

Let a single positive charge move over a small distance $\Delta r \ll R_1$ (so that the field can be considered uniform). The work done by the electric field on this section of the path is

$$\Delta A = E \Delta r = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\Delta r}{r^2}.$$

(*) See, for example, the article by L. P. Bakanina and S. M. Kozel “Principles of superposition in electrostatics”, «Kvant», 1973, No. 3.

The total work $A$ of electrical forces when the distance changes from $R_1$ to $R_2$ can be expressed in terms of the field potential of the exact charge:

$$A = -(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1) = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R_1} - \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R_2} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{R_2 - R_1}{R_1 R_2}.$$

Hence the capacitance of the spherical capacitor is equal to

$$C = \frac{q}{\Delta \varphi} = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_2 - R_1}.$$

Let us now return to problem 5 and complete its solution, using the results of problem 6. Let us compare the expression (*) for the capacitive work during gas expansion and the expression (**) for the capacitive work of moving a unit charge in an electrostatic field. It is easy to see that these expressions are similar (in problem 5 the variable is the volume of gas $V$, and in problem 6 it is the distance $r$). This allows us to conclude that complete works $A$ in obiha servants are expressed in the same way through the variables $V$ and $r$, respectively. Therefore, by analogy with expression (***), we can write down an expression for the work of a gas when its volume changes from $V_1$ to $V_2$:

$$A = R \beta \frac{V_2 - V_1}{V_1 V_2}.$$

Taking into account that $V = \frac{\beta}{T}$, we get:

$$A = R \frac{\frac{V_2}{\beta} - \frac{V_1}{\beta}}{\frac{V_1}{\beta} \frac{V_2}{\beta}} = R \frac{\frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1}}{\frac{1}{T_1} \frac{1}{T_2}} = -R(T_2 - T_1).$$

The minus sign in this formula is determined by the fact that gas rolling ($A < 0$) occurs in the process under consideration.
