To the article “Physical analogies”

1. The gas pressure at equilibrium is $p_0 = p_A + \frac{mg}{S}$.
Let us consider a small displacement of the piston from the equilibrium position. Let $\Delta V$ be the change in gas volume in this process. Let's find the new pressure $p$:

$$p = \frac{RT_0}{V} = \frac{RT_0}{V_0} + \Delta V = \frac{RT_0(V_0 - \Delta V)}{V_0^2 - (\Delta V)^2} = \frac{RT_0}{V_0} \cdot \frac{1 - \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}}{1 - \left(\frac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right)^2}.$$

Note that the ratio $\frac{\Delta V}{V_0}$ enters the numerator in the first column, and the denominator in the second. If $\left(\frac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right)^2 < 1$, then in the denominator with a good approximation we can neglect the quantities $\left(\frac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right)^2$ compared to unity. Then

$$p = \frac{RT_0}{V_0} \left(1 - \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right) = p_0 \left(1 - \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}\right),$$

where $p_0 = \frac{RT_0}{V_0}$.

Therefore, the change in pressure $\Delta p$ is equal to

$$\Delta p = p - p_0 = -\frac{RT_0}{V_0^2} \Delta V = -\frac{p_0^2}{RT_0} Sx.$$

Here $x$ denotes the small displacement of the piston from the equilibrium position. The force acting on the piston due to the pressure difference $\Delta p$ is

$$F = \Delta p S = -\frac{p_0^2 S^2}{RT_0} x.$$

This force is a quasi-elastic force. By analogy with the load, the external ones can now be written for the oscillation period $T$:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{mRT_0}{p_0^2 S^2}} = 2\pi \frac{1}{p_A S} \cdot mg.$$

As follows from the solution, the condition for small oscillations is related to the inequality $\left(\frac{\Delta V}{V}\right)^2 < 1$. If this condition is not met, the force acting on the piston is no longer quasi-elastic, and the analogy with the external load is lost. For example, a mathematical (and physical) matnik has the same property.
