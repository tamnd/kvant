⟦folio 34⟧

By dividing its digits - the most, then all its digits are either ones or nines.

Let there be a number consisting of $k$ nines and $n - k$ ones. The product of the digits of such a number is equal to $9^k$, therefore, in order for the difference to be the greatest, it is necessary that the number itself be the greatest average of all numbers composed of $k$ nines and $n - k$ ones.

It is clear that such a number is the number $\underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k}$.

Let us denote the difference between it and the product of its digits by $A'$:

$$A' = \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k} - 9^k.$$

Let's now see what $k$ should be so that the difference $A'$ between the number $\underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k}$ and the product of its digits is the greatest.

Let's increase $k$ by one: take the number $\underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k + 1} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k - 1}$.

To get the number the difference is

$$A'' = \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k + 1} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k - 1} - 9^{k + 1}.$$

Let's decrease $k$ by one: we get

$$A''' = \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k + 1} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k + 1} - 9^{k + 1}.$$

Imsem:

$$A' - A'' = \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k} - 9^k - \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k + 1} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k - 1} + 9^{k + 1}$$

$$- 8 \cdot 10^{n - k - 1} + 9^k (9 - 1) = 8 \left( -10^{n - k - 1} + 9^k \right) \geq 0,$$

(3)

since $A'$ is by assumption the largest difference.

Likewise

$$A' - A''' = \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k} - 9^k - \underbrace{9 \dots 9}_{k + 1} \underbrace{1 \dots 1}_{n - k - 1} + 9^{k + 1}$$

$$= 8 \cdot 10^{n - k} - 9^{k + 1} (9 - 1) = 8 \left( 10^{n - k} - 9^{k + 1} \right) \geq 0.$$

(4)

From (3) and (4) we obtain a system of inequalities:

$$\begin{cases} 9^k \geq 10^{n - k - 1}, \\ 10^{n - k} \geq 9^{k + 1}, \end{cases}$$

from which

$$\begin{cases} k \geq \frac{n - 1}{\lg 9 + 1}, \\ k \leq \frac{n + \lg 9}{\lg 9 + 1}, \end{cases}$$

that is

$$\frac{n - 1}{\lg 9 + 1} \leq k \leq \frac{n - 1}{\lg 9 + 1} + 1.$$

(5)
