After all, Oersted's experiments confirm what he thought about back in Bourg-en-Bresse - the unity of the forces of nature, the interconnection of phenomena.

Ampere was mainly a theoretician and rarely turned to experiments. But he realized that a server-based study of electromagnetic phenomena is impossible without setting up experiments that should test or refute his ideas. However, the Academy of Sciences did not allocate funds for these experiments. Ampère had to hire a mechanic who made everything necessary at his expense. Much was done by Ampere himself. First of all, he repeated Oersted’s experiment, trying to better understand the nature of the phenomenon discovered by the Danish physicist. He experimentally proved that statistical electricity does not act on a magnetic needle. Only moving electricity - electric current - is able to cause such an effect. “What is the reason for this phenomenon? - Ampere asked himself. “Why does a current-carrying conductor act on a magnetic needle?”

It would be natural to assume that an electric current passing through a conductor turns it into a magnet. This is what physicists believed, in particular, the famous French physicist Biot. Ampere had a different point of view. He expressed a general idea: the only principle of action of a conductor with current on a magnetic needle is moving electricity; magnetism is just one of its many manifestations. It is not the conductor through which the current flows that becomes the magnet, but, on the contrary, the magnet is a collection of currents. In a magnet there are many elementary circular currents flowing in a plane peredicular to its ss.

Ampere's hypothesis at that time seemed extremely bold and unjustified, so it was met with critical criticism by scientists.

Ampere developed a new view of the nature of magnetic phenomena as a result of a whole series of experiments. Already at the end of the first week of directed work, he made a discovery of no less importance than Oersted - he discovered the interaction of currents. If two electrified bodies mutually accept or repel, then wouldn’t conductors through which current flow behave similarly? Ampere placed parallel straight sections of two conductors connecting the ends of two Volga columns. One conductor was fixed, the other was made movable (Fig. 1). Having passed a current through the conductors, it is observed from the interaction: when the currents are in the same directions, they are attracted, when they are opposite, they are repulsive. It also turned out that the forces acting between current-carrying conductors are not central, that is, radial differences from electrostatic forces. Ampere proposed to reflect such a sharp difference in the manifestations of static electricity and electric current in terms of correspondence. He called the area of ​​phenomena associated with charges at rest 电磁electrostatics, and with moving charges - electrodynamics.

Another week has passed. Ampere carried out new experiments that developed and confirmed his ideas. If a magnet is a system of circular parallel currents directed in one direction, then a spiral of metal wire through which the current passes should behave like a magnet - it has two poles and takes a certain position under the influence of the Earth’s magnetic field (Fig. 2-3). The experiment confirmed these assumptions.
