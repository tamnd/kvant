His father found him doing this. “What are you reading, Andre?” he asked. “I’m reading an article about aberration,” answered the boy, who was 11 years old at the time. “But what do you understand about this?” - the father asked again. And Andre, without a shadow of embarrassment, explained to him the essence of this complex phenomenon.

Believing in his son’s exceptional abilities, his father gave him free access to his library. Andre eagerly read the Encyclopedia, volume after volume, and the unknown horizons of science were revealed to him. Such reading, although it was devoid of a certain system, did not pass without a trace: it was during this period that young Andre developed a real interest in various areas of man. He had never been to school or completed the required classical course of study that systematically introduces children to the basics of science. In his childhood he knew a lot. But this knowledge was scattered, and there were significant problems.

Andre showed particular interest in the physical and mathematical sciences. But it was precisely in this area that his father’s library was clearly lacking, and Andre began visiting the library of Linon College to read the works of great mathematicians - Euler, Bernoulli, Lagrange. However, many of these works were written in usernames that he did not own. It took him several months to study this language, and he learned it almost on his own and so well that he could easily comprehend the works of the classics of mathematics and physics of the 17th-18th centuries.

The parents invited Andre's math teacher. Already at the first meeting, he realized what an extraordinary student he was dealing with. “Do you know how roots are extracted?” - he asked Andre. “No,” the boy answered, “but I can interpret it skillfully!” Soon the teacher abandoned the lessons, since his knowledge was clearly not enough to teach such a student.

The history of science knows similar cases when great scientists did not receive a systematic school education for one reason or another. Nevertheless, their natural intelligence, passionate desire for knowledge, and hard work did their job. As an example, we can recall such famous self-taught people as Faraday and Edison.

Studying the works of the classics of mathematics and physics was a creative process for the young Ampere. He not only read, but also critically perceived what he read. He had his own thoughts, his own original ideas. It was during this period, at the age of 13, that he submitted his first works in mathematics to the Linone Academy.

The year 1789 arrived. The Great French Bourgeois Revolution began. On July 14, Bastlia, the symbol of Bourbon royal power, was captured. This news quickly reached Lyon. Andre perceived it as something known about a real new era, which the enlighteners dreamed of. XVIII century. However, the revolutionary events played a tragic role in Ampere’s life. In 1793, a rebellion broke out in Lyon, which was soon suppressed. Jean-Jacques Ampère was beheaded for sympathizing with mathematicians. Andre took the death of his father very hard; He was out of his habit for a long time and was close to losing his mind. Only a year later, with difficulty, he regained his peace of mind and was able to return to his studies.
