On April 18, the theoretical round of the Olympiad was held in the spacious classrooms of KSPI. Each participant in the Olympiad was offered 5 problems, which were given 4 hours to solve.

Most of the problems proposed at the Olympiad were published in the “Problem Book «Kvant» (see «Kvant» No. 7, 8 for this year). We provide the texts of problems that are not included in the “Problem Book «Kvant».

1. The rocket has two engines that can give it constant accelerations $a_1$ and $a_2$, directed vertically upward. The first engine is designed to operate for time $t_1$, the second - for time $t_2$, with $a_1 > a_2$ and $t_1 < t_2$. The engines can be turned on either simultaneously or sequentially. What order of turning on the engines should be chosen so that by the time the engines stop working the rocket will rise to its maximum altitude? (8 grades)

2. The spacecraft moves in a circular orbit around the Earth in the plane of the Moon's orbit with an angular velocity equal to the angular velocity of the Moon's rotation around the Earth. While moving, the ship is on a straight line connecting the centers of the Moon and the Earth. The distance from the ship to the Earth is such that the gravitational forces acting on the ship from the Earth and the Moon are equal to each other. Are the ship's engines working? What is the weight of the astronaut on the ship? The mass of the astronaut is 70 *kg*, the period of revolution of the Moon around the Earth is 27.3 *days*. The mass of the Earth is 81 times the mass of the Moon, and the distance from the Earth to the Moon is approximately 60 Earth radii. The radius of the Earth is taken to be 6400 * km *. (8 grades)

3. Two regular hexagonal prisms of transparent material $ABCDEF$ and $A'B'C'D'E'F'$ (Fig. 1) are stacked closely with blackened faces $ED$ and $E'D'$. The remaining edges have an anti-reflective coating, that is, no light is reflected on them. Face $AB$ is illuminated by a wide parallel beam of light, and the plane of incidence coincides with the plane of the base of the prism. At a certain value of the angle of incidence, the entire light flux incident on face $AB$ exits through face $A'B'$ of the second prism. Determine the refractive index of the prism material (10 cells).

When solving Problem 1, it was necessary to carefully understand the values ​​of the initial velocities at the second stage of the rocket’s movement under different engine operating modes, write down expressions for the height of the rocket’s rise in time $t_1 + t_2$ under different modes, and show that the optimal mode is in which the first engine is turned on first, and then the second.
