Despite all the efforts and savings, the funds earned by private lessons were not enough. Finally, in 1802, Ampère was invited to teach physics and chemistry at the Central School of the ancient provincial town of Bourg-en-Brés, 60 kilometers from Lyon. From that moment on, his regular teaching activities began, which lasted throughout his life.

Ampera dreamed of restructuring the traditional teaching of physics courses. Instead of presenting disparate facts and hypotheses, theories and experiments, he wanted to unfold before the Chatelaine case a grand picture of the universe, where everything is interconnected and interconnected. But these are just dreams. Boring teachers-officials, a wretched laboratory and a poor physics classroom, everyday everyday worries - this is the reality that he found in Bourcan-Brés. However, he worked hard and filled in the gaps in his knowledge. At the same time, he was left with the desire to return to Lyon to his wife and son. And soon she disappeared.

Changes were brewing in school education. In old royal France, most educational institutions were under the leadership and influence of the clergy. The French Revolution swept away this system. Universal education was proclaimed, teaching was liberated from church dogmatism, and a three-level education system was established: primary, secondary and higher. In each department, persons were established to provide secondary education with a practical bias. On April 4, 1803, Ampere was appointed teacher of mathematics at Lyons. Happy, he returned to Lyon, but soon a heavy blow fell on Ampere - his wife died.

At the end of 1804, Ampera left Lyon and moved to Paris, where he took up the position of teacher at the famous Polytechnic School. This higher school organization was founded in 1794 and soon became the national urban school of France. The main task of the school was to train highly educated technical specialists with deep knowledge of physical and mathematical sciences. Its middle teachers were also famous scientists like Lagrange, Monge, Berthollet. It is true that among the pupils of the Polytechnic School we meet precisely famous scientists and engineers, such as Fresnel, Gay-Lussac. Clapeyron, Poncelet, Arago, Dulong.
