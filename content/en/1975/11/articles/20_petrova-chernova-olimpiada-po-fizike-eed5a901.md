The final round of the IX All-Union Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics took place from April 17 to 23 in the city of Kaluga, where K. E. Tsiolkovsky lived and worked.

Kaluga is an ancient Russian city. More than 600 years ago the first mention of this city appeared, then a small fortress. Today's Kaluga is a large modern city with huge new residential areas, schools, cinemas, and universities.

This year, 43 8th grade students *), 47 9th grade students and 51 10th grade students took part in the final round.

April 17 in the new building of the Kaluga State Pedagogical Institute (KSPI) named after. K. E. Tsiolkovsky held the grand opening of the final round of the IX All-Union Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics. On this day, the city newspaper “Young Leninist” published greetings from pilot cosmonaut G.V. Sarafanov and presentations from the Organizing Committee of the All-Union Schoolchildren Olympiad, academician I.K. Kikon. Addressing the “Olympians” on the pages of the newspaper, I. K. Kikon said: “I greatly congratulate you that you yourself do not realize how happy your life is. If this were not a fantasy, I, like my peers, would be happy to be in your position, to occupy

At the grand opening, the first secretary of the Kaluga Regional Committee of the Komsomol, Associate Professor of the Department of General Physics of KSPI M. I. Tulchinsky, Academician I. K. Kikoin, Chairman of the Jury of the IX All-Union Olympiad in Physics L. N. Usachev and others addressed the participants with words of best wishes.

After the grand opening, a concert of amateur performances by students of the Pedagogical Institute took place.

On April 18, the theoretical round of the Olympiad was held in the spacious classrooms of KSPI. Each participant in the Olympiad was offered 5 problems, which were given 4 hours to solve.

Most of the problems proposed at the Olympiad were published in the “Problem Book «Kvant» (see «Kvant» No. 7, 8 for this year). We provide the texts of problems that are not included in the “Problem Book «Kvant».

1. The rocket has two engines that can give it constant accelerations $a_1$ and $a_2$, directed vertically upward. The first engine is designed to operate for time $t_1$, the second - for time $t_2$, with $a_1 > a_2$ and $t_1 < t_2$. The engines can be turned on either simultaneously or sequentially. What order of turning on the engines should be chosen so that by the time the engines stop working the rocket will rise to its maximum altitude? (8 grades)

2. The spacecraft moves in a circular orbit around the Earth in the plane of the Moon's orbit with an angular velocity equal to the angular velocity of the Moon's rotation around the Earth. While moving, the ship is on a straight line connecting the centers of the Moon and the Earth. The distance from the ship to the Earth is such that the gravitational forces acting on the ship from the Earth and the Moon are equal to each other. Are the ship's engines working? What is the weight of the astronaut on the ship? The mass of the astronaut is 70 *kg*, the period of revolution of the Moon around the Earth is 27.3 *days*. The mass of the Earth is 81 times the mass of the Moon, and the distance from the Earth to the Moon is approximately 60 Earth radii. The radius of the Earth is taken to be 6400 * km *. (8 grades)

3. Two regular hexagonal prisms of transparent material $ABCDEF$ and $A'B'C'D'E'F'$ (Fig. 1) are stacked closely with blackened faces $ED$ and $E'D'$. The remaining edges have an anti-reflective coating, that is, no light is reflected on them. Face $AB$ is illuminated by a wide parallel beam of light, and the plane of incidence coincides with the plane of the base of the prism. At a certain value of the angle of incidence, the entire light flux incident on face $AB$ exits through face $A'B'$ of the second prism. Determine the refractive index of the prism material (10 cells).

When solving Problem 1, it was necessary to carefully understand the values ​​of the initial velocities at the second stage of the rocket’s movement under different engine operating modes, write down expressions for the height of the rocket’s rise in time $t_1 + t_2$ under different modes, and show that the optimal mode is in which the first engine is turned on first, and then the second.

The main thing in solving problem 3 was to correctly construct the path of the ray through the signs. After all the work of the theoretical type was done, the Olympiad participants were given a tour of the city, and a viewing of demonstration performances by sportsmen from the region of the Yunost children's sports school took place.
On April 19, during the day, the “Olympians” worked on the Communist subbotnik, and in the evening, a departmental evening took place in the area of ​​the Palace of Pinners.
For the jury, these days were very focused - it was necessary to carry out all the work of a theoretical type.
On April 20, an experimental round was held in which all the Olympiad participants took part (unlike in previous years, when only those who most successfully completed a theoretical type task were allowed to participate in the experiment). The main goal of the experiment is to use simple equipment

conduct a series of experiments, evaluate the result obtained, its reliability and measurement error. I also attach a lot of importance to the design of the work. Here are the texts of the experimental problems.

8th grade
Eighth graders were given two problems.
1. Determine the density of the unknown liquid.
The experimenter has at his disposal beakers, liquids with known densities (water, copper sulfate), and test tubes.
When evaluating this work, I take into account how many ways the measurements were carried out, how the accuracy of measurements was assessed in various ways, the accuracy of the work and, of course, the result obtained.
2. Determine the maximum acceleration imparted to the puck by the campulma.
The experimenter is given a washer, campulma, and ruler.
When performing this work, it was necessary to determine the friction coefficient between the washer and the table surface, taking into account the dependence of friction on speed. It was important to carry out a sufficient number of measurements and correctly assess the accuracy of the results obtained.

9th grade
Determine which elements may be in the “black box” and how they are connected.
The experimenter has at his disposal a restam, a voltmeter, and a battery connected by wires. It is necessary to accurately determine the electrical circuit, take the current-voltage characteristic, detect and explain its non-captivity.

**10th grade**

*1. Determine the focal length of the diverging lens in all possible ways.*

*2. Determine the resolution of your eye (resolution is characterized by the minimum distance between two points at which these points are perceived by the eye separately).*

*For the experiment, a light source, converging and diverging lenses, a screen, foil, a ruler, and a measuring scale are provided.*

When evaluating the work, the choice of method and the accuracy of measuring the focal length of a converging lens, the accuracy of determining the focal length of a diverging lens, and the correct construction of the path of rays in a system of two lenses were taken into account.

In the evening after the experiment, the “Olympians” visited the memorial House-Museum of K. E. Tsiolkovsky and the State Museum of the History of Cosmonautics. Traditionally, Soviet cosmonauts come here after their next space flight.

On April 21, an excursion to the world's first nuclear power plant, built in the city of Obninsk in 1954, took place.

And then the day came, which everyone had been so impatiently waiting for: the jury members, and, of course, the “Olympians” themselves. On April 22, the closing ceremony of the IX All-Union Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics took place, at which the final results were summed up.

The winners of the Olympiad were awarded diplomas and prizes. A special prize «Kvant» - a binder of the magazine for 1974 with autographs of members of the editorial board - was awarded to *N. Vasiliev* (Kaluga). A subscription to the magazine «Kvant» for 1976 was awarded to *G. Abdulaev* (Kirovabad), *E. Ponomarev* (p/o Chernogolovka, Moscow region), *A. Smirnov* (Semibratovo village, Yaroslavl region) and *R. Sharipov* (Karakul).
