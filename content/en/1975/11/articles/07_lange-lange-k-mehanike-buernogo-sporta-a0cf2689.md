On the mechanics of ice boating

V. N. Lange, T. I. Lange

A buer is a platform mounted on skates, on which a mast with a sail is mounted.

The first sports races on ice boats in Russia took place in 1890 in St. Petersburg. Since the beginning of the 20th century, iceboating has become very popular, especially in the Baltic region. Since 1947, USSR iceboat championships have been regularly held.

Iceboat racing is a very beautiful sight. Light white-winged platforms glide swiftly on metal runners across a smooth ice surface. The speed of the iceboat with skillful control reaches 100 km/h. And here’s what’s amazing: if you change the wind speed at this time, it can be two or even three times less!

It turns out that the iceboat, which has no movement of its own, overtakes the wind! How? After all, acceleration occurs due to the force of pressure on the sail from the air. Everyone, the highest speed that can be achieved is the speed of the wind. But we must not forget that a boat sail can be controlled. It is precisely “sailing maneuvers” that allows one to achieve “superwind” speeds.

To understand the mechanism for accelerating a boat, consider the following example.

Let a block ABCD, whose faces AB and AD form an angle α, be able to slide along rectilinear guides MN and PQ (see figure). The friction between the block and the guides is so small that an insignificant amount of sleep leads to movement of the block. Let at point O in the direction indicated by figure by the arrow, some constant force acts on the block. For example, a rod presses on face AD. Due to the insignificant resistance forces, the block moves even with a slight impact, and during the time until the end of the rod passes the path OOO', the block will move along the guides a distance equal to AA', taking position A'B'C'D'. It's not hard to see that

$$AA' = \frac{OO'}{tg\alpha}.$$

For simplicity, we will assume that the motion of the rod and the block is uniform. Dividing both sides of the equality by the time of movement t, we obtain the relationship between the speed of the block and the rod:

$$v_{бр} = \frac{v_{с}}{tg\alpha}.$$

From this it can be seen that at 0 < α < 45° the speed of the block will exceed the speed of the rod, since tg α < 1.

Now let's get back to the boat. If we assume that the iceboat's positions are located along the lines MN and PQ, its sail is set in the direction AD, and the wind is blowing along OOO', then, in fact, for the correspondence between the speed of the iceboat and the wind, we can obtain a similar expression:

$$v_{\sigma} = \frac{v_{в}}{tg\alpha}.$$

We have considered only the case when the wind blows perpendicular to the iceboat's course; The reader is left to analyze the rest on his own.
