## A point, a straight line... - what is it?

Have you ever wondered what a point and a line are? At first glance, everything is clear here: a point is a point, a straight line is a straight line, what could be incomprehensible here? Well, but still, how to interpret this?
to someone who doesn’t know this at all and, in addition, understands everything very
literally? Is it really that simple? It turns out not at all! That's what it is
A fantastic story happened in one school.

### 1. Martian studies “terrestrial” geometry

A Martian boy, Inko, flew to one of the Moscow schools. When he got to the geometry lesson, he didn’t understand anything. The guys decided to help him, explain what was what. Vasya took charge of this. And he had to start... from a point. Slightly
touching the board with chalk, Vasya says:

- This is the point. And this is the point, and this is also the point,” he continues, leaving
tiny traces of chalk on the blackboard. - Do you understand? Further...

“Wait, wait,” says the Martian. - I need to take a better look
these points. - And he takes out a microscope. - So the dot is a bunch of white
substances. Interesting! We call it differently... And if this substance is
green - will these be dots? And what size can these piles be?

“A point has no dimensions,” says Vasya.

- Why not? You yourself said that the piles of white matter are dots! —
Inko answers.

Other students hand the Martian sheets of paper with traces of touch
pencil, with pin holes.

- These are placers of graphite! It's a torn hole in the paper! - Inko answers,
looking through a microscope.

Vasya is at a loss. The rest too. “Clear” and “understandable” have become unclear
not only for an alien from another planet.

Then Vasya takes a ruler and uses it to draw a line.

- It's straight! - he says. — It consists of dots.

Having carefully examined the “straight line”, the Martian says:

— A straight line is a ridge of chalk or graphite placers. — The class is upset.
What to do?

- Maybe this Inko is a loser? - some of you are thinking. Don't rush to conclusions! The Martian saves the day. - When on Mars they want
to tell about something new, they call its properties. What properties
do your straight lines and points have?

— These properties are indicated in our textbook! — the sixth grader will answer this.—
And they call them axioms!

— At the end of the 8th grade textbook, all these axioms are listed! - will add
eighth grader. - Let's use them!

What's left to do? We must agree. Let's start reading the axioms.

### 2. Axioms of belonging

Axiom I₁. *Each straight line is a set of points.*

Axiom I₂. *For any two different
points there is one and only one line containing them.*

Axiom I₃. *There is at least one straight line, and each
at least one point belongs to the line.*

### 3. Points and lines, which are described by the axioms of belonging

After reading the axiom I$_1$, Inko perked up.

“Stop,” he said. - What could we mean by a point and a line if there were no other properties? This axiom does not say what a point is. Means. By dots you can mean anything! For example, numbers
1, 2, 3. What lines will we have then? Let's find them together.

Together we found straight lines $\{1\}$, $\{2\}$, $\{3\}$, $\{1,2\}$,
$\{1,3\}$, $\{2,3\}$, $\{1,2,3\}$.

- Then there is another straight line! - one of you exclaims. It's empty
set - $\varnothing$! After all, axiom I$_1$ does not say that a set of points that is a straight line cannot be empty.

There is noise in the class. What are these straight lines? There are infinitely many points on a straight line, but here there are one, two, three, and even none!

The unperturbed Martian listens to the argument. Then he says:

— You are confused that I called the numbers 1, 2, 3 dots, and the ones listed
sets, including the empty one, straight? And this doesn’t surprise me at all.
Apparently, the fact is that you also know other properties of points and lines,
but I don’t know them. Therefore, those “points” and “straight lines” that are described
one and only axiom I$_1$, I will call it this way: “point
one-one", "straight one-one" and write: point (I$_1$), straight
(I$_1$). Are the others true for the point (I$_1$), line (I$_1$)
axioms don't interest me. Let's come up with more examples of points (I$_1$)
and straight lines (І$_1$), suggests Inko. There was silence. Here are some examples
came up with it.

sentences “*Vika really liked the city of Kyiv*” (many letters,
