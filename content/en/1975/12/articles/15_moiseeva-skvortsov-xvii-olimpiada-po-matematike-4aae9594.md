XVII Olympiad in Mathematics

The XVII International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren took place in Bulgaria in July 1975 in Burgas. Teams from 17 countries took part in it: Australia, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, East Germany, Greece, Democratic Republic of Vietnam, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, France, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia. As usual, each country's team consisted of 8 schoolchildren (the DRV team had seven people due to the illness of one participant). A team of Greek schoolchildren performed at the Olympics for the first time.

The Soviet team was mainly composed of the winners of the IX All-Union Mathematical Olympiad.

This year, the All-Union Olympiad was held according to new regulations, which are currently being clarified. This provision provides for the holding of republican competitions, based on the results of which a team of union republics will be formed. However, restrictions on the formatting of the RSFSR team and shortcomings in the organization and conduct of the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad led to the fact that many schoolchildren who showed high results at the Moscow City Olympiad did not qualify for the All-Union Olympiad. In this regard, additional competitions were held among Moscow schoolchildren to select candidates for the USSR team for the International Mathematical Olympiad. The winners of the All-Union Olympiad, together with the winners of the Moscow selection, were invited to participate in training camps, which are traditionally held near Moscow in Gorki Leninskiye. Here the composition of the team was finally determined. It included:

Alexander Kornyushkin, a graduate of Physics School No. 18 at Moscow State University, a prize-winner of the region and Resource Olympids in mathematics at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad, he received I premio in 1973, and II premio in 1974;

Yuri Neretin, a graduate of secondary school No. 91, Moscow, winner of the Moscow City Olympids, at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad he received in 1974 - II premio;

Alexander Reznikov, a graduate of Physics School No. 145 at KSU, a prize-winner of the city and resource olympids in mathematics (I premio in 1974 and 1975), at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad he received II premio in 1974, and in 1975 - I premio;

Sergei Finashin, a 9th grade student at Physics School No. 45 at Leningrad State University, winner of the Leningrad City Olympiads (1973 - 1st prize, 1974 and 1975 - 2nd prize), at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad he received 1st prize in 1974 and 1975;

Tatyana Khovanova, a 9th grade student at secondary school No. 444 in Moscow, winner of the Moscow City Olympiads in Mathematics, she received 1st prize at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad in 1974 and 1975;

Georgy Shmelev, a graduate of secondary school No. 20 in Yaroslavl, a winner of regional and republican Olympiads, received the 2nd prize at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad in 1974, and the 1st prize in 1975;

Boris Yusin, a graduate of the part-time evening (shift) school No. 180 in Moscow, a prize-winner of the Moscow City Olympiads, took first place in the qualifying competitions for schoolchildren for the XVII International Mathematical Olympiad;

Ilya Yunus, a graduate of secondary school No. 27 in Kharkov, a prize-winner of regional and republican Olympiads, received the 3rd prize at the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad in 1974, and the 1st prize in 1975.

In addition, candidates for the team took part in the training camp: Konstantin Rybasov (graduate of Physics School No. 145 at KSU), Alexey Muzykantov (graduate of School No. 130 in Novosibirsk), Alexander Goncharov (9th grade student at boarding school No. 18 at Moscow State University), Vladimir Geisel (graduate of Physics School No. 18 at Moscow State University).

For readers «Kvant», the problems of one of the qualifying Olympiads, the results of which were taken into account when forming the team, may be of interest.

On July 3, our team left for Bulgaria, where both rounds of the competition took place in Burgas on July 7 and 8.

The problems for the Olympiad were pre-selected by the International Jury, which included the scientific supervisors of all teams; the chairman of the jury was Sofia University professor I. Prodanov. On each day of the competition, as in previous years, 4 hours were allotted for solving problems (the texts of the problems are given at the end of the article; in brackets it is indicated which country proposed the problem and how many points were given for its complete solution).

The closing ceremony of the Olympiad took place in Sofia, where the winners were presented with awards.

**Tasks of the first day of competition**

**1.** Let $x_i$, $y_i$ and $(i = 1, 2, 3, \ldots, n)$ be real numbers such that $x_1 \geqslant \geqslant x_2 \geqslant \ldots \geqslant x_n$ and $y_1 \geqslant y_2 \geqslant \ldots \geqslant y_n$. Prove that if $z_1$, $z_2$, ..., $z_n$ is any permutation of the numbers $y_1$, $y_2$, ..., $y_n$, then

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - y_i)^2 \leqslant \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - z_i)^2$$

(Czechoslovakia, 6 points).

**2.** Let $a_1$, $a_2$, $a_3$, ... be an arbitrary infinite sequence of positive integers such that $a_k < < a_{k+1}$ $(1 \leqslant k)$. Prove that infinitely many terms $a_m$ of this sequence can be represented as $a_m = x a_p + y a_q$, where $x$ and $y$ are positive numbers and $p \neq q$ (UK, 7 points).

**3.** On the sides of an arbitrary triangle $ABC$ outside it (in the plane $ABC$) triangles $BPC$, $CQA$ and $ARB$ are constructed so that

$$\widehat{PBC} = \widehat{CAQ} = 45°, \qquad \widehat{BCP} = \widehat{QCA} = 30°,$$
$$\widehat{ABR} = \widehat{BAR} = 15°.$$

Prove that $\widehat{QRP} = 90°$ and $|QR| = = |RP|$ (Netherlands, 7 points).

**Tasks of the second day of competition**

**4.** Let $A$ be the sum of the digits in decimal notation of the number $4444^{4444}$, and $B'$ be the sum of the digits of the number $A$ ($A$ and $B$ are also considered in decimal notation). Find the sum of the digits of the number $B$ (USSR, 6 points).

**5.** Is it possible to mark 1975 points on a circle of radius 1 so that the lengths of the chords connecting any two of them are expressed in rational numbers? (USSR, 6 points).

**6.** Find all polynomials $P$ with respect to two variables that have the following properties:

1) $P$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $n$, that is, for all real $t$, $x$, $y$ $P(tx, ty) = t^n P(x, y)$;

2) for all real $a$, $b$, $c$
$$P(a + b, c) + P(b + c, a) + \\ + P(c + a, b) = 0;$$

3) $P(1, 0) = 1$
(UK, 8 points).
