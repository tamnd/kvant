### 1. The distance from one point to another. Axioms of distance

Axiom II₁. *For any two points $A$ and $B$ there is
a non-negative quantity $|AB|$, called the distance from $A$ to $B$.
The distance is zero if and only if the points $A$ and $B$
match.*

Axiom II₂. *The distance from point $A$ to point $B$ is
distance from point $B$ to point $A$: $|AB|=|BA|$.*

Axiom II₃. *For any three points $A$, $B$, $C$
the distance from $A$ to $C$ is not greater than the sum of the distances from $A$ to $B$ and from $B$
to $C$: $|AC|\le|AB|+|BC|$.*

Axioms II$_{\text{1—3}}$ define the concept of distance from one point to another. When solving problems, we will use notation of the form “distance
(II$_1$)" (distance described only by axiom II$_1$), "distance
(II$_{1,2}$)" (distance described by axioms II$_1$ and II$_2$), "point
(II$_1$)” (the point described by axiom II$_1$), etc.

### 2. Axioms of order

Axiom III₁. *Any point $O$ of straight line $p$ splits
the set of points of the line $p$ different from $O$ into two non-empty sets so that
What:*

1. *for any two points $A$ and $B$ belonging to different sets,
point $O$ lies between $A$ and $B$;*
2. *if points $A$ and $B$ belong to the same set, then one
of which lies between another point and point $O$.*

The sets referred to in Axiom III$_1$ are called *open
rays*, and the union of each of them with the point $O$ is called *ray with origin* $O$.

Axiom III₂. *For any distance $a$ at a given
ray with origin $O$ there is one and only one point $A$, the distance
which from the point $O$ is equal to $a$: $|OA|=a$.*

Axiom III₃. *If point $C$ lies between points $A$ and $B$, then points $A$, $B$, $C$ belong to the same line.*

Axiom III₄. *Any straight line $p$ splits the set of points of the plane that do not belong to it into two non-empty sets so that:*

1. *any two points belonging to different sets are separated by a straight line
$p$;*
2. *any two points belonging to the same set are not separated by a straight line $p$.*

### Conclusion

We examined several simple models in which certain axioms are satisfied. There are many models in which all axioms are satisfied
geometry. This is explained by the fact that the point is not a pin prick, but a straight line - not a tightly stretched thread, mentally extended indefinitely in both directions.
Points and straight lines can be called any objects for which
properties specified in axiom groups. For example, under dot we can
mean a pair of numbers $(x,y)$, and by a straight line any set of pairs $(x,y)$ that are a solution to the equation $ax+by+c=0$.

So, *a point and a straight line are those geometric concepts, properties
Which are described in axioms*. This could be your short answer
to the teacher during the exam.
