For schoolchildren about solid state physics

The physics editors of the Prosveshcheniye publishing house began publishing a series of books under the general title “For schoolchildren about modern physics.” This was done in a very timely manner, since the secondary school switched to a new physics program, in which the weight of the sections of modern physics was replaced and the scientific level of the entire course was raised.

In 1974, the first book in this series was published, which talked about some fundamental problems of modern physics. The solution to this book was published in the journal «Kvant»*). A second book dedicated to solid state physics** has recently been published. Solid state physics in our time has turned into a vast, branched science. Each direction of this science has great theoretical significance and broad practical applications. It is almost impossible to indicate a branch of industry where the achievements of modern physics are not applied

The properties of semiconductors provide a wide range of possibilities for the use of semiconductors in technology. A lot of such examples could be given.

In the school course, physicists consider some issues in solid state physics. But it is very difficult to talk about the current state of solid-state physics in a way that is understandable to a schoolchild. However, this difficult task was largely successfully solved by the authors of the articles included in the book.

The book contains six articles devoted to the most important problems of solid state physics, and one article telling about some features of the connection between modern physics and mathematics.

The book begins with the articles by A. F. Andreev, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, senior researcher at the Institute of Physical Problems of the USSR Academy of Sciences, professor at the Moscow Physical Institute, “Superfluidity of a liquid gel.” It talks about the phenomenon of superfluidity of liquid gel, discovered in 1938 by the leading Soviet physicist, Academician P. L. Kapitsa.

From this article, readers will learn not only about the amazing properties of liquid gel II, but also why a gel cannot be turned into a solid even at ultra-low temperatures and normal pressure.

The extraordinary properties that liquid gels exhibit at temperatures close to absolute zero are not the only remarkable phenomena in this temperature region. The second unique phenomenon characteristic of some metals and alloys at low temperatures is superconductivity. This phenomenon is in the book “For Schoolchildren about Modern Physics”. Solid state physics is given special attention. In the article “On Superconductivity,” written by Doctor of Physics and Mathematics, Professor V.Z. Kresny, he talks about how in the physical laboratory of Leiden University, the Dutch scientist G. Kamerlingh-Onnes discovered an amazing property of mercury in 1911: at a temperature close to 4°K, the electronic resistance of this metal abruptly turns to zero. This is how one of the most interesting and important areas in modern solid state physics arose.

The phenomenon of superconductivity is observed only at very low temperatures. Currently, it has been possible to achieve a transition to the superconducting state of the Nb₃Ge alloy at a temperature of T=22.3°K. This is the highest transition temperature yet. The article describes the phenomenon of superconductivity, discusses the directions of searches for high-temperature superconductivity, and examines the influence of magnetic fields on superconductivity. With great interest, readers will become familiar with already existing superconductors, as well as with the connection of superconductivity with other areas of physics (superconducting states of atomic nuclei, superconductivity and neutron stars, superconductivity and biophysics).

emission and absorption of $\gamma$-rays by atomic nuclei.

Like electrons in atoms, nucleons in nuclei (protons and neutrons) are at specific energy levels. Transitions of nucleons from one level to another cause the emission or absorption of gamma rays by nuclei. In 1958, the young German physicist R. Mössbauer studied the resonant absorption of $\gamma$ rays. The quantum energy of these rays is equal to the difference in the energy levels of the nucleons. Therefore, such absorption is called resonant. Mössbauer discovered that if the absorption or emission of $\gamma$-rays occurs in a crystal lattice where the atomic nuclei are tightly bound, then nuclear recoil does not occur. (If a person jumps into the water from a ship, then the ship does not experience recoil, since the mass of the ship is much greater than the mass of the person. If a person were jumping from a boat, then the recoil would be noticeable.) It turned out that under the conditions of the Mössbauer effect, the spectral lines of emission and absorption of $\gamma$ rays are very narrow. This allows the Mössbauer effect to be used to measure very small differences in energy and frequency with enormous precision. The article by V. A. Belyakov talks about the possibility of using the Mössbauer effect in various fields of physics and chemistry (observation of effects in the theory of relativity, studying the structure of complex molecules of solids, etc.).

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, senior researcher at the Institute of Physical Problems of the USSR Academy of Sciences, professor at Moscow State University M. I. Kaganov, in the article “Magnetic Properties of Matter,” fascinatingly talks about modern views on the magnetic properties of solids. Starting with a consideration of the magnetic properties of the elementary carriers of magnetism—electrification

rons, protons and neutrons, the author gradually moves on to the magnetic properties of atoms, molecules and crystals. The article contains a presentation of the nature of paramagnetism and exchange interaction. When studying the magnetic properties of matter, the greatest difficulty for schoolchildren is understanding the properties and physical nature of ferromagnetism. In the article by Professor M.I. Kaganov, they will not only find an accessible and modern explanation of these issues, but will also become acquainted with antiferromagnetism, the behavior of substances with magnetic properties near absolute zero temperatures, and many other interesting problems.

Article by candidate of physical and mathematical sciences, associate professor of the Moscow Pedagogical Institute. V. I. Lenin Yu. A. Gurvich is dedicated to semiconductors. In the school physics course, the study of the electrical properties of semiconductors is given a prominent place. Therefore, the content of this article, to a greater extent than the materials of other articles, will seem familiar to readers. However, the article “Semiconductors” significantly expands the information that schoolchildren receive in the compulsory physics course. All properties of intrinsic and impurity semiconductors are considered on the basis of the band theory of solids, analysis of the nature of the arrangement and filling of energy levels in crystals with electrons. This approach allowed the author to consider all the basic properties of electron and hole semiconductors and talk about their most important applications.

Prokhorov at the Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Moscow and the American scientist Charles Townes at Columbia University in New York. The article by A. A. The last, seventh article is called “Physics and Mathematics”. It was written by a candidate of physical and mathematical sciences, senior researcher at the Institute of Atomic Energy. I. V. Kurchatova V. P. Smilgoy. Thematically, this article is not related to solid state physics. However, it is directly related to the entire series of books “For Schoolchildren about Modern Physics”. The article talks about how mathematics and modern physics are intertwined in our time, the role of mathematics in the achievements of physics, the importance of mathematical beauty in physical theories, and much more. The article is masterfully written, using examples from eight specific stories covering a large period of time.

Let's summarize. With great pedagogical tact, at an accessible and at the same time serious level, schoolchildren are told about the most complex issues of modern physics. One can only wish that the next books in this series will bring the same benefit to students as those already published.
