Medians and midlines

E. G. Gotman

...It’s easier to solve many problems together than just one of them, if more of the problem is well coordinated, and one problem is isolated in itself.

D. Sing a. "Mathematics and Plausible Reasoning"

This article contains problems on the properties of the medians of a triangle and the midlines of a quadrilateral. The tasks are available to students in grades 7-8. To solve them, you need to come up with an auxiliary construction, apply parallel translation or central symmetry. The proof uses a small number of theorems, most often the theorem about the midline of a triangle. The problems are arranged in such a sequence that it is enough to select the keys to solve the first of them in order to be able to solve the next one.

Some of the data problems with solutions or short directions (at the end of the magazine). Before reading the solution to a particular problem, try to solve it yourself. In case of difficulty, build on figure. Find out what role auxiliary lines play and what they are needed for. Read the solution directions. Compare different possible ways to solve the same problem. When starting to solve subsequent problems, find their similarity with the solved problems and try to use it.

Medians of a triangle

Problem 1. In triangle ABC, the median CM is drawn. Prove that

a) if $|CM| > \frac{1}{2}|AB|$, then $\hat{C} < 90^\circ$;

b) if $|CM| = \frac{1}{2}|AB|$, then $\hat{C} = 90^\circ$;

c) if $|CM| < \frac{1}{2}|AB|$, then $\hat{C} > 90^\circ$.

Let us denote $A\hat{CM} = \alpha$, $B\hat{CM} = \beta$ (Fig. 1).

a) $|CM| > |AM|$ and $|CM| > |BM|$. Consider the triangles $ACM$ and $BCM$. Since in a triangle there is a larger angle opposite the larger side, then $\hat{A} > \alpha$, $\hat{B} > \beta$. Adding these equalities term by term, we obtain $\hat{A} + \hat{B} > \hat{C}$. It follows that $\hat{A} + \hat{B} + \hat{C} > \hat{C} + \hat{C} = 2\hat{C}$ and, since $\hat{A} + \hat{B} + \hat{C} = 180^\circ$, $\hat{C} < 90^\circ$.

Problem 2. The median CM of triangle ABC is formed from the sides AC and BC corresponding to the angles α and β. Which of these angles is greater if |AC| < |BC|?

Let us denote |BC| = a, |AC| = b, then by condition a > b.

Let's look at two ways to solve this problem.

First way. Let's construct point D, symmetrical to point C relative to the middle M of side AB, and connect point D to vertex A (Fig. 2). The constructed triangle ADM is symmetrical to the triangle BCM with respect to the point M, therefore, |AD| = |BC| = a and ADM = BCM = β. We have obtained a triangle ACD, which contains the intersection of angles α and β, and the opposite sides AD and AC are equal to the correspondence of a and b. According to the condition, a > b, therefore α > β.

Second way. When solving problem 2 in the first way, we constructed a triangle $ADM$, a symmetric triangle $BCM$ relative to the middle $M$ of side $AB$. To solve problem 4 you can use the same technique. Construct a quadrilateral $AMN_1C_1$, a symmetric quadrilateral $BMNC$ relative to the point $M$ (Fig. 4). Let $|AD| = a$, $|BC| = b$, $|MN| = m$, then $|AC_1| = b$, $|DC_1| = |NN_1| = 2m$. If $(AD) \parallel (BC)$, then $a + b > 2m$; if $(AD) \parallel (BC)$, then $a + b = 2m$.

Third way. Connect the midpoint $E$ of one of the diagonals of the quadrilateral from the points $M$ and $N$ (Fig. 5). Then, as in solving Problem 2 using the second method, we obtain a triangle $EMN$ with sides equal to $a/2$, $b/2$ and $m$. Therefore, $m < (a + b)/2$. In the special case when $(AD) \parallel (BC)$, the triangle $EMN$ degenerates, the point $E$ will belong to the segment $MN$ and, therefore, $m = \frac{1}{2} (a + b)$.

Task 5. Opposite sides $AD$ and $BC$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ are constructible. Prove that the straight line connecting the other two sides requires congruent angles with the straight lines $AD$ and $BC$.

This problem, like the previous one, can be solved in various ways, using the same techniques. Which solution do you like best?

Task 6. Construct a triangle using two sides and a median drawn to the third side.

Task 7. Construct a quadrilateral, knowing all its sides and a segment connecting two opposite sides.

The next task is worth remembering. This is a very famous problem that is often used to solve other problems.

Task 8. Prove that the midpoints of any quadrilateral are the vertices of a parallelogram. Under what conditions will this parallelogram be

a) rhombus,
b) a right angle,
b) square?

Task 9. Prove that the midlines of any quadrilateral and the segment connecting the diagonals intersect at one point and divide it in half.

If the points $A, B, C$ and $D$ do not belong to the same plane, then the six segments connecting these points in pairs are the edges of a triangular parallelogram (tetrahedron).

Task 10. Prove that the segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges of the tetrahedron intersect at one point and divide it in half.

Please note that problem 9 is a special case (or better yet, a limiting case) of problem 10.

Task 11. The diagonal of a quadrilateral is mutually perpendicular. Knowing their lengths $e$ and $f$, calculate the lengths of the middle lines of the quadrilateral.

Task 12. The midpoints of the side $AB$ and $CD$, $BC$ and $DE$ of the convex pentagon $ABCDE$ are connected by segments. The midpoints $H$ and $K$ of the resulting segments are connected again. Prove that the segment $HK$ is parallel to the segments $AE$ and $|HK| = |AE|/4$.
