## VIII Olympiad in Physics

In July 1975, the VIII International Physics Olympiad among schoolchildren was held in the city of Güstrow (GDR). Teams from Bulgaria, Hungary, East Germany, Poland, Romania, USSR, Czechoslovakia, France, and Germany took part in the Olympiad.

Members of the USSR team were schoolchildren - winners of the All-Union Physics Olympiad, who showed the best results at the training camp:

Leonid Avdeev - graduate of physics and mathematics boarding school No. 165 in Novosibirsk;

V a d i m B o r u - graduate of secondary school No. 28 in Zaporozhye;

S ergey Korshunov is a graduate of secondary school No. 1 of the village. Monino, Shchelkovsky district, Moscow region;

Yuriy M a k e d o n o v - graduate of secondary school No. 37 in Kalinin;

Evgeniy Shakhnovich is a graduate of secondary school No. 6 in Kalinin.

On July 7, all participants of the Olympiad gathered in Berlin and headed to Güstrow by bus.

On July 9, a theoretical round was held, at which participants were offered three tasks (the texts of the tasks are given at the end of the article). The maximum number of points in this round was received by: Radu Oprea (Romania), Sergei Korshunov (USSR), Antoni Lachkovsky (Poland), Domenic Delande (France). July 11 at the experimental round, where participants

On July 14, the closing ceremony of the Olympiad took place, where the final results were summed up. The winners were awarded diplomas and valuable gifts.

The teams of the GDR, USSR, Hungary and Poland performed most successfully, all of whose members were awarded diplomas of I, II, III degrees.

The winner of the Olympiad was *Sergei Korshunov* (USSR), who scored the highest number of points (43).

In their free time, the young physicists were given the opportunity to get acquainted with the cultural and historical monuments of the GDR and meet with schoolchildren and students.

Task 2. For only a glass line with radii of curvature $r_1$ and $r_2$ and thickness $d$ (Fig. 2) in the air, the focal length $f$ is determined by the following expression:

$$f = \frac{n r_1 r_2}{(n - 1) [n (r_2 - r_1) + d (n - 1)]},$$

where $n$ is the refractive index (air - glass).

For certain purposes, it is desirable that the focal length is independent of the light wavelength λ.

Task 3. A beam of positive singly charged ions (charge + e) ​​of equal and constant mass $m$ is emitted from point Q in one plane. Ions accelerated by voltage U are deflected by a single magnetic field, which is directed perpendicular to the plane of ion propagation. The magnetic field induction is equal to B. The boundaries of the magnetic field must be such that the beams converge at one point A ($QA = 2a$). The trajectories must be symmetrical relative to the line perpendicular to the segment QA and passing through its middle. From the possible boundaries of the magnetic field, you should choose those that would be in the vicinity of a line perpendicular to the middle of the segment QA, but would not include points Q and A. The areas should be single-connected, i.e., without holes and discontinuities.

a) Express the radius of curvature R of the trajectories of the part in a magnetic field as a function of voltage U and induction B.

6) Indicate to the characteristic the properties of the trajectories of the part in the resulting installation.

b) Find the boundaries of the magnetic field by geometric construction for the case: $R < a; R = a; R > a$.

d) Find a mathematical expression for the boundary of the magnetic field.
