F331. A flat spiral with a very large number of turns and an outer radius $R$ (Fig. 15) is in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the spiral and varying according to the law $B = B_0 \cos \omega t$. Named $э. d. c. индукции, наводимую в спирали. Расстояние между витками спирали посмотрено.

Рис. 15.

Рис. 16.

Ф332. Наимену рассмотрение между источником света $S$ и его изображением в оптической системе, приведенной на рисунке 17. Фокусные рассмотрения линии $A$ и $B$ одинаковы и равны $F$.

Рис. 17.

Величина $e. d. c. induction excited in a coil of radius $r_i$ is equal to

$$\mathcal{E}_i = -\frac{\Delta \Phi_i}{\Delta t},$$

where $\Delta \Phi_i$ is the change in magnetic flux through the plane limited by the coil for a short period of time $\Delta t$:

$$\Delta \Phi_i = S \Delta B = \pi r_i^2 B_0 \left\{ \cos \left[ \omega (t + \Delta t) \right] - \cos \omega t \right\} = -2 \pi r_i^2 B_0 \left[ \sin \omega \left( t + \frac{\Delta t}{2} \right) \sin \omega \frac{\Delta t}{2} \right] \approx -\pi r_i^2 B_0 \omega \Delta t \sin \omega t$$

since $\Delta t$ is small, then $\sin \omega \left( t + \frac{\Delta t}{2} \right) \approx \sin \omega t$, $\sin \omega \frac{\Delta t}{2} \approx \omega \frac{\Delta t}{2}$. The e.m.f. induced throughout the entire spiral differs in the amount of e.m.f. d.s. induced in turns, that is

$$\mathcal{E} = \sum \mathcal{E}_i = \sum -\frac{\Delta \Phi_i}{\Delta t} = B_0 \omega \sin \omega t \sum \pi r_i^2.$$

The quantity $\pi r_i^2$ can be considered the volume of a cylinder, the height of which is equal to one, and the area of ​​the base is $\pi r_i^2$. Then $\sum \pi r_i^2$ is the sum of the volumes of such cylinders. Since the difference between the radii of the bases of “neighboring” cylinders is small (the number of turns $n$ is large), the volume of all such cylinders is close to the volume of a cone with a height $n$ and a base area $\pi R^2$ (Fig. 16):

$$\sum \pi r_i^2 = V = \frac{1}{3} \pi R^2 n.$$

Thus, e. d.s. induced in the spiral is equal to

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{3} \pi R^2 n B_0 \omega \sin \omega t.$$

The image $S_1$ of source $S$ given by lens $A$ is obtained to the right of the lens at twice its focal length (Fig. 18). Indeed, from the lens formula it follows that

$$\frac{1}{2F} + \frac{1}{f_1} = \frac{1}{F},$$

whence $f_1 = 2F$. This means that the lens $B$ is hit by a converging beam of rays. This beam of rays is located at point $S_2$ - the image of the source.

For lens $B$, points $S_1$ are a minisource. Therefore (see Fig. 18),

$$-\frac{1}{d_2} + \frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{F},$$

where $d_2 = F$. From here

$$f_2 = \frac{F}{2}.$$
