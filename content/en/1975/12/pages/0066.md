For schoolchildren about solid state physics

The physics editors of the Prosveshcheniye publishing house began publishing a series of books under the general title “For schoolchildren about modern physics.” This was done in a very timely manner, since the secondary school switched to a new physics program, in which the weight of the sections of modern physics was replaced and the scientific level of the entire course was raised.

In 1974, the first book in this series was published, which talked about some fundamental problems of modern physics. The solution to this book was published in the journal «Kvant»*). A second book dedicated to solid state physics** has recently been published. Solid state physics in our time has turned into a vast, branched science. Each direction of this science has great theoretical significance and broad practical applications. It is almost impossible to indicate a branch of industry where the achievements of modern physics are not applied

The properties of semiconductors provide a wide range of possibilities for the use of semiconductors in technology. A lot of such examples could be given.

In the school course, physicists consider some issues in solid state physics. But it is very difficult to talk about the current state of solid-state physics in a way that is understandable to a schoolchild. However, this difficult task was largely successfully solved by the authors of the articles included in the book.

The book contains six articles devoted to the most important problems of solid state physics, and one article telling about some features of the connection between modern physics and mathematics.

The book begins with the articles by A. F. Andreev, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, senior researcher at the Institute of Physical Problems of the USSR Academy of Sciences, professor at the Moscow Physical Institute, “Superfluidity of a liquid gel.” It talks about the phenomenon of superfluidity of liquid gel, discovered in 1938 by the leading Soviet physicist, Academician P. L. Kapitsa.

From this article, readers will learn not only about the amazing properties of liquid gel II, but also why a gel cannot be turned into a solid even at ultra-low temperatures and normal pressure.

The extraordinary properties that liquid gels exhibit at temperatures close to absolute zero are not the only remarkable phenomena in this temperature region. The second unique phenomenon characteristic of some metals and alloys at low temperatures is superconductivity. This phenomenon is in the book “For Schoolchildren about Modern Physics”. Solid state physics is given special attention. In the article “On Superconductivity,” written by Doctor of Physics and Mathematics, Professor V.Z. Kresny, he talks about how in the physical laboratory of Leiden University, the Dutch scientist G. Kamerlingh-Onnes discovered an amazing property of mercury in 1911: at a temperature close to 4°K, the electronic resistance of this metal abruptly turns to zero. This is how one of the most interesting and important areas in modern solid state physics arose.

The phenomenon of superconductivity is observed only at very low temperatures. Currently, it has been possible to achieve a transition to the superconducting state of the Nb₃Ge alloy at a temperature of T=22.3°K. This is the highest transition temperature yet. The article describes the phenomenon of superconductivity, discusses the directions of searches for high-temperature superconductivity, and examines the influence of magnetic fields on superconductivity. With great interest, readers will become familiar with already existing superconductors, as well as with the connection of superconductivity with other areas of physics (superconducting states of atomic nuclei, superconductivity and neutron stars, superconductivity and biophysics).
