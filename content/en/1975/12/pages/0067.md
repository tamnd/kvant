Like electrons in atoms, nucleons in nuclei (protons and neutrons) are at specific energy levels. Transitions of nucleons from one level to another cause the emission or absorption of gamma rays by nuclei. In 1958, the young German physicist R. Mössbauer studied the resonant absorption of $\gamma$ rays. The quantum energy of these rays is equal to the difference in the energy levels of the nucleons. Therefore, such absorption is called resonant. Mössbauer discovered that if the absorption or emission of $\gamma$-rays occurs in a crystal lattice where the atomic nuclei are tightly bound, then nuclear recoil does not occur. (If a person jumps into the water from a ship, then the ship does not experience recoil, since the mass of the ship is much greater than the mass of the person. If a person were jumping from a boat, then the recoil would be noticeable.) It turned out that under the conditions of the Mössbauer effect, the spectral lines of emission and absorption of $\gamma$ rays are very narrow. This allows the Mössbauer effect to be used to measure very small differences in energy and frequency with enormous precision. The article by V. A. Belyakov talks about the possibility of using the Mössbauer effect in various fields of physics and chemistry (observation of effects in the theory of relativity, studying the structure of complex molecules of solids, etc.).

Kaganov, in the article “Magnetic Properties of Matter,” fascinatingly talks about modern views on the magnetic properties of solids. rons, protons and neutrons, the author gradually moves on to the magnetic properties of atoms, molecules and crystals. The article contains a presentation of the nature of paramagnetism and exchange interaction. When studying the magnetic properties of matter, the greatest difficulty for schoolchildren is understanding the properties and physical nature of ferromagnetism. In the article by Professor M.I. Kaganov, they will not only find an accessible and modern explanation of these issues, but will also become acquainted with antiferromagnetism, the behavior of substances with magnetic properties near absolute zero temperatures, and many other interesting problems.

Article by candidate of physical and mathematical sciences, associate professor of the Moscow Pedagogical Institute. V. I. Lenin Yu. A. Gurvich is dedicated to semiconductors. In the school physics course, the study of the electrical properties of semiconductors is given a prominent place. Therefore, the content of this article, to a greater extent than the materials of other articles, will seem familiar to readers. However, the article “Semiconductors” significantly expands the information that schoolchildren receive in the compulsory physics course. All properties of intrinsic and impurity semiconductors are considered on the basis of the band theory of solids, analysis of the nature of the arrangement and filling of energy levels in crystals with electrons. This approach allowed the author to consider all the basic properties of electron and hole semiconductors and talk about their most important applications.

It's called "Lasers". These optical quantum light generators were created in 1954-1955 by Soviet scientists academicians N. G. Basov and A. M. Prokhorov at the Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Moscow and the American scientist Charles Townes at Columbia University in New York. The article by A. A. Samokhin significantly expands and deepens the information presented in the school textbook. It talks about the physical principles of laser operation, their main types, mode of operation, as well as the numerous and ever-expanding possibilities for using lasers in a wide variety of fields of science and technology.

The last, seventh article is called “Physics and Mathematics”. It was written by a candidate of physical and mathematical sciences, senior researcher at the Institute of Atomic Energy. I. V. Kurchatova V. P. Smilgoy. Thematically, this article is not related to solid state physics. However, it is directly related to the entire series of books “For Schoolchildren about Modern Physics”. The article talks about how mathematics and modern physics are intertwined in our time, the role of mathematics in the achievements of physics, the importance of mathematical beauty in physical theories, and much more. The article is masterfully written, using examples from eight specific stories covering a large period of time.

Let's summarize. With great pedagogical tact, at an accessible and at the same time serious level, schoolchildren are told about the most complex issues of modern physics. One can only wish that the next books in this series will bring the same benefit to students as those already published.
