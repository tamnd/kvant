Indeed, if $\frac{\sin \alpha_j}{c_j} \neq \frac{\sin \alpha_{j+1}}{c_{j+1}}$ for some $j$, then according to Fermat’s principle, light cannot propagate along such a broken line: the vertex of the broken line at the boundary of the corresponding media can be moved so (without changing the other vertices) that the total time spent by light decreases.

If in some inhomogeneous optical medium the speed of light changes continuously, but in such a way that at horizontal points (i.e. at points with the same ordinates) it is the same: $c(y)$ (the value $y = 0$ corresponds to the initial position of the point from which the beam emerges), then by passing to the limit we obtain that in this medium the path of light propagation between two points is a curve $L$ such that

$$\frac{\sin \alpha(y)}{c(y)} = \text{const},$$

where $\alpha(y)$ denotes the angle that the tangent drawn to the curve $L$ at a point with ordinate $y$ makes with the vertical.

To move on to the problem of the brachistochrone, then now that we obtained correspondence (1) from Fermat’s principle, using only the fact that at a fixed point in our inhomogeneous medium the speed of light is fixed and does not depend on the direction of light propagation (in our examples it was built on horizontals).
