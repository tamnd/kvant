Point, straight... - what is it?
E. Semenov

In «Kvant» No. 11 he talks about a fantastic situation - in one of the Moscow schools, Marsinin Inko flew in for a geometry lesson. The guys could not “tell” him what a point or a straight line is, and only with the help of the axioms of belonging did he begin to understand the properties of these concepts. In this issue, we invite you to continue your acquaintance with the properties of points and lines - we present two more groups of axioms and problems, by solving which you can check whether you understood these axioms correctly.

1. Distance from one point to another

Distance axims

Axima II₁. For any two points A and B, there is a non-negative quantity |AB|, called the distance from A to B. The distance is zero if and only if point A and B coincide.

Axima II₂. The distance from point A to point B is equal to the distance from point B to point A: |AB|=|BA|.

Axima II₃. For any three points A, B, C, the distance from A to C is not greater than the sum of the distances from A to B and from B to C: |AC|≤|AB|+|BC|.

Axims II₁-₃ define the concept of distance from one point to another. When solving problems, we will use notations of the form “distance (II₁)” (a distance described only by axiom II₁), “distance (II₁,₂)” (a distance described by axioms II₁ and II₂), “point (II₁)” (a point described by axiom II₁), etc.
