To the article “Point, straight line... - what is it?”
(see «Kvant», No. 11)

1. θ) Sasha has six lines (I₁): {θ, u, κ, e}, {o, u, e, n, b}, {n, o, n, p, a, θ, u, l, c, i}, {e, o, p, ∂}, ∅, {κ, u, e, θ}, of which five are different, since the first and last lines (I₁) coincide: {θ, u, κ, e} = {κ, u, e, θ}. Note that in the word “egorod” the letter o appears twice, but in the line (I₁) defined by this word, the point (I₁) is indicated once: {e, o, p, ∂}. The lines {o, u, e, n, b} and {e, o, p, ∂} have a single common point o. The lines {e, o, p, ∂} and {κ, u, e, θ} do not have common points (they are “parallel”). Various Sasha points: θ, u, κ, e, o, u, n, b, n, p, a, l, c, i, e, ∂.

2. Kolya indicated 4 different straight lines, since the non-toronic sets he wrote are equal (consist of the same elements): {a, b} = {b, a}, {a, θ} = {θ, a}, {b, θ} = {θ, b} {a, b, θ} = {b, θ, a}, = {θ, b, a}.

2. In the first example given by Marcinin, the sets {1}, {2}, {3}, {1, 2}, {1, 3}, {2, 3}, ∅ or {1}, {2}, {3}, {1, 2, 3}, ∅ can be considered straight lines (I₁,₂). For example, it is impossible to consider the sets {1, 2} and {1, 2, 3} as lines (I₁,₂) at the same time, since then it turns out that there are different lines containing points 1 and 2.

For example, for Sasha’s points o and p, there are two different straight lines containing them: {n, o, n, p, a, θ, u, l, c, i} and {e, o, p, ∂}. Thus, the axiom I₂ for Sasha’s “straight lines” is not satisfied, therefore it is impossible to consider Sasha’s lines as straight lines (I₁,₂).
