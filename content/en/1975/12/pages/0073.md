Conclusion

We looked at several simple models in which other aximons are fulfilled. There are many models in which all aximons of geometry are satisfied. This is explained by the fact that the point is not a stick, but a straight line - not a tight thread, mentally extended indefinitely in both directions. Point and straight lines can be called any objects for which the properties specified in the groups by the axim are satisfied. For example, by a point we can mean a pair of numbers $(x, y)$, and by a line we can mean any set of pairs $(x, y)$ that are a solution to the equation $ax + by + c = 0$.

So, a point and a straight line are not geometric concepts whose properties are described in axims. This could be your short answer to the teacher during the exam.

— But what about images of points and lines using the method and a pencil? - you ask. Well, this is all about us and depicts both points and straight lines, which helps in our reasoning. And that's all. But in no case are points or straight lines. And who knows, maybe on some distant planet students depict points and lines in a completely different way and do not know that they can be depicted the way they are done here in “earthly” schools.
