If we consider the sun's rays to be approximately parallel, then the optical property of a parabola (Fig. 10) can be used to focus them. However, the relatively small arc of a very elongated ellipse is practically no different from other parabolas.

Parabolic reflectors (reflectors) are also used in modern telecopes. Of course, in this case, the light from a distant star is focused not for the purpose of heating, but so that the star can be seen (for example, so that the light collected by the reflector is enough to appear on photographic film).

If in solar installations and telecopes light coming from a distant source (practically “from infinity”) is collected at the focus, then in a projector it is the other way around: the light from a powerful lamp placed at the focus, after reflection from a parabolic reflector, leaves in a parallel beam of rays (black lines on Fig. 11)*). If the lamp is slightly removed from the mirror, then the reflector acts like an elliptical one - you get an “almost” converging beam of rays (red lines). Approaching the lamp to the mirror gives an example of the same pattern of rays as in hyperbolia

Finally, we indicate one more - this time mechanical - application of the optical property. figure 12 shows the ellipse $E_1$ and the ellipse $E_2$, symmetrical to it with respect to the tangent $l$. From the drawing it is clear that points $A, M$ and $C$ lie on the same straight line, and

$$|AC| = |AM| + |MC| = |AM| + |MB|,$$

that is, the distance between points $A$ and $C$ is equal to the greater axis of the ellipse. Let us now fix the ellipses at points $A$ and $C$ so that they can rotate around these points. If you rotate the ellipse $E_1$ around the point $A$, then for each of its positions there is a point $M$ at which the ellipse intersects the segment $AC$; By drawing a tangent at point $M$, we find the only corresponding position of the ellipse $E_2$. In other words, the ellipse $E_2$ will also rotate (around the point $C$), carried away by the ellipse $E_1$. This is the basis of the elliptical gear structure: it converts the uniform rotation of the ellipse $E_1$ around the point $A$ into the uneven rotation of the ellipse $E_2$ around the point $C$.
