**M319**. *A circle $\gamma$ and a point $P$ inside it are given on the plane. Consider the set of tetrahedra $ABCD$, in which all four faces are congruent, and the triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in the circle $\gamma$ such that its medians intersect at the point $P$.*

*a) At what position of the point $P$ inside $\gamma$ do such tetrahedra exist?*

*b) Prove that the vertices $D$ of such tetrahedra are located in one of two fixed points in space (symmetrical with respect to a given plane).*

◆ We will call tetrahedra whose faces are all congruent $K$-tetrahedra. Let us establish several properties of such tetrahedra.

$1^\circ$. *The sum of three plane angles at each vertex of a $K$-tetrahedron is $180^\circ$.*

Indeed, these three angles are congruent to the angles of the same triangle $ABC$, opposite its three different sides $AB$, $BC$, $CA$ (this reasoning is also valid for triangles $ABC$, in which some two sides are equal in length), and the sum of the angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$ (Fig. 3).

$2^\circ$. *The faces of the $K$-tetrahedron are acute triangles.*

In fact, in any trihedral angle, each plane angle is less than the sum of the other two. For the angles of a triangle, this condition is satisfied if and only if it is acute.

$3^\circ$. *The development of the $K$-tetrahedron has the form of an acute triangle, in which the middle lines are drawn.*

This follows immediately from $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ (Fig. 4). Of course, the converse is also true: *any acute-angled triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ with midlines $AB$, $BC$, $CA$ drawn in it can be considered as a development of a $K$-tetrahedron.* (Think why it is impossible to form a tetrahedron from an obtuse $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$.)

Note that when we add a tetrahedron from the development, fixing the face $ABC$ and rotating the other three faces relative to the edges $AB$, $BC$, $CA$, then the points $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$ will move along circles (lying in planes perpendicular to $ABC$), and their projections onto the plane $ABC$ will move along the altitudes of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ up to the point of their intersection $H$. So, we also got the following property:

$4^\circ$. *The projection $H$ of the vertex $D$ of the $K$-tetrahedron $ABCD$ onto the plane $ABC$ lies at the point of intersection of the altitudes of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$.*

Note that in any tetrahedron its “medians” - the segments connecting the vertices with the centers of gravity (the intersection points of the medians) of opposite faces - intersect at one point M - the “center of gravity” of the tetrahedron - and are divided by this point in the ratio $3:1$, counting from the vertex (Fig. 5; the segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges also pass through the same point M).

$5^\circ$. *In a $K$-tetrahedron, the center of gravity coincides with the center of the circumscribed sphere.*
