sy. If such a surface, called a rotation ellipsode, is covered from the inside with a mirror layer, and an exact light source (“sun”) is placed at one of the focuses, then you will see that if you are inside the ellipson, you will see two “suns”.
In fact, turning your gaze to the first focus, you will directly see the “sun” placed there. But, looking in the direction of the second focus (where in reality there is nothing), he will also see the “sun” (Fig. 8). And no matter how the observed ellipson moves inside, two “suns” will shine on it almost directly.
A similar picture can be observed inside an elliptical ellipson, I decided to take measures that would rid it of the “mine sun”, and placed a small opaque body (“screen”) at the second focus, blocking the path of reflection of the rays. The result turns out to be somewhat unexpected: now all the rays emanating from the “sun” A, after reflection from the ellipson, are collected (“focused”) on the “screen” B, and this can cause its intense heating. And note that for such focusing it is not necessary to have a mirror ellipse as a target, but only part of its surface can be used.

Fig. 10.

And what will the “non-windowed” observer see at the second focus B of the mirror ellipson, if a light source is placed at its first focus A? All rays emanating from point A and reflected from the ellipson end up at point B. Thus, for the observed scale, the ellipsoid will glow.

You can see an illustration of this phenomenon on the first page of the cover - it is used as a light source from the “mine sun”, and a small opaque body (“screen”) is placed in the second focus, blocking the path of reflections of the rays. The result is unexpected: now all the rays emanating from the “sun” A, after reflection from the ellipson, are collected (“focused”) on the “screen” B, and this can cause its intense heating. And note that for such focusing it is not necessary to have a mirror ellipse as a target, but only part of its surface can be used.

If, for example, you make an elliptical elliptical reflector, in one focus of which is the Sun (real!), and in the other - a boiler with water, then you can achieve boiling of water in the boiler due to the radiation of the Sun focused by the reflector.

There is nothing surprising in this: after all, the reflector has a large area, and from its entire surface solar energy is directed to heating the boiler. Such solar devices have some applications. And in the future (who knows?), maybe, by building a huge elliptical reflector (Fig. 9), use the energy of the rays that pass through the Earth?
