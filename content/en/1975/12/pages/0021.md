## Optical properties of ellipse, hyperbola and parabola
V. G. Boltyansky

The well-known problem is to find a point $M$ on the line $l$ for which the sum of the distances from two given points $A$, $B$ lying on the same side of the line $l$ is the smallest. The required point will be the intersection of the segment $A_1B$ with the straight line $l$, where $A_1$ is a point symmetrical to $A$ relative to the straight line $l$ (Fig. 1). In fact,
$$|AM| + |MB| = |A_1M| + |MB| = |A_1B|,$$
whereas for any other point $M'$ of the line $l$ we have
$$|AM'| + |M'B| = |A_1M'| + |M'B| > |A_1B|.$$
In other words,
$$|AM'| + |M'B| > |AM| + |MB|. \quad (1)$$

All this is good, an impatient reader may object, but this task is simple and well known. And what does all this have to do with an ellipse or a parabola? A moment of patience! Let's take another look at the drawing:

$\angle 1 \cong \angle 3$, and since, in addition, $\angle 2 \cong \angle 3$, then $\angle 1 \cong \angle 2$. Therefore, from the solution of the problem considered, the following statement follows:

*Let $A$ and $B$ be two points lying on the same side of the line $l$, and $M$ be a point on the line $l$ such that the segments $AM$ and $MB$ form equal angles with the line $l$. Then for any point $M'$ of the line $l$, different from $M$, inequality* (1) is valid.

Now we are sufficiently “armed” to consider the *optical properties of the ellipse*. Let us take an arbitrary point $M$ (Fig. 2) on the ellipse having foci $A$ and $B$. For any point $N$ belonging to the ellipse, the equality is true

$$|AN| + |BN| = |AM| + |BM|, \text{*)}$$

and for the point $N$ lying inside the ellipse, the sum $|AN| + |BN|$ will be less than $|AM| + |BM|$. Let's construct a straight line $l$ passing through $M$ and forming equal angles with the segments $AM$ and $BM$. If now $M'$ is an arbitrary point of the line $l$, different from $M$, then, as we have seen, the sum of $|AM'| + |BM'|$ will be greater than $|AM| + |BM|$, and therefore the point $M'$ lies outside the ellipse.
