1. "Knight's Spurs"

F. Klein was born in 1849 in Düsseldorf. Here he graduated from high school; in 1865 he entered the University of Bonn. The very next year, Professor Julius Plücker (1801–1868) hired a seventeen-year-old student as an assistant in physics. Plücker began his scientific career as a geometer, but gradually switched to experimental physics. However, in the last years of his life, after a long break, Plücker returned to geometry. “This turn played a decisive role in my own development,” wrote Klein. A posthumous edition of Plücker's last memoir (1869) was prepared by Klein. Perhaps this is the last reason that his dissertation (1868), with which, according to Klein himself, he “earned knightly spurs,” and his first publication (1869) were geometric.

Having only become a teacher, Klein becomes a “knight errant.” He visits the main mathematical centers of Germany (Gettigren, Berlin), establishes personal contacts with Clebsch, Weber, Weierstrass. A promising young scientist who wants to learn is immediately noticed. Klein's contacts with his peers are no less important. Klein's friend was especially happy with the great Norwegian Sophus Lie (1842-1899); they met in 1870 in Berlin. S. Lee was seven years older than Klein, but in 1870 he took only his first steps in geometry.
