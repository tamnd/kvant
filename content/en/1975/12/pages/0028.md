⟦folio 26⟧

Fig. 4.

The parabola has the form
$$z = \frac{r^2}{4f} - f,$$
(1)

where $f$ is the focal length, and $r$ and $z$ are the “abscissa” and “ordinate” of an arbitrary point of the parabola. The incoming ray, “meeting” the paraboly at point $a'$ at a distance $r'$ from the axis of symmetry, will be reflected, pass through the focus and again meet the paraboly at point $a''$ at a distance $r''$ from the axis $OZ$ (see Fig. 3). Let us denote by $\theta$ the angle between $OZ$ and the “intermediate” segment $a'a''$ of our ray. The equation of the straight line $a'a''$ is written as
$$z = -r \text{ ctg } \theta.$$ (2)

The points $a'$ and $a''$ belong simultaneously to both the parabola (1) and the straight line (2). Therefore, for them we can equate the right-hand sides of (1) and (2):
$$r^2 + 4fr \text{ ctg } \theta - 4f^2 = 0.$$ (3)

Solving this equation, we find:
$$r_1 = -2f \text{ ctg } \frac{\theta}{2}, \quad r_2 = 2f \text{ tg } \frac{\theta}{2}.$$ (4)

The roots $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the $r$ coordinate values ​​for the point $a'$ and $a''$. Thus, $r' = |r_1|, r'' = r_2$. From (4) it follows
$$r'r'' = 4f^2.$$ (5)

Formula (5) can be obtained using Vieta’s theorem without solving equation (3). This result confirms the conclusion obtained above from qualitative considerations: the distances of the parabolic and emerging rays from the $OZ$ axis are inversely proportional to each other.
