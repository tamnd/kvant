**Area of ​​triangle and quadrilateral**

Problem 13. *Prove that lines drawn through the vertices of a convex quadrilateral parallel to its diagonals bound a parallelogram whose area is twice the area of ​​the quadrilateral.*

Let $ABCD$ be the given quadrilateral (Fig. 6). To solve the problem, it is enough to note that the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ of the quadrilateral divide the described parallelogram $EFKL$ into four parallelograms, and the diagonal of each parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles.

Problem 14. Using the result of Problem 13, prove that the area of ​​any convex quadrilateral is equal to half the product of the lengths of its diagonals and the sine of the angle between them.

Note that any triangle is divided by a median into two equal triangles. Does the midline of a quadrilateral have a similar property?

Problem 15. Prove that the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ is divided by the midline $MN$ connecting the midpoints of the sides $AB$ and $CD$ into two equal quadrilaterals if and only if the sides $AB$ and $CD$ are parallel.

Problem 16. Given a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$. The points $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of its sides $AB$ and $CD$. Prove that the area of ​​the quadrilateral $AMCN$ is equal to half the area of ​​the quadrilateral $ABCD$.

Problem 17. The midlines of a quadrilateral divide it into four quadrilaterals. Prove that the sum of the areas of one pair of non-adjacent quadrilaterals is equal to the sum of the areas of the other pair.

Problem 18. *Prove that the area of ​​a convex quadrilateral is twice the area of ​​a quadrilateral whose vertices are the midpoints of the given sides.*

We present two solutions to this problem.

First solution. Let $E$, $F$, $K$, $L$ be the midpoints of the sides of the given quadrilateral $ABCD$ (Fig. 7). The midline $EF$ of triangle $ABC$ cuts off triangle $BEF$ from it, the area of ​​which is $\frac{1}{4}$ of the area of ​​triangle $ABC$: $S_{\triangle BEF} = \frac{1}{4} S_{\triangle ABC}$. Likewise, $S_{\triangle DLK} = \frac{1}{4} S_{\triangle ACD}$. Adding these equalities term by term, we get:

$$S_{\triangle BEF} + S_{\triangle DLK} = \frac{1}{4} S_{ABCD}.$$

In the same way we will prove that

$$S_{\triangle AEL} + S_{\triangle CKF} = \frac{1}{4} S_{ABCD}.$$

Thus, the sum of the areas of the triangles cut off from the quadrilateral $ABCD$ by the sides of the parallelogram $EFKL$ is equal to $\frac{1}{2} S_{ABCD}$.

Therefore, $S_{EFKL} = \frac{1}{2} S_{ABCD}$.
