Let us first explain this fact using visual considerations. Let $OZ$ be the axis of symmetry, $r'$, $r''$ the distances from the axis to the incident and corresponding outgoing ray (Fig. 3). The farther from the axis $OZ$ the beam falls, the closer to it it comes out, that is, the more $r'$, the less $r''$. Therefore, the energy of the “peripheral” regions of the incoming beam is “transferred” to the axis of the outgoing one. If the incident beam is wide enough (large radius of the mirror), then this will lead to a significant concentration of energy along the axis. As a result, the energy comes out practically in the form of a narrow “light cord”, and the wider the incoming beam, the narrower the outgoing cord.

So, we come to the conclusion: a paraboloid can concentrate energy not only at the focus, but also along the axis of symmetry.
