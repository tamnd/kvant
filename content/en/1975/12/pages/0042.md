There is a displacement of space in which the tetrahedron $ABCD$ is juxtaposed with itself such that the face $DCB$ becomes $ABC$ (i.e. $A—D$, $B—C$, $C—B$, $D—A$). During this movement, only two points of the tetrahedron surface remain motionless: these are the midpoints of the edges $AD$ and $BC$. It is clear that with such a movement, the center of the circumscribed ball $O$ and the intersection point of the medians $M$ are motionless (since the tetrahedron itself is combined with itself). If points $M$ and $O$ are different, then all points of the line $MO$ must also be fixed. This means that this line passes through the midpoints of the edges $AD$ and $BC$. In exactly the same way (considering other self-alignments of the tetrahedron) we will prove that the line $MO$ also passes through the midpoints of other edges. But this is impossible. This means that points $M$ and $O$ coincide.

Let us denote the center of gravity of the triangle $ABC$ by $P$, the center of its circumcircle (projection of the point $O=M$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$) by $Q$, and the projection of the vertex $D$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$ by $H$. figure 6 shows all these points lying in a plane perpendicular to the face $ABC$ and passing through the center of the ball $O$ and the vertex $D$; $E$ and $F$ are the points of intersection of this plane with the circumscribed circle of the triangle $ABC$, the radius of which will be denoted by $\rho$. Since $|DP| = 4|OP|$, $|OF|^2 = |OQ|^2 + \rho^2 = |OD|^2 = 9|OQ|^2 + |QH|^2$, we get

$$|QH|^{\mathbf{2}} + 8|OQ|^{\mathbf{2}} = \rho^{\mathbf{2}}.$$

Let us formulate the property we need:

6°. *In any K-tetrahedron* $|QH| = 3|PQ|$ and $|QH|^2 + |DH|^2/2 = \rho^2$ (since $|DH| = 4|OQ|$).

Now it is easy to solve both points problem M319.

a) The set of centers of gravity of acute triangles inscribed in a given circle $\gamma$ with center $Q$ and radius $\rho$ will be the interior of a concentric circle of radius $\rho/3$. (Prove this using figure 7 and the fact that for a right triangle the center of gravity is exactly at a distance $\rho/3$ from the center of the hypotenuse $Q$.) According to 2° and 3°, this is exactly the set of positions that point $P$ can occupy.

b) According to 6°, for a given circle $\gamma$ and a given position of point $P$ the position of the projection $H$ of the vertex $D$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$ and its distance $|DH|$ from this plane are uniquely determined.

In conclusion, let us make a few comments.

I. For those who want to understand in detail how the “symmetry group” of the $K$-tetrahedron (found in the proof of 5°) is structured, we give advice to consider a parallelepiped, the non-adjacent vertices of which are the vertices of the tetrahedron (Fig. 8). *For a K-tetrahedron, this parallelepiped is rectangular*. From this we can obtain a number of other characteristic properties of $K$-tetrahedra (see solution problem M9 in «Kvant» No. 9, 1970).
