⟦folio 56⟧

One geometric inequality

In «Kvant» No. 7 for this year (p. 65) the following sentence was expressed:

$$\frac{9}{20} (ab + bc + ca) \leq m_a m_b + m_b m_c + m_c m_a,$$

where $a, b, c, m_a, m_b, m_c$ are the lengths of the sides and the corresponding medians of an arbitrary triangle.

Below is a “geometric” proof of this statement*).

Lemma 1. $m_a + m_b + m_c < a + b + c$.

Lemma 2. $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 < 2(ab + ac + bc)$.

Lemma 3. $m_a^2 + m_b^2 + m_c^2 = \frac{3}{4} (a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$.

Prove these lemmas yourself.

Lemma 4. $m_a m_b + m_b m_c + m_a m_c \leq \frac{5}{4} (ab + bc + ac)$.

Prove this lemma yourself.

According to Lemma 1, $(m_a + m_b + m_c)^2 < (a + b + c)^2$, that is, $m_a^2 + m_b^2 + m_c^2 + 2m_a m_b + 2m_b m_c + 2m_a m_c < (a^2 + b^2 + c^2) + 2ab + 2bc + 2ac$.

*) The issue had already been sent to the printing house when the editor received a letter from Peter Takch from the town of Safarnikovo (Czechoslovakia) with a purely algebraic proof.

whence, by Lemmas 3 and 2:

$$2m_a m_b + 2m_b m_c + 2m_a m_c < \frac{5}{4} (a^2 + b^2 + c^2), \quad \leq \frac{5}{2} (ab + bc + ac),$$

Q.E.D.

Lemma 5. If we construct medians in a triangle with sides $\frac{1}{3} m_a, \frac{1}{3} m_b$ and $\frac{1}{3} m_c$, then their lengths will be equal to $\frac{1}{4} a$, $\frac{1}{4} b$ and $\frac{1}{4} c$.

Proof. Let $G$ be the centrifuges of the triangle $ABC, AM, BK$ and $CP$ the medians, $R$ the midpoint of the segment $BG$.

By construction, $|RG| = \frac{1}{3} m_b, |PG| = \frac{1}{3} m_c$, and $PR$ is the middle line in triangle $ABG$, whence $|PR| = \frac{1}{2} |AG| = \frac{1}{3} m_a$. Thus, triangle $PRG$ is the desired one. Let us draw the median $PQ$ in it. It is the midline of the triangle $ABK$, and therefore $|PQ| = \frac{1}{2} |AK| = \frac{1}{4} a$.

The statement of the lemma is proven.

Now applying Lemma 4 to the triangle $PRG$, we obtain the required inequality.

A. Reznikov
