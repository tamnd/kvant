We just have to repeat: don’t rush to finish your homework and close your textbook and notebook. Look at the task again: what did they tell you that was new? Is there a particular fact hidden in them or does it allow for generalization? what tasks were solved similarly? Is it possible to state the problem without changing the method of solution? But what happens if we get rid of some of the conditions of the problem? or replace them with their opposite? strengthen the requirements of the task? add new restrictions? All this is a great way to set in motion the imagination, agreement and curiosity that may be dormant in you.

Exercises
1. Find at what coefficients $a_1, b_1, \dots, c_3$ the equation
$$\frac{1}{a_1x + b_1y + c_1z} + \frac{1}{a_2x + b_2y + c_2z} + \frac{1}{a_3x + b_3y + c_3z} = 0$$
takes the form $Ax^2 + By^2 + Cz^2 = 0$.

2. Solve for number purposes the equation $6x^2 = 2y^2 + z^2$. Try to find other equations like $Ax^2 + By^2 + Cz^2 = 0$ that can be solved for number purposes and how to solve them.

3. Try to find equations like $Ax^2 + By^2 + Cz^2 = 0$ that are not solvable for number purposes (not counting the zero solution).

4. Solve the equation $xyz = yzv + xzv + xyv$ for number purposes.

5. Come up with a generalization of the following problem: “It is known that for any values ​​of $x$
$$a_1x^2 + b_1x + c_1 > a_2x^2 + b_2x + c_2$$.
Prove that $a_1 \geq a_2$, $c_1 \geq c_2»$.

6. Come up with a generalization of the following problem: “Find the sum of all irreducible fractions with value 3 between the positive integers $m$ and $n > m$.”

7. The same for the problem: “Find an arithmetic programming for which the arithmetic mean $n$ of the first terms for any $n$ is equal to their number.”

Quests
1. At figure you see two tangent circles $l_1$ and $l_2$ of radii $R$ and $r = R/2$ corresponding $AB \perp O_1O_2$, $BC \perp O_1O_2$, tangent to circle $l_1$, $AC \perp$ tangent to circle $l_2$.

Prove that the length of the segment $BC$ is equal to the length of the circle $l_1$ with a relative error of less than 0.005.

L. M. Dubinsky

2. Given a parabola—the graph of the function $y = x^2$. On the axis $Oy$ the point $A$ is taken. Find the shortest distance from point $A$ to the parabola.

You can use the following definition: a parabola is a set of points equidistant from some point $F$ and some line $l$.

M. L. Preezer

3. Prove that numbers of the form
$$7^{2n+1} + 2^{2n+1} + 5,$$
where $n$ is a natural number and cannot be represented as the sum of three cubes of natural numbers.

N. I. Mikhailov
