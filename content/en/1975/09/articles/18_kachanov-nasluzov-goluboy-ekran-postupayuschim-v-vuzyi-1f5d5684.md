## Blue screen for university applicants

In September, classes begin at the Moscow preparatory television courses, created in 1971 by Central Television together with the Ministry of Higher and Secondary Special Education of the USSR.
Every year, telecourses receive tens of thousands of listeners. The experience of the courses shows that the majority of students who study systematically and conscientiously during the academic year enter higher education institutions.
This note describes how the work of preparatory telecourses will be organized in the 1975/1976 academic year.

Television classes are conducted throughout the academic year in three disciplines: mathematics, physics and Russian language. The main goal of teleclasses is to systematize the knowledge of students, bring them into line with the requirements for entrance exams, and create conditions conducive to the development and strengthening of independent work skills necessary both for successful study at a university and for the future activities of every specialist, engineer, and scientist.

The preparation of educational television programs and the organization of classes together with the Main Editorial Board of popular science and educational programs of Central Television is carried out: in the departments of mathematics and physics - the Moscow Engineering Physics Institute, in the department of the Russian language - Moscow State University. M. V. Lomonosov. Along with these universities, the Moscow Economic and Statistical Institute, the Moscow Energy Institute, the Moscow Institute of Geodesy, Cartography and Aerial Photography Engineers and other Moscow universities take part in the courses. The work of telecourses is supervised by the Council of Rectors of Higher Educational Institutions of Moscow.

Telecourses mainly take the lecture form, but a significant part of the telelessons is devoted to practical exercises, consultations and review programs. Television classes are conducted by leading scientists and teachers of Moscow universities; educational programs are accompanied by demonstrations of experiments, physical instruments, showing fragments from films, photographs, etc.

To consolidate knowledge, students are regularly offered homework, consisting of problems, examples and texts of varying difficulty. As a rule, the assignments correspond to the topics of the lectures; the completion of some assignments requires the use of knowledge acquired during the entire previous period of classes. In the coming academic year, homework assignments will be published in the weekly review of Central Television and Radio programs “Moscow Speaks and Shows.”

Students' answers to homework assignments are checked by course instructors. The results of the check are entered into a card index, after which postcards with answers are returned to the addressees. In addition, tests on covered topics are regularly published throughout the year and quizzes are conducted during television programs.

In the 1975/1976 academic year, as in previous years, telecourses will conduct full-time tests: in winter (approximately January - February) and spring (late April - May). As the experience of the courses shows, meetings between students and teachers significantly increase the effectiveness of telelearning: future applicants, being in conditions similar to those found during entrance exams, not only test their knowledge, but also undergo a kind of psychological preparation. On the other hand, teachers, as a result of direct contact with television viewers, can determine how the presented material is perceived by listeners, and, if necessary, make appropriate adjustments to their work. Periodic face-to-face consultations are also included.

Servants call for face-to-face tests and consultations; they actively and systematically study on telephones. Those who successfully pass the tests will receive a Certificate of Completion from the Moscow Preparatory Telephones at the end of the academic year.

In the coming school year, ministers will offer two daily programs in mathematics, two in physics and one in the Russian language. For the convenience of students (especially those who work different shifts), these programs will be repeated in the same week, but on different days and at different times.

Classes on telephones will consist of two cycles: the main one (it will be legislated in May 1976) and the defense-consulting one (it will be broadcast in June 1976). In the lessons of the second cycle, they consider issues that cause the most difficulties when studying and repeating educational material.

The reception area for programs “For applicants to universities” in the 1975/76 academic year expanded compared to previous years - these programs will be able to be watched by residents of Moscow, the Belarusian USSR, the Lithuanian USSR, the region - Moscow, Volgodsk, Gorky, Ivanovo, Kalinin, Kaliningrad, Kaluga, Kostroma, Lipetsk, Ryazan, Smolensk, Tula and Yaroslavskaya.

Moscow preparatory telephones accept everyone - both those who have already completed secondary education, and students - graduates of school, vocational schools, and secondary specialized institutions. Admission to telephones is carried out without a competition.

To enroll, the minister must send in one envelope to the course address the “Questionnaire - Application”, “Registration Card” and an envelope with his return address.

The “application form” is drawn up on a general postal card. On the front side of it you should write your address and telephone number (home or office), if available. The reverse (blank) side of the card is filled with answers to questions, a sample of which we provide. All entries must be made parallel to the narrow side of the card. In the upper right corner, look at the letters corresponding to the departments in which you are going to fill out (if you intend to fill out in all three departments, then write three letters: “F” - physics, “M” - mathematics, “R” - Russian language). Under the answers to the questions, write the text of the application for admission (see sample) listing the departments you have chosen, put the date and signature.

The “registration card” is also issued on a postcard. Be sure to write your address on the front side; clean design as indicated on the sample. Glue a photo (size 3 cm × 4 cm) to the upper left corner. The inscription “Registration Card” is written one centimeter below the edge of the postcard. All recordings are performed parallel to the narrow side. For each branch, a separate “Registration Card” is filled out; in the upper right corner, one of the letters “F”, “M”, “R” corresponding to the selected branch is written.

On letters with applications addressed to courses, the minister must write in the upper left corner of the envelope the same letters “F”, “M”, “R”) as in the “Application Questionnaire”.

In an envelope with the minister’s return address, he will be sent a “Notice of Enrollment on Telephones” and “Annotation of Telephone Knowledge.” The “Notification of enrollment on telephones” indicates the number of the code attached to the employee, the “Annotation” contains the lesson program, literature recommendations, guidelines for completing homework and registration rules, tips on how to independently record your success, etc.
