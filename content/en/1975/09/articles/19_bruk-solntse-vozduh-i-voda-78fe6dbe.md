Sun, air and water

Please, these three words: sun, air and water are the main ones in the science of weather - meteorology. Meteorology has long ceased to be a purely descriptive scientific science; now its problems are dealt with by representatives of the exact sciences - geophysics and mathematics.

For example, the study of processes occurring in the atmosphere of our planet is the most important part of geophysical research. Knowledge of the basic laws of air mass movement, the structure and evolution of the atmosphere, the laws of propagation and absorption of electromagnetic and sound waves in it is necessary for sailors and radio operators, pilots and astronomers. But we can say that the main goal of atmospheric physics - one of the branches of modern geophysics - is the systematic study of weather and climate, which in one way or another affects all aspects of our lives.

T. Chandler’s short book “The Air Around Us”* begins with a story about weather observations. It is devoted to describing the physical processes that lead to changes in weather and climate, describing the dynamics of the impact of the shell of our planet. The author talks about the structure of the atmosphere, its chemical composition and temperature distribution over altitude, and the heat balance of the Earth and the atmosphere.

The presentation is structured in such a way that the reader does not require any prior knowledge of atmospheric physics. All special (not too numerous, however) terms are explained in detail, with the main attention being paid to their physical meaning. For example, the concept of albedo of various surfaces (the so-called fraction of solar energy reflected by the surface) is explained in detail. The reader learns that about 19% of solar energy is absorbed as it passes through the atmosphere, about 34% is reflected back into space from cloud tops and the earth's surface, and 47% reaches and is absorbed by the surface of our planet.

The absorption of solar energy leads to the continuous movement of the earth's atmosphere. Complex movements of air masses, characteristics at different heights and heights, stability of winds - these issues are among the main ones in atmospheric physics. A full description of them in strict mathematical language is shown in the future. Chandler talks about all this qualitatively, using his story with graphic diagrams and maps.

Describing the types of clouds observed at different altitudes, the author introduces the reader to the theories of evaporation and condensation, convection and the formation of ice crystals. He gives a diagram of the formation of precipitation and talks about various hypotheses and theories in this area. Understanding the physical nature of the processes of evaporation and condensation is a critical prerequisite for planning and implementing a drainage and watering program. The Earth's atmosphere contains over 13 billion tons of moisture. The rate of moisture circulation in the atmosphere is about 16 million tons per second or 505 billion tons per year. Chandler also gives a few more interesting figures. The atmosphere completely renews its moisture supply 36 times a year; in other words, a molecule of water vapor reaches the Earth on average 10 days after evaporation. These calculations are not difficult to make and are very useful.

It is natural to include in the book a section “Synoptic meteorology”, dedicated to the formation of air flows, the interaction of warm and cold air fronts, the formation of cycles and anticycles. At first glance, synoptic maps and diagrams are not so easy to understand. After reading Chandler’s book, these difficulties seem to disappear by themselves, and the reader will be able to draw up simple diagrams of the movement of air flows himself.

The final sections of the book are devoted to climates. It talks about how physical factors are essential for the formation of local climates, how and why local winds blow in coastal and forest areas, how natural conditions (terrain, the presence of reservoirs, etc.) influence the climate.

T. Chandler's book "The Air Around Us" introduces the fascinating and promising field of physics; readers «Kvant» will no doubt read it with great interest and benefit, etc.
