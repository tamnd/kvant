F317. When molecules arise under normal conditions, they collide with existing molecules, and let the distance between the molecules (the length of the free gap) be on average λ = 10⁻⁵ cm. Estimate the size of the air molecules.

The size of a molecule can be estimated if the free gap length and the average number of molecules per unit volume are known.

We will consider the molecules to be elastic balls of radius r. Let us assume that all molecules except one are at rest. A single moving molecule, moving with a certain average speed v, seems to “cut out” a volume of radius 2r in cylindrical space and collides with all the molecules whose centers happen to be of this volume. With each collision, the direction of movement of the molecules changes, and its path is represented by an egg-zag line, shown in figure 16.

The total volume “cut out” of molecules in space in 1 s is equal to 4πr²v. The average number of collisions in 1 s is v = 4πr²vn, where n is the number of molecules per unit volume. Under normal conditions n = 2.7·10¹⁹ cm⁻³.

Now we can write down an expression for the average length of the free gap λ. The average speed of movement of molecules is v, and the average time between events is τ = v/1. That's why

$$\lambda = v\tau = \frac{v}{v} = \frac{1}{4\pi r^2 n}.$$

From here we find the radius of the molecules:

$$r = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi n}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3,14\cdot10^{-5}\cdot2,7\cdot10^{19}}} \approx 1,7\cdot10^{-8} \text{ (см)}.$$

The above calculation does not take into account the movement of molecules, so the result obtained is only an approximate estimate of the size of the molecules. A large calculation, taking into account not only the fact of the movement of all molecules, but also the law of the distribution of molecules by speed, gives a correction of about 20% to this result. Note also that the quantities 2r play an important role in molecular physics and are called the gas kinetic diameter of molecules.

S. M. Kobel
