During the spring holidays of the 1974-75 academic year, the first All-Russian meeting of active student societies was held in Moscow, organized by the Ministry of Education of the RSFSR, the Central Committee of the Komsomol, the All-Union Council of Scientific and Technical Societies and the Board of the Knowledge Society of the RSFSR.

More than 500 schoolchildren took part in the meeting: representatives of school scientific societies, pioneer palaces, small academies and clubs for young science enthusiasts.

On March 24, the doors of the Concert Hall of the Moscow City Palace of Pioneers and Schoolchildren hospitably opened to the participants of the rally. The Minister of Education of the RSFSR A.I. Danilov addressed the participants of the rally with the words he brought. He noted that members of scientific societies are studying only yet scientific creative work. It is important to remember that specialists should succeed only when they strive in science and do not admire themselves, when they are modest in assessing their place and role, and when they devote all their strength and knowledge to serving the people and the Motherland.

The participants of the rally were also greeted by the Secretary of the Komsomol Central Committee 3. G. Novozhilova. She reminded the congregation of the words of Comrade L. I. Brezhnev, spoken by him at the XVII Congress of the Komsomol: “A country where people who began their working life as a roofer’s apprentice and a writer’s student master the heights of knowledge, become human beings! The destinies of Korolev and Gagarin are the clearest example of what wide open spaces and opportunities the social opens up for the working man, for our youth.”

On behalf of the Soviet cosmonauts, the delegates of the meeting were greeted by twice Hero of the Soviet Union, pilot-cosmonaut N. N. Rukavishnikov. He expressed confidence that classes in youth scientific and technical societies bring great benefits to many schoolchildren. This benefit, first of all, is that the children get concrete results and acquire important skills in creative work, learn to work with special scientific literature.

The head of the section of mathematics and cybernetics at the meeting, associate professor of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University and member of the editorial team of the journal «Kvant», candidate of physical and mathematical sciences N. Kh. Rozov, in his speech, cited two aphorisms addressed to outstanding scientists of scientific youth. The first aphorisms belong to I. G. Petrovsky, who was for many years the rector of Moscow State University: “See the Northern Stripe of the modern Austrian physicist L. Boltzmann: “There is nothing more practical than a good theory.”

Let us now move on to the work of the section of mathematics and cybernetics. On March 24, she worked at the Moscow City Palace of Pioneers and Schoolchildren, on March 25 in the morning at the USSR Exhibition of Economic Achievements in the Computer Engineering pavilion and in the afternoon at Moscow Physics and Mathematics School No. 1140, and on March 26 at the Institute of Solid State Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

During this time, 18 reports out of 32 presented by the participants of the meeting were heard. These reports were selected by jury members and section leaders as deserving the greatest attention. All of them were the result of independent work of schoolchildren.

The first group of reports concerned the solution of various problems on a computer.

“Calculating the length of a segment inside a polygon” was the title of the report by Mikhail Antonov (Moscow, s. sh. No. 1140, 10th class). It described a program compiled by the author to solve the following problem: *on a plane in the Cartesian coordinate system, the coordinates of two points through which a line is drawn and the vertices of a convex polygon are given; find the length of a straight line segment contained inside this polygon.* The work was performed on a BESM-4M computer. Antonov’s classmates, German Zubkov (“Calculating a double integral using the Monte Carlo method”) and Lev Drozdov (“Calculating the length of a route passing along a sphere through points specified by polar coordinates”), carried out their work on the same machine.

A number of reports were presented by participants in the mathematics and cybernetics section of the Moscow City Palace of Pioneers and Schoolchildren. This is Boris Mandel (Moscow, s. sh. No. 5, 8th grade, “Solving quadratic equations using the Mir-2 computer”), Stanislav Rachinsky (Moscow, s.sh. No. 149, 8th grade,

“Simulation of a game between a machine and a person” was the title of the report by Alexander Ivashchuk (Omsk, s. sh. No. 125, 9th grade). Sasha compiled a program (for the Razdan-2 computer) for the popular game “tic-tac-toe” on a 6x6 board. The program is based on the same principles as the program for playing chess on a machine (described in «Kvant», 1974, No. 11, 12).

Now let us note two purely mathematical works. Evgeniy Grishin, Anatoly Koromyslov and Vadim Shubin (school of young mathematicians at Perm State University, the guys study in the 7th grade at school No. 65) presented a report “Some properties of geometry on the Mobius cylinder.” It was read by V. Shubin at the meeting. The guys found out that on the Mobius strip there are pairs of points that can be connected by three segments of equal length, as well as some other interesting facts.

Vadim Kaimanovich (Leningrad, Physics School No. 30, 10th grade) read the report “Mathematical laws of money issue.” Vadim applied to the study of money emission (the release of money into circulation) in the period 1918-1921. techniques and methods that are usually used in the mathematical study of physical processes.

Gennady Avidon (Volgograd, s. sh. No. 68, 8th grade) spoke about the application of optimization methods to specific production problems in the report “Three Linear Models.”

Andrey Rusakov (Novosibirsk, school no. 130, 10th grade) spoke about game theory and the application of graph theory to it.

The last two reports are partly abstract in nature, although their authors did a lot of work on their own.

Let us also mention the report of Yuri Bakharev (Chelyabinsk, s. sh. No. 2, 10th grade) “Discrete radio remote control system.”

Four reports presented at the meeting described the work of mathematical societies.

For example, in the town of Malaya Vishera, the mathematical society “Search” for students from school No. 2 has been operating for ten years. This society maintains contacts with scientists from Moscow and Leningrad, and retired teachers also help it. The society has an extensive network of clubs, in whose classes various topics are discussed, and holds quizzes, KVN competitions, and exhibitions. Members of this society regularly win regional mathematical Olympiads.

More than 120 children who are passionate about mathematics are united by the mathematical society “Integral” of students from school No. 17 in Severodvinsk. They are helped by scientists from Moscow and Leningrad, teachers from the Arkhangelsk Pedagogical Institute; The boys themselves give lectures. Every year this society holds a mathematical conference, and in order to receive a membership card for the society, students must read one or two lectures and conduct discussions themselves. At the initiative of the society, after the 9th grade, practice on computers was organized.

At school No. 68 in Prokopyevsk, Kemerovo region, the mathematical society of students holds lectures and seminars, mathematical evenings, school Olympiads, helps equip classrooms, and organizes excursions to the Novosibirsk Academic Town. It is not surprising that representatives of this society successfully perform at city and regional Olympiads.

The Automation and Cybernetics Club at the Gatchina House of Pioneers is supported by the Leningrad Institute of Nuclear Physics. The guys study the theoretical foundations of cybernetics and make visual aids and devices that demonstrate the work of modern enterprises. The works of club members “Industrial Aluminum Production”, “Nuclear Power Plant”, “Electronic-Cybernetic Game “Labyrinth”” were demonstrated at the USSR Exhibition of Economic Achievements.

The work of the mathematics and cybernetics section was not limited to listening to reports. The guys toured the “Computer Technology” pavilion at the USSR Exhibition of Economic Achievements and visited Physics and Mathematics School No. 1140 in Moscow, which, by the way, held a physics and mathematics evening for them. The children were especially interested in visiting the Noginsk Scientific Center of the USSR Academy of Sciences. Inspection of the operating modern research center, its laboratories, computer center - what could be more interesting for future physicists and mathematicians.

The message of Serey Golub (Chelyabinsk, s. sh. No. 69, 10th grade) “Study of the light resistance of polymers” aroused great interest. Currently, polymer materials are widely used in technology and in everyday life. To obtain oxidized polymers during the polymerization process, water-antorganic dyes are added as additives, which affect the physicochemical properties of the polymers, in particular their light fastness. Under the influence of light, processes of destruction of both the dye and the polymer itself occur. To study the light resistance of polymers, that is, their ability to retain their original properties under the influence of light, was the goal of the work done by a group of students under the guidance of employees of the Department of Experimental and Theoretical Physics of the Chelyabinsk State Pedagogical Institute.

The next day the section was held at the Institute of Atomic Energy named after. I. V. Kurchatova. The institute's senior researcher, Doctor of Physics and Mathematics N.S. Babaev, told the participants about the main directions of the institute's work.

The most interesting messages of this day were the messages of Dimitry Morozov (Dzerzhinsk, Gorky region, rural highway No. 2, 9th class) and Aleksadr Kipchatov (Saratov, rural highway No. 9, 10th class).

D. Morozov's report is called “Electronic instruments for measuring mechanical quantities in chemical engineering.” First of all, Dmitry spoke about the work of the experimental physics section of the Young Physicists Club, of which he is a member. This section was created at secondary school No. 2 in Dzerzhinsk 10 years ago, led by physics teacher Lev Vasilyevich Pigaltsin. The children at the school have created a programmed learning room in which they can monitor the knowledge of students in all grades and in all subjects. Many demonstration and laboratory equipment in physics were made during the work of the section. And this academic year, contact was established with the Research Institute of Chemical Engineering. In particular, the work described in the report is being carried out (it has not yet been completed) on the instructions of this institute. One of the departments of the institute is working on the creation of an explosion meter - a device for measuring the explosion of various liquids. The design being developed resembles a medical syringe. D. Morozov and S. Zolotov were asked to design electronic devices to measure the speed of movement of the explosion meter from the capillary and the fluid pressure on the explosion meter piston. Now these devices have already been made and are being tested.

A. Kipchatov, in his report “MHD generation of electric energy,” spoke about the magnetohydidynamic principle of obtaining electric energy. What is this principle? It is known that when a conductor moves in a magnetic field, an electric current will appear in the conductor. d. With. induction. This is how a common electric current generator works. If the solid conductor is replaced by an electrically conductive gas moving at high speed, then it will also have an inductance of electrical current. The report discussed the history of creation, various types of MHD generators, and the challenges that scientists faced when working on the creation of such generators.

The third day of the section's work took place at the Institute of Creative Body Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences. The participants were warmly greeted by the scientists of this institute. The head of the laboratory of physics and mathematics, E. M. Nadgorpin, and Doctor of Physics and Mathematics, Lenin Prize laureate V. L. Brode, told the children what the institute’s scientists are working on, what problems exist today in the physics of the creative body. For example, studying the properties of quasistries (phonons, holes, extons, dislocations) is one of the areas of scientific research. The fundamental practical problem that the institute is working on is to learn how to make creative bodies with directional macro properties.

During the experiment, we visited various laboratories and departments of the institute and got acquainted with the work of the computer center.
