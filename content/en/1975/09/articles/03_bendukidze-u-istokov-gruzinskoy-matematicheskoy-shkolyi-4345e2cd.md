Wistock's Georgian mathematical school is inextricably linked with the founding of the Georgian university.

In 1907, preparations for the founding of a Georgian university, which began in the 19th century, were headed by a young private assistant professor at St. Petersburg University, historian Ivan Aleksadrovich Javakhshi Vili. He and his few brethren had to offer many obstacles. And so on January 26, 1918, the Georgian University was opened in Tbilisi in a solemn ceremony.

At the beginning, the university had only one faculty - philosophy; The teaching staff of the university consisted of only 17 members. Among them was one mathematician, private associate professor at Moscow University, Andrei Mikhailovich Razmadze.

Soon, the teaching staff was replenished with another mathematician - in May 1918, the professorial council granted the request of Archil Kirillovich Kharadze to provide him with a position as a university employee. Later, in 1919, at the suggestion of A. M. Razmadze, Georgiy Nikolaevich Nikoladze was seconded to the university to conduct scientific work, and in the fall of 1920, Nikolai Ivanovich Mukhelli Vili was invited to the post of head of the department of theoretical mechanics.

These scientists - A. M. Razmadze, G. N. Nikoladze, N. I. Muskhelishili, A. K. Kharadze - stand at the origins of the Georgian mathematical school and are rightfully considered the founders of higher mathematical education and scientific work in Georgia. Let's talk a little about each of them.

1

After the opening of the university in Tbilisi, Razmadze returned to Georgia; Since then, all his work has been connected with Tbilisi University.

Razmadze’s multifaceted activities were disrupted by his premature death, which followed a serious illness in the fall of 1929.

A. M. Razmadze is the first leader of Georgian mathematicians, and it is quite natural that the Tbilisi Mathematical Institute of the Academy of Sciences of the GSSR bears his name.

2

Georgy Nikoladze, according to the outstanding French mathematician Elie Cartan, was born a geometer. Nikoladze himself wrote in 1907: “I consider myself a person gifted with a fairly large geometric intuition, and it seems to me that my every thought should be clothed in a specific image. I must certainly understand any new law (or theorem) intuitively—feel its geometric necessity. Until then, no logical proof will clarify anything. But when I felt it, I begin to intuitively imagine it so clearly that the proof for me personally often becomes unnecessary, although “my mathematical feeling” is satisfied with the wave only after the law (or theorem) has been a) comprehended, b) logically proven.”

However, after graduating from high school, Nikoladze did not enter the university, but the St. Petersburg Technical Institute: G. N. Nikoladze, the son of a prominent Georgian social activist N. Ya. Nikoladze, understood that at that time Georgia, with its natural resources, needed more engineers than mathematics. An exceptional sense of duty to the homeland, inherent in Nikoladze’s strong-willed and purposeful nature, predetermined his choice - he decided to become a metallurgical engineer.

Having brilliantly graduated from the Technological Institute in 1913, Nikoladze went to Donbass to acquire practical skills, where he began working at a metallurgical plant. In addition, Georgiy Nikolaevich studies mathematics, works on Georgian technical terminology, organizes gymnastics clubs (Georgiy Nikolaevich has been interested in sports since childhood; in 1912 he took part in a rally in Prague and received his first awards with large diplomas for his performances on gymnastic apparatus in the athletics hexathlon).

N. I. Muskhelishvili was born in 1891. In 1914, he graduated from the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of St. Petersburg University and was left at the Department of Mechanics to prepare for a professorship.

In 1915, the first scientific work of Nikolai Ivanovich appeared (done by him together with Professor G.V. Kolosov), which laid the foundation for a whole cycle of works on the theory of elasticity. In the same year, his teaching career began - he became a teacher at the university, as well as at the Electrotechnical Institute.

In 1920, Muskhelishvili occupied the department of theoretical mechanics at Tbilisi University and was soon elected professor. Nikolai Ivanovich takes an active part in the work of the university, in particular in the organization of the Polytechnic Faculty (1922), on the basis of which the Georgian Polytechnic Institute was created in 1928. Nikolai Ivanovich was the dean of the faculty, and later the vice-rector of the institute.

In 1922, “Lectures on Analytical Geometry” were published, which served as the basis for the “Course of Analytical Geometry” that went through several editions. At the same time, Muskhelishvili is working on creating a textbook on theoretical mechanics in Georgian. Volume I of this course was published in 1926, and volume II in 1928.

In 1933, Nikolai Ivanovich was elected a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, and in 1934, without defending a dissertation, he was awarded the degree of Doctor of Science. Since 1939 he has been a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

In 1941, the Academy of Sciences of the GSSR was founded; N. I. Muskhelishvili, a world-famous scientist, is elected its president. He held this position for more than 30 years and carried out enormous public work. At the moment, Nikolai Ivanovich is the honorary president of the Academy, and his former student, now academician I. N. Vekua, widely known for his scientific works, was elected president.

Nikolai Ivanovich twice received state prizes - for the monographs “Some Basic Problems of the Mathematical Theory of Elasticity” and “Singular Integral Equations.” In 1945 he was awarded the title of Hero of Socialist Labor.

N. I. Muskhelishvili’s contribution to Georgian science and culture is invaluable; It should be especially noted that after the death of A. M. Razmadze and G. N. Nikoladze, he took upon himself the entire burden of scientific and organizational work in mathematics and coped with this difficult and responsible task with honor.

Professor A.K. Kharadze has been teaching at the university for more than half a century. He is a veteran of the mathematics department - all Georgian mathematicians who received a university education are his students.

A.K. Kharadze was born in 1895. After graduating from a gymnasium in Tbilisi, in 1912 he entered the mathematical department of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University.

At this time, at Moscow University, under the leadership of D. F. Egorov and N. N. Luzin, the famous Moscow school of function theory was created, which played a huge role in the development of mathematics in our country.
