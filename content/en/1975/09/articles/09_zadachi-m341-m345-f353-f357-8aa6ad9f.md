M341. 20 teams are participating in the world championship. Among them are $k$ European teams, the results of the meeting between them at the World Championship count toward the European Championship. The championship is held in one round. In which direction $k$ can it turn out that the European team that scores strictly the most points in the European Championship will score strictly the least number of points in the World Championship, if it is a) a hockey championship (ties are allowed),
b) * volleyball championship (there are no draws)? What will be the answers to these questions if there are not 20 teams, but $n$?

Yu. A. Schrader

M342*. a) Prove that from the digits 1 and 2 it is possible to form $2^{n+1}$ numbers, each of which is $2^n$-digit and each of which differs in at least $2^{n-1}$ digits.

6) Prove that there cannot be more than $2^{n+1}$ such $2^n$-digit numbers.

S. V. Fomin

M343. In some countries, cities have gray roads. The length of any road is less than 500 km and you can get from any city to any other by driving along roads less than 500 km. When one road was closed for repairs, they found out that you can drive from any city to any other along the remaining roads. Prove that this can be done by driving no more than 1500 km.

S. L. Eliseev

M344. The centers of all 64 squares are marked on the chessboard. Is it possible to draw 13 lines on a board so that in each of the parts into which these lines divide the board there is no more than one marked point? (Straight lines must not pass through the centers of the fields, Fig. 1).

A. N. Pechkovsky
