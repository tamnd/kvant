## A nickel in the hands of a resourceful student

This is what happened one day in geometry class. The teacher proposed a “construction” problem. A circle is drawn (its center is not indicated), on which the point $A$ is marked. You need to find the diametrically opposite point $B$.

“I don’t have a compass,” said the student. “Can I use a nickel?”

The teacher allowed, but demanded to give a reasoned decision. As a result of deep thought

⟦column⟧

student using a nickel:

1) constructed a circle $O_1$ and marked an arbitrary point $A$ (Fig. 5) on it;

2) constructed a circle $O_2$ intersecting the circle $O_1$ at point $A$ and at some point $P$;

3) through the point $P$ draw a circle $O_3$ intersecting $O_2$ at some point $Q$;

4) through the point $Q$ draw a circle $O_4$ intersecting $O_1$ at some point $R$, and a circle $O_3$ at some point $S$;

5) applied a nickel to points $R$ and $S$ so that the circle $O_5$ drawn with the help of a nickel passes through these points; the intersection of circles $O_5$ and $O_1$ gave the desired point $B$.

When justifying the construction, the student noted that if equal circles with centers $O_1$ and $O_2$ intersect at points $M$ and $N$, then $O_1MO_2N$ is a rhombus.

At home, using the same coin, the student

a) constructed a circle tangent to a given “patch” circle at a point arbitrarily chosen on it;

b) given a ray $OA$, construct a point $B$ belonging to an imaginary ray $OB$, making an angle $30°$ with the ray $OA$.
