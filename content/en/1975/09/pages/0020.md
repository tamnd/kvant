⟦folio 18⟧

Meeting Tal was not an easy task. For several months in a row, he moved from one international tour tournament to another (and took first place everywhere!). They managed to “catch” the grandmaster in Leningrad on the last free day of the 42nd national championship (major league). During the conversation, Tal admitted that for the first time in his life he was giving an interview to a correspondent of a special physics and mathematics journal...

— Mikhail Nekhemievich, what you don’t have in common today is the Fischer-Karpov match. My first question is somewhat related to mathematics. Is it true that you had exceptional math skills in school and always aced math quizzes?

- Of course, it’s difficult for me to judge my own abilities. But, as far as I remember, I solved algebraic problems almost instantly.

- What about geometrically?

— Things were worse with geometry. In it, as I was taught, the most important thing is the drawing. In my drawings, no matter how hard I tried, the catheget always turned out to be the length of the hypotenuse.

- It's clear. So your confession was algebra, and you had to become an algebraist. How did your mathematical career develop in the future?

— My math teacher couldn’t believe that I solved problems so quickly. She had no doubt that I was copying the answers from the assignment and constantly gave me two marks. This went on for two years, until finally my parents couldn’t stand it and transferred me to another scale. As a result of such upheavals, the love for mathematics cooled noticeably, and the path to chess was completely “cleared.”

— My first chess question is this. Why do people become interested in chess, and for many this game becomes the meaning of life?

- It all starts simple. A child encounters chess for the first time somewhere—at home or at school. He starts playing and at first he just wants to win - against his brother, friend, neighbor. The spirit of competition captivates him. After all, chess is first and foremost a competition. No one thinks about a sports career first. Struggling. Rivalry draws in, and you become infected with the chess germ. Some people cope with this disease more easily; chess occupies a certain place in their lives - important, but not the most important. Others become chronically ill. A qualitative change is coming. What was entertainment, a game, a pleasant pastime, becomes a passion - an absolutely important matter.

— Let chess become, as you say, an absolutely important matter in life for a young man. Should he, in this case, strive for higher education?

— First of all, it seems to me that at the age of 17 it is still difficult to decide what place chess will take in life. But let us still assume that the decision to become a chess player is final and wireless. Should I then spend time on other activities?

I am convinced that chess can and should become professional. The Botvinnik phenomenon is not at all typical for our time. Nowadays grandmasters have to deal with a huge flow of chess information.
