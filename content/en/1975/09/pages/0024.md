⟦folio 22⟧

The noise in the hall does not bother me. When the hall starts buzzing after my move, it inspires me - I know that I played interestingly.

But the chess world is not closed for me. It is connected by many threads to other worlds. Many of my friends, perhaps mostly instrumentalists, do not play chess or play completely amateurishly. And I also have a common language with them. Only my friendly interests - theater, literature, music - could never compete with chess; chess existed, and all the others existed next to it. That’s why I don’t wish for anything, despite the losses, failures, and difficulties. I don’t want other prospects, unrealistic opportunities. I just love playing chess.

- Let me ask you two more, so to speak, mathematical questions. Do you think math skills can help in practical play?

- Already when we first get acquainted with the chessboard, we are dealing with numbers - 8x8=64! I remember that I was once strongly impressed by a chess proof (using a chessboard) of the Pythagorean theorem. Many endgame positions are purely mathematical in nature. But I don’t find a direct connection between chess strength and mathematical abilities. True, in chess analysis, technical education can be useful. Let’s say, I can’t imagine how Yu. Averbakh would have been able to achieve such a clear systematization in his three-volume work on the endgame if he had not had experience in the exact sciences.

— The first world computer chess championship took place recently. Are you afraid of competition from machines?

— True, I am not familiar with the technical side of the matter, but I am skeptical about the chess capabilities of computers. I doubt that she will look like an ordinary master. We believe that the chess machine will be prepared to solve other, non-chess problems, but chess is too versatile, complex, and there are no clear answers...

Of course, this will not be said for «Kvant», but I must admit that I primarily associate chess not with mathematics, but with music. By type of reception, by type of experience. There is something very close here. For me, this similarity is striking, that certain outstanding chess players are associated with composers. For example, Smyslov is Tchaikovsky: instrumentality, melody, directionality and occasionally a magnificent explosion, an arbitrary huge impression, Petrosyan is Liszt, absolute virtuosity, Botvinnik, sorry, is Bach, and Keres is Chopin...
