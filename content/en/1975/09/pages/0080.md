To the article “Constructing equations from function graphs”

(see «Kvant» No. 8)

1. a) (see Fig. 5 of the article). On the “main” interval $(0,\pi)$, the function $u(t)$, not defined at the points $t=\pi n$, has the equation $u_0=t$, $t\in(0,\pi)$. Shifting the graph $u_0$ by $n\pi$ along the x-axis, we get

$$u_n=t-n\pi,\quad t\in(n\pi,(n+1)\pi)$$

For integer values ​​of $n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots$ this equation gives the equation of the desired function $u(t)$. Note that

$\mathrm{arcctg}(\mathrm{ctg}\,t)=t-n\pi,\quad t\in(n\pi,(n+1)\pi)$
$(n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots)$.

If the function is defined at the points $n\pi$ on the left, then $u(t)=t-n\pi$, $t\in(n\pi,(n+1)\pi]$; if at the same points the function is defined on the right, then $u(t)=t-n\pi$, $t\in[n\pi,(n+1)\pi)$. In both cases $n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots$

The entire line is described by two equations

$$\begin{cases}u(t)=\mathrm{arcctg}(\mathrm{ctg}\,t),\\t(u)=n\pi,\ u\in[0,\pi]\end{cases}$$

($n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\dots$).
