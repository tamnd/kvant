To our readers

The subscription for 1976 to the popular scientific and mathematical journal «Kvant» continues.
«Kvant» is addressed to all schoolchildren in grades 6–10 who love mathematics and physics and love solving problems.
Our magazine is also useful for those schoolchildren who have not yet become interested in physics or mathematics, but would like to become better acquainted with these sciences.
Now there will be a radical revision of school curricula in mathematics and physics. «Kvant» is actively involved in this work. In 1976, articles explaining the material of the new programs will be systematically published in the pages of the magazine.
Many materials will be published for 10th grade students preparing to enter college.
In 1976, «Kvant» will continue to print articles for younger students. The scope of these publications will be significantly expanded.
«Kvant» is also useful as a teacher. The materials published in «Kvant» can be used in mathematics and physics classes and in elective classes.
Read in «Kvant» in 1976:
Strata dedicated to experiments that can be proven using models of both physical and mathematical origins.
A large number of articles will be devoted to the new program at school. Thus, in the articles by A.G. Mordkovich, schoolchildren will become familiar with the concept of a derivative and its application in solving problems.

For the fifth year now, the section of the magazine “Problem Book «Kvant»” has been encouraging schoolchildren to solve problems of increased difficulty in mathematics and physics. Schoolchildren from cities and villages take part in the work of this section. Readers who regularly link correct solutions earn the right to participate in the Olympiads field. The editors advise schoolchildren who are connecting solutions to problems.

For applicants to universities in 1976, articles on the following topics will be published. Mathematics: identical transformations of rational equations; checking the solution; equations with parameters; proof of inequalities; use of monotonicity of functions when solving equations and inequalities; solving trigonometric equations; solving the problem on bodies of revolution; rectangular projection when solving stereometric problems; “hidden” data in the task conditions.

Physics: laws of ideal gases; electronic current, transmission lines; transformers, image construction (plastic, lens, mirror); optical instruments; eye; interface.

We will continue to provide our readers with new books that are of interest to them, talk about the work of physics and mathematics schools, about Olympiads, about various types of work with schoolchildren, etc.
