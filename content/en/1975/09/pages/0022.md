⟦folio 20⟧

- Naturally, I am biased and cannot objectively evaluate what I have devoted my whole life to. In my opinion, every person who loves chess can find a place for themselves in the chess world. Many of those who do not achieve outstanding results in practical play become tri-herers, commentators, and theorists.

Of course, in chess, as in any other sport, success and points are valued above all. If you do not show major results, do not take on the challenge of changing the world, then in the eyes of others you may look like a loser, and this must be taken into account. Alas, the chess profession cannot yet be classified as prestigious. People far from chess often ask grandmasters what they do and what their main profession is. That is why I would not risk recommending the chess path to a young man who has both mathematical and chess abilities and “chooses to live.” Not a single vice can guarantee you high chess achievements, and you can devote yourself entirely to chess only if you love it endlessly and are ready to “sacrifice” social management in the name of this love. I also warn you that chess is a very nervous activity, and a chess player’s bread is not always sweet.

— In science, there is a certain competition, as a result of which everyone is left in the dust. In chess, only the winner triumphs. Recently, there has even been a theory according to which, for the winner or the match, it is necessary to specifically evoke a feeling of anger towards the enemy...

— The goal of a chess game is to defeat your opponent. A chess player without character, soft, weak-willed, has no chance. A chess player is first and foremost a fighter. And in a fight, two aggressions are natural. But still, most often the usefulness of aggressiveness is limited to the chessboard and is not transferred to the personality of the enemy. Spassky once compared chess players to bosses, but leaders with gloves are not really bosses anymore. In the end, in chess, after a fight, both sides often remain alive... By the way, Spassky himself is a very gentle person in life and hostility towards his opponent rather hinders him. He plays great with those with whom he has friendship or at least a good relationship, and plays less well with those with whom he has antipathy. For myself, I also can’t say that I “charge up” before the game. And at times, when I like my partner and I know that this game is very important for him according to some principles, it is difficult for me to gather myself and play at my best. I think this is rather my minus.

Hayka is not a sport, and ideally, apparently, there shouldn’t be such “strictness” as in sports. In our country, even the title of grandseiter does not guarantee a capable life. Eternal struggle, eternal selection, competition. But I must say that chess players have joys that are not available to people in other professions. Having played one beautiful game, the path is not in a very large tournament -
