This means that we have a system of three first-degree equations with third unknowns $a, b, c$. It can be solved *). The solutions are again rational numbers. And then $a^2, b^2, c^2$ will automatically form a rational progression.

A. No, let's go straight to the general case: consider the progression $\alpha - \beta, \alpha, \alpha + \beta$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are arbitrary numbers. To determine the numbers $a, b, c$ we obtain the following system of equations:

$$b + c = \frac{1}{\alpha - \beta},$$

$$c + a = \frac{1}{\alpha},$$

$$a + b = \frac{1}{\alpha + \beta}.$$

D. Add all the equations and divide the sum by two; we get

$$a + b + c = \frac{3\alpha^2 - \beta^2}{2\alpha(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)}.$$

Subtract from (7) sequentially (4), (5), (6); we get:

$$a = \frac{\alpha^2 - \beta^2 - 2\alpha\beta}{2\alpha(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)}, \quad b = \frac{\alpha^2 + \beta^2}{2\alpha(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)},$$

$$c = \frac{\alpha^2 - \beta^2 + 2\alpha\beta}{2\alpha(\alpha^2 - \beta^2)}.$$

This means that whatever $\alpha$ and $\beta$ we take *), the numbers $a^2, b^2, c^2$, where $a, b$ and $c$ are calculated using these formulas, must form a progression. Let's check it out.
