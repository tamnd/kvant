Fischer is often cited as an exception. It may be difficult to call him an encyclopedist, but he is not a person, so to speak, with a low intellectual quotient. Suffice it to remember that he is fluent in many languages. And his “strange” actions are more likely related to his character, his view of chess, etc.

Most of my colleagues write a lot and have become professional writers. And, perhaps, it is no coincidence that former MSTU graduate student Yuri Averbakh and talented engineer Alexander Kotov have now become famous chess writers. By the way, I also graduated from the Faculty of Philology at the University of Riga and worked as a teacher of Russian language and literature at school for six months. I won’t lie, I taught with great pleasure, but after my students had “windows” in two quarters for several months, I was forced to leave school.

— So, you think that it is most reasonable to first try to combine chess with another profession and only later make a final decision. How long do you think such a combination is possible?

— I think that, at least up to the master’s level, combining chess with study or work in another field is quite possible.

— Taking into account the readership of the magazine, let's try to draw a parallel between the professions of a mathematician and a chess player. Both mathematics and chess require constant and intense mental effort. There is an opinion that 8 hours is the daily minimum for doing mathematics. “A truly virtuous person,” as the famous English mathematician J. Littlewood writes with a bit of humor, must find 10 hours of work a day through sleep. Is it conceivable to play chess under such conditions? By the way, some professors avoid accepting mathematicians who play chess well into graduate school.

— I can only agree that chess is a very dangerous related profession, and, apparently, especially for mathematics.

— Don’t you think that the profession of a mathematician, so to speak, is “more reliable” than the profession of a chess player? If you do not become a major scientist in the field of “pure” mathematics, you can find yourself in a related profession, becoming an “applied scientist.” In chess, sporting success comes first. And if by the age of thirty a chess player begins to understand that the audience will never burst into applause in response to his moves, and in Palma de Mallorca he is not destined to shine with the sacrifice of a queen, that his youthful dreams have not come true, then what remains for him in the chess world?
