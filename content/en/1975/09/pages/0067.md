## Blue screen for university applicants

In September, classes begin at the Moscow preparatory television courses, created in 1971 by Central Television together with the Ministry of Higher and Secondary Special Education of the USSR.
Every year, telecourses receive tens of thousands of listeners. The experience of the courses shows that the majority of students who study systematically and conscientiously during the academic year enter higher education institutions.
This note describes how the work of preparatory telecourses will be organized in the 1975/1976 academic year.

Television classes are conducted throughout the academic year in three disciplines: mathematics, physics and Russian language. The main goal of teleclasses is to systematize the knowledge of students, bring them into line with the requirements for entrance exams, and create conditions conducive to the development and strengthening of independent work skills necessary both for successful study at a university and for the future activities of every specialist, engineer, and scientist.

The preparation of educational television programs and the organization of classes together with the Main Editorial Board of popular science and educational programs of Central Television is carried out: in the departments of mathematics and physics - the Moscow Engineering Physics Institute, in the department of the Russian language - Moscow State University. M. V. Lomonosov. Along with these universities, the Moscow Economic and Statistical Institute, the Moscow Energy Institute, the Moscow Institute of Geodesy, Cartography and Aerial Photography Engineers and other Moscow universities take part in the courses. The work of telecourses is supervised by the Council of Rectors of Higher Educational Institutions of Moscow.

Telecourses mainly take the lecture form, but a significant part of the telelessons is devoted to practical exercises, consultations and review programs. Television classes are conducted by leading scientists and teachers of Moscow universities; educational programs are accompanied by demonstrations of experiments, physical instruments, showing fragments from films, photographs, etc.

To consolidate knowledge, students are regularly offered homework, consisting of problems, examples and texts of varying difficulty. As a rule, the assignments correspond to the topics of the lectures; the completion of some assignments requires the use of knowledge acquired during the entire previous period of classes. In the coming academic year, homework assignments will be published in the weekly review of Central Television and Radio programs “Moscow Speaks and Shows.”

Students' answers to homework assignments are checked by course instructors. The results of the check are entered into a card index, after which postcards with answers are returned to the addressees. In addition, tests on covered topics are regularly published throughout the year and quizzes are conducted during television programs.

In the 1975/1976 academic year, as in previous years, telecourses will conduct full-time tests: in winter (approximately January - February) and spring (late April - May). As the experience of the courses shows, meetings between students and teachers significantly increase the effectiveness of telelearning: future applicants, being in conditions similar to those found during entrance exams, not only test their knowledge, but also undergo a kind of psychological preparation. On the other hand, teachers, as a result of direct contact with television viewers, can determine how the presented material is perceived by listeners, and, if necessary, make appropriate adjustments to their work.
