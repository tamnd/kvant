The work of the sections was extremely intense and intense.

Let us first tell you how the work of the astronomy and cosmonautics section went.

On the first day, before the section began, the Moscow guests got acquainted with the astronomy and cosmonautics department of the Palace of Pioneers. Most of the kids gathered at the summer simulator for young cosmonauts. Professional discussions began at stands with photographs of astronomical objects.

By the time the section meetings opened, the hall of the Pioneer Planetarium, where these meetings took place, was overcrowded. According to the list there should have been 38 delegates to the meeting, but there were more than 70 in the hall. The “doubling” occurred due to the young astronomers of the capital.

From the stories of *Sergei Gusakov* (Moscow, s. sh. No. 31, 9th grade) and *Anna Lapshinskaya* (Moscow, s.sh. No. 408, 9th grade) about the work of the section of astronomy and atmospheric physics of the Southern Scientific Organization of the Moscow City Palace of Pioneers and Schoolchildren, guests learned about the topics of amateur scientific observations, about expeditions, experiments and abstract conferences, on the organization of clubs and circles in schools and pioneer camps by members of the circle.

*Nikolai Vlasov* (Moscow, s. sh. No. 710, 9th class) reported on long-term research into the nature of mysterious noctilucent clouds. This report was awarded the journal's «Kvant» Prize.

After these reports, guests were offered a “mini-quiz”: five questions and one minute for reflection. Here are two of those questions.

**1.** *Two satellite ships are moving in the same orbit by inertia at a distance of several tens of kilometers from each other. The second ship received additional instantaneous acceleration in the direction of orbital motion. How will the position of this ship relative to the first one change in the end?*

ANSWER OPTIONS:

a) *catch up and dock with the first one,*

b) *overtakes the first one*

c) *lags behind the first.*

**2.** *A vessel with water is taken out into outer space. Let's imagine that the vessel has disappeared. What will happen to the water?*

ANSWER OPTIONS:

a) *freeze*

b) *quickly dissipates in space*

c) *gathers into a ball, maintaining its state of aggregation.*

After the quiz, the section participants were divided into two groups. Astronomy enthusiasts were invited to the Palace's astrophysics laboratory, where they were presented with the tasks of the workshop. The young cosmonauts had to undergo tests on simulators.

At the end of the first day of the section, the guys listened to a report by *Oleg Ivanenko* and *Evgeniy Kuzmin* from the Moscow Palace of Pioneers “Complex for the study of planets” and a message by senior researcher A. A. Bolshakov “Space for humanity.”

The children were lucky enough to visit Star City, where they met with cosmonaut Yu. P. Artyukhin and toured the museum of the Cosmonaut Training Center.

The second and third days of the meetings were held at the Moscow Planetarium. 18 reports were heard here. Most of them are the results of amateur scientific observations, the results of practical work on the manufacture of astronomical instruments and models of space complexes, and theoretical abstracts. The report of *Alexander Maltsev* (Kaliningrad, s. sh. No. 32, 10th grade) “Gravitational collapse, singular points, their role in the Universe” aroused the greatest interest and lively discussion; This report, which showed the author's deep insight into the essence of the problem of relativistic astrophysics, was awarded the journal prize «Kvant». The «Kvant» prize was also awarded to the report of a member of the Chelyabinsk Scientific Society of Students *Andrey Sychikov* (Chelyabinsk, s. sh. No. 58, 9th grade) “On the results of processing observations of comet Kohoutek.”
