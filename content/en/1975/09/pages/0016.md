At the same time, Nikoladze conducts scientific work in the field of electrometallurgy, organizes the Shevardeni (Falcon) sports society, leads climbs to the peaks of Kazbek and Elbrus, and participates in the creation of the Polytechnic Faculty; later at this faculty he taught not only mathematics, but also metallurgy. On his initiative, a lot of work is being done to develop Georgian scientific mathematical terminology. As a result of this work, in 1925, the “Russian-Georgian and Georgian-Russian Lexicon of Mathematical Terms” compiled by N. I. Muskhelishvili, G. N. Nikoladze and A. K. Kharadze was published.

In 1926, Nikoladze was sent to Western Europe for two years to do scientific work in mathematics and study some issues in the field of electrochemistry. In the spring of 1928, at the International Congress in Bologna, he gave a report on continuous systems of geometric figures, and in the summer of the same year he submitted a dissertation at the Sorbonne and received a doctorate in mathematics.

In January 1931, under the leadership of Nikoladze, a series of melts began at the Tbilisi experimental ferromanganese plant. One cold night, Georgy Nikolaevich, rushing to the factory, caught a bad cold and contracted pneumonia. On February 5, 1931, G. N. Nikoladze died; he was not yet 43 years old.

During his short life, G. N. Nikoladze managed to do a lot. This cannot be explained by talent alone: ​​Nikoladze was an exceptionally organizational and disciplined person. His gymnastics students remember that he scheduled section classes at 7 a.m.
