⟦folio 47⟧

Samuel Loyd's Puzzle

Tea party game

This game can be played by two or more partners. Each of them is stocked by physicists with “its own” color. The player, as soon as his turn comes, puts the physics on the free axe. After all the physics questions are closed, scoring begins. The number of points of each player is equal to the number of squares with vertices in tsashkas covered by his physical figures. The physicists appointed by his partners are not taken into account by him. For example, if your physicist is placed on the fields A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, then you have built 3 squares: ABCD, BEFD and GFHI.

The game consists of both blocking opponents' attempts to build squares and building your own squares. The winner is the one who scores the most points. The letters at figure are placed only to explain the rules of the game.

Task. Try to determine the number of squares if all 33 fields of the playing board are occupied by your physicists.
