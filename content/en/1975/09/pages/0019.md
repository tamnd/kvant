Mathematics and chess have many similarities. The forms of thinking of a mathematician and a chess player are quite similar, and it is no coincidence that mathematical abilities are often combined with chess ones. It is enough to mention the names of world chess champions. The mathematician was interested in the first chess king, W. Steinitz. A famous mathematician was his successor, Dr. Em. Lasker. The current chairman of FIDE, ex-world champion M. Euwe, headed the largest computer center in Holland. The first Soviet world champion, Doctor of Technical Sciences M. Botvinnik, has been working on a chess game algorithm for a computer in recent years.

Many prominent mathematicians showed purely scientific interest in the chess topic. As you know, the great mathematician Carl Gauss was working on the “arrangement of queens,” and another great mathematician Leonard Euler was working on the problem “on the move of the knight.” In our country, mathematicians from different countries work in the field of chess programming (of course, the mathematical methods developed in this case can be used to solve other, more pressing problems). The chessboard, pieces, and the game itself are often used to explore a variety of mathematical concepts and problems.

Thus, the publication in «Kvant» of various articles and problems involving chess is completely natural language. We are confident that most people who are passionate about mathematics and physics also love to play chess. By the way, in the Moscow State University chess championships, students from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics win more often than others, and only recently have they had gray competitors, and those are students of the Faculty of Cybernetics.

Today «Kvant» with great pleasure provides its pages to one of the most popular chess players of our time, ex-world champion Mikhail Tal. Our regular “chess and mathematics” author Evgeniy Gik conducted an interview with the grosser.
