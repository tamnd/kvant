⟦folio 48⟧

So, the school holidays are over and the new school year has begun. It also begins in the “Applicant's Workshop,” a traditional section of our magazine addressed to those who are preparing to enter a university.

In the current academic year, we will continue to publish articles on mathematics and physics, devoted to various issues of the entrance exam program, and will introduce readers to the typical mistakes that applicants often make during entrance exams. On the pages of the magazine, readers will find the texts of problems in physics and mathematics offered for admission to various countries in 1975, as well as information about books that are useful to get acquainted with while preparing for exams.

We hope that the “Applicant's Workshop” will become a good adviser to applicants, clear up the incomprehensible, and warn against possible mistakes. It is possible that during the lessons our readers will have some questions, comments, and suggestions. Write to us about it. The editors will be happy to answer you, and will publish the most interesting suggestions and messages from readers in the “Readers Advice” section.

Of course, the materials from the Applicant's Workshop cannot replace school textbooks. These materials should be considered only as additions to the textbook, unraveling the theoretical principles of the school course and illustrating them with solutions to the problems of the entrance exam options. When outlining the topics of the articles, the editors took into account the experience of entrance exams and the numerous messages expressed by readers in their letters. In addition to the materials that will be published in the current academic year, when preparing for exams it is useful to get acquainted with those articles that have already appeared in previous issues of the journal; thematically, a list of such articles can be found in «Kvant», 1974, No. 1, p. 52.

You should not think that the materials of the “Applicant Practicum” are considered only for tenth graders. Many articles in this section are immediately accessible to nine classicists, and they will understand a lot from other articles. So everyone can read the “Applicant's Practicum”, and this will greatly contribute to a better assimilation of the material that is included in the entrance exam program.

A few words about one more issue - about tutors. Some applicants count on the fact that the help of a tutor before the exam allows them to quickly and easily repeat the necessary material and successfully pass the competition. This is deeply misguided! Oltiple shows that applicants “trained” by tutors receive only formal and superficial knowledge; this usually shows up in the entrance exam. The key to success in admission exams is systematic, regular independent work. Anyone who really wants to become a student will achieve this if, without wasting time, he begins now to actually repeat theoretical material and actively push himself in solving problems.
