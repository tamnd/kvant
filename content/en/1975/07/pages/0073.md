4. To give the wave rigorous proofs of the stated statements, you need to master a rather complex topological technique. However, in general the wave is very obvious that a flat closed curve without self-intersection points (that is, a knot diagram without intersection points) can always be "inflated" by a circle. It is also obvious that if a knot diagram has a single crossing point, then it is always a flat figure-eight (do not confuse with a figure-eight knot!), that is, a “bent” circle (see Fig. 2, a). By drawing two crossing points with string “cuts” coming out of them and trying to connect these “cuts” to each other (to get a knot diagram with two crossing points), you can easily see that this way you can get either two intersecting circles (that is, a loop diagram, not a knot), or a double-bent circle (that is, a trival knot) - see figure 2, b, c. Carry out similar, but slightly shorter, arguments with diagrams of nodes with the crown and four points of crossing on your own.
