⟦folio 51⟧

F299. A liquid of density p flows through a common hose S with a speed v. Find the tension of the thread AB connecting ends A and B of the hose, if it is known that it is the diameter of the semicircle that forms the hose (Fig. 14).

Let's first consider another problem. Let the flexible hose referred to in the conditions be rolled into a ring in such a way that line AB is the diameter of the circle. Let the liquid flow in the ring evenly without cracking, and let there be no gravity.

Then the walls of the hose must grow so that the resultant of the tension forces applied to a small section of the hose just balances the force of the fluid pressure. In this case, the tension forces are directed everywhere tangentially to the wall of the hose, and therefore, the lower half of the ring acts on the upper only with forces directed along the top.

According to the general principles for solving mechanical problems, the lower half of the hose can now be discarded, assuming that the upper half is subject to forces equal to the forces that the lower half of the hose exerted on the upper half. Since these forces do not have components directed along the diameter AB, thread AB is not needed to keep the hose in the shape of a semicircle with the flowing liquid in equilibrium.

Therefore, its tension is zero.
