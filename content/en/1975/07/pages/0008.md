§ 1. Could Alexander the Great discover the theory of knots?

The books of ancient historians have preserved for us a legend about how the Macedonian king Alexander, walking with his army during his victorious campaign into the depths of Asia through the Persian city, visited the temple and saw one of the attractions of Percy - a knot woven by the sage Gordius; legend said that the one who unties this knot will become the king of the universe. The young king cut the knot with a sword.

Should we admire Alexander or keep in mind that with the same blow of the sword he deprived himself, through guilt, of the opportunity to become the founder of the theory of knots?
