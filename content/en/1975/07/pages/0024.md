Let's talk about some of the phenomena observed in the world of superconductors, phenomena in which, perhaps, the laws of quantum physics are most clearly manifested.

Let's start with the following experiment. Let us excite an electric current in a metal ring (for example, using electromagnetic induction). At room temperature, the current in the ring will quickly decay. The flow of current is accompanied by thermal losses, and these losses lead to current attenuation.

A completely different picture is observed in the case when the temperature is close to absolute zero and the metal is a superconductor. Under these conditions, the once energized current becomes undamped. In one of the optics, the current circulated in the superconducting ring for 2.5 years, and if the experiment had not been stopped, it could have circulated indefinitely.

At first glance, the described picture of undamped current in a superconducting ring seems easy to explain. In fact, in superconductors, current flows without any resistance. There are no joyle losses in them. Therefore, electrons that do not lose their energy properties can move around the ring for as long as desired.

However, the situation is much more complicated. Of course, there are no thermal losses in a superconductor. Nevertheless, the picture remains mysterious and completely inexplicable from the point of view of classical physics.

Let's consider this issue in more detail. The movement of electrons forming a current in the ring is not uniform. Electrons move in a circle and, therefore, accelerate. A problem has already arisen in the history of physics.

In 1911, Rutherford, based on his classical experiments on α-particle scattering, created a planetary model of the atom. He proposed (we have now reduced ourselves to such a planetary model that the word “suggested” even sounds strange) that an atom consists of a nucleus and electrons rotating around it in circular orbits.

The main difficulty of the planetary model of the atom was associated with the acceleration of the motion of electrons around the nucleus. An electron moving with centripetal acceleration should emit electromagnetic waves, while its energy should decrease, and this should lead to its fall onto the nucleus. In other words, from the basic principles of the physics of electromagnetic phenomena, the conclusion follows that the planetary model of the atom is unstable. But in reality, atoms are stable.

The study of this fundamental difficulty standing in the way of the recognition of the planetary model of the atom led to the creation of Bohr's quantum theory.
