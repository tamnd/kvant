13. The proof is carried out by induction. Let $p$ be the number of handles. To make the reasoning more visual, we will imagine the sphere as oblate, that is, a circle, while the edge (equator) will turn into the circumference of this circle; the handles will turn into strips, and their edging will turn into the edges of these strips. When gluing the handle strips (see Fig. 5), they are allowed to be twisted and intertwined with each other; the only requirement is that the number of “twists” for each strip must be even, otherwise from one side of the circle-sphere we will be able to use the strip-handle to get to the other without crossing the knot-edge, that is, the knot will not cut the sphere into two parts.
