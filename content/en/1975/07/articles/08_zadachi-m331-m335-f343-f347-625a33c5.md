Tasks

M331—M335; F343—F347

M331. a) Triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ was obtained from triangle $ABC$ by rotating around the center of the circumcircle by some angle less than 180°. Prove that the intersection points of the corresponding lines: $(AB)$ and $(A_1B_1)$, $(BC)$ and $(B_1C_1)$, $(CA)$ and $(C_1A_1)$, are the vertices of a triangle similar to triangle $ABC$.

6) The quadrilateral $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ was obtained from the quadrilateral $ABCD$ circumscribed in a circle with center $O$, rotated around the center $O$ by some angle less than 180°. Prove that the intersection points of the corresponding lines: $(AB)$ and $(A_1B_1)$, $(BC)$ and $(B_1C_1)$, $(CD)$ and $(C_1D_1)$, $(DA)$ and $(D_1A_1)$ are the vertices of the parallelogram. (8-10 grades)

FOR. Eunuch

M332. Is it possible to construct a cube of size $k \times k \times k$ from white and black cups $1 \times 1 \times 1$ such that for each cube size two of its neighbors have the same color as itself? (Two cubes are considered adjacent if they have a common edge.) (9 cells)

A. G. Gein

M333. When flies crawl along the sides of the triangle $ABC$ so that the center of gravity of the triangle remains in one place. Prove that it coincides with the center of gravity of the triangle $ABC$ if it is known that one of the flies crawled along the entire boundary of the triangle. (The center of gravity of a triangle is the point of intersection of its medians.) (8 cells)

S. V. Fomin

M334. Given a polynomial $P(x)$ with a) natural coefficients, b) * integer coefficients.

⟦folio 41⟧

For each natural number $n$, let $a_n$ denote the sum of digits in the decimal notation of the number $P(n)$. Prove that you will find a number that appears in the sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ an infinite number of times. (9 grades)

I. N. Bernishtein

M335 *. a) In the square of $7 \times 7$ cells the centers of $k$ cells are offset. Moreover, no four offset points are the vertices of a direct line from the side, parallel to the sides of the square. At what maximum $k$ is this possible?

6) Solve the same problem for a square of $13 \times 13$ cells. (8-10 grades)

S. A. Gashkov and A. A. Grizdoryan

F343. The angle under which the Sun is visible from the Earth (angular diameter) is approximately $\alpha \approx 10^{-2}$ rad. The radius of the Earth is $R_1 \approx 6400$ km. Acceleration of free fall on Earth $g \approx 10 \text{ m/c}^2$. Determine from these data the ratio of the average densities of the Earth and the Sun, taking into account that $1 \text{ eo} \delta \approx 3 \cdot 10^7 \text{ c}$.

Notes. The volume of the ball is calculated by the formula $V = \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3$, where $R$ is the radius of the ball. (8 grades)

F344. The charge $Q$ is uniformly distributed. along a thin dielectric ring that lies on a smooth horizontal plane. The induction of the magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the ring varies from 0 to $B_0$. What angular velocity does the ring acquire in this case? The mass of the ring is $m$. (9 grades)

F345. Estimate the engine power required to maintain a helicopter of mass $M = 500 \text{ kg}$ in the air with blades of length $l = 3 \text{ m}$.

Assume that the weight of air under the flapping blades moves in a uniform downward flow. (8-9 grades)
