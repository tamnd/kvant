## Tasks for our readers

1. Find the angles of a right triangle if it is known that the ratio of the height lowered to the hypotenuse to the median drawn to the leg has the greatest possible value.

E. A. Bokov

2. A circle is drawn on a piece of paper, its center is not indicated. Using a double-sided ruler, inscribe a square in this circle.

A. V. Alyaev

3. No help. tables to prove that

$$\mathrm{tg}\ 50°>\frac{13}{11}.$$

S. I. Mayzus

4. Prove inequality

$$\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{9}\right)\cdots\times\left(1+\frac{1}{n^2}\right)<2.$$

S. T. Berkolaiko

5. Prove that an angle in $n°$, where $n$ is an arbitrary natural number not divisible by 3, can be divided into $n$ equal parts using a compass and ruler.

L. M. Zemskov

6. The sum of the angles at the base of a trapezoid is 90°. Prove that the distance between the midpoints of the bases of a trapezoid is equal to the distance between the midpoints of its diagonals.

B. I. Aleinikov

7. What are the limits of the ratio of medians drawn to the legs of a right triangle?

F. Gainullin

8. Given a triangle $ABC$, in which angle $A$ is twice as large as angle $B$. Determine the height $CK$ lowered from the vertex $C$ to the side $AB$, if it is known that $|AC|=3$ cm, $|AB|=5$ cm.

A. V. Khusainov
