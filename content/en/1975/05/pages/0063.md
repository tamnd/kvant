Problem 1. A block of height h slides rapidly along the slope of a mountain. Which half of the block, right or left, presses harder on the mountain if the surface of the mountain is rough?

Analysis. 1) The reference system is connected to the Earth, that is, it is practically inertial. 2) The speed of the block is apparently much lower than the speed of light in a vacuum, and the block itself is a macrosolid body - the laws of classical mechanics can be applied. 3) The block, of course, cannot be considered a material point in this problem, since the conditions speak about its dimensions and consider the pressure of its individual parts - it is necessary to apply the laws of dynamics and kinematics for extended bodies, in this case - for a rigid body. 4) The Earth-bar system of bodies is closed, the law of conservation of momentum can be applied. 5) Internal forces in the system - friction forces - are not potential, therefore the law of conservation of mechanical energy cannot be applied.

Thus, we have established what laws can be used to solve this problem. But this does not mean that all these laws need to be applied. None of the conservation laws are related to the pressure of the parts of the block, so you should try to solve the problem using

Solved and e. The block moves translationally, therefore, the equal force of all forces acting on it passes through the center of mass of the block. The block is acted upon by the gravitational force mg applied at the center of mass, the friction force Ftr directed upward along the inclined plane, and the support reaction force N perpendicular to the plane. The equal force R of the last two forces applied to the base of the block must also pass through its center of mass.

If the block moves uniformly, then the sum of all forces is equal to zero, that is, the force R is equal in magnitude to the gravity force of the block and is directed with it in the same straight line (Fig. 1). Consequently, the force R, and therefore the force N, is applied at point B, which lies below point O - the middle of the base of the block. According to Newton's third law, the force of pressure of the block on the mountain is also applied at point B. This means that the right part of the block presses harder than the left.

If the block slides with acceleration, then the equal force of all forces is directed in the same direction as the acceleration, that is, along the inclined plane. This means that the force R in this case was turned to the right by a certain angle (Fig. 2).
