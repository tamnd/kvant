Leningrad physicists, led by corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences P. P. Kobeko, together with sailors of the navy during the siege of Leningrad, had to solve an unusual problem: to develop methods to ensure the safe passage of transport along the ice road. As you know, during the blockade the city was supplied with food, raw materials and shells along the only road passing through the ice of Lake Ladoga. In the incredibly difficult conditions of the besieged city, which was under shelling and bombing, physicists and sailors ensured the uninterrupted operation of the “road of life.”

The results of the work of Ural physicists who developed a magnetic flaw detection method, which makes it possible to detect dangerous flaws in the steel casings of artillery shells, turned out to be effective. The method is based on the fact that for products that have “internal” defects in the metal, the magnetic field near these defects differs from the magnetic field in the “healthy” parts of the product. These deviations were detected by special devices called magnetic flaw detectors. Millions of shells, rejected due to external mechanical characteristics, but in fact quite suitable for firing, were declared suitable with the help of these devices and returned to the front.

The exceptional role that radars played in the war is well known. The idea of ​​​​creating this device belongs to academician Yu. B. Kobzarv.

The merits of Soviet scientists were highly appreciated by the party and government: many of them were named Hero of the Soviet Union, Hero of Socialist Labor. Many are laden with orders and medals of the Soviet Union and other countries, and have been awarded state prizes.

The task of mastering military equipment, in the development of which Soviet scientists are actively involved, has now become even more urgent and more complex. General Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee L. I. Brezhnev noted: “Military equipment today is not the training paris “farmans” of the 20s and not even the “hawks” and “ducks” of the period of the Patriotic War. Today, defenders of the Soviet Motherland must master the art of managing international packages, driving supersonic aircraft, nuclear submarines, and be familiar with many other complex types of weapons. Today we need not only just brave, trained, masculine guys with a sharp eye and a steady hand, but also engineers, mathematicians familiar with the secrets of electronics and cybernetics. The tasks, therefore, in this area have become more complex, responsible, and have risen to a new level. But Komsomol acumen, enthusiasm, rural youth, masculine qualities and courage are needed today just as during the civil war and the first five-year plans, as in the fiery years of the Great Patriotic War" *).

It has been difficult years since the end of the war. But the feat of the Soviet people remained forever in the memory of man. Our people highly appreciate the contribution that scientists made to the cause of victory. Honor to them!
