Any two opposite sides of the hexagon $A_1B_1C_1D_1E_1F_1$ are located in the same plane (prove it yourself). Consequently, any two diagonals connecting opposite vertices of the hexagon $A_1B_1C_1D_1E_1F_1$ intersect, and hence all three diagonals of the hexagon $A_1B_1C_1D_1E_1F_1$ (they do not lie in the same plane) intersect at one point. Only the previously proven Brianchon theorem turns out to be valid for a hexagon described around an arbitrary finite section (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola), since an arbitrary finite section can be projected with respect to some point onto another plane so that its projection will be a circle. In this case, the lines tangent to the final section will turn into straight lines tangent to the circle.
