Problem 2 (similar to problem F278). *The Earth satellite moves in a circular orbit of radius $R_1$. They want to transfer it to an elliptical orbit with a maximum distance from the center of the Earth $R_2$ and a minimum $R_1$. How much do you need to change the satellite's speed for this? What will be the circulation period?*

Analysis. 1), 2) - the answers are the same as in problem 1. 3) The satellite can be considered a material point - Newton’s laws and the equations of the kinematics of the point can be applied. 4) The Earth-satellite system of bodies is closed (we neglect the interaction with other cosmic bodies) - the law of conservation of momentum can be applied. 5) Only attractive forces that are potential act between the bodies of the chosen system; the law of conservation of mechanical energy can be applied. 6) In addition, the motion of the satellite obeys Kepler's laws.

Solution: Let us first consider a satellite in a circular orbit. Conservation laws do not allow us to find the speed of the satellite, since in a circular orbit all states of the satellite are equal. Therefore, you can try to apply Newton’s laws and the equations of kinematics of a point:

$$F = ma, \text{ где } F = \gamma\frac{mM}{R_1^2}$$

($m$ is the mass of the satellite, $M$ is the mass of the Earth);

$$a = \frac{v_1^2}{R_1}, \quad T = \frac{2\pi R_1}{v_1}$$

($v_1$ is the speed of the satellite in a circular orbit). Then

$$v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma M}{R_1}} \text{ и } T_1 = \frac{2\pi R_1}{v_1}.$$

Newton's laws turn out to be inconvenient to solve, since the gravitational force at different points of the orbit is different. Let's try to apply the law of conservation of mechanical energy and Kepler's second law:

$$\frac{mv_1'^2}{2} - \gamma\frac{mM}{R_1} = \frac{mv_2^2}{2} - \gamma\frac{mM}{R_2},$$

$$v_1'R_1 = v_2R_2$$

($v_1'$ is the speed of the satellite in an elliptical orbit at perigee). From here

$$v_1' = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma MR_2}{(R_1+R_2)R_1}} = v_1\sqrt{2\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}}$$

The required increase in speed is $\Delta v_1 = v_1' - v_1$. The orbital period can be found using Kepler's third law

$$\frac{T_2^2}{T_1^2} = \frac{(R_1+R_2)^3}{2^3R_1^3},$$

where $\dfrac{R_1+R_2}{2}$ is the semimajor axis of the elliptical orbit.

So, finally,

$$\Delta v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma M}{R_1}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2R_2}{R_1+R_2}} - 1\right),$$

$$T_2 = \pi(R_1+R_2)\sqrt{\frac{R_1+R_2}{2\gamma M}}.$$
