And multiplication and division become simply overwhelming operations! That is why, more than the 15th century in Europe, almost all calculations were divided into abacuses. Thus, at the same time there were two ways of notating numbers - one, extremely cumbersome and inconvenient, used when writing numbers, and the second, convenient and practical, used when calculating. This second method, in fact, does not differ from the modern recording method that is familiar to us. Apparently, it was not the abacus that arose after the invention of the positional number system, but the positional system that arose thanks to the abacus.

The first to introduce the Indo-Arabic number system in Europe was Leonardo of Pisa, known as Fibonacci. He outlined it in his beautiful book “Liber Abaci” (1202). The spread of Arabic numerals in Europe led to the emergence of computational algorithms that completely abandoned the Roman system of notation for numbers in favor of a more advanced Indo-Arabic system. The name algorithm comes from the name of the 9th century Arab mathematician Al-Khwarizmi and is the predecessor of the modern word algorithm. Abacusists, who adhere to Roman numerals in written calculations, but counted mainly on abacuses, and algorithms were often compiled among themselves in the speed of calculations. On figure, taken from the 1504 book Margarita philosophica (see second cover page), you see this condition.

Wholesalers soon discovered that the new numbers were extremely convenient for keeping accounts. However, in some European countries the use of immi was prohibited by law, so the use of Arabic numerals could only be used subtly. For example, in 1299 a decree appeared in Florenzini prohibiting the use of Arabic numerals, and even in 1594 in Antwerp merchants were not allowed to write Arabic numerals in contracts and bills. The new system of recording numbers finally won only in the 16th-17th centuries, when the mass production of paper began, and soon Arabic numerals acquired a standard type thanks to printing.

But let's get back to the bills. The modern abacus is essentially a modified counting board on which counters slide along wires or rods. Currently, three types of abacus are used: Chinese suan pen, Japanese soroban and Russian abacus.
