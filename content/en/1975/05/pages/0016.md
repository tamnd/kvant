Wave mechanics

About fifty years ago, quantum mechanics was born - the science of the laws of motion of microscopic particles of materials (atoms, atomic nuclei, electrons, etc.). These laws turned out to be so surprising, so different from what physicists had encountered so far, that at first even leading scientists refused to believe the conclusions of quantum mechanics. And even today, discussions about the physical foundations of this science continue, although many of its sections have already become engineering disciplines.

In the early years of quantum mechanics, it was called wave mechanics. Now this name has already begun to be forgotten, but meanwhile it contains an indication of the most important feature inherent in the phenomena of the microworld. After all, the quantum, that is, discrete nature of the processes in which microparticles participate, is associated precisely with their wave properties. For example, the fact that the energy of electrons in an atom can only take on a certain discrete set of values ​​is a consequence of the wave nature of the electron.

In this article we will try to understand what the words “wave nature of an electron” mean and why it leads to discreteness of its properties. We'll have to start from afar.

Particles and waves

How do we describe the movement of large bodies, for example, the movement of an artillery shell, a satellite of the Earth, a planet around the Sun? Such bodies are called microsoptic (as opposed to microparticles), and the features of good motion are known. Of course, the Earth's satellites and artillery shells harmed the wave can be considered offended things. But the laws that govern their movement are easy to understand from our everyday experience.

We are accustomed to the fact that each body occupies a certain place in space, and this place can be specified by specifying the coordinates of the body. If a body moves, then over time it changes its position in space. To describe the movement of a body, you can specify the positions that it occupies at successive moments of time, that is, (to be more precise) you can specify the dependence of the coordinates of the body on time. This is the language of mechanics, or rather, even its alphabet.
