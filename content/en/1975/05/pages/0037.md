Origin of the named ellipse and hyperbola. Second order curves

Let us call the main chord of an ellipse, hyperbola, or parabola the chord that passes through the focus peredicularly to the focal axis (this is the name of the axis passing through the focus of the curve). An ellipse and a hyperbola, unlike parabolas, have two main chords. We will denote the length of half of the main chord by the letter p and call it the focal parameter of the end section *).

For any point M of the finite section, we construct a yellow rectangle ABCD (whose height is equal to the main chord) and a blue square NMEH (Fig. 7, a, 6, b). As we know**), for parabolas this rectangle and square have the same area.

Below we will see that for an ellipse, a square has less area than a rectangle, that is, this square does not have enough (elleipsis). For a hyperbola, a square has a larger area than a rectangle, that is, it has an excess (hyperbole). This fact, known to Apollo, is the basis for the name of both curves.
