Here's a new way to construct an ellipse and hyperbola using foci and one point! We can start from any node in the network, and we get two families of “confocal” ellipses and hyperbolas. This construction is more accurate the smaller $d$.

The diagonals of a rhombus are mutually perpendicular, which means that each pair of confocal ellipse and hyperbola intersect at a right angle $*$). The diagonal of a rhombus divides the angle of the rhombus in half, which means that the tangent to the ellipse (hyperbola) is a bissertrix of each angle of the rhombic network within which it passes. From the fact that each radius of the circle intersects it at a right angle, it follows that the tangent $MN$ at each point $M$ of the ellipse forms equal angles with the lines $F_1M$ and $F_2M$. This means that according to the law “the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection”, the ray $F_1M$ falling from $F_1$ to point $M$ of the ellipse is reflected from it along the straight line $MF_2$ (Fig. 13, a). This will be for each point of the ellipse.
