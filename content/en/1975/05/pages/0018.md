Their horizon dimensions are smaller than the height and width of the surface teeth, so such particles can be considered as material points. Each of these particles hits a certain area of ​​the surface, a certain slope of a tooth, and is reflected from this slope according to the same law: the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. But since each tooth is inclined at its own specific angle to the direction of the primary flow of particles, particles falling on different teeth fly off in different directions. As a result, the reflected particles no longer form a parallel beam, but are placed randomly at different angles.

If large particles fall on the surface, which, when in contact with the surface, cover many teeth at once, then the picture turns out different (Fig. 3). “From the point of view” of such a particle, a surface with small teeth differs little from an ideal plane. For example, a ping-pong table is called (the plywood is unevenly processed, the paint is not applied in an equal layer, etc.), however, the ball bounces almost the same way as a leveling plane. The fact is that a large ball, touching many small surface teeth at once upon impact, seems to upsaverage their actions, and this upsaverage action turns out to be almost the same as for an ideal plane. In such cases they talk about specular reflection.

Reflection of waves from a rough surface can also occur in two ways. Long waves (wavelength greater than the size of the irregularity) are diffused by the irregularities; As a result, the reflection is close to ideal. For example, the surface irregularities of an ordinary mirror are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. Reflection of light occurs directionally (specular). However, hard RENT rays, the wavelength of which is thousands of times shorter than that of visible light, are reflected from the same mirror by scattering (diffusely) - the reflected rays go in different directions.

So, when particle flows or waves are reflected from surface irregularities, the decisive role for particles is played by the correspondence between the sizes of irregularities and the particle sizes, and in the case of waves, the correspondence between the sizes of irregularities and the wavelength.
