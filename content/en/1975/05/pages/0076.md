glyph "suan" - "to count". It depicts an abacus held by two "hand" characters above a "bamboo" character.
The origin of suan pen is unknown. Its exact description dates back to the 16th century, but it undoubtedly appeared many centuries earlier.

The Japanese soroban (Fig.4) also dates back to the 16th century, when it is believed to have been borrowed from China. Its bones have pointed edges: two cones connected at the bases. On each rod there is only one bone (five) above the divider (the Japanese call this area *sky*), and only four bones (units) below - on the ground. The original design had five bones at the bottom, like the Chinese suan pen, but the fifth was thrown out around 1920.

In Japan, they still conduct annual compilations of the soroban score. Comparisons were often made between Japanese or Chinese calculators on the abacus and American ones on the arithmeter. The most famous such composition was compiled in 1946, when Thomas Wood was placed next to Kioshi

Mazzaki. It turned out that the Mazzaks performed all the calculations, except for multiplying small numbers, faster.

Russian abacus differs sharply from Eastern ones. Perhaps they were borrowed from the Arabs. Until now, the same abacus is used in some places in India and the Middle East, where the Turks call them *kulba*, and the Armenians call them *khoreb*. The rods with four bones that you see at figure 5 are used to count fractional parts of rubles and kopecks.

The best abacus is designed for addition and subtraction. Multiplication and division in abacus is carried out by repeating these operations many times. Addition and subtraction on accounts is done in the same way as on paper when performing operations in a column. To quickly work on the abacus, finger movements must be completely automatic: you cannot stop and report! If you thoroughly learn the finger movements needed for addition, then the movements needed for subtraction need not be learned. The point is, Let, for example, you need to subtract 9213 from 456,789:

$$456\ 789 - 9213 =
= 456\ 789 + 786 - 999 =
= 456\ 789 + 786 + 1 - 10\ 000.$$

Thus, we have reduced subtraction to addition and subtraction of some power of ten. (Sort it out)
