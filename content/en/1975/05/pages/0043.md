Experiments with water jet
M. P. Golovey

The science that studies the laws of equilibrium and fluid motion and how to apply these laws to solve various practical problems is called *hydraulics*. It is difficult to name a field of technology where the laws of hydraulics are not applied. They are used in the construction of hydroelectric power stations, places, water pipelines, canals; they are needed for the construction of irrigation machines and jedeval installations.

We also encounter various hydraulic devices at home: water supply, sewerage, heating. The flow of water from a tap, a hose, a connection in a vessel - in all these, at first glance, simple phenomena, it is not so easy to determine patterns.

You don't have to look far for examples. What shape do you think the cross-section of the stream of water coming from the square-shaped outlet has? And from the answer in the shape of a triangle? circle? Take your time with your answer. Try to see how water flows out of pipes with different sections, and you will get a completely unexpected result.

It turns out that the cross section of the jet flowing through the square outlet has the shape of a cross with four thin edges (Fig. 1, a). If the response is triangular, the cross-section of the jet takes the shape of a cross with thin ribs at the crown (Fig. 1, b), if it is round, it takes the shape of an ellipse (Fig. 1, ​​c).

The cross-sectional shape of the jet continuously changes as it moves away from the wall of the sauce. figure 1 shows the cross-sectional shapes of the jet at different distances from the vessel. Particularly noticeable changes will occur with square and triangular responses. Thus, the cross-section of the jet emerging from the square outlet first has the shape of an octagon, and then becomes cruciform. This phenomenon is called jet inversion.
