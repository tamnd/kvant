Great feat of the Soviet people

On May 9, the Soviet people and together with them all progressive humanity celebrate the thirtieth anniversary of the victory over Hitler's Hermann. This great victory was won not only by the Soviet armed forces fighting on the front line, but also by the entire Soviet people under the leadership of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union.

Soviet scientists, flesh and blood of the people, did not stand aside either. They made a significant contribution to the victory, fighting at the front and working in scientific laboratories.

Let’s not forget the rally in the Communist Auditorium of Moscow State University on Mokhovaya, when the university Komsomol organization declared itself mobilized “to carry out any task of the party and government - at the front, in factories, in transport, on collective and state farm fields.” Front-line scientists fought selflessly against the enemy; all their thoughts were aimed at victory. But even then, in the days of hot battles, they remained faithful to science. The remarkable Soviet mathematician and teacher A. Ya. Khinchin wrote to one of them in March 1945:

“...You made me think about why during this war such very young fighters like you, with every little break, rush with all passion to their favorite work - to the work to which they devoted themselves before the war and from which they were torn off by the war. This was not the case during the last peaceful war. Then the young man who went to the front almost always felt that his life had changed, that everything he had lived before had become impossible for him. But now there are those who, in between battles, write dissertations and defend them when they come on a short vacation! Is it because you feel with your whole being your PATH work and your favorite pastime - science, art; practical activity - as two links of one and the same great cause? And if this is so, then isn’t this feeling one of the main driving forces behind your victories, which we here in the rear so admire?..”

Not everyone lived to see the day of the great victory. Many scientists gave their lives for the independence of our Motherland. We say their names with reverence.

Hero of the Soviet Union Evgenia Maksimovna Rudneva is a graduate of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University. Before the war, she headed the department of the All-Union Astronomer and Geodetic Society. Guard senior lieutenant, shtuman of the Guards Aviation Regiment, she flew 645 combat missions. The diaries and letters of Evgenia Maksimovna Rudneva are collected in the book “While the Heart Is Beating,” published by the Molodaya Gvardiya publishing house.

Those who are interested in popular science literature should know the names of talented popularizers of science - Rostislav Nikolaevich Bonchkovsky and Gorgiy Nikolaevich Berman. Rostislav Nikolaevich owns a book about the first mathematical Olympids for schoolchildren, held in our country. He was the main editor of the collections “Mathematical Enlightenment” published before the war. And all of you, of course, have read the wonderful popular science books of Gorgiy Nikolaevich Berman: “Cyclopda”, “Number and the Science of It” and others. Seriously wounded, he found the strength to continue his favorite work and literally two days before his death he was correcting the proofs of his book.

In 1937-1941, one of the sections of the mathematical circle for schoolchildren at Moscow State University was led by a young talented mathematician David Oskarovich Shklyarsky. From the first days of the war, he went to the front and in 1944 died a heroic death. But until now, D. O. Shklyarsky’s influence on mathematical education has been exercised. Since 1946, a series of books “Library of the Mathematical Circle” has been published, which used materials from the circle, which was led by David Oskarovich. He never held these books in his hands, but in recognition of his special merits, the name of D. O. Shklyarsky is in first place among the authors.

The names of scientists who died in battles with the Nazis are alive in the memory of the people. Eternal memory and words to them!

The contribution of scientists who, without wishing for strength, worked day and night, bringing all their experience, knowledge and talent to work, helping the armed forces and the defense industry is invaluable. The fact that the Soviet Army was founded with modern weapons is a great merit of our scientists and designers. We mention only some of the works of modern mathematicians and physicists.

Artillery is called the “God of War.” Science helped artillery to more accurately “cover” the target and communicate force to the outside. A major role in the accomplishment of this task was played by the work of Soviet mathematicians and mechanics, in particular, the school of Academician A. N. Kolmogorov on the theory of shooting, Academician M. A. Lavrentiev on explosions, and corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Ilyushin on the plastic flow of metals.

Even before the war, a group of specialists from the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology, headed by Academician A.F. Ioffe, began to develop a problem that was urgent for the fleet - demagnetization of ships. At that time, many navies around the world adopted so-called magnetic mines, which automatically exploded when a ship passed over them. The fact is that any steel body (in this case, the hull of a ship) is magnetized to a greater or lesser extent and thereby creates a magnetic field around itself. This field was what the mines “felt.” To protect the ship from magnetic mines, it needs to be demagnetized. The demagnetization of ships was carried out by a system for determining the way wires were arranged through which an electric current was passed.

During the war, work on demagnetizing ships was carried out directly at naval bases. This work was carried out by a group of physicists under the leadership of academicians A.P. Aleksandrov and I.V. Kurchatov. Demagnetization has proven to be so effective that our fleet suffers virtually no loss of magnetic mines. Thanks to this, the lives of the respective sailors were saved.

Scientists dealt not only with the problems of protection from enemy mines - they also did a lot of work on creating explosive shells. So, a group of physicists developed

anti-vehicle mines used to blow up German tanks, trains and cars. German mine throwers were powerless in their attempts to neutralize these mines.

Leningrad physicists, led by corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences P. P. Kobeko, together with sailors of the navy during the siege of Leningrad, had to solve an unusual problem: to develop methods to ensure the safe passage of transport along the ice road. As you know, during the blockade the city was supplied with food, raw materials and shells along the only road passing through the ice of Lake Ladoga. In the incredibly difficult conditions of the besieged city, which was under shelling and bombing, physicists and sailors ensured the uninterrupted operation of the “road of life.”

The results of the work of Ural physicists who developed a magnetic flaw detection method, which makes it possible to detect dangerous flaws in the steel casings of artillery shells, turned out to be effective. The method is based on the fact that for products that have “internal” defects in the metal, the magnetic field near these defects differs from the magnetic field in the “healthy” parts of the product. These deviations were detected by special devices called magnetic flaw detectors. Millions of shells, rejected due to external mechanical characteristics, but in fact quite suitable for firing, were declared suitable with the help of these devices and returned to the front.

The exceptional role that radars played in the war is well known. The idea of ​​​​creating this device belongs to academician Yu. B. Kobzarv.

The merits of Soviet scientists were highly appreciated by the party and government: many of them were named Hero of the Soviet Union, Hero of Socialist Labor. Many are laden with orders and medals of the Soviet Union and other countries, and have been awarded state prizes.

The task of mastering military equipment, in the development of which Soviet scientists are actively involved, has now become even more urgent and more complex. General Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee L. I. Brezhnev noted: “Military equipment today is not the training paris “farmans” of the 20s and not even the “hawks” and “ducks” of the period of the Patriotic War. Today, defenders of the Soviet Motherland must master the art of managing international packages, driving supersonic aircraft, nuclear submarines, and be familiar with many other complex types of weapons. Today we need not only just brave, trained, masculine guys with a sharp eye and a steady hand, but also engineers, mathematicians familiar with the secrets of electronics and cybernetics. The tasks, therefore, in this area have become more complex, responsible, and have risen to a new level. But Komsomol acumen, enthusiasm, rural youth, masculine qualities and courage are needed today just as during the civil war and the first five-year plans, as in the fiery years of the Great Patriotic War" *).

It has been difficult years since the end of the war. But the feat of the Soviet people remained forever in the memory of man. Our people highly appreciate the contribution that scientists made to the cause of victory. Honor to them!

*) Br. Zhnev L.I. Speech at the solemn Plenum of the Komsomol Central Committee on October 25, 1968 - In the book: “Lenin’s course.” Speeches and articles, vol. 2. M., 1973, p. 273.
