How to attend to a physics problem?
G. I. Rosenblat

Solving a physical problem is a very complex process. It requires the solution to have a deep knowledge of physics, the ability to analyze a problem and correctly apply the laws of physics and their consequences to solve it. It is often useful to apply the experience of previous solutions to solve a given problem, carry out analyses, make simplifications, etc.

It is difficult (or even impossible) to develop universal prims for solving any physical problem. However, some general rules can be proposed that provide the most important part of the decision, namely, the selection of those laws that can be applied to the decision. These rules, as applied to mechanical problems, will be discussed further in the article.

When solving a problem, students often choose one or another law on the grounds that its formula includes quantities that are present in the conditions of the problem. This approach can lead to serious errors. The fact is that each physical law has certain boundaries and conditions of application, that is, it cannot always be applied. So, for example, the law of conservation of momentum can be applied only if two conditions are met simultaneously: the reporting system is inertial and the system of bodies under study is closed. If at least one of these conditions is not met, the law of conservation of momentum cannot be used to solve the problem. Therefore, before applying any law, it is necessary to check whether the conditions for its application are met. Moreover, for each law it is possible to establish a certain sequence of questions that need to be answered, that is, to build a “recognition algorithm” *). So, for the law of conservation of momentum, the sequence of questions should be as follows:

1. Is the reporting system inertial?
2. Is the system of bodies under study closed?
3. Can the classical mechanism be used?
4. Is the system under consideration closed?
5. Are the internal forces of the system placed?
6. Which parts of the laws of the mechanism to use?

*) An algorithm is a set of actions performed in an established order to solve a problem of a certain type.

Let us explain using specific problems how to use such a “plan” for solving a problem.

Problem 1. A block of height h slides rapidly along the slope of a mountain. Which half of the block, right or left, presses harder on the mountain if the surface of the mountain is rough?

Analysis. 1) The reference system is connected to the Earth, that is, it is practically inertial. 2) The speed of the block is apparently much lower than the speed of light in a vacuum, and the block itself is a macrosolid body - the laws of classical mechanics can be applied. 3) The block, of course, cannot be considered a material point in this problem, since the conditions speak about its dimensions and consider the pressure of its individual parts - it is necessary to apply the laws of dynamics and kinematics for extended bodies, in this case - for a rigid body. 4) The Earth-bar system of bodies is closed, the law of conservation of momentum can be applied. 5) Internal forces in the system - friction forces - are not potential, therefore the law of conservation of mechanical energy cannot be applied.

The block is acted upon by the gravitational force mg applied at the center of mass, the friction force Ftr directed upward along the inclined plane, and the support reaction force N perpendicular to the plane. The equal force R of the last two forces applied to the base of the block must also pass through its center of mass.

If the block moves uniformly, then the sum of all forces is equal to zero, that is, the force R is equal in magnitude to the gravity force of the block and is directed with it in the same straight line (Fig. 1). Consequently, the force R, and therefore the force N, is applied at point B, which lies below point O - the middle of the base of the block. According to Newton's third law, the force of pressure of the block on the mountain is also applied at point B. This means that the right part of the block presses harder than the left.
