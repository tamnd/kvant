## Institute begins
## from school
K. I. Kashin

Since you picked up «Kvant», you probably decided after graduating from school to go study at a higher educational institution where one of the most important subjects is mathematics. If this is the case, then everything written in this short article is for you.

When you graduate from high school, you have a hard time remembering how you were in first grade. You have grown up, matured and look at today's first-graders somewhat ironically (unless, of course, you are a future teacher). After all, they still do not know how to think logically, abstractly, and they are not able to solve any complex problems. They only listen, remember and tell, and they tell poorly. You, of course, know how to retell much more complex things, because you know how to think logically, prove, and transform data. And they, first-graders, only know how to obey and imitate. Everything is new to them, they are still just taking a closer look at everything. Isn't that right?

Now take a closer look at yourself - a future student of a technical university. Very soon you will again become a first-class... that is, I wanted to say, a first-year. You will also try things on and take a closer look at the teachers, trying to understand what they want from you. And they want, since you have learned to think well logically, to give you many times more knowledge in five years than you received in ten at school. And this knowledge is qualitatively different - it prepares you for independent activity.

⟦column⟧

The institute educational process includes the following elements: lectures; practical classes; laboratory classes; consultations with teachers giving lectures and conducting practical classes; doing homework; performing control work; tests; exams. And all these elements of the educational process require independence.

Let's start in order, with lectures. The student’s work during the lecture is divided into three stages:

1) listen to a lecture;

2) retell for yourself the thoughts expressed by the lecturer (this stage is necessary both because it contributes to a deeper understanding of the material being presented, and because the student simply will not have time to write down everything he says after the lecturer);

3) write down a brief retelling (make notes).

As practice shows, a very, very significant proportion of first-year students do not know how to take lecture notes. But taking notes is a very important part of the work. A lot depends on how you deal with it.

If the notes are poorly written, then it may well happen that the student, due to lack of time to comprehend the notes, goes to the exam with preparation that does not guarantee a favorable outcome.

If the outline is written well, then in many cases it is enough to look through it (even without looking at the textbook) in order to

pass the exam successfully. In other cases, it is a guide to any textbook, even if the textbook is not written according to the program of your institute.

So, if you want to save time in college to study your favorite subject—mathematics—then be sure to learn how to take notes.

I will not teach you how to solve problems, but problem solving is what you do in practical classes. In «Kvant» you come across many good problems designed specifically to give you an idea of ​​independent research work. Completely compare your own solutions with those given in the magazine. Get used to finishing what you start. This habit will be very useful to you at the institute.

Laboratory classes in mathematics usually involve working on electronic or keyboard computers. It may very well be that your school does not have such machines, and you don’t know where to look for them.

However, you are not a first-grader who is taught everything by a teacher. If you intend to work in applied mathematics in the future, you need to take the initiative, go to exhibitions where computers are exhibited, or at a research institute or design bureau where these machines are used, and figure out what a computer is. Here, “open days” held in many cities in the spring can help you. These days, schoolchildren - future students - are introduced to the institute.

Why is consultation needed at the institute? Of course, not just for the teacher to give you advice. The teacher knows how to give advice, but do you know how to ask questions? Asking questions is very important to check whether you understand everything correctly, whether there is some kind of trained process - an exam.

You will pass it with dignity if you know the subject well and treat the indicated moments of the educational process with integrity.

The knowledge of the subject acquired in high school, of course, will also help you significantly, but this is a topic for another conversation. Now it is important that you prepare not only for the more intense flow of knowledge that will rush at you as soon as you enter college, but also for the educational process so that you do not find yourself again in the role of a first-class student.
