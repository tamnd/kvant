Tasks

M321—M325; F333—337

M321. There is a rectangular table of area 1. Prove that for any ε>0 it is possible to indicate a system of rectangular napkins covering this table (the edges of the napkins are parallel to the edges of the table), such that any of its subsystems consisting of non-overlapping napkins has an area less than ε.

A. V. Brailov

M322. a) A figure consisting of more than one point is the intersection of N circles. Prove that the boundaries of this figure can be represented as a union of 2N - 2 circular arcs.

b) There are N letters in the alphabet. Several letters are written around a circle so that no letter appears twice in a row and for any two different letters a, b one can carry out

straight line so that all the letters a will be one from the straight line, and the letters b will be on the other (Fig. 1). Prove that no more than 2N - 2 letters are written.

S. V. Fomin

M323*. Prove that any function defined on the entire number line can be represented as the sum of two functions, the graph of each of which has a center of symmetry.

V. A. Serdeev

M324. There are several piles of stones. Two people play a game, the course of which is

⟦folio 51⟧

consists of the player breaking each pile of more than one stone into two smaller piles. Moves are made one stone at a time. The winner is the player who made the last move. How should a beginner play if at first there were from 80 to 120 stones in each pile?

S. V. Fomin

M325. In a number triangle, the top number is 1 and the outer numbers in each row are also 1, and each of the remaining numbers is not less than the sum of the two numbers above it (in particular, the “Pascal triangle” shown in Fig. 2 satisfies this condition). Let the natural number $a$, greater than 1, appear in this triangle $k$ times. Prove that $2^k < a^2$.

F333. A wire of length $l$ is fixed along a chord on a non-conducting disk of radius $R$. The disk rotates with a constant angular velocity $\omega$. A magnetic field with induction $B$ (Fig. 3) is directed perpendicular to the disk.

F334. The bead slides without friction along a vertical wire of length $l$, which is bent into an accordion of length $\frac{l}{2}$ (Fig. 4). How many times is the time the bead slides greater than the time it takes to fall from a height $l$? The amplitude of the wire bends is much less than its length. The dimensions of the bead are negligibly small compared to the length of the “knee” of the accordion.

B. N. Breizman

F335. A cylindrical vessel of radius $R$, the axis of which makes an angle $\alpha$ with the vertical, is filled with water. A well-lapped piston, the material of which is permeable to air but impermeable to water, is lowered into the cylinder. At what minimum weight of the piston will its bottom surface always touch the water?

V. V. Mirnov

F336. A cylindrical cup with a handle rotates around a vertical axis with an angular velocity $\omega$. In this case, the surface of rutini forms a parabolic mirror. Determine the focal length of this mirror. Rutini density $\rho$, free fall acceleration $g$.

F337. When observed with a telephony, the stars are visible even during the day. Explain why.
