Tasks

M311—M315; F323—F327

M311. From one bacterium, 1000 were obtained in the following way: first, the bacterium was divided into two, then one of the two resulting bacteria was divided into two, then one of the three resulting bacteria was divided into two, and so on. Prove that at some moments there existed such a bacterium, the number of flows of which among the 1000 bacteria obtained at the end is between 334 and 667.

D. Yu. Grigoriev

M312. The parallelogram $P_1$ contains a parallelogram $P_2$, which in turn contains a parallelogram $P_3$, with sides $P_3$ parallel to sides $P_1$. Prove that although one side of the parallelogram $P_3$ is no less than half the length of the side $P_1$ parallel to it.

A. A. Grigoryan

M313. Given an angle with vertex $O$. Consider the set of four vertices $M$ of ONML parallelograms, the vertices $N$ and $L$ of which lie on the sides of a given angle, and the area is equal to the constant value $k$. (This set is called a zigerbola.) Prove that on the bissertrix of this angle and on its extension there are also points $F_1$ and $F_2$ (where $|F_10| = |0F_2|$) for which the distance difference $|F_1M| - |F_2M|$ does not depend on the point $M$.

I. N. Bronstein

M314. Among all 9-digit numbers whose decimal notation does not have the digit 0, find one for which the difference between the number itself and the product of its digits is:
a) the smallest;
b) most.

What is the answer for $n$-digit numbers for any $n$?

A. G. Leiderman
