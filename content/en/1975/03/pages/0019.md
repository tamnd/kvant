## The shape of a hyperbola, its vertex and asymptotes

Hyperbolas with different values ​​of the parameter $k$ are similar to each other, but they are not congruent - they cannot be combined by superposition. However, it is easy to prove that they are all similar, even located similarly with the center of similarity $O$. The reader is invited to prove this using figure 3.

Let us describe the form of a hyperbola (more precisely, one branch of an equilateral hyperbola), that is, we will list its properties that are primarily striking.

*Hyperbola has infinite extension* - it cannot all fit on a limited piece of plane. No matter how large the number $A$ is given, it is possible to indicate a point on the hyperbola that is at a distance greater than $A$ from the point $O$ (prove).

⟦column⟧

*The hyperbola is symmetric with respect to the bisector $OT$ of the angle $XOY$* (Fig. 4), that is, for any point $M_1(x_1,y_1)$ of the hyperbola, the symmetric point $M_2(x_2,y_2)$ also belongs to this hyperbola (prove).

The point $A$ of intersection of a hyperbola with its axis of symmetry $OT$ is called the *vertex* of the hyperbola. Each vertex coordinate of a hyperbola with parameter $k$ is equal to $\sqrt{k}$. The distance of the vertex from the center of the hyperbola (we will denote it by $a$) is equal to $OA=\sqrt{2k}$. The vertex is the closest point of the hyperbola from the center (prove).
