⟦folio 9⟧

After the defeat of the Paris Commune, Victor Jacqular found himself in prison. To save his son-in-law, V.V. Korvin-Krukovsky himself, who personally knew Thiers, came to Paris. The old general managed to somewhat ease the fate of his daughter’s husband, and soon, with the help of the Kovalevsky couple and his wife Anna, Jacqular managed to escape from prison and move to Switzerland. Anna followed her husband.

So, three years later, everyone gathered together again in Palibino. An atmosphere of universal love and friendship reigned in the old estate. Sophia would be happy if it were not for thoughts about the future. What will be her future fate? Will she get a teaching position at the university? After all, according to the laws of the Russian Empire, a woman could teach mathematics only in the elementary grades of a gymnasium.

After resting in Palibino, the Kovalevskys left for St. Petersburg. Efforts began to obtain a teaching position in some higher educational institution. They ended in failure. Sofya Vasilievna was not allowed to teach even at the Higher Women's Courses.

It was said that during her wanderings through the biocratic institutions of St. Petersburg, Sofya Vasilievna somehow ended up in the office of an official, who, in response to her request for permission to teach at the university, refused, rudely adding: “In our country, men have always done this. They cope with their responsibilities, the words are rich, well, and therefore we don’t need any innovations!》 To this, the indignant Sofya Vasilievna said: “When Pythogoras revealed his famous theorem, he sacrificed 100 bulls. Since then, all the cattle have been rich in new things...”

An important event takes place in the life of the Kovalevskys. Friendship and mutual respect that bind Sophia Va-
Sil'evna and Vladimir Onufrievich, fall into love and their marriage becomes de facto. In 1878, the Kovalevskys gave birth to a daughter, who was named Sophia in honor of her mother.

At this time, Sofya Vasilievna somewhat retreats from her busy mathematics. Now she is a journalist and a diligent housewife. Her popular science articles, critical notes, and theatrical receptions appear in periodicals. In the Kovalevsky guest house there are many scientists and writers: D. I. Mendeleev, I. M. Sechenov, S. P. Botkin, A. M. Butlerov, P. L. Chebyshev, A. G. Stoletov, I. S. Turgenev, F. M. Dostoevsky and others.

At that time, a certain part of Russian society was captured by the “spirit of profit and various commercial enterprises.” This current captured both my husband and my husband, and I must show myself in my sins,” Sofya Vasilievna later recalled. The Kovalevskys wanted to provide for themselves financially, so that later, without thinking about bread, they would be employed by science.

His passion for commerce ended tragically. Vladimir Onufrievich was a dedicated scientist and a worthless businessman. He was deceived by partners, contractors, workers - everyone with whom he dealt. Soon he became completely entangled in debt and, seeing no way out, amassed a life of selfishness.

The sad news found Sofya Vasilyevna in Paris, where she was applying for a teaching position at the Higher Women's Courses. Having received the news of her husband's death, she fell ill. Having recovered, she hurried to St. Petersburg and added to establishing the non-involvement of Vladimir Onufrievich in the dark affairs of his companions.
