⟦folio 10⟧

Mathematician and writer

In 1883, Sofya Vasilyevna Kovalevskaya received an invitation from the Swedish mathematician G. Mittag-Leffler (with whom she studied with K. Weierstrass) to take the position of privatdozent at Stockholm University. In November of the same year she left for Sweden. Since Sofya Vasilievna did not know Swedish, she was allowed to lecture in German during the first semester. After a few months, she was already proficient in Swedish and could teach in it.

In the summer of 1884, Sofya Vasilievna was appointed professor at Stockholm University. Over the course of 8 years, she took 12 different courses, including the theory of partial arbitrary equations, a course in mechanics, the theory of algebraic, Abelian and elliptic functions, and others.

But Sofya Vasilievna was not only studying mathematics in those years. She also had extraordinary literary talent: she wrote a number of stories, the novel “The Nihilist,” and the drama “The Struggle for the Score” (together with Mittag-Leffler’s sister, who left the most interesting memories of Sofya Vasilyevna). Kovalevskaya's house became one of the centers of intellectual life in Stockholm - the famous polar explorer Nansen, the Mittag-Lefflers lived here, they wrote a lot, and professors.

Sofya Vasilievna devoted a lot of effort to the liberation movement. She participated in socialist congresses, knew many Russian political emigrants, and actively fought for equal rights for women.

In Stockholm, Sofya Vasilyevna completed her main work - a study of the movement of a top. For this, as already mentioned, in 1888 she received the premio of the Paris Academy of Sciences. The following year she receives a prize of 1,500 kroner from the Stockholm Academy of Sciences for new work, also on rigid body rotation.

Russian mathematicians repeatedly tried to attract S.V. Kovalevskaya to scientific and pedagogical work in her homeland, but to no avail. The petition submitted by Sofia Vasilievna’s relative, General A.I. Kosich, addressed to the president of the Academy, in which he, in particular, cited the words of Napoleon that “every state should value the return of outstanding people more than the conquest of a rich city,” did not help either.

But new trends also penetrated the Academy of Sciences. On November 4 (16), 1889, the Academy fundamentally resolved the issue of “admitting the Lizhian field to election as corresponding members.” 20 people voted for this resolution (6 against). And so, on November 7 (19), at the Department of Physics and Mathematics, at the proposal of academician P. L. Chebyshev, V. G. Imshenetsky and V. Ya. Bunyakovsky, Doctor of Mathematics, Professor of Stockholm University Sofya Vasilievna Kovalevskaya was elected as a corresponding member of the Russian Academy. On December 2 (14), 1889, the general meeting of the Academy approved this decision.

Now Sofya Vasilievna enjoyed world slavery. Mathematicians were waiting for her new works on the motion of a rigid body. It was full of scientific and literary notes. But all these notes remained unimplemented. Returning to Stockholm from France in January 1891, where she spent the winter holidays, Sofya Vasilievna caught a cold and died of pneumonia on January 29 (February 10).

At her funeral there were teachers from Stockholm University, students, and numerous friends.
