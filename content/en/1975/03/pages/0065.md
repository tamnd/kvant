As a consequence of formulas (1), (2), (3), we can find the relationships between $\gamma$ and $\varphi$, $\beta$ and $\varphi$, $\gamma$ and $\beta$. Prove formulas (4) (6) yourself.

We also recommend that the reader derive formulas (1)–(6) for regular triangular and quadrangular pyramids, and then check the results obtained by substituting the values ​​$n=3$ and $n=4$ into the general formulas. This exercise is all the more useful because it is important to understand the inference methodology rather than memorize the final results. The ability to quickly and clearly derive relationships between angles in a regular pyramid is the key to solving many problems. The “saucepan method” is the basis for solving these problems; below, we will show how it “works” using a number of problems. But before moving on to the problems, we introduce a number of additional notations for a regular pyramid:

$a$ - base side,
$h$ — height of the pyramid,
$V$ — volume of the pyramid,
$S$ - lateral surface area,
$S_{осн}$ — base area,
$R$ is the radius of the circumscribed ball,
$r$ is the radius of the inscribed ball.

**3. Calculating the volume of a pyramid**

Problem 1. *Find V, knowing a and* $\alpha$.

We have $V=\dfrac{1}{3}S_{осн}\cdot h$, $S_{осн}=2n\times$
$\times S_{\triangle AKH}=2n\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}AK\cdot KH=\dfrac{na^2}{4}\,\mathrm{ctg}\,\dfrac{\pi}{n}$,

$h=AH\cdot\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha=\dfrac{a}{2\sin\dfrac{\pi}{n}}\,\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha$,

where

$$V=\frac{na^3}{24\sin\dfrac{\pi}{n}}\,\mathrm{ctg}\,\frac{\pi}{n}\cdot\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha. \tag{7}$$

Problem 2. *Find V, knowing a and* $\beta$.

We already know how to find $V$, knowing $a$ and $\alpha$. Let’s apply the “saucepan method”: using formula (3), we move from angle $\beta$ to angle $\alpha$ (“we’ll hang the saucepan-

$$V=\frac{na^3}{24}\,\mathrm{ctg}^2\,\frac{\pi}{n}\cdot\mathrm{tg}\,\beta.$$

Problem 3. *Find V, knowing a and* $\gamma$.

Let's use the same technique as in the previous problem. From formula (2) it follows that

$$\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{\cos\gamma-\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{n}}}{\sqrt{2}\sin\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}.$$

Substituting this expression for $\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha$ into formula (7), we obtain

$$V=\frac{na^3\,\mathrm{ctg}\,\dfrac{\pi}{n}\cdot\sqrt{\cos\gamma-\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{n}}}{24\sqrt{2}\sin\dfrac{\pi}{n}\sin\dfrac{\gamma}{2}}.$$

Problem 4. *Find V, knowing a and* $\varphi$.

From formulas (3) and (5) it follows that

$$\mathrm{tg}\,\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{2}\cos\dfrac{\varphi}{2}\cos\dfrac{\pi}{n}}{\sqrt{-\cos\varphi-\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{n}}}.$$

Using formula (7), we obtain

$$V=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,na^3\,\mathrm{ctg}^2\,\dfrac{\pi}{n}\cos\dfrac{\varphi}{2}}{24\sqrt{-\cos\varphi-\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{n}}}.$$

**4. Calculation of the lateral surface area of ​​a pyramid**

The simplest cases are when $a$ and $\beta$, $a$ and $\gamma$ are known. We will begin by considering these cases.

Problem 5. *Find S, knowing a and* $\beta$.

Using the known relation $S=\dfrac{S_{осн}}{\cos\beta}$, as well as the fact that $S_{осн}=\dfrac{na^2}{4}\,\mathrm{ctg}\,\dfrac{\pi}{n}$ (see Problem 1), we obtain

$$S=\frac{na^2\,\mathrm{ctg}\,\dfrac{\pi}{n}}{4\cos\beta}. \tag{8}$$
