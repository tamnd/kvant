5. A charged particle in a uniform magnetic field moves in a circle whose radius $R$ is inversely proportional to the charge-to-mass ratio:

$$R = \frac{mv}{eB},$$

where $B$ is the magnetic field induction, $e$ is the particle charge. Thus, in non-relativistic mechanics this radius must increase without limit in proportion to the speed $v$. The charge $e$ does not depend on speed, but the mass in relativistic mechanics grows in accordance with formula (1). That's why

$$R = \frac{m_0 v}{eB\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}.$$

Assuming that $\dfrac{v}{c} = 1 - \delta$, where $\delta \ll 1$, we obtain at speeds $v$ close to $c$,

$$R = \frac{m_0 c}{eB} \cdot \frac{1-\delta}{\sqrt{1-(1-\delta)^2}} =$$

$$= \frac{m_0 c}{eB} \cdot \frac{1-\delta}{\sqrt{2\delta - \delta^2}}.$$

Discarding small corrections in the numerator and denominator, we find

$$R \approx \frac{m_0 c}{eB} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\delta}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{m_0 c}{eB} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v/c}}.$$

Thus, at $v \to c$ the radius of the circle along which the particle moves increases without limit in proportion to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-v/c}}$.

2. We take the vertices of the regular $n$-gon $A_1 A_2 \ldots A_n$ as the first system of points, and the center of the circle circumscribed around it as the second system.

Since the distance between the centroids is zero, then from formula (9) (p. 42) we have

$$0 = \frac{1}{n} \cdot nR^2 - \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i<j} |A_i A_j|^2.$$

Hence $\displaystyle\sum_{i<j} |A_i A_j|^2 = n^2 R^2$, which is what was required.
