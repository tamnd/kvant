II. GENERAL HYPERBOLE

Definition of a general hyperbola

From the article “Ellipse”, readers know that if a circle is subjected to a stretching-rolling transformation (RS)*, then it will turn into an ellipse. We will be interested in the figure in which each of the equilateral hyperbolas depicted on figure by 9 dashed lines will go, as a result of PC relative to the symmetry axis $A'A$.

The fundamental square $pqr$ of these hyperbolas will transform into the rectangle $PQRS$ (at Fig. 9), its diagonals $pr$ and $qs$ will also transform into straight lines $PR$ and $QS$.

Conjugate (red and blue) equilateral hyperbolas will transform into red and blue solid lines as a result of PC. Each of them also consists of two branches and a symmetry with respect to the midlines of the functional rectangle $PQRS$, the diagonals of which serve as asymptotes of these curves. But these new lines will no longer be congestive. We will call them still conjugate hyperbolas.

A curve consisting of two branches and obtained as a result of PC of a “complete” equilateral hyperbola relative to the midline of a functional square is called a general hyperbola or a simple periola.

*) See «Kvant», 1975, No. 1, p. 2
