It turns out that the properties of ordinary kinetic energy were distributed in relativistic mechanics between two different concepts. Which one should be called kinetic energy? This is largely a matter of taste. It is customary to call the quantity $T$ kinetic, and $E$ the total energy of the body.

So far we have not talked about unification, but rather about the “splitting” of concepts. Now the time has come to justify the words spoken in the epigraph.

Let us consider the question of the relationship between mass and energy. Taking into account the above, it is necessary to clarify what mass and what energy we are talking about. In what follows, unless a special reservation is made, we will understand by mass the relativistic mass $m$, and by energy the total energy of a free body $E$. Then from relation (3) it follows that they differ only in the factor $c^2$, and this factor is a world constant, that is, a quantity that can never change under any conditions. In this case, any statement relating to mass automatically refers to energy and vice versa. For example, any increase or decrease in the energy of a body, say by heating and cooling or by radiation,

The fact that mass and energy are measured in different ways (for example, mass by weighing**, and energy in a calorimeter) cannot, of course, be considered an objection to the coincidence of the physical meaning of these concepts. After all, we constantly encounter situations where the same quantity can be measured in several different ways. Only theory can definitively decide whether two different experimental procedures measure the same quantity or whether they provide information about different quantities.

The difference in units in which mass and energy are expressed is also unimportant. Thus, body temperature is usually expressed in degrees, but it is often expressed in ergs or electronvolts (energy units for measuring temperature are widely used in thermonuclear research). The difference in units does not mean a difference in the physical meaning of the quantities.
