The same calculations are made as on similar scales of a normal slide rule.

There are six scales on the back of this ruler: $L$ ($\lg x$), $LL_1$, ($e^{0,01x}$), $DF$ ($\pi \lg x$), $D$ ($x$), $LL_3$ ($e^x$) and $LL_2$ ($e^{0,1x}$) on the body and four scales $CF$ ($\pi \lg x$), $CIF = (1 : \pi x)$, $CI$ $(1 : x)$ and $C$ $(x)$ on the slider.

The double logarithmic scales $LL_3$, $LL_2$ and $LL_1$ are continuations of one another. When working with these scales, it should be taken into account that the length of the corresponding segment on the scale $D$, equal to $m \lg x$ on the double logarithmic scale, will be

$$m \lg x = m (\lg \lg N - \lg \lg e),$$

A

$$\lg x = \lg \frac{\lg N}{\lg e},$$

whence $\lg N = x \lg e$ or $N = e^x$.

To determine the results on the scales $LL_1$, $LL_2$ and $LL_3$, the value of the degree of the base is found on the scale $D$ of the body and, aligning the main pointer of the sight with it, read the value of the value $e^x$ under it on the corresponding scale $LL_1$, $LL_2$ or $LL_3$.

So, for example, to determine $y = e^{0,08}$, combine the main pointer of the sight with the stroke corresponding to the number 8 on the scale $D$, and under this pointer on the scale $LL_3$ read the answer $y = 1,0833$. The same is done when using scales $LL_1$ and $LL_2$.

To calculate $y = e^x$ when $x > 10$ do this. The exponent is divided into several parts, each of which must be less than 10, that is, the expression $y = e^x$ is represented as a product

$$y = e^{\Delta x_1} e^{\Delta x_2} \ldots e^{\Delta x_n},$$

where $\Delta x$ is a component of the exponent.
