What if the metal contains a small admixture of “foreign” ions? Let the alien's charge be $|e|Z'$. Due to the fact that the dimensions of the impurity ion are different, the number of electron collisions with it will also change. If the cross-sectional area of ​​the impurity ion is $\sigma'$, and the “friend” ion is $\sigma$, then the stranger will be subjected to $\dfrac{\sigma'}{\sigma}$ times more impacts than the friendly ion. The force acting on the impurity ion from the electrons will change by the same amount. Therefore, the sum of the field forces and the electron wind for the stranger is equal to (Fig. 5)

$$\mathbf{F}' = Z'\,|e|\,\mathbf{E} + Z\,\dfrac{\sigma'}{\sigma}\,\mathbf{f}_1.$$

Substituting $-|e|\,\mathbf{E}$ instead of $\mathbf{f}_1$, we get

$$\mathbf{F}' = |e|\left(Z' - Z\,\dfrac{\sigma'}{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{E}.$$

If $Z' > Z\,\dfrac{\sigma'}{\sigma}$, then the impurity ions will move in the direction of this force, their speed will be directed along the field. If $Z' < Z\,\dfrac{\sigma'}{\sigma}$, then the ions will move against the field.

How can we explain the fact that in pure mercury the ions move in the direction of the electron wind? We have shown that in pure metal the ions must be stationary! The fact is that all ions were assumed to be completely identical, but this is not entirely true. Although most ions are indeed in the same "normal" energy state, some ions always have energy greater than normal.

Such ions are called "activated". The probability of collisions between electrons and ions increases with increasing ion energy. We can say that the cross sections of activated ions seem to have increased compared to normal ones. Thus, activated ions can be considered foreigners with the same charge as a normal ion, but with a larger cross-section. Such ions are carried away by the electron wind and are carried towards the positive pole of the source. This is the basis for the separation of mercury isotopes in an electric field. Ions of different isotopes have almost the same charge and cross section, but activated ions of light isotopes are carried away by the electron wind faster than activated ions of heavy isotopes due to their lower mass. This leads to an increase in their concentration at the “plus”, that is, if mercury is sucked out near the positive pole, it will be enriched with a light isotope.

The “paradoxical” transfer of ions in mercury amalgams was first discovered in 1907. At the same time, the concept of mutual friction of ions and electrons was introduced. Subsequent theories, which lasted until 1959, took a step back: the movements of ions and electrons were considered independent. This was partly due to the inconclusiveness of the experimental results. In 1953, the effect of separating mercury isotopes by direct current was discovered. After this, a number of works appeared on the study of ion electrical transport. The first theoretical works, where the physical mechanism of action of the electron wind was revealed, date back to the beginning of 1959.

A whole group of interesting and important effects in both liquid and solid metals and semiconductors is associated with the electron wind.
