Focal property of hyperbole

It is known that the ellipse has a remarkable “focal” property: in its plane there are also points $F_1$ and $F_2$ (foci), which from the sum of the distances of each point $M$ of the ellipse to its foci is the same - it is equal to the length of the major axis of the ellipse:

$$F_1M + F_2M = 2a.$$ (6)

Hyperbole has a similar property. In its plane there are two points (foci of the hyperbola), such that the difference in distances of each point $M$ of the hyperbola for these lobes is the same - it is equal to the distance between the vertices of the hyperbola:

$$F_1M - F_2M = 2a.$$ (7a)

Here $F_1$ is the focus more distant from $M$ than $F_2$ (Fig. 12). Both foci lie on the symmetry axis of the hyperbola passing through the vertices $A$ and $A'$; the distance of each focus from the center of the hyperbola is

$$F_1O = F_2O = c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2},$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the semi-axes of this hyperbola and its conjugate **). Important connection between the vertex "hyperbole elements $a$, $b$ and $c$

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2$$ (8)

is analogous to the $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$ connection for an ellipse.
