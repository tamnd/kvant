Devices

Of the devices used for calculations, slide rules are of particular importance, without which not a single engineering calculation can be done. Since their creation*) hundreds of different designs have been proposed, both universal and specifically designed for calculations in certain ranges of values ​​containing special functional scales.

Universal slide rules are the most widely used. Of the reporting universal rulers, the most advanced is the Leningrad ruler with double logarithmic scales, which allows you to use natural logarithms, exponential functions in calculations, solve exponential and logarithmic equations, and calculate powers with fractional exponents faster than on conventional slide rules (GOST 5161-57). When using double logarithmic scales, it should be taken into account that on them the length of the segment $l$ from the beginning of the scale to the stroke corresponding to the number $N$ is equal to

$$l = m (\lg \lg N - \lg \lg e),$$

where $m$ is the modulus of the double logarithmic scale, and $e = 2,718$ ... is the base of natural logarithms.

On the front side of the case of the Leningrad line there are four scales: $K(x^3), A(x^2), D(x)$ and $D1(1:x)$. Using these scales
