More than 80 years have passed since then. The name of Sofia Vasilievna Kovalevskaya occupies a worthy place among the names of great Russian scientists of whom our Motherland is rightfully proud. The President of the USSR Academy of Sciences S.I. Vavilov said on January 13, 1950 at a ceremonial meeting dedicated to the 100th anniversary of the birth of S.V. Kovalevskaya: “The contribution made to science by Kovalevskaya during her short life is unusually complete and significant. Her fundamental research on the rotation of a rigid body served as the basis for the further development of the most important issues in mechanics in our country and throughout the world. Her subtle research on the theory of differential equations and on some issues of mathematical physics and theoretical astronomy remains important today. In the history of mankind, before Kovalevskaya, there was no woman equal to her in strength and originality of mathematical talent... Kovalevskaya was not only a mathematician. She distinguished herself as a writer - the author of novels, dramatic works and short stories - and as a publicist. She did not confine herself to a narrow mathematical specialty and deeply understood her civic duty to the country, to the people...”

The outstanding significance of Kovalevskaya’s scientific creativity was highly appreciated by all advanced Russian intelligentsia. An expression of this was an exceptional event for old pre-revolutionary Russia - the election of Kovalevskaya as a corresponding member of the Academy of Sciences in 1889. To do this, the Academy had to break the charter, first solve the fundamental issue of admitting females to election as corresponding members... The Great October Socialist Revolution forever wide opened the doors of the country's highest scientific institution for women, and every year the number of female scientists working in the Soviet Academy increases. The enormous creative scientific powers of the Russian woman were first revealed to the whole world by S. V. Kovalevskaya.”

S.V. Kovalevskaya showed the whole world the possibilities of women in the field of mathematical creativity. Two years after her death, the Prussian government allowed women to attend lectures as free listeners. In 1895, the Englishwoman Grace Chisholm received, following Sofia Vasilievna, the title of Doctor of Mathematics there, in Göttingen. Outstanding works on mathematics were written in the 20th century by Emmy Noether, who is one of the founders of modern algebra. In the Soviet Union, the names of academician P. Ya. Polubarinova-Kochina, professors O. A. Ladyzhenskaya, O. A. Oleinik and many other women are known, who have achieved remarkable results in various fields of mathematics.
