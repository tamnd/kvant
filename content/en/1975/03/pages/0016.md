Why don't we take into account collisions between electrons? The fact is that they do not lead to a change in the average speed of electrons. Indeed, let us consider the collision of two electrons. Let their speeds before the impact be $v_1$ and $v_2$, and after the impact $v'_1$ and $v'_2$. According to the law of conservation of momentum

$$mv_1 + mv_2 = mv'_1 + mv'_2,$$

where

$$\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2} = \frac{v'_1 + v'_2}{2},$$

that is, the average speed before the impact is equal to the average speed after the impact (Fig. 3).
