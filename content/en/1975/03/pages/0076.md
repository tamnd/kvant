## To the article “Mass and energy in the theory of relativity”

1. Let's substitute the expression for $m$ from formula (1) into (2) *) and square both sides of the equality. Resolving the resulting equality with respect to $v^2$, we find

$$v^2 = \frac{p^2}{m_0^2 c^2 + p^2}.$$

Substituting this expression again into formula (1) and taking into account that $E = mc^2$, we obtain

$$E = c\sqrt{m_0^2 c^2 + p^2}.$$

Since at $a \ll 1$ $\sqrt{1+a} = 1 + \dfrac{a}{2}$,

then at $p \ll m_0 c$

$$c\sqrt{m_0^2c^2+p^2} = m_0c^2\sqrt{1+p^2/m_0^2c^2} \approx$$
$$\approx m_0c^2\left(1 + \frac{p^2}{2m_0^2c^2}\right).$$

Since the rest energy is $E_0 = m_0c^2$, we get

$$E = E_0 + \frac{p^2}{2m_0}.$$

Although the second term is small compared to the first, it cannot be discarded, since it is this term that depends on the momentum.

For $p \gg m_0c$ we have

$$\sqrt{m_0^2c^2+p^2} \approx p\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_0^2c^2}{p^2}\right),$$

whence it follows that

$$E \approx pc + \frac{1}{2}\frac{m_0^2c^3}{p} \approx pc.$$

In this case, the energy turns out to be proportional to the magnitude of the impulse.

2. Putting $v = c$ in formula (2) and taking into account that $m = E/c^2$, we obtain a linear dependence of energy on momentum

$$E = pc,$$

where $p$ is the absolute value of the vector $\mathbf{p}$. In this case, it is better to rewrite formula (1) in the form $m_0 = m\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$. It follows that for a photon the rest mass is zero. From the result of solving Problem 1 it follows that the energy is proportional to the momentum of all particles whose rest mass is zero (in addition to photons, these also include neutrinos).

3. Considering that

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}} \approx \frac{1}{1-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{v^2}{c^2}} \approx$$
$$\approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{v^2}{c^2},$$

That

$$T = m_0c^2\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}-1\right) \approx \frac{1}{2}m_0v^2.$$

4. Since $\dfrac{v}{c} \ll 1$, we can use the approximate calculation formulas given in the problem statement. Up to terms $v^4/c^4$ inclusive (this means that terms including $v^6/c^6$ are discarded) we obtain

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{v^2}{c^2}}} \approx 1 - \frac{1}{2}\frac{v^2}{c^2} - \frac{1}{8}\frac{v^4}{c^4} \approx$$
$$\approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{v^2}{c^2} + \frac{1}{8}\frac{v^4}{c^4} + \frac{1}{4}\frac{v^4}{c^4} =$$
$$= 1 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{v^2}{c^2} + \frac{3}{8}\frac{v^4}{c^4},$$

$$T = m_0c^2\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}-1\right) \approx$$
$$\approx \frac{m_0v^2}{2} + \frac{3}{8}\frac{m_0v^4}{c^2}.$$

The first term on the right side of this expression is equal to the classical kinetic energy, the second is the relativistic correction. Their ratio is $\dfrac{3}{4}\dfrac{v^2}{c^2} \approx 0{,}53 \cdot 10^{-9}$ for the example under consideration.
