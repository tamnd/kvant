Electrostatic field

The problems in the “Electrostatics” section have much in common with problems on the weight of bodies in mechanics, but there are also fundamental differences between them. The difference between electrostatic problems and mechanical ones lies, first of all, in the fact that to describe the interaction of electrostatic charges, a special concept is introduced - the electrostatic field, as well as new quantities for the quantitative characteristics of this field - intensity and potential. If the electrostatic field is created by stationary charges, then we speak of an electrostatic field and the correspondence of the strength and potential of the electrostatic field.

The purpose of this article is to draw the attention of readers to the most difficult issues related to the concept of an electrostatic field, and to protect against possible errors.

Field strength

First of all, you need to get used to handling this value, learn how to calculate it in various cases. The principle of superposition of fields *) helps with this. It is enough to know the formula for the field strength of an exact charge in order to calculate the field strength of a charged body with an arbitrary distribution in the

It is equally important to reduce the properties of understandable field lines, which give a qualitative picture of the field distribution in space. Many questions can be answered by clearly imagining how the lines of force flow.

It is interesting to note that the value of the dimensions of field strength and electrostatic charge allows in some cases, with an accuracy of up to a numerical coefficient, the dependence of field strength on distance.

Often when solving problems in electrostatics, the fact that the field strength inside a conductor is zero and the electrostatic field does not penetrate through the conductor is used. This means that the geometric sum of the fields of all charges at any point inside the conductor vanishes. From here we can draw conclusions about the correspondence of the distribution of charges in space.

The fundamental law of electrostatics, Coulomb's law, is very similar to the law of universal gravitation. This, of course, facilitates the solution of a number of electrostatic problems. However, you need to remember about these various

Let's consider several specific tasks.

Task 1. Can a powerful electric charge placed in an electrical field be in a state of stable equilibrium? It is necessary that when the charge is mixed in any direction, a force acts on it, returning the charge to the equilibrium position. Consequently, the field lines must converge at the point where the charge is located. But the lines of force of the electrical retinal field begin on positive charges and end up on negative ones. At the point where the charge in question is located, there are no negative charges, and, therefore, the field lines external to the charge cannot converge at the point where it is located.

Task 2. Knowing the dimension of the electric field strength, find (with power up to a numerical coefficient) the field strength of an infinite plane located in a vacuum and having a charge surface density σ.

The electric field strength at a point located at a distance R from the plate can only depend on σ and R. According to the superposition principle, the dependence on should be linear, that is, E = |σ| f(R), where f(R) is an unknown function. Dimensions of field strength E and charge density σ, respectively

$$[E] = \frac{[q]}{[\varepsilon_0] [R^2]} \text{ и } [\sigma] = \frac{[q]}{[R^2]}.$$

Posny E = \frac{k |\sigma|}{\varepsilon_0}, where k is a dimensionless coefficient. The field strength does not depend on the distance. Exact calculations give the value $1/2$ for the coefficient k.

Problem 4. The properties of electrical dynamics (a system of two powerful charges +q and -q, located at a distance l from each other) are characterized by its electric moment p = ql.

Find the direction of the electric-dynamic field with a moment p in the power spaced from the center of the dynamism axis R >> l, in two cases:

1) the power lies on a straight line passing through the dynamism axis;
2) the power lies on a straight line perpendicular to the dynamism axis.

$$E_A = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left\{ \frac{1}{(R-l/2)^2} - \frac{1}{(R+l/2)^2} \right\}.$$

$$\frac{1}{(R-l/2)^2} - \frac{1}{(R+l/2)^2} \approx \frac{2l}{R^3}.$$

Potential and potential energy

Wash away more difficulties in solving electrostatic problems usually arise when using the concept of potential. Potential is an energy characteristic of a field, and problems using the concept of potential are primarily problems based on the law of conservation of energy.

The application of the law of conservation of energy in mechanics causes significant difficulties, mainly due to the complexity of the concept of potential energy of interaction between bodies *). In electrostatics we encounter a new type of potential energy. Moreover, due to the presence of charges of opposite signs, the potential energy P and, therefore, the potential $\varphi = \frac{\Pi}{q}$ can be both positive and negative.

You can choose arbitrarily, but once you select the zero level for a given problem, you cannot change it later when solving the problem.

Often when solving problems, the fact that all points of a conductor have the same potential is used. Indeed, the field strength inside the conductor is zero, and no work is done when a charge moves inside the conductor. A typical mistake that is quite often made is this: they choose a constant potential inside the conductor equal to zero and forget that then it is no longer possible to consider the potential of a charged body at infinity to be zero (it is at infinity that the zero level of potential energy, and therefore potential, is most often chosen).

Problem 5. *What is the potential at the center of a conducting sphere of radius $R$, carrying charge $q$?*

The electric field strength inside the sphere is zero. Therefore, the potential difference between any two points inside the sphere or on its surface is zero. In other words, the potential at an arbitrary point inside the sphere, and therefore at the center of the sphere, is equal to the potential of the surface of the sphere,

Problem 6. *Positive charge $+q_0$ is uniformly distributed over a thin wire ring of radius $R$. In the center of the ring there is a point charge $-q$, the mass of which is $m$.*

*The charge is given an initial speed $v_0$ along the axis of the ring. Determine the nature of the movement of the charge depending on the value of the initial speed. The ring is motionless.*

The total energy of the charge at the initial moment is equal to the sum of the kinetic energy and potential energy in the electrostatic field of the ring:
$$E_{\text{пол}}=\frac{mv_0^2}{2}-\varphi_0 q=\frac{mv_0^2}{2}-\frac{qq_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R},$$
since the potential is at the center of the ring
$$\varphi_0=\frac{q_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R}$$
(this follows directly from the principle of superposition).

At $E_{\text{пол}}\geqslant 0$ the charge will go to infinity. Moreover, its speed at an infinitely large distance will be equal to zero if $E_{\text{пол}}=0$. If $E_{\text{пол}}>0$, the speed of the charge at an infinitely large distance from the ring is
$$v=\sqrt{v_0^2-\frac{qq_0}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 Rm}}.$$

If $E_{\text{пол}}<0$, then the charge will perform periodic motion along the axis of the ring. The greatest distance $r$, by which the charge will move away from the center of the ring, can be found from the law of conservation of energy:
$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2}-\frac{qq_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R}=-\frac{qq_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 \sqrt{R^2+r^2}}.$$
From here
$$r=R\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{2\pi\varepsilon_0 mv_0^2 R}{qq_0}-1\right)^{2}}-1}.$$
