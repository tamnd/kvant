The article below by Tom Stevenson from the American magazine “Electronic Design News” does not actually hide the problem, but in order to expose the “Stevenson Paradox”, you need to remember some of the basics of the doctrine of electricity. This is what T. Stevenson writes in the article “High Voltage Generator”.

“While working on some articles recently, I got distracted by an interesting thought. Let's take two large copper disks. We attach a wire and an insulating sleeve to each of them. Let's give a hand to two small kama.

Next, have the boys hold the discs at a very close distance, but so that they do not touch each other. Let's charge the resulting capacitor from a 12-volt automatic battery. Let's disconnect the battery. If the boys stand quietly, the voltage between the disks remains equal to 12 volts. But what will happen when they wonder?

From the elementary equation $U$ (charge) = $\frac{Q}{C}$ (capacitance) we can see that if they halve the distance between the disks, then the voltage will also halve, because the charge cannot change, but the capacitance will double.
