## Schoolchildren about modern physics

Anyone who loves physics and wants to choose physics as their profession must know a lot and be able to do a lot. Solving problems, working in a physics laboratory, the student studies what is called a school physics course. But you always want to know not only what is in school textbooks, but also what physicists are working on in their laboratories.

First of all, popular science literature can help with this, especially if it is written by scientists who are themselves actively involved in the study of nature. Even if not everything in their story is clear, you can be sure that the reader will not be given erroneous, incorrect information, erroneous, for example, because the views of scientists have recently changed or reports of new experimental facts have appeared.

You cannot learn to solve problems from such books or articles; One should not even try to enter into arguments with the author, trying to come up with a new theory. Usually they present only the final results, and therefore it is almost impossible to understand why theories of phenomena in modern physics do not (almost never) have different versions,
why physicists are so confident in the correctness of their constructions. To understand this, you need to know a lot and feel how many demands nature places on theory and how many unexpected consequences arise from any changes in theory. Therefore, only those who have worked a lot in science and have personally experienced the complexity of the interweaving of natural phenomena can appreciate the significance and, of course, the reliability of theoretical explanations of what experience reveals.

It was these authors who wrote the articles included in the book that opens the new series “For Schoolchildren about Modern Physics” *). This book includes six articles; all of them were written by famous Soviet physicists.

The first article is called “Modern Astrophysics”. Its author, academician and Lenin Prize laureate V.I. Ginzburg, talks about new branches of astronomy - gamma-ray astronomy, which emerged in the last few years, and X-ray astronomy, which was born a little earlier. The article talks about neutron stars, pulsars and black holes, and new hypotheses for the evolution of the Universe.

The article “Universal Gravity and Gravitational Waves” was written by Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V. B. Braginsky and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V. N. Rudenko. This article talks about how it is possible to register gravitational waves that come to us from the depths of our galaxy, and talks about experiments that so far (contrary to the first statements) have not yielded any results. The article by candidate of physical and mathematical sciences V. A. Ugarov “The principle of relativity and the special theory of relativity” briefly but clearly talks about the main ideas of the theory of relativity.

The article by Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V.B. Berestetsky “Heavy Electron” is very interesting. It tells in detail about one of the elementary particles - the $\mu$-meson, or muon, as it is now called. This particle, with a mass approximately 200 times the mass of an electron, is very similar in its properties to an electron, but it “lives” only two millionths of a second (at rest).

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences I. S. Shapiro in the article “The Mechanism of Nuclear Reactions” talks about how physicists decipher the processes occurring in atomic nuclei, how information about nuclear forces and nuclear reactions is selected and systematized from a large number of experiments.

The last article in the book “Controlled Thermonuclear Fusion,” written by Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Lenin Prize laureate M. S. Rabinovich, is devoted to the problem of the practical application of physical discoveries of our century. The issue of using nuclear fusion energy is very complex. The article discusses all the arguments that led physicists to confidence in the successful practical completion of the idea of ​​“fusion.”

The publishing house "Prosveshcheniye" plans to publish several more collections "For schoolchildren about modern physics" (the second collection is currently being prepared for publication).
