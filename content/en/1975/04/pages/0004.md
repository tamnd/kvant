Did you know?
What curve does Foucault draw on the magnetic plane? In what direction does a stone thrown vertically upward from the surface of the Earth deflect?
Can a projectile fly strictly along the meridian?
Can you answer these questions? All these problems have a common feature: it is increasingly convenient to solve them in non-inertial reporting systems *), more precisely, in reporting systems rotating at a constant angular velocity. One can, of course, consider all phenomena in some inertial system, such as, for example, the system of a distant star or even the Sun (the unevenness of its motion relative to the distant star can be neglected). Although this path is absolutely correct and, following it, we will not leave the solid ground of Newton’s laws, nevertheless, it is often more convenient (and even more visual) to consider a phenomenon from the point of view of what is observed, located in the rotating system of reporting.

If the Earth did not rotate around its axis, then the trajectory of the projectile would lie in the meridian plane. Such a picture would be seen both as observed while on the Earth, and as observed while stationary outside the Earth.

However, the Earth rotates! From the point of view of a stationary observer (who finds the freshness of the Earth), the Earth rotates counterclockwise (if viewed from above at its north pole), therefore the plane of the fixed meridian rotates with the Earth from west to east, and the plane of movement of the projectile in space remains unchanged.

Observed on Earth (moving observable), sees a stationary plane of the meridian and a flying projectile that deviates from this plane to the west. From any point of view, the plane of motion of the projectile does not coincide with the plane of the prime meridian; only one observed attributes this discrepancy to the motion of the Earth, and the other to the motion of the projectile itself.

An Earthly observer (for example, an artilleryman) knows, of course, that the cause of the “deviation” is actually the rotation of the Earth.
