This description, of course, does not completely define a parabola. There are many curves that have the same form, but only some of them have the right to be called parabolas.

Of all the possible lines depicted on figure 2 and having a common axis $OY$, a vertex $O$ and a pair of symmetrical points $A$ and $A'$, only one - red - is a parabola. It passes through two "middle" points $B$ and $B'$ such that $x_B=\dfrac{1}{2}x_A$ and $y_B=\dfrac{1}{4}y_A$. This will be established from the definition of a parabola.

**Definition of a parabola and its first construction from points**

From the school course we know that the graph of the function $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ at $a\neq0$ is called a parabola. It is enough for us to consider the function

$$y = ax^2 \tag{1}$$

and restrict ourselves to the case $a>0$. Adding terms $bx$ and $c$ does not change either the shape or size of the graph, but only shifts it. The graph of function (1) for $a<0$ is the inverted graph of function $y = |a|x^2$. Therefore, we *define a parabola as the graph of function (1) for $a>0$* (of course, any

there is another curve that can be combined with this graph).

Instead of saying “a parabola is the graph of function (1),” one can say: “equation (1) is the equation of a parabola*). For a parabola (and for any line that intersects every line parallel to the $Y'Y$ axis at most one point) both expressions are equivalent. Let us also note the accepted turn of phrase: instead of the words “parabola having equation $y = ax^2$”, they say briefly: “parabola $y = ax^2$”.

From this definition of a parabola, we can deduce all the features of its shape that were mentioned: its infinite extension, symmetry, smoothness, the presence of a vertex, straightening of the parabola as it moves away from the vertex, the nature of the deviation from the axis of symmetry, proof that a body thrown at an angle to the vertical moves along a parabola. We will not conduct this evidence here. Let us dwell only on how the points $B$ and $B'$ on figure 2 were obtained and how to generally construct several points of a parabola if its vertices, the direction of the axis and one more point are given.
