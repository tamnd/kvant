1. The wave coming from the speaker, interfering with the wave reflected from the piston, forms a system of standing waves if the condition is met:

$$l = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}(2n-1),$$

where $l$ is the distance between the piston and the speaker, $\lambda$ is the sound wavelength, $n$ is an integer. On the dynamics - antinode - maximum amplitude, on the piston - node - minimum amplitude of oscillations. Let the microphone record the sound. If you move the piston, the standing waves disappear. They will appear again when the piston is moved a distance $l_1 = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}[2(n\pm1)-1]$, that is, a distance $\Delta l = \lambda/2$ from its previous position (Fig. 1). When detuned, due to the randomness of the phase difference of the summed signals, no sound is heard.

The value of $\Delta l = \lambda/2$ is the wavelength. Knowing the frequency $\nu$ and wavelength $\lambda$, we can determine the speed of sound $u = \lambda\nu$.
