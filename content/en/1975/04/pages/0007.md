We considered two cases of body motion relative to a rotating reporting frame. In the first example (rotating room), the body was moving in a non-inertial system in a circle, at any moment the speed of the body was directed tangentially to this circle. As we have seen, in this case the body is acted upon by the Korpolis force, which is directed along the radius of the circle, that is, perpendicular to the velocity vector.

In the second example (a ball on a disk), we showed that if at some moment of time the speed of the body is directed along the radius, then at that moment the body is acted upon by the Korpolis force, in a direction perpendicular to the body’s speed vector. The absolute value of the Korpolis force in random terms is directly proportional to the product $v \omega$, where $v$ is the absolute value of the body velocity relative to the rotating system at a given time, and $\omega$ is the value of the angular velocity of the rotating system relative to the iterative inertial.

And what will be the Korpolis force acting on a body whose instantaneous speed in a rotating reporting system is directed in an arbitrary manner? It is not difficult to show that the magnitude of the Korpolis force in this general case will be proportional to the product of $|v|$ and the magnitude of the angular velocity of the system (to do this, you only need to decompose the velocity vector into two components, one of which is directed along the radius, and the other - perpendicular to it along the tangent).

Now we still need to determine the direction of the Korpolis force. To do this, first of all, note that the angular velocity can be specified as a vector. Vector $\omega$ is parallel to the rotation axis. the direction of this vector is connected with the direction of rotation of the system using the rule of the gimlet (or screw): if you turn the gimlet with your hand in the same way as the system is rotated, then the translational movement of the gimlet indicates the direction of the angular velocity vector. You can also use another rule - the right hand rule: if you agree with the four right hands in the direction of rotation of the system, then the bent thumb indicates the direction of the vector $\omega$ (see figure 2).
