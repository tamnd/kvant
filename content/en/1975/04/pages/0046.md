Problem 4. The properties of electrical dynamics (a system of two powerful charges +q and -q, located at a distance l from each other) are characterized by its electric moment p = ql.

Find the direction of the electric-dynamic field with a moment p in the power spaced from the center of the dynamism axis R >> l, in two cases:

1) the power lies on a straight line passing through the dynamism axis;
2) the power lies on a straight line perpendicular to the dynamism axis.

In the first case, as can be seen from figure 3, the directionality at point A is equal to

$$E_A = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left\{ \frac{1}{(R-l/2)^2} - \frac{1}{(R+l/2)^2} \right\}.$$

Since R >> l, then

$$\frac{1}{(R-l/2)^2} - \frac{1}{(R+l/2)^2} \approx \frac{2l}{R^3}.$$

Hence,

$$E_A \approx \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2q l}{R^3} = \frac{2p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}.$$

In the second case (see Fig. 3) the directionality of the field created as

$$E_1 = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{1}{R^2 + l^2/4}.$$

The total directionality vector $E_B$ is directed along the dipole axis. Its magnitude is

$$E_B = 2E_1 \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \times \frac{q l}{(R^2 + l^2/4)^3} \approx \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3}.$$

Note that in both cases the directionality kills as $\frac{1}{R^3}$, that is, faster than the directionality of the exact charge (proportional to $\frac{1}{R^2}$).
