6. Let us introduce the distance between particles $r$ and the magnitude of the charge $q$. The particles will move around the center of mass (see Fig. 7) in a circle with radii $r_m = r M/(M+m)$ and $r_M = r m/(M+m)$ at speeds $v_m$ and $v_M$ such that

$$mv_m = Mv_M.$$ (1)

Based on P. Newton's law

$$\frac{q^2}{r^2} = \frac{mv_m^2}{r_m} = \frac{m (M+m) v_m^2}{M r},$$

from here

$$\frac{q^2}{r} = \frac{m (M+m) v_m^2}{M}.$$ (2)

Let the particle speed be increased by $n$ times. Then, based on the law of conservation of momentum, both particles will continue to move even at the smallest $n$ with a certain speed $u$ after removing the part to a very large distance (their relative speed in this case is zero). The law of conservation of momentum gives the equation

$$m (n-1) v_m = (M+m) u.$$ (3)

According to the law of conservation of energy

$$- \frac{q^2}{r} + \frac{m n^2 v_m^2}{2} + \frac{M v_m^2}{2} = \frac{(m+M) u^2}{2}.$$

Substituting here $v_M$ from (1), $r$ from (2) and $u$ from (3), we obtain a quadratic equation for $n$: $n^2 + 2n \frac{m}{M} - \left[ \left( \frac{m}{M} \right)^2 + 2 + 4 \frac{m}{M} \right] = 0,$

where

$$n = \left( \frac{m}{M} \right) \left[ \pm \left( 1 + \frac{M}{m} \right) \sqrt{2} - 1 \right].$$

Both roots make sense, since the new speed of the particle $m$ can be directed both along the speed $v_m$ (then the + sign) and against the speed (the - sign).
