## Focus and focal property of a parabola

From this method of constructing the points of a parabola follows its most remarkable property, which is often taken as the definition of a parabola.

Let us pay attention to the point $F$ of intersection of the straight line $QR$ and the main chord (see Fig. 7, a). It is easy to realize that this point also lies on the axis of symmetry $OY$. Indeed, triangles $RNQ$ and $ROF$ are equal, like right triangles with the same legs ($NQ = OF$, $OR = = RN$). Therefore, no matter what point $N$ we take, the straight line $QR$ constructed along it will intersect the main chord in its middle $F$. Now it is clear that $\triangle FMQ$ is isosceles. Indeed, the segment $MR$ is both the median and the height of this triangle. It follows that $MF = MQ$. The distance from any point of the parabola to the middle of the main chord is equal to its distance to the directrix. The midpoint of the main chord of the parabola thus acquires special significance for the parabola; it is called the focus of the parabola *), and the proven property is called the focal property of the parabola. The vertex of the parabola is the midpoint of the perpendicular drawn from the focus to the directrix.

The converse is also true: only the points of a parabola have this property (try to prove this yourself).

Thus, a parabola is a set of points equally distant from the focus and directrix. This characteristic property of a parabola is its simplest geometric definition. The third method of constructing the points of a parabola (Fig. 8) is based on this definition. Let us construct a series of concentric circles with a center at the focus $F$ (starting from the radius $\dfrac{p}{2}$) and a series of lines parallel to the directrix (starting from the one located at a distance $\dfrac{p}{2}$ from the directrix) so that the radii and these distances increase by the same amount.
