The distance $x_0$ is denoted by $p$; then $y_0 = \frac{p}{2}$. From equation (1) we have $\frac{p}{2} = a p^2$, whence $a = \frac{1}{2p}$ and equation (1) takes the form $y = \frac{1}{2p} x^2$ or $x^2 = 2py$. (2) It is called the canonical parabolic equation.

The segment $p$ takes the name parabola parameter *). The chord $M'_0 M_0 \parallel X'X$ (Fig. 6), passing at a distance $\frac{p}{2}$ from the tangent $X'X$ at the vertex, has length $2p$; we will call it the “lava chord” of the parabola; its meaning will be clarified later.

Equation (2) can be given the following geometric interpretation. For any point $M(x, y)$ of the parabola, the area of ​​the rectangle $ABCD$, one side of which is equal to the main chord $2p$, and the other to the ordinate $y$, is equal to the area of ​​the square $ONEH$, built on the abscissa.

In approximately this form, ancient Greek geometry, without knowledge of the language of formulas and letter notations, expressed equality (2).

*) More precisely, $p$ should be called the focal parameter of the parabola for a reason that will soon become clear.

The ancient Greeks called the construction of a rectangle with given sides, equal in size to a given square, “applying a given segment to a square.” The Greek word for “application” is parabole, hence the origin of the name of the curve.
