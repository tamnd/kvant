Parabolograph

It is not difficult to make a device (let's call it a parabolograph) for drawing other parabolas of any parameter by continuous movement (and, moreover, the most characteristic part of the parabola containing the vertex). A parabolograph consists of two identical squares and a thread. The longer the side of the squares, the larger the drawn arc will be. In addition to the parabolograph, you will need a ruler and 3 pushpins for drawing.

Glue both squares together (Fig. 9, a), sewing a preliminary thread between them so that one part of it is firmly included, and the other, free, comes out of vertex A less than an acute angle. On the free part of the thread, make a knot with a small (“point”) loop at such a point F of the thread so that the length of this part AF is equal to the length of the large roll AC of the square *). On a piece of paper, draw the focus and directrix of the parabola. Let's apply a ruler to the director so that it lies on the other side of the focus. We clamp the ruler between two buttons, turning on at its ends, insert the sharp thirds of the button into the loop F of the thread and stick this button into the focus (Fig. 9, b).

Let us apply a short parabolograph to the directrix ruler. We point a pencil and press the thread to the long leg at its point M so that the thread is taut. Point M lies on the parabola since AC = AM + MF and MC = MF. If you now move the parabolograph along the fixed ruler with one hand, and with the other press the thread with a pencil to the long leg, then the point of the pencil will sweep the arcs of the left half of the parabola. Turning the parabolograph over to the other side, we draw the second thread.
