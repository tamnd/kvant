We see that the Corolis force depends on two vector quantities - on the speed $v$ of the movement of the material point relative to the colliding coordinate system and on the angular speed $\omega$ of the reporting system itself. The magnitude of this force is proportional to the product of the velocity of the point and the angular velocity of the system, and its direction is uniquely determined by the direction of the vectors $v$ and $\omega$.

The formula for corolis force in modern books is written as follows:

$$F_K = 2 m v \times \omega,$$

where $X$ is the sign of the so-called vector product of two vectors.

The vector product of two vectors $A$ and $B$ perpendicular to each other is the third vector $C$, which is constructed as follows (Fig. 5). Its magnitude is numerically equal to the area of ​​the rectangle built on the vectors $A$ and $B$, that is, $|C| = |A| \cdot |B|$. the direction of the vector $C$ is perpendicular to the plane of the specified line/lib and is chosen such that the vectors $A$, $B$ and $C$ (in that order) constitute a right-handed vector system.
