As a result, the speed of rotation of the vessel decreases, but the vessel still rotates in the direction of the rotation of the Earth.

At some point in time, the angular velocity of rotation of the vessel becomes equal to zero, and then, under the influence of the moment of elastic deformation forces, the vessel begins to rotate in the opposite direction. The angular speed of rotation increases. Now the force of deformation begins to decrease (the thread unwinds). The action of the moment of the Corolis force (this force is still decreasing) is now directed against the rotation of the vessel and leads to a decrease in the rotation speed. . . At some point in the rotation, the decreasing moment of the deformation forces will “catch up” in magnitude with the moment of the Corolis force, after which the rotation speed begins to decrease, etc.

When conducting the experiment, we used a cylindrical vessel with a diameter of 25 cm and a height of 30 cm (see Fig. 1). The vessel was filled with a funnel with a response diameter of 8 mm. A coupling with four symmetrically located screws was found on the top tube of the funnel.

Soft rubber on a hard base was tightly pressed to the end of the tube using a thread thrown through two opposite screws. The thread was tied in a knot on one of the screws. We suspended the vessel from the ceiling on a double badminton string 2.5 m long (you can take two cords twisted in opposite directions to determine side effects during rotation). Water was poured into the vessel almost to the top edge, and when it calmed down, the thread knot was burned out. In all cases, we observed that as water flowed out through the outlet in the funnel, the vessel rotated first counterclockwise, and then into the opposite string.

The experiment can also be carried out with glass funnels suspended on double thin scaffolding. But it turns out that the angle of rotation of the vessel significantly depends on the diameter of the funnel outlet. On the one hand, the effect of the Corolis force is more noticeable, the smaller the response. But on the other hand, as the response decreases, the role of liquid viscosity increases. We were able to observe a noticeable rotation of the funnel, together with a cord of 1 liter of water, with a response diameter of 5 mm.
