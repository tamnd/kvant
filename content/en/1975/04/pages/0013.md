Let the following be given: the vertex of the parabola $O$, its axis $OY$ and the point $M_1$ belonging to the parabola (Fig. 3); the point $M_1'$, symmetrical to $M_1$ with respect to $OY$, also lies on the parabola. If $M_1$ is close to $O$, then double $x_1 = ON_1$ and quadruple $y_1 = OL_1$. (see Fig. 1). Points $M_2 (2x_1, 4y_1)$ and $M_2' (-2x_1, 4y_1)$ also belong to a parabola (prove). If point $\widetilde{M}_1 (\widetilde{x}_1, \widetilde{y}_1)$ was far from $O$, then take point $\widetilde{M}_2 \left(\dfrac{\widetilde{x}_1}{2}, \dfrac{\widetilde{y}_1}{4}\right)$ - it also lies on the parabola. We do the same with the resulting point $M_2$ (or $\widetilde{M}_2$). Having received several points of the parabola, we connect them with a smooth line by hand or using a pattern. Below we will indicate other ways to construct a parabola, which are practically more convenient.

**Coefficient $a$ of the parabola and its role**

There are an infinite number of different parabolas; each value $a$ corresponds to its own parabola *). figure 4 shows several of these

---

*) The number $a$ is one of the parameters of a parabola (for the definition of a parameter, see the article “Hyperbola”, p. 16.) But we deliberately avoid this word here, since for a parabola the word “parameter” is usually used in a special sense (see below).

⟦column⟧

parabolas - at $a = \dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{3}, \dfrac{1}{2}, 1, 1\dfrac{1}{2}, 2, 3, 5$.

It is remarkable that *all parabolas are similar to each other*, and if they are arranged as on figure 4, then they are similarly located with the center of similarity $O$. Indeed, let us take two such parabolas $y = ax^2$ and $y = a'x^2$ (Fig. 5) and draw an arbitrary ray $OMM'$ meeting the parabolas at points $M$ and $M'$, respectively. From the similarity of triangles $ONM$ and $ON'M'$ we have, on one side,

us, $\dfrac{OM'}{OM} = \dfrac{ON'}{ON} = \dfrac{x'}{x}$ or

$$\dfrac{OM'^2}{OM^2} = \dfrac{x'^2}{x^2}, \qquad \text{(a)}$$

and on the other,

$$\dfrac{OM'}{OM} = \dfrac{N'M'}{NM} = \dfrac{y'}{y} = \dfrac{a'x'^2}{ax^2}. \qquad \text{(б)}$$

Dividing (a) by (b), we get $\dfrac{OM'}{OM} = \dfrac{a}{a'}$,

that is, this relation is the same for all rays $OMM'$. And this is the definition of such an arrangement of two figures. All parabolas have the same shape: they differ from each other only in size. And the size of each parabola is determined by the coefficient $a$ in equation (1).
