## WHY ARE STOVES FIRED?
V.N. LANGE

⟦figure⟧

If you ask a heat engineering specialist and a person not experienced in this science why we heat stoves in winter, the first will probably answer: in order to increase the internal energy of the air in the room; while the second will almost certainly be expressed more simply: to make the room warmer. And a non-specialist will give a more accurate answer.

As implausible as it may seem at first glance, the internal energy of the air in the room after the combustion of wood in the stove will remain unchanged.

Let's assume that you arrive at your dacha in late autumn. The rooms are about 0°C, and to keep warm, you decide to light the stove. While the thermometer rises to 20°, let’s calculate how the internal energy of the air in the rooms changes during this time.

Under conditions close to normal (that is, at a temperature not very different from 0 ° C and a pressure of the order of $10^5 \, н/м^2$), air is similar to an ideal gas, the internal energy of which is proportional to the mass $m$ and the absolute temperature $T$:

$$U = \alpha m T,$$

($\alpha$ is the proportionality coefficient, constant for a given gas).
