We can say that to the right of the plate the field is created by two precise charges +q and -q and the charges (negative and positive) induced on both sides of the plate. (In fact, the total directivity of the electric field from the exact negative charge and induced positive charges is zero.) If the plate is thin (its thickness is small compared to the distance d), then the directivity of the field of induced charges outside the plate is zero.

Thus, it turns out that to the right of the plate, the electric field created by the charge +q and the induced negative charges coincides with the field created by two exact charges +q and -q, located at a distance of 2d from each other (Fig. 2). This means that the directivity of the field of induced charges at the point where the charge +q is located is equal to the directivity of the field of the exact charge -q.
