Let's consider several specific tasks.

Task 1. Can a powerful electric charge placed in an electrical field be in a state of stable equilibrium? It is necessary that when the charge is mixed in any direction, a force acts on it, returning the charge to the equilibrium position. Consequently, the field lines must converge at the point where the charge is located. But the lines of force of the electrical retinal field begin on positive charges and end up on negative ones. At the point where the charge in question is located, there are no negative charges, and, therefore, the field lines external to the charge cannot converge at the point where it is located.

Task 2. Knowing the dimension of the electric field strength, find (with power up to a numerical coefficient) the field strength of an infinite plane located in a vacuum and having a charge surface density σ.

The electric field strength at a point located at a distance R from the plate can only depend on σ and R. According to the superposition principle, the dependence on should be linear, that is, E = |σ| f(R), where f(R) is an unknown function. Dimensions of field strength E and charge density σ, respectively

$$[E] = \frac{[q]}{[\varepsilon_0] [R^2]} \text{ и } [\sigma] = \frac{[q]}{[R^2]}.$$

Posny E = \frac{k |\sigma|}{\varepsilon_0}, where k is a dimensionless coefficient. The field strength does not depend on the distance. Exact calculations give the value $1/2$ for the coefficient k.
