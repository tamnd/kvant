- There's not much left. Let's depict these inequalities graphically. Let us introduce the coordinate axes $R_1$ and $R_2$ (Fig. 2).

The inequality $R_1 \leqslant 1$ means that the permissible values ​​$R_1$ should lie not to the right of the line $R_1 = 1$, but $R_2 \leqslant 1$, which is not above the line $R_2 = 1$ - we denote this by shading. Putting $R_1 = 0$, from the third inequality of system (7) we find $R_2 \geqslant -3$, and putting $R_2 = 0$, we get $R_1 \leqslant 3/2$. Drawing a straight line through the points with coordinates $(0, -3)$ and $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, 0\right)$ and shading it from above, we obtain a graphical representation of the third inequality. Finally, we depict the fourth inequality in the same way.

- But it seems that not all of these lines are needed.

- Right! The most significant is presented on figure 3, where the permissible range of values ​​of the forces $R_1$ and $R_2$ is shaded. Points inside this region, including the boundary, satisfy all inequalities. But that's not all. In the admissible region, we need to find a point at which $P$, defined by equality (6), takes on the greatest value. First, let's draw a line

$$P = \frac{8}{3}\,(R_1 + R_2) = 0.$$

It passes through the origin as shown in figure 3.

“But this doesn’t give us much: it’s clear that the platform will withstand zero force.”

- Of course. But why will it survive? Because line $P = 0$ intersects the admissible region.

- Wait! It's not entirely clear to me. There are points on the line $P = \dfrac{16}{9}$ that lie outside the feasible region, say point $K$. If a distribution of forces corresponding to this point occurs in the pillars, then the platform will collapse and...

- You are wrong. Although I cannot prove this, I feel: if the material of the pillars is plastic, then the structure will not collapse at $P = \dfrac{16}{9}$. A distribution of forces will arise in it, expressed by some point $E$ inside the admissible region. True, it is not known which point exactly, but that doesn’t matter.

Let us now consider the family of lines $P = \dfrac{8}{3}\,(R_1 + R_2) = \mathrm{const} > 0$. They are parallel to line $P = 0$ (after all, they have the same angular coefficients). From these lines, you need to choose the one that is furthest from line $P = 0$, but at the same time intersects the permissible region. This is line $P = \dfrac{8}{3}\,(1 + 0) = \dfrac{8}{3}$ passing through point $B$ with coordinates $R_1 = 1$, $R_2 = 0$. It's clear?
