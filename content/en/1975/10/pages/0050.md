⟦folio 48⟧

Learn to make additional constructions
S. B. Belie

Almost every geometry problem can be solved in several ways. It is always interesting to try to find the simplest, “beautiful” solution - especially since such solutions are highly rated by examiners. Additional constructions often help with this. In one case, these constructions are directed by themselves, in others the dreams are not so obvious and require a lot of experience, ingenuity, and geometric intuition from the decider.

Let's move on to consider specific tasks.

Problem 1. On the sides AC, BC of a right triangle, outside its construction are the squares ACDE and BCKF. From points E and F, the redistributions EM and FN are dropped onto the extension of the hypotenuse. Prove that EM + FN = AB.

In the right triangle ACB we draw the height CL (see Fig. 1).

Obviously, ΔACL = ΔEAM, ΔBCL = ΔFBN. Therefore EM = AL, FN = LB, EM + FN = AL + LB = AB.

Problem 2. (MIPT, 1970). The height of an equilateral boundary is greatest if its diagonals are mutually redistributed and the area of ​​the boundary is equal to S.

Let us draw a line through vertex C parallel to the diagonal BD until it intersects with the continuation of the base of the trapezoid AD at point E (see Fig. 2). Since the trapezoid is equilateral, and its diagonals are mutually redistributed; then triangle ACE is equilateral and right-angled (prove it!). In triangle ACE we draw the height CF and denote its length by h. Clearly AF = CF, but AF is equal to the midline of the trapezoid, so S = h². Therefore, h = √S.
