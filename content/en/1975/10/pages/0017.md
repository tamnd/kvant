Using the concept of negative resistance, we study, for example, the stability of an electric arc (Fig. 6, a), connected in series with the usual resistance $R$ and a source of constant e. d. c. $\mathcal{E}$ (we will assume that the internal resistance of the current source is included in the resistance $R$). This task is of practical importance, since when using an arc for welding or as a light source, it must burn steadily, without fluctuations in current.

First of all, let's find what direct current can flow in such a circuit. According to Ohm's law, for the entire circuit the sum of the voltage drops is equal to the electromotive force:

$$U(i) + iR = \mathcal{E}.$$

Crossing the term $iR$ to the right side, we find that the voltage on the arc $U(i)$ is equal to $\mathcal{E} - iR$. On the other hand, $U(i)$ is determined by the current-voltage characteristic. Therefore, possible currents can be found graphically as the intersection points of the current-voltage characteristic and the dependence $\mathcal{E} - iR$, the latter, obviously, being represented by a straight line with a negative slope (Fig. 6, b).

As can be seen, in principle, there are two intersections and, accordingly, two current values ​​$i_1$ and $i_2$ are possible in the circuit. Are both current stable?

Fig. 6, b.

When alternating current occurs, its amplitudes at the arc and at the resistance $R$ are the same (they are connected in series). Therefore, comparing the energy $(\Delta i_\pi)^2r$ generated at the negative resistance of the arc and the energy loss at the general resistance $(\Delta i_\pi)^2R$, we see that the condition for the increase in oscillations will be $|r| > R$. Let us now turn again to figure 6, b. It is easy to see that at current $i_1$ the arc has a larger negative resistance in abscyl value than $R$ (the tangent slope is greater than that of the straight line), and the condition for the occurrence of oscillations is fulfilled. With current $i_2$, on the contrary, $R > |r|$, and oscillations cannot occur. Thus, we can say that the arc will burn stable if the circuit impedance is positive.

If the arc is included in an oscillatory circuit, then minor vibrations may occur in it. To do this, it is necessary that the circuit resistance and the negative arc resistance) be negative. However, to produce insignificant electromagnetic oscillations, they now use not a voltaic arc, but more reliable electrical systems with negative resistance.
