“And I don’t remember, although I studied their works.” But let's continue our reasoning. The forces $R_1, R_2, R_3$ and $R_4$ cannot be any, they must satisfy the conditions of equilibrium. Two of them are obtained if we equate to zero the moments from all forces acting on the platform relative to the lines 1-4 and 1-2. Considering that the moment is the product of forces on the surface, we obtain two equations:

$$4R_2 + 4R_3 - P = 0,$$

$$4R_3 + 4R_4 - 2,5P = 0.$$

The third equilibrium equation must be obtained from the equality to zero of the sum of all forces acting on the platform - otherwise this platform would fall down or ascend to the sky.

$$R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + R_4 = P,$$

Of the three equations (2), (3) and (4), four unknowns $R_1, R_2, R_3, R_4$, of course, cannot be determined. But you can add the following condition: the force $P$ must be maximum. “Let’s leave aside the shadows of the great ones and solve this simple problem,” continued Jean-Baptiste. “To do this, let’s express $R_3$ and $R_4$ from equations (2) and (3):

$$R_3 = \frac{1}{4}P - R_2,$$

$$R_4 = \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{5}{2}P - 4R_3\right) = \frac{3}{8}P + R_2$$

$$R_1 + R_2 + \frac{1}{4}P - R_2 + \frac{3}{8}P + R_2 = P.$$

$$P = \frac{8}{3}(R_1 + R_2)$$

$$\begin{cases} R_1 \leqslant 1, \\ R_2 \leqslant 1, \\ \frac{2}{3}R_1 - \frac{1}{3}R_2 \leqslant 1, \\ R_1 + 2R_2 \leqslant 1. \end{cases}$$
