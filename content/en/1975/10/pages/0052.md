Problem 6. (MSU, Mechanics and Mathematics, 1973). *In a trapezoid, the diagonals are 3 and 5, and the segment connecting the midpoints of the bases is 2. Find the area of ​​the trapezoid.*

First of all, note that the points $E$ and $F$—the midpoints of the bases $BC$ and $AD$ of the trapezoid $ABCD$ (see Fig. 6)—and the point $O$ of the intersection of the diagonals lie on the same straight line (prove it!). Through point $C$ we draw two straight lines: one parallel to the diagonal $BD$ until it intersects with the continuation of the base $AD$ at point $L$, the other parallel to $EF$. Since $OF$ is the median of triangle $AOD$, then $CK$ is the median of triangle $ACL$. Now we will complete the triangle $ACL$ to a parallelogram, and the median $CK$ to its diagonal. $S_{\square ABCD} = S_{\Delta ACL} = S_{\Delta ACQ}$ (why?). It remains to note that $\Delta ACQ$ is rectangular, $S_{\Delta ACQ} = 6$.

Problem 8 (MIPT, 1973). *Two circles of radii $R$ and $r$ $(R > r)$ have an external tangency at point $A$. Through the point $B$, taken on the larger circle, a straight line is drawn, touching the smaller circle at the point $C$. Find the length of the segment $BC$ if the chord $AB$ is equal to $a$.*

Through the center $O$ of the smaller circle and the point $B$ we draw a straight line (see Fig. 8). Then $BC^2 = OB^2 - r^2$. By the cosine theorem

$$OB^2 = (R + r)^2 + R^2 -$$
$$-2R\,(R + r)\cos\sphericalangle AO_1B,$$

where

$$\cos\sphericalangle AO_1B = \frac{2R^2 - a^2}{2R^2}.$$

Hence,
$$BC^2 = (R+r)^2 + R^2 - 2R\,(R+r)\times$$
$$\times\ \frac{2R^2 - a^2}{2R^2} - r^2 = a^2\left(\frac{R+r}{R}\right),$$

$$BC = a\sqrt{1 + \frac{r}{R}}.$$
