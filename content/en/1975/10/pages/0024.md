Finally, the peculiarity of secret devices requires that the currents and voltages in them be subject to restrictions
$I_1 \geq 2, I_2 \geq 1, |U_3| \geq 1, 5$. (11)
The problem of finding values ​​of $I_1, I_2, I_3, E_1, E_2$ that satisfy constraints (9)–(11) and provide a minimum of the objective function (8) is a linear programming problem. Having expressed from equation (9) and (10) the currents through the emf $E_1$ and $E_2$: $I_1 = \frac{1}{5} (3E_1 + E_2)$,
$I_2 = \frac{1}{5} (E_1 + 2E_2)$, $I_3 = \frac{1}{5} (-2E_1 + E_2)$, and substituting the EMF values ​​into inequalities (11), we obtain three restrictions:
$$\begin{cases} 3E_1 + E_2 \geq 10, \\ E_1 + 2E_2 \geq 5, \\ |-2E_1 + E_2| \geq 7, 5. \end{cases}$$

“He doesn’t understand,” said Vitya, who was also familiar with linear programming. - There are model marks here, what will you do with it?

— I’ll write the third constraint in the form of two inequalities
$E_2 - 2E_1 \geq 7, 5; E_2 - 2E_1 \leq -7, 5,$
- Kolya sent. — Now let us draw straight lines corresponding to all the constraint inequalities (see Fig. 5), and obtain two admissible regions $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ ($E_1$ and $E_2$ positive)
