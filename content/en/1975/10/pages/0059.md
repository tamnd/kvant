Puzzles
Dudeney

The names of many famous popularizers of mathematics have been mentioned more than once on the pages of our magazine: Hugo Steinhaus, Szma Loyd, Martin Gardner, Lewis Carroll (the author, apparently, of the well-known and beloved “Alice in Wonderland”). Now we want to tell you about another wonderful master of puzzles - Henry E. Dudeney (1857-1930): this year, in the same series as three books by Gardner, books by Steinhaus and Carroll, his wonderful book “520 Puzzles” * was published.

Composing and solving puzzles was not only a professional thing for Dudeney (Dudeney is a talented selfie: he never went to college) - it was his recognition, his life’s work. If some interesting idea came to his mind during dinner, he would begin to draw thoughtfully... right on the tablecloth. The answers that Dudeney himself gave to his puzzles (we will publish them in the next issue «Kvant») very often cannot even be called “solutions” - rather, they are more brilliant than a contract. The book “520 Puzzles” will certainly be of interest to all mathematics lovers. You guys will find it especially useful: it has a lot of tasks available to you. Below we publish a selection of problems from the book by G. E. Dudeney. The publication was prepared by I. N. Klimova.

Subway stairs

One day, leaving Curley Street tube station, we ran into the young athlete Percy Longman. He stayed on the ecalator and said:

— I always go up the ecalator. You know, extra training never helps. This ecalator is the longest on the line - almost a thousand steps. But here’s what’s interesting - and this also applies to another, changing ecalator, along which I often have to climb: if, while climbing up, I step through two steps, then the last step takes one step; if I take three steps, then two steps; if in four, then five; if in five, then four; if after six, then five and, finally, if I step through seven steps, then the last step takes six steps. Why this happens, I don’t know.

When Percy went further up, stepping over three steps at once, we laughed and my companion said:

“He hardly suspects that if he took a step of 20 steps, then for the last step he would have 19 left!”

How many steps are there in the ecalator at Curley Street station, if the top platform is considered a step and the bottom one is not? **)
