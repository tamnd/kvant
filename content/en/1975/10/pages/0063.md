To the article “Why does the violin sound”

1. Vibrations are excited in the same way as in a string with a bow, but with a decreasing dependence of the moment of force trem on the relative angular velocity of the pendulum and the axis.
2. The current in the circuit is determined by the graphical points of intersection of the straight line $U(i) = \mathcal{E} - iR$ with the current-voltage characteristic of the lamp (Fig. 1). Current surges occur at resistance values ​​$R_1 = tg\alpha_1$ (with a decrease in resistance) and $R_2 = tg\alpha_2$ (with a decrease in resistance), determined by the overlap of the tangents to the current-voltage characteristic. The dependence of current on resistance is shown in figure 2.
3. When the current deviates from the specified value $i$, an emf appears in the circuit. With. induction equal to $-L \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t}$, where $L$ is the inductance of the volt arc. Ohm's law in this case looks like: $\mathcal{E} - L \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} = U(i) + iR$.
From here we find the rate of change of current: $\frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} = \frac{\mathcal{E} - iR - U(i)}{L}$. Let us now turn to figure 6, $\delta$ in the article and examine the emission of currents $i_1$ and $i_2$. It is easy to see that when the current changes near the value $i_2$, the increase in current corresponds to a negative value of the expression $\mathcal{E} - iR$ - $U(i)$ and, consequently, a negative rate of change of current. This means that after the deviation the current will decrease, tending to return to the value $i_2$. When the current decreases, on the contrary, $\frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} > 0$, and the current will increase to a value of $i_2$. Therefore, the current value $i_2$ is stable. Similar reasoning for current $i_1$ shows that this value is unstable - deviations will increase. Thus, we obtain (as well as with the help of energy reasoning) that a stable state is a state with a total positive resistance of the circuit.
4. Fluctuations in the blowing current cause fluctuations in its temperature. As a result, the temperature and density of the air must also change periodically, and a sound wave will arise.
