**The Birth of Linear Programming**

Only in his declining years, in 1823, the recognized mathematician Jean-Baptiste Fourier (1768-1830), a former friend of Napoleon, former prefect of the Isère department and creator of the “Theory of Heat”, gave a somewhat strange for those times report on linear inequalities at the French Academy, in which he explained the formulation and indicated the path to solution

Nevertheless, the name of this problem - *linear programming* - was born only in the forties of our century. The most famous applications of linear programming are related to economics \*). However, the need to find the extremum of a linear function under restrictions in the form of a system of linear equations and inequalities arises in a wide variety of problems, including those very far from economics. An example is the mechanics problem discussed above about the action of a concentrated force on a square plate - the first application of linear programming known to us.

In a more general form, the linear programming problem is formulated as follows. *There are n unknowns $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$. It is required to find their values ​​that satisfy the system of restrictions*

$$\left\{\begin{array}{l} a_{11}x_1 + \ldots + a_{1n}x_n \leqslant a_1, \\ a_{21}x_1 + \ldots + a_{2n}x_n \leqslant a_2, \\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot \\ a_{r1}x_1 + \ldots + a_{rn}x_n \leqslant a_r, \\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot \\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot\ \cdot \\ a_{m1}x_1 + \ldots + a_{mn}x_n \leqslant a_m \end{array}\right.$$

*and deliver maximum functionality*

$$z = p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 + \ldots + p_nx_n,$$

*called “target”. Coefficients $a_{11}, a_{12}, \ldots, a_{mn}$ and free terms $a_1, \ldots, a_m$ are considered given.*
