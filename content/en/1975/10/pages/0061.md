Pat Murphy

Many years ago such an incident occurred. Participants of one expedition fell into the clutches of bloodthirsty savages. Their wax, having received rich gifts, finally relented and allowed the captives to leave, but on the condition that half of them would be flogged. The expedition included 5 Englishmen and 5 native porters. The English decided to avoid flogging by standing in a circle as shown in figure, and instructing Pat Murphy (No. 1) to name a number, counting down to which would be used as a count.

The one who received the named number left the circle and was sent to execution, and the counting continued from this point again until the named number fell on the next person, etc.

If Pat had filled out the number correctly and started counting with the right person, then the big deletes would have been a great idea. But poor Pat got the number wrong and the man started counting from the wrong place. As a result, all the English were flogged, but they didn’t rush around.

Could you please indicate:
1) the number that poor Pat called, and the person from whom the counting began;
2) the number to be called and the person to start counting with?
In each case you need to find the minimum number.

Cats and mice

One morning, at Professor Rackbrane's table, the issue of exterminating rodents was being discussed, when suddenly the professor said:

— If a certain number of cats ate a total of 999,919 mice, and all the cats ate the same number of mice, then how many cats were there in total?

Someone suggested that perhaps one cat had eaten all the mice, but Rackbrain countered that he had said "mice." Then someone else kept the sentence that each cat ate one mouse, but the professor noticed that he said "mice." He also added, to help those present, that each cat ate more mice than there were cats.

Which answer will be correct?
