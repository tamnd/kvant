The dry friction forces depending on the speed in this case are shown in Fig. 1. In the violin, the friction forces between the bow and the string have exactly this character. The vertical section at $v=0$ corresponds to the slim resting friction. If the relative speed of the string and bow $v$ corresponds to the falling section $0<v<v_0$, then an increase in the relative speed by a certain small value $\Delta v$ corresponds to a decrease in slim friction, and vice versa, with a decrease in speed the corresponding change in slim friction is positive (see Fig. 1). As you can see now, it is thanks to this ability that the energy of the string can be increased due to the work of slim dry friction.

When the bow moves, the string deflects along with it. In this case, the fusion of rest is balanced by fusions of string tension (Fig. 2). Equilibrium tension $F$ if tension is proportional to the deviation of string $x$ from the equilibrium position:

$$F = 2T_0 \sin \alpha = \frac{4T_0}{l} x,$$

where $l$ is the length of the string, and $T_0$ is the merging of the string, which can be considered constant for small deviations. Therefore, when the string moves with the bow, the fusion $F$ will increase, and at the moment when it becomes equal to the maximum fusion of rest $F^0_{tp}$, slipping begins.

Fig. 2.

For now, for simplicity, we will assume that at the moment of the beginning of sliding, the change in slim friction occurs abruptly: it decreases from the maximum value of the slim friction of rest to a small slim friction of sliding. In other words, after the start of sliding, the movement of the string can be considered almost free.

At the moment of breaking, the density of the string was equal to the density of the bow, and at first the string will continue to move in the direction of the movement of the bow. But now the balancing slim tension is not constrained by anything, so it will inhibit the movement of the string, slowing it down. At some point, the density of the string will drop to zero, then the string will begin to move in the opposite direction, and after the maximum deviation from the equilibrium position, it will again move in the direction of the bow movement. And the bow continues to move evenly with speed $u$. At some points the strings and the bow will be equal. In this case, there is no longer any slippage between the string and the bow, and a fusion of rest appears, equal to the equilibrium of the fusion of tension.

With further movement, the tension slim decreases and, accordingly, the rest merge decreases. After the string passes through the equilibrium position, it is easier to repeat.
