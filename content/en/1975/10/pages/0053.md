Through point B we draw a straight line parallel to AM until it intersects with the continuation of side AD at point E, and through point A we draw a straight line AF parallel to BD (see Fig. 9), $S_{\triangle EBD} = \frac{3}{4}$ (why?). Now, due to the similarity of triangles EFA and EBD, we can write: $\frac{S_{\triangle EFA}}{S_{\triangle EBD}} = \frac{EA^2}{ED^2}$.

But $\frac{EA^2}{ED^2} = \frac{1}{9}$, from here we find $S_{\triangle EFA} = \frac{1}{12}$. But $S_{\triangle EFA} = S_{\Delta BOM}$, and now we can find the area of ​​the OMCD quadrilateral:

$S_{OMCD} = S_{\triangle BDC} - S_{\triangle BOM} = \frac{5}{12}$.

Let's draw some conclusions. When starting to solve a geometric problem, you need to keep in mind that a general geometric problem can be solved in several ways. Therefore, if you have an idea for solving a problem, but you see that the path to the solution is quite long, then try to find a different approach to the solution. It should be remembered that additional constructions can provide significant assistance.
