Soviet science headquarters

In October 1975, a solemn anniversary of the USSR was held in Moscow, dedicated to the 250th anniversary of its founding. The USSR holds it as a spring national holiday of Soviet science and a review of its outstanding achievements.

The Academy of Sciences of our country was created by decree of Peter I, issued on January 28, 1724 (old style). However, the grand opening of the Academy took place after the death of Peter I in August 1725.

The celebration of the anniversary of the USSR Academy of Sciences began back in 1974. During this time, scientific research was carried out in its institutes and scientific institutions. Ceremonial meetings were also held at the Republican Academies of Sciences. Numerous exhibitions and reviews of the results of scientific work were organized, held under the motto “Achievements of science for the national economy.” Meetings of major scientists with work teams and collective farmers took place. The current anniversary of the USSR concludes these celebrations.

The history of the Academy of Sciences is inseparable from the history of Russian science. 250 years is a long time. And during this time, the role and place of the Academy in our science underwent significant changes. These changes inevitably affected the state of all areas of Russian science.

In the period preceding the Great October Socialist Revolution, the state of mathematics and physics in Russia was significantly different. The works of N. I. Lobachevsky, M. V. Ostrogadsky, P. L. Chebyshev, A. M. Lyapunov, A. A. Markov, V. A. Steklov created worldwide authority in Russian mathematics. Physics in Russia was much less developed. True, during this period it was also adorned by A.G. Stoletova, P.N. Lebedeva, N.A. Umova, but the general level of development of Russian physics was significantly lower than abroad, and it did not have a noticeable influence on the development of world physics. It is not for nothing that the outstanding physicist A.F. Ioffe wrote in his memoirs about pre-revolutionary Russian physics that the majority of talented Russian physicists were forced to carry out their research in foreign institutes and laboratories.

The October Revolution radically changed the position of science in our country. Science was turning into one of the most important means of building a new socialist society. V. I. Lenin paid great attention to the successful development of young Soviet science. In the face of the difficult conditions of the civil war and economic devastation, Lenin sought to do everything possible to help scientists who, together with the entire people, suffered severe poverty and deprivation. He spent a lot of effort to unite the efforts of scientists and direct them to solve the most important problems of the national economy.
