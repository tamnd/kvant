History of mathematics

In the three-volume collective work “History of Mathematics” (ed. “Nauka”, M., 1970-1972), in the preparation of which professors A. P. Yushkevich, who is also the editor of the entire publication, I. G. Bashmakova, B. A. Rosenfeld and other prominent specialists in this field participated, the history of mathematics is described from ancient times to the 19th century.

To properly understand modern science, knowledge of its history is necessary. All the more useful is a story that contains a statement of the essence of the main discoveries, ideas, and methods. The book under review introduces the reader to the world of science, which sometimes resembles a world of fantasy. It is written in such a fascinating and vivid language that it is read with unusual ease.

The first volume consists of two parts: “Mathematics in Antiquity” and “Mathematics in the Middle Ages”. First, the development of mathematics in prehistoric times, the development of counting, the emergence of numerical notations, and geometric concepts are covered. Then the reader becomes acquainted with the mathematics of the ancient Egyptian kingdom, with the first papyri, the Savylonian six-decimal number system and the clipart notation of the solution of some quadratic equations, with the change in areas and volumes and the first applications of the Pythagorean theorem in the general case. The description of the history of mathematics in ancient Greece given at the end of the first part is extremely interesting. If before that individual problems were solved, then science was born in ancient Greece. The book introduces the reader to the most prominent ancient Greek scientists and their works. From the second part of the first volume, the reader learns about the mathematics of ancient and medieval China, ancient and medieval India, then a description of mathematics in the countries of the 8th-10th centuries, in particular Central Asia, is given. Next, the mathematics of feudal Europe and Transcaucasia, its emergence and the influence on its development of the mathematics of ancient Greece, Byzantium and the Arabs are considered. The description of mathematics and stories about mathematicians of the Renaissance are interesting.

The second volume introduces the reader to mathematics in the 17th century. Discoveries in each field of science are discussed in detail. Here the reader gets acquainted with Descartes, his geometry, the mathematical works of Fermat, Desarates and many other mathematicians. An interesting and fully accessible chapter introduces the reader to the discovery of differential and integral calculus by Newton and Leibniz.

The third volume introduces the reader to the mathematics of the 18th century, a time of rapid development of various branches of mathematics and, first of all, mathematical analysis.

This chapter describes the works of one of the greatest mathematicians of the 18th century, St. Petersburg academician Euler, as well as Maclaurin, D'Alembert, D. Bernoulli, Lagrange, Gauss and others.

More advanced chapter on probability theory: Reading the mathematical part requires knowledge of the basic concepts of probability theory. But the descriptive part itself is integral.

A separate chapter is devoted to geometry. It examines analytical and differential geometry on the plane and in space, curves of higher orders. The works of Clairaut, Monge, and Euler are especially consecrated. The history and basic concepts of spherical geometry and trigonometry are presented in an interesting way.

The final two chapters deal with the solution of ordinary differential equations and partial differential equations with arbitrary invariant calculus. Most of the material in these chapters requires special mathematical training. However, readers «Kvant» can get acquainted with the history of the issue and the formulation of problems from these chapters.

Unfortunately, fucking this interesting and well-designed book is not good and she has already become a bibliographic editor. It is necessary to reprint the book in a large print run for the mass reader: students of physics and mathematics schools, teachers, students and general mathematics lovers.

N. S. Petrakov
