1. Vibrations are excited in the same way as in a string with a bow, but with a decreasing dependence of the moment of force trem on the relative angular velocity of the pendulum and the axis.
2. The current in the circuit is determined by the graphical points of intersection of the straight line $U(i) = \mathcal{E} - iR$ with the current-voltage characteristic of the lamp (Fig. 1). Current surges occur at resistance values ​​$R_1 = tg\alpha_1$ (with a decrease in resistance) and $R_2 = tg\alpha_2$ (with a decrease in resistance), determined by the overlap of the tangents to the current-voltage characteristic. The dependence of current on resistance is shown in figure 2.
3. When the current deviates from the specified value $i$, an emf appears in the circuit. With. induction equal to $-L \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t}$, where $L$ is the inductance of the volt arc. Ohm's law in this case looks like: $\mathcal{E} - L \frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} = U(i) + iR$.
From here we find the rate of change of current: $\frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} = \frac{\mathcal{E} - iR - U(i)}{L}$. Let us now turn to figure 6, $\delta$ in the article and examine the emission of currents $i_1$ and $i_2$. It is easy to see that when the current changes near the value $i_2$, the increase in current corresponds to a negative value of the expression $\mathcal{E} - iR$ - $U(i)$ and, consequently, a negative rate of change of current. This means that after the deviation the current will decrease, tending to return to the value $i_2$. When the current decreases, on the contrary, $\frac{\Delta i}{\Delta t} > 0$, and the current will increase to a value of $i_2$. Therefore, the current value $i_2$ is stable. Similar reasoning for current $i_1$ shows that this value is unstable - deviations will increase. Thus, we obtain (as well as with the help of energy reasoning) that a stable state is a state with a total positive resistance of the circuit.
4. Fluctuations in the blowing current cause fluctuations in its temperature. As a result, the temperature and density of the air must also change periodically, and a sound wave will arise.

**To the note “Perpetual motion machine?”**

(see p. 54)

The ball $A$, whose mass $m_1 = \rho_1 V_1$, is affected by the gravitational force

$$F_1 = \gamma \frac{m_1 m_2}{R^2} - \gamma \frac{m_1 m_2'}{R^2} = \gamma \frac{m_2 - m_2'}{R^2} m_1 -$$

the resultant of the cauterization force on the side of the ball $B$ ($m_2 = \rho_2 V_2$) and the cauterization force on the side of the “mirror reflection” of the ball $B$ - ball $B'$ ($m_2' = \rho V_2$). The force $F_1$ can be called the weight of the ball $A$ in the field of gravity of the ball $B$. By analogy with the “ordinary” weight - the weight of bodies in the gravity field of the Earth - we can assume that in the field of forces of the gravity of the ball $B$ all bodies acquire “gravitational acceleration”

$$g' = \gamma \frac{m_2 - m_2'}{R^2}.$$

However, $F_1$ is not the only force acting on the ball $A$: we have scored another force - the buoyant force of Archimedes. As is known, this force $F_1'$ is equal in magnitude to the weight of the fluid displaced by the ball $A$, that is, $F_1' = \gamma \frac{m_2 - m_2'}{R^2} m_1',$ where $m_1' = \rho V_1$ is the mass of the displaced fluid. For example, this force is opposite to the direction $F_1$. So, the resulting force acting on the ball $A$ is

$$F_A = F_1 + F_1'; F_A = \gamma \frac{m_2 - m_2'}{R^2} (m_1 - m_1').$$

In a similar way, we can find the resulting force acting on the ball $B$:

$$F_B = \gamma \frac{m_2 - m_2'}{R^2} (m_1' - m_1).$$

It can be seen that $F_B = -F_A$, that is, the interaction forces between the balls satisfy Newton’s third law.

At $\rho_1 < \rho < \rho_2$ the forces $F_A$ and $F_B$ are directed outward, that is, the balls are stretched. If $\rho_1 > \rho$ and $\rho_2 > \rho$, then the forces $F_A$ and $F_B$ are directed internally, that is, the balls are aligned.

Eternal wanderer. The secret of the impossibility of getting into “house D” is that all cells are divided into two sets: type $\nabla$ and type $\Delta$. All cells of the form $\nabla$ are filled with odd numbers, and cells of the form $\Delta$ are filled with even numbers. When moving from a cell to an adjacent cell, the cells are crossed. Obviously, if a path takes a route consisting of the number of balls indicated in the “odd” cell ($\nabla$), it inevitably ends up in the “even” cell ($\Delta$). If a series of balls makes a path from cell $\Delta$, in which an even number is written, then it again ends up in the “even” cell ($\Delta$). And the traveler’s house belongs to the set of “odd” cells of the form $\nabla$. Consequently, the traveler never gets home.

We determine all these parts and subtract the resulting number from 55. The number of tens contained in the difference indicates the number of the box with the defective parts.
