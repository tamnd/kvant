## Scientists about Lenin

... We, the people of science, especially appreciate in the majestic appearance of Lenin his tireless concern for the prosperity of Soviet science. From the very first days of the existence of Soviet power, Lenin was closely involved in issues related to the development of science. Lenin's well-known directives back in 1918 clearly defined the tasks of the Academy of Sciences in the economic revival of the country, in the development of the productive forces of our Motherland. We are proud that the Academy of Sciences in all its activities was invariably guided by Lenin’s instructions. (From the speech of the President of the Academy of Sciences, Academician V.L. Komarov on February 14, 1944)

...At present, our historians, studying authentic materials and documents from the first years of the revolution, are convinced of the enormous active role of Vladimir Ilyich in the reform of the Academy of Sciences. In short, the further development of the Academy, the enormous dimensions of which we now know, was already foreseen in the first instructions of Vladimir Ilyich. In the very first months of the revolution, Vladimir Ilyich gave permission for the organization of a number of research institutes in Moscow and Leningrad...

... From the very first steps of the revolution, V.I. Lenin took all measures for the further broad growth of both scientific personnel and scientific institutions. This was required by the country, which was to turn into a socialist country. At the same time, Vladimir Ilyich’s exceptional attention to science was determined, of course, not to a small extent by the fact that he himself was the greatest scientist and left us an unforgettable example of both the importance of the results achieved and the methods of scientific work. (From the speech of Academician S.I. Vavilov, dedicated to the memory of V.I. Lenin, January 20, 1940)

...Building a new life, carrying out the world's greatest social revolution, fighting for a new Soviet state in a war-torn, poor and ignorant country, Lenin did not stop thinking about cultural construction and scientific growth. He treated science and scientists with great attention and exceptional care. Everyone knows the facts of his direct intervention in issues related to scientific construction; Probably, many already know how much he contributed to our Academy of Sciences in its desire to initiate and develop research work that was disrupted during the years of war and revolution. (From the speech of the President of the Academy of Sciences, Academician A.P. Karpinsky on January 21, 1928)

... The range of his knowledge is clearly evidenced by his rich, invaluable literary heritage, and by the notes that he left in the margins of the enormous number of books he read. Look through Lenin’s “Philosophical Notebooks”, and it will not be difficult for you to see what a deep, thoughtful character his notes were, what a roll call he kept in them with outstanding representatives of the scientific world. So tirelessly, day after day, Lenin’s sharp thought was honed, equally strong in both subtle analysis and powerful synthesis...

... Vladimir Ilyich was not just a bearer of world knowledge, he was the brightest representative of an advanced science that does not separate itself from the people and their needs, that science that is created not only to understand the phenomena of life around us, but also to transform them for the benefit of the working people, the science of revolutionaries, replacing the science of doctrinaires. (From the memoirs of Academician G. M. Krzhizhanovsky)
