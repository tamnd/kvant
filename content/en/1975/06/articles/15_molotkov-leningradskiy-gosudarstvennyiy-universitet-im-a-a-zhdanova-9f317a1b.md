## Leningrad State University
them. A. A. Zhdanova

Leningrad State University has three physics and mathematics departments: the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics, the Faculty of Applied Mathematics - Control Processes, and the Faculty of Physics.

The most distinctive feature of university education is the breadth and fundamentality of the knowledge acquired, which represents the basis on which certain applied disciplines are built. A university graduate has ample opportunities to apply his knowledge and easily adapts to the constantly changing tasks posed by practice.

The Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics has received 400 students in recent years and trains graduates in four specialties - mathematics, applied mathematics, mechanics and astronomy. In accordance with this, the faculty has 4 departments, admission to which is carried out through a separate competition.

In the mathematics department, students specialize in modern areas of analysis, algebra, number theory, geometry, differential and integral equations, probability theory and mathematical physics.

The Department of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics prepares graduates in computational methods, operations research, computer software and automated control systems, and theoretical cybernetics.

At the mechanical department, along with an in-depth study of mathematics, students receive specialization in one of the modern areas of mechanics: analytical mechanics, theory of vibrations, automatic regulation and control, flight dynamics, theory of elasticity and plasticity, hydro- and aerodynamics (including the aerodynamics of rarefied gases), physical mechanics.

In the astronomical specialty, training is carried out in the following specialties: astrophysics, radio astronomy, astrometry, celestial mechanics, stellar astronomy.

The Faculty of Applied Mathematics - Control Processes trains highly qualified personnel in applied mathematics with deep general theoretical training and experience working on modern computers. Annual reception - 200 people.

Main specializations: mathematical support for computers and automated control systems and the use of computer technology. At the faculty, scientific research is conducted on the management of technical objects, control of technological processes and design processes of complex systems, distribution of forces and means, general mathematical and mechanical issues of control systems.

The Faculty of Physics admits students to the following specialties: physics, radiophysics and geophysics. The total number of students enrolled in the first year is 300 people. Graduates from 14 departments are trained in physics. Of these, four are purely theoretical, where students study issues of quantum mechanics, field theory and elementary particles, statistical physics and mathematical physics. The remaining departments mainly produce experimental physicists - specialists in nuclear physics, solid state physics, optics and spectroscopy, polymer physics, atmospheric physics, etc. The scientific work of students, in which they are involved, starting from the 2nd-3rd year, takes place in the laboratories of the Research Physics Institute at Leningrad University and other research institutes of Leningrad. Currently, the faculty has moved to new premises, where excellent conditions have been created for fruitful work. This year, a dormitory for 1000 people is being rented near the faculty, in which students will live.

Problem a 1. We place a bow arrow at a distance of 40 m from a fortress wall 30 m high. The initial speed of the arrow is 40 m/s. Under what conditions is the arrow pointing towards the horizon and at the moment of firing the arrow moving through the wall? The reduction of air, the growth of the boom, the thickness of the fortress wall, the influence of the operation and m. d. we do not take into account. Free pressure acceleration is equal to 10 m/s². We do not consider the limiting case of an overhead boom on a wall.

The task is not difficult. To solve it, you only need to know the formulas for uniformly accelerated motion and the rule for adding velocities. Let t be the moment in time when the arrow reaches the horizontal distance l = 40 m to the wall, α is the initial angle of the arrow overhead to the horizon. For an arrow to fly over a wall, its height at moment t must be greater than the height of the wall h = 30 m. Hence

$$v_0 t \cos \alpha = l$$
$$v_0 t \sin \alpha - \frac{g t^2}{2} > h$$

($v_0 = 40 \text{ m/c}$). Eliminating t and substituting the numerical values ​​$v_0$, l, h, we obtain the inequality of relative α:

$$tg^2 \alpha - 8 tg \alpha + 7 < 0$$

whence 1 < tan α < 7. Since, according to the meaning of the problem, the angle α lies in the range from 0 to $\frac{\pi}{2}$, then $\frac{\pi}{4} < \alpha < \arctg 7$.

Problem a 2. A uniformly moving ball collides with a resting ball with a central impact. During a collision, 25% of the kinemic energy is transferred to the mello. What is the proportion of speed between the mass of balls so that the moving ball continues to move in the original direction with a speed of at least 50% of the initial one?

To solve the problem, you need to correctly write the laws of conservation of energy and momentum at the moment of collision. An additional complexity is that these laws are formulated for the materials of points, and when solving the corresponding equations, a second “non-physical” answer appears, corresponding to the penetration of one ball through another. This answer should be discarded. Overall the task is difficult.

Let M and m be the masses of the ball at rest and in motion, $v_1$ and $v_2$ the initial and final velocities of the moving ball, u the final velocity of the ball at rest. From the conservation laws and the conditions of the problem it follows that

$$\frac{3}{4} mv_1^2 = Mu^2 + mv_2^2$$
$$mv_1 = Mu + mv_2$$
$$u \geq v_2 \geq \frac{1}{2} v_1 > 0$$

(the condition u ≥ v₂ excludes the “non-physical” answer). Let us introduce new variables x = $\frac{v_2}{v_1}$, y = $\frac{u}{v_1}$, t = $\frac{m}{M}$. In them the system will take the form

$$\frac{3}{4} t = y^2 + tx^2$$
$$y = t (1 - x)$$
$$y \geq x \geq \frac{1}{2}$$.

Eliminating y from the equation

$$(t + 1) x^2 - 2tx + \left(t - \frac{3}{4}\right) = 0$$

relative to the x value. From here

$$x_{1,2} = \frac{t \pm \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{3-t}}{t + 1}$$

$$y_{1,2} = \frac{t \left(1 \mp \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{3-t}\right)}{t + 1}$$.

Thus, we come to the limitation t ≤ 3. These physical conditions mean that with a larger mass ratio (at t > 3) 25% of the energy cannot be lost for any type of impact. From the condition y ≥ x it follows that a solution only with a lower sign in front of the radical is suitable. It remains to satisfy the condition x ≥ $\frac{1}{2}$, which, taking into account the found expression for x and the very strong inequality t > 0, takes the form $t^2 - t - 2 \geq 0$. This inequality holds for t ≥ 2. Answer: 2 ≤ $\frac{m}{M} \leq 3$.

Problem a 3. Spormsten No. 1 we start from point A to point B up the river. After him, after 5 minutes, sportsten No. 2 is sent. Their speeds are maximum, which

they would have reached point B at the same time, but halfway through the journey athlete No. 1 turned back and, meeting athlete No. 2 150 m from point A, returned to point A 5 minutes after athlete No. 2 started. Find the speed of athlete No. 2 if the speed of athlete No. 1 in still water is 1 m/s.

If the problem is solved in general form, then it leads to an equation of the third degree, but the given numerical values ​​of the quantities greatly simplify the solution. The difficulty of the task is medium.

Let $u$ be the speed of the river flow (in $м/с$), $v$ be the desired speed of athlete No. 2 (in $м/с$), $s$ be the distance from $A$ to $Б$ (in $м$). The following system of equations follows from the conditions of the problem:

$$\begin{cases} \dfrac{s}{1-u} = \dfrac{s}{v-u} + 300, \\[2mm] \dfrac{s}{2} \\ \dfrac{1-u}{} + \dfrac{s}{2} \\ \dfrac{1+u}{} = 600, \\[2mm] \dfrac{\dfrac{s}{2}}{1-u} + \dfrac{\dfrac{s}{2} - 150}{1+u} = \dfrac{150}{v-u} + 300. \end{cases}$$

Subtracting the 2nd from the 3rd equation, we find

$$v = \frac{u+1}{2u+1} + u.$$

On the other hand, equating the expressions for $s$ found from the 1st and 2nd equations, after simple, albeit lengthy calculations, we obtain that

$$v = \frac{u+2}{2u+1}.$$

From here, excluding $v$, we arrive at the equation
$$2u^2 + u - 1 = 0,$$
which has only one positive

root $u = 1/2$. Therefore $v = 1\dfrac{1}{4}$ m/s.

Let $\beta$ be the desired inclination angle of the second plane, $l$ be the length of the path of the material point along this plane (see Fig. 1). Obviously $l = s/\sin(\alpha+\beta)$. Acceleration $g\sin\beta$ acts on the second inclined plane. Therefore, the time of movement of a material point along the second plane is equal to

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2l}{g\sin\beta}} = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{g\sin\beta\sin(\alpha+\beta)}} =$$

Task 6. Naming real solutions of the equation

$$\frac{x^2}{x-1} + \sqrt{x-1} + \frac{\sqrt{x-1}}{x^2} = \frac{x-1}{x^2} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{x-1}} + \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x-1}}.$$

Despite the simple formulation of the problem, its solution raises some difficulties. Trying to get rid of irrational thoughts “at any time” does not lead to anything good. The symmetry of the given equation should be used. His ODZ: $x > 1$. Let $a(x) = x^2, b(x) = x-1, c(x) = \sqrt{x-1}$. then the given equation can be written in the form $\frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a} = \frac{b}{a} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{a}{c}$, from which

$$\frac{(c-b)(b-a)(a-c)}{abc} = 0.$$

Thus, solving the original equation is equivalent to solving three equations: $a(x) = b(x), b(x) = c(x), c(x) = a(x)$, which in this case are easily solved. Answer: $x = 2$.
