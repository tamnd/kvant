Entrant-1975

A. Ya. Yakovlev

After graduating from high school in Kursavka (Stavropol Territory), Lyuda F. decided to enter the mathematics department of the Stavropol Pedagogical Institute. Moreover, her mathematics teacher gave her a B in algebra and an A in geometry on her certificate.

During the written exam, Lyuda was given 4 problems (we will give the answers to them at the end of the magazine).

1. The name of the surface of a regular triangular paramid, the side of the base of the side is equal to a, the angle between the lateral borders is equal to β.

2. Solve inequality
$$\log_2^2 x - \log_2 x - 6 \leq 0.$$

3. Solve the equation
$$\sin x + \sin 2x + \sin 3x = \cos x + \cos 2x + \cos 3x.$$

4. At what values ​​of a are both roots of the equation
$$x^2 - (a+1)x + (a+4) = 0$$
negative?

Alas! While solving the first problem, Lyuda could not remember what the angle between the lateral boundaries was. Then I designated by $\frac{\beta}{2}$ the angle between the lateral edge and the height of the paramid. Of course, the answer was wrong.

Solving the inequality, Luda found the following roots of the left side: $x_1 = \frac{1}{8}, x_2 = 4$. “But $x = \frac{1}{8}$ is not suitable,” writes Luda, “since the graph at this point is positive. This means there is only one root: $x = 4$.》 Then Lyuda formulates the answer: “For $x = 4$ the graph is negative.”

Is it possible to specifically come up with such an incomplete one?

When solving a trigometric equation, Luda writes: “sin 2x = cos 2x - there are no roots, since sine and cosine cannot simultaneously equal the same number.” Obviously, Luda stated that sin 45° = cos 45°, that is, that the sine and cosine of an angle of 45° are equal. And this happened many times in the eighth grade, and in the ninth, and in the tenth.

Finally, solving the fourth problem, Lyuda writes: “The roots are negative if the discriminant is less than zero,” and begins to transform the discriminant.

Let's leave Lyuda's grades in the certificate to the teachers' own. There are quite a few errors in written work and other applicants. Celebrate those who received fours and fives in mathematics on their certificates.

Of the 225 who entered the mathematics department of the Stavropol Pedagogical Institute, only ... four completely coped with the work. If they failed (they got D's) - sixty, although the grades became liberal for everything.

At the nearby Stavropol Agricultural Institute, almost 50 percent of applicants failed. Including exactly half of the residents of the regional center, including six out of seven graduates of school No. 6, four out of four graduates of school No. 17.

The results of written exams in mathematics in Moscow universities are slightly better. Every fourth applicant received an unsatisfactory grade at the Moscow Institute of Chemical Engineering, almost every third in the Automotive Institute, and up to forty two percent in the Standard Instrumental.

And this despite the fact that the requirements for applicants are not very high. The problems offered in the entrance exams are exactly the same as in school problems. If the inspector has doubts, a higher score is given. And the mathematical preparation of applicants, as evidenced by examiners of all types, is declining from year to year. “Every year we are forced to simplify the tasks for written exams,” says the projector of the Moscow Standard Instrumental Institute. This is also evidenced by a comparison of the options for university entrance assignments.

In technical, economic and many other universities, the entrance exam in mathematics is the main one. It is the mathematics exam that shows the quality of an applicant’s preparation for studying at a university, shows whether a given applicant can successfully study at a given university. Very often it is at the mathematics exam that the question is decided: to be or not to be a student. Why does the math exam also play an important role?

If a student at a chemical university did poorly in chemistry at school, this is, of course, bleak, but it is predictable: he will study the chemistry course at the university “from the very beginning.” If he has poorly cleared trigonometric equations and transformations or makes mistakes in operations with degrees, believing, for example, that $x^{-3} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}x}$, he will not be able to successfully study at an engineering or economics school. Physics and chemistry, theoretical mechanics and strength of materials, probability theory and statistics, as well as other engineering and economic sciences will require clear, fast, automated skills in calculations and transformations. And the university won’t teach all this again. Otherwise, it is fair to say that the university program is built on the mathematical foundation laid in high school.

It’s not so bad if the student doesn’t know the formula for volume parameters: it can be found in any reference book; it is much worse if the student cannot use the correct formula to solve the problem or does not know which formula to apply.

“Matematika is a language,” said the leading American physicist W. Gibbs. This is the language in which the conditions of arbitrary, technical, economic, and military problems are expressed, and solutions to these problems are obtained; it is also an apparatus with the help of which the same chemistry, the same strength of materials, the same economic sciences are applied. However, one of the most important tasks of today's engineer (economics, researcher, scientist) is the formalization of the problems to be solved, their formulation in the language of mathematics.

Further, a hundred years ago, the engineer Cyrus Smith, having found himself on the “Tannsteven Island” (remember Jules Verne!), could apply his mathematical knowledge very widely and not without crawling. And science, technology and economics today are simply unthinkable without the use of mathematics, without the ability to use it correctly, quickly and accurately.

The importance of mathematics and its applications has especially increased in recent decades. Every engineer, economist, researcher is forced to use the help of a computer; engineers and economists cannot do without linear programming and other mathematical methods; Probability theories and mathematical statistics are playing an increasingly important role.

How does a written entrance exam in mathematics differ from a school test, from problems solved in class? Methods of solution? Difficulty of the task? The scope of the program?

Not at all. Neither one nor the other, nor the third.

Mathematics entrance exam programs are absolutely no different from high school programs. Any problem proposed in the entrance exam can be solved by consistent application of the rules, formulas, theorems that were studied in high school. Often the exam simply presents problems from school problem books, sometimes from other well-known problem books. The tasks given above are also no different from school ones. The most time-consuming of them is the first task. To solve it, it is necessary to sequentially perform several separate stages, each of which could be a separate task in grades VII-IX of the school. However, many schoolchildren solve individual individual problems quite satisfactorily, and only a very few complete a complex task of 5-6 stages.

It is no secret that a student often solves problems not entirely independently: the teacher asks a leading question, reminds them of the required formula, corrects a mistake, and indicates a path or plan for solving it. It happens that a student simply copies the solution from the board or even (let’s be honest?) from a neighbor. The homework problems are similar to those discussed in class. And during an exam at a university, yesterday’s schoolchild is given a sheet of paper with problems that he has to solve on his own: no one asks leading questions, no one reminds about the missing coefficient, “similar” problems for some reason don’t come to mind, and on the neighbor’s sheet the problems are not at all similar.

Almost all applicants know the necessary formulas and theorems and even know how to prove them. But, firstly, not everyone knows how to apply these formulas and theorems. And, secondly, a large number of arithmetic errors and oversights

ditch leads to a sharp (but quite fair!) decrease in grades for written work in mathematics.

After all, the overwhelming majority of problems facing a specialist are to find a number: the number of tons, the number of rubles, the number of workers, the number of kilometers per hour, etc. Some school teachers sometimes do not lower their grade for arithmetic errors and oversights: just think, this is arithmetic! But if a newly built bridge collapses under the very first truck, will the engineer be able to justify that, when designing the bridge to carry a load of 50 tons, he “inadvertently” placed the comma one place to the left?

...When calculating the sine of the angle, the applicant received a value of 2.8 instead of the required 0.28. An obviously ridiculous answer was supposed to attract the applicant’s attention and force him to look for an error. But he didn't. I couldn't (or didn't want to) think. And received an unsatisfactory grade. Those who cannot (or do not want) to think are not accepted into universities!

The lack of clarity of calculations and transformations is especially unpleasant when solving complex problems. When performing a separate stage, the applicant is forced to concentrate attention on the action itself, often losing the thread of reasoning, forgetting which stage comes next. Very often, a mistake due to inattention (a missed number, an incorrect sign, a carelessly performed or unclearly recorded action) leads to a deliberately incorrect answer. And it takes much more time to find an error than to check each action performed.

Therefore, developing clear skills in calculations and transformations, fluency and automaticity in elementary actions is a necessary condition for both successfully passing a written exam in mathematics and for successful study at a university.

In conclusion, some advice to the 1975 Entrant.
