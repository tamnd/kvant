## Beta transformations of nuclei and properties of neutrinos
B. G. Erozolimsky

The entire world accessible to our knowledge, as is known, consists of particles of matter interacting with each other—the so-called elementary particles. These are, first of all, electrons, protons, neutrons, photons and dozens of other particles. It has now become obvious that the term “elementary particles” is inappropriate. In fact, these particles cannot be considered as elementary building blocks of the universe; they themselves are complex formations. The main proof of this is that all currently known elementary particles are capable of transforming into each other. Apparently, it is impossible to understand the “structure” of particles without understanding the features of their interactions with each other, which, in particular, lead to mutual transformations of particles.

Currently, four types of interaction of matter particles are known: gravitational, electromagnetic, nuclear (strong) and the so-called weak.

The reader is certainly familiar to one degree or another with the first three types of interaction. Let us only recall that the most intense is the nuclear interaction (which is why it is also called the strong interaction). The attractive forces between nucleons, that is, protons and neutrons in the nucleus, determine the stability of the atomic nucleus. To destroy the core, to overcome nuclear forces, it is necessary to

The electromagnetic interaction is somewhat weaker, but still quite intense. This is the usual attraction or repulsion of electrically charged particles.

Gravitational interaction is the least intense of all interactions; it has no practical significance in the life of elementary particles.

The last type is weak interaction. It was called that because it is really much weaker than nuclear and electromagnetic interactions. However, its role in the microcosm is great. It turns out that weak interactions account for most of the transformations of some elementary particles into others.

For the first time, science encountered such transformations in the study of natural radioactivity, discovered at the very end of the 19th century. Research by scientists, primarily Marie and Pierre Curie and Ernest Rutherford, showed that the nuclei of some chemical elements are capable of spontaneous disintegration, that is, turning into the nuclei of other elements. Such transformations are accompanied by radiation. Three types of radiation are known, which are designated by the letters $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. As it turned out,

α-rays are streams of nuclei of helium atoms, β-rays are streams of electrons, and γ-rays are streams of photons. We will talk in detail only about β-decays (when the transformation of nuclei is accompanied by the emission of fast electrons).

Where do the electrons that fly out at great speed come from? Apparently, from the nucleus, especially since during β-decay the electric charge of the nucleus changes by just one. So there are electrons in the nucleus? The first nuclear model was indeed built from protons and electrons. Only later did it become clear that such a structure of the nucleus is impossible; the properties of atomic nuclei and elementary particles are incompatible with this hypothesis. From a modern point of view, the nuclei of all elements consist of protons and neutrons. How then to explain β-decay? This is where the transformation of particles into each other begins!

It turns out that during β-decay of a nucleus, one of the neutrons in the nucleus turns into a proton, which is accompanied by the birth of an energetic electron *):

$$n \rightarrow p + e^-. \tag{1}$$

In this case, the serial number of the element (nuclear charge) increases by one accordingly. For example,

$${}_{82}\mathrm{Pb}^{214} \rightarrow {}_{83}\mathrm{Bi}^{214} + e^-$$

- an isotope of lead is converted into an isotope of bismuth with the same atomic weight.

It should be especially emphasized that process (1) contains a complete and decisive break with the idea, deeply rooted in the minds of scientists of the last century, that particles of matter cannot be born or disappear. The emission of an electron from the nucleus during a β-transformation is considered here not as a consequence of the fact that it (the electron) was there before, but

Over the past two decades, with the help of accelerators, a large number of new elementary particles have been discovered: mesons (particles lighter than a proton, but heavier than an electron), hyperons (particles heavier than a proton). All of them, as it turned out, are short-lived and also turn into each other, like a free neutron.

The advent of nuclear reactors, as well as isotope separation techniques, led to the discovery and production of a large number of new artificial radioactive substances. Among them there are many whose β-decay results in the emission of not an electron, but a positron ($e^+$). The nuclear charge (atomic number) decreases by one accordingly. The fact is that the mass of the neutron, and therefore its own energy, is greater than that of the proton. And therefore, the transformation of a free proton into a neutron would violate the law of conservation of energy.

Currently, this area of ​​physics—the physics of weak interactions—is rapidly developing. Since the 30s, when E. True, the existing ideas are very far from the harmony and completeness that distinguish, for example, the current picture of electromagnetic interaction.

According to modern views, any interaction can be described using a certain field, and the emergence of forces of attraction or repulsion between particles is due to their ability to “give birth” and absorb quanta of this field. The theory establishes a deep connection between the mechanism of interaction of forces acting between particles - the sources of a given field, and the resulting “transformations” of these particles, which are accompanied by the birth of quanta of this field.

For example, the interaction between electric charges is caused by an electromagnetic field, the quanta of which are photons. The same interaction leads to the birth of a quantum (photon), say, when a rapidly flying charge (electron) is decelerated. The “transformation” reaction here is:

$$e \rightarrow e' + \gamma,$$

where $e'$ is the electron with the changed energy.

A similar situation occurs in the case of weak interaction. The very forces of interaction between particles, for example nucleons, are columnar against the background of nuclear and electromagnetic forces, so that their manifestations could only be detected indirectly, and then in very subtle experiments. But the processes of transformations during which quanta of this field are born have been known to us for a long time - they are β-transformations of nuclei and weak decays of electromagnetic particles.

In this sense, reactions (1) and (2), reactions of transformation of a proton and a neutron into each other, can be interpreted as processes of the creation of weak interaction field quanta ($e^{-}$ and $e^{+}$). A detailed study, however, showed that the situation here turns out to be significantly more complicated. The field of weak forces is actually represented by two types of quanta: electrons (positrons) and particles with absolutely amazing properties, namely neutrinos, so reactions (1) and (2) are written incorrectly. Along with an electron or positron, neutrinos must also be born.

The history of the discovery of neutrinos is one of the most brilliant pages of science of the 20th century and deserves it.

If the β-decay process is represented according to scheme (1) or (2), then the presence of a continuous spectrum of electrons is completely incomprehensible. In fact, the β-transformation process is accompanied by the release of a completely definite energy, which is distributed between the two resulting particles (an electron and a recoil nucleus - the nucleus of a new element) in the only possible way, so the energy of the electron must be completely definite. This follows directly from the laws of conservation of energy and momentum. Since the initial nucleus accumulates, its momentum (momentum p) is equal to 0. The total momentum of the resulting particles should also be equal to zero, that is, the electron and the recoil nucleus should fly apart in opposite directions. Let us denote the mass of the electron and its speed by $m_e$ and $v_e$, and the mass of the recoil nucleus and its speed by $M$ and $u$ and write the law of conservation of momentum:

$$m_e v_e - Mu = 0.$$

(3)

On the other hand, the sum of the kinetic energies formed by the particles must be equal to $E_0$ - the total energy released during β-decay:

$$E_e + E_M = E_0.$$

(4)

For simplicity of calculations, we can assume that we relate the energy and momentum of a particle to the classical formula $E = \frac{p^2}{2m}$.

From equations (3) and (4) it follows that the speed $v_e$, and therefore the energy of the electron $E_e$, are uniquely determined ($E_e = \frac{E_0}{1+m_e/M}$). Therefore, the β spectrum should be one single line. By the way, it is easy to verify that since $M \gg m_e$, then $E_e \approx E_0$, that is, the recoil nucleus carries away a very small fraction of the total decay energy.

What is the value of this expected electron energy? For example, consider the β-decay of a free neutron. So that the changes show that the mass of the neutron is slightly greater than the mass of the proton, namely:

$$m_n - m_p = 2,5 m_e.$$

According to one of the functional provisions of the theory of relative energy, it is associated with the correspondence $E = mc^2$, where $c$ is the speed of light. Then the energy that should be released during the transformation of a neutron into a proton and electron is equal to

$$E_0 = (m_n - m_p - m_e)c^2 = 1,5m_e c^2 = 780 \text{ кэв.}$$

This means that practically always the electron does not carry away all the decay energy (up to the energy of the recoil nucleus), but only part of it, and the magnitude of this part takes on different values ​​from time to time.

The first theory of this process was proposed in the early 30s by E. Fermi.

Modern theory has combined the phenomena of $\beta$-decay of nuclei with similar transformations of other particles into a general class of phenomena of the universal weak interaction. One of the most remarkable statements of the theory was the prediction of the possibility of processes inverse to processes (5) and (6):

$$\overset{\sim}{\nu} + n \rightarrow p + e^-, \tag{9}$$
$$\overset{\sim}{\nu} + p \rightarrow n + e^+. \tag{10}$$

The theory also predicted the intensity of such processes. It turned out that before a neutrino (with an energy of 1 MeV) is captured, this particle can (on average) pass through a layer of matter (for example, water) about $10^{15}$ km thick!!! A neutrino can freely pass through the entire Earth so that only one of the $5 \cdot 10^{12}$ particles is absorbed by the Earth, that is, it causes “reverse” $\beta$ decay. That is why no calorimeters in early experiments on $\beta$ decay could detect this “elusive” particle.

The study of the properties of this particle continues to this day. For example, it was found that the neutrinos emitted during the decay of a neutron (process 5) and the neutrinos emitted when a proton transforms into a neutron (process 6) are actually different particles. Both types of neutrinos, as it turned out, have their own rotational moment - spin directed along the axis of rotation. However, for neutrinos from decay (5) the spin direction corresponds to right-handed rotation, and for neutrinos from reaction (6) it corresponds to left-handed rotation. Accordingly, the first type of neutrino is called an “antineutrino” and is designated $\overset{\sim}{\nu}$.

Further, studies of meson decay carried out at large accelerators in Brookhaven (USA) and Cern (Switzerland) showed that there is another pair of neutrinos - antineutrinos, which is somehow significantly different from the neutrinos involved in decays of types (5) and (6). In any case, under the influence of those neutrinos (called muons), the reverse $\beta$-Reines-Cowan process (process 10) does not occur. But similar processes of neutrino capture by a neutron or proton occur, and instead of an electron or positron, $\mu$-mesons of the corresponding sign are born:

$$n + \nu_\mu \rightarrow p + \mu^-, \tag{11}$$
$$\overset{\sim}{\nu}_\mu + p \rightarrow n + \mu^+. \tag{12}$$
