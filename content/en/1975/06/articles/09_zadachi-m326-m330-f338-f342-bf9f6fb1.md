M330. There are two convex polygons $M_1$ and $M_2$ on the plane. Let us denote by $M$ the set of points where the midpoint of a segment can fall, one end of which belongs to $M_0$, the other to $M_1$. Prove that $M$ is a convex polygon.

a) How many sides can $M$ have if $M_0$ has $n_0$ and $M_1$ has $n_1$?
b) What can be the perimeter $M$ if perimeter $M_0$ is equal to $P_0$ and $M_1$ is equal to $P_1$?
b)* What can be the area of ​​$M$ if the area of ​​$M_0$ is $S_0$ and the area of ​​$M_1$ is $S_1$?

N. B. Vasiliev

F338. Two rods from different metals with linear expansion coefficients $a_1$ and $a_2$ at a temperature $0^\circ C$ have slightly different lengths $l_1$ and $l_2$ and cross sections $s_1$ and $s_2$. At what temperature will the rods be the same a) length? b) cross sections? b) volumes?

B. B. Bukhovtsev

F339. The tennis ball hits a heavy racket and rebounds elastically from it. The mass of the ball is much less than the mass of the racket, and the speed of the ball before colliding with the racket is equal to $v$ and makes an angle $\alpha = 60^\circ$ with the perpendicular to the racket. At what constant speed must the racket move in order for the ball to be reflected from it at right angles to the direction of movement? Both before and after collision with the racket, the ball does not rotate.

F340. An Earth satellite with a mass of $m = 10$ kHz, moving in a circular orbit in high layers of the atmosphere, experiences resistance from rarefied air. Resistance force $F = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$ n. Determine how much the satellite's speed will change during one revolution around the Earth. The satellite's flight altitude above the Earth's surface is considered small compared to the radius of the Earth.

S. M. Kozel
