The error in measuring the AgI layer thickness using the Fizeau method, as shown above, is equal to $\frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{\lambda_0}{4n}$. The thickness of the Ag layer is 4 times less than the thickness of the AgI layer, so the error in determining the thickness of the silver layer is $\frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{\lambda_0}{4n} = \frac{\lambda_0}{34,4}$.

To measure the thickness of the mirror coating wholesale, the easiest way is to use a ball from a set of Christmas tree decorations. Such a ball must be carefully broken into pieces with dimensions of about $1 \times 1$ cm. You can also use an ordinary small mirror. The silver layer in mirrors is usually coated with paint, but it can be easily removed with a cotton swab dipped in turpentine or acetone. Experimental tests can also be carried out with mica plates coated with a thin layer of silver. Such plates are found in complex capacitors with a value of 100–1000 $n\phi$.

Grains under can be found in the school chemistry classroom. For wholesale, grains the size of a poppy seed or somewhat larger are suitable. If the grains are not enough, then you can use a real hearth. The match is moistened in a real hearth, and after the solution is absorbed, a piece 1-1.5 mm long is cut off. It is placed on the silver layer being examined.

Divisibility problems

Below are a few problems that we recommend you try to solve using the method of mathematical induction. This method often allows us to solve many problems of this type “at ease” and saves us from writing more “tricky” solutions. True, these “tricky” solutions are often shorter, but they are also more difficult to come up with!

Let us immediately give one indication - sometimes apply induction of arrival many times.

1. Prove that $n^3 + 5n$ is divisible by three and $n^4 + 6n^3 + 11n^2 + 6n$ is divisible by four for any natural number $n$.

2. Prove that $4^n + 15n - 1$ is divisible by 9, $5^n - 4n - 1$ is divisible by 16, $7^n - 6n - 1$ is divisible by 36, $8^n - 7n - 1$ is divisible by 49, $(m + 1)^n - m \cdot n - 1$ is divisible by $m^2$ for any natural numbers $m$ and $n$.

3. Prove that $3^{n+1} + 2^{n+1}$ is divisible by 7, $5^{2n} - 3^n$ is divisible by 16, $5^{2n+1} + 3^{n+2}$ is divisible by 19 for any natural number $n$.

4. Prove that $n^2 - n$ is divisible by 2, $n^3 - n$ is divisible by 3, $n^5 - n$ is divisible by 5, $n^7 - n$ is divisible by 7, ... $n^{p-1}$ where $p$ is a prime number and is divisible by $p$ for any natural number $n$.
