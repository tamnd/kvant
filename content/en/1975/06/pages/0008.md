If it could be divided with a circle and a ruler into three equal parts at $10^\circ$, then we could, based on the configuration $\{A, B\}$, construct an angle at $10^\circ$, and therefore a segment of length $s = \sin 10^\circ$ (see Fig. 4, where $\Rightarrow C = 10^\circ$, $CD = 1$, $DE \perp CE$, and therefore $DE = \sin 10^\circ$).

Let us prove that this cannot be done, that is, the number $s = \sin 10^\circ$ cannot be obtained using a finite number of basic actions from the number 1.

Using the formula $\sin 3\alpha = 3\sin \alpha - 4\sin^3 \alpha$, we obtain the equation $\frac{1}{2} = \sin 30^\circ = 3s - 4s^3$, or $s^3 - \frac{3}{4}s + \frac{1}{8} = 0$. (1)

By Theorem 2, it is enough for us to prove that (1) has no rational roots. You can verify this directly, but it is easier to use the following general technique.

Lemma. Let the equation $x^n + a_1x^{n-1} + a_2x^{n-2} + \dots + a_{n-1}x + a_n = 0$ (2) with the target coefficient $a_1, \dots, a_n$ have a rational root $x_0$. Then $x_0$ is a required number; number $u$ $|x_0|$ is a divisor of $|a_n|$.

Proof. Let's write $x_0$ as an irreducible fraction $p/q$ ($p$ is an integer, $q$ is a natural number, in part, if $x_0$ is an integer, then $q = 1$). Let's substitute $x_0 = p/q$ into (2), multiply all terms by $q^{n-1}$ and intersect the first term on the right side. We get $a_1p^{n-1} + a_2p^{n-2}q + \dots + a_{n-1}pq^{n-2} + a_nq^{n-1} = -p^n/q$.

There is an integer on the left, which means $p^n/q$ is also an integer, and since $p$ and $q$ are coprime, then $q = 1$ and $x_0$ are an integer. Rewriting (2) for $x = x_0$ in the form $a_n = x_0(-a_{n-1} - a_{n-2}x_0 - \dots - a_1x_0^{n-2} - x_0^{n-1})$, we find that $|a_n|$ is divisible by $|x_0|$. The lemma is proven.

To use the lemma, we make the change $x = 2s$ in (1). We obtain the equation $x^3 - 3x + 1 = 0$. (3)

Since 1 and -1 are not its roots, then (by the lemma) it has no rational roots, and therefore equation (1) has no rational roots. By Theorem 2, the number s cannot be expressed in terms of 1 using a finite number of basic actions, and therefore the problem of trisection of an angle in $30^\circ$ is unsolvable.

8. Proof of Theorem 1

We will say that the number $x$ can be constructed based on a given configuration if, based on it, it is possible to construct points $C$ and $D$ such that $CD = |x|$ (that is, $CD = x$ if $x \ge 0$, and $CD = -x$ if $x < 0$).

Exercise 8. Prove that a) if the numbers $z$ and $t$ can be constructed, then the numbers $z + t$, $z - t$, $zt$, $z/t$ (for $t \neq 0$) and $\sqrt{z}$ (for $z \ge 0$) can also be constructed, b) the point $(x, y)$ can be constructed if and only if it is possible to construct its coordinates - the numbers $x$ and $y$, c) based on the configuration with parameters $p_1, \dots, p_n$, it is possible to construct the numbers $p_1, \dots, p_n$.

From Exercises 8c and 8a it follows that, based on this configuration, you can construct any number that is expressed through its parameters $p_1, \dots, p_n$.
