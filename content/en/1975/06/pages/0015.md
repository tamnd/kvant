There is no need to talk about it briefly. The existence of neutrinos was theoretically predicted by W. Pauli as a result of attempts to comprehend a strange fact discovered in the beta decay of nuclei. Measurements of the energy of electrons emitted during β-decay showed that electrons do not have a fixed energy that would be characteristic of this β-transformation. There is a wide range, or, in other words, a spectrum of energies, extending from zero to a certain maximum value.

The energy of electrons is measured using special devices - beta spectrometers, in which electrons of different energies differ either in trajectories in magnetic or electric fields, or in the amplitude of electrical pulses arising in spectrometric-type detectors (for example, in scinitlation counters). figure 1 shows a diagram of a simple magnetic spectrometer with a continuous field. Electrons from the source And fall into the magnetic field formed by the solenoid C. From figure 2 and 3 it is clear that the β spectrum is continuous, electrons with any energies from 0 to $E_{\text{max}}$ are present.
