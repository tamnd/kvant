⟦folio 31⟧

Applied mathematics and mathematicians

L. E. Sadovsky

We turned out to be contemporaries of the powerful progress of mathematical knowledge, the formation of new branches of mathematics, new areas of its applications and such scientific directions as “mathematical economics”, “mathematical linguistics”, “mathematical psychology”, “mathematical methods in biology”.
The invasion of mathematics has introduced millions of areas of intellectual and practical human activity, the creation of electronic digital computers (EDC) over the past decades are essential features of the worldwide production of the process called the scientific and technological revolution.
This process required a restructuring of mathematical education at all levels: in schools, technical colleges. Willingly or unwittingly, we are all participants in this perestroika.
Primary and secondary school students are already learning mathematics using new textbooks, teaching new concepts and programs. In 1975-1976, 3,000 students will graduate from the country's experimental schools and classes, and in 1977, all graduates of our schools will be trained according to new programs.
Teachers. schools are already teaching in a new way, restructuring teaching methods. Mathematics departments of higher education institutions and universities, in turn, are forced to take into account real situations and also change a lot in long-established procedures. Scientists and engineers, in order to keep up, are forced to master new branches of applied mathematics and widely use digital computers.
Thus, a huge part of the actively working society is directly or indirectly involved in the process of mathematization of sciences.
In this case, many large and small problems will arise. Covering them, even partially, in a small country is another problem (and an insoluble one at that!).
Therefore, keeping in mind the natural interests of graduates of secondary and special physics and mathematics schools and boarding schools, it remained in a new language, generated by the growing need of modern society for “mathematized personnel”. Here, firstly, I mean the significant expansion of mathematical courses in traditional and new faculties of technical universities and, secondly, the departments (streams) of applied mathematics created in recent years in a number of the largest universities in the country.
By the end of this century, current graduates will become an active scientific and engineering force. In conditions of rapid development of technical, technical production and professional skills, an engineer is obliged to prepare for meaningful new phenomena, for changes in professional orientation, for search work at the intersection of various fields.
