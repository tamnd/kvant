Here $k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ is the number of the dark ring. The first is the outermost dark ring.

From relations (1) and (2) it follows that where a dark ring with number $k$ is observed, the thickness of the AgI layer can be calculated using the formula

$$d = (2k-1)\,\frac{\lambda}{4}. \qquad (3)$$

If the last dark ring with number $k_{\max}$ coincides with the edge of the central transparent spot, then the maximum thickness of layer $d_{\max}$ is equal to

$$d_{\max} = (2k_{\max}-1)\,\frac{\lambda}{4}. \qquad (4)$$

In reality, the last dark ring usually does not coincide with the edge of the transparent spot, but is located at some distance from it (as, for example, at Fig. $a$). Therefore, in fact, $d_{\max}$ is slightly larger than the value calculated using formula (4). Let's try to evaluate this inaccuracy. The difference in the thickness of the “lens” in the places corresponding to two adjacent dark rings is equal to half the wavelength. Consequently, the error in determining $d_{\max}$ does not exceed $\lambda/4$. Then, we can assume that $d_{\max}$ is equal to approximately

$$d_{\max} = (2k_{\max} - 1)\,\frac{\lambda}{4} + \frac{\lambda}{4} =$$

$$= k_{\max}\,\frac{\lambda}{2}. \qquad (5)$$

The wavelength of light in a substance $\lambda$ is related to the wavelength of light in a vacuum $\lambda_0$ by the relation $\lambda = \dfrac{\lambda_0}{n}$, where $n$ is the refractive index of the substance. Taking this into account, we write

$$d_{\max} = k_{\max}\,\frac{\lambda_0}{2n}. \qquad (6)$$

$$h = \frac{d_{\max}}{4} = k_{\max}\,\frac{\lambda_0}{8n}. \qquad (7)$$

For silver iodide $n = 2{,}15$. Substituting this value into (7), we obtain a formula for determining the thickness of the silver layer:

$$h = k_{\max}\,\frac{\lambda_0}{17{,}2}.$$

Let the picture depicted at figure $a$ be observed through a red filter (red light wavelength $6{,}2 \cdot 10^{-5}$ cm); Then

$$h = \frac{3 \cdot 6{,}2 \cdot 10^{-5}}{17{,}2} \approx 10^{-5}\ см.$$

If the observation is carried out in white light, then $k_{\max}$ is understood as the number of colored rings, and $\lambda$ is taken equal to $5{,}6 \cdot 10^{-5}$ cm (the wavelength of green light to which our eye is most sensitive).
