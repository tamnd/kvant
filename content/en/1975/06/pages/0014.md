⟦folio 12⟧

This strange property, manifested in the β-decay of nuclei, has become an integral characteristic of practically all known elementary particles. Currently, this area of ​​physics—the physics of weak interactions—is rapidly developing. Since the 30s, when E. Fermi created the first theory of β-decay of nuclei, a lot has already been done to ensure that all known facts receive a theoretical justification. True, the existing ideas are very far from the harmony and completeness that distinguish, for example, the current picture of electromagnetic interaction.

According to modern views, any interaction can be described using a certain field, and the emergence of forces of attraction or repulsion between particles is due to their ability to “give birth” and absorb quanta of this field. The theory establishes a deep connection between the mechanism of interaction of forces acting between particles - the sources of a given field, and the resulting “transformations” of these particles, which are accompanied by the birth of quanta of this field.

For example, the interaction between electric charges is caused by an electromagnetic field, the quanta of which are photons. The same interaction leads to the birth of a quantum (photon), say, when a rapidly flying charge (electron) is decelerated. The “transformation” reaction here is:

$$e \rightarrow e' + \gamma,$$

where $e'$ is the electron with the changed energy.

A similar situation occurs in the case of weak interaction. The very forces of interaction between particles, for example nucleons, are columnar against the background of nuclear and electromagnetic forces, so that their manifestations could only be detected indirectly, and then in very subtle experiments. But the processes of transformations during which quanta of this field are born have been known to us for a long time - they are β-transformations of nuclei and weak decays of electromagnetic particles.

In this sense, reactions (1) and (2), reactions of transformation of a proton and a neutron into each other, can be interpreted as processes of the creation of weak interaction field quanta ($e^{-}$ and $e^{+}$). A detailed study, however, showed that the situation here turns out to be significantly more complicated. The field of weak forces is actually represented by two types of quanta: electrons (positrons) and particles with absolutely amazing properties, namely neutrinos, so reactions (1) and (2) are written incorrectly. Along with an electron or positron, neutrinos must also be born.

The history of the discovery of neutrinos is one of the most brilliant pages of science of the 20th century and deserves it.
