⟦folio 39⟧

Entrant-1975

A. Ya. Yakovlev

After graduating from high school in Kursavka (Stavropol Territory), Lyuda F. decided to enter the mathematics department of the Stavropol Pedagogical Institute. Moreover, her mathematics teacher gave her a B in algebra and an A in geometry on her certificate.

During the written exam, Lyuda was given 4 problems (we will give the answers to them at the end of the magazine).

1. The name of the surface of a regular triangular paramid, the side of the base of the side is equal to a, the angle between the lateral borders is equal to β.

2. Solve inequality
$$\log_2^2 x - \log_2 x - 6 \leq 0.$$

3. Solve the equation
$$\sin x + \sin 2x + \sin 3x = \cos x + \cos 2x + \cos 3x.$$

4. At what values ​​of a are both roots of the equation
$$x^2 - (a+1)x + (a+4) = 0$$
negative?

Alas! While solving the first problem, Lyuda could not remember what the angle between the lateral boundaries was. Then I designated by $\frac{\beta}{2}$ the angle between the lateral edge and the height of the paramid. Of course, the answer was wrong.

Solving the inequality, Luda found the following roots of the left side: $x_1 = \frac{1}{8}, x_2 = 4$. “But $x = \frac{1}{8}$ is not suitable,” writes Luda, “since the graph at this point is positive. This means there is only one root: $x = 4$.》 Then Lyuda formulates the answer: “For $x = 4$ the graph is negative.”

Is it possible to specifically come up with such an incomplete one?

When solving a trigometric equation, Luda writes: “sin 2x = cos 2x - there are no roots, since sine and cosine cannot simultaneously equal the same number.” Obviously, Luda stated that sin 45° = cos 45°, that is, that the sine and cosine of an angle of 45° are equal. And this happened many times in the eighth grade, and in the ninth, and in the tenth.

Finally, solving the fourth problem, Lyuda writes: “The roots are negative if the discriminant is less than zero,” and begins to transform the discriminant.

Let's leave Lyuda's grades in the certificate to the teachers' own. There are quite a few errors in written work and other applicants. Celebrate those who received fours and fives in mathematics on their certificates.

Of the 225 who entered the mathematics department of the Stavropol Pedagogical Institute, only ... four completely coped with the work. If they failed (they got D's) - sixty, although the grades became liberal for everything.

At the nearby Stavropol Agricultural Institute, almost 50 percent of applicants failed. Including exactly half of the residents of the regional center, including six out of seven graduates of school No. 6, four out of four graduates of school No. 17.

The results of written exams in mathematics in Moscow universities are slightly better. Every fourth applicant received an unsatisfactory grade at the Moscow Institute of Chemical Engineering, almost every third in the Automotive Institute, and up to forty two percent in the Standard Instrumental.
