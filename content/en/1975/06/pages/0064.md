**To the article “Exit into space”**

(see «Kvant», 1975, no. 5)

1) Let us introduce Cartesian coordinates $(x, y, z)$ in space so that the axes $x$ and $y$ are located in our plane. The position of the pedestrian at the point with coordinates $(x, y)$ at the moment of time $t$ corresponds in space to the point $(x, y, t)$. The path of movement of each pedestrian in space will be a straight line. The fact that two pedestrian codes meet means that the straight lines corresponding to them in space intersect.

2) Let's take points corresponding to circles, as was done when solving Apollonius' problem (see article). Then, for example, the three points corresponding to the intersection of pairs of common external tangents belong to our plane and the plane passing through the three corresponding points located on one side of our plane.

3) Let us consider a triangular prism, the edges of which are projected into given lines and three points on its various faces are projected into given points (two straight lines can be left in place). The required triangle is a projection of a triangle obtained by the intersection of straight planes passing through three selected points on its faces.

4) Consider a plane that intersects the given plane along the straight line $ABC$ and does not coincide with the given plane. In this new plane we take a fixed circle $O$ passing through $AB$. Let us take an arbitrary sphere to which the circle $O$ belongs. The point of contact of all possible tangents to the sphere passing through $C$ lie in the same plane. This means that if $M_1$ and $N_1$ are the points of tangency of these tangents with the circle $O$, then $M, N, M_1$ and $N_1$ lie in the same plane. Moreover, $M_1$ and $N_1$ are fixed, and the lines $(AB)$ and $(M_1N_1)$ intersect.

5) Let's take a sphere whose center is located at the center of a given circle and whose radius is equal to its radius. Let us draw in this sphere through $A$ a chord $KL$ perpedicular to the plane of the given circle. Then $M$ belongs to the plane tangent to the given sphere at points $K$ and $L$.

6) Let's take an arbitrary plane, then a hyperplane intersecting it along a straight line, and in this hyperplane another plane intersecting this straight line.
