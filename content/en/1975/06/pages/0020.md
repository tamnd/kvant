How can I change the thickness of the mirror layer?

N. M. Rostmovtsev

When making mirrors, a thin layer of metal is applied to the polished surface of the glass, which reflects light well. Mirrors made of silver chemically deposited on glass are especially widespread.

But silver, as you know, is an expensive metal. How to control the consumption of silver, that is, change the thickness of its layer on a mirror surface? You will be able to answer the question posed and make changes in the thickness of the mirror layer if you get acquainted with the method proposed by Fizu in 1861. This method is based on the chemical interaction of odor with silver and is still used in practice.

To change the thickness of the mirror layer of silver, a grain of light is placed on it (beam 1 at Fig. 6), then it is partially reflected from the outer surface of the AgI layer (point A, ray 2), partially passes inside the layer, reflected from its second surface, that is, from the boundaries with silver (point B), and comes out (point C, ray 3). The resulting reflected waves (beams 2 and 3) are coherent, so they will interfere. The result of interference depends on the geometric path difference δ of rays 2 and 3, which can be calculated using the formula

$$\delta = AB + BC.$$

If the angle of incidence of light is zero or close to it, then $AB = BC$, so

$$\delta = 2AB = 2d,$$

(1)

where $d$ is the thickness of the “line” at the point of incidence of the light beam. From the edge to the center of the layer, its thickness $d$ gradually increases, and the path difference $\delta$ also increases. When illuminated with monochromatic light (for example, red), illumination maxima (red rings) are observed where the difference in the path of the rays is equal to an integer wavelength of light in the underlying silver.
