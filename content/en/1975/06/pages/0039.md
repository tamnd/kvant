⟦folio 37⟧

In reality, there is nothing unusual in constructing a model: every engineering, economic and other calculation performed using the means and language of mathematics turns out to be, in essence, mathematical modeling.

The concepts of numbers, functions, and equations that are well known to you serve, under certain conditions, as mathematical models of certain real objects. There are economic and mathematical models of the use of mathematical resources in various sectors of the economy, models of production and consumption, and others.

The problems associated with choosing a model, its study, optimization of some criteria for its quality, inevitable simplifications, assumptions, necessary calculations, and much more, are very diverse, difficult and cannot be considered here. Everything that has been said is only illustrative: here, for example, is what applied mathematics does. There is currently no consensus on the question of whether applied mathematics is an independent discipline. However, this circumstance is not existent for us. Especially in the absence of an exhaustive definition of all mathematics as such.

Apparently, there are no indisputable features by which mathematical constructions can be classified into theoretical (“numerical”) and applied. Everything is relative. For example, in the time of K. Gauss, complex numbers were considered by most mathematicians as highly abstract objects. But years passed, the theory of functions of a complex variable arose, its apparatus found applications in hydrodynamics and aerodynamics (in calculating the lift of an aircraft wing), in theoretical electrical engineering and other fields. Abstract group theory, which originated from the works of Lagrange (1736-1813), has found educational application in specific problems of crystallography, theoretical physics, quantum mechanics, coding theory (in the mathematical science of transmitting messages along communication lines), and problems of solvability of algebraic equations in radicals. The existence of some particle elements was anticipated by the group's theory long before their actual discovery *).

In terms of group theory, F. Klein classified various geometrines. The concept of a group, along with the concepts of set, function, limit, has become one of the main ones in mathematics. The history of mathematics is very rich in similar examples of the development of the pure into the applied and reverse processes.

In the already mentioned book, R. Courant wrote: “In fact, between “numerical” and “applied” mathematics it is impossible to check the clarity of the face. That is why in mathematics there should not be a division between the high priests, who worship the infallible mathematical beauty and pay attention only to their own inclinations, and the workers who serve them. Such “casteism” is, at best, a symbol of human limitations, which keeps most people from freely wandering through the vast simple human interests.”

F. Klein wrote about this in his book “Elementary Mathematics from a Higher Point of View” (Gostekhidat, 1934): “Purely logical concepts should constitute, so to speak, the solid skeleton of the organism of mathematics, giving it stability and reliability. But the life of mathematics itself, the most important guidance and its productivity are predominantly concerned.”

*) For example, the existence of the positron and meson was postulated in the works of P. Dirac and H. Yukawa. The properties of these parts were studied using mathematical methods before their experimental confirmation.
