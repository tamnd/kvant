The chronometer has three hands - hour, minute and second, and the second hand jumps sharply every half second with a clear blow that can be heard at a distance of several meters. Chronometers are very convenient because they can be carried without disturbing their movement. To do this, they are placed in a box on a gimbal suspension (a suspension with two mutually perpendicular axes), which allows them to maintain a horizontal position at all times. Soon, the method of transporting chronometers was widely used on land - when traveling, to determine the longitudes of populated areas when drawing up geographic maps, and to determine the longitudes of the main observatories in different countries. For example, after the founding of the Pulkovo Observatory in 1839, its first director V. Ya. Struve organized a special expedition in 1843, equipped with 60 chronometers to determine the difference in longitude between Pulkovo and Greenwich.

The problem of determining longitude received a completely new solution with the invention of the telegraph in the middle of the last century, which made it possible to transmit time signals of the prime meridian and determine the difference in local times of two points with previously unattainable accuracy - up to a fraction of a second. True, for this there must be a telegraph connection between the points, which obviously precluded the use of this method for ships on the high seas. Nevertheless, the laying of submarine cables made it possible to connect different continents with each other. Thus, for example, the longitude of the observatory in Washington was determined very accurately. In Russia, the main time (by which, for example, the train schedule was compiled) was St. Petersburg, or rather, the time of the Pulkovo meridian.
