The theme “Pushkin and Humboldt**” attracts attention and has also been developed.

However, the breadth of Pushkin’s interests and his understanding of the role of science in the life of society were most clearly manifested in his journalistic activities and were associated with the publication of the Sovremennik magazine - an enterprise on which he placed so much hope and into which he contributed so much effort in the last one and a half to two years of his life.

For Russian journalism 20-30 years. Over the past century, the publication of popular science articles has, one might say, a traditional character. Pushkin’s “Contemporary” was given a new look in this regard too. His publisher did not want to simply reprint popular science reviews from foreign magazines - he involved his correspondent, P. B. Kozlesky, a professional diplomat and a deeply educated person, in writing them. Kozlovsky’s articles were published in two of the four lifetime issues of the Russian Sovremennik. The first of them is “Analysis of the Paris Yearbook”, in modern terms, decisions for this publication, published under the supervision of the Secretary of the French Academy of Sciences, Francois Arago. In his detailed solution, Kozlovsky not only talks about the latest successes of French science, but also ardently advocates for the spread of education in his homeland, in Russia. The task is both noble and courageous, if we remember that in the eyes of the autocracy, enlightenment was synonymous with freedom. Kozlovsky’s next article, published in the 3rd volume of Sovremennik, was called “On Hope.” This was the first popular presentation of the foundations of probability theory in Russian literature. At that time we still did not have the last term, and Kozlovsky proposed calling this theory “convenience theories.” It is interesting to see on the pages of Sovremennik, next to the position of Pushkin, Zhukovsky, Tyuchev, algebraic formulas, Pascal’s triangle and even the graph that Kozlovsky completed his article.

**) Alexander Humboldt (1769-1859) - German naturalist and geographer. Traveled extensively in South and Central America. In the spring of 1829 he came to Russia. In St. Petersburg, he met with many scientists and writers, including P. A. Vyazemsky and A. S. Pushkin. In the same year, he made a half-day trip to Siberia. His scientific connections with Russian scientists continued throughout his long life.
