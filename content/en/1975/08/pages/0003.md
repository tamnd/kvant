Mixture (p. 48, 61)

The device that you see on the first page of the cover is called a pincompass (prin in English means rod, pin); This device, which was used by sailors back in the 18th century, is now kept in the Altona Museum in Gambur. It is impossible to determine coordinates with its help, but it is possible to “record” the ship’s course. On each roumier (1 roumier=360°:32) compass roses are made in response. All answers form concentric circles together. So the “dial” of the pincompass is, as it were, covered with a coordinate network. Every half hour the watchman leaves a pin in response corresponding to the definition of the direction. In this direction, the ship will maintain a course of approximately half a hour. But in order to determine the desired direction, it is necessary to know the coordinates of the ship at a given moment. Read about how these coordinates will be determined in the article by Academician A. A. Mikhailov (p. 11).
