Pravanice 6. Prove that if the area of ​​a figure is less than $n+1$, then it can be translated so that it defines no more than $n$ grid nodes ($n$ is an integer).

Using Blichfeld's theorem, we obtain the result we need.

Theorem 3. If a system of congestive squares with mutually parallel sides covers a figure of area $S$, then from it we can select a subsystem of disjoint squares (without common internal points) with each other, the sum of areas of which is not less than $S/4$.

What kind of figure the squares cover does not matter here, as well as in the Rado problem. Only its area is important.

Proof. We deliberately formulated the theorem somewhat differently than the previous ones (the area of ​​the “stoll” is now $S$, not 1) in order to be able to choose a different unit of length. We will assume that the side length of each “napkin” is equal to $1/2$. According to Blichfeld's theorem, we can place our figure (area $S$) on checkered paper (on the side of cell 1) so that it covers $n \geq S$ grid nodes. Let's leave one “napkin” covering each of these $n$ nodes. It is clear that the selected “napkins” cannot overlap each other, and their total area is equal to

$$\frac{1}{4}n \geq \frac{S}{4}.$$

Sokolin's theorem has been proven.
