To get out of this situation, you need to replenish the set of rational numbers by adding irrational numbers to it.

In the same way, the sequence of functions obtained by approximate solution of an integral equation does not always converge to some “good” function. To be sure of the existence of a solution, it is necessary to expand the set of functions by adding discontinuous functions to it. But if you add random discontinuous functions to the set of continuous functions, then you will not be able to build a good theory. It turned out that we need to add functions whose square we can integrate according to Lebesgue. Then we get a space very similar in its properties to our Euclidean space, only infinite-dimensional. This space is called Hilbert space.

The triumph of the ideas of Lebesgue, Baer and Borel led to the fact that even Darboux changed his mind about the new mathematics, and, speaking in 1908 at the Mathematical Congress in Rome, he spoke about the fiery and inquisitive spirit of mathematics of the 20th century, about a science conducting its research in a completely new field with unknown prospects. He emphasized that 20th-century science is not afraid to attack the foundations of constructs that for so long seemed unshakable.

Later, the ideas that led to the creation of the Lebesgue measure and integral allowed A. N. Kolmogorov to construct the axiomatics of probability theory and N. Wiener to define the concept of measure for function spaces. Wherever ideas of measure penetrated, constructions and concepts dating back to Lebesgue were used. They turned out to be very useful in statistical physics, allowing A. Ya. Khinchin and J. von Neumann to substantiate some of the ideas of the American physicist Gibbs.

Remarkable works in the field of the theory of action functions

⟦column⟧

The development of a real variable (as the field of mathematics that studied the general theory of functions, measures and integrals came to be called) was made by Moscow mathematicians D. F. Egorov and N. N. Luzin, as well as their numerous students.

During these years, Lebesgue himself was interested in the set-theoretic topology that was emerging at that time, anticipating in one of his works the later research of P. S. Uryson on the theory of dimension of topological spaces, potential theory and other questions of mathematics. And although he no longer created anything equal to the achievements of his mathematical youth, they turned out to be quite enough to immortalize his name.

Lebesgue devoted the last twenty years of his life to problems of history and teaching mathematics. Naturally, he paid most attention to the theory of measuring quantities in high school. In a series of articles, later collected in the book “On the Measurement of Quantities” (it was also translated into Russian), he wittily criticized common misconceptions in the teaching of these issues. In his book, he showed the inconsistency of common definitions of length, area and volume, and criticized textbooks for striving for rigor in some places while making gross logical mistakes in others.

75 years have passed since Lebesgue created his theory of measure and integration. These concepts have forever entered mathematics - without them it is impossible to think of probability theory, functional analysis, statistical physics, or topological algebra.
