About the Rado problem

In «Kvant» No. 5 for 1975, in the section “Problem «Kvant»” problem M321 was published [author A.V. Brailov]. Let us recall its conditions.

There is a rectangular table of area 1. Prove that for any ε > 0 it is possible to indicate a system of rectangular napkins covering this table (the edges of the napkins are parallel to the edges of the table), such that any of its subsystems consisting of non-overlapping napkins has an area less than ε.

This note talks about various variants of the problem of “non-overlapping napkin systems”. Some of them have a whole history, others are quite simple and can be solved with the help of one idea, like ordinary “oplypad” problems.

Apparently, a hypothetical (and related only to mathematics, and not to biology) example of living creatures for which the external world is a two-dimensional plane, and not three-dimensional space, takes the famous German naturalist and mathematician G. Helmholtz: these are water striders - “water spiders”, the sliding movement of which along the surface of the water can be seen in any stagnant body of water. Helmholtz’s “water striders” are just as unable to imagine the “three-dimensional” world that has been instilled in us, just as a person cannot imagine “four-dimensional space” *).

*) An imaginary “living creature” that has the shape of a flat geometric figure and inhabits a two-dimensional world is the subject of the book “Fletland” by English school teacher E. E. Abbott, published back in the 80s and, unfortunately, never translated into Russian. Compiled by C. G. Hinton, “The Episode in Flatland” (1907), which continues the same theme, is briefly summarized in «Kvant», 1970, No. 4, p. 28-31.

We will start with an external world that is even more primitive than that of water striders: with a one-dimensional analogue of problem M321. Further, in such a “light” version, it does not become meaningless.

Now our “tables” and “napkins” are not rectangular, but straight segments. We are interested in such a Statement. Let a segment o of length 1 (“table”) and a number ε > 0 be given. Then we can specify a system of segments (“napkins”) covering the “table” o such that any subsystem of disjoint segments (non-overlapping “napkins”) has a total length less than ε*).

Is this Statement true or false?
