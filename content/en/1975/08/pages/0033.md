Let us give an example to which Puskin’s remark is no less suitable.
In the famous poem “Portrait”, dedicated to the beautiful Zakrevskaya, he wrote:

With your flaming soul,
With your stormy passions
O wives of the North, between you
She appears sometimes
And past all the conditions of the world
Strives until he loses strength,
Like a lawless comet
In the calculated circle of luminaries.

It is obvious, however, that the movement of a comet is as well calculated (regular), as is the movement of the heavenly bodies: there is no greater “legislator” than nature. If it sometimes seems to us that the behavior of its objects is contrary to the laws, it simply means that we do not yet know other more complex laws that they obey!

Let us finally point out Pushkin’s well-known epigram, in which he reproduces a dispute between representatives of various philosophical schools of antiquity concerning the existence of the physical substantive concept of movement. This epigram, written in Mikhailovsky in the fall of 1825, is called “Movement”:

There is no movement, said the sage bradata.
The other fell silent and began to walk in front of him.
He could not have objected more strongly;
Everyone praised the answer to plan.
But, gentlemen, this is a funny incident
Another example comes to mind:
After all, every day the sun walks before us,
However, stubborn Galileo is right.

The “sage of bradata” is the ancient Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea (5th century BC), and his “other” opponent is Antisthenes (born around 440 BC). In eight lines of this epigram, the great pose with amazing capacity and expressiveness managed to convey the essence of the dispute; the final lines are intended to warn those who considered Antisthenes’s “answer to plan” exhaustive: Pushkin recalls the Galilean (Copernican) heliocentric system of the world, according to which the movement of the Sun is apparent, illustrating the relative nature of this movement.
