M336 — M340; F348—F352

M336. In the given plane there is a finite set of polygons, each two of which have a common point. Prove that there is a line that has a common point with each of these polygons. (10 grades)

S. V. Fomin

M337. Given an equilateral triangle ABC with side length 1. The first player chooses point X on side AB, the second player chooses point Y on side BC, then the first player chooses point Z on side AC.

a) The goal of the first player is to obtain a triangle XYZ with the largest area, the second player - with the largest area. What is the largest area the first one can provide?

b) The goal of the first player is to get the XYZ triangle with the largest perimeter, the second player - with the largest perimeter. What is the smallest perimeter that the first one can provide? (8-9 grades)

M. D. Bronstein

M338. Several zeros, ones and twos are written on the board. It is allowed to erase two unequal numbers and write one number together, different from the erased ones (2 together 0 and 1, 1 together 0 and 2, 0 together 1 and 2). Prove that if, as a result of several such operations, only one number remains on the board, then it does not depend on the order in which the erases were made. (8th and 10th grades)

S. V. Fomin

**M340**. Each cell of the rectangular table contains a real number. Some table cells are called its *saddle* cell if the number in it is not less than the other numbers in its line.

a) Let it be known about table $T$ that any table $2 \times 2$ that receives table $T$ at the intersection of two rows and two columns has a saddle cell. Prove that then table $T$ also has a saddle cell.

b) Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_m, b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n$ be arbitrary numbers, $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m, q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n$ be positive numbers. Prove that the table $m \times n$, at the intersection of the $i$th row and the $j$th column of which contains the number $\frac{a_i + b_j}{p_i + q_j}$, has a saddle cell.

**F348**. The spacecraft approaches the Moon along a parabolic trajectory, almost touching the surface of the Moon. To move to a creeping circular orbit (i.e., a circular orbit very close to the surface of the Moon), at the moment of the greatest approach to the Moon, the deceleration engine is turned on. Determine how much the ship's speed will change when performing this maneuver. Acceleration of gravity on the surface of the Moon $g_{\perp} \approx 1,7 \text{ m/c}^2$, radius of the Moon $R_{\perp} \approx 1,7 \cdot 10^6 \text{ m}$.

Additional question. Try to estimate what part of the first mass of the ship should be stored fuel if the engine ejects products with a relative speed $v = 4 \cdot 10^3 \text{ m/c}$. (10 grades)
