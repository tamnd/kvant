It was a New Year's surprise sent by a sea captain to his son - a box that opens only after a certain six-digit code number of the form *****0* is dialed using six disks with a full set of numbers on each. The fifth digit is known - zero, and all other digits are encrypted with asterisks.

In an accompanying letter to his son, the captain wrote: “... The simultaneous code number also indicates the size of the contents (in rubles) of the box. Take $\frac{1}{**}$ part of the money for books and vacation entertainment, and the rest for household expenses. When the contents of the box (number *****0*) are de-

poured onto the denominator of the indicated fraction (**), then the partial will receive a four-digit number, and the division process will have the following form:

$$\frac{****0}{**} \frac{**}{***}$$

If you show determination and wit, then, having deciphered the indicated division operation, you will also find the code number that opens the box...”

All the classmates of the sea captain’s son are included in the search for the “zest” put into the task by the cheerful captain. So far, alas, it’s flawless. Moreover, many have come to the conclusion that the recording of the action was incorrect. Some, however, believed that the “highlight” was in the deliberate

a typo that needs to be identified.
For example, is there an extra “tail” on the deuce? If you remove it, then a nine appears in place of the two, and then you can even find two solutions. However, of these - (1), which is the second?

Another version of the "slip" came from the proposal that the zero as the fifth digit is needed only for dialing the code number, in reality it maximizes the comma in writing the number. If the zero in the record of the dividend is replaced by a comma, and the remaining asterisks are brought closer together, then the result is the division of the decimal fraction by an integer, and the record of actions takes the form (2).

Supporters of this version found two options for deciphering the above recording. Which?

Unfortunately, none of the four solutions found by the edges opened the boxes.

The captain's message contained another piece of paper. The captain wrote to his son that their craftsman, who, by the way, helped make the box, was showing his comrades a funny “number trick”: he added the same numbers four times and each time he got a new result:

$$
\begin{array}{c c c c}
+ & 267 & + & 267 \\
\hline
267 & 534 & 267 & 545 \\
\hline
\end{array}
\begin{array}{c c c c}
+ & 267 & + & 267 \\
\hline
267 & 556 & 267 \\
\hline
\end{array}
$$

At the same time, it is understood that the sums were found evenly and accurately. The entire class still has not revealed the “secret” of this trick and the captain’s task.
