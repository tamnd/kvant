Fig. 18. Optoelectronics using laser ultrasonic pulses: a) optoelectronic switch; b) the principle of electro-optical gating of an electrical signal; c) optoelectronic oscilloscope

The basic idea is that one ultrasonic pulse triggers an electronic circuit, and with the help of a second ultrasonic pulse, delayed for a certain time interval, the electrical signal is measured at the desired point in the circuit. An essential part is the optoelectronic switch, with the help of which the circuit under study is launched. The basis is a semiconductor plate with high resistivity, on which conductive metal strips are applied - electrodes (Fig.18,a). The top strip has a cut in the form of a slit that breaks the electrical circuit. A voltage source can be connected to the ends of the electrodes. When a semiconductor is illuminated through a slit with laser radiation in the form of ultrashort pulses with a certain wavelength, a large number of current carriers (electron-hole pairs) appear in the surface layer of the semiconductor during the irradiation period, and the semiconductor becomes conductive. In this way, the electrical circuit is quickly closed, equal to the duration of the ultrasonic pulse. In the absence of light, spontaneous and fairly rapid destruction of carriers occurs (recombination of electrons and holes), and hence the restoration of high resistance in a time of less than 1 ps. It turns out that you can quickly turn the voltage on and off, i.e. generate an ultra-short electrical pulse that triggers the integrated circuit. Registration of electrical pulses is carried out by the method of electro-optical gating using an electro-optical probe (Fig.18,b). This

The probe is based on the effect of birefringence in an electro-optical crystal when an electric field is applied to it. If such a crystal is placed between crossed polarizers, then the transmission of light through them will depend on the strength of the electric field. When an electrical impulse passes through a circuit, a rapidly changing electric field appears around the conductor. It penetrates into a nearby crystal, and by transmitting light through crossed polarizers, the magnitude of the electric field can be determined. If this is light in the form of a USP, then the electric field is recorded only at the moment the USP passes through the crystal. By varying the amount of delay between the startup of the electronic circuit under test and the induced birefringence signal, the magnitude of the electrical signal can be measured as a function of the delay time. This produces an oscillogram of the electrical signal propagating in the integrated circuit. Based on this principle, a stroboscopic optoelectronic oscilloscope was created. Its circuit diagram is shown on figure 18th century. A very small electro-optical crystal was used, with a dielectric reflective coating applied to its bottom face. Since the launch of the circuit under study and the recording of the signal are carried out by the same laser ultrashort pulse, “jitter” of the signal relative to the launch is eliminated. This allows you to accurately record electrical signals.
