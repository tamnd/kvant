KVANT 2007/№4

Tasks

1.

Let us denote P(n) the product of the digits of a natural number n, and if the number n itself is a digit, then P(n) = n. For example: P(5) = 5, P(25) = 10. The following numbers are written sequentially:

1 + P(1), 2 + P(2), 3 + P(3), …

Can 2007 consecutive numbers in this sequence turn out to be consecutive natural numbers? S.Dvoryaninov

2.

On each sheet of a notebook of 96 sheets, Petya drew a cheerful face (from the front) either on one or the other side of the sheet, so that if you put the closed notebook on the table, some of the faces would “look” at him, and the rest would look away from him. Is it true that you can open the notebook (or not open it at all) in such a place that the same number of faces “looks” at Petya and from him? I. Akulich

The island is inhabited by aborigines, each of whom either always tells the truth or always lies. Once three aborigines met: Ah, Oh and Uh. One of them said: “Ah and Och are both liars,” the other said: “Ah and Uh are both liars” (but who exactly said what is unknown). How many liars are there among these three natives? E. Barabanov

Professor Mumbum-Plumboom dreams of choosing ten different natural numbers such that their greatest common divisor coincides with their arithmetic mean. Will he be able to do this? A. Zhukov
