The case of Tolya Klyukvin

S. DVORYANINOV

MY FRIEND TOLYA STUDYED LIKE EVERYONE else. NOT BAD. FINE.

Illustration by V. Ivanyuk

There were, of course, problems. They didn’t want to transfer him from elementary school to fifth grade. Or rather, everyone wanted to, but the math teacher didn’t want to. It was because of the multiplication table. It didn’t work out for Tolik. He could do everything in mathematics, but he couldn’t multiply. And on the very last day of the school year, when everyone received their annual grades, but Tolik did not, our Ivan Petrovich called Klyukvin into the empty teacher’s room to give him an assignment for the summer. The guys and I were in no hurry to go home and waited for Tolik in our class. Finally, Tolya appeared on the threshold. - Well, what's there? How was the issue resolved? - almost in unison

we turned to him. - Yes, I haven’t decided yet... Or rather, I need to solve one problem... - Which one? Tell us! What task did the teacher give you? “Ivan Petrovich looked and looked at me,” Tolik began, “sighed and sighed, and then said: “Here is an interesting problem. Take, Tolya, 2000 consecutive natural numbers 1, 2, ..., 2000. Each of them can be multiplied by itself. This is called calculating the square. So, look at all these 2000 squares. And you must tell me how many of the resulting squares have an odd tens digit. Summer is not just for kicking the ball! If you cope with the work, you will go to fifth grade. But no, in the second year.”
