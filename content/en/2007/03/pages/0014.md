Types of lasers

After the creation of the first ruby ​​lasers, intensive and very fruitful work began on the creation and research of new types of lasers. Let us indicate some of them that have become widespread. Lasers on luminescent crystals, glasses and dye solutions. Along with ruby, other crystals are also used, such as sapphire (crystalline aluminum oxide with titanium ions). A distinctive feature of this laser is its exceptionally wide gain bandwidth. This feature is extremely important for the generation of extremely short laser pulses. Glasses with an admixture of certain elements in the form of ions, for example some rare earth elements, are also used. The advantage of these materials is that they have high optical quality (the absence of any inclusions often present in crystals); laser parts of almost any size can be made from them. It also turned out to be possible to use solutions of certain luminescent dyes. The laser element in this case is not a rod with polished ends, but a cuvette - a cylindrical tube with plane-parallel windows filled with a dye solution. An important feature of these dyes is the very large gain bandwidth (same as sapphire). Thanks to this, it was possible to create a new type of laser with smooth tuning of the radiation wavelength. In modern lasers, the cavity often consists of not only two mirrors, but also a rather complex optical system between them. Above we talked about the mode selector. So, if a device based on the phenomenon of dispersion (a prism or a diffraction grating) is placed in the resonator and deflects the rays depending on the wavelength, then parallelism of the mirrors is ensured only for a narrow range of wavelengths, and it can be smoothly changed by tilting one of the mirrors. This results in a so-called selective resonator. As a result, the laser becomes a source of radiation with a very narrow spectral line, the wavelength of which can be continuously adjusted. Such lasers play an important role in spectroscopy. Semiconductor lasers. Population inversion can also be achieved in semiconductor bands using electric current. As a result, stimulated emission occurs at the radiative transitions of the semiconductor. Semiconductor lasers appeared, or, as they are now called, laser diodes. These lasers directly convert electrical current into laser radiation with very high efficiency exceeding 50%. In addition, they are distinguished by their very small sizes (on the order of a millimeter).

Thanks to these features, they have found wide application (laser printers, CD and DVD systems, pointers, etc.). For his work on creating lasers of this type, Russian physicist Zh.I. Alferov was awarded the Nobel Prize in 2000. Currently, these lasers are often used as pump sources for other solid-state lasers, in particular fiber lasers. As a result, fairly compact and efficient laser systems with laser radiation power of up to tens of kilowatts were created. They are beginning to find increasing use in the automotive and aviation industries. Fiber lasers. features of optical fiber. It consists of a thin thread (core) of one type of glass surrounded by glass of another type. The refractive index of the core glass is slightly greater than the refractive index of the cladding. As a result, light propagating in the core experiences total internal reflection at the core-shell boundary and can travel vast distances up to tens and hundreds of kilometers with virtually no loss. This is the basis of fiber-optic communications, the lines of which have already covered the entire globe. If luminescent additives (rare earth element ions) are introduced into the core glass, then with appropriate excitation by a pump source, an active medium can be obtained in the core. This pump source is usually radiation from a laser diode. The result is a fiber laser, which has a number of very important advantages. Since the radiation travels along the light guide, its bending does not affect the operation of the laser. Fine adjustment of the mirrors for parallelism is not required, and the laser is practically insensitive to external mechanical influences. The fiber can be rolled into a spool, which makes the laser very compact. Essentially, the active medium of a fiber laser is a cylinder with a diameter of several micrometers and a length of up to several meters. This means that the heat that is released in the active medium when it is pumped is transferred to the outer shell material through a large surface and to a significant volume (the diameter of the shell is tens of times larger than the diameter of the core).
