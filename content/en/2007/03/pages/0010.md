The laser is a remarkable achievement of the 20th century

P. KRYUKOV

Introduction

In the twentieth century, advances in physics and high technology led to achievements that radically influenced the life of human society. This is the creation of the atomic bomb, the mastery of atomic energy using nuclear reactors and the invention of the transistor. Here we can add the creation in 1960 of a fundamentally new light source - a laser, which has amazing properties. The laser is capable of emitting light with a very narrow spectrum (monochromatic radiation) in the form of a directed beam. High directivity of the laser beam, i.e. The small increase in cross-sectional area of ​​the beam as the distance over which it propagates increases means that the radiation energy can be transferred over considerable distances and concentrated by focusing with a suitable optical system. Advances in the development of lasers of various types have made it possible to obtain significant energies and powers of laser radiation. The legend about Archimedes burning the Roman fleet with focused solar rays and the fantastic descriptions of H. Wells and A. Tolstoy are becoming reality. Indeed, a powerful chemical laser is currently being created in the United States, placed on a Boeing 747 aircraft. This system is supposed to destroy ballistic missiles on takeoff at a distance of about 100 miles. In everyday life, lasers are also becoming widespread. They are used in medicine, industry (precision cutting and welding), in the latest electronics devices (DVD players, computers), in fiber optic communication systems and in laser shows. But perhaps the main applications are scientific research. High scientific results are recognized by Nobel Prizes, and their awarding is a definite indicator of the importance of the work. In 1964, C. Towns, N.G. Basov and A.M. Prokhorov were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for their contribution to the creation of the laser. Since then, almost a third of the Nobel Prizes in physics have been awarded for work involving the use of lasers. The scientific use of lasers is largely due to the fact that the radiation can be concentrated in a very narrow range of wavelengths, in other words, it is highly monochromatic and represents

an extremely narrow spectral line. This ensures extremely high accuracy of spectral measurements, which, in turn, is very important for studying the structure of matter. On the other hand, laser radiation can be concentrated in a very short flash - a pulse. This allows for high peak radiation power (energy divided by the time interval in which it is concentrated). Due to the high directivity of the light beam, it is possible to focus the radiation in a spot whose dimensions are close to the wavelength of laser radiation. As a result, it is possible to concentrate enormous radiation power in a small volume of matter and obtain very high temperatures - on the order of millions of degrees. Thus, for a short period of time of a laser pulse, it is possible to realize in laboratory conditions states of matter close to those that occur during a nuclear explosion or near astrophysical objects (stars, black holes). You can ask the question: where is the coldest place in the world? The possible answer - in the depths of space - will be incorrect. The lowest temperatures (differing by millionths of a degree from absolute zero) are obtained, not surprisingly, also with the help of lasers. The fact is that laser light is capable of imparting movement to atoms. You can choose the interaction of laser light with an atom in such a way that its movement will slow down and it will stop. It is possible to create a whole system of such “stopped” atoms. Since the temperature is determined by the speed of movement, such a system will have an ultra-low temperature. The substance at such low temperatures transforms into a special state - the so-called Bose-Einstein condensate. Note that research in this area was also awarded the Nobel Prize. The article presented to the attention of readers outlines a brief history of the creation of the laser, discusses the principle of operation and features of the laser, and lists some types of lasers indicating their scope of application. Particular attention is paid to lasers capable of generating ultrashort pulse durations and applications related to this feature of lasers.
