After two reflections and double passage of the active medium layer, the intensity will become $I_{0} R_{1} R_{2} e^{2} \alpha^{l}$. Generation is obtained under the condition that the intensity of the returned light is not less than the initial intensity, i.e. if $I_{0} R_{1} R_{2} e^{2} \alpha^{l}$ ³ $I_{0}$, where the equal sign means the generation threshold condition. Usually the value is $\alpha l = 1$, therefore $e^{\alpha} l$ » $1 - \alpha$, then the generation threshold condition takes the form $R_{1} R_{2} ( 1 - 2 \alpha l ) = 1$. However, it must be borne in mind that with successive rounds the waves will begin to interfere. The result of interference depends on the phase difference after reflections, which is determined by the distance between the mirrors. For those waves whose lengths fit an integer number of times along the length between the mirrors, interference will lead to the addition of the wave amplitudes, i.e. to constructive interference (Fig.4,c). It is for these waves that the generation threshold condition can be satisfied. On the contrary, waves with a different length will be attenuated. Thus, a system of two parallel mirrors is a resonator, and the resonant wavelengths $\lambda$ res are determined by the distance between the mirrors: $n \lambda_{рез} 2 = L$, where L is the distance between the mirrors and n is an integer. The corresponding frequencies $\nu_{n} = nc ( 2 L )$ are called modes of the optical cavity. Since $\lambda = L$, there are many modes, and the frequency interval between neighboring ones is that the waves of all modes propagate in one direction - perpendicular to the plane of the mirrors. This explains the high directivity of the laser beam. So, using a resonator, you can implement positive feedback and obtain lasing at optical frequencies. Since the self-excitation condition can be satisfied for many modes, the laser produces radiation at many equidistantly located ones, i.e. located at the same distances from each other, frequencies. This mode of laser operation is called multimode lasing. The spectral linewidth in one mode is extremely small, because with multipath interference and amplification, the dependence of the resulting amplitude on wavelength sharpens. In order to obtain highly monochromatic laser radiation, it is necessary to force the laser to generate radiation in only one mode. This mode of laser operation is called single-mode lasing. In order to achieve this mode, a special device is introduced into the resonator - two more mirrors with low reflection coefficients and a distance between them less than L. An additional resonator is obtained with a larger interval between its modes. Their resonances overlap each other, and generation occurs only at coinciding resonances. The frequency interval between them can be comparable to the width of the amplification line. This device is called a mode selector. It makes it possible to obtain lasing from only one mode with highly monochromatic laser radiation.
