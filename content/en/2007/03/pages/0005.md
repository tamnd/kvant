Leonard Euler

Life path

On April 15, 1707, in the city of Basel (Switzerland), a boy was born to Paul Euler and his wife Margarita Brückner, who was named Leonard. The following year the family moved to the village of Rügen, near Basel, where Paul Euler became pastor of an Evangelical Reformed church. In his youth, Paul Euler studied mathematics under the guidance of Jacob Bernoulli, the eldest of the mathematicians of the famous Bernoulli family. Paul and Jacob's younger brother, Johann Bernoulli, who played a big role in Leonard's life, even lived together for some time in Jacob's house. The name Bernoulli will appear more than once in this article, and a little later it will be discussed in more detail. In early childhood, Leonard received initial information about mathematics from his father, and then he was sent to the Latin school in Basel. They didn't teach mathematics there. In 1720, a thirteen-year-old boy was admitted to the Faculty of Philosophy at the University of Basel. Euler receives a university diploma and then tries for some time (without success) to take the position of professor of logic and law there. Then he becomes a student at the Faculty of Theology, fulfilling the wishes of his father, who dreamed of guiding his son along his own path. In parallel with theology, the young man began to seriously study mathematics. This is what he wrote in his autobiographical essay: “I soon found an opportunity to be introduced to the famous professor Johann Bernoulli. He was really very busy, so he flatly refused to give me private lessons, but he gave me very valuable advice - to start reading more complex mathematical books on my own, studying them as diligently as possible; in case of obstacles or difficulties, I received permission to visit him freely every Saturday afternoon, and he

kindly explained to me everything that I could not understand.” In 1724, Euler defended a dissertation in which he compared the concepts of natural science of Descartes and Newton, and received a Master of Arts degree. At the same time, he meets two members of the Bernoulli clan of a younger generation - Nikolai and Daniel. Here, perhaps, it is appropriate to give a brief background on the Bernoulli family. The roots of this family lay in Holland, but due to religious persecution, part of the family moved to Germany and then to Switzerland. There, the founder of the family of famous scientists, Nicholas Bernoulli (1623–1708), was a member of the Basel High Council and Court. Nicholas had four sons: Jacob (1654–1705), who became a professor in Basel, Johann (1667–1748), who was a professor in Groningen and then in Basel, and two more who were not involved in science. Johann had three sons, two of whom, Nikolai (1695–1726) and Daniel (1700–1782), became friends of Euler. Johann Bernoulli convinced Euler's father that Leonard's true calling was as a mathematician and that his desire to become a scientist should not be hindered, and his father agreed with this. In 1726, Leonhard Euler's first publication on the theory of sound appeared. From that moment it is reasonable to calculate the beginning of his unparalleled creative biography. The list of Euler's works, compiled in 1910, contains 886 titles of various books, articles and notes. In 1909, the Swiss Society of Naturalists decided to publish Euler's complete works. It was initially assumed that the collection would consist of 75 volumes, and there was an intention to complete the publication by the middle of the century, then it was planned to complete the work by Euler's three hundredth anniversary. It now seems possible to complete this enormous work by 2009, the centenary of its beginning. 72 volumes of scientific works have already been published (29 on mathematics, 33 on mechanics and astronomy and 10 on physics) and part of the final eight
