ice, water and steam

Many people will answer how familiar they are. Water is the most abundant substance on Earth. All living things, including ourselves, mainly consist of water. Its physical properties have been studied and re-studied, they are even used as standards. All this is true, but water in its various states of aggregation still presents many surprises. The features of this seemingly simple substance are not yet fully understood, and the behavior of water cannot always be predicted. Scientists, of course, do not want to come to terms with this state of affairs and stubbornly continue to understand more and more subtle details of the structure of water and its participation in mysterious effects. And it stimulates researchers to understand the exceptional importance of water for life on Earth. After all, more complete knowledge about it is necessary to solve environmental problems, and to create new drugs and treatment methods, and to counter global climate change, and to improve human production activities... The scale of these tasks prompts us to turn to a conversation about the physical properties of one of the four main elements. Look, even among school questions and tasks there are many that will make you doubt the simplicity of this simple substance - water.

In this case, the number of strange ones is equal to [ N 2 ] - L, and by choosing L it can be made any natural number ranging from 1 to [ N 2 ] - 1. Problem 18. Prove that in any tournament the strong together with the strange ones make up less than 2/3 of the total number of players, and that the same is true for the weak along with the strange ones. If there are no averages at the tournament, then the strong and weak each make up more than a third of the tournament participants, and the strange ones make up less than a third. More precisely, if N has the form 3M, 3M + 1 or 3M + 2 (M is natural), then the number of strange ones does not exceed, respectively, M – 2, M – 1, M. Solution. Let's consider the “arithmetic average” strong player (i.e., add up the points scored by the strong ones and divide by the number of such participants). This conditional player won against as many strong people as he lost to. At the same time, he lost to all the strange ones. Therefore, the sum of the points of the “arithmetic average” strong player is no more than the number of average and weak players plus half the number of strong ones. Likewise, the result of the “arithmetic average” of a strange player is no less than the number of strong ones plus half the number of strange ones. But this amount is less than in the first case. As a consequence, the total number of average and weak ones is more than half the total number of strong and strange ones. And this means that the strong and strange make up less than two-thirds of the entire roster of players. The statement about the number of strange people along with the weak ones is proven in a similar way. Problem 19. Any two participants scored different numbers of points. Prove that there are no strange players. Solution (found by participants of the Tournament of Cities conference). Let's assume that there is a strange player. Since he is alone, he takes neither first nor last place. Consequently, the first player lost to the strange one and, therefore, scored no more than n – 2 points, and the last player won against the strange one and, therefore, scored at least one point. On the other hand, since there was exactly one draw in the tournament, two players have half-integer scores and n – 2 players have integer scores. Thus, there are players who scored 1, 2,..., n – 2 points. Let's look at the last player. Since he won against the strange one, he scored exactly 1 point, i.e. lost to everyone else. Then the penultimate player (if it is not the strange one) won against the strange one and the last one and, therefore, lost to everyone else. Repeating this reasoning, we find that none of the weak players could play a draw. Likewise, none of the strong players could play a draw. Contradiction. (End to follow)

Screw and nut. It is very possible that this puzzle was invented around the same time as the pieces that make it up. The place of her birth has not yet been established even to the accuracy of the continent. Its variants have been found in Africa, Europe and Asia, consisting of sticks with rings attached to them. Among the Koryaks living on the Kamchatka Peninsula, this toy is considered a national puzzle and has been known since time immemorial. The task is to move one of the rings onto the loop with the other ring. The design shown in the photo requires connecting a screw and a nut. Like most good puzzles, the problem seems impossible at first. And it is solved, one might say, “head-on”! You need to stubbornly push the nut along the rope until it connects with the screw. Just don't try to push a large nut through a small hole. Instead, pull the rope knot through the hole and slide the rope over the nut, not the other way around. Bolt and washer. This wonderful puzzle was created by Moscow inventor Kirill Grebnev. To appreciate it, you should keep in mind that it is much more difficult to come up with a puzzle that is apparently simple but difficult to solve than one consisting of many parts. Not every puzzle inventor can do this. Based on the totality of its advantages, the Bolt and Puck toy can be called the best puzzle of 2006, and not only in Russia. It turned out to be the most popular at the annual October meeting of the Dutch club NKC, which unites more than 400 puzzle lovers from 38 countries. The task of the puzzle is obvious - you need to remove the washer from the bolt. To make this possible, you need to unhook the cord holding it from the washer. The loop that encloses the puck can be moved in two directions, but only one direction leads to the goal. Let's hope this hint is enough for you. I wish you success! A. Kalinin
