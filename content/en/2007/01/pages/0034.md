Are you really familiar with this?

?

ice, water and steam

Many people will answer how familiar they are. Water is the most abundant substance on Earth. All living things, including ourselves, mainly consist of water. Its physical properties have been studied and re-studied, they are even used as standards. All this is true, but water in its various states of aggregation still presents many surprises. The features of this seemingly simple substance are not yet fully understood, and the behavior of water cannot always be predicted. Scientists, of course, do not want to come to terms with this state of affairs and stubbornly continue to understand more and more subtle details of the structure of water and its participation in mysterious effects. And it stimulates researchers to understand the exceptional importance of water for life on Earth. After all, more complete knowledge about it is necessary to solve environmental problems, and to create new drugs and treatment methods, and to counter global climate change, and to improve human production activities... The scale of these tasks prompts us to turn to a conversation about the physical properties of one of the four main elements. Look, even among school questions and tasks there are many that will make you doubt the simplicity of this simple substance - water.

Questions and tasks 1. Will a sealed glass bottle filled to the top with water remain intact if it is lowered into melting ice? 2. A ball of ice is frozen to the bottom of a vessel with water from the inside. Will the water level in the container change when the ice melts? 3. Can tin be melted in hot water? How about freezing water with molten metal? 4. Fog droplets can remain liquid even at a temperature of –30 $^\circ$ C. Why?

5. At the first frost, water bodies freeze more easily if it snows. How to explain this? 6. What physical mistake was made in the poem about a drop: “It lived and flowed along the glass, but suddenly it was encased in frost, and the drop became a motionless piece of ice, and the warmth in the world decreased”? 7. Why does the melting of ice floating in the sea into water not lead to floods, whereas melting the ice resting on the island of Greenland would cause a catastrophic rise in sea levels? 8. The specific heat of fusion of copper is significantly less than that of ice. Does this mean that melting a copper plate in winter conditions will require less energy than melting a piece of ice of the same mass? 9. Why are steam burns more dangerous than boiling water burns? 10. What will extinguish the flame faster - boiling water or cold water? 11. Boil water in a saucepan with a heavy lid. Having removed the pan from the stove, it was allowed to cool slightly, then tea leaves were poured into the calm water, and the water boiled vigorously. Why? 12. Wanting to speed up the cooking process, the housewife increased the heat under the pan in which the water was boiling. Did the hostess do the right thing? 13. Are the readings of thermometers the same, one of which is placed at the surface of boiling water, and the other in its thickness? 14. A glass with a small amount of water was placed under the bell of the air pump and the air began to be pumped out. Why did the water first boil and then freeze? 15. Water was boiled in a round-bottomed flask, the flask was sealed and inverted. If you now put a little snow on the bottom of the flask or pour cold water over it, the water in the flask will boil. How to explain this? 16. In what case does the “dew point” become a “frost point”?
