The final stage of the competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

The recreation center "Berendeevy Polyany" near Sudislavl, Kostroma region, has already become a traditional venue for the final competitions of the A.P. Savin competition. In the summer of 2006, the XII final summer tournament of mathematical battles took place here, which once again broke the record for the number of participating teams: thirty-six. Schoolchildren from fifteen cities attended the tournament. Separate teams arrived from Ivanovo, Kirov, Kostroma, Magnitogorsk, Perm, Troitsk, Kharkov. And, of course, Moscow presented the most teams. A notable feature of the latest tournaments is that the competitions have become attractive to ninth-graders. The loser is eliminated

As you know, round-robin tournaments are not the only existing form of competition. In contrast, in the cup (Olympic) system, the loser is eliminated and draws are impossible. In such competitions, the role of chance is much higher, but the struggle is more intense. If the circular tournament corresponds to a complete graph (vertices are players, edges are duels, any two vertices are connected by an edge), then the graph of the Olympic tournament is a binary tree (there are no cycles, and on the path leading from a hanging vertex to the root, two edges enter each intermediate vertex and one exits). Problem 27. The boxing tournament was held according to the Olympic system (losers in each round are eliminated, there are no resters). How many boxers participated in the tournament if at the end of the tournament it turned out that 32 people won more fights than they lost? Solution. Under the Olympic system, most fights are won by those and only those boxers who reach at least the third round. Participants in the third round make up a quarter of the total number of participants. Consequently, 128 boxers participated in the tournament. It is interesting to compare the results of possible tournaments with the same participants, but held using different systems. Let us present two problems on this topic (author K. Feldman, see article by B. Frenkin “Draw for the champion” in «Kvant» No. 5 for 2000). To highlight what depends on the form of the game, and not on the players, we will accept the “stable game assumption”: in each pair of players the winner is always the same. Draws in the round-robin tournament between such players are excluded, since they do not occur in the cup tournament. However, we assume that one player wins against another, another – against a third, and at the same time the third wins against the first. Problem 28. The championship was held in a round robin with the participation of $2^{N}$ players. Now the same athletes will have to compete for the cup. The assumption of a stable game is fulfilled. Prove that there is a cup draw in which the round-robin champion will advance to the final. Solution. Let's number the players as follows. At the beginning of the list we will put those who defeated the champion, i.e. "dangerous". Next - those who lost to him, i.e. “non-dangerous”. We'll give the last number to the champion. In each round of the cup draw we will make pairs in numerical order. The champion performed no worse than the “average” participant, who won as much as he lost. This means that the number of “dangerous” ones is not greater than the number of “non-dangerous” ones. All “dangerous” ones will be in the first half of the list, while the champion will be in the second. Therefore, the champion will not meet the “dangerous” player before the final, which is what was required. Problem 29. Using the conditions of the previous problem, prove that there is a draw in which the champion of the round-robin tournament will receive the cup. Solution. Each of the “dangerous” (who beat the champion) lost in the championship to one of the “non-dangerous” (otherwise he would have won more matches than the champion, which is impossible). Let’s make the first pair of the cup draw from the “dangerous” one and the “non-dangerous” one to which he lost.

We make the next pairs in the same way, as long as possible. Let’s say there are “dangerous” ones left who cannot be included in such pairs (they lost in the championship to those “non-dangerous” ones who were already included in the previous pairs). Then let these “dangerous” ones play among themselves. If one of them is left without a pair, then we will do the following. The total number of “dangerous” ones is no greater than the number of “non-dangerous” ones (since the champion performed no worse than the “average” player, who won and lost equally). In the paired pairs there are no more “non-dangerous” players than “dangerous” ones, and one more “dangerous” one was left without a pair. This means that one of the “non-dangerous” ones has not yet been included in the pair - let the remaining “dangerous” one play with him. The remaining players are paired up randomly. The champion will advance to the next round because he is playing against a “non-dangerous” player. If in other rounds the pairs are built according to the same rule, then the champion will receive the cup. This is certainly possible if in each round less than half of the participants are “dangerous”. Let us assume that up to a certain round we ensured the fulfillment of this condition. We will show that this can be achieved in the next round. Since half of the participants from the previous one advance to the next round, it is enough to show that at least half of the “dangerous” ones are eliminated. But in matches between them, every second participant is eliminated. Only one “dangerous” one can win against a “non-dangerous” one. Therefore, it is enough that in the same round at least one “dangerous” player loses to a “non-dangerous” one. As we have seen, this was accomplished in the first round. Only those “dangerous” ones who lost to one of the “non-dangerous” ones who also made it to this round advanced to subsequent rounds. And the first pair was made up of a “dangerous” and a “non-dangerous” one to whom he loses. Our statement has been proven. To conclude this section, we present a problem that considers another form of competition - the knockout game. Despite the simplicity of the formulation and solution, the task turned out to be difficult for the participants in the selection for the 1998 Russian Olympiad. Problem 30. A group of schoolchildren plays ping-pong. They set up a queue, first and second play first, and then each next participant plays with the winner of the previous pair. The next day, the same schoolchildren play for elimination again, but the line goes in the opposite direction - from last to first. Prove that there are two schoolchildren who played with each other both on the first day and on the second. Solution. The opponent of the last player on the first day plays with everyone who is behind him in the queue. He plays his first game with one of them on the second day.

A few more tasks

Some of these problems have not been solved. Problem 31. a) Prove that in a tournament without draws, out of n participants, you can number them so that the 1st one beats the 2nd one, the 2nd one beats the 3rd one,..., (n – 1)th one beats the nth one, and the 1st one beats the nth one. b) Prove that in a tournament without draws, either there is a cycle that includes all participants, or it is possible to divide the participants into two groups so that any player from the first group defeats any player from the second.
