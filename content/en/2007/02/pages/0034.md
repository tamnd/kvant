"THE MOST FAMOUS SCIENTIST"

THIS is how one of the prominent mathematicians of the 18th century, Professor at the University of Basel Johann Bernoulli, ADDRESSED HIS FORMER student. There is no exaggeration in this respectful address - it was addressed to Leonhard Euler (1707-1783).

Euler set an example of selfless service to science. Having lost one eye in 1738, and in 1766 he became blind in both eyes, he nevertheless continued to work productively, dictating the results of his numerous studies to secretaries and students. Half of his works were created during this period.

Euler presented his results with amazing clarity, revealing the paths leading to them and providing calculations with valuable examples. “Read, read Euler, he is our common teacher,” advised the famous French mathematician P. Laplace. Another famous mathematician, K. Gauss, agrees with him: “The study of Euler’s works remains the best school in various fields of mathematics, and nothing else can replace it.”

Scientists from many countries recognized Euler not only as “the most famous,” but also as “super-witty,” “incomparable,” and “the leader of mathematicians.” The breadth of scientific interests, the depth of the results obtained, and most importantly, Euler’s incredible creative productivity still inspires admiration in our time. Euler wrote about 750 articles, 40 books, and 15 of his works were prepared for various competitions. To this should be added several thousand letters to various countries

Europe, often representing genuine scientific works. The list of his works covers all contemporary sections of mathematics, mechanics, optics, astronomy, technology and other branches of knowledge.

Statistical calculations show that Euler made on average one discovery per week.

Here are some of Euler's findings:

• In any triangle, the point of intersection of the heights H (orthocenter), the point of intersection of the medians M and the center O of the circumscribed circle lie on the same straight line - the Euler straight line. Point M lies between points O and H, while OM: MH = 1: 2 (Fig.1).
