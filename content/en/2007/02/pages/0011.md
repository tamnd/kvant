Wind force - Beaufort scale

The wind is of interest not only to sailors and pilots, it also greatly influences the quality of astronomical data.

Its intensity is perceived by several senses at once - eyes, skin, ears, and is also objectively measured by an anemometer as air flow speed (which meteorologists prefer to call wind force). To visually assess wind strength, there is a special 13-point scale developed by the English admiral F. Beaufort in 1806. The Beaufort Point has a verbal definition of wind strength - calm, fresh wind, storm, hurricane, etc. and the visual, descriptive manifestation of its action on land and sea - the pattern of swaying trees, the character and height of waves. At first, this scale was used only by its author himself. But in 1874, the Standing Committee of the First Meteorological Congress adopted the Beaufort scale for use in international synoptic practice. Later, the scale was changed and refined, and in 1963 it was adopted by the World Meteorological Organization as shown in Table 3 (at a standard height of 10 m above an open, flat surface).
