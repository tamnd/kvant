The ambiguity of bels and decibels makes the “dex” unit more convenient, which is increasingly used in mathematics, astronomy and many areas of physics. If we perceive the amplitude of a sound wave as loudness, then its frequency characterizes the pitch of the sound. And in this case, the Weber-Fechner law is valid: different sounds are perceived by us as equally spaced in height if the ratios of their frequencies are equal. Logarithmic units are used to measure pitch intervals. The main one among them is the octave, limited by frequencies whose ratio is equal to two. The concept of an octave is becoming increasingly popular outside the musical field, as numbers of the form $2^{n}$ are widely used in pulsed electronics, particularly in computing.
