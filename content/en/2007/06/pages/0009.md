EXPLOSION

kah. Blasting operations using gunpowder to extract ores were first carried out in Russia (1617) and only then became widespread in Europe. In the construction business - the demolition of structures, the planning of roads and sites in mountainous areas, the creation of drainage canal systems, the formation of dams and dams using powerful explosions. In the water industry - deepening the bottom of reservoirs and river fairways, destroying rapids and riffles, eliminating ice jams during floods. By the way, the first use of explosives for peaceful purposes recorded in history occurred in the middle of the 15th century, when the bed of the Lithuanian Neman River was cleared of stones and rapids with the help of gunpowder charges. These works were presumably carried out by Italian engineers. In the oil and gas industry - eliminating accidents of a drilling tool stuck in a well, increasing oil recovery by creating additional fracturing of the formation, creating underground oil and gas storage facilities, eliminating fires in oil and gas wells by explosively pinching them. In the metalworking and metallurgy industries - explosive hardening of metal, explosive stamping and welding, crushing of slag and large scrap metal. In scientific research - the study of the properties of gases, liquids and solids at high explosive pressures and temperatures, the characteristics of chemical kinetics, transport phenomena and phase transitions under these conditions, the production of ultra-strong magnetic fields for a short time (with an induction of up to 1500 Tesla), the study of “stellar matter” in the initial stage of a nuclear explosion. But perhaps the most important use of explosions for scientific purposes is seismic probing of the Earth's interior using strong explosions. In this way, information was obtained about the internal structure of the Earth - the crust, mantle, liquid and solid core, which made it possible for geology from a descriptive science to become a full-fledged science - geodynamics. What unites all these different aspects of the use of explosion? What is most characteristic of this unusual and seemingly mysterious natural phenomenon?

What is an explosion?

The “Physical Encyclopedic Dictionary” (1960 edition) gives the following definition: “An explosion is a sudden change in the physical or chemical state of a substance, accompanied by an extremely rapid transformation (release) of energy, which leads to heating, movement and compression of the transformation products and the environment, the occurrence of an intense pressure surge, destruction and scattering. A special kind of disturbance is formed and propagated in the environment – ​​a shock wave.” The author of this definition is A.F.
