In nuclear explosions, in addition to the usual damaging factors (destruction of everything nearby, the effect of a shock wave in the distance), there are additional factors - the impact of strong thermal, x-ray and gamma radiation, as well as the radioactivity of the explosion products and the soil that has become radioactive, spreading with a dust cloud (“mushroom”) over long distances from the explosion. 3) Electrical energy. These are the oldest explosions known to people - thunderstorm phenomena. Lightning is very fast. In a fraction of a second, a long, broken channel is formed, which is a plasma with high temperature and pressure. This plasma, expanding, forms a shock wave in the surrounding air, which, spreading and weakening, turns into a strong acoustic wave. We perceive it as thunder. Near lightning, you can feel, in addition to its thermal radiation, the effect of a shock wave. A lightning explosion resembles the explosion of a long cord of explosive material (called a detonation cord for initiating explosive charges from a long distance). In addition to the explosive effect, a lightning discharge can also cause electric shock. In laboratory conditions, an electrical explosion is produced by passing a strong current through a thin metal wire. 4) Kinetic energy of fast moving bodies. This energy, after a series of transformations, is released in the form of kinetic energy of an obstacle or air. This is the nature of the explosions that occur when meteorites fall. At small angles of inclination to the horizon, an explosion can occur in the air with the formation of a strong shock wave. At large angles of inclination, the path of the meteorite in the atmosphere is much shorter, and kinetic energy is converted into thermal energy already in the thickness of the earth. This is an analogue of an underground explosion with the formation of an explosion crater (meteor crater). This has happened on Earth - geologists count several huge craters, now filled with sedimentary rocks. On the Moon, where there is no atmosphere, each meteorite forms an explosion crater - the lunar face is dotted with meteorite craters. 5) Potential energy of elastic deformations in the earth's crust. A huge mass of rock can be compressed by the environment and have strong elastic deformations (compression or shear). Sometimes a small movement of rocks is enough for the energy of elastic deformation to transform into kinetic energy of the medium (by analogy with an expanding spring). This is the internal mechanism of earthquakes, which are a wave consequence of the initial unloading of deformation, which was of a sudden, explosive nature. 6) Internal energy of compressed gases. If the walls of a vessel containing compressed gas suddenly collapse, then the internal energy, according to the first law of thermodynamics, will “work” on the environment, first of all expanding it and forming a shock wave. These are explosions of compressed gas cylinders (often accompanied by ignition of the gas if it is flammable), explosions of steam boilers when the rules of their operation are violated. In nature, these are volcanic explosions. For example, the explosion of the island of Santorini in the 15th century BC. - a possible prototype of the death of Atlantis. In 1883, during the enormous explosion of the Krakatoa volcano, a cloud of volcanic dust raised by the explosion spread throughout the atmosphere. The classic explanation of such an explosion through the lips of his hero, engineer Cyrus Smith, was made by Jules Verne in the novel “The Mysterious Island.”

What do all six considered cases of explosive transformation have in common? First of all, this is a sudden transition of stored potential energy (or even kinetic - for meteorites) into the main explosive effect - the kinetic energy of movement of the explosion products and the environment, which is accompanied by the scattering of fragments of an explosive device and surrounding objects and the formation of an explosive shock wave (this will be discussed later). To conclude this article, I will focus on the use of powerful (high energy) explosions for peaceful purposes.
