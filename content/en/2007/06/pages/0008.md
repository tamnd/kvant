Explosion began to work in earnest after the invention of dynamite at the end of the 19th century. The fact is that powder explosions often turned out to be insufficiently effective for crushing hard rocks such as granite and diabase. Chemists invented more powerful explosives - salts of explosive acid, nitroglycerin and others. But they were very dangerous to use - with small mechanical impacts, an explosion could occur. A good example of such an explosive is nitrogen iodide, easily obtained by dissolving crystalline iodine in ammonia. The resulting white powder can be filtered and moved until it loses moisture. But as soon as the moisture evaporates, a slight shake is enough for an explosion to occur. The energy of such an explosion is low, and it does not cause harm, but the noise is great. Substances of this type are now used in cartridge primers. They explode from the impact of the firing pin of a pistol or rifle and provoke an explosive transformation of the main charge in the cartridge. Dynamite is safer. Dynamite is a variety of flammable fibrous materials (such as cotton wool) that absorb nitroglycerin (with some additives). Nitroglycerin, which fills the pores of the filler, turns out to be much less sensitive to mechanical stress. In terms of its explosive power, dynamite is much more effective than gunpowder. This invention was made by the Swedish chemist and engineer Alfred Nobel (whose father was a military engineer in Russia during the Crimean War, and whose brothers were the main creators of the oil industry in the Caucasus). The rapid spread of dynamite in mining and construction brought capital to the inventor, the interest from which now amounts to several million euros a year and, according to A. Nobel’s will, is the financial basis for international Nobel Prizes. After the invention of dynamite, there was a belief that powerful explosives could be quite safe to handle and, alas, in the manufacture of artillery shells, mines and bombs. Explosives such as trinitrotoluene (TNT or tol), tetranitropentaerythritol (PETN), cyclotrimethylenetrinitroamine (RDX) and their mixtures appeared. This happened during A. Nobel’s lifetime, and he, feeling responsibility and regret for this aspect of the use of new explosives, bequeathed the prize “For Activities to Preserve Peace” among the award nominations.
