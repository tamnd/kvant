Main types of initial explosion energy

1) Chemical energy of solid or liquid explosives, explosive gas or dust-air mixtures. These are the most common cases of explosions. The essence of energy conversion in this case is the transition of part of the electrical binding energy of the electrons of molecular orbitals into the kinetic energy of repulsion of reaction products, the binding energy in the molecules of which is greater in magnitude than that of the original substance. At the same time, they “work”, i.e. give kinetic energy and, therefore, higher temperature, electrical repulsive forces of the outer electron orbitals of the products. The reaction mechanism during combustion is exactly the same. An ordinary flame is transferred from one section of gas to another due to the processes of thermal conductivity and diffusion. An important role in this case is played by the diffusion transfer of active reaction centers - molecules in which the vector sum of their own rotational characteristics (spins) of the outer electrons is not equal to zero. Such molecules are spoken of as particles with an unsaturated spin. They are very active chemically, since the unsaturated spin wants to find a particle with the opposite spin. Because of their chemical activity, these particles are called radicals (in politics, a radical is an active politician who does not act according to the standard). Free radicals start and maintain a chemical reaction. The rapid transfer of active centers creates a chain (avalanche) process of involving new particles in the reaction and, consequently, increasing the temperature. The combustion process under certain conditions can lead to an explosion. It is no longer possible to explain this transition only by transport phenomena, since the chemical transformation during an explosion, which is called detonation, requires an understanding of the laws of gas dynamics. And, in particular, knowledge of a special type of movement of matter - a shock wave. 2) Nuclear energy. Its main difference from the chemical one is the value of the specific energy. The widespread prejudice against the dangerous radioactive consequences of “peaceful” underground nuclear explosions is based on ignorance of the issue. During an explosion in the massif, radioactive products remain securely buried in the source of the explosion in the form of a vitrified substance, practically insoluble in water. This is precisely the scheme of modern disposal of nuclear waste - sintering before vitrification and underground burial. The nature of energy transformations in nuclear explosions also lies in the conversion of part of the potential binding energy of nucleons in atomic nuclei into the kinetic energy of reaction products. In the case of a uranium or plutonium explosion, electrical repulsive forces “work” between positively charged nuclei - the products of the division of the original nucleus into two parts. The initial distances of action of these forces have a nuclear scale, and it is $10^{5}$ times smaller than the distances of the atomic (molecular) scale. And besides, the share of conversion of potential energy into kinetic energy during nuclear fission is much greater than during the destruction of a molecular complex. As for the strong short-range interactions, which are actually called nuclear forces, they, being forces of attraction, only interfere with the fission process in its initial stage and make the process of spontaneous fission extremely unlikely. The fission process begins when a uranium-235 or plutonium nucleus captures a neutron and becomes highly unstable. In the droplet model of the nucleus, it changes shape - instead of a ball, it becomes an elongated ellipsoid, in which the role of nuclear attractive forces is greatly reduced, since the size of the ellipsoid exceeds the critical threshold for the action of nuclear forces (2 H $10^{-} 15$ m). This is where the electrical repulsive forces of the protons come into play, tearing the nucleus into two parts. The rapidity of the nuclear transformation of uranium in a bomb is also caused by a branched chain reaction, where the role of active centers is played by neutrons, “multiplying” in each fission event. The kinetics of this process is described by the same equation as the kinetics of chemical chain reactions. The process can also develop stationary (nuclear reactors) or exponentially (nuclear bomb). The mechanism of energy conversion during a thermonuclear (“hydrogen”) explosion is different. The essence of nuclear reactions in this case is the formation of helium during the fusion of hydrogen nuclei (more precisely, its isotopes - deuterium and tritium). The kinetic energy of the resulting helium and neutrons is high due to the work done during fusion (fusion) by attractive forces. And these can only be nuclear forces - after all, electric repulsive forces only prevent hydrogen nuclei from combining.
