Department of “Morality, Law, Law” (law and civics)

This is the twelfth recruitment for the department. Schoolchildren in grades 8–11 and “Collective Student” groups are offered two courses. 1) One-year course “Conversations about human rights, morality, law, law and state.” The course provides modern ideas about the basic concepts related to law, law and the state, talks about the basics of Russian legislation, and human rights. Examples of court cases are analyzed and general cultural examples related to the focus of the course are given. 2) One and a half year course “Conversations about the foundations of democracy.” We suggest taking the courses in this order. And only high school students, if they do not have time to complete two courses in a row, but want to master the second course, can start directly from it. Those wishing to enroll must provide their full postal address (email address, if any), last name, first name and patronymic, how many classes have been completed. When assessing the entrance work, we take into account the age (basic education) of the applicant. Be sure to include a stamped, self-addressed envelope in your letter (so we can respond to you) and the answers to the questions below. The deadline for submitting the entry work is June 1, 2008. “Collective student” groups are accepted without introductory work.

Department of History

The department opened in 1998. Studying at the department will allow everyone, including residents of the most remote cities and villages, to expand their horizons and prepare

for admission to university. Those who successfully complete the course receive a diploma. Why do you need to study history? First of all, it's just interesting. It is interesting to know how people once lived, what they dressed in, what they ate, what they read, how they got married, what they fought for and “what they got into.” Secondly, it's useful. Only by knowing the past can one understand the present and predict the future. We will help you figure it out. Experienced teachers write books especially for you. You will be one of the first to know the latest news from the world of history! We will keep in constant contact with you. According to our books, you will perform special tasks and tell us what you have unearthed. After all, in essence, the work of a historian consists of these excavations. A historical archaeologist, digging through the earth and sand, finds grains of bygone times; the historian-archivist digs through a pile of papers and takes out from the archives and even from private correspondence everything that can allow him to understand the image of time; a theoretical historian, like a fascinating novel, reads archaeological tables, dry reports, statistics and uses them to reconstruct the living fabric of a bygone life. The historian has a special profession: he is an investigator, prosecutor and lawyer of the time in one person. The introductory task for the department is completed on a double sheet of paper. The deadline for submitting the entry work is June 1, 2008. “Collective student” groups are accepted without introductory work.
