At least he remembered to take the lamp with him when he left (although by that point it was no longer working). Another memorable “adventure” with the participation of the IKK was my trip to Sweden in 1984 (my first individual business trip to a capital country) to the International Physics Olympiad as a correspondent for the magazine «Kvant». The entire registration procedure began with a direct call from the ICC to the First Deputy Minister, who had to confirm to Kikoin the inclusion of my name in the list of secondees (I remind you that at that time I was an ordinary assistant at Moscow State University) and the consent of the leadership of the USSR Ministry of Education to allocate the appropriate foreign currency funds. The actual paperwork took at least three months, but there was still not enough time - nothing was clear until the departure. Finally, the moment came when the Olympics began in three days. At the same time, a team of schoolchildren - participants in the Olympiad and two leaders, in the company of whom I was supposed to fly, was registered for Sweden with me. Everything went according to plan for them - their passports with visas, plane tickets, and cash were already waiting for them at the ministry. And my situation, as often happened, froze - neither yes nor no. And then the IKK, without delay, called Minister M.A. Prokofiev via the famous “turntable” (as far as I know, they had a warm informal relationship). IKK knew how to achieve results without unnecessary words and emotions even in almost hopeless situations (he had an amazing knowledge of our system from the inside). As it later turned out, despite the earlier assurances of a second person in the ministry, without another direct call from Kikoin to the minister, nothing would have happened - the system worked in a mode of constantly waiting for an opportune moment to refuse. The car that had stopped started spinning again. I flew to the Olympics late, all alone, but I flew. This, as it turned out later, had its own charm at that time. Throughout the entire business trip, I belonged only to myself (maybe I was too naive). This was a reward for patience. We all returned to Moscow together. I won’t retell my feelings from that trip, I’ll just say that everything was at the level of a miracle, given to me by one gesture (which might not have happened) from IKK. And all because he promised. If someone else had been in my place, the IKK would have done the same (and did, I know that for sure). After returning from Sweden, I called IKK, and he invited me to come to his house during lunch hours to talk about the trip and discuss it in an informal

situation of accumulated cases. The ICC listened with great attention to my impressions of the Olympiad. I asked if the younger Sigban (Kai Sigban - Nobel Prize laureate in physics 1981, son of Mann Sigban - Nobel laureate in physics 1924) headed the International Organizing Committee of the Olympiad. He turned out to be absolutely right in his guess. When I, on behalf of the International Organizing Committee, presented the ICC with a special folder for the Olympiad participant, he quickly leafed through its contents and began to look at the map of Stockholm with interest. Together we found the building of the City Hall, where a reception was held for the participants of the Olympiad by the King of Sweden. The ICC reacted to this fact with great approval, agreeing that the International School Olympiads could well be classified as events of national importance. Having learned that the Olympiad participants were placed in a picturesque suburb of Stockholm (Sigtuna) in a very prestigious educational institution, where many outstanding politicians and public figures were educated, the IKK remembered the amazing details associated with the history of this city, and again appreciated the royal scope of the organizers of the Olympiad. IKK left suddenly. At least for us. He was buried in severe frost on December 30, 1984. I still remember that his relatives and those who simply loved him (and some of those who didn’t) stood without hats throughout the farewell ceremony. A feeling of hopelessness, abandonment and, I would even say, insecurity came over me. He devoted so much effort and attention to the magazine, at all times he was his reliable support and protection, and suddenly, instantly, without warning, everything changed, his legs stopped feeling the ground. Time has stood still... It seems to me that, despite various premonitions and expectations, the veil of mystery that hung over the journal for about a month, associated with a short-lived intra-academic confrontation of interests, was dealt with fairly by history. The Presidium of the Academy of Sciences appointed the director of the Institute of Solid State Physics, the creator of one of the science cities near Moscow, as it is commonly called today, academician Yuri Andreevich Osipyan, as the editor-in-chief of the journal «Kvant». Twenty-odd years have passed since January 1985. During this time, a lot has changed both around us and in our magazine. If a miracle happened and I had a chance to meet with the ICC today, I would not easily find answers to all of the questions posed by the creator of the magazine «Kvant». Especially when it comes to circulation, which, compared to the best years of the Kikoin era, has fallen by almost 100 times. But I would like to hope that on the whole, taking into account all the cataclysms that have taken place, the IKK would be pleased with us. At least, all these years, both the editorial board and the editorial board, having undergone inevitable changes both in number and in composition, tried to do everything possible, and sometimes to the brink of the possible, so that the journal «Kvant» remained faithful to the principles that the IKK laid down in it.
