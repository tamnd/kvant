Is it so familiar to you?

?

jet propulsion

This question is quite appropriate now, when we celebrate the 50th anniversary of the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite - an event that marked the beginning of a new era, the era of human exploration of outer space. The fulfillment of a long-standing dream became possible thanks to the development of jet technology. Its long, millennium-long history has made an unusually rapid leap, moving from predictions and calculations to the direct implementation of the idea of ​​unsupported movement beyond the Earth. And here, without a doubt, we can be proud of the decisive contribution to the theory and practice of astronautics by domestic scientists, engineers and designers. Time passes, and achievements that seemed miraculous - the first satellite, the first manned flight around the Earth, the first spacewalk - become routine, repeated episodes many times over. Now people go to the orbital station both for work and even... for a tourist trip. However, new plans related to interplanetary flights, the prevention of asteroid danger, the construction of an industrial belt around the Earth and lunar bases, the improvement of satellite communications and the removal of astronomical instruments beyond the atmosphere seemed to have opened a second wind for astronautics. And none of its emerging areas can do without these unusual machines - rockets. You can not only get acquainted with many issues of jet propulsion, but also seriously understand them, relying on the well-known laws of mechanics. This is what we invite you to do today.

Questions and tasks

1. Will the Segner wheel shown at figure be able to rotate in a vacuum (for example, in very rarefied air under the bell of an air pump)? 2. From the platform of the rear carriage of a moving train, a person throws a stone against the movement of the train so that the stone falls vertically to the ground. What was the work done by the person during the throw, if as a result the stone lost the kinetic energy that it possessed while moving with the train? 3. A cart filled with sand rolls along the rails in a horizontal direction. Through a hole in the bottom, sand is poured between the rails. How does the speed of the cart change? 4. While jumping through a swamp, Baron Munchausen, noticing that he would not make it to the opposite shore, turned back right in the air “by an effort of will” and returned to the shore from which he jumped. Why is this “true case” impossible? 5. Can the following be attributed to jet propulsion: a) rollback of a gun when fired; b) the rise of a balloon after dropping ballast? 6. In what case could a gun, when fired, acquire greater speed than the projectile fired from it? 7. On a railway platform that can move along the rails, two identical cannons are mounted, the barrels of which are directed along the rails in opposite directions. When fired simultaneously, both projectiles hit their target. Will the shells hit their targets if one of the guns fires a little earlier than the other? 8. When launching a rocket mounted at the tail of the aircraft to protect against attack from behind, it was discovered that the projectile turned around and caught up with the aircraft. How can this be explained? 9. The astronaut, being in a state of weightlessness, “hung” inside the cabin of the Earth satellite. How can it turn around its longitudinal axis? 10. An astronaut is at some distance from the spacecraft, carrying two identical single-shot pistols. He can fire two pistols at the same time or use them in turn. What should an astronaut do to return to the ship faster? 11. The spacecraft moves around the Earth in such a way that it is always at the point where the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Earth compensate each other. Are the ship's engines running?

It's interesting that...

...the possibility of using the reactive power of a steam jet, at least in the form of a toy, was discovered back in the first century of the new era by Heron of Alexandria. And in 1750, the Hungarian scientist Janos Segner invented one of the first hydraulic jet turbines on this principle - the “Segner wheel”. Its effect today can be observed on lawns irrigated using nozzles rotating on water columns. ...powder rockets, known in China since the 11th century, were used not only for fireworks, but also in military affairs - as incendiary and explosive shells, and also as lighting means. However, truly combat rockets were created in 1817 by the Russian artillery scientist, General A.D. Zasyadko and were successfully used in the defense of Sevastopol in 1854–55 during the Crimean War. ...the recoil phenomenon that caused ancient guns to roll back was eventually used to reload firearms, for example in machine guns, automatic pistols and rapid-fire cannons. ...in the theory of multistage rockets developed by K.E. Tsiolkovsky in 1926, it was shown that the last stage of the rocket is capable of reaching the first escape velocity. It followed from the theory that it would be advisable to discard tanks, pipelines and engines of spent stages with fuel consumption, and ideally, to continuously get rid of the unnecessary mass of the rocket, which, alas, is not yet structurally feasible. ...the giant among multi-stage rockets still remains the Saturn 5, which launched the Apollo 11 spacecraft into orbit, delivering American astronauts to the Moon on July 20, 1969.

The launch mass of the system is 2950 tons, its height is 111 meters. ...in addition to powerful propulsion engines, rocket and space technology uses so-called detonation automation, which, with the help of explosive charges of “jewelry” weight and dimensions, solves the problem of instant separation of structural elements, cutting electrical cables, shooting parachutes and launching various devices. ...the atmosphere helps slow down the speed when landing a spacecraft on Earth: braking in it allows you to use a parachute at the final stage of descent. This possibility completely disappears when descending to the Moon - the absence of an atmosphere on it forces the speed to be reduced only by reactive impulses, and the last meters of the journey the device lands on a stream of gas from the nozzle. ...to be transported from continent to continent at a speed of over ten thousand kilometers per hour in rarefied layers of the atmosphere, to launch payloads from conventional airfields into low-Earth orbit must be new generation aircraft with hypersonic air-breathing engines, created today by foreign and domestic specialists. ...the answer to the unexpectedly high speeds of newborn super-dense neutron stars, reaching 1500 kilometers per second, can probably be a natural jet engine - the radiation of neutrinos, which carry away enormous energy and are capable of creating the necessary recoil impulse. ...ideal for interstellar flights would be a hypothetical rocket, the role of a gas jet in which would be played by a beam of photons, in other words, the speed of the “jet” would be equal to the speed of light. However, the power of such a rocket weighing only one ton when moving with an acceleration equal to g would have to exceed the power of a large power plant such as the Bratsk hydroelectric station by about 1000 times. ...all space powers are preparing the next breakthrough into space in the next fifteen years. This is, first of all, a series of lunar expeditions. Our neighbor is already being considered as a testing ground for technologies necessary to conquer Mars, as a base on the way to other planets, as a new astronomical observatory, and even... as an open-air museum for space tourists to visit.
