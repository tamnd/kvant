The stability of a geodesic manifold is greatly influenced by its “sectional curvatures along two-dimensional directions.” Namely, the negativity of curvature causes geodesics (with close initial conditions) to diverge exponentially over time. Euler's theory allows one to calculate these sectional curvatures (for groups with left-invariant or right-invariant metrics). By applying these calculations to groups of incompressible diffeomorphisms of surfaces, I obtained many two-dimensional directions of strongly negative curvature. For example, applying these estimates to two-dimensional hydrodynamics on the surface of a torus (and to trade wind-type flows), I was convinced that initially small disturbances of the initial velocity field grow by approximately $10^{5}$ times (from a kilometer-sized thunderstorm to planetary-scale weather changes) over a period of about a month. This means that dynamic weather forecasts for much longer than a week will remain impossible, no matter how much computers, calculation methods, and sensors that record the initial weather state are improved. Indeed, by slightly changing the initial velocities in each cubic kilometer (even so that the average over the neighboring ten cubic kilometers does not change), we will arrive at a new initial condition that the sensors will not distinguish from the old one, but which will lead the typhoon in a couple of weeks not to New Orleans, where it was supposed to go according to the old scenario, but, say, to Bombay. One can only be amazed at how significant the applications of Euler's fundamental theories and ideas turn out to be, even in those cases when he himself limited his presentation to the first meaningful case (the SO(3) group in our example), and all further generalizations were obtained only recently.

Attention to our readers!

March 28, 2008 marks the hundredth anniversary of the birth of the outstanding physicist, Academician Isaac Konstantinovich Kikoin. In this regard, numerous special events are planned in our country at a high state level. For us, Isaac Konstantinovich Kikoin was, first of all, the creator and first Editor-in-Chief of the magazine «Kvant». The editorial board, editorial council and editorial staff of the magazine «Kvant» declare the next year in the magazine «Kvant» to be “the year of Kikoin”. And this is what is meant by this. We plan to publish various memoirs about Academician I.K. Kikoin in the next six issues of the magazine, and in scientific articles and other materials of the magazine to pay special attention to those areas of physics and technology in which the scientific and organizational talent of this amazing person was revealed in an outstanding way, in particular - atomic and nuclear physics and energy. We are preparing to publish a special issue of the series “Library «Kvant»” dedicated to the anniversary of I.K. Kikoin. Those of us who knew Isaac Konstantinovich closely keep his unforgettable bright image in our souls with gratitude. We really want this image to be imprinted in the memory of new generations of our readers.
