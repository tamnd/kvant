Having resolved it, we will find ourselves face to face with a completely new picture and new tasks. This stage of research—the discovery of a paradox in the process of theoretical research and the search for a way out of it—is inherent in many physical problems, including some problems in modern physics. This prompted the author to recall the story described below. During my student years, I read the memoirs of a famous Russian mechanic. Unfortunately, the seven decades that have passed since then have erased the name of this scientist from memory. There is only one vivid episode left that he told. The author of the memoirs discovers an amazing thing - statics methods give in to such a simple task as calculating the forces of the legs of a table on which a weight rests, if the table has four legs. And this despite the fact that the problem of a table with three legs can be solved in an elementary way. What's the matter? Don't think that the mathematical problem of a four-legged table is so difficult that no one can solve it. On the contrary, the problem is elementary simple, and, as any mathematician will say, finding its complete solution is not difficult. However... Let's start solving the problem of a table with four legs. In addition to the forces of gravity (the table and the weights standing on it), four more forces act on the table, which prevent any of the four legs from falling through. These are support (floor) reactions applied to the table legs at the points of their contact with the floor. All these forces are directed vertically.
