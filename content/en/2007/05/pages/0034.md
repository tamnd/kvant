jet propulsion

This question is quite appropriate now, when we celebrate the 50th anniversary of the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite - an event that marked the beginning of a new era, the era of human exploration of outer space. The fulfillment of a long-standing dream became possible thanks to the development of jet technology. Its long, millennium-long history has made an unusually rapid leap, moving from predictions and calculations to the direct implementation of the idea of ​​unsupported movement beyond the Earth. And here, without a doubt, we can be proud of the decisive contribution to the theory and practice of astronautics by domestic scientists, engineers and designers. Time passes, and achievements that seemed miraculous - the first satellite, the first manned flight around the Earth, the first spacewalk - become routine, repeated episodes many times over. Now people go to the orbital station both for work and even... for a tourist trip. However, new plans related to interplanetary flights, the prevention of asteroid danger, the construction of an industrial belt around the Earth and lunar bases, the improvement of satellite communications and the removal of astronomical instruments beyond the atmosphere seemed to have opened a second wind for astronautics. And none of its emerging areas can do without these unusual machines - rockets. You can not only get acquainted with many issues of jet propulsion, but also seriously understand them, relying on the well-known laws of mechanics. This is what we invite you to do today.

Questions and tasks

1. Will the Segner wheel shown at figure be able to rotate in a vacuum (for example, in very rarefied air under the bell of an air pump)? 2. From the platform of the rear carriage of a moving train, a person throws a stone against the movement of the train so that the stone falls vertically to the ground. What was the work done by the person during the throw, if as a result the stone lost the kinetic energy that it possessed while moving with the train? 3. A cart filled with sand rolls along the rails in a horizontal direction. Through a hole in the bottom, sand is poured between the rails. How does the speed of the cart change? 4. While jumping through a swamp, Baron Munchausen, noticing that he would not make it to the opposite shore, turned back right in the air “by an effort of will” and returned to the shore from which he jumped. Why is this “true case” impossible? 5. Can the following be attributed to jet propulsion: a) rollback of a gun when fired; b) the rise of a balloon after dropping ballast? 6. In what case could a gun, when fired, acquire greater speed than the projectile fired from it? 7. On a railway platform that can move along the rails, two identical cannons are mounted, the barrels of which are directed along the rails in opposite directions. When fired simultaneously, both projectiles hit their target. Will the shells hit their targets if one of the guns fires a little earlier than the other? 8. When launching a rocket mounted at the tail of the aircraft to protect against attack from behind, it was discovered that the projectile turned around and caught up with the aircraft. How can this be explained? 9. The astronaut, being in a state of weightlessness, “hung” inside the cabin of the Earth satellite. How can it turn around its longitudinal axis? 10. An astronaut is at some distance from the spacecraft, carrying two identical single-shot pistols. He can fire two pistols at the same time or use them in turn. What should an astronaut do to return to the ship faster? 11. The spacecraft moves around the Earth in such a way that it is always at the point where the gravitational forces of the Moon and the Earth compensate each other. Are the ship's engines running?
