Competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

We continue our next competition on solving mathematical problems for students in grades 6–8. Send solutions to the problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» (marked “Competition “Mathematics 6–8”). Be sure to include your name, class, and home address. As before, we welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by mathematical clubs. We ask circle leaders to provide an email address or contact phone number. According to tradition, the winning clubs of the correspondence competition are invited to the final in-person tournament.

6. Holding hands, 64 dancers lead 4 circular dances with a common center. 16 people take part in one round dance - 8 boys and the same number of girls. The round dances rotate evenly, so that 4 dancers - one from each round dance - fall into each of the 16 identical sectors of the circle in time with the dance. Is it possible to distribute boys and girls in such a way that no matter in which direction the round dances circle, at any beat of the dance there is a sector containing 4 boys? K. Kaibhanov

9. The first 100 natural numbers are written in such a way that of each pair of numbers with a sum of 101, one number is written in red ink and the other in blue, and the sum of all red numbers is equal to the sum of all blue ones. The weight of a number of any color is the number of numbers of a different color that are less than this number. Prove that the sum of the squares of the weights of the red numbers is equal to the sum of the squares of the weights of the blue numbers. V.Proizvolov

Three Nuts of a Traveling Mathematician

N. SHILOV

WHAT DO MATHEMATICS DO DURING SCIENTIFIC TRIPS? Most often, they get acquainted with new scientific results and - more importantly! – with new methods for obtaining new scientific results. Well, what do mathematicians do while they are getting ready for a business trip, flying on planes or traveling on trains? For example, I come up with problems and really hope that someone will be interested in solving them. From my last trip from Novosibirsk to Frankfurt, I returned with three new “nuts” problems. Try to figure them out! In all problems we will assume that our home planet Earth is a ball.
