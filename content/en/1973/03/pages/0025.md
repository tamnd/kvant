Practice 4. Let the vectors **OA** and **AB** (Fig. 3) have complex coordinates *z*1 and *z*2, and the sum of these vectors (vector **OB**) have complex coordinates *z*. How can we express the number *z* through the numbers *z*1 and *z*2?

Solution. Let *z*1 = *a*1 + *b*1 *i*, *z*2 = *a*2 + *b*2 *i*. Then the projections of the vectors **OA** and **AB** onto the *Ox* axis are equal to *a*1 and *a*2, respectively, and the projection of their sum **OB** onto the *Oy* axis is *a*1 + *a*2. Similarly, it is shown that the projection of the vector **OB** onto the *Oy* axis is equal to *b*1 + *b*2. But then the complex coordinate *z* of the vector **OB** is equal to (*a*1 + *a*2) + *i* (*b*1 + *b*2), that is

*z* = *z*1 + *z*2.

So, when adding vectors, their complex coordinates are added.

In practice 5. Two points *Z*1 and *Z*2 of the coordinate plane *xOy* (Fig. 4) have complex coordinates *z*1 and *z*2. What complex coordinate does the vector *Z*1 *Z*2 have?

Solution. Let us denote by *z* the complex coordinate of the vector *Z*1 *Z*2. Since *OZ*2 = *OZ*1 + *Z*1 *Z*2, then *z*2 = *z*1 + *z*, whence

*z* = *z*2 — *z*1.

So, the complex coordinate of a vector is equal to the difference between the complex coordinate of its end and the complex coordinate of its beginning.
