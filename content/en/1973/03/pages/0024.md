⟦folio 22⟧

Imaginary numbers and geometric problems

G. D. Balk, M. B. Balk

What relation can square roots of negative numbers have to those given in the field of geometry? Is it possible for a person solving a planning problem to realize any benefit from the fact that in algebra numbers of the form $a + b\sqrt{-1}$ are considered?

The answer will be obvious: “It cannot.”

Meanwhile, it turns out that the use of such “minimum” numbers often exists and facilitates the solution of a difficult planning problem - even if in the conditions of such a problem there is no question of any “minimums”. This will be discussed in this article *).

It is assumed that teachers have initial ideas about complex numbers and operations with them (in algebraic form), say, in the volume of the school textbook by E. S. and E. S. Kochetkov “Algebra and Elementary Functions”, part 2, M., “Prosveshchenie”, 1969, pp. 216-220.

Below we'll provide more information about complex numbers and exercises, and then look at a number of geometry problems.
