etc. (figure out figure 3 how this scale works!). The lower part of figure 3 shows how to construct graphs of functions of the form (1) on two points $(t=0, y=y_0)$, $(t=1, y=y_0a)$ on semi-garified paper.

On semi-garified paper, the increment $\Delta t=1$ corresponds to 10 cm. We chose the same scale on figure 3 along the axis $y$. Therefore, the slope of our graphs is $k=\lg a$. This is nothing more than the relative rate of change of $y$ as $t$ changes:

$$\frac{1}{y} \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t} = \frac{y'}{y} = \lg a = k$$

(here $y'$ is the derivative function $y = f(t) = y_0a^t$). If the scale along the $y$ axis is chosen differently, then a recalculation is needed, which you can easily handle on your own.

Graphs on semi-garified paper are drawn when it is necessary to find out whether the dependence $y=f(t)$ specified by the table can be at least approximately considered to obey the law of exponential growth (or decrease).
