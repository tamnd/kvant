Ball collision

G. L. Komkin

Considering the elastic collision of balls, we will limit ourselves to only rough estimates, that is, when calculating the forces acting during the collision and the time of collision of the balls, we should not be subject to an error of several times.

The main thing is to correctly imagine what is happening to the balls. It is clear that after contact they are deformed. But even at the moment of stopping, the deformation is relatively large only by the size of the plane of contact of the balls. As a result of deformation, a force arises that tends to return the balls to their original state. This force, like the elastic force of a spring, throws the balls away from each other.

Let's try to determine the elastic force of this “spring”. Let us consider the central collision of two balls *). We learn that the region of deformation penetration is small, and we replace the highly deformed region of the ball with a cylinder, the radius of which is equal to the radius $r$ of the plane of contact of the balls and the height (that is, the depth of deformation penetration) is also equal to $r$ (Fig. 1).

Let the displacement of the center of the ball during the time from the moment the balls touch until they stop is equal to $x$. The elastic force of the “spring” is determined by Hooke’s law:

$$F = ES \frac{\Delta l}{l},$$

*) That is, one in which the centers of the balls move along a straight line connecting these centers.

where $E$ is Young’s modulus, $S = \pi r^2$, $l = r$, $\Delta l = x$. Thus,

$$F \sim Erx.$$

(Here the value $Er$ plays the role of the elasticity coefficient $k$). The sign ~ means equality in the order of magnitude, that is, with a possible error of several times.

Let us now express $r$ in terms of $x$. To do this, let us assume (again approximately) that the ball is “bending” only in the section $ABC$ (Fig. 2). From the similarity of triangles $ABD$ and $ADC$ the correspondence follows

$$\frac{r}{x} = \frac{2R - x}{r},$$

where $R$ is the radius of the ball. Since $x \ll R$ and celelem 2 can be neglected, we get

$$r \sim \sqrt{Rx}.$$

(1)

Substituting (1) into the expression for force $F$, we find

$$F \sim ER^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{3}{2}}$$
