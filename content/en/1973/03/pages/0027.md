(4'). We will prove this formula for natural numbers $n$ using the method of mathematical induction. For $n = 1$ formula (4'), very indo, is correct. Let (4') be valid for some $k$:

Let us show that in this case formula (4') is also true for the next number (that is, for $n = k + 1$). Indeed, by virtue of formulas (5) and (2') we have

$$(e^{i\varphi})^{k+1} = (e^{i\varphi})^k \cdot e^{i\varphi} = e^{i(k\varphi)} \cdot e^{i\varphi} = e^{i(k\varphi)+\varphi}.$$

So, from (5) follows (5'). According to the principle of mathematical induction, formula (4') is true for all natural $n$.

Let now $n$ be a negative integer; then $n = -m$, where $m$ is a natural number, and therefore $(e^{i\varphi})^m = e^{im\varphi}$.

Hence,

$$(e^{i\varphi})^n = (e^{i\varphi})^{-m} = \frac{1}{(e^{i\varphi})^m} = \frac{1}{e^{im\varphi}} = e^{-im\varphi} = e^{in\varphi},$$

that is, (4') is also true for all negative integers $n$ (for $n = 0$, as usual, $z^0 = 1$).

Formulas (2')—(4') allow one to perform arbitrary transformations with expressions of the form $\cos \varphi + i \sin \varphi$ as if they were ordinary powers.

Formula (1) is called Euler's formula, and formula (4') is called Moivre's formula *). Another notation of formula (4') is widely used:

$$(\cos \varphi + i \sin \varphi)^n = \cos n \varphi + i \sin n \varphi.$$

Euler's formula allows each complex number $z$ to be written compactly in the form $z = re^{i\varphi}$, where $\varphi$ is the argument of the number $z$, and $r$ is its model (exponential or exponential form of writing a compact number).
