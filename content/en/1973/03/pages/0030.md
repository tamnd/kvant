Exercise 13. Given a square OABC (Fig. 10). The vector OA has a complex coordinate $z=3-i$. Calculation of complex coordinates of vectors OB (we denote it by $w_1$), AC ($w_2$).

Solution. The vector OB is obtained from OA by rotating it through an angle of $+\frac{\pi}{4}$ radians and stretching it with a stretch factor of $\sqrt{2}$. That's why

$$w_1 = \sqrt{2} e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}} \cdot z = \sqrt{2} \left(\cos \frac{\pi}{4} + i \sin \frac{\pi}{4}\right) (3-i) = (1+i) (3-i) = 4+2i.$$

If the vector OB is rotated by $90^\circ$ in the positive direction, then we obtain a vector equal to the vector AC. Therefore $w_2 = iw_1 = i \cdot (4+2i) = -2+4i$.

Exercise 14. On figure 11 $Z_1C=CZ_2=CZ$, $CZ \perp Z_1Z_2$. Known complex coordinates $z_1$ and $z_2$ of points $Z_1$ and $Z_2$. Calculate complex coordinate $z$ power $Z$.

Solution. The vector OC has a complex coordinate $\frac{1}{2}(z_1+z_2)$. The CZ vector is obtained from the CZ$_2$ vector by rotating it by $+\frac{\pi}{4}$ radians. But the vector CZ$_2$ has a complex coordinate $\frac{1}{2}(z_2-z_1)$, so the complex coordinate of the vector CZ is $i \cdot \frac{1}{2}(z_2-z_1)$. From the equality OZ = OC + CZ we find $z = \frac{1}{2}(z_1+z_2) + \frac{1}{2}i(z_2-z_1)$.
