Problem 5. On the sides of the triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ (Fig. 13) look at the squares that do not have common internal lobes with the triangle; their centers $B_1, B_2, B_3$ are marked in the squares. Check the validity of the correspondence suggested by the drawing: $B_1B_2 = A_3B_3, B_1B_2 \perp A_3B_3$.

Solution. Let us choose a Cartesian coordinate system $xOy$ in the plane of the triangle. The complex coordinates of the point $A_1, A_2, A_3; B_1, B_2, B_3$ and the vectors $B_1B_2$ and $A_3B_3$ will be denoted by $a_1, a_2, a_3, b_1, b_2, b_3, z$ and $w$. Let's express $z$ and $w$ through $a_1, a_2, a_3$. We have $z = b_2 - b_1$, $w = b_3 - a_3$. Let's find $b_2$ (as in exercise 14):

$$b_2 = \frac{a_3 + a_1}{2} + i \frac{a_3 - a_1}{2}.$$

Likewise

$$b_3 = \frac{a_1 + a_2}{2} + i \frac{a_1 - a_2}{2},$$

$$b_1 = \frac{a_2 + a_3}{2} + i \frac{a_2 - a_3}{2}.$$

Now it is easy to calculate that

$$z = \frac{a_1 - a_2}{2} + i \frac{2a_3 - a_1 - a_2}{2},$$

$$w = \frac{a_2 + a_1 - 2a_3}{2} = i \frac{a_1 - a_2}{2}.$$

This shows that $w = iz$. This means that when we rotate the vector $B_1B_2$ by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise, we obtain a vector equal to the vector $A_3B_3$. But in this case $A_3B_3 = B_1B_2, A_3B_3 \perp B_1B_2$.

Problem 6. The vertices of two regular triangles on the plane are read counterclockwise. Vertices with the same numbers are connected by segments. Prove that the midpoints of these segments are the vertices of a regular triangle (a komor triangle can degenerate into a point).

Solution. Let $A_1A_2A_3$ and $B_1B_2B_3$ be the given triangles, $C_1, C_2, C_3$ the midpoints of the segments $A_1B_1, A_2B_2, A_3B_3$ and $a_k, b_k, c_k$ (where $k = 1, 2, 3$) are the complete coordinates of the nines under consideration. Then the vector $A_1A_3$ has the complex coordinate $a_3 - a_1$, and the vector $A_1A_2$ has the complex coordinate $a_2 - a_1$. Since the triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ is regular, the vector $A_1A_3$ can be obtained from the vector $A_1A_2$ by rotating it by $\frac{\pi}{3}$ radians counterclockwise, so

$$a_3 - a_1 = e^{i \frac{\pi}{3}} (a_2 - a_1).$$

Likewise

$$b_3 - b_1 = e^{i \frac{\pi}{3}} (b_2 - b_1).$$

Since $C_1$ is the midpoint of the segment $A_1B_1$, then

$$c_1 = \frac{1}{2} (a_1 + b_1).$$

Likewise

$$c_2 = \frac{1}{2} (a_2 + b_2), c_3 = \frac{1}{2} (a_3 + b_3).$$

From the obtained relations it follows that $c_3 - c_1 = e^{i \frac{\pi}{3}} (c_2 - c_1)$. But this means that the vector $C_1C_3$ is obtained from the vector $C_1C_2$ by rotating it by an angle of $+\frac{\pi}{3}$ radians, that is, $\triangle C_1C_2C_3$ is correct.
