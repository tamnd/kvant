Solution. The vectors $Z_1Z$ and $ZZ_2$ are equal, and therefore they have the same complex coordinate; let's denote it by $c$. Since $OZ = OZ_1 + Z_1Z$ and $OZ_2 = OZ + ZZ_2$, then $z = z_1 + c$, $z_2 = z + c$.

Let us recall the concept of modulus and argument of a complex number. If the number $z = a + bi$ is the complex coordinate of the vector $AB$ (Fig. 1), then the length $r$ of this vector is called the modulum of the complex number $z$ (denoted as follows: $|z|$). Euler's formula and Myabra's formula
An expression of the form $\cos \varphi + i \sin \varphi$ appears frequently in mathematics and its applications. Various abbreviations are used for it. For example, in potatoes it is denoted by the sign $1_\varphi$, and in works on mathematics - by $exp(i\varphi)$ or $e^{i\varphi}$. Thus, by definition

$$e^{i\varphi} = \cos \varphi + i \sin \varphi.$$

Rule 10. What complex numbers are given by the expressions:

$$e^{i\cdot 0}; e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}; e^{i\frac{\pi}{3}}; e^{i(-\frac{\pi}{4})}?$$

Notation (1) is determined by the similarity in the properties of expressions of the form $e^x$ (a power with a real exponent $x$) and expressions of the form $e^{i\varphi}$. In fact, it is known that for real $x$, $x_1$ and integer $n$ the following formulas are true:

$$e^x \cdot e^{x_1} = e^x + x_1$$

$$e^{-x} = \frac{1}{e^x},$$

$$(e^x)^n = e^{nx}.$$

In other words, with a variable degree with the same base, the indicators are added; When any power is raised to a whole power, the indicators change. For example, for an expression of the form $e^{i\varphi}$, similar formulas (below $\varphi$, $\varphi_1$ are arbitrary real numbers, $n$ are any integer $^*$):

$$e^{i\varphi} \cdot e^{i\varphi_1} = e^{i(\varphi + \varphi_1)}$$

$$e^{-i\varphi} = \frac{1}{e^{i\varphi}},$$

$$(e^{i\varphi})^n = e^{in\varphi}.$$

Are these equalities true? It turns out yes. You can verify this by using the formulas for adding trionometric functions. Validity of formulas $(2') - (4')$ and definition, introduction of notation (1).

Let us prove the formulas $(2') - (4')$.

$$e^{i\varphi} \cdot e^{i\varphi_1} = (\cos \varphi + i \sin \varphi) (\cos \varphi_1 + i \sin \varphi_1) = (\cos \varphi \cdot \cos \varphi_1 - \sin \varphi \cdot \sin \varphi_1) + i (\sin \varphi \cdot \cos \varphi_1 + \cos \varphi \cdot \sin \varphi_1) = \cos (\varphi + \varphi_1) + i \sin (\varphi + \varphi_1) = e^{i(\varphi + \varphi_1)}$$.

*) Records of the form $e^{-i\varphi}$, $e^{\varphi i}$, $e^{ni\varphi}$ should, naturally, be understood as $e^{i(-\varphi)}$, $e^{i(n\varphi)}$.
