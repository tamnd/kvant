⟦folio 2⟧

1. In the 12th issue of «Kvant» for 1972, an article by “Exponent” was published. It shows that many quantities change over time in such a way that the rate of their change is proportional to the value they have already achieved. The general form of this indicator of dependence $y$ on $t$ is written by the formula

$$y = y_0 a^t,$$

(1)

where $y_0$ is the value of $y$ at $t = 0$. Putting

$$k = \lg a, \quad z = \lg y, \quad b = \lg y_0,$$

from (1) we obtain $z = kt + b$.

We see that the dependence of $z$ on $t$ is linear. Its graph is a straight line. Having drawn this graph in the usual way, we can mark the corresponding values ​​$y = 10^z$ on the basis of $(n)$ or on a line parallel to it). Then, according to our straight-line graph, it will be possible to directly read the values ​​of $y$ for any given $t$. At figure 1, this graphical representation method is applied to functions $y = 100^t$ and $y = e^t$ ($e = 2,718 \ldots$). Their graphs in the Cartesian coordinate system are shown on figure 2.
