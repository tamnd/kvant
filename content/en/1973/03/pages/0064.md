To the article “Collision of balls”
1. $t \sim 10^{-3}c, F \sim 10^5H$
2. The pressure $p$ inside the ball remains almost unchanged. Strength $F = \pi r^2 p = 2\pi Rxp$, that is, stiffness coefficient $k = 2\pi Rp$. According to formula (5) $t = \pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi Rp}} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi m}{2Rp}}$. For a soccer ball, $m \approx 0,4$ kts, $R \approx 10$ cm, $p = 1amm$, so $t \approx 10^{-2}c$.
3. When a steel and rubber ball collide, the rubber ball is deformed significantly more. The collision time of rubber and steel balls is much longer than the collision time of steel balls at the same speed. Therefore, after a collision, the steel balls move approximately as if we gently pushed them with our finger, that is, at the same speeds. Using the laws of conservation of momentum and energy
$$mv_0 = mv_1 + 2mv_2,$$
$$\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_1^2 + mv_2^2,$$
where $v_1$ is the speed of the rubber ball after the collision, $v_2$ is the speed of the steel balls, we find
$$v_1 = -\frac{1}{3}v_0, v_2 = \frac{2}{3}v_0.$$
