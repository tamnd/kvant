⟦folio 20⟧

At the moment the ball stops, kinetic energy always turns into potential deformation energy. The potential energy of deformation is equal to the work of the elastic force on the path $x$:

$$U \sim Fx \sim ER^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{5}{2}}.$$

This means $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = U \sim ER^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{5}{2}}$. But the mass of the ball is $m = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\rho$. Thus,

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{2\pi}{3}R^3\rho v^2 \sim R^3\rho v^2.$$

Hence,

$$R^3\rho v^2 \sim ER^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{5}{2}},$$

where

$$x \sim R \frac{\rho \frac{2}{5}v^{\frac{4}{5}}}{E^{\frac{2}{5}}} = R \left(\frac{\rho v^2}{E}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}}.$$

It is convenient to denote $\frac{E}{\rho} = u^2$), then $x \sim R\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{\frac{4}{5}}$. Let's substitute the found value $x$ into formula (2):

$$F \sim ER^2\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{\frac{6}{5}}.$$

Let's estimate the collision time of the balls:

$$t \sim \frac{x}{v} \sim \frac{R}{u}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}$$

(4)

*) The quantity introduced in this way coincides in order with the speed of sound in the substance of the ball. For example, for steel $E = 2;1.10^{11}h/m^2$, $\rho = 7,8.10^3\kappa_2/m^3$, $u = 5,2.10^3m/c$, the speed of sound in steel is about $6.10^3m/c$.

**) A more accurate, but much more complex calculation gives for $t$ a value approximately twice as large, and for $F$ - somewhat less.

Let two steel balls of radius $R = 1cm$ collide at a speed $v = 5cm/c$ (the mass of such a ball is ~30 $e$). Using formulas (4) and (3) we obtain

$$t \sim \frac{10^{-2}}{5 \cdot 10^{-3}}\left(\frac{5 \cdot 10^3}{5 \cdot 10^{-2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}c \sim 2 \cdot 10^{-5}c,$$

$$F \sim 2 \cdot 10^{11} \cdot 10^{-4}\left(\frac{5 \cdot 10^{-2}}{5 \cdot 10^{-3}}\right)^{\frac{6}{5}}n \sim 20n.$$

Note that the higher the speed of the balls, the shorter the time of their assistance.

There is another way to estimate the time of judging. Following the analogy with the spring, we will assume that the time of impact of the balls corresponds to the half-period of oscillation of the ball of mass $m$ on the stiffness spring $k \sim Er$, that is

$$t \sim \frac{T}{2} = \pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}},$$

(5)

The half-period is less, the greater the rigidity *).

Our estimate is not applicable if the ball speed is too high. In this case, the deformations are so great that Hooke’s law ceases to apply. For steel, Hooke's law is valid at relative strains $\frac{\Delta l}{l} \leq 10^{-2}$. From this condition we can determine the maximum collision speed of the balls. The greatest deformation of the balls (“springs”)

$$\frac{\Delta l}{l} \sim \frac{x}{r} \sim \left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}} \leq 10^{-2},$$

where

$$v \leq 10^{-5}u \sim 5 \cdot 10^{-2}m/c = 5cm/c.$$

One can make a similar assessment of the time and force of interaction when two identical cylinders of length $l$ and radius $R$ collide at the ends. In this case, the region of relative greater deformation extends to the volume of each cylinder.
