M191. On the plane, given are two points A and B and a line l passing through point A but not through point B. An arbitrary circle is drawn through points A and B. Let O be its center, C its point of intersection with line l, different from A. Find the locus of the midpoint of the segments OC.
P. Paramonov, 9th grade student

M192. Given numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., 1000. Find the most m that have the following property: no matter which m numbers are crossed out, among the remaining 1000 - m numbers there will be two, of which one is divisible by the other.

M193*. Prove that the sum of the areas of the heel of the triangles formed by the parameter and the diagonals of a convex pentagon (see Fig. 1) is greater than the area of ​​the entire pentagon.
