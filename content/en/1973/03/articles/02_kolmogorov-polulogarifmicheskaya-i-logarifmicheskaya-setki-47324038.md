1. In the 12th issue of «Kvant» for 1972, an article by “Exponent” was published. It shows that many quantities change over time in such a way that the rate of their change is proportional to the value they have already achieved. The general form of this indicator of dependence $y$ on $t$ is written by the formula

$$y = y_0 a^t,$$

(1)

where $y_0$ is the value of $y$ at $t = 0$. Putting

$$k = \lg a, \quad z = \lg y, \quad b = \lg y_0,$$

from (1) we obtain $z = kt + b$.

We see that the dependence of $z$ on $t$ is linear. Its graph is a straight line. Having drawn this graph in the usual way, we can mark the corresponding values ​​$y = 10^z$ on the basis of $(n)$ or on a line parallel to it). Then, according to our straight-line graph, it will be possible to directly read the values ​​of $y$ for any given $t$. At figure 1, this graphical representation method is applied to functions $y = 100^t$ and $y = e^t$ ($e = 2,718 \ldots$). Their graphs in the Cartesian coordinate system are shown on figure 2.

Polygonal paper is sold in stores. On this paper, vertical lines are drawn at intervals of 1 mm, as on ordinary graph paper, while horizontal lines are drawn so that their distance from the bottom edge is 100 lgy, $y$ takes the values

$$1 \quad 1,05 \quad 1,10 \quad 1,15 \ldots$$

$$2 \quad 2,1 \quad 2,2 \quad 2,3 \ldots$$

⟦folio 4⟧

etc. (figure out figure 3 how this scale works!). The lower part of figure 3 shows how to construct graphs of functions of the form (1) on two points $(t=0, y=y_0)$, $(t=1, y=y_0a)$ on semi-garified paper.

On semi-garified paper, the increment $\Delta t=1$ corresponds to 10 cm. We chose the same scale on figure 3 along the axis $y$. Therefore, the slope of our graphs is $k=\lg a$. This is nothing more than the relative rate of change of $y$ as $t$ changes:

$$\frac{1}{y} \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t} = \frac{y'}{y} = \lg a = k$$

(here $y'$ is the derivative function $y = f(t) = y_0a^t$). If the scale along the $y$ axis is chosen differently, then a recalculation is needed, which you can easily handle on your own.

Graphs on semi-garified paper are drawn when it is necessary to find out whether the dependence $y=f(t)$ specified by the table can be at least approximately considered to obey the law of exponential growth (or decrease).
