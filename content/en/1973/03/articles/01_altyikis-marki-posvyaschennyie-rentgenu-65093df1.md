February 10, 1973 marked the 50th anniversary of the death of the outstanding German physicist Wilhelm Conrad Roentgen (1845-1923). He was one of the best experimenters of his time. Many of the records he produced for the accuracy of measurements of various physical characteristics of matter have retained their significance to this day.

X-ray belongs to classical studies satisfying the heat capacity of gases, the skimability and vaporization of liquids, the relationship of electrical and optical phenomena in crystals. But what brought him most fame was his discovery in 1895 of invisible short-wave electromagnetic radiation called RENT RAYS. Together with the phenomenon of radioactivity, discovered a little later, RENTNOVSKIY RAYS LAID THE FUNDAMENTALS OF atomic physics. They have found many useful applications in medicine, physics and technology. For this outstanding discovery, Roentgen was awarded the first Nobel Prize in Physics on December 10, 1901.

X-ray was a very modest person. He was not green about any transactions with his property and died in poverty, abandoning the money that he could have made with his great discovery. He also responded with a proposal to award him a dual title. It should be said that the first monument to Roentgen was erected not in his homeland, but here in Leningrad during the scientist’s lifetime (in 1920).

The first stamp with a portrait of Roentgen was issued in 1939 by the undulating city of Danzig. This is the brand you see in the photo. Next to the portrait is the inscription: “Fight against cancer. We can cure cancer." Then stamps with Roentgen's portrait are issued repeatedly. Thus, in 1950, Surina (Dutch Guiana) issued two postage stamps with a portrait of Roentgen. These stamps had an additional denomination (to fund the fight against cancer). In 1951, in connection with the 50th anniversary of Roentgen. Nobel Prize, a stamp was issued in Germany with the image of a bust of the scientist. We see X-ray on three Swedish stamps of the average scientists who received the Nobel Prize in 1901 (one of them is shown in the photo). The photo also shows stamps with a portrait of Roentgen, issued in 1965 in the GDR and in 1967 in Spain (this stamp is associated with the VII European RENTN Congress in Barcelona).
