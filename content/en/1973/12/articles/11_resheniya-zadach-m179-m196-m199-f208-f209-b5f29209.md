M179. In «Kvant» No. 8, 1973, the solution to problem M179 was published. At the same time, we invited readers to complete the solution of the problem on their own. We hope you did and can compare your solution with ours.

Let us recall the formulation of the problem.

For each triangle $T$ a sequence of triangles is constructed
$$T_0 = T, T_1, T_2, T_3, \dots,$$
food $T_n$ is a triangle whose vertices are the bases of the altitudes of triangle $T_{n-1}$. We need to find out how many pairwise dissimilar friends of friends of triangles $T$ exist such that $T_n$ is similar to $T$ (for each fixed $n$).

Let us denote by $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma), (\alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1), \dots, (\alpha_n, \beta_n, \gamma_n)$ the angles of the triangle $T$ and the corresponding triangles $T_1, \dots, T_n$. We need to find out how many tracks $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ there are such that the track $(\alpha_n; \beta_n, \gamma_n)$ coincides with $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ or is obtained from it by a permutation, and $\alpha \geqslant \beta \geqslant \gamma$.

Triples $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ and their transformations
$$\begin{align*}
(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) &\rightarrow (\alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1), (\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \rightarrow \\
h^n &\rightarrow (\alpha_n, \beta_n, \gamma_n)
\end{align*}$$

It is convenient to imagine the geometric ones as follows:

Let's draw a triangle $Y$, for which at each height is equal to $\pi$, and we will draw a trio of non-negative numbers $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$, for which $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = \pi$, by a point $y$ inside this triangle, the distances from which to the side are equal to the corresponding $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ (Fig. 1). This correspondence
$$(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \rightarrow y$$

between the tracks $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ and the points $y \in Y$ will be one-to-one. Permutations of numbers in the trike $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ correspond to the movements of the triangle, combining it with itself (turns and symmetries with respect to heights). Recall that the transformation $h$ is described geometrically as follows. On the midlines of the triangle $Y$ (points $y$ of the midlines correspond to right triangles $T$) $h$ is not defined, and each of the four triangles into which the midlines divide the triangle $Y$, $h$ maps onto the entire $Y$, and this mapping is a similarity transformation with a coefficient 2, which transforms each segment into a segment parallel to it. Here, by indication of $n$, we find that the transformation $h^n$ is arranged similarly: on straight lines parallel to the sides and dividing the heights into $2^n$ equal parts, $h^n$ is not defined; in each of the $2^{2n}$ triangles into which these lines are divided $Y$, $h^n$ - a similarity transformation with a coefficient $2^n$, mapping this triangle onto the entire $Y$. (On figure 2 $h^2$ is depicted.) Conditions $\alpha \geqslant \beta \geqslant \gamma$ select one of the six triangles on which the triangle $Y$ of its altitude is divided. This one is straight

we denote the angle triangle by $Y_1$, the rest - as shown in figure 3 - we denote the corresponding triangle by $Y_2$, ..., $Y_6$. Let $f_j$: $Y - Y$ be the displacement $Y$ such that $f_j(Y_j) = Y_1$. If each of the $2^{2n}$ small triangles, which are mapped by the transformation $h^n$ onto the entire triangle $Y$, is divided by heights into six triangles, then $Y_1$ will be divided into $2^{2n}$ similar (with a coefficient $2^n$) triangles, which we denote by $X_j$, $1 \leq j \leq 2^{2n}$ (Fig.4). After mapping $h^n$, each $X_i$ will go into some $Y_j$, and if you then rearrange the numbers in the same ($\alpha_n, \beta_n, \gamma_n$) in descending order - apply $f_j$, then the product of two transformations $f_j \cdot h^n$ maps $X_i$ to $Y_1$ (similarity transformation with coefficient $2^n$).

We claim that in each of the $2^{2n}$ triangles $X_i \subset Y_1$ there is exactly one “fixed point” $y_i$, as well as $y_i \in X_i$ and $f_j(h^n(y_i)) = y_i$. From the answer it is also necessary to exclude the fixed points of those triangles $X_i$ that have more category $Y_1$ (there are exactly a column of them, what is the similarity coefficient), since for these (and as is easy to see, only for these) triangles, the “fixed point” falls on the boundary where the mapping $h^n$ is not defined. It immediately follows that the answer in our problem is $2^{2n} - 2^n$. (On figure 4 in each of the 12 head triangles there is one point ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$), for which ($\alpha_n, \beta_n, \gamma_n$) after rearrangement in descending order coincides with ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$).)

All that remains is to prove our statement. To do this, we apply the following theorem to the triangle $Y_1$ and the map $p$ of this triangle onto $X_i \subset Y_1$, inverse to $f_j h^n$.

Theorem. Let $Z$ be a non-similar figure on the plane (and all boundaries of the point $Z$ are considered to belong to $Z$), $p: Z \rightarrow Z$ be a similarity transformation with coefficient $k < 1$. Then there is exactly one “fixed point” of the transformation $p$ belonging to $Z$, that is, there is exactly one $z_0 \in Z$ for which $p(z_0) = z_0$.

The proof of the theorem for the bounded figure $Z$ (namely, this is the case we need) is easiest to check this way. Let $Z_n = p^n(Z)$ (the result of $n$ applying $p$ to $Z$). Then $Z \supset Z_1 \supset Z_2 \supset Z_3 \supset \dots$ and the intersection of all $Z_n$ consists of a single point $z_0$ (Fig. 5). This point will be the fixed point of the transformation $p$.

Another proof that makes it possible to explicitly indicate the fixed point and is suitable for unbounded figures $Z$ can be obtained using complex numbers. Any transformation of the similarity of a plane can be written as the result of a sequential rotation, stretching by $k$ times, parallel transfer: $z| \rightarrow az + b$ and, perhaps, even previously symmetrically with respect to a straight line: $z| \rightarrow az + b$ (here $a$ and $b$ are complex numbers, $|a| = k$, $z = x - iy$ - conjugation $k = z + iy$; see article “Imaginary numbers and geometric problems”, «Kvant» No. 3, 1973, p. 22). In the first case, the fixed point is found as a solution to the equation equation.

$$z_0 = az_0 + b > z_0 = \frac{b}{1 - a} = \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - k^2},$$

in the second - as a solution to a system of equations

$$\begin{cases} z_0 = \bar{a}z_0 + b \\ \bar{z}_0 = \bar{a}z_0 + \bar{b} \end{cases} > z_0 = \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - \bar{a}},$$

$$= \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - k^2}.$$

F208. At the end of automation, the tire bursts. Estimate at what speed the examb automation should be used so that the tire does not wrinkle.

We will assume that the vehicle moves uniformly and without slipping, and consider what forces act on the section of the tire of one wheel that is in contact with the road: In the inertial coordinate system associated with the center of the wheel, this section of the tire moves along a circle of radius $R$ (wheel radius) with a linear speed equal in magnitude to the speed of the vehicle v. The centripetal acceleration of this section is $a_u = \frac{v^2}{R}$.

What forces act on such a section of the tire (let’s call it “leading”)? If we ignore the deformation of the tire and the forces associated with it, then there is only one force in the leading area of ​​action - the road reaction force $N$.

(The force mg acting on this area is much less than $N$, and it can be avoided).

Since conventional automatics have 4 wheels,

then this force (on average) is equal to $N = \frac{1}{4} Mg$,

where $M$ is the automatic mass. The acceleration imparted to the leading section of the tire of mass $m$ by the force $N$ is the centripetal acceleration, that is

$$\frac{1}{4} Mg = m \frac{v^2}{R}.$$

(1)

Since the left side of equality (1) was constructed (for a given machine gunner), then the conditions $R =$ const (the condition of the tire’s invariability) and, as a consequence, $m =$ const uniquely determine the required speed:

$$v = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{M}{m} g R}.$$

(2)

To obtain a quantitative assessment, we substitute the following values ​​into this formula: $g = 10 \, m/c^2$, $R = 0,5 \, m$, $M = 2 \cdot 10^3 \, \text{кг}$. The value of $m$ needs to be estimated. The tire weighs about 30 kg, and a small section of it collides with the road. We will assume that its length is $l = 10 \, \text{см}$. Then

$$m = m_{\text{ш}} \cdot \frac{l}{2\pi R} \approx 30 \cdot \frac{10}{2 \cdot 3,14 \cdot 0,5} \approx 1 \ (\text{кг})$$

(we assume that the radius of the tire is small compared to the radius of the wheel).

Now substituting all these quantities into formula (2), we obtain

$$v = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{2000}{1} \cdot 10 \cdot 0,5} = 50 \, m/c = 180 \, \text{км/ч}.$$

For geological automation, this speed is quite low.

F210. Two identical open connections with two identical tubes and filled to the top with water. The tubes are closed with taps $K_1$ and $K_2$ (Fig. 12). The temperature of the water in the vessels is maintained constant, and $t_1^\circ > t_2^\circ > 4^\circ$ C. What will happen to the water in the vessels if first the tap $K_2$ is open, and then (with the tap $K_2$ open) the tap $K_1$ is open?

After opening tap $K_2$ the vessels will become communicating. Therefore, such water levels will be established in them at which the pressures at the lower points of the vessels will be the same (the content would flow from one vessel to another):

$$\rho_1 g h_1 = \rho_2 g h_2$$

($\rho_1, \rho_2$ - density of water in vessels with temperatures corresponding $t_1^\circ$ and $t_2^\circ$, $h_1$ and $h_2$ - corresponding water levels).

Since $t_1^\circ > t_2^\circ > 4^\circ$ C, then $\rho_1 < \rho_2$. This means that $h_1 > h_2$, that is, water will flow from the right vessel to the left and part of it will be thrown out.

What will happen to the water when tap $K_1$ is opened depends on the correspondence between the water pressures in the vessels at the level of the tube. And since the pressure is linearly related to the height of the level and $h_1 > h_2$, then at any level $0 < h < h_1$ the pressure in the left vessel is greater than in the right (Fig. 13). Therefore, $p_1(h_0) > p_2(h_0)$. Therefore, when we open the tap $K_1$, water begins to flow through the upper tube from the left vessel to the right. In this case, the pressure in the right vessel will increase, and in the left - decrease. Thanks to this, a flow of water from the right vessel to the left will appear in the lower tube.

This task is directly related to technical issues. Water heating batteries are connected to one tube, and yet the water circulates through such a battery, and does not just flow through the pipe without entering the battery.
