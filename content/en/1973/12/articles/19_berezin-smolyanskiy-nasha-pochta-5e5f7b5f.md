Our mail

This year, 1973, Quanta's mail continued to grow. So, in the first 5 months of this year, we received 1.7 times more letters (4 thousand) than in the same period of time last year.

It is pleasant to note that the calculation of the number of letters received from rural schoolchildren. Their total number is three times the number of letters received from Moscow schoolchildren.

As before, the lion's share of letters (more than 75% of all correspondence) falls on the “Kvant Problem Book”. Almost all of the authors of the solutions to the problems in this section that we received are schoolchildren (although there are also workers who want to continue their education or simply learn more about mathematics and physics; there are also students who became interested in solving the problem from the “Kvant Problem Book” while still schoolchildren and remained devoted to it, engineers and other lovers of mathematics and physics), so we can assume that the problems find exactly those readers to whom they are addressed. Already by their active and regular participation in the work of the magazine, these readers inform us: the tasks attract them, they can use and use “Quant” as their own imaginative self-teacher. We find evidence of this in the letters of some readers and in the responses to the questionnaire. For example, tenth-grader Mikhail Ozhigov from the Ponyri station in the Kursk region writes to us that he regularly solves problems from the “Kvant Problem Book”, although he does not send solutions. Tenth-grader Andrei Sofrin from Leningrad writes: “Task respect is systematic. At least they look at everything. I compare my solution with those given.” Tenth-class student Sergei Belolipetsky from the city of Kirzhach, Vladimir region, agrees with him.

Not everyone, of course, immediately selects the answers to the rather difficult and sometimes complex problems from Kvant’s “Problem Book”. Increase in one’s powers does not come immediately. Thus, ninth-graders Andrei Pogulyai from Ufa and Elena Kalyagina from Grozny Pshut that they began to systematically solve problems from this section only last year. Not everyone has the time, experience, or passion to slip into task conditions. When you have to give all your strength to successfully graduate from high school and prepare to enter a higher educational institution. But even among those readers to whom the problems from Quant’s “Problem Book” still seem too tough a nut to crack, many benefit from them by getting acquainted with the natural solutions in the magazine (for example, tenth grader Aleksadr Bulanovich from the village of Kosino, Moscow Region, wrote about this). Some readers, mainly those who have already graduated from high school, give explicit preference to problems in physics, like Andrei Nevolin of Moscow, others, like his fellow countryman Mikhail Zhukov, to problems in mathematics.

The Quanta Problem Book also helps the reader in working with schoolchildren. For example, mathematics teacher L.S. Kotikova from the city of Nelidovo, Kalinin region, writes about this. Mathematics teacher N.A. Vokhmyanina from the city of Kotelnitsa, Kirov region and reader O.T. Orlovsky from the city of Krasnoirskaya Pshut, who uses the material from the “Kvant Problem Book” in elective classes.

What attracts the reader to the problems from the Quantum Problem Book? Of course, the prospect of taking part in an Olympiad also plays an important role for a student, for which he becomes eligible if he succeeds in the Quantum Problem Book competition. But the main thing is in the backs themselves and their features. Quanta’s “Problem Book” contains, as a rule, problems proposed at the Olympiad or “Olympid-type” problems. The procedure for solving such a problem requires the student to actively and inventively use minimal knowledge about the conditions of the problem. In order for the solution to appear after it is found, it is necessary to view the successful one. At the same time, the readers of “Kvant” find themselves in a much larger position than the participants of the Olympiad, who must complete the task within a short time frame. The reader has more time to solve problems.

The problems from the “Quanta Problem Book” are compiled with the aim of helping the reader of the magazine cultivate in themselves the qualities that are necessary for active scientific work. These qualities include ingenuity, that is, a combination of restraint in guessing and modesty in conclusions, a love of scientific findings and the ability to work independently. Problems are usually posed in such a way that they allow several approaches to solution, which helps to show the student’s inductiveness and contributes to the development of certain tasks, taste and style in the method of work. Finally, the freshness of the solutions obtained and the romance of overcoming difficulties, combined with the naturalness of the results, are necessary.

The activity of readers can be seen in the fact that more and more often they are sending their own problems and small studies to the editorial office. In mathematics, for example, in the first five months of the new year there are about 240 such letters.

This year, many readers have given us their opinions on the design of «Kvant». The overwhelming majority responded to the questions - what are your comments and suggestions regarding the design of the magazine? - expressed satisfaction. Docent V.E. Stepan from the Krasnoyarsk Polytechnic Institute writes: “The magazine has its own appearance, which is different from other popular science magazines. In my opinion, the example should be presented in this form in the future.” Moscow State University student Valery Grobovsky believes: “The magazine is designed with taste (it does not turn into either a series of reports or decorated paintings) and with some, one might say, flavor and fantasy.” Here is the opinion of two schoolchildren. Ninth-grader Vladimir Khasanshin writes: “The magazine is designed in an original way. It would be nice if there were funny ones, but they didn’t take up too much space.” Ninth-grader Lydia Bashkatava from Voronezh believes: “Only positive things can be said about the design of the magazine. All statements are clear and understandable. It is believed that multicolor plays a big role in this.” Opinion of mathematics teachers N. Ya. Bushmina from the village. Out of more than four questionnaires from readers of questionnaires, only two contain dissatisfaction with the design. Thus, physics teacher V. A. The letters and answers to questions contain many wishes, which are edited to take into account and for which the reader is very grateful.
