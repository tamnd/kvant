The last formula does not depend on the direction of operation of the reverse heat engine, that is, it remains valid for a heat pump (although it is inappropriate to talk about efficiency in this case).

For irreversible engines
$$\eta = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1} \text{ является верхним пределом, то есть}$$
$$\eta = \frac{Q_1 - Q_2}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{Q_2}{Q_1} \leq 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1},$$
whence it follows that in irreversible engines
$$\frac{Q_2}{T_2} > \frac{Q_1}{T_1}.$$

From the old dictionary

In 1842-1843. Four volumes of the “Dictionary of the Church Slavonic and Russian Language”, compiled by the Academy of Sciences*, were published. It is interesting and instructive to see how physical announcements were used in those years; After all, physics has changed its appearance very much over the past 100 years, so many things may now seem curious. But we must not forget that physics developed rapidly in the first half of the last century and the explanations that we will talk about have been reached in recent years.

So let's see what heat is. The dictionary says: “... The same as caloric, caloric.” Let's look: “Telloride is the material cause of heat, warmth and cold; an unacceptably thin liquid pouring out from the Sun and permeating the entire physical world, invisible, imperceptible and only erroneous by perception.”

Lomonosov also explained heat by movement; philosophers of antiquity and the Middle Ages also spoke about this; but when they began to try to attribute a quantitative measure to heat, to talk about the transfer of heat from one body to another, then by the middle of the 18th century they came to the theory of floristic-therpor: the idea of ​​heat as a liquid, described above. Only after it was discovered that heat can be converted into other types of energy (the word “energy” is not yet in the dictionary!) and that bodies consist of moving atoms and molecules, did physicists abandon thermorad.
