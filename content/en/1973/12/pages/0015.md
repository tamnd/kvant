But what is most often used is the fact that the internal energy of an ideal gas (that is, essentially, the kinetic energy of the xaotic movement of its molecules) under certain conditions externally manifests itself as the potential energy of elastic deformation: a gas rolled up in a cylinder by a piston is quite similar to a rolled spring, it can do work (when we were talking about the impossibility *directly* of obtaining work from internal energy, only solid and liquid bodies were meant).

Let's try to mentally construct the simplest thermal engine, the working fluid of which is an ideal gas. Let the rolled gas be in the cylinder under the piston (Fig. 3). If the piston is able to move, the gas expands and does work.

When the gas is thermally conductive (that is, surrounded by a heat-conducting shell), it cools during adnabatic expansion, and work is done due to its internal energy ($A = -\Delta U$). But if the walls of the cylinder are held well enough

heat is generated, and the cylinder itself is in a thermostat, then the temperature, and therefore the internal energy of the gas, is maintained constant - the work is done due to the supplied heat ($A_1 = q_1$). Here there is already a transformation of heat into work, but this is not yet thermal movement, since there is no cyclicity in its work.

To return the gas to its initial state, it must be done. But this will require the expenditure of exactly the same work $A_1$ that was obtained during expansion, and will lead to the return of the heat $q_1$ previously received from it to the thermostat. To avoid this, the gas should be cooled before compression. For the same volume, the pressure of the cold gas is lower; so the compression process will require less work ($A_2 < A_1$) and will return less heat $q_2 = A_2$ to the thermostat. To cool the gas, thermal contact is established between the cylinder and a second thermostat with a lower temperature. Having compressed the gas to its original volume, the contact of the cylinder with the first thermostat is again restored.
