IV Mathematical Olympiad MESI

The Moscow Economic-Statistical Institute currently occupies a leading position in the country in training specialists in computer information processing and in the application of mathematical methods in economics. That is why mathematics plays a particularly important role for those entering MESI. For the last three years, entrance exams in mathematics and physics have been taken by the Minsk-32 electronic computer. This year, MESI holds the Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren for the 4th time.

The Olympiad is held in 2 rounds: 1st round - correspondence and 2nd - full-time.
Schoolchildren who have successfully compared the problems of the first round and sent solutions no later than January 25, 1974 (by postmark) will be allowed to participate in the second round, which will be held at MESI.
Each Olympiad participant admitted to the 2nd round will be aware of this. To speed up receiving a response from the institute, please attach an envelope with your address written on it to the letter. Success in the Olympiad will be taken into account when entering our institute.
MESI address: 119435, Moscow, B. Savvinsky lane, 14, “Olympiad-74”.
Solve problems in Russian in student terdini. All works submitted to the Olympiad will be checked by a computer. Therefore, we ask you to place the answers to the problems at the end of the terdini, fill out the following table

1st round task
1. Find the remainder when dividing the number 1973$^5$ - 1973 by 1680.
2. Calculate without table
   $3 \text{ ctg}^6 80^\circ - 27 \text{ ctg}^4 80^\circ + 33 \text{ ctg}^2 80^\circ$
3. Find the sum of the real roots of the equation
   $x^4 - 8 x^2 - 3200 x = 159984$.
4. A circle is drawn in the quadrilateral $ABCD$ with its center in the middle of side $AD$ and touching the other three sides. Find side $AD$ if $AB = 12$ cm, $CD = 75$ cm.
5. From the middle of the height of a right quadrangular paramid a perpendicular is dropped onto the side edge equal to 4.2 cm and a perpendicular to the side edge equal to 3 cm. Find the volume of the paramid.

I. G. Venetsky, Yu. I. Sorkin
