F210. Two identical open connections with two identical tubes and filled to the top with water. The tubes are closed with taps $K_1$ and $K_2$ (Fig. 12). The temperature of the water in the vessels is maintained constant, and $t_1^\circ > t_2^\circ > 4^\circ$ C. What will happen to the water in the vessels if first the tap $K_2$ is open, and then (with the tap $K_2$ open) the tap $K_1$ is open?

After opening tap $K_2$ the vessels will become communicating. Therefore, such water levels will be established in them at which the pressures at the lower points of the vessels will be the same (the content would flow from one vessel to another):

$$\rho_1 g h_1 = \rho_2 g h_2$$

($\rho_1, \rho_2$ - density of water in vessels with temperatures corresponding $t_1^\circ$ and $t_2^\circ$, $h_1$ and $h_2$ - corresponding water levels).

Since $t_1^\circ > t_2^\circ > 4^\circ$ C, then $\rho_1 < \rho_2$. This means that $h_1 > h_2$, that is, water will flow from the right vessel to the left and part of it will be thrown out.

What will happen to the water when tap $K_1$ is opened depends on the correspondence between the water pressures in the vessels at the level of the tube. And since the pressure is linearly related to the height of the level and $h_1 > h_2$, then at any level $0 < h < h_1$ the pressure in the left vessel is greater than in the right (Fig. 13). Therefore, $p_1(h_0) > p_2(h_0)$. Therefore, when we open the tap $K_1$, water begins to flow through the upper tube from the left vessel to the right. In this case, the pressure in the right vessel will increase, and in the left - decrease. Thanks to this, a flow of water from the right vessel to the left will appear in the lower tube.

This task is directly related to technical issues. Water heating batteries are connected to one tube, and yet the water circulates through such a battery, and does not just flow through the pipe without entering the battery.
