All that remains is to prove our statement. To do this, we apply the following theorem to the triangle $Y_1$ and the map $p$ of this triangle onto $X_i \subset Y_1$, inverse to $f_j h^n$.

Theorem. Let $Z$ be a non-similar figure on the plane (and all boundaries of the point $Z$ are considered to belong to $Z$), $p: Z \rightarrow Z$ be a similarity transformation with coefficient $k < 1$. Then there is exactly one “fixed point” of the transformation $p$ belonging to $Z$, that is, there is exactly one $z_0 \in Z$ for which $p(z_0) = z_0$.

The proof of the theorem for the bounded figure $Z$ (namely, this is the case we need) is easiest to check this way. Let $Z_n = p^n(Z)$ (the result of $n$ applying $p$ to $Z$). Then $Z \supset Z_1 \supset Z_2 \supset Z_3 \supset \dots$ and the intersection of all $Z_n$ consists of a single point $z_0$ (Fig. 5). This point will be the fixed point of the transformation $p$.

Another proof that makes it possible to explicitly indicate the fixed point and is suitable for unbounded figures $Z$ can be obtained using complex numbers. Any transformation of the similarity of a plane can be written as the result of a sequential rotation, stretching by $k$ times, parallel transfer: $z| \rightarrow az + b$ and, perhaps, even previously symmetrically with respect to a straight line: $z| \rightarrow az + b$ (here $a$ and $b$ are complex numbers, $|a| = k$, $z = x - iy$ - conjugation $k = z + iy$; see article “Imaginary numbers and geometric problems”, «Kvant» No. 3, 1973, p. 22). In the first case, the fixed point is found as a solution to the equation equation.

$$z_0 = az_0 + b > z_0 = \frac{b}{1 - a} = \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - k^2},$$

in the second - as a solution to a system of equations

$$\begin{cases} z_0 = \bar{a}z_0 + b \\ \bar{z}_0 = \bar{a}z_0 + \bar{b} \end{cases} > z_0 = \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - \bar{a}},$$

$$= \frac{b + \bar{a}b}{1 - k^2}.$$
