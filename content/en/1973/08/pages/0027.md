The Golden Ratio Ratio and Its Remarkable Properties

If we put $x = a\tau$ in proportion (2), then with respect to $\tau$ we get the following equation:

$$\tau^2 + \tau - 1 = 0$$

(3)

The Greek sculptor Leochares (4th century BC) created a statue of Apollo Belvedere, who inspired the ideas of the ancient Greeks about male beauty. The lines drawn in the photograph determine the basic proportions of the body. These proportions are related to the golden ratio.

The positive root of this equation is equal to the ratio of the golden ratio:

$$\tau = \frac{x}{a} = \frac{a-x}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}$$

This concept is a remarkable number that has a number of interesting properties. Here are some of them.

1. Direct calculations show that

$$\tau = 0,618033989\ldots, \frac{1}{\tau} = 1,618033989\ldots$$

—the reciprocal of $\tau$ is one greater than $\tau$ itself. It is easy to verify that this is the only positive number with this property. Indeed, if the positive $z$ satisfies the correspondence $\frac{1}{z} = 1 + z$, then $z$ must be the root of the equation $z^2 + z - 1 = 0$. But this equation has a single positive root: $z = \tau$.
