June 19, 1973 marks the 350th anniversary of the birth of Blaise Pascal, one of the most significant people in human history. Pascal is one of the great Frenchmen whose porters are reproduced on banknotes (along with Cornelim, Racine, Voltaire and Pasteur). The collected statements of great people about Pascal look very internal, and it is tempting to at least list some of them, but we are stopped by the warning of Pascal himself: “... when we quote authors, we quote their evidence, not their names...”. We will only note that different people at different times reproduced Pascal - thinkers and writers - as their contemporary.

Is it possible to correctly evaluate Pascal - mathematics and physics - from a historical perspective. Serdnyaya talks about Pascal's discoveries on the pages of school textbooks. In order to understand great discoveries, one must learn to be surprised by what his contemporaries were surprised by. At the same time, we can notice how different the collections of “efforts” of natural scientific and humanitarian discoveries are.

Let us mention one more facet of Pascal’s legacy—his practical achievements. Some of them received the highest distinction - today few people know the name of their author. How many people know that the most objective point was invented by Pascal (and not by a nameless craftsman in Ancient Egypt or China)? Pascalo also came up with the idea of ​​omnibucos - public carriages (“for 5 sous”) with fixed routes - the first type of regular urban transport.
