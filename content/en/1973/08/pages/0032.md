$$W = \alpha 4\pi r^2 \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}} a_0 b_0,$$

(1)

where $\alpha$ is the dimensionless proportionality coefficient between the number of encounters and the number of chemical transformations.

The value $\alpha$ essentially means the probability that when two interacting particles meet, a chemical reaction will occur between them. What this probability is and how it is determined is already a task for the theory of an elementary act of chemical transformation, and we will not dwell on this question.

Relationship (1) expresses the law of mass action: the rate of a chemical reaction at any time is proportional to the product of the concentration of the reacting substances at that time. The proportionality coefficient before the concentration product is called the rate constant of a chemical reaction at a given temperature; Let's denote it by the letter $K$.

In the reaction of interaction of two particles with the same mass, the value $K$ is equal to

$$K = 4\pi r^2 \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}.$$

Let's try to calculate the speed of some simple chemical reaction.

Task 1. In a medium that is an ideal gas under normal conditions (pressure 760 mm cm, temperature 0°C), a biomolecular reaction occurs. The concentrations of substances are equal to each other and to other foreign substances in the environment.

This shows that the reaction rate constant is expressed in units ($cm^3\text{c}^{-1}$). This means that in every cubic centimeter of gas, 6.5 $\cdot 10^{28}$ collisions of $A$ and $B$ molecules occur per second.
