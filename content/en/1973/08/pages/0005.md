M. D. Millionshchikov was born on January 16, 1913 in the city of Grozny. In 1932 he graduated from the Grozny Oil Institute, and in 1938 he completed graduate school at the Moscow Aviation Institute. Subsequently, he worked at the Central Aerodynamic Institute named after N. E. Zhukovsky, and from 1949 until the last days of his life - at the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I. V. Kurchatov.

Mikhail Dmitrievich began independent scientific research early. Even at the age of twenty, he investigated a number of problems in the theory of oil and gas filtration in a porous medium, directly related to the needs of the developing oil industry of the Soviet Union. While studying and working at the Moscow Aviation Institute, M. D. Millionshchikov began studying the theory of turbulence under the guidance of Academician A. N. Kolmogorov. The turbulent movement of liquids and gases is a weight propagation natural phenomenon. With this movement, the particles of the medium move in a disorderly manner, forming vortices. Turbulent motion plays a vital role in many technical processes (flow around an airplane wing or ship hull, fluid movement through pipes, etc.). However, theoretical analysis of such a movement is associated with great difficulties. M. D. Millionshchikov has developed a subtle mathematical apparatus that allows the study of turbulence to be advanced.

In 1949, at the invitation of Academician I.V. Kurchatov, M.D. Millionshchikov became involved in the development of problems of using nuclear energy. The research he conducted made a significant contribution to the development of Soviet nuclear technology. In particular, he obtained very important results in the field of creating high-temperature nuclear reactors.

M.D. Millionshchikov spent a lot of time on the issues of magnetic hydrodynamics, that is, the study of the movement of conducting gases in a magnetic field. Research on these issues is of great importance in the development of methods for the direct conversion of thermal energy into electrical energy.

In the works of M.D. Millionshchikov, the intuition of an engineer with a deep mathematical culture was considered.

Taking into account the major contribution made by M.D. Millionshchikov to science, the USSR Academy of Sciences elected him in 1953 as its corresponding member. In 1962 he was elected academician and vice-president of the Academy.

For more than thirty years, M.D. Millionshchikov spent time in a number of high educational institutions, training scientists.

The activities of M.D. Millionshchikov as Chairman of the Supreme Council of the RSFSR, as well as as Chairman of the Soviet Pagush Committee, uniting those studying in the struggle for peace, disarmament and international détente, enjoyed international fame.

The bright memory of Mikhail Dmitrievich Millionshchikov remains in the hearts of Soviet people.
