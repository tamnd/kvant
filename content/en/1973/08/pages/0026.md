⟦folio 24⟧

Luca Pacioli (c. 1445 - later 1509) was an Italian mathematician who wrote a treatise on the golden ratio, The Divine Proportion.

The portrait of Monna Lisa (Gioconda) was painted by Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519) - the general Italian artist and scientist of the Renaissance. The painting attracted the attention of researchers, who discovered that the composition figure is based on “golden triangles” (more precisely, on triangles that are pieces of a regular stellate pentagon).

It was caused, first of all, by the numerous applications of the golden ratio both in geometry itself and in art, especially in architecture. The consequence of this was the appearance of the book “Divine Proportion”, the author of which was the largest mathematician of the 15th century, Italian Luca Pacioli. In his work, Pacioli gives thirteen properties of the golden ratio, which he first called such epithets as “exceptional”, “ineffable”, “most excellent”, “supernatural” and so on. However, the title of the book itself speaks about the author’s attitude towards the subject being described. It is interesting that the book was illustrated by one of the initiators of its writing, Pacioli’s friend, the great Leonardo da Vinci. By the way, it was he who coined the term “golden ratio”.

Observations show that, from a natural point of view, the golden ratio has certain advantages. This is confirmed by an experiment that was conducted at the end of the last century: out of ten rectangles, among which were “golden” (on the side, the ratio of the lengths gave the golden ratio), the subject had to choose one. And so, about 22% of the total number of subjects chose the “golden rectangle”. We cannot ignore the fact that books, postcards, wallets, chocolate bars and many other items are shaped like a golden rectangle. Let us note here that if you cut a square from the “golden” rectangle or attach a square to the larger side of the “golden” rectangle, you will again get a “golden” rectangle.

Widely used in architecture and art. Many architectural masterpieces are built according to the proportion of the golden ratio. The same proportion underlies many of the immortal creations of Phidias, Tishchan, Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael.
