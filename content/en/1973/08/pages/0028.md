2. Rewriting equality (3) in the form $\tau = \frac{1}{1 + \tau}$ and substituting $\frac{1}{1 + \tau}$ instead of $\tau$ on the right side of this equality, we obtain:

$$\tau = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \tau}}.$$

This process is supported and as a result we obtain the following representations of the number $\tau$ in the form of an infinite continued fraction*):

$$\tau = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \dots}}}$$

One cannot help but note the extreme simplicity of this presentation!

3. Here is another representation of the number $\tau$:

$$\tau = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1 + \dots}}}}.$$

To give meaning to equality (5), let us study the sequence $\sqrt{1}, \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1}}, \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1}}}, \dots,$

the common term of which (we denote it by $\varphi_n$) contains $n$ radicals.

It is immediately clear that $\{\varphi_n\}$ is an increasing sequence. Moreover, it is limited. Indeed, since $\varphi_1 = 1 < 2$ and $\varphi_{n+1} = \sqrt{1 + \varphi_n}$, then from $\varphi_n < 2$ it follows that $\varphi_{n+1} < \sqrt{3} < 2$, and by induction we conclude that $\varphi_n < 2$ for any (natural) $n$.

*) Continued fractions are discussed in the articles of N. M. Beskin («Kvant» No. 1, 8, 1970). A more detailed presentation can be found in the book by A. Ya. Khinchin “Continued Fractions” (M., Fizmatgiz, 1961), as well as in the textbook for 9th grade secondary schools with a mathematical specialization “Algebra” by N. Ya. Vilenkin et al. (M., “Prosveshchenie”, 1968).

So, $\{\varphi_n\}$ is an increasing, bounded sequence. And as we know, such a sequence is convergent. Denoting the limit of the sequence $\varphi_n$ by $\varphi$, we can write: $\varphi = \lim_{n \to \infty} \varphi_n = \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1}}}.$

On the other hand, passing to the limit in the equality $\varphi_{n+1} = \sqrt{1 + \varphi_n}$, we obtain: $\varphi = \sqrt{1 + \varphi_n}$.

Thus, the (positive) number $\varphi$ is the root of the quadratic equation $\varphi^2 - \varphi - 1 = 0$, and the number $\psi = \frac{1}{\varphi}$ is the root of the equation $\left(\frac{1}{\psi}\right)^2 - \frac{1}{\psi} - 1 = 0$, or multiplying by $-\psi^2$, $\psi^2 + \psi - 1 = 0$, from which $\psi = \tau$, $\varphi = \frac{1}{\tau}$, we obtain equality (5). And here the extreme simplicity of the presentation will jump into the head!

Approximation of the number $\tau$ by rational numbers

Representation (4) is very convenient for approximating the irrational number $\tau$ by rational numbers. For this purpose, let's turn to “suitable” fractions:

$$\tau_1 = \frac{1}{1}, \quad \tau_2 = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1}}, \quad \tau_{(3)} = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1}}}$$

and in general for any $n$

$$\tau_n = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \dots}}} + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1}}$$
