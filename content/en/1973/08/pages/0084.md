In the article by A. Yu Soifer “Checkered boards and polyominoes” (“Quant”, 1972 No. 11), he talks about polyominoes and various problems related to them. Recall that a polymine is a figure consisting of a finite number of cells, and the cells can border on a vertex or on a side, and from each cell you can get to any other (you can only go from a cell to a cell that has a common side with it). We will call two polymines the same if they are combined by superposition.

Pentomine is a polymine consisting of a spot of cells. There are exactly a dozen different pentominoes (check it out!). The American mathematician S. W. Golomb managed to arrange a chessboard in thirteen polyominoes, including a dozen different pentominoes.
