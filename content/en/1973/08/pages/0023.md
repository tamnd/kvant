And if a tube filled with water were a hundred times wider or a hundred times narrower, but the water stood in all cases at the same height, then the same weight would always be used to balance the water. As soon as this load is reduced, the water will descend and lift the reduced load.

If you drink water into a tube at double height, then to balance the water, a double weight will act on the piston. In the same way, if you make the answer in which the piston is inserted twice the size, then you will need to double the force required to hold the double piston. From this it is clear that the force needed to prevent water from flowing out of the answer is proportional to the height of the water, and not to the width of the vessel, and that the measure of this force is always the weight of the water contained in a column with a height equal to the height of the water, and a base equal to the size of the answer.

What I said about water applies to all other types of liquid.
