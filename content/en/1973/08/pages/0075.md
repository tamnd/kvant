AND

We came across the conjunction “AND” in a sentence about dividing natural numbers (in Klaus’s written work). With this word we can combine several separate statements into one compound statement. Such a connection of statements is called defusion or conjunction. Let's look at an example.
The number 3 satisfies the inequality x < 7 And the number 3 satisfies the inequality x > 2.
There are two parts to this statement:
1) Number 3 satisfies the inequality x < 7 (true),
2) The number 3 satisfies the inequality x > 2 (true).
A union (conjunction) of statements will be true if and only if both parts from which it is formed are also true. In the case when one of the parts is true and the other is true, or, even more so, when both parts are true, the unification will be true. For example, the statement
The number 300 immediately following the number 200 AND the number 1000 immediately preceding the number 2000 is incorrect because both parts are incorrect.
0 is the smallest natural number AND 10000000000 is the smallest natural number - false because the second statement being combined is false.
Let's return to the question of dividing natural numbers.
Division a : b in the field of natural numbers is possible if the number a is a multiple of the number b AND b ≠ 0.
In this case, it is impossible to put OR together with AND.
It's easy to understand why the teacher was not satisfied with Klaus's answer:

"Division a : b in the domain of natural numbers is possible if a is a multiple of b OR if b is not zero." Indeed, empty, for example, a = 15, b = 4. Here b ≠ 0; the condition formed by Klaus is fulfilled, but division is completely impossible.
