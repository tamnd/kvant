Gulden's theorem *) talks about the volume of a bracing body that results if a plane figure braces around an axis lying in its plane. But what if the oatmeal does not lie in the plane of the figure? How to calculate the volume of the bracing body in this case? In this case, the following two theorems can help.

Theorem 1. Let α and β be two variable planes and l be their intersection line. Let, further, F be some plane figure whose plane γ is a variable of the plane β, and F₁ is the projection of the figure F onto the plane α. Then the volume of the body obtained by bending the figure F around the l axis is equal to the volume of the body obtained by bending the figure F₁ around the l axis.

Proof. Let us draw a plane p, an alternating plane line l, and let this plane intersect the figure F along the segment AB (if the intersection of the plane p with the figure F consists of several segments, the intersections do not change). The segment AB is variable in the plane β and therefore its projection onto the plane α will be the segment A₁B₁, equal to the segment AB.
