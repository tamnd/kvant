Golden ratio

Johannes Kepler said that geometry has two treasures - the Pythagorean theorem and the golden ratio. And if the first of these treasures can be compared to a measure of gold, then the second can be compared to a precious stone.

Every schoolchild knows the Pythagorean theorem, but not everyone knows what the golden ratio is. So we decided to tell readers about this gem.

What is the golden ratio?

The point $C$ is said to produce the golden ratio of the segment $AB$ if $AC : AB = CB : AC$. (1)

So, the golden ratio is a division of the whole into two unequal parts, in which the larger part is related to the whole as the smaller part is to the larger. In geometric terms, the golden ratio is also called the division of a segment in extreme and average ratio.

If the length of the segment $AB$ is denoted by $a$, and the length of the segment $AC$ by $x$, then the length of the segment $CB$ will be $a - x$ (Fig. 1), and proportion (1) will take the following form:

$$x : a = (a - x) : x$$ (2)

From this proportion it is clear that with the golden ratio, the length greater than the segment is the geometric mean, or, as is often said, the mean of

proportional to the lengths of the entire segment and its smaller part:

$$x = \sqrt{a(a - x)}$$

It is easy to say that the converse is also true: if a segment is divided into two unequal segments so that the length of the larger segment is the geometric mean of the lengths of the entire segment and its smaller part, then we have the golden ratio of this segment.

The geometric golden ratio of the segment $AB$ can be constructed as follows: at point $B$ we reconstruct the perpendicular to $AB$ (Fig. 2) and plot $BD = 0,5AB$ on it; further, connecting the points $A$ and $D$, set aside $DE = BD$ and, finally, $AC = AE$. The point $C$ is the desired one - it produces the golden ratio of the segment $AB$. In fact, we note that according to the Pythagorean theorem

$$(AE + ED)^2 = AB^2 + BD^2$$

and building

$$AE = AC, ED = BD = 0,5AB.$$

From these equalities it follows that

$$AC^2 + AC \cdot AB = AB^2,$$

and from here equality (1) can easily be obtained.

Solving equation (2) for $x$, we find that

$$x = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{2} a \approx 0,62a.$$

So, $a - x \approx 0,38 a$. Thus, the parts of the golden ratio are approximately 62% and 38% of the entire segment.
