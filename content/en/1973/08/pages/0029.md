$$\tau_1 = \frac{1}{1}, \tau_2 = \frac{1}{2}, \tau_3 = \frac{2}{3}, \tau_4 = \frac{3}{5}, \dots$$

This series of dobys is built according to a very simple law: the numerator of each doby is equal to the denominator of the previous doby, and the denominator is the sum of the numerator and denominator of the same doby. What will happen next? Does this pattern hold true? It's easy to prove that this is true. Indeed, as can be seen from formula (6), the neighboring suitable dobies $\tau_n$ and $\tau_{n+1}$ are related by the relation

$$\tau_{n+1} = \frac{1}{1 + \tau_n},$$

and therefore from the equality $\tau_n = \frac{m}{k}$ it follows that $\tau_{n+1} = \frac{k}{m + k}$.

This simple law of the formation of suitable additional numbers makes it possible to easily write out their sequence:

$$\frac{1}{1}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{3}{5}, \frac{5}{8}, \frac{8}{13}, \frac{13}{21}, \frac{21}{34}, \dots$$

From the theory of chain dobies it is known that suitable dobies with odd numbers decrease and approach the number generating these dobies on the right, and dobies with even numbers increase and approach the same number on the left. Applying this property in our case, we can write:

$$\frac{1}{2} < \frac{3}{5} < \frac{8}{13} < \frac{21}{34} < \frac{55}{89} < \dots < \tau < \dots < \frac{34}{55} < \frac{13}{21} < \frac{5}{8} < \frac{2}{3} < \frac{1}{1}$$

Recalling the approximations of the number m by suitable additions, we will notice that the ratio of any term of the Fibonacci sequence to the subsequent term is a suitable addition of the number $\tau$, that is, an approximate value of the ratio of the golden ratio. This approximation is better, the larger the number of the term taken.

If three consecutive terms are taken: $u_n, u_{n+1}, u_{n+2}$, then the numbers $\frac{u_n}{u_{n+1}}$ and $\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n+2}}$ are neighboring suitable extensions of the number $\tau$, and one of these extensions is greater than $\tau$, and the other is less.

Finally, we pose the following question: how to divide the integer $a$ into two integer parts so that they are relative to $\tau$?

Since $\tau$ is an irrational number, such a division is, of course, impossible; the one we are interested in can only approximately equal $\tau$. What is this approximation? The answer to this question is given by the theory of chain dobes.

Let the denominator of a suitable doby $\tau_n$ be $a$. Let us consider the set of all dobes with denominators not greater than $a$. It turns out that out of the set of these dobes, the one closest to the number $\tau$ is precisely $\tau_n$.

No denominator of a suitable dobei is a term of the Fibonacci sequence, so if $a$ is a term of the Fibonacci sequence, then dividing $a$ by $\tau_n$ will be a good approximation of the golden ratio.

Thus, numbers that are members of the Fibonacci sequence can be divided into two integer parts by the golden ratio with a good approximation. For example, the golden ratio of the number 8 gives (3.5,) the number 13 - (5.8), etc.
