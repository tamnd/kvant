2. 《Mystical Hexagonist》 or “Pascal’s Great Theorem.”

At the age of 13, B. Pascal already had access to Mersenne’s mathematical circle, which included a large number of Parisian mathematicians, including E. Pascal (Pascal lived in Paris since 1631).

The French monk Marin Mersenne (1588-1648) played a large and important role in the history of science as a scientist-organizer*). His main merit is that he conducted extensive correspondence with most of the world's leading scientists (he had several hundred correspondents). Mersenne skillfully concentrated information and communicated it to interested scientists. This activity required its own agreement: the ability to quickly understand new things, and set tasks well. Possessing high government qualities, Mersenne enjoyed the trust of correspondents. Along with the absentee team of correspondents, there was also a very small circle - the “Mersenne Thursdays” - in which Blaise Pascal ended up. Here he found himself a worthy teacher. He was Gerard Desargues (1593-1662), engineer and architect, creator of the original theory of prospects. His main work, “Rough sketch of an invasion into the field of what happens when a cone meets a plane” (1639), found only a few readers, and B. Pascal occupies a special place in the middle, who managed to make significant progress.

*) When assessing Mersenne’s work, one must keep in mind that the first scientific journal, the “Journal of Scientists,” was founded in 1665.

Although at that time Descartes was breaking new ground in geometry by creating analytical geometry, in general geometry had barely reached the level it was at in ancient Greece. Much of the legacy of the Greek geometers remained unclear. This primarily applied to the theory of finite sections. The most outstanding teaching on this topic - the 8 books of Apollo's Copika - was only partially known. Attempts were made to give modernized expositions of the theory, among which the most famous is that of Claude Midorge (1585-1647), a member of the Mersenne circle, but this work actually did not contain new ideas. Desargues noted that the systematic application of the perektata method makes it possible to construct theories of finite sections with completely new positions.
