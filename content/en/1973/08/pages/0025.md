A little history

The oldest literary monument in which the division of a segment in relation to the golden ratio is found is Euclid’s “Elements” (3rd century BC). Already in Book II of the Elements, Euclid constructs the golden ratio, and later uses it to construct some regular polygons and polyhedra.

But the golden ratio was known before Euclid. In particular, Pythogor and his students knew about him (VI century BC). In addition to philosophy and mathematics, in the philosophical school of Pythagoras they also studied harmony. While studying the theory of harmony, the Pythogoreans came to the conclusion that qualitative differences in sounds are due to quantitative differences between the lengths of the strings. This inspired them, and they tried to go further - to express all the patterns of the world through numbers, believing that God put numbers at the basis of the world order. The Pythogoreans in numbers and their relations (and the latter were considered as relations of segments) calculated the magical, the supernatural. And geometry is not without its revenge. Of particular note here are any Pythogoreans to the stellate pentagon, composed of the diagonals of a regular pentagon.

Here is what the famous mathematician and historian of mathematics van der Waerden writes about this in his excellent book “Awakening Science”: “This figure, a symbol of health, served as an identification mark for the Pythogoreans. When in a foreign land one of them was lying on his deathbed and could not light up the man who looked after him until his death, he ordered him to draw a star-shaped polygon on his home; If a Pythogorean ever passes through mismo, he will not fail to inquire about it. Indeed, several years later, a Pythogorean saw this sign, and the owner of the house received a rich reward" *).

The star pentagon is interesting for us primarily because each of the heels of the lines that make up this figure divides the other in relation to the golden ratio. In fact, since triangles ACD and ABE are similar (Fig. 3), then AC : AB = AD : AE. But AD = BC, and AE = AC, and therefore AC: AB = BC: AC - the proportion of the golden ratio already known to us. It was precisely this property of the stellated pentagon that the Pythogoreans could use to construct a regular pentagon, or they certainly knew how to construct the golden ratio.

By the beginning of the Renaissance, interest in the golden ratio had flourished.

*) B. L. van der Waerden. "Waking Science". M., Fizmatiz, 1959, p. 139.
