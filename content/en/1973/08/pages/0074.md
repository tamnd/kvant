⟦folio 72⟧

Small, almost imperceptible words, such as AND, OR, EXACTLY, can have a big meaning. And not only the small words themselves, but also their place in the sentence is very important (for example, the place of the words IF, SO, NOT).

In order not to make the same mistakes as our Klaus made, let's consider these small words with great meaning.

NO MORE, NO LESS, EXACTLY, ONE AND ONLY ONE, AT LEAST ONE

Consider the statement:
Which natural number corresponds to a point on the number axis.
This statement is correct. It expresses the same meaning as the statement:
Which natural number corresponds to a point on the number axis.
These sentences do not establish that each natural number corresponds to only one point on the number line. There may be several of these points, but in no case less than one.

As you can see, the information in these statements is much less than in the sentence:
Which natural number corresponds to a point on the number axis.
Now it is easy to find out whether the following sentences are true:

1. There are EXACTLY 3 single-digit natural numbers that are divisible by 3.
2. There are 3 single-digit natural numbers divisible by 3.
3. There are at least three single-digit natural numbers divisible by 3*).

The first statement, of course, is incorrect, since it has more than 3 single-digit natural numbers divisible by 3, namely 4 numbers: 0, 3, 6, 9. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, they have the same meaning.

In the same way, we can establish that the statement between 1 and 100 has at least 50 numerical natural numbers - incorrect, since in fact there are only 49 of them (that is, less than 50).

Surely there are readers among you who think that the statement

The GDR on January 1, 1971 had no more than 50 million inhabitants - this is incorrect, since the number of inhabitants of the GDR is only about 17 million. This argument is unconvincing: the turnover is “no more than 50 million.” means that it may be less, but in no case more, which means this statement is correct.

Check if the following statements are true:
a) There are at most 9 single-digit natural numbers.
b) There are at most two even prime numbers.
b) A triangle has no more than three acute angles.
d) The equation 3* = 27 has at most one solution in the domain of natural numbers.
d) Between 1 and 1000 there are at least 50 natural numbers.
e) The equation 3* = 27 has one and only one solution.
g) The number 60 has exactly 10 natural divisors.
h) Any given natural number corresponds to at most one point on the number line.
i) Equation 3* = 27 has at least one solution.
k) A triangle has at least one rectangle.

*) Don't forget that zero is considered a natural number. (Approx. Transl.)
