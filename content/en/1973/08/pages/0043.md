⟦folio 41⟧

The train dives into tunnels every now and then. It's breathtaking. All the wise men have gathered in the Varon’s corridor, looking out the open windows, and they can’t get enough of it.

Suddenly in one tunnel there is a roar, smoke, dust! Some kind of dirt fell into the windows. We passed the tunnel and the conductor entered. “Someone got dirty here,” he says. — Unfortunately, there is no water on the train. But now there will be more stops in a row, so we can get off the train and wash ourselves.”

Now I need to warn you - the wise men in Varona gather as a selection: as smart as they are lazy. No one, say, will wash in vain (unless he knows for sure that he has gotten dirty). And they won’t ask the neighbors if his place is clean and dirty - why should they rightfully disturb the people and worry themselves? - please report.

What will the wise men do after the announcement of the guide?

It is stated that if n of them have dirty faces, then at the nth stop all these n wise men will get off the train to wash themselves.

Let us prove the statement made by induction.

Case n = 1. When n = 1 the statement is very indo. The sage $M_1$ with a grim face will learn from the conductor’s announcement that there are passengers with dirty faces in the varona. Looking around at his neighbors, he discovers that their faces are clean. So, it’s him who has a dirty face. Therefore, at the 1st stop he goes to wash himself.

Transition from n = k to n = k + 1. Let us show that if the statement is true for n = k, then it is also true for n = k + 1

Let the faces of the wise men $M_1$, ..., $M_{k+1}$ be stained. Then $M_{k+1}$ sees k griz faces ($M_1$, ..., $M_k$) around and reasons like this:

“Two cases are possible:
1) I have a clean face;
2) my face is dirty.

In the 1st case, all k wise men with grisly faces that I see around will go out to wash at the kth stop (since, by the indukini assumption, the statement is true for n = k).

Since the 1st case is possible, I should not get off either at the kth stop or earlier: if I am clean, this would be an unforgivable waste of energy! Who came to this conclusion in my place, a violent person who is not prone to the fuss of a person *).

But, of course, the second case is also possible: maybe I also have a grim face. Then, however, each of the dirty sages $M_1$, ..., $M_k$ sees k dirty faces around him. In this situation, none of them - wise and leisurely - will go to wash at the k-th stop (see the previous paragraph).

So, if the wise men $M_1$, ..., $M_k$ do not go to wash at the k-th stop, then I am grizny, and I should go wash. I'll wait for the kth stop. If you can’t wash your face there, come and get off at the next (k + 1) stop and wash your face!”

All wise men with grisly faces reason in the same way. Consequently, at the (k + 1)th stop they will all go to wash, which is what needed to be proven.
