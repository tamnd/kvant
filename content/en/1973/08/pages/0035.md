If the reaction is bimolecular, the change in the concentration of the reactants over time should satisfy equation (3).

Let's rewrite equation (3) a little differently:

$$\frac{1}{[N]_t} = \frac{1}{[N]_0} + Kt. \quad (4)$$

Equation (4) is the equation of a straight line in inverse concentration - time coordinates. The rate constant of a bimolecular reaction is equal to the tangent of the angle of inclination of this straight line to the $t$ axis.

Taking the concentration values ​​from Figures 3, $a$, $b$, we construct curves of the inverse concentrations of the starting substances versus time (Fig. 4, $a$, $b$). It can be seen that in the case of cyclohexade flopping, a straight line is actually obtained. This means that this is a bimolecular reaction. The rate constant for this reaction is:

$$K = \text{tg}\varphi \approx 0,02 \text{ л/моль} \cdot \text{с.}$$
