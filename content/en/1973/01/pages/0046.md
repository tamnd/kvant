Problem 3. Air is pumped out of a vessel with volume $V$, the pressure in the chamber is $P$. How much pumping should a piston pump be pumped about $v$ for the pressure in the seal vessel to be $k$ times? The air temperature does not change.

Solution. Before the piston makes the first “swing”, we can write for the gas in the vessel

$$PV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

When the pump piston is in its extreme position, the volume of gas is $V + v$, the pressure is $P_1$, and

$$P_1 (V + v) = \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

This means that

$$P_1 = P \frac{V}{V + v}.$$

Similarly, we find that after the second swing the pressure in the vessel will be equal to

$$P_2 = P_1 \frac{V}{V + v} = P \left( \frac{V}{V + v} \right)^2.$$

After $n$ swings the pressure becomes equal

$$P_n = P \left( \frac{V}{V + v} \right)^n.$$

According to the conditions of the problem

$$P_n = \frac{1}{k} P.$$

It follows that

$$\frac{1}{k} = \left( \frac{V}{V + v} \right)^n,$$

that is

$$n = - \frac{\lg k}{\lg V / v} = \frac{\lg k}{\lg \frac{V}{v} + V / V}.$$

If there is a mixture of several gases in a vessel, then according to Dalton’s law, the pressure of this mixture on the walls of the vessel is equal to the sum of the partial pressures. The pressure that each gas individually would create if the remaining gases were removed from the container is called partial pressure. This means that the state of each gas in the mixture is independent of the state of the other gases. However, only volume and temperature are constant (since the gases are in equilibrium). Therefore, for each of the gases it is possible to write the equation of gas state differently. Let's take an example.

Problem 4. Determine the density of a mixture consisting of $m_1 = 50$ oxygen and $m_2 = 20$ hydrogen at a temperature of $t = 20^\circ C$ and a pressure of $P = 0,9$ atmospheres.

Solution.

$$P = P_1 + P_2,$$

where $P_1$ is the partial pressure of oxygen and $P_2$ is the partial pressure of hydrogen.

Let us denote the volume of the mixture $V$, then

$$P_1 V = \frac{m_1}{\mu_1} RT,$$

$$P_2 V = \frac{m_2}{\mu_2} RT,$$

And

$$PV = \left( \frac{m_1}{\mu_1} + \frac{m_2}{\mu_2} \right) RT.$$

The density of the mixture is

$$\rho = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{V} = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{RT} \left( \frac{m_1}{\mu_1} + \frac{m_2}{\mu_2} \right) \approx 0,23 \text{ ке/м}^3.$$
