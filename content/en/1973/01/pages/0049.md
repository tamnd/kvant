If the process occurs at a constant volume, then the gas does no work. In this case, the amount of heat imparted to the gas is equal to the change in its internal energy. The heat capacity of a gas during an isochoric process is called the heat capacity at constant volume and is denoted $c_V$.

Since in any process in which the initial and final temperature of the gas are the same, the changes in the internal energy of the gas are the same - the internal energy is determined by the temperature of the gas - then in any process the change in the internal energy of the gas is equal to $c_V m \Delta T$. Therefore, the first law of thermodynamics can be written in this form

$$cm \Delta T = c_V m \Delta T + A.$$

This means that in order to find the heat of a gas in each given process, you need to find an expression for the work done by the gas. And we already know how to do this.

Let's solve the following problem.

Problem 7. Find the specific heat of a gas at constant pressure if its specific heat at constant volume is $c_V$.

Solution. We have shown that

$$c_P m \Delta T = c_V m \Delta T + A.$$

No gas work is equal to

$$A = P (V_2 - V_1).$$

Because

$$PV_1 = \frac{m}{\mu} RT_1$$ and $$PV_2 = \frac{m}{\mu} RT_2$$, then

$$V_1 = \frac{1}{P} \frac{m}{\mu} RT_1$$ and $$V_2 = \frac{1}{P} \frac{m}{\mu} RT_2$$ and

$$A = P \frac{1}{P} \frac{m}{\mu} R (T_2 - T_1) = \frac{m}{\mu} R \Delta T.$$

Fig. 6.

This means that

$$c_P m \Delta T = c_V m \Delta T + \frac{m}{\mu} R \Delta T.$$

From here

$$c_P = c_V + \frac{R}{\mu}.$$
