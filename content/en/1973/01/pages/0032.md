We will proceed as follows. Divide the disk into thin hollow cylinders. Consider one of these cylinders located at a distance $r$ (Fig. 8) from the center of the disk. Its thickness is $\Delta r \ll r$, so we can assume that when the disk is rotated by an angle f, each point of the selected cylinder will move by fr. This means that the friction force $F_{TP}$ has done work
$$\Delta A = F_{TP} \varphi r = k \Delta m g \varphi r,$$
(2)

where $\Delta m$ is the mass of the selected cylinder, which can be expressed through the mass of the disk $M$. Obviously, the ratio of the mass of the cylinder to the mass of the disk is equal to the ratio of their areas
$$\frac{\Delta m}{M} = \frac{\pi (r + \Delta r)^2 - \pi r^2}{\pi R^2}.$$

From here
$$\Delta m = M \frac{2r \Delta r + (\Delta r)^2}{R^2}.$$

Since $\Delta r \ll r$, then $(\Delta r)^2 \ll 2r \Delta r$ and
$$\Delta m = 2M \frac{r \Delta r}{R^2}.$$

Substituting this expression for $\Delta m$ into formula (2), we obtain
$$\Delta A = \frac{2\varphi M g k r^2 \Delta r}{R^2}.$$

The work done by the friction force when rotating the entire disk is equal to the sum of the following expressions, which applies to different cylinders:
$$A = \sum \frac{2\varphi M g k r^2 \Delta r}{R^2} = \frac{2\varphi M g k \Sigma r^2 \Delta r}{R^2}.$$
(3)

The sum $\Sigma r^2 \Delta r$ can be calculated as follows. Consider the graph of the function $y = r^2$ (Fig. 9). The product $r^2 \Delta r$ is the area of ​​the rectangle divided by figure, and the sum $\Sigma r^2 \Delta r$ is equal to the area of ​​the stepped figure consisting of similar rectangles. At $\Delta r \rightarrow 0$ the broken line tends to the parabola $y = r^2$. Therefore, the expression $\Sigma r^2 \Delta r$ is numerically equal to the area of ​​the figure under the parabolas graph. To find this area, you can use the method outlined in the article by A. D. Bendukidze “Archimedes and the quadrature of paraboles” (“Quantum” No. 7, 1971), and arrive at the expression *)

$$\Sigma r^2 \Delta r = \frac{1}{3} R^3.$$

Let's substitute this expression into formula (3)
$$A = \frac{2\varphi M g k R^3}{3 R^2} = \frac{2}{3} \varphi M g k R.$$

Let us now equate this value to the kinetic energy of the disk:
$$\frac{2}{3} \varphi M g k R = Fl.$$

From here we find the angle of rotation of the disk
$$\varphi = \frac{3}{2} \frac{Fl}{M g k R}.$$

The number of revolutions of the disk is
$$n = \frac{\varphi}{2\pi} = \frac{3}{4} \frac{Fl}{\pi M g k R}.$$
