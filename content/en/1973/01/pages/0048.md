Solution. Let's draw a graph of the dependence of $P$ on $V$ (Fig. 4). In section 1-2, the pressure changes according to the law $P = \alpha \cdot T^{\frac{1}{2}}$, where $\alpha$ is some constant. Let us express the gas temperature in terms of pressure $T = \frac{P^2}{\alpha^2}$ and substitute it into the Clapeyron-Mendeleev equation:

$$PV = RT.$$

We get

$$V = R \frac{P}{\alpha^2}; \quad P = \frac{\alpha^2}{R} \cdot V,$$

that is, pressure is directly proportional to volume. In this section, the gas does positive work. Process 2-3 is isochoric; during this process, work is zero. During the 3-1 isobaric process, work was done on the gas, that is, the gas did negative work. Thus, the total work done by the gas is equal to the area of ​​the triangle 1 2 3:

$$A = \frac{P_2 - P_1}{2} (V_2 - V_1).$$

Because

$$V_2 = \frac{1}{P_2} RT_2 \text{ и } V_1 = \frac{1}{P_1} RT_1,$$

That

$$A = \frac{P_2 - P_1}{2} \left( \frac{T_2}{P_2} - \frac{T_1}{P_1} \right) R.$$

The heat capacity of an ideal gas (as opposed to liquids and solids) depends on what process occurs with the gas. For example, that specific heat is the amount of heat that must be added to a gas in order to increase its temperature by $1^\circ$. When the mass of a gas is equal to one unit of mass, the heat is called specific, and when the process involves one mole of gas, it is called molar.

If the gas is given an amount of heat $Q$, then

$$Q = cm\Delta T$$

(c is the specific heat and $m$ is the gas mass).

According to the First Law of Thermodynamics, the amount of heat imparted to the gas is equal to the sum of the change in the internal energy of the gas and the work done by the gas:

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Therefore, the heat of a gas is related to the amount of work done by the gas.

For example, if a gas becomes a gas due to a decrease in its internal energy, the specific heat of the gas is zero. The heat capacity of a gas can also be negative if the gas, due to a decrease in its internal energy, not only acts as a gas, but also gives off a certain amount of heat to the external environment, so that $Q$ is negative.
