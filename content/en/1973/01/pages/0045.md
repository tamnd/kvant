⟦folio 43⟧

Equation of gas state.
Work and heat of gas.

I. A. Zaitsev

Problems of changing the state of an ideal gas are solved using the equation of gas state - the Clapeyron - Mendeleev equation, which relates the parameters of the state - pressure, volume and temperature:

$$PV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the gas, $\mu$ is the molecular mass (gram-mol) of this gas, $\frac{m}{\mu}$ is the number of moles, $R$ is the universal gas constant. We will start this article by solving several common problems. Then we will consider how the work done by the gas or on the gas is determined, and the heat of the gas in various processes.

Problem 1. How did the volume of a certain mass of gas change during its transition from state 1 to state 2 (Fig. 1)?

Solution. From the equation of gas state it follows that when constructing the volume of a gas, its pressure is proportional to temperature:

$$P = \frac{1}{V} \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

The graph of $P$ versus $T$ is a straight line whose equation is

$$P = aT,$$

where $a = \frac{1}{V} \frac{m}{\mu} R$. The larger $V$, the smaller the proportional coefficient $a$ and the smaller the angle $\alpha$ of the graphic to the $T$ axis. Let's draw two isochores passing through points 1 and 2. Since $\alpha_1 > \alpha_2$, then $V_2 > V_1$, that is, the volume of gas increases.

Problem 2. How did the mass of gas in a cylinder with a value of $V$ change if, when the gas was heated from temperature $T_1$ to temperature $T_2$, the pressure in the cylinder changed from $P_1$ to $P_2$? The molecular mass of the gas is $\mu$.

Fig. 1.

Solution. This problem can be solved using not the equation of gas state, but the laws of Boyle - Mariotte, Gay-Lussac and Charles. However, such a solution is cumbersome and requires a certain amount of ingenuity. Using the equation of gas state, the problem is solved quite simply.

Let us write down the equation of the gas in the cylinder at the beginning and at the end of the process:

$$P_1V = \frac{m_1}{\mu}RT_1,$$

$$P_2V = \frac{m_2}{\mu}RT_2.$$
