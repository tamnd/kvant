Thermal balance of the Earth

B. M. Smirnov

Almost all the non-nuclear energy we deal with on Earth is the result of the conversion of solar radiation energy. This includes the chemical energy of fossil fuels, water energy, wind energy, and much more.

The main source of solar radiation is thermonuclear reactions occurring in the depths of the Sun.

The main purpose of these reactions can be determined by the following scheme:

$$2p \rightarrow d + e^+ + v + 0,42 \text{ Mэв},$$
$$d + p \rightarrow He^3 + \gamma + 5,49 \text{ Mэв},$$
$$2He^3 \rightarrow He^4 + 2p + 12,85 \text{ Mэв}.$$

Here $p$ is a proton, $d$ is a deuteron, that is, a nucleus consisting of a proton and a neutron, He$^3$, He$^4$ are gel nuclei containing three and four nucleons of the corresponding one (a nucleon is a proton or neutron), $e^+$ is a positron, $v$ is a neutrino, $\gamma$ is photon The particle energy written on the right side of the equation turns into heat, is transferred to the surface of the Sun, and then radiated. The only exception is the neutrino, which immediately leaves the Sun, carrying away an energy equal to 0.26 MeV. The positron enters into an angulation reaction with electromagnetic waves, and the wavelengths corresponding to the maximum energy of this radiation are shorter, the higher the body temperature.

The total flux of radiation from the surface of the Sun at all wavelengths is determined by the Stefan-Boltzmann law: $j = \sigma T^4$, where $j$ is the flux of radiated energy, $T$ is the absolute temperature of the surface, $\sigma = 5,69 \cdot 10^{-12} \text{bm/cm}^2$ erad$^4$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.
