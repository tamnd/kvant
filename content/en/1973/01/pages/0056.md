⟦folio 54⟧

Television physics and mathematics courses for university applicants

I. A. Dyyakovov, A. G. Mordkovich, I. I. Nasluzov

For the fourth year, there have been physical and mathematical courses organized by the Main Editorial Board of popular science and educational programs of Central Television for applicants to universities.

For 9 months (from October to June), according to the 3rd program of Central Television, classes are held for employees of television physics and mathematics courses.

The physics cycle consists of 39 television programs, of which 26 are lectures, 10 are consultations on solving problems, and 3 programs deal with issues included in the new school curriculum. Lectures are accompanied by demonstrations of physical instruments and experiments and various uses of materials: diagrams, photographs, drawings, and slides.

The mathematics course contains 50 television programs of a theoretical and practical nature. Five of them are related to the discussion of tests that are carried out after the completion of each main section of mathematics.

Every day, based on the topics covered, the course provider offers homework assignments for independent work. In mathematics there will be 20 such problems, in physics - 30. Homework problems and tests will be regularly published in the daily book "PTR".

The editors of the magazine «Kvant» and the Main Editorial Board of popular science and educational programs of Central Television, starting from January 1973, will publish on the pages of the magazine integral educational and methodological materials related to the work of the television program: additional tasks and examples on topics discussed on television, guidelines for their solution, various current information, etc. These materials will be useful not only for television physics and mathematics courses, but also for all applicants in 1973.

The first test in physics will be held in February. The following tasks are submitted to it: “Mechanics”, “Liquids and Gases” and “Molecular Physics and Heat”.

The procedure for conducting the test is as follows:

60 minutes are allotted for solving problems. (We publish typical options below).

Then the nouns break down the solutions with the teacher and answer a number of additional questions on the material included in the test program.

Based on the solution to the problem and answers to oral questions, the nouns are given a final assessment.

We invite the reader of the magazine to try their hand at solving typical options for the first-year test.

Option 1

1. A bullet flying out of a horizontally mounted coil hits exactly the center of the target, located at a distance of $L = 200$ m from the coil. The target was moved $l = 20$ m and lowered $h = 25$ cm. Determine at what distance from the center the bullet will hit the target, above or below the center? The initial speed of the bullet when leaving the coil is $v = 600$ m/s. Air resistance claim.

2. Three weights of equal mass $A$, $B$ and $C$ are fastened on a non-stretchable thread, thrown over a block, and weight $C$ is secured (see Fig. 1). Construct and explain a graph of the speed of the load $C$ versus time after its release. The thread and block are weightless. Do not take into account the resistance force and thickness of the loads.

3. A sled with a person with a total mass of $m = 80$ kg rolls down from the top of a mountain (angle of inclination to the horizon $\alpha = 30^\circ$) and, having traveled a distance of $l = 40$ m along a horizontal plane, stops. How much snow melted during snow movement, if the snow temperature is $t = 0^\circ$ C, the friction coefficient $k = 0,02$ is constant all the way and all the work against the friction forces goes towards melting the snow?
