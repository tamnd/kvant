Exercise

1. The two friends haven't seen each other for many years. They met, they started talking, and one bragged to the other that he already had three children. “How old are they?” asked the second one. “The product of their years is equal to 36, and the sum is the number of this passing tram.” After looking at the tram number, the second owner said that this data was not enough. “And my eldest son is red-haired,” “Then I know how old they are,” said his friend and accurately named the age of each child.

How old was each child?

2. At the next math club lesson, each student received 8 cards with numbers. It was necessary to arrange the cards in two lines (4 cards per line) so that the sums of the numbers in the lines were equal to each other.

One of the players arranged the cards in one line as shown in figure, and immediately told the commissions that the problem had no solution. Why did the student, without doing detailed calculations, make such a statement? And how do you still solve the problem?

3. Decipher the example for addition:

$$\frac{+88}{88}$$

4. Turn over a non-specialized bottle of kefir and tap the bottom so that the kefir splashes (to be careful, continue this all over a deep plate). What happens to the lid and why?

5. Two people carry a log, holding it at equal distances from the ends. The third, wanting to help, takes hold of the log in front of the first. Will it be easier for those carrying the log? (Or: what will the second one say?)
