$m_1 = 30$ e of hydrogen, in the other $m_2 = 160$ e of oxygen. Hydrogen can diffuse through the partition. What pressures will be established in the vessel after the diffusion process stops, if the vessel is always at a constant temperature $t = 27^\circ\text{C}$.

Solution. The diffusion process will stop when hydrogen is evenly distributed throughout the entire volume. The first half of the vessel will contain only hydrogen, the second half will contain hydrogen and oxygen. Moreover, the hydrogen pressure

![Рис. 2.](image)

in the first half is equal to the partial pressure of hydrogen in the mixture in the second half of the vessel. If the hydrogen pressure denotes $P_1$, then

$$P_1 \frac{V}{2} = \frac{m_1}{2} \mu_1 RT = 73,8 \text{ amm}$$

($\mu_1 = 2 \text{ e/mоль} — молекулярная масса водорода$).

In the second half of the vessel the pressure is equal to

$$P = P_1 + P_2,$$

Where

$$P_2 \frac{V}{2} = \frac{m_2}{\mu_2} RT$$

($\mu_2 = 32 \frac{e}{mol} — молекулярная масса кислорода$).

Thus

$$P = \left( 2 \frac{m_2}{\mu_2} + \frac{m_1}{\mu_1} \right) \frac{RT}{V} \approx 123 \text{ amm}.$$

Now let's focus on another important issue - the work done by the gas. If a gas expands at constant pressure, it does work

$$A = P \Delta V,$$

where $\Delta V$ is the change in gas volume. It is not at all difficult to prove this formula. Let the gas be in a cylinder with a piston whose area is $S$. Then the force $F$ acting on the side of the gas is equal to $PS$ and when the piston is mixed over a distance $\Delta l$ the gas does work

$$A = F \Delta l = PS \Delta l.$$

Because

$$S \Delta l = \Delta V,$$

That

$$A = P \Delta V.$$

As the volume of gas decreases, $\Delta V < 0$ and the gas does negative work. This means that external forces acting on the piston do positive work.

What to do if the pressure in the vessel is not constant? For example, does it change with volume as shown in figure 2? The change in gas volume can be divided into small sections, such that during each of them the pressure can be considered constant. In each of these sections, the pressure exerted by the gas is equal to the product of the gas pressure and the change in its volume. This product is equal to the area of ​​the straight line allocated on figure. The work done by the gas during the entire process is equal to the sum of these products. At $\Delta V \rightarrow 0$ this sum tends to the area of ​​the figure limited by the graph $P(V)$ and the axis $V$.

So, to find the work done by a gas during a certain process, you need to draw a graph of its pressure versus volume and find the area of ​​the figure under the graph.
