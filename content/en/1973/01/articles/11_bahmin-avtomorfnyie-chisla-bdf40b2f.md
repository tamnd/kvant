Since the force acting on an electron is proportional to the distance of the electron from the center of the atom, the average force is equal to half the force acting on the electron on the surface of the atom (it is equal to $\frac{e^2}{R^3} R = \frac{e^2}{R^2}$):

$$F_{cp} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{e^2}{R^2}.$$

Because

$$A_2 = F_{cp} R = \frac{1}{2} \frac{e^2}{R^2} R = \frac{1}{2} \frac{e^2}{R}.$$

The total work required to remove an electron from the center of an atom over a large distance is equal to

$$A = A_1 + A_2 = \frac{e^2}{R} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{e^2}{R} = \frac{3}{2} \frac{e^2}{R}.$$

This work by condition is equal to $W$:

$$\frac{3}{2} \frac{e^2}{R} = W.$$

Automorphic numbers

Have you heard anything about automatic numbers? These are numbers that reappear at the end of their squares. Some of these numbers are familiar to you: 5 (5x5=25), 6 (6x6=36), 25 (25x25=625). However, the values ​​of the automaton numbers are 625 and 376. It turns out that automaton numbers of a higher order are obtained from automaton numbers of the previous order, if a re-unit digit is added to them (which, however, can be zero, so there is only one four-digit automaton number: 9376, and 90625 is the only five-digit automaton number).

Automorphic numbers can be arbitrarily large. Here, for example, is a 100-digit automatic number: 395300 731910816980293850989006216 650958086381100055742342323 089610900410661997739225625 9918212890625.

This number was discovered in 1964. You can write a couple of them yourself. It turns out that there is a fairly simple relationship between automata numbers of the same order, and knowing one of them, you can easily discover the second. Try to find this dependence and write the second one in any number system: the base of the system should not be a prime number or its powers. The first suitable system will be six-digit; try to find a couple of two-digit machine numbers in it.

V. I. Bakhmin
