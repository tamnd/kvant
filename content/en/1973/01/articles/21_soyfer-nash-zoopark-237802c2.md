Quantum for junior schoolchildren

Our zoo

Our zoo has opened! We are in a hurry to share this news with you. The first to appear were two cheerful geese, and then a Bactrian camel. How did we get them? In order to answer this question, make 12 figures from cardboard, shown in figure 1, and try to make them first into geese (Fig. 2) - both white and gray - two cheerful geese, and then a camel (Fig. 3).

You have probably already noticed that each of the figures from which we put together the inhabitants of our zoo consists of six identical regular triangles, and all six triangles can be bypassed by a “triangular rook”, which moves as shown in figure 4. We will call such figures a triangular hexamino. There are exactly 12 different triangular hexaminoes, and for a camel there are only 11. Having completed this task, try to form a new little animal from the same figures. Our zoo welcomes new residents!

If you are interested in this game, then you can read more about this kind of games and the mathematical problems associated with them in M. Gardner’s book “Mathematical Puzzles and Entertainment” (Mir Publishing House, M., 1971).

*) Two figures that transform into each other when flipped are considered to be one and the same figure.

A. Yu. Soifer.
