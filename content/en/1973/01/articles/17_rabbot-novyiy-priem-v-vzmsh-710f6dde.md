New example in VZMSH

Unlike previous years, this year 7th grade students will be admitted to the All-Union Correspondence Mathematics School. This is due to the fact that now training at VZMSH will be not two-year, but three-year.

Schoolchildren admitted to VZMS will, regularly once a month, receive assignments and brochures that outline various issues in mathematics.

Working on VZMS problems will help children take a deeper, and often even a new look at the issues they study at school. Much attention is also paid to improving logical culture. Some standard problems, the coordinate method (including the concept of four-dimensional space), functions and their graphs, sequences, limits, trigonometric functions, various questions of geometry, methods for solving equations and inequalities - this is an incomplete list of VZMS tasks. For each topic, you need to complete a test, which the teacher will check and explain in detail all the mistakes, tell you what else needs to be improved, and what questions to answer.

Studying at this school is not easy, but it is interesting. It is especially necessary for those children who live in rural areas and small towns - after all, they do not have the opportunity to attend mathematics clubs and schools at institutes, like schoolchildren living in large scientific centers.

Since VZMSH does not have the ability to accept all applicants (their number is usually 5-6 times greater than the number of places), we have to check the competitive admission. To get into the VZMSH, you need to solve the problems of the introductory test well. The competition is the smallest for children from cela, a little more for schoolchildren from small towns and working-class villages, and quite high for residents of large cities and scientific centers.

Children who complete all the tasks of the VZMSH receive a certificate of completion.

J. M. Rabbot

New measurement of the speed of light

Well known formula
$$c = \lambda v$$

where $c$ is the speed of light, $\lambda$ is its wavelength, $v$ is the frequency. The natural way to measure the speed of light would seem to be to measure $\lambda$ and $v$. However, until recently, the accuracy of such measurements was not sufficient for this method to compete with the accepted method of measuring the time it takes light to travel a known path.

The difficulty is that they were able to measure frequency well in the region of the microwave spectrum, and wavelength in the visible region.

Last year, it was possible to measure the frequency of light emitted by a red laser (wavelength 6330 Å = 6330·10⁻¹⁰ m). The frequency was measured to 7 digits:
$$v = 473 \times 1216 \pm 29 \text{ Мц}$$
(1 Mgacycle is a million vibrations per second).

From this we obtain the value for the speed of light
$$c = 299 \times 792 \pm 18 \text{ м/c}$$

(“18” is a possible error). Pleasant speed of light value
$$c = 299 \times 792 \pm 100 \text{ м/c}$$

has an error 6 times larger.
