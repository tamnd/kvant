In front of you are five unusual chessboards. In each of the positions you need to complete the task below, related to the rearrangement of the pieces, and it is enough to know only how they move. Such problems are called permutation problems on a chessboard (the word “permutation” is a synonym for permutation). Remutation problems are composed by many famous chess composers. Among these problems there are very complex ones, the solutions of which consist of more than a few solutions. In the proposed positions, the dimensions of the “chessboards” are not large, and the tasks will be completely within your power. Of course, in each position the task must be completed in a minimum number of moves.

Fig. 1. Swap the white and black knights. The order of moves is arbitrary (a knight of the same color can make several moves in a row), but knights of different colors must not hit each other.

Fig. 2. The same task as in the first position (knights of different colors should not fall under 碍), but here white and black knights move alternately.

Fig. 3. Move the white king to square a1, do not become it on b2.

Fig. 4. Eat the black knight with the white king, provided that the knight is not mobile and the king cannot be in check (on square c2).

Fig. 5. Swap the places of the white and black complexes so that on no move the complex colors threaten each other (white and black complexes move in turns).
