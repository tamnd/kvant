Thermal balance of the Earth

B. M. Smirnov

Almost all the non-nuclear energy we deal with on Earth is the result of the conversion of solar radiation energy. This includes the chemical energy of fossil fuels, water energy, wind energy, and much more.

The main source of solar radiation is thermonuclear reactions occurring in the depths of the Sun.

The main purpose of these reactions can be determined by the following scheme:

$$2p \rightarrow d + e^+ + v + 0,42 \text{ Mэв},$$
$$d + p \rightarrow He^3 + \gamma + 5,49 \text{ Mэв},$$
$$2He^3 \rightarrow He^4 + 2p + 12,85 \text{ Mэв}.$$

Here $p$ is a proton, $d$ is a deuteron, that is, a nucleus consisting of a proton and a neutron, He$^3$, He$^4$ are gel nuclei containing three and four nucleons of the corresponding one (a nucleon is a proton or neutron), $e^+$ is a positron, $v$ is a neutrino, $\gamma$ is photon The particle energy written on the right side of the equation turns into heat, is transferred to the surface of the Sun, and then radiated. The only exception is the neutrino, which immediately leaves the Sun, carrying away an energy equal to 0.26 MeV. The positron enters into an angulation reaction with electromagnetic waves, and the wavelengths corresponding to the maximum energy of this radiation are shorter, the higher the body temperature.

The total flux of radiation from the surface of the Sun at all wavelengths is determined by the Stefan-Boltzmann law: $j = \sigma T^4$, where $j$ is the flux of radiated energy, $T$ is the absolute temperature of the surface, $\sigma = 5,69 \cdot 10^{-12} \text{bm/cm}^2$ erad$^4$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

We will assume that the internal energy of the Sun is released only as a result of the described nuclear reactions associated with the conversion of hydrogen into helium. (In fact, it is possible for helium to further transform into heavier elements with the release of energy.) Then, since the processing of one proton results in the release of 6.3 MeV energy, the total energy reserve of the Sun is 6.3 × 6.6 10⁵⁶ MeV = 6.7 10⁴⁴ J. Every second, energy j⊙·4πR²⊙ = 3.9·10²⁶ W is emitted from the surface of the Sun, where j⊙ = 6.4 kW/cm² is the radiation power per unit solar surface, R⊙ = 7·10¹⁰ cm is the radius of the Sun. It follows that with a constant amount of energy emitted by the Sun per unit time, the nuclear reserves of solar energy will last for 55 billion years. In this regard, a person has no reason to worry.

On the surface of the Sun, all the radiation power comes to an area of ​​4πR²⊙, and at a distance l from the Sun - to an area of ​​4πl². Consequently, the energy flux of solar radiation at a distance l from the Sun is equal to j⊙·R²⊙/l², where j⊙ is the energy flux on the surface of the Sun, R⊙ is the radius of the Sun. From here we find the power of radiant energy falling on the surface of the Earth:

πR²⊙·j⊙/R²⊙/d²⊙ = 1.75·10¹⁷ W,

 where R⊙ = 6.4·10⁸ cm is the radius of the Earth, d⊙ = 1.5·10¹² cm is the average distance from the Earth to the Sun.

A small part of the power of solar radiation reaches the surface of the Earth (less than a billionth part), but on our earthly scale this power is large. For clarity, let's give an example. The main part of the energy consumed by humans is released during the combustion of fossil fuels (oil, gas, coal). If we take into account all the earth's fossil fuel reserves, we can get an example of 2·10²³ J. The Earth receives such energy from the Sun in less than 14 days. From the same example we can see how little of the solar energy sent to the surface of the Earth over a long period of time was converted into fossil fuel energy.

What happens to the solar radiation energy that hits the Earth's surface? Some of it is reflected from the surface, the other part is reflected from the surface by the Earth's atmosphere and is converted into other types of energy.

How is absorbed energy converted? The mass of the Earth is M⊙ = 6·10²⁷ e, the average specific temperature of the Earth is 0.5 √2f/ε·radd. This means that heating the Earth by one degree requires energy of 3·10²⁷ J. Such energy is absorbed by the surface and atmosphere of the Earth in about 1000 years. Thus, the solar energy absorbed by the Earth during its existence (4.5 billion years) is not accumulated, but is dissipated in the surrounding space.

Let's find out how solar energy absorbed by the Earth returns to space. There are two ways to position the Earth. One of them is associated with the escape of atoms and molecules beyond the atmosphere, the other with radiation from the Earth's surface. In the first case, for a molecule to exit the Earth’s surface, it is necessary that its speed in the direction perpendicular to the Earth’s surface exceeds the second speed √2gR⊙ ≈ 11√κm/s. For a nitrogen molecule, for example, this corresponds to the energy mv²/2 = mg R⊙ = 2.9×10⁻¹³ grains (m is the mass of molecules). At room temperature, the average final energy of molecules is 2×10⁻¹⁴ grains. There are very few molecules in the Earth's atmosphere. Moreover, if

They appear at the surface of the Earth, then, when they collide with other molecules, they give off excess energy and cannot go beyond the Earth’s atmosphere. Therefore, the mechanism of energy dissipation associated with the escape of atoms and molecules beyond the Earth's atmosphere turns out to be ineffective.

The second way to return solar energy into space is radiation. The temperature of the Earth's surface (≈300 °K) is significantly lower than the temperature of the solar surface (6000 °K). The maximum energy of solar radiation corresponds to wavelengths in the visible part of the spectrum, and the wavelengths of Earth's radiation lie in the far infrared region. Let's find out what temperature the Earth would have if it radiated energy with a power of 10^17 m as a completely black body. From the Stefan-Boltzmann law we find that the power of radiation emitted by the Earth in this case is equal to σT⁴4π R². In this case, the temperature of the Earth should be 240 °K.

The difference between the obtained value and the actual temperature of the Earth's surface is not so great, especially if we take into account that we can only very approximately assume that the Earth radiates as a completely black body.

The model used makes it possible to present a general qualitative picture of the processes occurring in the Earth's atmosphere during the conversion of solar radiant energy into Earth radiation energy. It is clear that the energy of the Sun is unevenly distributed over the surface of the Earth. In this regard, energy flows from warmer to colder areas appear in the Earth's atmosphere: winds arise. Wind movement concentrates approximately 2% of the radiant energy absorbed by the Earth.

Most of the radiant energy (approximately 4·10^16 Vm) goes to the evaporation of water. Water vapor is carried away by directed atmospheric currents, so that the amount of water evaporation and precipitation at each point on the Earth does not coincide. This is compensated by flows of water on the surface of the Earth - rivers, which carry considerable power - approximately 3·10^15 m.

Water plays an important role not only in absorbing solar energy, but also in absorbing radiation from the Earth. Water molecules effectively absorb photons emitted by the Earth's surface. As a result, thermal radiation is delayed. This explains the two types of climates - marine and continental.
