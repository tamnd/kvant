In the “Syllabus for entrance examinations in mathematics” there is, in particular, the following item: “Complex numbers and arguments for actions on them. Modulus and argument of a complex number." Applicants are not required to strictly construct the theory of complex numbers, but they must be able to manipulate these numbers and know their geometric interpretation.

This article examines several tasks taken from application experience. Each problem essentially uses only basic definitions and a geometric interpretation of some concepts associated with complex numbers.

Task 1. Draw a bowl on the plane corresponding to the number $z = \frac{2+i}{i-2}$.

Solved and e. First of all, let's represent this number in algebraic form:

$$\frac{2+i}{i-2} = -\frac{(2+i)(2+i)}{(2-i)(2+i)} = -\frac{3+4i}{5} = -\frac{3}{5} - \frac{4}{5} i.$$

Now it is no longer difficult to depict the number $z$ on the complex plane - you get a point in the third square (draw it yourself!).

Task 2. The complex number $z = a+bi$ is represented by the point $M(a, b)$. Where is point $z_1 = z + 3 - 2i$?

Solved and e. We have $z + 3 - 2i = (a + 3) + (b - 2) i$.

The positional number $z_1 = (a + 3) + (b - 2) i$ is represented by the point $M_1(a + 3, b - 2)$, which is obtained from the point $M$ with two dimensions to the right by three units and downwards by two units.

Task 3. Complex numbers $z$ are considered as well as $|z| = 2$. Where are the points $2z - 3i + 1$ located?

Solved and e. Points $z$ for which $|z| = 2$ are located on a circle of radius 2 with center at point $(0, 0)$ (Fig. 1). Then the points $2z$ will lie on a concentric circle of radius 4. In fact, the point $2z$ lies on the same ray emanating from the origin as the point $z$, but is 2 times farther from the origin than the point $z$.

Point $2z + 1 - 3i$ is obtained from point $2z$ with two dimensions to the right by one and two dimensions down by three units. Therefore, the points $2z + 1 - 3i$, where $|z| = 2$, form a circle with a center at the point $(1, -3)$ and radius 4.

Task 4. Which set of points in the complex plane is given by the condition $2 < |z + 1 - 2i| \leq 3$?

Solved and e. Let $z_1 = z + 1 - 2i$; then this condition takes the form

$$2 < |z_1| \leq 3. \quad (1)$$

Points $z_1$ satisfying conditions $|z_1| = 3$ lie on a circle of radius 3 centered at the origin. Condition $|z_1| < 3$ is satisfied by all interior points of a circle of radius 3 centered at the origin. Condition $|z_1| > 2$ is satisfied by all points lying in

A circle of radius 2 with center at the origin. Thus, the set of points $z_1$ satisfying condition (1) is a ring between concentric circles with a center at the origin and radii 2 and 3 (Fig. 2); the points inside the circle do not belong to this set.

Since $z = z_1 - 1 + 2i$, the point $z$ is obtained from the point $z_1$ by moving one to the left and two units up. Therefore, the final result is a ring between concentric circles of radii 2 and 3 with centers at point $(-1, 2)$ (the inner circle is excluded).

Task 5 (MSU, Faculty of Economics, 1970). Indicate the powers of the plane corresponding to those complied numbers that satisfy the condition $|z| + i + z = 2 + i$.

Solution. Let $z = a + bi$; then this condition will take the form

$$i\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} + a + bi = 2 + i.$$

By defining the equality of complex numbers, from here we obtain a system of equations

$$\begin{cases} a = 2, \\ \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} + b = 1, \end{cases}$$

which is easily solved. As a result, we find the number $z = 2 - \frac{3}{2}i$ that satisfies the assignment conditions.

Problem 7. Complex numbers $z$ satisfy the conditions

$$|z+2+i| = |z-1-4i|.$$ (4)

Where are the dots representing these numbers located?

Solution. As is known, $|z_1-z_2|$ is the distance between the points representing complex numbers $z_1$ and $z_2$. Therefore the number

$$|z+2+i| = |z-(-2-i)|$$

 is the distance from the point representing the number $z$ to the point $M(-2, -1)$, and the number

$$|z-1-4i| = |z-(1+4i)|$$

 is the distance of the point representing the number $z$ to the point $N(1, 4)$. Condition (4) then means that the required points are equidistant from the points $M$ and $N$. Consequently, the required points lie on a line perpendicular to the segment $MN$ and passing through its middle.

Problem 8. Indicate the set of points representing numbers, representing complex numbers that simultaneously satisfy two conditions

$$|z-2i| \leqslant \frac{3}{2}, \frac{\pi}{3} < \arg z < \frac{2\pi}{3}.$$ (5)

Solution. The dots representing those complex numbers that satisfy the first of conditions (5) fill a circle (including a circle) of radius $3/2$ centered at the point $(0,2)$ (Fig. 4). The second condition is satisfied by complex numbers representing the points that are located between the rays emanating from the origin and the composition

$$0 < |z|^2 - |z| + 1 < 6 + 3 |z|,$$ (6)

Solution. Inequality (6), according to the properties of logarithms, is equivalent to the double inequality

$$0 < \frac{|z|^2 - |z| + 1}{2 + |z|} < 3,$$

which can be rewritten (since $2 + |z| > 0$ for any $z$) in the form

$$0 < |z|^2 - |z| + 1 < 6 + 3 |z|.$$

But the inequality $|z|^2 - |z| + 1 > 0$ is valid for any $z$ (since the discriminant of the square trinomial $x^2 - x + 1$ is negative), and the inequality $|z|^2 - |z| + 1 < 6 + 3 |z|$, that is, $|z|^2 - 4 |z| - 5 < 0$ is satisfied for $|z| < 5$ (the inequality $x^2 - 4x - 5 < 0$ is satisfied for $-1 < x < 5$, but $|z| \geqslant 0$ for any $z$). Therefore, conditions (6) are satisfied by complex numbers $z$ satisfying conditions $|z| < 5$; these numbers fill the interior of a circle of radius 5 centered at the origin.
