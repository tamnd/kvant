Martin Gardner

In «Kvant» No. 5 for 1972, we talked about the published book by the bright and original American popularizer of mathematics and physics Martin Gardner, “Mathematical Puzzles and Entertainment” *). Let us remember that this book contains forty-six sequences on various mathematical topics, previously published at different times in the Scientific American magazine (section “Mathematical Games”)).

Now the publishing house "Mir" has published thirty-eight more lines of M. Gardner, outlined in one book called "Mathematical Documents**). Looking ahead, let's say that we enjoyed the second book even more than the first.

First, a few words about M. Gardner himself.

In 1946, a busy seminar of the famous American philosopher Rudolf Carnap, specialized mainly in mathematical logic, probability theory and physics, was attended, among other students, by a certain Martina Gardner, who a few years later appeared with tape recordings of his speech and offered himself as ... editor.

Gradually, Martina Gardner, who began to write to a mature, developed reader, who did not necessarily have a special mathematical or physical education, came to several creative guidelines, which are especially clear in his “Mathematical Doctrines.”

Firstly, in presenting ideas one should rely only on what the general reader knows well and what he will be able to reproduce independently, without trickery. The more work the reader does on his own, the more necessary the results and conclusions turn out to be for him, the greater the satisfaction he will receive.
