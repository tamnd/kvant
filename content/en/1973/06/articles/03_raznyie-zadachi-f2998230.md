Finally, we note (for the reader familiar with the concepts of multidimensional geometry) that the above general formulation of Gülden’s theorem is applicable to the $n$-dimensional ενClidean space - only together with the plane over we consider the $(n-1)$-dimensional plane, and as the $l$ we must take the $(n-2)$-dimensional plane. The number $k$ in this theorem can (in the case of a $n$-dimensional space) belong to any of the values ​​1, 2, . . . , $n$.

And so as not to upset readers who are not familiar with the concepts of multidimensional geometry, we formulate the resulting theorem for $n=2$ (that is, for the case of a plane).

Gylden's theorem on the plane. Let there be a $(k-1)$-dimensional figure $F$ (here $k$ is any of the numbers 1, 2) located on a straight line on one side of the point $O$. By $S$ we denote the $(k-1)$-dimensional volume of the figure $F$, and by $R$ we denote the distance of its center by masses from the point $O$. When rotating the figure $F$ around the point $O$, a $k$-dimensional figure is obtained, the $k$-dimensional volume of which is calculated by the formula

$$V = S \cdot 2\pi R.$$

In particular, $k$ for $k=1$ (that is, in the case when the figure $F$ consists of several segments, Fig. 10), this formula allows you to calculate the sum of the areas of several concentric wheels.

Miscellaneous tasks

1. Eliminate $x, y, z$ from equations
   $$xy (x+y)=c,$$
   $$yz (y+z)=a,$$
   $$zx (z+x)=b,$$
   $$xyz=d.$$

2. Construct a triangle $ABC$ ($BC>AB>CA$) given the angle $A$ and the sums of the lengths of the sides $AB+BC$ and $AB+AC$.

3. Solve system of equations
   $(ax)^{1/2}a=(by)^{1/2}b,$
   $b^{1/2}x=a^{1/2}y.$

4. Find the quotient of division by $p-1$ of a number written in the $p$ number system using $p-1$ units (that is, the number 111...1). How is this quotient written in the $p$-ary number system?

5. Three points $A, B$ and $C$ lie on the same line. A circle with center at point $A$ is constructed. Find the geometric location of the intersection point of the lines connecting the points $B$ and $C$ with the dynamic opposite points of the circle.

6. Three circles pass through the points $M$ and $N$. Find the locus of points $P$ such that the tangents $PA, PB$ and $PC$ drawn from the point $P$ to these circles ($A, B, C$ are points of tangency) satisfy the relation $PA \cdot PB = k \cdot PC^2$.

7. Prove that if $p$ is a prime number, then the remainder when $C_{p-1}^m$ is divided by $p$ is $(-1)^m$.

8. Prove that if $p$ is a prime number greater than three, then $\frac{1}{2} C_{p/2}^p - 1$ is divisible by $p^3$.
