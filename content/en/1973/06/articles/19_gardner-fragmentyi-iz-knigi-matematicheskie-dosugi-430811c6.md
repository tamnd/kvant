Execution by surprise and the logical paradox associated with it

The convict was thrown into prison on Saturday.

“You will be hanged,” the judge told him, “on one of seven days next week.” But on what day this should happen, you will only find out on the morning of the execution.

The judge was famous for always keeping his word. The convicted person returned to his cell, accompanied by a lawyer. As soon as they were left input, the defender grinned with satisfaction.

- Isn’t it clear? - he exclaimed. - After all, the judge’s sentence cannot be carried out!

- How? “I didn’t understand anything,” the prisoner muttered.

- Now it will be explained. Obviously, you cannot be hanged next Saturday: Saturday is the last day of the week, and on Friday afternoon you would already know for sure that you would be hanged on Saturday. Thus, you would have known before the official notification on Saturday

in the morning, therefore, the judge's order would have been violated.

“That’s right,” according to the conclusions.

“So, Saturday, of course, disappears,” the lawyer continued, “so Friday remains the last day when you can be hanged.” However, you cannot be hanged on Friday, or after Thursday there would be only two days left - Friday and Saturday. Since Saturday cannot be the day of execution, you must be hanged only on Friday. But since you become aware of this on Thursday, then the judge’s order again also disappears. So, the last day you could still be executed is Thursday. However, Thursday is also not good, because if you remain alive on Wednesday, you will immediately understand that the execution must take place on Thursday.

- Everything is clear! - he exclaimed in conclusion, perking up. - In the same way, I can exclude Wednesday, Tuesday and Monday. Only tomorrow remains. But tomorrow I will probably not be hanged, because I know about it today!

And suddenly, to the great surprise of the condemned man, on Thursday morning a cane appears in the cell. The condemned man, of course, did not want this, but the most amazing thing is that the sentence turned out to be perfect - it can be carried out in full accordance with the wording.

What is the contradiction here?

Game of rope

It is worthy of regret that the art of making patterns has not become widespread;

It could be especially useful in the hands of teachers of language classes, medical secretaries, caring for patients forced to stay in bed for a long time, and psychiatrists who recommend manual labor as a form of therapy.

To satisfy the appetites of the readers, I will explain how to make one of the simplest and most famous diamond patterns. It is called "Osage diamonds" because this pattern was first shown by Indians using Ssige flames, but in the USA it is more often called "Jacob's lectice". The reader must take a meter and a half of soft cord, tie its ends and “show what he can do.” With a little practice, you can build Jacob's lectice in less than 10 seconds.

Using the index finger of your left hand, separate a section of the cord between the thumb and thumb of your right hand (the index finger of your left hand should pass between the sections of the cord thrown over the index finger of your right hand) and move your left hand to the right. Another rope task

Of a heel of these objects, no two weigh the same. These heels of objects must be arranged in a row as their weight increases. We have a lever scale with two bowls, but no weights. How to solve the problem with no more than seven ropes?

It is clear that for two objects one rope is enough. The item will have to be roped three times. Let the first rope determine that A is also hers than B. Put B and C on the scales. If B turns out to be heavier, then the problem will be solved with the help of two ropes; if C turns out to be heavier, then a third rope will be required, with the help of which we will compare objects C and A. If you have four objects in front of you, then you can easily get by with the fifth rope.

When the number of objects reaches the heel, the task ceases to be correct, and with a further increase in the number of objects to be roped, the difficulty of the task increases very quickly. As far as I know, there is no general method yet for ordering n items using the smallest possible number of strings.
