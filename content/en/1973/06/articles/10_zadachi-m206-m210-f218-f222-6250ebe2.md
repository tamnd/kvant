Problem "Quenta"

Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than August 30, 1973 to the address: 117071, Moscow, V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka Publishing House, Quantum magazine. After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: “Problem “Quaenta”, M206, M207” or “... F218”.

Solutions to problems in each subject (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be sent in separate envelopes. Original problems proposed for publication are assigned along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope place: “Problem “Quaenta”, a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”). Please send assignments from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes.

Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking your decisions).

Problems of increased difficulty are marked with an asterisk.

After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Tasks

M206-M210, F218-F222

M206. Given an infinite sequence of numbers. Prove that for any natural number $n$ coprime with the number 10, it is possible to indicate in the sequence a group of consecutive digits such that the number written by these digits is divisible by $n$.

V. Ufnarovsky

M207. Given two triangles $A_1A_2A_3$ and $B_1B_2B_3$. Describe the vector of triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ triangle $M_1M_2M_3$ with the largest area, similar to triangle $B_1B_2B_3$ (in this case, vertex $A_1$ must lie on line $M_2M_3$, vertex $A_2$ on line $M_3M_1$, vertex $A_3$ on line $M_1M_2$).

N. D. Nazaev

M208. It is known that the difference between the most areas and the most notified numbers

$$x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots, x_{10}$$

is equal to 1. Which

a) most,
b) most can be the difference between most and most of 10 numbers

$$x_1, \frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \ldots; \frac{x_1+x_2+\ldots+x_{10}}{10}$$

What will be the answer if the numbers are not 10, but $n$?

V. B. Peller

M209. For any triangle $ABC$ we can calculate the following sum:

$$S = tg^2 A/2 + tg^2 B/2 + tg^2 C/2.$$

Prove that

a) $S < 2$ for all ostrular and right triangles;

b) $S > 2$ for obtuse triangles with an obtuse angle greater than 2 argument $^4/3$;

c) among triangles with an obtuse angle $\phi$ such that $\frac{\pi}{2} < \phi < 2\arctg \frac{4}{3}$, there are those such that $S > 2$ and those such that $S < 2$.

M. L. Gerver

**M210*. A distance sequence consisting of 3000 digits 1 and 2. In such a sequence, any two adjacent triples of digits are placed in places. Two sequences are said to be equivalent if one of them can be transformed into the other by several such permutations. How many non-equivalent sequences are there?

**G. A. Gurevitz**

**F218. A train travels at a constant speed $v = 72 \text{ km/ч}$ along a horizontal section of track. How much should the power developed by the locomotive change so that the train continues to move at the same speed during a strong vertical rain? Consider that every second $m = 100 \text{ кг}$ water falls on the composition, which then flows to the ground along the walls of the varons. Neglect the change in friction forces.

**WITH. M. Kozel**

**F219. A vertical cylinder contains $n$ moles of an ideal monatomic gas. The cylinder is closed on top by a piston of mass $M$ and area $S$. Initially, the piston is held motionless, the gas in the cylinder occupies a volume of $V_0$ and has a temperature of $T_0$. The piston was then released, and after a few vibrations it came to rest. Neglecting in the calculations all forces of friction, as well as the heat capacity of the piston and cylinder, find the temperature and volume of the gas at the new position of the piston.

The entire system is thermally ash-lined. Atmospheric pressure is $P_a$.

**F220. The screen is illuminated by a parallel point of rays perpendicular to the plane of the screen. How does the illumination of the screen change if in the path of light we place a face $ABC$ with a small angle $\alpha$ and a refractive index $n$, and the face $AB$ is parallel to the screen (Fig. 1)? Neglect the reflection of light from the sign.

**F221. On a stationary circular cylinder of radius $R$ lies a doxa, as shown in Fig. 2. The thickness of the doxa is $h$. At what ratio between $h$ and $R$ will the doxie equilibrium be stable? The friction between the dock and the cylinder is great.

**IN. D. Krivenkov**

**F222*. The lightning rod is connected to the ground using a thin-walled tube with a diameter of 2 cm and a wall thickness of 2 mm. After the udram, the tube instantly turned into a round rod. Explain this phenomenon and estimate the strength of the discharge current if it is known that when rolling a cylindrical sample with a diameter of 3 mm, made of the same materials as the tube, it is destroyed at a force of 140,000 N.

**P. L. Kapsha**
