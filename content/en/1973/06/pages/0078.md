Already in the Middle Ages, a method was known for multiplying numbers from 6 to 10, that is, numbers greater than 5. In 1492, the general rule for using two additional numbers up to 10 was defined. (The complement of the number $n$ is equal to 10 - $n$.) To multiply 7 by 8, their complementarity is taken: 3 and 2. The difference between any of the two original numbers and its unpaired complement determines the digit tens, that is, in our case, 5, and the product of the additional one determines the second digit of the result, that is, $2 \times 3 = 6$, the final result is 50+6=56. The application of this method to multiplication on fingers boiled down to the following. The fingers of each hand are numbered from 6 to 10, starting with mintzitz. To multiply 7 by 8, the number 7 finger of one hand is connected to the number 8 finger of the other hand (Fig.2). Complementism 7 is the top three fingers of the left hand, and complementism 8 is the top two fingers of the right hand. The sum of all the remaining fingers of both hands determines the tens digit, that is, 5. To the pentadesty we add the product of the upper fingers 2 and 3, the answer is 56. This simple method of using the fingers to calculate the product of any pair of numbers from 6 to 10 was widely used during the Renaissance period and, speaking of saying, was still used by peasants in some areas of Europe.
