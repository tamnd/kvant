The usual solution is quite cumbersome: you need to find the volumes of two truncated cones with generators BC and CD (Fig. 5) without the volumes of two full cones with generators AB and AD. However, if we continue sides BC and CD until they intersect with the rotation axis at points M and N, then the required volume of a significant crust is calculated as the sum of the volumes of two complete cones with generatrices CM and CN without a satisfactory volume of the body formed by rotation of the triangle ABM (the sum of the volumes of complete cones with generators AB and BM). Double volume because it would be necessary to subtract the sum of the volumes of the bodies obtained from the rotation of two triangles ABM and ADN. But one of these triangles can be combined with the second using a carrier parallel to the axis. Therefore, the volumes of these bodies of rotation are equal to each other.

In the same way, when calculating surfaces in this problem, the lateral surfaces of truncated cones can be replaced by the lateral surfaces of full cones, which also reduces the calculations.
