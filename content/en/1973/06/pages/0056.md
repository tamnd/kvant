⟦folio 54⟧

In the “Syllabus for entrance examinations in mathematics” there is, in particular, the following item: “Complex numbers and arguments for actions on them. Modulus and argument of a complex number." Applicants are not required to strictly construct the theory of complex numbers, but they must be able to manipulate these numbers and know their geometric interpretation.

This article examines several tasks taken from application experience. Each problem essentially uses only basic definitions and a geometric interpretation of some concepts associated with complex numbers.

Task 1. Draw a bowl on the plane corresponding to the number $z = \frac{2+i}{i-2}$.

Solved and e. First of all, let's represent this number in algebraic form:

$$\frac{2+i}{i-2} = -\frac{(2+i)(2+i)}{(2-i)(2+i)} = -\frac{3+4i}{5} = -\frac{3}{5} - \frac{4}{5} i.$$

Now it is no longer difficult to depict the number $z$ on the complex plane - you get a point in the third square (draw it yourself!).

Task 2. The complex number $z = a+bi$ is represented by the point $M(a, b)$. Where is point $z_1 = z + 3 - 2i$?

Solved and e. We have $z + 3 - 2i = (a + 3) + (b - 2) i$.

The positional number $z_1 = (a + 3) + (b - 2) i$ is represented by the point $M_1(a + 3, b - 2)$, which is obtained from the point $M$ with two dimensions to the right by three units and downwards by two units.

Task 3. Complex numbers $z$ are considered as well as $|z| = 2$. Where are the points $2z - 3i + 1$ located?

Solved and e. Points $z$ for which $|z| = 2$ are located on a circle of radius 2 with center at point $(0, 0)$ (Fig. 1). Then the points $2z$ will lie on a concentric circle of radius 4. In fact, the point $2z$ lies on the same ray emanating from the origin as the point $z$, but is 2 times farther from the origin than the point $z$.

Point $2z + 1 - 3i$ is obtained from point $2z$ with two dimensions to the right by one and two dimensions down by three units. Therefore, the points $2z + 1 - 3i$, where $|z| = 2$, form a circle with a center at the point $(1, -3)$ and radius 4.
