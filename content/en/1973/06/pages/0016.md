Introduction
The word “friction” brings to mind the moving parts of machines, sleds sliding over a snowy slope, and metorites heating up as they fly through the atmosphere. In one case, it is impossible to do without trenya, in the other, they do everything possible to get rid of it. The slalom skier and the design engineer have to reckon with everything; Because a passerby complains about the lack of traffic, and physicists at LAB are looking for ways to reduce traffic by at least a fraction of a percent. But, perhaps, no matter who thinks about friction, everyone imagines some kind of macroscopic body (a leg, a wheel, a steamboat) interacting with the environment or with other bodies.

Is it possible to talk about friction during the movement of a microsoptic particle, for example, an electron?

In this article we will look at the movement of electrons in a metal. It is known that electrons perform random, chaotic movement. But if a current flows through a conductor, then, in addition to the otic thermal movement, there is also a directed movement of electrons.

It is very difficult to describe the behavior of each electron individually. Therefore, we will replace the movement of all particles with a stream of identical “average” particles moving in the same direction with the same average speeds.
