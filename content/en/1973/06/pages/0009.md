Problem 3*. A plane broken line consisting of n equal segments of length a and connected in the form of a snake at an angle α friend to friend. Determine the surface formed by this line about the axis that passes through one of its ends parallel to the bicsectrix of α*).

The traditional solution to this problem is quite cumbersome; it involves summing an arithmetic progression (more precisely, the first n odd numbers) and fully justifies the “asterisk” - a sign of increased difficulty, which appears next to its number. Meanwhile, Golden's theorem allows you to solve the problem very briefly, almost in your head.

In fact, the center of gravity of the broken line is, as is easy to see, at a distance of $\frac{na}{2} \sin \frac{\alpha}{2}$ from the axis of rotation, and the length of the broken line is equal to na (Fig. 4). Hence,

$$S = P \cdot 2\pi R = na \cdot \pi na \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} = \pi n^2 a^2 \sin \frac{\alpha}{2}.$$

Otherwise, from figure 4 it is not difficult to see an elegant solution to the problem using the traditional method: it is easy to prove that the desired rotation surface is equal to the lateral surface of the cone with generatrix AM, which serves as a continuation of the last link AB.
