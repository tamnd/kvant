Let us now move on to the proof of Golden's first theorem. First of all, we will prove that this theorem is valid if the plane line discussed in the theorem is a $n$-link broken line in which all links have the same length $m$. We denote the midpoints of the links of the broken line by $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$, and the distances of these points from the straight line $l$ by $r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n$ (Fig. 2). When rotating the polyline under consideration, the spicy vector $l$ produces a surface (Fig. 3) consisting of $n$ parts, each of which represents the lateral surface of a truncated cone. Since the lateral surface of a truncated cone is equal to the product of the length of the generatrix and the circumference of the middle section, the area of ​​the resulting rotation surface is equal to
$$S = m \cdot 2\pi r_1 + m \cdot 2\pi r_2 + \dots + m \cdot 2\pi r_n.$$

Noting that the length of the broken line in question (Fig. 2) is equal to $P = nm$, we can rewrite the expression for the area as follows:
$$S = P \cdot 2\pi R,$$
Where
$$R = \frac{r_1 + r_2 + \dots + r_n}{n}.$$

But the center of gravity of the broken line, that is, the center of gravity of the system of points $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$, in each of which the mass $m$ is concentrated, according to the lemma, is separated from the straight line $l$ at a distance $R$. This means that in the particular case under consideration, Golden’s first theorem is valid.
