The sudden appearance of red patterns from the chaos of rope weaves is one of the most pleasing features of most rope manipulation.

Those who have perfectly mastered the art of making rope patterns can enjoy performing in “doubles,” when two players hold the same loop: one with the right hand, the other with the left hand. When playing together, it is not difficult to simultaneously weave two identical patterns on two loops (the first loop will be placed on the right hand of the first player and on the left hand of the first player and on the second hand of the second). It is much more difficult to weave two different patterns at the same time. Such a trio requires enormous skill and precise coordination of movements.

Another rope task

Of a heel of these objects, no two weigh the same. These heels of objects must be arranged in a row as their weight increases. We have a lever scale with two bowls, but no weights. How to solve the problem with no more than seven ropes?

It is clear that for two objects one rope is enough. The item will have to be roped three times. Let the first rope determine that A is also hers than B. Put B and C on the scales. If B turns out to be heavier, then the problem will be solved with the help of two ropes; if C turns out to be heavier, then a third rope will be required, with the help of which we will compare objects C and A. If you have four objects in front of you, then you can easily get by with the fifth rope.

When the number of objects reaches the heel, the task ceases to be correct, and with a further increase in the number of objects to be roped, the difficulty of the task increases very quickly. As far as I know, there is no general method yet for ordering n items using the smallest possible number of strings.
