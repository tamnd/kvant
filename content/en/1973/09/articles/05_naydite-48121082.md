⟦folio 23⟧

Chu can be divided into five equal parts. Let's take out four cell nuts again and one for the monkey. It worked again!

Let's do this six times. Four cell nuts will remain and come out unscathed. They only played the role of a catalog and helped us reduce the problem to the one already considered: we need to find the smallest pile of nuts (with four cells!) such that $1/5$ of the nuts can be separated from it six times. The smallest number of nuts in such a pile is $5^6$, so the sailors had to collect $5^6 - 4 = 15621$ nuts.

Problem 11. We added four nuts, but they never arrived. Nevertheless, the monkey received six nuts. Where did they come from? After all, in a simpler problem there were no extra nuts left!

Puzzle design

Find 4 identical cubes in your children's toys (or glue them together from thick paper) and paint them with four colors as shown in figure 9 - each face is painted with one paint. On our figure the front faces of the cubes are not completely painted so that the coloring of all other faces is visible. As a result, you will get a fun, constructive puzzle for yourself and your friends.

Problem 12. From the four cups made, we create a composite parallelenid $1 \times 1 \times 4$ so that all 4 colors are represented on each of its side faces.

This is a difficult puzzle, since for a given color of the edges there is only one of 41,472 different multi-colored parallelepipeds, satisfying the conditions of the puzzle, is there a choice from 41,472 options?

Problem 13. What calculations led to the conclusion that in a practical solution to the puzzle there is a choice of 41,472 options?

Problem 14. To prepare a puzzle with four cups, the following coloring of the faces was presented: 7 red, 6 blue, 6 green and 5 cells. Instead, come up with a new coloring for the sides of the four cups, which would result in 6 single-sided sides, and so that the puzzle has one solution.
