VII ALL-UNION OLYMPIAD FOR SCHOOLCHILDREN IN MATHEMATIC AND PHYSICS

In April, the VII All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics (in Chisinau) and physics (in Leningrad) took place.
Below we place materials dedicated to these Olympiads.

M. L. Smolyansky

All-Union Olympiads

Various mathematical competitions in the history of mathematics have been known for a long time. History has brought to us information about mathematical show jumping held back in the 13th century (Kingdom of Naples). However, these were mathematical modernities, in which the greatest scientists of those times took part.

The history of school physics and mathematics Olympiads dates back to 1894, when the first mathematics Olympiad for schoolchildren (the so-called “Eotvos competition”) was held in Hungary. This, which has already become traditional, Olympiad is held annually in Hungary in October.

In the Soviet Union, the first mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in Leningrad in the spring of 1934 *). The initiators of this Olympiad were Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences B. N. Delaunay (Chairman of the Olympiad Organizing Committee) and Professor V. A. Tartakovsky.

At the initiative of the Moscow Mathematical Society, the First Moscow Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in the fall of 1935. Moscow mathematicians greeted her with great enthusiasm. The organizing committee included all the major Muscovite mathematicians (academician P. S. Aleksandrov - chairman of the organizing committee, academicians A. N. Kolmogorov and S. L. Sobolev, corresponding members of the USSR Academy of Sciences L. A. Lyusternik and L. G. Shinirelman, professors V. F. Kagan, S. A. Yankovskaya and others).

314 schoolchildren took part in the first (qualifying).type of the Moscow Olympiad, and 120 schoolchildren took part in the second (final).type.

Until the Great Patriotic War, mathematical Olympiads in Moscow were held annually and very soon gained universal recognition. During the war, Moscow mathematicians held Olympiads in Ashgabat and Kazan. After the Patriotic War, holding mathematical Olympiads became a tradition in many cities of the Soviet Union. These Olympiads were organized through the joint efforts of local public education authorities, universities and pedagogical institutes.

The total number of mathematical Olympiads held in our country is very large. The Olympics are held in many cities across the country. Often, in one city during one academic year, several Olympiads are held by different educational institutions. The number of Olympiads held in Moscow is not easy to count: Olympiads are held by Moscow State University, Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, Moscow Automobile and Road Institute, Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys, Moscow Economic and Statistical Institute and much more.

Following the mathematicians, physicists, chemists, astronomers, geographers, biologists, and then linguistics began to hold their olympiads.

In 1960, the organization of the XXIII Moscow Mathematical Olympiad, together with the Ministry of Education of the RSFSR, held a mathematical Olympiad on a larger scale. Teams from the 13th region of the RSFSR and 9 union republics took part in it. These Olympiads were called the All-Russian Mathematical Olympiads.

This experience seemed very successful, and since 1961, All-Russian Mathematical Olympiads began to be held regularly. They are held in four rounds: school, district, regional and final.

The first Olympiads in physics were held by Moscow University back in 1938. Since then, these Olympiads have become a tradition for Moscow schoolchildren.

Subsequently, such Olympiads began to be held by the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT) and other universities in Moscow. The initiative of Muscovites was picked up in other cities of the country.

In February 1962, the first general physical and mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren took place, conducted on the initiative of MIPT. Over 6,500 schoolchildren from 58 cities of the Soviet Union took part in it. It was held in one round during the winter holidays.

The form of holding the Olympics is interesting. Students and graduate students of the institute held it during the winter holidays in their hometowns. And then solving reduction problems in Moscow, where they were tested. All work on organizing the Olympics was headed by the Komsomol committee.

As a result of the Olympiad, more than 400 schoolchildren received awards and honorable mentions. In 1963, MIPT and the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University jointly held the second Physics and Mathematics Olympiad. Schoolchildren from the European part of the USSR and Transcaucasia took part in it. The first round of this Olympiad was correspondence, and the second was full-time.

Since 1962, the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences began to hold All-Siberian Physics and Mathematics Olympiads.

Since 1964, physics and mathematics Olympiads have been held jointly under the leadership of the Ministry of Education of the RSFSR. These Olympiads were called the All-Russian Physics and Mathematics Olympiads*).

Since 1967, the All-Union Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry Olympiads (All-Union Olympiads) have been held.

All-Union Olympiads by the Ministry of Education of the USSR together with the Ministry of Higher and Secondary Specialized Education of the USSR, the Central Committee of the Komsomol, the USSR Academy of Sciences, the All-Union Society "Knowledge", the All-Union Central Council of Trade Unions and scientific and technical societies. For the direct management of the All-Union Olympiad, the Central Organizing Committee of the All-Union Olympiad for Schoolchildren was created, the transmitter of which is Academician I. K. Kikoin. The organizing committee elects the central authority of the Olympiad by subject.

*) In 1965, chemists joined the All-Union Physics and Mathematics Olympiad.

All-Union physics and mathematics olympids are held in four rounds: school olympids, regional olympids, resource (or regional) olympids and, finally, the final round.

Four winners from 8-10 classes from each region, region, autonomous and united resource and winners of the first and second prizes of the final type of the All-Union Olympiad are invited to the final round of the next All-Union Olympiad.

The final round of the All-Union Olympiad is held in April separately (in different cities) for mathematicians and physicists.

The winners of the Olympids are awarded special prizes and certificates of achievement. From among the winners of the All-Union Physics and Mathematics Olympiad, the USSR team is formed - a participant in the International Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Mathematics and Physics.

By decision of the Ministry of Higher and Secondary Specialized Education of the USSR, participants in the International Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Mathematics and Physics are accepted in accordance with the above educational information without exams.

Beginning with the IV All-Union Mathematical Olympiad (1963), special badges are issued that are awarded to all participants in the Olympiad.
