The force of friction often causes serious difficulties for university applicants, especially the force of static friction. What is its magnitude? How is it directed? Let's try to answer these questions by looking at a few specific examples. The problems discussed in the article were offered at the entrance exams to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology in different years, and many applicants were unable to cope with it.

First of all, let us recall some features of the forces of dry friction that arise between two solid bodies. With the direct interaction (contact) of these bodies, forces arise that act on each of them. According to Newton's law of friction, these forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. The components of these forces, directed perpendicular to the contacting surface, are called normal pressure forces. The components directed along the surface are called friction forces.

Let the body lie on a horizontal table. We will act on it with a horizontal force, the magnitude of which gradually increases. As long as this force is less than a certain value $F_{\text{max}}$, the body will maintain a state of rest because the static friction force, equal in magnitude to the applied force, acts on the body from the side of the body. the direction of the friction force is opposite to the resulting displacement. If there was no friction, the body would immediately begin to slip. You can say that the body is starting to slip, but friction keeps it in place. If the magnitude of the impact is large $F_{\text{max}}$, the possibility arises that does not depend on the magnitude of the forces acting on the body along the surface:

$$F_{\text{ck}} = kN.$$

The magnitude of the normal pressure force $N$ does not depend either on the magnitude of tangential interactions or on the properties of the collapsing surfaces.

Experience shows that the total $F_{\text{ck}}$ is somewhat less than $F_{\text{max}}$. However, the difference between this is not great, and when solving almost all problems it is assumed that $F_{\text{ck}} = F_{\text{max}}$. This approach has become so familiar that its generality is not even limited. The dependence of friction force on speed is applied in the same way. Let us now move on to the analysis of specific problems in the solution of which the features of friction forces play a significant role.

Problem 1. The train, approaching the station at a speed of $v = 72$ km/h, begins to freeze evenly. What is the shortest time for a train to freeze until it comes to a complete stop that is safe for sleeping passengers? Coefficient of passenger friction on the shelf $k = 0,2$.

When the train slows down, the speed of movement of the shelf on which the passenger is lying decreases, and if the passenger maintained the same speed, he would begin to slide forward along the shelf, in the direction of the train. However, as soon as it starts, or rather, as soon as it “attempts” to slip, a frictional force arises. She informs the passenger of the braking acceleration. If this acceleration is equal to the acceleration of the train, the speed of the passenger is always equal to the speed of the shelf on which he lies, and the passenger does not slide along the shelf. The maximum possible acceleration can be achieved by the maximum static friction force, which, as we have already said, is approximately equal to $F_{ck} = kN$. According to Newton's second law
$$ma_{max} = kN$$
where $m$ is the mass of the sleeping passenger, and $N$ is the force of his normal pressure on the shelf. For horizontal shelf $N = mg$ and $a_{max} = kg$. This means that the acceleration of the train, at which passengers do not fall from the shelves,
$$a \leqslant kg$$
Braking time to a complete stop
$$t \geqslant \frac{v}{a} = 10 \text{ с.}$$

Problem 2. A car, starting from a standstill, uniformly picks up speed, moving along a horizontal section of the road, which is a duo of a circle of $30^\circ$ radius $R = 100$ m. At what maximum speed can the car drive onto a straight section of the road? Co-

friction coefficient of wheels on the ground $k = 0,3$.

The only external horizontal force acting on car miles is the friction force. Acceleration, as we assume, occurs without slipping, therefore, we are dealing with the force of static friction. Only this force can provide the car with the necessary acceleration.

Since the movement of a car in a circle is an accelerated movement, the friction force must be directed at an angle to the speed (Fig.2). In this case, the component $F_k$, directed along the speed, imparts to the car the acceleration necessary for acceleration, and the component $F_u$, directed along the radius of the circle, changes the direction of speed so that the car moves in a circle. The centripetal acceleration is $a_u = \frac{v^2}{R}$, therefore $F_u = m \frac{v^2}{R}$.

The speed is maximum in the horizontal, which means that $F_u$ is also maximum then. Since, according to the conditions of the problem, the car picks up speed uniformly, the force $F_k$ is constant.

As is known, the distance traveled, acceleration and speed at the end of the path are related by the relation
$$2a_kS = v^2.$$
Hence $a_k = \frac{v^2}{2S}$, and strength $F_k = m \frac{v^2}{2S}$.

The geometric sum of forces $F_k$ and $F_u$ must not exceed the maximum static friction force $F_{tp} = kmg$. Since these forces are perpendicular to each other

Problem 3. A small cube of mass m rests on a rough plane inclined to the horizontal at an angle α. Friction coefficient k = 2tgα. Determine with what minimum horizontal force F, lying in the plane of the slope (Fig. 3), the cube must be silenced so that it begins to move.

When k > tanα, under the influence of only the gravity force, the cube will not slide along the inclined plane, since the projection of the gravity force $F_T = mg\sin\alpha$ directed along the inclined plane is less than the maximum static friction force $F_{TP} = kmg\cos\alpha$. If we apply a horizontal force F, then the resulting force acting along the inclined plane will be equal to (see Fig. 3)

$$F_p = \sqrt{F^2 + F_{TP}^2}.$$

If $F_p \geq F_{TP}$, the cube begins to slip, since the static friction force can no longer balance it. The minimum value required for this force F can be found from the condition

$$F^2 + (mg\sin\alpha)^2 = (kmg\cos\alpha)^2.$$
