THOMAS JUNG

and 200th anniversary of his birth

The outstanding English physicist Thomas Young was born in Milverton on June 13, 1773. Jung's childhood was unusual. At the age of two he read fluently, at the age of six he began to study Latin, at the age of eight he became interested in land surveying and learned to determine distances and calculate heights. At first he studied at home, then in private schools. By the age of fifteen, he had learned several modern and ancient languages.

In 1792, nineteen-year-old Jung decided to devote himself to medicine. A year later, he presented to the Royal Society*) his work “Observations on the Process of Vision,” which examined the question of accommodation*) of the eye. At that time there were different opinions on this matter. Some scientists believed that the eye as a whole is capable of deforming (stretching or contracting); others suggested that the lens could change its location; still others linked the ability to accommodate with changes in the curvature of the cornea; fourth - with a change in the curvature of the lens. Through numerous experiments on his own eyes, Jung refuted the first three points of view and showed that the accommodation of the eye is due to the deformation of the lens. Unfortunately, under pressure from the then authorities

*) Royal Society - Academy of Sciences of England.

*) Accommodation of the eye - adaptation of the eye to clearly seeing objects located at different distances.
