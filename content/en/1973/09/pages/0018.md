The density of the oxide film is greater than the density of air, and the density of the metal is greater than that of the oxide; but if the density of the medium above and below the film is simultaneously either greater or less than the density of the film, then different surfaces change the state of the wave differently, and a thin layer of the film becomes dark instead of being light, one stream of light destroys another instead of uniting with it *).

If you get a soap film and hold it upright, the top edge of the film will become very thin and almost black, while the lower areas will be lined with horizontal lines in a series of colored stripes (Fig.2).

When two glasses, one of which is slightly convex, are pressed together with some force, the layer of air between them causes the appearance of colored rings, starting with a dark spot in the center and becoming narrower and narrower as the curvature of the glass rapidly increases the thickness of the layer of air between the glasses. The black spot gives way to such a faint violet color that it is barely noticeable; followed by orange-yellow, dark red and blue (Fig. 3).
