9th grade

Measure the average specific heat of transformer oil in the temperature range 45-50°C or 50-55°C.

Available devices:
1. Calorietter is a chemical mill with heat-insulating foam.
2. Thermometer 0 - 50°C or two thermometers: 0 - 100°C (rough) and 50 - 100°C (fine).
3. Electric heater - stirrer. Heater resistance at test temperature 200 °m; permissible power released by the heater in liquid is 30 °m.
4. Milliammeter or voltmeter.
5. Rheostat 30 °m (approximately), permissible current 5 a.
6. Laboratory tripod.
7. Stopwatch.
8. The unit is powered from a 50V DC network (approximately).
9. Laboratory scales, oil, distilled water are placed on separate tables. Their use is with the permission of the jury members on duty.
The calibration of all devices can be considered ideal.

10th grade

1. Determine which of the diagrams given (see Fig. 11) is contained in your box (do not forget to justify your device).
2. Determine the values ​​of the elements included in the circuit.

To solve this problem, you have at your disposal: an alternating voltage source (sound generator) capable of generating a sinusoidal voltage with a frequency from 20 2c to 200 kHz, and a tube voltmeter. The sound generator is connected to pins 1 and 2 (see Fig. 11). The lamp voltmeter can be connected either to pins 2 and 4, or 3 and 4 (it is already connected to pin 4). The circuit assembled for your arrival is shown in figure 12. In addition, you have at your disposal three resistances of different sizes, which you can connect between the hatch pair of contacts.

When evaluating experimental work, taking into account independence in performing the experiment, reducing the need to correctly draw up a report on the work, which should describe all stages of the experiment, justify the methods used, process the experimental results, and evaluate the accuracy of the results obtained.

The maximum score that could be obtained for completing the experimental task was 15 points. Moreover, the jury developed a very detailed scale of main, deductible and penalty points. For example, when evaluating ninth-grader experimenters, the main points were given for the correct selection and calculation of the electrical circuit, the use of forced heating (maximum current through the resistance when the measuring device is turned off), for taking into account heat transfer losses, correct accounting of the heat capacity of the calorimeter, and for assessing the error of the experiment. Incentive points were given for maximum calculation of the heating rate, for explicit consideration of thermal inertia, and for correct calibration of the calorimeter (with great accuracy and minimal time consumption). Penalty (negative) points were awarded for conditions or actual pressure of any type, for using a thermometer as a measure, for errors in graphs, for computational errors.
