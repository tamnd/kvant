how already in his early youth he took an active part in the political life of Kyat, occupying a prominent position under the two Khorezm Shahs.

They returned in 1004 to Khorezm, to its new capital - the city of Gurganj *), Beruni holds the position of adviser to the Shah and heads the academy created by that time. Brilliant scientists invited by the Khorezm Shah from different countries are grouped around Beruni. The decoration of the academy was the Bukhara scientist Abu Ali and bn Sina (Avicenna, 980-1037), a natural scientist, doctor and philosopher.

After the capture of Khorezm by Sultan Mahmud of Ghaznavid in 1017, Beruni, along with other scientists, was forced to move to Ghazna (Afghanistan), where he lived until his last days. Here he studies astronomy, mathematics and geodesy. During the same period, Beruni visited India twice; There he not only carried out work to determine the size of the globe, but also collected general material on the history, geography, ethnography and philosophy of this country.

At the same time, he wrote the history of the development of mathematics in India, drew up a map of the world and developed a theory of the origin of the seas.

Very little is known about the last years of Berunia’s life: loneliness, old age and, as always, focused work. He had no family, very few students. Probably, Beruni saw his purpose in passing on his knowledge not to individuals, but to all people. “All my books are my children, and most people are blessed by their children and poems,” the scientist wrote. Hence his careful attitude towards every hour, every minute of his life - he tried to leave people everything he had, everything to the last thought.

Peru Beruni owns about 150 scientific works, relating to a variety of fields of science: astronomy, mathematics, mineralogy, geography, geodesy, history, linguistics and others. However, throughout his life he studied most closely astronomy, geodesy and mathematics.

His first major work, “Monuments Remaining from Past Generations,” briefly called “Chronology” (about 1001), includes a description of the calendar systems of various peoples (Greeks, Romans, Khorezmians, Arids, Ebvres, Persians, etc.), the history of the development of science, culture, customs, etc.

Very interesting information is that in ancient times the Arabs considered the time of day from sunset to sunset, and the Greeks, Byzantines and Persians - from east to east. The book reveals about the astronomical types of mezzans (lunar, solar and temporal), about the origin of the named mezzans among different peoples.

Twelve chapters of the “Chronology” are devoted to famous days and religious holidays of the ancient Persians, Greeks, Romans and others. This book did not reflect scientific value even today: it very fully reflects the history, religion and ethnography of the peoples of the Ancient East.

In 1025, Beruni completed his treatise “Defining the boundaries of places for studying distances between points”, the terms called “Geodesy”. In it, Beruni, the first of the Muslim astronomers, gives a detailed development of a method for determining geographic longitude by simultaneously observing a lunar eclipse from two points with a known longitude of one of them.

In India, communicating with scientists and artisans captured by Mahmud of Ghaznavid, Beruni learned details about their amazing homeland, its religion, customs.
