⟦folio 47⟧

Exercise
in mathematics

L. G. Limanov

This article will provide the texts of all the problems offered at the Olympiad. Almost all of them have already been published in «Kvant» (No. 7, 8, 1973) and their solutions will appear in accordance with the issues of the magazine. Here we also developed a path for problems that were not included in the Quantum Problem Book.

8th grade

First day

1. At the trial, 14 coins were presented as evidence. The expert found that coins 1 to 7 were counterfeit, and coins 8 to 14 were real. The court only knows that counterfeit coins weigh the same, real coins weigh the same, and that counterfeit coins are lighter than real ones. The Expert has at his disposal a scale without a scale.

a) The expert wants to prove to the court that coins 1 to 7 are counterfeit. How can he do this, using only three weighings?

6) Show that with three weighings he can prove even more: that coins 1 to 7 are counterfeit, and coins 8 to 14 are real.

2. Prove that a nine-digit number that contains all digits except zero and turns out to be the number 5 cannot be a perfect square of an integer.

3. Given $n$ points, $n > 4$. Prove that you can connect them with arrows so that from each point you can get to each point by following either one arrow or two (each two points can be connected by an arrow only in one direction; you can follow the arrow only in the direction indicated on it).

Second day

4. On the sides of the acute triangle $ABC$, three similar acute triangles $AC_1B, BA_1C, CB_1A$ (in this case $AB_1C = ABC_1 = A_1BC, BC_1A = B_1CA, CAB_1 = C_1AB$) are built to the outside.

a) Prove that the circles circumscribing triangles $AC_1B, BA_1C$ and $CB_1A$ intersect at one point.

6) Prove that the lines $AA_1, BB_1$ and $CC_1$ intersect at the same point.

5. $N$ people do not know each other. It is necessary to introduce some of them to each other in such a way that no three people have the same number of acquaintances. Prove that this can be done for any $N$.

6. The king walked around the chessboard $8 \times 8$, hitting each square once and with the last move returned to the original square (the king moves according to the usual rules). When we drew his path, connecting with segments the centers of the fields that he sequentially passes through, we got a closed broken line without self-intersection.

a) Give an example showing that the king could make exactly 28 moves along the horizontal and vertical lines.

6) Prove that he could not have made less than 28 such moves.

9th grade

First day

1. At the trial, 14 coins were presented as evidence. The expert found that coins 1 to 7 were counterfeit, and coins 8 to 14 were real. The court only knows that counterfeit coins weigh the same, real coins weigh the same, counterfeit coins weigh the same, and counterfeit coins are lighter than real ones. The expert wants to prove to the court by weighing on cup scales without gratings that all the counterfeit coins he discovered are indeed counterfeit, and the rest are real. Will he be able to do it?

2. Given an angle with vertex $O$ and a circle touching its side at points $A$ and $B$. A ray is drawn from point $A$ parallel to $OB$, intersecting the circle at point $C$. Line segment $OC$ intersects the circle at point $E$, and lines $AE$ and $OB$ intersect at point $K$. Prove that $OK = KB$.

3. Indeed, the numbers $a, b, c$ are such that for all numbers $x$ satisfying the condition $-1 \leq x \leq 1$ the following inequality holds:

$$|ax^2 + bx + c| \leq 1.$$

Prove that for these values ​​of $x$ the inequality also holds

$$|cx^2 + bx + a| \leq 2.$$
