Indeed, if we assume that the height of mountain $AM = H$ is known (Fig. 1), then, using astrology, changing the angle of the horizontal $\alpha$, we can (from $\triangle CBA$) write that

$$R = (R + H) \cos \alpha;$$

from here

$$R = \frac{H \cos \alpha}{1 - \cos \alpha}.$$

At the same time, to determine the point $B$, when sighting at which the angle $\alpha$ will be measured, you need to find “... such a place by the sea or in the desert and observe the Sun from the mountain at east or sunset until half of its disk disappears from our time.”

The radius of the globe, determined by Berunni using formula (1), turns out to be $R = 6399.58$ km, which differs little from the size of the radius of the Earth ($R = 6371.11$ km) that is currently used.

To determine the height of $H$ (say, a mosque), Berunni used an altimeter he created, with which he measured the segments $ND$ and $FQ$ (Fig. 2). Having positioned the shield $MNPQ$ of the altimeter in the vertical plane so that the side $QM$ would be directed to the point $A$ (sight through the diopters $m$ and $n$), using the alias $PZ$, rotating around the point $P$, sight through the diopters $P$ and $Z$ to point $A$. In this position, the shield is secured and the alias $PZ$ is attached to it.

Since $\triangle PND$ is similar to $\triangle AQP$ and $\triangle AFQ$ is similar to $\triangle ACQ$, then

$$\frac{AQ}{PQ} = \frac{NP}{ND}, \quad \frac{AC}{AQ} = \frac{FQ}{PQ},$$

where

$$AQ = \frac{NP \cdot PQ}{ND}, \quad AC = \frac{AQ \cdot FQ}{PQ}.$$

The height of $H = AC$ will be $H = AC = AQ \cdot \frac{FQ}{PQ} = \frac{NP \cdot PQ \cdot FQ}{ND \cdot PQ} = \frac{NP \cdot FQ}{ND}.$
