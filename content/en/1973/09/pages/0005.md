ABU RAYKHAN BERUNI
to the 1000th anniversary of his birth

The first half of the 11th century and the two centuries preceding it were characterized by the rapid development of the culture of the peoples of the Middle East and Central Asia. The creation and development of large green cities, flowering oases, irrigation systems, the formation of international trade routes, the construction of palaces and temples stimulated research in the fields of mathematics, geodesy, and astronomy.

Central Asia is one of the oldest centers of world culture. The works of Central Asian scientists had a great influence on the development of not only science in the Middle East, but also all of European science.

The works of Central Asian astronomers and mathematicians of the 9th-16th centuries are widely known. One of the middle places of honor belongs to the significant scientist Abu Rayhan Muhammad ibn Axmad al-Beruki.

This Great Man combined the best features of a scientist - dedication to science, an unquenchable passion for knowledge, determination and dedication.

Abu Raikhan Beruni was born on September 4, 973 in Uzbekistan in the city of Kyat *), the then capital of Khorezm.

He spent the first years of his life in the family of the Khorezm Shah Mamu and A.

Thus, widespread in the lower reaches of the Amu Darya, it is called Berunni (previously Berunni was considered an Arab scientist, and in Arabic his name sounds like Berunni).

There, his outstanding abilities were noticed by the famous scientist Abu Nasr Mansur and bin Ali and bin Iraq (approximately 950-1034), who instilled in young Beruni an interest in the natural sciences and especially in mathematics and astronomy. Remembering this period of his life, Berunni wrote in one of his last poems:

“The Iraqi family fed me with their milk, and their Mansur undertook to raise me...”

Having received an excellent education in his homeland, Berunni, already in his youth, made independent observations and around 995 he was the first in Central Asia to build a globe that made it possible to determine the geographical coordinates of settlements with sufficient accuracy for that time, was engaged in the construction of astronomical instruments (wall square, etc.) and determined the geographical coordinates of various places in Khorezm.

His inquisitive mind and constant desire to acquire knowledge could not be satisfied by the education received in the Iraqi family, and Berunni devotes a lot of time to the study of philosophy, mathematics, astronomy and other sciences. For the same purpose, he traveled to such large scientific centers as Baghdad and Bukhara and various cities of Khorezm and Afghanistan.
