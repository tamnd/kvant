⟦folio 51⟧

No. 4 (L). Given 11 natural numbers. Prove that it is possible to write out several of them in a row so that each digit appears an even number of times in the resulting number.

No. 5 (M). A $k$-gon is inscribed in a circle of diameter $D$. Draw $k - 3$ pairwise non-intersecting diagonals in it so that it splits into triangles. A circle is inscribed in each of the triangles; their radii are $R_1, R_2, \dots, R_{k-2}$. Prove that $R_1 + \dots + R_{k-2} < D$.

This is quite a difficult and beautiful task. Nevertheless, both teams did it.

No. 6 (L). Are there 99 distinct odd natural numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{99}$ such that $\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_{99}} = 1$?

During correspondence, the word “odd” was omitted from the conditions of this problem, which is why the solution turned out to be too simple. In the form in which the problem is presented here, the participants were unable to solve it completely.

No. 7 (M). The company "Horns and Hooves" purchased a counting machine on which to perform only one operation: $a*b = 1 - \frac{a}{b}$. Nevertheless, O. Bender learned to perform all four arithmetic operations on it. How?

No. 8 (L). A triangle is called “good” if all its angles are no more than 120°. A “good” triangle is divided into several triangles. Prove that among them there is at least one “good” one.

At this point, both teams simultaneously ran out of solved problems. The following tasks remained unsolved.

No. 9. See problem 1 of the first day of 9th grade with the following change: the expert knows that 6 may be fake, and the remaining 8 are real (and knows which ones are fake).

No. 10. In the square table $100 \times 100$ the diagonal cells are colored blue. A set of table cells is called a bing if it does not contain synchronized cells and, after some rearrangement of rows and columns, turns into a rectangle. What is the minimum number of bings (maybe overlapping) needed to cover

a) the entire document, except the diagonal,
b) just the bottom half of the dock under the diagonal (and, if necessary, part of the top, but without the diagonal).

No. 11. At figure there is a street map of the city of Ensk. It is required to launch several bus routes around the city so that exactly one bus passes along each section of the route (and only in one direction). How many ways can this be done? (Route directions are not taken into account).

Matoi participants also had the opportunity to score additional points by sharing their messages about unsolved or incompletely solved problems. The Leningraders thoroughly analyzed problem No. 6 in the correct formulation, and the Amockoviches explained part of the problem about “bings”.

In the end, the Muscovites won, having wisely structured their battle not only from a mathematical, but also from a tactical point of view. Score 28:22.
