2. After releasing the load $C$ (see figure in the conditions of the problem), the system begins to move with acceleration $a = g/3$. This occurs until the weight $A$ touches the stand. We consider the interaction of the weights with the stand and with each other (in the collision of weights $A$ and $B$) to be absolutely inelastic. The movement time at this stage is $t_1 = 2 \sqrt{\frac{3h}{g}}$; final speed $v_1 = 2 \sqrt{\frac{gh}{3}}$ (on the graph in coordinates $v, t$—an inclined straight line going up).

Then the load $C$ moves uniformly at a speed $v_1$ until the load $B$ touches the load $A$. Movement time $t_2 = t_1 = \frac{h}{v_1} = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3h}{g}} = \frac{1}{4} t_1$ (on the graph - a straight line parallel to the abscissa axis).

Then the load $C$ moves with acceleration $g$ directed downward (as in the case of the movement of a body thrown vertically upward). Having passed the highest point, the load $C$ lowers and at the moment $t_3$ begins to lift the load $B$.

$$t_3 - t_2 = 2 \frac{v_1}{g} = 4 \sqrt{\frac{h}{3g}} = \frac{2}{3} t_1.$$

The tangent of the angle of inclination of the velocity graph to the horizontal axis in absolute value is second greater than at the first stage (in the graph there is a straight line going down and crossing the abscissa axis).

At moment $t_3$ there is a boring loss of speed of the load $C$ (we consider the thread inextensible) in accordance with the law of conservation of momentum: $mv_1 = 2mv_2$; $v_2 = \frac{1}{2} v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{gh}{3}}$ (on the graph—a straight line parallel to the abscissa axis).

At moment $t_4$ the load $A$ begins to rise, and again there is a boring change in the speed of the load $C$. The law of conservation of momentum has the form: $2mv_2 = 3mv_3$; $v_3 = \frac{2}{3} v_2 = \frac{1}{3} v_1 = \frac{2}{3} \sqrt{\frac{gh}{3}}$ (straight line parallel to the ordinat axis).

Then the load $C$ moves equally slow (an inclined straight line going up and crossing the x-axis).

$$t_5 - t_4 = \frac{v_3}{a} = 2 \sqrt{\frac{h}{3g}} = \frac{1}{3} t_1$$

Starting from moment $t_5$ the pattern will repeat. In this case, the periodic movements of all systems decay, since the initial speed of the load $C$ tends to zero at $t \rightarrow \infty$.

2. We will solve the problem based on the law of conservation of energy. Some conditions are associated with the uncertain characteristics of the interaction of bodies upon impact. Since the ball drains after the impact, it is clear that the impact is elastic. But it may not be completely elastic, that is, part of the mechanical energy can turn into non-mechanical forms, for example, into thermal energy.

Law of conservation of energy upon impact:

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = \frac{MV^2}{2} + \frac{mv^2}{2} + Q,$$

where $Q$—non-mechanical types of energy,

$$v_0$$—ball speed before impact,

$$v$$—ball speed after impact,

$$V$$—velocity of the body after impact.
