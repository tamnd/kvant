Why do the centers of the episcicles of Mercury, Venus and the Sun always lie on the same straight line? Why do the orbital periods of Mars, Jupiter and Saturn in their episccles equal one earthly year?

There are many such problems of connection with the Ptolemaic system.

Copernicus's hypothesis was simple. It is necessary to place the Earth and the Sun in places in the old Ptolemaic system, leaving only the Moon to revolve around the Earth (Fig. 4). But this simple hypothesis was inaccessible to the understanding of most of Copernicus' contemporaries. For the first time in the history of science, the observed was only in his privileged position, and the question of the picture of the world from the point of view of another (moving relative to the observed) coordinate system was discussed. Such a step was revolutionary not only from the point of view of the church - the Earth and man ceased to be the main ones in the Universe - but also from the point of view of mechanics - nothing regarding movement had yet been used to solve specific problems. Having placed the center of the planetary system on the Sun, Copernicus immediately simplified its diagram.

After what has been said, the reader will understand in the Small Commentary, in which Copernicus briefly formulates his idea (see page 9).

According to Copernicus's scheme, the daily movement of the sky was declared to be the rotation of the Earth around its axis, and the annual movement - its revolution around the Sun. The second inequality has disappeared - the retrograde motion of the planets was a consequence of the different angular speeds of the Earth and other planets in their orbits. In a remarkable way, the need for the hypothesis about the rotation of the celestial sphere also disappeared: to declare the movement of the stars, it was enough to assume that the plane of the Earth’s orbit slowly rotates in the direction opposite to the movement of the Earth itself in its orbit.

But one should not think that Copernicus’ simple scheme completely solved the problem. Let us remember that the natural movement of the planets must achieve everything that the ancients tried to achieve for each planet with the help of episccles. However, it is a matter of opinion of Apollonius and the ancients that the motion of the planets does not turn out to be uniform, even after attributing this unevenness to the rotation of the Earth. Consequently, the planets do not move in concentric circles, but in a different way, which we will show later...”

Now we know that the variability of the speed of movement of planets in their orbits follows from Coperre's laws and does not need to be avoided in theory. But Copernicus remains captive to his laws of kinematics and strives to achieve uniformity of motion by introducing the same episccles and eccentrics (but without equants). As a result, his diagram of the planetary system turns out to be quite complex.
