February 19, 1973 marks the 500th anniversary of the birth of the Great Polish astronomical scientist Nicolaus Copernicus (1473-1543). This date is widely celebrated all over the world.

Most people will usually trust their practical experience. Convention says, for example, that in the morning the Sun rises in the east, moves across the sky and sets in the west in the evening. This practical experience was reflected in the doctrine of the construction of the Universe by the ancient Greek scientist Claudius Ptolemy, who argued that the Earth is at the center of the Universe, and the Sun, planets and stars do not move around. The teachings of Ptolemy are in good agreement with the Bible, which said that in creating the world, God first worked with the Earth. For many centuries, scientists made amendments to the Ptolemaic tables predicting various celestial phenomena, but the geocentric system of the world itself was never questioned.

Copernicus will respond with ethnological ideas about the structure of the Universe. He came to the conclusion that all planets move in circular orbits around the Sun and established that the Earth goes around the Sun in a year, while healing around its axis. Copernicus' theory revolutionized the structure and undermined the foundations of the teachings of the church.

About 50 stamps and blocks are associated with Copernicus. It is quite clear that the purest of such stamps were issued in his homeland in Poland. The first two stamps with a portrait of Copernicus were issued in Poland in 1923 (one of them is shown in the photo). In our magazine (No. 1, 1971) 17 stamps associated with Nicolaus Coperni were reproduced.
