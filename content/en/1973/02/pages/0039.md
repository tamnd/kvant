Let's consider a rocket engine fixed motionless, the so-called bench engine. When the mass μ is removed (we count both the fuel and the descriptor together), energy qμ is released, but only part of it is converted into productive kinetic energy μ $\frac{V^2}{2}$. Therefore $\frac{V^2}{2} < q$. If we enter the efficiency factor of the bench engine $\eta_0$ - the ratio of useful *) energy to expended energy - we get

$$\mu \frac{V^2}{2} = \eta_0 q \mu,$$

$$V = \sqrt{2 \eta_0 q},$$

that is, the flow rate for a given chemical reaction cannot exceed its maximum flow rate $V_0 = \sqrt{2 q}$ (since $\eta_0 < 1$).

Let's consider several chemical reactions and calculate the maximum flow rates for them.

1. Acetylene combustion:

$$2C_2H_2 + 5O_2 = 4CO_2 + 2H_2O + 611 \text{ ккал.}$$

The number of kilocalories on the right side of the reaction equation shows how much energy is released when a mass is added equal to the sum of the moles of fuel and the descriptor. In this case, with the side of two moles $C_2H_2$ and the heel of moles $O_2$, that is, only $2 \cdot 26 + 5 \cdot 32 = 212 \text{ e}$. Thus, the calorific value of this fuel

$$q = \frac{611}{212} \frac{\kappa \alpha l}{2} = 2880 \frac{\kappa \alpha l}{2} = 1,2 \cdot 10^7 \frac{m^2}{c^2},$$

and the maximum exhaust velocity

$$V_0 = \sqrt{2 q} = 4,9 \frac{\kappa m}{c}.$$

*) Note that the energy $\frac{\mu V^2}{2}$ is not truly useful. After all, the main task is to accelerate the useful mass to the required speed, and not the exhaust gases.

2. $2H_2 + O_2 = 2H_2O + 115,6 \text{ ккал.}$

We calculate similarly

$$q = 3220 \frac{\kappa \alpha l}{\kappa e}; V_0 = 5,2 \frac{\kappa m}{c}.$$

3. $2Al + \frac{3}{2}O_2 = Al_2O_3 + 394 \text{ ккал;}$

$$q = 3860 \frac{\kappa \alpha l}{\kappa e}; V_0 = 5,6 \text{ км/c.}$$

The highest possible maximum exhaust velocity is achieved in the combustion reaction of metallic beryllium:

$$Be + \frac{1}{2}O_2 = BeO + 146 \text{ ккал;}$$

$$q = 5840 \frac{\kappa \alpha l}{\kappa e}; V_0 = 7 \text{ км/c.}$$

The practical use of this reaction, however, is hampered by the fact that beryllium metal and its compounds are very poisonous.

$$\eta = \frac{mv^2}{2M_0 q} = \frac{v^2}{2q} e^{-v/V}.$$
