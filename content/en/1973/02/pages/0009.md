To the two circles a third was added - the equant. The center of the equant has the peculiarity that an observer located in it would see the uniform movement of the planet. In other words, although the angle $\phi$ (Fig. 3) changes unevenly with time, the angle $\phi$ increases proportionally with time. Thus, it was as if a fictitious planet was seen moving uniformly along the equant. The equant hypothesis (with the additional condition that the center of the equant is bisected) improved the theory, but it also turns out to be insufficient. Twice additional assumptions had to be made, for example, that the center of the equant itself moves along a circle or that each epicycle rolls along an epicycle.
