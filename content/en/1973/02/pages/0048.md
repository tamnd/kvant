(1) points $A_1, A_2, A_3, \dots$ lie on line $a$, points $B_1, B_2, B_3, \dots$ on line $b$;

(2) $A_1B_1 = B_1A_2 = A_2B_2 = B_2A_3 = A_3B_3 = \dots = 1$;

(3) the point $A_n$ is not the same as $A_{n+1}$ unless $A_nB_n \perp a$ (and similarly, $B_n$ is the same as $B_{n+1}$ only if $B_nA_{n+1} \perp b$). (It is easy to see that conditions (1) - (3) determine the sequence of jumps, starting from $B_1A_2$, uniquely.)

Prove that if the angle between straight lines $a$ and $b$ varies by a rational number of degrees, then the path of the bluebird will be periodic, that is, at some moment it will hit the initial point $A_1$ and then will successively pass the same points $B_2, A_2, B_3, \dots$ as at the beginning of the path, and if by an irrational number, then the bluebird will not hit any point more than twice.
