Rocket efficiency coefficient

A. V. Byalko

Let's start with a small task. How long does it take for an electric vehicle to consume as much energy as is necessary to launch it into Earth satellite orbit? (Power $P = 1 \text{ квт}$, weight $m = 1 \text{ ккэ.}$)

The kinetic energy of a satellite moving in a circular orbit is $\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{mgR}{2}$, where $v$ is the first escape velocity, $R$ is the radius of the Earth. The potential energy $mgh$ for slightly high orbits can be neglected compared to the Kinetic Energy of a Sputtering Kite $P_t$, we find that

$$t = \frac{mgR}{2P} = 3,2 \cdot 10^4 c \approx 9 \text{ u.}$$

Only 9 hours! 9 kilowatt-hours, 36 kopecks are needed to launch every kilogram of mass into orbit of an Earth satellite. Why does the implementation of a space program require costs commensurate with the national budgets of the countries involved?

Because this energy, 9 kWh, is necessary, but not enough. Because the racket as a telling machine has a very low efficiency. Let's try to find him.

First, we will derive a formula connecting the mass and speed of a rocket, that is, we will solve the “Tsiolkovsky problem.” The difficulty here is that the mass of the rocket decreases all the time during acceleration - the fuel burns out. Such processes are studied by solving differential equations. To avoid this, let us imagine that combustion does not occur continuously, but in certain small portions. Let's say, each time the rocket ejects the $\frac{1}{N}$th part of its mass, as if firing a projectile, the mass of which is $N$ times less than the mass of the rocket at this moment (Fig. 1).

The speed of such a “projectile” relative to the rocket will be considered constant and called the exhaust velocity $V$. With each such “shot” the law of conservation of momentum is fulfilled. Let the rocket's mass initially be $M_0$ and its speed equal to zero. Then $\frac{M_0}{N} V$ is the amount of motion of the mass chosen after the first push and $(1 - \frac{1}{N}) M_0 v_1$ is the amount equal to it.
