§ 5. Solution of the problem

Now it is easy to answer the question of the problem: at what $C_0$ does the dog reach the fox? — Only for $C_0$ lying on the half-line $l$ in front of the point $J_0$. The entire plane, except for this bow, is a safe zone for the fox. Indeed, if point $C_0$ lies in the indicated zone, then relative to the coordinate system $x, y$, moving with the fox, the dog will run along a parabola. After some time, she will cross the $x$ axis and end up behind the fox. And in this case, she will not be able to appear.

Thanks to the introduction of motion of the coordinate system, we can easily understand the finer details.

Moving from point $C_0$ along the parabola (A), the dog is constantly approaching its directrice $d$, and thereby its focus $O$. In other words, the distance $CJ$ between the dog and the fox is decreasing all the time. In this case (see Fig. 7) it tends to the limit equal to $a/2$. Let the coordinates of $C_0$ be $x_0, y_0$; the distance $C_0J_0 = \sqrt{x_0^2 + y_0^2}$ will be denoted by $r_0$; It’s quite easy to check that then $a/2 = (r_0 - y_0)/2$. Thus, the distance $CJ$, originally equal to $r_0$, cannot in any way become less than $s = (r_0 - y_0)/2$.

Can it become equal to $s$? This question will help us solve figure 6. The speed of the dog’s movement along the parabola decreases all the time. And although (in the coordinate system $x, y$) the dog needs to run a path of finite length so that $CJ$ becomes equal to $(r_0 - y_0)/2$, it cannot do this in a finite time. An accurate proof of this fact will require some calculations.

Let $C$ be a point on the parabola (A) with coordinates $(x, y)$, $E$ be the projection of the point $C$ onto the axis $x$; $u(x)$ is the speed at which $E$ approaches $O$. From figure 10 it is clear that $u(x)/v = x/\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. Using equation (A), we find that $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = y + a = (x^2 + a^2)/2a$, hence $u(x) = 2avx/(x^2 + a^2) < 2vx/a$.

Thus, at $a/2 \leq x \leq a$ the speed is $u(x) < 2v$; from $a/2$ to $a/4$ projection $E$ moves at speed $u(x)$, less than $v$; from $a/4$ to $a/8$ - at a speed of $u(x) < v/2$, etc. That is, it takes time to complete each of these segments, more than $T = \frac{a}{2}:2v = \frac{a}{4}:v = \frac{a}{8}:\frac{v}{2}$, etc.). This means that $E$ cannot reach the point $x = O$ in any finite time. In other words, $CJ$ is always greater than $(r_0 - y_0)/2$.
