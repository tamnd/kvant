This was the task that faced Plolem. How to combine Aristotle's principle of inertia with the real world? How to describe the real world based on kinematics alone?

Movement sphere

Ancient observers saw that the motion of the planets was complex. They divided this complex movement into several simpler ones. The first main movement was the daily movement of the sky, the second was its annual movement. All seven spheres, which pulled the planets along with them, participated in these two movements. The eighth sphere, to which the stars were attached, was motionless. Hipparchus also improved this system. To take into account the movement of the vernal equilibria point (the point of intersection of the equatorial plane with the Earth’s orbit), he attributed to the eighth sphere a slow movement of one degree per 100 years (36″ per year) *).

However, these simple movements did not satisfy Aristotle's principle of inertia. Their speeds were inconsistent. Therefore, they talked about the existence of two inequalities. The first is the uneven movement of the planets in their orbits; the second is the observed “retrograde” movement of the planets - a change in the direction of their movement across the sky to the opposite. Only the Sun and Moon did not have a second inequality. Therefore, the theory of Hipparchus already allows their position with an error that changes one minute. This inequality had to be dealt with, and Plolemey dealt with it magnificently.

Eccenter and equant

Spring had a witty way out of a seemingly hopeless situation. It must be assumed that it is not the planet itself that moves in a circle around the center of the Universe (the Earth in the Plolemian system), but only the center of another circle, called the epicycle, moves along the main orbit, called the deferent *). As a result of such construction, it turns out (a good exercise for the reader!) that the planets still move in a circle (called the eccenter), but its center is shifted relative to the Earth.

Thus, the important kinematic equivalence of the movement pattern of the excycle along the deferent and the equant was established.
