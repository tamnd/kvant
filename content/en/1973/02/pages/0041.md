About the fox and the dog

M. L. Gerver

This note is unrelated to this:
“fox” and “dog” are simply convenient conditions for breeding for two moving lobes, one of which is “pushing to water” the other.

§ 1. Formulation of the problem
A fox runs in a straight line at constant speed. The dog chases the fox at the same speed, and runs in such a way that he sees the fox all the time in front of him. The fox runs from point L₀ (Fig. 1) in the direction indicated by the arrow. The dog is at the initial moment of being at point C₀. At what C₀ will the dog catch up with the fox?

Now it is difficult to establish who came up with this beautiful problem - it has been offered for many years at the Moscow State University to students who have not yet learned what a derivative is. After the theme “Derivative” was included in the mathematics school curriculum, they began to tell schoolchildren about the fox and the dog, warning them in advance that this was a “problem with two stars.”

At the beginning of 1972, we proposed a problem about a fox and a dog in the 10th “b” class of the 57th school in Moscow. The first in the class to solve it (in different ways) were Kolya Nadrashiel, Volodya Lidsky and Serge Glyadkov*). However, the solution (thought up by S. Glyadkov) was simplified so much that it became completely “elementary.”

This solution (which does not require familiarity with the concept of derivative) will be given below. Before reading it, try, of course, to solve the problem yourself. maybe it didn’t work out (I already warned you that the task is difficult) - then try to at least guess the answer. It's quite unexpected.
