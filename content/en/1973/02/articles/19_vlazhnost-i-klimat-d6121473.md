Humidity and climate

It is known that water has a greater heat capacity than air. When heating water by 1° C, it is necessary to expend 3000 times more heat than when heating the same mass of air.

The average surface temperature of the world's oceans is 17.4° C, while the average air temperature over the entire globe is 14° C. Thus, in winter, the world's oceans serve as a kind of giant “stove” that warms the land.

True, heat exchange between water and air occurs somewhat in a timely manner. There are several ways to help in the air: air humidity plays a significant role. Let us cite this very loved child from the diary of one of the participants in the experiment on Elbrus in August 1927.

“On August 23, after 5 o’clock, one of us brought a bucket of water for the household needs of the women from the stream that flowed from the Maly Azay glacier. By 6 o’clock the water was covered with an ice sheet at an air temperature of +8.8°, which shows a large terrestrial radiation caused by the low water content in the atmosphere.”

In fact, we should say the glorious words “large terrestrial radiation” instead of the more correct ones - “integis evaporation”. In such conditions as, say, in Murmansk, the “warming” of the climate is inevitably accompanied by an increase in humidity. This happens because the climate of Murmansk is determined by the warm Gulf Stream, which heats the coastal waters of the Barents Sea. Few people know that if water, in the process of heat exchange with air, lost heat by another 1 degree more, then the average air temperature for Murmansk would increase by 10 degrees!
