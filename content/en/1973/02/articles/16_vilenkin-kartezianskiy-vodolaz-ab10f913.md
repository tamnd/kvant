⟦folio 69⟧

CARTHUSIAN DIVER

A paper boat floats easily on the water, but when the paper gets wet, the boat sinks. The dry boat holds the air on the surface of the water, staying for a year with a dome. If the dome gets wet and spreads, the air will escape from under it and the boat will sink. Is it possible to make it so that the air either comes out from under the dome, then enters, and the ship either sinks or suffers - according to our desire?

It turns out that it is possible. For the first time such a toy was made by the great French scientist and philosopher Rene Descartes, and now it is called a “carte”.

Press the film and the “diver” will be pulled to the bottom. Let go and the “diver” will float to the surface. That's why he's drowning. When you press on the film, the air underneath it is compressed, increasing the pressure in the bottle and forcing some more water into the bottle. The bubble becomes heavier and sinks. As soon as you release the film, the pressure in the bottle decreases, the air rolled into the bubble expels excess water, and the “diver” floats up.

Now try to solve the following problem (if you do the “diver”, you can check the experiment).

Task. As air pressure increases above the surface of the water, the “Cartesian diver” drowns. Does the “diver” go down to the day with a slight increase in pressure, or can it stop “on the way” before reaching the day (that is, can you remove the rubber film and use this toy as a barometer, marking on the bottle the dive depths of the “diver” corresponding to different pressures above the water)?

A. N. Vilenkin
