⟦folio 67⟧

Television physics and mathematics courses for university applicants

In this issue we continue to publish materials from television courses.
The following topics were studied in the physical department of courses during January - February:
1) Electric charges. Electric field.
2) Conductors and dielectrics in an electric field. Electrical capacity.
3) Electrostatics. Problem solving.
4) Electric current. Conduction mechanisms. Ohm's law for a section of a circuit.
5) Electromotive force. Ohm's law for a complete circuit. Joule-Lenz law.
6) Electric current. Problem solving.
We invite you to use your strengths in solving some typical problems.
1. Two metal balls have the same charges. The magnitude of each charge is $q = +10^{-9}$ k. After connecting the balls with a thin conductor, their potential became equal to $\phi = 120$ v. Determine the radius of the first ball if the capacity of the second is $C_2 = 10$ kkf.
2. The horizontal arrangement of the tonxe of the prickly radius $R = 20$ cm is uniformly charged, the charge density is $q = +10^{-7}$ k/cm. A body of mass $m = 5$ e and charge $q_1 = -10^{-8}$ k begins to fall from the center of the ring. Determine the acceleration of the body at the moment when it is at a distance of $h = 30$ cm from the plane of the ring.
3. Until February, during telecourses in mathematics, in addition to the topics listed in the previous issue of the Kurnal, the following topics were also studied:
in planimetry - triangles and polygons similar to plane figures, metric relationships between the elements of plane figures, circles, areas, the use of trigonometry in solving planimetric problems;
in the “functions and graphs” order - properties of functions and their application to constructing graphs of functions, application of graphs of functions, mutually inverse functions, exponential and logarithmic functions, logarithms and their properties.

We offer the reader of the magazine a test on these topics.
CONTROL WORK No. 2
1. The bases of a trapezoid are $a$ and $b$. Find the length of the segment connecting the midpoints of the diagonals.
2. On sides $AB$ and $BC$ of triangle $ABC$ squares $ADKB$ and $BEPC$ are constructed. Prove that the segment $KE$ is 2 times larger than the median $BM$ of the triangle $ABC$.
3. The bissertrix of an angle of a triangle divides the opposite side into segments 4 cm and 2 cm, and the height dropped to the same side is equal to $\sqrt{15}$ cm. Find the side lengths of the triangle if it is known that they are expressed in integers.
4. A regular triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in a circle. A projection point $M$ is taken on arc $BC$ and connected by chords to the vertices of the triangle. Prove that $AM = BM + CM$.
5. A square is constructed on the hypotenuse $AC$ of a right triangle $ABC$. The center of the square is connected to the vertex $B$. What segments is the hypotenuse divided into if it is known that the legs of the triangle are equal to 21 cm and 28 cm?
6. In the trapezoid $ABCD$ the diagonals intersect at the point $O$. The area of ​​triangle $BOC$ is $b^2$, the area of ​​triangle $AOD$ is $a^2$. Find the area of ​​the trapezoid ($AD$ and $BC$ are the bases).
7. In a triangle, one of the angles is equal to $60^\circ$, and the side opposite to this angle is divided by the point of tangency of the inscribed circle into segments $a$ and $b$. Find the area of ​​the triangle.
8. Calculate $491-0,25\log 25$
9. Compute $\log_{49}16$ if it is known that $\log_{14}28 = a$.
10. Construct a graph of the function $y = \sqrt{\frac{x}{x^2+1}}$.
