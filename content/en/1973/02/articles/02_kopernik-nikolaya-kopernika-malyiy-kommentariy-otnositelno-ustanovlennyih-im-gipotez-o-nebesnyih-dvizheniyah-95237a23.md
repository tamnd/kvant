Our ancestors ordered many celestial spheres, I believe, in order to preserve the principle of uniformity to explain the visible movements of the luminaries. It seemed to them that they would not always move evenly. However, they considered the possibility that when several movements of the luminary are added or combined, the luminary will appear to move unevenly in relation to any place.

This could not be achieved by Calippus and Eudoxus, who tried to obtain a solution by means of concentric circles and with them to explain all the features of the movements of the planets, not only applies to the visible rotation of the stars, but even those when, as it seems to us, the planets either rise to the upper parts of the sky, then descend, which, of course, concentricity cannot allow in any way. Therefore, it was considered best that this could be reproduced with the help of eccentric circles and epicycles, with which, finally, most scientists agreed.

So, having paid attention to this, I have often wondered whether it would be possible to find some more rational combination of circles that could explain all the visible irregularities, and each movement in itself would be uniform, as required by the principle of perfect movement. When I began to tackle this, of course, difficult and almost insoluble task, I still had an idea how this could be achieved with the help of a smaller number of spheres and more convenient combinations compared to what was done before, if only we agreed with some of our requirements, which are called axioms. They follow below in that order.

First requirement. There is no one center for all celestial orbits or spheres.

Second requirement. The center of the Earth is not the center of the world, but only the center of gravity and the center of the lunar orbit.

Third requirement. All spheres move around the Sun, which is located, as it were, in the middle of everything, so that the center of the world is located near the Sun.

Fourth requirement. The ratio that the distance between the Sun and the Earth has to the height of the firmament is less than the ratio of the radius of the Earth to its distance from the Sun, so that in comparison with the height of the firmament it will not even be perceptible.

Fifth requirement. All movements noticed near the firmament belong to it itself, but to the Earth. It is the Earth with the elements closest to it that are all healed in daily movement around its constant stripes, while the firmament and the highest sky remained motionless all the time.

Sixth requirement. All the movements we notice in the Sun are not peculiar to it, but belong to the Earth and our sphere, together with which we move around the Sun, like any other planet; thus the Earth has several movements.

Seventh requirement. Each direct and polar movement of the planets does not belong to them, but to the Earth. Thus, this movement alone is sufficient to explain a large number of irregularities visible in the sky.

Using these premises, I will try to briefly show how the uniformity of movement can be maintained in a completely orderly manner. However, here, for the sake of brevity, I believe it is necessary to omit the mathematical proofs, since they are intended for a more general discussion. However, when describing these circles, we will narrow the magnitude of the resulting orbits, with the help of which anyone luminous in mathematics can easily see how well such a composition of circles will suit numerical calculations and observations.

No one believes that we, together with the Pythagoreans, frivolously affirm the mobility of the Earth; for this he will find gray evidence in my description of the circles. After all, those arguments that, with the help of the natural, mainly try to establish its immobility, rely for the most part on appearance; they will all immediately give up if we also force the Earth to heal on the basis of visible phenomena.
