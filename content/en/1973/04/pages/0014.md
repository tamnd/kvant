About the profession of mathematics

Below we publish excerpts from the well-known brochure by A. N. Kolmogorov “On the Profession of Mathematics” *)
The publication was prepared by M. Smolyansky and T. Kislova

1. The importance of mathematical methods in sciences such as mechanics, physics or astronomy is well known. Everyone also knows that mathematics is necessary in the practical work of engineers and technicians. Basic knowledge of geometry and the ability to use alphabetic formulas are necessary for almost every craftsman or skilled worker. But less clear to many is the question of what it means to have a specialization in mathematics and to engage in mathematics itself as a primary profession.

Many people imagine the matter in such a way that textbooks and mathematical reference books have already collected a wave of sufficient formulas and rules for solving all kinds of mathematical problems encountered in practice. Further, very educated people often express bewilderment: is it really possible to do anything new in mathematics?

Pozmota and mathematicians are sometimes imagined as a boring person who has memorized a large number of formulas and theorems, and they believe that his task is to transfer the memorized, ready-made knowledge to others.

In all this, the only thing that is true is that the mathematical information imparted in secondary school and at the first stages of studying mathematics in higher education was added by man a long time ago. But even these simple mathematicians

The proposer of mathematics in both higher and secondary schools is required not only to have a thorough knowledge of the science he proposes. Only a person who is passionate about it and perceives it as a living, developing science can offer a good proposal for mathematics. Probably, many high school students know how exciting and, thanks to this, easy and accessible mathematics becomes with such students.

To an even greater extent, independence and the ability to take a new approach to the mathematical formulation of a task are necessary for those who use mathematics in solving technical problems. This applies to every engineer's job. But since this requires mathematical knowledge and not everyone has the ability, most of our research technical institutes and even some large factories began to successfully attract mathematicians to work together with engineers on technical problems.
