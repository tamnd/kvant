To “Probabilistic Problems”

(cm. cmp. 59)

1. Wrong. Let us imagine that this experiment was carried out many times. Then, as the case may be, the ball will be taken from the first box, that is, it will be white. In the other case, the ball will be taken from the second box, and in the position from this half of cases it will be white. Consequently, the white ball will be drawn in $3/4$ cases, that is, the required probability is $3/1$.

2. Let's imagine that another game will be played. If the first player wins it, he will take all the money (since the score will be 10:8), and if he passes, the score will be 9:9, after which the players’ chances will be equal. This means that half of the money belongs to the first player, and he or his opponent will take the second half, depending on the outcome of the intended game. Therefore, the second half must be divided in half, that is, the first player must be given $3/4$ of all the money, and the second one - $1/4$.

3. Let there be two children in the family. When listing them, we will first indicate the eldest child, and then the youngest (we exclude families with twins). Four equally likely cases are possible:

MM, MD, DM, DD.

Consequently, in families where there are two children, among whom there is a boy, three equally probable cases are possible:

MM, MD, DM.

We see that in two of these cases the second child is a girl, and in one, a boy. This means that it is twice as likely that the second child is a girl.
