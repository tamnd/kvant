With this method, above the point $M$ of the terrain, a mensula is installed with points $a, b$ and $c$ marked on its tablet, corresponding to points $A, B$ and $C$ (Fig. 6) on the terrain. Cover the tablet with wax (tracing paper) and mark a point $m$ on it, located approximately above the desired point $M$ of the area. Then, through point $m$ on the wax, one sights sequentially at points $A, B$ and $C$ of the area and other directions $mA, mB$ and $mC$. After this, the wax is moved along the scale tablet so that the directions $mA, mB$ and $mC$ read on it pass through points $a, b$ on the tablet. If now, holding the wax in this position, pierce it at point $m$, then the true position of the desired point will be obtained on the tablet.

To control the actions performed, it is recommended to check the orientation of the tablet at the point $A, B$ or $C$ that is farthest from it.

In a quiet haven

The Tripirata share the booty, consisting of 10 pistres, 10 dvolons and a barrel of wine. They have containers for pouring wine, but, alas, each pirate has his own opinion about the comparative value of pistres, dvolons and wine.

Everyone, however, agrees that a barrel of wine is worth more than four pistres and more than four dvolons.

Prove that it is possible to divide the booty so that each pirate gets a part that, in his opinion, is worth no less than the part of each of the others.

A. Toom
