⟦folio 8⟧

Andrey Nikolaevich Kolmogorov
(on the seventieth birthday)

April 25, 1973 marks the seventieth anniversary of one of the most outstanding modern mathematicians - Hero of Socialist Labor, Academician Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov.

A. N. Kolmogov developed an interest in mathematics early. Already at the age of five, he independently discovered through study that
$$1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + \dots + (2n + 1) = n^2.$$
In senior high school, other interests prevailed, in particular, in Russian history.

He returned to mathematics only in his student years and for the rest of his life. Going to university in 1920 was easy, but studying was much more difficult. These were heady years; lectures were given in classrooms where the temperature was below zero. It was considered a great blessing that, starting from the second year, each student received 16 kg of bread and 1 kg of butter per month.

Under the influence of the lectures of Academician N. N. Luzin, A. N. Kolmogov began independent scientific work, constructing in 1922 a very general “theory of operations on a set,” which has not lost its significance even today. During his student years (1920-1925), A. N. Kolmogov studied the theory of trionometric series and, in particular, solved the very difficult problem of convergence of series, constructing for the first time an example of a divergent Fourier series.
