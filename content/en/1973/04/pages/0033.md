⟦folio 31⟧

The mensula is a table for drawing a plan. It consists of a shativ and a tablet - a square drawing board, which is mounted on a stand. The stand has three lifting screws allowing for alignment on the stand. The stand together with the tablet is attached to the tripod using a screw. Stands can be wooden or metal. The centering fork (see Fig.nok) is used to center a point on the tablet above the corresponding point on the terrain: a plumb line is suspended from the lower end of the fork, the upper end of the fork is on the same vertical as the lower one.

And yes, yes, I licked it for sighting from the mensula point to the terrain point. It consists of a ruler on which the transverse scale is engraved, and two diopters with slits and hairs; A cylindrical level is usually placed on the alidade ruler to bring the scale tablet into a horizontal position. Telery, together with alidads with diopters, uses Cypregel.

The kipregel consists of a ruler with a transverse scale and level, and a rubbing tube rotating on the axis of the column so that the plane described by the sighting axis of the tube (collimation) passes through the beveled edge of the kipregel ruler. On the mensula, a horizontal position of the terrain should be obtained, therefore, to change the angles of inclination, kipregels with vertical circles are made, with the help of which the angles of inclination are changed with an accuracy of 1°. Two additional threads (rangefinder), located symmetrically about the middle axis, are visible in the field of view of the kipregel tube. They are used to determine distances on the ground.

The compass (improved compass) is used to orient the compass according to the cardinal points. The compass has the form of a round or rectangular box with degree divisions; a magnetic needle rotates in the center. The compass is always constructed in such a way that the line connecting the divisions 0°-0° or 0°-180° and passing through the center of the compass is parallel to the outer edges of the box.

Graphical solution to the Potenot problem

If there are three points on the scale tablet corresponding to those points on the terrain (that is, if there is, for example, a map of the area), you can determine the position of the fourth point by observing only in it, that is, solve the Potenot problem.
