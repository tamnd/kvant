2. Pedestrian speed is $3$ km/h, bicycle speed is $15$ km/h.
3. 12.5 m. Solution. Let $ABC$ be the given triangle (see Fig. 1), $AK$ be the bicsectrix of angle $A$, and point $O$ be the center of the circle inscribed in triangle $ABC$. According to the conditions, the points $C, O$ and $K$ lie on the same circle, the center of which $Q$ lies on the side $AC$. Let $L$ be the second point of intersection of this circle with the side (prove that this circle intersects the side $AC$, and not its extension!), so $LC$ is the diameter of this circle. Connect the points $O$ and $C$ with a segment; then by the property of an angle with a vertex on a circle
$$\cup OK = \cup LO = \times KCL,$$
$$\cup KC = \pi - 2 \times KCL$$
(give reasons for these equalities!). Next, since angle $KAC$ is an angle with a vertex outside the circle, we can write
$$\frac{1}{2} \times BAC = \frac{1}{2} (\cup KC - \cup OL) = \frac{1}{2} (\pi - 3 \times KCL),$$
that is, $\times A = \pi - 3 \times C$, from where $\times B = 2 \times C$. By the theorem of sines from triangle $ABC$ we have
$$\frac{AB}{\sin 4C} = \frac{AC}{\sin 24C},$$
where does $\cos \angle C = \frac{3}{5}$ come from, and therefore $\sin \angle C = \frac{4}{5}$ (why $\sin \angle C > 0$?). It remains to use the formula $R = \frac{AB}{2\sin 4C}$.
4. $\sqrt{3}$ m. Solution. Let $SH$ be the height of the given paramid $SABC$ (Fig. 2), and $a$ be the length of the edge $SC$. From triangle $SHC$ we have $HC = a/2$,
$$SH = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$
