How fast does the onna move?

V.V. Mayer, R.-E. E. Shafir

To answer the question at what speed onns move, we can perform an experiment in which the movement of charge carriers is directly visible. A simple device for testing such an experiment was proposed in 1948 by S. A. Artsybyshev and I. A. Bildzyukevich.

On a glass plate (1) measuring 50 × 60 mm, place a frame (2) cut out of thick paper with a window measuring approximately 25 × 40 mm (see figure). Place two aluminum electrodes (3) measuring 20x40 mm on the frame parallel to each other at a distance of about 15 mm. Press the electrodes with spring contacts (4). Place a few drops of an aqueous solution of table coli between the electrodes and place a glass tray (5) on the paper frame so that a thin layer of electrolyte is obtained. The plastic should be such a width that there is a gap of approximately 1 mm between its edge and the cathode.

If you now apply a direction to the electrodes, then a current will flow through the NaCl electrolyte solution. In this case, Na⁺ and Cl⁻ onns will move in the solution, but their movement is observed.

The speed of movement of the oxidized ions varies depending on the experimental conditions. It is influenced by the concentration of the solution, the direction supplied to the electrodes, and many other reasons. Try, by slightly changing the experimental conditions, to establish at what speed onns can move.
