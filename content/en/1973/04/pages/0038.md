But according to the condition, the sum of their areas is equal to 1. So,

$$x^2 + (\sqrt{2} - x)(h - x) \leq 1,$$

where

$$h \leq \frac{1 - x^2}{\sqrt{2} - x} + x = \sqrt{2} \left[ 3 - \left( y + \frac{1}{y} \right) \right],$$

Where

$$y = 2 - x \sqrt{2}.$$

Consequently, since the quantity $y + 1/y$ in parentheses is not less than 2 for any $y$, $h \leq \sqrt{2}$.

We will describe here a way of packaging the perfect formal. The description of the "algorithm" - the instructions according to which we propose to pack the squares - is contained in the next paragraph. After the operation described there is done $k$ times, the $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}$ square is placed first above the $k$ squares (we still assume that they are lined in descending order of sides), and the rest of the $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}$ square is divided into $(k + 1)$ rectangles, which are assigned certain numbers (from 0 to $k$). Some rectangles are declared "closed" (meaning that no more squares will be inserted into them), and others are declared "open" (any open rectangle will fit a $(k + 1)$-square). First, “rectangle No. 0” declares the entire square $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}$. So, the algorithm for placing squares:

$$k - \text{шаг} (k = 1, 2, 3, \dots).$$

From all the unclosed rectangles, we select the one with the highest number. (Let this number be $m$). We place the $k$th square in its corner. We continue the side of this square parallel to the smaller side of the rectangle $m$ (if the sides of the rectangle are equal, choose any of them) so that the part of the rectangle $m$ not occupied by the square $k$ is split into two rectangles. Of these two rectangles, we assign the number $k$ to the one that makes up the rectangle together with the square $k$, and the number $m$ is now reserved for the remainder of the “former” rectangle $m$ (Fig. 4). All squares and rectangles, except rectangles $m$ and $k$, retain the old numbers. If the $k$th square we placed is not the last one, then we check whether the $(k + 1)$th square fits in the rectangle $k$ and in the rectangle $m$, and if not, then we declare the corresponding rectangle (or both) closed.
