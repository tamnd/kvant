Ticket No. 1
Let us write down the equilibrium conditions for the ladder:
$$N - P = 0,$$
$$f - N_1 = 0,$$
$$N_1 l \sin \alpha - P \frac{l}{2} \cos \alpha = 0.$$

Here $N$ is the reaction of the horizontal support, $N_1$ is the reaction of the smooth wall, $P$ is the gravitational force, $f$ is the friction force (see Fig. 5). From equation (1), (2) and (3) we find
$$N = P, N_1 = f = \frac{P}{2} \text{ ctg} \alpha.$$

The required force $F$ (total reaction of the support) is equal to $F = \sqrt{l^2 + N^2}$.

This force makes an angle $\varphi$ with the horizontal such that $tg \varphi = \frac{N}{f}$.

Thus,
$$F = \frac{P}{2} \sqrt{4 + ctg^2 \alpha},$$
$$tg \varphi = 2 \text{ tg} \alpha, \quad \varphi = \arctg (2 \text{ tg} \alpha).$$

Ticket number 2
Let us write down the equation of motion of the body before separation from the sphere:
$$mg \cos \alpha - N = m \frac{V^2}{R}.$$

Fig. 6.

Here $m$ is the mass of the body, $V$ is its speed at a given point, $\alpha$ is the angle between the vertical and radius vector of this point, $N$ is the support reaction force. At the moment of separation (Fig. 6)
$$N = 0,$$
$$h_1 = R - R \cos \alpha_0,$$
$$V_0^2 = 2gh_1.$$

Substituting (2), (3), (4) into equation (1), we find
$$\cos \alpha_0 = \frac{2}{3}.$$

After the body is lifted off from a height $\frac{2}{3} R$, it flies freely, having an initial speed $V_0$. Equation of body motion vertically:
$$\frac{2}{3} R = (V_0 \sin \alpha_0) t + \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

From here
$$500t^2 + 260t - 120 = 0, \quad t \approx 0,3c.$$

Ticket number 3
The luminous flux is related to the luminous intensity of the source and the solid angle within which this flux will propagate, respectively
$$\Phi = I\Omega.$$

In the first case
$$\Phi_1 = I\Omega_1 \approx I \frac{\pi d^2}{4 L^2}$$

(since $d \ll L$).

In the second case, the luminous flux $\Phi_3$ reflected from the mirror is added, that is, $\Phi_2 = \Phi_1 + \Phi_3$.
