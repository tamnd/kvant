In October 1957, with the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite, man began to systematically explore outer space. Very little time has passed since then, but how surprisingly far space research has stepped forward! Soviet automatic stations explored the Moon, Venus and Mars. Science has learned much more about these celestial bodies than in the entire previous century of persistent observation.

The first rafted orbital station “Salyut”, intended for conducting long-term scientific research in space, was successfully tested. A variety of artificial satellites of the Earth serve humans by studying meteorological processes in the atmosphere, transmitting information over vast distances, and studying the water regime of our planet.

To date, more than a thousand different devices have been launched into space, half of which were made by the hands of Soviet scientists, engineers, and workers. Our space program clearly distinguishes three main areas of research: systematic research in near-Earth space, which has not only scientific but also great practical significance; study of the Moon and outer space; exploration of the planets of the solar system. In each of these areas, tremendous progress has been made and exciting prospects are opening up.
