Andrey Nikolaevich Kolmogorov
(on the seventieth birthday)

April 25, 1973 marks the seventieth anniversary of one of the most outstanding modern mathematicians - Hero of Socialist Labor, Academician Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov.

A. N. Kolmogov developed an interest in mathematics early. Already at the age of five, he independently discovered through study that
$$1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + \dots + (2n + 1) = n^2.$$
In senior high school, other interests prevailed, in particular, in Russian history.

He returned to mathematics only in his student years and for the rest of his life. Going to university in 1920 was easy, but studying was much more difficult. These were heady years; lectures were given in classrooms where the temperature was below zero. It was considered a great blessing that, starting from the second year, each student received 16 kg of bread and 1 kg of butter per month.

Under the influence of the lectures of Academician N. N. Luzin, A. N. Kolmogov began independent scientific work, constructing in 1922 a very general “theory of operations on a set,” which has not lost its significance even today. During his student years (1920-1925), A. N. Kolmogov studied the theory of trionometric series and, in particular, solved the very difficult problem of convergence of series, constructing for the first time an example of a divergent Fourier series.

In 1922-1925, in parallel with his studies at the university, Andrei Nikolaevich taught in an experimental

exemplary school of the People's Commissariat of Education of the RSFSR in Potylix, on the present-day Lenin Hills. At first, he entered this school to earn a living, since student styles were not enough in those days, but the work captured him. While working at school, A. N. Kolmogorov was the secretary of the school council, led the biology club, and was even the head of the school walking tour in the Crimea. Life at the Potylixin school was of great importance for Kolmogorov also because there were no physical education sections, and Andrei Nikolaevich did not even know how to swim until he was twenty. Now he decided to make up for lost time and achieved some, albeit modest, successes in swimming, pole walking, and rowing.

The school instilled in A. N. Kolmogorov a lifelong love of teaching. This probably explains the fact that later Kolmogorov began compiling school textbooks, organized a boarding school at Moscow State University, etc.

Entering graduate school in those years for a young man unencumbered by a family meant complete freedom from earning money. Since 1925, Kolmogorov has directed his main efforts to scientific work, combining it with teaching at universities, mainly at Moscow University, where in 1931 he became a professor.

These were the years that laid the foundation for fruitful scientific work that has not weakened to this day. It was at this time that Andrei Nikolaevich created his main works in the field of probability theory, topology and mechanics.

Mathematicians engaged in scientific work spend relatively little time in the office offices of scientific institutes; scientific public international activities occur both at home, and during vacations, and on tourist trips. A. N. Kolmogorov covered many thousands of kilometers of dust on pars and rowing boats and kayaks with his closest friend, academician P. S. Alexandrov, and with students of different generations.

Problems of pedagogy occupy a very significant place in the life of A. N. Kolmogorov. He does not think of his creativity in isolation from the selection and education of students, from the transfer of his knowledge and scientific ideas to them. For him, working with young people, creating scientific schools and directions is an integral and natural part of his own scientific research. The issues of educating the scientific generation, the choice of conditions under which mathematical abilities would not be suppressed, but would receive an incentive for development, constantly worry Andrei Nikolaevich, and he devoted many oral and written presentations to this important problem. This is connected with his active participation in holding school mathematical Olympiads, speaking to schoolchildren with stories about ideas and the results of mathematical science. The famous brochure by A. N. Kolmogorov for schoolchildren and teachers, “On the Profession of Mathematics,” which went through several editions and was published in many thousands of copies, talks about this.

In our country, knowledge of mathematics has ceased to serve only as a whole for the general development and acquisition of skills in the elements of calculations: for many young people attending secondary school, mathematics has become one of the most important tools in everyday work. That is why Andrei Nikolaevich defends the thesis that the skillful use of mathematical information is possible only when it is not only fixed in memory, but also the conditions for creativity. Questions in the life of this plan make certain demands on those who will implement it. Only a person who is passionate about it and perceives it as a living, developing teacher can teach mathematics really well. Probably, many high school students know how exciting, and, thanks to this, easy and accessible mathematics becomes with such teachers.

Nurturing a mathematical style of thinking is necessary not only for those who subsequently become mathematicians, but also for those who choose the profession of physicist, engineer, economist, or one of many others. Some ideas on this matter were expressed by A. N. Kolmogorov in the article “Search for Talent” (Izvestia No. 83 (14246), 6.4.1963). The most important question facing modern teaching—what and how to teach—is of deep interest to A. N. Kolmogorov. Even before the Great Patriotic War, he published a number of articles on teaching algebra in high school and wrote, together with P. S. Alexandrov, the first part of an algebra textbook.
