The resulting force of interatomic interaction reaches its maximum value when the centers of the atoms are at a distance of $r_1$ from each other (see Fig. 1). As the stretch increases further, the interaction forces become so small that the bonds between the atoms are broken.

Let us denote the magnitude of the strongest attraction between atoms (that is, the magnitude of the strength of a single bond) by $F_{\text{max}}$, and the number of bonds per unit area of ​​the section perpendicular to the direction of the external force by $N_{\text{cb}}$. then the readability limit of the crystal is

$$\sigma = F_{\text{max}} N_{\text{cb}}.$$

figure 2 shows the simplest model of a table salt crystal (NaCl consists of positive sodium ions and negative chlorine ions). Each crystal has six nearest neighbors with opposite charge signs. When such a crystal breaks, each atom has one bond inverted. The breaking of a crystal is schematically depicted in figure 3. In the model under consideration, the number of bonds broken in each square centimeter is equal to the number of atoms coming to this area ($N_{\text{cb}} = N_{\text{at}}$). For simplicity, we neglect the interaction between atoms located at distances greater than $r_0$ from each other (the rest are at distances no less than $r_0\sqrt{2}$). These interactions provide a relatively small correction. To estimate the readability limit σ, it is sufficient to multiply the largest value of the interaction force between atomic values ​​per unit area in the discontinuity plane:

$$\sigma = F_{\text{max}} N_{\text{at}}.$$
