   b) Let us exclude the two points $A$ and $B$ that are most distant from each other, let $C$ and $D$ be the most distant from the remaining points. Then all points lie on the sphere $S$ with diameter $AB$ and the sphere $S$ with diameter $CD$, that is, all points belong to the intersection of $S$ and $S$; Next, we need to separately consider the case when $S$ and $S$ coincide and when they do not coincide.

In the first case, the considered set (“geometric locus”) of points $M$ such that $\triangle ABM$ is rectangular immediately leads to the conclusion that, except for $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, we do not have a single point. In the second case, using the Pythagorean theorem, it is easily established that if the points $A$, $B$, $M$ and $N$ satisfy the conditions of the problem, then either $AMN = BNM = 90^\circ$ or $ANM = BNM = 90^\circ$, from which it can also be deduced that there is no point other than $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$, the set of points under consideration does not contain points.

So the number of dots here is also 3 or 4.

2. How much angular is large. In fact, let us say that $n$ points $A_1$, $A_2$, ..., $A_n$ have already been selected on the plane, satisfying the conditions of the problem. Let us connect each two points $A_i$ and $A_j$ and through $A_i$ and $A_j$ we draw lines perepredcular to $A_i A_j$ that bound a certain “strip”, and all the points lying in this strip form a triangle with $A_i$ and $A_j$. The constructed stripes cannot fill the entire plane, or each straight line, not parallel to any of them, intersects the set of all stripes along a finite number of segments and, therefore, contains points that do not belong to any stripe. By joining such a point $A_{n+1}$ to the existing $n$ points, we obtain a set of $n+1$ points that satisfy the conditions of the problem.
