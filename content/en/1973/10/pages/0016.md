One of the most important achievements of modern physics is the special properties of liquid gel. These properties are unexpected and paradoxical. Currently, they are all united by the understandable superfluidity - a phenomenon discovered by the Soviet physicist P. L. Kapitsa in 1938. The phenomenon of superfluidity, of course, cannot be reduced to a purely quantitative assessment, that is, to the fact that liquid gels significantly better than other liquids. The properties of a superfluid gel *), as shown in 1941 by the Soviet theoretical physicist L. D. Landdu in his theory of superfluidity, are the most striking manifestation of the general patterns in the behavior of any substance at sufficiently low temperatures. That is why the influence that research on liquid gel had on other seemingly distant areas of physics was so great.
