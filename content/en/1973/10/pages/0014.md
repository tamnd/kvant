⟦folio 12⟧

No Evariste did not remain free for long. On July 14, 1831, the day of the storming of the Bastille, several hundred demonstrators with waving banners marched through the New Place. They protested against Lun-Phillp's ban on demonstrations. In the forefront is Galois. He is wearing the uniform of a National Guardsman, in his hand is a carbine, and under his uniform is a dagger. As soon as the demonstrators left the area, they were surrounded by police. Galois was arrested along with many other demonstrators. This time he was sentenced to six months in prison for illegally carrying military uniforms and weapons.

In the Sainte-Pélagie prison, Galois edited his most important works. In the preface to one of them he wrote:

“First of all, the title page of this work is not cluttered with names, surnames and titles and is not accompanied by greetings to the stingy nobles, whose wallet opens when you smoke fimam for them, and threatens to slam shut as soon as the censer runs dry. No one will see here a title written in capital letters and expressing respectful reverence for a luminary of science or some learned patron, very useful (and I would even say necessary) for someone who at twenty wants to write.

I'm not saying that everything good in my work has been achieved thanks to the advice and encouragement of such and such. I’m not saying this because it would mean laty...”

But Evariste couldn’t do the lyats. In the Sainte-Pélagie prison, he received another “hello” from the prefect of police, Monsieur Gisquet - a bullet, fired on July 29 from the attic of a neighboring house, flew into the cell and flattened against the wall a few centimeters from the prisoner’s head.

Here, in prison, Galois received a letter from the Academy of Sciences. It contained his manuscript and a note from the secretary of the academy, Franus Arago:

“Dear Monsieur Galois!

Your manuscript has been sent to Monsieur Poisson for review. He returned it to us with the comments that we present here:

,,We have made every effort to understand the proofs of Monsieur Galois. His reasoning is not clear enough, it is not enough to expand and does not give the opportunity to judge how accurate they are...”

The Academy once again, without understanding, rejected his work...

However, Evariste himself was partly in this. In his haste, he did not express his thoughts very clearly, and formulated some theorems that had not been proven by him as proven. And Galois’s style of work was unusual for mathematicians in the 19th century. A new style guides the mathematics of the 20th century. Instead of long calculations to solve application problems, completely unexpected ideas; Moreover, there were too many new concepts in his work. It is true that to Poisson, whose scientific interests were far from the theory of algebraic equations, these works seemed insufficiently clear.

Evariste's health is deteriorating. On March 16, 1832, he was transferred to the prison hospital. It was here that he met his wife (her name remains unknown), who played a fatal role in the fate of Galois.

On the last night of his life, he put his manuscripts in order and wrote several letters. In one of them, addressed to his only friend Auguste Chevalier, he briefly outlined the content of his research and asked to contact the most prominent mathematicians to assess the importance of these results - he did not doubt their truth.
