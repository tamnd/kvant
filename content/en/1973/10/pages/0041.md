Let us assume that the deformation remains elastic up to the deformation corresponding to rupture; in other words, the most elastic deformation corresponds to a stress equal to the tensile strength. But the maximum elastic deformation approximately corresponds to the maximum value of the force of mechanical attraction (see Fig. 1).

Experiments with the strongest crystals have shown that their maximum relative elastic deformation $\varepsilon_{\text{max}}$**) before destruction usually does not exceed 10-20%. Let's put $\varepsilon_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{6} \approx 17\%$. This relative deformation corresponds to the displacement of atoms from the equilibrium position to a distance of $\Delta r = \varepsilon r_0 = \frac{1}{6} 3 \text{\AA} = 0,5 \text{\AA}$.

Thus, when calculating the forces of mechanical attraction for a tunable crystal model, the distance between the ions should be equal to
$$r = r_0 + \Delta r = 3 \text{\AA} + 0,5 \text{\AA} = 3,5 \text{\AA}$$
If we take into account that the charge of each ion is equal in magnitude to the charge of the electron, that is, $q = 1,6 \cdot 10^{-19} \text{\AA}$, then the maximum value is silipropional to the distance.
