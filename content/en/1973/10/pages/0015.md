On the morning of May 30, 1832, some peasants near a pond in the town of Gentilly came across a young man seriously wounded in the stomach. The wounded man was transferred to the hospital, where he died the next morning in the arms of his brother.

“Don’t cry,” Evariste asked his brother before his death, “don’t cry.” I need to save all my courage to die at twenty years old.

In one of his last letters, he wrote: “I am dying as a victim of a sublayer coquette and two dupes devoted to her.” But Evariste did not know everything. New proof

Probably all our readers are familiar with the theorem about the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean. It is formulated as follows: if there are two positive numbers a

and b, then $\frac{a+b}{2} \geq \sqrt{ab}$, and equality is achieved when a = b.

Let us give a very simple and clear proof of it.

Let's construct a square CDEF from the side CD = a + b (see figure). Let's assume for now that a ≠ b. On the sides of the square from points C, D, E, F, we will plot equal segments: CK = DL = EM = FN = b. Through points L and N we draw straight lines, parallel sides CD, and through points K and M - parallel sides CF. We denote the intersection points of these lines by R, T, S, P. As a result of these constructions, we obtain the square RTSP and four equal rectangles, so that the area of ​​the square CDEF is equal to the sum of the area of ​​the square RTSP and quadruple the area of ​​the rectangle KDLP.

The area of ​​the square CDEF is equal to $(a+b)^2$, the area of ​​the rectangle is equal to the product ab, we obtain the following equality: $(a+b)^2 = 4 ab + S_{\text{RTSP}}$. But since S_{\text{RTSP}} \geq 0 (obviously), then $(a+b)^2 \geq 4 ab$, hence $\frac{a+b}{2} \geq \sqrt{ab}$.
