figure at the top shows an intricate figure lying on the face of checkered paper. Nine nodes fell into its “territory” (we call nodes the points of intersection of horizontal and vertical straight lines, paper lines). Is it possible to fold it over the paper so that fewer nodes fit into its occupied area? It turns out that it is possible: if the area of ​​an arbitrary figure lying on checkered paper is less than n (the area of ​​one square is taken as one), then it can be folded so that no more than (n-1) nodes fall on its territory. This statement is called Blichfeld's theorem. Try to prove it. After this, you just have to make sure that the area of ​​our figure is less than one, and, therefore, it can actually be folded from the nodes.

On the first page of the cover you see a model of an ideal ionic crystal. The properties of crystalline substances are determined by the structure of their crystal lattice. How to find the mechanical parameters of a substance using microsopper characteristics is described in the article by G. B. Kuperman and E. D. Shukin “Mechanical properties of crystals.”
