⟦folio 67⟧

Now about the contents of the steels. As it should be, again, the most detailed and complete statistics are related to the sections of elementary mathematics, and, what is especially important, the authors were very attentive to the definitions in elementary mathematics, while in various other publications - school textbooks and even the Great Soviet Encyclopedia - the definitions are not always accurate. For example, the following definition of prism is quite common: “A prism is a polyhedron whose two faces are equal polygons with corresponding parallel sides, and all other faces are parallelograms.” This definition is incorrect (why? - check it yourself), as stated in the corresponding article in the reference book.

Of course, not all terms of elementary mathematics are declared sufficiently fully; in particular, the article “Cosine theorem” would be nice to supplement with the cosine theorem for a quadrilateral, but in general this section of the word deserves the highest rating.

As for the terms relating to various branches of higher mathematics, unfortunately, they are sometimes not explained so thoroughly. Here is a not entirely correct definition of the number “e” (article “Remarkable Proposals”), and an erroneous formulation of the Heine-Borel lemma. A number of articles contain links to stats that are not in the dictionary. So, in the article “Random Variables” there are references to the missing article “Distribution Law”, but in the dictionary there is an article “Distribution”, which should have been referenced. In the article “Reflexivity” there is a link to the article “Binary Relations”, which is not in the dictionary.

In a number of articles connected to each other by interconnections, identical elements are designated differently.

Thus, in the articles “Group”, “Groupodid”, “Quasigroup” the elements of the set are denoted by malmi Latin letters (a, b, c,...), and in the article “Semigroup” they are also denoted by capital Latin letters (A, B, C,...). The list of inaccuracies, errors and typos could, unfortunately, be continued. But they define the face of this interesting and very necessary word. Without giving discounts to the authors, we can assume that, in general, the “first pancake” is not attributed to higher education.

WHAT would I like to wish the author of the word?

First, take a more careful approach to the designated mathematical expressions that need to be included in the dictionary. Maybe it would be worth turning to leading specialists in various branches of mathematics with a request to review the terms and add to it if they consider it necessary. In response, take into account the shortcomings noted both in this decision and in other critical statements, written and oral, and it is especially important to take into account those for whom this dictionary is primarily intended. And then, let's hope, the dictionary will receive an even higher rating.

And finally, a few words to the publisher. It would be advisable to continue this valuable initiative and republish this very necessary and useful book as soon as possible. We hope that in the near future it will be possible to buy the “Explanatory Dictionary of Mathematical Terms” in a store without the help of friends.

Well, what a “centroid” is and much more, you will learn by reading this book.

K. I. Kashin

Another Olympics

On April 15, 1973, the XVI Republican Mathematical Olympiad for Macedonian schoolchildren was held in the city of Skoplje. Below we present some of the tasks proposed at it.

1. Prove that if 1+x+y=0, then 1+x^3+y^3=3xy.

2. Given a line p and two points A and B not lying on it. The medium of triangles ABX, where X is a point on the line p, find the triangle with the smallest perimeter.

3. It is known about equilateral triangle ABC that it can be divided into two equilateral triangles. Find the angles of this triangle.

4. Given the equation a^4x^2+6a^2bx+5b^2=0; a,b≠0 a) Prove that its roots are valid for any values ​​of the parameters;

6) Find out at what parameter values ​​the matching is performed.

$$\frac{x_1+x_2}{3}=\frac{x_1x_2}{5}+1.$$

5. A tangent is drawn at point B of a circle with diameter AB. Find the point on the circle equidistant from point A and the tangent.

6. Let AB be the diameter of a point that does not lie on the circle or on the line AB. Let A_1 and B_1 be the intersection points of the lines MA and MB and the circle, and S be the intersection point of the lines AB_1 and A_1B. Prove that MS is perpedicular to AB.

7. Let k be an odd positive number. Prove that the product of k positive odd numbers is divisible by k.
