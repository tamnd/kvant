We can say that in the experiment with a slit the superfluid component manifests itself, and in the experiment with the disk it manifests itself as a normal one.

Body secretion in Gelinii II is a process of emitting sound waves. The transfer of a body in a certain direction occurs because the energy of sound waves propagating in this direction is greater than in the opposite direction. Therefore, the direction of body transfer is at the same time the direction predominantly of the propagating sound waves and the normal component associated with it.

The transfer of a body in gelini II is thus always accompanied by the movement of the normal component, that is, the mechanism of body transfer is always convective. That is why in the experiment, the diagram of which is shown in figure 3, the thermometers shown were identical, and the petal was constantly deflected in the direction of the body flow under the influence of the normal component flowing around it.

If the body flow takes place in gelini II, filling some closed vessel, as in figure 4, then the non-primary transfer of liquid mass in one direction does not occur, since the pressure difference generated by the flow causes the movement of the superfluid component in the opposite direction. Moreover, the speed of superfluid motion corresponds to the fact that there is no total mass transfer, so the liquid level does not change. The deflection of the lobe shows that the normal component flows out of the inner region, and the superfluid motion remains unnoticeable, since it does not exert pressure on the bodies flowing around it.

Points on a plane

Here is a problem, the solution of which, at first glance, seems completely impossible. It is required to find a finite set of points on the plane such that a perecular, constructed in the middle of a segment connecting any two points, will certainly intersect at least two more points from this set. Nevertheless, the problem has an interesting solution. And a set with such an amazing property consists of eight points. Old-tecb obticata him!
