**M164.** On the white cells of an infinite chessboard filled with the upper half-plane (Fig. 5), some numbers are written so that for each black cell the sum of the numbers in the two cells adjacent to it, to the right and left, is equal to the sum of two other numbers, the sum of them from the cells adjacent to it, above and below. The number in one cell of the $n$th line is known (blue cross on figure), but we need to find out the number above it in the $(n + 2)$th line (red question mark on figure). How many more numbers in the bottom two lines (blue dots on figure) do you need for this banner?

Let us introduce the following notation (see Fig. 6): $x$ is an unknown number in the $(n + 2)$th line, $d_n$ is a given number in

$n$th line. The numbers $a$ and $b$ are not given to us, but we know that $x + d_n = a + b$, whence $x = d_n + (a - d_n) + (b - d_n)$. Since $b + d_{n-1} = d_n + b_n$, then $b - d_n = b_n - d_{n-1}$. Likewise

$$b_n - d_{n-1} = b_{n-1} - d_{n-2} = \dots = b_1 - d_0.$$

Thus, $b - d_n = b_1 - d_0$. Exactly the same

$$a - d_n = a_n - c_{n-1} = a_{n-1} - c_{n-2} = \dots = a_1 - c_0.$$

So, $x = d_n + (a_1 - c_0) + (b_1 - d_0)$.

So, to determine $x$, you need, in addition to $d_n$, to know only 4 numbers $a_1, b_1, c_0$ and $d_0$ in the first and zero lines.

Is it possible to get by with less than four numbers?

In the expressed case (at $n = 0$) it is possible: then $x = a_1 + b_1 - d_0$ (see Fig. 7), and, in addition to $d_0$, it is enough to know only two numbers ($a_1$ and $b_1$).

With $n \geq 1$ it is impossible to get by with less than four numbers, and besides $d_n$, you need to know the numbers $a_1, b_1, c_0$ and $d_0$: if at least one of these numbers is known, then the value of even all the other numbers in the first and zero lines does not make it possible to determine $x$. Let's prove it.

Fig. 7.

Let, for example, the number $a_1$ be unknown (see Fig. 6). Then nothing prevents you from setting it arbitrarily. This would be meaningful if the number $d_n$ was not known. In this case, the numbers of the first and zero lines are not connected by any correspondence - they can be assigned to all the contents arbitrarily, and from them all other numbers can be determined (first in the second line, then in the third, etc.). Moreover, $d_n = P - N$, where $P$ is the sum of the numbers of the first line, standing between $a_1$ and $b_1$ (excluding $a_1$ and $b_1$ themselves), and $N$ is the sum of the numbers of the zero line, standing between $c_0$ and $d_0$ (excluding themselves $c_0$ and $d_0$); the equality $d_n = P - N$ is easily proven by induction on $n$; in this case, you need to use the previously derived formula $x = d_n + (a_1 - c_0) + (b_1 - d_0)$.

Thus, $d_n = P - N$ is the only match connecting $d_n$ and the numbers of the zero and first row; $a_1$ is not included. This means that, knowing $d_n$ and all the numbers of the zero and first row, except for $a_1$, we cannot determine $a_1$; on the contrary, it can be specified arbitrarily. But by fixing $b_1, c_0, d_0$ and $d_n$ and giving $a_1$ different meanings, we will get different meanings for $x$.

So, knowledge of $a_1, b_1, c_0$ and $d_0$ is necessary and sufficient to determine $x$ given $d_n$.

F181. The satellite moves around the Earth in a steep orbit with a speed of $v$. The change in its orbit is due to the fact that the frictional force $F = av^\alpha$ acts on the satellite from the side of the microescissa, while $a$ and $\alpha$ are constants. Named $\alpha$ if it is known that the radius of the satellite’s orbit changes very slowly.

Let us immediately stipulate that we will solve the problem for the case when the orbital radius changes at a constant speed.

Work against the friction force is performed due to a decrease in the mechanical energy of the satellite.

Let a satellite of mass $m$ move with speed $v$ in a circular orbit of radius $R$.

Its kinetic energy is equal to $W_K = \frac{mv^2}{2}$, and its potential energy is $W_\pi = -\gamma \frac{mM}{R}$, where $M$ is the mass of the Earth, $\gamma$ is the gravitational constant (see, for example, the article by N. M. Speransky “Potential energy of bodies in a gravitational field”, «Kvant» No. 6, 1972).

Centripetal acceleration imparts to the satellite due to gravity. Therefore, according to Newton's second law

$$\frac{mv^2}{R} = \gamma \frac{mM}{R^2},$$

where

$$v = \left( \gamma \frac{M}{R} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.$$

The Theler expression for $W_K$ can be written as follows:

$$W_K = \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R}.$$

Then the total mechanical energy of the satellite is

$$W = W_K + W_\pi = -\frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R}.$$

If the orbital radius during time $\Delta t$ decreases by a small amount $\Delta R (\Delta R \ll R)$, then the change in the energy of the satellite will be equal to

$$\Delta W = -\frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R} - \left( -\frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R - \Delta R} \right) =$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R(R - \Delta R)} \Delta R \approx \frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R^2} \Delta R.$$

The work against the friction force during this time can be calculated as follows:

$$A = F_{\text{тр.}} v_{\text{cp}} \Delta t = av^{\alpha+1} \Delta t \approx$$

$$\approx a \left( \gamma \frac{M}{R} \right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}} \Delta t = \frac{1}{2} \gamma \frac{Mm}{R^2} \Delta R.$$
