⟦folio 14⟧

I would like to note that these conclusions were made on the basis of very large experimental material, from hundreds of geophysical uplifts, and, of course, processing of geophysical material for much more information than is mentioned here.

Thus, a new approach to the problem of searching and creating a mathematical apparatus has already shown its viability and high efficiency in the problem of assessing reliability in the search for local uplifts. However, the same methods make it possible to construct a reliability function for assessing whether a point belongs to the properties of a local uplift (this is essential when choosing the point for laying the first exploration well) and a reliability function for the oil-water contact zone, that is, the boundary of the oil deposit. Next, using the confidence function of belonging to a point of a local uplift, we can construct a good estimate of the average area occupied by a local uplift. The same technique makes it possible to approach the most important problem of calculating oil (or gas) reserves in a field from a new perspective. Thus, in the field of petroleum geology and geophysics, methods based on the reliability function have already brought significant benefits, and their consistent use will lead to even greater success.

But here I would like to emphasize that probabilistic search methods can be successfully used in solving other search problems in geology, and many similar problems from other fields of science and technology.
