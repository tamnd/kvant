4. Answer: $\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}(2 + \sqrt{4 - \sqrt{3}})$.

Solution. Since pl. $SAC \perp$ pl. $ABC$, then the height $SO$ of the paramid $SABC$ is the height $\Delta SAC$ lowered to the side $AC$ (pi. 2). Then $SO = \frac{1}{2}AS = \frac{a}{2}$, $AO = AS \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = a \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (and $\Rightarrow SAC = 30^\circ$!).

Let $O'$ be the center of the circle of the base of the cone, $S'$ its vertex, and $S'M$ the generatrix of the cone passing through the vertex $S$ of the paramid. Then, obviously, the point $O$ lies on the radius $O'M$ of the base of the cone and $\Delta S'O'M \sim \Delta SOM$. From the similarity of these triangles we find that $S'O' = SO \frac{O'M}{OM}$.

By the sine theorem for $\Delta ABC$ we have
$$O'A = O'M = \frac{AC}{2\sin \Delta ABC} = \frac{a}{2 \cdot \sin \frac{180^\circ - 30^\circ}{2}} = \frac{a}{2 \cos 15^\circ}.$$

By the cosine theorem for $\Delta AO'O$ we have
$$O'O = \sqrt{AO^2 + (AO')^2 - 2AO \cdot AO' \cos \Delta OAO'} = \frac{a}{2 \cos 15^\circ} \times \sqrt{1 + (3 - 2\sqrt{3}) \frac{1 + \cos 30^\circ}{2}} = \frac{a \sqrt{4 - \sqrt{3}}}{4 \cos 15^\circ}.$$

Then $OM = O'M - OO' = \frac{a}{4 \cos 15^\circ} \times (2 - \sqrt{4 - \sqrt{3}})$.

Now all that remains is to calculate the height $S'O'$. We have
$$S'O' = \frac{a}{2} \cdot \frac{a}{2 \cos 15^\circ} \cdot \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{a}{2 \sqrt{4 - \sqrt{3}}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}(2 + \sqrt{4 - \sqrt{3}}).$$
