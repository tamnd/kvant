⟦folio 8⟧

Living the old fashioned way in a “lucky or unlucky” situation. And it seems that we should shout at the geologists, demand that they work for sure, since “all conditions are created” for them...

Geologists cite not klad, hidden by a pirate from prying eyes, but underground storerooms, where coal and oil, gold and tungsten, iron and uranium are riches incomparable in scale to any treasures. And, of course, when searching, they somehow evaluate their chances. These assessments are still of a qualitative nature, although they are based on various types of information, both qualitative and quantitative.

But now in all industries the problem of optimization is being solved: everything must be done as quickly as possible, as cheaply as possible, with as many errors and errors as possible. And there is a need to take into account all the causes that create interference, the means to overcome them, the need to quantify the quality of the work performed.

So, we will talk about a new approach to the problem of searching for minerals, more precisely, about the problem of searching for oil and gas deposits.

What to look for?

The surface of the Earth, many kilometers deep, looks like a multilayer sandwich: through

There are layers of sand, clay, limestone, etc. Oil or gas can only occur in porous layers where there are adequate spaces between the rock particles. Under the influence of all kinds of catalytic layers of sedimentary rocks changed their geometry, peaks and rises, mountain ridges and faults appeared. In most cases, oil and gas fields are confined to underground hills with a seal impermeable to liquid or gas; it can be clayey or salty. Under this dense dome there are porous layers in which liquid or gas lies. Therefore, the first task of geologists when searching for oil or gas is to detect underground domes, or, as geologists call them, local *) uplifts. This is what we are talking about now about searching for local uplifts.

Model

How to get an idea of ​​the structure of the earth's layers at a depth of several kilometers on the Earth's surface? The answer is clear: it is necessary to drill wells, select pieces of rock - core - from different depths, and then compare the results of studies of different wells. But drilling many wells is very expensive; drilling a well to a depth of 2-4 km costs hundreds of thousands of rubles, and now deeper wells are being drilled. And therefore, to obtain preliminary information, it uses various indirect methods, such as gravity surveying or electrical prospecting. The most informative among them is now freeze-breeding.
