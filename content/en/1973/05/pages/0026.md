Let us now use the fact that
$$1 - C_{m+1}^1 + C_{m+1}^2 - \cdots + (-1)^{m+1} C_{m+1}^{m+1} = 0$$
and therefore
$$C_{m+1}^2 - C_{m+1}^3 + \cdots + (-1)^{m+1} C_{m+1}^{m+1} = C_{m+1}^1 - 1 = m.$$
Noticing also that
$$C_{m+1}^k \frac{m-k+1}{m+1} = C_{m}^k$$
and uses property (4) of the operation*, we obtain the following expression for $S_m(n)$:
$$S_m(n) = \frac{n^{m+1} + nm}{m+1} + [C_m^2 S_{m-2}(n) - C_m^3 S_{m-3}(n)]^*.$$
By fielding $q = m-1$ in (3), we find that the expression in square brackets is equal to $mS_{m-1}(n) - n^m$, therefore, $S_m(n) = \frac{n^{m+1} + nm}{m+1} + [mS_{m-1}(n) - n^m]^* = \frac{n^{m+1} + nm}{m+1} + mS_{m-1}^*(n) - \frac{n^{m+1} - n}{m+1} = n + mS_{m-1}^*(n).$
Thus, (5) is also true for $q = m$. The theorem has been proven.
Example. Suppose you know that
$$S_3(n) = \frac{1}{4} (n^4 + 2n^3 + n^2).$$
Let's find an expression for $S_4(n)$:
$$S_4(n) = n + 4S_3^*(n) = n + (n^4 + 2n^3 + n^2)^* = n + \frac{n^5 - n}{5} + 2 \frac{n^4 - n}{4} + \frac{n^3 - n}{3} = \frac{n^5}{5} + \frac{n^4}{2} + \frac{n^3}{3} - \frac{n}{30}.$$
§ 3. General formula
Newton's binomial, however, can give much more than identity (3) proved in § 1.

Consider the following transformations (m and n are assumed to be natural numbers):
$$m^n - 1 = [(m-1) + 1]^n - 1 = (m-1) C_n^1 + (m-1)^2 C_n^2 + \cdots + (m-1)^n C_n^n.$$
Let $m \neq 1$. Dividing both sides of the identity by $m-1$, we get
$$1 + m + m^2 + \cdots + m^{n-1} = C_n^1 + (m-1) C_n^2 + \cdots + (m-1)^{n-1} C_n^n.$$ (6)
This equality is true for all natural $m$ and $n$, including $m=1$ (in this case it is very indo). Since on the left and on the right we have polynomials of degree $n-1$ relative to $m$, this is only possible if the polynomials are identically equal, that is, their coefficients coincide (in this case, they are all equal to one). If we open the parentheses on the right side of (6) and equate the coefficients at $m^k$, $k=0, 1, \ldots, n-1$, to one, we obtain a series of identities connecting the biomp coefficients. The following is important for us: if the degrees $m^k$, $k=0, 1, \ldots, n-1$ on the left and right in (6) are replaced by any numbers $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1}$, the equality will not be violated: after all, the coefficients in front of the numbers $a_k$ will remain equal to one! Thus, we come to the following identity, true for any numbers $a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1}$:
$$a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_{n-1} = C_n^1 + (a_1 - a_0) C_n^2 + (a_2 - 2a_1) + a_0) C_n^3 + \cdots + [a_{n-1} - C_n^1 a_{n-2}) + C_n^2 a_{n-3} - \cdots + (-1)^{n-1} C_n^n.$$ (7)
