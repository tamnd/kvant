For sufficiently small values ​​of $v_0$, the safety ellipse will intersect with the earth's surface along a certain parallel. From figure 4 it is clear that the major axis of the safety ellipse, equal to $4a - r_0 = M_0M + R$, increases with increasing angle $\varphi$. At $\varphi = 180^\circ$
$$4a - r_0 = r_0 + 2R,$$
that is, $a = \frac{1}{2}(r_0 + R)$.

From equality (7) the corresponding value of the initial speed is found

$$\hat{v}_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma m_0 R}{r_0 (r_0 + R)}},$$
or, given that $\gamma m_0 = gR^2$,

$$\hat{v}_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2gR^3}{r_0 (r_0 + R)}}.$$

This is the lowest initial speed that allows you to hit any point on the earth's surface. In particular, if $r_0$ approaches $R$, then $\hat{v}_0$ tends to $\sqrt{gR}$, that is, to the first escape velocity, which is completely natural. With an increase of $r_0$ the speed of $\hat{v}_0$ kills, which is also completely natural. With $v_0 > \hat{v}_0$ it will be possible to get to any point on the Earth along two different trajectories, and with $v_0 = \hat{v}_0$ the same is the case for all points on the earth’s surface except the South Pole.
