Physics

Each applicant was given 5 problems during the written exam.

No. 1. “Experimental” task, which required an explanation of a physical phenomenon demonstrated by examiners on a laboratory bench.

No. 2. An “easy” problem that every averagely prepared applicant can solve.

No. 3 and 4. Tasks that require a fairly confident knowledge of the basic physical laws and some intelligence.

No. 5. A task of increased difficulty that requires skill in an unprivileged or physical situation.

Below are most of the problems (the number of each problem in the exam card is indicated in parentheses).

Task 1. (1). Two coils of wire are raised so that their axes are horizontal and lie on the same straight line. The gap between the ends of the coil is about 3 cm. When the coil is connected to an electrical grid through a laboratory author, the coil transformer is accepted.

Why does a sensitive coil start to respond when a copper sheet is inserted between them? Why does the picture change if an iron sheet is inserted together with copper?

Task 2. (1). There are two speakers and two sound generators. When each speaker is alternately connected to its generator, in both cases sounds of the same loudness and frequency are heard. When connected at the same time, the sound is of the same frequency, but with periodic increase and decrease in volume. Explain the phenomenon.

Task 3. (1). A black disk with one narrow white sector is rotated. When the disk is illuminated with a periodically drying lamp, three motionless white sectors are visible, located at 120° intervals. Find the rotation speed of the disk from the observed pattern at a known frequency of the illuminating lamp.

Task 4. (2). The double magnet rotates around a vertical axis so that both threads lie in the same plane with the axis and make constant angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Fig. 1) with it. The lengths of the thread are the same and equal to $l$. Find the angular speed of rotation of the magnet. The acceleration due to gravity is $g$.

Task 5. (2). In a cylindrical vessel of radius $R$, directed by a liquid of density $\rho$ to a height $h$, there is a hole in the side wall near the bottom, plugged with a stopper. What work needs to be done to screw the rod into the vessel to a length of $l$?

The plug has the shape of a cylinder of radius $r$, length greater than $l$. Ignore training. The vessel is high enough so that water does not spill out of it.
