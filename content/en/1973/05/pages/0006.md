3. Getting from the initial position to the specified one.
"Safety" ellipse

Let's solve the following problem.
What direction of the initial velocity must be reported at a given value $v_0$ in order for the material point to move from the initial position $M_0$ to the given position $M$?

Point $M$ must lie on an ellipse, so the initial initial velocity means finding

This is the direction of the tangent to the ellipse at the point $M_0$. This can be done by using the optical property of the ellipse, according to which the initial velocity must be directed (in one direction or another) along the bissertic of the angle adjacent to the angle ($r_0, r'$) (Fig. 2), where $r_0$ and $r'$ are radius vectors drawn from the foci to the point $M_0$.

One of the ellipse's foci is known (coincides with the point $O$), and the other needs to be determined. It is obvious that the distances $r'$ and $r'$ from the point $M_0$ and $M$ to this focus satisfy the conditions

$$r_0 + r' = 2 a,$$

$$r + r' = 2 a.$$

It follows that

$$r' = 2 a - r_0$$ and $$r' = 2 a - r,$$

that is, $r_0$ and $r'$ are satisfied by the position of the point $M_0$ and $M$ and the magnitude of the initial speed. Therefore, the desired focus lies at the intersection of two circles with centers at points $M_0$ and $M$ and with radii equal to the corresponding $r'$ and $r'$.

If the distance is $M_0 M > r' + r' = 4 a - r_0 - r$, then the problem has no solution, since the circles do not intersect.

If $M_0 M < r' + r'$, then the above circles will have two intersection points $F_1$ and $F_2$ as shown in figure 2 and the problem will have two solutions.
