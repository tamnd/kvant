13. As the magnetic field decreases, the magnetic flux through the ring changes. The resulting electric field spins the ring.

The magnetic flux through the ring is equal to
$$\Phi = \pi a^2 2B + \pi (R^2 - a^2) B = \pi B (a^2 + R^2).$$

According to the law of electromagnetic induction
$$E_{\text{инд}} = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = \pi (a^2 + R^2) \frac{\Delta B}{\Delta t}.$$

Let the magnetic field uniformly decrease to 0 in time $t$; Then
$$E_{\text{инд}} = \frac{\pi (a^2 + R^2) B}{t}.$$

E.m.f. induction is equal to the work of moving a unit charge along the entire ring in the resulting electric field with the direction $E$,
$$E_{\text{инд}} = 2\pi RE.$$

From here
$$E = \frac{(a^2 + R^2) B}{2Rt}.$$

For each element of the ring length
$$\Delta l \ll R$$ (charge of such an element $\Delta q = \frac{\Delta l}{2\pi R} q$, mass $\Delta m = \frac{\Delta l}{2\pi R} m$) the force acts
$$\Delta qE$$, which creates acceleration $a = \frac{\Delta qE}{\Delta m}$ and in time $t$ increases the speed from 0 to
$$v = at = \frac{\Delta qE}{\Delta m} t = \frac{q}{m} \frac{(a^2 + R^2) B}{2R}.$$

To the “Problem” (see «Kvant» No. 4, 1973, cmp. 45)
Let the urns contain a correspondence of $a, b, c$ and $d$ balls. Then the losers for the beginner are those and only those positions for which the sums of all digits corresponding to one digit in the notation of the numbers $a, b, c$ and $d$ in the double number system are multiples of trem for all digits.
The considered game can be generalized to the case when there are $n$ urns, and balls can be taken from no more than $k$ urns ($k \leq n$). In this case, the losing positions will be those in which the sums of all digits corresponding to one digit in the double entry of the number of balls in the given urn are divisible by $k + 1$. With $k = 1$ we get the game “nim” (see the article by I.M. Ya Glom “Two games with matches”, «Kvant» No. 2, 1971).
