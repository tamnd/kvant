Let us now choose a value for $x$ to ensure that $x$ is incommensurable with $a$. For example, $x = a\sqrt{2}$. Then

$$\cos [a^2(1 + \sqrt{2})^2] = \cos 2a^2,$$

from somewhere

$$a^2(1 + \sqrt{2})^2 + 2a^2 = 2\pi k,$$

or

$$a^2(1 + \sqrt{2})^2 - 2a^2 = 2\pi k.$$

Taking advantage of the fact that $a^2 = 2\pi n$ and putting it into these two equalities, we get either $5 + 2\sqrt{2} = \frac{k}{n}$ or $1 + 2\sqrt{2} = \frac{k}{n}$ ($n \neq 0$, since $a$ is, by assumption, a period). In both cases, we come to the conclusion that $\sqrt{2}$ is a different number. The resulting contradiction proves that the function $\cos x^2$ is non-periodic.

1. Answer: at $a \cdot b \cdot c > 0$

$$x = \pm \frac{b + c}{2bc} \sqrt{abc}, \quad y = \pm \frac{a + c}{2ac} \sqrt{abc},$$

$$z = \pm \frac{a + b}{2ab} \sqrt{abc}.$$

Solution. The original system is easily reduced to the form

$$\begin{cases}
(x - y + z) \cdot (x + y - z) = a, \\
(y - z + x) \cdot (y + z - x) = b, \\
(z - x + y) \cdot (z + x - y) = c.
\end{cases}$$

Assuming $x + y - z = u$, $x - y + z = v$, $-x + y + z = w$, we obtain for $u, v, w$ the system

$$u \cdot v = a, \quad uw = b, \quad vw = c,$$

from where $(u \cdot v \cdot w)^2 = a \cdot b \cdot c, \quad u = \frac{u \cdot v \cdot w}{v \cdot w} = \pm \frac{\sqrt{abc}}{c}; \quad v = \pm \frac{\sqrt{abc}}{b}; \quad w = \pm \frac{\sqrt{abc}}{a}$

(only at $a \cdot b \cdot c > 0$). Here, for $u, v, w$, the expressions are either all with the sign “+” or all with the sign “-”. Returning to old variables

$$\left( x = \frac{u + v}{2}, \quad y = \frac{u + w}{2}, \quad z = \frac{v + 2w}{2} \right),$$

we find the answer.
