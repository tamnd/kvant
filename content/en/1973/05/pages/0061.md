To the article “Heads with infrared radiation”

1. The filament of an incandescent lamp connected to an alternating current network with a frequency of 50 cycles heats up whenever the current passing through it reaches its maximum (in absolute value) value, that is, 100 times per second.

2. The receiver responds to the flow of infrared radiation, but the sound strength in the loudspeaker is proportional to the change in this flow. In 0.005 s, the lamp filament does not have time to cool down much, so the change in flux is only a small part of it. To increase the change in flux, it is enough to turn the light beam falling on the phototransistor with a rotating fan. It is not difficult to verify this by checking the phototransistor with the palm of your hand with “opened” fingers: a much stronger sound will be heard from the speaker than when simply illuminating the phototransistor with an incandescent lamp.

5. When one of the Polaroids is rotated, the illumination of the phototransistor drops to zero, while the volume of the sound coming from the speaker remains unchanged. It follows that polaroids polarize only the visible region of the spectrum, leaving infrared rays unpolarized. (This is a property of polaroids; in general, infrared rays also polarize.) The phototransistor reacts only to infrared rays, since otherwise, when the illumination of the phototransistor changes, the volume of the sound would also change.

6. Since the phototransistor reacts only to infrared rays, and for visible light the law of reflection has been proven, to check this law in the infrared region it is enough to insert the sensor into the beam of light reflected by the mirror.

2. The main period of the cos 3πx function is $\frac{2\pi}{3\pi} = \frac{2}{3}$. The fundamental period of the function sin 2πx is $\frac{2\pi}{2\pi} = 1$.

For segments of length $2/3$ and 1, the least common multiple is a segment of length 2.

Let us prove that 2 is the main period. For this purpose, consider the roots of the equation cos 3πx = sin 2πx on the interval [0,2]. There will be six ethic roots: 0.1; 0.5; 0.9; 1.3; 1.5; 1.7. The function cos 3πx - sin 2πx at x = 0 is equal to 1 and kills for many nights, at least on the interval [0,0,1]. Consequently, it cannot have a period less than 0.1, that is, according to Theorem 3, it has a main period.

Let's assume that 2 is not the main period. In this case, it will be a small number greater than 0.1, which corresponds to a segment that fits into 2 an integer number of times (Theorem 2). The found values ​​of the roots suggest only one such number - 0.4. However, it is not a period, since, for example, at x = 1.5 + 0.4 = 1.9 this function does not vanish. Therefore, 2 is the main period.
