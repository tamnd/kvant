Real local uplifts in platform areas are fairly well-structured surfaces: they change slowly, and the network step is chosen so that there are no sharp fluctuations of this surface between nodes. Then, with sufficient accuracy, we can replace the previous definition of a local lift with the following: the point $Q_0$ belongs to a local lift if there is at least one closed polygon enclosing the point $Q_0$, at all nodes $Q_i$ of which the surface under study is located lower than at the point $Q_0$: $H(Q_i) < H(Q_0)$ for all $i = 1, 2, \dots, n$.

The diameter of the polygon is assumed to be limited, just as above for arbitrary lines.

Probabilistic approach

According to the definition, the point $Q_0$ either belongs to the local lift or does not belong. But our knowledge about the position of points on the surface under study contains errors that are statistical in nature. Therefore, the statement that the point $Q_0$ belongs to a local lift can only be stated using Let us introduce the corresponding characteristic.

Namely, we define at each studied point $Q$ with coordinates $(x, y)$ of the studied domain of the function and up to $d(Q) \equiv d(x, y)$, which represents the probability that the point $Q$ belongs to a local lift.

The reliability function can be visually specified in the same way as a structural map - by a set of its level lines. Points located on this level line are equally likely to belong to a local rise. Now the question arises of how to construct a reliability function based on the information available to geophysicists. Deviations of the depth values ​​obtained as a result of survey work from the actual values ​​of the depth of the surface under study at each point $Q$ are random variables. To study their probabilistic characteristics, they were compared with data from subsequent drilling, which, being much more accurate, was accepted as valid. Data processing in large regions of the Volga-Ural oil and gas province for the occurrence of della is an opportunity to find out the probabilistic characteristics of these random variables.

In accordance with the last definition, it is necessary to find at least one closed polygon enclosing the point $Q_0$, in all nodes $Q_i$ of which the values ​​of the functions $H(Q_i)$ will be less than $H(Q_0)$. Since the quantities $H(Q_i)$ themselves are random, then the own “there is at least one closed polygon enclosing the point $Q_0$” is also random. Its probability is the probability that the point $Q_0$ belongs to a local lift, that is, the value of the confidence functions $d(Q_0)$.

Knowing the probabilistic properties of the random variables $H(Q_i)$, we can write formulas for calculating the values ​​of the reliability functions. However, these formulas are very cumbersome and are not practical to use for calculations.
