Problem "Quenta"

Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than June 30, 1973 to the address: 117071, Moscow V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka Publishing House, Quantum magazine.
After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: “Problem “Quenta”, M201, M205” or “... F215”.

Solutions to the problem for each subject (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be sent in separate envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking your decisions). Send the orceal problems proposed for publication along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Problem “Quaenta”, a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).

Send problems from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Problems of increased difficulty are marked with asterisks.

Tasks
M201—206; F213—217

M201. Line $l_1$ intersects sides $a$; $b$ and $c$ triangles (or their extensions) at points $A_1$, $B_1$ and $C_1$ respectively; line $l_2$ intersects them at points $A_2$, $B_2$ and $C_2$. Prove that if points $A_1$ and $A_2$ are symmetrical relative to the midpoint of side $a$, and points $B_1$ and $B_2$ are symmetrical relative to the midpoint of side $b$, then points $C_1$ and $C_2$ are symmetrical relative to the midpoint of side $c$.

Nuem Cong Qui (Hanoi)

M202. Prove that from the sequence $a, a + d, a + 2d, ..., a + nd, ...$, is an infinite arithmetic progression ($d \neq 0$), if and only then can we choose a subsequence that is an infinite geometric progression when the relation $a/d$ is heterononal.
