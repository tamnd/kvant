§ 2. Instead of $q$ members there is one

So, each of the formulas for calculating the sums $S_q(n)$, $q = 1, 2, \ldots$ can be successively applied using the general correspondence (3). Such correspondences, which determine a new member of a certain sequence based on its previous members, are called *recurrent*.

Of course, recurrent correspondences that contain as many terms as possible on the right side are also more preferable—at best, only one: the one preceding the one being defined.

For a sequence of sums $S_q(n)$ such a simple recurrence corresponds.

In order to write it down, we use the following notation. Let $P_m(n)$ be some polynomial of degree $m$ with respect to $n$:

$$P_m(n) = a_0n^m + a_1n^{m-1} + \ldots + a_{m-1}n + a_m.$$

Then $P_m^*(n)$ is a polynomial of degree $m+1$, obtained from the polynomial $P_m(n)$ by replacing the degree $n^k$ in it with the expressions:

$$\frac{n^{k+1}-n}{k+1} \quad (k=0, 1, \ldots, m).$$
