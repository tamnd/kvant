If we assume that the boundary from which the reflection is recorded is flat, and the speed of propagation of elastic waves along the entire path of the wave is constant, then we can explicitly write out the dependence of the times of arrival of the wave on the daylight surface on the coordinates of the seismic receiver and the parameters of the environment. This dependence is called `зодоредфа отраженной волны` in seismic exploration, and the problem of reconstructing the reflection of boundaries according to a given hodograph is called the inverse kinematic problem of seismic exploration. Under the assumptions made, this problem is uniquely solvable, and this method is used in practice under the name `метода эффективной скорости`.

The assumptions introduced above together form the so-called model of the real environment. The model used in the effective velocity method only approximates the actual signal. Of course, it could be conditioned to bring it closer to the real environment. But it is important for us to support that building a model of the environment is an integral part of solving the inverse kinematic problem of seismic exploration. In one form or another, it is always present when interpreting seismic data. However, any accepted model only approximately reflects the actual state of things. This, of course, is not the only difficulty that has arisen in the interpretation of seismic observations: there are also hardware errors, distortions in the registration of the reflected wave by oscillations reflected from other boundaries, etc. are observed.
