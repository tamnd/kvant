Some results and prospects

The thought is directed by itself: the values ​​of the reliability functions are also plotted on the structural map (that is, on the map of the level lines of the horizon being studied, constructed according to the data of the georeconnaissance survey). You can even use the general methods of interpolation, drawing lines of the level of reliability functions (at Fig. 2 and further the level lines of reliability functions are drawn with red lines).

Now we can begin to discuss the new things that the reliability function brought to the structural map.

It turned out that in areas where the values ​​of the reliability functions were quite high (more than 0.8), there is, as a rule, a coincidence of the local uplift constructed according to geological survey data (we will henceforth call it the geophysical uplift) with the real uplift (see Fig. 4, where the local uplift is shaded).

At the highest values ​​of the reliability functions of the order of 0.5 - 0.6, a mixture of the geophysical uplift and the real one was often noted, and the areas with the highest values ​​of the reliability functions tended in location to the real uplift (Fig. 5 and 6). An important practical conclusion follows from this: the exploration well should be located not at those points that turned out to be the highest according to the geophysical construction (that is, in the arch of the geophysical uplift), but at the points with the highest values ​​of the reliability functions, or with the most confidence in the surveyed area belong to the real local uplift.

Finally, geophysical structures for which the maximum values ​​of the confidence functions were more than 0.35, as a rule, turned out to be false; their presence was refuted by subsequent drilling. In this case, all the costs of drilling out such false structures turned out to be involuntary.
