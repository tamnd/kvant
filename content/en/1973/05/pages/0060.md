To the article “Ballistic task in space”

1. Let $O$ be the focus of the parabola, $A$ the vertex of ($OA = h$), and $OB = 2OA$ the perpendicular dropped to the directrix. For the initial point $M_0$ we have $r_0 = OM_0 = M_0N$, where $M_0N$ is the distance from the point $M_0$ to the directrix (Fig. 1). From the optical property of a parabola it follows that the tangent to the parabola at the point $M_0$ is a bisector of the angle $\angle OM_0M$. From figure it is clear that

$$r_0 = M_0N = OB + OM_0 \cos 2\alpha_0 = 2h + r_0 \cos 2\alpha_0.$$

From here

$$h = \frac{r_0}{2} (1 - \cos 2\alpha_0).$$

2. The material point goes along the hyperbola to infinity. In this case, its maximum speed has (according to formula (1)) the following values:

$$v_\infty = \lim_{r \to \infty} v = \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}$$

and is directed along an asymptote.

Since the tangent of the angle of inclination of the asymptote to the hyperbola axis is equal to $\frac{b}{a}$ (Fig. 2), it is easy to show that the distance from the focus to the asymptote is equal to $b$. Therefore, the law of conservation of angular momentum gives

$$mv_\infty b = mv_0r_0 \sin \alpha_0.$$
