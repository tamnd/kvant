9. After the call is opened, the resistance $R$ flows current $i = \frac{u}{R}$, where $u$ is the potential difference across the capacitor, which is equal to $\frac{q}{C}$. Since we are interested in very small changes in charge, we will not take into account that both the current and the potential difference depend on time. During time $\Delta t$, the charge of the capacitor will decrease by amounts $i \Delta t$. Then

$$\Delta q = i \Delta t = \frac{u \Delta t}{R} = \frac{q \Delta t}{RC}.$$

From here

$$\Delta t = \frac{\Delta q}{q} RC = 10^{-3} RC.$$

10. When hitting a block, the jet changes the direction of movement, and therefore the momentum: $\Delta (mv) = F_1 \Delta t$.

The change in the momentum of the fluid $\Delta (mv)$ during time $\Delta t$ is due to the fact that the mass of water $\rho Sv \Delta t$ at a height $h$ changes the vertical speed from $v_1$ to 0, that is

$$\Delta (mv) = \rho Sv v_1 \Delta t.$$

From the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{\mu v_1^2}{2} + \mu gh = \frac{\mu v^2}{2} (\mu - \text{масса небольшой порции воды}) \text{ найдем } v_1 = \sqrt{-2gh + v^2}.$$

Then the strength is $F_1 = \rho Sv v_1 = \rho Sv \sqrt{v^2 - 2gh}$. The same magnitude of force acts on the block from each scab. Since there are $N$ scabs in total, then the total strength

$$F = N \rho Sv \sqrt{v^2 - 2gh}.$$

From the equilibrium condition of the block $P = F$:

$$P = N \rho Sv \sqrt{v^2 - 2gh}.$$

Finally we get

$$h = \frac{v^2}{2g} \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{P}{\rho SNv^2} \right)^2 \right].$$

11. Let the capacitor have already been charged, and the potential difference across it is equal to $U$. Then, when the call is next closed to position $I$, at the initial moment a current $I_1 = \frac{E_1 - U}{R_1}$ flows through resistance $R_1$. During switching on, the charge of the capacitor will change by values ​​$I_1 \tau$, where $\tau$ is the switching time. When the call is closed to position 2, it will receive a current of $I_2 = \frac{E_2 - U}{R_2}$. Since $U$ is the steady-state voltage, it must satisfy the relation $I_1 \tau = -I_2 \tau$, that is, after two switchings, the charge on the capacitor remains unchanged:

$$\frac{E_1 - U}{R_1} = -\frac{E_2 - U}{R_2}.$$

From here

$$U = \frac{E_2 R_1 + E_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}.$$

Charge on capacitor

$$q = CU = C \frac{E_2 R_1 + E_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}.$$
