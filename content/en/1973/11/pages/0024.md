Magnetization of lunar samples

There is no core magnetic field on the Moon. But then lunar rock samples were brought to Earth, and measurements showed that these samples were magnetized.

How did this magnetization occur?

The first natural explanation is the hypothesis that the Moon had a magnetic field in the distant past, which subsequently disappeared. Let us assume that the Moon once had a molten core, which created a strong magnetic field (Fig. 7a). This field magnetized rocks on the surface containing iron. Then, in the absence of the Moon and its daily rotation slowing down, the magnetic field of the core disappeared. However, the magnetized strata of the lunar surface retained their magnetic properties - the so-called remanent magnetization (Fig. 76).

The rock acquires the main share of residual magnetization when it remains: therefore, when the Moon remained, the core field was there. Based on the residual magnetization, knowing the composition of the sample, one can judge the magnitude of the magnetic field in which this sample cooled. On the other hand, the age of rocks can be determined by the relative daily average of radioactive elements and their decay products*).

Such combined changes in radioactivity and magnetization of lunar rock samples showed that approximately 3.1 billion years ago the Moon had its own magnetic field of at least 1000γ.
