The problem, however, can be solved more simply if we assume that the object simultaneously participates in two independent movements: it rises uniformly with a speed of $v_1$ and falls freely. Let's connect the reference system to the ground, and direct the coordinate axis upward. Then the equation of motion of an object from a height $h_1$ to the ground will be written as follows:

$$0 = h_1 + v_1 t_2 - \frac{gt^2}{2}$$

Problem 5. A body is thrown vertically upward with a certain initial speed. When it reaches a height of $H = 100$ m from the ground, from the same initial point and with the same initial speed a body is thrown into the sky. At what altitude do they occur? What speeds will they have at the moment of meeting? At what initial speed were the bodies thrown? Air resistance is a concern.

Let us first consider some features of the motion of a body thrown vertically upward. This complex movement is the sum of two simple ones - uniform movement and free fall. Moreover, each movement occurs independently of the other and regardless of whether the body rises or falls. Therefore, we can say that the time it takes a body to travel the same section of the path up and down is the same and that the speeds of the body at a certain height when moving up or down are the same in magnitude.

Let us show, for example, that the time the body rises to its maximum height is equal to the time it falls to the initial position and that the final speed is equal in magnitude to the initial one.
