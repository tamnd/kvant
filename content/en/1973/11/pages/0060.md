To solve problems on this topic, it is enough to know the equations for speed and displacement. In scalar form they are written like this:

$$v = v_0 + at,$$
$$x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{at^2}{2}.$$

Here $v_0$ is the initial speed of the point, $x_0$ is the initial coordinate, $a$ is the acceleration, $v$ and $x$ are the corresponding speed and coordinate of the point at time $t$. The quantities $v_0$, $a$, $v$ and $x$ will be considered positive when their direction coincides with the positive direction of the selected coordinate axis $OX$, negative otherwise.

It is useful to begin solving a problem with a brief recording of its conditions, if possible completely translating the problem into the language of notation conditions. In this case, it is necessary to ensure that the units of measurement of all quantities are given in the same system of units. It is better to carry out all calculations in general form, that is, in letter notation, and substitute numerical values ​​into the final result.

Problem 3. Two values ​​of the unity of friendship at a glance: one of them, having a speed of 5.4 km/h, descends from the mountain with an acceleration of 0.2 m/s²; the other, having a speed of 18 km/h, rises uphill with an acceleration of 20 cm/s². How long will it take for them to meet?

Let the origin coincide with the initial position of the first quantity, and the positive direction of the coordinate axis coincide with the direction of its initial velocity. Then a short statement of the condition will look like this:

$$v_{01} = 5,4 \text{ km/ч} = 1,5 \text{ м/c}$$
$$v_{02} = -18 \text{ km/ч} = -5 \text{ м/c}$$
$$a_1 = 0,2 \text{ m/c}^2$$

$$a_2 = 20 \text{ cm/c}^2 = 0,20 \text{ m/c}^2$$
$$x_{02} = 195 \text{ m}$$
$$t = ?$$

Let us write the equation of motion for each quantity:

$$x_1 = v_{01} t + \frac{a_1 t^2}{2},$$
$$x_2 = x_{02} + v_{02} t + \frac{a_2 t^2}{2},$$
$$x_1 = x_2.$$

and $a_1 = a_2$ by condition. At the moment of meeting

$$t = \frac{x_{02}}{v_{01} - v_{02}} = 30c.$$

This could be the end of the solution, but in this case you should make sure that the answer you receive has a physical meaning. To do this, we find the speed of the second magnitude 30 s after the start of movement:

$$v_2 = v_{02} + a_2 t = -5 \text{ m/c} + 0,2 \text{ m/c}^2 \cdot 30 \text{ c} = 1 \text{ m/c}.$$

It turns out that the second velinistic by this time will affect the mountain, and not rise up the mountain. Obviously, this task is composed of an inconclusive result.
