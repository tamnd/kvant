This device is essentially a special type of transformer.

The described transformer consists of two windings, primary and secondary, wound on a common ferromagnetic core *. In the SG magnetometer, the core is a thin strip of permalloy, a special alloy of iron (20%) and nickel (80%).

Induction B of the resulting magnetic field in the core consists of two parts: induction $B_0$ of the field created by the current of the magnetizing coil, and induction $B_\phi$ of the field created by oriented elementary currents in the substance itself. The ratio $\frac{B}{B_0} = \frac{B_0 + B_\phi}{B_0} = \mu$, called the magnetic permeability of a substance, for ferromagnetic materials is much larger (that is, $B_\phi \gg B_0$) **).

The product of induction B and the area S of the core cross-section perpendicular to vector B, that is, BS, is called magnetic flux.

According to the law of electromagnetic induction, a change in magnetic flux over time generates an electromotive force of induction in the secondary winding, the magnitude of which is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux and the number of turns in the secondary winding. If the number of turns in the secondary winding is greater than in the primary, we will get a voltage in it greater than in the primary (step-up transformer). The purpose of a transformer is to increase (or decrease) the voltage applied to the primary winding.

*) Let us recall that ferromagnetic are materials that are capable of strongly magnetizing, even when they are in a relatively weak external magnetic field (mainly iron, nickel and some of their compounds and alloys).

**) The value of magnetic permeability can change greatly when the external magnetic field changes. In particular, permaloy differs in that, at very weak external fields, the value of μ changes sharply with changes in the external field.

In the magnetometer, as in the transformer, there are primary and secondary windings, wound on a common permalloy core (for the magnetometer diagram, see Fig. 8 in the Appendix). An alternating current with a frequency of 5000 2Hz is supplied to the primary winding from a special generator. This current creates an alternating magnetic flux F in the permaloy core, and a change in time of this flux induces e in the secondary winding. d.s. induction. However, the purpose of a magnetmeter is not to increase or decrease the voltage, but to change the time-constant weak external magnetic field in which the device is located.

The external field changes the magnetic permeability of the core and thereby affects the secondary voltage (the principle of operation of the magnetmeter is described in more detail in the Appendix). Transformer cores are made of transformer steel, an alloy of iron and silicon; weak external magnetic fields do not change the magnetic permeability of this material.

The magnetometer has three mutually perpendicular coils; they change the field components in three mutually perpendicular directions.

The sensitivity of the device is very high. For example, a magnetmeter installed on the artificial lunar satellite “Luna-10” records changes in the magnetic inductance of 1 gamma. (γ is a unit of measurement of magnetic induction; 1γ = 10⁻⁹ mL. The magnetic field at the Earth’s surface is 30÷60 thousand gamma, the interplanetary field is 5γ.)
