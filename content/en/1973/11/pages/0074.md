⟦folio 72⟧

The International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren quickly gained greater international authority.

Thus, in the first four Olympids only teams from most European socialist countries took part, in the V MMO the team of Yugoslavia took part, in the VI - Mongolia, VII - Finland, in the IX - England, France, Italy and Sweden, in the XI - Belgium and the Netherlands, in the XII - Australia and in the XIII - Cuba.

At the IV MMO, the Charter of the International Mathematical Olympiads was adopted, which has remained virtually unchanged to this day.

Main provisions of the Charter:
1. The International Mathematical Olympiad is held daily in one of the participating countries.
2. Each team consists of 8 participants: students of secondary general education and special schools, no older than 19 years, a team leader and his deputy.
3. All team leaders (one from each country) form the International Jury.
4. Before the Olympics, each participating country must send its tasks to the organizing committee of the organizing country.
5. The team leader and his deputy will be directly involved in the work of the jury in selecting the task and checking the work.
6. The organizing country bears all costs associated with hosting the Olympiad. Participating countries bear the cost of paying for the project from the Voinx delegation to the Olympic venue and back.
Typically the Olympics last 2 days. On each of these days, participants solve 3 problems, which will take 4 hours to solve.

Despite the fact that MMO tasks are very interesting, they are inferior in difficulty to the tasks offered at the All-Union Olympids.

All problems of the International Mathematical Olympiads (from I to XI inclusive) are placed in the book “International Mathematical Olympiads”*.

Starting from the XI MMO, the magazine «Kvant» regularly publishes detailed information about the progress of the competitions and analysis of the tasks proposed at them**.

By its status, the Olympics is not a team championship. Diplomas (I, II and III degrees) are awarded individually to Olympiad participants for the best solutions to problems. But, as a rule, the number of points scored by the team is published.

In honor of the MMO, special badges are issued that are awarded to all MMO participants (see page 4 of the cover).

The Soviet team has been involved in MMOs since their organization.

I MMO (1959, Romania). The Soviet team consisted of 4 participants: Andrey Toom (III degree diploma), Viktor Fedorets (certificate of honor), Valery Frolov (certificate of honor) and Alexander Chemev.

In II MMO (1960, Romania) and III MMO (1961, Hungary), the Soviet team did not take part.

IV MMO (1962, Czechoslovakia). The Soviet team consisted of 8 participants: Nosif Bernishtein (1st degree diploma), Lydia Goncharova (1st degree diploma), Alexey Pomenun (2nd degree diploma), Grigory Marrushis (2nd degree diploma), Gennady Kuranov (3rd degree diploma), Denmark Muitari (3rd degree diploma), Evgeny Pankramov and Alexander Shermenev.
