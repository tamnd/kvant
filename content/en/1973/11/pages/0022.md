The driving nona is the current. A current in a magnetic field is acted upon by a force F, perpendicular to the direction of the field and the velocity vector.

figure 4 shows how the Earth's magnetic field affects the movement of solar wind ions. From figure it is clear that the rectilinear trajectory, when it hits the Earth’s magnetic field, is bent and turns into a circle.

However, a stream of fast ions can distort the magnetic field into which it strikes. The theory shows that the result of the interaction of the solar wind with the Earth’s magnetic field depends on the correspondence between the kinetic energy of the ions found

per unit volume of space “filled” with solar wind - $n \frac{mv^2}{2}$ (n is the density of ions) and the energy density of the magnetic field (energy per unit volume of space), which is proportional to the value $B^2/2$ (B is the induction value of the Earth’s magnetic field at a given height from its surface). Far from the Earth, at distances greater than 100,000 km, the Earth's magnetic field is small and $n \frac{mv^2}{2}$ » $\frac{B^2}{2}$ - the solar wind moves freely. At smaller distances $n \frac{mv^2}{2} < \frac{B^2}{2}$, ion trajectories are bent by the Earth’s magnetic field, and the solar wind does not penetrate to distances less than ~ 60 thousand km from the Earth’s surface.

However, not being able to “pierce” the magnetic shield, the solar wind deforms the Earth’s magnetic field. The region of the Earth's magnetic field, somewhat deformed by the solar wind, is called the magnetosphere.
