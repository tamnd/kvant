Uniform linear movement
Uniform linear motion is a motion in which a material point undergoes equal movements over any equal periods of time. The equation of such motion in vector form is written as follows:
$$S = vt$$
where $S$ is displacement, $v$ is movement speed, $t$ is time.

The movement of a material point is always considered relative to some body, which in this problem is taken to be stationary and is called the reporting body. A coordinate system is associated with it; Together with the body of the report, they form the reporting system. For rectilinear motion, it is enough to select one coordinate axis, for example $OX$. Then the position of the point will be determined by its coordinate $x$. The equation of uniform motion in scalar form will look like this:
$$x = x_0 + vt$$
where $x_0$ is the coordinate of the point at time $t = 0$.

The correct choice of reporting system often makes the task easier. Let's consider several specific tasks.

Problem 1. A passenger sitting at the train window, induce its speed $v_1 = 72$ km/h, we see an oncoming train, induce its speed $v_2 = 31,4$ km/h, for 10 seconds. Determine the length of the oncoming train, and direct the coordinate axis in the direction of the speed of the oncoming train. The values ​​of velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$ are specified relative to some fixed reference system, for example the earth. In relation to the passenger moving at a speed of $v_1$, the oncoming train has a so-called relative speed of $v_2$, which is equal to
$$v_2 = v_2 - v_1$$
or in scalar form
$$v_2 = v_2 - (-v_1) = v_2 + v_1.$$
Then the required length of the oncoming train is
$$l = v_2t = (v_1 + v_2)t = 290\text{ м.}$$

Problem 2. A fisherman is sailing a boat up the river; While driving under the bridge, he dropped his straw hat into the water. After half a century he discovered this and, turning back, found the hat 5 km below the bridge. What is the speed of marking the river if the fisherman, moving down the river, rowed the same way?

Let's connect the reporting systems with the water in the river, that is, with the hat. The fisherman moves away from the hat and approaches it at the same speed, therefore, he reaches it halfway after he discovered the loss, or an hour after the hat fell into the water. During this time, the hat traveled 5 km relative to the ground. This means that the river tagging speed is 5 km/h.
