Uniform linear movement
Uniform linear motion is a motion in which a material point undergoes equal movements over any equal periods of time. The equation of such motion in vector form is written as follows:
$$S = vt$$
where $S$ is displacement, $v$ is movement speed, $t$ is time.

The movement of a material point is always considered relative to some body, which in this problem is taken to be stationary and is called the reporting body. A coordinate system is associated with it; Together with the body of the report, they form the reporting system. For rectilinear motion, it is enough to select one coordinate axis, for example $OX$. Then the position of the point will be determined by its coordinate $x$. The equation of uniform motion in scalar form will look like this:
$$x = x_0 + vt$$
where $x_0$ is the coordinate of the point at time $t = 0$.

The correct choice of reporting system often makes the task easier. Let's consider several specific tasks.

Problem 1. A passenger sitting at the train window, induce its speed $v_1 = 72$ km/h, we see an oncoming train, induce its speed $v_2 = 31,4$ km/h, for 10 seconds. Determine the length of the oncoming train, and direct the coordinate axis in the direction of the speed of the oncoming train. The values ​​of velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$ are specified relative to some fixed reference system, for example the earth. In relation to the passenger moving at a speed of $v_1$, the oncoming train has a so-called relative speed of $v_2$, which is equal to
$$v_2 = v_2 - v_1$$
or in scalar form
$$v_2 = v_2 - (-v_1) = v_2 + v_1.$$
Then the required length of the oncoming train is
$$l = v_2t = (v_1 + v_2)t = 290\text{ м.}$$

Problem 2. A fisherman is sailing a boat up the river; While driving under the bridge, he dropped his straw hat into the water. After half a century he discovered this and, turning back, found the hat 5 km below the bridge. What is the speed of marking the river if the fisherman, moving down the river, rowed the same way?

Let's connect the reporting systems with the water in the river, that is, with the hat. The fisherman moves away from the hat and approaches it at the same speed, therefore, he reaches it halfway after he discovered the loss, or an hour after the hat fell into the water. During this time, the hat traveled 5 km relative to the ground. This means that the river tagging speed is 5 km/h.

If the speed of a material point is not constant, but at any equal

between periods of time it changes by the same amount, then in this case we speak of uniform motion. Movement is called uniformly accelerated if the speed increases, and uniformly slowed down if the speed decreases.

To solve problems on this topic, it is enough to know the equations for speed and displacement. In scalar form they are written like this:

$$v = v_0 + at,$$
$$x = x_0 + v_0 t + \frac{at^2}{2}.$$

Here $v_0$ is the initial speed of the point, $x_0$ is the initial coordinate, $a$ is the acceleration, $v$ and $x$ are the corresponding speed and coordinate of the point at time $t$. The quantities $v_0$, $a$, $v$ and $x$ will be considered positive when their direction coincides with the positive direction of the selected coordinate axis $OX$, negative otherwise.

It is useful to begin solving a problem with a brief recording of its conditions, if possible completely translating the problem into the language of notation conditions. In this case, it is necessary to ensure that the units of measurement of all quantities are given in the same system of units. It is better to carry out all calculations in general form, that is, in letter notation, and substitute numerical values ​​into the final result.

Let's solve the following problem.

Problem 3. Two values ​​of the unity of friendship at a glance: one of them, having a speed of 5.4 km/h, descends from the mountain with an acceleration of 0.2 m/s²; the other, having a speed of 18 km/h, rises uphill with an acceleration of 20 cm/s². How long will it take for them to meet?

Let the origin coincide with the initial position of the first quantity, and the positive direction of the coordinate axis coincide with the direction of its initial velocity. Then a short statement of the condition will look like this:

$$v_{01} = 5,4 \text{ km/ч} = 1,5 \text{ м/c}$$
$$v_{02} = -18 \text{ km/ч} = -5 \text{ м/c}$$
$$a_1 = 0,2 \text{ m/c}^2$$

$$a_2 = 20 \text{ cm/c}^2 = 0,20 \text{ m/c}^2$$
$$x_{02} = 195 \text{ m}$$
$$t = ?$$

Let us write the equation of motion for each quantity:

$$x_1 = v_{01} t + \frac{a_1 t^2}{2},$$
$$x_2 = x_{02} + v_{02} t + \frac{a_2 t^2}{2},$$
$$x_1 = x_2.$$

and $a_1 = a_2$ by condition. At the moment of meeting

$$t = \frac{x_{02}}{v_{01} - v_{02}} = 30c.$$

This could be the end of the solution, but in this case you should make sure that the answer you receive has a physical meaning. To do this, we find the speed of the second magnitude 30 s after the start of movement:

$$v_2 = v_{02} + a_2 t = -5 \text{ m/c} + 0,2 \text{ m/c}^2 \cdot 30 \text{ c} = 1 \text{ m/c}.$$

It turns out that the second velinistic by this time will affect the mountain, and not rise up the mountain. Obviously, this task is composed of an inconclusive result.

Problem 4. An object is taken vertically from the ground with an acceleration of 2.45 m/s². After 8 seconds he begins to move, an object falls out of his condol. After how much time and at what speed will the object hit the ground? Air resistance is great.

Since at first the object moves together with the aerostat, then after $t_1 = 8 \text{ c}$ it rises to a certain height $h_1$ and we will have a speed $v_1$, and

$$h_1 = \frac{at_1^2}{2} \text{ и } v_1 = at_1.$$

The further movement of the object can be described in different ways.

The problem, however, can be solved more simply if we assume that the object simultaneously participates in two independent movements: it rises uniformly with a speed of $v_1$ and falls freely. Let's connect the reference system to the ground, and direct the coordinate axis upward. Then the equation of motion of an object from a height $h_1$ to the ground will be written as follows:

$$0 = h_1 + v_1 t_2 - \frac{gt^2}{2}$$

($t_2$ - time of movement of the object). Substituting the expressions for $h_1$ and $v_1$ into this equation, we obtain

$$\frac{gt^2}{2} - at_1 t_2 - \frac{at^2}{2} = 0,$$

from where $t_2 \approx 6,5$ s.

Problem 5. A body is thrown vertically upward with a certain initial speed. When it reaches a height of $H = 100$ m from the ground, from the same initial point and with the same initial speed a body is thrown into the sky. At what altitude do they occur? What speeds will they have at the moment of meeting? At what initial speed were the bodies thrown? Air resistance is a concern.

Let us first consider some features of the motion of a body thrown vertically upward. This complex movement is the sum of two simple ones - uniform movement and free fall. Moreover, each movement occurs independently of the other and regardless of whether the body rises or falls. Therefore, we can say that the time it takes a body to travel the same section of the path up and down is the same and that the speeds of the body at a certain height when moving up or down are the same in magnitude.

Let us show, for example, that the time the body rises to its maximum height is equal to the time it falls to the initial position and that the final speed is equal in magnitude to the initial one.
