Why is the pillow soft? Why is it comfortable to lie on a feather bed or on an air mattress? Why is lying on boards or hard ground uncomfortable?

If you simply say that fluff and air are soft, and boards and earth are hard, you will not be entirely right. The point is not at all in the properties of the materials - both boards and hard clay can be “soft”. And from solid materials you can make a comfortable “spoon” if you give it the shape of a human body.

Imagine that you lie down on soft clay and leave a depression in it that matches the shape of your body. Once dry, the clay will become hard as rock. If you now lie in the resulting depression, then you will be very comfortable, despite the fact that your “spoon” cannot be called soft (although when it dries, the size will change somewhat, but we will not take this into account).

So what's the matter? It turns out that the impression of softness or hardness depends not on the properties of the materials, but on the amount of pressure on the surface of the body. Let's do a little calculation.

Let us assume that the mass of an adult is about 60 kg, and the body surface is approximately 2 m². If a person lies in a bed that bends and seems to “encompass” the body, approximately a quarter of the entire surface of his body is in contact with it.

It is not difficult to calculate that in this case there are only 12 G per square centimeter of body surface. And if the same person is lying on a hard, inelastic surface, the contacting area will be only about one hundred square centimeters. Then for one square centimeter there will already be 600 G, that is, the pressure will increase 50 times!
