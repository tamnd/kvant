What is the excess pressure inside such a bubble? A cylindrical surface has different curvature in different directions. Along the generatrix of the cylinder, the curvature is zero (the generatrix is ​​a straight line) *), and in the section perpendicular to the cylinder axis, its curvature is equal to 1/R, where R is the radius of the cylinder. What value of p should we substitute into the previous formula? It turns out that the pressure difference on different sides of any surface is determined by its average curvature. What are these quantities?

*) What is the curvature of a plane curve? The curvature of a circle is defined in the same way as the curvature of a sphere: $\rho_{\text{окр}} = 1/R$, where R is the radius of the circle. If the curve is not a circle, then, nevertheless, its individual small sections can be approximately considered arcs of circles of certain radii. The reciprocals of these radii are called the curvatures of a plane curve at its various points.

Let us draw through the normal to the surface at point A of the plane. Sections of a cylindrical surface by these planes (they are called normal sections) can be a circle, an ellipse or a straight line (Fig. 4). It is easy to see that the curvatures of these sections at point A are different: the maximum curvature has a cross section - a circle, and the minimum curvature, equal to zero, is a straight line (longitudinal section). The average curvature $\rho_{\text{сред}}$ is defined as the result of the maximum and minimum curvature of the normal sections:

$$\rho_{\text{сред}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{max}} + \rho_{\text{min}}}{2}.$$

This definition is suitable not only for a cylinder, and this way one can determine the average curvature at a given point of any surface.

A cylindrical surface at any point has a maximum curvature $\rho_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{R}$, where R is the radius of the cross section of the cylinder, and $\rho_{\text{min}} = 0$. Therefore, the average curvature is $\rho_{\text{цил}} = \frac{1}{2R}$, and the excess pressure inside the cylindrical bubble is $\Delta p_{\text{цил}} = \frac{\sigma'}{R}$.

As you can see, the cylindrical bubble has an excess pressure - the same as that of a spherical injection bubble of large radius. Therefore, the radius of the spherical “caps” of a cylindrical bubble will be larger than the radius of the cylinder, and they are spherical segments, not received ones.

What if we completely destroy the excess pressure in such a bubble, forcing, for example, the “caps” to burst?
