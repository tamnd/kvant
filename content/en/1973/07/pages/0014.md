The tendency of surface tension to reduce the surface of a liquid is usually counteracted by other forces. For example, a drop of liquid is almost never a sphere, although a sphere has the smallest surface area of ​​all figures for a given volume. When a drop accumulates on a stationary horizontal surface, it becomes flattened. A drop falling in the air also has a complex shape. And only a drop in weightlessness takes on a perfect spherical shape.

The Belgian scientist J. Plateau first agreed to eliminate the effect of gravity when studying the surface tension of a liquid in the middle of the last century. Of course, at that time they did not even dream of real weightlessness, and Plato simply proposed balancing the force with gravity using the Archimedean buoyancy force. He placed the liquid under study (oil) in a solution of the same density, and, as his biographer writes, “he was surprised to see that the oil took on a spherical shape; he immediately accepted his rule “to be surprised in time,” and this phenomenon then served as the subject of long reflection for him.”

Plato used his method to study various phenomena. For example, he studied the process of formation and separation of a drop of liquid at the end of a tube.

Obviously, no matter how slowly we grow the drop, it comes off the tube so quickly that the eye cannot follow the details of this process. Plato placed the end of the tube into a liquid whose density was only slightly less than that of a drop. In this case, the effect of gravity is weakened; you can grow a very large drop and see how it comes off the tube.

figure 2 shows photographs showing various stages of the red process of formation and separation of a drop (the photographs were obtained using a modern method - using high-speed film). Let's try to explain this phenomenon. As long as the drop grows slowly, we can assume that it is in equilibrium at every moment of time. Then, for a given volume of a drop, its shape is determined from the condition that the sum of the surface energy and the potential energy of the drop due to gravity is minimal.
