⟦folio 32⟧

Under the influence of the weight of the load, the system will always move. You can directly calculate the amount of heat released during movement, but for this you need to know a lot of additional data - the amount of water, its viscosity, the shape of the connecting tube, the nature of the movement of water, etc. The solution is greatly simplified if you use the law of conservation of energy.

Since at the initial and final moments both water and weight accumulated, heat is released only due to a decrease in the potential energy of the system $W$

$$Q = W_{\text{н}} - W_{\text{к}},$$

(1)

where $W_{\text{н}}$ and $W_{\text{к}}$ are the initial and final values ​​of the potential energy. It is easy to see that the potential energy of the weight has decreased, and that of the water has increased.

Let’s write down the expressions for $W_{\text{н}}$ and $W_{\text{к}}$ (let’s take up to the vessels as the zero level):

$$W_{\text{н}} = \rho g \frac{h^2}{2} (S_1 + S_2) + mgh$$

(2)

(h is the initial water level in everyday vessels),

$$W_{\text{к}} = \rho g \frac{h^2}{2} S_1 + \rho g \frac{h^2}{2} S_2 + mgh_2$$

(3)

($h_1$ and $h_2$ are the final levels in the first and second vessels, respectively).

The values ​​$h_1$ and $h_2$ can be found from the condition of equilibrium of the system and constant mass of water:

$$\rho gh_1 = \rho gh_2 + \frac{mg}{S_2},$$

(4)

$$\rho h_1 S_1 + \rho h_2 S_2 = \rho h (S_1 + S_2).$$

(5)

Solving the system of equations (1)–(5), we find

$$Q = \frac{m^2 g S_1}{2 \rho S_2 (S_1 + S_2)} \approx 0,08 \text{ дж.}$$

This task, which at first glance has nothing in common with the previous one, is very similar to the transition of a situation - on the contrary. Consequently, the process of recharging the capacitor occurred, and an electric current passed through the circuit. If the resistance of the wires and current sources is known, then the current can be determined as a function of time and the heat generated in the circuit can be calculated.

The law of conservation of energy makes it possible to obtain a charge even without knowing the magnitude of the process, which means that the energy of the capacitor has not changed (in the first case, as in the second, the total e.d.c. of the sources charging the capacitor is equal to $E$). Therefore, all work done by current sources must turn into heat. And the work of this is not difficult to calculate. Source having e. d. c. $2E$, does not do any work, since in position 2 it is disconnected from the circuit. The source from e. d. c. $E$ does work in crossing the charge $q = 2CE$.

Thus, the heat released in the circuit is equal to

$$Q = A = E \cdot 2CE = 2CE^2.$$
