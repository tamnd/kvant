At $H = d$ ($2ad = -v_0^2 \cos^2 \alpha$) the trajectory of the electric plane contacts the plate at point $x_2 = -\frac{v_0^2 \sin \alpha \cos \alpha}{a} = -\frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\alpha}{2a}$. This is half the flight range of a body thrown at an angle to the horizon.

In order to find the speed of an electrop at any moment of time, you need to find only the vertical component $v_y$ of its speed at this moment, since $v_x = \text{const}$. The value $v_y$ changes over time according to the formula

$$v_y = v_0 \cos \alpha + at.$$

At time $t = t_1$

$$v_y = v_0 \cos \alpha + at_1 = \sqrt{v_0^2 \cos^2 \alpha + 2ad}.$$

The speed of the electrop at this moment is

$$v_1 = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2ad} = \sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{e\sigma d}{\varepsilon_0 m}}.$$

In order to find the speed of the electrop, one could calculate differently - use the fact that the change in the kinetic energy of a body is always equal to the work done by a force acting on the body. This means that

$$\frac{mv_1^2}{2} - \frac{mv_0^2}{2} = Eed.$$

From here

$$v_1 = \sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{2Eed}{m}} = \sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{e\sigma d}{\varepsilon_0 m}}.$$

This speed is made at a vertical angle $\beta$ such that

$$\sin \beta = \frac{v_x}{v_1} = \frac{v_0}{\sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{e\sigma d}{\varepsilon_0 m}}} \sin \alpha.$$
