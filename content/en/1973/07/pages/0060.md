To the article “How many operations on sets are there?”

Let $f$ be a non-linear operation; the distance is the polynomial $\tilde{f}(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ that flows from it. It will be required to include single beams above the first degree. There may be several of them. Let's choose one of them that has a minimum degree (but greater than 1). Let it be $x_1 \dots x_h$, $k \geq 2$. Let us substitute together $A_3, A_4, \dots, A_k$ the universal set $I$, and together $A_{k+1}, \dots, A_n$ the empty set 0. An operation will arise on a pair of the set $A_1, A_2$, which will result in a polynomial of the form $x_1x_2 + ax_1 + bx_2 + c$, where $a, b, c$ can take values 0, 1 (in $f$ you need to substitute $x_3 = x_4 = \dots = x_h = 1$, $x_{k+1} = \dots = x_n = 0$; show that in this case all monorays above the first degree, different from $x_1 \dots x_h$, are formed in 0 form of the minimal degree $k$.
