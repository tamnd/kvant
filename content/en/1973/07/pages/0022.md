Interestingly, the formation of stable foam is only a side effect of the decrease in surface tension when detergents dissolve. Foam is formed from air bubbles that fall inside the water (when mixed with a stream of water). Then they float to its surface and find themselves surrounded by a film of liquid. If the surface tension is not high, then the excess pressure inside the bubble is low, and it should not burst. Of course, the high strength of soap films also plays a significant role here.

The mechanism of the washing action, which was discussed here, is also of interest in connection with other important technical tasks - coating surfaces with varnishes and paints, bonding, dyeing fabric with suspensions, making waterproof fabrics.

For example, in order to make a fabric water-repellent, its educational specialized substance that forms a thin film around each fiber. This film significantly increases the surface tension at the water-fabric interface, but little changes the tension at the fabric-air interface. If the formula for the contact angle θ is formed, then it is easy to see that the angle θ increases. As a result, water does not wet the fabric, but collects on it into drops that roll off the materials.

Adsorption films are used to reduce the evaporation of water from the surface of reservoirs, which is an important problem in arid areas. A protective film is easily created over the entire surface of a reservoir, since adsorption always reduces surface tension (otherwise adsorption would not produce surface energy at all). The surface tension force of pure water acts on the film boundary, tending to stretch the film, and

the tension force of the film itself is directed in the opposite direction. The surface tension of clean water is greater, and as a result the film covers the entire clean surface.

To create protective films, a special substance is used - hexadecanol. Under normal conditions, this is a solid substance that floats on the surface of the water. If the entire surface of the water is covered with an adsorbed monomolecular layer, then the substance is not consumed. However, if the film gets damaged somewhere. As the surface tension increases in this place, it pulls the film from neighboring areas, etc. As a result, the film is broken and near the floating piece of substance, which restores the film.

It was found that to create a protective film over an area of ​​1 EA, about 2 EA of hexadecanol is required, and the loss is on average 2-3 monomolecular layers per day. Therefore, the consumption of substance to maintain the film is only about 60 ea per day per 1 ea, and this method turns out to be economically profitable. For example, in Australia, it saves about 10 million liters of water per hectare of water surface every day.
