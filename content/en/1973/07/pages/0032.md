⟦folio 30⟧

The law of conservation of energy is one of the fundamental laws of nature. He argues that energy neither arises nor disappears; in any physical processes it only transforms from one type to another or passes from one body to another. We can say that this law establishes the conditions for the energy balance of physical processes.

The law of conservation of energy is not a consequence of any other physical laws. It was obtained as a result of a generalization of the entire set of experimental facts, which cover both large-scale phenomena on a cosmic scale and their own origins in the world of molecules, atoms and elementary particles. Facts characterizing mechanical, thermal, electromagnetic, nuclear, chemical, and biological processes give no reason to doubt the validity of the law of conservation of energy.

In its most general mathematical form, this law can be written as follows:

$$\Delta E + \Delta U = A + Q.$$  (1)

Here $\Delta E$ is the change in the mechanical energy of the system, equal to the sum of changes in the kinetic and potential energy of the bodies included in the system under consideration; $\Delta U$ — change in the internal energy of the bodies of the system; $A$ - work of external forces; $Q$ is the heat received by the system.

When considering any specific processes, the law of conservation of energy is often written in a simpler form. Thus, when solving mechanical problems, internal heat transfer ($Q = 0$) is usually not used. In this case the equality holds:

$$\Delta E + \Delta U = 0.$$

An example of such a process in which mechanical energy is converted into internal energy is inelastic impact.

If mechanical forces act in the system, depending only on the relative position of the body, then

$$\Delta E = A,$$

and in isolated systems

$$\Delta E = 0.$$

The last two equalities can be obtained directly from Newton's laws. (Consider, for example, the free fall of bodies in a gravity field, as is done in a school physics textbook for grade 8). Therefore, we can say that the law of conservation of energy in mechanics is not independent, although the general law of conservation and transformation of energy (let us note this again) cannot be derived from any other physical laws.

In the theory of thermal processes, problems are usually considered in which the change in mechanical energy c can be neglected.
