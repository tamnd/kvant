the Tin word “catena” - chain; This surface can be obtained by rotating around a curve that has the shape of a chain suspended horizontally from the ends - a catenary line). What's the matter?

Take a closer look at this surface (Fig. 5). Pay attention to its bottleneck - interception. It is easy to see that this interception is both convex and concave. Its cross-section is a circle, and its longitudinal is a chain line. The curvature of the direction inward should increase the pressure inside the bubble, while the curvature of the direction outward should decrease it. (The pressure under the concave surface is greater than above it). In the case of a cataenode, these curvatures are equal in magnitude, and since they are directed in opposite directions, the average curvature is zero. Consequently, there is no excess pressure inside such a bubble.

Fig. 6.

There are many other surfaces that are curved in all directions, but still their average curvature is zero, and these surfaces do not produce any pressure. To get them, you just need to take any bent wire frame and immerse it in soapy water. Taking out the frame, you can see a variety of surfaces with zero average curvature, the shape of which depends on the shape of the frame. However, the kataenode is the only rotation surface other than the plane with zero mean curvature.

One of the tasks of the special mathematical science of differential geometry is the determination of such surfaces with zero mean curvature, spanned by closed spatial curves. There is an exact mathematical theorem stating that the area of ​​such surfaces is always minimal, and it will now seem very convenient to us.

Soap bubbles can connect with each other to form foam. Despite the chaos in the arrangement of soap films in captivity, the following law is always followed: the films intersect each other only at equal angles (see photo on the cover).

Consider, for example, two bubbles in contact with each other and having a common partition. Excessive (compared to atmospheric) pressures inside the bubble are located and determined by Laplace’s formula:

$$\Delta p_1 = \frac{2\sigma'}{R_1}, \quad \Delta p_2 = \frac{2\sigma'}{R_2}.$$

Therefore, the partition must be such as to create additional pressure equal to the pressure difference of the bubble. Therefore, it must have a certain curvature. The radius $R_3$ of the curvature of the partition is determined from the correspondence
