Since the direction of light is determined by random factors, energy flows in different directions must be equalized. Isotropic (uniformly distributed in all directions) radiation occurs in the chamber.

Let us write down the energy balance condition for such radiation. Let a light flux (energy per unit time) $P_0$ enter the chamber, a flux $P_1$ exits and is absorbed - $P_2$. According to the law of conservation of energy

$$P_0 = P_1 + P_2.$$ (1)

Let's express $P_2$ through $P_1$. Since the radiation is isotropic, any area receives a luminous flux proportional to the size of this area. If a flow $P_1$ comes out through the reciprocal area $\sigma$, then a flow equal to $\frac{P_1}{\sigma}(S - \sigma)$ falls on the walls of the chamber with an area $S - \sigma$.

$$P_2 = k \frac{P_1}{\sigma}(S - \sigma) \approx kP_1 \frac{S}{\sigma}.$$ (2)

From (1) and (2) we find the desired quantity

$$\frac{P_1}{P_0} = \frac{1}{1 + k \frac{S}{\sigma}} = \frac{1}{3}.$$
