Movement of charged particles in an electric field

The nature of the movement of a body (the type of its trajectory, formulas, the expression of the dependence of the movement and speed of the body on time, etc.) does not depend on the nature of the force acting on the body. It is determined only by how this force depends on the coordinates of the body and on time. Therefore, problems that differ in appearance, in which the dependence of force, for example, on coordinates, are the same, are solved in the same way today. This allows us to use what we know about the movement of a body in other “analogoric” fields when solving many problems involving the movement of charged particles in an electric field.

Consider, for example, the following problem.

Problem 1. When considering a house with an end-to-end flat monk horizontal uniform charged flat electron speed was equal to $v_0$, and the vector $v_0$ made an angle a with the vertical (Fig. 1). The charge density of flat planes is $\sigma$. What is the trajectory of the electron if we pass through the plane for it? What will be the speed of the electron when it is at a distance b below the plane?

Fig. 1.

When solving the problem, ignore the force of gravity and the influence of the electron on the charge distribution along the lassine.

Solution. A non-finite plane, uniformly charged with a surface charge density $\sigma$, creates a uniform field with the direction

$$E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0},$$

and above and below the plate the vector is directed in different directions. Therefore, when considering the motion of an electron, it is convenient to separately consider the motion of the electron above and below the plate.

In a uniform electric field with direction $E$, a constant force acts on the electron

$$F = eE,$$

(where $e = -1,6 \cdot 10^{-19} \text{ к} - \text{заряд электрона}).$ This force imparts constant acceleration to the electron

$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{e}{m} E,$$

$$a = \frac{e}{m} E = \frac{e\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0 m}$$

(m is the electron mass). But, as we know, with constant acceleration of the particle’s movement near the Earth’s surface, where the gravitational force can be considered constant. Therefore, the electron in our case will move in the same way as a particle thrown from the Earth at an angle to the horizon moves.
