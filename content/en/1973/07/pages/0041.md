Problem 4. Two identical powerful charges $q$ move in a circle of radius $R$ around charge $Q$. In this case, the charges $q$ are located at the ends of the single-band diameter (Fig. 5). The named velocities of the charges $q$ and the ratio of the total kinematic energy of the charges to the instantaneous one.

Solution. Charges $Q$ and $q$ must have different icons, otherwise charges $q$ will fly apart. Each of the charges $q$ is acted upon by a force

$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{Qq}{R^2} - \frac{q^2}{(2R)^2} \right).$$

This force imparts to each charge a centripetal acceleration equal to $\frac{v^2}{R} = \omega^2 R$, where $\omega$ is the angular velocity of the charge. That's why

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{Qq}{R^2} - \frac{q^2}{(2R)^2} \right) = m\omega^2 R.$$

From here

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{Qq}{R^2} - \frac{q^2}{4R^2} \right)} \frac{1}{mR} =$$

$$= \frac{1}{4R} \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon_0 mR}} \left( 4Qq - q^2 \right).$$

The kinetic energy of the system is the sum of the kinetic energies of the charges $q$:

$$W_{\text{киин}} = 2 \frac{mv^2}{2} = m(\omega R)^2 =$$

$$= \frac{1}{16\pi\varepsilon_0 R} \left( 4Qq - q^2 \right).$$

The potential energy of the system is numerically equal to the work required to transform $q$ from infinity to the distance $R$ from charge $Q$. To redefine one of the charges in the field of charge $Q$ it is necessary to perform work

$$A_1 = -\varphi \cdot q = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Q}{R} q,$$

(f is the potential of the field of charge $Q$ at a distance $R$ from the charge), and to re-establish the second charge (teler already in the field of charges $Q$ and $q$) it is necessary to do work

$$A_2 = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Q}{R} q + \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{2R} q.$$

Posny's potential energy of the system is equal to

$$W_{\text{пот}} = A_1 + A_2 =$$

$$= \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{q^2}{2R} - 2 \frac{Qq}{R} \right),$$

And

$$\frac{W_{\text{киин}}}{W_{\text{пот}}} = -\frac{1}{2}.$$
