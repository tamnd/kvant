Moscow Institute of Electronic Engineering

The Moscow Institute of Electronic Engineering (MIEM) is one of the youngest universities in Moscow; it was created in 1962 and trains highly qualified specialists for the electronics and radio industries. Its creation is a consequence of the rapid development of radio-electronic technology, which required a significant number of specialists combining deep engineering knowledge with advanced physics and mathematics training.

MIEM has the faculties of semiconductor and electric vacuum engineering, automation and computer engineering, radio engineering, applied mathematics, evening and two special faculties of applied mathematics and automated control systems.

The Faculty of Semiconductor and Electro-Vacuum Engineering prepares mechanical engineers to work on the creation and operation of new technological equipment for the electronics industry, as well as to develop advanced methods for manufacturing devices for automating production processes. Specialists who have graduated from this

faculty, can also independently solve modern problems of welding production of electronic equipment products.

The Faculty of Automation and Computer Engineering graduates electrical engineers specializing in the design of computers, telecontrol and telecontrol systems, information and measuring automatic devices using modern discrete and continuous computers. Engineers who graduate from MIEM in this specialization can calculate and design computer elements, including creating and using machine methods for calculating elements. They can also conduct research into technologies for manufacturing processes of elements in order to find optimal modes.

The Faculty of Radio Engineering trains radio equipment design engineers. Graduates of this faculty can work as design and technological engineers in the fields of the radio-electronic industry, develop and organize the production of radio-measuring equipment in design bureaus, instructions and factories, work as technological engineers at the college, within which the Faculty of Applied Mathematics was organized in 1967 with day and evening departments. Following MIEM, departments of applied mathematics were organized at MIPT, MAI, MEPhI and many universities.

The curriculum of the specialization “applied mathematics” provides for the training of mathematical engineers in the mathematical support of electronic computers,

providing high mathematical training and deep knowledge of special disciplines.

Modern computers are a complex complex: they are able to implement in a short time not only the most complex computational, but also logical operations, organize arrays of information and classify information. But to effectively use the capabilities of a computer, it is necessary to give the machine a program that ensures automatic planning of the work of its individual devices and the organization of its work as a whole. To do this, it is necessary to develop computer software, which represents modern transport, operating systems, libraries of standard subroutines and other software elements.

A characteristic feature of the training plan for mathematical engineers and systems engineers is that from the very first days of study at the institute, the student, along with the classical sections of mathematics, studies computer programming, mathematical logic, methods for solving problems on a computer, and a number of special mathematical disciplines. This plan makes it possible, already in the first year, to carry out a semester-long practice on numerical methods and algorithms, which included solving problems on the use of computational methods of linear algebra, interpolating functions, calculating the roots of algebraic and transcendental equations. At the same time, computational methods and algorithms are discussed from the point of view of their implementation on a computer.

The existing system of mathematical education of mathematical engineers and systems engineers at MIEM is a factor determining the mathematical training of students of all specialties of the institute.

Naturally, this circumstance is taken into account when selecting applicants for entrance exams. But one should not think that options for written or oral exams go beyond the scope of the school curriculum. For example, any applicant who has firmly agreed on the school curriculum can successfully cope with it.

1. Two factories must jointly process a certain amount of raw materials. If the randomness of labor in the second factory were doubled, then the time required for the factories to do the work would be reduced by $2/15$ parts of the time required for the first factory alone to do the work. In which of the factories is the randomness higher and by how many times, if it is known that each factory processed at least one third of the raw materials.

2. Solve the equation
$$\sin^4 x - \cos^4 x = a (\sin^8 x - \cos^8 x).$$
