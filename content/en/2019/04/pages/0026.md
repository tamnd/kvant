Now let's go back to problem 3 about circles. It turns out that the partition of the polygon we need can be formed not by perpendicular bisectors, but by the so-called radical axes of circles. The radical axis of two non-intersecting circles is the locus of points, the tangents of which, drawn to two given circles, have equal lengths. It is easy to show that the radical axis of two circles of any radius with divergent centers is a straight line (see Appendix). If the circles do not intersect, they will be entirely on opposite sides of the radical axis. Let us then divide the points of the polygon (lying outside the circles) into areas consisting of points, the tangents of which to one circle are shorter than to the others. Similarly, we obtain that each circle lies entirely in one region and this region is a convex polygon.
