Problems like this: “A light bulb was connected to a flashlight battery, on which 4.5 volts is written, the resistance of which, when measured, turned out to be 2 ohms. What current will flow? Because the penultimate answer is two diameters of the Earth, and you have already read about short distances in this article. In addition, it is useful to simply look at the problem statement. The student who solved the problem of an object floating on the boundary of two liquids, the densities of which are 900 kg $м^{3}$ and 2700 kg $м^{3}$, and received an answer for the density of the object 100 kg $м^{3}$, did not do it in vain. As a sad anecdote, we can add that hundreds of schoolchildren joyfully wrote that it was oil and aluminum, although this was not asked in the problem. Why did you have to demonstrate the terrifying abstractness of your thinking? Further, within a task you often have to add, oddly enough. If you get something like 0.515 ◊ $10^{15} + 2,75$ as an intermediate result, you should be wary. As well as receiving as an answer to the question “how many times” the answers “1.2 ◊ $10^{18}$ times” or “0.4 ◊ $10^{26}$ times”. If the problem was created by a minimally intelligent person, this will not happen - adding and subtracting comparable quantities at school makes sense (if only because it is allowed to use calculators). Note that in serious physics (and this should be explained to schoolchildren) the situation is more complicated - it happens that very different quantities have to be added. And more often they have to be compared - to choose a model, to justify what we take into account and what we neglect in this case. So, numerical estimates are of great importance in physics - and long before the answer is received! They allow you to build a model and determine the direction of its development.
