Are you really familiar with this?

?

physics+technology

How could a person engage in construction and process various materials - and this is exactly what was discussed in our previous physical and technical issues - if it were not possible to move various loads and even move around? So he had to rack his brains to find devices that would allow him to transport more and faster, in other words, to solve TRANSPORT PROBLEMS. For many centuries, wheeled carts, oars and sails helped us out - with their help we were able to explore vast spaces and even discover new continents. But “horsepower” and tailwinds were replaced by new cars, much more powerful and faster. Actively “absorbing” the achievements of science, transport technology, literally over the last two centuries, has radically changed the idea of ​​terrestrial scales and the time of reaching any point on our planet. Today we can no longer be surprised by drones and unmanned vehicles, hydrofoils and hovercraft, solar-powered aircraft and tourist flights in low-Earth orbits. What will happen tomorrow? Let's try to imagine our transport future, however, starting so far only from well-known means of transportation.

It's interesting that...

...the invention of the wheel was preceded by a long mastery of rotational movement - from spinning a wooden stick to create fire, using a potter's wheel and lifting devices to using a round log when transporting heavy loads. ...already the sailors of Ancient Egypt knew how to control the power of the wind: the sails of their ships gradually took on the optimal shape for those times. ...Newton, who was also involved in aerodynamics, managed to establish the quadratic dependence of the drag force of the medium on speed, which was later so important for subsonic aviation. At higher speeds, this dependence becomes cubic, which was noticed by artillerymen about a hundred years ago, when airplanes had just begun to overtake birds.

...the appearance of the locomotive - a steam engine on rails - and the invention of the steamship at the beginning of the 19th century brought about a complete revolution in transport technology. ...when transporting a huge stone for the pedestal of the Bronze Horseman in St. Petersburg, cast iron balls were used to place it under it - prototypes of future ball bearings, which became widespread only in the 80s of the 19th century. Thanks to this, sliding friction was replaced by tens of times less rolling friction. ...D.I. Mendeleev, who flew in a hot air balloon without a pilot, argued that the conquest of the atmosphere would be carried out with the help of balloons, and not heavier-than-air machines. K.E. Tsiolkovsky, before starting to study the theory of rocket propulsion, developed the principles of constructing an all-metal airship. ...engineers at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology have created a two-seater flying car. On the ground it moves with folded wings, and in the air it reaches speeds of up to 185 kilometers per hour with a payload of up to 195 kilograms. ...less than a year ago, a robotic boat, controlled by an autopilot, crossed the Atlantic Ocean. The on-board electronics were powered by solar panels installed on the deck, and the propulsion was a trapezoid-shaped sail.

...the idea of ​​a device mounted on a drone that allows it to track dangerous proximity to other vehicles was borrowed by its creators...from locusts. These insects manage not to collide with each other or with the birds that get in their way - and this despite the fact that in swarms of locusts the density of individuals reaches 80 million per square kilometer!
