PHYSICS DAYS IN DUBNA

In mid-April, the VI All-Russian scientific festival “Days of Physics” was held in Dubna, Moscow Region. It was organized and conducted by the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research, the JINR Educational and Scientific Center and the Interschool Physics and Mathematics Elective. For several days, the Mir House of Culture turned into one large laboratory: experiments were demonstrated in the small hall, and the quest “Periodic Law” was held in the large and exhibition halls. Then the traditional mathematical festival and master classes took place, and Cosmonautics Day was marked by the launch of rockets at Molodezhnaya Polyana. High school students demonstrated and explained to visitors various physical laws using equipment they had designed. These experiments, always bright and sometimes unexpected and thought-provoking, invariably attract a large number of viewers - from preschool to retirement age. They are also attractive for participants, since, judging by their reviews, these events are not held in this format anywhere except Dubna. In addition, non-resident participants were able to get to know Dubna by going through an exciting quest around the city and visiting the JINR Museum of the History of Science and Technology, and playing the intellectual game “What? Where? When?" Here are a few excerpts from interviews taken from festival participants. Chairman of the festival organizing committee M.V. The participants were Moscow gymnasium No. 1514, physics and mathematics lyceum from St. Petersburg, and a team from Bryansk. For the first time, guys came from Volgograd, Glazov and Istra near Moscow. From the Dubna teams, guys from the physics and mathematics electives of the UC JINR and the Dubna Lyceum always actively participate. All the experiments presented by the guys are not easy. We always welcome experiments related to particle physics or nuclear physics. In the year of the anniversary of the discovery of the Periodic Law, our traditional quest for Physics Days participants is dedicated to this event. The guys receive a map - the Periodic Table - and, going through stations in the large hall of the House of Culture, solve various tasks on history, geography, even linguistic tasks, learn what role chemical elements play in our everyday life, which of them were synthesized in Dubna, and in the end receive a well-deserved prize. Physics teacher M.V. Akimova (Lyceum No. 1 of Volgograd): We are participating in the festival for the first time, and I learned about Physics Days a year and a half ago at the school of physics teachers held by the UC JINR (after which I also went to school at CERN). We sent here two papers that my girls prepared and waited for the decision. When they found out that both jobs had passed, the children were very happy. We have a physics and mathematics lyceum, and physics and mathematics are taught in depth, and project work is also carried out in physics. We participate in regional festivals and competitions. But here it’s more research work, beautiful experiments. We prepared them for about four months.

Olga Golikova (Volgograd): Our project “Slow Dance” is based on the idea of ​​Jeff Lieberman, an American inventor and artist. It's all about the stroboscopic effect. The flower attached to the solenoid with coils vibrates rapidly, and the LED strip, programmed by a special chip, flickers at a frequency of 80 Hz, which our eyes do not notice. It seems to us that the lighting is constant, and we simply don’t see some of the flower’s movements - we get the feeling that it is dancing a slow dance. I.A. Lomachenkov (UC JINR) is engaged in experimental physics with Dubna schoolchildren: We have submitted seven experiments for today’s Physics Days. In fact, there are much more of them, but we have already shown many of them at previous holidays and did not want to repeat them. Two experiments are carried out with accelerators, which, on the one hand, is interesting, and on the other, they are related to the topics of JINR and emphasize the direction in which the Institute is working. An experiment related to electromagnetism was demonstrated by Ivan Pachkolin (Lyceum No. 15 in Glazov): I am demonstrating a model of a Faraday generator. I am in the eighth grade, this is a physics and mathematics class, and I plan to enter a physics or technical university. Before demonstrating the experiments to the audience, we ourselves looked at what the other teams had prepared. Some experiments interested me. This is our first time participating in Physics Days, but we will definitely come again. The children's chemical experiments “Foam Party” by MIPT students evoked an enthusiastic reaction from the kids.

Daria Plashchinskaya: I am a first-year student at the Faculty of Biophysics. We were preparing for these experiments, we found some reagents ourselves, some were given by our laboratory assistant, who was even glad that we ourselves would do a little science and demonstrate something to the children. We rehearsed a lot. It seems that it worked. <Judging by the emotions of the children - how it turned out!> My partner Masha has been coming here for several years, she is delighted with the holiday, she loves Dubna very much. And I really liked the city, and any event related to science, especially with my line of work, is interesting. Some other guys came with us, they are helping to carry out the quest. We will definitely come next year. At the origins of the Days of Physics in Dubna was a member of the editorial board of the journal «Kvant» teacher of the Interschool elective A.A. Leonovich. At this celebration, he, together with the JINR Universal Library, presented three of his books from the series “Simple Science for Children”: “Physics without Formulas”, “Miracles of Technology”, “Bionics: Suggested by Nature”. Books could be obtained by winning the “Physics for Growth” blitz quiz, which attracted many children. A.A. Leonovich: The AST publishing house <which publishes books in the “Simple Science for Children” series> is interested in popularizing its work, and where, if not at our Physics Days, can you get these books. We don’t give them away just like that - you need to answer the questions of a quiz designed for children up to the sixth grade, i.e. who have not yet started studying physics at school.
