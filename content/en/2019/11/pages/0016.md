The frequencies of both pairs are determined, and the found probabilities are multiplied. The probabilities of one-step typos are different: for example, rearranging adjacent letters in a word is much more likely than replacing a letter with a distant one on the keyboard (for example, replacing b with r is not so easy). Secondly, you can encounter a correct, meaningful phrase that is not in the corpus, and then the product of the probabilities will be equal to zero (example: the phrase working correction, which we used at the end of the previous paragraph, is not yet in the corpus). Thirdly, the considered version of the Markov chain connects a word only with its closest neighbors, although in language there are dependencies over long distances. For example, in the phrase the sleeve of the shirt that Vasya bought at the airport turned out to be too short, when choosing a replacement sleeve or sleeves, you will have to rely not on neighboring, but on distant words that turned out to be short. Fourthly, by making typos, you can get a phrase with words from the dictionary, but erroneous: for example, My cunning is torn. The algorithm will not suspect such a phrase of anything. However, increasing the complexity of the algorithm makes it possible to cope with such difficulties. Computational linguistics.
