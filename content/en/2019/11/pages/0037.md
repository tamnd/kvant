What's wrong with guessing hats?

K.KOKHAS

–P

RIVET, FRIENDS, I'M GLAD YOU'RE AGAIN on TV! However, I can’t see you at all from here. Gargoyles with you, scientific observer of the Very First Channel. The next program “What’s Wrong?”, dedicated to breaking stereotypes and overthrowing the standards of the scientific worldview, is ready to delight viewers with an unforgettable enchanting episode. By tradition, we choose the topic of the program among the questions sent by viewers. "What's wrong with guessing hats?" – TV viewer Kick Tukh asks us. Our wonderful expert on issues of inadequacy, Woodpecker Spyatel, answers the question! Please, maestro. – Good evening, dear TV viewers. First of all, for the correct placement of emphasis, I would like to clarify that I am an expert on issues of adequacy. If our host continues to be confused, then in the next episode we will have to discuss the question “What is wrong with our host?” Clear? - Sorry, sorry, dear maestro. I hope to make amends, look: there are several black and white hats in this box, they can significantly improve the adequacy of our proceedings. “Then let’s start the Guessing competition.” I'll ask the cameraman to show a close-up of the Musca domestica insect. - This is an ordinary house fly! - Absolutely right. So, dear TV viewers, while you were admiring the fly, the assistants put a hat on both me and Gargoyle. You can clearly see the colors of both hats, but I can clearly see the Gargoyle hat,

but, alas, I can’t figure out what color the hat I’m wearing. “I also don’t know the color of my hat,” Gargoyles picked up, “but at the same time I can clearly see the hat of the woodpecker Spyatla.” So what are we going to guess? – According to the rules of the competition, we must, without talking or giving each other any signals, try to guess the color of our hat. Each of us has a remote control with “black” and “white” buttons, we must discreetly press one of the buttons! “But maestro, it’s unlikely that both of us will be able to guess the color of our hat.” After all, we have no information about the color of our hat! - Right. But if at least one of us guesses right, it will be considered our victory in the competition! - Something is wrong here. Each of us knows nothing at all about the color of our hat. How can we even guess anything? – You forget that one of us is an outstanding expert on issues of adequacy! Of course we can overcome this competition! And the point here is not at all that big mirror, which is not visible to television viewers, but is clearly visible to us. We can win this competition... with the help of intelligence! In fact, dear TV viewers, agree that there are only two possibilities - either our hats are the same color or different. Now we will press the buttons, and I will press the button based on the assumption that our hats are the same color, and my colleague will assume that the colors are different! We don't know which of these hypotheses actually came true, but one of the hypotheses definitely came true, and the one who adhered to this hypothesis will guess the color! - Maestro, this is amazing. But what will we do if our TV viewer Kick Toot asks for a competition in which the hats will be not two, but, say, eight colors? – We will invite 6 more friends and hold a similar competition! The assistants will put on a hat of one of 8 colors for each of the eight participants; each will see the hats of the others, but not their own. And in the same way, at least one participant needs to guess the color of his hat!
