Signals excited on the surface propagate deep into the Moon, where they are scattered and reflected on the internal structure. Using a line of seismic receivers installed on the lunar surface, acoustic signals scattered by the internal structure were recorded and then transmitted via a radio communication channel to Earth. It is worth noting that although seismic and other experiments on the Moon were stopped in 1977, the data obtained from lunar seismic sensors was reprocessed in 2010 using modern computing tools. Interestingly, this processing revealed a new kind of lunar core: a solid core surrounded by a liquid outer core, in turn surrounded by a layer of partially molten magma (Fig.3).

The Luna automatic stations delivered cores of lunar regolith to Earth, obtained as a result of drilling to a depth of more than 2 meters. To ensure such drilling and obtain undamaged cores, ultrasonic disturbance of the drill was additionally used. This ultrasonic lunar drilling technology made it possible to obtain high-quality core samples that showed the structure of the lunar regolith. Analysis of the results of these flights provided for the first time convincing evidence of the presence of water on the Moon. Much later, in the 1990s, the Americans were able to obtain similar results. Water has been discovered in the structure of the lunar soil! During research under the Apollo program, various physical fields on the surface of the Moon were measured, but a fairly detailed picture of the internal structure of the Moon was obtained using the acoustic method.
