Escape velocity is the minimum speed that allows a body to overcome the influence of the Earth's gravitational field. It is clear that this speed is different for each planet, because it depends on its mass and radius. Let now c > 0, i.e. the initial speed is greater than the second escape velocity. Then for any positive value of R there are two values ​​of the speed R¢. The corresponding points ( R; R¢ ) lie on two branches of the phase trajectory, which on figure 4 are shown in blue - the color of the bottomless sky. The lower branch describes the fall of a body from infinity. Each branch has its own horizontal asymptote. Again speaking figuratively, let's say that any body that started with a speed $v_{0} > v_{2}$ will go to infinity and will move there with some speed. If c < 0 (and $v_{0} < v_{2} ),$ then the values of R are in some finite interval. This means that a body launched from the Earth can move away from it only a finite distance, stop for a moment and then return to the Earth. It is impossible to escape from the gravitational field of the Earth with such a low speed. The corresponding phase trajectories are shown in figure 4 in green - the color of green grass. The trajectories should be considered in a half-plane $R \ge R_{З}$. All trajectories of three colors make up a phase portrait. The name of the red trajectory is a separatrix. It separates trajectories of one type from trajectories of another type.

⟦column⟧

Taking any point (R; R¢) in the right half-plane and considering the trajectory passing through it, you can easily see the evolution of our dynamic system. Note that each trajectory can be easily constructed using elementary methods, assuming that the equation of motion defines, for each value of the parameter c, a function R that depends on the argument R¢. It comes down to “dividing unity by a quadratic function.” You can read about phase velocity and phase portrait in «Kvant» in the articles of V. Arnold “Evolutionary processes and ordinary differential equations” («Kvant», 1986, No. 2) and A. Veselov “On the mathematics of harmonic oscillations” («Kvant», 1986, No. 5).
