Left, right - where is the side?

S. DVORYANINOV

THE RUSSIAN POET VASILY Ivanovich Sirotin LIVED IN THE 19TH CENTURY. Most of his work is irretrievably lost. His most famous poem, which has survived to this day, is called “Street”. It was published in 1859. Here are his first lines: Since I am returning home to my place, the street seems strange to me. Left, right side where? Street, street, you, brother, are drunk. Now I’ll explain why I remembered this. I'm riding home on the Sokolnicheskaya line of the Moscow metro. I should get off at Lubyanka station. Above the exit from the car is a diagram indicating the stations of this line (Fig.1). Okay, very convenient.

The next day the same thing. Evening, metro, approaching Lubyanka. I stood at the door, getting ready to go out. Above the door there are the usual names, but now on the diagram the stations are indicated in a different order for some reason (Fig.2). He looked back, looked back. Above the opposite door is a diagram, as in figure 1. Here the names of the stations are like a reflection in the mirror figure 2. But then the train stopped, the doors swung open, and I stepped out onto the platform. And now - questions. Why are the names of stations on different diagrams in the same carriage?

are they in different order? From which direction did my train approach Lubyanka in case figure 1? Which station was the previous one: Chistye Prudy or Okhotny Ryad? And in the case of figure 2? We advise readers to stop reading now, turn on their spatial imagination and think about these questions. Just in case, let us remind you that if a passenger train has a “Moscow-Samara” sign on it, then this train goes from Moscow to Samara. And on the way back, on the same carriage there will be another sign “Samara-Moscow”. Yes, still. On the Sokolnicheskaya Line, metro trains move on the right. At each station, exit to the platform on the left (taking into account the direction of the train). And we continue our story. Reflecting on what has been said, one should take into account the psychology of a passenger in a subway car. This passenger wants, of course, to get to the desired station as quickly as possible. From time to time he glances at the movement diagram, mentally noting his movement on it. Naturally, stations should be listed from left to right - this is how we read and write. The diagram A - B - C means that we have passed station A, we are approaching B, then there will be C. At the same time, it is more pleasant if the diagram

The direction of the stations is coordinated with their location on the train route. At the same time, the passenger also looks at the door - whether other people in the carriage will interfere with his exit or whether he should stand closer to the door. This means that the circuit he needs should be located above the door through which the passenger exits. Now it becomes clear why inside the same carriage we see two different diagrams of the same metro line. Both diagrams correspond to the actual locations of stations along the metro line. When we move our gaze from one diagram to another, we turn to $180\infty$. Figure 1 corresponds to the case when the train approaches Lubyanka from Chistye Prudy, and figure 2 – when from Okhotny Ryad. In the new metro trains, instead of paper diagrams, electronic ones appeared, on which the movement of the train is displayed by running lights. They give reason to remember other lines of the same poem “Street”:

And the lanterns are burning so dimly, They can’t stand still. So they flicker here and there...

car at the Barrikadnaya station through the door shown at figure 3. Looking at the diagram, tell me: where is the train moving - left or right? This question cannot be answered! Electronic displays in a subway car (possibly to save production) are of only one type; the lights on them run from right to left. Such displays are located on both sides of the car. In this case, the direction of movement of the lights coincides with the direction of movement of the train only on one side of the car. If they do not coincide, a person experiences a condition called cognitive dissonance. You know the phenomenon of resonance; dissonance, one might say, is its opposite. Dissonance in music is a violation of harmony, an incorrect combination of tones that makes an unpleasant impression on listeners. In general, dissonance is something that brings discord into something, comes into conflict with something. We hope that our readers - future engineers and designers - will take into account all circumstances in their work, and everything that they create will not lead to any dissonance.
