dinats. When a wheel rolls along an axle, each marked point along which the wheel has passed leaves a dotted mark on it. Prove that you can choose a real R such that if you roll a wheel of radius R along the axis, starting from zero, then on each arc of the wheel of size 1q there will be a trace of at least one marked point.

Let's divide the wheel circumference into n = 720 arcs of size 0.5q and paint each arc in its own color (we do not paint the ends of the arcs). Let's roll the wheel in a straight line so that zero corresponds to the boundary between the 1st and nth colors. We assume that each point of the straight line is colored in the color of the arc that passes through the point. Then the straight line will be divided into intervals of n different colors corresponding to the arcs. We need to find such a radius R, or such a length of the wheel S 2 S R, that for each of the n colors there is a marked point of this color. Suppose we have already selected S such that for some k < n the following k-condition is satisfied: for the 1st, 2nd, ..., kth colors there is a marked point of this color. To fulfill the 1st condition, we take S large enough so that the first segment of the 1st color covers the marked point. Let us show that, while maintaining the k-condition, we can slightly change the length of the wheel so that some marked point is colored in the k 1st color, i.e. so that the k 1 -condition becomes fulfilled. By repeating this procedure, we will eventually achieve the fulfillment of the n-condition, which is what we need. So, let the k-condition be satisfied for some wheel length S for some k < n. Let's fix the marked point of each of the colors 1, 2, ..., k. A small change in the length of the wheel corresponds to the homothety of a straight line with a center at point 0 and a coefficient close to 1. It is clear that we can choose a homothety coefficient so close to 1 that the picture for our fixed k marked points will not change: they will all remain inside the “stretched” intervals of the same color.
