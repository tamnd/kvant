Task 4. The spectator writes n numbers on the board from left to right. The magician's assistant covers one of the numbers with a red card, another with a blue card, and another with a green card. After this, the magician enters and calls the closed numbers. For what smallest n is such a trick possible? Answer: for n = 12. Solution. Let $V_{1}$ and $V_{2}$ be the sets of those “pictures” that can be seen by the assistant and the magician, respectively. Consider a bipartite graph $G V_{1}, V_{2}$, the edges of which describe possible transitions from pictures from $V_{1}$ to pictures from $V_{2}$. The graph is semiregular, $V_{1} 10^{n}, V_{2} n n 1 n 2$ • $10^{n} 3$. By Corollary 2, for a perfect matching from $V_{1}$ to $V_{2}$ to exist, it is necessary and sufficient that the share $V_{2}$ has no fewer vertices than $V_{1}$. Hence $n n 1 n 2 t t 10^{3}$, which is equivalent to the inequality n t 12. Thus, the smallest value of n is 12. We will show how to perform the trick practically with n t 12. We number the places where the first 12 digits are written from left to right: 1, 2, ..., 9, 0, 11, 12 (we do not number the remaining places, if any!). Let S be the sum of all digits recorded by the viewer. We will act for the assistant as follows. Let's cover with a red card the place whose number is the last digit of the number S, after which we'll mentally delete this place, and with a blue card we'll cover the place with a number equal to the digit that is hidden under the red card. Then, with a green card, we will cover the place whose number (calculated after “removing” the numbers under the red and blue cards) is hidden under the blue card. Now the magician will understand by the position of the green card which number is under the blue one, and by the position of the blue card - which number is under the red one. All numbers, except the one under the green card, are already known to the magician, and the position of the red card indicates the last digit of the sum of all the numbers written down by the spectator. Therefore, the magician will be able to determine the number under the green card.

Let us present a selection of $задач^{1}$, in the solution of which it is possible to apply Hall’s theorem.
