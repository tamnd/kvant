Sufficient condition

In this article we will talk about one (simple, but little-known) sufficient condition for the existence of a perfect matching in a bipartite graph. Theorem 2. Let in a non-empty (i.e., having at least one edge) bipartite graph $G V_{1}, V_{2}$ the degree of any vertex from part $V_{1}$ be no less than the degree of any vertex from part $V_{2}$. Then there is a perfect matching from $V_{1}$ to $V_{2}$ in this graph. Proof. Let q be a number that is not greater than the degree of any vertex in $V_{1}$ and not less than the degree of any vertex in $V_{2}$. Let us take arbitrary k vertices of the first part $b_{1}, b_{2}$, … $, b_{k}$ and adjacent vertices $g_{1}, g_{2}$, … $, g_{l}$ of the second part. Consider the subgraph G c of the original graph generated by these k + l vertices (G c includes the indicated vertices and all the edges between them that are present in the original graph). It is clear that in the graph G c the degree of any vertex from the first part is at least q, and from the second part no more than q. Problems about magicians

There are a number of problems that can be easily solved using Theorem 2 and its corollaries. The plot of these tasks is as follows. The audience presents the magician's assistant with some randomly selected object (for example, several playing cards), after which the assistant somehow changes this object according to his choice. Next, the magician uses the new object to restore the original one. Let us construct a bipartite graph $G V_{1}, V_{2}$ as follows. Let the vertices from $V_{1}$ be all possible configurations that the audience can present to the magician’s assistant, and the vertices from $V_{2}$ be the configurations that the assistant can receive. If configuration b $V_{2}$ can be obtained from configuration a $V_{1}$, then the graph must contain an edge ab; there are no other edges in the graph. If in a given graph there is a perfect matching from $V_{1}$ to $V_{2}$, then the magician and his assistant, using it, will be able to perform the trick: the magician, based on configuration b, will uniquely determine configuration a.
