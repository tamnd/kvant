Instead of cells, we will consider their centers, which we will call nodes. Thus, we have a finite set of initially black nodes and a template - the set of centers of cells of a paper polygon M. The following process occurs in the problem: if we can put the template in such a way that exactly one of its nodes is white, then we can recolor this node black. Let us prove that all initially white nodes lying in the opposite half-plane relative to m will always remain white. Let's assume the opposite and consider the moment when the first such node A became black. This means that it is covered by the shift K of our template. Let us draw a line a through A parallel to the line l. On one side of a, all nodes are still white (since A is the only recolored node), which means that a is the supporting line for K. But then the shift that takes K to $K_{0}$ takes the line a to l, therefore, on the line a, in addition to A, there is at least one more node belonging to K. As we see, at the moment of recoloring node A, pattern K covers at least two white nodes. We obtain a contradiction that completes the solution of the problem. Comment. As we see from the solution, the pattern can be arbitrary, so the connectivity of the polygon M from the problem statement is not important. It is also easy to deduce from the solution of the problem that if the template does not fit into either a horizontal or vertical row, then the repainting process will be finite (i.e., in the end there will be a finite number of cells on the plane). Note that the problem has a natural generalization to a spatial lattice (in a similar process one is allowed to recolor two white nodes) or a lattice of a finite-dimensional space. D. Zakharov
