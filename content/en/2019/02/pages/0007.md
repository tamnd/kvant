Rule N – K = 1

The concept of the dimension of a physical quantity is introduced when some basic physical quantities have already been selected and units for their measurement have been established. Quantities not included in the basic ones are called derived quantities. Expressing the units of measurement of a derivative physical quantity through the units of measurement of the basic quantities means dimension. In general, the choice of basic quantities and units for their measurement can be done in different ways. Here, of course, a lot depends on convenience, traditions and existing standards and agreements. In mechanics, the basic quantities are mass, length and time.

Such systems of units are sometimes said to belong to the MLT class. The dimension of force in this class is determined by the expression $MLT^{2}$, the dimension of energy – $ML^{2} T^{2}$, etc. Replacing M by kg, L by m, T by s in these expressions, we obtain the dimensions of force and energy in the SI system. If we consider problems in which non-mechanical quantities appear, for example, electric charge, current, potential, then we can increase the number of basic quantities and the corresponding basic units of measurement. In the SI system, current is added to the number of basic quantities, which is measured in amperes (A). Similarly, in thermodynamics, one of the basic quantities can include temperature, its unit of measurement is kelvin (K). It is important to note that the dimensional method can be used in any system of units. Each time, of course, expressions for the dimensions of various quantities must be written in one pre-selected system. Suppose that in some problem we are looking for a functional relationship between N quantities. Assuming a power-law dependence of one of the quantities on the others and writing out the dimensions of these quantities, we can try to construct the formula that interests us. If all dimensions are expressed through the dimensions of K basic quantities and if N – K = 1, then it can be argued that the sought formula is the only possible one. This rule is clearly illustrated by the example discussed above with a ball oscillating on a string. For this case we had four parameters: Z, T, M and a; the dimensions of all these quantities were expressed through the dimensions of the basic quantities, i.e. through kg, m, s. In other words, N = 4, K = 3 and N – K = 1. The fact that the formula $Z \sim T^{1} 2 M^{1} 2 a^{1} 2$ is the only possible one follows from the fact that the system of equations for determining the exponents x, y, z has a unique solution.
