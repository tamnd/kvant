Magician problems and the theorems of Hall and Sperner

A. EVNIN

Hall's theorem

The following problem has become widely known in the popular mathematical literature. Problem about weddings. There are many boys, each of whom knows some girls. Under what conditions is it possible to marry all the young men at the same time so that each of them marries a girl he knows? The answer is: the problem is solvable if and only if any k boys from a given set are collectively acquainted with at least k girls. Note that the need here is obvious. Indeed, if for some k young men the number of potential brides is less than k, then these k young men will not be able to get married at the same time. Sufficiency is not so easy to prove! In order to formulate the problem of weddings in mathematical language, we present some definitions from graph theory [1]. The vertices of a graph are adjacent if they are connected by an edge. Edges of a graph are called adjacent if they have a common vertex, and non-adjacent otherwise. Pairwise nonadjacent edges of the graph form a matching.
