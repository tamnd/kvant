The professor conducts a spectral analysis of photographs of apple slices. It uses a 3-bit RGB palette to encode the resulting images. Let us remember that white is the presence of all colors, black is the absence, yellow does not have blue, violet does not have green, and blue does not have red. To optimize his work, the professor, using a special utility, sorted the binary representations of colors and wrote them down in a table, numbering them starting from one, so that later he could use the color number. Sorting was performed on each bit, but it is not known in what order they were analyzed and whether ascending or descending sorting was performed. The calculations were made so that the most popular color in the study was in first place. But here’s the problem: while the professor was collecting material for the study, the table was lost. However, the professor remembered that blue and black were in different halves of the table, and red was number 5.
