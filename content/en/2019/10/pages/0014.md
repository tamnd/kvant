The chaotic (turbulent) movement in the atmosphere of air flows with different temperatures, and therefore with different densities and refractive indexes, causes trembling and blurring of the image. Astronomers have such a concept - astroclimate, i.e. atmospheric conditions that affect image quality, which includes air uniformity. Liquids with high refractive indices n are called “immersion” (from the Latin immersio - immersion). They are used to determine refractive indices, for which a transparent particle of the test substance is placed in immersion liquids with known n - when the refractive indices are equal, the particle becomes invisible. Standard sets consist of 30–100 of these liquids with refractive indices ranging from 1.4 to 2.1, but solids take the cake. Although glass barely reaches n = 1.9, diamond has n = 2.4, next to it are calcium and strontium titanates and thallium iodide (n = 2.4), titanium oxide (n = 2.5) and almost the record holder silicon carbide SiC (n = 2.6). That is why SiC, moissanite, is used as a diamond simulator in jewelry (for this, cubic zirconia $ZrO_{2}$ with additives is also used, but its n is smaller). Recently, compound $Al_{3} C_{2} B_{48}$ was synthesized with a record value of n = 2.9–3.1, but little is known about it.
