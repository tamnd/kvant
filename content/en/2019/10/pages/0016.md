The reflection coefficient of Au is 0.39–0.95, for Cu – 0.55–0.96, for Al – 0.92–0.90, for Ag – 0.97–0.99. Therefore, Au and Cu are of a different color than Al and Ag. A person with good color vision or a metallurgist will be able to distinguish between nickel and chrome plating - the former is slightly yellower. Sometimes color variations are completely unexpected. Thus, it is generally accepted that tantalum has a blue tint. But this is only tantalum, which has an admixture of niobium Nb of about 3%, and pure tantalum is gray. As for reflection from powders, the powder appears white if the size of the powders is such that reflection dominates absorption. Therefore, the stronger the absorption, the finer the substance must be crushed in order for the powder to turn white. The absorption of graphite is so great that for realistically achievable particle sizes it prevails over reflection. A special case is reflection from inhomogeneous media or very rough surfaces - for example, from natural surfaces. After all, to describe their shape, it is necessary to introduce different scales - “roughness” on the scale of meters and kilometers can have a different effect than on a small scale. therefore, the edge of the Moon’s disk, which we observe precisely tangentially, should be dark. One hypothesis is that the flat areas of the Moon's surface are darker than the mountain slopes, and therefore the edge of the disk, where we see mostly the slopes, appears relatively brighter (Galileo's hypothesis). Later models took into account relief of different orders - both mountains and individual stones. Another hypothesis suggests that in the surface layer of the Moon there are many glass balls (melting during meteorite impacts?), which act as reflectors, i.e. reflect light towards the incident ray, towards the Sun. And on a full moon, this will be exactly the direction to Earth. But this explanation does not apply the rest of the time. Liquids are used very rarely as “optical materials”. In addition to the above cases of immersion liquids, the use of certain liquids in astronomy for filling cavities in lenses is known. The optical properties of liquids in the infrared region are used very widely, not by opticians, but by chemists - to determine the composition. Accordingly, the optical properties of gases are used to study mixtures of gases. These properties are also important for calculating thermal processes in the atmosphere.
