PETER

LEONIDOVICH

It is difficult even briefly to talk about the life and fate of P.L. Kapitsa. His father Leonid Petrovich Kapitsa came from the Polish Moldavian noble family of the Kapitsa-Mislevskys. He graduated from the Nikolaev Military Engineering Academy and became a naval engineer, a builder of port facilities, one of the creators of the fortifications of the Kronstadt naval port, and had the rank of major general in the engineering corps. He died in 1920. Kapitsa's mother Olga Ieronimovna Stebnitskaya, the daughter of a military cartographer and chief geodesist of the Caucasus, graduated from the famous Bestuzhev women's courses. She became a specialist in children's literature and folklore and taught at the Leningrad Pedagogical Institute named after A.I. Herzen. She died in 1937. Kapitsa's serene childhood and adolescence were decorated with summer travels around Europe with his mother or father. At the age of 18, having graduated from the Kronstadt Real School, Kapitsa entered the electromechanical department of the St. Petersburg Polytechnic Institute, created and directed by the physicist A.F. Ioffe. He soon attracted the talented sophomore to scientific work in the physics laboratory. It is interesting that Kapitsa spent her first summer holidays in 1913 with her brother Leonid, a geography student, in the Russian North, on Solovki and on the coast of the Kola Peninsula, collecting ethnographic materials. The first journal work appears - the popular science article “Fish Oil”. But already during his second vacation, he was interested not in ethnography, but in engineering issues of ship construction. And he spends these holidays in Scotland, lives in the family of a shipbuilder entrepreneur and, following the example of Peter the Great, masters the working professions of a shipbuilder. Due to the outbreak of the World War, he was able to return to Russia only in 1915 and immediately enlisted as a volunteer - a driver and mechanic in a front-line sanitary detachment. Institute. At this time, he meets 18-year-old Nadezhda Kirillovna Chernosvitova, the daughter of a major politician, a member of the Central Committee of the Cadet Party, who was executed in 1918. She becomes his bride. The girl lives in Shanghai, in the family of her brother, an employee of the Russian-Asian Bank, and studies at the Shanghai Russian Gymnasium. In the summer of 1916, Kapitsa travels to Shanghai and brings his bride to Russia. The wedding took place on the estate of the bride's father. At the same time, the young student’s first two scientific works were published, one theoretical, the other experimental. The experimental one is especially interesting. In it, Kapitsa outlines the method he created for making very thin metal threads. The method consisted of shooting upward from a device similar to a boy’s slingshot with a piece of solidifying molten metal, which, as it flew, turned into a frozen thin but strong thread. The summer of 1917 was eventful for Kapitsa - internship in the radiotelegraph department of the Siemens and Halske plant-concern and the birth of his son Jerome. Another year and a half of study and scientific work, and Kapitsa is an electrical engineer, a teacher at the Polytechnic Institute and a researcher at the Physico-Technical Institute (in the laboratory of A.F. Ioffe). The winter of 1919-20 is coming. Within a month, an influenza epidemic (“Spanish flu”) takes away a father, son and wife with a newborn daughter. Salvation from the blow of fate lies only in scientific work and the tender care of the mother. Friends don't leave either. With his closest friend, Nikolai Nikolaevich Semenov, Kapitsa calculates the interaction of atoms with a highly inhomogeneous magnetic field and discusses the idea of ​​an experimental setup to study this interaction. But the publication of this work did not take place - Russia did not yet have its own physics journal, and it was not possible to get to European scientific journals.
