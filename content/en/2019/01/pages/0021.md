Thus, according to the definition, the Voronoi region of a point for a lattice on a plane is a parallelogon. Quite similarly, for a lattice in space, the Voronoi region is a three-dimensional polyhedron - a parallelohedron, now called the Voronoi parallelohedron. It must be said that the idea of ​​a Voronoi domain for discrete sets dates back to the times of Descartes (1596–1650). In the 19th century, the concept of a Voronoi region in the two-dimensional case was actively used by the famous German mathematician Dirichlet, and for more than a century these regions were called Dirichlet regions. But the most profound results about these areas, and for an arbitrary dimension, were obtained by G.F. Voron, and currently these areas are named after him. The number of types for these dimensions is 2, 5 and 52, respectively, and it has been established that each of these types can be implemented as a Voronoi domain. Georgiy Feodosievich suggested that this is true for parallelohedra of any dimension d, i.e. any parallelohedron of dimension d has the same structure as some Voronoi parallelohedron. For those who are familiar with the concept of an affine transformation, let us clarify the meaning of the Voronoi conjecture: for every parallelohedron there is an affine transformation that transforms this parallelohedron into some Voronoi parallelohedron. If the hypothesis is confirmed, then the Voronoi method, with which one can enumerate all types of Voronoi parallelohedra, also solves the problem of enumerating the types of all parallelohedra in general. If the Voronoi conjecture is incorrect, then the general problem of describing all parallelohedra remains open. A significant part of Voronoi's memoirs is devoted to this hypothesis. In particular, in it Voronoi proved a conjecture for a very important class of parallelohedra, the so-called primitive ones. However, despite the efforts of many mathematicians, the problem has remained unsolved for a century for all dimensions $d \ge 5$. The seemingly mysterious mention in Kaleidoscope «Kvant» of “at least 110244” types of five-dimensional parallelohedrons means that, on the one hand, all 110244 types of five-dimensional Voronoi parallelohedrons have been found. On the other hand, there may be more of all types, since it is not known whether there are any other five-dimensional parallelohedrons of a different structure.
