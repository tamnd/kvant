Parallelohedra

Voronoi's latest memoir, as was said, is devoted to the theory of parallelohedrons. A parallelohedron is a convex polyhedron, copies of which, applied to each other along entire faces, can fill the space without gaps or pairwise overlaps; it is assumed that the parallelohedra are not only congruent, but also parallel to each other, i.e. are obtained from each other by parallel transfers. Due to the parallelism of the polyhedra to each other and their adjacency along entire faces, each face of a parallelohedron has an equal and opposite face parallel to it. Hence the name parallelohedron, i.e. parallelograne, which was given to them by the great crystallographer Evgraf Stepanovich Fedorov (1857–1919). Fedorov began studying parallelohedrons in 1885 because of their importance in crystallography. Indeed, the division of space into parallelohedrons well models the structure of a crystal, which, as is known, is periodic in three dimensions. The two-dimensional analogue of parallelohedra is called a parallelogon. A parallelogon is a convex polygon, parallel copies of which can be tiled on a plane, applying them to each other along entire sides. Therefore, a parallelogon is a polygon with an even number of sides, in which each side has an equal and parallel pair. A parallelogon, as can easily be shown, is a centrally symmetrical polygon with four sides (i.e. a parallelogram) or six sides.
