Within two years, Kapitsa created a pulse generator to produce magnetic fields. The physical essence of this work is simple - a highly charged battery is discharged through a small solenoid. The current strength in the solenoid reaches tens of thousands of amperes, and the magnetic field generated by it reaches tens of teslas, which is ten thousand times greater than the induction of the Earth’s magnetic field. The discharge was pulsed, lasted hundredths of a second, and the solenoid did not have time to heat up to a destructive temperature. But during this time it was possible to record everything that happens to charged particles in a magnetic field. It is curious that these were alpha particles, i.e. nuclei of helium atoms. And a few years later, Kapitsa began experiments with liquid helium, experiments that made him famous. Kapitsa becomes a doctor at Cambridge University, deputy to E. Rutherford.
