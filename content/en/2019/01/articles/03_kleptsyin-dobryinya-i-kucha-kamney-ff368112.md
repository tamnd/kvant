Dobrynya and a pile of stones

V.KLEPTSYN

AT THE MATH CLASS, the TEACHER has just finished writing down the conditions of the problem: There is a pile of 1001 stones. Every time the hero Dobrynya divides a pile of stones into two, the king pays him as many coins as the product of the numbers of stones in the two resulting piles. How should Dobrynya act to get as much as possible?

And immediately the students began to argue. – It’s clear that the first thing you need to do is divide the pile almost in half: then Dobrynya will immediately receive as many as 500•501 coins. Then we will divide the smaller heaps in half, etc. And we will need to add up all this earnings. But how? – Zhenya said. – Who told you that this path is the most profitable? He'll hit the jackpot a couple of times, and then you'll have, say, ten-coin piles divided in half, receiving 25 coins at a time. And Dobrynya will take one stone at a time from me. A thousand coins on the first move, 999 on the second, 998 on the third... The slower you go, the further you will go! – the thorough Mika answered her. – So, do we now need to add it all up to compare? – Zhenya was clearly not happy about the prospect of calculating the sum of a thousand terms. “Take your time,” the teacher intervened. – You haven’t gotten used to the task yet.
