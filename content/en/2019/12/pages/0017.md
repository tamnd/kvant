PROBLEM WORKER

The boards must have an even number of short sides adjacent to them. (i) We have 2 k = 10 short sides adjacent to the edges of the board, which means that on one side of the board there are at least 3, and therefore at least 4 short and at most 2 long sides. It is clear that then among the short sides on the perimeter there will be two adjacent ones - a contradiction. (ii) We have 2 k = 12 short sides adjacent to the edges of the board ABCD (Fig.1).

Since there should be no pairs of adjacent long sides along the perimeter of the board, at least two short sides are adjacent to each side of the board. This means that to some side of the board, for definiteness to the horizontal segment AB, there are exactly two short sides adjacent, in which case the adjacency to AB is defined as follows: long-short-long-short-long. Then each of the segments AD and BC is adjacent to at least 4 short sides, which means exactly 4; the CD then has exactly two short sides adjacent to it. Further, both cells marked with red dots cannot be covered by horizontal dominoes, otherwise there will be two adjacent long segments on AD. To be specific, let the top cell with a red dot be covered with a vertical domino, so that figure 1 is filled with the currently fixed dominoes. Now, since no dominoes can be adjacent to each other with their short sides, we consistently uniquely determine the position of b) the estimate is exact, and the problem is completely solved.
