AT THE CROSSROADS OF IDEAS: THE HISTORY OF THE DISCOVERY OF MAGNETIC RESONANCE

The path to recognition

We can safely say that the methods whose discovery relay celebrates its 75th anniversary this year have a happy fate. The fate of their discoverers turned out to be different. E.M. Parcell and F. Bloch very soon after the discovery received the Nobel Prize and became one of the most eminent physicists in Western science, heading large physical research centers or societies at different periods. E.K. Zavoisky continued to work in the field of EPR at Kazan University (where he worked from his student days and where his outstanding discovery was made) only for three years after his discovery. The lack of even simple instruments for conducting research and the impossibility of obtaining them through official channels of state supply of science (and there were practically no others) made the situation hopeless. Due to the impossibility of continuing work, as well as due to unbearable living conditions that threatened the health of the family, in 1947 Zavoisky accepted I.V. Kurchatov’s offer to leave Kazan and begin work on the creation of Soviet nuclear weapons. For the next four years, he worked at the nuclear center in the city of Sarov, but bitterly regretted this activity. As soon as the opportunity arose, he went to work at the Laboratory of Measuring Instruments of the USSR Academy of Sciences, now known to us as the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I.V. Kurchatov, and took up peaceful applications - the creation of ultra-fast electron-optical converters, beams of nuclei with polarized spins and, finally, plasma research, which was necessary for the implementation of controlled thermonuclear fusion. But Zavoisky, fortunately for Russian science, managed to publish his work on EPR before the moment when all scientific work in the country began to be considered secret. Therefore, they were translated into English and published in special English-language publications

magazines that the USSR published to disseminate information abroad about the work of Soviet scientists. (Very soon the publication of these magazines ceased.) Thus, Zavoisky’s works became known throughout the world, and his priority was not lost for our country. In 1957, Zavoisky received the Lenin Prize for the discovery of the EPR; his portrait and article were published in the Pravda newspaper. From that moment on, foreign scientists, having finally seen that the mysterious author of the world's first works on EPR, who disappeared ten years ago from the pages of scientific journals, actually exists and is alive, began annually nominating him for the Nobel Prize. From 1958 to 1966, it was nominated 15 times by scientists from Sweden, Switzerland, Great Britain, the Netherlands and the GDR, as well as 7 times by compatriots (the “Nobel History” of E.K. Zavoisky is described in more detail in the journal “Science and Life” No. 12 for 2019). But the number of “Nobel level” works in these years was very large, the lists of applicants contain dozens, or even hundreds of names that are now known to us from textbooks, and only a few people could receive the prizes (let us recall that the Nobel Prizes during this period were received by Soviet physicists I.E. Tamm, I.M. Frank, P.A. Cherenkov, L.D. Landau, A.M. Prokhorov and N.G. Basov). Perhaps the fact that Zavoisky was almost never allowed to leave the USSR for international conferences also played a negative role, and live communication with foreign colleagues always contributed to the international recognition of the scientist. Or maybe Zavoisky simply did not have time to receive his well-deserved prize: he died in 1976, before reaching the age of 70. Nevertheless, Zavoisky’s priority in the discovery of EPR is recognized throughout the world, his name is remembered and appreciated. More than a quarter of a century ago, the prestigious International E.K. Zavoisky Prize for scientific achievements in the field of magnetic resonance was established and awarded annually to scientists from different countries of the world.
