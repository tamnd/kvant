PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2586–M2589 were offered at the XLI Tournament of Cities. The author of problems F2593–F2596 is S. Dmitriev.

Problem M2586–M2589, F2593–F2596

M2586. Given a polygon in which every two adjacent sides are perpendicular. Let's call two of its vertices not friendly if the bisectors of the polygon emerging from these vertices are perpendicular. Prove that for any vertex the number of vertices that are not friendly with it is even. M. Skopenkov, A. Zaslavsky

M2587. In each cell of a strip of length 100 there is a chip. For 1 ruble you can swap the places of any two adjacent chips, and you can also swap for free any two chips between which there are a) exactly three chips; b) exactly four chips. For what is the least amount of rubles you can rearrange the chips in reverse order? E. Bakaev

$M2588^{*}$. Point M lies inside a convex quadrilateral ABCD at the same distance from the lines AB and CD and at the same distance from the lines BC and AD. It turned out that the area of ​​quadrilateral ABCD is equal to

MA ◊ MC + MB ◊ MD.

Prove that the quadrilateral ABCD is a) cyclic; b) described. N. Sedrakyan

$M2589^{*}$. Given an increasing sequence of positive numbers … $< a_{-} 2 < a_{-} 1 < a_{0} < a_{1} < a_{2} <$ …, infinite in both directions. Let $b_{k}$ be the smallest integer with the property: the ratio of the sum of any k consecutive terms of a given sequence to the largest of these k terms does not exceed $b_{k}$. Prove that the sequence $b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}$, ... either coincides with the natural series 1, 2, 3,..., or is constant from some point on. I. Mitrofanov

F2593. Two paths of equal length in the park are covered with snow. Two janitors, Vasya and Petya, must clear the paths of snow. They started cleaning the first path from both ends, and when it was cleared, it turned out that Vasya had cleared 20 meters in length. They also started cleaning the second track from both ends, but now Vasya increased the speed 3 times, and Petya maintained the same pace. When both tracks were cleared, it turned out that Vasya's share was only 50 meters. How many meters of paths did Petya clear?
