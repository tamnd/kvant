In clean air, a person sees a horizon, which (if you stand on the surface of an ideal sphere) is about five kilometers away (and on the Moon?). Moreover, a person sees the horizon by climbing the highest mountain, and in this case it is more than three hundred kilometers away. In ideally clean air, due to the scattering of light by density fluctuations, the range of vision is limited to approximately the same value, but this is a coincidence. In other words, the scattering of light in the atmosphere, the very thing that makes the sky blue, at a distance of three hundred kilometers creates such a background that objects will not be visible in this haze. But if the thickness of the atmosphere were 30 times greater than it is now, we would not have astronomy, and the picture of the world would be different for a long time. It is precisely this – scattering by density fluctuations – that determines the minimum losses in an optical fiber, in light guides, the so-called Rayleigh losses. In general, losses in optical fibers are a relevant and actively researched area. For one simple reason: fiber optic lines carry most of everything—everything at all! – information that humanity sends.

And if the refractive indices, for example, coincide, then what? There is a very simple, well-known example of an emulsion that scatters light perfectly - milk. Solids also scatter light if they are inhomogeneous, and the size of the inhomogeneities is comparable to the wavelength or larger. An example is glass. Ordinary amorphous glass is transparent (unless light-scattering particles are added to it). If glass is crystallized, it will become light-scattering, matte, and light will be scattered at the boundaries of the crystals. But if you crystallize glass so that the crystals are very small, tens of nanometers or less, i.e. much less than the wavelength, then if certain other conditions are met, it will become transparent. Nowadays it is called “nanostall”, all sorts of nonsense is written about it and – if it is colored – it is advertised as a jewelry “stone”. Radiation scattering is characterized by a radiation pattern, i.e. dependence of power on angle; They draw it, naturally, in polar coordinates. With a purely specular reflection, it is a narrow “needle” at the same angle at which the radiation arrived. For purely diffuse reflection from a chaotically rough surface, the power is proportional to the cosine of the angle relative to the normal (Lambert's law). In a real situation, there is always a mixture of both; even a perfectly polished surface does not reflect purely specularly. Some sources indicate that perfectly polished glass scatters $10^{3}$ percent, perfectly polished metal - $10^{2} 10^{1}$ percent. Let's assume the opposite: Sharik ate all the cutlets in some order and at the same time ate no more than a million flies. Let us present two possible arguments that provide a solution to the problem. 1. Note that from some point on, Sharik will only eat cutlets without flies. From this moment on, the number of cutlets without flies cannot increase after Sharik's move: if he formed some new cutlet without flies (by moving a fly from it), then before that he ate some cutlet without a fly. Then, at some point, the number of cutlets without flies will stabilize. After this moment, Sharik eats a cutlet without a fly with each move and transfers a fly from a cutlet that has exactly one fly to a cutlet that already has flies. Thus, a cutlet will appear with at least two flies on it, and Sharik will never eat this cutlet again (he no longer removes flies from such cutlets, and he no longer eats cutlets with flies). Contradiction. The problem is solved. 2. Let's number the cutlets in the order in which Sharik will eat them. In the first $10^{6}$ moves, Sharik will transfer some flies to some cutlets. Let n be the largest number among the numbers of these cutlets. Consider the first n cutlets. During the first $10^{6}$ moves, the total number of flies on them did not decrease (not counting the flies that Sharik ate). This means that before eating the nth cutlet, Sharik will have time to transfer no more than $n^{1} 10^{6}$ flies to cutlets with higher numbers.
