when there are a lot of bubbles. But in all cases, we need to know the optical properties of all materials involved in the situation. Question 7. And if the refractive indices, for example, coincide, then what? There is a very simple, well-known example of an emulsion that scatters light perfectly - milk. Solids also scatter light if they are inhomogeneous, and the size of the inhomogeneities is comparable to the wavelength or larger. An example is glass. Ordinary amorphous glass is transparent (unless light-scattering particles are added to it). If glass is crystallized, it will become light-scattering, matte, and light will be scattered at the boundaries of the crystals. But if you crystallize glass so that the crystals are very small, tens of nanometers or less, i.e. much less than the wavelength, then if certain other conditions are met, it will become transparent. Nowadays it is called “nanostall”, all sorts of nonsense is written about it and – if it is colored – it is advertised as a jewelry “stone”. Radiation scattering is characterized by a radiation pattern, i.e. dependence of power on angle; They draw it, naturally, in polar coordinates. With a purely specular reflection, it is a narrow “needle” at the same angle at which the radiation arrived. For purely diffuse reflection from a chaotically rough surface, the power is proportional to the cosine of the angle relative to the normal (Lambert's law). In a real situation, there is always a mixture of both; even a perfectly polished surface does not reflect purely specularly. Some sources indicate that perfectly polished glass scatters $10^{3}$ percent, perfectly polished metal - $10^{2} 10^{1}$ percent. Probably, the specific figure depends on the degree and meaning of “ideality”.
