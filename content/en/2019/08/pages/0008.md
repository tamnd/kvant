These results are absolutely necessary for the logic of quantum mechanics. Quantum mechanics must undoubtedly be considered an important component of what Einstein called a simple and clear picture of the world. Of course, one cannot assume that all natural phenomena are explained with its help. You can focus your attention on what is not explained. This is what professionals do. But not only that. Many people who are far from physics feel the world around them is full of mysteries and secrets. And they are right. Indeed, at almost every step one can encounter a phenomenon or event, the explanation of which is either unknown to us or unknown at all - even to those who in their profession could know the explanation if there was one. Those who look at the science of the world as if from the outside often draw the wrong conclusion from such observations about the fundamental impotence of science. The other extreme is exaggeration of the capabilities of science. There are people who believe that science can do everything. Naturally, this view of science is also wrong. I will refer to the statement of one of the creators of new physics, Erwin Schrödinger: “Science cannot tell us a word about why music makes us happy, why and how an old song can make us cry. We believe that science can, in principle, describe... But science knows nothing about the feelings of joy and regret that accompany the process [of learning], so it is silent about them.” The important words in this quote are: “science can in principle describe.” Science always describes. It would only be more correct to clarify: “natural sciences can, in principle, describe.” But let's not dwell on extreme points of view. The three words in the title of the article can be placed in different ways. This may change the meaning somewhat. Let it be, for example: surprise - admiration - understanding. Surprised and delighted by what he saw, the scientist comprehends the nature of the phenomenon and, according to Einstein, turns the miracle into triviality.

⟦column⟧

The word order adopted in the title: surprise - understanding - admiration means something else. Namely: i Surprise at the impossibility of explaining the observed phenomenon forced the scientist to go beyond the boundaries of the accepted theory. i The creation of a new theory followed. An understanding emerged that embraced many facts and phenomena. i The new picture of the world evokes true admiration. Admiration also extends to scientists, who literally expand the field of knowledge every day. Sharing admiration is very difficult. Neither exclamation marks, nor oohs, nor aahs will help. I'll tell you what fascinates me. I recently wrote an article entitled “What everything is made of” (it was published in three issues of the journal “Science and Life”: No. 10,11,12 for 2003). The article describes how atoms, molecules, solids are built from particles of three types - electrons, protons, neutrons, i.e. everything that makes up the world around us, including ourselves. The article ends with the following words: “Admiration and explanation do not get along well. By explaining how you can eliminate admiration. I really want that, having understood in general terms the principles of the structure of everything in the world, you do not lose the ability to admire the fact that to create all this fabulous diversity, only three types of particles are enough - electrons, protons and neutrons. “Just think,” the skeptic will say, “with the help of two symbols (zero and one) you can encode any text: a boring instruction and a beautiful poem.” Right! But someone created the text, as well as any device. When someone creates a unique machine or a brilliant poem, he certainly deserves admiration. But nature also evokes admiration, naturally creating an infinite variety of electrons, protons and neutrons.” Let us add to this that the natural path is surprisingly monotonous.
