Let's assume the opposite: Sharik ate all the cutlets in some order and at the same time ate no more than a million flies. Let us present two possible arguments that provide a solution to the problem. 1. Note that from some point on, Sharik will only eat cutlets without flies. From this moment on, the number of cutlets without flies cannot increase after Sharik's move: if he formed some new cutlet without flies (by moving a fly from it), then before that he ate some cutlet without a fly. Then, at some point, the number of cutlets without flies will stabilize. After this moment, Sharik eats a cutlet without a fly with each move and transfers a fly from a cutlet that has exactly one fly to a cutlet that already has flies. Thus, a cutlet will appear with at least two flies on it, and Sharik will never eat this cutlet again (he no longer removes flies from such cutlets, and he no longer eats cutlets with flies). Contradiction. The problem is solved. 2. Let's number the cutlets in the order in which Sharik will eat them. In the first $10^{6}$ moves, Sharik will transfer some flies to some cutlets. Let n be the largest number among the numbers of these cutlets. Consider the first n cutlets. During the first $10^{6}$ moves, the total number of flies on them did not decrease (not counting the flies that Sharik ate). This means that before eating the nth cutlet, Sharik will have time to transfer no more than $n^{1} 10^{6}$ flies to cutlets with higher numbers. Therefore, in the first n steps he will eat at least $10^{6} 1$ flies. Contradiction. The problem is solved. Can I. Mitrofanov's number of cars travel in 1 hour around a certain mark on the road?
