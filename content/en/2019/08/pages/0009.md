SURPRISE, UNDERSTANDING, ADMIRATION

Universe, the same. “The observable part of the Universe” sounds very mundane. But it occupies a sphere whose radius is about $10^{10}$ light years, i.e. light from the boundary of the observable region of the Universe moves towards the observer $10^{10}$ years. And the observable part of the Universe is filled with many billions of galaxies - multibillion-dollar clusters of stars. I would like to call this unimaginably huge sphere terra cognita - known land, in contrast to terra incognita - unknown land. Of course, this is a deliberate exaggeration: the world is full of secrets. But it is necessary to emphasize the following two circumstances. 1) A peculiar dismemberment of the world. The world is designed in such a way that you can explore and comprehend it in parts. Without this, the world would be completely unknowable. 2) Based on the objectively existing dismemberment of the world, the natural sciences have developed a unique professional sense among scientists. The specialist knows what belongs to his “department” and what does not. The history of science knows significant “aggressions”. First chemists and then physicists took up biology. Their intervention brought enormous fruit: it helped to advance the understanding of the problems of the activity and development of living organisms - right down to the atomic-molecular level. It is less known that only the intervention in cosmology of specialists in microphysics (particle physics) made it possible to raise the question of the development of the Universe and obtain encouraging answers that give hope to find out how the Universe developed from the moment of the Big Bang to the present day. Attempts to identify the most impressive achievements of science are usually of a specific nature - radio, electricity, atomic energy... All this is truly admirable. But what fascinates me most about science is its methodology. Each science has developed strict rules for handling the object of research. These rules are very diverse -

us, but they have common features. One of the main requirements is objectivity. When it comes to an experiment or the derivation of a formula, science strictly requires repeatability, the possibility of independent verification. It is scientific methodology that evokes special respect. Even pseudoscience, posing as science, uses scientific terminology, trying to emphasize its objectivity and the reliability of the methods it uses. Let us return mentally to the immensely vast region that I called “the known land.” Suppose we have created a model - something like a globe with “icons” printed on it, depicting objects of the world around us. Let the color of an object depend on the degree of understanding of the nature of this object. Then the “globe” would change its color all the time: new “icons” would continuously appear, and existing ones would change their color. Scientific knowledge about the world around us is constantly being improved and deepened. A mysterious phenomenon suddenly receives a detailed description, its new, previously unknown features are discovered, it enters into scientific use, and often leads to new discoveries. Science is one of the most dynamic areas of human activity. At the same time, science is characterized by strict selection: it does not “attack” any secret. Science, as a rule, sets itself problems that it can solve. There is a certain logic in the development of science: what was outside science yesterday due to inaccessibility is today on the agenda. Inaccessibility is by no means always a consequence of unpreparedness of the tools of scientific knowledge. More often, inaccessibility means that the worldview of scientists is unprepared to pose the corresponding problem. One of the important features of great scientists is a sense of change in the situation, a change that allows one to move from ignorance to knowledge. So science is dynamic.
