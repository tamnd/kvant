In a reference frame where the body of the cockroach is motionless and the legs are moving, the speed of the fly will be the sum of the relative speed with respect to the previous frame of reference, equal to 2 S R 60 s ª 1 cm s, and the transfer speed, equal in magnitude to the speed of the cockroach v, but directed in the opposite direction. Therefore, the speed of a fly relative to a cockroach can range from $v_{min} ª 9$ cm s to $v_{max} ª 11$ cm s. More precisely, from v - 2 S R 60 s to v + 2 S R 60 s. The speed of the cockroach in the fly's frame of reference can be either 2 S h 60 s - v ª 10.9 cm s, or 2 S h 60 s + v ª 30.9 cm s. The amount of speed depends on which direction the cockroach runs in relation to the wall. And the answers do not depend at all on the distance L.

F2570. Between two houses with flat roofs, which are located at the same height above the ground, the distance is 3 meters. Vasya brought two light and strong boards of the same width and thickness to one of the roofs, each of which is 2 meters long. Having tied these boards with ropes as shown in figure, Vasya connected the roofs of the houses with a bridge. The boards, with their free ends, rest on the edges of the roofs, “extending” 2.5 cm onto each roof. And each of the ends tied to the other board is covered with a rope, forming a “single” ring. Vasya's mass is 60 kg. At what minimum tensile strength of the ropes can Vasya, without fear of falling and in no hurry, move from one roof to another?

Since the boards are considered to be light, only Vasya’s mass needs to be taken into account in the load of the bridge. No matter where Vasya is on the bridge, the tension force of the ropes should not exceed the value at which the rope breaks. Therefore, we need to find such a position for Vasya

on the bridge, in which the rope of one of the rings is stretched the most. It is easy to guess that the rope ring shown in figure on the right is not stressed at all, since the upper board, when loaded from above, directly rests on the lower board. In other words, the tension force of the rope of this ring does not affect the safety of the transition from roof to roof and this rope can simply be ignored. And the rope shown on figure on the left will be stretched, and the force of its tension depends on Vasya’s position on the bridge. Neglecting the difference in lengths of 0.95 m and 1.05 m, we will assume that these segments have the same lengths. When Vasya is on the top board, the tension in the rope increases as he moves away from the left end of the bridge all the way to the far right end of the top board. At this moment, the total tension force of the vertical sections of the rope on one and the other side of the boards should be equal to 2 Mg 3, where M = 60 kg is Vasya’s mass. With further movement to the right end of the bridge along the bottom board, the tension force of the rope ring on the left becomes less and less. Thus, the rope must withstand a tension force of F = Mg 3 = 200 N.
