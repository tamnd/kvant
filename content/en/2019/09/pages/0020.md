Let's do the first 2n – 3 weighings according to the algorithm. Let's assume that in each of them the coin with the higher number outweighed. According to the Dirichlet principle, among the coins with numbers 1, ..., n - 1 there is one that “lost” (turned out to be lighter) no more than once during 2n – 3 weighings; Let's denote the number of this coin by k. Of course, the coin with number n has never “lost.” Let us show that such weighing results are possible. Indeed, this could happen in at least the following two situations. (A) The coins are ordered by increasing masses and all the scales (including the damaged ones) showed the correct results in all weighings. (B) The coins are ordered by increasing mass, with the exception of coin number k, which is the heaviest. At the same time, those scales on which coin number k “lost” (if such a weighing took place) were damaged and showed the wrong result in this weighing, but in the remaining weighings all the scales showed the correct results. Let's consider two cases. 1. The last ( 2 n - 2 ) -th weighing does not involve the coin with number k. Let's assume that the coin with the higher number wins again. Then each of situations (A) and (B) is still possible. 2. The last weighing involves the coin with number k. Let's assume she has the upper hand. Then, on the one hand, it is possible that situation (A) occurs and the last weighing was performed on damaged scales. On the other hand, it is possible that situation (B) occurs and in the last weighing the scales showed the correct result.
