Finally, systems are needed to store excess energy so that it can be spent at the right time. Nuclear energy is ideally suited for the role of a “constant energy background”. Its best indicators correspond to constant generation capacity, so it is advisable not to reduce the current share (5% in the world energy sector), but rather to increase it. Hydroelectric power, the current level of which (3%) is also undesirable to reduce, and the use of biomass for electricity production are also suitable. Thus, it is possible to provide a “background” level of at least 20% of global electricity consumption (better - even more). Gas generation can become an important component. On the one hand, it is the cleanest of the current “big three” (gas, oil, coal). On the other hand, gas reserves are quite large (within the 21st century). Finally, gas power plants can increase the constant “background” of electricity production and, very importantly, make it possible to easily and quickly change the power supplied to the network. Options based on pumped storage power plants - pumped storage power plants - are also important in covering power peaks and troughs. According to the principle of operation, a pumped storage power plant pumps water upward into the pool when there is excess power, and discharges water downward, generating electricity when there is not enough power. In addition, the use of biomass for energy production cannot be discounted. It can be used to maintain the “background” and to compensate for peaks in power consumption. What if the level of wind and solar generation becomes very large? In this case, more radical solutions will be needed. The literature suggests the following set of measures:

i create a reserve of capacity of non-renewable sources; i increase the installed capacity of renewable sources (RES generation reserve); i pump power from regions where it is abundant to those regions where it is lacking (energy transmission networks); i accumulate excess energy for the purpose of using it during times of insufficient wind and solar generation (storage). Let's briefly look at these measures. If the energy system is based only on wind and solar generation, then a reserve of non-renewable sources of the same power is required. This effectively results in duplication of power generation devices, with each element being used sub-optimally. If the installed capacity of wind and solar generation is significantly increased so that even in low winds and low light there is enough energy, then much more generation devices will be required. It was calculated that with a 60% share of wind and sun, these devices need twice as many, with 80% - 6 times more, and with 100% - 10 times more (!). This is a huge waste of resources, again used inefficiently. The development of trunk energy transmission systems to other regions requires a radical update of networks and an increase in their capacity by 7 times with a share of wind and solar of 60% and 12 times with 100%. The task is very ambitious. The use of energy storage or accumulators simplifies the task of balancing the power produced and consumed and, in principle, can make it possible to completely dispense with non-renewable energy sources. However, storing excess energy on a large scale faces still unsolved problems - storage capacity, resource, i.e. number of charge-discharge cycles, losses during charging and discharging.
