Now we will show how to modify the solution from point a) in the situation under consideration. Let us assume that there is an algorithm that allows us to determine the heaviest coin in 2n – 2 weighings. Let's number the coins with the numbers 1, ..., n. Let's do the first 2n – 3 weighings according to the algorithm. For each of these weighings, let's call the main coin for this weighing the coin with the lowest number of all participating in this weighing. According to the Dirichlet principle, among the coins with numbers 1, ..., n – 1 there is one that was the main coin no more than once during 2n – 3 weighings; Let's denote the number of this coin by k. Of course, the coin with number n could never be the main one. Let us assume that in each of the weighings performed, the bowl on which the main coin of this weighing lies “lost” (turned out to be lighter). Let us show that the indicated weighing results are possible. Indeed, this could happen in at least the following two situations. (A) The coins are ordered by increasing masses and all the scales (including the damaged ones) showed the correct results in all weighings. (B) The coins are ordered by increasing mass, with the exception of coin number k, which is the heaviest. In this case, those scales on which the weighing was carried out, in which k was the main one (if such weighing took place), were damaged, and in the remaining weighings all the scales showed correct results. Let's consider two cases. 1. In the last ( 2 n - 2 ) -th weighing, the main one is not the coin with number k. Let’s assume that the cup on which the main coin lies “loses” again.

Then each of situations (A) and (B) is still possible. 2. In the last weighing, the coin with number k is the main one. Let's consider all the other coins involved in this weighing. Let coin number l be the coin with the lowest number among them. On the other hand, it is possible that situation (B) occurs and in the last weighing the scales showed the correct result. A fly sits on the tip of the second hand and does not move relative to the arrow, and a cockroach runs along the horizontal floor along the wall with a clock at a distance L = 10 cm from it at a constant speed v = 10 cm s. At some point the cockroach is closest to the clock. What is the maximum speed of a cockroach at this moment in the fly’s reference frame, where it and the second hand of the clock are at rest, and the whole world rotates around the axis of the clock?
