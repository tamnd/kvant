Table 2 is ambitious. Let us separately note the situation in Germany and Denmark. After the nuclear accident in Fukushima, Japan, Germany decided to completely shut down nuclear power generation in its country and accelerate the transition to renewable energy sources. In 2014, the total share of renewable energy sources in Germany was already 28% (wind - 10%, solar - 6%, hydro - 3%, bio - 9%). In “tiny” Denmark, a pioneer in the development of wind energy, they have reached a wind generation level of 50% and are not going to stop there. As for the “heavyweights” in terms of population (India and China), India expects to reach 40% renewable energy by 2030. Judging by the fact that in China there is already a gradual reduction in nuclear generation to the level of 50% (by 2035). What is the reason for such attention to renewable energy sources? There are several components here. Firstly, it is environmental. When producing electricity using renewable energy sources (wind, sun, hydro), there are no harmful emissions and, especially, the formation of carbon dioxide $( CO_{2} ),$, which affects the climate and global warming. Secondly – ​​resource. The reserves of non-renewable energy sources on Earth are limited. The resource of wind and solar energy is very large and is maintained as long as our heavenly body shines.
