physics+technology

Even a casual glance at the epigraphs will notice a key concept that points to the theme of this issue – ENERGY. Indeed, how can we manage our engineering series without discussing the possibilities and contributions of science to the production, storage, transformation, transmission and use of various types of energy? Physics undoubtedly played a decisive role in this, and every section of its school course contains energy issues. The importance of their development is emphasized by the following forecast: the economic development necessary to maintain the current standard of living until 2050, taking into account population growth, will require a manifold increase in global energy production and the search for new, unconventional ways to obtain it. This is a serious challenge that creates a whole range of physical and technical problems. Well, let's energetically, without delay, tackle them!

It's interesting that...

...the desire to harness the forces of nature already thousands of years before the new era led to the construction of water and windmills - the prototypes of modern turbines. ...the ancient Greek scientist and engineer Heron of Alexandria invented and built the aeolipile - the first working steam engine. ...the discovery of atmospheric pressure in 1643 by the Italian Evangelista Torricelli led to the invention of air pumps and steam-atmospheric installations. In 1675, the Dutchman Christiaan Huygens demonstrated a machine powered by the explosion of gunpowder, and his collaborating Frenchman Denis Papin in 1690 was the first to use steam to lift a piston and describe the closed thermodynamic cycle of a steam engine.
