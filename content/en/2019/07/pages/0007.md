Four years later, having broken the warlike axis, the victorious Allies created an international organization - the United Nations - as an instrument for maintaining peace. After the disappearance of the axis, however, “axial” symmetry rapidly transformed into antisymmetry, and the first nuclear explosions marked this transformation. In Hiroshima and Nagasaki, science visibly entered politics. And the physicists involved in this realized faster than the politicians that the new antisymmetry is fraught with the self-destruction of humanity. The largest theoretical physicists of the 20th century, Einstein and Bohr, addressed the newborn world parliament - the UN, calling for the creation of a world government so that the nuclear age would not become the last century of civilization. Alas, at that time it was too theoretical an idea.

Theorist-inventor

The outlined global picture of world symmetries was also too theoretical for 20-year-old Andrei Sakharov. In ominously practical circumstances, when the Nazis approached Moscow, the fourth-year student was evacuated along with the university to distant Ashgabat. And after an accelerated graduation from the university, he went to a military plant in Ulyanovsk. The plant made cartridges - for the front, for victory. And a young specialist in “defense metal science,” trying to help the victory, invented a magnetic device for checking the quality of bullets. And his first independent task in theoretical physics was born from reflection on this invention. In order for a physical problem of dubious importance to arise from cartridge production, a person with the vocation of a theoretical physicist is needed. Otherwise, the invention of a very specific device will not lead to a strange question: what if magnetic forces are replaced by electric ones? This was not a production issue, it was a question about nature, in which there are countries

Forty years earlier, an employee of the patent office in Bern, Albert Einstein, reflecting on the apparent asymmetry of moving charges, created the most famous theory of physics - the theory of relativity. When starting to think about some asymmetry in nature, the theorist does not know whether he will come to a new theory, just a new formula, or not come to anything reasonable at all. But reflection is driven by the same desire for knowledge. So, in the warmest and brightest room of the Ulyanovsk Cartridge Plant - in the party office, next to the shelves of political books, the young engineer studied theoretical physics and finally figured out how to solve his electromagnetic problem. This problem was followed by others - unnecessary for cartridge production, but interesting for a novice theorist. The theorist asked Nature questions, the answers to which he received using physical reasoning and mathematics, skillfully managing symmetries and asymmetries. Only one of these problems can be understood without explanation: “At what rate does the thickness of ice increase when surrounded by ice water?” The aspiring theorist sent the solutions to the other two problems to his father in Moscow, and he showed them to Igor Evgenievich Tamm, the chief theorist at the Physical Institute of the Academy of Sciences. As a result, at the beginning of 1945, Andrei Sakharov became a graduate student at the Lebedev Physical Institute and devoted himself entirely to theoretical physics. History gave him only a few years for pure science. The atomic explosion in Hiroshima in August 1945 was heard in the Soviet Union. Nuclear asymmetry demanded an answer. Not only politicians, but also Russian physicists thought so.
