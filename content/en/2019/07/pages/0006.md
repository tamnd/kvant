Right and left

In the first months of America I was struck by the abundance of people writing with their left hand. In the forty-five years of my previous – Russian – life, I had not seen anything like this. Of course, I knew that such people existed, and of course I read Leskov’s “Lefty”, but for some reason I never met them in everyday life and even somehow connected the sad fate of Lefty with his innate peculiarity. Sakharov was lucky that he belonged to the even rarer minority of ambidextrous people and therefore was not so vulnerable to the overwhelming majority of right-handed people. But what if his parents had not provided him with home education until the 7th grade, but had immediately sent him to a Soviet school for re-education? And there - according to Mayakovsky - they would command: “Who there writes with the left hand?! Right! Right! Right!” According to Andrei Sakharov himself, long-term home schooling increased his “lack of contact, from which [he] later suffered both at school and at university, and indeed almost all his life.” Who knows, early close contacts with Soviet life might have undermined the self-esteem of this gentle man with strong moral principles, and deprived him of the ability to be in the minority. But in science, a new word is always spoken by the smallest minority - one person.

Symmetries in physics and politics

The word “symmetry” usually describes the shape of a building, a pattern, a geometric figure - this is always some kind of pattern of form. However, in the 20th century, it was possible to discern manifestations of the symmetries of the universe in physical laws. It turned out, for example, that a spinning top does not fall for a long time simply because it “doesn’t know” which way to fall: all horizontal directions in space are equal

– all sides are symmetrical relative to the vertical $оси.^{1}$ And in general, every fundamental physical law reveals a certain symmetry of the Universe. If an experiment reveals some kind of asymmetry, then the theoretical physicist receives a difficult but excitingly interesting task - to find the place of this asymmetry in the harmony of the universe. Sakharov became interested in a similar problem in the 60s. Butterfly symmetry, or mirror symmetry, is involved in his most significant idea in cosmology - in the physics of the Universe. However, before taking up this absolutely peaceful idea, Sakharov devoted the previous two decades of his life - the most fruitful years for a theoretical physicist - to the creation of the most destructive weapon in the history of mankind. Why? The asymmetries that shaped his life were not related to physics, but to politics. Andrei Sakharov entered the university in 1938, on the eve of World War II. The world then seemed to him extremely asymmetrical. The country in which he was lucky enough to be born was ahead of the rest. She was moving towards a bright future, a society of planetary social justice and limitless possibilities. Left behind were the countries of capitalism and among them the most sinister Hitlerite Germany, in which they abused the word “socialism” with the prefix “national-” and rejected scientific theories because of their “non-Aryan” origin. This asymmetry was abolished by the World War.
