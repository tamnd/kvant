It's interesting that...

...the desire to harness the forces of nature already thousands of years before the new era led to the construction of water and windmills - the prototypes of modern turbines. ...the ancient Greek scientist and engineer Heron of Alexandria invented and built the aeolipile - the first working steam engine. ...the discovery of atmospheric pressure in 1643 by the Italian Evangelista Torricelli led to the invention of air pumps and steam-atmospheric installations. In 1675, the Dutchman Christiaan Huygens demonstrated a machine powered by the explosion of gunpowder, and his collaborating Frenchman Denis Papin in 1690 was the first to use steam to lift a piston and describe the closed thermodynamic cycle of a steam engine.

...pupils of the Paris Polytechnic School Gaspard-Gustave Coriolis and Jean-Victor Poncelet, trying to apply theoretical methods to applied problems, clearly defined the concept of work, and also introduced an assessment of energy losses due to the presence of friction. ...the world's first electric motor was built by Michael Faraday in 1821, and a decade later he discovered the law of electromagnetic induction, which formed the basis of modern electric power engineering. ...one of the most outstanding inventors in the field of electrical engineering, Nikola Tesla, who developed a number of designs for electric motors, electric generators and transformers, also became famous for the construction of the Niagara hydroelectric power station. ...recently Japanese engineers transmitted 2 kilowatts of power to 55 meters using a beam of microwave radiation. This is the next step towards the implementation of a geostationary power plant, when at an altitude of 36 thousand kilometers satellites “collect” and transmit solar energy to Earth. ...a technique has been developed for spraying any flexible materials - fabrics, plastic and even toilet paper - with solar photocells that generate noticeable voltage without loss of efficiency. ...a student from Germany managed to create an “electromagnetic harvester” that captures energy literally from thin air, namely background fields of mobile communications, Wi-Fi signals and other household sources, then charging batteries of a certain type with it.
