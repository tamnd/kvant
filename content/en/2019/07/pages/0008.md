Inventive theorist or practical politician?

Andrei Sakharov with his daughter, summer 1948 (asymmetrically aimed camera) defending his dissertation in pure science, returned to physical and technical invention. For almost two decades. He considered this work necessary, he liked it, and he succeeded in it. In a matter of weeks, Sakharov would come up with the idea for the Sloika, the first Soviet thermonuclear bomb. “I remember this summer with the sparkle of the water, the sun, the fresh greenery, the yachts gliding across the reservoir... Despite the summer time, we all worked very hard. The world we were immersed in was strangely fantastic, in stark contrast to everyday city and family life outside our workroom, with ordinary scientific work.” Invention is compatible with theoretical physics. The famous theorist Enrico Fermi said about the invention of the superbomb: “Excellent physics!” After all, a thermonuclear explosion with its astronomical temperatures and pressures gives the theorist the opportunity to “penetrate” inside the star. All this is true. But forty years later, Sakharov sadly wrote in a letter: “I’m trying to study what smart people have done in the field of quantum field theory... it’s an extremely difficult thing, and I often despair of ever reaching the proper level - too much has been missed since 1948, there are continuous gaps, and in all subsequent years I’ve only been able to do something due to luck and “impudence”, often getting caught

No matter how exciting it is to see an artificial star in a hydrogen bomb, by the end of the 50s the goal was achieved and the scientific problem was exhausted. It's time for sophisticated technology. There is also creative scope there, but not for a theoretical physicist. But what about a theorist who knows that he invented the most powerful weapon in history and - by the will of history - became a very influential scientist in the country's military-scientific complex? Responsibility for what is happening around – the ancestral feeling of the Russian intelligentsia – led the theoretical physicist into practical politics. Sakharov began his political biography in the field of his profession: he was concerned about nuclear tests in the atmosphere - harmful to humanity and unnecessary for maintaining the nuclear balance. The novice politician was successful - in 1963, an international treaty banned above-ground testing. This confirmed Sakharov in the importance of his new role. At the same time, communication with the highest leaders of the state gave him experienced knowledge about the Soviet government that was not available to others. The very problem of strategic balance is related to the overall potential of the state and society. In the mid-1960s, the development of nuclear missiles led to an unusually dangerous turn in the arms race, although it was about defense. Missile defense has proven to be more dangerous to the world than offensive weapons. Sakharov, as one of the highest military-technical experts, tried to explain this non-obvious, but quite probable conclusion to the government on July 21, 1967.
