KVA NT 2019/No.5

If the segments are of different colors, then they are separated by a grid line - for example, a horizontal one (see example at figure 6, d). Then the segment with number t moved to the top row of the m u n rectangle, and the segment t + 1 moved to the bottom, but to the next column. The sides of the rectangle m u n are odd, so the top row is colored the same as the bottom. This means that the segments with numbers t and t + 1 will be of different colors. So, adjacent segments of the same color go to cells of the same color, and adjacent segments of different colors go to cells of different colors. This means that all red segments lie in cells of one color, and blue segments lie in cells of another. The rectangle m u n has odd sides, so the number of black and white cells in the chess coloring differs by 1. Then the number of blue and red segments differs by 1, which is what needed to be proved.
