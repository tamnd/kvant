$1. (3)^{1}$ Several natural numbers are written out in a row with a sum of 20. No number and no sum of several numbers written in a row is equal to 3. Could more than 10 numbers be written down? A. Shapovalov

2. (4) There are 2n + 1 coins with heads facing up in a circle. Moving clockwise, they make 2n + 1 turns: they turn over a coin, one coin is passed and the next one is turned over, two coins are passed through and the next one is turned over, three coins are passed through and the next one is turned over, etc., finally, 2n coins are passed through and the next one is turned over. Prove that now exactly one coin is heads up. V. Rastorguev

3. (4) The product of natural numbers m and n is divided by their sum. Prove that $m n d n^{2}$. B.Frenkin
