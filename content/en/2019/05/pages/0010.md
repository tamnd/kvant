At the same time, due to a valve failure, the ball rose above the clouds to an unplanned height of 3.5 kilometers, but, alas, the eclipse was already over. Mendeleev managed to fix the valve and land safely at a distance of 100 kilometers from the starting point. This touch of Mendeleev’s biography illustrates his extraordinary courage, which manifested itself not only in the feat of creating the periodic table. Mendeleev was courageous and principled in all his affairs, including in relations with the “powers that be.” At the end of the 19th century in Russia, the authorities began to pursue a policy of “strengthening discipline and law and order” in society and, above all, in universities, where students began to strive for educational reforms. Mendeleev appealed to the “authorities” several times, standing up for the “rebels” expelled from the university. The result was his dismissal from the university and resignation from almost all the commissions in which he actively participated. Mendeleev was twice denied election as a member of the Russian Academy of Sciences, although he was already a member of several dozen prestigious academies and scientific societies around the world. Twice the government insisted on withdrawing Mendeleev's Nobel Prize nominations made by prominent Russian chemists. And this, of course, influenced the Nobel committee, which never awarded Mendeleev this award, to the bewilderment of the entire world chemical community. An important place in Mendeleev’s life at that time was occupied by weekly evenings, where colleagues and friends gathered, including artists I.E. Repin, A.I. Kuindzhi, I.I. Shishkin and other Wanderers. In this salon, all events in the scientific and political life of society were casually discussed. Mendeleev's wife pointedly did not take part in these meetings. became increasingly complex and hopeless. At the end of 1876, 42-year-old Mendeleev met 16-year-old Anna Ivanovna Popova (1860–1942) at one of his salon evenings. The young girl chose an unusual fate for that time. Having grown up in the old Testament family of the Don Cossack ataman, she somehow accepted the advanced ideas of the emerging Russian intelligentsia, choosing for herself the path of enlightening the people. Secretly from her father (but with her mother’s blessing), she left for the capital to study at women’s courses (the prototype of the famous “Bestuzhev courses” - the first women’s university in Russia). She studied painting, was accepted into the Mendeleev salon, felt free and at ease in it and, of course, could not help but pay attention to its head, the famous chemist. And neither Mendeleev’s worldwide fame nor the 26-year age difference prevented her from feeling love, along with immense respect. And Dmitry Ivanovich could not help but be interested in Anechka, as they began to call her in the salon. Not wanting to be the reason for the destruction of the family, Anya “escaped” to Italy. Having learned about his departure, Mendeleev dropped everything and left after Anna. A month later they returned together. After his divorce from his first wife, Mendeleev's life changed dramatically. Anna Ivanovna was an attentive and caring wife and shared all the views of her husband. This marriage produced four children. Not far from Mendeleev’s country house near Moscow there was the village of Shakhmatovo, where in the summer the family of the daughter of the rector of St. Petersburg University, botanist A.N. Beketov, a great friend of D.I. Mendeleev, lived. And there Mendeleev’s eldest daughter, Lyubov Dmitrievna, and Beketov’s grandson, Alexander Alexandrovich Blok, the future great Russian poet, met. They became husband and wife. Anecdotal stories are also woven into the biographies of many famous people. They are also present in the biography of Mendeleev.
