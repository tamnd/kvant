Is it so familiar to you?

?

inertia

It’s even strange why this concept, denoting one of the basic properties of matter, has not yet become the main character of our issues. Let’s restore justice and give her the right to a “solo performance.” Inertia surprisingly combines apparent simplicity and intricate complexity. It manifests itself both in nature and in the creations of our hands. Sometimes it can be a hindrance, but often it is definitely useful. So, for example, the well-known top and spinning top, it turns out, behave like sophisticated gyroscopic devices that serve to stabilize the movement and orientation in space of many vehicles. Drivers often resort to “coasting” driving, accelerating and then spending the accumulated energy for a long time, in common parlance – “inertia”. Children's cars with a handwheel and beetle lanterns that shine by pressing the handle are prototypes of energy storage devices that use rotational inertia. It would seem that what is common between a dog shaking off water and inertial thermonuclear fusion? Or between the elementary falling of a stone to the ground and the fundamental properties of space and time? This is all inertia... Let’s add a few more stories to the collection of our ideas about it in the hope that we will be able to amend the direct translation of the Latin word “inertia,” meaning “lethargy, immobility, inaction.”

It is curious that... ...the ancient Greeks distinguished between two types of movement - natural and violent. Today, natural movement that occurs without the application of force means inertial movement in a straight line. However, ancient scientists believed that such a movement should be eternal and unchanging only in the “supralunar world”, i.e. circular and uniform movement of the heavenly bodies. ...Newton, who called inertia the “innate force of matter,” interprets this term only as a “property”, without thinking of it as a real physical force. He calls the external force applied to the body “another force.” Clarification was required due to the uncertainty of the concept of force in the 17th and 18th centuries, when its meanings sometimes had nothing in common with the modern understanding of force introduced by Newton. ...Heinrich Hertz, dissatisfied with the constructions of Galileo and Newton, created his own so-called force-free mechanics, which, however, was not accepted by the scientific community. It followed, for example, that the law of inertia is valid when applied to the Universe as a whole, that there is a “world ether” that has the properties of ordinary inert matter, and that the movement of planets around the Sun can be explained by inertia without involving the action of forces. ...the concept of the moment of inertia, responsible for the inertia of a body during rotation, was introduced at the beginning of the 18th century when developing the theory of the pendulum by such celebrities as Jacob Bernoulli and Christiaan Huygens.

...Christian Huygens explained the change in the period of oscillation of a pendulum when moving along the earth's surface by the rotation of the Earth, leading to its flattening at the poles and swelling at the equator. To confirm, he quickly rotated a ball of soft clay placed on an axis and observed its flattening. This experience, by the way, significantly influenced the cosmogonic views of Immanuel Kant and Pierre Laplace, who developed hypotheses of the origin of the Solar system. ...the very rapid rotation of Jupiter - one revolution every 10 hours - leads to its strong oblateness at the poles. The Moon, making one revolution around its axis in a whole month, on the contrary, is practically not flattened. ...the oldest flywheel on Earth, discovered during archaeological excavations, was made five and a half thousand years ago. It was a potter's wheel - a massive disk of baked clay about a meter in diameter and with a round hole in the center. After spinning up, the disk rotated for some time, consuming the accumulated energy, i.e. played the role of a flywheel. ... back in 1791, Russian mechanic Ivan Kulibin invented a “scooter” with a flywheel that smoothed out the unevenness of its movement. And in 1860, the Russian engineer Karl Shubersky developed a “fly-wheeler,” which was a railway platform with huge flywheels weighing hundreds of pounds. Accelerating on horizontal sections and descents, the rotating flywheels then helped the train overcome fairly steep climbs with their inertia. ...the two roles of mass - as a measure of inertia and “gravitational charge” - were studied by Newton, conducting experiments with pendulums. The period of the pendulum, according to his data, did not depend on the weight of the load, as it should have been in the case of equality of the inertial and gravitational masses of the bodies. The identity of the concepts of inertia and gravity was then elevated to the rank of a fundamental principle of physics and formed the basis of Einstein’s general theory of relativity. ...about a hundred years ago a series of experiments was carried out to identify charge carriers in metals. When the conductor suddenly stopped, the free charges in it moved for a short time by inertia, creating a current pulse. Its direction showed that electrons are charge carriers. ...photons, having no rest mass, have inertia. Thus, they are attracted to other bodies: flying past the Sun and stars, they turn, creating cosmic mirages, or, flying towards the Earth, they increase their energy, which affects the change in their frequency. But, alas, it is impossible to stop, slow down or speed up photons.
