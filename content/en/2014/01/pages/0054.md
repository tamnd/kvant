Winter quirks of the sun's rays

(See the beginning on the 4th page of the cover)

...It is known that a rainbow is a consequence of the refraction and internal reflection of sunlight in tiny droplets of water floating in the air after rain. In cold winter, instead of droplets, ice crystals appear in the air, which become especially numerous immediately after a sharp cold snap. These crystals, as a rule, have the shape of hexagonal flat plates or prisms. Everyone knows how a piece of paper falls. His fall is an alternation of floating and sliding down (Fig.1), but

This leads to the fact that the brightness of all parts of the halo becomes the same. In calm weather, ice crystals float above the ground so that their planes make very small angles with the horizontal. Therefore, parts of the halo on a horizontal line passing through the Sun become very bright. The peoples of the Arctic, where these two side flares of the halo were especially often observed, called them solar dogs - for their long tails going to the sides (Fig.3).
