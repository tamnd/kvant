Let the tangents drawn at points A and B to the circle $\omega$ intersect the line $A_{1} B_{1}$ at points X and Y, respectively, and intersect at point Z (Fig.5). Let us prove a lemma that is important for what follows.

The considerations mentioned are a powerful tool. Say, from statements 3, 4, 5 it follows that the triangle formed by the tangents drawn to $\omega$ at points A, B, C, and the orthotriangle are homothetic, and the straight line OH (it connects the centers of the inscribed circles of these triangles) passes through the center of homothety. Using auxiliary statements and Lemma 1, we can solve, for example, the following difficult problem.

Exercise 4 (Yu. Blinkov, A.P. Savin Mathematical Fights Tournament, 2008). Lines MH, $A_{1} B_{1}$ and $ZC_{1}$ intersect at one point $( C_{1}$ – the base of the altitude from vertex C.) Prove it.

Lemma 1. M is the center of the inscribed circle of triangle XYZ. Proof. 1) Since ZA = ZB, then ZM is the bisector of the angle XZY (see Fig.5). 2) Note that $MA = MB_{1}$, i.e. M lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment $AB_{1}$. From the tangency, angle XAC is equal to angle B, but from Proposition 3, angle $XB_{1} A$ is also equal to angle B. Hence, triangle $XAB_{1}$ is isosceles, and point X also lies on the perpendicular bisector of segment $AB_{1}$, which is the bisector of $\angle AXB_{1}$. So, M lies on the bisector $\angle AXB_{1}$, as required.

Well, what about point P? It turns out that it has the most direct relation to this design. Problem 5 (Yu. Zaitseva, Moscow Oral Olympiad in Geometry, 2012). Prove that the circumcircles of triangles ABC and XYZ touch at point P. To solve this difficult problem, we again need to recall two more facts and prove the lemma. Statement 6. Points $H_{a}, H_{b}$ and $H_{c}$, symmetrical to the orthocenter with respect to the corresponding sides of triangle ABC, lie on its circumcircle and form a triangle homothetic to the orthotriangle. Statement 7 (main property of the symmedian). Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle. The tangents to the circle drawn at points A and B intersect at point Z. Then the line CZ contains the symmedian (a line symmetric to the median CM relative to the bisector of angle C) of triangle ABC. Lemma 2. Points Z, P and $H_{c}$ lie on the same line. Proof. According to the basic property of the simedian, PZ is the simedian of the triangle PAB. It remains to understand that $H_{c}$ also lies on the symmedian. It is clear that the point $H '$ from Statement 1 (symmetrical to H relative to M) is symmetrical to the point $H_{c}$ relative to the perpendicular bisector to AB (Fig.6). Then the arcs $AH '$ and $BH_{c}$ are equal, so the lines $PH '$ (median) and $PH_{c}$ are symmetrical with respect to the bisector of the angle APB. The lemma is proven.

Exercise 3 (Yu. Blinkov, A.P. Savin Mathematical Fights Tournament, 2011). Prove that the circumcircles of triangles $AMA_{1}$ and $BMB_{1}$ pass through the points X and $Y соответственно.^{6}$

Further we will repeatedly encounter triangles with correspondingly parallel sides. Two such triangles are homothetic (that is, there is a center of homothety that takes one of the triangles to the other), or there is a parallel translation that takes one of the triangles to the other. The lines connecting the corresponding vertices of two homothetic triangles intersect at the center of the homothety. Moreover, the following is true: Statement 5. Lines connecting the corresponding points (for example, points of intersection of altitudes, bisectors, medians, etc.) of two homothetic triangles intersect at the center of homothety.
