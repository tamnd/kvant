6th grade

1. Prove that in any six-digit natural number divisible by 101, two digits can be swapped to form a six-digit number also divisible by 101. 2. There are 20 consecutive natural numbers. Could it be that the first number is divisible by 1, the second by 2, the third by 3, ..., the nineteenth by 19, and the twentieth by 400? 3. In a natural number that does not end in 0, two zeros were crossed out in a row. As a result, the number decreased by k times, where k is a natural number. Find the largest possible value of k. 4. Is it possible to color each natural number blue, green or red so that all three colors are present and the color of the sum of any two different colored numbers does not coincide with the colors of the terms? 5. The Ministry of Propaganda employed honest employees (who always told the truth) and pathological liars (who always lied). Every employee knew about everyone else, whether he was honest or a liar. The newly appointed Minister of Propaganda asked each subordinate about some other subordinate, whether he was honest or a liar. At the same time, the Minister did not ask twice about any subordinate. After the survey, the Minister expelled from the Ministry all employees who were called honest. Could the Minister have exactly 2013 subordinates left? 6. In prehistoric times, there were 2013 villages on the coast of the round island and every two neighboring villages were connected by two different one-way roads in different directions. The builder leader can build a new one-way road between any two villages, and can also build a new village in the middle of any road. Prove that at any given time there is a cyclic route through an even number of villages that passes through each village at most once. 7. The chess knight (indicated at figure by the letter K) can make moves in eight directions. Let's call the moves to the cells marked with the number 1 Kazan, and the moves to the cells marked with the number 2 - Vyatka. Can a knight go around the chessboard $8\times 8,$ by visiting each of its squares exactly once and with the last move returning to the original square so that the Kazan and Vyatka moves alternate? 8. There are 100 straight lines drawn on a plane. It turned out that among any four of them there are two parallel ones. Prove that among any seven of them there are three parallel ones. 9. Inside a square with side 20, several segments with a total length of 160 are drawn, which divide it into several rectangles. In this case, the ends of each drawn segment belong to

7th grade

1. For any pairwise distinct integers x, y and z, prove the inequality

$( x - y )^{3} + ( y - z )^{3} + ( z - x )^{3} \ge 6$.

2. There are 100 consecutive natural numbers written on the board. Vasya multiplied 50 numbers: 25 largest and 25 smallest. Then he multiplied the 50 remaining numbers. Could the sum of the resulting products be equal to 100! (i.e. $1 \cdot 2 \cdot$ … $\cdot$ 100)? 3. A natural number is written on the board. Every minute, double the number of its natural divisors is added to it (counting 1 and the number itself) and the resulting number is written on the board instead of the original one. What is the greatest number of exact squares that can appear on the board in 24 hours? 4. On the path between Winnie the Pooh’s house and Piglet’s house, an Owl settled in the middle of the path, and Tigger settled somewhere else. Passing by Owl's house, impressed by what he saw, Winnie the Pooh reduces his speed by 20% compared to his usual speed. Passing by Tigger's house, impressed by what he saw, Winnie the Pooh increases his speed by 20% compared to his usual speed. It turned out that the appearance of new neighbors did not change the time it took Winnie the Pooh to get to Piglet and return home. Where could Tigger live? Winnie the Pooh walks away from Piglet's house at the same speed with which he approached him. 5. Diego wants to cover the square $6\times 6$ with eighteen dominoes $1\times 2.$ Can he arrange three dominoes in the square (without overlaps) so that the rest of the square can be covered with the remaining dominoes in the only way? 6. For what natural values ​​k can a triangle be cut into k quadrilaterals of equal area? 7. In an acute triangle ABC, the bisector BD is drawn. In triangle ADB the altitude DE is drawn, and in triangle BCD the median DF is drawn. It turned out that BC + FD = 2BE. Find the angle DFC. 8. There are 32 squares marked on the chessboard. What is the largest number of knights that can be guaranteed to be placed on these squares without them hitting each other? 9. 300 schoolchildren came to the summer camp. It turned out that the number of triplets of pairwise acquaintances of schoolchildren is greater than the number of pairs of acquaintances of schoolchildren. Prove that there is a schoolboy who knows at least five others. 10. In the first grade of the Prostokvashinsky secondary school
