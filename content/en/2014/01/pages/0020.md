KVANT 2014/№1

The Old World (Europe) decided to overtake the New World (USA). European countries joined forces, and the LHC was designed and built - the Large Hadron Collider, which is significantly more powerful than the American accelerator. And although the LHC will finally reach its designed particle energy level only by 2015, already now, having worked for a little over a year at half power, the LHC has made it possible to obtain a number of important results, the main one of which is proof of the existence of the Higgs boson.

Site of the Large Hadron Collider at CERN

On the Fourth of July 2012, CERN announced the discovery of the Higgs boson. The boson mass turned out to be (with 99% reliability) equal to $125,3 \pm 0,6$ GeV, the boson spin number is zero, the lifetime of the order is 1.6 ◊ $10^{-} 22$ s, various ways of decay into pairs and quadruples of particles and antiparticles - all these and other characteristics completely confirmed the theoretical predictions. From now on, all doubts about the validity of the Standard Model disappeared. It has taken its place as the theory underlying modern physics. What's next? And then - a whole series of problems, the solution of which, perhaps, will move physics to a higher level of knowledge. Among the related tasks are the refinement of the Standard Model, obtaining new information about quarks and gluons and, as a result, about the first moments of the existence of the Universe. But there are even more important tasks - to find out what dark matter and dark energy are, whether supermatter and superparticles exist, what is the state of matter in black holes. Science expects a lot of new things from the work of the LHC in the next 10–20 years. The beneficial competition between the Old and New Worlds will continue. In the USA, it is planned to build an electron positron collider, whose capabilities are an order of magnitude greater than the LHC. It’s not for nothing that Academician V. Rubakov ended his article a year ago with the words: “So stay tuned for the news!” In conclusion, let us dwell on some characteristics of the LHC - a real wonder of the world of the 21st century. On the border of France and Switzerland, in the rock mass at a depth of about 120 m from the surface of the earth, there is an almost circular tunnel 26.7 km long with a main section diameter of 12 m. In addition to the main tunnel, there is a series of smaller rings, where protons gradually gain energy in areas with an accelerating electric field and are divided into two counter beams. In the main tunnel, two streams of protons move in opposite directions through two pipes. The speed of protons is almost equal to the speed of light. With such

speed per second, protons circumscribe the circumference of the 27-kilometer ring 10,000 times. The essence of the colliding beam method (collider) is that during a collision the relative energy increases not by 2 times, as happens at low energies, but by several hundred times. This violates the calm (symmetry) of vacuum fields, as a result of which new particles appear (are born). Every second, about a billion such events occur at the site of proton collision. Some of them are reliably identified with the production of Higgs bosons. The tunnels maintain a very strong vacuum. The residual pressure does not exceed $10^{-} 8$ Pa, which is one hundred billion times less than atmospheric pressure. At the same time, there are still 200 million molecules left in $1 см^{3}$, but their effect on flying protons can already be neglected. To maintain the required vacuum, an entire “army” of pumps must operate continuously. But the main task, of course, is to ensure the circular motion of protons. Only a magnetic field can do this. At the enormous speeds of protons, even for such a large circle, the magnetic field induction should be 15 Tesla. There are 1624 solenoids along the flight path, creating the main magnetic field that ensures the circular trajectory of particles, as well as fields for correcting and focusing protons. Protons do not fly in a continuous stream, but in the form of bunches, each of which contains 100 million particles. Such a clot looks like a needle about 5 cm long and hundredths of a millimeter thick. At individual points of the trajectory, the acceleration of the bunches by a rapidly alternating electric field continues. There are 2808 clots on the flight path at the same time. In order to create a magnetic field of the required intensity, the current in the windings of the solenoids must have a value of 11000 A. With such a current, under normal conditions, any conductor almost instantly melts and evaporates. To prevent this from happening, the resistance of the solenoid windings must be not just small, but strictly zero, i.e. the windings must be in a superconducting state. This can only happen at very low temperatures. Therefore, the temperatures of the windings and the entire tunnel do not exceed 1.9 K. Maintaining this temperature is achieved by three-stage cooling - first with liquid argon, then with liquid nitrogen and, finally, with liquid helium. Since the heat of the Earth tends to penetrate into the tunnel, it is necessary to constantly pump liquid coolant gases through the cooling cylinders. The LHC consumes 30,000 liters of liquid nitrogen per day (several heavy-duty tanks) and 3,000 liters of liquid helium (medium-sized tank). Of no small importance is the accuracy of fit of all metal parts of the structure. It should not exceed a few thousandths of a millimeter, because the “meeting place” of protons “cannot be changed,” and the meeting needles are very thin. It is interesting that in this case it is necessary to take into account even such an unusual circumstance as a tidal wave in the earth's crust. On a full moon, the amplitude of this wave where the LHC is located is 25 cm. And although the tidal wave is very smooth, it slightly deforms the tunnel, and this must be taken into account. Continuous instrument monitoring of the particle flight path and its correction are required. There are 4 meeting points for proton beams and 4 measurement laboratories on the flight route.
