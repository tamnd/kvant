M2309. On the board 8 ¥ 8 there are 8 rooks that do not attack each other. All squares of the board are distributed into the possession of these rooks according to the following rule. The square on which the rook stands is given to that rook. A square captured by two rooks is taken by the rook closest to that square; if these two rooks are equidistant from the square, then each of them receives half a square. Prove that the areas of possession of all rooks are the same.

Rook A captures only 15 squares (on figure - pink) - in its vertical and its horizontal. Let's consider another cell C in the same rank in which A is located. It is beaten by exactly one more rook B, located in the same rank with it. The same rook captures one square D, located in the same vertical as A. Then A, B, C, D are the corner cells of the checkered rectangle. If this rectangle is a square, rooks A and B will each get half of squares C and D. If it is not a square, then one of squares C and D will go to rook A, and the other to rook B. From here it is clear that out of 15 pink squares, rook A will get 8 squares in total: the one on which it stands and half of the remaining 14 squares (the remaining rooks will “take” one of the remaining 7 pink squares). Likewise, each rook will have 8 squares. L. Mednikov, A. Semenov, I. Rubanov, A. Shapovalov
