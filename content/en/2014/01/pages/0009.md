Amazing Properties of Electrons

A. SERGEEV

The main thing for them is to come up with a name. Until I came up with it - it’s a pity to look at him, he’s a fool. Well, when he comes up with some kind of gravity concentrate, it’s as if everything becomes clear to him, and life immediately becomes easier for him. Arkady and Boris Strugatsky. Roadside Picnic

A SEVERAL YEARS AGO, THEORETICAL PHYSICISTS predicted the existence of unusual substances – topological insulators. A little later they were discovered experimentally. These materials have a whole range of amazing properties, a detailed description of which would take dozens of pages. Therefore, today we will talk about only one, but in a sense the main, property that has become the hallmark of topological insulators: in the thickness of a substance, a topological insulator is a dielectric, and its surface conducts electric current. In other words, electrons move along the surface of a dielectric as freely as in a metal. The reason for such strange behavior of electrons is the prohibition of reflection back. This prohibition, in turn, has deep mathematical premises (it is better not to wonder where the mathematical premises themselves come from). In this article we will try to figure out why an electron sliding along the surface of a topological insulator, having encountered an obstacle, cannot rebound in the opposite direction. To do this, let's look at several loosely related stories from different areas of physics and mathematics. Look closely at each piece of this puzzle and you will see how they fit together to form a complete picture. For experiments - where would we be without them? – you will need a sheet of paper, scissors, a pencil, a globe without a stand, a mug with water and an imaginary balloon.

As well as the fact that the plane of oscillation of a Foucault pendulum will not rotate at any point on the globe - at the equator, for example, this will not happen. Do you want to know how to explain the rotation of the plane of oscillation of a Foucault pendulum without a single formula and, moreover, study the angular dependence of the speed of this rotation on geographic latitude? Then make a cut along the radius and bend a certain sector of the circle. Now close the edges of the empty sector - you will get a cone, along the edge of which there are parallel vectors. However, no, now they are not so parallel! When walking along the base of the cone, the vector rotates. It is easy to see that the angle of rotation is equal to the angle of the empty sector of the cone scan. Now, if the piece of paper was large enough or if your globe is small enough, you can put the cone on the globe so that it touches the globe along some parallel (see Fig. 1 on the right). The angle of rotation of the vector drawn on the cone will be equal to (minus $2\pi )$ the angle by which the plane of the Foucault pendulum located on this parallel will rotate during the day. On another parallel, the angle at the vertex of the tangent cone will be different, which means the angle of rotation will also change.
