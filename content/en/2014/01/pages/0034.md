inertia

It’s even strange why this concept, denoting one of the basic properties of matter, has not yet become the main character of our issues. Let’s restore justice and give her the right to a “solo performance.” Inertia surprisingly combines apparent simplicity and intricate complexity. It manifests itself both in nature and in the creations of our hands. Sometimes it can be a hindrance, but often it is definitely useful. So, for example, the well-known top and spinning top, it turns out, behave like sophisticated gyroscopic devices that serve to stabilize the movement and orientation in space of many vehicles. Drivers often resort to “coasting” driving, accelerating and then spending the accumulated energy for a long time, in common parlance – “inertia”. Children's cars with a handwheel and beetle lanterns that shine by pressing the handle are prototypes of energy storage devices that use rotational inertia. It would seem that what is common between a dog shaking off water and inertial thermonuclear fusion? Or between the elementary falling of a stone to the ground and the fundamental properties of space and time? This is all inertia... Let’s add a few more stories to the collection of our ideas about it in the hope that we will be able to amend the direct translation of the Latin word “inertia,” meaning “lethargy, immobility, inaction.”

Questions and tasks

1. Why can you use a hammer to break a brick in your palm without feeling any pain from the impact? 2. Why, wanting to shake off the mercury in a medical thermometer, do we make a sharp downward movement with our hand and immediately stop our hand?

3. Why does a ruler suspended on paper rings break when hit hard, but the rings remain intact? 4. Which car, loaded or empty, has a longer braking distance at the same speed? Assume the coefficient of friction is the same; air resistance can be neglected. 5. Why does the coupling of the train cars sometimes break when the electric locomotive suddenly moves away? In which part of the train is the rupture most likely to occur? How to avoid it? 6. A heavy body is suspended by a spring from the ceiling of the elevator car. What will be the motion of the body relative to the cabin if it suddenly begins to fall freely under the influence of gravity? 7. a) A uniform block hangs on a thread, and the thread is cut at some point. b) The same block lies on a horizontal stand, and the stand is suddenly removed. Determine in both cases which particles of the block will have greater acceleration at the initial moment of time: those located in the upper section of the block or in the lower one. 8. Does the car settle on the right or left springs when turning left? 11. The plane makes a “dead loop”.
