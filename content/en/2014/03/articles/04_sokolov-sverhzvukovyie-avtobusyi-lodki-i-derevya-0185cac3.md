ARTICLE “SUPERSONIC AIRCRAFT AND MAX CONE”

(«Kvant» No. 3, 2010) it was shown how useful it is to use the concept of the Mach cone when studying the kinematics of supersonic aircraft. Let us recall that this is a cone whose angle $\alpha$ at the apex is determined by the relation sin $\alpha = c v$, where the numerator and denominator are the speeds of sound and the aircraft, respectively. Mach cones are very real formations that accompany fast moving objects: jets, bullets and shells, meteorites. They appear as air compactions that can be seen in photographs. The first photograph of this kind was taken by Ernst Mach himself (interestingly, he had to construct a special camera for this). Mach cones can be seen in everyday life (Fig.1), you just need to look carefully. They are especially visible in shallow water. For example, after rain, wide but small streams of water run through the streets. If a stone gets in their way, the water flowing around it forms two lines of elevation on the surface of the stream - the Mach cone. And sometimes a wave will run onto the sandy shore and as it retreats, each shell will form its own little Mach cone. “And I saw a crow walking through a shallow puddle, looking for a place to drink, and the waves from its paws formed a Mach cone. “And I saw how drops of water falling from the oar formed Mach cones on the surface of the water. – These are very good examples. I will add that you can also simply drag a twig across the surface of the puddle, and this will also form a Mach cone. – And the fact that the tops of the trees have a conical shape, are these also Mach cones? – If botanists confirm that the shoots grow at a constant speed, then yes. But in problems, Mach cones are encountered not only during the movement of supersonic aircraft, meteorites and bullets, but also during the movement of ordinary buses or boats.
