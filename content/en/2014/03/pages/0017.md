The first is the Pontecorvo hypothesis about neutrino oscillations, which I will talk about now, and the second is the Majorana hypothesis. So, now I want to say a few words about Bruno Pontecorvo and his 1957 hypothesis. This is the oscillation hypothesis, it was published again in a preprint, only now in Dubna. Remembering the work of Majorana (Pontecorvo was to some extent his student), he kept thinking about whether neutrinos and antineutrinos are the same thing or not? If they are one and the same thing, then the same processes are obtained; if they are different things, then different processes are obtained. And so, thinking about this, Bruno Pontecorvo formulated this very interesting hypothesis. In the conclusion of his article, he writes that if you do not have neutrinos and antineutrinos, but there is a violation of the conservation of the lepton charge (i.e., if the neutrino charge is not conserved), then it is possible for the neutrino to transform into an antineutrino in the process of motion, $( \nu$ ´ $\nu ɶ ),$ and then processes similar to those that were discovered in $K^{0} -$ mesons will occur. And in $K^{0}$ -mesons at this time oscillations were discovered, when $K^{0}$ goes into anti- $K^{0}$ and anti- $K^{0}$ goes into $K^{0}$. And Pontecorvo even wrote that, apparently, this process can very likely be discovered, at least in astrophysics, on an astrophysical scale. Moreover, he even gave an estimate of the length of these oscillations. The average energy of neutrinos, which, for example, is emitted by the Sun, is 1 MeV, and then the length of the oscillations is about $10^{3}$ km. But today the oscillations are open, and their magnitude is about 100–200 km. In other words, the neutrino mass is not zero (Pontecorvo assumed a value of $10^{-} 11$ eV for the neutrino mass). Thus, if everyone at that time was carried away by Weyl and believed that the neutrino mass was zero and there was nothing to talk about, then Pontecorvo expressed the following hypothesis. This time, approximately from 1956 to 1966, was a “theoretical” time, a time of discussions, a lot of things happened there, a lot of all sorts of ideas were expressed. After this, the theorists fell silent and experimenters began to work. Over the next 20 years, experimenters prove that the Standard Model actually works. Three main directions in neutrino physics are emerging - now we have learned to work with neutrinos. Firstly, low-energy neutrinos are mainly reactor neutrinos or beta decay. Research is underway on the properties of neutrinos, estimates are being made on the neutrino mass, neutrino polarization, and evidence is being sought that the neutrino is not equal to the antineutrino. And finally, the so-called neutral reaction channel was discovered, when a neutrino does not produce an electron in the reverse process, but simply scatters. It dissipates and that's it. Moreover, it can scatter on nucleons - well, on a deuteron, for example, and on electrons. At the same time, it turned out that in such processes physicists are forced to work with absolutely extremely small cross sections: $10^{-} 43 см^{2}$ is still good. Now they are working on the verge of $10^{-} 46 см^{2}$. Fantastically small values ​​- we cannot measure anything lower. But it was then that it was realized. The second direction is high-energy neutrinos. It arose, in particular, due to the fact that physicists learned to work with accelerator beams. In beams of pi-mesons, for example, neutrinos are born from the decay of pions, and these neutrinos have an energy that is already much higher - not on the order of 1 MeV, as in reactor physics, but on the order of 1 GeV and even higher. And the cross section for the interaction of neutrinos with nucleons, and in general for all weak processes in this region, is proportional to the square of the energy of the incident neutrino. If so, then you can now, by taking neutrino beams of sufficiently high energies, reach large cross sections. Indeed, this direction has developed, new methods of recording neutrinos have appeared, in particular, all sorts of cameras, and it has been proven that the electron neutrino is not like the muon neutrino, that the muon neutrino is not like its antineutrino, and that there is a third type of neutrino, the so-called tau neutrino, from the decay of the tau meson. Thus, three families of neutrinos appeared. The processes of neutrino scattering on electrons and on nucleons were investigated, and in parallel with this, new quarks, c, b, t, were discovered, and a certain analogy was found between neutrinos and quarks. There are three types of neutrinos, and there are three families of quarks.
