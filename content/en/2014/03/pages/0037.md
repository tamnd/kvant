We would like to immediately warn the reader about this. In calm water (on a lake), where we direct the boat (row), that’s where it moves. But on the river these are already different directions. figure 7 shows what happens on the river with a boat in one second. We row (steer the boat) at an angle $\beta$ to the shore and move relative to the water at a distance c. But besides this, there is a current, and it carries us a distance v in a second. As a result, we move relative to the shore at a completely different angle $\alpha$ and with a completely different speed u.

Here are a few problems associated with crossing the river. Problem 5. In what minimum time can our boat (see Fig.7) cross the river? What course (angle $\beta )$ should the rower choose? What distance d will it drift along the shore? What is the path s of the boat relative to the shore? Discussion. We think that overly self-confident guys have personal experience in this situation, and they will immediately guess how to drive the boat so that the crossing time is minimal. “Overconfident” we call those guys who, having barely learned to swim in a pond or pool, begin to conquer real rivers with a fast current. It’s good if the river is small and there is enough strength. But if things take a serious turn: the strength is running out, and the opposite bank is still far away? This is where we find ourselves in a situation where we need to get to the other side as quickly as possible. How should we behave? Of course, keep the course $\beta = 90 ^\circ$. And the main thing is that every stroke brings us closer to the desired shore. So, together with you we have answered the most important question of the task. We suggest you get the rest of the answers yourself.

And now - a more difficult task. Problem 6. The boatman needs to cross the river so that the drift of the boat is minimal. What course (angle $\beta )$ should he choose for this? What angle (angle $\alpha )$ is formed by the trajectory of the boat with the shore? How much time (t) will the boatman need for such a crossing? How far (d) will the boat drift? What path (s) will the boat take relative to the shore? In this situation, personal experience is unlikely to help us. Even if someone crossed the river, trying to swim with with the least drift, it is not a fact that he has found the correct course. Therefore, when solving this problem, it is better to carry out reasoning when figure is immediately visible. - Is there such a miracle - figure? - And its main element is the Mach cone. And not from the speed c, but from the speed v (Fig.8). In a second, the river will move the boat to the right at a distance v. After that, we ourselves must move the boat at a distance c, and we can direct it in any direction. Therefore, we draw a semicircle with radius c. And the speed of the boat relative to the shore is a vector connecting point O and the point we have chosen on the circle.

Problem 7. When crossing, the boatman drove the boat in such a way as to cross the river as quickly as possible. And he returned back so that the demolition of the boat was minimal. It is known that he spent twice as much time on the return journey. Find how many times the speed of the current is greater than the speed of the boat. How many times longer is the boat's journey there than the journey there? Problem 8. Usually a boatman crosses a river in 12 minutes. And when he chooses a new course for crossing, making an angle $90^\circ$ with respect to the first one, he spends only 9 minutes on the crossing. 1) How much time will the boatman spend crossing if he keeps his course perpendicular to the coastline? 2) What is the ratio of the speed of the current to the speed of the boat, if both with the old and new course the boatman is carried away the same distance? Problem 9. Two boatmen choose different courses for their boats, but cross the river in the same time $t_{0}$. In this case, when crossing, the second boat is carried n times further than the first. 1) In what minimum time could the boatmen cross the river if the speed of the current is M times the speed of the boat? 2) At what maximum M is this possible? The speeds of both boats in calm water are the same.
