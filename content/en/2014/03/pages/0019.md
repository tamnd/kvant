Here I want to draw your attention to a very interesting analogy between the Newtonian theory, which everyone knows, and the Standard Model. In the Standard Model you have massless particles, to the zeroth approximation. Massless particles are the thing that describes geometry. No physics is needed to describe massless particles. Pure geometry, from the point of view of modern higher geometry. In the same way, in Newtonian theory you have inertial motion - if you have no interaction, everything is turned off, then the particles will move by inertia in straight lines. But straight lines are again pure geometry. Both theories are based on pure geometry. And then physics comes into play. In Newtonian mechanics, forces are turned on, and all particles begin to move along certain trajectories, i.e. the geometry begins to break. In the same way, in elementary particle physics, gauge interactions, the Higgs mechanism, are turned on, and everything begins for you, physics arises. Physics differs from mathematics in that it involves symmetry breaking. These are the little thoughts... Well, now we move on, we take a step, to modern theory, to modern physics. But another path is possible - and, most likely, Nature has taken this path: what if neutrinos (precisely neutrinos in this case) have non-standard properties? For example, there is a Majorana neutrino, i.e. neutrino is equal to antineutrino. Or you have oscillations - the transition of electron and muon neutrinos into tauneutrinos. And so on, i.e. the second path is the path associated with the non-standard nature of the neutrino. And how do experiments begin to answer this? It must be said that a supernova explosion turned out to be a very interesting moment associated with the development of the experiment. In 1987, especially for physicists, a supernova exploded in the Large Magellanic Cloud. On Soviet Army Day, February 23. It is for this reason that Russian physicists did not all register this explosion. For example, in Krasnoyarsk underground there was a day off in honor of February 23rd. And for the first time in the history of mankind, a supernova explosion was observed not only in the light region, but also in the neutrino region. Namely, approximately two dozen neutrino bursts and neutrino traces were recorded in the five main detectors that were operating on the ground at that time. But even here a very funny situation arose, another neutrino mystery. Everyone recorded these outbreaks at approximately the same time. Although, I must say, a fantastic situation arose - the Japanese(!) watches broke. And they didn’t just break - there was a difference for a minute somewhere, although all flashes are counted in seconds. But, in the end, we agreed, moved the Japanese clock to the right place, and everything seemed to come together. So, five hours before this flash - this was recorded by five detectors - four more events were recorded. This is a mystery that still exists, although recently we are beginning to unravel it. Nobody simply believed it for all of twenty years. Next are reactor neutrinos. By this time, the interactions of neutrinos with protons, with deuterons, with electrons were measured, the magnetic moment of neutrinos was measured, and they began to look for neutrino oscillations in reactor neutrinos. And finally, a very interesting thing was invented. It turns out that with the help of neutrinos you can diagnose the operation of reactors - place detectors at a distance of 100 m and watch how the reactors operate. In Iran, for example. And report to the IAEA whether today they are producing plutonium or not. This is called neutrino diagnostics. Unfortunately, it has not yet been implemented, but in principle it exists, it is possible. Next are solar neutrinos. The first experiments, as I already said, were Davis's experiments.
