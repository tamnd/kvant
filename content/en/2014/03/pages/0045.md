MATHEMATICAL

i What the next question is and who it is asked of may depend on the answer to the previous question. i You cannot ask paradoxical questions that can be answered with both “yes” and “no” or cannot be answered at all. For example, “Are you going to answer ‘ya’ now?” i The sly man answers questions completely arbitrarily. We can assume that every time he mentally tosses a coin and if it comes up heads, he answers the truth, and if it comes up tails, he lies. Let's add to this that, of course, Mr. A, B and C know each other and know which of them is a liar, which is a knight, and which is a cunning one. Before presenting the solution, let us introduce two mental constructs.

Mr. Ya, Mr. Wa and Mr. Hee Above, we called a knight a man who always tells the truth. In other words, we can say this: a knight is a person who answers “yes” if the statement formulated in the question is true. At the same time, a liar is someone who answers “no” to such a statement. Let us remember that the islanders use their own language. Thus, to a question that states a true statement, both the knave and the knight answer either always “ya” or always “va”. The one who answers “Ya” will be given a middle name – Mr. Ya. And the one who always answers “va” will be called Mr. Va. Finally, we will call the cunning man Mr. He. Note that we cannot know about Mr. Ya whether he is a knight or a liar, but the islanders A, B and C themselves must know which is which. Therefore we can use new names in questions. For example, we can ask Mr. A, “Are you Mr. Ya?” or ask him: “Mr. B is Mr. Va?”

Deck of cards Now take three identical pieces of paper and write “Mr. Ya” on the first one, “Mr. Wa” on the second one, and “Mr. Hee” on the third one. We will receive three cards - the Ya card, the Wa card and the Hi card.

There is a natural correspondence between people and cards. Thus, if the concept of “Mr. A's card” comes up in a question, the islanders should understand it this way: they need to look up who Mr. A is - Mr. Ya, Wa or Hi - and find a card with the corresponding name written on it. The same should be done if the question contains the concept of “your card”. Now let’s put these three cards in a deck: the Ya card on top, then the Wa card and the bottom one – the Hi card. If we don't have paper and can't make such a deck, we have to ask the islanders to do a thought experiment: imagine these three cards in that order. Why was this order of cards chosen? Solution to problem 3

First question

Let's ask Mr. A the following: "Is your card in the deck on Mr. B's card?" Let us prove that if the answer is “ya” to this question, Mr. B is not a cunning person (he is a knight or a liar), and if the answer is “va” to this question, Mr. B is not He. Note that if A is Mr. Xi, then neither B nor C can be Xi, which is what we need. Let's assume that Mr. A is not He and consider his options for answering the question. Let A answer "ya". Option 1: Mr. A is Mr. Ya. Then the statement in the question is true, and Mr. A's card is Y's card. Since in the deck the Ya card lies on the Wa card, then Mr. B is Mr. Va. Option 2: Mr. A is Mr. Ba. Since Mr. Wa answered “yah,” then the statement “Mr. A’s card is on Mr. B’s card” is false. This means that the Wa card does not lie on Mr. B's card. By definition of the deck, the Wa card lies on the Chi card. So Mr. B is not He. Let A answer "va". Option 1: Mr. A is Mr. Ya. The statement in the question is false. The Ya card lies in the deck on the Wa card and does not lie on the Chi card. So Mr. B is He and Mr. C is Mr. Wa. Option 2: Mr. A is Mr. Ba. The statement is true. The Wa card is in the deck on the Chi card. So Mr. B is He, and Mr. C is Mr. Ya.

Second question

With the first question, we identified from Mr. B and Mr. C the one who is definitely not a cunning person. The second question needs to be asked specifically to this islander, who is not a cunning one. In both cases, the question will be the same and will be very similar to the previous one: “Is your card in the deck on Mr. A’s card?” As we saw above, the answer to this question shows us among the two islanders (those to whom the current question is not addressed) one who is definitely not Mr. He. To be specific, consider the case when the question is asked by B. Then either A or B is not Xi. Let B. Then Hee is Mr. A. Similarly, in all possible cases we recognize who Mr. Hee is.

Third question

So, with the previous two questions we found Mr. He among the islanders. As in solving problem 2, let’s ask any of the two remaining inhabitants of the island the question “indicator” of knights and liars “Ya - is it Yes?” and thereby find out who is who.
