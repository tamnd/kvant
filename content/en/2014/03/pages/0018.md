This is the first time physicists have asked themselves this question. But in order to talk about it, I will remind you what the Standard Model is from today's point of view. The standard model of the electroweak interaction, which I will mainly talk about, is a unique thing from a mathematical point of view. From time to time in physics, models arise that combine mathematics and physics. And these are precisely the special points when some kind of explosion of our understanding really occurs. The standard model from a mathematical point of view is the so-called SU(2)–U(1) symmetry, which has been well studied by mathematicians for a long time, since the end of the last century. From the point of view of physics: there are leptons that form doublets of the left and singlets of the right $частиц.^{8}$ Accordingly - for the electron, muon and tau families. Here are the masses of these particles: 0.5 MeV for an electron, 105 MeV for a muon, 1777 MeV for a tau particle. Neutrinos exist only on the left; there are no right-handed neutrinos in the Standard Model. Do neutrinos have mass? This is the first question that arises. According to the Standard Model, at this level, there is no mass, zero, neutrinos are only left-handed, and there are no questions. Quarks have the same thing, there are quark doublets and there are singlets, there are right-handed ones and there are left-handed ones. There are u- and d-quarks, c- and s-quarks, t- and b-quarks. Here are the quark masses today: from 2–5 MeV for u- and d-quarks to 180 GeV for t-quarks. They opened not so long ago, by the way. It turns out (and this is the first question for the theory), some of these quarks, namely d, s, b, are not included in the theory by themselves, but are mixed. And there is a purely phenomenological, not yet explained, Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix that describes these mixings. Where did it come from, what is it? Question. But the following question arises: is there mixing of neutrinos? Further. All interactions in the Standard Model are very simple. There is a so-called gauge interaction and there are constants connecting various interactions. In the zeroth approximation, in theory, everything is massless, nothing is massive. Then the mechanism for the appearance of mass, the so-called Higgs mechanism, is activated. The source of mass is this Higgs boson, which is still not $открыт^{9}$, and then some of these particles, in particular bosons, receive masses. The gamma quantum remains massless, and the Z- and W- bosons receive a certain mass.
