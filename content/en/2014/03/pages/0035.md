It is curious that... ... Aristotle already guessed about the existence of the centrifugal force of inertia, and Ptolemy believed that if the Earth rotated around its axis, then because of the centrifugal force we would not be able to stay on its surface. Kepler and Galileo refuted this point of view, but believed that the centrifugal force should decrease with distance from the center of rotation. ... while studying the movement of a pendulum, Huygens established that if a massive body suspended on a thread moves in a circle, then the thread begins to stretch, as if by another force, additional to the force of gravity. He called it centrifugal tendency and cited the laws defining it, which have retained their significance to this day. Moreover, for Huygens it was not at all fictitious, but a completely real force of the same nature as the force of gravity. ...that in the process of falling onto the rotating Earth a body should shift to the east, Newton pointed out back in 1679 at a meeting of the Royal Society of London. The first Astronomer Royal, John Flamsteed, spoke at the discussion and argued that this effect had long been known in artillery. So, with an elevation angle of the gun of $87 ^\circ$, the fired cannonball falls back into the crater, while on a resting Earth this angle should be equal to $90 ^\circ$. ...perfectly accurate, rhythmically running clocks are called inertial. The course of all such clocks in all inertial frames of reference is the same - in the world there is a single, universal and universal time, called absolute. This position is the most important result of the classical mechanics of Galileo–Newton, accepted in it as an axiom. ...the great French scientist D'Alembert was a widely educated man - a mathematician, mechanic and educational philosopher. He was elected to all the Academies of Sciences existing at that time, including the St. Petersburg Academy. The famous principle given in the epigraph, which made it possible to reduce dynamic problems to static ones, was formulated by D’Alembert in 1743 in the Treatise on Dynamics, when he was only 26 years old. ...in non-inertial reference systems, when taking into account the forces of inertia, Newton’s third law cannot be applied to them, since there are no counterforces applied to another body by the given one, and there is no “other” body itself. Also, in these systems, the consequences of the third law, including the law of conservation of momentum, are also unfair. ...disputes about the truth or fictitiousness of inertial forces continued for more than two hundred years. Indeed, when moving to an inertial reference frame, you can simply get rid of them. On the other hand, they cause very real events - ruptured flywheels, derailments and train crashes, and so on. ...the Austrian physicist and philosopher Ernst Mach considered the Universe rotating around the observer to be the source of the origin of inertial forces. With this approach, the inertial mass of any body was due to the influence of all bodies in the surrounding world. This point of view, called Mach's principle, was initially shared by Einstein, but later abandoned it. ...in nature, the effect of inertial forces is especially pronounced during the movement of huge masses of water and air in the ocean and in the atmosphere. Thus, the Gulf Stream, moving north in the northern hemisphere, is deviated to the right under the influence of the Coriolis force of inertia, depleting Canada of heat and warming Europe. The appearance of giant cyclonic vortices, spinning counterclockwise in the northern hemisphere and clockwise in the southern hemisphere, owes its appearance to the same force. ...without inertial navigation, both the flights of spaceships and airliners, and the navigation of surface and especially submarine fleet vessels are unthinkable today. One of the devices used in inertial navigation is the most sensitive accelerometer, which allows one to measure the acceleration of moving objects with an accuracy of $10 -7 g$.
