And we form... a Mach cone (Fig.4) with an opening angle $\alpha =$ arcsin ( c v ), where c is the speed of the mushroom picker, and v is the speed of the bus. At each point inside the cone there will be two mushroom pickers, at the very edge there will be one, and beyond the edge there will be no one. The Mach cone will be the area from which the traveler can catch the bus. Prove? Please. Any traveler located inside or on the edge of the Mach cone can catch the bus. To do this, he just needs to go in the direction opposite to the one in which one of the mushroom pickers came to a given point. Moreover, the traveler will come out onto the road just as the bus is approaching. In the same way, it is not difficult to prove that not a single traveler standing outside the Mach cone we have constructed will never catch the bus (conduct this proof yourself). So, the answer to our problem is this: the reachable region of the bus is the Mach cone with the vertex at the bus and the angle at the vertex $2 \alpha = 2$ arcsin ( c v ). Each traveler located inside this cone has a choice - he has two paths along which he can enter the road exactly as the bus approaches, and he can also choose any path lying between them. True, in this case he will have to wait for the bus for some time. For a traveler standing on the generatrix of the cone, there is only one option for returning - he must go perpendicular to this generatrix.
