⟦folio 18⟧

KVANT 2014/№3

Solar neutrinos were studied using the chlorine-argon method, then the large Kamiokanda detector was created in Japan, and it was proven that there is a deficit of neutrinos from the Sun. This deficiency was later confirmed in our experiments at Baksan, at the Super-Kamiokand detector, and, finally, at the Solar Neutrino Observatory in Canada. Next are accelerator neutrinos. Indeed, they began to work with high energies in accelerators, and an effect was discovered that still remains unclear. In America, at the Los Alamos accelerator, unusual neutrino oscillations were recorded. Whether they exist or not, we still don’t know. Maybe it's a mistake, maybe it's not. Finally, in accelerator neutrinos at large distances they also work with oscillations; today this is already becoming trivial. Next are atmospheric neutrinos. Neutrinos that are born in processes in cosmic rays can also be detected, and in 1998–99 oscillations of these neutrinos were found. So far this remains a mystery, although there is a lot of research on neutron beta decay. Either he fits into the Standard Model, then the next experiment is done and it turns out that there is a discrepancy of several $сигма^{11}$. And so it is until now. Until recently, we were confident that the lifetime of a neutron was measured with an accuracy of one second. And recently Anatoly Serebrov from Leningrad did an experiment and showed that he got a difference of 10 seconds. And, finally, the neutrino mass. Pontecorvo also proposed using tritium to measure the mass of neutrinos. Now these experiments are being carried out, and they allowed us to say that the mass of the electron neutrino that escapes from tritium is, in any case, less than 2 eV. Which is also not bad. I am finishing my story about the history of the development of our ideas about neutrinos. As you can see, this area has brought a lot of new and unexpected things, many key ideas for physics. I think that this is where we should expect surprises, new ideas and turning points in the development of physics in the future.
