Let points $I_{a}, I_{b}, I_{c}$ be the centers of excircles tangent to sides BC, CA, AB respectively (see figure). Since the lines $CI_{c}$ and $I_{a} I_{b}$ are the bisectors of the internal and external angles ACB, then the line $I_{c} C$ is the altitude in the triangle $I_{a} I_{b} I_{c}$. Likewise, the lines $I_{a} A$ and $I_{b} B$ are its heights. Therefore, triangle ABC is an orthotriangle (i.e., a triangle with vertices at the bases of the altitudes) of triangle $I_{a} I_{b} I_{c}$. Since point I is the orthocenter of triangle $I_{a} I_{b} I_{c}$, triangle ABC is also the orthotriangle of triangle $II_{a} I_{b}$. Note that the points $A, B, I_{a}, I_{b}$ lie on the same circle (with a diameter $I_{a} I_{b} ),$, therefore the triangles $II_{a} I_{b}$ and IAB are combined by a similarity transformation - symmetry with respect to the bisector of the angle AIB followed by homothety with the center I. Therefore, the circle (ABC) (circumscribed about the orthotriangle of the triangle $II_{a} I_{b} )$ and the circle circumscribed near the orthotriangle of the triangle AIB, are combined by the same similarity transformation. Hence, both their centers O and $O_{C}$, and the rays $IO_{C}$ and IO are translated into each other by the same transformation. Therefore, the point $E_{C}$ lies on the line IO. Similarly, the points $E_{A}$ and $E_{B}$ lie on the line IO. S.Ilyasov

$M2333^{*}$. A graph is a finite set of vertices, some pairs of vertices are connected by edges. A graph is said to be correctly colored in m colors if each of its vertices is assigned one of m given colors such that any two vertices connected by an edge are colored in different colors. The vertices of a certain graph are divided into three sets A, B, C so that the vertices from set A are not connected by edges to the vertices from set C. It is known that if all vertices of set C are removed from the graph (together with outgoing edges), then the resulting graph can be correctly colored in k colors, and if all vertices in set A are removed from it, then in n colors. What is the minimum number of colors that such a graph can be correctly colored with a guarantee?

"QUANTA"

⟦column⟧

Answer. k + n – 1. Let us show that in k + n – 1 color all vertices can always be correctly colored. Let's color B » C with n colors, call one of these colors the first, and let D be the set of all vertices from B colored with the first color. Now let's color A » D in k colors (at the same time we recolor the vertices from the set D), using the first color as one of them (and taking the remaining k – 1 colors different from the n colors already used). We keep the same coloring for vertices not included in A » D. Since no vertices from A » D are connected by edges with vertices from C colored in the first color (from D – due to the correct coloring of B » C in n colors, from A – by condition), we obtain a correct coloring of the graph in k + n – 1 color. Now we will show that fewer colors may not be enough. Let's arrange kn points in the form of a rectangle of n rows and k columns. Let's take this set to be B. Add a zero row of k points at the bottom, take it to be A, add a zero column to the left, take it to be C. Using edges in B, connect any two points that do not lie in any row or column. In A we connect every two points with edges, and in C too. In addition, we connect by edges each point from A with all points from B that do not lie in the same column with it, and each point in C with all points from B that do not lie in the same row. The correct colorings of A » B in k colors (in columns) and C » B in n colors (in rows) are obvious. Let us show that the resulting graph cannot be colored in less than k + n – 1 color. Let's say there is a regular coloring A » B » C in k + n – 2 colors. Since the points of set A are colored in k different colors, and the points of set C are colored in n different colors, then there are $m \ge 2$ colors (let’s call them common) in which the points of both of these sets are colored. Let us mark the rows and columns in which such points are located; at their intersection there are $m^{2}$ points of the set B. Note that each of the m common colors can be colored at most one of these $m^{2}$ points, namely, the point located at the intersection of the corresponding column and row. Therefore, among these $m^{2}$ points there are $m^{2} - m$ points painted in different colors. These cannot be common colors, but neither can they be other colors used in coloring the points of sets A and C, because points from the marked rows and columns are connected by edges to all points of such colors. In total, k + n – m colors were used to color the points from A and C. From the $m^{2} - m$ points mentioned above, m points can be selected, no two of which lie in the same row or column. Each two of these m points are connected by an edge, so they are colored in at least m colors. It turns out that at least k + n colors were used in total. Contradiction. I. Bogdanov
