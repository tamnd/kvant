OLYMPICS

XXXV Tournament of Cities

OBJECTIVES OF THE SPRING TOUR

Basic option

8–9 grades

1 (3). 100 numbers are given. When each of them was increased by 1, the sum of their squares did not change. Each number is increased by 1 again. Will the sum of squares change this time, and if so, by how much? A. Shapovalov

1

2 (4). Mom baked identical-looking pies: 7 with cabbage, 7 with meat and one with cherries, and laid them out in a circle on a round dish in that order. Then I put the dish in the microwave to heat it up. Olya knows how the pies were laid, but does not know how the dish turned. She wants to eat a cherry pie, but considers the rest to be tasteless. How can Ole surely achieve this after biting no more than three tasteless pies? A. Khachaturian

3 (4). See problem M2350 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

4 (5). On side BC of triangle ABC, a point L is chosen so that AL is twice the median CM. It turns out that angle ALC is equal to $45\infty$. Prove that AL and CM are perpendicular. R.Zhenodarov

5 (6). A queue lined up to cross the Bosphorus Strait: the first was Ali Baba, followed by 40 robbers. There is only one boat, two or three can sail in it (you cannot sail alone). Among those sailing in the boat there should not be people who are not friends with each other. Will they all be able to cross if every two people standing next to each other in the queue are friends, and Ali Baba is still friends with the robber standing one from him? A. Shapovalov

10–11 grades

1 (4). Cheburashka has a set of 36 stones with masses of 1 g, 2 g,..., 36 g, and Shapoklyak has superglue, with one drop of which you can glue two stones into one (accordingly, you can glue 3 stones with two drops, and so on). Shapoklyak wants to glue the stones together so that Cheburashka cannot choose one or more stones with a total mass of 37 g from the resulting set. What is the smallest number of drops of glue that will be enough for her to accomplish her plan? E. Bakaev

2 (4). In a convex quadrilateral ABCD, the diagonals are perpendicular. On sides AD and CD, points M and N are marked, respectively, so that angles ABN and CBM are right angles. Prove that lines AC and MN are parallel. A. Polyansky

3 (5). See task 5 for grades 8 – 9.

4 (5). The natural numbers a, b, c, d are pairwise coprime and satisfy the equality ab + cd = ac – 10bd. Prove that among them there are three numbers, one of which is equal to the sum of the other two. B.Frenkin

5 (5). Given a convex quadrilateral ABCD. Pedestrian Petya leaves vertex A, walks along side AB and then along the contour of the quadrangle. Pedestrian Vasya leaves vertex A at the same time as Petya, walks along the diagonal AC and simultaneously with Petya arrives at C. Pedestrian Tolya leaves vertex B at the moment when Petya passes it, walks along the diagonal BD and simultaneously with Petya arrives at D. The speeds of pedestrians are constant. Could Vasya and Tolya arrive at the intersection point of the diagonals O at the same time? B.Frenkin

Complex option

8–9 grades

1 (3). Santa Claus distributed 47 chocolates to the children, so that each girl received one more chocolate than each boy. Then Santa Claus distributed 74 jelly beans to the same children, so that each boy received one more jelly bean than each girl. How many children were there in total? E. Bakaev

2 (5). On the checkered board $5 \times 5$ Petya marks several squares. Vasya will win if he can cover all these cells with corners of three cells that do not overlap and do not extend beyond the border of the square (corners can only be placed “in cells”). What is the smallest number of squares that Petya must mark so that Vasya cannot win? A. Shapovalov

3 (6). A square tablecloth is placed on a square table such that no corner of the table is covered, but a triangular piece of tablecloth hangs from each side of the table. It is known that some two adjacent pieces are equal. Prove that the other two pieces are also equal. (The tablecloth does not overlap itself anywhere; its dimensions may differ from the dimensions of the table.) E. Bakaev

4 (7). The king called two wise men. He gave the first 100 blank cards and ordered him to write a natural number on each (the numbers are not necessarily different), without showing them to the second. Then the first can tell the second several different numbers, each of which is either written on some card, or equal to the sum of the numbers on some cards (without specifying how exactly each number was obtained). The second must determine which 100 numbers are written on the cards. If he fails to do this, both will have their heads cut off; otherwise, as many hairs will be torn out of everyone’s beard as the number of numbers the first told the second. How can the sages, without agreeing, stay alive and lose a minimum amount of hairs? I. Bogdanov

5 (7). Several white and several black dots are given. From each white dot there is an arrow to each black one,

6 (9). From cubes $1 \times 1 \times 1$ cube $3 \times 3 \times 3$ is glued. What is the largest number of cubes that can be thrown out of it so that a figure with the following two properties remains: i from the side of any face of the original cube, the figure looks like a square $3 \times 3$ (looking perpendicular to this face, we will not see a gap - 9 cubes of the figure are visible); i moving in a figure from cube to cube through their common face, is it possible to get from any cube to any other? A. Marachev

7 (9). There are 10 points marked on the circle, numbered clockwise: $A_{1}, A_{2},..., A_{10}$, and it is known that they can be divided into pairs that are symmetrical about the center of the circle. Initially, a grasshopper sits at each marked point. Every minute one of the grasshoppers jumps along the circle over its neighbor so that the distance between them does not change. At the same time, you cannot fly over other grasshoppers and get to the point where a grasshopper is already sitting. After some time, it turned out that some 9 grasshoppers were sitting at points $A_{1}, A_{2},..., A_{9}$, and the tenth grasshopper was sitting on the arc $A_{9} A_{10} A_{1}$. Can we say that he sits exactly at point $A_{10}$? E. Bakaev

10–11 grades

1 (3). Dunno boasts that he wrote several units in a row, put a “+” sign or a “$\times$” sign between each adjacent units, placed parentheses and received an expression whose value is 2014; Moreover, if in this expression we simultaneously replace all the “+” signs with “$\times$” signs and the “$\times$” signs with “+” signs, we will still get 2014. Could he be right? V. Kleptsyn

2. Is it true that any convex polygon can be cut along a straight line into two smaller polygons with equal perimeters and a) (4) equal longest sides; b) (4) equal smallest sides? A. Shapovalov

The second must determine which 100 numbers are written on the cards. If he fails to do this, both will have their heads cut off; otherwise, as many hairs will be torn out of everyone’s beard as the number of numbers the first told the second. How can the sages, without agreeing, stay alive and lose a minimum amount of hairs? I. Bogdanov

6 (9). Each city in a certain country is assigned an individual number. There is a list in which for each pair of numbers it is indicated whether the cities with these numbers are connected by railway or not. It turned out that no matter how you take two numbers M and N from the list, you can renumber the cities in such a way that the city with number M will receive number N, but the list will still be correct. Is it true that if you take two numbers M and N from the list, you can renumber the cities in such a way that the city with number M will receive number N, the city with number N will receive number M, but the list will still be correct? A. Pakharev, M. Skopenkov, A. Ustinov

7 (10). The polynomial P ( x ) satisfies the conditions: $P ( 0 ) = 1 ; 2 ( P ( x ) ) = 1 + x + x^{100} Q ( x )$, where Q ( x ) is a certain polynomial. Prove that the coefficient of $x^{99}$ in the polynomial 100 ( P ( x ) + 1 ) is zero. D. Zvonkin

ORAL TOUR FOR 11TH CLASS

1. In the set {1, 2, 3,..., 2014} we chose a subset A. It turned out that no square trinomial, all three coefficients of which belong to A, has real roots. What is the greatest number of elements that A could have? G. Zhukov

2. Given triangle ABC. A ray drawn from vertex B through the midpoint of AC intersects the outer bisector of angle A at point P. Line PC intersects the line containing the inner bisector of angle A at point Q. Prove that BA = BQ. F.Ivlev

3. See problem M2349 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

4. In the isohedral tetrahedron ABCD, the points $A ', B ', C ', D '$ are the centers of excentric spheres. Prove that A, B, C, D are the centers of the excentric spheres of the tetrahedron $A ' B ' C ' D '$. (A tetrahedron is called isohedral if its faces are equal triangles. An excircle sphere is a sphere that touches one of the faces and the extensions of the remaining faces.) A. Zaslavsky

5. In a white checkered rectangle, the sides of which are more than 10, K cells are painted black. Next, during a move, choose a row (horizontal or vertical) in which there are at least 10 black cells, and paint all the white cells in this row black. After several such moves, all the cells became black. Prove that $K \ge 100$. P. Kozhevnikov

6. Prove that there is no polynomial in two variables P ( x, y ) for which the set of solutions to the inequality P ( x, y ) > 0 is the quadrant { ( x, y ): x > 0, y > 0 }. L.Stunzhas
