Now we present the solution problem M2339 of the Problem Book «Kvant». Problem 7 (V. Mokin, problem M2339, generalization problem M1295). Given a board $m \times n$, divided into single cells. First, a chip is placed in ( m - 1 ) ( n - 1 ) + 1 cells. Suppose that at some moment there were four cells on the board, the centers of which are the vertices of a rectangle with sides parallel to the edges of the board, such that exactly one of these cells contains a chip; then this chip can be removed. Prove that at least one chip cannot be removed from the board by performing only the operations described. Solution. Let's build a graph: the vertices are vertical and horizontal, there are m + n in total. An edge connects a vertical to a horizontal if there is no token at their intersection. Initially, mn - ( m - 1 ) ( n - 1 ) - 1 = = m + n - 2 cells are free, i.e. the number of edges is less than m + n – 1. According to the theorem, our graph is not connected. The cell at the intersection of the horizontal h and the vertical v will be denoted by (h, v), and if this cell is free, then (h, v) will also denote the edge from h to v. What happens when the chip is removed as described in the condition? Let the four centers of the cells be located at the vertices of the rectangle at the intersection of the horizontals p, q and the verticals r, s, and let a piece be removed from the cell (p, r). Then, by condition, the cells (p, s), (q, s) and (q, r) are free. This means that at the moment before the chip was removed, the graph had edges (p, s), (q, s) and (q, r). They formed a path from p to r, i.e. p and r were in the same connected component. By removing a token, we add an edge ( p, r ) to the graph, but this does not change the connected component. Therefore, the graph was and remains unconnected. Note now that on an empty board, each vertical is connected to each horizontal. Such a graph is connected: the horizontals in it are connected in a two-link way through any vertical, and vice versa. But it has already been proven that we cannot obtain a connected graph, so we cannot remove all the chips. The path to the solution. The constructed graph seems unusual. However, for mathematicians, a table of zeros and ones is a fairly familiar way of representing so-called bipartite graphs: in such graphs, the vertices are divided into two sorts (two parts), and edges can only connect vertices of different sorts.
