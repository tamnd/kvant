It turned out that if we look at the processes of interaction of matter with neutrinos, the cross section is at the level $10^{-} 43 - 10^{-} 46 см^{2}$. While the cross section for nuclear reactions is $10^{-} 24 - 10^{-} 28 см^{2}$. Well, it's easy to understand these estimates. If the size of a nucleus is $10^{-} 12$ cm, then the square of the size of this nucleus is $10^{-} 24 см^{2}$. Here is a normal nuclear cross section. And $10^{-} 28 см^{2}$ is a very small value. Now let's imagine what $10^{-} 42 см^{2}$ is. This means that the size of the region where neutrinos interact with other particles that carry out weak interaction is approximately $10^{-} 21$ cm. Today we estimate it a little differently, as $10^{-} 17 - 10^{-} 18$ cm, but still very small cross sections are obtained - you should hit a coin with a size of about $10^{-} 20$ cm with your flow. It is clear that even compared to nuclear cross sections, this is an incredible task. And this was the next problem that needed to be solved. But if so, then you will never be able to detect neutrinos. Having such small cross sections, the neutrino easily passes through the globe. Interacting once during the entire movement across the globe. Once! How will you record this? This is the problem physicists are faced with. To finish with this period, I will show the main participants - theorists who worked during this period. Dirac writes his equation in 1928 (the relativistic wave equation of the electron). By the way, the Dirac equation has one interesting limiting case: let’s put the mass equal to zero in the Dirac equation. As a result of the reduction, the Weyl equation is obtained. So, the most fantastic, the most mysterious and strange thing was that the Dirac equation, when you turn the mass to zero, can lead to P-parity violation. This case, the Weyl equation (i.e., the equation of motion for a massless particle with spin 1 2, a special case of the Dirac equation), was considered unphysical. They returned to him only after 20–25 years. It turns out that it was immediately possible to discover the unusual properties of neutrinos, please - Weyl wrote this equation. But they were so unusual that no one believed it. I will say more, there was an experiment in 1929, which actually repeated - well, did not repeat, but preceded the experiments of 1956-57, where the polarization of electrons was measured (i.e. mutual orientation of the spin and momentum of particles) emitted during beta decay. There they found that they were polarized, but no one could believe it. They put the experiment aside and said: this cannot be. That's all. This is such an interesting thing. I also want to tell you about double beta decay (i.e., the decay of a nucleus that is accompanied by an increase in the charge of the nucleus by two units and the emission of two electrons). Double beta decay was first proposed by Goeppert-Mayer in 1935. And only today we are starting to study it experimentally. You had to wait 70 years to start doing it. And the most interesting thing is that we are just beginning to study today – this is the Majorana hypothesis. The fact is that the neutrino as a Dirac particle (i.e., what was invented by Fermi by analogy with the Dirac equation) had normal properties: there was a neutrino, there was an antineutrino - just as an electron and a positron exist, there are neutrinos and antineutrinos. However, who told you that this is the only possibility? And so Majorana in 1937, literally a few months before his death, published a work in which he proposed the following: he takes the Dirac equation, makes some mathematical transformations and shows that it contains solutions in which a neutrino can be identical to an antineutrino. Then there is no electronic charge, or rather, it is zero. And such a neutrino can exist. This baton was picked up by Pontecorvo. Pontecorovo was the man who listened to Majorana in 1937. By the way, the fate of Majorana is still unknown; apparently he committed suicide just a few months after he wrote this article. This is the mystery we have been left with since 1937. It must be said that she was simply ignored at that time. Not only can neutrinos not be registered, but they also have some completely abnormal properties. So, from 1932–33 a completely new period began - the experimental period of studying beta decay. Until this time, nuclear physics did not yet exist, and all that physicists studied were radioactive nuclei that gave electrons - i.e. beta minus decays occurred (from our current point of view). And since 1932, after the neutron was discovered, the rapid development of nuclear physics began, many different nuclei were discovered, you can drive a neutron into nuclei and see what happens. If you drive an alpha particle inside a nucleus using a cyclotron, then you can find positive nuclei with an excess of protons and observe beta-plus decays.
