Nominal comment

Wolfgang Ernst Pauli (1900–1958) – Austrian physicist, introduced the concept of spin of an elementary particle, predicted the existence of neutrinos and formulated the “exclusion principle”. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1945. Niels Henrik David Bohr (1885–1962) – Danish physicist, one of the founders of modern physics (in particular, the creator of the first quantum theory of the atom and an active participant in the development of the foundations of quantum mechanics). Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1922. Antoine Henri Becquerel (1852–1908) - French physicist, one of the discoverers of radioactivity. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1903. Ernest Rutherford (1871–1937) – British physicist, “father” of nuclear physics, creator of the planetary model of the atom. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for 1908. Charles Drummond Ellis (1895–1980) – English physicist. James Chadwick (1891–1974) – English physicist, student of Rutherford; discovered the neutron. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1935. Lise Meitner (1878–1968) – Austrian physicist and radiochemist. Otto Hahn (1879–1968) – German chemist, “father of nuclear chemistry”, discovered the fission of uranium. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for 1944. Erwin Rudolf Joseph Alexander Schrödinger (1887–1961) was an Austrian theoretical physicist and one of the founders of quantum mechanics. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1933 (shared with Paul Dirac). Paul Adrien Maurice Dirac (1902–1984) - English physicist, one of the creators of quantum mechanics. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1933 (shared with Erwin Schrödinger). Werner Karl Heisenberg (1901–1976) – German physicist, one of the creators of quantum mechanics. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1932. Enrico Fermi (1901–1954) – Italian physicist, one of the founders of quantum physics. Winner of the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1938. Dmitry Vladimirovich Skobeltsyn (1892–1990) – Soviet experimental physicist, specialist in the field of cosmic radiation and high energy physics. Dmitry Dmitrievich Ivanenko (1904–1994) – Soviet physicist; work in the field of nuclear physics, unified field theory, gravitation theory, etc.
