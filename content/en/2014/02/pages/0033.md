Collisions of neutrons with atoms

S. VARLAMOV

"IN

DEPENDING ON THE INITIAL ENERGY OF NEUTRONS, they are divided into 5 groups: ultrafast, with an initial energy of 10–50 MeV; fast, with energy 100 keV - 10 MeV; intermediate, with energy 1–100 keV; slow, with energy less than 1 keV; thermal, having a thermal motion energy of the order of 0.025 eV. The main contribution to the ionization of matter is made by ultrafast, fast and intermediate neutrons. In most substances, neutrons have a high penetrating ability and, accordingly, a low ionizing effect. However, in biological tissues, where the content of hydrogen atoms is high, the penetrating ability of neutrons is low due to their interaction with protons. Therefore, in biological structures, fast neutrons are characterized by a high ionization density, which determines the extremely high susceptibility of living objects to neutron irradiation.” This text, taken from the Internet and slightly corrected, contains information about the ionizing ability of neutron radiation. It indicates the transition range of neutron energies (intermediate) - from effective in the sense of ionization of matter by these neutrons (fast and ultrafast) to ineffective in the sense of ionization (slow). It is known, in particular, that there is a special type of nuclear weapon - a “neutron bomb”, designed to destroy enemy personnel. At the same time, “non-living” matter under the influence of neutron radiation from such a bomb does not lose its properties. Why exactly is the range 1–100 keV transitional (intermediate)? To answer this question, let’s solve the following problem using school methods: A neutron approaches a stationary hydrogen atom. After a head-on collision of a neutron with a proton, the hydrogen atom becomes ionized. What was the kinetic energy of the neutron before the collision? A nuclear reaction did not take place during such an attack. There are two separate parts to this problem-task: a) the impact of nucleons; b) expansion of a proton and an electron. With the impact of nucleons (neutron and proton) during a failed nuclear reaction, everything is quite simple: it is an absolutely elastic impact of two bodies of almost the same mass. In this case, after the interaction, the proton can acquire recoil energy in the range from zero (glancing impact) to the energy that the neutron had before the impact (frontal impact).
