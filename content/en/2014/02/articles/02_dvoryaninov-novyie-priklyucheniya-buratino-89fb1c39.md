New adventures of Pinocchio

Geometric fairy tale

S. DVORYANINOV

WHEN BURATINO WAS VERY LITTLE, dad Carlo took him for walks. But Pinocchio grew up and began going to elementary school. And old Carlo just got a lot of orders for the manufacture of wooden furniture, his work increased, and his free time, accordingly, decreased. That’s when Pinocchio began to ask to be let go for a walk alone. – Papa Carlo, our town is surrounded on all four sides by such beautiful forests, groves and parks that look like forests! It's so wonderful there! Let me go there! And in order not to get lost, I will take a compass with me! “Well,” his father answered, “you’re not little, but I won’t give you a compass, but a GPS navigator.” Tomorrow, for starters, visit the Square Forest, and I will set up the navigator so that it shows you the direction perpendicular to the sides of this forest. And so Pinocchio went to the Square Forest after school. - Well, how was the walk? – Dad Carlo asked him. Did you like it? - Yes, of course, only I walked the same path from edge to edge twice, back and forth (Fig.1,a). Left the starting point S and returned to it. Can I choose a more interesting route? - Can. Go to the Quadrangle Forest tomorrow. And the navigator will show you directions along the diagonals of this quadrangle. Buratino came out of the city gates, immediately found himself at the edge of the forest and turned on the navigator. I reached one side of the quadrangle, then the other, and so on, all the time moving in parallel

A week later, Pinocchio went to the Triangular Forest. The name is clear: the forest had the shape of a triangle. – Papa Carlo, set the navigator so that I return to the starting point of my journey again. - asked Pinocchio. “I’m used to such travels, and the song says: but it’s so nice to return under the roof of your home!” – Okay, tomorrow the navigator will take you parallel to the sides of the Triangular Forest. Again Buratino came to the edge of the forest and hit the road. After two turns, he again found himself at the city wall, but not where he began his journey (Fig.1,c). “The navigator must have gone astray,” Buratino suggested. – It happens that technology fails. Well, I'll check and walk through the forest one more time. His new route consisted of three new segments parallel to the sides of the triangle. But then, at the end of the journey, his finishing point coincided with the starting point! - No, the navigator is working. A few more days passed, and Pinocchio went to the Properly Triangular Forest. This forest had the shape of a regular, in other words, an equilateral triangle. Now the navigator indicated the direction perpendicular to the side of the triangle. – And I will return to the starting point again? - Pinocchio asked in the morning. “Yes, but only for a special starting point,” answered Papa Carlo. – There are exactly two such points at the edge of the forest. They divide the edge into three equal parts. From one you must move clockwise, and from the other counterclockwise. After completing three straight sections, you will certainly return to the beginning of the path. Pinocchio did just that (Fig. 1, g). He walked along one three-link broken line (red) and another (blue). And one day he decided to find out what the path along the perpendiculars would be for an arbitrary starting point. And so, having put a piece of pie and a flask of water in your school bag, what if the journey turns out to be long? – he went on the road. At first there was nothing interesting. Having passed three segments (they are shown in blue on figure 2), he again found himself at the edge of the forest near the city wall. Here he, without stopping, continued moving, following the directions of the navigator. Now he has completed three more blue sections. The next three sections of his path are red. Every time he got to the edge, he marked in the sand the point at which he found himself. Eventually he got tired and returned home. - Papa Carlo, what would happen if I continued and continued my path along perpendiculars to the sides of an equilateral triangle? – Pinocchio asked after talking about his observations. – Would I end up on a closed triangular trajectory? How many laps would I take in the forest? “The answer is this, my young traveler and lover of geometry,” began Papa Carlo. – Speaking theoretically, it is impossible to get onto a triangular trajectory. Your path would consist of an infinite number of links. But the longer you walked, the closer the three successive links of your path would be located near the sides of the triangle that you passed, moving from a special point at the edge. Accordingly, at the edge of the forest you would find yourself closer and closer to a special starting point, dividing the length of the edge in a ratio of 1:2. - Very interesting! - Buratino exclaimed. – Can I prove this strictly mathematically? - Try! This requires knowledge of geometric progression. So the proof of what I told you can be put aside for now. And conducting a mathematical experiment on paper is not at all difficult.
