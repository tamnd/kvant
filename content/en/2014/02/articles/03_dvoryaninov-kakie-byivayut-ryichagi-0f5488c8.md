Tolya Vtulkin remembered this problem in the seventh grade, when they began to study levers (Fig.2) and blocks in physics. He especially liked, when answering a teacher’s question, to say: “A fixed block serves to change the direction of a force.” He pronounced the three words at the end of words with special pleasure, as if emphasizing them. Tolik felt the usefulness of changing the direction of force very well. It’s one thing to inflate a bicycle wheel with a hand pump, and another with a foot pump. Here you press the pedal with your foot, in fact you simply stand on it, and that’s it. Easy and convenient. And when using a stationary block, you just need to pull yourself up on it, like on a rope in the gym. Then another beautiful word appeared - polyspast (Fig.3). A pulley block is a system of movable and fixed blocks, which already results in a gain in strength.

Let's assume that the number of wheels is odd, for example 13. If the first wheel rotates clockwise, then the second one rotates counterclockwise, the third one rotates clockwise, the fourth one rotates counterclockwise, and so on. Each odd numbered wheel rotates clockwise. Thirteenth too. If it is connected to the first one, then the first one should rotate counterclockwise. But that's not true. Consequently, all the wheels of this system will not be able to rotate simultaneously. We can say that the rotation is hampered by an arithmetic obstacle, namely, the odd number n.
