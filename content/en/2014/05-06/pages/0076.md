Tasks

1. Many plants brought from distant countries are grown in household plots. And most of them survive safely in our conditions. It is clear that these plants have every chance of spreading and living independently of humans. But only a small part of them are represented in reliably decent numbers in the “wild” nature. What reasons may limit the spread of such foreign plants? If you can, use specific examples to support your points. 2. Explain whether the following are genetic clones: a) all apples from one apple tree; b) all potatoes from one bush; c) all seeds from one watermelon. To support your point, describe the biological processes that produce genetic diversity (or uniformity) in each case. 3. Describe the possible mechanisms of action of antidotes (substances that are introduced into the body to minimize the negative consequences of poisoning by a particular poison). 4. A routine blood test, which you do during periodic examinations at the clinic, includes determining a standard set of parameters. What diseases and physiological disorders can be identified based on this analysis? List for what diagnostic purposes it is necessary to determine additional characteristics of blood composition. What parameters should be measured in each of these cases? 5. How do you understand why the Nobel Prize in Biology and Medicine was awarded in the fall of 2013? State in your own words the essence of the discovery and its significance for the further development of medicine. 6. What determines the lifespan of certain human cells or other multicellular organisms? Explain what may necessitate their death and when it occurs. Try to offer as many answer options as possible.
