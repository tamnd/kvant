It's interesting that...

...long before the term “field” appeared, Newton introduced the idea of ​​the force inherent in a given place in space, called the “acceleration magnitude” of the force. ...the use of the term “gravitational acceleration” is often confusing because this value allows one to find the weight of a body that is suspended or lying motionless and does not experience acceleration at all. From the point of view of the “field approach”, this quantity can equally well be called the gravitational field strength. ...tidal accelerations on the Moon, caused by the gravitational field of the Earth, are approximately 20 times greater than tidal accelerations on Earth, generated by the Moon. ...for decades, debates have continued in science either about the existence of certain “agents” - intermediaries that transmit action “along the chain” from one body to another, or about the direct, immediate influence of one body on another through emptiness. Commitment to only one of these concepts - short-range action or long-range action - was often uncritically attributed even to such scientists as Newton and Faraday. ...the aspirations of scientists in the first half of the 19th century can serve as a herald for current attempts to unify physical interactions. Thus, Faraday, being “convinced that all the forces of nature are in mutual connection,” looked for a similar connection between electricity and gravity, between light and electricity. And the German scientist Wilhelm Weber created a popular theory that united the views of that time on electricity and magnetism. But the method successfully applied by Newton for gravity, when extended to electromagnetism, led to contradictions. ...an alternating magnetic field, as Faraday discovered, generates an electric one. Isn't there a reverse process in nature, Maxwell suggested. The hypothesis of an alternating electric field exciting a magnetic field and called “displacement current” by Maxwell was the last fundamental link in the construction of a quantitative theory of the electromagnetic field. For the first time, displacement currents were experimentally discovered by the Russian physicist Alexander Alexandrovich Eikhenwald in 1903. ...Maxwell's theory formed the basis of classical electrodynamics. According to it, the electromagnetic field, although different from physical bodies, has some of their properties. For example, it can be characterized by momentum and energy and propagate in space. ...the special theory of relativity created by Einstein most fully revealed the role of the reference system in electrodynamics. Electric and magnetic fields appear in it only as partial manifestations of a single electromagnetic field, depending on the choice of the reference system. ...in 2012, a new particle was discovered at the Large Hadron Collider in Geneva - the Higgs boson, indicating the existence of another field in nature. It fills all space and is responsible for the appearance of mass in elementary particles. The authors of the theory, who had been waiting for its confirmation for almost half a century, were awarded the Nobel Prize a year after the discovery of the new particle. ...the energy released during the combustion of one match is many times greater than the energy of gravitational field waves emitted during the same time by the entire solar system. However, this year, astrophysicists apparently managed, using equipment installed in Antarctica, to register evidence of the presence of these waves, generated by the expanding Universe in its very first moments.
