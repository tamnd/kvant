field actions

This selection of epigraphs is like a summary of the almost two-hundred-year history of the origin and formation of one of the fundamental physical concepts. But why does the title mention not just “fields”, but specifically “field actions”? There is more than one reason for this. The presence of a particular field can be determined primarily by how it influences – acts – on the body placed in it. The influence of gravity, electricity and magnetism is well known. These are the first things you come across and study in some detail in your school physics course. But they do not exhaust the physical fields discovered today and their properties. And if we move from the word “action” to “interaction,” then the concept of the field will expand and be filled with the idea of ​​it as an intermediary, a carrier, transporting forces from one body to another. It is then natural to wonder what might play a role in these carriers and whether there are commonalities between the different types of interactions. Further development of the field idea will lead us straight to modern science, where new ones will be added to the old ones - strong and weak interactions. And later – such very exotic characters as, for example, “quantized wave fields of elementary particles.” But let’s take our time and first discuss those types of fields that we have already dealt with, including in Kaleidoscope. However, if previously they performed separately, now they will gather together “on the same field.”
