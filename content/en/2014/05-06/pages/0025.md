FROM THE HISTORY OF SCIENCE

How I remember P. L. Kapitsa

Y.TSIPENYUK

ON THE 8TH OF JULY 2014, THE 120th anniversary of the birth of the outstanding scientist Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa was celebrated. I was lucky enough to work next to him for more than 20 years at the Institute of Physical Problems (IPP), which he created, which now bears his name. The Institute of Physical Problems is very small - it has about 50 researchers, and, having become an employee of the IPP, I, naturally, often met with Pyotr Leonidovich. Communication with him left a very vivid impression, since he was a wise man, his actions and judgments were always extraordinary. Pyotr Leonidovich does not have students in the usual sense, when the leader constantly discusses with the student methods for solving the problem, the results obtained and ways for further development of research. But in reality he has a lot of students - those who inherited the style of his work, the ability to critically analyze both their results and the results of other scientists, those for whom his active civic position became their own position. The portrait of Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa for me comes together like a puzzle from many episodes. Some of them are given below.

* * * I first appeared at the IPP in 1960, while still a fourth-year student at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT). The fact is that the training of senior students in research institutes was laid down by the creators of MIPT, one of whom was P.L. Kapitsa. He believed that in order to educate creative young researchers, it is necessary that they become involved in scientific work as early as possible under the guidance of scientists actively working in science. I ended up in the group of his son Sergei Petrovich, who was recruiting students to work on the electron accelerator he created - the microtron. At first, I dealt with the issues of measuring high-frequency fields in accelerating cavities, and as a thesis, S.P. Kapitsa suggested that I consider the question of how to study the recently discovered Mössbauer effect on a microtron. It took 3 months of studying this phenomenon, but I was unable to suggest any reasonable experiments. Sergei Petrovich called for help from Academician Yu.M. Kagan, who spoke categorically: “You won’t succeed.” A stalemate has created. Sergei Petrovich talked with his father, who said: “He did a good job in the fifth year, even if he defends it ahead of schedule. There was already a precedent with Andreev, a student of Landau. Why can theorists defend themselves ahead of schedule, but experimenters cannot?” That’s how I successfully defended myself, and they left me to work at the institute. * * * In 1960, Niels Bohr came to Moscow, and, naturally, he visited the Institute of Physical Problems, with whose director, P.L. Kapitsa, he had long years of friendship. There was a fear that many people would want to come to Bohr’s speech, and “face control” was installed at the entrance, i.e. Only familiar research assistants were allowed through. The conference room was, of course, filled, but there was no rush. When this became clear, we students were allowed to sit in the back rows. I will forever remember the kind smile on Niels Bohr’s face, Kapitsa’s sparkling wit, and the fantastically friendly atmosphere in the hall. Introducing Bohr to the audience, Pyotr Leonidovich said: “Every schoolchild knows that a Bohr atom is not a boron atom, but a hydrogen atom.” I especially remember one episode from this seminar. Kapitsa asked Bohr why young people from all over the world were so eager to work at his Institute for Theoretical Physics in Copenhagen. Evgeny Mikhailovich Livshits translated the entire discussion. As Lifshits translated, Bohr responded: “We are never afraid to tell a young man that he is talking nonsense.” And then the hall exploded, they heard: “Zhenya, you translated incorrectly.” Livshits asked Bohr again and, apologetically, corrected himself: “Nils said that they are never afraid of looking like fools.” Kapitza immediately reacted to this, saying that the difference between Bohr and Landau is that when the interlocutor is wrong, Landau says: “You are a fool,” and Bohr says: “This is amazing.”
