Spectral measurements confirmed theorists' predictions that at the moment the bomb exploded the temperature would be about 20 million degrees. Almost simultaneously, in countries where nuclear weapons were being created (USA, USSR and Great Britain), projects arose for a hydrogen bomb - a reservoir with a hydrogen isotope, inside of which there was a uranium or plutonium bomb. At first, such devices were so bulky that they could not be used as bombs. But on November 22, 1955, a hydrogen bomb with the equivalent of 1.6 megatons of TNT was dropped from an airplane and exploded at an altitude of 1000 m above the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site in the USSR. Soon similar bombs were made in the USA. And an arms race between the USSR and the USA began, enormous in its scale and terrible in its possible consequences. The culmination of this race was the test on October 30, 1961, when a “Tsar Bomb” with a TNT equivalent of 58 megatons was detonated at the Novaya Zemlya test site in the USSR.
