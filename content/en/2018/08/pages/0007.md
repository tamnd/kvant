Lev Andreevich Artsimovich gave her ideas for obtaining thermonuclear energy. This idea was to create a circular electric discharge in hydrogen, the thermal insulation of which would be provided by electrical repulsive forces. O. Lavrentyev’s letters were transferred to organizations involved in nuclear explosions, and eventually came to I.E. Tamm and A.D. Sakharov, who at that time were working on the creation of a hydrogen bomb. They organized the demobilization of O. Lavrentyev and his arrival in Moscow, his receipt of secondary education and admission to the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. But the very idea of ​​Lavrent-

Eva turned out to be insolvent. Electric fields could not ensure a stable state of a circular discharge. Lavrentyev himself was convinced of this. Subsequently, having received a physics education, he worked in scientific institutes, doing nuclear physics. Tamm and Sakharov used the rational grain of the Laurentian idea. In a hydrogen plasma, the electric current should indeed be ring-shaped, and the plasma itself should be a torus (steering wheel). But only a magnetic field of a special configuration could hold a circular current in space - the induction lines of this field should seem to wrap around a plasma torus. Scientists called this field toroidal. The implementation of this idea was associated with the solution of many scientific and engineering problems. And back in 1951, in the secret scientific institute created by the chief scientific director of the uranium problem in the USSR, I.V. Kurchatov, then called Laboratory No. 2 of the USSR Academy of Sciences or the Laboratory of Measuring Instruments, or simply LIPAN or “two,” a division appeared that began to implement the idea of ​​Tamm and Sakharov. Today, the “two” has turned into a huge National Research Center “Kurchatov Institute”, and the division in which work on thermonuclear fusion began has become the ITER design center, part of the Russian national agency for ITER and the state corporation Rosatom. In 1951, I.V. Kurchatov’s employees L.A. Artsimovich and I.N. Golovin, one of the main coordinators of the “brainstorming” of the problem, became the leaders of the work on the implementation of laboratory thermonuclear fusion. Since 1973, B.B. Kadomtsev became the head of the work, and since 1975, E.P. Velikhov.
