If, when the system deviates from the equilibrium position, a force appears that returns the system to its original state, then this equilibrium position is called stable. An example is a ball located in a hole (or hollow). To compress or stretch a spring, some work is required. This work results in an increase in the potential energy stored in the spring. A deformed spring tends to return to its original undeformed state, which corresponds to a minimum of potential energy. The undeformed state of the spring is stable because its stretching or compression causes the appearance of an elastic force, which tends to return the spring (and the system associated with it) to its original state. And now - the design task. figure 7 shows two tea jars. Their locks operate according to the principle outlined in our article. Where is the spring here? It turns out that the role of a spring is played by an elastic rubber gasket reinforced around the perimeter of the lid (at the same time, this gasket ensures the tightness of the can). When closing the jar, the lid is first pressed against it, and then slightly (about a millimeter) rises up. Both levers are so rigid that they practically do not deform. The gasket, being deformed and then freed from external influence, easily lifts the lid. If the gasket is removed, the system stops working.
