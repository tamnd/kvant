planetary physics

First, man learned to distinguish planets from stars, then he discovered the laws of their motion, but until the 20th century, he could only speculate about what they were, how similar or different they were from the Earth. All kinds of bold assumptions and wild fantasies appeared until, finally, the talent of scientists and engineers made it possible to create incredibly complex delivery vehicles and the instruments themselves, capable of transmitting reliable information to us from the vicinity of the planets, or even directly from their surface. Planetary physics - part of a whole complex of sciences called planetology - has made a giant leap in recent decades, greatly enriching our knowledge of the Solar System. Armed with this knowledge, people have already been able to visit the Moon, are preparing for a manned flight to Mars, and are assessing the possibilities of developing planetary satellites and asteroids. But most of all they hope to discover the presence of any forms of life on the planets closest to us. And, perhaps, on exoplanets orbiting stars other than the Sun... For now, let’s go on a mini-tour of the planets, hoping that it will serve as another introduction to the astronomy course that awaits you in the new academic year.
