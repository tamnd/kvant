PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses.

Problem M2498–M2501, F2505–F2508

M2498. There are 100 pairwise distinct positive numbers written on the board. For each pair of written numbers, their sum was calculated. What is the largest number of pairs that can have this sum among the numbers written on the board? Folklore (based on the Olympiads of the Czech Republic and Slovakia)

M2499. Is it true that any tetrahedron has a cross section that is an isosceles trapezoid? V. Letsko

$M2500^{*}$. The cells of the n ¥ n table are numbered (in random order) with the numbers 1, 2, …, $n^{2}$. Find the smallest natural number k with the following property: for any numbering there are two adjacent cells whose numbers differ by at most k. N. Sedrakyan

M2501. Let natural numbers $a_{1}, a_{2}$, … $, a_{n}$ be given, satisfying the condition: for any 1 £ i < j £ n, the number j – i is divisible by gcd $a_{i}, a_{j}$. Prove that there is a positive integer N such that N + i is divisible by $a_{i}$ for all i = 1, 2, …, n. P. Kozhevnikov, A. Tolpygo
