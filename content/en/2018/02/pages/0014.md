Ordinary data refers to ordinary conditions - inserted a sample, loaded, torn, recorded, inserted, loaded... In short, the characteristic times are seconds and hours. If we are talking about significantly shorter times, thousandths of a second or less, it is called an impact. The ability to work in such conditions is naturally called impact resistance. If we are talking about significantly longer times - hours, years and centuries, we will be able to notice a change in the size of the long-suffering rod under constant load. This effect is called creep or creep. Naturally, in order to avoid it, it is necessary to reduce the loads - and we came to the concept of long-term strength. Another condition is temperature: if it is heated well, then the question about mechanical parameters significantly changes the meaning. Everything is melting - no matter how hard you step into a puddle! With increasing temperature, all the characteristics of substances change, and in many cases this change determines the performance of devices. The maximum efficiency of heat engines and engine thrust increase with temperature, so technology is constantly trying to move into the region of high temperatures. But many physical processes (chemical reactions, diffusion) require interacting particles to have a certain energy, and the proportion of particles with this energy grows exponentially with temperature. Therefore, as we move into the region of high temperatures, the interaction of materials accelerates and their mechanical parameters deteriorate, i.e., simply put, everything corrodes, burns, creeps

Moreover, sometimes heat resistance and heat resistance are specified separately: Heat strength is the ability to maintain strength at high temperatures, i.e. work when heated, and heat- Resistance is the ability to withstand at this temperature without load, and work with a load later, after cooling. For example, steel will not oxidize when heated if it is alloyed with Cr, Al, Si, which will create protective oxide films on the surface, and for heat resistance it must be alloyed with Cr, V and Nb. It is impossible to characterize heat resistance or heat resistance with any exact number - heat resistance cannot be described by any one parameter; however, even for heat resistance this is impossible, since heat resistance will be different in different environments. There is nothing to say about heat resistance until the criterion is determined. But people need to compare materials, and the situation turned out to be tied to practice, i.e. to dominant real-life application. These turned out to be engine elements, in particular aircraft engine turbines, where high operating temperatures, high mechanical loads and a long service life are combined. And the criterion turned out to be preservation of form, i.e. a combination of strength, low creep and low oxidation rate at high temperatures and in the environment of fuel combustion products. According to this not entirely clear combined criterion, the most heat-resistant alloys were those based on nickel Ni and chromium Cr with various additives: aluminum Al, titanium Ti, molybdenum Mo, tungsten W, niobium Nb, tantalum Ta, hafnium Hf, rhenium Re.
