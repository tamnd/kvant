Conventionally, we will denote autonomous stress in Old Russian words in the usual way (ry ’ - li), and automatic stress - with a dash before the word ( -byli). Let's take three triplets of nouns (possibly with a preposition), which have retained the Old Russian accent to this day: gresha - gresha - for gresh, zhenb - zhene - for zhene and nogb - nuga - zb leg. Something familiar comes to mind: the stress is always on the base, the stress is always on the ending and the movable stress - these are the same patterns that we have already seen in Zaliznyak’s works about the modern Russian language. In his book “From Proto-Slavic Accentuation to Russian” (1985), he clearly explained where and how these patterns came from. Let us assign each morpheme (root, ending, etc., as well as prepositions, conjunctions and particles) one of three markings: p, o or –. Let's agree that the emphasis is determined by the leftmost arrow. If it is p, then the autonomous (“real”) stress falls on the syllable marked by it; if it is o, then the autonomous (“present”) stress falls on the syllable to the right of it; if there are no arrows in the word, but only minuses, the word has an automatic (“fake”) stress. How to determine stress in a manuscript

The diagram presented above describes the ancient Russian situation, which then changed noticeably. We ourselves notice this: for example, now it’s quite possible to call it nougat. In his works, Zaliznyak described in great detail how the Old Russian accent changed. To do this, he analyzed 89 ancient manuscripts and printed books with accents marked. Stresses were not always placed in texts - but sometimes you can get information about stress even if there is no emphasis. Let's take, for example, this phrase from “The Golden Calf” by Ilya Ilf and Evgeny Petrov: At sunset, Ostap distributed the promised gifts. With what stress is the verb read? The answer follows from knowledge of the rules of Russian spelling: ruzdal, because otherwise it would have been written distributed. Zaliznyak solved a similar problem based on the material of one ancient Russian manuscript - only, of course, it was much more difficult, because he had to first discover the rules.
