Strength and rigidity

The question of strength has always interested people - starting with the first stone ax. If we talk about materials that are actually produced and used on an industrial scale, and only about massive materials, not films or fibers, then steel with a maximum yield strength of 1.6 GPa, a slightly higher (1.7 GPa) temporary strength and a Young’s modulus of 200 GPa will claim first place. Specific figures strongly depend not only on the composition, but also on complex thermomechanical processing - a sequence of heating, deformation and cooling. The same strength values, but with half the Young's modulus, can be achieved for alloys of titanium Ti with molybdenum Mo, chromium Cr and aluminum Al. These alloys are twice as light (4.5 g $см^{3}$ versus 8 g $см^{3} ),$, however, the technology for their production is even more complex and “stricter” and they are significantly more expensive. Therefore, what stands on the ground is almost never made from them, but only what flies, swims under water, or that a person wears.
