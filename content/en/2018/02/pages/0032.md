True, in mathematics, a polyhedron is another object - a frame together with polygon faces (Fig.4). In this case, the common segments of adjacent faces are called edges - they correspond to our tubes. And the points where several edges converge are called vertices - we can say that these are our rubber bands. Here are two more remarkable polyhedra: an octahedron is assembled from 12 tubes, it has 8 faces, and an icosahedron is assembled from 30 tubes, it has 20 faces (Fig.5). All three assembled polyhedra have triangular faces, and these polyhedra are inflexible. Now let's try to assemble a cube from the tubes. Look at figure 6: this cube does not hold its shape at all - it bends, and bends in an absolutely monstrous way. We called it the "loose" cube. And jokes aside, here it arises
