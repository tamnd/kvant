Ultrasound in the megahertz frequency range propagates quite well in biological tissues. As you know, living organisms consist of almost 90% water. Therefore, the speed of sound propagation in such conditions is close to 1500 m/s, which corresponds to the speed of sound propagation in water. The wavelength of ultrasound at a frequency of 1 MHz is equal to 1.5 mm, which provides a sufficiently high spatial resolution of ultrasonic methods. The use of ultrasound in medicine for the diagnosis and examination of internal organs and joints (ultrasound) is well known. Less well known are advances in the field of ultrasound surgery, although there are significant results here too. First of all, this is the crushing and removal of kidney stones using focused shock waves - the so-called lithotripsy. Since the 1980s, lithotripsy has been the most common procedure for removing kidney stones. Another rapidly developing area of ​​research is the therapeutic area of ​​​​ultrasound, the main advantage of which is the therapeutic effect inside the body without damaging surrounding tissue. The wide possibilities of various types of ultrasound therapy have been demonstrated experimentally, and some of them have already found application in clinical practice. One example is intense focused ultrasound.
