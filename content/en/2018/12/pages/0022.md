KVANT 2018/№12

And if I didn’t have any acquaintances, how would I know that such schools exist? – First of all, we – your students – are lucky. – I don’t know about you – I’m definitely lucky! – How do you teach lessons - according to what program, what textbooks? Tell us a little about the leaf system. – I rather stick to the program for math classes, and I hardly assign anything from the textbook. I like to make up sheets of paper myself, I give them to the students and tell them the numbers of the problems from this sheet of paper that need to be solved. Sometimes I ask you to read something from a textbook. But this happens rarely even in geometry, and even less often in algebra. When we prepare for the 9th grade exam, we at least stick to the textbook first. For example, in geometry, Pogorelov’s textbook is used - the axiomatics are taken from it. We need to agree on the basics. And I give tasks from pieces of paper. Of course, my lessons are different from analysis lessons, in which many teachers take on problems at the same time. But I walked around, looked at who was doing what, called them to the board - in general, I somehow adapted. The main principle of the leaves is from simple to complex. Of course, I tried to select beautiful problems that would be solved with pleasure. I wanted the problems to be interesting to solve. There are such tasks that you don’t even want to read the conditions to the end. I tried to prevent such things from happening. Although it’s impossible to say that I didn’t have any cumbersome tasks at all - you also need to be able to solve cumbersome tasks. But still there were not so many of them. – Which of your colleagues influenced you? First, of course, Kronrod. He forced me to solve analysis problems. He then wanted me to go to graduate school with him. But he himself could not formally be my supervisor: he worked at a research institute, working on oil (computer calculations for explosion and wave propagation). Nevertheless, he assigned me to graduate school. At first it was the All-Union Correspondence Pedagogical Institute. I took courses there

English language and philosophy, passed the candidate minimum. Then he assigned me to graduate school at the Pedagogical Institute. I was there first with the head of the department Shchegolkov, then they gave me another scientific supervisor - Dmitry Abramovich Raikov, a very famous professor at the Pedagogical Institute. Then he unexpectedly died, and I was transferred to Meer Feliksovich Bokstein, a major specialist in algebraic topology. But at some point, mathematics in my planned dissertation nevertheless turned into methodology. I wrote something, but it was unsuccessful - those who read it could not understand where the psychology was. And as a result, I dropped out of graduate school (although I passed all the candidate minimums). But I learned how to type on a typewriter. – Was it the second half of the 70s? - Yes, somewhere around 75, 76. A weight has been lifted from my shoulders - I don’t need any dissertation! And I began to focus only on school. And to the question of who influenced me: I met (I don’t remember how) Igor Fedorovich Sharygin - he greatly influenced me. And also Arnold Yakovlevich Bloch. That is, three people - Kronrod, Sharygin and Bloch. None of them, alas, are any more. Boris Petrovich Heidman also taught me a lot. – Has it ever happened that you learned something from your students? – This happens all the time! Surely someone will say something interesting. Igor Pukhov (he died, I feel very sorry for him), he twice received medals at international Olympiads - that’s how he solved problems! He looked and looked, and then in a quiet voice he gave out solutions - and they were always so beautiful! Of course, I learned a lot from children. And now sometimes schoolchildren come up with some kind of solution - I immediately write it down and remember it. Today, in an open lesson, I told original (at least from my point of view - I had never seen them before) solutions to problems that our students came up with - either during lessons or while solving homework.
