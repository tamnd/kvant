There were two technical schools in Novogrudok, one of them was trade and economics. I came there and worked there for three years and was a class teacher. Mostly girls from villages entered the technical school, because the specialty is good - either you are a commodity specialist (I had a group called “food commodity specialist”), or you are an accountant. There was only one boy in my group - his name was Lenya Kernogha. I taught him to play the bass guitar, but I didn’t teach him anything about mathematics. He played normally, we had an ensemble; I didn’t perform in it myself, but I taught them – and they played at the dances. It is surprising that among these children there were very capable ones. – How did you get from there to Moscow and start working at school No. 57? – I also tried to enroll in graduate school (I think even twice). Of course, nothing came of this, and even Springjuk could not help me, although he tried to do something. As a result, I moved to Moscow because I got married. My wife’s father was a serviceman, he was called up from Moscow and, according to the law of that time, he had to return to Moscow to retire. The whole family came to Moscow, and so did I. But I had to look for a job. A friend of my father introduced me to Alexander Semenovich Kronrod, who at that time was engaged in computer recognition of X-ray images of the lungs. On this basis, Kronrod knew a friend of my father. And this friend asked me to get a job - somewhere in a computer center. Although I was very bad at programming. I was even given a test at the university because I promised never to do programming. But I needed to work somewhere, I already agreed to everything. Kronrod undertook to arrange for me, I came to work with him every morning, he called different places, made arrangements, and I went to these different places, they talked to me, but didn’t take me anywhere. This happened several times and continued for a month. As a result, I once told him: “I’m ready for any job,

even to school." “To school? Well, sorry, this is very difficult. I have to take your exam. Do you agree to take it?” We talked on the phone in the evening, and he asks: “Can you come now?” Around ten o'clock in the evening I went to him to take the exam. I didn’t pass the first time: I decided on some things, I didn’t decide on others. He gave me homework problems and said: “Decide - call.” In the morning I got up and solved everything except one problem - to prove that there is no function that is discontinuous at all irrational points and continuous at all rational points. I didn't know any of this then. I called him and said that I could solve everything, but I couldn’t solve this problem. He replied: “That’s right, that’s how it should be.” I came to him and told him the solutions to the remaining problems. He said: “Okay, now I recommend you,” and gave me the phone number of Nikolai Nikolaevich Konstantinov. I called, we met with Kolya, and he took me to school 179. There was no place there, there were no hours, but somehow he and Kronrod persuaded the director to hire me for at least some hours. They gave me a few hours of mathematical analysis. It was November 1973. I came with students to analysis lessons [at school 179, as in some other math schools, recent graduates come to part of the lessons and take over solutions to the students’ problems]. I was “overgrown” there - I was already 24 years old, and they were all fifth-year students. For a long time I could not understand what was happening - after all, to an outsider, what is done in analysis classes in schools like 57 and 179 is completely incomprehensible. But gradually I got used to it, then they began to give me replacements - algebra and geometry lessons. I even received some money, very small - 30 rubles a month. So several months passed, and suddenly in February 1974 Kolya called me and said that there was a vacancy in school 57, it was 17 hours. Kolya brought me there, the school director Nina Evgenievna Lapushkina and the head teacher Lev Nilovich Bukhman talked to me. They gave me the exam anyway.
