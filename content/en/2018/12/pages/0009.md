CONTEST

NAME

heh. Voronoi himself clearly understood that hard work had a negative impact on health, but abandoning research was beyond his strength. “Only my wife knows that mathematics is the main goal of life for me, it is everything to me,” he wrote in his diary. Georgy Feodosievich spent the summer of 1908 in his native Zhuravka, despite doctors’ recommendations to go to Carlsbad for treatment. By the beginning of the school year, he feels better and returns to Warsaw. At the beginning of September he writes, as it turned out, his last letter to Academician V.A. Steklov, in which he briefly reports

A.P. SAVINA

⟦column⟧

about his research on the theory of parallelohedrons, and also expresses a desire to move to St. Petersburg University. However, in October there was a sharp exacerbation of the disease. In the last month of his life, suffering from pain and bedridden, Voronoi managed to write down “Notes on Fermat’s Last Theorem.” Two weeks later, on November 7 (20), 1908, at the age of forty, Georgy Feodosevich Voronoi died. He was buried according to his will in his beloved Zhuravka.

15. Let A be the smallest and B the largest of the eight-digit numbers, which contain each of the digits 1, 2, ..., 8 and which are divisible by 137. Prove that A + B is divisible by 73. S. Kostin

16. Let us denote as $P_{N}$ the number of ways to choose from N different objects any number of objects that gives a remainder of 1 when divided by 3, and as $Q_{N}$ the number of ways to choose from N different objects any number of objects that gives a remainder of 2 when divided by 3. Prove that $P_{N}$ and $Q_{N}$ differ by no more than 1. E. Bakaev
