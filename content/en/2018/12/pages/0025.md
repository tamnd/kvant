PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2538, M2539 were offered at the XL Tournament of Cities. Problem M2541 was proposed at the correspondence round of the XIV Geometry Olympiad named after I.F. Sharygin.

Problem M2538–M2541, F2545–F2548

M2538. Petya places 500 kings on squares of the board 100 and 50 so that they do not beat each other. And Vasya places 500 kings on white squares (in a chess coloring) of a 100 and 100 board so that they do not beat each other. Who has more ways to do this? E. Bakaev

M2539. a) Prove that any number of the form 3k – 2, where k is an integer, can be represented as the sum of one square and two cubes of integers. b) Prove that any integer can be represented as the sum of one square and three cubes of integers. N. Sedrakyan

M2540. In an acute triangle ABC the altitudes $AH_{a}, BH_{b}, CH_{c}$ are drawn.

Fig. 1

⟦column⟧

Let $H_{ab}$ be the projection of the point $H_{a}$ onto the line AC (Fig.1). Let us similarly define the points $H_{ac}, H_{ba}, H_{bc}, H_{ca}, H_{cb}$. Prove that six points $H_{ab}, H_{ac}, H_{ba}, H_{bc}, H_{ca}, H_{cb}$ lie on one circle, the center of which coincides with the center of mass of a triangular contour $H_{a} H_{b} H_{c}$ made of uniform wire. I. Weinstein

$M2541^{*}$. The plane is divided into convex heptagons of unit diameter. Prove that any circle of radius 200 intersects at least a billion of these heptagons. (The diameter of a polygon is the maximum among the distances between pairs of its vertices.) A. Kanel-Belov

F2545. Two solid balls of different sizes from one chemically pure substance are located in space far from other massive bodies and interact with each other only by gravitational means. They rotate around a common center of mass, making one revolution during one lesson T = 45 minutes. What is the minimum density of the material from which the balls are made? What metal does this density correspond to? A. Burov, E. Shalimova
