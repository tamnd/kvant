ON W I

– Please tell us about the database of geometry problems (zadachi.mccme.ru). – In 1991, a graduate of the 81st year, Misha Businier, came to me and said that he and his comrades wanted to write a program with which one could search for problems using different criteria. Misha said that they had several seminars, that Lesha Kanel wrote a book in which he outlined the theoretical foundations of how such a system should be structured: what you need to know about the problem in order to find it, what is most interesting, how it all should be arranged from his point of view. They wrote such a program, but it needed to be filled with tasks, and they turned to me - so that there were at least 100 tasks. – Only in geometry? - Yes. They wanted to see if the system worked, whether it was interesting or not. I became interested and enjoyed doing it. I did it, checked it - yes, it really worked well (but slowly). Slow because, in particular, it was the ChiWriter editor (now not in use). The program took almost 5 minutes to load, but it worked and found tasks based on the attributes that I set. Then we did another 600 problems. It probably took a year. Everything works. The problem was drawing pictures. The program code itself was written by Misha Businier and Seryozha Trifonov, Dima Teiblyum and many others also participated in this. At first, only I was involved in filling the database with tasks. Then, when all this began to develop (and there were about 1000 problems), Volodya Protasov, a student of Sharygin - now he is a professor at the Independent Moscow University, began to participate. Sharygin was also enthusiastic - he did not participate himself, but he was very positive about the idea of ​​​​a task database. They even tried to sell this program, there were some exhibitions and lectures, books were written about this base. This went on for several years. Then everything suddenly died out. Then the TeX editor appeared, the Internet began to develop, and someone had an idea - why not transfer the database to TeX. Dima Shkolnik wrote a program that converts a document from ChiWriter to TeX. U

This program, of course, had terrible errors, so then I had to edit it manually. Then Misha Raskin appeared, who generally did it all perfectly. There are already 3000 tasks, 5000 tasks. Today we have more than 10,000 tasks, and the database works perfectly. I instantly find everything I need there. I know that a lot of people use this database: on average, we have about 500 requests per day (of course, there are fewer unique users, but 500 times a day this or that task is requested from the database). For a non-entertainment program this seems to be ok. Of course, they send us feedback and find errors and typos. We react instantly and correct. Now Misha Panov does this - he draws pictures when he has time. I am still the only one who enters tasks into the database. The biggest problem is pictures; now only about 20% of tasks have them. But adding pictures is difficult - you need 10-20 people to sit for a month and write it all. When we had practice at school, Misha led a circle on MetaPost, taught the children to draw - and the children did figure, 40-50 tasks each. Of course, Misha still leads the circle, but only a few people go there. Sometimes I select tasks from the system, and he draws pictures for them. It turns out that figure are added, but I introduce new tasks at a faster rate than tasks for the old ones are drawn. Unfortunately, there is such a gap. I like this system. I would love to work with her: she is ideal for selecting tasks for a circle, for a lesson, for a test. – So, the main point of the database is that it allows you to search for problems by topic? – By topic, by complexity, by various attributes (for example, a problem on proof, on construction, on calculation, on the geometric locus of points, on geometric inequalities, on maxima and minima, a beautiful problem, “perhaps beautiful,” olympiad, educational, cumbersome...). Of course, a solution graph is written down - what facts, objects and methods are used; how tasks are related to each other - ancestors, descendants, analogues; all this is instantly found.
