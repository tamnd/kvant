Study at St. Petersburg University

From the diary we know with what great desire and persistence Georgiy began to study mathematical courses, which “increasingly fascinated” him. He learned to get up around 5 a.m. and begin his studies, which continued until the evening. Georgy had a needle with him, which he used to prick himself in order to relieve the wave of fatigue that rolled in from time to time with acute pain. Father, it was clearly not enough. And after my father retired in 1887, this help was reduced. Since Voronoi began receiving a university scholarship only in his last year, he was forced to give lessons for little money. The lessons exhausted him, and the difficult conditions in the dormitory further complicated his rest and mathematics. Under these conditions, Voronoi concentrated all his strength and will on mathematics. “The main thing that interests me is whether I have enough abilities,” he writes in his diary. Fortunately, Voronoi had an abundance of mathematical abilities, and the “ever-increasing passion for mathematics” captured him completely. In “the moments when the mind embraces an idea that had previously slipped away like a ball, I forget that I exist,” Voronoi writes in his diary in 1887. In the same place: “I owe my recent successes to the habit of thinking without pen and paper. All the propositions I proved arose completely independently. …I hope that this habit of thinking in this way will serve me well.” To further develop his mathematical abilities, Voronoi arranged mathematical training for himself: he consistently solved difficult educational problems on the calculation of complex integrals and symmetric functions, on the integration of differential equations. Fortunately, at the St. Petersburg University at that time there was a brilliant mathematical school of the brilliant mathematician Pafnuty Lvovich Chebyshev (1821–1894), the famous St. Petersburg School of Number Theory. Chebyshev and his outstanding students A.A. Markov (senior), A.M. Lyapunov, A.N. Korkin and others maintained a very high level of mathematical education at St. Petersburg University. Academician Andrei Andreevich Markov became Voronoi’s scientific supervisor.
