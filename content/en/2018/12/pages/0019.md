My father remembered all his life: “What a stupid thing you did to leave.” – Were your parents ready for you to stay, live and go to school so far away? – Yes, my parents were ready, but I said “no” and went home. Then I skipped through 8th grade. And while I was studying at school, an 11-year-old girl appeared. I did not study in the 11th grade, after the 10th grade I passed all the exams - so I studied for 9 years instead of 11. Then I participated in various Olympiads, but I did not have much success. But I became the winner of the Belarusian Olympiad, which was held specifically for selection to the university. And I was accepted - but I still had to take exams. I passed mathematics normally, but in physics I got such a ticket! I didn’t know at all what to say – saturated pairs... I had to calmly give a two. I began to say something to the examiners, they replied: “We will now take you to the laboratory, and you will see what is really happening.” In general, since I was on the list of winners of the Olympiad, they gave me a 4. the special course was taught by Vladimir Gennadievich Sprindzhuk - there was such a mathematician, unfortunately, he died early. He worked on number theoretic problems, solved Mahler's problem, and was quite a famous person in his field. I worked with him on analytical functions in p-adic fields, wrote a thesis on this topic. He wanted to take me to graduate school. Then it was time for distribution. The distribution depended on the average score. I had a GPA of about 4.5 - not brilliant, but it was considered very good. I was in about the first five dozen (out of 250 people). The rector, academician Anton Nikiforovich Sevchenko, a terrible anti-Semite, was present at the distribution, he specially came so that God forbid any Jew would be taken somewhere other than school. They could have sent me to some research institute to do some calculations - no, they definitely had to send me to school. But in the end it worked out well. Sevchenko was busy with something else at that time. And then they brought me. I sit down, he is still continuing another conversation, then looks: “Who is it?” He reads the last name, first name and patronymic and no longer looks in my direction: “To the Ministry of Education.” At the Ministry of Education, I was one of the first, and they offered me a choice of several good, in their opinion, places (near Minsk, they said that from there it would be possible to go to the university to study as a graduate student).
