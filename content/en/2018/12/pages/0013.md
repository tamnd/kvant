PHYSICS

SOUND

⟦figure⟧

Fig. 2. Diagram of sound rays emerging from a sound source in the atmosphere in the presence of wind

The air flow increases the speed of sound propagation in the wind and slightly reduces it in the opposite direction. Generally, surface air flow or wind increases its speed with height. The speed of sound propagation in the wind at high altitudes is greater than at the ground, so the front of the sound wave, when rising upward, turns around and the wave is directed downward, where the speed is lower. Sound refraction occurs. Thanks to this, a sound waveguide is formed in the near-surface layer of the atmosphere, in which sound is concentrated, and acoustic signals that propagate at an altitude of several tens of kilometers can be recorded on the surface of the earth. The effect of refraction when propagating against the wind leads to the fact that sound quickly travels to a high altitude (tens of kilometers). Therefore, we hear poorly against the wind and well downwind. A surface sound waveguide can be formed not only as a result of wind. On a quiet, windless frosty day, somewhere outside the city you can hear the barking of dogs or the noise of a car far away. In such weather, a so-called temperature inversion is possible in the surface atmosphere. Typically, air temperature decreases with height, but on a frosty day the temperature at the surface of the earth, especially in the lowlands, may be lower than at some altitude. The minimum temperature in the surface air layer corresponds to the minimum
