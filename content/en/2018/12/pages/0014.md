Via for the existence of atmospheric sound channels. Sound waves from volcanic eruptions or ground explosions travel through these channels over vast distances and can even circle the globe. Therefore, the middle atmosphere (from 20 to 120 km altitude) is a good conductor of infrasound. This property of the atmosphere allowed scientists to develop a technique for infrasound sounding of the atmosphere, based on the phenomenon of scattering of acoustic pulses on layered inhomogeneities of wind speed and atmospheric temperature up to altitudes of the lower thermosphere of the order of 140 km. Using this technique, it is possible to determine wind speed fluctuations in the altitude range from the upper stratosphere to the lower thermosphere (90–140 km). Seismic waves in the ground. Seismic waves propagate in the ground in a similar way. They can be either natural or artificial. We can name earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and mountain collapses as natural sources of seismic waves. Seismic waves are most effectively excited artificially by an explosion or special multi-ton vibrators. If only longitudinal sound waves propagate in the ocean and atmosphere (there is no shear elasticity in liquids and gases), then seismic waves can be both longitudinal and transverse. Transverse waves, depending on the plane of oscillation, can have different polarization. The speed of propagation of transverse waves is, as a rule, 2–3 times less than the speed of propagation of longitudinal waves. The presence of seismic waves of two types expands the capabilities of seismic sounding in comparison with sounding of the ocean or atmosphere. The central task of seismic sounding is to study the structure of the earth and search for minerals. Both of these tasks require conflicting approaches. On the one hand, it is interesting to look as deep as possible under the surface of the earth. This is possible

On the other hand, a decrease in frequency leads to an increase in the emitted wavelength, and this reduces the resolution of the remote sensing method. Increasing demands on the quality of mineral exploration force us to look for ways to increase the resolution and, consequently, the accuracy of seismic exploration. The contradiction that arose was resolved through the development of methods for receiving seismic signals. It is known that the larger the receiving antenna, the higher its spatial resolution. If signals are received by a large number of receivers combined into a single network, the spatial accuracy of remote sensing can be increased. But this requires complex signal processing from many hundreds or even thousands of receivers. Modern seismic exploration provides sufficient sensing accuracy to identify productive deposits of minerals, such as oil or gas, at depths greater than 10 km. Modern technologies ensure that the well passes horizontally along the formation to increase the efficiency of oil production. The thickness of the formation is about 10 m at a depth of several kilometers. In this case, the length of the well can be more than 10 km. The accuracy of laying a well is commensurate with the accuracy of placing a rocket on a trajectory for interplanetary flight.
