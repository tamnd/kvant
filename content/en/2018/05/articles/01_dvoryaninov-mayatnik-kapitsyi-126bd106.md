Kapitsa pendulum

S. DVORYANINOV

Wonder is the mother of knowledge. Attributed to Aristotle

NOW WE WILL TELL ABOUT ONE INTERESTING physical phenomenon. Moreover, it is amazing, and on this basis it was included in V.I. Arnold’s book “Mathematical Understanding of Nature. Essays on amazing physical phenomena and their understanding by mathematicians (with drawings by the author).” We will talk about a pendulum. Usually a pendulum is drawn as shown in figure 1. A small body is suspended from a long thread, which oscillates. Such a pendulum is called mathematical. The lower equilibrium position of the pendulum is stable. Instead of a thread, you can take a rod - we will have just such a pendulum. Now let’s turn the pendulum over, Fig. 1, make it stand, as Arnold writes, “upside down” (Fig.2). In such a pendulum, the previous point of suspension turns into a fulcrum, but according to tradition we will call it the point of suspension. It is well known that it is impossible to place such a pendulum vertically - either to the left or to the right - Fig. 2 it will definitely fall. If you still manage to fix it in a vertical position, then the slightest shock will

Losing the support or blowing air will cause the pendulum to tip over. This is a consequence and manifestation of the instability of the upper equilibrium position of an inverted pendulum. Academician and future Nobel laureate Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa (2019 will mark his 125th birthday) conducted such an experiment. He forced the suspension point to rapidly oscillate along the vertical. What could be the source of such fluctuations? The time was post-war, not rich, but Kapitsa’s family had an electric sewing machine, which means there was an electric motor. The suspension point must perform a translational movement up and down, and the electric motor creates a rotational movement. How to turn one thing into another has long been well known. For example, in a steam locomotive, the translational movement of the piston in the cylinder is converted by a crank mechanism into rotation of the wheels. The design diagram of the Kapitsa pendulum is shown in figure 3. On the axis of the electric motor

1, a ball bearing 2 is eccentrically mounted, a rod 3 is attached to its cage, which causes the lever 4 to oscillate. One end of the lever rotates relative to a fixed axis, and the pendulum rod 5 is suspended on the other so that it can swing freely. The pendulum with a vibrating suspension point stood stably upside down. An attempt to tilt it to the side resulted in it starting to swing around the vertical left and right, but not falling. The problem of considering such a pendulum arose in connection with the theory of accelerators. When developing a new project, it was necessary to determine whether the movement of an accelerated particle along a ring of huge diameter would be stable. It is clear that an experiment with a pendulum costs many, many times less than building an accelerator. You can watch a recording of V.I. Arnold’s story about this pendulum on the website “Mathematical Etudes” (http:// etudes.ru). He did not have an electric sewing machine, and therefore Vladimir Igorevich used a vibrating electric razor “Neva” as a source of vertical oscillations of the pendulum. The pendulum was attached to the tongue of the electric razor using a pencil collet clamp. It is well known that it is better to see once than to hear a hundred times. Better yet, hold it in your hands. Surprisingly, you can verify from experience the fundamental possibility of stability of an inverted pendulum with the corresponding vibration of the suspension point almost instantly, without getting up from your desk. You have everything you need to carry out the experiment at your fingertips. Take a wooden (or plastic) ruler with a hole in the end and place it on a smooth, polished table. Place an unsharpened pencil in the hole and quickly, like a violinist with a bow, move the pencil back and forth in a direction that does not coincide with the direction of the ruler (Fig.4). You will immediately see how the ruler
