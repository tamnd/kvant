COMPLEX OPTION

8–9 grades

1. (4) The line contains 39 numbers that are not equal to zero. The sum of any two adjacent numbers is positive, and the sum of all numbers is negative. What is the sign of the product of all numbers? B.Frenkin

2. (5) Aladdin has several identical gold bars, and sometimes he asks the genie to increase their number. The genie adds a thousand of the same bars, but after that he takes exactly half of the resulting total mass of gold for the service. Could Aladdin win after ten such requests, if not a single ingot had to be sawed? A.Perepechko

3. (6) See problem M2511 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

4. (6) See problem M2510 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

5. (8) On the street, houses stand opposite each other, 50 pairs in total. On the right side of the street there are houses with even natural numbers, on the left - with odd natural numbers, the numbers increase from the beginning of the street to the end on each side, but they are not necessarily in a row (gaps are possible). For each house on the right side of the street, they found the difference between its number and the number of the house opposite, and it turned out that all the numbers found were different. The largest house number on the street is n. Find the smallest possible value of n. M. Didin

6. (10) In the country of knights (they always tell the truth) and liars (they always lie), 10 people, among whom there are liars, sit at a round table at the vertices of a regular decagon. A traveler can stand somewhere and ask those sitting: “What is the distance from me to the nearest liar among you?” After that, everyone answers him. What is the minimum number of questions a traveler must ask in order to be guaranteed to know who the liars are at the table? (There are no strangers nearby, you can’t stand on the table.

⟦column⟧

Consider people as dots. Everyone, including the traveler, can accurately measure any distance.) M. Didin

3. (6) The table 10 u 10 contains 100 different numbers. During a move, you can select any rectangle made up of cells and rearrange all the numbers in it symmetrically relative to its center (“rotate the rectangle by 180q”). Is it always possible in 99 moves for the numbers to increase in each row from left to right and in each column from bottom to top? A. Shapovalov

4. A regular triangle lying in plane D was orthogonally projected onto a non-parallel plane E, the resulting triangle was orthogonally projected onto plane J and a regular triangle was again obtained. Prove that: a) (4) the angle between planes D and E is equal to the angle between planes E and J; b) (4) plane E intersects planes D and J along straight lines perpendicular to each other. L. Emelyanov

6. (10) Quadrilateral ABCD is inscribed in a circle. Rays BA and CD intersect at point P. A line passing through P and parallel to the tangent to the circle at point D intersects at points U and V the tangents drawn to the circle at points A and B. Prove that the circles circumscribed about triangle CUV and quadrilateral ABCD are tangent. A. Zaslavsky
