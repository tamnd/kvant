Determine whether this hypothesis is correct, and if so, determine how long after the first decay the second decay occurred. Assume that the particles move freely. I.Vorobiev

3. A wedge of mass M = 250 g with an angle D of 30 q at the base rests on a smooth horizontal plane. A block of mass m = 100 g was placed on the wedge and a certain velocity was imparted to it with an impact, directed upward along the inclined surface of the wedge. Find the amount of heat Q released as a result of the friction of the block on the wedge, if it is known that the maximum height to which the block rose from its initial position is h = 20 cm. The coefficient of friction of the block on the inclined surface of the wedge is P = 0.6. Take the free fall acceleration equal to g 10 m $с^{2}$. S. Chesnokov

4. On a horizontal section of the field, Vasya cleared an area measuring 1 m x 1 m from pure white snow. Under the rays of the spring sun, the black earth warms up and heats the air located above it. The thermal power received by the air from the site is W = 0.3 kW, since the sun is quite low above the horizon in winter. In calm and dry weather, at an air temperature of $T_{0} = 273$ K and a pressure near the earth's surface of $p 10^{5}$ Pa, a column of warm air rising above the site has a temperature of $T_{1} = 275$ K and a cross section of $S 2 м^{2}$ at a height h = 10 m. The ambient temperature does not depend on altitude and is equal to $T_{0}$. The molar mass of air is M = 29 g/mol, its molar heat capacity at constant pressure is $C_{p} 7 R 2$. 1) Estimate the speed at which the air flow rises at a height h if the process has already established itself. 2) Estimate the height to which warm air would rise if there were no heat exchange between the warm air and the cold air surrounding it.
