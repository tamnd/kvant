Oral round for 11th grade

1. The housewife baked a square cake and cut several pieces from it. The first cut is made parallel to the side of the original square from edge to edge. The next cut is made in the remaining part from edge to edge perpendicular to the previous cut, then - similarly (several times). All cut pieces have equal area. Could the rest of the cake be a square? B.Frenkin

2. Let X be some fixed point on side AC of triangle ABC (X is different from A and C). Arbitrary surrounding

⟦column⟧

The line passing through X and B intersects the segment AC and the circumcircle of triangle ABC at points P and Q other than X and B. Prove that all possible lines PQ pass through one point. M. Panov

3. 16 cards with integers from 1 to 16 are laid out face down in the form of a 4 u 4 table so that the cards on which adjacent numbers are written lie next to each other (touching on the side). What is the smallest number of cards that must be turned over at the same time in order to accurately determine the location of all the numbers (no matter how the cards are laid out)? M. Evdokimov

4. There is a natural 1001-digit number A, and a 1001-digit number Z is the same number A, written from end to beginning (for example, for four-digit numbers it could be 7432 and 2347). It is known that A > Z. At what A will the quotient A/Z be the smallest (but strictly greater than 1)? A.Tolpygo

5. Is it possible to arrange five spheres in space so that for each of the spheres it is possible to draw a tangent plane through its center to the other four spheres? The spheres can intersect and do not have to have the same radius. M. Murashkin
