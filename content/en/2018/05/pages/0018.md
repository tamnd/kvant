This means that the plane KLMN is parallel to the line AC, whence KL P AC P MN. Now let's prove this lemma. Lemma. Let the tetrahedron ABCD have AB < BC and CD < AD. Then it cannot have a section that is an isosceles trapezoid and parallel to the edge AC (or BD). Proof. Let us assume the opposite - let there be a section KLMN such that the vertices K, L, M, N lie on the edges AB, BC, CD, DA, respectively, and KLMN is an isosceles trapezoid, KL P MN P AC. Next, let P, Q, R denote the midpoints of the segments KL, AC, MN, respectively (see figure), and let B c, P c, D c, R c be the projections of the points B, P, D, R, respectively, onto the line AC. Since AB < BC, then AB c B c C, so point B c lies on the ray QA. Since the point P lies on the segment QB, the point P c lies on the segment QB c and, therefore, P c lies on the ray QA. Similarly, from the condition CD < AD it follows that R c lies on the ray QC. From here, in particular, we see that the points P c and R c cannot coincide. The straight line PR is the axis of symmetry of the isosceles trapezoid KLMN, hence PR A KL and, therefore, PR A AC. But in this case, the projections of P c and R c onto the line AC must coincide. Contradiction. The lemma is proven. In the tetrahedron ABCD, we will conditionally call the pair of crossing edges AB and CD the first pair, the pair AC and BD the second pair, and

the pair AD and BC are the third pair. Suppose we have a tetrahedron in which any edge from the first pair is shorter than any edge from the second pair, and any edge from the second pair is in turn shorter than any edge from the third pair. Then it follows from the lemma that in such a tetrahedron there is no section for any edge that is an isosceles trapezoid and parallel to this edge. The tetrahedrons we need exist. For example, an isohedral (inscribed in a rectangular parallelepiped) tetrahedron ABCD is suitable, in which AB = CD < AC = BD < AD = BC. We can generalize our lemma and prove the following necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a section that is an isosceles trapezoid and parallel to the edge AC. Such a section exists exactly in the following cases: 1) AB = BC and AD = DC; 2) AB < BC, AD < < DC and BD are not perpendicular to AC; 3) AB > BC, AD > DC and BD are not perpendicular to AC. (In the case of AC A BD, there are rectangular sections parallel to AC.) Using this, we can indicate other classes of tetrahedra that do not have a section in the shape of an isosceles trapezoid. For example: pyramid ABCD, at the base of which lies a regular triangle ABC, and the side edges AD, BD, CD are not equal to each other; orthocentric tetrahedron ABCD (i.e. in which AB A CD, AC A BD, AD A BC) without equal edges.
