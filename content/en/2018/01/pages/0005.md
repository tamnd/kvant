Biology

Astrobiology, bioastronomy, exobiology... These disciplines have existed for decades, although they never had an object of practical study and still don’t have one. Life beyond Earth has not yet been discovered. They searched for her on both the Moon and Mars; in the form of intelligent life they were looking for it in the vastness of the Galaxy and even beyond its borders. But life and intelligence have not been found in space, and this gives us a lot to think about. However, pessimism is inappropriate here. Astronomers regularly discover places in space favorable for life, therefore, sooner or later extraterrestrial life itself will be discovered. For biology, this would mean a colossal breakthrough in understanding the very phenomenon of life and its origin. Therefore, biologists are extremely interested in the findings of astronomers. Among the planets around other stars, astronomers have already discovered dozens of Earth-like ones, and some of them apparently have a climate close to that of Earth. In the near future, the construction of several giant telescopes with a diameter of 25–40 meters will be completed, which will make it possible to obtain the spectra of these planets and search for traces of life in them. But still, studying someone else’s life from afar is a thankless task. Would we learn much about the structure of proteins and DNA if we studied earthly life by looking at our planet from the Moon through a telescope? Therefore, now the closest attention is paid to objects in the solar system where conditions for life have been discovered. Unfortunately, there are no favorable conditions on the surface of any of them, except the Earth. But there may be some underneath the surface. Ecology

The state of the environment directly affects our lives. Typically, environmentalists are concerned with the quality of air, water, food, noise levels and other local parameters within the Earth. But our planet is a particle of the larger cosmos, which reminds us of itself from time to time. We ourselves have already filled the near outer space with thousands of artificial bodies, which sometimes collide with each other, explode and thus multiply as space debris. A significant part of astronomical observations (and astronomers' earnings) is associated with tracking space debris to warn earthlings and workers of the International Space Station. The ISS has already avoided dangerous debris dozens of times, thanks to an accurate astronomical forecast of its movement. The graph on the next page shows how quickly the amount of space debris has grown since the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite. These are failed satellites, the last stages of launch vehicles, transition compartments, parts of exploded rockets or satellites destroyed during mutual collisions. Only large objects larger than 10 cm in size are taken into account here, which can be tracked by radar methods and which pose a fatal threat to “living” satellites and spacecraft and stations.
