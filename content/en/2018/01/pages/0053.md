2. (4) Are there 99 consecutive natural numbers such that the smallest of them is divisible by 100, the next is divisible by 99, the third is divisible by 98, ..., the last is divisible by 2? P. Kozhevnikov

2. (4) In triangle ABC, draw the bisector CL. The perpendicular bisector to side AC intersects segment CL at point K. Prove that the circumcircles of triangles ABC and AKL are tangent. M. Panov

3. (4) There are 100 identical coins in a row. Among them there are exactly 26 fake ones, and they lie in a row. Real coins weigh the same, fake coins don't necessarily weigh the same, but they are lighter than real ones. How can you find at least one counterfeit coin in one weighing on a two-pan scale without weights? R.Zhenodarov

3. (4) There are 21 non-zero numbers. For each two of them, their sum and product are calculated. It turned out that half of all sums are positive and half are negative. What is the greatest possible number of positive products? B. Frenkin, S. Kudrya

4. (5) On one of the squares of the 8 ¥ 8 field there is a treasure buried. You are with a metal detector in the center of one of the corner cells of this field and move, moving to the centers of the cells adjacent to the side. The metal detector goes off if you find yourself on the cell where the treasure is buried, or in one of the cells adjacent to it on the side. Is it possible to reliably indicate the cell where the treasure is buried by walking a distance of no more than 26? M. Evdokimov
