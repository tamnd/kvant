From the three-cap theorem (now it can be called a theorem, because we have proven it) many consequences follow about the tangency of circles and balls. We will highlight only three of them, and formulate a few more in exercises 5–9. Let's start with the fact that if for two of the three pairs of circles the external tangents are replaced by internal ones, then the theorem will remain true. Nothing will change in the proof. Only the plane will now touch two balls on one side, and the third on the other. We call the corresponding points internal vertices. So, the outer and two inner vertices of three circles (or three balls) lie on the same straight line. If two balls touch, the inner vertex will become the point of contact. This limiting case gives the following consequence. Corollary 1. Two balls externally touch a third one. Then the vertex of the two balls lies on the straight line connecting the two points of tangency. The same is true, of course, for circles on a plane. Then the line connecting the points of tangency passes through a fixed point of the line AB, independent of the drawn circle. Proof. Note that the square of the tangent AA c is equal to AD AC, and therefore does not depend on the circle. Same with tangent BB c.
