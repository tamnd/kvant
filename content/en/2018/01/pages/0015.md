This remarkable fact was apparently known even before Desargues. Renaissance painters must have been familiar with it, since they knew the basics of perspective and applied them. Desargues, however, was the first to formulate it as a theorem and, most importantly, prove it. Moreover, the idea of ​​its proof is still used almost unchanged! Look at figure 16. Now imagine that this is not a flat, but a spatial drawing and the shaded triangles actually lie in different planes. Then the statement of the theorem is obvious: the planes of the shaded triangles intersect along a straight line, and the points K, L, M belong to both of these planes, and therefore lie on the intersection line. That's it, the proof is essentially finished. All that remains is to carefully move from space to plane. This can be done using projection, or using passage to the limit. Let's take the second path.
