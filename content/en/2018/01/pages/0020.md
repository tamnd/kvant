In an effort to describe the language in an optimal way, Zaliznyak posed and solved mathematical problems. One of them is suggested by the example with which we began. Let us recall that the word lantern has the stress pattern b, i.e. The emphasis is always on the ending. Before you read further, think: is there some contradiction here that needs to be resolved? Let's ask ourselves a strange question: where does the emphasis fall in the shape of a lantern - on the base or on the ending? There are three possible answers to this question: 1) to the stem: it is clear that the vowel a is included in the stem, and the ending here is zero; 2) we don’t know where: the stem and ending do not compete for stress here, since the ending is zero; 3) to the ending, but let’s introduce an additional rule: if the stress falls on the zero ending (and it turns out that there is nowhere for it to sound), it is transferred to the stem. Let’s denote the stress on the base by +, the stress on the ending by –, and “we don’t know” by 0.
