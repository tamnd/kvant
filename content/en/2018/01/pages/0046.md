Solution. 1) The brightest stars have a negative magnitude, so Sirius is the brightest star. 2) The stars can be the same distance from the Sun (Earth), but this does not mean that they are in the same constellation. For example, the star Vega (D Lyrae) and the star Fomalhaut (D Pisces Southern) are located at the same distance from the Sun, but belong to different constellations. 3) The evolution of stars depends on their mass. The greater the mass of the star, the more intense the evolution of the star. Therefore, the evolution of Deneb (its mass $21 M_{C} )$ will occur faster than that of Sirius (its mass $2 M_{C} ).$ 4) The spectral classes of stars are closely related to the temperature (to a lesser extent, to the density and chemical composition) of stellar atmospheres. The spectral class of a star does not depend on its mass.
