These diagrams turned out to be very convenient and visual in all cases of interaction of particles through an electric field. Feynman used the concept of the electric field as a continuum of virtual photons and developed a quantum electrodynamic method for calculating the probabilistic production and absorption of photons. At first glance, these concepts seem simple and obvious, but behind this simplicity lies a deep understanding of the essence of electromagnetic interactions. Together with M. Gell-Mann in 1958, he developed a quantitative theory of weak interactions, and in the 60s he proposed the idea of ​​the internal structure of nucleons, which led Gell-Mann to the creation of the quark model of hadrons. Feynman made a great contribution to the development of quantum mechanical concepts of superfluidity and superconductivity. In quantum mechanics, he created a method of integration along trajectories, which is increasingly used in various issues every year. This list alone is already enough to rank Feynman among the most outstanding physicists of the twentieth century. But this is not the only reason why he became a legend and remained in the memory of his contemporaries. Here are brief extracts from Feynman's biography. His parents were Jewish immigrants who moved to the United States at the turn of the 20th century, his father from Minsk, his mother from a small Polish town. They lived in Queens, New York's largest suburban borough. Thanks to hard work and abilities, the family became quite wealthy. Feynman's father did not have a systematic education, but was able to convey to his son (and daughter) an interest in natural phenomena and a desire to explain them. From his mother, Richard inherited an inexhaustible sense of humor in relation to all life's collisions. The parents' secret dream was for Richard to become

Moreover, not only the son, but also their daughter Joan also devoted her life to science and became an outstanding astrophysicist. At an early age, Richard became interested in physics, and at the age of 13 he gained fame (and even income) as a repairman of radios, the structure of which could then be understood “at a glance,” since all their parts (radio tubes, coils and capacitors) were large. As Feynman later joked, his main difficulty was opening the cover of the radio. He amazed customers by the fact that, having opened the device, he did not just climb inside with a screwdriver, but began to think (and sometimes walk around the room while doing so). At the age of 15, Richard independently mastered trigonometry and the basics of mathematical analysis. Despite this, he was denied admission to Columbia University in New York because of his nationality (in the 1930s, many US universities had quotas for admitting people of certain nationalities, including Jews).

Feynman began studying physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The sophomore's scientific papers appeared in many physics journals. He completed his master's and doctoral studies at the prestigious Princeton University and began teaching physics there.
