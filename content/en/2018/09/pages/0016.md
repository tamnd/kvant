FROM THE HISTORY OF SCIENCE

Man-legend of the 20th century

(on the 100th anniversary of the birth of Richard Feynman)

L. BELOPUKHOV

IN 2018, unnoticed by the scientific community, let alone the general public, the centenary of the birth of Richard Phillips Feynman passed. Almost everyone who comes into contact with physics knows about the famous “Feynman Lectures on Physics”. In our country in 1965–67, these nine small books were a subscription publication, and when they appeared on the public market, they instantly disappeared from the shelves of the scientific departments of bookstores. In fact, “Lectures” are notes edited by Feynman of lectures on general physics that he gave in the early 60s to junior students at the California Institute of Technology, the famous Caltech. Physicists Robert Layton and Matthew Sands, who were present at all lectures, carefully took notes on their colleague’s lectures, and Feynman considered them full co-authors of his course. In 1963–64, the Lectures were published as a three-volume course in the United States and England, and then translated and published in 16 additional countries. A little later, in 1965, a natural addition to the Lectures was published - a collection of problems and exercises that were used in student seminars at Caltech. The total circulation of the Lectures in English alone exceeded one and a half million copies.
