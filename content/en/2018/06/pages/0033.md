Where is the error?

(Logic, combinatorics, probability)

THE SUGGESTED TEXTS MAY HAVE ERRORS (both in the terms of the tasks and in the answers and solutions). Try to find them where they are. Problem 1. Knights and knaves live on the island. One day, 12 islanders gathered around a round table. Each of them said: “Both my neighbors are liars.” How many liars were there at the table? Answer: 6. Solution. Everyone sitting at the table could not be liars, since then for each of them the statement would be true. This means there was at least one knight at the table. His right neighbor is a liar, since the knight told the truth. The liar's right neighbor is a knight, since the liar lied. The right neighbor of this knight is a liar, and so on. Thus, knights and knaves sit through one, therefore, there were 6 knaves. The reasoning looks plausible. Are there any errors? Problem 2. Consider “words” of length 100, composed only of the letters A, B and C. Which words are larger: those in which each of the fragments AB and AC occurs an even number of times, or those in which each of such fragments occurs an odd number of times? Answer: equally. Solution. Consider a word in which both fragments occur an odd number of times. Let's replace the first of the fragments with another (AB with AC or vice versa).
