The set $C_{1} C_{2}$ is, of course, convex. Problem 5. Prove that the length of the boundary of the set $C_{1} C_{2}$ is the half-sum of the lengths of the boundaries of the sets $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$. Probability and last digit of the product

Our reader E. Znak noticed and proved that the number “1” has the following interesting property: it is no more probable than any other number, as the last digit of the product of two random natural numbers, and it is no less probable than any other number, as the last digit of the power of a random natural number with a random natural exponent. First of all, we need to decide in what sense we are talking about random natural numbers. We will assume that the probability of choosing a number with a given last digit is the same for all digits and is equal to 1/10. Similarly, the probability of choosing a number with a given remainder when divided by 4 is 1/4, etc. This arrangement is fully consistent with the following model for choosing a natural number: from a given large number of N consecutive natural numbers, one is chosen with equal probability so that the probability of choosing a particular number is 1/N.
