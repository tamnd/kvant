PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2514–M2517 were offered at the final stage of the XLIV All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics.

Problem M2514–M2517, F2521–F2524

M2514. Given natural numbers a and b. Prove that there are infinitely many positive integers n such that the number $a^{n} 1$ is not divisible by $n^{b} 1$. A. Golovanov

M2515. In the card game, each card is assigned a numerical value from 1 to 100, with each card beating the smaller one, with one exception: 1 beats 100. The player knows that there are 100 cards with different values ​​lying face down in front of him. The dealer, who knows the order of these cards, can tell the player about any pair of cards which one beats which one. Prove that the dealer can make one hundred such messages, so that the player can then know the exact value of each card. I. Bogdanov, K. Knop, Y. Kuzmenko

$M2516^{*}$. Initially, a natural number is written on the board. Then, every second, the product of all its non-zero digits is added to the current number. Prove that there is a natural number a such that the addition of the number a happens an infinite number of times. D. Krachun
