It dawned on the capable student: even if the accepted assumption that the magnitude of the friction force is independent of the speed of the body is correct, then it is still necessary to take into account the dependence on the direction of the latter. This means that the ball, moving to the left under the action of the restoring force of the elasticity of the spring and having overshot the equilibrium position (x = 0) due to inertia, will compress the spring, stop for a moment, and then begin to move to the right. But at the same time, the sign of the friction force will also change: now it will be directed to the left, again opposite to the direction of velocity. So in equation (1), it is more correct to write the friction force as follows: $F_{тр} F_{x} mg_{y}$ P sign $v_{x}$, where sign $v_{x}$ is a discontinuous function, meaning simply “sign $v_{x}$”. It is also called stepwise, piecewise constant, relay (switching). The graph of this friction force is shown in figure 2, c. Thus, the vector of the dry friction force is very dependent on speed, although one of the physical models assumes the constancy of its modulus. According to figure 2,a (see colored lines), having completed a finite number of oscillations, the ball will find itself in the stagnation band x x x with zero speed (point C in Figures 2,a,b), while part of the energy stored at the initial moment will remain in the form of potential energy of the still slightly compressed spring. At all points of this band, the spring deformation is not enough to overcome the friction force. A finite number of oscillations is a characteristic sign of the presence of dry friction.
