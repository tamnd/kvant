MANY YEARS AGO, an imported bottle of some strong drink fell into my hands completely by accident. Naturally, it was very different from the unchanged glass containers of Soviet vodka, as I would say today, in its design, but most of all I was surprised by some kind of knocking sound when shaking the empty bottle. Having disassembled the cork, I found a nice glass ball in it, which I was sorry to throw away. Someone came up with it and did it so carefully! I was then working on a shift for gifted children at a health camp and decided to make a percussion musical instrument from bottles with different amounts of water - that’s why I needed empty bottles. And different people constantly came to the ravine under our mountain to scatter empty dishes in the forest. It must be said that our compatriots have strong principles in life and always carefully close the bottle when it can no longer serve them, and only then throw it into the bushes. Having brought these various bottles to the camp in a quiet hour and washed them, I began to disassemble the corks. Many of the corks had balls in them - some were plastic, some were glass. The plastic ones didn’t look so beautiful, but they had cavities and you could “play” with them around Archimedes’ law - their average density turned out to be close to the density of cold water. It didn’t occur to me for a long time what glass balls are good for. But finally, a “physics job” was found for them. Everything turned out to be very simple. In a small elegant “penicillin bottle,” as we called it about sixty years ago, I poured sifted dry river sand, filling the bottle about halfway, put in a glass ball and closed it tightly with a plastic stopper. This means that a denser glass ball floats up in plain sand, violating the law of universal gravitation! Not every physical experience or simple observation can be immediately explained. So in this bubble with a ball there may be some secret and a corresponding physical problem hidden. It seems to me that inertia is to blame for everything. A glass ball is denser than dry sand, and inertia helps maintain its position in space during a sharp push. And this is where the great Force of Friction comes into play. Below the ball, the sand is a little denser than above it, where the layer is thinner and there is no hard bottom of the bubble. I suspect that because of this, the friction force in this place is a little weaker. With each push, the competition “inertia-friction-gravity” gives a tiny advantage of inertia, the sand flows around the ball, and now it has already missed the top of the ball, and then the movement becomes easier and easier.
