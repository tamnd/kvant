PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2531 and M2532 were offered at the III Olympiad of Megacities.

M2530. In an acute triangle, the points $A_{1}, B_{1}, C_{1}$ are taken on the sides BC, CA, AB, respectively, so that the segments $AA_{1}, BB_{1}, CC_{1}$ intersect at one point. It is known that BC $AA_{1} CA$ $BB_{1} AB$ $CC_{1}$. Prove that $AA_{1}, BB_{1}, CC_{1}$ are the altitudes of the triangle. P. Kozhevnikov

M2532. Anya and Maxim play a game on a checkered board 100 and 100. First, Anya fills the squares of the board with integers from 1 to 10,000 so that each number occurs once. Maxim then places a chip on one of the cells of the leftmost column of his choice. Then, in a few moves, he moves the chip to the rightmost column. In one move, he can move a piece from a cell to any cell adjacent to it along the top or side. For each cell visited (including the initial one), Maxim pays Anya the number of coins that is written on this cell. Maxim wants to pay as little as possible, but Anya wants to get as much as possible. How many coins

$M2533^{*}$. Given a tetrahedron. Let a and b be the lengths of two opposite edges of the tetrahedron, and D and E be the values ​​of the dihedral angles at these edges; c and d are the lengths of the edges in another pair of opposite edges, and J and G are the values ​​of the dihedral angles at these edges. Prove that a + b = c + d if and only if D E J G. M. Panov

F2537. Two identical bars are coupled by a rigid weightless rod and move along a smooth horizontal surface of the table (see figure). At some point, the bars begin to drive onto the rough area of ​​the table. Find the coefficient of friction P of the bars on a rough surface if they had a speed v at the beginning of their movement, and after passing through the rough section of the table they move with a speed u. The length of each block is l, the rod is s, and the rough section of the table is l + s. A. Ivanov
