KVANT 2018/№10

The huge building near the center is impressive. But this is just a prefabricated workshop in which individual bulky parts of the cryostat are built: the tray, the lid and the walls of the refrigeration “thermos”. It turns out that reliable welding of these parts, consisting of dozens of individual steel sheets, is possible only in their horizontal position with each weld carefully checked. Once welded, a giant crane will move these parts to their permanent location. This year, the reinforced concrete base for the cryostat tray has been fully prepared, which is already ready in the neighboring building and is waiting to be installed in its place. Preparing the reinforced concrete base and the entire foundation for the reactor is an extremely responsible operation. After all, the multi-thousand-ton cryostat and vacuum chamber should not experience the slightest deformation. All possible natural vibrations of the soil must be absorbed and dampened by the foundation and foundation. Between the cryostat tray and its reinforced concrete base there must be space for installing various technological devices. Therefore, the foundation under the cryostat has a very complex shape, resembling a crown. The builders called him that. This summer, the ITER press announced the completion of the “crown” construction. It is made of completely unusual concrete. Its density is one and a half times greater than that of ordinary building concrete. For the first time, the heavy gravel used for it was delivered from Lapland. The problem of feeding a plasma cord with fuel - a mixture of deuterium and tritium - is very difficult. ITER, like all previous tokamaks, is a pulsed energy generator. Each pulse lasts about 8 minutes. During this time, one gram of “fuel” “burns”, such is the mass of the plasma cord. And in terms of energy released, this gram is equivalent to 10 tons of coal. Towards the end of the pulse period, the temperature in the cord begins to drop - replenishment is necessary.

But at the same time, the vacuum in the entire cryostat cannot be broken. The next portion of fuel in the form of “ice” balls with a temperature of 10 K is shot into the vacuum chamber. Simultaneously with the balls, a pulse of a high-frequency electromagnetic field generated by special devices - electron cyclotron resonators (gyrotrons) is introduced into the chamber. A field with a frequency of 50 MHz is introduced into the chamber through 60 windows made of polycrystalline artificial diamond (it is transparent for this frequency). Absorbing the energy of the field, the fuel balls are accelerated, ionized and then, under the influence of a magnetic field, fall into the plasma cord, heating it to the temperature at which a thermonuclear reaction begins. Again, the cord has 150 million degrees and 46 thousand amperes. The water flows inside the “petals” of the vacuum chamber are heated again to a boil. And this is repeated hundreds and thousands of times until the time comes to replace the beryllium screens in the blankets that transfer the neutron energy to the water and liquid nitrogen that cools the cryostat. One cannot help but dwell on the task of creating magnetic fields of tens of teslas in the chamber. For such fields, the current in the magnet windings must be at least 60,000 amperes.
