"Ghost" pipes

E.BAKAEV, A.VESNIN

The SENJU THERMAL POWER PLANT in Tokyo operated from 1926 to 1963. Its “ghost” chimneys remained in many photographs and films of the time and became one of the symbols of the region. The power plant had 4 huge chimneys, 83.5 meters high and 5–6 meters wide. At the time of construction, they were the tallest structure in Tokyo. Why did they come to be called “ghostly”? Two possible explanations are offered. According to the first, the smoke from these pipes looked like a ghost - this power plant was a spare one, and the smoke appeared rarely and unexpectedly. According to another version, the pipes were so named because of their interesting property: depending on which side you looked at the power plant from a distance, it seemed that there were 4, 3, 2 or even only 1 pipes.
