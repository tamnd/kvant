M2519. Let us call a method of partitioning a set of 2n natural numbers into n pairs square-free if in no pair the product of numbers is the square of a natural number. It is known that for given 2n different natural numbers there is a square-free way of partitioning into pairs. Prove that in this case there are at least n! square-free ways to partition these 2n numbers into pairs.

For each natural number m we associate a set of prime numbers P m, each of which is included in the expansion of the number m to an odd power. For example, $P 2^{3}$ $7^{8}$11^2; 11 ` $, P 3^{4}$ $5^{6}$ . Obviously, the product of natural a and b is a perfect square if and only if the sets P a and P b coincide. Let's color the given 2n numbers in this way: numbers for which the sets P m coincide are colored in the same color, and numbers for which the sets P m are different are colored in different colors. Since there is a square-free partition into pairs, at most n numbers are colored in each color. Let us prove the following lemma, from which the solution of the problem immediately follows.

Lemma. Let 2n (different) objects be colored in several colors so that at most n objects are colored in each color. Then there are at least n! ways to split these objects into multi-colored pairs (i.e., so that each pair contains objects of different colors). Proof of the lemma. Let's carry out induction on n. The base n = 1 is obvious. Let us prove the transition from n – 1 to n. Let's choose the color c that occurs among these objects the greatest number of times, i.e. Let there be k objects of color c, and at most k objects of any other color. Let's take object A of color c and select for it a pair B of any color d other than c; this can be done in 2n – k ways. Moreover, 2n k t n, since k d n. By removing the pair A, B, we get a set of 2 n 1 objects. Let us check that their coloring satisfies the conditions of the lemma. Indeed, let us assume the opposite, and objects of some color e are not less than n. This means that for each way to select object B for the resulting set of 2 n 1 objects, there are at least n 1! partitions into multi-colored pairs.
