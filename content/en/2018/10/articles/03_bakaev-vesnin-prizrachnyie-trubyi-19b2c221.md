"Ghost" pipes

E.BAKAEV, A.VESNIN

The SENJU THERMAL POWER PLANT in Tokyo operated from 1926 to 1963. Its “ghost” chimneys remained in many photographs and films of the time and became one of the symbols of the region. The power plant had 4 huge chimneys, 83.5 meters high and 5–6 meters wide. At the time of construction, they were the tallest structure in Tokyo. Why did they come to be called “ghostly”? Two possible explanations are offered. According to the first, the smoke from these pipes looked like a ghost - this power plant was a spare one, and the smoke appeared rarely and unexpectedly. According to another version, the pipes were so named because of their interesting property: depending on which side you looked at the power plant from a distance, it seemed that there were 4, 3, 2 or even only 1 pipes. astrophysics

“Astron” is Greek for “star,” but astrophysics studies more than just stars. However, today, in the final astronomical issue of Kaleidoscope, we will talk mainly about them. Let's hope that in these issues we were able to show the close connection between physics and all branches of astronomy, including those devoted to stars. Indeed, how can one understand the processes occurring in stars without knowing the properties of light, electromagnetic and gravitational interactions, without understanding how matter can behave if it finds itself in extreme conditions not found on Earth? Of course, physics has made a huge contribution to the study of cosmic objects of various scales, given an idea of ​​the unity of the world around us, and made it possible to build a picture of the Universe. But the opposite is also true: new information about distant stars has added to our knowledge of what can be happening on Earth, and about ourselves and our capabilities. Judge for yourself the progress that contributes to these hopes. The Voyager 1 probe, launched in 1977, “only” reached the boundaries of the solar system for 35 years. And today “nanoprobes” are already being designed that can reach our closest star, Alpha Centauri, located thousands of times further away, in just 20 years! The scientific assault on space, which will inevitably be followed by its exploration, continues. You may well take part in it. Per aspera ad astra!
