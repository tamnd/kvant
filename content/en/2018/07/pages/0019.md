Let's assume that at some step Bob still wins, i.e. an oriented cycle C has appeared. Let us find the leftmost of the vertical grid lines on which there are nodes of the cycle C, and among these nodes we select the lowest one. Let us denote this node by X, i.e. X – “lowest of the leftmost”. Similarly, we define the “topmost among the rightmost” nodes of the cycle C. If X lies strictly to the left of the midline, then X is the vertex of some LN-corner in which both segments are oriented. By the definition of node X, both of these segments are included in the cycle C. But, according to Anna's strategy, these segments are oriented in such a way that they cannot be in an oriented cycle. Otherwise, node Y lies strictly to the right of the midline, and similar reasoning for node Y leads to a contradiction. So Bob will never win. The problem is solved. A simpler version of this problem can be achieved if Bob goes first. In this case, it is enough to use a pair of initial moments, both rods are directed to the right. What trajectory does the point of intersection of the rods O follow? Find the speed and acceleration of this point after time t = S ( 6 Z ) after the start of motion. Justify your answer.

Let after the start of the movement (when the rods were directed to the right) some time t has passed, which is less than the time of half a revolution of the right rod, i.e. t < S ( 2 Z ). Consider the triangle ABO, where A and B are the ends of the rods around which they rotate, O is the point of intersection of the rods at this moment. Obviously, this triangle is isosceles, in which AB = BO. Indeed, since the angle ABO is equal to S - 2 Z t and the angle OAB is equal to Z t, then the angle AOB is equal to S - Z t - ( S - 2 Z t ) = Z t. Thus, in triangle OAB, the angles AOB and OAB are equal, and therefore this triangle is isosceles, in which AB = BO at any time. So the distance from point B to the point of intersection of the rods O remains the same during the movement and equal to l.
