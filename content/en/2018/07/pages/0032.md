The case of two piles

If m = 1, then there is only one pile and the beginner wins by taking all the stones from it on his first move. If there are two piles (m = 2), then the strategy can be obtained by arranging the “+” and “–” signs. The state of the game is determined by two numbers - the number of stones in the first and second piles, so each state can be assigned to a cell of a two-dimensional table. The horizontal coordinate of the cell corresponds to the number of stones in the first pile, and the vertical coordinate - in the second. After the player’s move, either the first or second coordinate of the cell decreases, i.e. the cell corresponding to the new state is located either lower or to the left of the original one. Arranging the signs “+” and “–” according to the rules formulated above, we obtain the table shown at figure 1.

It can be seen that the minuses are located along the main diagonal, i.e. in cells whose first coordinate coincides with the second. Therefore, the rule “Bet on minus!” leads to the following strategy. If the piles are different, the first player can ensure victory for himself. With his first move, he must equalize the number of stones in both piles, and then he should repeat the opponent’s move, but on the other pile, leaving after his move piles with an equal number of stones in them. Neem with three piles

Now consider the case m = 3. Then the state of the game is determined by three numbers, and to develop a strategy, you should fill in the cells of the three-dimensional table with “+” and “–” signs. The table cell with coordinates (a, b, c), where a, b, c are non-negative integers, corresponds to the state of the Nim game, in which the first pile contains a, the second contains b, and the third contains c stones. Of course, after spending some time, you can, according to the rules formulated above, arrange the signs in the layers of the table and thereby develop a strategy for playing Nim with three piles (given fixed a, b, c in the initial position). Let's do it differently. Let us assume that the “+” and “–” signs in the cells of a three-dimensional table have already been placed in accordance with the above rules. Let's prove the following $лемму.^{Лемма} 1.$ In any table column in which two coordinates are fixed and the third takes all non-negative values, there is exactly one cell with a “–” sign. Proof. Let the first two coordinates a and b be fixed. First, let us show by contradiction that a column cannot contain two or more cells with a “–” sign. Let each of the two cells with coordinates $a, b, c_{1}$ and $a, b, c_{2}$ contain a “–” and $c_{1} c_{2}$ sign. Then, by taking $c_{2} c_{1}$ stones from the third pile, in one move the game can be transferred from the state corresponding to cell $a, b, c_{2}$ to the state specified by cell $a, b, c_{1}$, i.e. from minus to minus. We have a contradiction: all moves from a cell with a “–” sign must lead to cells with a “+” sign. Therefore, a column cannot have more than one minus sign.
