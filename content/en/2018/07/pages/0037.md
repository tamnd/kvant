About plant patterns and good models

V. PTUSHENKO, A. BAZYKINA

ON PAGE 4 OF THE COVER OF THE EIGHTH issue «Kvant» for last year there was a photograph of a simple pattern formed by moss under a wooden bench. In the accompanying text it was mentioned that the reason for its appearance can be various factors: the availability of light, moisture and the effect of trampling. This “toy” situation is actually a good model for much larger and more complex problems of ecology and biogeography - problems of the distribution of different species of plants and animals across regions of the Earth. For some plants, their distribution area is limited by the amount of available moisture in the form of precipitation during the growing season. Others have adapted to dry conditions through deep root systems or a very short development period, but require specific temperatures throughout the year. Still others are “tied” to the distribution of their pollinators or seed dispersers (a classic example is coconut palms, which grow mainly in coastal areas and have fruits that float freely on the water and are carried far by ocean currents). In addition, competition with other species plays an important role: remember how plants from “warm regions” grow well at higher latitudes in open ground in botanical gardens, where the competition factor is excluded. However, in all these cases, the area of ​​distribution is determined by the play of a number of factors. And it is not always possible to single out the main one - the so-called limiting factor. Let's look again at the example we started with: the uneven growth of moss under the bench (Fig.1). How could one determine what is the limiting factor for moss growth here: light or moisture?
