It's interesting that...

... the first who tried to argue that the center of the Universe is not the Earth, but the Sun, was the ancient Greek astronomer Aristarchus of Samos in the 3rd century BC. – 18 centuries before Copernicus! Aristarchus' heliocentric doctrine brought upon him the charge of apostasy, and he was forced to leave Athens. ... to find out the true motion of the planets among the stars, Archimedes combined observations with calculations carried out with the mechanical model of the solar system he created. However, in solving such a difficult problem of celestial mechanics, he, like his predecessors, failed. ... the heliocentric model achieved complete victory only in the 17th century with the advent of Kepler’s theory of elliptical trajectories of planets, supported by calculations using integrals and verified by direct measurements using a telescope.

⟦column⟧

... both Kepler and Galileo were professional astrologists, drawing up horoscopes both for clients and for their loved ones. At that time, this was common practice among astronomers and doctors, and astrology was not considered a pseudoscience, but rather an art. ... in 1577, 30-year-old Tycho Brahe, observing another comet, for the first time managed to calculate the distance to it. At this time, the mother of six-year-old Kepler, who had unusually sharp eyesight, showed her son this comet as the greatest miracle of nature. ... Aristotle believed that comets arise from “earthly vapors”, and their tails are flames driven by the wind; For a long time, astronomers believed that comets fly through the solar system only once. Finally, Newton established that these celestial bodies move in closed but highly elongated elliptical orbits. ... the theory of the motion of the Moon turned out to be one of the most difficult problems of celestial mechanics, the solution of which took, counting only Newton, two and a half centuries and required the participation of such outstanding scientists as Euler. ... the presence of the equatorial “bulge” of the Earth must be taken into account when calculating the trajectory of spacecraft and even the orbit of the Moon. ... according to the general theory of relativity, any planet describes a slowly rotating ellipse in its motion. This effect was first discovered on Mercury, and with the development of radar - on other planets, which experimentally confirmed the theory with great accuracy.
