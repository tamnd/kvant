Second way. This solution uses rotation. Let's explain what it is. Let's make a copy of the drawing on a transparent film and put it on the drawing so that the original and the copy match. Let's stick a needle into the table, piercing the drawing and copy. If you now move the film, the copy of the drawing on it will rotate around the point O into which the needle is stuck (Fig.1,d). Let us list the main properties of rotation. Let the rotation be made around point O, point A goes to point A c, point B goes to B c. Then: 1) Angles AOA c and BOB c are equal, in other words, all points rotate by the same angle. It is called the rotation angle (Fig.1,e). 2) The figures transform into equal figures. Specifically, OA OA c, AB A c B c, ' OAB ' OA c B c. 3) The angle between straight lines (more precisely, rays) AB and A c B c is equal to the angle of rotation.

Armed with the properties of rotation, let's return to our problem. Let's rotate the structure by 90q around the common vertex of the squares (Fig.1,з). Then in each square one vertex will go into another (because the neighboring sides of the square are equal and perpendicular). Accordingly, one dotted segment will go into another. Problem 2. Now consider the combination of three squares shown in figure 2,a. Prove that the center of the blue square is the midpoint of the segment connecting the vertices of the two red squares. In our construction there are the same pairs of squares, which means there are also pairs of equal triangles corresponding to them. Combining two such pairs, we obtain that the three triangles are equal (Fig.2,b).
