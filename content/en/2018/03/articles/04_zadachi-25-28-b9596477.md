25. a) Petya writes the letters A or B in each square of the 100 ¥ 100 board so that there are equal numbers of letters A and B. Then Vasya moves a piece along this board, moving it all the time only to the cell adjacent to the side and each time writing down which letter it is on. Can Vasya always place a chip in such a way and go around all the cells exactly once, so that the resulting sequence of letters is read equally from left to right and from right to left? b) The same thing, but the board is 101 ¥ 101 and there are one more letters A than letters B. G. Nikitin

26. Each vertex of a rectangle (not a square) was reflected symmetrically with respect to the diagonal not passing through this vertex. We got four points. a) Prove that these four points form a rectangle. b) Can they be the vertices of a rectangle equal to the original one? c) Can they be the vertices of a rectangle similar but not equal to the original one? S.Dvoryaninov

27. “I once divided one natural number by another with a remainder,” Petya told Kolya. - When I divided the square of the first number by the second, the remainder turned out to be twice as large as it was during the first division. And when I divided the cube of the first number by the second, the remainder became three times larger. - Well, you’re the one pouring it in, this can’t happen! - exclaimed Kolya. – I actually had a similar story. I also divided one natural number by another with a remainder. And when I divided the cube of the first number by the second, the remainder turned out to be twice the original, and when I divided the square of the first number by the second, the remainder became three times larger. – Now you’re composing! - Petya said. Who is right in each case? I. Akulich

28. Which natural numbers are greater: 10-digit numbers with a sum of digits of 9 or 9-digit numbers with a sum of digits of 10? How long? S. Kostin
