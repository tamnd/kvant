SCIENCE NEWS

The Birth of Gravitational Wave Astronomy

L. BELOPUKHOV

In 2017, the Nobel Prize in Physics “for decisive contributions to the development of the LIGO (Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory) detector and the observation of gravitational waves” was received by American scientists Rainer Weiss, Barry Barish and Kip Thorne. One billion three hundred million years ago, at a great distance from the Earth, two solid black holes, in their circling around the Universe, came to such a distance that the force of their gravitational interaction began to actively attract them to each other. One of them had a mass 36 times greater than the mass of the Sun, the other - 29 times more. As they approached, they rotated around their center of mass so that they moved as if in a spiral. The speed of this rotation eventually became enormous - one revolution every few milliseconds. And finally, the black holes merged, emitting a short but terrible sound.

Rainer Weiss, Kip Thorne and Barry Barish
