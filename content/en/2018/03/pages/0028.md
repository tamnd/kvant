What, then, is optimality expressed in? Here's the thing. It is easy to see that no matter how we cut the cheese, this cut will always be correct for small R and incorrect for large ones. One could say it more precisely. Let a piece of cheese be cut into a finite number of pieces in an arbitrary manner, but in compliance with rule (i); Let's calculate all possible ratios of the weights of the pieces after the first, second,......, last cutting - this is a finite number of positive numbers. Let $R_{0}$ be the smallest of these numbers. Then it is obvious that our cutting was correct for any $R R_{0}$ and incorrect for all others. (The same applies if at first there was not one piece, but several.) The advantage of optimal cutting over others (its optimality) is that for optimal cutting the number $R_{0}$ is the largest possible. Thus, if we need to solve an inverse problem (the number R is not given, but the cutting method is specified and we need to find out for which R it is correct), then the optimal method is preferable: if some R is suitable for another method, then it is also suitable for the optimal one (but not vice versa). And one more important note: the definition of optimality does not guarantee either existence or uniqueness: the optimal cutting method may not exist at all or there may be many of them. Suppose, for example, that at first there is only one piece of cheese. Then it can be optimally cut into two pieces: cutting exactly in half will be optimal (the ratio of the smallest piece to the largest is 1, and it certainly cannot be greater). We claim that if s d r, then there is an optimal way for this, which is as follows: you need to sequentially divide each existing piece exactly in half, starting with the largest and then in descending order - first piece a, then b, then c and so on. Indeed, with this method, for example, after two cuts, the largest piece is c, and the smallest is b 2.
