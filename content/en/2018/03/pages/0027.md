If cheese can be correctly cut into (2n – 1) parts, then it can also be correctly cut into 2n parts. To prove this, we introduce the concept of optimal cutting. Let us first note that the condition “the ratio of the weights of any two pieces must be greater than R” is sufficient to check for the smallest and largest of the available pieces; if it is true for them, then for all others it is carried out automatically. Let some natural numbers s, r be given; Let us assume that the cheese is already somehow correctly cut into r pieces, and we want to make an additional s cuts, i.e. get r s pieces. Of course, this can be done in different ways. Let us call a cutting method optimal if after each of the s cuts two conditions are simultaneously satisfied, namely the minimax condition: the smallest piece obtained by this moment is not smaller than that obtained by any other method of correct cutting (“the minimum piece is maximal,” hence the concept of “minimax”), the maximin condition: the largest piece obtained by this moment is not larger than that obtained by any other method of correct cutting. Obviously, if these conditions are met simultaneously, then at each step the ratio of the weight of the smallest piece to the weight of the largest will be greater (or equal) than at any other. It immediately follows that if a correct way of cutting exists at all, then the optimal way is also correct. Before moving on, let's make a few comments about optimality itself.
