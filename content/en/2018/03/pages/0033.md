The tightrope walker is blindfolded. And the third settled down... in a basin! Sitting in it, he moves forward with the help of his legs. But everyone has an indispensable balance pole in their hands. Tsovkri produced many tightrope walkers who performed all over the world and brought glory to our country. Then one of us remembered about modern cable cars, popular in the mountains. Basically they come in two types. Some are chairlifts. On other, more powerful ones, entire cars are suspended from ropes, like tram cars. Only the wheels are not below, but above the carriage, or rather, above the rope. And they roll along the rope, like on a rail. And here everyone can take a ride - either in a chair or in a trailer. Quite comfortable and without any risk. But then our teacher Ivan Petrovich suggested that we think: how else can we...

but walking on a tightrope? Let's say over the river. The duration of the project is a week. The task is not for everyone, but for those who want it. A week later, at a meeting of the school physics club, we defended our projects. I suggested that everyone stepping onto the rope be given a balloon filled with helium in their hand (such balloons can often be seen now). There were other developments as well. But everyone recognized Tolya Vtulkin’s design as the best. The operating principle of its design is clear from figure. A small two-wheeled cart (like a bicycle) rides along a rope (more precisely, along a metal cable). Each wheel has a groove in which the rope is located. And the cart does not fall left and right because a long rod with a balancing weight at the end is rigidly connected to it. It is clear that for any deviation of the trolley from the vertical plane, the counterweight load rises upward. As a result, the center of gravity of the entire system rises, therefore, the initial equilibrium position of the cart is stable. The person on the rope must hold on to this rod and at the same time push the cart forward. Tolik also proposed simple calculations, from which it follows that, wanting to reduce the weight of the load, we must increase the length of the rod. And vice versa. If the rod is short, then the counterweight must be large enough to ensure stability. This counterweight acts in the same way as the keel of a yacht, keeping it from capsizing. - So this turns out to be a staff for moving along a rope! Perhaps it’s not that difficult to use! – this was the result of our discussion. “You’re welcome,” Tolya agreed with us. If you want, say staff, if you want, tightrope walker!
