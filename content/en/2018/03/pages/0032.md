Rope walker Toli Vtulkina

S. DVORYANINOV

LAST WEEK WE ENTIRE CLASS WENT TO THE circus. There are a lot of impressions! On the way back, of course, they discussed what they had seen. What impressed everyone the most were the tightrope walkers. Someone remembered that in 2012, American aerial acrobat Nik Wallenda was the first in the world to walk a tightrope over Niagara Falls, and soon (without a safety net) conquered the Grand Canyon. A few years later, in 2016, the famous Chinese tightrope walker Adili Vuxor and his partner walked 800 meters on a tightrope over the Yellow River, walking towards each other. In this show, Vuxor's partner lay down on the rope at the moment of the meeting, and the rope walker crossed over him, stepping on his chest. A long pole helps a person maintain balance on a rope. The rope walker holds the pole horizontally in front of him. When tilting to the left, this pole should be moved to the right. And vice versa: when tilted to the right, the pole moves to the left. As a result, during the movement of the tightrope walker, the center of gravity of the “man plus pole” system invariably ends up above the rope. The men perform their tricks in calm, windless weather. Sometimes, to hold the rope along which the daredevil moves, additional stabilizing ropes are used to prevent swinging. It’s not only dangerous to walk on a swinging rope, but it’s also difficult to land on it, or rather “get on the rope”. Even birds know about this and therefore try to perch on that part of the wire that is closer to the pole - here the amplitude of vibrations is less. Every person has an organ that ensures his balance. The name of this important organ is the vestibular apparatus. It is a system of channels filled with liquid. When you tilt or turn your head, fluid flows back and forth, nerve cells react to the change in pressure and transmit a signal to the brain. Even with our eyes closed, we understand what position we are in and therefore can move in complete darkness. But tightrope walking is not only entertainment or an exciting spectacle. Legends say that for the inhabitants of the mountains, the ability to walk on a tightrope was a vital necessity. Imagine that you need to move from one edge of the gorge to the other. Or overcome a stormy mountain river. You can't build a bridge here. But it is quite possible to stretch a rope by tying it to trees or securing it with stones. Residents of the Dagestan village of Tsovkra gained particular fame in their skill in walking on a rope. You can easily find both photos and videos on the Internet.
