physics+geography

Since time immemorial, man has sought to explore uncharted lands. Expanding their searches, travelers, sailors and traders needed reliable landmarks. Sooner or later they wondered how the surface of the Earth is structured, how heavy the Earth is, what shape it has and how it moves. To do this, they invented instruments to help determine location, drew geographical maps, looked for ways to measure time more and more accurately, not forgetting to look at the starry sky. But the task of finding out where and when something happens and how it moves belongs to the “department” of physics - any of its courses begins with questions related to space and time. Is this not what is being discussed in the above quotes from outstanding scientists and naturalists? The connection of science to the solution of applied issues has made it possible, in a historically short time, not only to move from primitive navigation instruments to the most accurate satellite systems, but also to unprecedentedly expand our knowledge of both the structure of the Earth and the entire world around it. Speaking today about the union of geography and physics, we could not ignore the participation of astronomy in it. Thus, we have risen one more step on the way to the already sky-high heights, where we will rush in the next issue of Kaleidoscope.

Here is one typical example. The “magic propeller” is described to us as a stick with notches and a propeller on a nail driven into its end. The propeller spins when another stick is moved along the notches. How simple it is! Yes, I’ll do it now... And, having found a stick, I begin to make notches on it with a knife. But I remember that about seventy years ago I not only saw this same trick at recess in the hands of a classmate, but also repeated it on the same day in front of my comrades in the yard. But you didn’t have to do anything: a safety razor blade was put on the neatly sharpened pencil, with which we sharpened this pencil, the pencil was clamped horizontally in the hollow between the thumb and forefinger, and the straightened middle and ring fingers had to be drawn with some effort along the desk or along the bench in the yard. The blade was spinning like an airplane propeller! However, it is difficult to describe the experience in this form. Now, if you see it once, it’s a different matter; any boy will understand and repeat it. But here is an even simpler and more modern option, which is available to any girl.
