STRAIGHT LINES PASSING THROUGH THE vertex of an angle and symmetrical with respect to its bisector we will call isogonals with respect to this angle. The article will discuss the following important theorem and its applications. Theorem on isogonals. Let OB and OC be isogontals of angle AOD. Lines AC and BD intersect at point Q, lines AB and CD intersect at point P. Then OP and OQ are also isangular with respect to the angle AOD. figure 1 shows two of the possible cases of the location of the isogonals OP and OQ relative to the angle AOD. For convenience, we will highlight the sides of the original angle in blue, and the segments lying inside equal angles (segments AB and CD or AC and BD) in red and green.

Solution. Let us apply the isogonal theorem to the unfolded angle $BA_{1} C$ and the isogonals $A_{1} C_{1}$ and $A_{1} B_{1}$. Then $A_{1} A$ and $A_{1} P$ are the coincident isogonals of the angle $BA_{1} C$, i.e. $A_{1} A$ – bisector of angle $BA_{1} C$. Therefore, $A_{1} A$ is the height of triangle ABC.
