LA B O R A T O R I A

Here is one typical example. The “magic propeller” is described to us as a stick with notches and a propeller on a nail driven into its end. The propeller spins when another stick is moved along the notches. How simple it is! Yes, I’ll do it now... And, having found a stick, I begin to make notches on it with a knife. But I remember that about seventy years ago I not only saw this same trick at recess in the hands of a classmate, but also repeated it on the same day in front of my comrades in the yard. But you didn’t have to do anything: a safety razor blade was put on the neatly sharpened pencil, with which we sharpened this pencil, the pencil was clamped horizontally in the hollow between the thumb and forefinger, and the straightened middle and ring fingers had to be drawn with some effort along the desk or along the bench in the yard. The blade was spinning like an airplane propeller! However, it is difficult to describe the experience in this form. Now, if you see it once, it’s a different matter; any boy will understand and repeat it. But here is an even simpler and more modern option, which is available to any girl. In the lid of some old medicine I saw a finished propeller with six beautiful blades and cut it out using a penknife and scissors. In the photograph (Fig.1) it can be seen next to other “equipment” - a fountain pen without a writing unit into which the propeller is inserted, and a cordless razor - it vibrates well and trembles when turned on.
