OLYMPICS

XLIX International Physics Olympiad

This year the International Physics Olympiad (IPO) was held in Lisbon, the capital of Portugal. 431 schoolchildren from 87 countries took part in the competition. The Russian national team was represented by: Grigory Bobkov - Chernogolovka, Moscow region, Moscow, school 1589, Vyacheslav Kuznetsov - Moscow, school 192, Stanislav Tsapaev - Moscow, school 1589, Alexey Shishkin - Moscow, school 1589, Zakhar Yakovlev - St. Petersburg, Academic Lyceum "Physical and Technical School". The Russian team was led by teachers of the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (state university) A.A. Voronov (team leader), M.N. Osin, V.A. Shevchenko and F.M. Tsybrov. The selection of candidates began a year before the Olympiad. More than 30 tenth-graders – winners and prize-winners of the final stage of the All-Russian Schoolchildren Olympiad in Physics last year – became candidates for the team. The preparation of the team and the selection of the final composition took place over the course of a year at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology and the Sirius Educational Center. At the training camp, the skills of experimental work on complex equipment were practiced, topics were studied that are not included in the school curriculum in physics, but are necessary.

Dima to participate in MFOs. The training system that has developed over the past years also includes participation in international Olympiads. The opportunity to try yourself in the “combat” conditions of the Olympics is an important stage of psychological preparation for the decisive competition. The guys participated in the European Olympiad “Romanian Masters of Physics”, in the International Experimental Olympiad in Physics and in the Asian Olympiad in Physics. At the Olympiad in Lisbon, participants were offered three theoretical problems and two experimental tasks. The presented tasks reflect the modern development of science and technology and reveal the connection between the natural sciences. For example, in the first theoretical task, participants had to process data from the LIGO experiment to detect gravitational waves. The topic of the third theoretical problem was the physics of living systems and a tumor growth model. In the experiment, participants examined a TFT field-effect transistor “printed” on paper.
