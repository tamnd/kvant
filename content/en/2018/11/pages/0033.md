MATHEMATICAL CIRCLE

Circle of ten points of an inscribed quadrilateral

IT IS KNOWN THAT IN ANY TRIANGLE, the midpoints of the sides, the bases of the altitudes and the midpoints of the segments connecting the vertices of the triangle with its orthocenter lie on one circle, which is called the Euler circle or the circle of nine points of the triangle. An analogue of a circle of nine points for an inscribed quadrilateral can be called the circle described in the following problem, proposed in 2011 at the VII International Zhautykov Olympiad (Kazakhstan):

⟦column⟧

The diagonals of the cyclic quadrilateral ABCD intersect at point K, points M and N are the midpoints of diagonals AC and BD, respectively. The circumcircles of triangles ADM and BCM intersect at points M and L. Prove that points K, L, M and N lie on the same circle (all these points are assumed to be distinct). In this article we will show that on this circle, which we will further denote as P, in addition to the points K, L, M and N, there are six more points. This allows us to call it the circle of ten points of an inscribed quadrilateral.
