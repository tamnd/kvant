PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2534-M2537 were offered at the XL Tournament of Cities.

Problem M2534–M2537, F2541–F2544

M2534. Knights, liars and singers live on the island; everyone knows about everyone, which one is which. They lined up all 2018 inhabitants of the island and asked everyone to answer “Yes” or “No” to the question: “On the island there are more knights than liars?” The residents answered in turn and so that others could hear them. The knights answered the truth, the liars lied. Each singer answered in the same way as most of those who answered before him, and if there were equal numbers of “Yes” and “No” answers, he gave any of these answers. It turned out that there were exactly 1009 “Yes” answers. What was the largest number of people who sang along among the island’s inhabitants? M.Kuznetsov

M2535. You need to write down a number of the form 77...7, using only sevens (they can be written one at a time or several in a row), and only addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and exponentiation, as well as parentheses, are allowed. For the number 77, the shortest notation is simply 77. Is there a number of the form 77...7 that can be written using these rules using fewer sevens than in its decimal notation? S.Markelov

paper checkered polygon M, which has more than one cell. It can be moved, without turning, in any direction to any distance, but so that after the shift it lies “in the cells”. If after the next shift exactly one cell of M lies on a white cell of the plane, this white cell is painted black and the next shift is made. Prove that there is a white cell that will never be painted black, no matter how many times we shift M according to the described rules. D. Zakharov

$М2537^{*}$. There are infinitely many points with natural coordinates marked on the number axis. When a wheel rolls along an axle, each marked point along which the wheel has passed leaves a dotted mark on it. Prove that you can choose a real R such that if you roll a wheel of radius R along the axis, starting from zero, then on each arc of the wheel of size $1 ^\circ$ there will be a trace of at least one marked point. I. Mitrofanov
