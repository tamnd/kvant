MATHEMATICAL CIRCLE

Circle of ten points of an inscribed quadrilateral

IT IS KNOWN THAT IN ANY TRIANGLE, the midpoints of the sides, the bases of the altitudes and the midpoints of the segments connecting the vertices of the triangle with its orthocenter lie on one circle, which is called the Euler circle or the circle of nine points of the triangle. An analogue of a circle of nine points for an inscribed quadrilateral can be called the circle described in the following problem, proposed in 2011 at the VII International Zhautykov Olympiad (Kazakhstan):

⟦column⟧

The diagonals of the cyclic quadrilateral ABCD intersect at point K, points M and N are the midpoints of diagonals AC and BD, respectively. The circumcircles of triangles ADM and BCM intersect at points M and L. Prove that points K, L, M and N lie on the same circle (all these points are assumed to be distinct). In this article we will show that on this circle, which we will further denote as P, in addition to the points K, L, M and N, there are six more points. This allows us to call it the circle of ten points of an inscribed quadrilateral. In what follows we will use the following notation: Z ABC – circumcircle of triangle ABC; S X – degree $точки^{X}$ (see [2]) relative to $Z^{ABC}$. Main theorem. We can say that a random weak sound that gets into the microphone is amplified by the electrical system of the computer and speakers, comes back to the microphone, still passes through the amplification path, and one of the frequencies in this sound is amplified especially significantly, coinciding with some natural frequency of the system. At resonance, the signal becomes very loud. This is positive feedback. But for high-quality sound of any instrument, the design must also have negative feedback - to prevent unnecessary resonances, otherwise the sound will only irritate the ear. In essence, this is an extremely simplified model of the wonderful invention of Lev Sergeevich Theremin (1896–1993), which was called the “theremin”, only in this device the feedback was carried out electrically. The approach of the performer's hand changed the capacitance of the oscillatory circuit in the generator, and its frequency increased or decreased. This was then translated into sound, and the instrument (without touching it!) was able to reproduce melodies with a unique sound.
