F1037. A small hole is made in the hollow sphere, through which a narrow parallel beam of light penetrates inside. The inner surface of the sphere reflects light in all directions equally (diffusely) and does not interfere with it. How will the illumination differ in this case at the point diametrically opposite the hole and at all other points of the sphere?

Since, according to the conditions, the inner surface of the sphere does not absorb the light energy incident on it, after repeated reflections, after some time the installation system is in equilibrium. In this case, each small section of the inner surface of the sphere will dissipate all the light energy that hits it evenly in all directions. As a result of establishing equilibrium, the illumination of all internal points of the sphere, except for the point opposite the hole, will be the same. At equilibrium, the total light energy coming to the hole per unit time from all internal points of the sphere will be equal to the energy entering through the hole into the extracting sphere through the hole into the extracting sphere. Thus, after equilibrium is established (namely, this is the situation that is considered in the problem), the hole will be equivalent to a device in which all the energy coming to it from all internal points of the sphere is sent in a narrow beam to a diametrically opposite point.

It is easy to understand that, due to the smallness of the hole, the total energy coming to it from all the internal surfaces of the sphere is equal to the total energy scattered in all directions by any part of the sphere equal in area to the hole (this follows from the inverse nature of the equilibrium state - if all the scattered rays are “forced” to move in the opposite directions, then nothing will change); this reasoning does not apply only to the point diametrically opposite the hole.

So, due to the difference, the shares of energy sent by all internal points both to the hole and to the point opposite the hole will be the same. In addition, exactly the same energy will come to the point opposite the hole and from the hole. Consequently, the illumination of the internal points of the spherical surface will be exactly half the illumination of the point on the part of the sphere opposite the hole. (The smaller the hole area, the more accurately the 1:2 ratio is met.)
