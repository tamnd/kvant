F1033. Throw a single-pod flexible rope of mass m and length l over a block of radius R, attached to two hooks on the ceiling located at a distance of 2R (see figure). On the axis of the block there is a load, the mass of which together with the block is M. There are no trenches between the rope and the block. Find the minimum tension force in the rope.

Consider the left half of the rope ABD (see figure). It is clear that the lower the point of the rope in section AB, the less the tension force at this point. Therefore, the point of the rope with minimal tension should be looked for somewhere in the BD section. Let's take an arbitrary point C in this area, located at a distance H from the ceiling.

The section of rope ABC is affected by the direction of the vertical upward force F₁ from the side of the cry, the tension force of the rope F at point C (from the part of the rope located to the right of point C), as well as the pressure force of the block on the rope, distributed over the entire surface of contact of the rope with the block.

Let us mentally move the section of rope ABC by a small distance x so that it takes a new position A₁B₁C₁ (see figure). With such a movement, force F₁ will do work F₁x, and force F will do negative work Fx. The pressure forces of the block on the rope will not do any work (at each point of the rope they are directed perpendicular to the direction of movement of this point). The potential energy of the displaced section of the rope will increase by the same amount as the potential energy of a piece of rope with length x and mass $\frac{m}{l} x$ will increase when crossing it from position CC₁ to position AA₁. According to the law of conservation of energy

$$F_1x - Fx = \frac{m}{l} xgH.$$
