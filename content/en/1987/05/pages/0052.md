⟦folio 50⟧

Correct solution to a geometric problem

Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
E. G. GOTMAN

As you know, when solving a geometric problem, it is necessary to consider all possible cases of the relative arrangement of the elements of a figure. The solution to a problem that allows for various configurations will be incomplete and erroneous if we limit ourselves to considering only one of the possible cases. When solving such problems, it is important not to make the mistake of mistaking the part for the general.

Teachers in class and members of the Olympiad jury encounter similar mistakes. Problems with incomplete and incorrect solutions can also be found in literature for students.

Let us give a few examples taken from various collections of Olympiad problems.

Task 1. Inscribe a rectangle with the smallest diagonal in this triangle ABC.

Solution. Let the rectangle KLMN be inscribed in triangle ABC so that its side KL lies on the line BC, and the vertices M and N lie on the sides AC and AB (Fig. 1,a). Let AD be the height of triangle ABC. Let's construct a triangle A'B'C' with a right angle C' so that the sides BC and B'C' are equal and located on the same straight line, and so that the lines AA' and BC are parallel (Fig. 1,b). Then A'C'=AD=h.

Let us prove that if rectangles KLMN and K'L'M'N' are inscribed in triangles ABC and A'B'C' and have equal heights KN and K'N', then these rectangles are equal.
