Having a table of advantages and knowing the word A guessed by player A, player B can easily find the optimal choice of answer B - for this he only needs to find the maximum value in the column under the word A. For example, for the first column A=000 (Table 3), $d_{\text{max}}=7$, and you need to take B=100. This provides a sevenfold advantage. In Table 3, the maximum elements in the columns are circled - all of them are at least 2; - therefore, for any choice A, B can respond in such a way that y B will have at least a 2-fold advantage in the game.

How to choose a response word?

We have already obliged how to do this. But at the same time they assumed that a table of advantages had been compiled in advance. Is it possible, knowing the words A, to directly find the optimal answer B without calculating the entire table (or its entire column, the length of which is 2')?

It turns out that it is possible. Let's tell you how it's done.

Let player A choose the words A = $a_1 a_2 \dots a_l$ ($l > 2$, a_i is 0 or 1).

Then player B should choose the words $B = b_1 b_2 \dots b_l$,
$b_2 = a_1, b_3 = a_2, \dots, b_l = a_{l-1}$ (until the first letter $b_1$ of the word $B$ is fixed).

In this case, when the word $A$ is shifted by one position relative to $B$

$$B = b_1 b_2 b_3 \dots b_l$$
$$A = a_1 a_2 \dots a_{l-1} a_l$$

all letters located under each other are the same, so the first term in the polynomial $K_{BA}(t)$ at $t=1/2$ is equal to 1/2 - we thereby strive to maximize the value of $K_{BA}(1/2)$ in Conveñe’s formula. In general, we propose that the choice of the optimal answer is based on a (rather subtle) analysis of the Conven formula, which we will not conduct.

It remains to explain how $b_1$ is selected. Let's denote it by $A' = a_1 a_2 \dots a_{l-1}$. Let $r$ be the minimum number at which, after shifting $A'$ by $r$ positions, the letters written under each other coincide:

$$A' = a_1 \dots a_r a_{r+1} \dots a_l-1$$
$$A' = a_1 \dots a_{l-r-1} a_{l-r} \dots a_l-1$$

(i.e. $a_1 = a_{r+1}, a_2 = a_{r+2}, \dots, a_{l-r-1} = a_{l-1}$). So, the letter $b_1$ should be chosen according to the rules $b_1 \neq a_r$ (for example, with $A=100100$ we have $r=3$, and we should take $B=110010$).

American mathematicians Guibas and Odyzhko proved in 1981 that this choice of word $B$ is optimal. Moreover, they proved that the number $\min \max d(B, A)$ (i.e., the advantage of B over A given the best choice of word $A$ for A) tends to 2 as $l$ increases. In other words, given the given answer choice, the second player, on average, wins twice as often as the most cunning player.

All we have to do is provide the promised proof.
