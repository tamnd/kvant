F1034. A mole of ideal gas from the initial state 1 with a temperature $T_1 = 100$ K, expanding through a turbine into an empty vessel, does some work and transitions to state 2 (see figure). This transition takes place without the supply or removal of heat. The gas is then compressed in the $2 \rightarrow 3$ process, in which the pressure is a linear fixation of the volume, and finally, in the isochoric $3 \rightarrow 1$ process, the gas returns to its original state. This is the work done by the gas during expansion through the turbine in transition $1 \rightarrow 2$, if in process $2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 1$ $Q = 72$ J of heat is supplied to the gas in this process. It is also known that $T_2 = T_3$, $V_2 = 3V_1$; gas constant $R = 8,31$ J/(mol K).

When a gas expands through a turbine into an empty vessel, it does work using its internal energy (since there is no heat supply). During this transition, individual parts of the gas can have different temperatures and pressures - this is the so-called “nonequilibrium” expansion. However, after the equilibrium temperature $T_2$ and pressure $p_2$ are established in the gas, the work done by the gas is uniquely determined by the law of conservation of energy:

$$A_{1 \rightarrow 2} = c_V(T_1 - T_2),$$ where $c_V = \frac{3}{2} R.$

In the section $2 \rightarrow 3$ the initial internal energy of the gas is equal to the final one, therefore the heat $Q_{2 \rightarrow 3}$ supplied to the gas is determined by the work done by the gas (area of ​​the trapezoid):

$$Q_{2 \rightarrow 3} = A_{2 \rightarrow 3} = \frac{p_2 + p_3}{2} (V_3 - V_2) = RT_2 \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2\alpha},$$ where $\alpha = \frac{V_2}{V_3} = 3.$

In the section $3 \rightarrow 1$, the heat supplied to the gas is determined by the change in its internal energy, since the isochoric process of work by the gas does not occur:

$$Q_{3 \rightarrow 1} = c_V(T_1 - T_3) = c_V(T_1 - T_2).$$

According to the condition $Q_{2 \rightarrow 3} + Q_{3 \rightarrow 1} = Q \neq 72$ J, or

$$RT_2 \frac{1 - \alpha^2}{2\alpha} + c_1(T_1 - T_2) = Q.$$

The last equality allows us to find the temperature $T_2$, and then the work $A_{1 \rightarrow 2}$:

$$A_{1 \rightarrow 2} = \frac{Q + RT_1 \frac{\alpha^2 - 1}{2\alpha}}{1 + \frac{R}{c_V} \frac{\alpha^2 - 1}{2\alpha}} = 625$$ J.

F1035. An electric diopole of two rigidly connected exact charges $+q$ and $-q$, located at a distance $l$ from each other, flows through a flat capacitor, the layer of which is connected to a source with a constant EMF ($cm. рисунок$). In general, it should be noted that the diopole will be “pulled” into the capacitor - the resulting force acting on it from the plates is directed inside the capacitor, and therefore, the speed of the diopole in the capacitor will return.

Since the field in the capacitor is maintained constant, one can ignore the influence of the diopole field on the charges of the plates, i.e., consider the capacitor field to be external (independent) with respect to the diopole.
