The dream becomes reality
(Discovery of high temperature superconductivity)

“If you can’t, but really want to, then you can.” These words can describe the open ones that literally produced bodies today and which will be discussed in this article.

For the first time, superconductivity, one of the most striking and unusual phenomena in physics, became known on April 28, 1911. The Dutch physicist G. Kamerlingh-Onnes, at a meeting of the Royal Academy of Sciences in Amsterdam, reported his discovery of the effective - complete disappearance of electrical resistance of mercury cooled with a liquid gel to a temperature of T = 4.15 K. Soon, superconductivity at low temperatures was discovered in a number of other metals and alloys.

The practical prospects of the discovered phenomenon seemed limitless: linear transmission of electrical energy without losses, heavy-duty magnets, electric motors and transformers of a new type, batteries with unlimited capacity, etc. However, two insurmountable things at that time became in the way of the implementation of these plans. The first is the extremely low temperatures at which superconductivity appears in all known materials. To cool such temperatures one has to use expensive liquid gel, which makes many tempting projects for using superconductivity simply unrealistic. The second transformation - which Kamerlingh Onnes himself soon discovered with disappointment - is due to the fact that superconductivity turned out to be “capricious” in relation to the magnetic field: it disappears in strong fields (in ruth, for example, this happens when the magnetic field induction is above 0.04 Tesla).

For a long time, the phenomenon of superconductivity remained unexplained; only in 1957 did this phenomenon find a consistent theoretical explanation. As it turned out, superconductivity is associated with the appearance in some metals at low temperatures of a specific acceptance between electrons, due to their interaction through the surrounding crystal lattice.

The creation of the theory of superconductivity should be given a powerful impetus by its further research. Along with the discovery of fundamentally new quantum effects characteristic of superconductors, significant progress has been made in obtaining new superconducting materials. Thus, by the mid-80s, superconductors were created that were capable of withstanding enormous magnetic fields (up to 60 Tesla) and passing correspondingly large currents. However, in the area of ​​increasing the so-called critical temperature - the maximum temperature at which a superconducting current can still flow in a sample, over the past 15 years, despite the harsh conditions of scientists all over the world, it has not been possible to increase the reference value of 23 K (for the Nb₃Ge alloy) by a single degree.

But perhaps the phenomenon of superconductivity is fundamentally impossible at sufficiently high temperatures?

This conclusion has been reached regarding ordinary superconductivity (caused by the interaction of electrons through a lattice) and inclining scientists. The most optimistic forecasts predicted the maximum possible values ​​of critical temperatures of the order of 30-40 K.

And at the end of 1986, the scientific world was excited by a sensational message about the discovery of a new series of superconducting substances with critical temperatures of exactly 30-40 K and even higher. These substances turned out to be complex metal oxides such as La-Sr-Cu-O, La-Pb-Cu-O, La-Ba-Cu-O. They were first synthesized by Swiss scientists, but just a few months later they began to be “furnaced” in many laboratories around the world.

Like the “gold rush” of the Klondike, the post-conjunction fever with high critical parameters has gripped scientists. Information about new superconductors, with even higher critical temperatures, was sent out in a kind of abundance. Journal publications cannot keep up with the synthesis of more and more new substances with amazing properties; news about them is immediately transmitted from laboratory to laboratory by telephone or, as in the time of Heugen and Newton, in letters.

There have already been reliable reports of reaching a record critical temperature of 120 K in compounds of the Y-Ba-Cu-O type. Successful research in this direction is also being carried out in our country.

The substances of the discovered series are so-called metal oxide ceramics. And the conductivity in the normal state of spring is low - in terms of their electrical properties they are flat metals, and the concentration of electrons in them is low. The nature of the new, high-temperature superconductivity remains unclear. Is it related to the traditional interaction of electrons with each other (boiling point 77 K). As a result, we can expect a real revolution in general where physics and technology are possessed, the consequences of which are difficult to fully imagine today.
