Tasks

M1041 — M1045; F1053 — F1057

M1041. A) four, b) three vertices are given on the plane. Using a double-sided ruler, restore the remaining vertices. (With a double-sided ruler you can do the same as a regular ruler without divisions, and also draw a straight line parallel to this one at a distance equal to the width of the ruler.)

M1042. The class is limited to touring on a re-geared rope. All kinds of teams that can be made up of students of this class (of one, two, etc. people, except for the team of the entire class) should participate in the tour once at a time. Prove what each team will do with a team consisting of all the other students in the class.

I. I. Grinichuk

M1043. Is it possible to divide the set of all integers into three subsets so that for any integer n the numbers n-50, n+1987 belong to three different subsets?

S. V. Konyazin

M1044. Prove that from four numbers you can always choose two numbers x and y, also that

$$0 \leq \frac{x-y}{1+xy} \leq 1.$$

I. I. Sergeeev

M1045. In some kingdom, some state, the territory of which is shaped like a square with a side of 2 km, the king decides to invite all residents to his palace for a ball by 7 pm. To do this, at noon he sends a messenger with an order, who can convey any instruction to any resident, who in turn can convey any instruction to any other resident, etc. Before receiving the instruction, each resident is at home (in a known place) and can transmit at a speed of 3 km/h in any direction. Prove that the king can limit the notification so that all residents have time to arrive at the beginning of the ball.

S. V. Konyazin

F1053. In a thin smooth pipeline a flexible homogeneous cord (Fig. 1) slides (in a field of force and gravity). Sections AB and BC of the pipeline are semicircles of radius R, points A, B and C lie on the same vertical; cord length l=2πR. Find all points of the cord at which the tension is zero at the moment when the lower end of the cord is at point C.

I. Yu. Poteryako

F1054. Two rods of the same shape are made of materials with different elastic properties - the Young's moduli of the materials are equal to the correspondence of E₁ and E₂. The rods are glued together at their ends and painted with the same paint, so that the resulting sample looks like a uniform rod. What Young model of the “substance” of this rod would be modified by an experimenter who does not know what the rod constitutes?

V. A. Davydov

F1055. Electrical device P is connected to an alternating current network with a voltage of 220 V through a capacitor em with C = 0.5 μF (Fig. 2). The ammeter shows current I=0.01 A, the voltmeter reading is U=180 V. Find the power consumed by the device from the network. Consider the ampere and voltmeter ideal.

A. R. Zilberman

Problems

M1041 — M1045; P1053 — P1057

M1041. Let a) four, b) three vertices of a regular pentagon in the plane be given. Construct the other vertices by means of a two—sided ruler. (A two—sided ruler does anything a one—sided unmarked ruler can do and can also be used to draw a line parallel to a given one at the distance equal to the ruler’s width.)

M. I. Grinchuk

M1042. A tug-of-war competition is organized among the pupils of a certain class. Every possible team which can be made up of one, two, etc. pupils (but not the entire class) must participate exactly once. Prove that each team will compete against the team consisting of all the other pupils of the class.

I. N. Sergeev

M1043. Can the set of all integers be partitioned into three subsets so that for any integer n the numbers n, n−50, n+1987 will belong to three different subsets?

S. V. Konyagin

M1044. Prove that out of any four positive numbers it is always possible to choose two numbers x and y such that

$$0 \leqslant \frac{x-y}{1+xy} \leqslant 1.$$

I. N. Sergeev

M1045. Once upon a time the King of a small square Kingdom of side 2 km decided to invite all his subjects to a Ball at the Court at 7 PM. At noon he dispatched a Messenger who could transmit any message to any inhabitant of the Kingdom, who in turn could transmit any message to any other inhabitant, etc. Each inhabitant stays at his house (whose location is known) until he gets a message and then walks at 3 km per hour in the required direction. Prove that the King could have organized the transmission of messages so that all his loyal subjects could reach the Court in time for the Opening of the Ball.

S. V. Konyagin
