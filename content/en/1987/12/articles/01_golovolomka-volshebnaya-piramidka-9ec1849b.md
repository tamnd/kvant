The "Magic Paramid" - another part of our notes on globe puzzles - was invented before the Rubik's Cube, but for some reason it was not appreciated at that time. Especially voluminous ball-shaped globebreakers, paramids and cubes, among others, differ from the ones from the previous issues, with the exception of the “inverted” one (see “Quit”, No. 1, 2), is that their moving parts can not only move from one place to another, but also “sit” in a given place in different ways. So, in the paramidon on figure on the left there are 3 types of blocks - 4 corner, 6 edge and 4 central. The first ones can be turned in the same place in three ways, the second ones in two ways, the third one in three ways; However, the rotations of the central blocks appear to be indistinguishable. We produce several versions of the paramidka. The most common is the one shown on the right, and paramidal crowns are usually attached to its corner blocks, each healing on its own. But what’s more interesting is the paramida with central blocks.
