ХVIII International Physics Olympiad

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. S. KROTOV

This year, the International Schoolchildren Olympiad in Physics was held from July 5 to 13 in the city of Jena (GDR). In terms of the number of participants, this was the most representative Olympiad. Teams from 25 countries came to Jena: Australia, Austria, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Holland, Iceland, Italy, Canada, China, Cuba, Kuwait, Norway, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, Turkey, Finland, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia. In addition, observers from Belgium, Greece and Colombia attended the Olympiad. (As experience shows, perhaps next year these countries will become full participants in the Olympiad.) A representative of UNESCO, a correspondent for the magazine «Kvant» and correspondents from youth magazines from Iceland and Germany also took part in the Olympiad.

In accordance with the statute of the International Physics Olympiad, each participating team consists of two leaders and fifth participants. Team leaders, together with members of the organizing committee of the country hosting the Olympiad, form the International Commission - the main governing body of the Olympiad. This year, the International Commission was headed by Rudolf Hermann, a professor at Humboldt University (Berlin), a specialist in the field of solid state physics.

Based on the results of performance at the All-Union Olympiads and the results of winter and summer training camps, the USSR team included:

Anton Bibikov is a graduate of Physics School No. 18 in Moscow,
Dmitry Budko - graduate of the secondary school. No. 3 Belgorod,
Ginatas Vilklyas - graduate of the secondary school. No. 45 Vilnius,
Dmitry Glushenkov - graduate of the secondary school. No. 239 Leningrad,
Alexey Goldin is a graduate of Physics School No. 2 in Kyiv.

Before going to the Olympiad, all participants in the summer camp (including also Sergei Bobylev, a graduate of secondary school No. 9 in Berezniki and Pavel Karklin, a graduate of secondary school No. 49 in Moscow) were enrolled without exams in the universities of their choice.

The leaders of our team were appointed scientific employees of the Research Institute of Content and Methods of Teaching of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR O.F. Kabardin and V. A. Orlov.

The opening of the Olympiad took place on July 6 in the trade meeting hall of the University of Jena. Friedrich Schiller. In the evening of the same day, an official meeting of the International Commission was held, at which theoretical problems developed by a special commission were presented. After the need to adjust the terms of the task and clarify the official decisions of the commission, they approved the tasks.

On July 7, the participants were given three problems: the first on thermodynamics, the second on mechanics and electromagnetism, and the third on wheels and waves.

Here are the conditions for these tasks.

Theoretical tour

Problem 1

Adiabatic moist air flows around the mountain range (Fig. 1). Meteorological stations $M_0$ and $M_3$ record air pressure $p_0=100$ kPa, station $M_2-p_2=70$ kPa. Air temperature at point $M_0-t=+20$ °C. When air rises, cloud formation begins at a pressure of $p_1=84,5$ kPa. With further rise of air, in which condensation of water vapor occurs (the mass of this air over each square meter of surface is equal to 2000 kg), it reaches the top of the ridge (station $M_2$ after 1500 s. In this case, it will separate, per kilogram of air, $m=2,45$ g of water as precipitation (rain).

1) What is the temperature $T_1$ at cloud base?

2) At what height $h_1$ above station $M_0$ is the cloud base located, assuming that air density decreases linearly with height?

3) What temperature $T_2$ will change at the top of the ridge?

4) What height does the column of water reach (height of precipitation per unit area) falling from the air flow within 3 hours? The conditions for steam condensation are considered the same in the area from $M_1$ to $M_2$.

5) What is the temperature $T_3$ on the back side of the ridge at station $M_3$? Discussion of the air condition at station $M_3$ compared to its condition at station $M_0$.

Instructions and data:

Air should be considered as an ideal gas.

The influence of the presence of water vapor on the thermal conductivity and air density, as well as the temperature dependence of the thermal conductivity, should be neglected. Temperatures should be indicated with an accuracy of 1 K, the height of the cloud base with an accuracy of 10 m, and the height of precipitation with an accuracy of 0.1 mm.

The specific thermal conductivity of air in the temperature range is $c_p=1005$ J/(kg K); air density at station $M_0$ at $p_0$ and $T_0=-\varphi_0=1,189$ kg/m³; specific thermal conductivity of air in the cloud area - $q_V=2500$ kJ/kg; $c_p/c_1=x=1,4; g=9,81$ m/s².

Task 2

A beam of electricity flies out from a precise source $P$ and flies into the magnetic field $B$ of an annular (toridal) coil along the linear field (Fig. 2). Let the opening angle of the electric beam $2a_0$ be very small ($a_0\ll 1$). Electric injection occurs on the middle radius $R$ of the torid using an accelerating voltage $U_0$. It is assumed that model $B$ is built. Neglect the electrostatic interaction between the electrons in the beam.

1) To move the beam along the linear side, it is necessary to apply a uniform magnetic field with induction $B_1$, which deflects the beam. Calculate $B_1$ for an electrode that moves along a circular path of radius $R$ inside the torid.

2) Calculate the model $B$ of an inductive magnetic field facsing a beam at four points spaced by l/2 relative to each other (see Fig. 2). Note: when studying the trajectory of the electric field, the curvature of the linear magnetic field can be neglected.

3) Without a deflecting field $\vec{B}_1$, the electric beam does not remain in the toroid, but leaves it with a drift movement perpendicular to the toroid plane. Show that the radial deviation of the electron from the injection radius $R$ is finite. Determine the direction of the drift speed. Note: The opening angle of the electron beam should be neglected. Use the laws of conservation of energy and angular momentum.

Data: $e/m=1,76 \cdot 10^{11}$ C/kg, $U_0=3$ kV, $R=50$ mm.

Task 3

When sinusoidal oscillations of current and voltage propagate along an infinite $L-C$ chain (Fig. 3), the phases of voltage oscillations on the capacitors of two consecutive cells differ by a constant amount $\varphi$.

1) Determine the dependences of $\varphi$ on $\omega$, $L$ and $C$ ($\omega$ is the cyclic oscillation frequency).

2) Determine the speed of wave propagation if each cell has length $l$.

3) Under what conditions does the speed of propagation of oscillations weakly depend on $\omega$ and what is the value of the speed in this case?

4) Propose the design of a mechanical model similar to the electrical circuit described above, and derive equations that justify the validity of this model.

Auxiliary formulas:

$$\cos \alpha - \cos \beta = -2 \sin \left( \frac{\alpha + \beta}{2} \right) \sin \left( \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} \right),$$

$$\sin \alpha - \sin \beta = 2 \cos \left( \frac{\alpha + \beta}{2} \right) \sin \left( \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} \right).$$

Experimental tour

Determine the refractive indices of the prism and liquid.

1) When using only one recognition, determine its own refractive index $n_1$ in two different ways. Represent the problem in the form figure and use it to derive formulas for calculating the refractive index.

Auxiliary materials: marking sheet with angles of 30°, 60° and 90°, graph paper, ruler, white paper.

2) Using two such identical parameters, determine the refractive index of the liquid $n_2$; and $n_1 > n_2$. Represent the problem in the form figure and use it to derive formulas for calculating the refractive index.

Auxiliary materials: two rectangles with angles of 30°, 60° and 90°, graph paper, ruler, white paper, Petri dish, round stand, liquids.

Formulas:

$$\sin(\alpha \pm \beta) = \sin \alpha \cos \beta \pm \cos \alpha \sin \beta.$$

Aftermath of exams

Quanti, that one and the same event can be looked at from different points of view. In the exam, these are the points of view of teachers and applicants. These are the parallels we were able to draw between the statements of examiners and applicants, which we overheard in the midst of the entrance exams.

Teachers

One of my applicants got confused, but oh well. Pretended not to notice.

Eh, I shouldn’t have given one a B today. After all, I’ve already seen that I don’t know anything. I was just in a big hurry.

And this fake guy doesn’t understand anything.

I tell him: “You know, but not five points. There are people who know better.”

I have one question for her. Doesn't answer. I'm second. Doesn't answer. I give her a bad grade - she's in tears!

Applicants

I outsmarted the teacher. He made a mistake, but answered the question in such a way that he didn’t even notice.

Eh, bad luck! The examiner was in a hurry somewhere, otherwise I would have taken my rightful five points.

And this little guy doesn’t understand anything.

He tells me: “You know well, there are few guys who know better than you, but...”.

He asks a difficult question, I give him an answer right away. He is the second question - I am the answer again. And he suddenly says: “You, girl, don’t know anything.” Well, then I told him that he was wrong.
