MEKHMATOVO STUDENTS OF MSU
IN THE BATTLE FOR MOSCOW

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. Z. PIROGOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. A. TKOLINA

When asked what they remembered most from the last war, I always answered: the battle for Moscow.

G. K. Zhukov

Of the thirteen Heroes of the Soviet Union - rebels of Moscow University - seven people are from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics *).

The authors of this article - associate professors of mechanics (faculty of mechanics and mathematics) of Moscow State University Ivan Zinovevich Pirozov and Irina Aleksaidova Tyulina - were among those who began to buzz Moscow in the fall of forty-one.

I.Z. Pirozov worked in East Kazakhstan before the war. Despite the “svinitsov-chinkov” brother**) On August 18, 1941, he was sent to the Frunze Infantry School, from whose personnel one of the special Kursan rifle brizadas was soon formed. At the end of October, the 34th brizade, two services of I.Z. Pirozov, was sent to the Moscow region. As part of the 49th artillery, this brisade entered the battles near Serpukhov at the end of November, and in December these units were near Tarusa. On December 20, Pirozov was seriously wounded. Having followed an inescapable path under the pressure of evacuation, in the summer of 1942 he ended up undergoing treatment in Sverdlovsk, where at that time he was being evacuated as a mechanic from Moscow State University. Still admiring, Pirozov began to train for service and soon became a mechanic, with whom his entire future life was connected. I.Z. Pirozov was awarded the Order of the Patriotic War, 1st degree, the Red Star and medals of war and peace.

I. A. Tyulina, a 3-20 year mechanic student, entered in June 1941, and by the fall she graduated from a two-part nursing course. Its military road had its share: it began in Tula, stretched for thousands of kilometers through the Kaluga region, Belarus, Poland, Pomerando to the Elbe (in the spring of '45). At first, I. A. Tyulina was a medical instructor of the 330th Infantry Division, and then a senior operating nurse and parter of the medical unit. She returned to mechanic training in the summer of 1945; Having graduated from mechanics and his postgraduate studies, he also studied the history of mechanics there. She was awarded two Orders of the Patriotic War, II degree, medals, and a Certificate of Honor of the Supreme Council of the RSFSR.

**) Workers engaged in the production of lead and zinc were exempt from military service.

At the end of 1941, a third of all infantry and two-thirds of tank and motorized forces of the Eastern Front of Hitler Germany were thrown at Moscow. Nazi tank rams fell on Moscow along the Leningradskoye, Rogachevskoye, Dmitrovskoye, Volokalamsk, Minsk, and Warsaw highways. Muscovites got involved in defense work. By the autumn of 1940, 15 rifle divisions of the people's militia were formed; 12 of them occupied combat lines already in July.

Hundreds of students, graduate students and employees of Moscow University, together with workers of the Krasnopresensky district, students of the conservatory, artists of the Theater of the Revolution (Teler is the theater named after V. Mayakovsky) and other organizations enrolled in the 8th Infantry Krasnopresensky Division. Unlike regular troops, the vast majority of militia units were recruited with less careful age and medical selection. Therefore, people from purely peaceful professions, the so-called “white ticket workers,” ended up there. The militia's military training was very brief.

213 Mekhanites signed up for the 8th Krasnopresensk division of the Moscow people’s militia. Most of the students, graduate students and mechanic teachers were sent to the 975th Artillery.

In August, the 8th Krasnopresensk Division occupied a line in the Rzhevskaya Zemsky direction. Military training was combined with defensive work. By September, anti-tank loadings, firing points, and minefields had been created.

However, the enemy made changes to the course of expected events. At the beginning of October, the situation in Elnin-

This direction quickly became more complicated: the enemy threw in fresh troops, many times superior to our forces in tanks, guns and personnel. In this regard, the command of the Reserve Front transferred the 8th Krasnopresensk Division from the 32nd to the 24th Army, giving the Krasnopresensky soldiers a new combat mission. It was necessary to quickly withdraw from the prepared reliable positions and redeploy to the area of ​​the village of Uvarovo, Smolensk region, which is somewhat south of Yelnya.

In the autumn mud, the convoys with ammunition and products fell behind, but the soldiers of the 975th artillery regiment, like the entire 8th division, completed their task, delaying the enemy collapsing towards Moscow for several days. The militias of the Moscow multi-guards will fight here side by side with the personnel units of the Soviet Army. This battle near Yelnya caused great losses to the occupiers: about 45 thousand soldiers and officers. Our losses were also great: more than a third of the militia of the 8th Krasnopresensk Division died or were wounded and captured. Among the dead and missing were many young scientists - graduate students of mechanite: S. S. Kudashev, A. I. Gerchikov, S. Ya. Karnov, M. E. Glerman, M. I. Pesin, A. I. Vikhrov, V. N. Zasukhin, M. D. Broginsky, H. M. Milshtein, G. N. Alekseev and others.

Many graduates of the mechanics of the forty-first year were assigned to design bureaus and defense enterprises provided with reservations. However, they too voluntarily went to the front; many joined the militia. Among them were Mikhail Ivanov, who already had several working specialties at that time. Everywhere he was active in Komsomol and party work. In 1936, having extensive work experience, M.I. Ivanov entered the mechanical engineering department of Moscow University and graduated with honors at the beginning of the war. Mikhail Ivanovich Ivanov was enlisted in the 975th artillery regiment with the rank of captain. He did not return from the bloody battles near Yelnya in early October '41. His comrades who had just graduated from mechanical engineering also died there: Mstislav Dolgov, Oleg Sorokin, Evgeny Pavlov, Pyotr Sokolov and fourth-year students Alexander Sivkov, Vladimir Muranov, Viktor Podyachev and many others.

Among those who returned from these brutal battles near Yelnya was Gorimir Gorimirovich Cherny, now an academician and director of the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University.

Together with students and graduate students of mechanite, associate professor Nikolai Borisovich Vedenisov joined the militia. A love for exact sciences was instilled in him by his father B. N. Vedenisov, an outstanding scientist and scientist, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, laureate of the State Prize. In 1922, N.B. Vedenisov entered the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University. He studied together with A. N. Tikhonov, V. V. Nemyshka, L. A. Tumarkin, A. N. Cherkasov, famous mathematicians of the future. In their second year, these mathematicians took part in a seminar on topology organized by P. S. Urson and P. S. Aleksandrov.

After completing his studies at the university, N. B. Vedenisov taught mathematics at the Moscow State Pedagogical Institute, while simultaneously working at Moscow University. In the years preceding the Great Patriotic War, he was an assistant professor at the Military Artillery Academy. He had disciples everywhere; In the topological seminar of Moscow State University, he supervised the work of graduate and postgraduate students.

Together with employees of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, graduate students and employees, N. B. Vedenisov enrolled in the 8th Krasnopresensk Division. A few years before the war, he sprained his spine and doctors recommended that he stay in the rear. Nikolai Borisovich answered everyone succinctly: I should be where my students are - at the front. Like most Mekhanite militias, he was sent to the 975th artillery regiment of the 8th Krasnopresensk division. In the battles near Yelnya, N.B. Vedenisov was wounded, captured and died in October 1941.

Hard trials befell those who were captured. You can read about their fate in the book “Missing Without Meeting” by S.P. Zlobin, who endured four years in prison camps in Germany.

Volunteer from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics Vladimir Muranov was in his fourth year when the war broke out. He enlisted in the people's militia and was sent to the 975th artillery regiment. In October 1941 he went missing. For almost 40 years, Volodya’s four sisters searched for his traces. And finally a message came from the Red Cross Society that V.P. Muranov died on December 27, 1941 in the fascist camp No. 324 in Grodno. During the three years of “mastery” in the vicinity of Grodno, the occupiers shot and tortured 33 thousand civilians and prisoners of war in death camps.

For many years, nothing was known about the fate of Andrei Pavlov and his friend, the Mekhanites Ivan Lepekhin. Both were finishing graduate school; Andrey had most of his dissertation prepared when the war began. According to the recognition of the Moscow Party Committee, Andrei and Ivan were sent for retraining to Serpukhov (both appeared voluntarily at the military registration and enlistment office of the Krasnopresensky district of Moscow on June 28). In July they were assigned to the 48th Regiment of the 38th Infantry Division in the Smolensk direction. Andrei Pavlov was appointed commander of a platoon of 120-mm mortar batteries. In the autumn of '41, he found himself in the most dangerous direction of the autumn onslaught of the Nazis - near Yartsevo. K.K. Rokossovsky included the 38th Infantry Division in his memoirs as the most persistent. After some stabilization of the front line in this area, the situation deteriorated again in early October. Hitler's Operation Typhoon began. The 38th Division had to take on the onslaught of the strongest Nazi strike forces, covering the retreat of the 16th Army. The remaining units of the 30th Division were down to the last grenade. Now, on the site of the October 1941 battle, a grand memorial has been built in memory of the soldiers and commanders who held back the rapid pressure of the enemy’s tank and mechanized columns on the approaches to Moscow.

From the first days of the Great Patriotic War, the State Defense Committee paid special attention to the defense of our capital. Scientists, students, graduate students and employees of Moscow State University, including the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, took the most active part in the most complex problem of life in a front-line city.

By order of MSU dated July 12, 1941, the Local Air Defense (LAD) of MSU was organized. The 1st company's rank and file included the greatest scientists of the faculty: I. G. Petrovsky, P. S. Aleksandrov, S. L. Sobolev, B. V. Bulgakov, A. A. Ilyoshin, N. A. Slezkin, A. A. Kosmodemyansky, D. E. Menshov, A. G. Kurosh and others. Those who were not included in the MPVO were used through the MPVO for auxiliary and other defensive work.

Classes at the university began on August 1, 1941; The duration of training was shortened, the intensity of training increased greatly, many students were specialized. On October 13, due to the evacuation of the main staff of Moscow State University to Ashgabat, classes in Moscow stopped.

Moscow was preparing to repel the enemy's air attack. Scientists geometer N.A. Glagolev successfully solved the problem of the optimal placement of anti-aircraft batteries and pollution balloons around Moscow, S.V. Bakhvalov (also a geometer) at the very beginning of the war developed the theory of adding artillery fire (by the way, during the civil war he was an artilleryman). Mechanics specialist Kh. A. Rakhmatulin carried out theoretical and experimental work on the study of the aerodynamics of tethered balloons and the calculation of cables during a transverse impact. He also intensively carried out work on the study of the aerodynamics of parashtovs.

From the very first days of the war, the team of the Department of Probability Theory under the leadership of A. N. Kolmogorov conducted research on the development of the theory of artillery shooting. In a short time, the problem of the most advantageous dispersion of projectiles when firing over areas was solved. Particularly important in this initial period of the war was the development of bombing tables for small bombers of the PO-2 type.
