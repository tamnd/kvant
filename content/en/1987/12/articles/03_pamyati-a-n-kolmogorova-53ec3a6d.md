⟦folio 2⟧

On October 20, 1987, the life of the Great Scientist, the greatest mathematician of our time, Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov, ended.

A. N. Kolmogorov’s place in the history of mathematics is unique - he made fundamental discoveries in all areas of this science and its applications. But in addition to his own mathematical creativity, the desire to serve the Enlightenment, to serve the education of the younger generation, was of great importance in Andrei Nikolaevich’s life.

A. N. Kolmogorov was born on April 25, 1903 in Tambov. His rarity and versatility manifested itself early: at the age of seven he independently rediscovered the representation of squares of integers as the sum of primes, at twelve he began to study higher mathematics, then became interested in the history of Novgorod (where he made an important discovery), and was seriously interested in metallurgy. Having entered the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University in 1920, he finally connected his life with mathematics and the university. Already at the age of 19, he managed to construct an example of a “trigonometric series diverging almost everywhere,” which brought him peaceful fame. In 1931, A. N. Kolmogorov became a professor at Moscow University, where he headed three departments at different times and created several scientific schools. It is not possible to even list here those areas of knowledge where Andrei Nikolaevich made the most important discoveries. Let us only note the creation of the foundations of the theory of probability and statistics, the solution (with the teaching of V.I. Arnold) of Hilbert's XIII problems.

In the same years when Andrei Nikolaevich made his first discoveries, he became a school teacher and worked in a secondary school for several years. Since the 30s, he has given numerous lectures to schoolchildren and students, and has been actively involved in the formation of school mathematical optimids, first Moscow, and then Vsegosky and Vsegoozny. In the sixties, he created a physics and mathematics boarding school at Moscow State University (which everyone immediately dubbed “Kolmogorovsky”), and put a lot of effort into developing school curricula and textbooks. In 1970, together with I.K. Kikon, he created the magazine “Quant” and remained its author and active leader until his very last days.

 Andrei Nikolaevich once told one of his students that he sees humanity in the form of lights wandering in the fog, which only confuse the light scattered by others. But these words are not only for a great scientist, not only for a great teacher, but also for a great Enlightener. Andrei Nikolaevich belonged to those unheralded geniuses who brighten life by the very fact of their existence. A person who has a columnar mind and a columnar soul does not need to reap offspring - his works continue to appear on us, his name will not be forgotten.
