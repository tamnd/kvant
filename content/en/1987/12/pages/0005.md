MEKHMATOVO STUDENTS OF MSU
IN THE BATTLE FOR MOSCOW

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. Z. PIROGOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. A. TKOLINA

When asked what they remembered most from the last war, I always answered: the battle for Moscow.

G. K. Zhukov

Of the thirteen Heroes of the Soviet Union - rebels of Moscow University - seven people are from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics *).

The authors of this article - associate professors of mechanics (faculty of mechanics and mathematics) of Moscow State University Ivan Zinovevich Pirozov and Irina Aleksaidova Tyulina - were among those who began to buzz Moscow in the fall of forty-one.

I.Z. Pirozov worked in East Kazakhstan before the war. Despite the “svinitsov-chinkov” brother**) On August 18, 1941, he was sent to the Frunze Infantry School, from whose personnel one of the special Kursan rifle brizadas was soon formed. At the end of October, the 34th brizade, two services of I.Z. Pirozov, was sent to the Moscow region. As part of the 49th artillery, this brisade entered the battles near Serpukhov at the end of November, and in December these units were near Tarusa. On December 20, Pirozov was seriously wounded. Having followed an inescapable path under the pressure of evacuation, in the summer of 1942 he ended up undergoing treatment in Sverdlovsk, where at that time he was being evacuated as a mechanic from Moscow State University. Still admiring, Pirozov began to train for service and soon became a mechanic, with whom his entire future life was connected. I.Z. Pirozov was awarded the Order of the Patriotic War, 1st degree, the Red Star and medals of war and peace.

I. A. Tyulina, a 3-20 year mechanic student, entered in June 1941, and by the fall she graduated from a two-part nursing course. Its military road had its share: it began in Tula, stretched for thousands of kilometers through the Kaluga region, Belarus, Poland, Pomerando to the Elbe (in the spring of '45). At first, I. A. Tyulina was a medical instructor of the 330th Infantry Division, and then a senior operating nurse and parter of the medical unit. She returned to mechanic training in the summer of 1945; Having graduated from mechanics and his postgraduate studies, he also studied the history of mechanics there. She was awarded two Orders of the Patriotic War, II degree, medals, and a Certificate of Honor of the Supreme Council of the RSFSR.

*) About the Heroes of the Soviet Union who studied at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University, see: «Kvant», 1977, No. 11, p. 38-40 and 1980, No. 5, p. 10-17, as well as the journal “Mathematics at School”, 1985, No. 2, p. 7-13.

**) Workers engaged in the production of lead and zinc were exempt from military service.

At the end of 1941, a third of all infantry and two-thirds of tank and motorized forces of the Eastern Front of Hitler Germany were thrown at Moscow. Nazi tank rams fell on Moscow along the Leningradskoye, Rogachevskoye, Dmitrovskoye, Volokalamsk, Minsk, and Warsaw highways. Muscovites got involved in defense work. By the autumn of 1940, 15 rifle divisions of the people's militia were formed; 12 of them occupied combat lines already in July.

Hundreds of students, graduate students and employees of Moscow University, together with workers of the Krasnopresensky district, students of the conservatory, artists of the Theater of the Revolution (Teler is the theater named after V. Mayakovsky) and other organizations enrolled in the 8th Infantry Krasnopresensky Division. Unlike regular troops, the vast majority of militia units were recruited with less careful age and medical selection. Therefore, people from purely peaceful professions, the so-called “white ticket workers,” ended up there. The militia's military training was very brief.

213 Mekhanites signed up for the 8th Krasnopresensk division of the Moscow people’s militia. Most of the students, graduate students and mechanic teachers were sent to the 975th Artillery.

In August, the 8th Krasnopresensk Division occupied a line in the Rzhevskaya Zemsky direction. Military training was combined with defensive work. By September, anti-tank loadings, firing points, and minefields had been created.
