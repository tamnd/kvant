Volunteer from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics Vladimir Muranov was in his fourth year when the war broke out. He enlisted in the people's militia and was sent to the 975th artillery regiment. In October 1941 he went missing. For almost 40 years, Volodya’s four sisters searched for his traces. And finally a message came from the Red Cross Society that V.P. Muranov died on December 27, 1941 in the fascist camp No. 324 in Grodno. During the three years of “mastery” in the vicinity of Grodno, the occupiers shot and tortured 33 thousand civilians and prisoners of war in death camps.

For many years, nothing was known about the fate of Andrei Pavlov and his friend, the Mekhanites Ivan Lepekhin. Both were finishing graduate school; Andrey had most of his dissertation prepared when the war began. According to the recognition of the Moscow Party Committee, Andrei and Ivan were sent for retraining to Serpukhov (both appeared voluntarily at the military registration and enlistment office of the Krasnopresensky district of Moscow on June 28). In July they were assigned to the 48th Regiment of the 38th Infantry Division in the Smolensk direction. Andrei Pavlov was appointed commander of a platoon of 120-mm mortar batteries. In the autumn of '41, he found himself in the most dangerous direction of the autumn onslaught of the Nazis - near Yartsevo. K.K. Rokossovsky included the 38th Infantry Division in his memoirs as the most persistent. After some stabilization of the front line in this area, the situation deteriorated again in early October. Hitler's Operation Typhoon began. The 38th Division had to take on the onslaught of the strongest Nazi strike forces, covering the retreat of the 16th Army. The remaining units of the 30th Division were down to the last grenade. Now, on the site of the October 1941 battle, a grand memorial has been built in memory of the soldiers and commanders who held back the rapid pressure of the enemy’s tank and mechanized columns on the approaches to Moscow.

From the first days of the Great Patriotic War, the State Defense Committee paid special attention to the defense of our capital. Scientists, students, graduate students and employees of Moscow State University, including the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, took the most active part in the most complex problem of life in a front-line city.

By order of MSU dated July 12, 1941, the Local Air Defense (LAD) of MSU was organized. The 1st company's rank and file included the greatest scientists of the faculty: I. G. Petrovsky, P. S. Aleksandrov, S. L. Sobolev, B. V. Bulgakov, A. A. Ilyoshin, N. A. Slezkin, A. A. Kosmodemyansky, D. E. Menshov, A. G. Kurosh and others. Those who were not included in the MPVO were used through the MPVO for auxiliary and other defensive work.

Classes at the university began on August 1, 1941; The duration of training was shortened, the intensity of training increased greatly, many students were specialized. On October 13, due to the evacuation of the main staff of Moscow State University to Ashgabat, classes in Moscow stopped.

Moscow was preparing to repel the enemy's air attack. Scientists geometer N.A. Glagolev successfully solved the problem of the optimal placement of anti-aircraft batteries and pollution balloons around Moscow, S.V. Bakhvalov (also a geometer) at the very beginning of the war developed the theory of adding artillery fire (by the way, during the civil war he was an artilleryman). Mechanics specialist Kh. A. Rakhmatulin carried out theoretical and experimental work on the study of the aerodynamics of tethered balloons and the calculation of cables during a transverse impact. He also intensively carried out work on the study of the aerodynamics of parashtovs.

From the very first days of the war, the team of the Department of Probability Theory under the leadership of A. N. Kolmogorov conducted research on the development of the theory of artillery shooting. In a short time, the problem of the most advantageous dispersion of projectiles when firing over areas was solved. Particularly important in this initial period of the war was the development of bombing tables for small bombers of the PO-2 type.
