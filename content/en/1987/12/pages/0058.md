⟦folio 56⟧

Selected tasks
Leningrad City Olympics
in mathematics

1(7). There is an instruction for geometric constructions on a plane, which allows you to do the following:

a) if there are two points, then you can draw a straight line through them,
b) if a line and a point on it are given, then you can use the redistribution to this line at this point.

How can I use this tool to lower a redirection from a given point to a straight line that does not pass through this point?

D. Fomin

2(8, 10). There is an unlimited supply of coins in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 kopecks. and 1 rub. It is known that A kopecks can be placed using B coins. Prove that then B rubles can be placed using A coins.

F. Nazarov

3(8). Several crows are sitting on the branches of a large oak tree. At the signal, they begin to cross. Every minute one of the crows is driven away by neighbors sitting on the same branch, and this crow flies to the next highest (higher) branch; if there are no branches above, the crow flies away. All branches are located at different heights. Prove that the time it takes for the crow to finish (i.e., there will be no more than one vertical on each branch) depends only on the initial location of the crows, but not on the order of flights.

D. Fomin

4(9). The squares of the 8x8 board are colored in a checkerboard pattern. It is allowed to swap any two horizontal lines or any two vertical lines. Is it possible, using these operations, to obtain a board, the entire left edge of which is painted black, and the entire right edge is white?

V. Ufnarovsky

5(9, 10). At points A and B of the intersection of two circles, the tangents to these circles are mutually redefined. Let M be an arbitrary point on one of the circles lying inside the other circle. Let us continue the segments AM and BM beyond point M until they intersect at points X and Y with a circle containing M inside itself. Prove that XY is the diameter of this circle.

D. Fomin

6(9, 10). An astronomer, observing 50 stars in the sky, discovered that the sum of all pairwise distances between them is equal to S. An incoming cloud obscured 25 stars. Prove that the sum of pairwise distances between prominent stars is less than S/2.

S. Fomin

7(8, 9). Given positive numbers a, b, c, d. Prove that if cd=1, then on the interval with endpoints ab and (a+c)(b+d) there is at least one square of an integer:

L. Kurlyandchik

8(8). The plane was cut into several circles, among which there were intersecting ones. Prove that the pieces cut out of the plane cannot be folded into several non-intersecting circles.

A. Anjans

9(9, 10). Eight non-negative real numbers whose sum is 1 are placed at the vertices of a cube. For each edge there are numbers of variables at its ends. Prove that the sum of all products obtained in this way is no more than 1/4.

D. Fomin

10(9). Given a sequence of natural numbers a1, a2, a3, an, ..., in which a1<1987 and ai+1=ai+2 for all natural i. Prove that if for some n the numbers a1—an and a2+an−1 are divisible by 1987, then n is odd.

S. Genkin

11(10). Given a sequence of real numbers x1, x2, ... and a natural number T. Prove that if among all possible ordered T - elements of sets of the form (xk+1, xk+2, ..., xk+T) there are at most T different ones, then the sequence x1, x2, ... is periodic.

I. Lifshych

12(10). The diagonals of the cyclic quadrilateral ABCD intersect at point O. Prove that

$$\frac{AB}{CD} + \frac{CD}{AB} + \frac{BC}{AD} + \frac{AD}{BC} \leq \frac{OA}{OC} + \frac{OC}{OA} + \frac{OB}{OD} + \frac{OD}{OB}.$$

F. Nazarov

13(10). In the n-element set, S subsets were identified containing A1, A2, ..., An elements of the corresponding one. It is known that among these subsets, none is contained in the other. Prove that

$$\frac{1}{C_n^{A_1}} + \frac{1}{C_n^{A_2}} + ... + \frac{1}{C_n^{A_s}} \leq 1,$$

where $C_m^k = \frac{m!}{k!(m-k)!} - \text{бинимальный коэффициент}.$

A. Merkuryev

14(10). Continuous functions f, g: [0; 1] → [0; 1] have the property that f(g(x))=g(f(x)) for each x ∈ [0; 1]. Prove that if f returns, then f(a)=(a)=a for some a ∈ [0; 1].

D. Fomin
