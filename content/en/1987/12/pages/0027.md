M1057. Two cerroks alternately write down natural numbers on the board that do not exceed p. The rules of the cerrok prohibit writing divisors of already written numbers on the board. Produces a cerrok that cannot make another move.
a) Find out which of the cerroks has a winning strategy for p=10, and indicate it.
b) Find out which of the cerroks has a winning strategy for p=1000.

M1058. A non-empty set of nodes is marked on an integer lattice. In addition, a finite set of vectors with integer coordinates is given. It is known that if all given vectors are disconnected from any canceled node, then among their ends there will be more canceled nodes than non-cancelled ones. Prove that there are infinitely many excellent nodes.

M1059. The graph of the function y=f(x), which defines the entire number line, transforms into itself when rotated by an angle π/2 around the origin.
a) Prove that the equation f(x)=x has only one solution.
b) Give an example of such a function.

a) Answer: the beginner wins. The first move he must write is the number 6. After that, he can only write the numbers 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10. Divide them into pairs: (4, 5), (7, 9), (8, 10). It is easy to see that in response to any number written by the second player, the first player will always be able to write a paired number.

6) Answer: The beginner wins. Let's prove this by contradiction. Let the beginner have no winning strategy, i.e., for any of his moves, the second player has an answer that ultimately leads to winning. In particular, if the starter writes 1 with his first move, his partner can, as an irrevocable strategy, secure a win by first writing some number n. Then, having written n as the first move, the one who starts with the same strategy will ensure a win for himself, since 1 can no longer be written. Of course, this reasoning works for any p.

D. Ivanov (8th grade, s. sh. No. 57, Moscow)

Let us introduce a coordinate system on the plane, placing its origin at one of the lattice nodes and directing the abscissa axis to the right and the ordinary axis upward. The volume of this set of vectors is divided into two parts V₁ and V₂. To V₁ we include vectors with coordinates (x, y), where y>0 or y=0, and x>0. The remaining vectors are referred to as V₂. If the number of excellent nodes is finite, then among them you can choose the node that has the most abscissa of all the nodes with the most ordinata (the rightmost of the topmost). Let us set aside all vectors from it (see figure). Then the ends of the vectors of the set V₁ will fall into irreplaceable nodes. According to the conditions of the problem, it follows that the set V₂ contains more vectors than V₁. In the same way, plotting the vectors from the leftmost of the most excellent nodes, we find that there are more vectors in the set V₁ than in V₂. This contradiction shows that the set of canceled nodes is infinite.
