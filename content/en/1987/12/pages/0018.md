Reviewers generally praised Inception. However, the admiration that she deserved (from our modern point of view) was not there. Why? There were several reasons. First of all, he said the influence of the authority of Descartes and his theory in the whirlwind. That is why the hydrodynamics of the Elements were studied with greater attention than the theories of gravity.

The importance of the theory of gravitation was realized later, after the efforts of Euler, Clairaut, d'Alembert, Lagrange and Laplace turned celestial mechanics into the most developed branch of natural arcing. Another circumstance that left an imprint on the perception of “Principles” was associated with the “synthetic” method of presentation, which was already mentioned in the first part of the article (see «Kvant» No. 11, 1987). It was once said: “doing with ease what makes others difficult is talent; to do something that is beyond the reach of talent is genius.” From these positions, “Principia” is evidence of Newton’s genius. But any scientific work has another task - to help followerism further than the author. Newton's method of solving a mechanical problem made progress difficult. This is a beautifully correct Euler: “Although the reader is convinced of the truth of the statements of the provisions, he does not receive a sufficiently clear and precise understanding of them, so that if the same questions are slightly changed, he will hardly be able to solve them on his own... Although it seemed to me that I quite clearly understood the solution to many problems, however, I could no longer solve problems that deviated slightly from them.” Newtonian mechanics began to “work” in science after it was translated into the language of differential and integral calculus and differential equations. The main contributions here belong to Euler, d'Alembert and Lagrange.

And yet “big things are seen from a distance.” History has shown that Newot's “Principles” can actually be considered the beginning of a new science. In this book, for the first time, a system of analysis of physical (more precisely, mechanical) phenomena was built, based on qualitative reasoning, and on their mathematical description. The most important feature of the Principia is the consistent use of experimental data to build a theory. “Principles” is an example of an unchallenged wide scope of phenomena - from the movement of bodies on Earth to the movement of planets, their satellites and comets.

Any significant scientific book is interesting and important not only for the problems solved in it. It gives direction to the further development of science and properties problems. A whole scientific program was taught in “Principles”, which was carried out by scientists of the 18th - first half of the 19th centuries. Mention of this program can already be found in Newton’s preface to the first edition of the Elements:

“It would be desirable to deduce from the principles of mechanics and other natural phenomena, reasoning in a similar way, or much has led me to assume that all these phenomena are caused by certain forces, from which particles of bodies, the exact reasons are still unknown, either tend to each other and adhere to regular bodies, or mutually repel and move away from each other. Since these forces are unknown, until then the attempts of philosophers to explain natural phenomena remained free. I hope, however, that either this method of reasoning, or another, more correct one, the reasons presented here will provide unique illumination.”

Indeed, the laws of dynamics served as the basis for the implementation of Newot's program. Newton's creation had a long life ahead, full of interesting events that led in the middle of the 19th century to the formation of a mechanical picture of the world.
