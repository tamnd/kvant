M1076. The bissertis of the angle $A$ of an acute triangle $ABC$ intersects the side $BC$ at the point $L$, and the circumcircle of the triangle at the point $N$ (different from $A$); $K$ and $M$ are the bases of the perpendiculars dropped from $L$ to sides $AB$ and $AC$ (Fig. 1). Prove that the quadrilateral $AKNM$ is equal to the triangle $ABC$.

I. A. Kushinr

M1077. Let $p_n(k)$ be the number of permutations of a set of $n (n \geq 1)$ elements having exactly $k$ fixed points. Prove that:

a) $\sum_{k=0}^{n} k \cdot p_n(k) = 0 \cdot p_n(0) + 1 \cdot p_n(1) + \dots + n \cdot p_n(n) = n!$

b) $\sum_{k=0}^{n} (k-1)^2 p_n(k) = n!$

At less than A permutation of a finite set $S$ is a one-to-one mapping $f$ of the set $S$ onto itself; the number of permutations of a set of $n$ elements is $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot n$. The element $a$ of the set $S$ is called a fixed point of the permutation $f$ if $f(a) = a$.

M1078. The function $f$ is defined on the set $N_0$ of all non-negative integers and takes values ​​in this set. Prove that the equality $f(f(n)) = n + 1987$ cannot hold for all $n$ of $N_0$.

M1079. Let $n$ be a natural number, $n \geq 3$. Is it possible to arrange $n$ points on the plane so that the distance between any two expressions is an irrational number, and the area of ​​a triangle with vertices at any three is a rational number (non-zero)?

M1080. Let $q$ be a natural number, $q \geq 3$. Prove that if $k^2 + k + q$ is a prime number for all integers $k$, where $0 \leq k \leq \sqrt{q}/3$, then $k^2 + k + q$ is prime for all integers $k$, where $0 \leq k \leq q - 2$.

V. F. Lev

F1088. The weightless ladder is fixed in a weightless suspension rotating at a speed of $f=10$ rpm so that it can freely swing relative to the upper step (Fig. 2). A monkey weighing $m=30$ kg begins to descend the stairs. At what distance $l$ from the suspension will it be when the vertical position of the ladder ceases to be stable?

A. V. Andrianov

F1089. When flying from the Mir station to the Salyut-7 station, which were in the same orbit, our cosmonauts slowed down their ship, moved to a lower orbit, and in a time of $t=30$ hours onto the Salyut-7 gas, which was flying ahead of the Mir at a distance of $L=3000$ km. Assuming the orbits are circular, determine how many kilometers the intermediate orbit is below the main one? The orbital altitude is much less than the earth $R_3 \approx 6400$ km.

M. V. Semenov
