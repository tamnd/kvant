⟦folio 35⟧

Observations of the sun and rays through such azrosol they are seen as blue - the red component of white light is scattered, and only blue light reaches us.

So, the walls of colors that color the heavenly light are determined in each specific situation by a combination of Rayleigh scattering with the scattering of light on small parts.

“Blue mountains, blue distances, blue cities” - all these positive combinations of words are conditionally used as symbols of pristineness, extraordinary purity, and mystery. The most surprising thing is that physics was involved here too.

So, sometimes over an open area covered with greenery, not too polluted due to human activity, a very red, tinny blue haze appears. The blue mountains of the Caucasus are often mentioned in literature. The Blue Mountains in Australia are famous for their blue haze. The blue color of the “haze” is due to the scattering of light on the smallest parts, the size of which is much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. Such parts can be organic macromolecules emitted by the plant, as well as tiny particles swept off the sharp parts of plants by the earth's electric field. So, in places where tiny particles accumulate, an additional effect of scattering of the blue part of the spectrum occurs.

Concluding the article, we offer the reader plots for independent consideration.

1. Why does a New Year's tree from a distance (say, standing in the main square of your city) appear red in the evening, although it is illuminated with lights of different colors?

2. If you happen to be sitting by the fire on New Year's Eve, don't forget to pose and maybe show off to those around you by asking them the following question. Why does a bonfire appear blue against the background of trees, but above the treetops (against the light sky) it looks yellow?

3. Drop a few drops of milk into a glass of water and look through it at the glowing light bulb. The light bulb will appear reddish-yellow. If you look at the light reflected from the glass, it will be blue. Explain the observed color difference.
