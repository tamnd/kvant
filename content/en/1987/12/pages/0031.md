Lenino, the properties of the soap film are the same for both bubbles.

$$\frac{\Delta V}{V} \sim \frac{\Delta t}{r^4}.$$

If we have two bubbles of radii $r_1$ and $r_2$, then the ratio of time during which the volume of each of them will decrease by the same small part is

$$\frac{\Delta t_1}{\Delta t_2} = \frac{r_2^4}{r_1^4},$$

but at the same time, the ratio of the radii remains the same, which means that the ratio of the time of disappearance of the bubbles will be the same. So,

$$t_2 = 16t_1.$$

F1071. In the diagram shown in figure 1, the light bulb burns equally brightly both with the key K closed and with the key K open; $R_1 = R_3 = 90$ Ohm, $R_2 = 180$ Ohm, $U = 54$ V. Find the direction on the light bulb.

Note that the resistance of the light bulb depends on the direction on it: with increasing voltage, the heating of the filament increases, and the resistance of the light bulb increases. According to the conditions of the problem, the heating of the threads with the key closed and open is the same; therefore, the direction on the light bulb $U_\pi$ and its resistance $R_\pi$ are the same in both cases.

Figures 2 and 3 show equivalent circuits with the switch closed and open. Resistance of sections $BC$ and $AC$ —

$$R_{BC} = \frac{RR_3}{R + R_3}, \quad R_{AC} = R_2 + R_{BC}. \tag{1}$$

Substituting into (1) the values ​​of resistances $R_2$ and $R_3$ (in ohms), we obtain

$$R_{BC} = \frac{90R}{R + 90}, \quad R_{AC} = \frac{270(R + 60)}{R + 90}. \tag{2}$$

Voltage on the light bulb when the key is closed

$$U_\pi = \frac{U}{R_{AC}} R_{BC}.$$

Taking into account (2) and the fact that $U = 54$ B, we obtain

$$U_\pi = \frac{18R}{R + 60}. \tag{3}$$

A similar calculation with the key open gives

$$U_\pi = \frac{36R}{R + 150}. \tag{4}$$

From equations (3) and (4) the voltage across the light bulb is found:

$$U_\pi = 6 \text{ В.}$$

F1072. Why does the presence of an ultrafillet component in the spectrum worsen the sharpness of the image obtained on photographic film?

The index of glass prefill and, therefore, the focal length of the object depends on the wavelength of the radiation. By choosing the shape and materials of the object and the optimal distance to the film, the virtual elimination of blurring of the image when photographing will be added. The corresponding tricks do not concern wavelengths outside the visible part of the spectrum. Therefore, the contact of ultraphyllet rays with photographic film will lead to a general blurring of the image. To combat this disadvantage, filters are used that do not transmit ultraviolet images. For the same reason, on some objects a separate distance cabinet is applied for shooting in the infrared range.
