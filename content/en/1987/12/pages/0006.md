This direction quickly became more complicated: the enemy threw in fresh troops, many times superior to our forces in tanks, guns and personnel. In this regard, the command of the Reserve Front transferred the 8th Krasnopresensk Division from the 32nd to the 24th Army, giving the Krasnopresensky soldiers a new combat mission. It was necessary to quickly withdraw from the prepared reliable positions and redeploy to the area of ​​the village of Uvarovo, Smolensk region, which is somewhat south of Yelnya.

In the autumn mud, the convoys with ammunition and products fell behind, but the soldiers of the 975th artillery regiment, like the entire 8th division, completed their task, delaying the enemy collapsing towards Moscow for several days. The militias of the Moscow multi-guards will fight here side by side with the personnel units of the Soviet Army. This battle near Yelnya caused great losses to the occupiers: about 45 thousand soldiers and officers. Our losses were also great: more than a third of the militia of the 8th Krasnopresensk Division died or were wounded and captured. Among the dead and missing were many young scientists - graduate students of mechanite: S. S. Kudashev, A. I. Gerchikov, S. Ya. Karnov, M. E. Glerman, M. I. Pesin, A. I. Vikhrov, V. N. Zasukhin, M. D. Broginsky, H. M. Milshtein, G. N. Alekseev and others.

Many graduates of the mechanics of the forty-first year were assigned to design bureaus and defense enterprises provided with reservations. However, they too voluntarily went to the front; many joined the militia. Among them were Mikhail Ivanov, who already had several working specialties at that time. Everywhere he was active in Komsomol and party work. In 1936, having extensive work experience, M.I. Ivanov entered the mechanical engineering department of Moscow University and graduated with honors at the beginning of the war. Mikhail Ivanovich Ivanov was enlisted in the 975th artillery regiment with the rank of captain. He did not return from the bloody battles near Yelnya in early October '41. His comrades who had just graduated from mechanical engineering also died there: Mstislav Dolgov, Oleg Sorokin, Evgeny Pavlov, Pyotr Sokolov and fourth-year students Alexander Sivkov, Vladimir Muranov, Viktor Podyachev and many others.

Among those who returned from these brutal battles near Yelnya was Gorimir Gorimirovich Cherny, now an academician and director of the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University.

Together with students and graduate students of mechanite, associate professor Nikolai Borisovich Vedenisov joined the militia. A love for exact sciences was instilled in him by his father B. N. Vedenisov, an outstanding scientist and scientist, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences, laureate of the State Prize. In 1922, N.B. Vedenisov entered the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University. He studied together with A. N. Tikhonov, V. V. Nemyshka, L. A. Tumarkin, A. N. Cherkasov, famous mathematicians of the future. In their second year, these mathematicians took part in a seminar on topology organized by P. S. Urson and P. S. Aleksandrov.

After completing his studies at the university, N. B. Vedenisov taught mathematics at the Moscow State Pedagogical Institute, while simultaneously working at Moscow University. In the years preceding the Great Patriotic War, he was an assistant professor at the Military Artillery Academy. He had disciples everywhere; In the topological seminar of Moscow State University, he supervised the work of graduate and postgraduate students.

Together with employees of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, graduate students and employees, N. B. Vedenisov enrolled in the 8th Krasnopresensk Division. A few years before the war, he sprained his spine and doctors recommended that he stay in the rear. Nikolai Borisovich answered everyone succinctly: I should be where my students are - at the front. Like most Mekhanite militias, he was sent to the 975th artillery regiment of the 8th Krasnopresensk division. In the battles near Yelnya, N.B. Vedenisov was wounded, captured and died in October 1941.

Hard trials befell those who were captured. You can read about their fate in the book “Missing Without Meeting” by S.P. Zlobin, who endured four years in prison camps in Germany.
