Let us introduce a coordinate system with the origin at the point of impact of shard No. 3. Then the time dependences of the coordinates of the first and third shards have the form

$$y_1(t) = H + \frac{1}{2} vt - \frac{gt^2}{2},$$

$$y_3(t) = H - vt - \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

Since $y_1(T_2) = 0, y_3(T_1) = 0$, then from equations (2)

$$0 = H + \frac{1}{2} vT_2 - \frac{gT_2^2}{2}, \quad 0 = H - vT_1 - \frac{gT_1^2}{2}$$

we find

$$H = g \frac{T_2T_1}{2} \frac{T_1 + 2T_2}{2T_1 + T_2}.$$

F1070. A boy blows a soap bubble from a long coarse bar. Inflate the bubble, it releases the grub from the mouth, while the bubble blows back into the grub and time $t$ finally disappears. How long will it take to deflate an input bubble of a larger radius in the same way? Assume that the air moves through the coarse rather slowly.

The air flows through the coarse slowly, so the force of friction can be considered proportional to the speed of air flow. The excess pressure in the bubble is small compared to atmospheric pressure, so we neglect the change in air density in the bubble.

The force of friction at any moment almost balances the force of excess pressure, and $\Delta p$ is inversely proportional to the radius of the bubble. Then we can write:
