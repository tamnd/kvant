The apothesis of “Principles” is the third book, introduced “On the System of the World.” Here Newton demonstrated the full power of mechanics, analyzing many things, including scales and complex astronomical phenomena, with the help of the laws of motion he formulated and the law of universal gravitation, discussed at the beginning of the third book. Newton constructs his presentation in such a way that the law of universal gravitation is, as it were, deduced on the basis of a generalization of the results of observations. The formulation of this law that is familiar to us follows from Theorem VII (“Gravity exists for all bodies in general and is proportional to the mass of each of them”) and Corollary 2 of it (“Gravity for individual equal parts of bodies is figuratively proportional to the squares of the distances of places to the part”), as well as the third data law. In this part, Newton discusses the peculiarities of the movement of planets and Lunya, builds theories of tides and theories of the movement of comets. He boldly unites “earthly” and “heavenly” mechanics, thereby demonstrating the significant possibilities of science.
