Plane transformations in construction problems

1. Vertex $B$ of triangle $ABC$ goes to vertex $C$ under transformation $S_{l} \circ H_{k}$, where $l$ is the bisector of angle $A$, $k = AC/BC$.

2. The segment under consideration is symmetrical with respect to the perpendicular to the given line drawn through the center of the circle.

3. Figures $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ are arcs from the point of which the segment between the beacons is visible at given angles, transformation $F$ is a parallel transfer to a vector depicting the movement of the ship between the moments of measurement.

4. Construct the segment $AA_{1}$ and find the locus of the points $B$ and $C$; take advantage of the fact that they are symmetrical with respect to $A_{2}$.

5. The vertex of the rhombus goes into the neighboring vertex with the composition of a rotation by $90^\circ$ around the center $O$ and an inversion with the center $O$ and the coefficient $S/2$, where $S$ is the area of ​​the rhombus.

9. Construction. Let $A'$ be a point symmetrical to point $A$ with respect to a given line. Let us draw circles with centers $A$ and $A'$ tangent to the given line, and draw tangents to each of these circles from the point $B$. The points of intersection of these tangents with a given line will be denoted by $M_{1}$, $M_{2}$, $M_{3}$, $M_{4}$. The conditions of the problem can only be satisfied by the points $M_{1}$, $M_{2}$, $M_{3}$, $M_{4}$ (proof!) and each of them does or does not satisfy them depending on the location of the points $C$ and $D$. If, for example, points $A$ and $B$ are located as shown in figure 4, then $\angle BM_{1}X=2\angle AM_{1}Y$, $\angle BM_{2}Y=2\angle AM_{2}Y$, $\angle BM_{3}Y=2\angle AM_{3}X$ and $\angle BM_{4}X=2\angle AM_{4}X$. So, say, if the point $C$ lies between $M_{2}$ and $M_{3}$, and the point $D$ lies between $M_{3}$ and $M_{4}$, then there are three solutions: $M_{2}$, $M_{3}$, $M_{4}$; if point $C$ lies to the right of $M_{4}$, and point $D$ lies between $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$, then there are no solutions at all.

Sweeping. Point $B$ may fall inside one of the constructed circles; then it is impossible to check the tangent to the corresponding circle from it. If point $B$ falls on a circle, then one tangent is drawn to this circle. In addition, the drawn tangent can be parallel to the line $CD$. You can look into these cases on your own.

10. $(1/\sqrt{2})^{n}$. Solution. From condition $\sin \alpha_{1} \cdot \dots \cdot \sin \alpha_{n}=\cos \alpha_{1} \cdot \dots \cdot \cos \alpha_{n}$. Therefore $2^{n}(\sin \alpha_{1})^{2} \cdot \dots \cdot (\sin \alpha_{n})^{2}=\sin 2\alpha_{1} \cdot \dots \cdot \sin 2\alpha_{n} \leq 1$ and $\sin \alpha_{1} \cdot \dots \cdot \sin \alpha_{n} \leq(1/\sqrt{2})^{n}$. Equality is achieved at $\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{n}=\pi/4$.
