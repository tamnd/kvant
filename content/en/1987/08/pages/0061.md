X Tournament of Young Physicists

Sciences nourish the youth,
Joy is served to the old,
In a happy life
decorate,
In the unfortunate - employee
take care...
M. V. Lomonosov

The tournament is held by the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. M.V. Lomonosov from September 1987 to February 1988.

Solutions to the problems of the correspondence collective competition must be sent no later than November 16, 1987 to the address: 119899 Moscow GSP, Moscow State University, Faculty of Physics, Department of Flask Physics, Organizing Committee of TUV-X. In the “To” column, write: correspondence competition TUF-X and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending. Fill out the following forms in the envelope:

1. School mailing address.
2. Last name, first name, patronymic of the team leader.
3. List of solution authors.

At the beginning of solving each problem, be sure to indicate the city, school number, class, surnames and names of the authors of the solution. For experimental problems, attach detailed descriptions of the installation, their diagrams, preferably photographs and experimental data. Indicate the solution to the problem and independently formulated problems will be awarded with certificates from the tourist and proposed for publication in the journal «Kvant».

More detailed information about TUF-X can be obtained from the TUF Organizing Committee at the above address.

Tasks for the correspondence collective competition TUF-X

1. Come up with it yourself. Propose original projects for the technical and scientific use of high-temperature superconductivity.

2. "Evening" Design and manufacture a portable radio receiver that does not use power sources. Test parameter: $x=P/Lm$, where $P$ is the sound pressure at a distance of 1 m from the receiver, $L$ is the maximum linear size of the receiver, $m$ is the mass of the receiver.

3. Camera obscura. Take a group portrait of your team using the cover camera. Justify the physical principles of obtaining a high-quality signal using such a device.

4. Electric circuit. Several nodes ($n<10$) are connected to each other by batteries with known emf and $r$. Create a computer calculation program to determine the potential difference between the first and second nodes. The criterion for the quality of the program is the time from the start of data entry into the computer (tables of EMF values ​​and $r$) until the correct result is issued.

5. Metrology. Determine the maximum accuracy of measuring length with a steel ruler.

6. Vacuum seller. An astronaut entrepreneur decided to supply vacuum from outer space to physical laboratories around the world. What are the chances of his venture being successful?

7. Sun at sunset. The visible disk of the Sun at sunset is solid. Experimentally modify and characterize these distortions. Calculate the theoretical ratio of the horizontal and vertical dimensions of the solar disk, tangent linearly to the horizon.

8. Color television. You need to design a four-color TV. What flowers would you choose as your main flowers? Should the lighting equipment change in this case?

9. The ninth wave.
“In front of a lot of sea waves. There are many of them. They can’t imagine counting.”
V. Pasterak Does the “Ninth Wave” exist? Please clarify this issue. The ideas presented in the article “Three, Seven, Ace...” (Knowledge is Power, 1987, No. 1; pp. 97-104) can be used as a starting point.

10. Spontaneous combustion.
“But also from the wind, when the trees swayed with their branches,
Staggering greatly, they will learn to lean one on top of the other,
Their powerful rubbing exudes fire, and I flog,
Suddenly bursting into flames
a hot flame will sparkle and rise,
If in return they rub with both trunks and branches.”
Lukrets Kar.
This is how the Roman philosopher explained the origin of forest fires. Assess the likelihood of such a fire and its place among the factors that cause fires in nature, i.e., those that are not a consequence of human activity.

11. Incandescent lamp. It is said that two 60 W bulbs produce more light than three 40 W bulbs. Is this true? Explore how the light output and life of an incandescent lamp changes with small changes in supply voltage.

12. Spring in the city. Spring comes earlier in the city than in the countryside. Describe the main factors leading to this and make numerical estimates. In particular, what will happen if one day we take all the snow from Moscow out of town?

13. Heat transfer. Investigate heat transfer through a vertical column of water in two cases: $T_1 < T_2$ and $T_1 > T_2$.

1 - water column,
2 - heat transfer pipe.
