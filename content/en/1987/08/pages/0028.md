Lemma

If $f'(x) > g'(x)$ at $x \geq x_0$, then $f(x) > f(x_0) + g(x) - g(x_0)$ at $x > x_0$ (indeed, the function $f(x) - g(x)$ has a positive product and therefore strictly increases at $x \geq x_0$).

$$f(a+1) > 2a+1;$$

then, according to the lemma given in the fields for the functions $f(y)$ and $g(y) = y + a$ ($g'(y) = 1$), from (2) and (3) it will follow that for $y \geq a+1$
$$a^y - y^a = f(y) \geq f(a+1) + g(y) - g(a+1) > 2a+1 + y - (a+1) = a+y.$$

Let's evaluate the product $f(y)$:
$$f'(y) = \ln a \cdot a^y - ay^{a-1} = (\ln a - 1)a^y + (a^y - y^a) + y^{a-1}(y-a) > y^{a-1}(y-a)$$
(ln $a \geqslant \ln 3 > 1$, $a^y - y^a > 0$ as proven above). This estimate immediately implies (2), since $y^{a-1}(y-a) \geqslant (a+1)^{a-1}$ at $y \geqslant a+1$. To prove (3), note that $y^{a-1}(y-a)$ is the product of the function $g(y) = y^{a+1}/(a+1) - y^a$, and by the field lemma (for $x = a+1$, $x_0 = a$)
$$f(a+1) > f(a) + g(a+1) - g(a) = -g(a) = a^a - \frac{a^{a+1}}{a+1} = \frac{a^a}{a+1} = \left[ a^{a-2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{a+1}\right) \right]a.$$

The function in square brackets is obviously increasing and for $a \geqslant 3$ no less than $3(1-1/4) = 9/4$. That's why
$$f(a+1) > 2a + \frac{a}{4} \geqslant 2a + \frac{3}{4};$$
but the numbers $f(a+1)$ and $2a$ are integers, which means $f(a+1) \geqslant 2a+1$, and equality here is possible only for $a=3$, but also in this case $f(4) = 3^4 - 4^3 = 81 - 64 > 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7$.
