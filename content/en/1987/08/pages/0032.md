$$A_A = q(\varphi_\infty - \varphi_A), A_B = q(\varphi_\infty - \varphi_B).$$

Potential $\varphi_\infty$ at a great distance from the capacitor, as is usually done, we set equal to zero. Let's find potentials $\varphi_A$ and $\varphi_B$.

Since the charge of the particle $q$ is much smaller than the capacitor, we can assume that the particle practically does not change the total charge of the capacitor. The field inside the capacitor is uniform and its strength is $E = U/d$, where $d$ is the distance between the plates. Since the negatively and positively charged plates are equidistant from the center line $OO'$ inside the capacitor, the potential $\varphi_{OO'}$ on this line will be zero. Since the field is homogeneous, for points $A, B$ and $C$ we can write:

$$\varphi_A - \varphi_C = -E \frac{d}{2}, \varphi_B - \varphi_C = E \frac{d}{2}.$$

But $\varphi_C = 0$, and therefore

$$\varphi_A = -\frac{Ed}{2} = -\frac{U}{2}, \varphi_B = \frac{U}{2}.$$

From here comes $A_A$ and $A_B$:

$$A_A = -q\varphi_A = \frac{qU}{2}, A_B = -q\varphi_B = -\frac{qU}{2}.$$

Thus,

$$\Delta K_A = \frac{mv_A^2}{2} - \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{qU}{2},$$

$$\Delta K_B = \frac{mv_B^2}{2} - \frac{mv^2}{2} = -\frac{qU}{2},$$

From here is:

$$v_A = \sqrt{v^2 + \frac{qU}{m}}, v_B = \sqrt{v^2 - \frac{qU}{m}}.$$

It can be seen that if $v^2 < \frac{qU}{m}$, then the particle will not flow through the capacitor at all.

F1051. In a magnetic field, the induction of which is horizontal and equal to B, a thin metal ring rolls without breaking at a speed v, in which there is a very small gap of length l; vector B is perpendicular to the plane of the ring (Fig. 1). Find the induced emf at the moment when the AOS angle (see Fig.ok) is equal to α.

The direct way to find the EMF involves dividing the loop into small (almost rectilinear) sections and summing up all the elementary EMFs. We will do otherwise. If there is an additional loop to the whole, then the EMF in it will be equal to zero (the circuit is closed). On the other side,

$$0 = \mathcal{E}_0 + \mathcal{E}'$$

where $\mathcal{E}_0$ is the required potential difference, $\mathcal{E}'$ is the induction emf of the additional element. Hence,

$$\mathcal{E}_0 = -\mathcal{E}' = -uBl \sin \varphi$$

where $u$ is the speed of the element, $l$ is its length, $\varphi$ is the angle that the vector $\vec{u}$ makes with $\vec{B}$. As can be seen from figure 2,

$$u = 2v \sin \frac{\alpha}{2}, \varphi = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\alpha}{2}.$$

Thus,

$$\mathcal{E}_0 = -2Blv \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \cos \frac{\alpha}{2} = -Blv \sin \alpha$$

— The EMF changes according to a sinusoidal law.

L. G. Markovich
