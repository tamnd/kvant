To reduce the yield, the dye should be sprinkled onto the surface approximately 1 minute after the start. If the vessel is well centered (i.e., the axis of the vessel must coincide with the axis of rotation), an intermediate pattern is formed in the liquid - convective rings (Fig. 3). These rings are formed due to the action of two mechanisms - convection and uneven rotation of the liquid, due to the fact that not all of the liquid swirled at once at the same speed. Cold liquid falls down the wells, and warm liquid rises up between the wells. Due to this, the so-called sdigg instability arises on the rings, i.e. instability caused by the fact that on one side of the ring the liquid moves in one direction, and on the other in the other. This type of instability is observed everywhere in nature: in the ocean, in the atmosphere at the border of oncoming currents. In this case, the border is gradually destroyed. Thus, in our experiments, vortices begin to form on the rings, which are then built into a certain structure.

Note that one of the determining parameters in the system is the depth of the liquid. If you take a vessel in which the depth in one place is less than the depth in another (put a gap in one half), then vortices with different structures arise in different parts of the jar and intense vortex interactions will also occur at the interface between the lattices.

Theoretical studies of even the simplest vortex movements are very complex. The experiment allows us to study the basic process “building blocks” that make up a complex movement. Thus, the interaction of “dusty devils” in empty ones occurs similarly to what is observed in the simplest experiments, i.e., along a double helix. These same experiments make it possible to answer the question: when will vortices live in peace with each other, and when will one absorb the other, which is constantly observed in the atmosphere and is easily visible on the so-called weather maps.
