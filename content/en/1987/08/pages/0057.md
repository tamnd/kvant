Work, energy, heat

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. I. BUZDIN,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. S. KROTOV

A significant number of problems in entrance exams in physics concern the processes of converting mechanical energy into thermal energy and vice versa. For the first time, a series of such problems appears in the “Mechanics” section of a school physics course when studying mechanical movements in which it is necessary to take into account friction forces, when considering inelastic collisions of bodies, etc. The second time schoolchildren encounter similar problems in the section “Thermal phenomena. Molecular Physics" when getting acquainted with various thermal processes, the work of thermal engines, etc.

Conversion of mechanical energy into heat

The basis for solving problems on this topic is the law of conservation of energy, formulated in the form: when bodies interact in a closed system, the amount of heat released is equal to the difference in the mechanical energy of the system before and after the interaction.

Problem 1. A bullet of mass m, lying horizontally with speed v, sticks in a bag of sand moving towards it with speed u. Determine the amount of heat released.

When solving this problem, we will assume that the process of a bullet being lost in a sandbag occurs so quickly that the change in the potential energy of the bullet can be neglected (this will occur if the force acting on the bullet from the sandbag is significantly greater than the gravitational force of the bullet).
