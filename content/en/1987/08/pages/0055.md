⟦folio 53⟧

From anything - anything

L. A. STEINGARTZ

One day I needed to do some calculations. I reached my starting calculator, looked into the notebook - the first column is the number 23 - and pressed the key with the number 2. The key did not work. I tried to press the key with the number 3 - again it didn’t work. I pressed the “1” key - everything is in order, the display shows a green one. I checked the keys with operation signs - they worked. How can this be? It occurred to me that $23=11\times(1+1)+1$, and I quickly pressed the “1” key five times in the required sequence, twice on “+”, and once on $\times$, and saw 23 on the display.

Getting carried away, I soon forgot about the initial calculations I needed. I was already worried about the “principal” question - is it possible to represent a natural number with the help of any one product?

free number? Here I must explain to the reader what meaning I include in the words “represent with a single number...”. I think like a person who has a calculator in his hands: I think that in the notation $\sqrt{2}$ there is only one two, in the notation $\lg \sqrt{2}$ there is also one two, although in fact $\sqrt{2}$ is $\sqrt{2}$, and $\lg \sqrt{2}$ is $\log_{10}^2\sqrt{2}$. But I have keys on which, in particular, there are icons $\sqrt{2}$ and $\lg$: I clicked on them and got the result!

If the reader agrees to such “rules,” then I can invite him to play the game “from anything - anything.” I will present a whole series of arguments, and the reader is left to verify their “fairness.”

1. Represent any natural number using three twos:
$$n = -\log_2(\log_2\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^2})$$

2. Represent any natural number using two numbers, 2 and 10:
$$n = -\log_2(\lg \sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^10})$$
