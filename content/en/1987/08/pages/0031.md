F1049. The wooden raft was pushed away from the river bank so that at the initial moment its speed was equal to v and directed perpendicular to the bank. The trajectory of the raft is shown in figure. The red cross on the trajectory is the place where the raft is located after time T after the start of movement. Assuming the river speed to be constant and equal to v, find graphically the trajectory points at which the raft is located at times 2T, 3T, 4T.

Let us consider the motion of the raft in a reference frame associated with water, i.e., moving at a speed of $\vec{u}$. In this system, the raft has an initial speed of $\vec{v}' = \vec{v} - \vec{u}$ and moves in a straight line. In this case, the speed of the raft $\vec{v}'$ decreases due to the action of the water resistance force on it. (If there were no resistance, then after time T the raft would end up at point c with coordinates

$$x_c = uT, y_c = vT.)$$ The displacement of the raft relative to the shore during time T is the sum of its displacement $s_\pi = v'T$ relative to the water and the displacement of the water $s_B = uT$ (see figure). During time 2T, the water movement will be twice as large. Let us lay off a segment of length 2s_B from point O along the x axis and draw a straight line parallel to $s_\pi$ through the point with coordinate 2s_B. This straight line intersects the trajectory of the raft at the point located with it by the cross; the raft will be at this point 2T after the start of the movement.

A similar construction is used to find the points at which the plot will be after time 3T, 4T, etc.
