⟦folio 48⟧

About one recurrent sequence

V. V. GURARIY, K. A. STYKAS
(10th grade students)

In the magazine “In the World of Science” No. 3 for 1984, an article by Brian Hayes “The Rise and Fall of Gradient Numbers” was published. This article talked about one interactive problem about number sequences. We would like to talk about it and some interesting results associated with attempts to solve it.

The formulation of this problem is extremely simple. Let's take some natural number. If it is even, divide it by 2, otherwise multiply it by 3 and add 1. Repeat the same operations with the resulting number. The question of the problem is the following: how will the resulting sequence look depending on the initial number?

In general, we can expect two possibilities: either this sequence is divergent, that is, the resulting numbers will increase indefinitely, or after each number of operations we will receive a number that is already equal to that already encountered in our sequence. In this case, the numbers in it will begin to repeat periodically, and this sequence will become “cyclical.”

Let's consider the very first natural number - one. Our sequence will be like this: 1→4→2→1. Then everything is repeated from the beginning. Numbers 2 and 4 are considered meaningless, since they have already been encountered, and we will not receive a new sequence of numbers.

Let's move on to the number 3. As you can easily see, it is followed by the numbers 10, 5,16,8,4,2,1,... We again come to a closed cycle 1,4,2,1,...
