⟦folio 44⟧

Let us raise both sides of the vector equality $\vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = 0$ into a scalar square. We get

$$\vec{a}^2 + \vec{b}^2 + \vec{c}^2 + 2\vec{a}\vec{b} + 2\vec{b}\vec{c} + 2\vec{a}\vec{c} = 0,$$

where $\vec{a}\vec{b} = ab$ cos $\alpha$, $\vec{b}\vec{c} = bc$ cos $\beta$, $\vec{a}\vec{c} = ac$ cos $\gamma$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ are the angles between the corresponding vectors.

From here

$$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = -2(\vec{a}\vec{b} + \vec{b}\vec{c} + \vec{a}\vec{c}) \leqslant 2(|\vec{a}\vec{b}| + |\vec{b}\vec{c}| + |\vec{a}\vec{c}|) < 2(ab + bc + ac) = (a + b + c)^2 - (a^2 + b^2 + c^2) = 1 - (a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$$

(think about why strict inequality appeared in our reasoning).

Thus, $2(a^2 + b^2 + c^2) < 1$, $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 < 1/2$, which is what was required.

This inequality is also exact, i.e., it is impossible to take the constant $k < 1/2$ such that the inequality $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 < k$ holds for all vectors satisfying the conditions of the problem. In fact, let $k = 1/2 - \varepsilon$. Let's take three collinear vectors of length $\varepsilon$, $1/2 - \varepsilon$, $1/2$, respectively, with vectors of length $\varepsilon$ and $1/2 - \varepsilon$ in one direction, and a vector of length $1/2$ in the opposite direction. They satisfy the conditions of the problem, but at the same time

$$\varepsilon^2 + \left(\frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} + 2\varepsilon^2 - \varepsilon > \frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon = k.$$

The problem we discussed allows for the following natural generalizations:

Prove that the sum of the squares of the sides of a $n$-gon of perimeter 1:

a) not less than $1/n$;
b) less than $1/2$;
b) can the statement in point 6) be used?

Selected School Problems

Eighth grade

1. Find all integer values ​​of $x$ for which the expression $\frac{7x+1}{3x+4}$ takes integer values.
2. Prove that the number $2^{10} + 5^{12}$ is composite.
3. Prove that in any convex pentagon you can choose three diagonals so that you can form a triangle from them.
4. Given a regular pentagon $P_1$ inscribed in a circle, and a regular pentagon $P_2$ circumscribed around this circle and touching it at the vertices of the pentagon $P_1$. Let us draw through the vertices of the pentagon $P_1$ re-prediculars to its sides. They will form a regular pentagon $P_3$. Prove that if $a_1, a_2, a_3$ are sides of the pentagons $P_1, P_2, P_3$, then $a_3^2 = a_2^2 - a_1^2$.
5. The bases of a trapezoid have lengths 3 and 15. Is it possible that the radius of a circle inscribed in a trapezoid has length 4?

Ninth grade

6. Prove that the equation $xy + yz + zx = 1$ has an infinite number of integer solutions.
7. Solve the system of equations in positive numbers

$$\begin{cases} x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n = 3, \\ \frac{1}{x_1} + \frac{1}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{x_n} = 3. \end{cases}$$

8. In the acute triangle $ABC$ the altitudes $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ are drawn, intersecting at the point $H$. Prove the formula for the radius $R$ of the circumcircle:

$$R = \frac{1}{2}AH^2 \cdot \frac{A_1H}{B_1H \cdot C_1H}.$$

9. Points $C$ and $D$ lie on a line, but points $A$ and $B$ do not lie on it. Using a compass and ruler, find a point $M$ on this line such that $\angle BMD = 2\angle AMC$.
10. It is known that $tg a_1 \cdot tg a_2 \dots tg a_n = 1$. Find the most possible value of the product $sin a_1 \cdot sin a_2 \dots sin a_n$.

Tenth grade

11. Solve the system of equations

$$x_1^2 = x_3, x_2^2 = x_4, \dots, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1 = x_n, x_n^2 = x_n - 1$$

12. From the square $ABCD$ from the side $a$ they cut out the four flattest round ones, so that the straight line and the square had a common angle $A$. What are the flattest dimensions of a rectangle?

13. Is it possible to use a plane so that a regular triangle is obtained?
14. Four rays emerge from the point $O$ in space, such that the angle between any two rays is the same. Find this angle.
15. At what natural number $n$ will the number $\frac{n^2}{(1,1)^n}$ be the flattest?

Problems 2, 3, 6, 9-11, 13, 15 were suggested to us by L. D. Kurlyandchik, problems 1, 5 - P. P. Gornusha, problems 4 - T. N. Demina, problems 8 - Yu. N. Khokhlenko, problems 7, 14 - V. A. Yasinsky, problems 12 - E. G. Gotman.
