Sixteen geometric series

Finite simple groups are not limited to the series $Z_p$ and $A_n$. There are 16 other series of finite simple groups. They are already more complex. The groups of these series are, as a rule, finite subgroups of “Lie groups”. We can say that they are more geometric than algebraic in nature. Many mathematicians participated in their discovery. The fundamental role was played by the work of the Frenchman C. Chevalley, carried out shortly after the war and completed in the fifties. Infinite geometric series cover the bulk of typical simple groups and admit of many different descriptions, adapted for various purposes.

Sporadic simple groups

Long before the task of identifying all 18 infinite series of finite simple groups succumbed to a flood of researchers, the existence of isolated simple groups called “sporadic” was known. Before 1965, there were only five of them, and all of them were associated with the name of the French analytical mathematician E. Mathieu. The word "group" did not appear in the titles of his works of the period 1861-1873, where functions and structure combinators were described in spaces of dimensions 12 and 24 with very special and red symmetry. This symmetry is controlled by five groups, now called Mathieu groups (see table on page 3). It's interesting that in our time groups
