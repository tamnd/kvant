Example 2. In triangle ABC: AB=AC, $\angle BAC=40^\circ$. Outside triangle ABC, but inside angle ABC, a point M is taken such that $\angle AMB=30^\circ$, $\angle BMC=20^\circ$ (Fig. 4). Find $\angle ABM$.

Solution. By property IV, point A is the center of a circle circumscribed about triangle BCM. Therefore AB=AM and $\angle ABM=\angle AMB=30^\circ$.

Example 2 can be generalized to any triangle ABC in which AB=AC, $\angle BAC=2\angle BMC$, $\angle AMB=\alpha$. Then $\angle ABM=\alpha$.

Problem 3. In triangle ABC: AB=BC, $\angle ABC=80^\circ$. Point M lies inside the triangle, and $\angle MAC=10^\circ$, $\angle MCA=30^\circ$. Find $\angle BMC$ (Fig. 5).

Solution. Angles BAC and BCA are 50°. Let O be the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle AMC. Then by Property III $\angle AOM=60^\circ$, so the triangle AOM is right. Further, BO is the perpendicular bisector to segment AC (since AB=BC and AO=OC). Primogol triangles ABK and AOK are congruent because $\angle OAK=60^\circ-10^\circ=50^\circ=\angle BAK$. Therefore, AB=AO, but AO=AM, so AB=AM. In an isosceles triangle ABM, angles ABM and AMB are equal (180°-40°):2=70°. Finally, $\angle BMC=360^\circ-140^\circ-70^\circ=150^\circ$ (see Fig. 5).

Extra conditions in competition problems

In this note, we talk about two little-known problems in mathematics, proposed at the time (in 1971) of MEPhI and MIPT and published together with solutions in 1972 in «Kvant». These are the tasks:

Task 1 (MEPhI, 1971; «Kvant», 1972, No. 6, pp. 52, 75-76). If you divide a two-digit number by the sum of its digits, you get the quotient of 4 and remainder 3. If you divide this number by the product of its digits, you get the quotient of 3 and remainder 5. Find this number.

Task 2 (MIPT, 1971; «Kvant», 1972, No. 5, p. 43). If a two-digit number is divided by the product of its digits, then the quotient will be 3, and the remainder will be 9. If the product of its digits is subtracted from the square of the sum of the digits of this number, then this number will be obtained. Find this number.

In fact, to solve problem 1 it is enough to know only the second condition, and to solve problem 2 - the first condition.

Indeed, having written the required number in the form 10x+y (1≤x≤9, 1≤y≤9), in problem 1 from the second condition we obtain

10x+y=3xy+5 (xy≠0).

Let's express x in terms of y:

$$x = \frac{5-y}{10-3y}.$$
