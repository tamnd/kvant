a handsome, big-eyed man, who was intently writing tanish signs in a small notebook and speaking with an accent that was, of course, a German spinon.

In a prison near Paris, Sophus Lie's profound ideas about the application of group theory in analysis, in particular in differential equations, are ripening. The foundations of a theory that has been fruitfully developing to this day will emerge—the theory of Lie groups. And in his homeland, Klein is pondering the role of groups in geometry. This role turns out to be decisive for the very foundations of geometric science, and Klein explains this with a block in his meaning of the Ergantzen program.*)
