⟦folio 46⟧

he ran along the wires and made his way from one ball to another with a bright electric spark.

The spark burned for only a moment, but during this elusive world, a quick reflection from the mirror managed to fall on the photographic plate. He ran across the plate with the speed of an artillery shell and traced his path on it.

Right there on the spot, without leaving the device, Federaren developed the plate and printed a photographic SIM card. A narrow stripe was clearly visible on the SIM card - a mark left by a bunny.

Federaren measured the length of the strip - one and a half centimeters. He knew the speed of the bunny - 60,000 centimeters per second. How long does it take for someone to click on a record? The length of the strip - 1.5 must be divided by the speed of the bunny - 60,000, it turns out 0.000025. So, that means twenty-five millionths of a second—that’s how long it took for the record to click without a trace, and that’s how long the electric spark lived.

The Federaren watch performed its assigned task perfectly. Continuous sparks were measured. The task that Federaren set for himself was solved.

But, peering into his upward snare, Federaren became convinced that his watch had made another discovery. They not only measured the length of the spark's life, but also found out what the purpose of this short life was and compiled a detailed biography of the spark.

The trail drawn by the bunny on the SIM card turned out to be not continuous, but intermittent. It consisted of several light spots separated from each other by dark spaces.

This means that the electric spark that leaked between the balls of the spark gap did not burn with a uniform brilliance. During its entire life, which lasted only 25 millionths of a second, it flared up and went out several times. Short flashes came one after another so quickly that the human eye could not follow it - several flashes seemed to the eye of one. And only the wonderful Federaren watch was able to dissect the moment, decompose the spark into several separate flashes.

Federaren counted the number of towers on his symbol. There were eight of them, and each subsequent one was a little weaker than the previous one. Eight flashes in twenty-five millionths of a second! This means that the spark consisted of individual sparks that lit up and died out every three millionths of a second.

So, from the note made by the bunny, Federaren read the story of the electric spark, a story that lasted only one moment from beginning to end.

Experiments continue

Federaren repeated his experience more than once. He took either a single Leyden jar or a whole battery of ten, fifteen and even twenty Leyden jars. When I saw the balls almost closely, I moved them apart by a whole centimeter or one and a half. He changed and washed the balls - he took iron, copper, lead, and gold. In one experiment both balls were made of the same metal, in others they were made of different ones. Federaren took the wires that went from the Leyden jars to the spark gap balls, either short and thick, or long and thin. And each time he photographed the reflection of the spark in the rotating mirror.

Using photographic SIM cards, he measured how long the spark lasted, learned how it flashes, burns and goes out.

The sparks came out of different fury, different lengths, different durations, but each state was not from just one flash, but from many. The flashes followed each other every few millionths of a second and became weaker and weaker until the spark died out.

Why does the electric charge make its way between the spark gap balls in more than one jump?
