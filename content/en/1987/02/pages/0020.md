Oma's new stage?

The discovery of the quantization efficiency of cold resistance aroused great interest among physicists. This interest is understandable - currently there are not many ways to measure fundamental physical constants, and the emergence of new opportunities in this direction always ultimately leads to clarification of the values ​​of these constants, which is extremely important for all areas of physics. However, specialists in the field of measurement - metrology - showed no less interest in this discovery. The interest of metrologists is due to the fact that they saw in the efficiency of cold resistance quantization a way to maintain accurate values ​​of electrical resistance. You know, of course, that all basic physical quantities have etons. The unit of electrical resistance “Ohm” also has such a stage. The standard is a set of wire resistances, and when measuring any resistance with any instrument, we ultimately compare it with the resistance of that group of coils. But this approach has its drawbacks. Firstly, its resistance, while empty is insignificant, is still measured over time, which very significantly affects the accuracy with which resistance measurements can be trusted. Secondly, there can only be one such etaon - if we create two identical etaons, and after some time they change their meaning to coincide, then we will still have to choose which of them is “leading”. Thirdly, it can fail - say, it can die from a natural disaster, and this will immediately disrupt the integrity of the entire system for measuring electrical resistance. If the 1 Ohm eton is created based on the efficiency of quantization of cold resistance, then all of the listed disadvantages will disappear. Therefore, many countries are now working to create an ethan based on the quantized Hall efficiency. There is already an agreement that the transition to such etons begins as soon as laboratories in at least three countries achieve quantized values ​​of $R_x$, differing from each other by no more than $2 \cdot 10^{-7}$ Ohm. Apparently this will happen soon.

Archeology News

According to the latest data from archaeological excavations, the concept of a fraction as a part of a whole originated in the era of the primitive communal system, when no savage was able to eat a whole mammoth. As a result, with the disappearance of mammoths and in connection with the transition to smaller game, the need for fractions gradually disappeared, and soon they fell out of use altogether.

Simplicity of mathematics

John von Neumann, one of the greatest mathematicians of our century, speaking at the end of the 40s with a report on the future of electronic computers, said that

mathematics is only a very small and very simple part of life. When the audience responded to this, von Neumann added: “If people do not believe that mathematics is simple, it is only because they do not realize how complex life is.”
