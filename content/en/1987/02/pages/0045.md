⟦folio 43⟧

Inventors of the radiotelegraph
(Chapters from the book)

M. P. BRONSHTEIN

Who and when?
Who invented radio and when?

Some people answer this question: Aleksandr Stepanovich Popov invented it, and that was forty years ago. Others say: the radio was invented by the Pultanian Guglielmo Marconi.

And indeed: forty years ago, both Popov and Marconi simultaneously built the world's first radio stations and began sending the world's first radio telegrams.

But the history of radio began much earlier than the last radio telegram. The scientists who began the history of radio with their discoveries and experiments did not send or receive any radio telegrams. They did not strive to transmit any signals or music or human sounds over a distance. How surprised these first inventors of the radiotelegraph would have been if someone had told them that they were inventing the radiotelegraph!

The transmission of sounds, signals, images did not occupy them at all. They were interested in something else.

Have you ever seen electric sparks flying from electrified objects? Dazzling electric sparks that flare up for an instant and then immediately fade away again?

It was with these sparks that the history of radio began.

For many decades, physicists observed an electric spark, did experiments with it, and studied its properties. Finally, they wanted to know: how long does it take from the birth of a spark to its death? How long does an electric spark last?

The question was difficult. They usually answered it like this: it flares up and immediately fades away, it lives only for one moment. But what is a moment? A hundredth of a second or a thousandth or a millionth? How to find out, how to measure?

Every person feels the passage of time. We all distinguish a minute from two minutes, seconds from two seconds, and even ten hours of seconds from a whole.
