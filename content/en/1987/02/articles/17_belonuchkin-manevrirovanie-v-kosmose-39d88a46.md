Maneuvering in space

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. E. BELONUCHKIN

In the previous article by this author, “Kepler's Laws and School Physics” («Kvant», 1986, No. 2), neotary problems on the motion of various space objects were analyzed. This article discusses several more, now more complex, questions on the same topic.

It is likely that similar problems may be encountered in physics olympiads than in advanced exams. However, we believe that familiarity has an equal effect.

Large and small planets, comets, natural satellites of planets have been moving along the same trajectories for millions and millions of years. This is not the case with artificial spacecraft. Satellites move from orbit to orbit, dock and separate, and interplanetary stations are launched from them.

Every maneuver in space is a complex scientific and technical task. To solve it, it is necessary to take into account many different factors, carry out a lot of precise calculations, and carry out with pinpoint accuracy those operations that are prescribed by the calculation results.

We will consider the simplest maneuvers and make only the roughest estimates. Our most “sophisticated” ordinem will be Kepler’s second law, which states that the radius vector of a body freely moving in the vicinity of the gravitational center describes equal areas in equal periods of time.

Let's start with a well-known problem.

Problem 1. What should be the change in the speed of a satellite orbiting around the Earth so that it can leave the Earth forever?

To solve this problem we don’t even need Kepler’s second law. But we will try to remember several useful for the further formation of cosmic form and correspondences.
