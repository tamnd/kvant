How much energy do you think a student spends on homework? This question will probably confuse many readers: is it possible to count this energy? How? We answer: it is possible; here is the answer: on average about 500 kJ. And this is how we received this answer. We opened on page 118 a book with such a seemingly prosaic title - “Handbook of Physics and Technology”, and...

Further actions, reader, will become clear to you if you open this book. And we think that if you do this, you will no longer be able to open your eyes from its pages, although the main content of the book is tables, graphs, and numbers. “Numbers also have their own language, the ability to speak, to tell about various phenomena and natural phenomena...” It is impossible not to agree with these words of the book’s author, Anatoly Sergeevich Enochovich. The numbers will tell us a lot of new and interesting things...

You will learn, for example, that the length of the ciliate slipper is 300 micrometers, and the length of the Nile River - the longest river on Earth - is 6700 km, that the distance between telegraph poles is 50-60 m (Table No. 19).

Less time is spent running on the track, skating, and swimming. In which of these types of human movement does absolute speed show? Why is the speed of skating at a distance of 1000 m greater than at 500 m? Can a person in any sports exceed the speed of a swallow (17.5 m/s)? cheetah (31 m/s)?

From table No. 43, we learned that each point of the Earth on the spine of Moscow moves due to the rotation of the Earth at a speed of 259 m/s. At points of the equator this speed reaches 465 m/s. Why don't we notice this movement? Or are there still phenomena that allow us to notice it, just as we notice the movement of a train while in it?

Are there trees that drown in water? Such trees exist, these are bakut (“iron tree”) and ebony - the density of their wood is greater than the density of water. This information is provided by tables No. 49 and No. 55. The same tables report that of all types of wood, large wood has the lowest density: it floats “best” in water, and, as indicated in the footnote to table No. 55, the Kon-Tiki raft was made from it. That is why the participants of the voyage on the Kon-Tiki are looking for a ball tree in South America, overcoming enormous difficulties: during the jan season, they moved through the Andes to the jungle area and took from there twelve ball trees, nine of which were used to build a raft (an entire chapter in the famous book by T. Heyerdahl is devoted to the description of this journey).

And if a raft of the same volume as the Kon-Tiki is made not from balla, but from pine, then how many times less will its carrying capacity be (i.e., the mass of the cargo that the raft can hold until completely submerged)? Table No. 55 will help you get the answer to this question.

What distances do you think the air molecules in the room travel from one collision to another? Is it possible to deflect such a distance on paper using a sharpened pencil? It turns out that under normal conditions it is impossible for the average free collision of air molecules to be 60 nanometers (and the number of collisions of molecules per second is 7.5 billion). But at an altitude of 80 km above the Earth, the air is so rarefied that the distance between molecules is on average 4.4 mm (Table No. 105); such a segment can be put down with a pencil on paper.

For one kilowatt-hour of electric grain we pay 4 kopecks. But we learned about the real “price” of a saved kilowatt-hour from table No. 203. Such energy is spent on producing 1.5 kg of paper or 39 kg of granulated sugar, adding 30 kg of oil or smelting 0.5 kg of steel. To produce 1 kWh, about 600 g of coal is consumed.

The reference book will help you in solving various practical problems that may arise both at the level and at home, and when working in school workshops or in production, and when designing and manufacturing various devices, models...

Are there any other books like this? In any reference book you can find interesting information and tables of physical quantities necessary for solving problems, such as density, specific boiling point, etc. But the book “Handbook of Physics and Technology. A textbook for teachers" by A. S. Enochovich (M., Prosveshchenie, 1983) is a special reference book, a special book. She teaches, interests, perceives. Its significance is as great as the significance of the books of Ya. I. Perelman, M. I. Bludov.

The creation of the directory required a lot of work from its author. Thousands of data had to be found in many books, every number had to be checked, and if necessary, then updated - after all, technology is developing, the speed and power of machines and power plants, the range and duration of space flights, the efficiency of mechanisms are increasing...

The first reference book on physics also includes interesting tables such as “Movement speeds in living nature”, “Human physics”, data on automatic tractors, grain harvesting combines, and the meaning of their own events in the history of physics...
