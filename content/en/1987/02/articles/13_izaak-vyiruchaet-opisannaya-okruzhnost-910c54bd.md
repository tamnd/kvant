The circumscribed circle helps

It is well known that the magnitude of the central angle is equal to the magnitude of the arc enclosed between its sides, and the magnitude of the inscribed angle is half the magnitude of the arc enclosed between its sides. When solving planimetric problems, it is useful to first check the circumscribed circle of a triangle or quadrilateral. In this case, the properties noted above can be formulated as follows.

Fig. 1. Fig. 2.

I. If the quadrilateral ABCD is cyclic (Fig. 1), then ∠ABD=∠ACD, ∠ABC+∠ADC=180° (and also ∠DBC=∠DAC, ∠ADB=∠ACB, ∠BAC=∠BDC, ∠BCD+∠BAD=180°).

II. If points B and C lie on the same side of the line AD and at the same time ∠ABD=∠ACD or ∠ABC+∠ADC=180°, then the quadrilateral ABCD is cyclic (see Fig. 1).

III. If triangles ABC and AOC lie on the same side of line AC and point O is the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle ABC, then ∠AOC=2∠ABC (Fig. 2).

IV. If triangles ABC and AOC lie on the same side of the line AC, OA=OC and ∠AOC=2∠ABC, then O is the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle ABC (see Fig. 2).

Let us show with examples how these properties can be used to solve problems.

Example 1. In triangle ABC: ∠A=70°, ∠B=50°. Point M lies inside the triangle, and ∠MAC=∠MCA=40°. Find ∠BMC.

Solution. Since ∠AMC=100°=2∠B, MA=MC, by property IV point M is the center of a circle circumscribed about triangle ABC
