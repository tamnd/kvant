⟦folio 36⟧

The beautiful polyhedron shown on the cover has cubic symmetry. This means that it - like a cube - is symmetrical with respect to two planes, one center, and is also compatible with itself when rotated through angles that are multiples of π/2, around three axes, and through angles that are multiples of 2π/3, around four axes. An arbitrary face of a polyhedron can be alternately subjected to this transformation, and it will alternately be combined with 23 other faces of the same type.

Such polyhedra can be created using a technique developed by V.N. Gamayunov and associated with drawing elures of a special type (projectographic drawings, see «Kvant», 1982, No. 2, p. 39). We will not go into the construction of such elures here, but will tell you how you can construct a model of the shown polygon using a ready-made elure.

To do this, you should enlarge the elure (it is shown on the second page of the cover) to the desired size and carefully cut out which highlighted part (except red) from thin cardboard or thick paper in 24 copies. For ease of gluing, the sides of a detail can be spread out with “tongues”, which are glued to each other when the corresponding parts (faces) have a common edge on the polyhedron. (However, you can do without tongues and glue the edges along the edges with Velcro).

The details are cut out. How do you know what to merge data with? The black arms on the elure help with this. For example, to find out to which edge the larger element of the part is glued to the edge AB, you should find the black bow on the straight line AB (it is located at point O) and rotate the straight line near O by the angle specified by the bow (here 180°); then edge AB will go into edge A'B' of the yellow part: edge AB should be glued to it. In the same way for the edge EF: place the bow on the straight line EF, find it at point P and turn it around P by an angle of 90°, as a result of which EF is combined with E'F', which gives the necessary gluing.
