M1026—M1030, F1038—F1042

M1026. a) Five equal two AB, BC, CD, DE, EA are arranged so that each is divided adjacent into three equal parts (Fig. 1). Find the magnitude of each arc (in degrees).
6) The same question for a “rosette” of m equal arcs, each of which is divided into three equal parts by its neighbors.
A. V. Shvetsov

M1027. Prove that the number is 1985!! + 1986!! divide by 1987. (N!! denotes the product of all natural numbers not exceeding n and having the same parity, i.e. n!! = n (n − 2)(n − 4)....)
V. V. Proizvolov

M1028. a) Two intersecting lines are given on the plane, and one point (D and E) is offset on them. Construct a triangle ABC whose bisectors CD and AE lie on the given lines, and their bases lie on the given points D and E (Fig. 2).
6)* Prove that if ∠CDE = 30°, then one of the angles of triangle ABC is equal to 60° or 120°.
M. Volkevich, 10th grade student. (Moscow)

M1029. Among n terms of an arithmetic progression, we managed to select k terms that form an increasing geometric progression. Prove that n ≥ 2k−1.
V. F. Lev

M1030. For a convex polyhedron M, let us denote by S(M) the sum of the areas of its faces, and by P(M) the sum of the products of the lengths of all its edges and the corresponding external angles of the polyhedron (the external angle for a given edge is the angle between the perpendiculars to the edges adjacent to the edge and directed to the external region of the polyhedron; it is equal to 180° minus the value of the corresponding edge angle). Prove that the polyhedron M₁ lies inside the polyhedron M₂, then
a) S(M₁) ≤ S(M₂);
b) P(M₁) ≤ P(M₂).
A. V. Goncharov

F1038. When identical inextensible threads are attached at equal distances from each other to a ring of radius r₀ and similarly to a ring of radius 2r₀. The threads are passed through a triangle of radius r₀ (Fig. 3). Ring 1 is fixed in a horizontal plane, and all systems are located. Find the distance between the centers of rings 2 and 3. All rings are made of the same wire. Neglect training.
V. P. Borodin

F1039. A thread is attached to a block lying on an inclined plane and passed through a wire loop; at the other end of the thread there is a weight (Fig. 4). In this case, the block is motionless. When I lifted the weight, the block began to move. Explain the phenomenon.
G. V. Meledin
