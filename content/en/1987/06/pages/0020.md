Classification

In 1984, the Nauka publishing house published a book by the materialist of the USSR Academy of Sciences, L. B. Okun, “Physics of Particle Elements.” This book has a unique structure. To make it easier to read the main part, which is of a special nature, it contains layers of terms. One of the terms - “hadrons” - was once proposed by Lev Borisovich himself and received international recognition. The explanation of the term “classification” is interesting, which we will reproduce in full here.

Classification is the distribution of objects or phenomena into classes. Numerous examples of different types of classification are found in the pages of this book.

An interesting example of classification, lying in different

model of physics, given in the book “Words and Things” by the modern French philosopher M. Foucault. According to Foucault, he took this classification from the book of the Argentine writer Jorge Luis Borges, who in turn quotes the Neculo Oriental Encyclopedia. I did not find this quote in Borges’s books and present it as given by Foucault:

“Animals are divided into: a) those belonging to the Emperor, b) embalmed, c) tamed, d) suckling pigs, e) sirens, f) fabulous, g) stray dogs, h) included in this classification, i) two-eared, as in madness, j) innumerable, k) drawn with very fine acid from the camels six, l) and others, n) who have just broken a jug, o) the publisher of seemingly flies.”
