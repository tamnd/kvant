During boiling, the total vapor pressure in the bubble, equal to atmospheric pressure $p_0$, is, according to Dalton’s law, the sum of the partial pressures of saturated vapor only $p_T(T_K)$ and water $p_B(T_K) : p_0 = p_T(T_K) + p_B(T_K)$. Thus, the saturated vapor pressure of water and only each individually is less than atmospheric, which means that the boundary boiling point $T_K$ should be less than the boiling point of both water and only.

A similar demonstration experiment is especially successful if you pour a little carbon tetrachloride ($\text{CCl}_4$), which is at $76,7\degree\text{C}$, into a test tube, and pour water on top of it. In order to make the liquid interface easier to replace, carbon tetrachloride can be pre-tinted with a solvent.
