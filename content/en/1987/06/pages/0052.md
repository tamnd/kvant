The heat capacity of a mole of nitrogen at constant volume is $C_1=(5/2)R$, the temperature of a mole of argon is $C_2=(3/2)R$. The molar masses of nitrogen and argon are equal to $M_1=28$ g/mol and $M_2=40$ g/mol.

We find the initial temperatures of nitrogen and argon from the Mendelev-Clapeyron equation:

$$T_1 = \frac{p_1 V_1}{v_1 R} = 360 \text{ K}, \quad T_2 = \frac{p_2 V_2}{v_2 R} = 500 \text{ K},$$

where $v_1 = m_1 / M_1 = 50$ mol and $v_2 = m_2 / M_2 = 30$ mol are the number of moles of nitrogen and argon.

The temperature of the mixture $T$, established after opening the Ventilb, is determined from the first law of thermodynamics - the internal energy of the system when mixing gases remains unchanged (there is no heat exchange with surrounding bodies, the system is working)
