As the concentration increases, the number of active particles increases. As the area of ​​the vessel walls increases, their number decreases, since there are more particles captured by the vessel walls and eliminated from the game. If a vessel is gradually filled with reacting gases, then the chain reaction cannot proceed to a certain concentration - more active particles settle on the walls and calm down than are formed in the volume of the vessel. Continuing to further fill the vessel with gases (in other words, increasing the concentration), we must reach such a critical concentration at which more active particles are formed than die on the walls. The surface of the vessel is no longer able to cope with such an avalanche of active particles. There are too many of them. At this moment there is a transition from an inert state to a violent reaction. The critical concentration is the boundary separating the zone of “calm” reaction from the zone of “explosive” activity. It depends not only on which gases react, but also on the container in which these gases are located. Not one commander controls the chain reaction, but two at once. The first - concentration - brings the atoms into battle, the second - the surface of the vessel - tries to stop them. The judge is their critical concentration. Below is not - the second is stronger, above - the first wins.

If we, maintaining the same concentration, reduce the size of the vessel, then we will do so until such an exchange occurs when most of the chains do not have time to branch at all. then the number of active atoms dying on the wall turns out to be greater than the number of newly formed atoms as a result of the reaction. The avalanche will no longer be able to develop, and the practical reaction will stop.

The walls of the vessel are not the only force capable of calming active particles. This can also be done by foreign molecules of impurities, upon contact with which the parts lose their activity. Small amounts of impurities can greatly influence the speed of chain processes.

The results of the experiments were necessary, and many scientists simply did not believe them. For example, the famous German chemist Max Bodenstein explained the results obtained as an experimental error. Even his close colleagues were incredulous about the results of Semenov’s experiments. “I had to go through some unpleasant hours,” the scientist recalls.

This is today Academician N. N. Semenov - the founder of a new science - chemical physics, the author of the classic work “Chain Reactions”, a real book of polychemical and chemical scientists. And then, more than 50 years ago, it took him a lot of courage not to lose confidence in the correctness of his ideas in a difficult situation.

Chain reactions are the basis of multichemical industrial technologies. This includes combustion, the production of flammable materials from oil, and the production of plastics. Further, the explosion of an atomic bomb is a chain reaction, only the active particles in this case are not atoms, but neutrons. When Semenov's students, academicians Yu. V. Khariton and Ya. V. Zeldovich, created the theory of chain decay of uranium in 1939, they were greatly helped by ideas about chain reactions.

It took the world more than 20 years to fully understand the significance of branched chain reactions. In 1956, Academician N. N. Semenov was the first Soviet scientist to be awarded the Nobel Prize for his study of the mechanisms of chemical reactions. In his speech upon receiving it, he said: “The further task of chemistry is to create the possibility of rational control of the rate and direction of chemical transformation. The theory of chain reactions outlines the initial ways of approaching this issue.”
