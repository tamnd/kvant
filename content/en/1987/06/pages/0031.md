At the beginning of the century, England was alarmed by news of disasters in coal mines - terrible explosions claimed the lives of thousands of miners. They erupted due to the ignition of gas, which the miners called “mine gas.” It was not possible to understand the cause of sudden explosions for almost a century. Only in 1916, the German physicist-chemist Walter Nernst proposed that they would arise as a result of reactions that could be called chain reactions. And it took almost 50 more years to create a theory of this phenomenon.

...Have you ever seen rock falls in the mountains? One small pebble, accidentally falling out of its place, carries with it huge masses of other stones rushing down in an avalanche. Let's try to estimate how many stones are involved in such a rockfall. Let the first stone begin its journey at an altitude of 500 m and at every meter of its path involve another neighbor in its movement. Each new stone, in turn, becomes the initiator of the movement of others, others - the next ones, etc. The movement is, as it were, transmitted along a chain. After one meter there will be two stones in the stream, after the second meter - four, after the third - eight, etc. When the avalanche reaches the foot of the mountain, there should already be 2⁵⁰ stones in it. This is a huge number, more than 150 points! Of course, as a result of the situation, the number of stones turns out to be much smaller. Not all stones involve their neighbors in the movement; some stones encounter obstacles on their way and stop. Nevertheless, the number 2⁵⁰ gives some idea of ​​the scale of the chain process. Some chemical reactions also develop according to a similar pattern.

One of the most common substances in nature is water. To form a water molecule - an H₂O molecule - three reacting particles must meet - two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Therefore, for such a reaction, hydrogen and oxygen must be mixed in a quantitative ratio of 2:1. This mixture is called "explosive" gas.
