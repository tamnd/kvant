⟦folio 11⟧

The bodies of these stars are a liquid mixture of nuclei and electrons. Nuclei in such a “solution,” like particles in ordinary liquids, can be added to significant distances, and their movement could be chaotic. Ideas about the construction and stability of lattices of atomic nuclei immersed in a sea of ​​electrons appeared a little more than a few years ago.

Let us also propose that atomic nuclei with charges Ze actually form a crystal lattice, i.e., they are “tied” to certain places in space. The electrons surrounding them “wave”, of course, “walk” throughout this crystal, but on average there are Z electrons near each nucleus. Lattice destruction would obviously occur if the nuclei could be greatly displaced from their equilibrium positions. Small vibrations of nuclei do not lead to destruction of the lattice.

In ordinary solids, vibrations of the crystal lattice are mainly caused by temperature. For a nuclear crystal, the Coulomb interaction plays a very important role. Plasma-type oscillations will occur at any temperature, including very low ones. Our goal now is to estimate the frequency and energy of these oscillations and compare the latter with the potential energy of the nucleus in the crystal lattice and with the value of kT, which characterizes temperature oscillations. If the potential energy turns out to be much greater than kT and the characteristic energy of plasma oscillations, then we can say that the nucleus is “tied” to “its” node in the crystal lattice.

It is convenient to divide our entire “nuclei + electrons” system into conditional cells that have a spherical shape and such that each of them contains one nucleus with charge Ze and Z electrons. If a unit volume contains nₐ nuclei, then the radius r of the cell can be determined from the condition $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 n_\alpha = 1$; hence $r \sim n_\alpha^{-1/3}$. In general, each such cell is electrically neutral. Since the number of electrons Z >> 1, we can assume for estimations that the electrons forming clouds with a negative charge are evenly “smeared” throughout the cell. The oscillations of the nucleus in this negative charged cloud will occur at the plasma frequency

$$\Omega_0^2 \sim \frac{n_\alpha(Ze)^2}{\varepsilon_0 m_\alpha} \sim \frac{Z^2 e^2}{\varepsilon_0 m_\alpha r^3}.$$

This is essentially the same formula (*). But only now we are talking about the oscillations of nuclei with charge Ze and mass mₙ.

We have already mentioned that due to the high concentration of particles, the plasma in a white dwarf is described by quantum laws. Naturally, the oscillations in such a plasma must be pure, so that the total energy of oscillations of the crystal lattice, built from atomic nuclei, consists of individual portions - energy quanta. Given the vibration frequency, it is easy to estimate the energy of such a portion. To do this, frequency $\Omega_0$ must be multiplied by Planck’s constant $\hbar \left(\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}\right)$.

The value $\hbar \Omega_0$ is the characteristic energy of plasma oscillations, relating to one oscillating particle, in our case, to one nucleus. Here we also say that for typical conditions inside white dwarfs, the energy kT of thermal oscillations (again, per frequency) is much less than $\hbar \Omega_0$. This allows us to “forget” about temperature when talking about the fluctuations of the white dwarf’s crystal lattice.
