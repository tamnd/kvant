It can be shown that the described replacement by Robinson triangles is not periodic.

Let us outline the proof of this statement. Let's argue by contradiction. Suppose that the substitution of the plane of Robinson triangles is periodic with periods $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. Let's cover the plane with a network of parallelograms from the sides $\vec{u}$, $\vec{v}$ (see Fig. 1). Let us denote by $p$ the number of $P$-triangles whose lying vertex (relative to our network) is located in a shaded parallelogram; Let us define the number $q$ in a similar way. (The selected $p+q$ triangles form the so-called functional domain of a given periodic substitution.) Consider a circle of radius $R$ with center $O$. Let us denote by $p_R$ (corresponding to $q_R$) the number of $P$-triangles (corresponding to $Q$-triangles) lying inside this circle.

It can be proven that

$$\lim_{R \to \infty} \frac{p_R}{q_R} = p/q. \tag{1}$$

Indeed, the number of triangles intersecting a circle of radius $R$ is proportional to $R$, while the number of triangles inside a circle of radius $R$ is proportional to $R^2$. Therefore, in the limit, the ratio of the number of $P$-triangles to the number of $Q$-triangles in a circle is equal to this ratio in the functional domain (Fig. 8).

Let's now take our replacement and perform a deflation transformation. Then in the original functional domain there are $p' = 2p+q$ less than $P$-triangles and $q' = p+q$ less than $Q$-triangles (see Fig. 7). Let us denote by $p'_R$ and $q'_R$ the fewer triangles in a circle of radius $R$. Now it is easy to obtain a contradiction. In fact,
