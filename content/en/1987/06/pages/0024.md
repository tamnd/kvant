M1029. Among the terms of the arithmetic progression, we were able to select terms that form an increasing geometric progression. Prove

a) Note that line AC must pass through points D$_1$ and E$_1$, symmetrical to D and E with respect to these lines (Fig. 1). The following construction follows from this:

we reflect points D and E relative to the given lines OE and OD (O is the point of their intersection); draw a straight line through their images D$_1$ and E$_1$ until it intersects with DO - at point C and with EO - at point A (if D$_1=E_1$, then draw a straight line through D$_1$ arbitrarily, but so that it intersects the rays DO and EO, Fig. 2); we find the third vertex B of the exclusive triangle as the intersection point of lines AD and CE.

This construction is not always feasible: it may turn out that D$_1$E$_1$||DO, D$_1$E$_1$||EO or AD||CE. But even in the case when the triangle is “constructed,” the lines AE and CD may turn out to be bissetrices not of its internal, but of its external angles. From equalities

$$\angle DOE = \angle AOC = 180^\circ - \frac{1}{2} (\angle A + \angle C) = 90^\circ + \frac{1}{2} \angle B$$

it follows that the problem is solvable only for $\alpha = \angle DOE > 90^\circ$. But this condition is not yet worthy. Additionally, it is necessary to guarantee the existence of points A and C, and also that points D, O and E lie on the same side of the line AC. It can be shown that the problem is solvable if and only if

$$-2 \cos \alpha < DO : EO < -1/2 \cos \alpha \text{ при } \alpha \neq 120^\circ$$

(in this case the solution is unique) or when

$$DO = EO, \alpha = 120^\circ$$

(in this case D$_1 = E_1$, solved infinitely many). We leave the details of the study to the readers.

b) Construct points D$_1$ and E$_1$, as in solving problem a). Triangle DEE$_1$ is regular ($\angle EDE_1 = 2\angle EDO = 60^\circ, DE = DE_1$). If D$_1 = E_1$ (Fig. 2), then DO and EO are bissetrices of this triangle, therefore O is its center and $\angle DOE = 120^\circ$. By equality (1), angle B is equal to $60^\circ$.

Let now D$_1 \neq E_1$ (Fig. 3). Since EE$_1 = ED = ED_1$, triangle D$_1$EE$_1$ is isosceles, which means

$$\angle EE_1A = 180^\circ - \angle ED_1A = 180^\circ - \angle EDA$$

(here we take advantage of the fact that points E$_1$ and D$_1$ lie inside angle AOC, i.e. on one side of A, since angle DOE is obtuse). From equality (2) it follows that points A, D, E and E$_1$ lie on the same circle (Fig. 3). Therefore $\angle EAD = \angle EE_1D = 60^\circ$, and $\angle BAC = 2\angle EAD = 120^\circ$.

M. Volchekvich, V. N. Dubrovsky
