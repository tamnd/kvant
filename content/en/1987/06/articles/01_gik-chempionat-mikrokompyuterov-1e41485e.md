MICROCOMPUTER CHAMPIONSHIP

The last issue talked about the fifth world chess championship among mainframe computers. The sixth microcomputer championship was also played out according to the Swiss system: 14 machines competed for the “crown” in seven rounds. “Mephisto” won for the third time in a row, scoring 6 points. This time the championship, one might say, was personal and team in nature. Three “Mephisto” (Germany), three “Fidelity” (USA) - the winner of the first three championships, three “Rekom” (Holland), three “Cyrus” (England) and one each “Chess Monster” (USA) and “Kemplen Aatri” (Hungary) entered into the dispute. Representatives of the same company did not meet with each other. In the team championship, as well as in the individual competition, “Mephisto” won (first, third and fifth places).

Which machines play slower - big ones or small ones? Universal computers, capable of solving a wide variety of problems, surpass their micro-competitors in memory capacity and speed. Chess microcomputers are special - their hardware is designed specifically for playing chess, and these machines cannot do anything else. However, recently additional microprocessors and hardware models that implement certain chess functions have been connected to large machines, and the parameters of small computers have been improved through the use of more advanced microcircuits. As a result, the difference in the classification of machines decreases, and microcomputers are on an equal footing with more powerful computers. Their little colleagues played in the fifth world championship among all-rounders,

Moreover, the “Mephisto” micro-champion was only a point behind the “Cray Blitz” super-champion.
