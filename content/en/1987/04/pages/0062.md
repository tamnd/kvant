It follows that parallelepipeds with “square” bases can be inscribed in a paramid only when the cutting plane is removed from the plane of the base at a distance of $h$, no less than $\frac{2}{3}$ and no more than $1-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}$. The square of the diagonal length of the parallelepiped is equal to $d^2=\frac{1}{9}+h^2$.

If the base of the parallelepiped is a rectangle with sides $x$ and $y$ (Fig. 7, b), then $xy=\frac{1}{18}$, and the side of the section of the paramid by the plane in which the upper base lies is equal to $\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}\geqslant\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{xy}=\frac{1}{9}$; it follows that all such sections are removed from the plane of the base by a distance of $h$, not exceeding $\frac{2}{3}$.

At $0<h\leqslant\frac{2}{3}$ the square of the length of the diagonal is $d^2=h^2+x^2+y^2=\left(1-\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2+x^2+y^2=\frac{3}{2}(x+y)^2-(x+y)\sqrt{2}+\frac{8}{9}$.

The applied values ​​of $d^2$ are achieved at $x+y=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$, that is, at $h=\frac{2}{3}$.

A slightly larger value of the function $d^2$ is achieved at $x+y=\sqrt{2}$, but at the same time the parallelepiped ($h=0$) “disappears”.
