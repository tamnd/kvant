M1016. The polygon is described around a circle with center O. Let P be the center of mass of the polygon, K the center of mass of its contour. Prove that points P, O and K lie on the same line, and PO=2PK. (When determining the center P, we consider the polygon as a homogeneous plane, the center K - as a contour made of a uniform wire.)

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

Let us replace each side of the polygon with its center of mass (i.e., the middle) and place at this point a mass numerically equal to the length of the side. It is clear that point K coincides with the center of mass of the resulting system of materials points $(K_1, a_1)$, ..., $(K_n, a_n)$ (here n is the number of sides of the polygon, $K_i$ are their midpoints, $a_i$ are their lengths). To find the center of mass P of a polygon as a plane, we divide it into triangles by segments drawn from the center O of the circle to its vertices (Fig. 1), find the centers of mass $P_1$, ..., $P_n$ of these triangles $(P_i$ - the center of mass of the triangle adjacent to the side $a_i$), and place masses equal to the areas at these points $S_1$, ..., $S_n$ triangles. Then P coincides with the center of mass of the material system of points $(P_1, S_1)$, ..., $(P_n, S_n)$. The center of mass of a triangular plane lies at the point of intersection of the medians. (For proof, let’s consider a power-law figure of k rectangles of equal height, approximating our triangle - see Fig. 2 for k=4. Their centers lie on the median of the triangle, therefore, the center of mass of the entire figure also lies on this median. Going to the preposition at $k \rightarrow \infty$, we find that the center of mass of the triangle lies on its median.) Thus, the point $P_i$ at $i=1$, ..., n lies on the segment OK_i, and $OP_i/OK_i=2/3$, i.e. the system of points $K_1$, ..., $K_n$ transforms into the system $P_1$, ..., $P_n$ when prepared with center O and coefficient 2/3. And since the masses at points $P_i$ are proportional to $a_i$ ($S_i=a_i R/2$, where R is the radius of the circle), point K with the indicated preparation goes into P, which is equivalent to the statement of the problem.

A similar statement is true for a polygon described around a sphere with center O: if P is the center of mass of such a polygon (as a body), and K is the center of mass of its surface, then $OP=(3/4)OK$.
