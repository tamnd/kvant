In the second solution we will only prove that the required plane exists, without specifying it specifically. The advantage of this solution is that it is suitable for any set of curves on a sphere whose total length is less than π.

Let's consider the set of all large circles intersecting our curve, and for each we note its stripes. Let us prove that the plane of the set M of all marked stripes is less than the plane of the sphere, i.e. 4π. This will mean that there is a larger circle whose stripes do not belong to the set M, i.e., does not intersect our curve.

It is clear that the stripes of all great circles passing through some point X themselves form a larger circle ω_X with a stripe X. When point X runs past each AB of the great circle, the circles ω_X “sweep” two equal bigons (Fig. 2) on the sphere: the vertices of these bigons are stripes of the great circle AB, and the “raster angle” of the bigons is equal to the length s(A,B) of the other ABs. The area of ​​a bigon with angle α on a sphere of radius 1 is equal to 2α (obviously, it is proportional to α and equal to 2π for α=π). Consequently, the plane of the set of stripes of great circles intersecting each AB is equal to 4s(A,B), and the area of ​​the entire set M does not exceed 4(α₁+...+αₙ)<4π, where α₁,...,αₙ are the lengths of the stripes that make up a given curve (or several curves).

6) Let us select two points A and B on this curve and divide it into parts of equal length. Let us draw a larger circle through A and B and take the midpoint P on the smaller of its two ends A and B. Now, as in the first solution to problem a), we must prove that for any point X of our curve s(P,X)<π/2. And this inequality follows from the lemma given below (the sum of the lengths of the arcs AX and XB does not exceed the length of the section of the curve AXB and therefore is less than π).

Lemma. Let P be the midpoint of side AB of the spherical triangle ABX (on a sphere of radius 1). Then the length of its “median” s(P,X) is less than, equal to or greater than π/2 if the sum of the length is its side s(A,X)+s(B,X) corresponding to less than, equal to or greater than π.

Proof. Let first s(P,X)=π/2 and Y be a point on the sphere diametrically opposite to X (Fig. 3). Spherical triangles PXB and PYA are congruent (they are “spherically symmetrical” about the center P), so s(X,B)=s(Y,A) and

s(A,X)+s(X,B)=s(A,X)+s(Y,A)=π.

If s(P,X)>π/2, put on the arc PX a point X₁ such that s(P,X₁)=π/2 (Fig. 4), then by the proven sums s(A,X₁)+s(X₁,B)=π, but from the triangle inequality it follows (see Fig. 4) that it is less s(A,X)+s(X,B). The case s(P,X)<π/2 is treated similarly.

Problem 6) also admits a solution similar to the second solution to problem a). Let the set M be formed by the stripes of all great circles intersecting a given curve. Any of these circles intersects the curve at least 2 times (since it is closed), therefore the bigons considered in the solution to problem a) cover M in at least 2 layers, and therefore the area of ​​the set M is less than 4 2π/2=4π, i.e. less than the area of ​​the sphere.
