F1030. A body of mass $m$ is suspended on a weightless thread with rigidity $k$. The maximum tension that the thread can withstand is $T$. The body is raised to a height $x$ from the equilibrium position and released. At what minimum $x$ will the thread break?

Note that $T > mg$, otherwise equilibrium would be impossible. In the equilibrium position, the thread is stretched by $\Delta l_0 = mg/k$.

After the load has been lifted to a height $x$ from the equilibrium position and released, it will move downwards. Having passed the position corresponding to the length of the free thread, the load begins to stretch the thread. Let $\Delta l$ be the maximum stretch of the thread. Then the conditions for breaking the thread are $k \cdot \Delta l = T \Rightarrow \Delta l = T/k$.

Depending on the ratio of parameters $T$ and $m$, two cases are possible: 1) $x < \Delta l_0$, 2) $x > \Delta l_0$. Let's look at them separately.

1) $x < \Delta l_0$. Let us write down the law of conservation of energy, taking as the zero level of potential energy of a body in a gravity field the position when the thread is not stretched:

$$-mg(\Delta l_0 - x) + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l_0 - x)^2 = -mg \cdot \Delta l + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l)^2.$$

Substituting the values ​​$\Delta l_0$ and $\Delta l$ from (1) and (2), we find $x$:

$$x = \frac{T - mg}{k}.$$

It is easy to see that $x \leq l_0$ is equivalent to the condition $T \leq 2mg$. 2) $x > \Delta l_0$. From the law of conservation of energy -

$$mg(x - \Delta l_0) = -mg \cdot \Delta l + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l)^2$$

— taking into account (1) and (2) we find:

$$x = \frac{mg}{2k}\left(\left(\frac{m}{mg} - 1\right)^2 + 1\right) \text{ (при } T \geq 2mg).$$

V. G. Kharitonov

F1031. The conducting sphere is divided into several fragments, separated at great distances from each other. Do the fragments randomly connect systems of fragments or the electrical capacity of the sphere? Wire capacity claims.

Let us inform the system of some charge $q$ (let it be $q > 0$ for determination). Let's start collecting fragments connected by wires (whose capacity we neglect) into a sphere. It is clear that the surface charge density on any already assembled section of the sphere will be positive. Therefore, when fragments collide between them, repulsive forces will always act, and in order to collect the fragments into a sphere, work $A > 0$ must be done.

The charge of the collected sphere will be equal to $q$, its power station energy will be $W_{\text{сф}} = q^2/2C_{\text{сф}}$, where $C_{\text{сф}}$ is the efficiency of the sphere. The energy of the system was $W_{\text{ос}} = q^2/2C_{\text{ос}}$, where $C_{\text{ос}}$ is the electrical capacity of the system. It is clear that $A = W_{\text{сф}} - W_{\text{ос}}$, and since $A > 0$, $W_{\text{сф}} > W_{\text{ос}}$, i.e.

$$\frac{q^2}{2C_{\text{сф}}} > \frac{q^2}{2C_{\text{ос}}}.$$

This means that $C_{\text{сф}} < C_{\text{ос}}$ is the electrical capacity of the sphere is less than the electrical capacity of the system of fragments connected by wires.
