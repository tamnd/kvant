⟦folio 36⟧

Here are the numerical data Dalton has:
44% carbon + 56% oxygen = carbon monoxide,
28.1% carbon + 71.9% oxygen = carbon dioxide.
In such a record no patterns are visible. But let's determine how much oxygen comes to the same amount of carbon in different reactions. In the first, there are 44 parts by weight of carbon - 56 parts by weight of oxygen. How much oxygen is required for 44 parts by weight of carbon in the second reaction?
The answer is given by a simple proportion:
$$44 \cdot \frac{79,9}{28,1} \approx 112,6.$$
Almost twice as much as in the first one! Dalton explained this fact by proposing the corresponding atomic models of common substances (see figure 2):
1 atom of carbon + 1 atom of oxygen = 1 atom of double carbon dioxide, 1 atom of carbon + 2 atoms of oxygen = 1 atom of triple carbon dioxide.
From this explanation (absolutely correct!) it followed that the weight of the oxygen atom is related to the weight of the carbon atom as 56:44 (in good agreement with modern data).
Dalton's plan for determining atomic weights was proposed to be clear: first, using the reaction of water formation, calculate the weight of the oxygen atom, and then, based on oxidation reactions, calculate the weight of the remaining simple substances.
On September 6, 1803, a table of atomic weights of a number of substances appeared in Dalton's diary. We present part of this table along with modern data (in the same units):

| Atom | Atomic weight | Modern data |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hydrogen (H) | 1 | 1 |
| Oxygen (O) | 5,66 | 15,37 |
| Nitrogen (N) | 4 | 14 |
| Carbon (C) | 4,5 | 11,9 |
| Water (HO) | 6,66 | 17,87 |
| (complex atom) | | (H₂O molecule) |

It would seem that nothing is similar. But this is not entirely true. For example, the ratio of the weights of oxygen and carbon according to Dalton is 1.26. Modern methods give a very close value - 1.25. Please also remember that this is only the first version of the table. Subsequently, Dalton clarified it more than once. In particular, the weight of oxygen “increased” to 7. Unfortunately, one server defect remained in all variants: Dalton incorrectly determined the atomic weight of oxygen. And since the atomic weights of other substances were determined mainly by oxidation reactions, the error was automatically transferred to them.

The table shows that the chemical composition of water, according to Dalton, corresponds to the formula HO, i.e. for every oxygen atom there is one hydrogen atom. This is where the inaccuracy lay. Today's chemical formula of water is known to everyone - H₂O. But Dalton did not know this formula, and without it he could not move on. Therefore, he introduced the following principle: in cases where only one connection of atoms A and B is known, it is the simplest, that is, in Dalton’s terminology, a double atom AB. Therefore, the formula of water took the form HO.
