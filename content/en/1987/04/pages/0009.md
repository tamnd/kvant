The gas pressure turns out to be so high that the gas layer can withstand heavy loads - even the weight of the human body. But its main advantage is high thermal insulation.

Let us estimate how long a person can withstand contact with super-hot (and super-cold) objects if the thickness of the gas layer is $l = 10^{-4}$ m. (The surface layer of the skin can withstand cooling to $0^{\circ}\text{C}$ and heating to almost $100^{\circ}\text{C}$ without heat or frostbite).

Inside the gas layer, the temperature is distributed linearly - from the temperature of the hot object (or liquid nitrogen) $t_1$ to the contact temperature $t_0$. Therefore, the heat flux is $q = \chi_{\text{raza}} \frac{t_0 - t_1}{l}$. The temperature distribution at the end depends on time in the same way as with conventional contacts. Let us equate the heat flows:

$$\chi_0 \frac{t_T - t_0}{\sqrt{\chi_0^2}} = \chi_{\text{raza}} \frac{t_0 - t_1}{l}.$$

You see, in this case, the contact temperature $t_0$ depends on time - it increases when touching hot objects and falls when touching super-cold ones.

Skin tissue is not destroyed at $0^{\circ}\text{C} < t < 100^{\circ}\text{C}$, therefore, the contact temperature can change within these limits. Let's calculate the temperature, how long it takes for the surface temperature of the skin to reach these limits:

$$\tau_{\text{makc}} = \frac{1}{\chi} \left( \frac{\chi_0}{\chi_{\text{raza}}} \frac{t_T - t_0}{t_0 - t_1} \right)^2.$$

The thermal conductivities of various gases are close to each other. Therefore, to numerically estimate $\tau_{\text{makc}}$, we take the value of the air thermal conductivity coefficient $\chi_{\text{возд}} = 0,026$ J/(m·s×deg). Calculations show that when in contact with a hot object, the temperature of which is $600^{\circ}\text{C}$, the time for heating the skin through the steam-gas gap to $100^{\circ}\text{C}$ is equal to half a second - quite enough to shift the red-hot large one or take the next step over hot coals sprinkled with ash.

When the skin comes into contact with liquid nitrogen ($t_1 = -196^{\circ}\text{C}, t_0 = 0^{\circ}\text{C}$), the skin cooling time is 1.3 seconds. This is also quite enough to surprise the uninitiated or simply drop a drop of liquid nitrogen on the floor.
