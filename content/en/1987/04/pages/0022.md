⟦folio 20⟧

WITH BACKPACK
IN THE ARCTIC

Candidate of Technical Sciences
F. N. SKLOKIN

Eleven members of the polar expedition of the newspaper “Komsomolskaya Pravda” continued their false passage across the Arctic for thirty-eight days in the conditions of the polar night. Moving on ice, overcoming tors, cracks, channels with open water or covered with thin ice - all this was familiar from previous crossings. But the absence of light created completely unprivileged conditions. I remember how at the start of the transition, when the polar explorers who were accompanying us left, it was psychologically difficult to marvel at the night, and not at the people, at the visible fire of the driftwash station “Northern Strip-26”. But several days (or rather, nights) passed, and the eyes began to get used to the minimum illumination.

For the first two weeks there was no moon. The light of the stars helped, but frequent cloudiness deprived us of this light. A flashlight, and also an ordinary candle in the tent - only these sources created the possibility of effective activity. Without a flashlight, it was impossible to disassemble or assemble your own backpack - by touch, wearing gloves, you couldn’t sense what you came across in prepackaged bags, and you could only take off your gloves for a very short time - it was freezing.

The unprivileged conditions of the night crossing gave rise to an interesting visual effect. While walking, the surrounding area is visible within a few tens of meters, and the line of the horizon is guessed purely intuitively at the level of a horizontal gaze into the distance, where, I imagine, the icy wasteland meets the clear sky.
