Those who follow the puzzles on the back pages of our magazine are already familiar with the “changers” («Kvant» No. 1 and No. 2) and “magic rings” («Kvant» No. 3). And now we present a kind of zufrid of these two types of puzzles, derived by the authors of the “reversals” A. Dremov and G. Shevtsova (his idea was proposed by A. Kallin). The relationship of the puzzle shown here with “reversals” is very convenient. It consists of figures - 12 parmids glued together from balls - that can be rolled in a hexagonal box. For convenience, at its bottom there are located on a triangular lattice 3 × 13 = 39 round holes, three of them are empty. (Important polysene: to roll a paramida made of balls, you only need one empty hole next to it - the top ball of the paramida falls into this hole; two “support” ostatus balls in the old holes). As in other permutation puzzles, one given arrangement must be transformed into another; You can see several location options in the pictures. Please note, however, that this puzzle differs from the paramidian rollovers, if only in that as the game progresses, empty holes can scatter throughout the entire box, and for this reason, the rolling of the ball paramidocks cannot be replaced by the rolling of ordinary terrazdras. (Think carefully about whether it is possible to rearrange the puzzles of balls so that the empty holes form the base of one such paramida.) So, the strength of our puzzle with “reversals” is quite repeated. Its connection with the “magic rings” is deeper and is not immediately apparent. It turns out that there is a sequence of moves, as a result of which 7 paramidas are rearranged in a cycle - this is how one prickle is formed. Similarly, the second will arise, intersecting with the first, also from 7 paramidas. Try to find these rings! Doing this already or on paper is not very easy. We advise you to make a puzzle and number the paramides (see figure), otherwise confusion is inevitable. Thus, the solution to the “reversals of seven balls” is evidenced by otachetes, see «Kvant» No. 5) and the solution to the “magic rings” (which is located on the cover of «Kvant» No. 3).
