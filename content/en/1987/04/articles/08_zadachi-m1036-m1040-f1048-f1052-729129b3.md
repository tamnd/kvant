Tasks

M1036 — M1040; F1048 — F1052

M1036. Is there a (non-convex) pentagon that can be cut into two equal pentagons?

S. M. Hosid

M1037. Find all solutions in natural numbers x, y to the equation $x^y - y^x = x + y$.

A. I. Zaichik

M1038. a) Prove that if the product mn is divisible by 6 (where m and n are integers greater than 1), then the rectangle $m \times n$ cells can be cut into corners of three cells.

For what m and n can this be done so that the dividing lines are not cut out (Fig. 1)

6) not a single rectangular $2 \times 3$ cell?

b) not a single rectangle (less than m × n)?

M. Khovanov (9th grade student), A. P. Savin

M1039. Point A, B, C, D are the vertices of the tetrahedron. Prove that a) if $DA \cdot BC = DB \cdot CA = DC \cdot AB$, then all three scalar products are equal to 0;

6) if three angles between opposite edges of a tetrahedron are equal, then they are straight.

V. E. Matizen

M1040*. The numbers 1, 2, ..., 3n are randomly divided into three groups of n numbers each. Prove that you can choose one number from each group so that one of them is equal to the sum of the other two.

V. E. Alekseev, S. Savtsev (NRB)

F1048. The projectionist mistakenly inserted the film strip so that all the events on the screen “flowed in the opposite direction,” while automatically moving backwards. How did the speed and acceleration change automatically as a result of this error?

A. S. Butov

F1049. The wooden raft was pushed away from the river bank so that at the initial moment its speed was equal to v and directed perpendicular to the bank. The trajectory of the raft is shown in figure 2. The cross on the trajectory marks the place where the raft is located for time T after the start of movement. Assuming the river speed to be constant and equal to u, find the graphical points of the trajectory at which the raft is located at times 2T, 3T, 4T.

I. Yu. Poteryayko

F1050. A small particle of mass m, carrying a positive charge q, approaches the capacitor from a very large distance in a direction perpendicular to its plates and flies through the capacitor through small holes in the middle of the plates. Assuming that at a large distance from the capacitor the speed of the particle is equal to v, find its speed: 1) at point A inside the capacitor (Fig. 3); 2) at point B immediately after leaving the condenser. The capacitor is charged to a potential difference U, the distance between the plates is much less than the dimensions of the plates; The capacitor charge is much greater than q.

G. V. Grigoriev
