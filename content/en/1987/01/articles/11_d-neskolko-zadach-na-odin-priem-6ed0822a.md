Several problems for one example

We offer you several construction tasks using plane transformations - rotations, parallel translations, axial symmetries, homothetes. The method of applying the transformation in all tasks is the same. We will illustrate it with an example.

Given two circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ and a point $A$. It is required to construct a segment with ends on the given circle, which would be divided in half by the point $A$.

Solution. Let $X$ and $Y$ be the ends of the desired segment, belonging to $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, respectively. With central symmetry with center $A$, point $Y$ should go to $X$, i.e., fall on the circle $\omega_1$. At the same time, it should be on the circle $\omega_2$, symmetrical $\omega_2$ relative to $A$ (since $Y \in \omega_2$). Therefore, $X$ is the intersection point of circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. A problem may have two, one, or no solutions.

Think about what transformations need to be applied in the following tasks:

1. Given a straight line and two circles on opposite sides of it. Construct a square whose diagonal lies on a straight line, and the ends of the other diagonal lie on a circle.

2. Three parallel lines are given. Construct a regular triangle whose vertices lie (one at a time) on these lines.

3. Given a circle and a point $A$ outside it. Construct a ray with origin at $A$, intersecting the circle successively at points $B$ and $C$ such that $AB=BC$.

4. Given a point $A$, a circle and a straight line. Construct a square $ABCD$ such that its vertex $B$ lies on the circle, and on the line a) vertex $D$, 6) vertex $C$.

5. Given two intersecting lines and two points, $A$ and $B$. Construct a parallelgram $ABCD$ whose vertices $C$ and $D$ lie (one at a time) on the given lines.

6. Given two concentric circles. Draw chords in the larger circle, which would be divided by the smaller circle into three equal parts.
