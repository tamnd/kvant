Last year we talked about new computer achievements in the study of chess endings. In No. 2 it was reported that the computer had thoroughly analyzed the very complex ending “a king and two opposite-colored bishops against a king and a knight.” Until recently, it was considered a draw, but the computer proved that it was won for the stronger side, and in a record position, when both sides played in the best way, two bishops dealt with the knight in only 67 moves. K. Thompson, the author of the Bell programs, the third leader of the world among computers, in recent years has developed many computer programs for analyzing certain endings. For an endgame with two bishops against the end of his program, a little non-contractual (66 moves), it did server-side analytical work and identified characteristic winning positions, as well as nine classes of positions in which (contrary to the general rule) the stronger side cannot avoid exchanging one of the bishops for a knight or the matter ends in perpetual check (of course, positions where Black takes the bishop on the first move do not count).

Thus, we have here the first example of a computer program that not only has the ability to evaluate endings of a fairly complex type, but has also created a certain classification of endings useful for theory.

So, in the fight of two words against the end, nothing is achieved in rare cases. Using a computer, Thompson compiled tables with four fixed pieces, and the fifth - the white king - can occupy any field.

When the king is placed on an arbitrary square available to him, White, starting, wins. And how many moves this requires is indicated indefinitely on each field. It can be seen that winning here in less than 50 moves is not the exception, but the rule.

Previously, the chess code in the 50-move rule stipulated that for positions where such a number is not enough, he must be notified accordingly. However, I never used this clause, and it was later cancelled. Instead, the code made exceptions for three types of endings. Now that computers have made such internal progress in the study of the endgame, it is clear that this has been achieved, and there is direct sense in returning to the old position. More precisely, the code should contain a list of all “exceptional” types of positions and provide for the possibility of updating it.
