**M1003. In triangle ABC there are three altitudes AH, BK, CL. Prove the equalities AK·BL·CH = A·L·BH·CK = HK·KL·LH.**

Let the angles at the vertices A, B, C of this triangle be equal to α, β, γ, respectively. Then AK=AB|cos α|, AL=AC|cos α| (the modulus sign provides for a random angle α; see Fig. 1, 2), therefore, triangle AKL is similar to triangle ABC with coefficient |cos α| and KL=BC|cos α|. Substituting these expressions and analysis into the equalities from the problem statement), 6) Consider three cases of the location of the straight line l passing through A.

1) The straight line l contains vertex B or C. In this case, obviously, the required points are not on it (∠ABM can equal ∠ACM only when M=A).

2) Point B and C lie on the same side of l (Fig. 1). Then from the equality ∠ABM=∠ACM it follows that points A, B, C and M lie on the same circle, that is, M is the intersection point of the circumcircle of triangle ABC with straight line l, different from A. Such a point does not exist if line l touches circle ABC. For all other straight lines considered here there is a unique point M.

3) Points B and C lie on opposite sides of l. Let B₁ be a point symmetrical to B relative to l. If it does not lie on the line AC (Fig. 2), then the triangle ABC can be replaced by AB₁C (∠ABM=∠AB₁M for all points M of the line l), and B₁ and C already lie on the same side of l. Now from what has been said in case 2) it follows that on any line l there is at most one required point, and moreover, these points do not exist only when l touches the circumcircle of the triangle AB₁C. Let l be such a line (Fig. 3).
