Having convinced himself of the correctness of the experimental data, Comptor, despite the objections of some colleagues, published his results. However, the thought of the need to give them a physical interpretation never left the scientist. And soon Comptor publishes a new work, in which he gives a theory of the effect. An experimental physicist proposes a theory based on the most modern concepts of that time! Listen is extremely rare in the history of physics.

"Scattering of RENTen rays as particles"

This is how Comptor titled his memoirs, which tell about the events associated with the discovery of the effect named after him. This title reflects Comptor's subjective explanation of the effect.

Comptor received a good education at home, and then was sent for an internship to England, to the famous Cavendish laboratoire, where he became acquainted with the most important problems of physics in the 20th century. In particular, he knew about Einstein’s theory of light quanta, which was based on the idea of ​​light as a stream of particles characterized by frequency v and energy hv (h = 6.63·10⁻³⁴ J s - Planck’s constant). He also knew about the addition to this theory made by Einstein himself, according to which a light quanta has not only energy, but also momentum p=hv/c, where c is the speed of light. The idea of ​​photon momentum was proposed back in 1916, but it did not receive experimental confirmation until the early 1920s.

Comptor decided to use quantum learning concepts to explain the effect he discovered. He proposed that the scattering of RENTen quanta occurs on the free electrons of a solid body at rest, and the collision of a quantum with an electron is similar to an elastic collision of balls, as a result of which the values ​​of energy and momentum of both the quantum and the electron change. In fact, the momentum diagram (figure 5), corresponding to the elementary act of scattering, looks the same as in the case of colliding balls, since in vector form the equation reflecting the law of conservation of momentum is also written in the form $\vec{p}_1 = \vec{p}'_1 + \vec{p}'_2$ ($\vec{p}_2 = 0$). It is only necessary to take into account that the momentum of the quantum before and after scattering is corresponding to p₁=hv/c and p'₁=hv'/c, and the momentum acquired by the electron is $p'_2 = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} v$,

where $m_0$ is the rest mass of the electron (due to the high energy of RENTThen quanta, an electron during a collision can acquire a speed comparable to the speed of light; therefore, when writing the equation, relativistic formulas should be used). Based on the diagram, we can write an equation reflecting the law of conservation of momentum:

$$\frac{hv}{c}^2 = \left( \frac{m_0 v}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} \right)^2 - \left( \frac{hv'}{c} \right)^2 + 2 \left( \frac{h}{c} \right)^2 vv' \cos \theta.$$ (4)

The relativistic equation for the law of conservation of energy of RENTen quanta is related in the following form:

$$hv + m_0 c^2 = hv' + \frac{m_0 c^2}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}$$ (5)
