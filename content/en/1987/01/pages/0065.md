Observing a drop through an erythelium tube, at certain angles one can notice a bright dot on the drop, which changes color with slight mixing of the erythelium tube. This is the rainbow. The observation and calculation results for a drop of water are presented in figure 15 and 16.

Extrapolating the last plot to the value $n=2$, we get the value $\varphi = 304^\circ \pm 25^\circ$. The experimental results obtained can be verified theoretically. Denoting by $i$ the angle of incidence of the beam on the drop, and by $r$ the angle of refraction, we express the angle of rotation of the beam $\varphi$:

$$\varphi = k(\pi - 2r) + 2(i - r),$$

where $k(\pi - 2r)$ is the angle of rotation resulting from $k$ reflections, $2(i - r)$ is the angle of rotation resulting from two refractions. Substituting the value $r = \arcsin(\sin i/n)$ into the resulting formula, we get

$$\varphi(i) = k\pi - 2r(k+1) + 2i = k\pi - 2(k+1)\arcsin(\sin i/n) + 2i.$$

In this case, there is an angle $\varphi$ for which the rays emerging from the drop travel in a parallel beam without scattering. From this angle the rainbow will be visible. So, the conditions for observing the radig $\varphi(i) = 0$, from where, after transformation, we obtain
