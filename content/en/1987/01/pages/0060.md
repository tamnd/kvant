Option 1
1. Oscillations of the scale pan with mass $m_1$ occur relative to the equilibrium position in which the uniformity of the spring $\Delta x_0$ is determined by the conditions
$$m_1g = k\Delta x_0,$$
where $g$ is the free pressure acceleration, $k$ is the spring stiffness. In the lowest position, the scale pan is acted upon (according to Hooke’s law) by the spring from the force urruost $k(\Delta x_0 + A)$, the speed of movement of the scale pan at this moment is zero. If at this moment an overload of mass $m_2$ is placed on the cup, such that the merging of the cup with the overload is equal to the force of gravity, then, obviously, the oscillations will stop. Thus,
$$(m_1 + m_2)g = k(\Delta x_0 + A).$$
The given equalities allow us to find the spring stiffness: $k = m_2g/A$, from which for the first initial period $T$ of oscillations of the scale pan we obtain
$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{m_1/k} = 2\pi \sqrt{(m_1A)/(m_2g)}.$$

When solving this problem, many applicants made a characteristic mistake, forgetting that the oscillations occur relative to the static position on the cup ($m_1 + m_2)g = kA$.
