⟦folio 22⟧

Ku was connected to a source with a voltage of $U = 100$ V, and a current flowed through it, shown in the graph at figure. Estimate the initial inductance of the coil. Neglecting losses, find the speed of the core at instant $\tau = 10^{-3}$ s.

$$\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = \frac{d(LI)}{dt} = U.$$

Here $L$ is the inductance of the coil, depending on time, $I$ is the current in the coil, which also depends on time. That's why

$$\frac{d(LI)}{dt} = I \frac{dL}{dt} + L \frac{dI}{dt} = U.$$

In the initial period, when the core has not yet moved, we can assume that the inductance does not change. Then $\frac{dL}{dt} = 0$, and equation (*) has the form

$$L_0 \frac{dI}{dt} = U,$$

where $L_0$ is the initial inductance of the coil. From the graph we find that during this period $\frac{dI}{dt} = 10^6 \frac{A}{c}$. Hence,

$$L_0 = \frac{100 \text{ B}}{10^6 \text{ A/c}} = 10^{-4} \text{ Гн.}$$

The value $L_0$ is small enough to be ignored in the future (especially since the current, as can be seen from the graph, changes very little in the future). Therefore, for $t > 10^{-5}$ с, equation (*) takes the following form:

$$I \frac{dL}{dt} = U.$$

This shows that $L$ will stretch over time, since $U = 100 \text{ B} = \text{const}$ and $I = 10 \text{ A} = \text{const}$:

$$L = \frac{U}{I} t.$$

At moment $t = \tau = 10^{-3}$ s

$$L_\tau = \frac{100}{10} \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ Гн} = 10^{-2} \text{ Гн.}$$

Let's find the speed of the core at this moment. Neglecting losses, we write the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} + L_\tau \frac{I^2}{2} = IU\tau,$$

where $IU\tau$ is the work of the source in time $\tau = 10^{-3}$ s, $L_\tau I^2/2$ is the electromagnetic energy of the coil, $mv^2/2$ is the kinetic energy of the ferromagnetic core. From here we find $v$:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2IU\tau - L_\tau I^2}{m}} = 10 \text{ м/c.}$$

To solve this problem, it is necessary to make several comments. This problem belongs to the so-called “estimation” problems, in which, as a rule, some quantities are specified approximately or must be found from a graph, others must be found approximately, neglecting some small quantities. In this problem, we did not take into account the active resistance of the source, the loss in the core due to Foucault currents; they did not take into account that the current in the core after the first jump will not be constant over time, but will be slightly less; and of course, we did not consider the transition process from small to large inductance. The exact solution to this problem is difficult to achieve; however, our estimates are close to real values.
