Tasks

M1021. A climber wants to climb a rock 1000 m high. After camping at the foot of the rock, he can climb, using a rope, at a speed of 40 meters per hour, and after a cold rope on the rock - 30 meters per hour. He climbs the finished rope at a speed of 400 meters per hour. In how many days can he reach the top if he works on the rock (including climbing the rope) 6 hours a day? (The time of descent and other operations is pending.)
M1022. The first 8 natural numbers can be arranged in two rows so that the sum of the numbers in the top row is equal to the sum of the numbers in the bottom, and the sums of the numbers in the columns are also equal to each other. Is it possible to arrange in a similar way to exceed a) ten, 6) twelve natural numbers?

| 1 | 4 | 6 | 7 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 8 | 5 | 3 | 2 |

c) For what natural numbers $n$ can the numbers from 1 to $2n$ be arranged in this way?

M1023. Out of 100 triangles, is there always at least one such that it can completely cover the other 99?

G. A. Gurevich

M1024*. Prove that for any two triangles with angles $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ and $\alpha_1$, $\beta_1$, $\gamma_1$ the inequality
$$\frac{\cos \alpha}{\sin \alpha} + \frac{\cos \beta}{\sin \beta} + \frac{\cos \gamma}{\sin \gamma} \leq \text{ctg} \alpha + \text{ctg} \beta + \text{ctg} \gamma.$$
R. P. Ushakov

M1025*. Two straight lines drawn through one and the other intersection point of the extensions of opposite sides of a convex quadrilateral cut it into four smaller quadrilaterals. Prove that if circles can be written into two of them that do not have a common side, then a circle can be written into the original quadrilateral.

I. F. Sharyzin

F1033. A block of radius $R$ will be crossed by a uniform flexible rope of mass $m$ and length $l$, attached to two shouts on the ceiling located at a distance $2R$ (Fig. 1). On the axis of the block there is a load, the mass of which together with the block is $M$. There is no tension between the rope and the block. Find the minimum tension force in the rope.

V. I. Chuvilev

F1034. A mole of an ideal gas from the initial state 1 with a temperature $T_1=100$ K, expanding through a turbine into an empty vessel, does some work and goes into state 2 (Fig. 2). This transition occurs without the supply or removal of heat. The gas is then compressed in the $2 \rightarrow 3$ process, in which the pressure is a linear function of volume, and finally, in the $3 \rightarrow 1$ isochoric process, the gas returns to its original state. Find the work done by the gas during expansion through the turbine in transition $1 \rightarrow 2$, if in process $2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 1$ the gas is ultimately supplied with $Q = 72$ J of heat.
