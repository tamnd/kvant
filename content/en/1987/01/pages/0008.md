Fig. 3. Installation diagram of A. Compton.
A beam of X-rays emerging from the tube T was scattered on the object R. To isolate from the scattered radiation the part scattered at a given angle θ, a system of slits was used (figure corresponds to θ=90°). The radiation passing through the slit was analyzed by a cutting electrometer. The most important part of the spectrometer is the crystal, which forms a spatial diffraction grating for X-ray radiation. Due to diffraction on this grating, the rays reflected from the crystal propagated in directions depending on the wavelength of the reflected radiation. A conization chamber was used to analyze the spatial distribution of rays. Entering it, X-ray quanta caused conization of the gas filling the chamber. As a result, the current flowing through the gas increases. By changing the orientation of the camera relative to the crystal and monitoring the current, it is possible to determine the relative intensity of X-rays of different wavelengths arriving in the scattered radiation.

Fig. 4. Results of experiments by A. Compton.
a) is the dependence of the Compton shift on the scattering angle θ; A is the spectrum of the radiation above; B, C, D - spectra of scattered radiation at θ = 45°, 90° and 135°, respectively.
The mixing increases with increasing scattering angle.
b) — results of studies of the effect for various scatterers; A is the spectrum of the current radiation.
The more massive the atoms of the scatterer element, the lower the intensity of the displaced component and the higher the intensity of the non-displaced one. The amount of displacement remains constant.
