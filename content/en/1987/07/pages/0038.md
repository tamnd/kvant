Tasks

M1051 — M1055, F1063 — F1067

M1051. Two pieces are placed in the lower left corner of a 8x8 square chessboard in the shape of a 3x3 square. A chip can jump over any other chip, reflecting symmetrically from it, if the corresponding field is free. Is it possible to collect all the chips in the form of a 3×3 square with several such moves:
a) in the upper left corner?
b) in the upper right corner of the board?

A. E. Briskin

M1052. Prove that of the $n$ quadrilaterals cut off from a convex $n$-gon by diagonals, at most $n/2$ can be circumscribed. Give an example of an octagon that has 4 such quadrilaterals.

N. M. Sedrakyan

M1053. Prove that in the sequence of Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ..., where each number is equal to the sum of the previous two, at $m > 3$ there will be at least 4 and at most 5 $m$-digit numbers.

M1054. Prove that six points of pairwise tangency of four spheres always lie on the same sphere or on the same plane.

Yu. K. Koba

M1055. There are 21 points on a circle. Prove that among the arcs with ends at these points there are at least 100 arcs not exceeding 120°.

A. F. Sidorenko

F1063. The pedestrian needs to get from field point $A$ to field point $B$ in the shortest possible time, the distance between which is 1300 m. The field is crossed by a straight road so that point $A$ is located at a distance of 600 m from it, and point $B$ is at a distance of 100 m. The speed of movement of the pedestrian across the field is 3 km/h, and road - 6 km/h. Which path should a pedestrian take? What is the minimum time? Consider the case when points $A$ and $B$ lie on the same side of the road and when they lie on opposite sides of the road.

S. S. Krogov

F1064. The thread is attached to and wound around the rim of a heavy hoop. If the other end is attached to the ceiling and the hoop is released, it will move downward, rotating and unwinding the thread. Let three such hoops be connected “in series,” that is, the end of the next hoop is attached to the axis of the previous one (figure 1). All the hoops were released at the same time, and they began to move. Determine the acceleration with which the top of the hoop moves.

With what acceleration will the top of the hoop move if the number of hoops connected in series is very large?

Assume that the mass of the hoop is evenly distributed along its rim, the knitting needles are weightless, the threads are weightless and inextensible, and there is no training.

E. N. Yunosov, I. V. Yaminsky
