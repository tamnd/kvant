Fig. 4. Colorful dots are the values ​​of $s(n)$.

$$d_1 = 1, \quad d_2 = 1/2, \quad d_3 = d_4 = 1/4, \quad d_5 = \dots = d_8 = 1/8, \dots$$
(1) $(d_i = 2^{-m}$ for $2^{m-1} < i \leq 2^m$), i.e. for a sequence of points

$$0, 1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{1}{8}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{5}{8}, \frac{7}{8}, \frac{1}{16}, \dots$$

(2)

(fractions with the same values ​​can be rearranged in this sequence). It remains to estimate the sum $s(n) = d_1 + \dots + d_n$ for sequence (1). At $n = 2^m + k, 0 \leq k < 2^m, m \geq 0$,

$$s(n) = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{4} + 4 \cdot \frac{1}{8} + \dots + 2^{m-1} \cdot \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{k}{2^{m+1}} =$$

$$= 1 + \frac{m}{2} + \frac{k}{2^{m+1}},$$

i.e. $s(n) = 1 + (\log_2 n)/2$ at $n = 2^m$, and in sections from $2^m$ to $2^{m+1}$ the function $s(n)$ changes linearly. Since $\log_2 x$ is a convex function, $s(n) \leq 1 + (\log_2 n)/2$ for all natural $n$ (Fig. 4).

It is interesting that the optimal location of points (2) is given by a “greedy” algorithm (this is a completely official term): at each step it is enough to ensure the maximum of the next term, without worrying about what follows.

F1043. On a smooth horizontal surface, rotating, a stick of length $l = 10$ cm slides at a speed of $v = 10$ cm/s. At what catch speed of rotation will the stick hit the wall (see Fig. 4) fingers, if at a distance of $L = 50$ cm from the wall the stick was parallel to the wall?

The time it takes for the center of the stick to travel the distance $L$ to the wall is

$$\tau = \frac{L}{v} = 5 \text{ с.}$$

For the stick to hit the wall of the finger, it is necessary that during this time it completes an integer number of rotations around the axis. Hence,

$$\omega_n \tau = n\pi, \text{ где } n = 1, 2, 3, \dots,$$

where

$$\omega_n = n \frac{\pi}{\tau}.$$

Not all obtained values ​​of $\omega$ will be the answer to the problem. If the $\omega$ of the finger is large enough, when moving, one of its ends will “catch” on the wall, without the finger having time to hit it. In order to find the condition that the distorted values ​​$\omega$ satisfy, we note that during the impact the speed of its end $A$ (see Fig. 4) cannot be directed away from the wall (otherwise, at the same instants of time, the point $A$ “was” in the wall, which is impossible). The speed of point $A$ is the sum of the speed of movement of the center of the stick and the linear speed of rotation of the stick:

$$v_A = u - v = \frac{\omega l}{2} - v > 0.$$

From here we obtain the conditions $\omega$:

$$\omega < \frac{2v}{l} = 2c^{-1}.$$

Comparing these conditions with formula (*), we will see that the appropriate value $\omega_n$ is only the value

$$\omega_1 = 0,63 \text{ c}^{-1}, \omega_2 = 1,26 \text{ c}^{-1}, \omega_3 = 1,89 \text{ c}^{-1}.$$
