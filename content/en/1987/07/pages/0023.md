How did events develop further? Understanding the importance of magnetic measurements for the development of science, travel, and military affairs, three Academies of Sciences - St. Petersburg, Paris and Berlin - are announcing a competition for the best work explaining the phenomenon of terrestrial magnetism.
With the discovery of Coulomb's law, a new stage in the study of terrestrial magnetism began. In the wake of the famous German scientist - geologist, geographer, traveler - Alexander Humboldt, since 1800, almost all expeditions included the measurement of the magnetic field at various points on the Earth as one of the program items. By the way, the famous Russian seamen also took up such measurements. This was in the middle of the 18th century. Many types of educational postcards are called by the Academies. A number of fundamental works on the study of terrestrial magnetism were carried out precisely at this time. And among them is the work of the French physicist and engineer Charles Auguste Coulomb on the precise measurement of electrical and magnetic forces.
After conducting a series of amazingly accurate experiments for that time, Coulomb proved that the force of interaction between charged bodies is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them and directly proportional to their charges. By analogy with electric charges, to characterize the magnetic properties of matter, Coulomb used the concept of magnetic charges and showed that the force of interaction between magnetized bodies depends on the distance between them and on the magnitude of magnetic charges in the same way as the force of power interaction in the case of electrically charged bodies.*) Thus, thanks to Coulomb’s experiments, it became possible to measure the absolute value of magnetic force.
