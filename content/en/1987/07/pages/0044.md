F1044. A skydiver with a mass of $m=90$ kg makes a long jump. Before the parachute opened, its speed was $v_0=60$ m/s; after the parachute opened, the speed was $v=6$ m/s. What would be the maximum tension on the parachute lines if it deployed in a multi-fold fashion? Assume that the force of air resistance against a moving parachute is proportional to the square of the speed. The mass of the parachute and lines should be considered small compared to the mass of the parachute.

F1045. Two polar explorers met in the Arctic. They were dressed in seemingly identical combination suits, but in fact one of the combination suits was a tellee. When the temperature was placed on the Raman surface, it turned out to be higher on one than on the other. Which combination tellee? (Regular combinations, without undercut.)

F1047. *) The observer shoots a “sunbeam” with the help of a small mirror, standing in front of a large mirror in which he sees his image. What happens if the person being observed points the “bunny” at the image of the mirror in the mirror?

When the parachute opens, the steady parachute speed (when the sum of all forces acting on it is zero) is 6 m/s. In this case, the air resistance force $F_c$ is equal to the tension force of the slings $T_0$, which, in turn, is equal to the gravitational force $mg$, i.e. $F_c = mg$.

At the first moment after the parachute opens, the drag force $F_c$ is $(60/6)^2=100$ times greater. Thus, the resistance force becomes equal to 100mg. The tension force of the slings is the same:

$$T = 100mg = 80000\mathrm{H}$$

For.reason. With such a force $T$ the parachutist would acquire a rooting $a = \frac{1}{m}(T - mg) = 99g$, i.e. the overload $a/g$ would be unacceptably greater. In reality, the canopy does not open instantly, and the tension of the lines is not very high. Real values ​​of overloads $a/g$ never exceed 6-7. Therefore, when calculating the strength of slings, we can assume that it is always $T \ll 100\mathrm{mg}$.

I. F. Ginzburg

The temperature on the inner surface of the combination chambers is the same - it is equal to the temperature of the human body. The heat flow through the combine is also approximately the same (unless it is assumed that one of the polar explorers is engaged in heavy physical work, and the other will be lazy at this time). This means that the material of the zona suit, the surface temperature of which is lower, has a lower thermal conductivity, and it is precisely such clothing that we call the body. The limiting layer of this problem describes a person wearing a thin shirt - its surface temperature is only slightly different from the normal 36 degrees (until, of course, the person is very cold).

I. I. Mazin

The “bunny” that the observer can see in the large mirror is the image of the “bunny” that was formed on the “screen” standing in front of the large mirror. The screen can be the observer himself, the wall that he sees in the mirror, etc. There is a “hole” in the screen - this is a small mirror: if direct sunlight, reflected first from a small mirror, and then from a large one, hits the small one again, then there will be no “bunny”. But with such a course, the rays, after the first reflection from the small mirror, go perpendicular to the plane of the large mirror and, therefore, fall into the image of the small mirror in the large mirror.

Consequently, if an observer directs a “sunny bunny” to the image of a mirror, then he will not see the image of a “bunny” anywhere.

S. S. Krotov
