Tunnel microcoppe

In 1986, the Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to the Swiss physicists G. Binnick and G. Rohrer, the inventors of the scanning tunnel microcoppe - the device discussed in this article.

One of the most “productive” effects of quantum mechanics is the tunnel effect. In particular, it consists in the fact that electrons are able to “leak” from one conductor to another, separated from the first by a thin dielectric or vacuum layer.*) The phenomenon owes its origin to the wave property of electrons,

*) In the general case, the tunneling effect consists in the passage of a potential barrier by a particle when its energy is less than the height of the barrier. This is a phenomenon of an essentially quantum nature, impossible in classical mechanics.

thanks to which an electron can be detected with a certain probability outside a conductor.

The well-known tunnel effect is already more useful for the life of many useful devices and devices. One of them - a tunnel diode - has become a common radio element. And now the tunnel effect forms the basis of a new type of microcoppa, which will step into the world of sizes that are fractions of the diameter of the atoma. (Recall that the diameter of the atoma is of the order of one angstrom: 1A=10⁻¹⁰ m.) The tunnel microcoppe has a constant magnification exceeding 100 million, and allows you to study the details of the atomic structure of the surfaces of solid bodies.

Until recently, the cordsman was an electronic microcoppe with magnification up to 1 million. In it, unlike a conventional optical microcopper, the role of a light beam is played by a flow of electrons controlled by electron optics - an analogue of the optical lines system. In the tunnel microcoppa you will not find elements traditional for microcoppa. It contains inconsistencies similar in purpose to the line, and it works not according to the principle of “seeing”, but according to the principle of “sensing”.

The sensitivity of the elements, literally “groping” the surface, is the thinnest tungsten absence, the column is thin, so that at the end it is reduced to only a few atoms. Using high-precision mechanisms, the tip is brought perpendicular to the surface under study until noticeable (10⁻⁹ A) tunneling electrons appear through the tip-surface gap. Then the so-called peso-greens begin their work - plates made of special ceramics that change their length when an electric field is applied.*)

*) The action of such a pesoseliment is based on the inverse pesoselic effect - the occurrence of mechanical deformations in solid crystals under the influence of an electric field.

The electromechanical system of resin cells automatically maintains the distance from the tip to the surface constant and equal to 10 Å. A change in the distance by a small amount causes the tunneling current to deviate from the set value, and the electronic feedback circuit supplies an electrical signal to the resin cell, which moves the sharp relative surfaces of the tunnel current closer to or away from the set value, so that the tunnel current, and therefore the distance of the sharp surfaces, remains unchanged. The efficient operation of the automation system ensures that the tunnel current varies extremely sharply with the size of the gap. Thus, changing the tip-surface gap by 2 Å increases the tunneling current by 1000 times.
