Addition of points
on an elliptic curve

The secant method applied to the elliptic curve $C$ leads to the following result: it turns out that the points on it can be “added”. We define the operation of adding points on $C$, starting from its graph image (see figure 7). Let's take two points $P$ and $Q$ on $C$ and draw a straight line through them. This line has a third intersection point with curve $C$. Let us reflect this point from the axis $Ox$ and the resulting point by the sum of $P$ and $Q$ (designation $P+Q$, Fig. 7). The straight line passing through the points does not always intersect the curve $C$ in the third - to the right, a vertical straight line. Next we will look at this situation in more detail.

Let's study the properties of adding points on an elliptic curve. Let us take as an example the properties of the operation of adding numbers. This commutation operation, i.e., $a+b=b+a$, is also associative, i.e., $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$. In addition, this operation has a zero, i.e. a number $O$ such that $a+O=a$ for any $a$, and, finally, for every number $a$ it has its opposite, i.e. a number $(-a)$ such that $a+(-a)=0$.

But what about the elliptic curve? First of all, the operation of adding commutation points. In fact, to calculate $Q+P$ we use the same direct sum as for $P+Q$. Therefore, $Q+P=P+Q$.
