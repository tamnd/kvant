In 1862, the British Association for the Advancement of Science, whose members also included scientists like Lord Kelvin and J. Claire Maxwell, set the task of developing basic and derivative units of measurement for various fields of physics. But... she didn’t find anything better than Gauss’s idea of ​​choosing length, mass and time as the main quantities. But it was proposed to change the units of their measurements somewhat, “assigning” the centimeter, gram and seconds as the main ones. And one more thing: it was this authoritative Commission that proposed the unit of force - dyne and the unit of energy - erg. In fact, the well-known system of GHS units was developed. For electrical and magnetic units, Gauss's idea of ​​​​connecting mechanical and electromagnetic units through measurement procedures (SGSE and SGSM systems) was used.

For the first time, the Commission brought some clarity to the system of electrical and magnetic units, but, unfortunately, in practice it was very inconvenient to use such a system, because some of the values ​​turned out to be too large, and others too small. Since the Congress was international, the decisions it made had international significance. And these decisions were based on the proposals of the British Association for the Advancement of Science, i.e., the ideas of Gauss.
