If you manage to reduce the number of parts in any of the problems compared to the specified one, send your solutions to our editorial office.

Tasks *)

1. Herringbone. Cut the herringbone into 4 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

2. Of the two - one. Cut the square with two straight lines into 4 parts so that a new square can be folded from them and the second square.

3. Out of three - one. Cut the larger square into three parts so that the resulting five shapes can be folded into one square.

4. Two figures. From two figures, cut them, make five so that you can fold them into a square.

5. Squaring the figure. Cut the depicted figure into 4 parts, from which you could then fold a square.

6. Perforated square. Cut the holed square into 8 pieces that can be used to form a whole square.

7. Jagged square. Turn a jagged square into a regular square by cutting it into 5 pieces.

8. Holey jagged square. Cut the holey jagged square into 5 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

9. Ladder. Turn the “ladder” into a square by cutting it into three parts.

10. Steps. Make a stepped cut of the “step” into two pieces that can be folded into a square.

11. Maltese cross. Cut the “Maltese cross” into 5 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

*) We took this problem from Ya. I. Perelman’s collection “The Fun of the Problem” (Rudiments of Knowledge, Petrograd, 1919).

*) The “Herringbone” problem was proposed by N. I. Abilov, the “Out of two - one” problem was proposed by N. K. Antonovich, the remaining problems were proposed by L. P. Mochalov.
