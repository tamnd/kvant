We will require that all these infinities be the same point $E$, i.e. we will assume that $E$ is the intersection point of all verticals. This requirement correctly defines the point $E$—the zero point with respect to our addition operation. By our convention, a vertical line passing through the point $P$ passes through $P$ and $E$. Therefore, the point $R$ of intersection of this line with the elliptic curve satisfies the correspondence $P+R=E$, i.e., it is opposite to $P$. On the other hand, $R$ is a point symmetrical to $P$ relative to the $Ox$ axis. This means that any point $P$ has an opposite point - $P=R$. Thus, we are convinced that the addition of a point on an elliptic curve has the same properties as the addition of numbers.

How to calculate point $P+P$? When the points were different, we drew a secant. Since they have merged, it is clear that we need to check the tangent (Fig. 9).

What should you do to find 3$P$? Very simply, we take the sum of 2$P$ and $P$. Like this 4$P=3P+P$, 5$P=4P+P$ etc.

Search for rational points
Armed with the operation of addition, let us now deal with rational points. Let $P=(x_1, y_1)$, $Q=(x_2, y_2)$ be two rational points on the elliptic curve $y^2=x^3+ax^2+bx+c$, where $a, b, c$ are integers, and let the line passing through $P$ and $Q$ intersect this curve at another point $R=(x_3; y_3)$. then $R$ is also a rational point.

This statement is proven quite simply. If

$$y=kx+d$$

is the equation of the line passing through the points $P$ and $Q$, then $k$ and $d$ are rational numbers, since they can be expressed through the coordinates $(x_1; y_1)$ and $(x_2; y_2)$ of the points $P$ and $Q$ by the formulas

$$k=\frac{y_1-y_2}{x_1-x_2}, \quad d=y_1-kx_1=\frac{x_1y_2-x_2y_1}{x_1-x_2}.$$

Substituting (7) into the elliptic curve equation, we obtain for $x$ a third-degree equation with rational coefficients

$$(kx+d)^2=x^3+ax^2+bx+c,$$

i.e.

$$x^3+(a-k^2)x^2+(b-2kd)x+c-d^2=0.$$

By Vieta's theorem

$$x_1+x_2+x_3=k^2-a.$$

Since $x_1$ and $x_2$ are rational, then $x_3$ will be rational, and hence $y_3=kx_3+d$.

From this proof the formula for calculating the coordinates of the point $P+Q$ immediately follows. By definition, $P+Q$ is obtained from $R$ by reflection from the axis $Ox$, which means that the coordinates $(u; v)$ of point $P+Q$ can be found using the formulas

$$u=k^2-a-x_1-x_2, \quad v=-ku-d=-[k(u-x_1)+y_1].$$

Substituting here the values ​​$k$ and $d$, we get

$$u=\frac{(y_1-y_2)^2}{(x_1-x_2)^2}-(a_1+x_1+x_2),$$

$$v=\frac{y_1-y_2}{x_1-x_2}(x_1-u)-y_1.$$

It is clear that if $x_1=x_2$, these formulas do not make sense. In this case, the secant equation (7) must be replaced with the tangent equation and proceed according to the previous scheme. As a result we get

$$u=-2x_1+a-(\frac{3x_1^2+2ax_1+b}{2y_1})^2,$$

$$v=y_1+\frac{3x_1^2+2ax_1^2+b}{2y_1}(u-x_1).$$
