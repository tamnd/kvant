About renovation, art, sports and science
Those who are interested in chess know about a long-standing debate: what is chess - a debate, an art or a science? Many articles and books have been written on this subject, bringing together different points of view on this fascinating game.

There is no way to argue about mathematics in this way. This is science! Even “the science of all sciences,” as many authorities claim. But let's think a little.

What do you learn in math lessons at school? Compose and solve equations, handle powers, logarithms, arbitrary ones, etc. What do they teach, say, in cutting and sewing courses? Cutting and sewing circuit boards, handling sewing machines, materials and tools, etc. Here they teach repair skills, and in mathematics lessons they teach repair skills, the ability to carry out mathematical calculations, which is an integral part of the training of a technically educated person.

Craft is usually opposed to art. Note that in Ancient Greece, mathematics was classified as an art, on par with music, painting and architecture (counting, weighing, and measurement techniques were not classified as mathematics). And this was natural, since the considered problems were solved each separately, using their own method. The attitude towards mathematics as an art continued into the Middle Ages. Then, with knightly tournaments, states of poses and tubadours, mathematician tournaments were also held, where they demonstrated their skill in solving the problem.

Stop, stop! Where is the science of mathematics? What is her role? Mathematicians discover new connections between mathematical objects. As a result of this work, general methods are found for solving various classes of problems. And these problems receive “standard” methods of solution, moving from the category of “creative” problems to the category of “technical” ones, that is, those requiring the use of already known methods for their solution.

Of course, I am not saying that learning mathematics at school and applying the acquired knowledge in practice is completely devoid of elements of creativity. On the contrary, remontsement can create a work of art by rethinking the products it chooses in a new way; and a schoolchild, solving a “standard” problem at school, can find a new, beautiful way to solve it. Everything that was said had complete support for the following: problems are divided into those for which special solution methods have already been created, and those for which there are no such methods. The latter mostly include resolution tasks. Indeed, let's compare two problems.

Tasks *)

1. Herringbone. Cut the herringbone into 4 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

2. Of the two - one. Cut the square with two straight lines into 4 parts so that a new square can be folded from them and the second square.

3. Out of three - one. Cut the larger square into three parts so that the resulting five shapes can be folded into one square.

4. Two figures. From two figures, cut them, make five so that you can fold them into a square.

5. Squaring the figure. Cut the depicted figure into 4 parts, from which you could then fold a square.

6. Perforated square. Cut the holed square into 8 pieces that can be used to form a whole square.

7. Jagged square. Turn a jagged square into a regular square by cutting it into 5 pieces.

8. Holey jagged square. Cut the holey jagged square into 5 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

9. Ladder. Turn the “ladder” into a square by cutting it into three parts.

10. Steps. Make a stepped cut of the “step” into two pieces that can be folded into a square.

11. Maltese cross. Cut the “Maltese cross” into 5 pieces so that they can be folded into a square.

*) We took this problem from Ya. I. Perelman’s collection “The Fun of the Problem” (Rudiments of Knowledge, Petrograd, 1919).

*) The “Herringbone” problem was proposed by N. I. Abilov, the “Out of two - one” problem was proposed by N. K. Antonovich, the remaining problems were proposed by L. P. Mochalov.
