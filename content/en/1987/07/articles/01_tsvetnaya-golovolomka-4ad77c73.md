On figure 1 you see a “color puzzle” - the so-called one at the manufacturing plant (2. Lviv). Everyone knows that this is just a slightly modified game “15” by Szma Loyd, but in reality the box with chips is closed with a lid and you cannot call them back one by one, as in the game “15”. There are two ways to move chips. You can shake the box, and then two of the same horizontal rows (tot, whose left or right extreme place is free) will move one piece (respectively, blebo or to the right - the vertical movement of the barera between the horizontal rows). In addition, using the levers on the sides of the puzzle, you can move the outermost vertical rows, again side by side, one piece up or down; these rows can be in three positions each. Rassanovaka on figure 1 is considered “correct” and one must learn to return to it after arbitrarily moving the chips.

Readers who follow our publications about permutation puzzles know that the keys to solving them are often given by cyclic permutations and their commutators (see cover «Kvant» No. 5 or the article “Permutations” in this issue), and it is also convenient to study puzzles using a graph. The “color puzzle” graph is shown in figure 2. The lines on it indicate the permissible movements of the chips. For example, if in the correct position you move the left row down and the right up, “fuck the blebo box” and return the outer rows to their place, then 6 chips will move along the green arrows on the graph. It is easy to tell how to arrange cyclic permutations of chips, for example, along a beautiful or blue closed contour (an asterisk on the graph contains a free space through which the chip seems to jump - this is shown by the dotted line). The switch ABA$^{-1}$B$^{-1}$, where A and B are cyclic permutations of chips along the red and blue contours in a clockwise direction, simultaneously swaps two pairs of chips, as indicated by the black arrows on figure 2, and does not move the remaining chips. In the image on figure situchi, he almost completes the assembly of the three lower rows - all that remains is to send the white chip to the empty space. Other switches are also useful - AB$^{-1}$A$^{-1}$B, AB$^2$A$^{-1}$×B$^{-2}$, etc. But to understand how they operate and create a complete solution algorithm, we, as always, offer those who like to rack their brains as homework.
