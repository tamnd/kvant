Jan Hevelius

1987, at the initiative of UNESCO (United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization), was declared the Year of Hevelius. Who is he and what did he do to deserve such a high position?

Jan Hevelius is an outstanding user of the 17th century. He was born in Gdansk on January 28, 1611 and died in the same year on January 28, 1687, having lived 76 years. Using funds inherited from his father, a brewer, he built and equipped an observatory, which for a long time was the best in Europe. Hevelius had unusually sharp eyesight and excellent visual memory. And although his observatory contained telescopes that he made, he made most of the most accurate observations of the positions of the stars without resorting to their help.

Hevelius's first scientific work, published in 1647, was the famous "Selenography", which contained a detailed description of the visible part of the surface of the Moon. Hevelius printed this book in his printing house, making 133 skillful graphs for it, of which 60 depicted various parts of the lunar surface and the appearance of the Moon in its various phases.
