M1047. In a chess tournament played in one round, at least 3/4 of all games played ended at the bottom at some point. Prove that at this moment some two sections scored the same number of points.

In this problem, it is convenient to use the following scoring system: 1 point for a win, 0 points for a draw, - 1 point for a loss (obviously, it is equivalent to the general chess system). Let us denote the number of tourist sites by n and set k=n/2 for even n, k=(n-1)/2 for odd n.

Let's assume that at a given moment all areas have different results. Then among them it will find either k with positive results or k with negative results. It is enough to consider only one, say, the first case. Since all these k chess players scored different numbers of points, and the number of points of each is not less than the number of games they won, the total number of victories of these chess players is not less than 1+2+...+k=k(k+1)/2. The number of all games in the tournament is equal to (n-1)n/2, therefore the share of games that ended with the victory of one of the opponents at the moment in question is no less

$$\frac{k(k+1)}{(n-1)n} > \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4},$$

i.e., the proportion of numerical ones is less than 3/4, which contradicts the conditions of the problem. Thus, our assumption is refuted.
