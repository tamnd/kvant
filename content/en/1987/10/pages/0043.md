Return and death

Having fallen ill, Ramanujan begins to think about returning to his homeland. Only by the beginning of 1919 did his health improve enough to make a long trip by sea. A place was ready for him at the University of Madras - his words reached India. Ramanujan writes a letter of gratitude to the rector, apologizing for the fact that recently he has no longer given the opportunity to work with decent intensity. But he was never able to start working at the university. He managed to do it in his homeland for less than a year. After three months in Madras, Ramanujan moved to Kumbakonam. In January 1920, he sent his last letter to Hardy, where he reported on his work on a new class of theta function. Neither doctors nor relatives can persuade a terminally ill scientist to resume work. On April 26, 1920, Ramanujan died. He was not yet 33 years old.

Memory

The news of Ramanujan's death shocked his friends both in India and England. They felt it was their duty to understand the amazing phenomenon that Ramanujan was. Hardy writes:

“It is possible that the great days of formulas are over and Ramanujan should have been born 100 years earlier; but he was the greatest formula maker of his time.”

More than half a century has passed, and today we clearly see what Hardy and his contemporaries could not have foreseen. Ramanujan's genius turned out to be in tune not only with the past, but also with the future of mathematics. Ramanujan's arithmetic formulas rarely turned out to be key in the new stages of algebraic number theory, and one could only wonder how he could see them without knowing something without which they could not be seen. And then came a revival of interest in specific explicit formulas both within mathematics and in the field of its applications.
