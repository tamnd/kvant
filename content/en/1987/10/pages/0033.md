F1059. At some points in space it is necessary to create the maximum direction of the gravitational field (acceleration of free pressure), having at disposal a given mass of matter of constant density.

a) What shape should the veal be made from this substance?
b) What should be the relative position of the body and the calf?

Let a homogeneous body of mass $M$ create a grabituation field, the direction vector of which at some point $O$ is equal to $g$ and is directed along the axis $OX$, as shown in figure 1. According to the principle of superposition of fields, vector $\vec{g}$ is the sum of vectors $\Delta\vec{g}_i$ directed by the fields created at the point $O$ by all infinitesimal elements $\Lambda m_i$ of the body $M$. The contribution of each element to the total field $g$ is equal to the projection of the vector $\Delta\vec{g}_i$ onto the axis $OX$, i.e.

$$\Delta g_{ix} = \frac{G \cdot \Delta m_i}{r_i^2} \cos \theta_i,$$

where $r_i$ is the distance from point $O$ to element $\Delta m_i$, $\theta_i$ is the angle between the direction to the element and the axis $OX$. Therefore (if all elements of $\Delta m_i$ are the same),

$$g = |\vec{g}| = \sum_i \Delta g_{ix} = G \Delta m \sum \frac{\cos \theta_i}{r_i^2}.$$

Thus, for a given body mass $M$, the value $g$ will be maximum if the shape of the body and its orientation relative to the point $O$ is such that the value $\sum \frac{\cos \theta_i}{r_i^2}$ will be maximum.

Let us consider a body of mass $M$, bounded by a surface passing through point $O$, for all points of which (except point $O$) the relation $\frac{\cos \theta_i}{r_i^2}$ is the same, i.e.

$$\frac{\cos \theta}{r^2} = \text{const.}$$

We call such a surface a surface of equal contribution. A body bounded by a surface of equal contribution is a rotating body (around the $OX$ axis; see Fig. 2). Let us show that it is precisely such a body that creates a gravitational field, the direction of which $g_0$ is maximum at point $O$.

Elements $\Delta m$ located on the surface of equal contribution, by definition of heights, have equal contributions $\Delta g_{0x}$ to $g_0$. The contribution of any internal element $\Delta g_{bнутр}$ is greater than $\Delta g_{0x}$; if the element $\Delta m$ were behind the surface of the body, then its contribution $\Delta g_{bнутр}$ to the values ​​$g_0$ would be less than $\Delta g_{0x}$. Thus,

$$\Delta g_{bнутр} > \Delta g_{0x} > \Delta g_{bнутр}.$$

Any deformation of the body $M$ (without changing its density), which does not lead to a change in the direction of the directional vector at point $O$, coinciding with the transfer of mass from the internal regions to the external regions, beyond the surface of equal contribution. As can be seen from the correspondence (*), this leads to a decrease in the direction of the field at point $O$.

Consequently, the direction of the gravitational field created at point $O$ by a body of a given mass is maximum if this body is a rotation body limited to a surface of equal contribution, and point $O$ lies on the surface on the rotation axis.

Note that the shape of this body differs from a spherical creature. figure 2 shows sections of such a body and a ball of the same mass. You can see how the ball needs to be deformed in order to obtain the maximum possible field at one point (in this case at point $O$). It turns out that after such a deformation of the ball, the direction of the field at point $O$ will increase $\sqrt{27/25}$ times, i.e. by approximately 3%.
