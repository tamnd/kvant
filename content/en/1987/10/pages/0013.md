⟦folio 11⟧

The system could fly through a giant cloud of interstellar gas and capture the dust, gas, organic compounds it contains, and possibly even ready-made cometary nuclei.

By the way, using radioastronomical methods, several types of organic compounds were discovered in gas-dust clouds in the Galaxy - formaldetides, hydrocyanic acid, alcohols - the starting products for the formation of the most complex carbon derivatives - amino acids, which are the “building blocks” of protein. Traces of organizational compounds recorded during ground-based observations and in comets. Even amino acids themselves are found in meteorites.

Perhaps meteorites (like comets) are “garbage” from the building site where the Solar System was created. However, they are already fairly “damaged” by the Sun. Comets were stored at a great distance from it, as if in a refrigerator, without being exposed to radiation, and therefore had to retain their primary properties.

That's why scientists were so impatiently waiting for a date with Halley's comet.

The most important task in Project Veta was to study the physical characteristics of the comet's nucleus. Two approaches were used: firstly, remote measurements using optical instruments and, secondly, direct studies of matter (gas and dust) leaving the core and crossing the trajectory along which the device is moving.

The optical instruments were placed on a special platform, which rotated during the project of the core and automatically tracked the direction towards it. However, one of the main elements of the optical complex was the television system. The kit also included instruments that were supposed to provide detailed spectroscopic studies of the chemical composition of various atmospheric objects (coma) and the comet's tail. However, one of the most important directions of these studies was the search for primary, or, as they are also called, “parent” molecules. Ground spectroscopic changes

these molecules cannot be detected. The fact is that when cometary ice transitions from a solid state directly to a gaseous state, gas flies out from the surface of the comet almost at the speed of sound, and in the region of the nucleus its density is very high. Therefore, molecules intensively interact with each other and chemical changes occur in them. During these processes, so-called “daughter”, i.e. secondary, molecules are formed. They “record” ground-based spectroscopic changes.

Optical studies of the gas state of the comet's atmosphere should be supplemented by its changes using a special dust mass analyzer (PUMA).

As already noted, the cometary atmosphere consists not only of gas, but also of dust particles. With a relative speed of merging the spacecraft with the comet of almost 80 km/s, this dust poses a great danger. To protect the apparatus, the comet creates velocity and was used to determine the composition of the particles. If a target is placed in the path of such a “speck of dust,” then when it is removed, the particle will explosively evaporate, initize, and turn into a cloud of plasma. The mass spectrometer determines its composition, and therefore the composition of the nucleus of the comet from which this particle escaped.
