The specialist is referring to the internal energy of the air that fills our homes, and under typical residential conditions, the air behaves like an ideal gas (more precisely, like a mixture of ideal gases). The energy of an ideal gas is proportional to the absolute temperature $T$ and the amount of matter, i.e., the mass of the gas $m$. Therefore, for the internal energy of air we can write

$$U = amT,$$

where $a$ is some constant.

Now the mistake that the scientist made is becoming very serious. He stated that in this situation the air mass $m$ is a function of temperature. And the reality is that the living space is not isolated from the outside world; when the air is thawed, it heats up, expands and partially comes out through the cracks and pores in the walls.
