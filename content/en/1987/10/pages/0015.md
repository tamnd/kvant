Interesting results for measuring the quantitative characteristics of the dust flow. Experiments with dust counters have shown that about a million tons of cosmic dust leaves the cometary nucleus every day. The nature of the size distribution of dust particles turned out to be quite unexpected; an unexpectedly large number of small particles on the order of a hundredth of a micron in size were discovered.

Measurements have shown that the flow of gas leaving the comet contains the most water vapor. The second most abundant component is carbon dioxide. Most likely, CO₂ and H₂O are included in the core in the form of a clathrate - a crystalline compound in which the molecules of one substance (CO₂) are “embedded” in the crystal lattice of the other (ice H₂O).

Basic and completely new information about the chemical state of particles that were part of the nucleus and left it under the pressure of gas flows, for the PUMA experiment (on Vega) and a similar experiment on Giotto. In reality, particles with a diameter of hundredths and tenths of a micron were recorded, but there were a lot of them. The PUMA experiment obtained spectra of approximately 2000 such particles.

From the spectra it follows that, according to the chemical composition, solid particles of comet dust can be divided into three main types: the first is dominated by light elements, the second by carbon, and the third by metals. Soviet physicist L.M. Mukhin, who analyzed these spectra, came to two important conclusions. First, the carbon-rich particles exhibit C/Si elemental ratios that are substantially greater than those found in any type of metorite. The second conclusion is that a high C/Si ratio is always accompanied by the presence of nitrogen, and this can only be explained by the fact that carbon and nitrogen are part of organic molecules. This is an important indication of the presence of important technologies.

In fact, there are no insurmountable technical difficulties for carrying out such research even at the current level of space development, and scientists are already discussing this possibility. Among the comets there are also those whose nucleus could be approached even to the point of direct contact. Perhaps such a plenet was made at the end of this century or the beginning of the next.
