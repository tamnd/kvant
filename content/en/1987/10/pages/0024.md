delta the dependence of $m$ on $T$, using the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation for an ideal gas:

$$pV = \frac{m}{M} RT$$

(M is the molar mass of air, $R$ is the universal gas constant). The volume of the room $V$ does not change, the pressure $p$ remains constant, equal to the external air pressure. Consequently, the right side of equation (2) should also, despite the temperature change, maintain a constant value. This is only possible if the product $mT$ is constant.

Note that the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation is usually applied in the case when the mass $m$ is fixed. In the problem we are considering, this condition is not satisfied and, according to equation (1), the internal energy of the air, if the product $mT$ is constant, must remain unchanged. The increase in the average kinetic energy of an individual molecule is compensated by a decrease in the number of molecules.

The air temperature in the room will differ from the operating temperature of the human body. Too low a temperature causes severe hypothermia in the body, as a result of which it impedes the process of chemical reactions that ensure metabolism.
