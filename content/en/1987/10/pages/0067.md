COMPUTER IN THE COMPANY
GRANDMASTERS
The first meetings at the chessboard between man and machine date back to the early 70s. It is clear that in the era of “generalists” - computers occupying huge rooms, such meetings had to be specially organized. Now that the industry of personal computers, including chess microcomputers, is rapidly developing, anyone can play with a machine (they will also go on sale here soon). As for the grandsmeterers, they are usually approached with a challenge being carried out on one side and the other side of the game against a whole company of machines; sometimes machines are included in the number of participants in a regular session. However, all teams play in the same tournaments with chess, and spring seems to be in the tournaments.

Former world champion M. Tal recently played in an international tournament in West Berlin, in which “Mephisto”, the world champion among chess microcomputers, was also a participant. It is a pity that there was no meeting between them - the Swiss system did not bring them together. This tournament brought together 466 chess players from 77 countries, among them 15 grossmetres and 50 international masters. The composition included nine rounds. Tal came out the winner, which is not surprising. But the result of the car is sensational: “Mephisto” scored 5.5 points and shared 63rd place, and in the same company were grandsmeterers Radulov, Gorgiev and Lekhtinsky, and behind by half a point were grandsmeterers Bellon, Tringov and Spasov. Of course, the “Swiss” with its nervous composition often overcomes challenges, but in any case, those who are skeptical about the chess abilities of computers have reason to doubt.

The creative masterpiece of the tournament in West Berlin was the next game between “Mephisto” and the Hamburg player M. Fette, owner of a master rating of 2300.

"Mephisto" - M. Fette

Grionfeld's defense
1. c4 Kf6 2. Kc3 d5 3. cd K:d5 4. d4 g6 5. e4 K:c3 6. bc Cg7. The tournament took place simultaneously with the Kasparov-Karpov rematch, in which Grionfeld's defense was the most popular opening. True, in the present game the rivals have developed a varnat that was not encountered in the fight for the crown. 7. Kf3 c5 8. Cb5+ Kc6 9. 0—0 0—0 10. C:c6 bc 11. Ce3 Cg4 12. Rc1. The computer played the opening without much complaint, and now Black could quickly make a draw: 12...C:f3 13. Q:f3 cd 14. cd C:d4 15. C:d4 Q:d4 16. R:c6, etc. However, man always strives to defeat the machine. 12...Qa5 13. Qe2 Jlfd8 14. Jlfd1 Qa4. Now the threat to the d4 pawn is serious. White is forced to play e4 - e5, and Black's positional advantage becomes undeniable. 15. e5 cd 16. cd Ce6 17. Cg5 Jlfd7 18. Jlfd2 h6 19. Ce3 Jlb8 20. Jldc2 Cd5 21. Ke1!

The knight goes to point c5 - an excellent strategic alternative. It's amazing how well the computer performs in this difficult situation. 21...Jldb7 22. Kd3 Jlb1 23. h3 Kph7 24. Qd2 e6 25. R:b1 Jl:b1+ 26. Kph2 Ce4 27. Rc5. “Mephisto” sets a cunning trap: 27...Jld1 28. Kb2! Jl:d2 29. K:a4 Jl:a2 30. Kc3. The option is forced, so it’s completely elementary for a computer.

27...Qa6 28. Jlc3. The threat of Nc5 is no longer a joke. 28...Cd5 29. a3 Cf8 30. f3. Limiting the actions of the bishop d5. 30...Qa4 31. Kc5 Qd1. After the exchange on c5, due to the expanded bishops, the board would have been a broken draw. Black is slightly nervous and jokes about the possibility of outplaying his opponent in the backgame. But the situation is getting worse.

32. Q:d1 Jl:d1 33. Kd7 Ce7 34. Kpg3 g5 35. Kpf2 Kpg6 36. g4!? White takes control of the f5 square, but weakens the f3 and h3 pawns. 36...Cd8 37. Kf8+ Kpg7 38. Kd7. The machine agrees to a draw for now. 38...Ca5 39. Jlc5 Ce1+ 40. Kpe2 Jla1 41. Kf6. Black is playing with fire in search of a chance to win. Now the threat of Jlg8× has appeared in Mephisto’s project, so the black bishop must hold square a5 (shielding from Jlc5 - a5:a7), and the rook must hold the bishop itself. Unpleasant binding!

41...a5 42. f4 Cg2? (should have taken on f4) 43. fg hg 44. Cf2. I wonder why White didn’t take the g5 pawns? Last 44. C:g5 C:h3 (44...Kpg6 45. h4 with the idea of ​​Kf6-h5-f4+) 45. R:c6 White has a terrible threat Jlc6 - c8 - g8×. What if the machine foresaw countercheck 45...Cf1+! Then the king must take the bishop: 46. Kp:f1 Cd2+ 47. Kpe2 C:g5 and don’t look at the lack of a pawn to transfer again to black: 48. Kh5+ Kpg6 49. Jlc3 Cc1.

44...C:f2 45. Kp:f2 Jla2+ 46. Kpg1 Cd5 47. K:d5. The last opportunity to pose problems for Black was 47. Jl:a5. Now I lose 47...Jld2 48. Jla8 Jl:d4 49. Jlg8+ Kph6 with inevitable mate. Salvation, apparently, lies in 47...Jlg2+ 48. Kpf1 Jlh2 49. Jla8 Jlh1+ with perpetual check, since the white king cannot accelerate the black square b4 due to c6 - c5+.

47...cd 48. Jl:a5 Jlfd2 49. Jla4 Jlfd1+ 50. Kph2 Jld2+. Prostrate, the extra pawn doesn't matter. A party that blacks can be proud of!
