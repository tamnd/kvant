F1060. A mathematical matrix is ​​an inexhaustible rod of length l, to the free end of which a small massive handle is attached. A bead of the same mass is placed on the rod, which can slide freely along a horizontal guide passing to the level of the middle of the rod (see figure). Find the period of small oscillations of such a matrix. Trenz is missing.

$$v_m = \sqrt{2gl(1 - \cos \alpha_0)} = 2\sin \frac{\alpha_0}{2}\sqrt{gl}.$$

Under the condition of small fluctuations

$$\sin \frac{\alpha_0}{2} \approx \frac{\alpha_0}{2} \approx \frac{A}{2l},$$

where $A$ is the amplitude of matrix oscillations, and

$$v_m = A\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} = A\omega_0,$$

where $\omega_0$ is the oscillation frequency.

Let's find the maximum speed of the weight for our system (a matrix with a bead that is always on the same horizontal line). Let us write down the law of conservation of energy for the system at the moment when the weight passes the lowest point of the trajectory:

$$\frac{m}{2}v_m^2 + \frac{m}{2}\left(\frac{v_m}{2}\right)^2 = mgl(1 - \cos \alpha_0)$$

($\frac{v_m}{2}$ is the speed of the bead at this moment). From here

$$v_m = \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{2gl(1 - \cos \alpha_0)} = A \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\omega_0.$$

As can be seen from this result, the frequency $\omega$ of oscillations of our system is $2/\sqrt{5}$ times less than the frequency $\omega_0$ of oscillations of the mathematical matrix of length l. Therefore, the period of oscillation of the system is

$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \pi\omega_0\sqrt{5} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{5l}{g}}.$$

L. G. Markovich

F1061. The flame of the alcohol lamp, before going dark, begins to flicker and demand. Why?

The reason for the flickering of the flame is, most likely, the uneven supply of fuel - alcohol. This occurs when the alcohol runs out.

Indeed, when there is little alcohol left, they begin to tell, in addition to the capillary forces of “capturing” the fuel with the wick, the forces of interaction of the alcohol with the bottom and walls of the alcohol lamp (surface tension forces). Excessive fuel supply can also occur due to the presence of all kinds of “weights”, which are usually located at the bottom of the spirit lamp.

So, at some point in time, the alcohol supplied to the flame through the wick is not enough to maintain a stable size of the flame, and the flame seems to disappear; however, at the next moment, “excess” alcohol enters, and there is a sharp increase in the flame, accompanied by a crackling sound.

As a rule, with a wick diameter of 2-3 mm, such flickering occurs with crackling air, when the length of the wick is several millimeters, and the shorter the wick, the more often flashes occur, accompanied by a crackling sound.

A. S. Butov
