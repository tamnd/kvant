XIII All-Russian Schoolchildren Olympiad

According to established tradition, the final, zonal stage of the All-Russian Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry Olympiad for schoolchildren in grades 8-10 took place during the spring school holidays. Below are the conditions of the problems in mathematics and physics proposed at the final stage, and the names of the winners of the XIII All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren.

Mathematics
8th grade
1. Find all pairs of integers (x; y) satisfying the equation $xy=20-3x+y$.
2. On the number axis there is a set M, which can be covered with segments of unit length. Let $M'$ be the set of midpoints of all possible segments whose ends belong to the set M. Prove that the set $M'$ can be covered by six segments of unit length. Is it always possible to go around a path in segments?
3. When the circles have a common point M, intersect in pairs at points P, Q, R and are located on the plane as shown in figure 1. Through an arbitrary point A of one of the circles lying on the arc PQ not containing the point M, and points P and Q at which the circle intersects two other circles, straight lines are drawn that intersect these two circles a second time at points B and C (see Fig. 1). Prove that points B, C and R lie on the same line.
4. An infinite decimal non-periodic fraction is given. Prove that you can rearrange its digits so that you get a periodic fraction.
5. All angles of a convex pentagon ABCDE are equal. All angles at the vertices of the “star” ACEBDA are also equal. Prove that the pentagon ABCDE is right.
9th grade
1. Find all values ​​of n for which the number 144...4 is the square of a natural number.

2. Among the 99 outwardly identical coins, several were found that were counterfeit. It is known that each counterfeit coin differs in weight from the real coin by an odd number of grams and that the total weight of all these coins is equal to the weight of 99 real coins. There are two-pan scales with an arrow showing the difference in grams of the weights of the weights placed on the bowls. Prove that by carrying out only one weighing on such scales, you can find out about any pre-selected coin whether it is counterfeit or not.
3. Prove that if α, β, γ are the angles of a triangle, then the inequality is true
$$\cos^2 \alpha + \cos^2 \beta + \cos \gamma > \frac{3}{4}.$$
4. Two circles intersect at points M and N. Lines l1 and l2 are drawn through points M, forming identical angles with the cord MN and intersecting the circles, in addition to point M, at two more points: l1 - at points A and B, l2 - at points C and D. Prove that AB = CD.
5. The billiard table has the shape of a regular triangle. Prove that if the ball passed through a certain point seven times after being hit, then it will pass through it at least once more.
10th grade
1. Find all three-digit numbers abc whose squares end in abc.
2. Prove that for all x∈R the identity holds
$$\|...\|x|-2^n|-2^{n-1}|...|-2|-1|= \|...\|x|-1|-1|-...|-1|.$$
3. Prove that if the sum
$$\frac{1}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3}+\frac{1}{4\cdot 5\cdot 6}+\dots+\frac{1}{1984\cdot 1985\cdot 1986}$$ is represented as an irreducible fraction, then the numerator of this fraction will be divisible by 1987.
4. Let l be the length of the spatial broken line, a, b, c the lengths of its projections onto the coordinate planes. a) Prove that the inequality $(a+b+c)/l \leq \sqrt{6}$ is true. 6) Is there a closed broken line for which the equality $(a+b+c)/l=\sqrt{6}$ holds?
5. Four correctly look for numbers lying on the table with their dials facing up so that their centers are the vertices of the square. At some points in time, the ends of the minute hands turned out to be the vertices of the square. Prove that at any other time, a quadrilateral with vertices at the ends of the minute hands is also a square. (Neglect the thickness of the clock.)
