MEETING WITH A COMET
HALLEY HAS BEEN HAPPENED!

Candidate of Physics and Mathematics
T. K. BREUS

Comets are still the least studied objects in the solar system, although several are discovered every year. The reason is that they appear unexpectedly, and the body of the comet itself - its nucleus - is inaccessible to ground-based observations, since it is hidden by the glow of a colossal, hundreds of thousands of times larger than the nucleus itself, head, or “coma” - a gas-dust atmosphere that arose during the evaporation of cometary matter, heated by solar study as the comet approaches the Sun.

Halley's Comet has proven itself to be one of the brightest during its return. For example, in 837 it passed 6 million km from the Earth and was 6.5 times brighter than Sirius, the brightest star in the earth’s sky. In 1910, during the execution of the luminary, the comet found itself between the Earth and the Sun. And since the tails of comets are always directed away from the Sun and stretch for many millions of kilometers, the Earth flew through the comet’s tail for several hours.

At this time, Halley's comet was discovered at a record distance - about 1.6 million km from our planet and the Sun. It was October 16, 1982, when the comet was in the constellation Canis Minor and was 40 million times fainter than the faintest stars that a person can still see with the naked eye. Typically, comets are detected at distances 2-3 times smaller, but this time the most advanced technology was used, which makes it possible to register even individual light quanta. But due to the unfavorable location of the comet’s trajectory (during periods of the trajectory, the Sun found itself between the Earth and the comet), it was impossible to “promise” with it from the Earth properly. This shortcoming of the comet was immediately resolved by the launch of a heel of spacecraft to the comet at once - two Soviet (“Veta-1”, “Veta-2”)*, one Western European (“Giotto”) and two Japanese.

During the previous appearance of Halley's comet in 1910, spectroscopes were aimed mainly at it, with the help of which actromes clarified the composition of the head and tail of the celestial guest. Even earlier, in 1835-1836, the first astrophysical device was used to study Halley's comet - the invention of the outstanding French physicist and astronomer Arago - a polariscope. Then it was proven that the tails of comets shine mainly with light reflected from the Sun, that is, they contain a lot of dust - dispersed stone parts of the nucleus, driven in the form of a tail away from the Sun by some kind of force. It was only in 1910, after the Russian physicist P. N. Lebedev showed that light can exert pressure on particles, that the formation of dusty comet tails was explained.

In 1836, statements about the physical nature of the comet's tinsten nucleus appeared in the scientific literature. The German astronomer Bessel put forward the idea that the substance is volatile, that is, easily ignited by heat. A similar idea was expressed by the late Lapas. In the third edition of his Exposition of the System of the World (1808), he explained the formation of the colossal head and tail of a comet by the rapid evaporation of a “liquid” from the surfaces of the nucleus near the Sun. In lazy comets, the remaining “liquid” from our star must again turn into ice, and so on with each subsequent return.

In the 20th century, the idea of ​​the icy nucleus of comet novae was revived by the American astrophysicist F. Whipple. He proposes a model (1950), according to which the cometary nucleus is a cosmic iceberg - a monolithic body of irregular shape, consisting of frozen volatile substances (H₂O, CH₄, NH₃, CO₂, etc.) mixed with particles of meteorite matter. When the cometary nucleus approaches the Sun, volatile substances evaporate from its surface, and gas flows arise that carry along dust particles - forming a coma and tail.

Soviet astronomer B. A. Vorontsov-Velyaminov believed that the comet’s nucleus consists mainly of refractory substances (silicates, metals) and only on the surface there is a layer of frozen gases. The Dutch astrophysicist M. Greenberg generally agreed with Whipple, but believed that complex organizations of matter should be “added” to the hypothetical composition of the nucleus. He proposed that the comet's nucleus is not an iceberg, but rather a kind of snowdrift, and it consists of particles of interterrestrial matter, “compressed” into a complex structure - a cylinder several microns long, silicate inside, a layer of organics on top, and a layer of ice on top of the organics.

There were a large number of hypotheses about the origin of comets. According to one of them, first proposed by Lagrange (1788), and then developed by the Soviet scientist S.K. Vsesvyatsky (1955), cometary nuclei could be ejected from the bowels of giant planets and their satellites during volcanic eruptions. When the American Voyaders discovered volcanic activity on Jupter's moon Io, this assumption gained a kind of “second wind.” Nevertheless, the attitude towards this hypothesis is very skeptical - there are too many “buts”.

Most experts believe that the outstanding Dutch astronomer Jan Oort managed to get the answer to the question of the origin of comets. Of course, at the beginning the correctness of a particular hypothesis is not decided by a majority vote, and only experimental facts provide objective evidence. Nevertheless, Oort's hypothesis (1950) has virtually no flaws.

Oort suggested that the Solar System is surrounded by a giant “cloud” of comets, extending at distances from 20,000 to 200,000 astronomical units (1 AU = 150,000,000 km) from the Sun, i.e. far beyond the planetary system (the orbit of Pluto, the last planet, is about 40 AU).

What makes comets leave their “shelter”? Although the Oort region is estimated to contain 10¹¹÷10¹² comets, their collisions are so rare*) that they cannot be the cause that pushes them out of the cloud. It turns out that the close passage of a star is responsible for this. They change the speed of movement of comets and from time to time they are thrown either outside the Solar System or towards the Sun.

However, the origin of the Oort cloud itself, or, as it is called, the bank or safe of comets, remains unclear. Solving this problem is one of the main goals of the space experiment to Halley's comet. Scientists hoped that observing the comet's nucleus at close range, determining its composition, and examining part of the comet with the “hands” of automata would make it possible to understand how the Oort bank was formed. Is it part of the protoplanetary matter - the gaseous snout nebula, from which the Sun, planets and comets emerged at approximately the same epoch? Or maybe the Oort bank has a galactic origin? By the way, using radioastronomical methods, several types of organic compounds were discovered in gas-dust clouds in the Galaxy - formaldetides, hydrocyanic acid, alcohols - the starting products for the formation of the most complex carbon derivatives - amino acids, which are the “building blocks” of protein. Perhaps meteorites (like comets) are “garbage” from the building site where the Solar System was created. However, they are already fairly “damaged” by the Sun. Comets were stored at a great distance from it, as if in a refrigerator, without being exposed to radiation, and therefore had to retain their primary properties.

The most important task in Project Veta was to study the physical characteristics of the comet's nucleus. Two approaches were used: firstly, remote measurements using optical instruments and, secondly, direct studies of matter (gas and dust) leaving the core and crossing the trajectory along which the device is moving.

The optical instruments were placed on a special platform, which rotated during the project of the core and automatically tracked the direction towards it. However, one of the main elements of the optical complex was the television system. The kit also included instruments that were supposed to provide detailed spectroscopic studies of the chemical composition of various atmospheric objects (coma) and the comet's tail. However, one of the most important directions of these studies was the search for primary, or, as they are also called, “parent” molecules. Ground spectroscopic changes

these molecules cannot be detected. The fact is that when cometary ice transitions from a solid state directly to a gaseous state, gas flies out from the surface of the comet almost at the speed of sound, and in the region of the nucleus its density is very high. Therefore, molecules intensively interact with each other and chemical changes occur in them. During these processes, so-called “daughter”, i.e. secondary, molecules are formed. They “record” ground-based spectroscopic changes.

Optical studies of the gas state of the comet's atmosphere should be supplemented by its changes using a special dust mass analyzer (PUMA).

As already noted, the cometary atmosphere consists not only of gas, but also of dust particles. With a relative speed of merging the spacecraft with the comet of almost 80 km/s, this dust poses a great danger. To protect the apparatus, the comet creates velocity and was used to determine the composition of the particles. If a target is placed in the path of such a “speck of dust,” then when it is removed, the particle will explosively evaporate, initize, and turn into a cloud of plasma. The mass spectrometer determines its composition, and therefore the composition of the nucleus of the comet from which this particle escaped.

A number of instruments were intended to measure the physical characteristics of the dust flow - the number of parts of different masses, their sizes, and densities.

Numerous instruments were also installed on board the stations to study the interaction of cometary ions with the interplanetary medium, which, as is known, is filled with plasma emitted by the solar corona - the solar wind.

What new did we learn during this meeting with the comet?

Analysis of television images transmitted from the stations to Earth showed that the nucleus of Halley's comet is a monolithic body of irregular shape. Its dimensions are 8x8x16 km. This is much more than scientists assume (2-5 km). The volume of the core, according to rough estimates, is approximately 5·10¹¹ m³. The nucleus is rotated around an axis, which is located inside the cone at a distance of ±45° from the normal to the orbital plane. The direction of the doctor coincides with the direction of orbital motion. The reflectivity of the core is very low. The comet's nucleus is one of the darkest bodies in the solar system. Dust emission occurs only on the illuminated part of the core. There are several areas on the surface (approximately 10% of the area), from which dust escapes in relatively narrow streams (jets). Dust, however, leaves the surface and outside the chips. Above the surface there is a thin (about 1 km) layer, inside which the dust is accelerated by a gas flow from zero speed to several hundred meters per second. Features resembling ring craters are visible on the surface.

The photographs taken by the Western European Giotto apparatus added clarification to this picture only in the last question: ring craters are undoubtedly real; they are clearly visible on processed sigma images near the terminator (the boundary of shadow and light), where there is little dust above the surface and lighting conditions are most favorable.

Measurements of the thermal radiation of the core made by the Veni-1 spectrometer made it possible to determine the surface temperature. From preliminary calculations based on heat balance conditions, it followed that on the day side of the comet it should be 200 K. However, measurements for twice the values ​​of $T=380 \pm 30$ K.

This can be explained, believes the Soviet scientist V.I. Moroz, if we assume that the surface of the core is covered with a layer of “weight” (similar to what is often the case in cities with snowdrifts). This layer absorbs solar energy, part of it is re-emitted in the infrared range, and part of it is transferred by thermal conduction down to the inner “clean” surface of the core, where the temperature is about 200 K.

What is the structure of this layer? Its thickness is most likely no more than 0.1 mm, otherwise it greatly weakens the natural flow. At the same time, it must be solid, otherwise the core would not be so black. Apparently, there are many small particles in it, and gas passes freely through the pores. The surface layer in some places cracks from time to time, and then an active region is formed with a particularly powerful outflow of matter.

The formation of such a layer is an interweaving of natural ones, if we remember that cometary ice is transported with particles of refractory substances, and during evaporation after the gas leaves, their concentration on the surface increases.

The temperature of 380 K refers to the warmest point of the core, which is on the dayside at the equator. This point is shifted from the obtained one by approximately one hour due to the thermal inertia of the surface layer. It is much colder on the night side; According to indirect estimates, the temperature here is 180-200 K.

It was possible to estimate the mass and average density of the comet's nucleus. The mass was determined by the reactive effect, which is created by the emission of gas and dust towards the Sun. The calculations were carried out independently by the Swedish scientist G. Rickman and the Soviet scientists R. Z. Sagdeev, P. E. Elyasberg, V. I. Moroz. Results

Interesting results for measuring the quantitative characteristics of the dust flow. Experiments with dust counters have shown that about a million tons of cosmic dust leaves the cometary nucleus every day. The nature of the size distribution of dust particles turned out to be quite unexpected; an unexpectedly large number of small particles on the order of a hundredth of a micron in size were discovered.

Measurements have shown that the flow of gas leaving the comet contains the most water vapor. The second most abundant component is carbon dioxide. Most likely, CO₂ and H₂O are included in the core in the form of a clathrate - a crystalline compound in which the molecules of one substance (CO₂) are “embedded” in the crystal lattice of the other (ice H₂O).

Basic and completely new information about the chemical state of particles that were part of the nucleus and left it under the pressure of gas flows, for the PUMA experiment (on Vega) and a similar experiment on Giotto. In reality, particles with a diameter of hundredths and tenths of a micron were recorded, but there were a lot of them. The PUMA experiment obtained spectra of approximately 2000 such particles.

From the spectra it follows that, according to the chemical composition, solid particles of comet dust can be divided into three main types: the first is dominated by light elements, the second by carbon, and the third by metals. Soviet physicist L.M. Mukhin, who analyzed these spectra, came to two important conclusions. First, the carbon-rich particles exhibit C/Si elemental ratios that are substantially greater than those found in any type of metorite. The second conclusion is that a high C/Si ratio is always accompanied by the presence of nitrogen, and this can only be explained by the fact that carbon and nitrogen are part of organic molecules. This is an important indication of the presence of important technologies.

In fact, there are no insurmountable technical difficulties for carrying out such research even at the current level of space development, and scientists are already discussing this possibility.
