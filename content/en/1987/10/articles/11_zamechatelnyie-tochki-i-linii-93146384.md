Wonderful points and line

There is a point in a triangle such that the segments connecting it to the vertices form equal angles from the side of the Brocard point in the four-French mathematician Ani Brocard (1845-1922), the author of the most complete reference book on remarkable curves.

These equal angles are called Brocard's ulelli. There is also a second Brocard point in the triangle, which is obtained by going the opposite way around the vertices of the triangle. It is interesting that the value of Brocard's well for the second point will be the same as for the first.

In Brocard's reference book we find the logarithmic spiral. Its equation is r = aeᵣᵣ, where r is the distance of point M on the spiral to the selected point O, φ is the angle between the ray OM and the selected ray OX. If a logarithmic spiral is like increasing and turning at the same time, it will turn into itself.

The tangent of the Brocard angle is expressed by the formula: tanφ = $\frac{4S}{a^2+b^2+c^2}$, where S is the area of ​​the triangle, a, b, c are the lengths of its sides. The areas of the triangles into which the original triangle is divided are related as the reciprocals of the squares of the sides: $a^2S_{ABM} = b^2S_{BCM} = c^2S_{CAM}$.
