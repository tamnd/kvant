INTERVIEW
WITH ACADEMICIAN
R. Z. SAGDEV

— The first artificial Earth satellite and the Mir orbital complex are separated by 30 years. This is a whole era in technology. Current space technology has already become an integral part of the economy, one of the most promising sectors of the national economy of the USSR. What did the future of space science look like 30 years ago? What direction will space technology take in the coming years?

“It’s very difficult to compare what happened with what we expected to get, since there was no real, collectively conscious forecast. There were forecasts by individual scientists. For example, a very bright forecast by K. E. Tsiolkovsky (it was given several decades before the launch of the first satellite). It is known that S.P. Korolev thought a lot about the direction in which space science would develop. I believe that the space scientists understood the main technical, organizational, and psychological difficulties that arose later quite well.

At the same time, today space science, in my opinion, is more of a business, “routine” nature, rather than the romantic one that seemed dreamy in the first years of space exploration.

The emphasis in the forecasts was on free automatic devices. Many science fiction stories were, and still are, about the activities of crews in orbit or in deep space, or even the goals of space colonies. But on Earth, for decades now, the present of automatic machines and robotics has been going on. Why not expect the same thing to happen in space? So, in fact, this is already happening. For 30 years, machines have “learned” to do simply miracles. They constantly implement new advances in computer technology and information science. CERO-
