Our automaton, in essence, is the bearer of artificial intelligence. The number of commands “embedded” in the on-board computers of vehicles such as, for example, the American “Viking” or our “Vegas” amounts to tens of thousands. But that’s not all: thanks to the constant noise communication channel, the Earth - spacecraft (spacecraft) has the ability to reprogram during flight. When we discussed, for example, the problem of finding the small nucleus of Halley's comet among a giant bright coma, we decided to leave the freedom of detection and location of the nucleus to the on-board computer, since no one knew what shape it would have. Problems of this kind are rapidly advancing science and technology, and at the turn of two thousand, such spacecraft will determine the face of space technology.

I will not go so far as the famous American physicist F. Dyson does. He imagines the future spacecraft, probes leaving the solar system, as a kind of hybrid of ultra-modern, or rather, ultra-future electronics, computer science and molecular genetics - a kind of “cosmic butterfly in a protective cocoon”, using the principles of molecular biotechnology and bioelectronics. Although today even such fantasies probably deserve attention.

— What place does man still have in space flights?

- Of course, man, the astronaut has his own economic food space. For example, at orbital stations such as Mir. And in the coming years, perhaps until the end of the century, the trends that have emerged will develop. Man's stay in orbit is already permanent.

The everyday life accumulated by space medicine during long orbital flights, as well as the experience of long-term operation of space technology, would, in principle, allow our contemporary to go on a multi-year journey, for example, to Mars. And such a project has many supporters. At SISHA, scientific and technical societies and major scientists and specialists in the field of space exploration are encouraged to organize such an expedition. For this purpose, astrophysicist K. Sagan and his colleagues founded a powerful public organization, the American Planetary Society. The current level of technology really makes it possible to carry out such a flight. However, its cost would be several times higher than the cost of the most expensive space program to date - the Apollo lunar expedition, implemented in the late 60s - early 70s.

— With the help of space technology, it has become possible to carry out research that is insufficient for “terrestrial” technology, and to make many scientific discoveries. What are the most important ones?

— Today it is difficult to single out any of them. The future will show what place they will occupy in the golden background of human achievements. And we're not just talking about astronomy. The 30 years of the space age have enriched many things in terms of science and technology. In orbit, they obtain new materials and medicines, conduct observations of agricultural and forest areas, draw up maps of hard-to-reach areas of the Earth, and help geologists search for minerals.

As for astronomical research, the priority here is in two directions: the study of celestial objects using remote sensing methods and the direct study of the solar system with instruments installed on spacecraft. Having taken instruments beyond the Earth's atmosphere, space astronomy has the opportunity to specially work in ranges inaccessible on Earth due to the influence of the atmosphere - RENTEN, infrared, ultraviolet ranges, as well as in the range of galmasic teachings. These topics include quasars, pulsars, neutron stars and black holes. The infrared region also has its own pioneers. The study of extradalactic objects will lead to the creation of new models of the formation and evolution of the Universe, the formation of galaxies and star systems, in particular the Solar System.

- At the dawn of the space age, there was a lot of hope that going into space would make a real discovery of external life; even man’s dream of meeting his brothers in mind seemed about to happen. Perhaps this is what gave space flights a special romance. However, in actual fiction this theme was central (you can use the replacement of I. A. Efremov’s novel “The Andromeda Nebula,” which appeared at the same time). Years passed, but scientists not only failed to detect life on other celestial bodies, many of them even believe that life on Earth is unique. What's new for space exploration in this regard?

— In space research, the search for extraterrestrial life occupies an important place. If the flight program allowed for the opportunity to shed additional light on this issue, such an opportunity, as a rule, was not missed. Special instruments were installed on spacecraft (for example, on the landing block of the Martian Viking there was a biological laboratory). And although it was not possible to find signs of life of inaction anywhere, I thought it was too early to draw pessistic conclusions. Of course, what we know about Mars today makes the discovery of even the most primitive forms of life on it unlikely. And in general there is little chance that this will be removed anywhere in the Solar System. However, research has yielded a lot of important data about the so-called pre-biological evolution of molecules. An almost complete set of amino acid bases is present both in the lunar soil and in the metoritic substance that falls to Earth. This means that the so-called abiogenic synthesis—the preparation of the initial “building blocks” for further jumping to the gene level—takes place under cosmic conditions in the presence of all possible environmental radiation, such as X-rays. And of course, during future flights to Mars and other celestial bodies more distant from us, such a possibility of biological or at least pre-biological research will be explored.

— Many scientists consider it advisable to look for manifestations of the activity of external civilizations among celestial bodies, in the “behavior” of which there are any anomalies. At one time, the attention of astronomers was attracted by the Tunguska meteorite, the satellites of Mars, some comets... Are there any celestial bodies today that astronomers are “on suspicion”?

Unfortunately, today there are no objects that could be considered “suspicious” from such a point of view. To one degree or another, on the basis of astrophysical hypotheses and physical models, every anomaly in the behavior of certain cosmic bodies can be explained. At least to the extent that known facts allow us to do so. At the same time, radio astronomers consider one of their main tasks to be the search for objects with anomalous characteristics. This direction has a chance of detecting truly anomalous objects.

The main question of our time is the question of peace. Could international cooperation in space be an alternative to the Star Wars program? How do you imagine it?

Of course, peaceful international cooperation in any form is an alternative to the miliarization of outer space. It is impossible to saturate the near-Earth orbits of numerous military spacecraft and at the same time expand international scientific or, say, commercial projects into gray road construction. These two directions come into deep conflict. And it is no coincidence that the parties to peaceful space, the star world, actively oppose the use of space for military purposes.

Today there are already quite a lot of specific areas of space research in which international cooperation is carried out, in particular, this is the Intercosmos program, which includes the study of outer space by unmanned vehicles, rafted flights, etc. As part of international cooperation, research into Halley’s comet was successfully completed in 1986. In mid-1988, I learned how to implement the Phobos project. Its goal is to study one of the satellites of Mars. The initiators of the project were Soviet scientists, and the scientific equipment for Phobos is being prepared by specialists from different countries.

2001: A Space Odyssey is just around the corner. What will it be like? After all, it contains scientists and external readers of the journal «Kvant». What can you wish for them?

I think her script will be written when today's readers «Kvant» become specialists. There is not much left before that, maybe 5-7 years. And the earlier schoolchildren take an active creative position, the greater results they will achieve (and it is not at all necessary to wait until they reach their fifties!).

I wish them success!
