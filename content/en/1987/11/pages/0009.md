Immediately before the Great Patriotic War, L. V. Kantorovich considered a number of new problems for finding maximum and minimum values ​​related to the distribution of work, cutting materials, organizing transportation, etc. These problems served as the beginning of the creation of a new mathematical theory - linear programming, which received rapid development after the war. Now these first formulations of problems have received general formulations, solution methods have been proposed for them, an interesting theory has been built, and these results have been extended to more general cases (nonlinear programming).

A large area of ​​research, caused by the need for automatic control of technological processes, the movement of aircraft, spacecraft, etc., was developed by L. S. Pontgrin and his students. This direction is called the theory of optimal control. Theory and develop principles in it to immediately obtain numerous practical applications.

New tasks in the theory of random processes include such practical problems as managing product quality and determining when to set up equipment. These problems were brought to the attention of A. N. Kolmogorov, who directed the attention of a number of his students to them.

One of the greatest mathematicians of our time, N. N. Bogolyukov, having begun working under the guidance of Academician N. M. Krylov, published his first result at the age of seventeen. His research interests quickly expanded to include mathematical analysis, differential equations, expressions, expressed calculus, theories of functions, mathematical physics, and probability theory. Together with N. M. Krylov, he developed mathematical methods of nonlinear mechanics. After the Great Patriotic War, N. N. Bogolyubov switched almost exclusively to solving problems of modern physics and added major successes in this area.

It is impossible not to mention mathematics of an exceptionally broad profile, the results of which relate to topology, the theory of differential equations, mathematical physics, geophysics, computational mathematics, electrodynamics - A. N. Tikhonov. In the first half of our degree, under the influence of the views of the substitute French mathematician Hadamard, the conviction was formed that physics and practice are only interested in so-called well-posed problems, the solutions of which, obtained with close initial data, cannot diverge too strongly from each other. Constant interest in the problems of practice convinced A. N. Tikhonov of the fallacy of this point of view. It turned out that many natural problems in physics, economics and other fields of knowledge are essentially incorrectly posed. Tikhonov developed an approach to solving this type of problem.
