Note that system (4) can be solved for any finite value of $N$. It is clear that as $N$ increases, the “difficulty” of the calculation increases. What if $N \to \infty$? Here we will carry out arguments that are valid specifically for the case $N \to \infty$.

Let's assume that the acceleration of the first hoop is $a_1 = \chi g$, (5)

where $\chi$ is a certain number that we need to determine. Let us now move on to the reporting system, moving with acceleration $a_1$. In this system, describing the movement of all hoops, starting from the second, is a task similar to our original one, with the only difference that the movement occurs in a gravitational field with

$$g' = g - a_1 = g(1 - \chi)$$

and the total number of hoops decreased by one. Since there are still a lot of hoops, it is natural to assume that the acceleration of the upper (second) hoop is equal to

$$a'_1 = \chi g'.$$

Therefore, in a stationary reporting system, the acceleration of the second hoop is equal to

$$a_2 = a'_1 + a_1 = \chi g' + \chi g = \chi(g' + g) = \chi(g(1 - \chi) + g) = g(2 - \chi).$$ (6)

Substituting expressions (5) and (6) for $a_1$ and $a_2$ into the first equation of system (4), we find $\chi$:

$$\chi^2 + \chi - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \chi = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{2}.$$

Thus, the acceleration of the first hoop for a sufficiently large $N$ is equal to

$$a_1 = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{2} g.$$ (7)

Note that already at $N = 3$ expression (7) is valid with an accuracy of 0.5%, and for $N = 10$ - with an accuracy of $10^{-6}$%.

F1065. A saucepan containing 1 liter of water cannot be brought to a boil using a heated power of 100 W. Determine how long it will take for the water to cool by one degree if you turn off the heated one.

This is a very simple task - from the condition it is clear that the power of the heater is equal to the power leaving the pan of water in the surrounding space (the temperature of the water does not change over time). This means that if the heated one is turned off, the power output is 100 W and the water cools by one degree during the time

$$\tau = \frac{cm \cdot \Delta T}{P} = 4,2 \cdot 10^3 \frac{\text{Дж}}{\text{кг} \cdot \text{К}} \cdot 1 \text{ кг} \cdot \frac{1\text{K}}{100\text{Вт}} = 42\text{с}.$$

A. R. Zilberman
