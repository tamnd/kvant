5. Positive numbers a, b, c, A, B, C satisfy the conditions: a+A=b+B=c+C=k. Prove that
$$aB+bC+cA < k^2.$$
D. Flaas
6.* In each cell of the square table 1987×1987 there is a number not exceeding modulo 1. In any 2×2 square of this table the sum of the numbers is 0. Prove that the sum of all numbers in the table does not exceed 1987.
A. Merkuryev
7. Vertex B of angle ABC lies outside the circle, and rays BA and BC intersect it. From the intersection point K of ray BA and the circle, a straight line is drawn perpendicular to the bisector of the angle, intersecting the circle at points K and P, and ray BC at point M. Prove that the segment PM is twice the length of the perpendicular drawn from the center of the circle to the bisector of angle ABC.
S. Duzhin

a) Find out which player has a winning strategy for p=10 and indicate it.
6) Find out which player has a winning strategy for p=1000.
D. Fomin
9th grade
5. Every evening, out of 33 heroes, Uncle Chernomor assigns 9 or 10 to duty at his discretion. After what is the smallest number of days it can turn out that each of the heroes went on duty the same number of times?
V. Iblev
6.* A non-empty set of nodes is marked on an integer lattice. In addition, a finite set of non-empty vectors with integer coordinates is given. It is known that if all given vectors are plotted from any displaced node, then among their ends there will be more displaced nodes than non-displaced ones. Prove that there are infinitely many displaced nodes.
D. Flaas
7. A convex p-gon (p≥5) is cut along all diagonals. Prove that among the resulting parts you will find parts of different areas.
A. Anjans

S. Konyazin

A. Klyushin
7. All faces of a convex polyhedron are triangles. Prove that each edge of this polyhedron can be colored red or blue so that one can get from any of its vertices to any other by looking only at the red edges, and also only at the blue ones.

XXI All-Union Olympiad in Physics
A. R. ZILVERMAN

This year, the final stage of the All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics was held in the city of Tallinn from April 15 to 21. The grand opening of the Olympiad took place on April 16, and the very next day the participants wrote the theoretical round. Here are the conditions of the theoretical problems (as is generally known, some of these problems were published in Zadannik «Kvant» in two previous issues of the journal):
8th grade
1. A small elastic ball is released from a height H = 1 m above the floor, and a hard plate is fixed on its path, from which it opens. What speed will the ball have when it hits the floor? How should the plate be positioned so that the ball hits the floor as far as possible from the starting point? What is this maximum distance?
2. Papa Carlo made a cap for Pinocchio from thin tin. The cap has the shape of a cone of height H=20 cm with an angle a=60° at the apex. Will this cap stay on Pinocchio’s head if his head is a smooth ball with a diameter of d=15 cm?

$$25,2^\circ - 26,4^\circ - 27,6^\circ - 28,7^\circ - 29,7^\circ - 30,6^\circ - 31,5^\circ - 32,3^\circ - 33,1^\circ.$$
