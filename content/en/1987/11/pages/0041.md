All our arguments are valid for the case of an ideal gas. What if the gas is not ideal? Then its molecules interact with each other, and the internal energy of the gas consists of the kinetic energy of the movement of the molecules and the potential energy of their interaction.

figure 3 shows the dependence of the potential energy $\Pi$ of the interaction of two molecules on the distance $r$ between them. Where the potential energy is minimal (point $r_0$), the substance condenses, i.e., turns into a liquid state. Since, according to the conditions, we have a gas at the initial moment, the average distance between the molecules corresponds to the point $r_1 \gg r_0$. After doubling the volume, the average distance between molecules becomes $r_2 = r_1 \sqrt[3]{2} > r_1$.
