Law of Gravity

There are people whose lives and activities serve as a real watershed in human history. The great English thinker Newton, who discovered the law of universal gravitation in 1667, was just such a person. Let's try to reproduce the corresponding reasoning of Newton.

First, a few words about what preceded the opening. Kepler, studying for a number of years the tables of planetary motion, entrusted to him by his teacher Tycho Brahe, at the beginning of the 17th century formulated three extraordinary complete and elegant laws (which are now called Kepler's laws):

1. All planets move around the Sun in an ellipse, with the Sun at one of the focuses.
2. The radius drawn from the Sun to the planet “sweeps” equal areas in equal periods of time.
3. The squares of the periods of two planets ($T_1$ and $T_2$) are related as the cubes of the semimajor axes of their orbits ($R_1$ and $R_2$): $T_1^2/T_2^2 = R_1^3/R_2^3$ (or, otherwise, $T_1^2/R_1^3 =$ const for all planets).

Already from these laws it is felt that the Sun plays some special role in “planetary life”. However, Kepler himself, despite painful attempts, could not fully understand this role.

Around the same time that Kepler discovered the laws of planetary motion, Galileo formulated the law of inertia, which states that if nothing acts on a body, it will move at a constant speed and in a straight line.

It was from this level that Newoton started. First of all, developing the ideas of Galileo, he asks the question: if it is internally possible for bodies to maintain their speed, then by what means can it change? Answering this question, he formulates a law known to any schoolchild today (Newoto’s second law).
