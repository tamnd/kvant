⟦folio 13⟧

S. I. Vavilov.

Academician Vladimir Iosifovich Veksler discovered the principle of autophasing of particles accelerated in cyclic accelerators, which bears his name. The work of all modern cyclic accelerators - synchrotrons, phasotrons and synchrophasotrons - is based on it. Somewhat later, independently of Wexler, the same principle was discovered by the American physicist MacMillan. For this discovery, both of them were awarded an international scientific prize.

Complex molecules of the organization of substances have spectra consisting of wide continuous spectral bands. Professor Eduard Vladimirovich Shpolsky showed that by dissolving organic substances in specially selected solvents and cooling the resulting solutions to very low temperatures, it is possible to obtain absorption spectra consisting of very fine spectral lines that are uniquely related to the structure and properties of the molecules. Thus the new field of molecular optics was created. This phenomenon is widely called the “Shpolsky effect” in the scientific literature.

Semiconductors, like other substances, can absorb light whose wavelengths are within certain limits. For light with longer wavelengths they are transparent. Thus, there is a limit to the wavelength at which the semiconductor stops absorbing light. Academician Leonid Veniaminovich Kelysh theoretically said that under the influence of an external electric field this boundary should mix towards longer wavelengths. A similar prediction was made by the German physicist Franz. Soon, Professor Viktor Sergeevich Vavilov received experimental confirmation of this phenomenon, now known as the “Kelysh-Franz effect.”

Academician Sergei Nikolaevich Vernov and the American physicist Van Allen, using scientific equipment installed on the first artificial satellites, independently discovered the existence of the Earth's radiation bands. It turned out that the Earth is surrounded by several layers with an increased density of particle pollution. These zones are called “Vernov-Van Allen radiation floors.”

Academicians Alexander Mikhailovich Prokhorov and Nikolai Gennadievich Basov developed the foundations of a new field of physics - quantum electronics and created the first quantum molecular generator using aminac molecules. For these outstanding works, in 1964 they, together with the American physicist Townes, were awarded the Nobel Prize.

Scientists around the world who study physical processes in hot plasma widely use installations called the unusual Russian word “TOKAMAK”. This is the abbreviated name for “toroidal chambers in a magnetic field” - Soviet thermonuclear installations developed and built at the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I.V. Kurchatov under the leadership of Academician Lev Andreevich Artsimovich.
