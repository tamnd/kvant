"All", "some" and negation

1. There is at least one white ball.
2. All balls are white.
3. All balls are red.
4. Some equilateral triangles are not rectilinear.
5. Some students in the class were not at the meeting.
6. Not a single girl was at the meeting.
7. Some of the angles of a given hexagon are right or acute.
8. For at least one $x$ of $A x^2$ does not exceed 4.
9. All people are not children.
10. For all $x$ out of $A x^2 - 2x + 1 \neq 0$.
11. Some men are no taller than 2 meters.
12. Some prime numbers are even.
13. For at least one prime number $p$ the number $2^p - 1$ is not prime.
14. Wrong. There may be one white ball and the rest are blue.
15. Right.
16. To be sure to get a white ball, you need to take out at least 20 balls. A smaller number may not be enough, or red and black balls may be taken out first - there are 19 of them. To ensure that you get balls of all three colors, you need to take out at least 22 balls; 21 can grab if all the white and black balls are drawn first; there are just 21 of them.
17. We need to call 31 people. Less is impossible - it can be assumed that only triangles and good ones are taken to the board (there are $10 + 20 = 30$) and each answer is no more than 4. If you call 31 people, then at least one of them turns out to be an excellent student and will answer 5.

XXI All-Union Olympiad in Mathematics

8th grade

1. Note. Take advantage of the fact that $x_1 + y_1 = x_2 + y_2 = ... = x_{10} + y_{10} = 9$, and $x_1 + x_2 + ... + x_{10} = y_1 + y_2 + ... + y_{10}$.
2. Answer: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Instruction. If $x_1 \leq x_2 \leq ... \leq x_6$ is the mass of weights, then each mass that can be balanced using these weights is written in the form $\varepsilon_1 x_1 + \varepsilon_2 x_2 + ... + \varepsilon_6 x_6$, where $\varepsilon_i = 0,1$ and not all $\varepsilon_i = 0$. There are only 63 such amounts. Therefore, they all must be different. It is clear that $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = 2$, $x_3 = 4$. Let it be proven that $x_k = 2^{k-1}$, then the most mass balanced with the help of weights $x_1, ..., x_{10}$ is equal to $x_1 + x_2 + ... + x_4 = 2^{k-1}$. Therefore $x_{k+1} = 2^k$.
3. Let $A_1 A_2 A_3 \dots A_7$ be the given heptagon, $B$ the intersection point of the lines $A_1 A_2$ and $A_3 A_4$, $A_1 A_2 = x$, $A_1 A_3 = y$, $A_1 A_4 = z$. Triangle $BA_3 A_4$ is equilateral to $(\angle A_1 BA_3 = \angle BA_3 A_4 = 3\pi/7)$, and triangles $A_2 BA_3$ and $A_1 BA_4$ are similar to $(A_2 A_3 \parallel A_1 A_4)$. From here we immediately get that $\frac{y - x}{y} = \frac{x}{z}$, or $\frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z}$.
