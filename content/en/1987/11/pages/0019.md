Absolute space, by its very essence, regardless of anything external, remains always the same and motionless.”
The clear introduction to absolute space and absolute time was associated with Newton’s general views on the structure of the world: he believed that there was a fixed center of the Universe and even proposed experiments to prove this assumption. At the same time, the use of these understandable concepts allowed, to a first approximation, to solve many problems in mechanics correctly and, importantly, simply. It is worth noting that during the first formulation of a consistent theory of the motion of bodies, such ideas about space and time were inevitable. Only two centuries later, as a result of the development of physics, scientists within the framework of the relative theory came to ideas about space and time that more fully reflect reality.
Next come “Axims or Laws of Motion.” Here's how Newton formulates them:

“LAW I. Every body continues to be maintained in its state of rest or uniform motion and motion until and unless it is able to change this state by applied forces.

LAW II. The change in momentum is proportional to the force applied to the movement and occurs in the direction of the straight line along which this force acts.

LAW III. An action always has an equal and opposite reaction; otherwise: the actions of two bodies on each other are equal and directed in opposite directions.”
This ends the introductory part of the “Beginnings” and begins the presentation of the main material. It is divided into three books (parts).

Book one, entitled “On the Motion of Bodies,” begins with a consideration of mathematical theorems, on the basis of which further exposition is built. Following the mathematical introduction, the solution of many problems related to the movement of bodies under the action of so-called central forces, that is, forces acting in a straight line connecting the body with a certain center, is applied.
