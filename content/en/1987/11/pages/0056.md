The practical procedure for holding the Olympiad has not changed compared to last year, so we will not describe in detail the work of the jury (you can read about this in the 11th issue of “Kvat” for last year).

On Sunday, April 19, the experimental tour took place. The participant was given problems prepared by the hosts of the Olympiad, Estonian scientists. I would like to note two of them that do not require complex equipment. They can be repeated not only in the physical office, but also at home.

The first one was done by eighth graders. They were given irregular polygons cut from a sheet of metal, and they had to determine the ratio of their masses without weights. You could use a ruler (wooden), paper and a pencil. Try it yourself. It is curious that the accuracy can be obtained very well - up to 1-2%.

The second task was offered to ninth graders. The problem of a long rope thrown over a block and folded into coils on both sides of the block on stands located at different heights is quite widely known. In this problem, it is required to determine the rate of establishment of “flowing” through the block. The experimenters were asked to determine not only this speed (and its dependence on the height difference), but also the minimum vertical length at which the movement would be established, and the installation time. If you do the work yourself, then take a thin white rope 5-10 meters long; a block is not necessary - a standard rod will replace it if the friction is not great. The most interesting part of this problem is to explain the larger discrepancy between the theoretically derived answer $v = \sqrt{gH}$ and the experimental results.
