The current is determined by the Lenz rule (“Physics 9”, § 92, 93).

Farad himself thought it was perfection of nature, that both options are described by the same law. However, a close analysis shows that such a system is far from very good. Let's try to understand this issue in more detail.

When a circuit moves in a magnetic field that does not change with time, the role of an external force generating a current in the circuit is played by the Lorentz force, which acts from the magnetic field on any moving charge. Let us recall an example known to you (“Physics 9”, § 95). Let a rectangular circuit be placed in a uniform magnetic field with induction $B$, the plane of which is perepredcular to the induction line (Fig. 1). On one side, the contour is a jumper with sliding contacts. If you move the jumper at a speed $v$, then an induced emf $E = Bvl$ will appear in it (l is the length of the jumper). Indeed, the free charge $q$ in the moving jumper is acted upon by the Lorentz force $F = qvB$ (the direction of the force is indicated by the arrow); correspond to this side of the EMF force

$$E = \frac{A}{q} = \frac{Fl}{q} = \frac{qvBl}{q} = Bvl$$

where $A$ is the work done on the side of the force along the length of the jumper. Let's compare this result with the rate of change of magnetic flux:

$$\left| \frac{\Phi}{l} \right| = \left| \frac{\Phi(BS)}{l} \right| = \left| \frac{\Phi(Bxl)}{l} \right| = Bvl$$

It is also easy to check that the sign of the resulting EMF corresponds to the Lenz rule.

The analyzed example shows that in the case of the movement of conductors in a constant magnetic field, the occurrence of an induction current does not represent a fundamentally new physical phenomenon.

Since the free charges in the conductor are initially stationary (of course, we do not take into account the chaotic thermal movement), the magnetic field does not act on them and therefore cannot cause their directional movement. Consequently, an induced current can only appear under the influence of an electric field. How does it arise and what properties does it have?

It is clear that in many ways this electric field differs from the known electrostatic field. For example, it creates an EMF in a closed loop, which means that its work in moving charges along a closed path is not zero. This is an output field, i.e. its drain lines look like closed lines. Etc.

We see that the analysis of situations, the occurrence of an error in the case of a stationary circuit in an alternating magnetic field, leads to a wide range of completely new physical phenomena, indicating a direct fact, not supported at that time by any experiments: the movement of an electric field in time always, in turn, generates a magnetic field. Having given this physical statement a mathematical symmetrical form (Maxwell's equations), he completed the construction of a unified theory of the electromagnetic field.
