ABOUT MATHEMATICS
COUNTRIES OF THE SOVIETS

Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR V. V. GNEDENKO

The Great October Socialist Revolution was a powerful boost to the progress of mathematics in our country, creating exceptionally favorable conditions for the development of its abstract directions and the application of mathematical methods in natural sciences and engineering, and the organization of production.

Thus, in 1918, a decree was issued on the organization of TsAGI - the Central Aerodynamic Institute and on the appointment of N. E. Zhukovsky as its director. In the same year, the Nizhny Novgorod Radio Laboratory was created.

In 1921, at the height of the collapse, the Physics and Mathematics Institute of the Academy of Sciences was organized on the initiative of V. A. Steklov. For the first time in the history of our country, a special scientific institution began to develop theoretical and applied mathematics.

Further, a brief description of what Soviet mathematicians have done over the past seventy years would require a multi-volume essay and the work of a large group of specialists. Therefore, here we are forced to limit ourselves to only a cursory review of a small proportion of important areas of scientific research.

Please, the emergence of the Moscow mathematical scale of function theory and set theory should be considered the most significant phenomenon in the mathematical life of our country. At its origins stood two outstanding mathematicians and teachers - D. F. Egorov and N. N. Luzin. Their names are associated with the widespread involvement of students in scientific research. Numerous results, beautiful constructions, the formulation of a large number of new problems for further research, a lively presentation - all this summed up to captivate Luzin’s mathematical youth. His first students were D. E. Menshov, P. S. Alexandrov and A. Ya. Khinchin. Their first independent works were in the circle of ideas of the teacher - the theory of integrals, the convergence of trionometric series, the structure of the so-called Borel sets. These first works largely determined the further interests of these studies. D. E. Menshov's main works were on linking trionometric series and series of orthogonal functions; A. Ya. Khinchin - metric theory of functions and its application in number theory, probability theory and statistical physics; P. S. Aleksandrov - set theory, and later - topology.

Already the first admissions of students to Moscow University after 1917, away from the second generation of active students of Luzin, Luzin organized a seminar at which each participant was asked to abstract the definition of monografico, then establish a discussion of the abstract with posing questions, highlighting the possibility of petition and generalization of evidence.

In the twenties, Luzin’s students called their team “Lusitania”*). This ascent was emphasized by his teacher, his scientific ideas, and lectures. Luzin's students were proud of their involvement in the group developing his ideas, and sought to give the maximum of what they were capable of in science.

Of course, every excessive hobby also carries a negative element. The Lusitanians were overly concerned with only one direction of mathematical development and some of them were somewhat disdainful of the classical field

This was reflected in the comic names of a number of disciplines: the theory of probability was called the theory of inconsistency, equations in particular arbitrary ones - equations with unfortunate arbitrary ones, finite differences - different limbs, etc. However, this underestimation of other areas of mathematics did not last long, since within Lusitanina herself a process of expanding interests began. P. S. Aleksandrov and P. S. Urson became interested in the problems of fusion-theoretical topology and soon laid the foundation for the existence of the Moscow topological school. A. Ya. Khinchin in his twenties began to be interested in the problems of metric number theory, from there he approached questions of probability theory, and by the end of the twenties he completely directed his energy to these two areas of research. Probability theory soon became one of the essential parts of the interests of the Moscow mathematical school. The creation of the Moscow School of Probability Theory is the merit of two outstanding scientists - A. Ya. Khinchin and A. N. Kolmogorov.

Further, the interests of Moscow mathematicians turned to classical analysis. At the same time, we found out that the ideas of function theory provide the opportunity for serious advancement. Qualitative theory of differential equations, partial differential equations become the object of research in Moscow. Among the representatives of this direction should be named V.V. Stepanov and I.G. Petrovsky.

One should not think that only Moscow was the center of mathematical thought of that time. First-class research at this time was carried out in Ukraine - in Kiev and Kharkov. In Kiev, one should note, first of all, the research on abstract algebra cultivated by D. A. Grave and his students. At the beginning of the revolution, one of his students O. Yu. Shmidt, a versatile scientist and excellent lecturer, began promoting new algebraic ideas in Moscow. O. Yu. Shmidt was the founder of the Moscow algebraic school. In Kharkov, S. N. Bernstein developed the ideas of P. L. Chebyshev in the field of best approximation of functions by polynomials and successfully worked in the field of probability theory.

Research in the field of mathematical physics and number theory successfully developed in Leningrad. Both of these areas of research were traditional for Leningraders and originate from L. Euler, M. V. Ostrogadsky and P. L. Chebyshev. In analytical number theory, notable results were obtained by I.M. Vinogradov. In 1934, I.M. Vinogradov managed to completely solve the famous Goldbach problem: every odd natural number can be represented as a sum of at most three prime numbers.

New universities, formed in a number of cities in the country in the first years of the revolution, quickly turned into significant mathematical centers. First of all, we must name here Tbilisi and Tashkent, which were famous for their research -

By the beginning of the Great Patriotic War, Soviet mathematics had gained enormous scientific prestige throughout the world. Her research covered practically all areas of mathematical thought. Many mathematicians took part in solving problems in natural science, technology and production organization.

The results of mathematicians were especially significant in connection with the problems raised by the development of aviation. It was precisely the problem of flight in a compressed fluid that prompted the construction of the theory of quasiconformal mappings by M. A. Lavrentiev. A major success on the eve of the Great Patriotic War was achieved in the construction of the mathematical theory of “shimmy” and “corkscrew”, which were the causes of the death of multi-chemical aircraft. From the mathematical theory constructed by M.V. Keldysh, it was possible to draw practical conclusions that made it possible to radically combat both the “shimmy” and the “corkscrew”. Later, M.V. Keldysh studied rocket technology and became the Chief Theoretician of Soviet cosmonautics.

The mathematical problem also became of great importance in connection with ensuring high quality of mass industrial products. Individual quality control of each manufactured product required a huge amount of control. It was impossible to do this, since there was already a catastrophic shortage of labor. It was at this time that the idea of ​​constructing a theory of statistical product quality control arose. A. N. Kolmogorov took an active part in its creation. Currently, this theory is undergoing vigorous development along two paths: 1) checking the quality of already manufactured batches (acceptance control) and 2) managing the quality of the product during the manufacturing process (technical or operational control).

The post-war years are characterized by a rapid process of mathematization of knowledge and practical activities. Mathematics was required for solving problems of organizing production and for studying biological processes, for carrying out in the field of physics and in creating technical systems, for studying economic issues and for the rational placement of industrial enterprises. But this is not enough. It turned out that mathematical methods assist archeologists and historians, linguists and physicians. New directions of mathematical thought appeared. Mathematical logic acquired incomparably greater importance than before the war. Immediately before the Great Patriotic War, L. V. Kantorovich considered a number of new problems for finding maximum and minimum values ​​related to the distribution of work, cutting materials, organizing transportation, etc. These problems served as the beginning of the creation of a new mathematical theory - linear programming, which received rapid development after the war. Now these first formulations of problems have received general formulations, solution methods have been proposed for them, an interesting theory has been built, and these results have been extended to more general cases (nonlinear programming).

A large area of ​​research, caused by the need for automatic control of technological processes, the movement of aircraft, spacecraft, etc., was developed by L. S. Pontgrin and his students. This direction is called the theory of optimal control. Theory and develop principles in it to immediately obtain numerous practical applications.

New tasks in the theory of random processes include such practical problems as managing product quality and determining when to set up equipment. These problems were brought to the attention of A. N. Kolmogorov, who directed the attention of a number of his students to them.

One of the greatest mathematicians of our time, N. N. Bogolyukov, having begun working under the guidance of Academician N. M. Krylov, published his first result at the age of seventeen. His research interests quickly expanded to include mathematical analysis, differential equations, expressions, expressed calculus, theories of functions, mathematical physics, and probability theory. Together with N. M. Krylov, he developed mathematical methods of nonlinear mechanics. After the Great Patriotic War, N. N. Bogolyubov switched almost exclusively to solving problems of modern physics and added major successes in this area.

It is impossible not to mention mathematics of an exceptionally broad profile, the results of which relate to topology, the theory of differential equations, mathematical physics, geophysics, computational mathematics, electrodynamics - A. N. Tikhonov. In the first half of our degree, under the influence of the views of the substitute French mathematician Hadamard, the conviction was formed that physics and practice are only interested in so-called well-posed problems, the solutions of which, obtained with close initial data, cannot diverge too strongly from each other. Constant interest in the problems of practice convinced A. N. Tikhonov of the fallacy of this point of view. It turned out that many natural problems in physics, economics and other fields of knowledge are essentially incorrectly posed. Tikhonov developed an approach to solving this type of problem.

Of the mathematicians of the younger generation, Muscovites S.P. Novikov, V.P. Maslov and Leningrader L.D. Faddeev received the most recognition. Serrey Petrovich Novikov, coming from a family of professional mathematicians (his father is academician P. S. Novikov, a logician and algebraist, his mother is L. V. Keldysh, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, topologist), very early received a number of outstanding results in one of the most important disciplines at that time - differential topology. These results were highly appreciated not only in our country, but also abroad: S. P. Novikov became the first Soviet winner of the Fields Prize, the most prestigious mathematical prize.

V.P. Maslov began as a physicist, but his first significant results were associated with the development of the idea of ​​modern mathematical analysis. His work is characterized, on the one hand, by the depth of purely mathematical generalizations, and, on the other, by its focus on applied issues, in particular, mathematical physics.

L. D. Faddeev, who also began as a physicist and also comes from a mathematical family (his father, D. K. Faddeev, is an algebraist, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences), is known for his functional work on mathematical physics. The international authority of his work is significant at present L. D. Faddeev is the President of the International Union of Mathematical Modern Theorems, but also in the study of the phenomena of the world around us. As a rule, it happens that natural phenomena and economic processes are more limited and the means of mathematics are available. This is an eternal incentive for the development of mathematics itself, its concepts and theory.
