XXI All-Union Olympiad in Physics
A. R. ZILVERMAN

This year, the final stage of the All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics was held in the city of Tallinn from April 15 to 21. The grand opening of the Olympiad took place on April 16, and the very next day the participants wrote the theoretical round. Here are the conditions of the theoretical problems (as is generally known, some of these problems were published in Zadannik «Kvant» in two previous issues of the journal):
8th grade
1. A small elastic ball is released from a height H = 1 m above the floor, and a hard plate is fixed on its path, from which it opens. What speed will the ball have when it hits the floor? How should the plate be positioned so that the ball hits the floor as far as possible from the starting point? What is this maximum distance?
2. Papa Carlo made a cap for Pinocchio from thin tin. The cap has the shape of a cone of height H=20 cm with an angle a=60° at the apex. Will this cap stay on Pinocchio’s head if his head is a smooth ball with a diameter of d=15 cm?
3. While performing laboratory work, the student lowered a boiler into a vessel with water, plugged it into the network and began recording the temperature every 3 minutes. The data from this experiment are as follows:
$$25,2^\circ - 26,4^\circ - 27,6^\circ - 28,7^\circ - 29,7^\circ - 30,6^\circ - 31,5^\circ - 32,3^\circ - 33,1^\circ.$$
Then he cooled the water by placing a small metal sample in the vessel, and again

Determine the heat of the sample from these data. Mains voltage $U=35$ V, current through the boiler $I=0,2$ A, room temperature $t=+20^\circ C$.

4. To study the solar battery, a multi-range voltmeter is used (it consists of a sensitive microammeter and a set of additional resistors). Connecting it to the battery at the 1 V limit, we get a reading of $U_1=0,7$ V. Switching the voltmeter at the 10 V limit, we get $U_2=2,6$ V. What would happen at the 100 V limit? It is known that, under constant illumination, a solar battery behaves like an ordinary source, to which a high resistance resistor is connected in series.

The practical procedure for holding the Olympiad has not changed compared to last year, so we will not describe in detail the work of the jury (you can read about this in the 11th issue of “Kvat” for last year).

On Sunday, April 19, the experimental tour took place. The participant was given problems prepared by the hosts of the Olympiad, Estonian scientists. I would like to note two of them that do not require complex equipment. They can be repeated not only in the physical office, but also at home.

The first one was done by eighth graders. They were given irregular polygons cut from a sheet of metal, and they had to determine the ratio of their masses without weights. You could use a ruler (wooden), paper and a pencil. Try it yourself. It is curious that the accuracy can be obtained very well - up to 1-2%.

The second task was offered to ninth graders. The problem of a long rope thrown over a block and folded into coils on both sides of the block on stands located at different heights is quite widely known. In this problem, it is required to determine the rate of establishment of “flowing” through the block. The experimenters were asked to determine not only this speed (and its dependence on the height difference), but also the minimum vertical length at which the movement would be established, and the installation time. If you do the work yourself, then take a thin white rope 5-10 meters long; a block is not necessary - a standard rod will replace it if the friction is not great. The most interesting part of this problem is to explain the larger discrepancy between the theoretically derived answer $v = \sqrt{gH}$ and the experimental results.
