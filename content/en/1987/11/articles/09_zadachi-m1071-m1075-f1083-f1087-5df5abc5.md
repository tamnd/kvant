Tasks

M1071—M1075, F1083—F1087

M1071. A field for playing “numbers” is drawn on the board: (((((((((__*_)*_)*_)*_)*_)*_)*_)*_)*_)*

Two players take turns. The first player, with the initial move, writes down some number in place of the first (self-made) space (___). Each further move consists of writing a number in place of the next space and replacing the asterisk (*) on the left with an addition or multiplication sign; however, no number should appear twice. At the end of the game, the value of the resulting expression is calculated. If it is an even number, then the first player wins, if it is an odd number, the second player wins. Who wins the seasoned game?

S. A. Genkin

M1072. Factor the number 989·1001·1007+320 into prime factors.

S. V. Fomin

M1073. In the hexagon $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5A_6$ there is a point O, from which all sides are visible at an angle of 60°. Prove that if $OA_1 > OA_3 > OA_5$ and $OA_2 > OA_4 > OA_6$ then $A_1A_2 + A_3A_4 + A_5A_6 < A_2A_3 + A_4A_5 + A_6A_1$.

A. S. Merkuryev

M1074. Given a stack of 2n+1 cards, the following two operations are allowed:
(A) remove some cards from the top and place them down, maintaining order;
(B) the top n cards are laid out in the n spaces between the bottom n+1 cards, maintaining the order.
Prove that using the indicated operations it is impossible to obtain more than 2n(2n+1) different arrangements of cards from the initial arrangement of cards in the stack.

D. V. Fomin

M1075. Find the largest natural number, the tenth of which each digit (except for the outer ones) is strictly less than the two digits adjacent to it.

S. E. Rukshin

F1083. A homogeneous brick $ABCDA'B'C'D'$ (Fig. 1) has the following property: if you cut it with an arbitrary plane passing through the edge $AA'$, then each of the two parts of the brick, placed on a new face $AEE'A'$ (Fig. 2), does not fall. Find correspondences between brick sizes at which this is possible.

S. V. Shlosman

F1084. If you press a nickel against frozen glass for a short time, a circle will thaw on the glass along the edge of the nickel. Why?

L. A. Ashkinzi

F1085. figure 3 depicts a process performed on an ideal gas and transferring it from state A to state B. Find on the graph the sections of the process where the temperature of the gas increases (decreases).

I. Yu. Poteryayko
