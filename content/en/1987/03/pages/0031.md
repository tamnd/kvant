F1027. Light source at a distance of $l = 1$ m from the screen. A hole with a diameter of $d = 1$ cm is made in the screen of the source. How will the flux of light through the hole change if a strong cylinder made of materials with a refractive index of $n = 1,5$ is placed between the screen and the source, as shown in figure 1 (cylinder length $l = 1$ m, diameter $d = 1$ cm; the source on the base will pass through the hole in the screen.

Indeed, the limiting ray falling from the air onto the end of the cylinder at an angle $\pi/2$, after refraction, will form an angle of total internal reflection $\alpha$ with the axis of the cylinder such that $\sin \alpha = 1/n$; this ray hits the side surface of the cylinder at an angle $\varphi = \pi/2 - \alpha$ (Fig. 2). Because

$$\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{2}{3} < \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sin \frac{\pi}{4},$$

then $\alpha < \frac{\pi}{4}$ and therefore $\varphi > \frac{\pi}{4} > \alpha$. This means that when falling on the side surface of the cylinder, this ray will be reflected. Subsequently, the beam will go inside the cylinder, reflecting each time from its surface, and ultimately hits its far end.

Any other ray from the source incident on the input end of the cylinder at an angle less than $\pi/2$, when refracted, will make an angle $\alpha' < \alpha$ with the cylinder axis; This ray hits the side surface of the cylinder at an angle $\varphi' > \varphi > \alpha$ and is completely reflected.

Thus, the cylinder will “collect” into the hole the beam emitted by the source into a solid angle of 2n steradians. In the absence of a cylinder, a luminous flux concentrated inside the solid angle $\pi d^2/4l^2$ steradians enters the hole. Therefore, if there is a cylinder, the luminous flux through the hole will increase by

$$n = \frac{2\pi}{\pi d^2/4l^2} = \frac{8\pi l^2}{\pi d^2} = 8 \cdot 10^4 \text{ раз.}$$

A. I. Butkov
