ACOUSTICS IN THE OCEAN

Academician L. M. BREKHOVSKIKH,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V. M. KURTEPOV

A source of food resources, a class of minerals, the “kitchen” of the weather, the main road of the Earth... These words can generally characterize the role of the World Ocean in our lives. And every year it becomes clearer and clearer how important this role is, how enormous the influence of the Ocean is on all spheres of human activity, how important research work in the Ocean and the development of technical means for the development of its resources are.

Man is keenly interested in knowing as much as possible about the state of the Ocean, the movement of its waters, and the structure of the ocean day. This is necessary for surface and underwater navigation, to determine the area with the most biological productivity, to take into account the influence of the Ocean on the weather and climate of our planet, and for many other purposes.

Numerous research ships from different countries are constantly in the Ocean and in the seas attached to the continents. They record the state of water masses at different depths, the state of the atmosphere over the Ocean, and study the characteristics of the day. The results of the research flow into World Data Centers, the main of which are located in Moscow and Washington. This information is very valuable. However, in terms of its volume, it is too small to fully describe the current state of the entire World Ocean and the seas adjacent to it. For this purpose, the flow of information must be increased thousands of times. This is where the means of exploring the Ocean from space come to the rescue. Instruments installed on artificial Earth satellites and space stations quickly and immediately “collect” data on many characteristics of the Ocean from a large area. Measuring your own study of the Ocean in the information or centimeter range allows you to record its surface. By scattering electromagnetic waves in the centimeter range on the surface of the Ocean, one can determine the characteristic

Sticks of surface waves, surface current speed and surface wind. Based on the travel time of the electromagnetic pulse from the space station to the surface of the Ocean and back, it is possible to determine changes in the Ocean level over large distances. This is how a “trough” was first discovered on the surface of the Ocean, about 23 meters deep above the Puerto Rican platinum on the ocean floor.

However, all this data obtained from space refers to the surface of the Ocean. And it’s clear why - electromagnetic waves cannot penetrate deep into sea water to any significant extent. In the water column, the light fades at a distance of several tens of meters; Even for the most powerful laser beam, the limit is several hundred meters.

Aktika provides a tool for “enlightening” the depths of the Ocean. Only sound waves can travel a considerable distance in water. Thus, the sound of a relatively small research explosion in marine experiments was recorded at a distance of 22,000 kilometers.

Here we need to turn to discoveries that were discovered forty years ago. In 1946, Soviet scientists discovered a very interesting phenomenon in the Sea of ​​Japan. The sounds of explosions (anti-submarine bombs were detonated at a depth of 100 meters) spread without noticeable attenuation over very long distances - many hundreds of kilometers. Detailed processing and analysis of experimental materials showed that this occurs due to the free dependence of the speed of sound in the Ocean on depth. The speed of sound in sea water, generally speaking, changes with changes in temperature, salinity and hydrostatic pressure.
