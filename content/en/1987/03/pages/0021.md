⟦folio 19⟧

Tasks

M1031—M1035; F1043—F1047

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than May 15, 1987 to the address: 103006 Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». In the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics or “... a new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you are a teacher. Please write your first and last name in block letters.

M1031. On the data plane there is a straight line l and two points A and B on one side of it. On straight line l, point M is selected, the sum of the distances from which to points A and B is the smallest, and point N, for which the distances from A and B are equal: AN=BN. Prove that the point of the inverses of all the numbers written out is equal to n/2.
(For example, when n=3 $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 4} + \frac{1}{3 \cdot 4} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4} = \frac{3}{2}$).

A. A. Anjans

M1032. N numbers 2, 3, ..., n+1 are written down, their possible products are two, three, and so on until the product of all n of these numbers is completed. Prove that the sum of the inverses of all the numbers written down is equal to n/2.
(For example, when n=3 $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 4} + \frac{1}{3 \cdot 4} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4} = \frac{3}{2}$).

A. A. Anjans

M1033. The circle cuts four linear triangles from the square (the border of each consists of two circles and two segments). Let's paint two of them, adjacent to the opposite corners of the square, blue, the other two red. Prove that a) the sums of red and blue are equal;
b) the sums of the perimeters of the red and blue triangles are equal.

B. B. Prozovolyov

M1034. A rectangular piece of chocolate is divided into 50 square slices by longitudinal and transverse grooves. Two people play this game. The beginner washes the chocolate along a certain indentation into two straight indented parts. Then the players take turns breaking one of the resulting parts into two along a certain indentation. The one who first breaks off a square piece (without indentations)
a) loses;
b) wins.

Which of the players can secure a win for themselves - the beginner or his partner?

S. B. Fomin

M1035. On the segment [0; 1] points x0, x1, x2, ..., xn are displaced in turn. For each point k (k=1, ..., n), the distance dk from it to the closest point to it from the previously set points changes. Prove that d1+d2+...+dn≤1+\frac{\log_2 n}{2}.

B. S. Grieberg

F1043. On a smooth horizontal surface, rotating, a stick of length l=10 cm glides at a speed v=10 cm/s. At what angular velocity will the rotating stick hit the wall (see figure) flat, if at a distance L=50 cm from the wall the stick was parallel to the wall?

N. Yu. Poteryayko
