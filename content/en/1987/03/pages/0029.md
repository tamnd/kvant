We present the circuit diagram of this circuit in equivalent form (Fig. 2). We will consider the currents in the coils to be positive when their directions correspond to circling the external circuit counterclockwise. The processes in such a circuit can be easily analyzed by analogy, considering the mechanical system shown in figure 3: identical carts of mass m (analogous to a coil) are connected by a spring with stiffness k (analogous to a capacitor). Since the current through the spring of the benefit coil can only flow in the positive direction, the wheels of the second cart must be equipped with a stable one, allowing it to move freely only to the right. In this system, the cart velocities v₁(t) and v₂(t) will be analogues of the currents I₁(t) and I₂(t), and the spring deformation x(t) will be an analogue of the capacitor charge Q(t).

The spring first grows by x₀.

At time t=0, the left cart is released without an initial speed (when the key is closed, the capacitor begins to discharge through the left coil). Until the deformation of the spring goes to zero, the right cart is motionless (v₂=0), or the spring pulls it to the left, where it cannot roll (until the capacitor charge goes to zero, so the coil cannot flow through the spring: I₂=0). This first phase of movement from t=0 to moment t₁ corresponds to a quarter of the period of harmonic oscillations of a cart of mass m on a spring of stiffness k: t₁=T₁/4=
= 2π/4 √m/k (for the circuit t₁=T₁/4=2π/4 √LC). By time t₁, the left cart accelerates to speed v₁, which starts from the law of conservation of energy:
$$\frac{kx_0^2}{2} = \frac{mv_1^2}{2} \Rightarrow v_1 = x_0 \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$
(similar to the chain I₁=Q₀/√LC). The spring deformation x(t) (capacitor charge Q(t)) decreases during this time according to the cosine law from the initial maximum value x₀(Q₀) to zero.

Then the spring begins to compress and only the second trolley to the right, i.e., starting from moment t₁, the wheels of the right trolley are released (the charge of the lower plate of the capacitor becomes positive, the diode passes current through the coil spring). Subsequently, the center of mass of the carts moves to the right with a speed v₁/2, and this speed is constant, since at t>t₁ no external forces act on the system. This uniform motion is superimposed on the oscillations of the carts relative to the center of mass, passing in anti-phase with a frequency ω = √2k/m (the stiffness of the “half” of the spring is 2k). Since at t=t₁, when the spring is not deformed, the carts move relative to the center of mass towards each other at a speed v₁/2 = x₀/2 √k/m, then this will be the amplitude of the speed oscillations. The amplitude x₁ of spring deformation oscillations can be found from the law of conservation of energy, equating the kinetic energy of the bogies (in the center of mass system) at the moment t₁ to the potential energy of the spring at the moment of its maximum deformation:
$$\frac{2m(v_1/2)^2}{2} = \frac{kx_0^2}{4} = \frac{kx_1^2}{2} \Rightarrow x_1 = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt{2}}.$$
