M1013. Two points M and N are taken on sides AB and BC of triangle ABC. Three parallel lines passing through points M, B and N intersect the base AC at points K, D and L. Prove that the area of ​​a trapezoid (or parallelogram) is not greater than the area of ​​one of the triangles ABD and DBC.

M1014. Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be different pairwise mutually prime natural numbers. Prove that there are infinitely many natural b such that the numbers $b+a_1, b+a_2, \dots, b+a_n$ are also pairwise coprime.

Chinese remainder theorem
If the natural numbers $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m$ are mutually prime, then for any set of natural numbers $r_1, \dots, r_m, 0 \leq r_i < p_i (i=1, \dots, m)$ there is a number b that when divided by $p_i$ gives a remainder $r_i$ for all i.

Let's denote it by $\angle ADB = a, AD = a, BD = b, CD = c, AK = AD = k, CL = CD = l$. then MK: BD=k, NL: BD=l, i.e. MK=kb, NL=lb, and KL=KD+DL=(1-k)a+(1-l)c, and since the distance between the lines MK and NL is equal to KL sin $\alpha$, the area of ​​the quadrilateral KMNL is $\frac{1}{2} (MK+NL) KL \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2} (kb+lb) ((1-k)a + (1-l)c) \sin \alpha$.

Let $a \geq c$ be used to define, then the last expression does not exceed $\frac{1}{2} b (k+l)(2-(k+l)) a \sin \alpha \leq \frac{1}{2} ab \sin \alpha$ (since $x(2-x) \leq 1$ for all x), i.e., the area of ​​triangle ADB.

It is interesting to find out for which sets $A = (a_1, \dots, a_n)$ of different natural numbers the statement of the problem holds, i.e. the sums $a_1+b, \dots, a_n+b$ are pairwise mutually prime for at least one integer b (and therefore for an infinite set of numbers b). If, for example, the set $A$ contains two quadrilaterals, such a set does not suit us. In general, if among the remainders from the division of numbers $a_i$ by some prime $q$ each of the possible remainders 0,1, ..., $q-1$ occurs at least twice, then for any “shift” at least two numbers will be divisible by $q$. It follows that the set $A$ must satisfy the following conditions:

among the remainders from the division of numbers from the set $A$ by each prime $p$ at least one remainder $r_p, 0 \leq r_p < p$ must occur no more than once.

Let $D$ be the difference between the largest and most numbers in the set $A$. For each prime $p \leq D$ we find the remainder $r_p < p$ which appears at most once when dividing numbers from $A$ and $p$. Let us now select the number $b$, which, when divided by each prime $p \leq D$, gives an additional remainder to $r_p$ $r_p^* (r_p^* = p-r_p$ for $r_p > 0$ and $r_p^* = 0$ for $r_p = 0$). Then the numbers $a_1+b, \dots, a_n+b$ will be pairwise mutually prime, since each prime $p$ can be divided by at most one of them: for $p \leq D$ this is ensured by choosing $b$, and for $p > D$ it is done automatically, since all the remainders from dividing the numbers $a_1, \dots, a_n$ by $p$ will be different.

N. V. Vasiliev, V. F. Lev
