Physics
Written exam
Faculty of Physics
Each option consisted of five problems of three types.
The first three tasks are calculated, of varying difficulty: from almost standard to relatively complex, requiring ingenuity, deep knowledge, and the ability to understand an unusual or conventional physical situation.
The fourth task is an assessment task. To solve it, you need to understand the physical phenomenon under consideration, formulate a simple (since only an estimate is needed) physical model of this phenomenon, select reasonable numerical values ​​of physical quantities and, finally, obtain a numerical result that more or less corresponds to reality. In the text of the problem I assume that the applicant can himself choose the quantities necessary to solve the problem and their numerical values.
The fifth task is a demonstration task, in which you need to explain a physical phenomenon demonstrated in the classroom. Here it is important to understand the existing phenomenon and the environment of various factors to highlight the main one.
Five hours were given to solve the problem, starting from the completion of the demonstration.
Below, after the text of each problem, the average percentage of those who solved it is indicated in brackets.

Option 1
1. Find the lift of a balloon filled with gel. The mass of the gel is $m$. The pressures and temperatures inside and outside the balloon shell are the same. Molar mass of helium $M_r$, impact $M_b$, gravitational acceleration $g$. Shell mass claim (80%)
2. A Christmas tree decoration - a thin-walled ball - is broken when dropped onto a stone floor from a minimum height of $h$ (Fig. 1). At what minimum speed must the ball hit the same balls that are at rest before the collision for the balls to break? (49%)
3. Find images of the exact source $S$ produced by a system of a lens with focal length $F$ and a final mirror with an apex angle of 90° (Fig. 2). The axis of the cone coincides with the axis of the lens. The distance between the top of the cone and the lens is 2F, and between the source and the lens is 3F/2. (42%)
4. Estimate how much power is developed in LOC at the finish line. (51%)
5. The flask with boiling water is removed from the stove - the boiling stops. The flask is tightly boiled with a rubber stopper with a massive copper rod threaded through it - the water boils. Explain the phenomenon. (65%)

Option 2
1. Two balls of the same radius $R$, but one is made of aluminum and the other is wooden, held by a long thread, slowly sink in water, moving at a constant speed (Fig. 3). Find the force of water resistance acting on each of the balls. Density of aluminum $\rho_1$, wood $\rho_2$, water $\rho_0$. Gravity acceleration $g$. (61%)
2. A test tube of length $l$ containing air and saturated water vapor touches the open end of the water surface (Fig. 4). The test tube is immersed halfway in water, and the surface of the water in the test tube is at a depth of $a$. Find the saturated water vapor pressure. The temperature is constant, atmospheric pressure $p$, water density $\rho$, gravitational acceleration $g$. (55%)
3. Three identical flat metal plates $A$, $B$ and $C$ of area $S$ each are located parallel to each other at distances $d_1$ and $d_2$ (Fig. 5). The plates are insulated; on plates $B$ and $C$ there are zarars $+q$ and $-q$, and plate $A$ is not zararyn. Then the plates $A$ and $C$ are connected through the istor, the key $K$ is closed. Find the amount of heat generated in the resistor. (46%)
4. Estimate at what distance the railroad tracks appear to merge. (65%)
5. The conductive wheel can rotate freely around its vertical axis. A constant potential difference is created between the center of the wheel and its rim. If you put a magnet near the wheel, it starts to turn. When sweeping the shelf, the magnetic direction of rotation changes to the opposite. Explain the phenomenon. (52%)

Option 3
1. Along the main optical axis of a collecting lens with a focal length of $F=5$ cm, two fireflies, located on opposite sides of the lens, move towards each other. The speed of fireflies is the same and equal to $v=2$ cm/s. How long will it take for the image of the second person to cross the light, if at the initial moment they were at distances of $l_1=20$ cm and $l_2=15$ cm from the lens? (70%)
2. The two sides of a regular triangle are formed by identical, uniformly charged rods. In this case, at the center $O$ of the triangle, the potential is equal to $\varphi_0$, and the strength of the electric field is equal to $\vec{E}_0$. Find the potential and the pattern and direction of the direction vector that will be at point $O$ if one of the sticks is removed. (63%)
3. Two identical elastic balls $A$ and $B$ move towards each other at speeds $v$ and $2v$, and the straight lines passing through the centers of each ball in the direction of their movement touch the other ball (Fig. 6). Find at what angle to the original direction the ball $A$ will move after the collision. (38%)
