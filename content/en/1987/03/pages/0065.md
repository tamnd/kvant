The wavelength $\lambda$ at which the circuit is tuned is related to the natural frequency of oscillations of the circuit $\omega = 1 / \sqrt{LC}$ by correspondence

$$\lambda = \frac{2 \pi v}{\omega} = 2 \pi v \sqrt{LC},$$

where $v = 3 \cdot 10^8 \text{ m/c}$ is the speed of light in vacuum. Using the last formula, you can find the values ​​of inductance $L_1$ and $L_2$, necessary for tuning the circuit to wavelengths $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, as well as the change in inductance $\Delta L$, necessary for the rearrangement:

$$\Delta L = L_2 - L_1 = \frac{\lambda_2^2 - \lambda_1^2}{4 \pi^2 v^2 C} = \frac{d(\lambda_2^2 - \lambda_1^2)}{8 \pi^2 \varepsilon_0 v^2 S} = 1,4 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ Гн.}$$

10. Applying the lens formula

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F},$$

where $d$ is the distance from the source to the lens, $f$ is the distance from the lens to the image and $F$ is the focal length of the lens, and taking into account that $d = f - h$, we find: $F = h / 2$. A mini image of the ball will exist while the ball is between the focus and the lens. This time $\Delta t$ can be found as the difference between the time the ball falls to the lens $t_1 = \sqrt{2 h / g}$ and to the focus $t_2 = \sqrt{2 (h - F) / g}$:

$$\Delta t = t_1 - t_2 = \sqrt{\frac{h}{g}} (\sqrt{2} - 1) = 0,1 \text{ с.}$$
