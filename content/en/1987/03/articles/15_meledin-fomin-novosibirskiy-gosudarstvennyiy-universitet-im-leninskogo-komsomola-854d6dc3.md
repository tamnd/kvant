If $0 < b \leq 1$, then the second inequality will be satisfied for any $y$. If $b > 1$, then $b^2 - 1 > 0$, and then the inequality can be reduced to the form $y \leq b/(b^2 - 1)$. Thus, the resulting system of inequalities breaks down into two:

$$\begin{cases} y \geq b^3 - b, \\ 0 < b \leq 1 \end{cases} \quad \begin{cases} y \geq b^3 - b, \\ y \leq b/(b^2 - 1), \\ b > 1. \end{cases}$$

The first of the two systems describes the part of the area shaded by figure 6, located below the straight line $b = 1$, and the second describes the remaining part of the same area. The answer is justified.

The presented solution method allows you to be sure: if you can solve the equation corresponding to the inequality, then solving the inequality will not cause any difficulties.

Faculty of Physics
Each option consisted of five problems of three types.
The first three tasks are calculated, of varying difficulty: from almost standard to relatively complex, requiring ingenuity, deep knowledge, and the ability to understand an unusual or conventional physical situation.
The fourth task is an assessment task. To solve it, you need to understand the physical phenomenon under consideration, formulate a simple (since only an estimate is needed) physical model of this phenomenon, select reasonable numerical values ​​of physical quantities and, finally, obtain a numerical result that more or less corresponds to reality. In the text of the problem I assume that the applicant can himself choose the quantities necessary to solve the problem and their numerical values.
The fifth task is a demonstration task, in which you need to explain a physical phenomenon demonstrated in the classroom. Here it is important to understand the existing phenomenon and the environment of various factors to highlight the main one.
Five hours were given to solve the problem, starting from the completion of the demonstration.
