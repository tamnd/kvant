F1024. Evaluate the change in pressure in the steam room after water from a ladle is splashed onto the hot stones. It is assumed that you, having a good understanding of the phenomenon, can set the necessary quantities yourself, choose their numerical values ​​correctly enough and get a numerical answer.

After the evaporation of the water, which was splashed from the ladle onto the hot stones, the temperature in the room increases slightly (note that it differs at the top near the ceiling and at the bottom near the floor). The resulting steam spreads throughout the room, partially condensing on cold areas of the walls, ceiling and windows. Humidity changes, the heat transfer process changes; steam and air flows lead to different values ​​of physical parameters in different places in the steam room.

To estimate the change in pressure that created the additional portion of steam, a rough but fairly reasonable physical model will be used.

First of all, we assume that the doors and windows are tightly closed (otherwise it would not make sense to talk about changes in pressure: the pressure inside and outside would remain constant). We will assume that the parameters have established themselves (become constant) and that the temperature of the steam throughout the room is the same and equal to $T \approx 350$ K. (We will neglect the change in temperature in the room in this assessment.) We will consider water vapor to be an ideal gas, the state of which is described by the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation.

Putting the volume of the steam room $V \sim 100$ m³, the mass of water in the ladle and, accordingly, the mass of the formed water vapor $m \sim 1$ kg and taking into account that the molar mass of steam (water) $\mu = 18 \cdot 10^{-3}$ kg/mol, and the gas constant $R = 8,3$ J/(K mol), from the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation we can find the pressure $\Delta p$ of water steam generated by pressure from a ladle of water:

$$\Delta p \cdot V = \frac{m}{\mu} RT \Rightarrow \Delta p \sim \frac{mRT}{\mu V} \approx 1,6 \cdot 10^3 \text{ Pa} \approx 0,02 \text{ atm}.$$

Considering that the pressure in the steam room is usually $p \sim 1$ atm, we come to the conclusion that the change in pressure after steam supply is not large - only a few percent of normal pressure.

Note that taking into account the change in pressure caused by additional heating of the air in the steam room, according to Charles’s law, would lead to the correspondence $\Delta p/p \sim \Delta T/T$. For this additional pressure equation to be comparable to the estimate above ($\Delta p/p \sim 2\%$), at $T \sim 350$ K the temperature increase must be $\Delta T \sim 10$ K.

G. V. Meledin
