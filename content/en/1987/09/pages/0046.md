The first example (very simple) is a body on a spring (Fig. 1). The role of potential energy in this case is played by the energy of the deformation spring (k is the spring stiffness). From the general statement we obtain

$$W = \frac{k}{2} \Delta x^2 + \frac{m}{2} \left( \frac{\Delta x}{\Lambda t} \right)^2, \quad \omega^2 = \frac{k}{m},$$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}.$$

Similarly, the frequencies of any vibration springs are found: molecules in a crystal, the Ostankino TV tower or a place across the bay in San Francisco, etc.

The second example (a little more complicated) is a mathematical matrix (Fig. 2). When writing the potential energy of a material point in a gravitational field, it is necessary to take into account that in the expression $W_p = mgh$ the values ​​are $h = \Delta x^2/2l$ (this follows from the similarity of triangles ABD and ABC on figure 2). Then from the general statement it follows:

$$W = \frac{mg}{2l} \Delta x^2 + \frac{m}{2} \left( \frac{\Delta x}{\Lambda t} \right)^2, \quad \omega^2 = \frac{g}{l},$$

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}.$$

The vibration frequencies of a ball in a hole, a ship on water, etc. are found similarly.

Example three (electrical) - an oscillatory circuit (Fig. 3). At equilibrium, there is neither charge on the capacitor (C) nor current through the coils (L). When deviating from equilibrium, a charge $\Delta Q$ appears on the capacitor, and current $I = \Delta Q/\Delta t$ flows through the coils. The role of potential energy is played by the energy of a charged capacitor: $W_p = (\Delta Q)^2/(2C)$ (after all, it is precisely this energy that is associated with the mutual arrangement of charges). The role of kinetic energy is taken by the energy stored in the coils: $W_k = LI^2/2 = (L/2)(\Delta Q/\Delta t)^2$ (it is associated with current, i.e., with the movement of charges; $I = \Delta Q/\Delta t$ plays the role of speed). So,

$$W = \frac{1}{2C} (\Delta Q)^2 + \frac{L}{2} \left( \frac{\Delta Q}{\Lambda t} \right)^2,$$

$$\omega^2 = \frac{1}{LC},$$

Determining oscillation frequencies by type of energy turns out to be convenient in any other case.

In conclusion, I would like to say that oscillations are not one of the types of movement in a series of polygons, but represent a fundamental phenomenon, an element of that shadowy “alphabet” with the help of which nature creates everything that surrounds us.

E. E. Gorodetsky
