The next most important achievement of IPP scientists is the discovery of the intermediate state of superconductors. This is the name given to the special state of a superconducting sample in a magnetic field when it “breaks up” into normal and superconducting regions. This was followed by a series of replacement discoveries in the field of superconductivity. In the fifties, Lev Davidovich Landdu, who worked at the IPP, together with V.L. Ginzburg, created a phenomenological theory of superconductivity; A. A. Abrikovov discovers superconductors of the second type, which became the basis for all practical applications of this amazing phenomenon.

Then, after a series of studies of supertectics and superconductivity, a sudden decline occurred in this area. However, at present, due to a number of rapid discoveries in recent years, these studies have intensified again.

Another important area with which great achievements of the FPI are associated is the study of the energy spectra of electrons in metals. The most sophisticated techniques were developed for the production of extremely pure single-crystal samples in which the electron “ran” without so much distance of millimeters! In ordinary, “dirty” images at room temperature, this range does not exceed several meter distances. The institute's scientists developed many original methods for studying the behavior of electrons in metals, which made it possible to open a completely new and exciting field of low-temperature physics.

Interesting results were also obtained when studying the magnetic properties of solids at low temperatures. For example, a new physical phenomenon was discovered - pyzomagnetism - the occurrence in some substances of magnetization under pressure.

It should be noted that the last thirty-five years of scientific activity of Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsya himself were not associated with low temperature physics. He was engaged in high-power electrical wiring, so the record-breaking power microwave generator - nitron - studied plasma discharge and other physical phenomena related to plasma. And now the Institute has a laboratory that, under the leadership of S.P. Kapitsya, deals with electrical wiring and accelerator technology. Speaking about P. L. Kapitsa, one cannot fail to mention his engineering activities. All installations in the world for producing oxygen by cryogenic separation of air, all machines for burning gel work on the principles proposed by Pyotr Leonidovich.*)

The active collaboration between Kapitsya and Landdu led to a number of advanced discoveries in low temperature physics. Subsequently, a whole “Landdu school” was formed at the Institute, from which much of the best theoretical physics of our country was embroidered. In the sixties, the USSR Academy of Sciences organized the Institute of Theoretical Physics (now named after L.D. Landdu), and some of Landdu’s students and employees went to work. With this institute today we maintain the theory.
