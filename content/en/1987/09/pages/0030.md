F1057. A dumbbell consisting of two balls of mass m, fixed at the ends of a thin thread of length 2b, is suspended in a horizontal position on two non-stretchable threads of length l, the distance between which is 2a (Fig. 1). The period of small torsional vibrations of the dumbbell begins.

After closing the key K₁, a current appears in the coil L₁, which increases over time according to a linear law

$$I_1 = \frac{U_0}{L_1} t.$$

The same thing will happen in the L₂ coil after the K₂ key is closed. At the moment of opening the key K₃ (Fig. 2)

$$I_1 = \frac{U_0}{L_1} (t_1 + t_2) = 3 \text{ A},$$

$$I_2 = \frac{U_0}{L_2} t_2 = 4 \text{ A},$$

and the charge on the capacitor is zero.

At the moment when the charge of the capacitor becomes maximum (it will remain so in the future - the diode does not allow the capacitor to discharge), the same current I will flow through both coils, not equal to zero (Fig. 3). The circuit, consisting of two coils and two batteries, is superconducting, and the batteries cancel each other out, so the magnetic flux, an arbitrary circuit, does not change over time:

$$L_1 I_1 - L_2 I_2 = L_1 I + L_2 I.$$

From here we get the answer to the second question of the problem:

$$I = \frac{L_1 I_1 - L_2 I_2}{L_1 + L_2} = \frac{2}{3} \text{ A}.$$

Starting from the moment the key K₃ is opened and until the maximum charge qₘ is established on the capacitor, the batteries have done work

$$A = q_m U_0.$$

Then, having written the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{L_1 I_1^2}{2} + \frac{L_2 I_2^2}{2} + A = \frac{(L_1 + L_2) I^2}{2} + \frac{q_m^2}{2C},$$

or

$$q_m^2 - 2CU_0 q_m - C (L_1 I_1^2 + L_2 I_2^2 - (L_1 + L_2) I^2) = 0,$$

we find the answer to the first question:

$$q_m \approx 1,3 \cdot 10^{-2} \text{ KJ}, \ U_m = q_m / C \approx 1300 \text{ B}.$$

The solution may become more obvious if you notice that two batteries can be replaced with one (that is, the positive terminals of the batteries are combined).

During circular oscillations, the dumbbell rotates around the vertical axis OO₁. Let's find out how the potential and kinetic energy of a dumbbell depends on the angle of rotation φ (Fig. 2).

Let, when rotated through an angle φ, the center of the dumbbell's weight rise to a height Δh (relative to the equilibrium position). As can be seen from figure 2, Δh = l - h, where

$$h = \sqrt{l^2 - |BB_1|^2} \approx \sqrt{l^2 - a^2 \varphi^2}$$

(since the rotation angle φ is small, |BB₁| ≈ aφ). From here we get:

$$l^2 - h^2 \approx a^2 \varphi^2 \iff \Delta h \cdot (l + h) \approx a^2 \varphi^2,$$

where

$$\Delta h \approx \frac{a^2 \varphi^2}{l + h} \approx \frac{a^2 \varphi^2}{2l}$$

(for small φ Δh is small, and l + h ≈ 2l).
