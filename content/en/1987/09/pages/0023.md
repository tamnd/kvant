Tasks

M1061 — M1065, F1073 — F1077

M1061. In a country with more than two cities, some pairs of cities are connected by intersecting roads. It is known that for any three cities A, B, C, it is possible to travel along this network of roads from A to B without stopping at C. Prove that one-way traffic can be installed on all roads so that from each city one can drive to any other, following the established directions.

V. E. Kolosov

M1062. a) Points E and D are taken on sides AB and AC of triangle ABC. Lines BD and CE intersect at point M, AM and BC at point P, AM and DE at point N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NA} = 2 \cdot \frac{PM}{MA}.$$

6) Points D, E and F are taken on the edges SA, SB and SC of the terrahedron SABC. Planes ABF, BCD and CAE intersect at point M, line SM intersects planes ABC and DEF at points P and N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NS} = 3 \cdot \frac{PM}{MS}.$$

T. A. Dzhortmenadze, E. Ya. Gleibman

M1063. How many different integers are there that can be represented as the difference $a - \bar{a}$, where $a - n$ is a natural number ($10^{n-1} \leq a < 10^n$), $\bar{a}$ is the number obtained by writing the digits $a$ in reverse order? For example, if $a = 1917$, then $a - \bar{a} = 1917 - 7191 = -5724$. Provide the answer for: a) $n = 4$; b) $n = 5$; b) any natural $n$.

G. O. Elstin

M1064. What is the maximum number of self-intersection points that a closed $n$-plane broken line can have if: a) $n$ is odd; b) $n$ even? (It is assumed that no three vertices lie on the same line and that no three links intersect at the same point.) D. V. Fuchs

M1065.* We will consider vectors $(x; y)$ with integer non-negative coordinates, and at least one of the coordinates is different from 0. Let us isose such a generator vector if $|x - y| = 1$.

a) Prove that the vector $(x; y)$ under consideration can be represented as a sum of generators (or it itself is a generator) if and only if the quantity $k(x, y) = x + y - (x - y)^2$ is non-negative.

6) Prove that the number $n(x, y)$ different (up to order of magnitude) is represented by the vector $(x; y)$ as the sum of the generators of the table so that the ball hits the floor as far as possible from the starting point? What is this maximum distance?

A. V. Khelsis
