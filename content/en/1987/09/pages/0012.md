Waves that propagate without attenuation and maintain their characteristics constant due to the continuous supply of energy are extracted by the authors. A clear analogue of the author’s process is the propagation of a combustion wave in a medium that is capable of subsequent restoration of its properties. The simplest example of such a phenomenon is the burning of the steppe. Each time a wave of flame passes, all the grass burns, but then it slowly grows back, so that the steppe can be set on fire again. The steppe as an “excitable environment” has the property of recovery.

Individual muscle fibers of the heart are similar in electrical characteristics to nerve fibers. The muscle tissue of the heart consists of tightly woven fibers that are electrically connected to each other in areas of contact. The excitation impulse can pass from one fiber to another, so that excitation waves can propagate through the cardiac tissue in any direction. In this sense, cardiac tissue is an excitable environment.
