This effect is not difficult to explain. If we assume that the distribution of refractoriness over the medium is random, then the probability of the birth of a new spiral wave from a break in the front of one of the existing waves is approximately the same for any point in the medium. The total number of new spiral waves born in a medium per unit time is proportional to the area occupied by the medium and the number of already existing spiral waves: $n_+ = \alpha L^2 N$, where $L$ is the linear size of the medium (we consider it two-dimensional), $N$ is the number of already existing waves, $\alpha$ is a certain constant coefficient. The number of spiral waves that disappear at the boundaries of the medium per unit time is $n_- = \beta N L$ (it is proportional to the length of the boundaries), $\beta$ is a constant coefficient.
