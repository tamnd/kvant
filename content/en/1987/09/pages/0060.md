⟦folio 58⟧

There is a specially equipped laboratory for film development and photographic printing.
Mathematics - very often tests were carried out based on what the teacher said. They did them with honor!
Physics - throughout the lesson, the teacher must make all his conclusions using relevant experiments.
Teachers at Andover School behave very relaxed in class, explaining until everything becomes clear to the class, they are highly artistic, they don’t call you to the blackboard in class, but they often conduct not very difficult tests. During lessons (all of them) there is a discussion with the whole group about what the students have already read.
Andover schoolchildren are all involved in some kind of sport, studying furiously, proving themselves until the very day (they keep up with the teacher until he not only explains everything to them, but also proves it to them), very talented guys (this is especially true from school), the main thing is that they think.
Without a doubt, going to America is also an excellent practice in the English language. The first week in Andover we spoke English much better, as we very carefully used the rules and basics that we received in the lessons and special preliminary training (in English) that our teachers provided. But then, when the standard phrases were no longer enough, we began to get confused in such a way that the Americans understood us with great difficulty. Then we brought, adapted and easily perceived the information. Around the end of the third week, I could cook freely and understandably with my neighbors and friends.

S. Shalamov:
In Andover we helped American schoolchildren gain real and correct understanding of Soviet schoolchildren. It turned out that there are not many differences between us.

It would be very good to learn from Andover students their ability to work. They work out a lot. I was angry at the behavior of the schoolchildren and the teacher in class. For all their freedom and relaxedness, the guys serve his lesson, and there is silence in the class. The teachers explain the material very thoroughly and well, combining strict control with other neighbors.

A. Vasiliev:
All the Americans we met (especially in Andover, where the school is located) treated us very well. Here we met both a desire to sing and a desire to help where necessary. Of course, we had difficulties with the language, especially at the beginning, but they were also surmountable.

The education system at Phillips Academy is very different from ours. Much of what we saw would be useful for us too. For example, I liked the art lesson. In this lesson, the teacher is not about the skill of drawing or photography, but about intelligently seeing the world, looking at something in common with the required point. By the way, the word “teaches” here is not entirely correct; the teacher does not give instructions, but advises, without insisting on his point of view.

O. Ivanovenko:
During my month of study, I learned a lot not only from the courses I took, but also from the lives of Phillips Academy students.

The relationship between teacher and student in this school is freer than ours; there is no tradition of raising your hand or standing in front of the teacher. However, they felt great respect on both sides. I never noticed that students were distracted during lessons. A teacher can explain an incomprehensible message several times without ever causing confusion. Students choose the courses themselves, so they are more interested in studying them.

The school drama theater left an indelible impression. Andover schoolchildren are wonderful performers.

A. Kosykh:
I lived among Americans, young Americans. And I saw that they sometimes think the same thing about us as we think about them. Now I understood them well and proudly understood myself, my friends, and us better.

F. Boyarkin:
The attitude towards us from the American students and residents of Andover with whom we interacted was very good. Nowhere did we meet anyone who treated us less as Soviet people. In conversations with Americans, we always felt a desire to learn something new about our country, about our school and much more.

I realized that Americans have the same thoughts and interests, they want friends of our country.

I. Pavlova:
We received the opportunity for free, so to speak, “unofficial” communication with our secularists in the United States. We looked for ourselves, not second-hand, to get to know each other. For example, after two weeks of staying I felt like an ordinary student at this school. There was no barrier between us and the students at Phillips Academy that made communication difficult. I really liked the naturalness and unnaturalness of our relationship.

We got a great opportunity to learn English. Please exchange only for more intensive study of a foreign language at school. We have seen how necessary it is.

At Phillips Academy, a lot of students play some kind of musical instrument. We have music and art schools where children who have the ability study, but high school students can no longer enroll in them. It would be nice if we studied music in our schools, but not in the elementary grades, but in the senior grades.
