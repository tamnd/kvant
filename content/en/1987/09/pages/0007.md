— What can you say about today’s young generation of IPP scientists?

— Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa repeatedly emphasized that a real scientist cannot work without young people. He was one of the founders of the Moscow Physical Institute, a new type of university recognized for educating future scientists. Our ties with this organization are traditionally strong. Every day in laboratories and at scientific seminars at the IPP you can see students and graduate students of “physics”. Since the founding of MIPT, our institute has become the base for the department of low temperature physics. The best students of this department, who have proven themselves during the learning process, remain at our institute as interns and then as graduate students. We employ the best of the best after graduate school.

Unfortunately, this practice of working with young people is not universal. The current situation in our country is that the Academy of Sciences, the country’s highest scientific body, has become very separated from higher education. At the same time, in many countries, especially in the USA, Canada, England, Japan, science is done to a large extent in scientific institutions, which are laboratories of universities. At the same time, for every one researcher there are two or three graduate students and several undergraduates. At our institute, this correspondence is much worse - on the fifth day of employees, about thirty interns, graduate students and students arrive. But compared to other scientific institutions in the country, this is still very good.

I believe that if a mature researcher works without young people, without students, then he very soon loses his form. With technology, youth are affected by the effectiveness of communication, and its role may turn out to be much more important than just learning. The learning process always involves a deeper understanding of the subject by the teacher himself. And then, it is the youth who find the way in science. So in our country, over the past 10-15 years, a number of physics graduates who have become employees of the IPP have created new scientific directions, some of which I mentioned above.

And here's another thing I wanted to say. There is a lot of talk now about the harm of scientific achievements in technology, practice, and life. This is undoubtedly one of the main tasks today. But as for the Fundamental Sciences, then, from my point of view, the most important harm here is the transfer of accumulated knowledge, all accumulated experience. Fundamentals of sciences are precisely what are stored primarily in the brains of scientists and passed on from one generation to another.
