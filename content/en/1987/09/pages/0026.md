6) Obviously, it is enough to consider 2 cases: 1) given vertices A, B, C, 2) given vertices A, B, D. In case 1 (Fig. 3) we construct AE - the bisector of the angle adjacent to BAC, and AD - the bisector of the angle EAC; vertex D is at the intersection of AD and the bisector of the angle adjacent to ACB. We similarly construct E. In case 2 (Fig. 4) we can use the fact that AC and DC are the bisectors of the angles BAD and BDF, where DF is the bisector of the angle adjacent to ADB. This allows you to build vertex C and then E.
