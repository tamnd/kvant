M1061 — M1065, F1073 — F1077

M1061. In a country with more than two cities, some pairs of cities are connected by intersecting roads. It is known that for any three cities A, B, C, it is possible to travel along this network of roads from A to B without stopping at C. Prove that one-way traffic can be installed on all roads so that from each city one can drive to any other, following the established directions.

V. E. Kolosov

M1062. a) Points E and D are taken on sides AB and AC of triangle ABC. Lines BD and CE intersect at point M, AM and BC at point P, AM and DE at point N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NA} = 2 \cdot \frac{PM}{MA}.$$

6) Points D, E and F are taken on the edges SA, SB and SC of the terrahedron SABC. Planes ABF, BCD and CAE intersect at point M, line SM intersects planes ABC and DEF at points P and N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NS} = 3 \cdot \frac{PM}{MS}.$$

T. A. Dzhortmenadze, E. Ya. Gleibman

M1063. How many different integers are there that can be represented as the difference $a - \bar{a}$, where $a - n$ is a natural number ($10^{n-1} \leq a < 10^n$), $\bar{a}$ is the number obtained by writing the digits $a$ in reverse order? For example, if $a = 1917$, then $a - \bar{a} = 1917 - 7191 = -5724$. Provide the answer for: a) $n = 4$; b) $n = 5$; b) any natural $n$.

G. O. Elstin

M1064. What is the maximum number of self-intersection points that a closed $n$-plane broken line can have if: a) $n$ is odd; b) $n$ even? (It is assumed that no three vertices lie on the same line and that no three links intersect at the same point.) D. V. Fuchs

M1065.* We will consider vectors $(x; y)$ with integer non-negative coordinates, and at least one of the coordinates is different from 0. Let us isose such a generator vector if $|x - y| = 1$.

a) Prove that the vector $(x; y)$ under consideration can be represented as a sum of generators (or it itself is a generator) if and only if the quantity $k(x, y) = x + y - (x - y)^2$ is non-negative.

6) Prove that the number $n(x, y)$ different (up to order of magnitude) is represented by the vector $(x; y)$ as the sum of the generators of the table so that the ball hits the floor as far as possible from the starting point? What is this maximum distance?

A. V. Khelsis

**F1074.** Papa Carlo made a cap for Pinocchio from thin hard cloth. The cap has the shape of a cone $H=20$ cm high with an angle $\alpha=60^\circ$ at the apex. Will this cap stay on Pinocchio’s head if this head is a smooth ball with a diameter of $D=15$ cm?

S. S. Krotov

**F1075.** While performing laboratory work, the student lowered a boiler into a vessel with water, turned it on and began recording the temperature every three minutes. The data for this omission are in Table 1. Then he cooled the water, put a small metal sample in the vessel and took the measurements again. The results of this omission are in Table 2.

Determine the heat capacity of the sample from these data. Mains voltage $U=35$ V, current through the boiler $I=0,2$ A, room temperature $t_0=20^\circ$ C.

L. P. Bakanina

**F1076.** To study the solar battery, a multi-range voltmeter is used (it consists of a sensitive microammeter and a set of additional resistors). By connecting it to the battery at the 1 V limit, we get a reading of $U_1=0,7$ V. Switching the voltmeter to the 10 V limit, we get a reading of $U_2=2,6$ V. What would happen at the 100 V limit? It is known that under constant illumination, a solar battery behaves like an ordinary source, to which a high resistance resistor is connected in series.

A. R. Zilberman

**F1077.** According to one hypothesis, stars are formed from the interstellar medium (cosmic dust) by rolling under the influence of gravitational force. Estimate the time of formation of a star from a giant spherical cloud of cosmic dust with a density of $\rho=2\cdot10^{-20}$ g/cm$^3$. We can assume that when rolling, the particles do not overtake each other. Gravitational constant $G=6,67\cdot10^{-11}$ N m$^2$/kg$^2$.

V. E. Skorovarov

**M1061.** In a country of more than two cities some pairs of cities are connected by non-intersecting roads. It is known that for any three cities $A, B, C$ it is possible to travel from $A$ to $B$ on these roads without going through $C$. Prove that it is possible to choose directions of one — way traffic on all the roads so that any city may be reached from any other along the roads in the chosen directions.

V. K. Kolosov

**M1062. a)** Points $E$ and $D$ are chosen on sides $AB$ and $AC$ of triangle $ABC$. The lines $BD$ and $CE$ intersect at the point $M$, $AM$ and $BC$ at $P$, $AM$ and $DE$ at $N$. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NA} = 2 \cdot \frac{PM}{MA}.$$

b) Points $D, E$ and $F$ are chosen on the sides $SA, SB$ and $SC$ of the tetrahedron $SABC$. The planes $ABF, BCD$ and $CAE$ intersect at the point $M$, the line $SM$ intersects planes $ABC$ and $DEF$ at $P$ and $N$. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NS} = 3 \cdot \frac{PM}{MS}.$$

T. A. Djortmenadze, E. Ya. Gleibman
