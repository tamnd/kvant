II Scientific and Technical Conference of Schoolchildren at MIPT

In «Kvant» No. 11 for 1986, I talk about the first scientific and technical conference of Schoolchildren at MIPT and invited to send abstracts and works to the next conference, scheduled for April 1987. The students responded very quickly. At the beginning of March this year, 220 works from 33 cities of the country were sent to MIPT. Omsk and Lvov, Sverdlovsk and Tula, Kyiv, Chelyabinsk, Tbilisi, Moscow, Saratov, Zaporozhye and many other cities were indicated in the return addresses of large parcels with abstracts, graphs, diagrams and computer programs received by the conference organizer. And so on April 19, 1987, the II Scientific and Technical Conference of Schoolchildren was held at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. About 100 participants from the RSFSR, Ukraine, Belarus and Kazakhstan came to it.

After the grand opening of the conference, the sections of mathematics, physics, theoretical physics and computer science began, at which the participants made presentations on their work. Then there was a viewing of “poster reports” - works that were not heard at the sectional sessions.

The Schoolchildren's performances showed the main use of the conference before Olympiads and other competitions: the opportunity to develop a topic of interest for a long time and in depth. The best work came from the guys who were creative and carefully carried out the experiment or who worked through a large amount of literature on the topic of the essay, and not from those who rewrote a chapter from a university textbook in a few days. In addition, speaking at the conference is as close as possible to real scientific work, which requires a creative approach to the topic and a lot of time and labor.

After viewing the “poster presentations,” MIPT teachers and specialists from basic institutes spoke to the children. They answered numerous questions from schoolchildren. The conference participants were pro-

Various physical experiments were demonstrated, including the effectiveness of high-terparity superconductivity, discovered in early 1987, literally two months before the conference.

The conference ended with the loading of active participants with books on physics and mathematics (with the authors' names and wishes), diplomas from MIPT, diplomas and badges from the journal «Kvant». But the main reward for the Schoolchildren was probably self-study at the conference, famous with MIPT - one of the leading universities in the country, and meetings with scientists.

And now the conference organizing committee is waiting for new works. Any student can become a participant in the conference. To do this, you need to write an abstract (7-25 pages), independently analyzing an interesting question in physics, technical mathematics or programming. However, a copy of the abstract must be sent before February 1, 1988 to the address: 141700 Dolgoprudny, Moscow region, MIPT, organizing committee of the III Scientific and Technical Conference of Schoolchildren, the second one must be kept for yourself. All questions on the topics of abstracts and organization of the conference can be found out by calling ZFTSH: 408-51-45.

A. V. Sapozhnikov, member of the conference organizing committee

The Soviet delegation at the Rockefeller Center at NBC television (New York).

There was very great interest in the USSR representative in Andover. The vast majority of schoolchildren and teachers, with whom we promised, were very friendly towards our country and the Soviet people. American television, radio and newspapers paid great attention to the exchange program. Information about us and about the American delegation in Siberia was published in the Voston Globe, Lawrence Tribon, and local Andver newspapers. The information was mostly objective and friendly.

Summing up some of the results of the first exchange, it should be noted that the experience accumulated over 200 years of one of the most representative American schools can be useful for Soviet public education. Certain courses and textbooks deserve in-depth study, especially in science subjects and foreign languages, and the widespread use of computer technology not only in mathematics, but also in teaching art, languages, etc. A lot of interesting things can be learned in the field of specific methods of teaching various subjects. On the other hand, the American partners were more interested in the experience of the Novosibirsk FMIII in a large scientific and university center, in particular, the original courses in mathematics, physics, the completely new course “Mathematical modeling of physical processes using a computer,” the system of natural education and the organization of life of a large student team as a whole.

Exchange decisions to continue. Soon the next delegation of 10 students should travel to Novosibirsk and Andover.

O. Shueva:
Perhaps most importantly, our American peers became our very good friends. I can talk to him about literally anything, because they tried to understand, listen and express their point of view. Between the girls, we talked quite a bit about politics, discussing only some external aspects. But it was there that I realized that in the USA they were creating some degrees of our country, although in general we were treated by very different delegates and hotels.

About how we learned. Every day we had 5 lessons of 50 minutes.

English is a course specially prepared for us, where we read “The Old Man and the Sea” and talked with American children on a given topic, tried to read and understand poetry.

The programming was conducted by a remarkable teacher, not only a professional in his field, but also an amazing artist and improviser. A lot of work on machines, studying TURVO-PASCAL, and most importantly, we had the opportunity to find out what programming lessons could be like at school.

The art course struck me and interested me in its necessity. I would say eccentricity. We at FMIII also have a course in art history, but here we studied artists and sculptors purely theoretically (albeit very interestingly) using certain sources. In Andover, I was in a group in which we were taught to look at all objects from all sides, with all their features. I must say that this was not difficult, but it was important to learn. In general, the art lessons consisted of two parts: drawing and photography. For example, we needed to get different types of photos:

1. Capture a very interesting moment.
2. Take an abstract photograph.

Mathematics - very often tests were carried out based on what the teacher said. They did them with honor!
Physics - throughout the lesson, the teacher must make all his conclusions using relevant experiments.
Teachers at Andover School behave very relaxed in class, explaining until everything becomes clear to the class, they are highly artistic, they don’t call you to the blackboard in class, but they often conduct not very difficult tests. During lessons (all of them) there is a discussion with the whole group about what the students have already read.
Andover schoolchildren are all involved in some kind of sport, studying furiously, proving themselves until the very day (they keep up with the teacher until he not only explains everything to them, but also proves it to them), very talented guys (this is especially true from school), the main thing is that they think.
Without a doubt, going to America is also an excellent practice in the English language. The first week in Andover we spoke English much better, as we very carefully used the rules and basics that we received in the lessons and special preliminary training (in English) that our teachers provided. But then, when the standard phrases were no longer enough, we began to get confused in such a way that the Americans understood us with great difficulty. Then we brought, adapted and easily perceived the information. Around the end of the third week, I could cook freely and understandably with my neighbors and friends.

S. Shalamov:
In Andover we helped American schoolchildren gain real and correct understanding of Soviet schoolchildren. It turned out that there are not many differences between us.

It would be very good to learn from Andover students their ability to work. They work out a lot. I was angry at the behavior of the schoolchildren and the teacher in class. For all their freedom and relaxedness, the guys serve his lesson, and there is silence in the class. The teachers explain the material very thoroughly and well, combining strict control with other neighbors.

A. Vasiliev:
All the Americans we met (especially in Andover, where the school is located) treated us very well. Here we met both a desire to sing and a desire to help where necessary. Of course, we had difficulties with the language, especially at the beginning, but they were also surmountable.

The education system at Phillips Academy is very different from ours. Much of what we saw would be useful for us too. For example, I liked the art lesson. In this lesson, the teacher is not about the skill of drawing or photography, but about intelligently seeing the world, looking at something in common with the required point. By the way, the word “teaches” here is not entirely correct; the teacher does not give instructions, but advises, without insisting on his point of view.

O. Ivanovenko:
During my month of study, I learned a lot not only from the courses I took, but also from the lives of Phillips Academy students.

The relationship between teacher and student in this school is freer than ours; there is no tradition of raising your hand or standing in front of the teacher. However, they felt great respect on both sides. I never noticed that students were distracted during lessons. A teacher can explain an incomprehensible message several times without ever causing confusion. Students choose the courses themselves, so they are more interested in studying them.

The school drama theater left an indelible impression. Andover schoolchildren are wonderful performers.

A. Kosykh:
I lived among Americans, young Americans. And I saw that they sometimes think the same thing about us as we think about them. Now I understood them well and proudly understood myself, my friends, and us better.

F. Boyarkin:
The attitude towards us from the American students and residents of Andover with whom we interacted was very good. Nowhere did we meet anyone who treated us less as Soviet people. In conversations with Americans, we always felt a desire to learn something new about our country, about our school and much more.

I realized that Americans have the same thoughts and interests, they want friends of our country.

I. Pavlova:
We received the opportunity for free, so to speak, “unofficial” communication with our secularists in the United States. We looked for ourselves, not second-hand, to get to know each other. For example, after two weeks of staying I felt like an ordinary student at this school. There was no barrier between us and the students at Phillips Academy that made communication difficult. I really liked the naturalness and unnaturalness of our relationship.

We got a great opportunity to learn English. Please exchange only for more intensive study of a foreign language at school. We have seen how necessary it is.

At Phillips Academy, a lot of students play some kind of musical instrument. We have music and art schools where children who have the ability study, but high school students can no longer enroll in them. It would be nice if we studied music in our schools, but not in the elementary grades, but in the senior grades.
