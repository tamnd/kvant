About a year and a half ago, one of the central newspapers published a letter from a parent who, in the wake of a common hobby, bought a new gold grinder for their son. Of course, it wasn’t difficult to “confuse” her. But to “unravel”... Neither the son, nor the father, nor the teacher at school could solve the puzzle. Finally, in despair, the parents turned to the manufacturer of the ill-fated toy. The result can be judged by the fact that the next instrumental was a newspaper.

We have reason to believe that the item that caused so much trouble to its happy owner was the gold crusher chosen here (Fig. 1). These are 7 discs with 6 holes on each, all holes except one contain colored balls. Turn the wheel on the side of the gold breaker to a certain angle; using a system of gears, we simultaneously turn all the disks to the same angle in the direction indicated by the arrows. In this case, the “hole” (empty hole) must be placed opposite the ball on another disk, the ball must be inserted into the “hole”, the vacated hole must be installed opposite the new ball using the wheel doctor, etc. This will rearrange the balls. How to return to the starting position?

The gold crusher graph helps you find the algorithm (see covers «Kvant» No. 5 and No. 7). Let's mark 42 points on the plane - according to the number of holes - and connect the lines of each pair of points corresponding to the holes "connected" to each other.

It is easy to understand that any hole is connected exactly to one hole on each of the neighboring disks, i.e. the holes of the central disk (6 points in the center of the graph) are connected to 6 holes each, and the remaining holes have 3 “connections”. The holes directly adjacent to the central ones (“holes of levels 1-20”) form an internal spine on the graph. When turning the wheel at 60°, “holes of level 2-20” are put in their place - they are represented by the points of the next wheel. Next comes a series of “holes of levels 3-20”, they hit the 1st level when turning 120°, etc. Now the puzzle can be reduced to moving the chips along the lines of the graph. Such “travels through graphs” were discussed in detail in a one-dimensional article by D. Vakarlov («Kvant» No. 7, 1986, p. 50). The main difficulty arises when crossing into practice - there is too much difference in the arrangement of the balls in the gold crusher and on the graph (in the general drawings the same (!) arrangement of colors is depicted; a “hole” is placed in black on the graph).

Fig. 2.

Marriage of balls in a gold crusher and on a graph (in the general drawings the same (!) arrangement of colors is depicted; a “hole” is placed in black on the graph). The puzzle is easy to assemble by levels; The pre-estimated level is transferred to place 1-20. Let us explain how balls of levels 1-20 are rearranged. Let’s first transfer the “hole” through the 2nd level to one of the places of the 1st-20th level (following the red arrows), pass it in a circle (the blue of the arc) and return it in the same (red) way. As a result, 5 balls of levels 1-20 are cyclically rearranged, and the rest are returned to their places (check!). From these 5 cycles you can create any permutation of colors to the 1st level. Similarly, exchanges between different levels can be arranged.
