A. S. BOROVIK -

— Andrey Stanislavovich, please tell us about the Institute of Physical Problems, which you head. About its history, traditions, main directions of scientific research.

— Our institute is untrained compared to most of the physical process institutes existing in the USSR. Its main feature is that, if necessary, the number of employees in the IPP is collected by very strong researchers. It was founded in 1934 by Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa and was created under his direct leadership. It was created over quite a long time, not all at once, not by one.max. And during all this time he concluded the foundations of future directions of scientific research.

From the very beginning, the main subject of research at the IPP was low-temperature physics, physics associated with the manifestation of quantum laws in solids, as well as in liquid helium. It was here in 1938 that P. L. Kapitsa discovered one of the most amazing manifestations of quantum laws on a macroscopic scale - the phenomenon of superfluidity. The discovery of superfluidity is a whole cycle of untaught, ingenious and subtle experiments that led to the creation of a new field

The next most important achievement of IPP scientists is the discovery of the intermediate state of superconductors. This is the name given to the special state of a superconducting sample in a magnetic field when it “breaks up” into normal and superconducting regions. This was followed by a series of replacement discoveries in the field of superconductivity. In the fifties, Lev Davidovich Landdu, who worked at the IPP, together with V.L. Another important area with which great achievements of the FPI are associated is the study of the energy spectra of electrons in metals. The most sophisticated techniques were developed for the production of extremely pure single-crystal samples in which the electron “ran” without so much distance of millimeters! In ordinary, “dirty” images at room temperature, this range does not exceed several meter distances. The institute's scientists developed many original methods for studying the behavior of electrons in metals, which made it possible to open a completely new and exciting field of low-temperature physics.

Interesting results were also obtained when studying the magnetic properties of solids at low temperatures. For example, a new physical phenomenon was discovered - pyzomagnetism - the occurrence in some substances of magnetization under pressure.

It should be noted that the last thirty-five years of scientific activity of Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsya himself were not associated with low temperature physics. — What can you say about today’s young generation of IPP scientists?

— Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa repeatedly emphasized that a real scientist cannot work without young people. He was one of the founders of the Moscow Physical Institute, a new type of university recognized for educating future scientists. Our ties with this organization are traditionally strong. Every day in laboratories and at scientific seminars at the IPP you can see students and graduate students of “physics”. Since the founding of MIPT, our institute has become the base for the department of low temperature physics. The best students of this department, who have proven themselves during the learning process, remain at our institute as interns and then as graduate students. We employ the best of the best after graduate school.

Unfortunately, this practice of working with young people is not universal. The current situation in our country is that the Academy of Sciences, the country’s highest scientific body, has become very separated from higher education. At the same time, in many countries, especially in the USA, Canada, England, Japan, science is done to a large extent in scientific institutions, which are laboratories of universities. At the same time, for every one researcher there are two or three graduate students and several undergraduates. At our institute, this correspondence is much worse - on the fifth day of employees, about thirty interns, graduate students and students arrive. But compared to other scientific institutions in the country, this is still very good.

I believe that if a mature researcher works without young people, without students, then he very soon loses his form. With technology, youth are affected by the effectiveness of communication, and its role may turn out to be much more important than just learning. The learning process always involves a deeper understanding of the subject by the teacher himself. And then, it is the youth who find the way in science. So in our country, over the past 10-15 years, a number of physics graduates who have become employees of the IPP have created new scientific directions, some of which I mentioned above.

And here's another thing I wanted to say. There is a lot of talk now about the harm of scientific achievements in technology, practice, and life. This is undoubtedly one of the main tasks today. But as for the Fundamental Sciences, then, from my point of view, the most important harm here is the transfer of accumulated knowledge, all accumulated experience. Fundamentals of sciences are precisely what are stored primarily in the brains of scientists and passed on from one generation to another.

— How can you comment on the latest achievements in this area?

— Superconductivity, which we are talking about now, is extremely interesting both from the point of view of functional science and, mainly, from the point of view of emerging promising technical applications. Physicists face the difficult task of explaining the nature of the discovered high-temperature superconductivity in new materials. Today it is clear to everyone that it differs from the graft type; there are several alternative hypotheses about the mechanism of the discovered high-temperature superconductivity, but there is no unambiguous solution to the problems yet. It may soon be possible to increase the temperature of transition to the superconducting state by another 100-200 K. Now there is no longer any reason to deny this possibility. But even the available conditions open up unprecedented prospects. In the next few years, maybe five to seven, thanks to the discovered phenomenon, a colossal revolution will take place in many technical areas. But to implement it, special personnel are needed - not those who deal with physical problems, but personnel of chemical technologists who are looking for new materials. Their activities are sometimes even “anti-scientific,” that is, the train of thought of these scientists is incredible from the point of view of the usual scientific approach. As an example, I can cite a joke that the Inland theoretical physicist Hendrik Casimir recently told me. He worked for a long time in the research laboratories of a radio engineering company and is well versed in engineering. This joke, in a sense, reflects the way technologists think. For a long time it was not possible to spray the French on Germany. And then Casimir said that it was necessary to place rhenium as an intermediate layer. The logic behind the choice of Rhine was that... between France and Germany the Rhine flows as a “binding” natural element.

The essence of the matter is that the technicians who create new materials have their own vision, sometimes very strange (from the point of view of a physicist), some properties of the figure, their own logic, which is not clear to everyone, but nevertheless leads to the goal. As in any creativity, especially related to Japanese, here it turns out to be very important, in addition to the lasting, it has some kind of specificity. It must be said that in the West such scientists are, as they say, number one. I think that somewhere in our country, say, in metallurgy, similar unique products are known. But in the field of physics, I find it difficult to name such people. And not because they don’t exist. I'm sure there is. But they are not used, they are not isolated. This is a big flaw in the organization of our science.
