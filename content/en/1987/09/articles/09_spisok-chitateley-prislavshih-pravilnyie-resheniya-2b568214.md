⟦folio 29⟧

Therefore, the potential energy of the dumbbells (measured relative to the equilibrium position) depends on the angle of rotation as

$$\Pi_{\varphi} = 2mg \cdot \Delta h = \frac{mga^2}{l} \varphi^2.$$

(1)

Let us now calculate the kinetic energy. For small oscillations, we can neglect the vertical component of the balls’ velocities and assume that the velocity vectors lie in the horizontal plane and are equal in magnitude to $v = b\omega_{\varphi} = b\varphi'$ ($\omega_{\varphi}$ is the angular velocity of the balls at the moment when the angle of rotation of the dumbbells is $\varphi$). Thus, the kinetic energy depends on the rotation angle as

$$K_{\varphi} = 2 \frac{mv^2}{2} = mb^2(\varphi')^2.$$

(2)

Note that formulas (1) and (2) are similar to the formulas describing the potential and kinetic energy of a load oscillating with external rigidity $k$:

$$\Pi_x = \frac{1}{2} kx^2, \quad K_x = \frac{1}{2} M(x')^2,$$

where $x$ is the deviation from the equilibrium position. Receiving this analysis, we can write:

$$\Pi_{\varphi} = \frac{mga^2}{l} \varphi^2 = \frac{mga^2}{l} \left( \frac{x}{b} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} kx^2,$$

where $x = b\varphi$ is the deviation of the ball from the equilibrium position, and

$$k = \frac{2mg}{l} \frac{a^2}{b^2};$$

corresponds,

$$K = mb^2(\varphi')^2 = m(x')^2 = \frac{1}{2} M(x')^2,$$

Where

$$M = 2m.$$

Thus, the dumbbell will make round oscillations with a period

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2mlb^2}{2mga^2}} = 2\pi \frac{b}{a} \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}.$$

M. M. Tsypin
