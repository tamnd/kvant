But perhaps we are exaggerating the significance of the step taken by Euler? Most likely not. How unusual for the 18th century his approach to the definition of a physical quantity was is evidenced by the explanations that Euler considered necessary to make further: “Here, perhaps, doubt may arise about how one can make a path for time, since these are heterogeneous quantities, and, therefore, it is impossible to indicate how many times an interval of time, for example, 10 minutes, is contained in a path length, for example, 10 feet.〈...〉 If to measure paths we Let us choose length as units for definition, and in the same way for time we will choose time interval as units of definition, and if we constantly use this measure, then all paths and times will be expressed in abstract numbers, and then there will be no advantages for dividing the first into the second.” These are the kinds of processes things had to be unleashed in fundamental discussions on mechanics in the middle of the 18th century.

But Euler cannot be considered fully the author of the modern definition of speed - he did not use an understandable speed as a vector, but separately defined the model of speed and its direction. The idea of ​​speed as a direction of a segment entered physics roughly a few years after the publication of Euler's theory, when the foundations of vector calculus were developed in mathematics.

After talking about the long and complex history of the concept of speed, it is no longer surprising that the concept of acceleration entered science even later. For the first time, the physical quantity “acceleration” was used in lectures on mechanics by the French mathematician and engineer J. Poncelet (1788–1867) only in 1841. This does not mean, however, that the term “acceleration” does not appear in the works of authors of the 18th and even 17th centuries. It was used, but it did not mean physical quantities, but the process of changing speed. (Let us note in passing that in physics one can find other examples of such mixed usage of words - let us at least remember the terms “change”, “pressure”, “resistance”.) True, Euler had already considered a second arbitrary path in time, which from a modern point of view has the meaning of tangential acceleration, but he interpreted it differently. Euler wrote that this arbitrary “expresses that segment of the path along which the body moves beyond the distance that it has covered due to its equal movements.”

The widespread use of acceleration in work on mechanics began only after the publication (1851) of the work “Elements of Mechanics” by the Frenchman A. Rezal, a student of Poncelet.

So, the history of the basic concepts of kinematics shows that the expression of a basic language to describe movement required overcoming many difficulties of fundamental characteristics. And the fact that we now easily use this language only once again supports the merits of scientists.

Around one task

At one of our busy problem-solving circles, our teacher proposed the following problem:

In five seconds, the body moves 5 cm equally slow and stops. How far did the body travel in the third second?

None of us could approach her. Then the teacher said: “We will devote the heartiest lesson of the circle to solving this problem, although it is being solved... orally. To do this, you just need to know... However, solve another problem first.” Here are its terms:

In the last half-second, a freely falling body travels a distance of 30 m. Find the speed of the body at the moment of landing.
