Mathematics has a reputation as the most abstract science. At the same time, no one dares to consider it a useless science; on the contrary, it is a necessary science itself. And so we get a seemingly contradictory situation: on the one hand, science itself is abstract and divorced from life, and on the other hand, necessary science itself. This apparent contradiction is explained by the fact that mathematics has two spectrums: theoretical, where it talks about the most general and far from life things, and applied, where it concerns the most practical necessary things.

All my life I have been a representative of theoretical (or, as it is also called, pure) mathematics and have never dealt with its applications. At the same time, I don’t think that I’m doing something useless.

One of my students, now an academician, Andrei Nikolaevich Tikhonov began his scientific career - applied mathematics.
