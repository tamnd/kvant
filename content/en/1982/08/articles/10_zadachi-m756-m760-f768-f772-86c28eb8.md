Tasks

M756—M760; F768—F772

M756. In the country, besides the capital, there are more than 100 cities. The capital of the country is connected by air lines with 100 cities; Each of the remaining cities is connected by air line to 10 cities worldwide. It is known that from any city you can (perhaps with transfers) fly to any other. Prove that it is possible to close half of the airlines flying from the capital, so that it is possible to get from any city to any other.

M757. From the sequence 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, ... it is not difficult to isolate an arithmetic progression of length three: 1/2, 1/3, 1/6. Is it possible to select an arithmetic progression from this sequence?
a) length 4?
b) length 5?
c) length k, where k is any natural number?

G. Galperin

M758*. What is the smallest number of numbers that must be removed from the sequence 1, 2, 3, ... 1982 so that none of the remaining numbers is equal to the product of the other two remaining numbers? (9)

L. Kurlyandchik

M759. Inside a convex quadrilateral for which the sum of six pairwise distances between the vertices (that is, the sum of the lengths of all sides and diagonals) is equal to $S_1$, there is another for which this sum is equal to $S_2$.
a) Can the value $S_2$ be greater than $S_1$?
b) Prove that $S_2 < 4S_1/3$.
c) Prove that if inside an arbitrary tetrahedron with the sum of edge lengths $S_1$ there is another one for which this sum is equal to $S_2$, then $S_2 < 4S_1/3$. (10)

P. Gusyatnikov

F768. A top shaped like a cone (Fig. 2) moves along a smooth table, quickly rotating around its axis. At what speed $v$ of translational motion will the top not hit the edge of the table and drain from it? The axis of the top remains vertical. The dimensions of the top are indicated at figure 2. (8)

A. Zilberman

F769. A massive cylinder of radius $R$ rests on two supports of equal height (Fig. 3). One stand is stationary, and the other emerges from under the cylinder at a speed of $v$. With what force does the cylinder press on the stationary stand at the moment when the distance between the points of support is $R\sqrt{2}$? Consider that at the initial moment the stands are located very close to each other; there is no friction between the cylinder and the supports. (9)

S. Krotov

F770. In a heat-conducting cylinder under a light piston there is a mixture of equal quantities of water and ice: $m_B=m_\pi=1$ kg. The pressure on the piston slowly increases from the initial value $p_0=10^5$ Pa to $p_1=2,5 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Determine how much ice will melt and how much work will be done by the external force. It is known that to reduce the melting temperature of ice by 1 degree, it is necessary to increase the pressure to $14 \cdot 10^6$ Pa.
1) Solve the problem assuming water and ice are incompressible.
2) Evaluate the adjustment that comes from accounting for skimability.
It is known that to reduce the volume of a certain amount of water by 1%, the pressure must be raised to $20 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Take the compressibility of ice to be equal to half the compressibility of water.
Specific thermal properties of water and ice - $c_B = 4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K), $c_\pi = 2,1 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K); specific heat of melting of ice $\lambda = 3 \cdot 10^5$ J/kg, density of ice $\rho_\pi = 0,9\rho_B$, where $\rho_B$ is the density of water. (9)

A. Buedin

F771. In the circuit shown in figure 4, subsection D and a coil with inductance $L$ at time $t=0$ are connected to an alternating voltage source $u = U_M \cos \omega t$ using a key $K$. F772. The interplanetary spacecraft made a soft landing on the Moon. The ship has the shape of a disk of radius $r=4$ m; its surface is covered with black (non-reflective) paint. Is it possible to detect the application of a spacecraft using the world's largest Soviet television scale, the BTA, with an object diameter of $D=6$ m, if a photoplastic is installed in the focal plane and the area of ​​the lunar surface in which the expected application site is located is formatted?

Please note that the reliably distinguishable structure of an image on photographic plastic (that is, the minimum relative difference in illumination of the light and dark parts of the image) is equal to $k=0,05$.

Distance from Earth to Moon $L=4 \cdot 10^5$ km; photography is carried out in light with a wavelength of $\lambda=0,6$ µm. To estimate at what size the letters laid out by astronauts on the surface of the Moon can be read when observed from Earth using the BTA teleweight. (10)

S. Kozel

Problems

M756—M760; P768—P772

M756. There are more than 100 cities in a certain country, not counting the capital. The latter is joined by direct flights to 100 cities; each of the remaining cities is joined by direct flights to exactly 10 cities. It is known that one can fly from any city to any other (eventually, with transfers). Prove that half the airlines flying from the capital can be closed down so that it will still be possible to fly from any city to any other.

M757. It is easy to see that the sequence 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, ... contains a three-term subsequence which is an arithmetical progression: 1/2, 1/3, 1/6. Is it possible to find another subsequence, which is also an arithmetical progression,

a) of length 4?
b) of length 5?
c) of length $k$, where $k$ is any natural number?

G. Galperin
