Problem 6. A thin rod of length $l = 15$ cm is located along the main optical axis of the collecting line so that the distance between the lens and the tip of the rod closest to it turns its focal length. In this case, the images of the ends of the rod in the face are located at distances of $f_1 = 36$ cm and $f_2 = 60$ cm from it. Find the focal length of the line.

Let us construct an image $A'B'$ of the rod $AB$ in the face (Fig. 2). From figure 2 it is clear that the image of the end of the rod closest to the face (point $B$) is further from the line than the image further away from the end of the rod (point $A$). In the problem, the given distances are $f_1 = |OA'|$ and $f_2 = |OB'|$; therefore, $d_1 = |OA|$, $d_2 = |OB|$, rod alina $l = d_1 - d_2$.

From the lens formula $\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}$ we have $d = \frac{fF}{f - F}$.

Then

$$l = d_1 - d_2 = \frac{f_1F}{f_1 - F} - \frac{f_2F}{f_2 - F}.$$

Fig. 2.

This is the equation for determining the desired focal length $F$. Let's reduce it to the standard form of a quadratic equation:

$$(f_2 - f_1 - l)F^2 + l(f_1 + f_2)F - lf_1f_2 = 0$$

and find the roots:

$$F_{1,2} = \frac{-l(f_1 + f_2) \pm \sqrt{l(f_2 - f_1)(4f_1f_2 + lf_2 - lf_1)}}{2(f_2 - f_1 - l)}$$

Substituting the numerical values ​​and performing the appropriate calculations, we obtain $F_1 = 20$ cm and $F_2 = -180$ cm. The second root - negative - has no physical meaning, since the focal length of the coinciating line is positive. Thus, the desired focal length of the line is $F = 20$ cm.
