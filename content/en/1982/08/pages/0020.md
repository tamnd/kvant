$$E_{пов} \sim S, \text{ или } E_{пов} = \sigma S.$$

The proportionality coefficient is the surface tension. This coefficient is changed by relating the work A required to increase the surface area by ΔS to the value of ΔS. It is different for each liquid.

When we collapse a bubble, the size of the cavity (and therefore the surface of the film) increases, but the amount of liquid in the film naturally remains constant. It is clear that an increase in the surface is possible only due to the release of new molecules from the volume into the surface layer, that is, due to the performance of work and an increase in the surface energy of the liquid.

Now we can answer the question of why a soap bubble has the shape of a sphere, and not, for example, an egg, an ellipson or a polyhedron. The fact is that the sphere has a remarkable property: of all the geometric figures that limit a given volume, it has the smallest surface area. Therefore, if we try to give the bubble a non-spherical shape, then we will be forced to increase the surface area of ​​the layer, that is, to do additional work to bring molecules to the surface, and therefore to further increase the surface energy of the liquid.

In other words,
the spherical shape of the bubble removes the minimum potential energy of the film that forms its shell.

The question is, who is in the potential hole here? It is clear that this is not a separate part, since the very nature of the surface of tension is not associated with individual molecules, but with their interaction. It cannot even be said that there are molecules of the layer surface in the potential well, since when the radius of the bubble changes, a transition of molecules occurs from the volume to the surface or vice versa (and even with constant bubble sizes, due to the tempo movement, there is a continuous volume of molecules between the volume and the surface).

Thus,
The potential well contains the entire surface energy of the liquid.
