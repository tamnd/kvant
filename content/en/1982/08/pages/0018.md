What is a potential hole

A potential hole is a hole that has not yet been dug...
(from scientific folklore)

In physics, the phrase is often found: “The particle (or system) is in a potential well.” What is a potential hole and why does any physical object end up in it? Let's try to figure this out. The most convenient way to do this is with specific examples. On it from the simplest.

Yashik and ball

Let's take a box with nerve dynamium, in which there are bulges and dents (holes), and throw a metal ball into it. After rolling along the bottom, the ball will eventually remain at the bottom of one of the holes. Let us ask ourselves: why did the ball remain in the hole, and precisely at its bottom, and not on the slope? The obvious answer is this. The ball located on the wall of the hole (or convexity) is acted upon by two forces: the force of gravity $\vec{F}_\tau$ and the reaction force of the wall $\vec{F}_p$ (Fig. 1). The resultant force $\vec{F}$ is directed down the slope, so the ball, in accordance with Newton’s second law, acquires acceleration and rolls down to the bottom of the hole. At the bottom, the action of two forces ($\vec{F}_\tau$ and $\vec{F}_p$) is balanced, and the equivalent force vanishes, so here the ball will be in an equilibrium position.

Let's consider the same situation from an energy point of view. A ball resting at the bottom of a hole has zero speed and its kinetic energy is zero. It follows that all the energy he possesses is potential. Let's try to lift the ball from its equilibrium position. It is clear that any movement of the ball up the slope must require work against the force of gravity. Due to this work done on the body, its potential energy returns.
