The condition for equilibrium of a body with a fixed axis of rotation is the equality to zero of the algebraic sum of the moments of forces acting on this body. Relative to the bracing axis, moments create two forces: the force of gravity $m\vec{g}$, applied to the center of gravity of the rod, and the Archimedes force $\vec{F}_A$, applied to the center of gravity of the part of the rod immersed in water.

Let us denote by $l$ the length of the rod, by $S$ the area of ​​its cross section, and by $x$ the part of the length of the rod immersed in water. Then the gravity force of the rod is $mg = \varrho_1 l S g$, and the Archimedes force is $F_A = \varrho_2 x l S g$. The arms of these forces are $l/2$ cos $\alpha$ and $l(1-x/2)$ cos $\alpha$ respectively.

The equilibrium condition of the rod relative to the bracing axis has the form

$$mg \frac{l}{2} \cos \alpha - F_A l \left(1 - \frac{x}{2}\right) \cos \alpha = 0.$$

After substituting expressions for forces and some transformations for the desired value $x$ we obtain a quadratic equation

$$x^2 - 2x + \frac{\varrho_1}{\varrho_2} = 0.$$

The roots of this equation are equal

$$x_{1,2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{1 - \frac{\varrho_1}{\varrho_2}},$$

or

$$x_1 = 1,45 \text{ и } x_2 = 0,55.$$

Obviously, the length of the rod cannot exceed one, so the first root has no physical meaning.

Thus, 0.55 of the length of the rod is in water.

Let's apply the Boyle-Mariotte law to the volume of air enclosed in a tube:

$$p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2.$$

In the initial state, its pressure is the sum of the atmospheric pressure and the pressure of the core with a height of $h$:

$$p_1 = p_a + \varrho gh, \text{ или } p_1 = \varrho g (H + h),$$

where $\varrho$ is the density of the soil, $H$ is the height of the column of soil, creating a pressure equal to the atmosphere. The volume occupied by air in this case is equal to $V_1 = (L - h) S$, where $S$ is the cross-sectional density of the rough.

Let us denote by $x$ the height of the core of the core remaining in the tube after reorientation. Then

$$p_2 = p_a - \varrho g x = \varrho g (H - x)$$

and $V_2 = (L - x) S.$

Substituting the corresponding values ​​of pressure and volumes into the expression of the Boyle-Mariotte law, we obtain an equation for finding the desired value $x$:

$$\varrho g (H + h) (L - h) S = = \varrho g (H - x) (L - x) S,$$

or

$$x^2 - (H + L) x + h (H + h - L) = 0.$$

The roots of this equation are equal
