The famous explorer of the deep sea, the French scientist Jacques-Yves Cousteau (he invented scuba gear) in his book “The Mighty Lord of the Seas” writes: “It is difficult to describe the ears of a person who first met a whale in the water... First of all, you are stunned by the size of the whale. They surpass everything that man has come to experience in the animal world, they surpass everything that he imagined.”
Figure 1 gives an idea of ​​how many times the size of a blue whale is larger than the size of an elephant and a human. The length of this whale reaches 33 m, it is almost 10 m the length of a passenger monk! (It’s not for nothing that Russian fairy tales mention the “miracle fish-whale”, which has “a selo stone on its back.”) We have already talked about the mass of whales. The largest of the whales had a mass of 150,000 kg, and the largest land animal - the elephant - has a mass of 3000 to 6000 kg (like the tongue of some whales!). It is calculated that if an elephant had twice the mass, then it would need legs twice as thick, and they already have an area of ​​4 dm² each. (Think and explain why such legs would be necessary for a land animal.) A body floats in water if the Archimedean force and the force of gravity acting on it are equal. Let's calculate the Archimedean force acting on the whale and compare it with the force of gravity.
The Archimedean force is equal to the weight of the liquid displaced by a body immersed in it, that is
$$F_A = g \rho_{\text{ж}} V$$
where $g \approx 10$ N/kg, $\rho_{\text{ж}}$ is the density of the liquid, $V$ is the volume of the body. How do we calculate the volume of a whale? Let's do it this way: let's assume that the whale's body has the shape of a cylinder. Then the volume is equal to $V = \pi d^2 h / 4$, where $d$ is the diameter of the cylinder, $h$ is its height. In our case, the height $h$ is the length of the whale. What is the diameter of our whale-cylinder? Let's assume that this is the average diameter of the whale's body. And let’s estimate the average diameter using figure 1. To do this, let’s change to figure the diameter of the whale in several, for example, ten, different places: at the head, at the tail, at the middle. We will take the arithmetic mean of these changes as the diameter of the whale-cylinder. But just take into account that figure 1 is made on a certain scale: on figure the length of the whale is ~7.5 cm, but in fact its length is 30 m, so the scale figure is 1:400.

Do all the calculations further, and you will see that the Archimedean force that supports the whale in the water is calculated in millions of newtons. (Of course, your calculations are approximate, and it is impossible to say exactly how many newtons; but the fact that this is a number between one and ten million is certain.) It is clear that such a force keeps in balance a body with a mass of hundreds of tons, and this is precisely the mass that the blue whale has. A whale in water is weightless, because the force of gravity acting on it is also calculated in millions of newtons (to calculate the force of gravity, multiply 9.8 N/kg by the mass of the whale).

Of course, the whale will not be able to stay on land. There are known cases when a whale, for unknown reasons, washes up on the ocean shore.
