Archimedean force and knits

On land, the goose gives the impression of a sedentary, clumsy bird. “The goose is heavy on red paws...” - this is what A.S. Pushkin wrote, using the very expressive word “heavy” to describe the bird. But then the goose entered the water and swam... Now we see a light, graceful bird, moving quickly and freely. Even a breath of wind is enough to change the speed of its movement. Why such a change?

The peculiarities of the body's behavior in water are associated with low friction and the presence of buoyant - Archimedean - force.

Place a cork or plastic jar lid on the table and serve it on the side. She won't budge. Place the cork on the surface of the water - when you blow it, it begins to move easily. You will see that the friction force in water is much less than the friction force between solid bodies.

And the cork floats on the surface of the water because two forces acting on it in opposite directions are equal to each other: gravity force and Archimedean force.

The body of the largest animal on Earth, the whale, is perfectly adapted for life in water. The largest representatives of the cetacean order are blue knits. The mass of the blue whale reaches 130 tons, but it is capable of reaching speeds of up to 20 knots in the water (a knot is a speed equal to one nautical mile per hour, and since a nautical mile is 1.852 km, a knot is a speed equal to 1.852 km/h). For comparison, we point out that the MKM motor boat can reach speeds of up to 30 km/h, that is, about 16 knots.

A sperm whale, weighing about 60 tons, jumps out of the water and rises several meters above its surface.
