M731. Two people play this game: the first one names a natural number from 2 to 9; the second multiplies this number by an arbitrary natural number from 2 to 9; then the first one multiplies the result by any natural number from 2 to 9, etc.; The winner is the one who first produces a product greater than a) thousand, 6) million. Who wins when played correctly - the beginner or his partner?

V. Boltyansky

M732. a) Triangle ABC describes two different rectangles so that the base AC contains two vertices of each rectangle (and one vertex each on the sides AB and BC). The perimeter of each rectangle is equal to 10. Find the area of ​​triangle ABC and prove that the perimeter of any rectangle inscribed in triangle ABC, whose two vertices lie on [AC], is also equal to 10.

6) * The quadrilateral ABCD describes two rectangles with parallel sides (so that on each side AB, BC, CD, DA there is one vertex of each rectangle). The perimeter of each of the rectangles is equal to 10. Find the area of ​​the quadrilateral ABCD and prove that for any point on any of the sides of the quadrilateral ABCD it is possible to construct an inscribed rectangle with a vertex at this point, the sides of which are parallel to the sides of these rectangles and the perimeter of which is also equal to 10

M734. The bisector of angle $A$ of triangle $ABC$ intersects its circumcircle at point $K$. Prove that the length of the projection of the segment $AK$ onto the line $AB$ (or $AC$) is equal to half the lengths of the sides $AB$ and $AC$.

R. Mazov

M735. a) Prove that a circle of diameter 1 cannot be covered with several paper strips whose total width is less than 1 (Fig. 1).
b) On the ground, a layer of thickness $h$ is part of the space enclosed between parallel planes, located at a distance $h$ from each other. Prove that a ball of diameter 1 cannot be covered with several layers whose total thickness is less than 1.

F743. At what minimum speed can a stone be thrown over a wall of height $H$ and thickness $l$, if thrown from a height $h$?

O. Savchenko

F744. The spacecraft is a rigid thin-walled sphere of radius $R=2$ m, directed by gas. Inside the apparatus there is a ball of radius $r=R/2$, directed by the same gas as the weight of the apparatus, but at higher pressure. The ball touches the inner surface of the apparatus. As a result of the damage, the ball burst. Find how many times the pressure inside the apparatus changed if it turned out that the weight of the apparatus shifted by a distance of $a=0,5$ m. Neglect the mass of the shells; assume the temperature remains constant.

A. Buzdin

F745. The thermally insulated vessel is divided into two parts by a heat-conducting piston, which can move into the vessel without friction. The left side of the vessel contains 1 mole of an ideal monatomic gas, the right side contains vacuum. The piston is contained with the right wall of the vessel by a spring (Fig. 2), the length of which in the free state is equal to the length of the vessel. Neglecting the heat suit of the vessel, piston and spring, determine the heat cost of the system.

B. Klyachin

F746. An electric battery using β-radioactivity is a metal sphere, inside of which a piece of radioactive substance isolated from it is placed (Fig. 3). $v$ atoms decay every second. Assuming that the energy of the electrons produced during decay is uniformly distributed from the minimum value $W_{\min}$ to the maximum value $W_{\max}$, determine the emf of the battery. What is the maximum current that such a battery can produce? Up to what load resistance can a battery be considered a current generator?
