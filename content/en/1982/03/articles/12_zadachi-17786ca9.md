⟦folio 39⟧

Exercise

1. figure shows two number puzzles. In each of them, the same letters correspond to the same numbers, and different numbers correspond to different ones. Decipher these puzzles.

2. Cut the figure shown in the figure into four congruent parts and fold them into a square so that the red circles and blue squares are symmetrical with respect to all symmetry axes of the square.

3. From the three “patterns” shown on figure, assemble three cubes; then a rectangular parallelepid $1 \times 1 \times 3$ was glued together from these cubes. Four three-digit numbers were written on the four faces of the parallelepid (the position of the number in the square does not matter). What is the most possible value for the sum of these numbers?

4. There are seven boxes on the table. The middle box is empty, and the other six boxes contain two coins: a small one - a nickel, and on top - a “kopeck piece”.
It is allowed to transfer one coin at a time to the adjacent box, but you cannot put a nickel on a “kopeck piece”.
In addition, the fifth box should not contain more than three coins, and the rest - more than two.
Is it possible to rearrange the coins so that a) the first three boxes contain two “kopeck pieces” each, and the last three - two nickels each, b) the first three boxes contain two nickels each, and the last three - two “kopeck pieces” each?

These tasks were offered to us
N. Aktonovich,
F. Bartenev, A. Shvetsov
