bar. The work $A_1$, taking into account Hooke’s law, is equal to
$$A_1 = \frac{kx^2}{2} = \frac{k(F/k)^2}{2} = \frac{F^2}{2k},$$
and the ratio $A_2$ is given by
$$A_2 = Fs \cos \alpha.$$
The magnitude of the force $F$ depends, generally speaking, on how the block moves. We will assume that the movement occurs at a constant speed. (Obviously, at the initial stage, when the speed of the block increases from zero to some set value, our assumption is not valid. However, since the steady speed is small, this stage lasted very briefly and its contribution to the work does not exist.)
According to Newton’s second law, the sum of all forces acting on the block (force $\vec{F}$, gravitational force $mg$, normal support reaction force $\vec{N}$ and friction force $\vec{F}_{\tau p}$) during uniform motion is equal to zero:
$$\vec{F} + m\vec{g} + \vec{N} + \vec{F}_{\tau p} = 0,$$
or, in projections on the horizontal and vertical coordinate axes (make figure appropriate),
$$F \cos \alpha - F_{\tau p} = 0,$$
$$N + F \sin \alpha - mg = 0,$$
where $F_{\tau p} = \mu N$.
From here we will find
$$F = \frac{\mu mg}{\cos \alpha + \mu \sin \alpha}.$$
Finally
$$A = A_1 + A_2 = \frac{F^2}{2k} + Fs \cos \alpha =$$
$$= \frac{(\mu mg)^2}{2k(\cos \alpha + \mu \sin \alpha)^2} + \frac{\mu mgs}{1 + \mu tg \alpha} \approx 19 \text{ Дж.}$$

In the first case, the acceleration of the first body is provided by the tension force of the thread, and the acceleration of the second body is the difference between the projection of its gravity force on the direction of the tangent to the cylindrical surface and the tension force of the thread:
$$m_1 a = F_H,$$
$$m_2 a = m_2 g \sin \alpha - F_H.$$
From here we can find sin $\alpha$:
$$\sin \alpha = \frac{(m_1 + m_2) a}{m_2 g}.$$
In the second case, according to Newton's second law,
$$m_1 a' = F_H' - m_1 g \sin \alpha/2,$$
$$m_2 a' = m_2 g \sin \alpha/2 - F_H'.$$
where does the new acceleration of bodies come from?
$$a' = \frac{(m_2 - m_1) g \sin \alpha/2}{m_1 + m_2}.$$
The value of sin $\alpha/2$ can be found from the well-known trigonometric formula $2 \sin^2 \alpha/2 = 1 - \cos \alpha$:
$$\sin \alpha = \left(\frac{1 - \cos \alpha}{2}\right)^{1/2} = \left(\frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \alpha}}{2}\right)^{1/2}.$$
As a result we get
$$a' = g \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \left(\frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \alpha}}{2}\right)^{1/2} \approx 2.2 \text{ м/c}^2.$$
3. After the current in the circuit stops, the voltage on both capacitors will be the same (we denote it by $U$). It can be related to the initial voltage $U_1$ and $U_2$ using the charge conservation law:
$$C_1 U_1 + C_2 U_2 = (C_1 + C_2) U,$$
And
$$U = \frac{C_1 U_1 + C_2 U_2}{C_1 + C_2}.$$
According to the law of conservation of energy, the amount of heat released in the resistor is equal to the difference between the initial and final energies of the charged capacitors:
$$Q = \frac{C_1 U_1^2}{2} + \frac{C_2 U_2^2}{2} - \frac{(C_1 + C_2) U^2}{2} =$$
$$= \frac{C_1 C_2 (U_1 - U_2)^2}{2 (C_1 + C_2)}.$$
Note that the voltage difference $U_1 - U_2$ at the moment the switch is closed is equal to the voltage between the positive plates of the first and second capacitor. Then, according to Ohm's law for a section of the chain
$$U_1 - U_2 = IR.$$
Finally
$$Q = \frac{C_1 C_2 (IR)^2}{2 (C_1 + C_2)} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ Дж.}$$
4. Once on a grounded screw, each proton is decelerated and transfers its particle energy $mv^2/2$ to the target. Per unit time, $N = I/e$ protons approach the target. Therefore, thermal power
$$P = \frac{mv^2}{2} N = \frac{mv^2}{2} \frac{I}{e}.$$
The speed of protons $v$ can be found using Newton's second law:
$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = evB,$$
And
$$v = \frac{eBr}{m}.$$
Thus,
$$P = \frac{mv^2}{2} \frac{I}{e} = \frac{B^2 Ir^2}{2} \frac{e}{m} = 2 \text{ Вт.}$$
