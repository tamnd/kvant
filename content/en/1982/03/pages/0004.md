⟦folio 2⟧

Anniversary of a wonderful article

Sixty years ago, in March 1922, V. I. Lenin’s work “On the Significance of Militant Materialism” was first published in the magazine “Under the Banner of Marxism.”
It clearly identifies several very important tasks that have not received much activity in our day.

First of all, the task is to strengthen and develop the union of Marxist philosophy and natural philosophy. Back in 1909, in his book “Materialism and Empirio-Criticism,” Lenin convincingly showed that during the period of the greatest revolution in physics, associated with the discovery of radioactivity and the complex structure of atoms, the creation of the special theory of relativity, etc., many outstanding scientists were unable to correctly assess the essence of the events taking place. They perceived them as a deep crisis of physics, a vision of a number of its basic concepts and laws, the collapse of materialism and the triumph of idealism in a wide variety of forms. A scientific analysis of the revolution in physics and an accurate forecast of its further development could only be made from the position of dialectical materialism, which was done by V.I. Lenin.

The struggle between materialism and idealism became even more acute in subsequent years in connection with the emergence of quantum mechanics and the general theory of relativity. And here victory was on the side of dialectical materialism. The ideas developed by Lenin in his brilliant book, Lectureze, have stood the test of time.

To correctly assess the role and place of physics in the need to expand the family of natural sciences, in understanding the nature around us, knowledge of its laws and the ability to correctly solve specific physical problems is absolutely not enough. It is necessary to master the basics of the Marxist scientific worldview. This should be clearly understood by everyone who wants to become a physicist or mathematician. “...without a solid philosophical foundation, no natural sciences, no materialism can withstand the struggle against the onslaught of bourgeois ideas and the restoration of the bourgeois worldview,” as V.I. Lenin wrote in his work “On the Significance of Militant Materialism.” This means that even during school years, having successfully mastered physics and mathematics, one must study social sciences no less persistently.

The bourgeois worldview has always been dark with religion and mysticism, striving to reconcile the broad masses of the people with political and social inequality, continuously growing exploitation by entrepreneurs who recognize mortality, submission to fate, and non-resistance to the invading evil. And the more complex the specific social situation in each capitalist country turned out to be, the more actively religious ideas were propagated. Never, for example, in the United States has there been such activity among Eastern religious cults, various threshold-fiendish sects, fortune-tellers, prophets, and astrologers, as in our day.

Religious ideas still persist in the minds of some of our citizens. The church in the USSR is separated from the state, and each of you has complete freedom to choose one or another worldview. But this does not mean at all that those who are atheists should not conduct active anti-religious propaganda, be, in Lenin’s words, “militant atheists.” Modern science gives you a lot of convincing evidence of the inconsistency of religious ideas about the “creation of the world”, the emergence and development of man and human society. They should be widely used for the purposes of anti-religious propaganda. This propaganda must be carried out patiently, persistently, convincingly, individually, without offending the religious feelings of believers. Every citizen we win from the church is a real victory in the construction of a communist society. This is the second task posed by V.I. Lenin in this remarkable article.
