Complex numbers

Here I first of all very briefly talk about how complex numbers arose in mathematics and gradually established themselves in it. Then I give a definition of complex numbers, operations on them and their geometric interpretation. Along the way, formulas for cosine and sine of a sum are proved, which are closely related to the multiplication of complex numbers.

Historical background

From the course of mathematics it is known that negative numbers were introduced primarily so that the subtraction operation, the inverse of the addition operation, was always possible. For a similar reason, complex numbers appeared in mathematics. If we consider only real numbers, then the operation of extracting a square root, the inverse of the operation of squaring, is not always possible, since it is impossible to extract the square root of a negative number. This, however, is not enough to introduce new numbers in mathematics. It turned out that if the product of a calculation according to general rules over expressions in which the square root of a negative number occurs, then one can arrive at a result that no longer contains the square root of a negative number. In the 16th century, Kardanov found a formula for solving the cubic equation («Kvant», 1976, No. 9, p. 2). It turned out that it is precisely in the case when the cubic equation has three real roots that the square root of a negative number is found in the Cardan formula and that completely understandable results can be obtained. Therefore, these roots began to be used in mathematics. Extracting calculations with expressions containing the square root of a negative number can produce fully understandable results. Gauss gave full civil rights to at least the numbers on the verge of XVIII-XIX centuries («Kvant», 1977, No. 8, p. 2), who calls them complex numbers, gave them a geometric interpretation and, most importantly, proved the fundamental theorem of algebra, which states that every polynomial has at least one real or complex root.

Definition of complex numbers

We will assume that we know the real numbers. We know that there are two basic operations defined for them - addition and multiplication - and there are inverse actions to them - subtraction and division. For these actions, well-known rules are followed, which are usually used completely automatically - therefore they will not be formulated here. The set of objects for which the actions of addition and multiplication and their inverse actions of subtraction and division are defined, and the objects of the rules that apply to real numbers are fulfilled, is called a field in modern abstract algebra.

Thus, from the point of view of modern abstract algebra, the set R of all real numbers is represented as a field.
