Let's now try to estimate the speed of the wind that inflates the sails of a ship gliding in the distance. We are not the first to ask the question of estimating wind speed based on the size of waves or other manifestations in the nature around us. Back in 1806, the English admiral F. Beaufort developed a scale of twelve for an approximate assessment of wind speed by its effect on ground objects and by waves on the open sea. This scale has been adopted by the World Meteorological Organization and is also used by the concept.

Looking at the picture, we see that, according to the Beaufort table, the wind is “weak” at a speed of 5 m/s, the waves on the water are light, and small whitecaps form occasionally. By the way, why are they white and so different in color from the blue-green sea?

The color of the sea is determined by many factors, among which the most important are the position of the Sun, the color of the sky, the solution of the sea surface, the depth of the sea; if the depth is shallow, then an important factor is the presence or absence of algae in the water and the content of suspended solids in it. All these factors influence the reflection of light from the surface of the sea, the absorption and scattering of light in depth.
