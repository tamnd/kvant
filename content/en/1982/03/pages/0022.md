Math circle

M. Evgrafov

Mechanics of the magic cube

In response to numerous requests from readers, we are publishing an article about the structure of the “Hungarian hinged cube” («Kvant», 1980, No. 12). The article describes in detail how the “Hungarian Cube” can be made in a school workshop. We will return to the mathematical side of the matter in one of the following issues, when the cube made for you is in your hands.

Externally, the “magic cube” of the Hungarian architect E. Rubik appears to be a cube, as if cut into 27 equal small cups (of course, only 26 of them are visible). The small cubes are aligned in such a way that any layer of 9 cups adjacent to one face of the large cube can be freely rotated around its axis (see figure above). It is amazing that the entire system does not disintegrate; not a single one of the small cups is removed separately. The outer edges of the small cups have stickers in six different colors (nine stickers of each color); You need to rearrange the small cubes by rotating the layers so that each face of the large cube is painted the same color.

Many articles have been published on how to do this (for example, «Kvant», 1980, No. 12 and “Hayka and Life,” 1981, No. 3). The structure of the “Hungarian Cube” was not discussed in detail in these articles, which is quite natural. Having a cube, it is not difficult to take it apart and see how it works. My article was written for those who do not have the opportunity to solve the problem the hard way. I myself was in exactly this position, but I was lucky: I asked one of my acquaintances for the words “Hungarian Cube”, repaired it and returned it to the owner. With the knowledge I gained, it was no longer difficult for me to make my own cube.

How does he crit?

The “Hungarian Cube” is made up of 27 main parts (not counting colored stickers, springs and other little things). These parts are a three-dimensional cross hidden inside and 26 “cubes” extending out.

“Cubes” are not cubes at all. They were of three different types depending on their location on the faces of the large cube: central cubes (there are 6 of them, they are located in the center of each edge), middle cubes (there are 12 of them, they are located in the middle of each edge) and corner cubes (there are 8 of them, they are located at the vertices of the large cube).
