⟦folio 58⟧

Tasks
our readers

1. Prove that if $1 \leq x \leq 5$, then the equation $x^n + y^n = z^n$ has no solutions in natural numbers.

V. Duntsev

2. Factor the polynomial $x^8 + 4x^2 + 4$.

E. Turkewitz

3. Let $S(n)$ be the sum of the digits in double notation of the natural number $n$. Prove that among the pairs $(m; n)$ of distinct natural numbers $m$ and $n$ there is an infinite set such that $\frac{m}{S(m)} = \frac{n}{S(n)}$. Solve a similar problem for the decimal system.

L. Khanin

4. In a triangle, the sum of the squares of the side lengths is $m^2$, and the sum of their fourth powers is $n^4$. Find the area of ​​this triangle.

N. Trifonov

5. a) Prove that for each $n \geq 3$ in the plane there are $n$ circles with different centers such that any segment connecting the centers of two circles intersects at least one circle with another center.

6) Prove that there exists a $m$ such that it is impossible to arrange $m$ circles on the plane so that the lucentres of two circles intersect with the remaining $m-2$ circles.

7. A thin conducting ring rotates with angular velocity $\omega$ around an axis passing through the centers of the ring and lying in its plane (Fig. 3): The mass of the ring is $m$, its radius is $r$. Determine the kinetic energy of the ring.

E. Lifshits

8.** Let $p \neq 2$ be a prime number. Consider all natural numbers less than $p$. Let us denote by $S_1, S_2, S_3, \dots, S_{p-2}$ the sums of products made up of these numbers, taken one at a time, two at a time, three at a time, etc., that is, $S_1 = 1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + (p-1)$, $S_2 = 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 3 + \dots + (p-2) \cdot (p-1)$, $S_3 = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 4 + \dots + (p-3) \cdot (p-2) \cdot (p-1)$, $S_{p-2} = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \cdot (p-2) + 1 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \cdot (p-2) \times (p-1) + \dots + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \times (p-2) \cdot (p-1)$.

a) Prove that all these sums are divisible by $p$. Note. Use this statement: if a polynomial with integer coefficients of degree less than $p$ is divisible by $p$ at all integer points, then all its coefficients are divisible by $p$.

6) Prove that the sums $S_3, S_5, S_7, \dots, S_{p-2}$ are divisible by $p^2$.

This task is for those who have to deal with biomial coefficients (see, for example, the article by N. Vasiliev and A. Zelevinsky in No. 1).

I. Weinstein
