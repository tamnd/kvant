⟦folio 7⟧

V. Litsevsky

Daniel Bernoulli

...for me the true pleasure of my whole life lies in the Russian imperial service...
D. Bernoulli

They say that nature, having created a genius, then rests, creating his descendants. And this is mostly true. But there are no rules without exceptions, and one of them is the Bernoulli family. Over the course of a hundred years, the Bernoulli family gave man a tree of ten outstanding scientists - mathematicians, physicists, doctors, lawyers, rhetoricians... Many of them were members of various academies, five were academicians of the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences.

The more famous brothers are Jacob (1654-1705) and Johann (1667-1748) Bernoulli and Johann's son Daniel.

Daniel Bernoulli was born on January 29, 1700 in Groingen (Holland), where his father taught mathematics at the university. In 1705, the family moved to Basel (Switzerland), where Johann Bernoulli “inherited” the position of professor of mathematics after the death of his older brother Jacob.

Daniel studied at the Basel Gymnasium. After graduating from high school in 1713, he was sent to France to improve the meaning of the French language. After returning to his homeland in 1716, he received the title of Master of Philosophy.

At the insistence of his father, Daniel began studying medicine. He studied in Heidelberg, in Strasbourg, and after defending his dissertation “On Breathing” in 1720 he became a licentiate in medicine. But Daniel’s heart was not in medical practice. “...The example of his family members, namely his father and older brother Nikolai, as well as the inclinations of his own soul, attracted him to the mathematical sciences and to the study of nature,” this is how Daniel Bernoulli wrote about himself in the third person in his “Autobiography,” preserved in the archives of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

In 1724, Daniel Bernoulli's first scientific treatise, “Mathematical Exercises,” was published. In the same year he receives an offer to head the academy in Genoa. While Daniil is reasonable, an invitation came from Russia to enroll in the St. Petersburg Academy, which had just been created by Peter I.

The offer was replaceable, but Daniel did not want to part with his brother Nikolai, with whom he was connected by a touching friend. The difficulty was resolved very simply. The then president of the St. Petersburg Academy, L. L. Blumentrost, invited both Bernoulli, and in October 1725 he began to move to St. Petersburg. Daniil received the Department of Physiology, Nikolai - the Department of Mathematics.
