Problem 2. Light from the Sun hits a flat mirror with area $S = 1 \text{ m}^2$ at an angle $\alpha = 60^\circ$. Find the force of light pressure, assuming that the mirror completely reflects all the light shining on it. It is known that the average power of solar radiation arriving at 1 $\text{ m}^2$ of the earth's surface, overlying the radiation, is equal to $P = 1,4 \cdot 10^3 \text{ Bt/m}^2$.

First of all, let's discuss why light pressure exists at all and how to explain it from a quantum point of view.

Photons incident on the mirror are elastically reflected from it, and the momentum of the photons changes. This change is caused by the impulse of force acting from the mirror on the photons at the moment of reflection (interaction with the mirror). According to Newton's third law, exactly

**Fig. 2.**

the same force (in absolute value) will act on the mirror from the side of photons (Fig. 2). It creates light pressure.

Now let's do the calculation. Let us first find the impulse of force acting on the mirror during the time interval $\Delta t$ from photons with energies $h v_i$, where $v_i$ is some fixed frequency. Let the concentration of such photons in the incident flux be equal to $n_i$. Then in time $\Delta t$ $n_i c \Delta t S \cos \alpha$ photons will hit the mirror (c is the speed of light). Their total momentum before interaction with the mirror is $P_1 = (n_i c \Delta t S \cos \alpha) mc = n_i S \cos \alpha \cdot h v_i \Delta t$.

After reflection, the impulse changes in direction (the angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence), but remains the same in magnitude:

$$P_2 = P_1.$$

Thus, the change in momentum $\Delta P = |\vec{P}_2 - \vec{P}_1| = 2 n_i h v_i S \cos^2 \alpha \cdot \Delta t,$

and the force of pressure on the mirror from photons of a given type

$$F_i = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = 2 n_i h v_i S \cos^2 \alpha.$$

The total pressure force exerted by all photons

$$F = \sum F_i = 2 S \cos^2 \alpha \cdot \sum n_i h v_i.$$
