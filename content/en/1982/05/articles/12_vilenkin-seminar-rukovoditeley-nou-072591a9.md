Seminar head of NOU

The Board of the Ministry of Education of the USSR, the Secretary of the Central Committee of the Komsomol, the Predidum of the All-Union Council of Scientific and Technical Societies, the Board of the All-Union Society "Znanie" held an inter-republican seminar-meeting of the leaders and organizers of scientific societies of students on December 2-4, 1981 in Kishchinna. The purpose of the meeting was to further improve activities to develop the cognitive activity of schoolchildren, to promote among them the achievements of science, technology and production, literature and art, to generalize the experience of scientific societies of students, to strengthen connections between schools and industrial enterprises, challenges, and scientific institutions.

About 200 representatives from all resource groups came to the meeting. The program of the meeting included the work of sections, a round table discussion, and a visit to busy school sections of the Moldavian Non-Governmental Educational Institution “Vintorul”. The meeting was attended by not only representatives of such well-known NOUs as “Vintorul”, which supplies schoolchildren from all over Moldavin, Lenin Komsomol Prize winner Chelyabinsk NOU, Simferopol, Volgograd, but also the heads of very Moldovan school NOUs.

In the speech of the participants, it was noted that the NOU is very important, since members of the NOU working in sections related to the election, subsequently choose a profession related to the profession of their work in the NOU. There are great opportunities for non-state educational institutions in terms of small-scale automation in small enterprises that do not have qualified specialists in this field. These opportunities are used - an agreement is often concluded between such a representative and the NOU section, and a member of the section develops an instrument and device, which are then implemented at this enterprise. At the same time, students feel resistance to creative work and see the results of their work and its social usefulness. In total, during the meeting there were 100 proposals to improve and improve the work and management of non-state educational institutions. The organizers presented the participants of the seminar with methodological literature about the work of non-governmental educational institutions in Kishchinnev, Chelyabinsk, Volgograd, Ivanovo, Rostov-on-Don, Kyiv, Moscow and other cities.

Let us note that materials highlighting the work experience of the best educational institutions are regularly published on the pages of the magazines “Mathematics at School”, “Physics at School”, “Public Education”, “Education of Schoolchildren”, “Young Technician”, “Technology and Science” and «Kvant».

Correspondence School of Programming

The twentieth lesson, published in the previous issue «Kvant», was the last in the three-year cycle of the Correspondence School of Programming. The time has come to look back and take stock of the first results.

* * *

When in 1979 the “Art of Programming” column was opened in «Kvant» and the first lesson of the Correspondence School was published, we, of course, did not know what our readers’ response to this endeavor would be. It must be said that among the forecasts there were also skeptical ones. However, the results exceeded the most optimistic expectations. The flow of letters from all corners of the USSR and from abroad with the inscription “ZShP, lesson 1”, ever increasing, directly filled the premises of the schools in the Information Department of the Computer Center of the Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences, headed by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A.P. Ershov. More than 4,000 letters arrived, each of which had to be carefully checked and a response quickly sent - and this without special inspectors! Then the organizers helped by bringing Novosibirsk youth programs to the rescue, the same schoolchildren as ZShP students, only with a greater programming background.

The organization of checking assignments gradually improved, and although there were no more full-time employees, enthusiasts were found in different cities of the country - 5 regional centers (ZShP branches) were created. Of course, not everything went smoothly - the lack of programming expertise among the magazine’s editors, the huge distance between the editorial office and the Novosibirsk authors of the lessons led to certain difficulties (in particular, annoying typos). Too many people wishing to study at the ZShP did not allow us to run two courses in parallel since 1980, and three since 1981, as originally planned.

But still we are pleased with the results. About 1,000 children - our first graduate - will receive certificates of completion of the three-year ZShP course in June. The best students filled the Novosibirsk summer schools of young programmers for a long time (see «Kvant», 1979, No. 12 and 1980, No. 12, as well as the article by G. Zvenigorodsky in the next issue), where they had the opportunity to directly work on modern computer technology, and the Leningrad Conference on School Information («Kvant» will write about it later).

The correspondence school of programming will continue its work (from the fall) in the same way as the quantum section “The Art of Programming”. It will publish materials not only for ZShP students, but also for everyone who is interested in programming and computer technology. And materials on this topic available to all readers will be published in the general section of the magazine.

However, this sorting is slow. The algorithm uses approximately *k* calls to the *inclusion* procedure (k is the number of array elements). The *n*th time this procedure is called, approximately *n* comparisons are performed. In general, the approximate number of times the procedure *comparison* can be performed can be estimated

$$1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + k = \frac{(k+1)}{2} k.$$

A sorting algorithm that requires a number of comparisons comparable to *k^2/2* cannot be called fast: in practical problems the number *k* was very large. Recall («Kvant» #2) that merge sort only requires *k* comparisons.

The *distribution* sorting method approaches the problem from a different position. This method is not used at all in comparison. It is designed to work with elements whose keys, like integers or words, consist of finite sets of characters and have a general form.

$$a_1 a_2 \dots a_i \dots a_l.$$

Each symbol here takes on a finite set, the number of *p* elements of which is relatively infinite (up to several tens). This is, in particular, the alphabet alphabet.

The basis of sorting by the distribution method is a cycle with a variable *i*, which has an initial value *l*, a boundary of 1 and a step equal to -1: first, the last letter of the key words is considered, then the penultimate ones, etc. until the first. Each time this loop is executed, all elements of the sorted file are distributed into *p* files - “pockets”; for alphabetic texts in Russian *p*=33 (32 letters plus space). The “pocket” file corresponding to the letter *a* contains elements whose keys have the letter *a* in the *i*-th position, the next “pocket” contains elements with keys whose *i*-th position contains the letter *b*, etc. Each execution of the loop ends by merging *p* “pocket” files into a single file, which serves as the object of the next execution of the loop.

The algorithm assumes that all keys have the same length. However, this is not a limitation when working with texts: the length of each word can be taken equal to the number *l* of positions allocated for key values ​​in the source document or in computer memory. If length
