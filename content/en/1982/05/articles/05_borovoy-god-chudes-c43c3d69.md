How long is the gamma quantum itself invisible in a cloud chamber?

There are two mandatory conditions for the birth of a couple. First, the energy of the gamma quantum must exceed the sum of the rest energies of the components of the pair: $E_\gamma > 2m_e c^2 = 1,02$ MeV. And secondly, there must be a nucleus or an electron, which will take on excess energy and momentum. Otherwise, the conservation of energy and momentum $^*$) is violated.

The birth of $e^+e^-$ pairs was due to one incomprehensible effect that researchers encountered back in 1930. Experiments have shown that hard gamma rays are absorbed in matter much more strongly than predicted by theory. As it turned out, the “anomalous” absorption was associated with the formation of pairs. And it is quite likely that, if Anderson and Blackett and Occalini had hesitated, part of the discovery of the positron would have belonged to physicists dealing with the absorption of gamma rays.

"The melody turns into a violin"

These are the words of Academician S.I. Vavilov. At one of his lectures, he talks about the reverse process of the angulation reaction, when a flying gamma quantum produces an electron + positron pair:

$$\gamma \rightarrow e^+ + e^-.$$

This process of transforming an electromagnetic field into matter was discovered almost simultaneously in 1933 by four groups of physicists - in France, Germany, England and the USA. By 1932, several groups of scientists were on the verge of discovering artificial nuclear expansion. E. Lawrence and M. Livingston (USA) began to create a new device - a cyclotron. In this device, particles were accelerated by an electric field, and a magnetic field diverted their trajectory. Thus, the beam could pass through the accelerating gap many times. It moved in a flat spiral, gaining energy and increasing its radius. By the beginning of the “year of miracles,” the Lawrence laboratory had a proton beam with an energy of 2.2 MeV. But the researchers immediately began installing a cyclotron with a gigantic (at that time) magnet weighing 74 tons.

A remarkable electrostatic generator was built back in 1929 by Van de Graaff at Princeton University (USA). The model, created in 1931, could already accelerate particles up to an energy of 1.5 MeV at also 25 μA.

We will not dwell here on the work of other groups. They all walked towards the same goal, walked parallel or almost identical paths and almost “head to head.” But the first to arrive was a group from the Cavendish Laboratory.

In the thick volume of the proceedings of the Royal Society there is a detailed article by Cockcroft and Walton describing an electrostatic generator for accelerating protons and other heavy ions. She built the editorial office on February 23, 1932.

The experimenters removed a beam of protons with an energy of about 0.7 MeV from the tube through a thin mica window and... began to study their absorption in air depending on the magnitude of the applied voltage. At the same time, thoughts were raised about modernizing the installation and replacing its parts (in the initial design, scrap parts were used, in part biscuit tins).

So, all groups of researchers violated the above commandment to some extent. This did not last long in Cavendish. According to the recollections of various individuals, the magnitude of the “storm” that Rutherford created for his students reached different “scores”.

The most relaxed, “calm” version is given by the same Alibon: “Rutherford kept asking whether Cockcroft and Walton were putting a target under the beam; but since they were completely immersed in measuring the dependence of probe on energy, they didn’t even try to set a target. Rutherford insisted and was angry at their slowness. So they made a lithium target and did the experiment just to get Rutherford to stop grumbling.” As a result, artificial nuclear expansion was discovered:

$$\frac{7}{3} \text{Li} + p \rightarrow \alpha + \alpha.$$

This is reported in a letter to the editor of Nature, sent on April 16, 1932.

Now let's talk very briefly about the Cockroft and Walton experiment itself.

Artificial distribution of the lithium nucleus (April 1932)

The Cockcroft-Walton installation was located in a large, bright room. You see her in the photo. On the left are four sections of the electrical system generator, on the right is an accelerating tube of two sections. The remaining columns are condensers or insulating supports.

The idea of ​​obtaining high quality voltage is very simple. If you charge $n$ with identical em bones to a voltage $U_0$ and connect them in series, then the potential difference at the ends of the circuit will be $U=nU_0$. The most difficult thing is to come up with a mechanism for recharging such a system. And this problem was solved by Cockroft and Walton extremely wittily.

The second most important part of the installation was an accelerated tube, consisting of two sections, each of which was supplied with voltage from a generator (up to 350 thousand volts). Thus, the maximum energy of the proton beam could be ~0.7 MeV.

After the “suggestion” made by Rutherford, an installation for studying the artificial distribution of the lithium nucleus appeared at the lower end of the accelerating tube (in the photograph it is covered with black matter). At first, the experimental scheme looked as shown in figure 4. A beam of protons fell onto a lithium mine, turned at an angle of 45° to the direction of the beam. The a-particles escaping from the mine hit a screen made of zinc sulfide. When an energy (several MeV) alpha particle hits such a screen, a flash of light occurs, which can be seen using a microscope.

Having started the experiment, the researchers began to gradually increase the voltage on the tube. When it reached a value of 125 kV, the first flashes from a-particles appeared. There were still few of them (5 pieces per minute at a current of 1 μA), but the more the voltage increased, the more often light points appeared. The number of flashes turned out to be proportional to the proton current.

A little more about accelerators

After 1932, the accelerator advanced by leaps and bounds. At the end of 1936, α-particles accelerated in a cyclotron exceeded in energy anything produced by natural preparations. As time passed, new ideas were born, and new types of accelerators were created. It was once calculated that every five times the energy of the accelerated particles increased by almost an order of magnitude. The accelerator became crowded in rooms, halls, and then in hangars. The time has come when you can only see the accelerator in its entirety from an airplane. If Cockroff and Walton's installation was "terribly expensive" (approximately 1,000 pounds sterling), then this figure was overgrown with so many zeros that only rich countries could afford to build an accelerator. But they too unite to create new giants.

Physicists had at their disposal beams of a wide variety of particles, and among them - deuterons (d) - nuclei of heavy hydrogen, deuterium D. They consist of one proton and one neutron, that is, they have a charge of +1 and a mass of 2.

An amazing fate connects the neutron and deuteron. The existence of both particles was predicted by Rutherford in 1920, and both particles were discovered in 1932.
