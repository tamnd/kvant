5. $$\frac{1}{4} \left( S + \sqrt{2} \sigma k \sqrt{1-l} \right).$$ Solution. Distance metrahedron $ABCD$; Let us denote by $K, L, M, N$ the midpoints of the edges $AC, BC, BD, AD$, respectively, by $H$ the distance between straight lines $AB$ and $CD$, and by $S_1$ the area of ​​the middle section $KLMN$ (Fig. 4). Let us show that the volume $V$ of the $ABCD$ metrahedron is equal to $$\frac{2}{3} S_1 H.$$

To do this, it is enough to use the metrahedron $ABCD$ to the “wedge” (triangular feature) $AA_1 A_2 BB_1 B_2$, choosing points $A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2$ so that $A_1 B_1 \parallel A_2 B_2 \parallel AB$, $|A_1 B_1| = |A_2 B_2| = |AB|$ and $C$, $D$ are the midpoints of the segments $A_2 B_2$ and $A_1 B_1$, respectively (Fig.5). Then the distance $H$ from the line $AB$ to the line $CD$ is equal to the distance from any point of the line $AB$ to the plane $A_1 B_1 B_2 A_2$, and therefore the volume $V_2$ of the wedge is equal to $$\frac{1}{2} S_2 H,$$ where $S_2$ is the area of the parallelogram $A_1 B_1 B_2 A_2$ (see. Fig. 6); the volume of the direct sign $A_3 A'_2 A_1 B_3 B'_2 B_1$ is very little equal to $$\frac{1}{2} S_2 H$$ and coincides with the volume of the wedge $AA_2 A_1 BB_2 B_1$). Next, from the similarity of $KLMN$ and $A_2 B_2 B_1 A_1$ with a coefficient of 1/2 we have $S_2 = 4S_1$. It remains to show that $V_2 = 3V$. Let $F$ be the middle of $AB$; then the $CDF$ plane splits the $ABCD$ metrahedron into two equal volumes, each equal to $1/6V_2$ (e.g. $V_{CDFB} = \frac{1}{3} V_{CDFB_2 B_1 B_2} = \frac{1}{6} V_2$). Formula $V = \frac{2}{3} S_1 H$ has been proven.

The area of ​​the desired section is equal to $$\frac{1}{4} S + S_1,$$ where $S_1$ is the area of ​​the middle section of the metrahedron $BCDE$, parallel to the edges $BC$ and $DE$ (which is equal to the area $KLMN$), and $S$ is given (see Fig. 7). The volume of the metrahedron $BCDE$ is $V$, and the distance between the lines $BC$ and $DE$ is $H$, so it remains to calculate $V$ and $H$ to find $S_1$ from the formula $V = \frac{2}{3} S_1 H$. Let $r$ be the radius of the ball hanging at $BCDE$, $s$ the distance from the center of the hanging ball to the straight line $DE$. Then $$\frac{s}{H} = k, \quad r = s \sin \frac{\alpha}{2},$$ where $\alpha$ is the value of the dihedral angle at edge $DE$. Means,

$$V = \frac{1}{3} r \sigma = \frac{1}{3} s \left( \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \right) \sigma =$$

$$= \frac{1}{3} k H \left( \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \right) \sigma;$$

$$S_1 = \frac{3}{2} \frac{V}{H} = \frac{1}{2} k \left( \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \right) \sigma.$$
