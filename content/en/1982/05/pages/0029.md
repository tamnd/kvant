F715. How does the voltage between points A and B (Fig. 1) depend on the resistance of the resistor R?

Let's construct a vector diagram for the circuit (Fig. 2). The currents flowing through capacitor C and resistor R are the same; this means that the vectors $\vec{U}_{MR}$ and $\vec{U}_{MC}$ have been redistributed. Their sum is equal to the vector $\vec{U}_M$; this means that the end of the vector $\vec{U}_{MR}$ lies on the circle,

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

whose diameter is $|\vec{U}_M|$. The voltage $U_{MR}$ across a resistor with resistance $r$ is equal to $U_M/2$. Consequently, the desired stress $U_{MAB}$ is numerically equal to the radius of the constructed circle, that is

$$U_{MAB} = \frac{1}{2} U_M$$ and does not depend on the value of $R$.

When $R$ changes, the phase shift angle between vectors $\vec{U}_M$ and $\vec{U}_{MAB}$ changes, so that the proposed circuit can serve as a phase shifter. (Often in radio engineering it is necessary to change the phase of an alternating voltage, leaving the amplitude unchanged. In practice, instead of a resistor divider $r$, a transformer with a tap from the middle of the winding is usually used.)

F716. Hoops were made from a piece of thin steel strip of width d, in which a small hole of radius r was punched, and placed on the table so that the hole was at the bottom. From this position, the hoops were shifted a little and left to their own devices. What is the maximum swing speed of the hoop?

The description in the conditions of the initial position of the hoop is unstable. Left to its own devices, the hoops begin to move. If the friction is high enough, the hoops will lift without slipping. The speed of the hoop will be maximum at the moment when its potential energy is minimum, that is, when the center of gravity of the hoop is at its lowest position. This condition is satisfied at the moment when the hole is at the top at a distance $D$ from the table equal to the diameter of the hoop. It is not difficult to report that the decrease in the potential energy of the hoop when the hole moves from the lower position (at the beginning of the movement) to the upper is equal to

$$\Delta \Pi = \Delta m \cdot gD = \frac{M \pi r^2}{\pi Dd} gD = \frac{M r^2 g}{d},$$

where $M$ is the mass of the entire hoop, $\Delta m = \frac{M}{\pi Dd} \pi r^2$ is the “mass of the hole” ($\Delta m \ll M$).

This change in potential energy is equal to the kinetic energy of the hoop at the moment when the hole is at the top at a distance $D$ from the table equal to the diameter of the hoop. The energy of rotational motion of the hoop as a whole is $Mv^2/2$ ($v$ is the speed of the center of mass of the hoop at a given moment) and the energy of rotational motion is $Mu^2/2$ ($u$ is the linear speed of all points of the hoop).
