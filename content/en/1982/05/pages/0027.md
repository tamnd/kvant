This solution is also suitable for a more general case, when the role of a square is played by a black parallelogram (Fig. 2): we will prove that the red straight lines (respectively, the parallel sides of the black parallelogram) form a parallelogram that is similar to the black parallelogram.

To prove it, it is enough to check that $\frac{x}{a} = \frac{b}{v}$ and $\frac{a}{y} = \frac{u}{b}$ (for notation, see figure 3). Combining these equalities, we obtain $\frac{x}{y} = \frac{u}{v}$, which means that point K lies on EG.

The result obtained is reminiscent of Papna's theorem, which D. Hilbert and S. Cohn-Vossen formulate in their remarkable (recently republished in Russian) book “Visual Geometry” as follows (pp. 126-127): if the vertices of a closed six-link broken line lie alternately on two straight lines and two pairs of its opposite links are parallel, then the third pair of links is parallel (on figure 3 is also a broken AKBEFGA).

M705. On a rectangular sheet of flying paper there are several rectangular cards, the sides of which lie on the grid lines. The cards cover the sheet in two layers (that is, each card has dimensions of 1x2 cells. Prove that it is possible to select some of the cards so that they cover the sheet in one layer. (You cannot translate the cards).

Will this remain true if the cards b) can have arbitrary sizes, c*) have dimensions of 1x3 cells?

a) We will prove a more general statement: from covering any finite set of cells with cards of size 1 × 2 cells (subject to the conditions of the problem), we can select a part of the cards so that each card in the set is covered equal to one line. It is easy to see that the resulting chain has an even number of cards (make sure of this!). Color all the cards in the chain in two colors: with four numbers - in one color, with odd numbers - in another. It is easy to see that our set of cells with cards of each color turned out to be covered equal to one straight line.

Compose the uncovered cells of the set and do the same until all the cells of the original set are covered equal to one straight line.
