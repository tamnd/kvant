Physics

Written exam

Faculty of Physics

Each one creates a state of five tasks of three types. Five hours were given to solve the problems.

The first three tasks are computational, of varying difficulty: from standard to comparative, requiring ingenuity, deep knowledge, and the ability to understand an unusual or conditional physical situation.

The fourth task is an assessment task. To solve it, you need to understand the physical phenomenon under consideration, form a simple (since only an estimate is needed) physical model of this phenomenon, select mutual values ​​of physical quantities, and finally obtain a numerical result that more or less corresponds to reality. In the text of the problem, I suggest that the applicant can choose the physical factors necessary to solve the problem and highlight the main one.

The work was evaluated depending on the amount of points scored. For tasks of varying degrees of difficulty, different numbers of points were assigned: for $N_1=1-4$, $N_2=2-6$, $N_3=8$, $N_4=5$, $N_5=3$. The corresponding part of the points was awarded for a particular solution to the problem. Below, after the text of each problem, the percentage of those who solved it is indicated in parentheses (in this case, the problem was considered solved if at least half of the total number of points was scored for it).
