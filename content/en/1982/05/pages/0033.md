The concept of flow, of course, is not only relevant to the consideration of a case; it is very widely used in physics.

Let there be a beam of particles moving in space in the same direction with the same speed $v$. The number of particles per unit volume of the beam, as in the linear case, is also called concentration $n$ (or volume density) and has a dimension $1/\text{m}^3$. The product $\Phi = nv$ now has dimension $1/(m^2 \cdot c)$ and is equal to the number of particles passing every second through a unit sectional area perpendicular to the direction of motion of the beam. Therefore, the quantities $\Phi$ are called particle flux density; By multiplying its value by the cross-sectional area of ​​the beam, we obtain the total flow of particles in the beam.
