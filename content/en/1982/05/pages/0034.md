if $a = q$ is the electronic charge carried by each part, then $\Phi_q = qnv$ is the electron charge flux density (amount of electrons). The quantity $\Phi_q$, which has a dimension $KL/(m^2 \cdot c)$, is also called the electron current density, since the strength of the electron current is the amount of electrons passing per unit time through the cross section of the conductor. (This also follows from the messages of dimensions: $KL/(m^2 \cdot c) = (KL/c)/m^2 = A/m^2$.) Other densities of fluxes (energy, momentum, etc.) are determined similarly.

Let us now consider several specific problems that will help you better understand the concepts of flow or density.

1. Try to estimate the capacity of a highway, that is, the maximum number of automatic vehicles that can pass along a given road in an hour.

Such calculations are necessary when designing roads and for proper organization of traffic, especially in large cities and on major international highways.

Let the highway be two-way, and in each direction traffic occurs in two lanes with a maximum permissible speed of $v = 60$ km/h (these are, for example, the traffic conditions on the Moscow Automatic Ring Road). What interests us is the flow of automation, that is, the number of automations passing per unit of time mismo motionless is observed. Using the formulas obtained above, we will write $\Phi = Nnv$, where $N=4$ is the total number of traffic lanes, $n$ is the number of cars per unit length of the road, which can be associated with the distance $L$ between automatic machines: $n = 1/L$. Thus, $\Phi = Nv/L$. Manuals for operating automatic machines recommend keeping a distance between automatic machines equal in meters to half the speed expressed in kilometers per hour. In our case, at a speed of $v = 60$ km/h $L = 30$ m, and the highway capacity is

$$\Phi = N \frac{v}{L} = 8000 \text{ 1/ч.}$$
