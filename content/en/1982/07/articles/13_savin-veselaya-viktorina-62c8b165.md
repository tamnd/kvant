10. There are six glasses in a row on the shelf, like at figure: three with water and three empty. How to achieve an arrangement in which empty and full glasses alternate, if only one glass is allowed to be picked up at each move?

11. Find two numbers whose sum, product and quotient are equal.

We also offer several games that can be used in modern ones, for example, in captain competitions in mathematical KVN. In each of them there is a winning strategy for one of the players: the starter or the second. Try to determine who wins in a board game and find a winning strategy.

1. There are two piles of stones. Two players alternately make the following moves: they take one of the piles and divide the second into two parts. If the player cannot divide the pile during his move (since there is only one stone left in it), then he loses.

2. Several pegs are driven into the ground. The two take turns tying themselves together with twine from a pair of pegs. The winner is the game that results in the formation of a closed broken line made up of strings. (It is not allowed to connect the string between two previously connected pegs.)

3. Two people take turns picking off the petals of a chamomile, and at one time you can tear off either one petal or two adjacent petals. The one who makes the last move wins.
