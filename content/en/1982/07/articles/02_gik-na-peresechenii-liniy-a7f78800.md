AT THE CROSSING OF LINES

Today we will look at several practical examples of using geometric motifs on a chessboard.

The double strike (fork), an ancient motif familiar to every chess fan, also contains a geometric element.

F. Olafsson - A. Karpov (XXIV Chess Olympada, 1980)
34. F:f7. Linen in ceyton carelessly takes a pawn. 34. Bf2 was correct, and 34...R:f2 should be followed by 35. Qf5+ with perpetual check. 34...R:g2+ 35. Npf1 Rg6 36. Re2 (36. Bf2 Kd2+) 36...Qb1+ 37. Re1. Everything goes towards repeating moves, but suddenly geometry comes into play. 37...Qa2! A dab double strike. Horizontally there is a threat of 38...Qg2×, and diagonally at 38. Le2 solves 38...Kd2+ and 39...Qf7. Olafson considered the geometric theorem proven and sold it.

A. Karpov - I. Chom (Bad Lauterberg, 1977)

Black has an extra knight and pawn, the enemy rook is under attack - things, apparently, are not so bad. However, it is difficult to fight the laws of geometry. 50. Nf5! The blacks surrendered. Here the queens again act on two lines at once, and Black is helpless. After 50...Nd7 the white queen moves along the free diagonal - 51. Qh2+! (this check also resolves in the case of 50...ef) 51...Kg8 52. Qg3+ Kpf7 53. Qg7×. If the threat along the b8-h2 diagonal is interrupted - 50...Qb8, then the queen manifests herself along the back horizontal: 51. Rh7+! K:h7 52. Qg7×.

Theler will give three striking examples on the topics of overlap, coupling and distraction.

V. Simagin - D. Bronstein (Moscow, 1947)
Now a second black queen will appear on the board, and White seems to be forced to limit himself to perpetual check.
1. Bg5!! That's the overlap! The bishop cannot be taken either with a pawn - 1...fg 2. f6 with mate, or with a queen - 1...Q:g5 2. Qc8+, 3. Qc7+ and 4. Q:h2 with a decisive material advantage. After 1...h1 F2. Qe8+ Kg7 3. Qg6+ Kpf8 4. Q:f6+ Kg8 5. Qd8+ Kph8 6. Qe7+ Kg8 7. Qe8+ Black resigned, due to the inevitable mate - 7...Kpg7 (7...Kph7 8. Qg6+ Nph8 9. Bf6×) 8. f6+ Kh7 9. Qf7+ Kh8 10. Qg7×.

Handel - Sushkevich, 1956
1...R:g3+! 2. Rxg3 Rg8!!

Double bind! The white rook g3 is tied both vertically (there is no move 3. Rxh3) and horizontally (after 3. Rxg8 it loses its queens). V. at the same time threatens R:g3+. The Whites surrendered.

Long and unexpected queen moves always make an impression. Of course, a significant role is played here by the geometric element - the effective movement of the figure in space. And the longer the path the queen travels, the stronger the natural impact on the reader.

Panchik - Shurada, 1978
1. Qa8!! Excellent execution of the theme of distraction. The game ended immediately, because Black was left without a piece - both after 1...R:a8 2. N:e7+ and 3. N:c8, and after 1...Rb7 2. N:e7+ R:e7 3. Q:b8.
