Solar eclipse
July 31, 1981

The average rare astronomical phenomenon is the most impressive - a solar eclipse. I am sure that thousands of eyewitnesses of the total solar eclipse, which was observed on July 31, 1981 in the USSR, will agree with this statement. On this day, the lunar shadow covered a path of 8250 km across the Earth, of which about 7100 km was on land; There is rarely a hundred-dollar eclipse streak on the mainland. It is remarkable that along its entire path the moon’s shadow marveled across the territory of our country: from the coast of Georgia through the North Caucasus, Kazakhstan and Southern Siberia to the eastern borders of the Soviet Union. The distant shadow of the Moon marveled across the surface of the Pacific Ocean and ended its route near the Hawaiian Islands. The lunar shadow covered this entire long path in just 2.9 hours, and at each individual point on the Earth’s surface the duration of the total eclipse does not exceed 129 seconds. However, the author of this note, together with a group of astronomers and geophysicists, was lucky enough to observe the solar corona for 155 seconds from the Yak-40 aircraft, flying after the lunar shadow.

The diameter of the lunar shadow on the Earth's surface does not exceed 270 km. During the July 31 eclipse, the maximum size of the shadow was 108 km. By luck, the moon's shadow passed over many sections of the Baikal-Amur Mainline. Many amateur astronomers and professional astronomers and geophysicists gathered in the capital of BAM, Tynda, to observe the eclipse. But on the morning of July 31, the weather in Tynda was bad: cloudiness began almost at the very ground and stretched to a height of 7 km. Everyone was waiting for noon, when the eclipse was supposed to begin. The premonitions were the most gentle. It seemed that even our hope for the plane was not true: due to weather conditions, it was not allowed to take off. Not long before the eclipse, the fog near the ground cleared, and after desperate persuasion from the dispatcher, we were allowed to take off. We saw and formed the solar corona at an altitude of 7770 m against the backdrop of an almost black sky.

As they discovered after landing, in Tynda the eclipse began under completely cloudy conditions, but literally a few minutes before the moment of total eclipse, the clouds quickly cleared and the solar corona appeared in an almost clear sky. For their patience, the observers were rewarded with an amazing picture of a bright pearl halo that appeared for only 2 minutes around the disks of the Moon and the Sun, meeting for a short time, only to disperse again for a long time in their complex celestial dance.

Perhaps the reader will have a question: is this rare natural phenomenon worth the opportunity to keep people busy going to distant, sometimes uninhabited places and in these uninhabited places almost real astronomical observatories with complex expensive instruments, and all this for a few minutes of admiring the solar corona? And is it just love? It turns out that many astronomers who did not travel for eclipses never saw the corona of the Sun, although the weather was excellent. During the eclipse, scientists spent time at their instruments, afraid to even look away for a moment to look at the corona.

The answer to this question is undeniable: it’s worth it. The fact is that in short minutes, eclipsing the astronomical poll, one can obtain the most valuable observable material about the structure and physical conditions in the upper layers of the solar atmosphere. Research cannot be carried out during general hours either: the bright light of the sun's disk interferes. Therefore, solar eclipses will remain a desirable feature for astronomers and all lovers of celestial science for a long time.
