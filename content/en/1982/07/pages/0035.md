The plates consisted of mica plates (several centimeters in size with a thickness of 0.2-0.3 mm) and glass (9x12 cm photographic plates, washed from the emulsion, are very convenient). We did not use any deep knowledge about the nature of light, except one: that light is waves. We learned what kind of waves - continuous or transverse - from the analysis of the results obtained.

Disappearance of reflected light

Postponing the “mica effect” for later, we took up the effect of the disappearance of reflection and the presence of its methodical study.

We first noticed this phenomenon when looking at images of bright objects outside the window after two reflections. The first was in the window glass, the second in the glass plate that one of us was holding in his hands. Measuring the position of the glasses and following with our eyes the image of an object (for example, a light cloud low above the horizon), we discovered that with some images the image seemed to fade, greatly losing its brightness. The geometry of the ray path for this case is shown in figure 1. We will often encounter it, and for brevity we will call it geometry No. 1. Note that the planes of the first and second reflections are mutually perpendicular (we will call the reflection plane the plane in which the incident and reflected rays and the perpendicular to the glass surface lie), and the angles of incidence of the rays on both glasses (they were absent from the perpendicular to the glass surface) are close to 60° to the eye.

Understanding that the brightness of the image is determined by the amount of light reflected from the first and second glasses, we asked the question: at which of the two reflections - the first or the second - does the light reflectance decrease? By examining objects reflected once in glass, we were convinced that at whatever angle of incidence of the rays their reflection does not exhibit a minimum. Then we continued our experiments.

What and how does the effect depend on?

First of all, we changed the light source and repeated the experiment in geometry No. 1. We took an incandescent lamp (power 60 W) and placed it so that when you sit on a chair, the filament is there. slightly above the knees. The first reflection was in the glass from the bookshelf, which we placed vertically, and the second - from the glass plate, which the person sitting on the chair held in his hands in front of him at the level of the lamp filaments. Following the reflection of the thread with my eyes, I quickly managed to find a position in which the thread fades almost completely. The reflection weakened by eye at least several times*). We repeated the experiment with another light source - a candle - and made sure that the effect did not depend on the source.
