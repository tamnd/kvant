F731. Two identical particles with charges q and masses m fly out simultaneously from one point in the direction perpendicular to the confluence of the modulus line of a uniform magnetic field with induction B. Express the distance between the particles as a function of time if the initial velocities of the particle are in the same direction and are equal to $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$.

In a magnetic field, particles move in a circle (see figure), the radii of which are equal, respectively,

$$R_1 = \frac{mv_1}{qB}, \quad R_2 = \frac{mv_2}{qB}.$$

The angular velocities of the part are the same and equal

$$\omega = \frac{v_1}{R_1} = \frac{v_2}{R_2} = \frac{qB}{m}.$$

Let after time $t$ (from the beginning of movement in the magnetic field) one part is at point $A$, and the other at point $C$ (see figure). The distance $r(t)$ between them at this point in time can be determined from the triangle $ABC$. This triangle is equilateral: $|AB| = |BC| = R_2 - R_1$; $ABC = \omega t$. Hence,

$$r(t) = 2|AB| \sin \frac{\omega t}{2} = 2(R_2 - R_1) \sin \frac{\omega t}{2}.$$

Substituting the values ​​of $R_1, R_2, \omega$ from (1) and (2), we find:

$$r(t) = \frac{2m}{qB} (v_2 - v_1) \sin \left( \frac{qB}{2m} t \right).$$

(We consider the electrical interaction of particles to be negligibly weak. This is precisely the situation observed in a cloud chamber.)

F732. The turned object S is located on the axis of a hollow cone with a mirrored inner surface (Fig. 1). Rays, once reflected from the surface of the cone, propagate as if they were coming from a set of precise mini sources located around the circle; each such source is symmetrical to the source S relative to the corresponding generatrix of the cone. The image of these sources on the screen will be a colus (Fig. 3). It is important that the beam of rays coming from each mini-source on the face is flat: it does not pass through the entire surface of the lens, but intersects the line along the corresponding diameter (for example, from the source $S'$ the rays propagate in the vertical plane). Therefore, the degree to which such a beam is attenuated by the diaphragm depends on the shape and orientation of the diaphragm.

A symmetrical diaphragm, shown at figure 2, a, very indo, will equally attenuate the beams coming from all mini sources. In this case, the illumination of the wheel on the screen will decrease evenly. The diaphragm, shown in figure 2, b, will completely pass through the beams, the planes of which form angles $a < a_0$ with the vertical plane. Consequently, the illumination of the corresponding (i.e., upper and lower) parts of the wheel on the screen remains the same.
