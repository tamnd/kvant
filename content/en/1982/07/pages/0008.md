Concurrent Engineering
Near the surface of the Earth, the rays of the Sun are parallel with sufficient accuracy. Therefore, the appearance of a flat surface shadow from a flat object is an illustration of parallel projection (Fig. 7).

And the mathematical definition here is this: let the given planes π and π' (parallel or intersecting) and the line l intersecting both planes; to each point A∈π we associate the point A'∈π' of the intersection of the line l' passing through A parallel to l with the plane π' (Fig. 8); the resulting mapping of the plane π onto π' is called a parallel projection (π onto π' along the straight line l).

Obviously, parallel projection is a one-to-one mapping of the plane π onto the plane π', transforming parallel lines into parallel lines, and intersecting lines into intersecting ones.

Parallel projection not only changes the size of figures, but also distorts their shape. Thus, with parallel design, a circle can “solidify” into an ellipse (Fig. 9)*. Thus, not only distances change, but also their relationships.

However, the simple relationship of three points in parallel design is preserved. Indeed, when the planes π and π' intersect (Fig. 10), this immediately follows from the similarity of the triangles PAA', PBB' and PCC'; the case when π and π' are parallel is even simpler - analyze it yourself.

This simple but important fact has many interesting consequences.

*) By the way, this is one of the possible definitions of an ellipse - a curve obtained by parallel projection of a circle.
