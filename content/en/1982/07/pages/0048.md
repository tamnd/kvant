M717. Given natural numbers $n$ and $r$, $1 \leq r \leq n$. The distance of the entire multiplication of a subset of $[1, 2, \dots, n]$ consisting of $r$ numbers, and in each selection the smallest number. Prove that the arithmetic mean of all chosen numbers is $\frac{n+1}{r+1}$. (For example, with $n=3$, $r=2$ we get three subsets $[1, 2], [1, 3], [2, 3]$ and the arithmetic mean is $(1 + 1+2)/3 = 4/3$.)

◆ First of all, note that the set $[1, 2, \dots, n]$ has $C_n^r = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$ subsets consisting of $r$ numbers. We will present two properties of the numbers $C_n^k$), which we will need to solve our problem:
$$1^\circ. C_n^k + C_n^{k+1} = C_n^{k+1};$$
$$2^\circ. C_n^k + C_n^{k-1} + \dots + C_k^k = C_n^{k+1}.$$

We will call subsets of the set $[1, 2, \dots, n]$ consisting of $r$ numbers $r$-subsets. It is easy to say that the smallest elements of the $r$-subset of the set $[1, 2, \dots, n]$ can only be the numbers 1, 2, ..., $(n-r+1)$. Moreover, the number $k$ is immediately the smallest for $C_n^{k-1}$ such subsets (indeed, each $r$-subset for which the number $k$ is the smallest element contains, along with $k$ and $r-1$, an element from the set $[k+1, k+2, \dots, n]$, consisting of $n-k$ elements). Therefore the sum of all chosen numbers is equal to
$$S = 1 \cdot C_n^{r-1} + 2 \cdot C_n^{r-1} + \dots + (n-r+1) C_n^{r-1},$$
(*)

and we need to show that the arithmetic mean $\frac{S}{C_n^r}$ (there are $C_n^r r$-subsets in total) is equal to $(n+1)/(r+1)$, or that $S = C_n^r \cdot (n+1)/(r+1) = C_n^{r+1}$.

Taking advantage of the fact that $C_n^{k-1} = C_n^k - C_n^{k-1}$ (see $1^\circ$), we transform the sum $S$ as follows:
$$S = 1 \cdot C_n^{r-1} + 2 \cdot C_n^{r-1} + \dots + (n-r+1) C_n^{r-1} =$$
$$= 1 \cdot (C_n^r - C_n^{r-1}) + 2 \cdot (C_n^{r-1} - C_n^{r-2}) + \dots + (n-r) (C_n^{r+1} - C_n^{r+1}) + (n-r+1) C_n^{r+1} =$$
$$= C_n^r + C_n^{r+1} + \dots + C_n^{r+1},$$

Taking into account the property $2^\circ$, we obtain $S = C_n^{r+1}$, as required.

This problem can also be solved by induction by considering the set $[1, 2, \dots, n+1]$ and noting that
$$S(n+1, r) = S(n, r) + S(n, r-1)$$
(**)

(see Fig. 2); here $S(m, k)$ denotes the sum of the smallest numbers of the $k$-subset of the set $[1, 2, \dots, m]$.

Assuming proven equality
$$S(n, r) = C_n^{r+1}, S(n, r-1) = C_n^{r+1}$$

for the $r$- and $(r-1)$-subsets of the set $[1, 2, \dots, n]$ (the induction basis is contained in the conditions of the problem), we obtain, taking into account (**) and the property $1^\circ$,
$$S(n+1, r) = C_n^{r+1} + C_n^{r+1} = C_n^{r+1},$$

which is what is required.

*) You can get acquainted with the numbers $C_n^k$ - biompical coefficients - in more detail by reading the article by N. Vasilyev, A. Zelevinsky “Chebyshev polynomials and recurrence relations” («Kvant». 1982, No. 1) or the brochure by V. A. Uspensky “Pascal’s Triangle” (Popular easy-to-read mathematics, issue 43, M., “Science”, 1978); The first few lines of this triangle are depicted in figure 1.
