This led to the coloring of mica in different colors, although the color range was poorer than in the experiment with daylight. We observed how, when mica was rotated around an axis coinciding with the rays of light, the colors periodically replaced each other.

If you look at the polarized light that passed through the mica directly, that is, for about a second reflection, then no colors are visible. When the mica was moved, there was also no coloration in the area between the lamp and the first reflector.

In addition to mica, we tried other productive materials. It turned out that glass and plexiglass did not give any effect, but the cellulon film from the pack gave a similar effect. When the film was rotated, the reflection periodically increased and decreased (this produced one complete rotation several times), but in this case there was almost no color tint.

Finally, we took a much thinner mica, peeling off a layer from a thicker plate. Mica spreads quite easily, and after a little practice we managed to do it.
