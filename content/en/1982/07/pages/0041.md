⟦folio 39⟧

Tasks

M751—M755; F763—F767

M751. There are 3k offset points on a circle, dividing it into 3k arcs, of which k arcs have length 1, another k arcs have length 2, and the remaining k arcs have length 3. Prove that among the offset points there are two diametrically opposite ones. (9)

V. Proizvolov

M752. A square table of $n \times n$ cells is filled with integers. Moreover, in cells that have a common side, numbers are written that differ from one another by no more than 1. Prove that at least one number appears in the table:
a) no less than $[n/2]$ times ([a] is the integer part of a);
b) no less than $n$ times. (8)

A. Berzins

M753. The numbers a, b, c lie on the interval $]0, \pi/2$ [and satisfy the equalities:
$\cos a = a,$
$\sin \cos b = b,$
$\cos \sin c = c$.

Arrange these numbers in ascending order. (10)

S. Hesse

M754. a) Are there polynomials $P = P(x, y, z), Q = Q(x, y, z), R = R(x, y, z)$ in the variables x, y, z such that the identity $(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-2x+1)^3R = 1?$ holds?
b) The same question for the identity $(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-x+1)^3R = 1.$ (10)

P. Gusyatnikov, Y. Nesterenko

M755. A point M is chosen inside the tetrahedron. Prove that at least one edge of the tetrahedron is visible from point M at an angle whose cosine is not greater than $-1/3$. (9)

S. Gashkov
