M719. a) For which $n \geqslant 3$ there is a set of $n$ consecutive natural numbers having the following properties: the largest of these numbers is a divisor of the least common multiple of the remaining $n-1$ numbers?
6) For what $n \geqslant 3$ is there a unique set of $n$ consecutive natural numbers that have the indicated properties?

Any natural number $n$ for some $k$ satisfies the inequality $2^k < n \leqslant 2^{k+1}$. then $2^k < n < 3 \cdot 2^k < 5 \cdot 2^k$.
Consider the set
$$A = \{3 \cdot 2^k, 3 \cdot 2^{k-1}, \dots, 3 \cdot 2^k-n+1\}$$
of $n$ consecutive natural numbers. Since $n-1 \geqslant 2^k$, among $n-1$ consecutive natural numbers from $3 \cdot 2^k-n+1$ to $3 \cdot 2^k-1$ there is at least one, is done on $2^k$. For $n \geqslant 4$ at least one of them is divisible by 3. Therefore, the number $3 \cdot 2^k$ is a divisor of the least common multiple of the remaining $n-1$ numbers in the set $A$ for any $n \geqslant 4$.

For $n \geqslant 6$, it is easy to construct an example of another set that has the indicated properties:
$$B = \{5 \cdot 2^k, 5 \cdot 2^{k-1}, \dots, 5 \cdot 2^k-n+1\}$$
(check it out!). Thus, for $n \geqslant 6$ there are at least two sets $A$ and $B$ that have the indicated properties. It remains to consider the case $n=3$, 4 and 5.

For $n=5$ there are also two required sets: $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ and $\{8, 9, 10, 11, 12\}$.

Let us prove that for $n=4$ there is a unique set that has the indicated properties. Indeed, let the set $\{m, m+1, m+2, m+3\}$ have the required properties. Since $m+3$ is an LCM divisor, $(m, m+1, m+2)$ is divisible by $m+3$. But $m(m+1)(m+2) = (m+3)(m^2+2) - 6$; This means that 6 must be divisible by $m+3$, whence $m=3$, and we get the only example of a set of four consecutive natural numbers that has these properties: $\{3, 4, 5, 6\}$.

Finally, for $n=3$ there are no such sets $\{m, m+1, m+2\}$: the LCM of $(m, m+1) = m(m+1)$ ($m$ and $m+1$ are coprime), but $m(m+1)$ is not divisible by $m+2$, since $(m-1)(m+2) < m(m+1) < m(m+2)$.

M720. About the function $f$, defined on the set of all pairs of non-negative numbers $(x; y)$, the following is known:

1°. $f(0, y) = y+1$
2°. $f(x+1, 0) = f(x, 1)$
3°. $f(x+1, y+1) = f(x, f(x+1, y))$
for each pair $x \geqslant 0, y \geqslant 0$. Find the value of $f(4, 1981)$.

It is easy to see that $f(1,0) = f(0,1) = 2$. Applying the correspondence $3^\circ$, we find
$$f(1, y) = f(0, f(1, y-1)) = f(1, y-1) + 1,$$
whence it follows that
$$f(1, y) = f(1,0) + y + 2.$$
Similarly we get that
$$f(2, y) = 2y + 3.$$
Because what is equal to $f(3,y)$. We have
$$f(3,0) = f(2,1) = 5;$$
$$f(3,y) = f(2,f(3,y-1)) = 2f(3,y-1) + 3,$$
So
$$f(3,1) = 13, f(3,2) = 29, f(3,3) = 61, f(3,4) = 125, \dots$$
