The comet near the perihelion will be as if behind the Sun, and it can be observed in short periods of time at dawn and after sunset. In addition, Halley's comet, unlike most comets, has the so-called reverse motion - it flies in its orbit in the direction opposite to the Earth's revolution around the Sun. This makes the “Giotto” experiment very difficult, since the automaton and the comet will support each other as if on a collision course with a relative speed of 75 km/s, so the flight time near the comet’s nucleus will be very short, it is calculated in seconds. During this time, you need to have time to implement a huge scientific program: try to form a nucleus, establish the chemical and isotopic composition of the cometary atmosphere, the degree of ionization, the magnitude of the magnetic field, etc.

In parallel with “Giotto”, the USSR will carry out its own expedition to Halley’s Comet. Her project is very witty. Its author is academician R.Z. Sagdeev, director of the Institute of Space Research of the USSR Academy of Sciences. The essence of the project is to use the automata, which are supposed to be sent to Venus in 1984, to rendezvous with Halley's comet. The fact is that, unlike the Earth, Venus will be located very well in relation to the comet near its perihelion. Therefore, a small maneuver in the gravitational field of Venus will be enough to direct the automata, which have completed their research tasks near this planet, to Halley’s comet. Such a project requires virtually no additional cash costs.

If expeditions to Halley's comet continue successfully, then this comet alone in human history becomes undeniable.
