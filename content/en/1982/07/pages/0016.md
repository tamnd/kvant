What are comets according to modern scientific views? The masses of comets are small. As already mentioned, they are probably contained within $10^{15} \div 10^{22}$ g (Earth mass≈6·10$^{27}$ g). In the gravitational field of the colossal mass of the Sun ($\approx 2 \cdot 10^{33}$ g), they move according to the law of universal gravitation, along orbits that are finite sections, that is, curves obtained when a cone intersects a plane. These are circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola. The Sun is at the focus of the orbits. The gravitational field of the large planets (Jupter and Saturn) disturbs their orbits, changes them, so that a comet moving to the right along an elliptical trajectory can switch to a hyperbolic or parabolic one. Comets that have elliptical orbits (the appearance of a circle in such conditions is extremely unlikely) periodically return to the Sun. Such comets are called periodic. There are not many of them—several more than seventy.

*) During supernova explosions, the temperature rises so high that thermonuclear synthesis of chemical elements, in particular radioactive aluminum, begins in their substance. The products formed as a result of such synthesis, separating during an explosion, can “contaminate” a nearby sun-filled cloud.

Comet Borelli has a very long period, which passes through a period once every 493 years. Comet Encke has the shortest period - 3.3 years. (Comets usually bear the names of their first occupants.) In total, over five hundred cometary orbits are now known. One might think that since most comets move along open trajectories, they are born outside the Solar System, somewhere in the Galaxy. However, not like that. Research has shown that, in fact, the primary orbits of all comets were ellipses, most of which gradually turned into hyperbolas and parabolas under the influence of the juxtaposition of Jupter and Saturn. This means that comets originated within the solar system. Now the most popular point of view of the Dutch astronomer Jan Oort, according to which, at the border of the Solar system, at a distance of approximately 15,000 AU. That is, from the Sun, there is a cloud of comets (the so-called Oort cloud) - a “reservoir”, from which, under the influence of the gravity of nearby stars of time, any of the comets breaks out and rushes towards the Sun. We record it as another comet. Previously, there was a beautiful hypothesis according to which the comet cloud arose from the icy remains of the tenth planet of the solar system - Phaethon, which once existed between the orbits of Mars and Jupter and was destroyed by the explosion. Later, such an explanation began to seem far from random. Comets will also arise as a by-product during the formation of planets, as a result of other processes in a DOS cloud filled with gas and dust. It is important, however, that they most likely arose in the Solar System, evolved little and, therefore, carry information about the initial conditions.
