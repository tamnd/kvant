Unlike previous problems, where nuclear transformations were accompanied by the release of energy, in this problem the reaction occurs with the absorption of energy (that is, the total mass of nuclei after the reaction is greater than before the reaction). In this case, the threshold energy $E_n$ cannot be equal to the absorbed energy $\Delta E$, since the momentum of the system before the reaction is not equal to zero, which means that after the reaction the system has non-zero momentum and kinetic energy. The minimum kinetic energy that a system must have after a reaction is equal to the kinetic energy associated with the motion of its center of mass. After all, the speed of the center of mass cannot change, since during the reaction only internal forces act, which in total compensate each other. Consequently, the minimum energy of an incident particle at which the reaction can proceed, that is, the threshold energy $E_n$, is equal to the sum of the energy $\Delta E$ absorbed during the reaction and the kinetic energy of the center of mass of the system:

$$E_n = \Delta E + \frac{MV^2}{2},$$

where $M = m_N + m_a$ and $V = \frac{m_a v_a}{M}$. From here we find

$$\Delta E = \frac{M - m_a}{M} E_n = \frac{14}{18} E_n.$$
