M714. N friends learned N news at the same time, and each learned one piece of news. They began calling each other and exchanging news. Each conversation lasted one hour. You can convey as much news as you like in one call. What is the minimum number of hours required for everyone to know all the news? Consider three cases: a) N=64, 6) N=55, b) N=100.

Let us denote by $T(N)$ the minimum number of hours during which $N$ friends will be able to exchange news. It is interesting that the function $T=T(N)$ is not monotonic. This can be discovered by finding the values ​​of $T(N)$ for small $N$ - they are given in the table in the margin; next to it, at figure 1, is shown a method that allows $N=4, 5, 6$ and 8 to find out all the news in $T(N)$ hours. Of course, in order to solve the problem, we must not only indicate a way to exchange news in $T(N)$ hours, but also prove that this cannot be done in fewer hours—to obtain an estimate of $T(N)$. We will teach from her.

Let's consider an arbitrary news item. After the first hour, no more than two friends know her, after the second hour - no more than four friends, ..., after the $k$th hour - no more than $2^k$ friends. Therefore, if after $T$ hours all $N$ friends know all the news, then $2^T \geq N$ and $T \geq \log_2 N$. Since $T$ is an integer, then $T \geq \log_2 N$. (Here, $\Gamma a$ denotes the smallest integer that is not less than $a$. This notation is now used more and more often, especially specialized in applied and computational mathematics; however, for the general integer part - “rounding down” - instead of the notation $[a]$, the notation $[a]$ is used.) If $N$ is an odd number, then there will be one of the $N$ friends - let's denote him by $A$ - who does not participate in conversations in the first hour. Applying the above reasoning to that news $a$, which at first only $A$ knows, we find that at least $1 + \log_2 N$ hours are needed to exchange all the news.

Let us show that the found estimate

$$T(N) = \begin{cases} \Gamma \log_2 N, & \text{ если } N \text{ четно}, \\ \Gamma \log_2 N + 1, & \text{ если } N \text{ нечетно} \end{cases}$$

is achievable, that is, we will show that it is possible to organize the exchange of news in such a way that after $T(N)$ hours all friends will know all the news. Let's look at three cases.

1°. The easiest way is to present a way to exchange news in $k$ hours for $N=2^k$.

Let us associate to each druse a set of $k$ numbers 0 and 1 (“binary number”); various such sets from $(0,0, ..., 0)$ to $(1,1, ..., 1)$ are just $2^k$. Let pairs of friends whose numbers differ only in one $t$ place talk to each other at the $t$th hour. Then, if the numbers of two friends differ in $t_1, t_2, ..., t_s$ places ($1 \leq t_1 < t_2 < ... < t_s \leq k$), then as a result of talking at $t_1$, $t_2$, ..., $t_s$ o’clock they will know each other’s news. (This method for $N=4$ and $N=8$ is illustrated in Figure 1.)
