⟦folio 41⟧

F767. In one of the projects for obtaining electric energy, it was proposed to use sea currents and the Earth's magnetic field. The project will consist of the following.

Two horizontal metal plates are immersed in the sea, located one above the other at a distance of $l=100$ m; the area of ​​each plate is $S=1$ km$^2$. Sea water, whose resistivity is $Q=0,25$ Ohm m, flows between the plates from west to east at a speed of $v=1$ m/s. The Earth's magnetic field in a given place is uniform, directed from south to north; the field induction is $B=10^{-4}$ T. Determine the maximum electrical power that can be released to the load connected to the plates. (10)

A. Sheronov

Problems

M751—M755; P763—P767

M751. A circle is divided into $3k$ arcs by $3k$ points; $k$ arcs are of length 1, $k$ others of length 2 and the remaining $k$ are of length 3. Prove that at least two of the points are diametrically opposed. (9)

V. Proizvolov

M752. Integers are the entries in the $n \times n$ cells of a square table. Integers in cells with a common side differ by no more than 1. Prove that at least one number appears in the table

a) no less than $[n/2]$ times ([a] is the integer part of $a$); b) no less than $n$ times. (8)

A. Berzinsh

M753. The numbers $a, b, c$ in the interval $]0, \pi/2[$ satisfy the relations:

$$\cos a = a, \sin \cos b = b, \cos \sin c = c.$$

Write these numbers in increasing order. (10)

S. Gessen

M754. a) Do there exist polynomials

$$P = P(x, y, z), Q = Q(x, y, z), R = R(x, y, z)$$

in the variables $x, y, z$ satisfying the identity

$$(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-2x+1)^3R = 1?$$

b) Same question for the identity

$$(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-x+1)^3R = 1.$$ (10)

P. Gusyatnikov, Yu. Nesterenko

M755. The point $M$ is chosen arbitrarily inside a tetrahedron. Prove that at least one edge of the tetrahedron is seen from the point $M$ under an angle whose cosine is no greater than $1/3$. (9)

S. Gashkov

P763. A hoop of radius $R$ stands on a horizontal surface. An identical hoop rolls past it with velocity $v$ (see figure Pic. 1). Find the dependence of the upper “intersection point” of the hoops on the distance between their centers. The hoops are thin; the second hoop rolls past the first, almost touching it. (9)

S. Krotov
