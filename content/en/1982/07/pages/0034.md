Home experiments with polarized light

Instead of introducing
The experiments described in this article are not at all similar in nature to those that are usually done in school (or even in college). There, through experience, they either check the correctness of some correspondences arising from already known laws, or, by changing part of the quantities included in these correspondences, they calculate others. For example, from Hooke’s law and Newton’s second law it follows that the external oscillation period of the load is equal to $T = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$. You can independently measure the mass of the load $m$ and the elasticity of the spring $k$, calculate the period $T$ and compare it with the value changed experimentally. You can also measure the period and mass of the load and calculate the springs.

Such experiments are very necessary and are constantly being carried out not only in educational, but also in scientific laboratories. However, experiments with other approaches to the problem are no less important for science. Let's say you were filled with some unknown effect, and it aroused your beating. You begin to explore what factors and conditions this effect depends on. Moreover, the talk is not about checking any laws, because there is nothing to check yet. The laws are not yet known and may not be established soon. But you will make your contribution to the development of science: the future theory will have to be binding on your results. And this does not exhaust the significance of such research. Until a complete theory emerges, a number of important conclusions can be drawn. In addition, the discovered effect may even find practical application. Let us at least remember the magnetic needle, which served as a journey long before the nature of magnetism was understood.

What interested us?

A bright ray of sunlight falls on a glass plate and... is not reflected. We see illuminated specks of dust on the surface of the glass, but there is no bunny on the wall. When we introduced a piece of wireless mica into the rays going to the glass, a reflection appeared and the field of view was colored with all the colors of radgia. They shimmered, turning into each other when turning or showing mica. In our experiments we made these and several other discoveries. We are me and two eight classics.

Let’s say right away that for the experiments we took the most ordinary glasses, but the light was “prepared” in a special way. Otherwise, let's talk about everything in order.
