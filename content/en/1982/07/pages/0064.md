$$2hv_{\gamma} = 2m_e c^2,$$

$$E_{\gamma} = hv_{\gamma} = m_e c^2 \approx 0,51 \text{ MэВ}.$$
Quantum energy of visible green light
$$E = hv = hc/\lambda \approx 2,3 \text{ эВ}.$$
Thus,
$$E_{\gamma}/E \approx 2,2 \cdot 10^5.$$

Let us use the formulas for transforming the fields ($E_{\parallel} = E'_{\parallel}$, $E_{\perp} = \gamma_0 E'_{\perp}$, where $\gamma_0 = 1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2} \geq 1$) and taking into account that when moving from the system $K'$ to the system $K$, the longitudinal distances are reduced (by $\gamma_0$ times), and the transverse ostatics remain unchanged. Then for the transverse field in the system $K$ we obtain
$$E_{\perp}(b) = \frac{E'_{\perp}(b)}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 b^2 \sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} = \gamma_0 E_{\kappa\ell}.$$
— the transverse field $E_{\perp}$ of a moving relativistic particle is enhanced in comparison with the Coulomb field $E_{\kappa\ell} = q/(4\pi\epsilon_0 b^2)$ by $\gamma_0$ times.
For a longitudinal field in the same system we can write
$$E_{\parallel}(a) = E'_{\parallel} \left( \frac{a}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} \right) = \frac{q(1-v^2/c^2)}{4\pi\epsilon_0 a^2} = E_{\kappa\ell}/\gamma_0^2$$
— the longitudinal field of a moving particle is weakened compared to the Coulomb field by $\gamma_0^2$ times.

With type 6 flasks, two forces act on a carbon atom, identical in model and directed in the same direction. If a ball - a carbon atom - is divided into two equal parts, then they will oscillate equally, that is, at any moment they will have the same accelerations, velocities and coordinates. Thus, the task is to determine the frequency of the flask connecting crushed balls with masses $M$ and $m/2$. Such a system swings around a fixed center of mass. It is located at a distance $l' = \frac{m}{m+2M} l$ from the ball with mass $M$ (l is the length of the atom in an undeformed state), therefore we can assume that the oxygen atom (ball with mass $M$) is attached to the center of mass with a protracted length $l'$. The hardness of part of the crumb is greater than the rigidity of the whole crumb:
$$k' = kl/l' = k(m+2M)/m,$$
The postural frequency of the ball of mass $M$ and the violation of rigidity $k'$ is equal to
$$\omega_a = \sqrt{\frac{k'}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{k(m+2M)}{mM}}.$$

$$\omega_a = \sqrt{\frac{m}{m+2M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{11}}.$$
