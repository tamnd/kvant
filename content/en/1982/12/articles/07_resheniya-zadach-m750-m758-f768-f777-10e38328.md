We will need the following

Lemma. Let us be given a chain of $N$ cells of the table (each cell borders the previous one on a common side), in the outermost cells of which the numbers $a$ and $b$ (Fig. 1) are written. Then $|a-b| \leq N-1$; 2) any chain number $x$, enclosed between $a$ and $b$, is written in one of the cells of the chain.

Proof. Let $x_i$ be the number written in the $i$th cell of the chain, then the statement follows from the fact that

$$|a-b| = |a-x_2 + x_2-x_3 + \ldots + x_{N-1}-b| \leq |a-x_2| + |x_2-x_3| + \ldots + |x_{N-1}-b|,$$

and the difference of two adjacent numbers is not greater than 1. When proving the second statement, for the purpose of definition we will assume that $a < x \leq b$. Let's move along the chain from the first cell (with the number $a$) and find the last cell, which still contains a number not exceeding $x$. This cell is the one we are looking for: it contains $x$, since the next cell would also contain a number not exceeding $x$ (after all, the numbers in neighboring cells are different by no more than 1).

a) Let $M$ and $m$ be the most and most numbers in the table. Since any two cells can be connected by a chain of at most $2n-1$ cells, it follows from the inequality of the lemma that $|M-m| \leq 2n-2$. Therefore, there are no more than $2n-1$ different numbers in the table, which means that at least one of them is written in no less than $[n^2/(2n-1)]$ cells. But $n^2/(2n-1) > n/2$, from which statement a) follows.

b) Let $M_k$ and $m_k$ be the most and most numbers in the $k$th column. If there is a number $x$ such that $m_k \leq x \leq M_k$ at once for all $k$ from 1 to $n$, then by the second statement of the lemma the number $x$ appears in each column, that is, at least $n$ times. If there is no such number, then for some numbers $k$ and $l$ we have $m_k \leq M_k < m_l \leq M_l$, that is, any number in the $k$th column is less than the number in the $l$th column. Applying the same statement to the number $y$, contained between $M_k$ and $m_l$, and the horizontal chains between the $k$th and $l$th columns, we find that the number $y$ is written in each line, and therefore no less than $n$ times.

F768. A cone-shaped drag moves along the main table, quickly grinding its axes (see figure). At what speed v of translational motion will the die not hit the edge of the table, oozing from it? The axis of the drag remains vertical. The wolf's dimensions are indicated at figure.

Having jumped off the table, the wolf will move in a parabola (rotation only stabilizes the vertical position of the wolf's axis). The horizontal projection $v_x$ of the wolf's speed will be equal to the speed $v$ of its translational movement on the table; the vertical projection will change over time according to the law $v_y = gt^2/2$. The fiber will not hit the edge of the table if during the time $t$, during which it moves vertically by $H$, its horizontal displacement is greater than or equal to $r$ (see figure). Let's write this condition:

$$v_x\tau = v\tau \geq r, \quad \tau = \sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}.$$

Thus, the speed of the wolf’s translational movement must satisfy the condition:

$$v \geq \sqrt{\frac{r^2g}{2H}}.$$

1) Solve the problem assuming water and ice are incompressible.
2) Evaluate the correction provided by skimability accounting.
It is known that to reduce the volume of a certain amount of water by 1%, the pressure must be raised to $p' = 20 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Take the compressibility of ice to be equal to half the compressibility of water.
Specific thermal fluidity of water and ice - $c_B = 4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K), $c_\pi = 2,1 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K); specific heat of melting of ice $\lambda = 3 \times 10^5$ J/kg, density of ice $Q_\pi = 0,9Q_B$, where $Q_B$ is the density of water.

1) First, we find the decrease in the temperature of the mixture as a result of an increase in external pressure:

$$\Delta T = \frac{p_1}{p} \approx 0,17 \text{ K}.$$
Such a small change in temperature indicates that a small amount of ice will melt, that is, $\Delta m \ll m_\pi$.
Let's write down the law of conservation of energy:

$$A = \lambda \cdot \Delta m - (c_\pi + c_B)m \cdot \Delta T.$$
Let us estimate the value of the work $A$ of the external force. The change in volume of the mixture as a result of melting mass $\Delta m$ of ice is equal to

$$\Delta V = \frac{\Delta m}{Q_\pi} - \frac{\Delta m}{Q_B} = \Delta m \frac{Q_B - Q_\pi}{Q_B Q_\pi} \ll 0,1 \frac{m}{Q_\pi} \sim 10^{-4} \text{ m}^3,$$

$$A \approx p_1 \cdot \Delta V \ll 2,5 \cdot 10^2 \text{ Дж}.$$
The amount of heat $\Delta Q$ required to heat a mass $m$ of ice and a mass $m$ of water by $\Delta T$ degrees is

$$\Delta Q = (c_\pi + c_B)m \cdot \Delta T \approx 1,1 \cdot 10^3 \text{ Дж}.$$
It can be seen that $A \ll \Delta Q$; therefore, for the sake of estimation, we can assume that $\lambda \cdot \Delta m \approx \Delta Q$, whence

$$\Delta m = \frac{\Delta Q}{\lambda} \approx 3,7 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ кг}.$$
The change in volume due to the melting of this mass of ice is equal to

$$\Delta V = \Delta m \frac{Q_B - Q_\pi}{Q_B Q_\pi} \approx 4 \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ m}^3.$$
Considering that with a slow increase in pressure $\Delta V \sim \Delta p$, we find the work of the external force:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} p_1 \cdot \Delta V \approx 0,5 \text{ Дж}.$$
