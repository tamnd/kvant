Performer 350 years since the founding of the University of Tartu. This is one of the oldest and most famous universities in the Soviet Union.

Substitute mathematicians also worked in Tartu, such as M. Bartels - teacher of N. I. Lobachevsky, T. Clausen, F. G. Minding. The works of academician V. Ya. Struve and his students glorified the University of Tartu as a world astronomical center. The famous physicist E. H. Lenz (Lenz rule, Joule-Lenz law) was a graduate of the university. Professor of the University of Tartu B. S. Jacobi, a physicist and electrical engineer, invented galvanoplasty; he designed the world's first electric motor. Professor B.B. Golitsyn, one of the founders of seismology, worked here. A graduate of the University of Tartu was the famous natural center K. M. Baer (he discovered the law of erosion of river banks). The creator of the “Explanatory Dictionary of the Living Great Russian Language” V. I. Dal studied here. Outstanding surgeons N.I. Pirogov and N.N. Burdenko, the famous historian academician E.V. Tarle worked at the university.

Nowadays, the University of Tartu is a prominent center of education, culture and science of the Soviet Union, a source of personnel for the Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic.

An excellent physics institute has been created in the capital of the Latvian SSR, Rita, which has a research nuclear reactor used as a powerful source of neutrons.

Any nuclear reactor serves not only as a source of energy, but also as a relatively powerful source of neutrons. Research reactors are used to study a wide range of physical and chemical processes. The construction of such reactors requires significant funds, and only highly qualified personnel can experiment with them.

It is known that many substances, when irradiated with neutrons, noticeably change their physical and chemical properties. At the reactor of the Institute of Physics in Rita, scientists are studying changes in the structure of a number of dielectric crystals, leading to changes in their optical properties. These studies have not only scientific significance, but also practical application in solving a number of technical problems. Thus, recently the influence of neutron irradiation on substances that are supposed to be used in the construction of industrial nuclear reactors has been studied at the reactor.

Under the influence of neutron irradiation, molecules of neoctor chemical compounds dissociate (decay) at a greater or lesser rate. The search for compounds sufficiently resistant to neutron irradiation is an important task. The study of a number of chemical compounds in this direction is carried out at the research reactor of the Institute of Nuclear Physics in the capital of the Uzbek SSR, Tashkent. Trials carried out by Uzbek scientists together with scientists from the Institute of Atomic Energy named after. I.V. Kurchatov at the Tashkent Research Reactor made it possible to select chemical compounds suitable for use in technology, the molecules of which are sufficiently resistant to neutron irradiation.

The most important property of neutrons is that they are able to penetrate the nucleus of an atom and change its composition; in this case, either the nucleus of another chemical element or the nucleus of a radioactive isotone of the irradiated element is formed. A classic example of such a nuclear transformation is the process of formation of new chemical elements that underlies all nuclear technology when uranium is irradiated with neutrons. Radioactive isotones are widely used in medicine, technology, and agriculture. At the Tashkent reactor, Uzbek physicists obtain radioactive isotones of a number of elements.

Important scientific and practical work is carried out at the research reactor of the Institute of Nuclear Physics in the capital of the Kazakh SSR, Alma-Ata.

Neutrons can also perform another important “service”. They are indispensable for analyzing the chemical state of a substance, when determining the content of a particular element in a substance; especially when the amount of an element in a substance is so small that it is impossible to detect its presence using chemical analysis methods. An example is the analysis of rocks for the content of rare, and therefore expensive, elements. Here we come to the aid of electrons (β-particles), which come to use in basic cases. Modern instruments make it possible to register each individual γ-quanta and each electron and change their energy. The energy of an emitted quantum or electron is a quantity characteristic of the nucleus of a given element. Therefore, having irradiated the analyzed rock sample with neutrons and counted the number of γ-quanta over a certain time

Such an analysis can be carried out at any research reactor, but at the Institute of Nuclear Physics in Almaty this method has been developed to a good standard. The results obtained by the institute's scientists are very important when searching for deposits of rare metals.

The same institute has a cyclotron, an installation no less expensive than a research reactor.

The cyclotron, as is known, is used to accelerate charged particles, in particular α-particles. By irradiating a light metal, such as beryllium, with alpha particles, beams of neutrons can be obtained. True, the number of neutrons in this case is noticeably less than that obtained from a nuclear reactor, but, using a cyclotron, it is possible to regulate with great accuracy the energy of accelerated α-particles and, accordingly, the energy of “knocked out” neutrons. For a number of studies this advantage is invaluable.

The Soviet state places great emphasis on the importance of allied scientific institutions providing powerful modern research facilities. Thus, in the capital of the Armenian SSR, Yerevan, one of the most powerful cyclic electron accelerators in the world was built. This is a hail structure in which the kinetic energy of electrons reaches 6·10⁹ electrons (this energy would be received by an electron in an electric field at a voltage of 6·10⁹ V). In the Armenian SSR, in Biopakan, there is a world-famous astrophysical observatory, founded with the most modern scientific equipment.

Over the past decades, physical sciences have received great development in the Belarusian SSR. The Academy of Sciences of the BSSR includes a number of large physical institutes conducting important work in the field of optics. The Institute of Physics of the Academy of Sciences of the BSSR conducts extensive research on the physics and technology of lasers. One of the important areas of work at this reactor is the study of the possibility, using neutron irradiation, of improving the physical and chemical properties of a number of materials used in industry.

I had the opportunity to visit the capital of the Georgian SSR, Tbilisi, and get acquainted with the work of some of the scientific physics institutions there. At the Faculty of Physics of Tbilisi State University and in other scientific institutions of the city, extensive research is being carried out on the physics and technology of semiconductors, and on radio electronics. At the Institute of Physics, which has a research reactor and a large cryogenic installation, interesting phenomena related to the effect of neutrons on the physical properties of substances at low temperatures are being studied. These studies have made it possible to significantly clarify the physical nature of changes in the properties of materials that support neutron irradiation. The same institute studies the unique properties of the so-called superfluid He-II. The fact is that liquid helium, being cooled to a temperature of 2.17 K, jumps into a new state in which its viscosity becomes zero (liquid flow without friction). Due to the lack of viscosity, the movement of liquid helium has interesting features. They became the subject of study by Georgian physicists.

I do not have the opportunity to dwell in any detail on the state of the physical sciences in the largest of the allied resource centers - the RSFSR. Suffice it to say that large scientific physical centers are located throughout the vast resource area from Kaliningrad to Vladivostok. I had and still have to collaborate with many of them. Further, simply listing the named most important physical institutions of the RSFSR would take up too much space. The capital of the RSFSR is also home to the main headquarters of Soviet science - the USSR Academy of Sciences, which is headed by the outstanding Soviet physicist Academician A.P. Aleksandrov.

It is necessary to note the great merits of Soviet physicists in solving the most important problems facing the country. Thus, during the Great Patriotic War, they made a huge contribution to the assimilation of existing military equipment and the creation of new types.

The author of these lines, together with other physicists, scientists and engineers of many specialists, entered the technical science team created in our country. Before my eyes, the physical ideas underlying this technology received industrial implementation. Now the Soviet Union is one of the leading powers in this area. In a number of nuclear technologies, our country is ahead of the most industrially developed capitalist countries. It is well known that the first nuclear power system was built in the Soviet Union. Soviet nuclear icebreakers have no equal in the world.

Soviet physics greeted the six decades of the formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics with new successes both in the field of physiological research and in their application in various sectors of the national economy. Thus, the physicists of all allied resource workers are fulfilling the historical plans of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union.
