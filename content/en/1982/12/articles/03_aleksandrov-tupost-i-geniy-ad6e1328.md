Stupidity and genius

Let us now look back at the history of Euclid’s fifth step. Lobachevsky said about her: “Vain countries... for two thousand years.” But what countries! Many mathematicians waste them, and what mathematicians! Among them are famous names: attempts at discovery, arose by Archimedes, passing through Omar Khayyam and coming to an end with Gauss.

Attempts to prove the fifth step were, as we found out earlier, completely natural. But for 2000 years no one agreed that proof was impossible. No one could have thought that some geometry different from the attractive Euclidean one would arise. Its independent connection with our spatial experience and visual representation, its logical perfection and pronunciation, including its study and, one might say, use - all this made Euclid’s geometry indisputable, as if absolutely necessary, inherent in both the world and the mind. Its distortion from practice was eclipsed by the perfection and clarity of its logic. And it finally came to the point that in 1781 the great philosopher Kant, in his “Critique of Pure Reason”, considered geometry a priori - independent of experience - and based on this the conclusion about the apriority of space itself,

Geney

But just at the same time, problems began to emerge from attempts to prove the fifth step. Already in 1766, Lambert had the idea that the denial of the fifth step “takes place on some minimal sphere,” and that perhaps the strange conclusions to which this denial leads are not nonsense. The tension is rising. Kant’s “a priori” is spreading in the minds, especially after the second edition of his Critique in 1787.

But Lambert’s work appeared in 1786. Then, from equally persistent and impeccable attempts to prove the fifth step, in the first quarter of the 19th century the general idea finally emerged that a geometry other than Euclidean might be conceivable. Almost simultaneously, although with varying degrees of definiteness and clarity, this idea appears in several people - Schweickart, Taurinus, Gauss, Lobachevsky and Bolyai.

Getting to the point of testing the vaccine can be general in itself. But this is not hay yet, just an idea. Science requires the transformation of an idea into a theory, just as engineering requires the transformation of an idea into an object, into a completed invention.

Genya is not only a flight of thought, but also its perseverance - work, increases in income, and in income supported by work. Thus, Copernicus not only expressed the idea that not the Earth, but the Sun is in the center (the idea, by the way, is not new: it was expressed back in the 3rd century BC by Aristarchus of Samos), but also built the “Copernican system” - he gave an accurate description of the movement of the planets around the Sun, in accordance with observations. Lobachevsky affirmed the idea of ​​​​the unprovability of the fifth act and the possibility of non-Euclidean geometry, based on physical, conceptual-theoretical convictions. This is already expressed in his words quoted earlier (in the first part of the article) from the preface to “A New Beginning of Geometry...”. “The truth that they wanted to prove,” that is, the fifth arrived, does not lie “in the concepts themselves,” but in application to real space and is subject to verification by experience, like a physical law. By the way, it is worth noting that scientific methods believe that they and the sciences do not need philosophical thinking at all. All the great scientists from Newton and Galileo, if we talk only about modern times, were philosophical thinkers. Without philosophy, science does not develop: the position of its new paths, when they have not taken shape, is the philosophical movement of thought. The only question is what kind of philosophy it is, whether it is associated with exact logic and facts of experience or with the general phrases of a priori and pure speculative thinking located in airless space. Galilee, Newton, Lobachevsky not only expressed philosophical judgments, but also, starting from general convictions, built the tasks of scientific theories - solid foundations of entire vast areas of science.

The emergence of non-Euclidean geometry was the beginning of a revolutionary transformation of geometry. But also, which is typical for a revolution, along with the name of its forces, the reaction also grew. It was precisely when the discovery of a new geometry was already approaching that Kant’s philosophy appeared with its doctrine of the a priori nature of geometry, of space as an a priori form of contemplation. Any geometry other than that inherent in this form of contemplation seemed unthinkable.

Lobachevsky clearly opposes these views. The emergence of a new geometry defines them and opens up invisible, unthinkable equal paths for the development of science - a revolution is taking place. A genius is a revolution, a revolution is a genius in action.

When non-Euclidean geometry was discovered and discovered and the question of its real meaning arose, then the ingenuity showed itself in full force.

As early as 1827, Gauss developed the foundations of the general theory of geometry on a surface, in which the role of straight segments is played by the shortest lines located on the surface. In particular, he concluded that on some surfaces (surfaces of negative curvature) the sum of the angles of a triangle (whose sides are the shortest lines) is less than 180°. He knew at the same time that the same thing was true in non-Euclidean geometry. But he did not compare these two conclusions; he did not realize that non-Euclidean geometry should work on some surfaces. If he had figured this out, the proof would not have been particularly difficult for him, given his exceptional strength as a mathematician (this conclusion was obtained by the Italian mathematician Beltrami only 40 years later).

It is believed that Gaus’s thoughts in non-Euclidean geometry, on the one hand, and in the theory of surfaces, on the other, went as if in parallel, without intersecting. The phenomenon is quite general. Lyudmal often does not think of comparing things that have been perfected each time, but upon closer examination they turn out to be closely related or even coinciding. This is what happens to one person when he knows both “things”, but does not compare them. The same thing happens in a group of people, when they know one thing, others know others, but they don’t compare.

This is exactly what happened next with non-Euclidean geometry and geometrics on a surface of constant negative curvature. Character

Gayss, Bolshi, Lobachevsky - three mathematicians who discovered non-Euclidean geometry. A person has three characters.

Friedrich Gayss is a mathematician of extraordinary strength, about whom they say “the great Gayss”, “princeps mathematicorum” (that is, the king of mathematicians), “the foreman of mathematicians”*).

But Gayss, for all his mathematical strength, was characterized by intellectual caution, indecisiveness, which manifested itself in part in the fact that he studied the theory of parallels for more than 30 years before he decided to express, even to himself and in partial letters, his strong conviction of the validity of non-Euclidean geometry. Then another caution followed - a difficulty that did not allow him to come out with his conclusions for fear of “the cry of the security guards”...

The complete opposite of Gayss is presented to us by Janos Bolshy, the youngest of the three: when he figured out non-Euclidean geometry, he was only 23 years old (accordingly, Gayss was 47, Lobachevsky was 31). Lobachevsky speaks publicly at 32, Bolshi at 30, Gayss - not at all. Bolsha's work on non-Euclidean geometry is brilliantly written, if a bit too brief.
