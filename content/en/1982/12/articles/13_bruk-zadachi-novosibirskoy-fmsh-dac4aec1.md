5. M. D. Frank-Kamenets ki. The most important molecule (Q1). Price 40 k.

Just as the elucidation of the structure of the atom led to the birth of quantum physics, the discovery of the structure of DNA - the most important molecule of living nature - led to the emergence of a new molecular, biological one. The book talks about the physical, chemical and biological properties of DNA (in particular, about DNA as a carrier of genetic information). Much attention is paid to the discoveries of recent years that led to the emergence of genetic engineering.

6. V. S. Edelman. Near absolute zero (Q1). Price 40 k.

The book examines the range of issues that concern low temperature physics. Describes how temperatures close to absolute zero are achieved, and what phenomena are observed at low temperatures (superfluidity, superconductivity), how electrodust moves in liquid helium, and what a quantum crystal is. The book also describes technical applications of superconductivity, such as the creation of strong magnetic fields and high-Q oscillator systems.

M. I. Kaganov, V. M. Tsoukernik. The origin of magnetism. Price 40 k.

Although magnets were discovered at the dawn of civilization, understanding of the nature of magnetism came only recently - after the creation of quantum mechanics. However, among the founders of this science, Dirac expressed the conviction that there must be magnetic charges in nature. He called them monopoles. Scientists spent a lot of time searching for monopoles, but they were discovered. However, microscopic sources of magnetic fields in nature - this is discussed in detail in one of the chapters. Other chapters explain why different bodies behave differently when subjected to a magnetic field, what magnets are, and why magnets, or ferromagnets—microscopic sources of magnetic field—play an important role among them.

Tasks
Novosibirsk FMS

At the end of 1981, Siberian physicists offered schoolchildren in our country a good gift - a new problem in physics *). It is based on problems proposed during classes at the Physics and Mathematics School at Novosibirsk State University, at various Olympids and at summer schools.

This problem book has its own personality. Getting to know and working with him will be useful for everyone who wants to engage in physics as a service and, in the future, professionally.

The book contains over two thousand problems of complexity: from “obvious” problems that can be used to check the conditions of sections of the school curriculum, to tasks that require communication and conventional thinking. For larger values, only answers are given. All this allows you to use the book for classroom activities, physical circles, school competitions, and for independent reflection.

*) I.I. Vorobyov, P. I. Zubkov, G. A. Kutuzova, O. Ya. Savchenko, A. M. Trubachev, V. G. Kharitonov. Problems in physics (textbook). (M., “Nauka”, 1981). Price 95 kopecks.

There are qualitative problems in the book that require a clear understanding of physical phenomena and processes. You may have to think about this task for quite some time. Indeed, it is not so easy to give a reasoned answer to the question: when will the whites hanging in the kukuna dry out faster - if you open the window while the cold autumn rain is drizzling outside, or if you don’t open it? Or to this question: why do two light bodies floating on the surface, both wetted or not wetted by water, hide towards each other, and in the case when one body is wetted by water and the other is not, repels?

Most of the problems require not only reasoning, but also accurate calculations and drawing graphs of physical patterns. And a schoolchild can calculate a lot - and the speed that must be imparted to the body in order for it to fly away from the nebula. Andromeda; and the minimum and maximum pressures inside a spherical drop of liquid floating in another liquid; and the maximum magnetic pressure on the surface of the superconductor; and you never know what else!

Almost each of the 13 chapters of the books contains problems on topics that are usually not included in school problems. For example, you can also indicate topics such as “Gallium Transformation”, “Probability of Thermodynamic State”, “Superconductors in a Magnetic Field”, “Emission and Reflection of Electromagnetic Waves”. This listing alone suggests that the authors touch upon important and profound issues.

The most difficult problems, problems with non-standard formulations (and there are quite a few of them in the collection) are often the most interesting. The authors of the collection hope that solving such problems will encourage schoolchildren to read popular (and maybe not only popular!) books and to study physics in more depth.
