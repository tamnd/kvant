An excellent physics institute has been created in the capital of the Latvian SSR, Rita, which has a research nuclear reactor used as a powerful source of neutrons.

Any nuclear reactor serves not only as a source of energy, but also as a relatively powerful source of neutrons. Research reactors are used to study a wide range of physical and chemical processes. The construction of such reactors requires significant funds, and only highly qualified personnel can experiment with them.

It is known that many substances, when irradiated with neutrons, noticeably change their physical and chemical properties. At the reactor of the Institute of Physics in Rita, scientists are studying changes in the structure of a number of dielectric crystals, leading to changes in their optical properties. These studies have not only scientific significance, but also practical application in solving a number of technical problems. Thus, recently the influence of neutron irradiation on substances that are supposed to be used in the construction of industrial nuclear reactors has been studied at the reactor.

Under the influence of neutron irradiation, molecules of neoctor chemical compounds dissociate (decay) at a greater or lesser rate. The search for compounds sufficiently resistant to neutron irradiation is an important task. The study of a number of chemical compounds in this direction is carried out at the research reactor of the Institute of Nuclear Physics in the capital of the Uzbek SSR, Tashkent. Trials carried out by Uzbek scientists together with scientists from the Institute of Atomic Energy named after. I.V. Kurchatov at the Tashkent Research Reactor made it possible to select chemical compounds suitable for use in technology, the molecules of which are sufficiently resistant to neutron irradiation.

The most important property of neutrons is that they are able to penetrate the nucleus of an atom and change its composition; in this case, either the nucleus of another chemical element or the nucleus of a radioactive isotone of the irradiated element is formed. A classic example of such a nuclear transformation is the process of formation of new chemical elements that underlies all nuclear technology when uranium is irradiated with neutrons. Radioactive isotones are widely used in medicine, technology, and agriculture. At the Tashkent reactor, Uzbek physicists obtain radioactive isotones of a number of elements.

Important scientific and practical work is carried out at the research reactor of the Institute of Nuclear Physics in the capital of the Kazakh SSR, Alma-Ata.

Neutrons can also perform another important “service”. They are indispensable for analyzing the chemical state of a substance, when determining the content of a particular element in a substance; especially when the amount of an element in a substance is so small that it is impossible to detect its presence using chemical analysis methods. An example is the analysis of rocks for the content of rare, and therefore expensive, elements. Here we come to the aid of electrons (β-particles), which come to use in basic cases. Modern instruments make it possible to register each individual γ-quanta and each electron and change their energy. The energy of an emitted quantum or electron is a quantity characteristic of the nucleus of a given element.
