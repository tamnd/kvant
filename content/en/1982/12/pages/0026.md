M753. The numbers a, b, c lie in the interval [0, π/2] and satisfy the equalities
cos a=a,
sin cos b=b,
cos sin c=c.

Arrange these numbers in ascending order.

Answer: b<a<c. For proof, note that since sin x<x for all positive x, and cos x kills in the interval ]0; π/2 [, for all x∈]0; π/2 [the following double inequality holds:
sin cos x<cos x<cos x.

It follows from this that
b=sin cos b<cos b, cos c<cos sin c=c,
that is
cos b-b>0=cos a-a>cos c-c.

But the function y=cos x-x kills in the interval ]0; π/2 [, therefore b<a<c.

M754. a) Are there polynomials?
P=P(x,y,z),
Q=Q(x,y,z),
R=R(x,y,z)
from the variables x, y, z also, that the identity holds
(x-y+1)³P+(y-z-1)³Q+
+(z-2x+1)³R=1?
6) Same question for identity
(x-y+1)³P+(y-z-1)³Q+
+(z-x+1)³R=1.

a) Answer: does not exist. Note that the system of equations
x-y+1=0, y-z-1=0, z-2x+1=0
has a solution (x,y,z)=(1,2,1). When substituting these values ​​of the variables into the identity under consideration, the polynomials at the polynomials P, Q and R, and with them the entire part, vanish, and the identity is violated, no matter what polynomials we choose.

6) Answer: they exist. Let f=x-y+1,
g=y-z-1, h=z-x+1, then f+g+h=1 for all values ​​of x, y and z. Raising the last identity to the seventh power, we find that 1 is represented as a sum of terms of the form f⁵·g⁵·h⁵, where k, l, m≥0 and k+l+m=7, which means that at least one of the numbers k, l, m is not less than 3. Thus, each of these terms is divisible by either f³, or g³, or h³. Grouping all terms divisible by f³ and taking f³ out of brackets, we obtain f³·P. Grouping those of the remaining terms that are divisible by g³, we obtain g³·Q. The sum of the remaining terms is equal to h³·R (P, Q and R are some polynomials in x, y, z). As a result, we get an identity of the desired form:
f³·P+g³·Q+h³·R=1.

P. Gusyatnikov,
Yu. Nesterenko

Editorial. These problems suggest that the general theorem is true: if the polynomials F₁, F₂, ..., Fₙ (in one or more variables) do not simultaneously vanish, then one will also find the polynomials P₁, P₂, ..., Pₙ such that F₁P₁+F₂P₂+...+FₙPₙ=1. This theorem is indeed true if we take into account not only real, but also complex values ​​of the variables*).
