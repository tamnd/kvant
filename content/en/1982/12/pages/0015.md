Small notes

This simple but ingenious device for producing enormous pressure was invented by the American physicist Percy Williams Bridgem (1882-1961).

If two cones are brought together at their vertices (Fig. 1, a) and rolled along the axis, then a contact area (Fig. 1, b) is formed. The emerging direction σ is maximum in the middle of this area, and at the edges it is equal to zero (Fig. 1, ​​θ). As the rolling increases, the area increases, but the maximum direction σm remains the same (dotted lines in Figure 1, θ and θ).

Near the top of this device, called Bridgham's anvil, the material slopes not only vertically, but also laterally, that is, its slope is close to all-round (similar to the slope caused by hydrostatic pressure). In this case, the permissible direction is much greater than the usual tensile strength. (Strong all-round rolling does not destroy the material at all.) In addition, with increasing pressure, the strength increases, since it makes it difficult for layers of material to slide mutually (as with friction).
