Have you ever observed the following picture: a basin with a slightly convex house stands steadily on the table, but for some reason it begins to sway on a hot stove, and these vibrations do not die down for quite a long time? (Housewives know that some kasrol are also capable of “dancing” on the stove.) How can this be explained?

The stability of the pelvis on the table is quite understandable - its center of gravity C is below the center of curvature of the day O (Fig. 1). The reason for the unusual behavior of the pelvis on a hot plate is that at the point of contact with the plate it becomes very hot and bulges out, as a result of which the local center of curvature O' turns out to be lower than the center of gravity C (Fig. 2), and the balance becomes unstable. This is also facilitated by the fact that the thickness of the herab is uniform: its lower side is hotter, it expands more, and the bulge is directed precisely downwards. On figure 2, where the contact with the plate is shown, the hotter side of the basin is blackened. The effect is further enhanced if cold water is poured into the basin - this increases the temperature difference and the warping of the day. Having swung to the side, the pelvis stops for a moment, contact and bulge form in a new place, it is qualitative, etc.

A cold ball on a hot plate will also be unstable, but it begins to roll rather than oscillate in one case, it is of high quality, etc.
