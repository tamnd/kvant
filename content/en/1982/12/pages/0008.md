One of the important areas of work at this reactor is the study of the possibility, using neutron irradiation, of improving the physical and chemical properties of a number of materials used in industry.

I had the opportunity to visit the capital of the Georgian SSR, Tbilisi, and get acquainted with the work of some of the scientific physics institutions there. At the Faculty of Physics of Tbilisi State University and in other scientific institutions of the city, extensive research is being carried out on the physics and technology of semiconductors, and on radio electronics. At the Institute of Physics, which has a research reactor and a large cryogenic installation, interesting phenomena related to the effect of neutrons on the physical properties of substances at low temperatures are being studied. These studies have made it possible to significantly clarify the physical nature of changes in the properties of materials that support neutron irradiation. The same institute studies the unique properties of the so-called superfluid He-II. The fact is that liquid helium, being cooled to a temperature of 2.17 K, jumps into a new state in which its viscosity becomes zero (liquid flow without friction). Due to the lack of viscosity, the movement of liquid helium has interesting features. They became the subject of study by Georgian physicists.

I do not have the opportunity to dwell in any detail on the state of the physical sciences in the largest of the allied resource centers - the RSFSR. Suffice it to say that large scientific physical centers are located throughout the vast resource area from Kaliningrad to Vladivostok. I had and still have to collaborate with many of them. Further, simply listing the named most important physical institutions of the RSFSR would take up too much space. The capital of the RSFSR is also home to the main headquarters of Soviet science - the USSR Academy of Sciences, which is headed by the outstanding Soviet physicist Academician A.P. Aleksandrov.

It is necessary to note the great merits of Soviet physicists in solving the most important problems facing the country. Thus, during the Great Patriotic War, they made a huge contribution to the assimilation of existing military equipment and the creation of new types.

The author of these lines, together with other physicists, scientists and engineers of many specialists, entered the technical science team created in our country. Before my eyes, the physical ideas underlying this technology received industrial implementation. Now the Soviet Union is one of the leading powers in this area. In a number of nuclear technologies, our country is ahead of the most industrially developed capitalist countries. It is well known that the first nuclear power system was built in the Soviet Union. Soviet nuclear icebreakers have no equal in the world.

Soviet physics greeted the six decades of the formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics with new successes both in the field of physiological research and in their application in various sectors of the national economy. Thus, the physicists of all allied resource workers are fulfilling the historical plans of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union.
