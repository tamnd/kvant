6. Proof. Let us denote the numbers of the population $\{0, 1, 2, \dots, n\}$ by $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n$ so that the inequalities are satisfied
$$|a-a_0| \leqslant |a-a_1| \leqslant \dots \leqslant |a-a_n|.$$
For each natural number $k$, the interval $|x-a| < k/2$ contains at most $k$ numbers from our population. Therefore, for values ​​of $k$ from 1 to $n$ the following inequalities are valid:
$$|a-a_k| \geqslant k/2.$$
Moreover, it is obvious that $|a-a_0| \geqslant \langle a \rangle$. After multiplying all these inequalities we get

$$|a| \cdot |a-1| \cdot \dots \cdot |a-n| = |a-a_0| \cdot |a-a_1| \cdot \dots \cdot |a-a_n| \geqslant \langle a \rangle \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{2} \cdot \dots \cdot \frac{n}{2} = \langle a \rangle \frac{n!}{2^n},$$
