XIII International Physics Olympiad

The 1982 Olympics took place from June 19 to 29 in the Federal Republic of Germany, in the resort town of Malente near Hamburg. 17 countries took part in it: Australia, Bulgaria, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Greece, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, the Soviet Union, Finland, France, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden and Yugoslavia. For the first time, schoolchildren from Austria, Greece and the Netherlands came to the Olympiad. Thus, the circle of countries participating in the physical Olympiad has expanded this year.

As in previous years, each country was represented by a team of five members - students from secondary and vocational schools. Their age did not exceed 20 years. Each team was accompanied by two leaders.

The beautiful figure on the Olympiad emblem has a certain physical meaning to the wave - it illustrates the flow of liquid (or gas) around a rotating cylinder. In figure you can clearly see how, due to the rotation of the cylinder, the speed of the liquid appears to be lower than at the top. In accordance with Bernulin's law, where the speed of a fluid is less, its pressure is greater. Therefore, a lift force perpendicular to the direction of flow will act on the cylinder. This effect was discovered in 1852 by the German physicist G. Magnus.

Let us give an interesting example of the practical use of the Magnus effect. In 1924, a shunt was built at the shipyards of the city of Kila, on which turning cylinders were installed together with the masts. As a result of the wind blowing on them, a force arose that set the shuna in motion.

The Olympad was opened by the Minister of Education and Science of the Federal Republic of Germany B. Engholm. It read a greeting to the participants from the President of the Federal Republic of Germany, Karl Karsten. The history of international physics Olympiads was told by the honorable growth of the Olympiad, Professor Kunfalvi (Hungary), who took part in all previous Olympiads.

As usual, the Olympiad was held in two rounds - theoretical and experimental. The days of perfection were separated from each other by a day of rest. Five hours were allotted to solve each type of problem. At the same time, it was allowed to use electronic calculators, which all participants received as a gift from the organization of the Olympiad. The texts of the problems were written in four languages: English, German, Russian, French.
