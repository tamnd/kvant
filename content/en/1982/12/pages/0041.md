⟦folio 39⟧

Let us explain what has been said using specific tasks. Most of them were offered at the entrance exams to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology.

Problem 1. The force acting on the body periodically changes its direction to the opposite (Fig. 1, a). How does a body move under the influence of such a force?

Let's consider how the speed of a body and its movement along the line of action of the force (let this be the X axis) depend on time. To determine this, we will assume that the initial speed and coordinate are equal to zero.

In the 0-1 section, the force is constant both in modulo and direction; therefore, the movement of the body is uniformly accelerated: the speed changes in proportion to time, and the displacement is proportional to the square of time. In this case, the velocity graph (more precisely, its projection v onto the chosen direction) is a straight line passing through the origin of coordinates (Fig. 1, b), and the displacement graph (or rather, x coordinates) is a parabolic, the vertex of which is located at the origin of coordinates (Fig. 1, c).

In section 1-2 the force is constant, but directed in the opposite direction. The speed decreases linearly with time and at point 2 becomes zero (the time of action of the force is the same as in the first section; this means that the change in momentum is the same). The coordinate graph in this section is a parabola whose vertex is at point 2. At point 1 there is a smooth transition from one parabola to another, since there is no discontinuity in speed at this point.

Further consideration is carried out in a similar way. As can be seen from the resulting graphs, the speed does not change sign, only periodic ones turn to zero, and the coordinate increases all the time. In other words, the body is constantly moving away from its initial position, its movement is not at all periodic, although this is exactly the answer one often hears from applicants for entrance exams.
