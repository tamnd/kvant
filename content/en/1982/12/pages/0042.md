Problem 3. A small ball of mass $m$ moves along the “equator” of the inner surface of a spherical shell with mass $M$ at a constant absolute speed, making a full revolution in time $T$ (Fig. 3). Assuming that there are no external forces and there is no tension, determine with what force the ball presses on the sphere. The distance between the centers of gravity of the ball and the sphere is $d$.

Since external forces do not act on the sphere-ball system, the center of mass of this system must move (which immediately follows).

Fig. 3.

Blows from Newton's laws). This means that not only the ball moves, but also the sphere, and both of them rotate around an axis passing through the center of mass of the system.

Let us denote the distance from the ball to the center of mass by $r$, then

$$mr = M(d - r), \text{ и } r = d \frac{m}{M + m}.$$

According to Newton's third law, the force with which the ball presses on the sphere is equal in magnitude to the force with which the sphere acts on the ball. It imparts centripetal acceleration to the ball.

$$a_u = \omega^2 r = \left(\frac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2 \frac{Md}{(M + m)}$$

So, the required force is equal in modulus

$$F = ma_u = \frac{4\pi^2 Mm d}{T^2 (M + m)}$$

and is directed radially from the center of rotation.
