e) The recombination time of ions and electrons in a gas discharge is quite long - that’s why the lamp is on all the time.
g) The resistance capacitance of a capacitor with a capacity of $C=4,7$ µF is equal to $1/\omega C=677,3$ Ohm. The total system resistance in this case is
$$z' = \sqrt{\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2 + R^2} = 373,7 \text{ Ом.}$$

It is almost the same as that of a device without a capacitor; therefore, the lamp has the same expulsion properties and lights up according to the same principle. The only difference is that the phase drainage is now opposite in sign to the dwgy phases $\varphi$:
$$tg\varphi' = \frac{\omega L - 1/\omega C}{R} = -2,01, \text{ и } \varphi' = -63,6^\circ.$$
In applications with a large number of fluorescent lamps, additional capacitors are used to compensate for phase dwga.
Task 2.
Having denoted the distance from the fulcrum to the center of mass by $s$, the mass by $m$ and the moment of inertia relative to the fulcrum by $I$, for the period of oscillation $T$ we write
$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgs}}.$$

In this case, $I$ is determined through the moment of inertia $I_0$ relative to the center of mass:
$$I = I_0 + ms^2.$$

From equations (1) and (2) we have
$$I_0 + ms^2 = \left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^2 mgs,$$
