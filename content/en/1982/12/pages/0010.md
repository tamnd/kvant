As the centuries passed, this process of transformation of geometry, which began with Lobachevsky, began to unfold in full force; Lobachevsky was called the “Copernicus of geometry.” We must not, of course, forget that the new geometry was also developed and published by Bolyai, but the advantage will go to Lobachevsky, because he did it earlier and then significantly continued his research and publications.

Lobachevsky affirmed the idea of ​​​​the unprovability of the fifth act and the possibility of non-Euclidean geometry, based on physical, conceptual-theoretical convictions. This is already expressed in his words quoted earlier (in the first part of the article) from the preface to “A New Beginning of Geometry...”. “The truth that they wanted to prove,” that is, the fifth arrived, does not lie “in the concepts themselves,” but in application to real space and is subject to verification by experience, like a physical law. This is the negation of Kant’s “a priori”: geometry is not independent of experience, but is subject to verification by experience*). In other works, Lobachevsky clearly objected to Kantinism in a general form, when he wrote, for example, that “concepts are acquired through experience... drivers should not be trusted.”

*) Strictly speaking, the word “geometry” should be understood in two ways: as a purely mathematical theory and as a theory of real spatial relations. In this second capacity, it is subject to experimental verification (modern physics has proven that our space is not an exact ενClidean space). But from a purely mathematical theory in itself, first of all, logical harmony and necessarily consistency are required. In this form, this or that geometry is a set of propositions deduced from accepted premises. And what applications it is in - this in itself does not affect it.

By the way, it is worth noting that scientific methods believe that they and the sciences do not need philosophical thinking at all. All the great scientists from Newton and Galileo, if we talk only about modern times, were philosophical thinkers. Without philosophy, science does not develop: the position of its new paths, when they have not taken shape, is the philosophical movement of thought. The only question is what kind of philosophy it is, whether it is associated with exact logic and facts of experience or with the general phrases of a priori and pure speculative thinking located in airless space. Galilee, Newton, Lobachevsky not only expressed philosophical judgments, but also, starting from general convictions, built the tasks of scientific theories - solid foundations of entire vast areas of science.

The emergence of non-Euclidean geometry was the beginning of a revolutionary transformation of geometry. But also, which is typical for a revolution, along with the name of its forces, the reaction also grew. It was precisely when the discovery of a new geometry was already approaching that Kant’s philosophy appeared with its doctrine of the a priori nature of geometry, of space as an a priori form of contemplation. Any geometry other than that inherent in this form of contemplation seemed unthinkable.

Lobachevsky clearly opposes these views. The emergence of a new geometry defines them and opens up invisible, unthinkable equal paths for the development of science - a revolution is taking place. A genius is a revolution, a revolution is a genius in action.
