It is interesting that on the basis of the described phenomenon, the famous American scientist Benjamin Franklin (who discovered, in particular, atmospheric electricity) created an original musical instrument. The purpose of a series of well ground glass cups, drilled in the middle, at equal distances from each other, was attached to one common axis. Under the box in which this system was located, a teacher was attached (like a sewing machine), which was rotated. From the simple touch of wet fingers to the rotating cups of the environment, the sounds intensified to fortissimo or fell to a whisper.

Now it is difficult to imagine this amazing musical instrument, but people who heard it assured that the harmony of its sounds had a stunning effect on both the performer and the listener. In 1763, Franklin gave his sample of this instrument to the English woman Miss Davis. For several years she demonstrated it in many European countries, and then this amazing instrument disappeared without a trace.

A mirror that doesn't confuse the right and left sides

Take two ordinary mirrors without frames, place them with their edges at right angles to each other with the mirror side inward and look straight into the corner along its bisector. You will see your image. Then close one eye, for example the right one, and your image will also close the right eye; raise your left hand - in the mirror your left hand also appears raised. Why?

This is not difficult to understand. You cannot directly see the image of your left half in the left mirror, since (following the law of reflection) the rays reflected from it do not go towards you, but in the opposite direction and on their way they meet the second, right mirror. This is where the final image of your left half is obtained, which you will see, since the right mirror is positioned in the appropriate manner for this. In other words, one mirror imperceptibly “transmits” the image to the other, resulting in not one reflection, but two, so that the left half naturally turns back into the left, and the right into the right.

Teller, try to gradually increase the angle of the mirrors. First, the middle of the face with the nose will disappear, then only one ear will remain, and at some angles the image of the face will disappear completely, but at an angle close to 180°, a clear image of the face will appear in a flat mirror. If you again do it in the reverse order, then everything will happen the other way around: first, the face begins to blur in width, the nose will swell, stick out in length, then a third eye will appear on the bridge of the nose, and... the like.

To understand why the image behaves this way, try to draw the path of rays in such a mirror system for different angles between the mirrors.
