2) In this case, the beginning of the reporting system must be associated with the aircraft. Relative to it, the initial speed of the slug is equal to $u$, therefore the horizontal coordinate of the slug will change according to the law $x = ut$. The vertical coordinate will still have the form $y = gt^2/2$. Therefore, the transformation served as a change, we obtain the equation of the trajectory of the slug relative to the movement of the aircraft:

$$y = \frac{gx^2}{2u^2}.$$

It is obvious that here, too, the trajectory of the projectile is a parabola.

3) In order to describe the movement of the first projectile relative to the second, we will use the same reporting system as for the first projectile, that is, we will place the origin of coordinates at the point where the plane is at the moment of the first shot.

Let us write the equation for the coordinates $x_i$ and $y_i$ of the first slug and for the corresponding projections $v_{1x}$ and $v_{1y}$ of its velocity:

$$x_i = (u + v)t, \quad y_i = \frac{gt^2}{2},$$

$$v_{1x} = u + v, \quad v_{1y} = gt.$$

Here time $t$ is measured from the moment of the first shot, but according to the meaning of the problem it is not less than the interval $t$ between shots, that is $t \geq \tau$.

Similar equations for the second charge will look like:

$$x_2 = v\tau + (u + v)(t - \tau), \quad y_2 = \frac{g(t - \tau)^2}{2},$$

$$v_{2x} = u + v, \quad v_{2y} = g(t - \tau).$$

If connections with the second slug are observed, then relative to it the coordinates (or projections of sorisity) of the first slug are equal to the difference of the corresponding coordinates (or corresponding projections of sorosity) of the first and second slugs in the fixed reporting system:

$$x_{1OTH} = x_1 - x_2 = (u + v)t - v\tau - (u + v)(t - \tau) = u\tau,$$

$$y_{1OTH} = y_1 - y_2 = \frac{gt^2}{2} - \frac{g(t - \tau)^2}{2} = g\tau - \frac{g\tau^2}{2},$$

$$v_{1OTH} = v_{1x} - v_{2x} = (u + v) - (u + v) = 0,$$

$$v_{1OTH} = v_{1y} - v_{2y} = gt - g(t - \tau) = g\tau.$$

So, after both shots, the projectiles move horizontally at the same speeds, so that the distance between them does not change and remains equal to $x_{1OTH} = u\tau$. Vertically, the first slug moves relative to the second with the help of $v_{1OTH} = g\tau$, therefore the distance between the slugs changes with the currents according to the law $y_{1OTH} = g\tau - g\tau^2/2$.

Problem 2. In any tank moving at a speed of $v_\tau = 54$ km/h, a bullet flying at a speed of $v_0 = 1800$ km/h hits at an angle of $\varphi = 60^\circ$ to the direction of movement of the tank, and bounces off from it (Fig. 1, a). At what speed $\vec{v}$ will the bouncing bullet fly?

It is convenient to first solve the problem in the reporting system associated with the tank, and then go to the stationary reporting system.

Relative to the tank, the speed of the bullet before impact is $\vec{v}'_0 = \vec{v}_0 - \vec{v}_\tau$ (Fig. 1, b). After a spring impact, the bullet speed model $\vec{v}'$ remains the same ($\vec{v}' = \vec{v}_0'$), and the “reflection” angle $\alpha'$ will be equal to the “incidence” angle $\varphi'$.
