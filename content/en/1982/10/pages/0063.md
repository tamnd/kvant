3. Find the maximum power that the source can deliver to the load. What should the load resistance be for maximum power?

10th grade

1. A mathematical mat, in which the thread is replaced by a rigid, weightless rod, performs free oscillations of small amplitude. By what percentage, at most, can the period of oscillation be changed if we transform a mathematical mat into a type of physical one, by attaching a load of the same mass as at the end to a certain point on the rod?

2. A tube with a length of $l=1$ m and a radius of $r=0,08$ m has a mass of $m_1=4$ kg. The tube contains a piston with a mass of $m_2=10$ kg. At one end of the tube there is a sealing ring that does not allow air to pass through, and at the other there is a clamp that can hold the piston in its extreme position. When the piston is located with the piston ring on the bloody vertical wall (Fig. 4). After this, the piston was moved to the opposite end of the tube and secured with a clamp, and the tube was released. Will the tube stay on the wall? Friction coefficient of the sealing ring against the wall $\mu=0,5$, atomosphere pressure $p_0=10^5$ Pa.

3. A circuit is assembled from a battery, two identical voltmeters and two identical milliammeters (Fig. 5). Instruments connected in parallel display $U_1=0,26$ V and $I_1=0,75$ mA. The second milliammeter shows $I_2=1$ mA. Determine the direction of the battery terminals and the resistance of the device.

4. Two rigid weightless rods of length $l$ each are fastened at their ends in a hinge with mass $m$. At the ends of the rods there are balls, the masses of which are $m$ and $2m$. The structure is placed on a vertical table with the hinge up and begins to move apart with a small push so that the rods remain in the vertical plane all the time (Fig. 6). There is no friction. 1) Find the speed of the hinge before striking the table. 2) Find the speed of the hinge with mass $2m$ at the moment when the angle between the rods is $90^\circ$.

Experimental tour

8th grade

1. Determine the friction coefficient of the stand (body) on the table surface.

Equipment: a tent with a foot, a stand from a universal tent (or a piece of free-form metal weighing about 2 kg), a stiff thread, a wooden ruler, a dynamometer.

2. Determine the density of the unknown liquid.

Equipment: beaker, cork or real tennis ball, plasticine, vessel with water, vessel with an unknown liquid, thread.

9th grade

1. Determine the unknown resistance.

Equipment: current source BC-4-12, connecting rod with two legs, electronic demonstration, resistor (6 Ohm, 2 A) - 2 pcs., thread, iron nut, connecting wires - 6 pcs., unknown resistance, wooden ruler.

2. Determine the work required to inflate the balloon.

Equipment: balloon, U-shaped pressure gauge, glass tee, centimeter, rubber tube, graph paper.

Note. Before measurements, you should inflate and deflate the balloon 2-3 times.

10th grade

1. Plot and graph the dependence of the depth of field on the diameter of the diffraction at a fixed distance between the lamp and the lens.

Equipment: school lens, white screen, stand with a light bulb directed 2.5-6.3 V, current source (2.5-6.3 V), student ruler, black paper, scissors, graph paper.

Note. Sub-depth of field refers to the difference between the maximum and minimum distances between the lens and the screen at which the image is still sharp.

2. Determine how many times less heat is lost in a calorimeter with a coefficient than in a calorimeter without a coefficient.

Equipment: school calorimeter, thermometer, resistat (6 Ohm, 2 A) - 2 pcs., current source BC-4-12, connection wires - 8 pcs., school ampermeter (2 A), vessel with water (200 ml), heating coil on the block.
