Problem 3. A horizontal disk rotates around its axis, making $n = 5$ rpm. A person walks along the radius of the disk with a constant speed $u = 1,5$ m/s relative to the disk. How does the model of a person's speed relative to the ground change depending on the distance $r$ from the axis of the disk? What is the_module of this speed at a distance of $R = 3$ m from the disk axis?
At any point on the disk, the speed $\vec{v}$ of a person relative to the ground is equal to the vector sum of two speeds (Fig. 2, a): the speed $\vec{u}$ of the person’s movement along the disk, directed along its radius, and the linear speed $\vec{V}$ of the point of the disk in which the person is located, directed perpendicular to the radius, that is
$$\vec{v} = \vec{u} + \vec{V}.$$
Since the model $V$ of linear speed is associated with the angular speed $\omega$, and therefore with the frequency $n$ of disk rotation: $V = \omega r = 2\pi n r$, then the model of a person’s speed relative to the ground is equal to
$$v = \sqrt{u^2 + V^2} = \sqrt{u^2 + 4\pi^2 n^2 r^2}.$$
Graphic dependences of $v$ from $r$ are presented on figure 2, b.
At a distance $R$ from the disk axis, the model of the desired speed is equal to
$$v_R = \sqrt{u^2 + 4\pi^2 n^2 R^2} \approx 2,2 \text{ м/с}.$$
Problem 4. A wheel of radius $R$ moves along the ground translationally with a speed $\vec{v}_0$ and rotates with an angular speed $\omega$ (Fig. 3). Find the tangential and normal acceleration projections of a certain point $A$ on the wheel rim in a fixed frame of reference related to the ground.
Recall that the tangential (tangent) projection $a_{\tau}$ of acceleration $\vec{a}$ is a projection onto the direction

tangent to the trajectory, that is, to the direction of speed; $a_{\tau}$ is responsible for changing the speed model. The normal projection $a_H$ is the projection of the acceleration $\vec{a}$ in the direction perpendicular (normal) to the tangent, that is, perpendicular to the speed; $a_H$ is responsible for changing the direction of speed.
First, consider the motion of the point $A$ in the reference frame associated with the wheel. Here the point rotates uniformly around a circle of radius $R$, so that the speed changes only in direction. The acceleration of the point is radially aligned to the center of the wheel and is equal according to the $a = \omega^2 R$ model (this acceleration is also called centripetal).
