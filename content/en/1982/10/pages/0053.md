Problem 5*. A heavy disk of radius $R$ is placed on two inextensible threads wound around it (Fig. 4). The ends of the threads are secured. The threads are constantly tensioned as the disk moves. At some point the whole speed of the disk is $\omega$, and the speed between the threads is $a$. What is the speed of the center of the disk at this moment?

Let's choose two reporting systems: a fixed one, connected, for example, to the ground, and a movable one, connected to the center of the disk. Thus, the task comes down to finding the speed of the moving reporting system relative to the stationary one.

Let the threads touch the disk at points $A$ and $B$ at this moment. In the moving reporting system (connected to the center of the disk), the velocities $\vec{u}_A$ and $\vec{u}_B$ of these points are equal modulo $\omega R$ and are directed along the threads. In a system connected to the ground, the velocities $\vec{v}_A$ and $\vec{v}_B$ can only be directed perpendicular to the threads, since the threads are always tense.
