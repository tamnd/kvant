ONCE AGAIN ABOUT THE QUEEN’S SACRIFICE

Combinations in which the queen is sacrificed are attractive and arouse increased interest among chess fans. Therefore, on our “pages” we constantly love the collection of queens donated by grandmasters. They also listen to funny incidents. The next game was played in the 1981 national championship in Frunze (major league) between two Lviv grandmasters. A. Mikhalchishin - O. Romanshin

Grionfeld's defense
1. d4 Kf6 2. c4 g6 3. Kf3 Cg7 4. Kc3 d5 5. cd K:d5 6. e4 K:c3 7. bc c5 8. Ce3. In theory, other continuations are known - 8. h3, 8. Ce2, 8. Rb1, but the move made also does not immediately lose. 8...Qa5 9. Qd2 Kc6 10. Rb1 cd 11. cd 0—0 12. d5??? CC3. The whites have dealt.

If Grionfeld could see this position, he would be happy with such an impeccable implementation of his opening idea - it is simply impossible to ask more from the funked g7 bishop!

Mikhalchyshyn quickly left the stage. And Romanshin looked confused. He himself had not yet fully realized that he had inflicted a very disappointing defeat on his friend and coach. “What could I do? - he justified himself. — Hit on d2 and “stay”?” As you can see, grosseyers are people too...

The national championship in Frunze was extremely exciting, and not only thanks to the incident described. The average age of the participants was lower than in all previous championships, which affected the chess content of the tournament - pawns and pieces were sacrificed in almost all games, and in its final results - the young G. Kasparov and L. Psakhis became champions, the operational properties of the precedors by two and a half points!

E. Sveshchnikov - V. Tseshkovsky
(Frunze, 1981)

After the queen retreated - 22...Qg6 White would have played 23. 0-0! (the king and rook have not yet moved) with a decisive advantage. Unexpected follow-up 22...c3! Teler's capture of the queen leads to the rarest mating construction with two black epaulette pawns symmetrically guarding the indirect king - 23. hg R:h1×. White did not take the queen, but after three moves - 23. Kb5 cb 24. Q:b2 Qg5 25. a4 C:b5 - he dealt anyway, and the spectators were deprived of the pleasure of watching the “original number”.

Garry Kasparov sacrificed a lot in this tournament, but never parted with his queen. Let us present one episode from the game of the young national champion, which he played at the student world championship.
