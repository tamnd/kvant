Standard programming techniques

(Programming phraseology)

Today we are publishing the first lesson of the one-year cycle “Standard Programming Techniques”. This series is designed for schoolchildren who are familiar with the basics of programming and want to deepen their knowledge. Together with graduates of the Correspondence School of Programming, which worked in «Kvant» from September 1979 to April 1982, new participants can also perform control tasks. The classes are taught by the teacher of the Kuibyshev Aviation Institute L. Sternberg.

For each lesson, preparatory tasks are offered that you need to solve independently. In the lesson, possible solutions are discussed, and then test tasks are given, the solutions of which must be sent for verification. All sample programs are written in Pascal; control tasks must be performed on it. You can read about this language in the magazine «Kvant» (No. 10-11 for 1981 and No. 1-4 for 1982).

Schoolchildren who show the best results at the end of the one-year cycle will be invited to the VII All-Union Summer School of Programmers in 1983.

Lesson 1. Calculation using recurrence formulas

What does it take to speak a language? You need to know a certain number of words and be able to construct phrases correctly, that is, know the grammar of the language - that’s all, it seems. But no: you also need to know phraseology - that is, when and what decision phrases should be used.

How to greet a person? The British, for example, believe that the best phrase for this is “Hau do io do?”, but if you say hello in Russian with a literal translation of this phrase (“How are you?”), then you will, apparently, be considered impolite (in Russian this question can be asked after a greeting, but not as a greeting) or will be taught to answer in detail on the essence of the question, which in fact you did not need. As you can see, ignorance of phraseology can lead to troubles: you may not be understood or misunderstood.

What is programming phraseology? Programming is also a kind of conversation, only not with people, but with computers, and it is conducted in special languages.
