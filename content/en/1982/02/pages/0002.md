It turns out that the sound vibrations of a glass or metal plate can not only be heard, but also... seen. This was first demonstrated by the German scientist Ernst Chladni in 1787.
If fine sand is poured onto the surface of the plate and vibrations of the plate are excited, the sand will be redistributed, collecting along individual lines. These lines form various figures, which are now called cold figures. Some of them are depicted in the above figure.
Many scientists have tried to develop a mathematical theory of plate vibrations. Among them is Simeon Denis Poisson, whom he talks about in this issue of the magazine.
