2. $R = \sqrt{L/C}$ Note. See figure 2, which shows the current and voltage diagram vectors for the first and second sections of the circuit, as well as for the entire circuit. At any frequency, the current in the circuit will be in phase with the applied voltage if the angles $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ are equal.

3. $P_{\max} = \frac{\mathcal{E} U}{\omega L}$. Note. figure 3 shows a vector diagram of currents and voltages for a power line. Here there are $\overrightarrow{OA} = \overrightarrow{\mathcal{E}} - \exists D C$ generators; $\overrightarrow{OB} = \overrightarrow{U}$ — voltage on the receiving system; $\delta$ - phase shift between them; $\overrightarrow{BA} = \overrightarrow{U}_L = \overrightarrow{\mathcal{E}} - \overrightarrow{U}$ - voltage on the transmission line; $\overrightarrow{I}_L$ is the current in the line, spaced from $\overrightarrow{U}_L$ by $\pi/2$. It is convenient to transfer the vector $\overrightarrow{I}_L$ to the point $O$ and consider the triangle AOB. Its area, on the one hand, is $S = \frac{1}{2} U \sin \delta$, and on the other, $S = \frac{1}{2} |\overrightarrow{AB}| \times |OK| = \frac{1}{2} U_L U \cos \varphi$. Comparing these two expressions, we find that the active power is equal to

$$P = U I_L \cos \varphi = \frac{U U_L}{\omega L} \cos \varphi,$$

the maximum will be if $\delta = \pi/2$, and $\sin \delta = 1$.

4. $U_1 = U \frac{n_1(n_1 R_2 + n_2 R_1)}{n_1^2 R_2 + n_2^2 R_1}$;

$U_2 = U \frac{n_2(n_1 R_2 + n_2 R_1)}{n_1^2 R_2 + n_2^2 R_1}$;

$I_1 = \frac{U n_2(n_2 - n_1)}{n_1^2 R_2 + n_2^2 R_1}$;

$I_2 = \frac{U n_1(n_1 - n_2)}{n_1^2 R_2 + n_2^2 R_1}$.

Note. For this scheme the following equalities are also valid:

$$I_1 R_1 = U - U_1,$$

$$I_2 R_2 = U - U_2,$$

$$\frac{U_1}{U_2} = \frac{n_1}{n_2},$$

$$I_1 n_1 + I_2 n_2 = 0.$$
