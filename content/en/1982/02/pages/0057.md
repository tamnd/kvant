III Moscow Tournament of Young Physicists

...What does it mean to know?
That's where all the difficulties lie!
I. Goethe. "Faust"

From January 23 to April 26, 1981, the now traditional Tournament of Young Physicists was held in Moscow (see «Kvant», 1980, No. 8). This year it was organized by the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University; Chairman of the Tournament Organizing Committee - Head of the Department of Atomic Physics and Electronic Phenomena, Vice-President of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Academician E. P. Velikhov. High school students from nineteen schools in Moscow and the Moscow region took part in the Tournament.

The first round of the Tournament - the correspondence team competition - began on January 23 and ended on March 10. All participating schools were sent lists of 17 problems that anyone could solve. The proposed problems were devoted mainly to experimental or theoretical studies of real physical phenomena. Each such task is a problem whose solutions constitute scientific research. When forming the conditions, the authors sought to briefly outline only the subjects, giving the children the opportunity to specify the problem themselves, to make reasonable assumptions and simplifications, which, of course, requires the manifestation of physical intuition, the ability to approach the problem from different sides.

Below we present the conditions of some problems of the correspondence competition with brief comments on them.

The "Candle" task. A candle, burning, shines and warms. Measure the heat of combustion of a paraffin candle.

The simplicity of forming the problem and the opportunity to demonstrate their experimental abilities aroused keen interest of future physicists, and almost all schools sent a solution to this problem. The most interesting work was recognized as the work of I. Alekseev and D. Svirida (s.sh. No. 179) *).

Problem "Oscillations". A large, exposed test tube floats in water in a vertical position and can oscillate up and down (Fig. 1). Calculate the period of oscillation of the test tube and measure it. Explain the discrepancy between theory and experiment.

Theoretical calculation of the period of oscillation of a test tube, taking into account the thickness of water and its movement, is quite complicated. The period turns out to significantly depend on the correspondence of the cross-sectional areas of the test tube and the free surface of the water in the vessel; It is also necessary to take into account the wave formation process. The most reasonable theoretical estimates, combined with good experimental measurements, were given in their work by ten-class scientists V. Grebennik and S. Shavyrin (s.sh. No. 179).

Problem "Stirrer". To prepare betan, you need to mix sand with cement and other components. In practice, as a rule, this is done in cylindrical drums. How much and at what speed is it necessary to grind the drum (Fig. 2) to obtain a homogeneous mixture? Think through and consider simplifying the process model.

This task represents a server physical and technical problem that has not yet been solved to date. The most interesting messages were expressed in their works by the ten-classicists I. Kudryavtsev (school no. 2) and A. Polyakov (school no. 91).
