2. A massive cylinder of radius $R$ rests on two supports of equal height (Fig. 6). One of the stands is stationary, the other is a horizontal segment with a speed of $v$. 3. In a heat-conducting cylinder under a light piston there is a mixture of equal quantities of water and ice: $m_B = m_1 = 1$ kg. The pressure on the piston slowly increases from the initial value $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa to $p_1 = 2,5 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Determine how much ice will melt and how much work will be done by the external force. It is known that to decrease the melting temperature of ice by 1 degree, you need to bring the pressure to $14 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. First, solve the problem assuming that water and ice are incompressible, and then evaluate the correction that comes from taking into account the skimability. It is known that to reduce the volume of a certain amount of water by 1%, the pressure must be raised to $20 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Take the compressibility of ice to be equal to half the compressibility of water. The specific heat capacity of water is $c_B = 4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K), the specific heat of fusion of ice is $\lambda = 3 \cdot 10^5$ J/kg, the density of ice is $\rho_1 = 0,9\rho_B$, where $\rho_B$ is the density of water.
