F758. The rigid workpiece is clamped between two parallel guides moving in the horizontal direction at speeds $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$. At some instants of time, the points of contact of the workpiece with the guides lie on a straight line perpendicular to the vectors $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$. Which points of the workpiece have velocities at this moment equal in absolute value to $v_1$ and $v_2$?

The velocities of the workpiece points currently lying on the segment $AB$ uniformly change from $\vec{v}_1$ at point $A$ to $\vec{v}_2$ at point $B$. Consequently, the speed of point $O$ (see figure) at this moment is zero. Point $O$ is the instantaneous center of rotation. (Since the workpiece is volumetric, point $O$ lies on the instantaneous rotation axis, which is perpendicular to the drawing plane.) It is clear that speed $|\vec{v}_1|$ at a given moment has workpiece points lying on a circle of radius $OA$, and speed $|\vec{v}_2|$ has points lying on a circle of radius $OB$. (In a volumetric workpiece, points with such a speed lie on cylindrical surfaces whose radii are equal to $|OA|$ and $|OB|$, respectively.)

F759. A weightless rod of length $l$ with a small load of mass $m$ at the end is hinged at point $A$ (Fig. 1) and is in a vertical position, touching a body of mass $M$. With a little push the system starts to move. At what ratio between $m$ and $M$ will the rod at the moment of “separation” from the body $M$ make an angle $α = \pi/6$ with the horizon? What will be the speed of the body $M$ at this moment? Neglect the trence.

As long as the load touches the body, the speed of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the speed of the load, and the acceleration of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load.

Let $\vec{a}$ be the total acceleration of the load. Then we can write: $\vec{a} = \vec{a}_\tau + \vec{a}_u$, where $\vec{a}_u$ is the centripetal acceleration of the load as it moves along a circle of radius $l$, that is, $|\vec{a}_u| = v^2/l$, where $v$ is the modulus of the speed of the load. The horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load is equal to (see Fig. 2)

$$a_r = a_\tau \sin \alpha - \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

The body moves with such acceleration. Equation of body motion -

$$N = Ma_r = Ma_\tau \sin \alpha - M \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha,$$

where $N$ is the normal pressure force from the load. At the moment of lifting off the load $N = 0$, and

$$a_\tau \sin \alpha = \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

Acceleration $a_\tau$ at the moment of separation is imparted to the load only by the force of gravity and (see Fig. 2)

$$a_\tau = g \cos \alpha.$$

Thus, the speed at the moment of separation is equal to

$$v = \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha},$$
