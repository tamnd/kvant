⟦folio 40⟧

F767. In one of the projects for generating electricity, it was proposed to use sea currents and the Earth's magnetic field. The project will consist of the following.

Two horizontal metal plates are placed in the sea, located one above the other at a distance of $l=100$ m; the area of ​​each plate is $S=1$ km$^2$. Sea water, whose resistivity is $\varrho=0,25$ Ohm m, flows between the plates from west to east at a speed of $v=1$ m/s. The Earth's magnetic field in a given place is uniform, directed from south to north; the field induction is $B=10^{-4}$ T. Determine the maximum electrical power that can be released per voltage connected to the plates.

Sea water has a noticeable non-conductivity, so that when charged ions move in the Earth's magnetic field, they are deflected from the Lorentz fields to one of the horizontal plates. As a result, an uncompensated charge will appear on this plate, and an electric field appears between the plates. This field acts on naons with a force directed opposite to the Lorentz force. This filling of the charge on one of the plates will occur until the electric field of these charges balances the action of the Lorentz force. Note that for such equilibrium to occur, it is sufficient for a very small fraction of the total number of ions flowing between the plates to deviate towards one of the plates.

Determine the voltage $\mathcal{E}$ that arises between the horizontal plates in the absence of current between them, that is, the EMF of the resulting “electric generator”. If $q$ is a charge of ins, then under equilibrium conditions

$$qvB=q\mathcal{E}/L \Rightarrow \mathcal{E}=vBL.$$

If the plates are connected to an external load $R$, then current $I$ will flow through it; while the resistance of sea water between the plates. equal to $\varrho L/S$, will play the role of the internal resistance of the resulting electrical generator.

Let's determine the power released in the load:

$$P=I^2R=\frac{\mathcal{E}^2R}{(\varrho L/S+R)^2}.$$

To determine the maximum power, the following mathematical fact is used: for any $x>0$ the inequalities are satisfied

$$x+1/x \geq 2, (x+1/x)^2 \geq 4.$$

In the formula for power, we take the power $\varrho L/R/S$ out of brackets into the denominator:

$$P=\frac{\mathcal{E}^2R}{\varrho L/R/S(\varrho L/S+RS/L\varrho)^2}.$$

In the last formula, the expression in parentheses in the denominator is greater than or equal to 4. Therefore, for maximum power we have:

$$P_{\max}=\mathcal{E}^2S/4\varrho L=\frac{v^2B^2LS}{4\varrho}=1 \text{ Вт}.$$

(Note that this formula corresponds to the case when the external load is equal to the internal resistance of the generator.)
