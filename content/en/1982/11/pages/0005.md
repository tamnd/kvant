⟦folio 3⟧

During the difficult years of devastation, Civil War and famine, the revolution aroused a genuine passion for learning and scientific creativity. It is important to note that neither hunger, nor cold, nor material difficulties could extinguish this passion.

Academician P. S. Aleksadrov, one of the leading Soviet mathematicians, the creator of the Soviet school of set-theoretic topology, spoke beautifully about this time: “The period of the early 20s... was a rather unique phenomenon. An important positive factor of this era in the history of mathematics at Moscow University is the boundless scientific enthusiasm of young people. This enthusiasm was based on the great patriotic ideas of Soviet scientific culture, which aroused in students and scientists that genuine scientific fervor that did not manifest itself with such force within the walls of the pre-revolutionary mathematics department of Moscow University.”

The same picture is observed both in the few old universities and in the newly created ones. The first, revolutionary admissions of students to universities prepared and produced the older generation of Soviet mathematicians, who educated subsequent generations of Soviet scientists and laid the foundation for scientific mathematical schools.

Such schools arose in Moscow, Tbilisi, Tashkent, Kyiv, Kharkov. In Leningrad, the mathematical school continued to develop, the foundations of which were created by the works of P. L. Chebyshev, A. M. Lyapunov, A. A. Markov and other remarkable scientists and teachers. A school of theory of functions of a real variable was born in Moscow, which soon extended its interests to the theory of functions of a complex variable, probability theory, topology, differential equations, and geometry; The founders of this school were D.F. Egorov and N.N. Luzin.

The new university centers received serious assistance from the old universities, primarily from Moscow and Leningrad. In particular, young people who were brought up with boundless scientific enthusiasm moved to Tbilisi. For the mass students of plants and factories, engineers were needed - mathematicians were required to train them. There is an urgent need for scientists capable of assisting practice using mathematical methods. In the late twenties and early thirties, the construction of new types began throughout the country, admission to graduate schools was expanded, and places were allocated in the oldest colleges and institutes of the USSR Academy of Sciences for the training of specialists at all levels from among the youth of the indigenous national union and autonomous resource. This measure turned out to be extremely productive, since in a short time a larger number of talented young people were trained for active scientific work. Returning to their homeland, they became centers for accepting the scientific thought of young resource people and organizers of national scientific schools. As a result, a strong school of the theory of functions of a complex variable arose in Yerevan, a school of functional analysis and the theory of differential equations in Baku, and research on the qualitative theory of differential equations began to be developed in Alma-Ata and Frunze.
