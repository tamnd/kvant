Mathematics Olympiad

The final round of the XVI All-Russian Mathematics Olympiad for schoolchildren, dedicated to the 60th anniversary of the formation of the USSR, took place from April 14 to 22 in Odessa. 156 schoolchildren (43 eighth-graders, 60 ninth-graders and 53 tenth-graders), representing all the resources of the Soviet Union, took part in it. The Olympiad jury (about 50 people) included scientific and pedagogical workers from Moscow, Leningrad, Kyiv, Chisinau, Riga, Novosibirsk and other cities, including, of course, Odessa. The honorary representative of the jury was Academician A. N. Kolmogorov, the representative was Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B. V. Gnedenko, his deputy was Associate Professor of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University Yu. V. Nesterenko, Professor of Kyiv University M. I. Yadrenko and Professor of the Odessa Polytechnic Institute N. B. Divari.

The grand opening of the Olympics took place on April 15 in the huge hall of the House of Sailors, very close to the Odessa port and the banner Potemkin Stairs. The Olympid was opened by the deputy representative of the Odessa City Executive Committee, V. A. Cherkassky. Academicians A. N. Kolmogorov and B. V. Gnedenko listened with great interest; they talked about the applied significance of mathematics, about its age-related role in modern science and arbitrariness. The participants were greeted by Odessa schoolchildren and representatives of pioneer friends of the pioneer camp “Young Guard”, on the territory of which the Olympiad participants were accommodated and both rounds of the Olympiad were held.

The Olympics itself, as usual, took place in two rounds - April 16 and 18. In each class, a homogeneous task consisted of 4 problems, the solution of which was given 5 hours. Below we present the conditions of all problems, indicating their authors.

Quests

8th grade

First day

1. On a circle with center $O_1$ of radius $r_1$ points $M$ and $K$ are taken. A circle with center $O_2$ and radius $r_2$ is inscribed into the central angle $MO_1K$. Find the area of ​​the quadrilateral $MO_1KO_2$.

(B. Chinik, Chisinau)

2. In the numerical sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$, each term, starting with thirdERO, is equal to the sum of the previous two, with $a_1=1$, $a_2=2$ and $b_1=2$, $b_2=1$. How many numbers are there that appear in both the first and second sequence?

(A. Anjan, Riga)

3. Let $m$ and $n$ be natural numbers. Prove that if for some non-negative integers $k_1, k_2, \dots, k_n$ the number $2^{k_1}+2^{k_2}+\dots+2^{k_n}$ is divisible by $2^m-1$, then $n \geq m$.

(A. Gritsoryan, Moscow)

4. Each vertex of the cube is assigned a non-negative real number, and the sum of all these numbers is 1. Two people play the following game. The first one chooses any cube, the second one chooses another cube and finally the first one chooses the third cube. In this case, you cannot select faces parallel to already selected ones. Prove that the first player can play so that the number corresponding to the common vertex of the three chosen faces does not exceed 1/6.

(A. Berzins, Riga)

Second day

5. The natural numbers from 1 to 1982 are arranged one after the other in no particular order. The computer looks to the right from pairs of adjacent numbers (first and second, second, etc.) up to the last one and swaps the numbers in the pair being looked at if more of them is to the left. Then she looks through all the pairs, looking from right to left from the last pair to the first, swapping the numbers in the pairs according to the same law. At the end of this viewing, the operator working with the computer received information that the number in the hundredth place had not moved from its place both times. Find this number.

(Yu. Nesterenko, Moscow)
