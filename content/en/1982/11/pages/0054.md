7. A graph of the function $y = x^2$ was drawn on the Oxy coordinate plane. Then the coordinate axes were erased - only the parabola remained. How to use a compass and ruler to restore the coordinate axes and the unit of length?

(A. Anjan, Riga)

8. A square table of $n \times n$ cells is filled with numbers. Moreover, in cells that have a common side, numbers are written that differ from one another by no more than 1. Prove that at least one number appears in the table:

a) no less than $[n/2]$ times ([a] is the integer part of the number a);

b) no less than $n$ times.

(A. Berzins, Riga)

9th grade

First day

1. One day three boys met in the library. One of them said: “Now I will go to the library every other day.” The second said that he would go to the library in two days, and the third said that he would go to the library in three days. The librarian who started their conversation noticed that the library had a day off on Wednesdays. The boys replied that if one of them had a day of arrival on the library’s day off, he would come the next day and would continue to be absent from that day. That's what the boys did. One Monday they all met again in the library. On what day of the week did the above conversation take place?

(A. Savin, Moscow)

2. In the parallelogram $ABCD$, in which $|AB| \neq |BC|$, the ratio of the lengths of the diagonals is given: $|AC|/|BD| = k$. Let ray AM be symmetrical to ray AD relative to straight line AC, ray BM symmetrical to ray BC relative to straight line BD, M be the common point of rays AM and BM. Find the ratio $|AM|/|BM|$.

(B. Dubrovsky, Moscow)

3. There are 3k points offset on a circle, dividing it into 3k arcs, of which k arcs have length 1, if k arcs have length 2, and the remaining k arcs have length 3. Prove that among the offset points there are two diametrically opposite.

(B. Proizvolov, Moscow)

4. A point M is selected inside the terrahedron. Prove that at least one edge of the terrahedron is visible from point M at an angle whose cosine is not greater than - 1/3.

(S. Gashkov, Moscow)

Second day

5. Prove that for all positive values ​​of x the following inequality holds:

$$2^{12}\sqrt{x} + 2^{9}\sqrt{x} \geq 2 \cdot 2^{9}\sqrt{x}$$

(B. Kolotov, Novosibirsk)

6. What is the smallest number of numbers that must be crossed out from the set of numbers 1, 2, 3..., 1982 so that none of the remaining numbers is equal to the product of the other two remaining numbers? How can this be done?

(J. Kurlyandchik, Leningrad)

7. Each cell of an infinite sheet of checkered paper contains a real number. Prove that some cells contain a number that is not greater than the number written in at least four of the numbers raised?

(A. Gritsoryan, Moscow)

8. Prove that from any sequence of n real numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$ one can select a part of the numbers so that the following three conditions are satisfied:

a) no consecutive three numbers are selected;

b) out of every three consecutive numbers, at least one is selected;

b) the absolute value of the sum of the selected numbers is not less than

$$\frac{1}{6} \left( |a_1| + |a_2| + ... + |a_n| \right).$$

(A. Anjan, Riga)

Arrange these numbers in ascending order.

(A. Gritsoryan, Yu. Nesterenko, Moscow)

4. A closed broken line M has an odd number of vertices—sequentially $A_1, A_2, ..., A_{2n+1}$. Let us denote by S (M) a new closed broken line, the successive vertices $B_1, B_2, ..., B_{2n+1}$ of which are the midpoints of the links of the broken line M: $B_1$ is the middle of $[A_1 A_2]$, $B_2$ is the middle of $[A_3 A_4]$, ..., $B_{n+1}$ is the middle of $[A_{2n+1} A_1]$, $B_{n+2}$ is middle $[A_{2n} A_{2n+1}]$. Prove that the sequences are constructed in this way.

$$M_1 = S(M), M_2 = S(M_1), M_3 = S(M_2), ...$$

there is a closed broken line that is preparatory to the original broken line M.

(A. Kelarev, Sverdlovsk)
