Second solution. (This solution was proposed by S. Matyushov, a 10th grade student at Physics School No. 18 in Moscow.) For any point $A$

$$\overrightarrow{OA} = \frac{1}{n} \left( (\overrightarrow{OA}_1 + \overrightarrow{A_1A}) + \dots + (\overrightarrow{OA}_n + \overrightarrow{A_nA}) \right) =$$

$$= \frac{1}{n} \left( \overrightarrow{OA}_1 + \dots + \overrightarrow{OA}_n \right) + \frac{1}{n} \left( \overrightarrow{A_1A} + \dots + \overrightarrow{A_nA} \right) =$$

$$= \frac{1}{n} \left( \overrightarrow{A_1A} + \dots + \overrightarrow{A_nA} \right).$$

This immediately implies the inequality

$$|OA| \leqslant \frac{1}{n} \left( |AA_1| + \dots + |AA_n| \right).$$

Putting $A = A_1, A = A_2, \dots, A = A_n$ in it successively and adding up all the resulting inequalities, we find that

$$d = |OA_1| + \dots + |OA_n| \leqslant \frac{2}{n} \sum |A_iA_j|$$

(**)

(each term $|A_iA_j|$, $1 \leqslant i < j \leqslant n$ is included in the sum once, so it is represented by the sum of the lengths of all sides and diagonals of the polygon).

For odd $n$ there are $n$ sides, $n$ diagonals cutting off 1 vertex, $n$ diagonals cutting off 2 vertices, ..., $n$ diagonals cutting off $\frac{n-3}{2}$ vertices. The length of the diagonal cutting off $k$ vertices does not exceed the length of the $(k+1)$-link broken line from the side of the polygon connecting the ends of the diagonal. Therefore, the sum of the lengths of such diagonals is no more than $(k+1)p$, where $p$ is the perimeter of the polygon and (**) is rewritten as follows:

$$d \leqslant \frac{2}{n} \left( p + 2p + \dots + \frac{n-1}{2} p \right) = \frac{n^2 - 1}{4n} p,$$

that is, $p \geqslant \frac{4n}{n^2 - 1} d.$
