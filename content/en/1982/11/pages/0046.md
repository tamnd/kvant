⟦folio 44⟧

only with the energy of an artillery shell...
Swift made a small mistake in calculating the muscular strength of the giants. We have already seen in the first chapter*) that the power of animals is not proportional to their size. If we apply the messages given there to Swift's bikes, it turns out that although their muscular strength was 144 times greater than Gulliver's, their body weight was 1728 times greater**). And if Gulliver was able to lift not only the weight of his own body, but also approximately the same load, then the giants would not be able to overcome even the load of their huge body. They should lie motionless in one place, powerless to make any significant movement. Their nature, as pictured by Swift, could only appear as a result of incorrect calculation.

Several experiments taken (from the book “Physics at Every Step”)

Diving bell. An ordinary basin is suitable for this experiment, but if you can get a deep and wide jar, then it is more convenient to continue the experiment. You will also need a tall glass. This will be your “water bell”, and the bowl of water represents a smaller version of the sea or lake.

There is hardly any experience of the past. You hold the glass up, immerse it in the bottom of the basin, continuing to hold it with your hand (so that the water does not push it out). At the same time, it is easy to notice that the water inside the glass almost does not penetrate: the air does not allow it. This becomes much more clear when under our “bell” there is some easily wet object, for example a piece of sugar. Place a cork circle on the water, sugar on it and cover with a glass on top. Feel free to lower the glass into the water now. The sugar will clear below the water level, but will remain dry because the water will not penetrate under the glass.

The same experiment can be continued with a glass funnel, if you turn it with the wide end down, tightly plug its hole with your finger and then immerse it in water. The water does not penetrate under the funnel, but it stays away

*) This refers to the book “Entertaining Geometry”. (Note ped.)

**) Since the strength of muscles depends on their thickness, that is, cross-section, then as the size of the animal increases, strength increases in proportion to the square, and weight - to the cube of the increase.
