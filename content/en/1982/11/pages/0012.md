Please note that in 1926 I did not even see any of these vacancies. Here is a vivid example of abstract scientific thinking and movement. The vacancy was “seen” (the same, by the way, according to the idea expressed by Ya. I. Frenkel) much later - this was done by the Kharkov physicist B. G. Lazarev (he published his work in 1956). The idea is this: the concentration of vacancies increases strongly with temperature. Let's heat the metal almost to melting and sharply cool it to room temperature (“harden”). The vacancy will not have time to go to the surface and remain in the metal. The difference is large: in copper at 1075°C (copper melts at 1083°C) the proportion of vacancies is 1.3·10⁻⁴, and at 27°C - 10⁻¹⁹.
