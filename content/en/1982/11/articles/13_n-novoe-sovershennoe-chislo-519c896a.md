⟦folio 50⟧

New perfect number

The editor «Kvant» received a letter from our reader L. Kovgan (Frolovo, Volgograd region). He draws attention to the appearance in the magazine “Knowledge is Power” of a message about the receipt of a new prime number and notes that a new perfect number was actually indicated at the same time. “I would like,” L. Kovgan ends his letter, “for the magazine «Kvant» to publish short information about this.”
We fulfill the reader's request.
In «Kvant» (1971, No. 8, p. 1; 1972, No. 4, p. 39; 1973, No. 10, p. 71) I already talk about the curious history of the study of perfect numbers. A perfect number is a natural number equal to the sum of all its proper divisors (that is, divisors other than the number itself). As Euclid proved, if the number $2^k - 1$ is prime, then the number $2^{k-1}(2^k - 1)$ is perfect. Here are the first few perfect numbers:
6, 28, 496, 8 128, 33 550 336, ...
Until recently, only 24 perfect numbers were found (mainly thanks to computers).
Now let’s reproduce the information published in the journal “Knowledge is Power” (1980, No. 6, p. 10):
“A computer named “Cray-1” was able to find the largest prime number known to dotagmaticians [...] The largest prime number.”

$2^{44} \cdot 497 - 1$ contains 13,395 digits. It is many times greater than the number of all atoms in the Universe..."

The discovery of a new prime number allows, based on the Euclid theorem formulated above, to immediately indicate a new, 25th perfect number:

$2^{44} \cdot 496 \cdot (2^{44} \cdot 497 - 1)$.

This is a huge number: it requires 26,790 digits to write it in full decimal.

Apparently, finding the next perfect numbers using Euclid's formula will either be of purely sporting interest, or will pursue the goal of demonstrating the possibility of a particular computing complex.

H. X.
