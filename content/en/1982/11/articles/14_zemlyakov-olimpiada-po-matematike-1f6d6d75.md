Mathematics Olympiad

The final round of the XVI All-Russian Mathematics Olympiad for schoolchildren, dedicated to the 60th anniversary of the formation of the USSR, took place from April 14 to 22 in Odessa. 156 schoolchildren (43 eighth-graders, 60 ninth-graders and 53 tenth-graders), representing all the resources of the Soviet Union, took part in it. The Olympiad jury (about 50 people) included scientific and pedagogical workers from Moscow, Leningrad, Kyiv, Chisinau, Riga, Novosibirsk and other cities, including, of course, Odessa. The honorary representative of the jury was Academician A. N. Kolmogorov, the representative was Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B. V. Gnedenko, his deputy was Associate Professor of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University Yu. V. Nesterenko, Professor of Kyiv University M. I. Yadrenko and Professor of the Odessa Polytechnic Institute N. B. Divari.

The grand opening of the Olympics took place on April 15 in the huge hall of the House of Sailors, very close to the Odessa port and the banner Potemkin Stairs. The Olympid was opened by the deputy representative of the Odessa City Executive Committee, V. A. Cherkassky. Academicians A. N. Kolmogorov and B. V. Gnedenko listened with great interest; they talked about the applied significance of mathematics, about its age-related role in modern science and arbitrariness. The participants were greeted by Odessa schoolchildren and representatives of pioneer friends of the pioneer camp “Young Guard”, on the territory of which the Olympiad participants were accommodated and both rounds of the Olympiad were held.

The Olympics itself, as usual, took place in two rounds - April 16 and 18. In each class, a homogeneous task consisted of 4 problems, the solution of which was given 5 hours. Below we present the conditions of all problems, indicating their authors.

Quests

8th grade

First day

1. On a circle with center $O_1$ of radius $r_1$ points $M$ and $K$ are taken. A circle with center $O_2$ and radius $r_2$ is inscribed into the central angle $MO_1K$. Find the area of ​​the quadrilateral $MO_1KO_2$.

(B. Chinik, Chisinau)

2. In the numerical sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$, each term, starting with thirdERO, is equal to the sum of the previous two, with $a_1=1$, $a_2=2$ and $b_1=2$, $b_2=1$. How many numbers are there that appear in both the first and second sequence?

(A. Anjan, Riga)

3. Let $m$ and $n$ be natural numbers. Prove that if for some non-negative integers $k_1, k_2, \dots, k_n$ the number $2^{k_1}+2^{k_2}+\dots+2^{k_n}$ is divisible by $2^m-1$, then $n \geq m$.

(A. Gritsoryan, Moscow)

4. Each vertex of the cube is assigned a non-negative real number, and the sum of all these numbers is 1. Two people play the following game. The first one chooses any cube, the second one chooses another cube and finally the first one chooses the third cube. In this case, you cannot select faces parallel to already selected ones. Prove that the first player can play so that the number corresponding to the common vertex of the three chosen faces does not exceed 1/6.

(A. Berzins, Riga)

Second day

5. The natural numbers from 1 to 1982 are arranged one after the other in no particular order. The computer looks to the right from pairs of adjacent numbers (first and second, second, etc.) up to the last one and swaps the numbers in the pair being looked at if more of them is to the left. Then she looks through all the pairs, looking from right to left from the last pair to the first, swapping the numbers in the pairs according to the same law. At the end of this viewing, the operator working with the computer received information that the number in the hundredth place had not moved from its place both times. Find this number.

(Yu. Nesterenko, Moscow)

9th grade

First day

1. One day three boys met in the library. One of them said: “Now I will go to the library every other day.” The second said that he would go to the library in two days, and the third said that he would go to the library in three days. The librarian who started their conversation noticed that the library had a day off on Wednesdays. The boys replied that if one of them had a day of arrival on the library’s day off, he would come the next day and would continue to be absent from that day. That's what the boys did. One Monday they all met again in the library. On what day of the week did the above conversation take place?

(A. Savin, Moscow)

2. In the parallelogram $ABCD$, in which $|AB| \neq |BC|$, the ratio of the lengths of the diagonals is given: $|AC|/|BD| = k$. Let ray AM be symmetrical to ray AD relative to straight line AC, ray BM symmetrical to ray BC relative to straight line BD, M be the common point of rays AM and BM. Find the ratio $|AM|/|BM|$.

(B. Dubrovsky, Moscow)

3. There are 3k points offset on a circle, dividing it into 3k arcs, of which k arcs have length 1, if k arcs have length 2, and the remaining k arcs have length 3. Prove that among the offset points there are two diametrically opposite.

(B. Proizvolov, Moscow)

4. A point M is selected inside the terrahedron. Prove that at least one edge of the terrahedron is visible from point M at an angle whose cosine is not greater than - 1/3.

(S. Gashkov, Moscow)

Second day

5. Prove that for all positive values ​​of x the following inequality holds:

$$2^{12}\sqrt{x} + 2^{9}\sqrt{x} \geq 2 \cdot 2^{9}\sqrt{x}$$

(B. Kolotov, Novosibirsk)

6. What is the smallest number of numbers that must be crossed out from the set of numbers 1, 2, 3..., 1982 so that none of the remaining numbers is equal to the product of the other two remaining numbers? How can this be done?

(J. Kurlyandchik, Leningrad)

7. Each cell of an infinite sheet of checkered paper contains a real number. Prove that some cells contain a number that is not greater than the number written in at least four of the numbers raised?

(A. Gritsoryan, Moscow)

8. Prove that from any sequence of n real numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$ one can select a part of the numbers so that the following three conditions are satisfied:

a) no consecutive three numbers are selected;

b) out of every three consecutive numbers, at least one is selected;

b) the absolute value of the sum of the selected numbers is not less than

$$\frac{1}{6} \left( |a_1| + |a_2| + ... + |a_n| \right).$$

(A. Anjan, Riga)

First day

Arrange these numbers in ascending order.

(A. Gressen, Moscow)

3. Is there a natural number that is divisible by $11...1$ and whose sum of digits is less than what?

(A. Gritsoryan, Yu. Nesterenko, Moscow)

Let us denote by S (M) a new closed broken line, the successive vertices $B_1, B_2, ..., B_{2n+1}$ of which are the midpoints of the links of the broken line M: $B_1$ is the middle of $[A_1 A_2]$, $B_2$ is the middle of $[A_3 A_4]$, ..., $B_{n+1}$ is the middle of $[A_{2n+1} A_1]$, $B_{n+2}$ is middle $[A_{2n} A_{2n+1}]$. Prove that the sequences are constructed in this way.

$$M_1 = S(M), M_2 = S(M_1), M_3 = S(M_2), ...$$

there is a closed broken line that is preparatory to the original broken line M.

(A. Kelarev, Sverdlovsk)

These guys were among the candidates for the USSR team at the XXIII International Schoolchildren Olympiad (about this Olympiad, held in Hungary in July 1982, see «Kvant» No. 12).

Along with the main prizes, the honorees also received special prizes - for original solutions to problems, for the best results of the first day, for the will to win. A special prize was awarded to the best group of girls - eighth-grader A. Bogomolnaya from school No. 534 in Leningrad, who received a 2nd degree diploma. Prize «Kvant» - a file of numbers for 1981 - was awarded to eighth-grader Bulat Orazbekov from the school named after. Lunacharsky Kirov district of Taldy-Kurgan region of the Kazakh SSR. A subscription to «Kvant» for 1983 will be received by Sardon Gulomnabev from the school village. Shutnana, Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region, Tajik SSR (8th grade) and Taras Kitsmet from the school village of Gorokholno, Ivano-Frankovsk Region, Ukrainian SSR (9th grade).

The hero city of Odeska warmly welcomed its guests - the participants of the Olympiad. During the tours, in the evenings the guys went to the famous Odessa theaters - opera and ballet, musical comedy. Traditional suppers of other peoples of the USSR took place in various schools of Odessa, excursions around the city “Odeska - a hero city”, “Odeska - a feat”, “Literature Odessa”, an excursion by sea to the port of Ilchevsk. Like everyone else in our country, on April 17, Olympiad participants worked at the All-Union Leninist Communist Community Cleanup Day - planting trees in the beautiful park of the Young Guard pioneer camp.

On April 19, a “mathematical battle” took place between a team of schoolchildren and a jury team. The battle was stubborn and honorable - suffice it to say that before the final competition for captains the score was equal. The last match decided everything: the captain of the schoolchildren, Grisha Perelman, nevertheless lost to the captain of the team of jury members S.V. Konyagin (two-time winner of the national mathematical Olympiads, now a teacher at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University, the author of many problems from the Problem Book «Kvant»). As the chief judge, I assure you that I did not help the jury members at all - rather, on the contrary!

On April 20, the closing ceremony of the Olympiad took place at the Pioneer Palace of the Young Guard pioneer camp. Professor M. I. Yadrenko addressed the participants of the Olympiad with a welcoming speech. Academician A. N. Kolmogorov gave parting words to both the winners of the Olympiad and those who did not receive the prize. The heads of the jury presented prizes, diplomas and certificates to the participants. The symbol of the Olympiad was handed over to a representative of the city of Kshinev, where the final round of the next, XVII Olympiad will take place.
