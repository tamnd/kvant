Physics Olympiad

The final stage of the 16th All-Scholastic Olympiad in Physics for schoolchildren, dedicated to the 60th anniversary of the formation of the USSR, took place from April 14 to 21 in the capital of Soviet Azerbaijan, order-bearing Baku.

143 representatives of all allied resource workers took part in a very serious contemporary event. Among them are the winners of this year’s Resource Olympids and those recognized by the previous All-Olympics, who received diplomas of I and II degrees.

On the morning of April 15, Olympiad participants laid wreaths at the monument to V.I. Lenin and the memorial of the 26 Baku Commissars, after which in the assembly hall of the Azerbaijan Institute of Petroleum and Chemistry named after. M. Azizbekov, the grand opening of the Olympids took place. The Minister of Education of the Az. SSR E. M. Kafarov, the representative of the Jorni of the All-Oplympids, the corresponding member of the Academy of Sciences of the Az. SSR A. I. Mukhtarov and the deputy representative of the Jorni, Professor S. M. Kozel, addressed the audience with attractive words. Rector of the Institute of Petroleum and Chemistry, Academician of the Academy of Sciences of Az. SSR I. A. Ibragimov spoke about the weak history of the institute, the first higher educational institution in Europe and Asia, which began training engineers for all branches of the oil industry. It was said with pride that the outstanding physicist, Lenin Prize laureate Academician I.V. Kurchatov, Academician L.S. Leibenzon and Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences K.A. Kraussky worked within the walls of this institute; Among the graduates of the institute are Lenin Prize laureate, representative of the State Planning Committee of the USSR N.K. Baibakov, Hero of Socialist Labor, Lenin Prize laureate, Minister of Gas Industry S.A. Orudzhev and Hero of the Soviet Union, pilot-cosmonaut of the USSR V.M. Zholobov.

In the evening of the same day, the guests attended a performance at the Opera and Ballet Theater. M. F. Akhundova.

On April 16, the theoretical round of the Olympids was held. Eighth-class students were asked to solve four problems (4 hours were allotted for work), ninth-class and tenth-class students were asked to solve five problems each (time was given 5 hours). Let us present the conditions of the problem (most of them are included in the “Quenta” Problem Book in issues 7 and 8 of the magazine for this year).

8th grade

1. A small kettle is filled to the top with warm water ($t_1 = 30^\circ\text{C}$). The kettle remains at 1 degree for $t = 5$ min. In order to prevent the kettle from cooling down, hot water is dripped into it from the tap ($t_2 = 45^\circ\text{C}$). The mass of one drop is $m_k = 0,2$ g. How many drops per minute must be dripped into the kettle to maintain the temperature equal to $t = 30^\circ\text{C}$? How many degrees to maintain equal to $t_1 = 10$ mA, and the current flowing through the degrees to maintain equal to $t_2 = 2$ mA. Find the resistance ratio of resistors $R_1/R_2$.

Find the resistance ratio of resistors $R_1/R_2$.

Find the dependence of the speed of the upper point of “intersection” of the hoops on the distance between their centers. The hoops are thin, the second hoop “passes” close to the first.

2. A massive cylinder of radius $R$ rests on two supports of equal height (Fig. 6). One of the stands is stationary, the other is a horizontal segment with a speed of $v$. With what force does the cylinder press on the stationary stand at the moment when the distance between the points of support is $R\sqrt{2}$? There is no friction. At first, the stands were located very close to each other.

3. In a heat-conducting cylinder under a light piston there is a mixture of equal quantities of water and ice: $m_B = m_1 = 1$ kg. The pressure on the piston slowly increases from the initial value $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa to $p_1 = 2,5 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Determine how much ice will melt and how much work will be done by the external force. It is known that to decrease the melting temperature of ice by 1 degree, you need to bring the pressure to $14 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. First, solve the problem assuming that water and ice are incompressible, and then evaluate the correction that comes from taking into account the skimability. It is known that to reduce the volume of a certain amount of water by 1%, the pressure must be raised to $20 \cdot 10^6$ Pa. Take the compressibility of ice to be equal to half the compressibility of water. The specific heat capacity of water is $c_B = 4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K), the specific heat of fusion of ice is $\lambda = 3 \cdot 10^5$ J/kg, the density of ice is $\rho_1 = 0,9\rho_B$, where $\rho_B$ is the density of water.

4. For a drying lamp, a non-linear capacitor is applicable (it is filled with a dielectric whose dielectric constant depends on the field voltage). The dependence of voltage $U$ on the charge of plates $Q$ of this capacitor is shown in figure 7. The capacitor is charged from a battery with a voltage of $U_0 = 300$ V through a resistor with a resistance of $R = 10$ kOhm. Find the maximum number of searches in 1 minute. Assume that during discharge the capacitor is completely discharged. Minimum initial search voltage $U_1 = 250$ V.

5. A high-precision multi-range ammeter contains a separate shield for each measurement limit. The ammeter is connected to the circuit at a limit of 10 mA, and it shows the current strength $I_1 = 2,95$ mA; when it was switched to the 3 mA limit it showed $I_2 = 2,90$ mA. What was the current strength in the circuit before connecting the ammeter?

3. One of the science fiction novels proposed a power plant project that harnessed the energy of sea currents and the Earth's magnetic field. The project is as follows. Two horizontal metal plates with areas $S = 1$ km$^2$, located at a distance of $L = 100$ m one above the other, are immersed in the hole. Sea water, with a resistivity of $\varrho = 0,25$ Ohm m, flows from east to west at a speed of $v = 1$ m/s. The Earth's magnetic field in a given place is uniform, directed from south to north, and the indication of this field is equal in magnitude to $B = 10^{-4}$ T. As a result, voltage appears between the plates, and if they are connected to an external load, power is released in it. Determine the maximum value of this power.
