a) Let's draw an arbitrary straight line that is not parallel to any of the axes of the parabolas. Such a straight line can intersect with the interior region of any of the parabolas only along an interval. In fact, the equation of parabolas in a certain coordinate system has the form $y = ax^2$ ($a > 0$), and the equation of a straight line has the form $y = kx + l$; The abscissas of the points of the line belonging to the interior of the parabola satisfy the inequality $kx + l > ax^2$, the solution set of which is either empty. Or is the interval $x_1 < x < x_2$, where $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the roots of the equation $ax^2 - kx - l = 0$.

It follows from this that on the drawn line he will find points that do not belong to any of the interior regions of the parabolas.

6) Let $O$ be an arbitrary point in space and $K'$ be a cone obtained from the cone $K$ with vertex $Q$ by parallel translation taking point $Q$ to point $O$. Let us draw an arbitrary ray $l$ with its origin at point $O$. Let us consider the sections of the ends $K$ and $K'$ by a plane passing through the ray $l$ and the point $Q$ (Fig. 1). It intersects cones at two angles with parallel sides; Using this, it is easy to verify that the ray $l$ intersects the cone $K$ along the ray if and only if it is entirely contained in the cone $K'$. Otherwise, the ray $l$ intersects with the cone $K$ along a segment or at one point $Q$ or does not intersect at all.

We now intersect the vertices of all ends at the point $O$ and prove that no two cones $K'_1$ and $K'_2$ have a common point $A \neq O$. Then the ray $l = [OA)$ is contained in each of them. As was shown, in this case the ray $l$ intersects with the cone $K_1$ along some ray $l_1$, and with the cone $K_2$ along the ray $l_2$. But then the rays $l_1 \cap l_2$ belong to the common cone $K_1$ and $K_2$. Contradiction.

Let us now consider a sphere with center at point $O$. It intersects with each end of the “bouquet” along a certain “spherical cap” (Fig. 2). This means that for any location of the ends in space on the sphere, it will find a point that is not covered by “caps”. F759. A weightless rod of length $l$ with a small load of mass $m$ at the end is hinged at point $A$ (Fig. 1) and is in a vertical position, touching a body of mass $M$. With a little push the system starts to move. At what ratio between $m$ and $M$ will the rod at the moment of “separation” from the body $M$ make an angle $α = \pi/6$ with the horizon? What will be the speed of the body $M$ at this moment? Neglect the trence.

As long as the load touches the body, the speed of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the speed of the load, and the acceleration of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load.

Let $\vec{a}$ be the total acceleration of the load. Then we can write: $\vec{a} = \vec{a}_\tau + \vec{a}_u$, where $\vec{a}_u$ is the centripetal acceleration of the load as it moves along a circle of radius $l$, that is, $|\vec{a}_u| = v^2/l$, where $v$ is the modulus of the speed of the load. The horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load is equal to (see Fig. 2)

$$a_r = a_\tau \sin \alpha - \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

The body moves with such acceleration. Equation of body motion -

$$N = Ma_r = Ma_\tau \sin \alpha - M \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha,$$

where $N$ is the normal pressure force from the load. At the moment of lifting off the load $N = 0$, and

$$a_\tau \sin \alpha = \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

Acceleration $a_\tau$ at the moment of separation is imparted to the load only by the force of gravity and (see Fig. 2)

$$a_\tau = g \cos \alpha.$$

Thus, the speed at the moment of separation is equal to

$$v = \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha},$$

⟦folio 36⟧

and the speed of the body is

$$u = v \sin \alpha = \sin \alpha \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha}.$$

According to the law of conservation of energy, at the moment of time when the rod makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizon,

$$m g l = m g l \sin \alpha + m \frac{v^2}{2} + M \frac{v^2 \sin^2 \alpha}{2}.$$

Substituting into this equality the found expression for $v$ at the moment of separation and the value $\sin \alpha = \sin \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, we find the ratio $M/m$:

$$\frac{M}{m} = \frac{2 - 3 \sin \alpha}{\sin^3 \alpha} = 4.$$

The speed of the body when the load is lifted is equal to

$$u = v \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{g l}{2}}.$$

F760. A steel ball of mass $m_2 = 1$ g is introduced into a heat-insulated vessel containing $m_1 = 20$ g of greens at a speed of $v = 100$ m/s. Start changing the temperature into the vessel. ◆

After a long time, the ball will lose almost all its speed (its mass is many times greater than the mass of a green atom), and we can assume that the change in the internal energy of the gas into the vessel is equal to the initial kinetic energy of the ball:

$$\frac{m_2 v^2}{2} = \Delta U = \frac{3}{2} N k \cdot \Delta T = \frac{3}{2} k N_A \frac{m_1}{\mu} \cdot \Delta T,$$

(*)

$$\Delta T = \frac{m_2 v^2 \mu}{3 m_1 R} \approx 0,08 \text{ K}.$$

A more accurate solution should take into account the increase in the temperature of the ball. Then

$$\frac{m_2 v^2}{2} = c m_2 \cdot \Delta T' + \frac{3}{2} \frac{m_1}{\mu} R \cdot \Delta T'$$,

$$\Delta T' = \frac{m_2 v^2}{2 \left[ c m_2 + \frac{3}{2} \frac{m_1}{\mu} R \right]} \approx 0,08 \text{ K}.$$
