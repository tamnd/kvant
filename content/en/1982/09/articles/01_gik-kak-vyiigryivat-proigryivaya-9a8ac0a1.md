HOW TO WIN BY LOSSING

An amazing incident happened to the host of a page on New Year's Eve in one of the chess editorial offices.

— Shall we play a game of chess giveaways? - Grandmaster D. Bronstein suggested to me when he stopped by the editorial office.

It’s not every day that you get to meet a famous grandmaster at the dock, and without thinking twice, I agreed. The goal of the game is very simple and convenient - you need to get rid of all your pieces, or put them away. Capture, as in checkers, is mandatory, and if there is a choice, then you can capture any piece, including the king.

I got the white ones, and we began to give in.

E. Gik - D. Bronstein
1. e4 d5 2. ed Ф:d5
3. Ks3. Having made a move with my knight, I settled more comfortably in the chair, believing that a long and stubborn struggle lay ahead. But an unpleasant surprise awaited me.

“I have to disappoint you,” the grandmaster said softly. “Teler inevitably eats all my figures!”

What followed was the stunning 3...Qxg2. The further course of this fight no longer depended on me, and I barely had time to remove the black pieces from the dock. 4. C:g2 b6 5. C:a8 Kc6 6. C:c6 a5 (in giveaways, you don’t have to react to check) 7. C:e8 (kings are captured in this game) 7...Kf6 8. C:f7 Ce6 9. C:e6 Kd7 10. C:d7 e6 11. C:e6 Jlg8 12. C:g8 g6 13. C:h7 c5 14. C:g6 a4 15. K:a4 c4 16. K:b6 Cc5 17. K:c4 C:f2. I had to give up, because after the forced 18. K:f2 there was no trace left of the black pieces on the board.

Bronstein's combination was one of the longest in the history of chess! He claimed that the grandmaster knew the secret of the giveaways and nevertheless challenged him to a rematch. We turned the dock over and started a new batch.

D. Bronstein - E. Gik
1. d4 f5 2. e4 fe 3. Qh5 g6 4. Ce2. Actually, this move was wrong - the white queen should have taken one of my pawns g6 or h7. However, I tactfully did not make a replacement grandmaster. He himself continued to play strictly by the rules. 4...gh. There followed an instant answer 5. C:h5.

As in the first game, the white bishop entered the game and seemed to be about to destroy the black pieces. Revenge was close, all that remained was to find the most elegant way to bring the matter to an end. Suddenly Bronstein passed my search.

- What are you thinking about, maztro? — he showed sincere interest.

“I decided not to hide my numbers from my partner on how to end this meeting with a bishop sacrifice on f2.”

- Sorry, but mat to your king! - said the grandmaster.

- What a curse, because we are playing giveaway!

- Where did you get the idea? We were talking only about the first batch, and we didn’t finish cooking the second.

Only now did I realize what had happened. There was a natural mat on the dock. Mat, which in some mysterious way announced the white bishop to my king just five moves after the start of the game...

- Happy New Year! - Bronstein smiled slyly and left the room, leaving me this fabulous position as a New Year’s debater.

I was not myself for many days and calmed down only when I published this Christmas story in the very publication in which it originated. However, there was one more surprise ahead of me.

Soon I received a letter from a giveaway specialist, who reported that O. Wender’s favorite move was 1. e2-e4 after the answer 1...b7-b5! leads whites to formed death.
