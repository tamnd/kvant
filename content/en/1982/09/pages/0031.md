⟦folio 29⟧

The main idea of ​​de Broglie's theory - the likening of moving material objects to waves - went against the usual ideas about the surrounding world, and to most physicists it seemed absurd, wild, even crazy. There was only a little understanding of de Broglie's revolutionary views. But among these few were Langenin and Einstein, who said that with his work de Broglie “raised the edge of the great curtain.”

Two years have passed, and this curtain has been completely raised - a new science is represented on the stage of physics in its entirety and more - quantum mechanics, the most important part of which is the rethought and in-depth wave theory of de Broglie.

In 1929, de Broglie's services to science were recognized by awarding him the Nobel Prize in Physics; many scientific institutions and organizations in different countries of the world made the French scientist their member, Louis de Broglie is a foreign member of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

After his epoch-making discovery, Louis de Broglie continued to study fundamental issues of quantum theory, problems of ultra-high-frequency electromagnetic oscillations, physical science and the history of physics; he devoted a lot of time to teaching physics, in particular, at the same Sorbonne, where he once taught himself. The great scientist did not lose his keen interest in physics even to this day.

This is a fragile world

Every person who has the slightest interest in how our world works, sooner or later asks himself the question: “Why is our world structured this way and not otherwise?” In fact, why, for example, do not all objects develop into their constituent molecules and atoms? And if atoms and molecules like to hold on to each other so much, then why does our world consist of separate objects, and not be a single monolithic block?

Physics is primarily trying to answer these questions. Much is already clear to scientists. How would those who know too little physics just start studying it? Give up on everything: they say, it’s interesting, of course, but too tough? Don't despair. Physicists themselves willingly talk about everything they know, and try to do it simply and clearly, so to speak “on the fingers.”

So our “why?” You can find the answers in the book “Portrait of a Crack” (M., “Metallurgy”, 1981), written by physicist Professor V. M. Finkel. The author's scientific work directly relates to the problems discussed in the book.

Having opened the book, we immediately get acquainted with the main “characters” drawn by the artist R.V. Levitsky: this is metal with atoms, vacancies and dislocations, whiskers, direction, cracks, temperatures.

It would not be an overstatement to say that the present and future of humanity depends on what materials we use now and what nature will replace them. After all, our well-being, comfort, and sometimes even life depend on the strength and reliability of the objects around us: houses, roads, places, cars, trains, planes... Reading the book, we gradually begin to understand how complex and original the mechanisms of readability and destruction are, and certain physical properties of bodies.

The author examines these issues at the most modern level: the reader finds himself in a mysterious microworld - the world of atoms and molecules. It is not dry to consider a rather strict presentation, largely due to successful analogies, considered from literary works that figuratively illustrate complex physical problems.

Now let's give the floor to the author:

“The book lying in front of you is dedicated to destruction, its nature and its diverse manifestations: both when it is discovered evil, and when it is vitally important and successfully serves people... Perhaps it would be good if nature were simpler than it is, but how wonderful it is that there is still so much unknown, even dangerously unknown, to which you can apply your courage, mind and hands...

The book is intended for an inquisitive reader, and the author sought to write it in such a way that it would be interesting and not useless to both schoolchildren and specialists.

Filled with this hope, the author submits his book to the readers.”

It seems to us that the author’s hopes will be justified.
