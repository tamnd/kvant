The last bacton remained: the problem of complete capture.

V. M. Alekseev: full capture of opportunity!

The starting position for the last bakton's maneuver was the same equilateral three-body problem. Investigating it, V. M. Alekseev proved in 1968: the initial position and speed of a speck of dust can be chosen so that its subsequent movement will belong to the type of limited or oscillating movements, and full movement and speed of a speck of dust can be acquired even when it arrives from infinity.

In general, the picture of the movement looks like this: a speck of dust approaches from the end to the plane of elliptical motion of two massive bodies, produces this plane, and during this time the massive bodies come closer together, the forces of mutual attraction between all three participants in the movement increase so much that they tighten the speck of dust, turn it back and force it to subsequently oscillate with a limited or unlimited scope. The first of these two possibilities corresponds to complete capture, the second to oscillation capture, as the researchers say.

As usual, the very last steps are the most difficult. All those examples of motion that had a different character in the past and future, which were built before 1968, seemed to survive from a huge ocean of uncertainty, which is the entire set of possible motions in the three-body problem. Alekseev’s research shed light on how this totality is structured.

Alekseev’s result ended many years of debate about Shazi’s statement: it turned out to be completely wrong! The most controversial combinations of heterogeneous types from the Shazi classification, which describe the movement of three bodies in the past and future, turned out to be feasible and received a structural analogical justification.

Comet Kirnca-Qui: captured by Jupiter?

It cannot be said that with the refutation of Shazi’s statement in the problem of gravitational capture, no ambiguities remained. An attentive reader could notice some of them even with the help of this short article.

Well, for example: the possibility of capturing a comet, as Laplace saw it - due to its passage mismo of Jupiter or another giant planet? There is reason to think that the answer is yes.

Here's what that grip might look like. A comet, approaching Jupiter at a hyperbolic (relative to the Sun) speed, is slowed down by it and transferred to an elliptical orbit around the Sun so that at the next meeting with Jupiter it will move to another elliptical orbit, etc.

For some time it seemed that this was how the comet Kirnca-Kwi, discovered in 1963, was captured. Based on the results of the observations obtained, its current and previous orbits were calculated.
