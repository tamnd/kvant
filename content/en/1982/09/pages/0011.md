O. Yu. Shmidt (1891-1956). Outstanding Soviet mathematician, famous polar explorer. Proposed the first primer of partial capture in a three-body task.

slightly modified hyperbole with slightly increased speed.

This primer, perhaps, will become more understandable if we imagine the opposite situation of “decay”, when the body $P_1$ has been revolving around $P_0$ “for centuries”, but at some point the body $P_2$, which flew in at great speed, began to throw it out of orbit and threw it to infinity, and itself continued its path to infinity, losing a little in energy (Fig. 4, 6). To get an example of capture from here, it is enough to imagine time flowing backwards.

Schmidt's example had the same disadvantages as the exchange examples: the calculation covered only a finite period of time; Moreover, the errors in numerical integration turned out to be excessively large. When we compared the angular momentum values ​​at the ends of the integration, they did not coincide. Schmidt's example was not convincing. It seemed that Shazi's point of view had prevailed.

K. A. Sitnikov: partial capture is possible!

Several years have passed. Schmidt's example has been moved. The results showed that the scientist’s main idea was correct - a celestial body flying towards the Sun at great speed can “push” another body flying nearby from a hyperbolic orbit to an elliptical one, and then retire to infinity. Moreover, they found that this idea can be used to construct trajectories by purely theoretical means, without numerical integration.

In 1952, K. A. Sitnikov constructed an analytical primer of partial capture, and in 1956, V. M. Alekseev - examples of exchange. It turned out that such examples do not at all represent isolated exceptions - small changes in the parameters of celestial bodies did not lead to a change in the qualitative picture of the phenomenon.
