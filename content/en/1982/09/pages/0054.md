This, my brothers, is why concepts are needed in science. They simply save people time and effort. However, “save” is not the right word. They are the ones who make science possible. There was such a great scientist - Henri Poincaré. He was asked: “What is mathematics?” and he answered: “Mathematics is the art of calling seemingly different things with the same word.”
- Art!
— Yes, and concepts exist not only in mathematics. Every science has its own clear circle. And the “definition” is understandable and helps to master this circle.
— How does this definition help to master concepts? - Petya asked.
- Very simple, boys. An example from the same geometry. What is a square?
- Square? A rhombus in which all angles are right.
- Thank you, Peter Sharikov. So you pronounced the definition of a square. Well, what is a rhombus?
— A paraallogograph in which all sides are congruent.
- And again - the definition, this time of a rhombus. Now let's see how you did it. First you said: “A square is a rhombus,” that is, you reduced the concept of a square to the concept of a rhombus. A square is a special case of a rhombus, since every square is a rhombus, but not every rhombus is a square. If the first concept is a special case of the second, then it is called specific for the second, and the second is called generic for the first!
- I know that! Because in botany there are genera and species.
- Absolutely right! But let's continue our geometric reasoning. In the second definition, the paraallograph became the generic concept, and the rhombus became a specific concept for it, that is, here too you continued the same operation: you reduced the specific concept to the generic.

But that's not all! After all, you didn’t just indicate a generic concept. You noted which particular case of the generic concept is represented by a species concept. A square is not just a para-allograph, but a para-allog with constructive sides. Conclusion? In any definition of a new concept, two operations are performed: firstly, we indicate the closest generic concept, known previously, and secondly, we inform what particular case of a generic concept our new concept is*).
