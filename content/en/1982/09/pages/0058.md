the function $g$ is negative on some interval to the right of point 0 (why?). But for large $x$ (as mathematicians say, “on flatness”) $g(x) > 0$. This means that somewhere to the right of point 0 the polynomial $g(x)$ (a continuous function!) has a root $x_1$. Since the function $g$ is odd, $g(-x_1) = -g(x_1) = 0$. So, the polynomial $g(x)$ has roots $x_1, 0, -x_1$, forming an arithmetic progression. This means that the roots $a, \beta, \gamma$ of the given polynomial $f(x)$ also form an arithmetic progression.

So, the condition “$g$ is odd and $g'(0) < 0$”, that is

the existence of three roots forming an arithmetic progression is equally exclusive.
