Four planes drawn through the correspondence of the “raised” side of the rectangle and the rectagon $KLMN$ bound the tetrahedron $ABCD$. Let's perform a parallel projection, in which the “raised” rectangle goes into $K_1L_1M_1N_1$. Then the tetrahedron will be projected into the quadrilateral described by the enchanted rectangles, that is, into the original quadrilateral $ABCD$.

The faces $ABC$ and $ADC$ of the tetrahedron pass through the parallel segments $KL$ and $MN$ and intersect along the edge $AC$. Therefore, it is parallel to these segments and, therefore, to the drawing plane. It follows from this that any of its parallel projection onto this plane, in particular the diagonal $AC$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$, is equal to and parallel to the edge $AC$. Similarly, any projection of the edge $BD$, in particular the diagonal $BD$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$, is equal to and parallel to the edge $BD$. Now the tetrahedron $ABCD$ is projected onto the drawing plane in a different way - parallel to the edge $CD$. The projections of points $C$ and $D$ coincide, and we obtain a right triangle $ABC$, in which two rectangles of perimeter 10 are inscribed (see Fig. 4). In this case, the diagonals $AC$ and $BC$ of this triangle are equal and parallel to the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ of the original quadrilateral.
