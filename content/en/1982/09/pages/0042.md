Dayot number $c_1, c_2, c_3, \dots (c_k=a^k-1)$ when divided by $2^m$.
The number of different remainders is finite, so there are two average numbers $c_k$, say $c_n$ and $c_l$ ($n>l$), which give identical remainders when divided by $2^m$. Their difference is a multiple of $2^m$, and since $c_n-c_l=a^n-a^l=a^{n-l}-1=a^l\cdot c_{n-l}$ and $a$ are odd, $2^m$ is divisible by $c_{n-l}$. It also follows from the formula for the difference that, together with $c_{n-l}$, all terms of the infinite sequence $c_{n-l}, c_{2(n-l)}, c_{3(n-l)}, \dots$ are also divided by $2^m$.

We recommend the reader to prove that the generality of all numbers $c_k$ divisible by $2^m$ has the form $\{c_r, c_{2r}, \dots, c_{i.r}, \dots\}$, where $r=2^l$, $t\leq m$.

C) Let us denote by $d(N)$ the exponent of the most powers of two by which the integer $N$ is divided. Let $d(m)=n$, then $m=2^n\cdot s$, where $s$ is odd. Since $c_m=a^{2n}\cdot s-1=(a^{2n}-1)(a^{2n(s-1)}+a^{2n(s-2)}+\dots+1)$ and the sum in the second bracket is odd, $d(c_m)=d(a^{2n}-1)$. Let us now note that factors of the form $a^{2l}+1$ in expansion (1) for $l\geqslant 1$ are divisible by 2 and not divisible by 4 (indeed, the number $a$ is odd, therefore $a^{2l}$ is the square of an odd number and leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 4). It follows that $d(c_m)=d(a^2-1)+n-1$ at $n\geqslant 1$; if $n=0$, then $d(c_m)=d(a-1)$.

For divisibility of $c_m$ by $2^m$ it is necessary that the inequality $d(c_m)\geqslant m$ be satisfied, that is, $d(a-1)\geqslant m$ when $m$ is odd and $d(a^2-1)+n-1\geqslant m=2^n\cdot s$ when $m$ is even. The first inequality holds only for a finite number of numbers $m$, and the second inequality is reduced to the form

$$s \leq \frac{d(a^2-1)+n-1}{2^n}.$$

Since the right part of this inequality tends to 0 for $n\rightarrow\infty$, it can only hold for a finite set of pairs $(n;s)$ for any fixed $a$.

Returning to point a), we set $a=31$. The required numbers $m$ satisfy the inequality $m\leqslant d(31-1)=d(30)=1$, if $m$ is odd, and the inequality $s\leqslant(d(31^2-1)+n-1)/2^n$, if $m=2^n\cdot s$, where $n\geqslant 1$, $s$ are odd. The first inequality gives $m=1$. The second is reduced to the form $s\leqslant(5+n)/2^n$, since $d(31^2-1)=d(30\cdot 32)=6$. From here we get the possibility of the pair $(n;s)$: these are $(1;1), (1;3), (2;1)$ and $(3;1)$, that is, $m=2, 4, 6, 8$.

**M734. Bissertis point A of triangle ABC intersects the circumcircle at point K. Prove that the length of the projection of segment AK onto line AB (or AC) is equal to half the length of sides AB and AC.**

Let for definition $|AB| \leqslant |AC|$. On the side AC, consider a point P, symmetrical to B relative to the straight line AK (see figure). Obviously $|AP|=|AB|, |PK|=|BK|$. The equality of the angles BAK and CAK implies the equality of the arcs BK and CK on which they rest, and the equality of the arcs implies the equality of the chords BK and CK subtending them. Thus, $|CK|=|BK|=|PK|$, and therefore the projection K' of the point K on the side AC is the midpoint of the segment CP. Hence,

$$|AK'|=|AP|+|PK'|=|AB|+\frac{|AC|-|AB|}{2} = \frac{|AB|+|AC|}{2}.$$

A. Egorov
