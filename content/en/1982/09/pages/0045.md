◆ A stone thrown with a minimum initial speed $v_0$, sufficient for the stone to fly over the wall, describes a trajectory such as shown in Fig. At points A and B, the trajectory touches the edges of the wall; the speed $v$ of the stone at point A makes an angle $\alpha = 45^\circ$ with the horizon. The time $t$ during which the stone flies from point A to point B is determined by the conditions
$$v_y = v \sin \alpha = gt/2 \Rightarrow t = 2v (\sin \alpha)/g.$$

The magnitude of the velocity $v$ is related to the obtuse wall $l$ by correspondence
$$l = v_x t = 2v \cos \alpha \cdot v (\sin \alpha)/g = v^2/g \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{gl}.$$

The connection between $v_0$ and $v$ will be found from the law of conservation of energy:
$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} + mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2} + mgH,$$
where
$$v_0 = \sqrt{v^2 + 2g(H - h)} = \sqrt{g(l + 2H - 2h)}.$$
