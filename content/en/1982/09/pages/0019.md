$$\frac{\Delta u}{u} \left( \frac{3}{5} \frac{u^2 M}{RT} - 1 \right) = \frac{\Delta S}{S}.$$

Note that $^5/3RT/M$ is the square of some speed. Let's denote it by $a^2$, then the expression $u^2/a^2 - 1$ will be in the bracket. In fact, $a^2$ is the square of the speed of sound in a monatomic gas at a temperature $T$, but we won’t need this fact. Let's just remember that there is some important speed $a$ that depends on $T$. And that will be enough for us here.

So, what can be seen from the last equation? If $u < a$, then the bracket is negative, and for a positive speed increment ($\Delta u > 0$), the area increment must be negative ($\Delta S < 0$), that is, the area must be reduced. As $u$ increases we will reach a point where $u = a$. Here the bracket vanishes, and $\Delta S = 0$ for any $\Delta u$, including positive ones. And finally, when $u > a$, for further growth $u$ it is necessary to ensure a positive increase in area ($\Delta S > 0$), that is, the flow of the channel must increase.
