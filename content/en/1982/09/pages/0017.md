Imagine a reactor in which water flows at a speed of $u$ and which has a cross-sectional area (perpendicular to the speed) of $S$. Then, per unit time, a mass of water passes through this section equal to the product $quS$. This quantity is called mass flow. Look how it is “structured”: the density of water $q$ is multiplied by the volume $uS$ transferred per unit time. But each unit of mass (more precisely, the mass of each unit of volume) has both kinetic energy $qu^2/2$ and momentum $qu$. This means that the river carries not only mass, but also these quantities, and the flows of kinetic energy and momentum are equal, respectively ($qu^2/2)uS$ and $qu \cdot uS$.
