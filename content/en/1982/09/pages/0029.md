As of 1979 (date of publication of the last catalog of cometary orbits), the orbits of 659 comets have been reliably determined, of which only 98 are of the hyperbolic type. But where is the guarantee that they were hyperbolic even before the first observation of these comets?

It is currently believed that on the border of the solar system there is a Hypertian cloud of comets - the Oort cloud, which is 100-150 thousand times larger than the Earth’s orbit. It is believed that those comets' orbits that touch us in a parabolic or weakly hyperbolic manner are, for the most part, actually highly elongated ellipses, which can be traversed in up to 400 thousand years. But here’s the question: is the Oort cloud formed simultaneously with the entire solar system, or does it replenish from time to time due to gravitational capture in interstellar space? Here changing diverges. Let's hope that the answer to this question comes from the future.
