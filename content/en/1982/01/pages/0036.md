Between two media with refractive indices $n_0 > 1$ and $n_1 = 1$ there is an inhomogeneous layer with a height of $h = H \left(1 - \frac{1}{n_0^2}\right)$, where $H = \text{const}$, inside which the refractive index changes with height according to the law $n = n_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{y}{H}}$ (see figure). A light beam enters the inhomogeneous layer from a medium with a refractive index $n_0$. At what values ​​of el $a_0$ is the distance between the entry and exit points of the beam maximum?

From the law of light refraction it follows that with a changing refractive index of the medium, the angle φ of the beam’s deviation from the normal changes (to the interface between the conditions of sections of the medium with different values ​​of $n$), and
$$n \sin \varphi = n \cos \alpha = n_0 \sin \varphi_0 = n_0 \cos \alpha_0 = \text{const}, \quad \text{где } \alpha = \frac{\pi}{2} - \varphi$$
To find the trajectory of a ray in an inhomogeneous medium, we will use the following analogy: a body of mass $m$ (the dimensions of the body are extremely small) moves along the trajectory of the ray, the speed of the body at the point with ordinate $y$ is equal to $v(y) = v_0 n_y$ ($v_0$ is the value of the initial speed of the body at the point with ordinate 0). Then the velocity projection onto the interface (horizontal projection) remains constant. If at the same time a constant force $F = \frac{mv_0^2}{2H}$ acts on the point, directed vertically at any point of the trajectory
$$\frac{m(v(y))^2}{2} = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} - m \frac{v_0^2}{2H} y.$$
It can be seen that for a given law of change $n(y)$ the trajectory of the ray coincides with the trajectory of a body thrown at an angle to the horizon and moving under the influence of “gravity” $F = mv_0^2/2H$. Therefore, to answer the questions posed in the problem, we can use formulas for such motion, taking into account that the “acceleration of free fall” in our case is equal to “g” = $v_0^2/2H$.
So, the distance $l$ between the entry and exit points of the beam will be maximum if the beam enters the inhomogeneous medium at an angle $\varphi_0 = 45^\circ$. At the same time
$$I_{\max} = \frac{2vA_0^2 \sin \alpha_0 \cos \alpha_0}{v_0^2/2H} = 2H$$ (since $\alpha_0 = \frac{\pi}{2} - \varphi_0 = 45^\circ$).
In order for the beam not to pass into the upper homogeneous medium at $\alpha_0 = 45^\circ$, the height $h$ of the inhomogeneous region must satisfy the condition $h \geq \frac{H}{2}$. Otherwise ($h < \frac{H}{2}$), the maximum distance between the entry and exit points of the beam corresponds to the angle $\alpha'$ at which the height of the trajectory is equal to $h'$, that is, the trajectory touches the upper boundary of the interface. This angle $\alpha'$ is found from the condition
$$n_0 \cos \alpha' = n(y = h') \cos 0^\circ = n_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{h'}{H}};$$
$$\cos \alpha' = \sqrt{1 - \frac{h'}{H}}.$$
