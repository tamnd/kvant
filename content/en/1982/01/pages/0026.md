⟦folio 24⟧

M723*. Is there an infinite set of natural numbers such that none of the numbers in this set and no sum of several of them is a power of a natural number ($a^k$, where $k \geq 2$)?

L. Gurvits

M724. Several scoops crawl along the plane, the speeds of which are equal in magnitude but different in direction. Prove that, no matter how the scoops are located at the beginning, after some time they will be at the vertices of a convex polygon.

V. Prasolov

M725*. Let's put $e_n = \cos^n \frac{\pi}{7} + \cos^n \frac{3\pi}{7} + \cos^n \frac{5\pi}{7}$. Find a) $e_1$ and $e_2$, b) $e_3$ and $e_4$. C) Prove that $e_n$ is a rational number for any $n$.

N. Vasiliev

F733. On a “half-cylinder” with base radius $R$ lies a dumbbell of length $l$ (Fig. 1). Find the period of small oscillations of the dumbbell.

A. Frolov

F734. A rope, attached at one end to the side surface of the cylinder at its base of radius $r$, is wound around the cylinder $k$ times ($k$ is an integer). A weight is attached to the free end of the rope. The load is given a speed $v$, directed along the radius of the cylinder (Fig. 2). How long will it take for the entire rope to wrap around the cylinder again? The cylinder is fixed on a smooth surface.

B. Mukushev

F735. Two identical inductors are connected through switches $K_1$ and $K_2$ to a source with constant EMF $\beta$ and internal resistance $r$ (Fig. 3). At the initial moment of time, both keys are open. Then keys $K_1$ are closed first, and then keys $K_2$. Determine the magnitude of the current flowing through the keys $K_1$ at the moment of closing the key $K_2$, if it is known that after closing the key $K_2$ the steady-state current through the keys $K_1$ is twice as large as the steady-state current through the keys $K_2$. Neglect the active resistance of the coil.

V. Mozhaev

F736. A beam of electrons falls onto a homogeneous rod, both ends of which are grounded, and the same number of electrons per second hits each centimeter of the length of the rod. Rod resistance $R$. The current in the borrowing section is $I$. Find the potential difference between the core of the rod $A$ and its end $B$ (Fig. 4).

N. Dikasky
