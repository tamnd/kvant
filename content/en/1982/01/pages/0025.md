⟦folio 23⟧

This section has been maintained since the founding of our magazine. In each issue we place five problems in mathematics and five problems in physics. (For foreign readers, we provide the texts of the problem in English.) The degree of difficulty of the problem varies. Along with relatively easy ones, problems that require lengthy thinking are published; There are problems that are not easy for specialists to solve. The most difficult tasks are given stars.

If a task interests you, but you can’t solve it right away, don’t give up, try to return to it again and again. Do not open it even after it has been solved—think about how to write down the solution most rationally and convincingly, how to generalize the problem, clarify its result, what related problems it allows you to solve. Send your decisions to the editorial office at the address: 117071, Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Fizmatlit, «Kvant». On the envelope, in the “To” column, write Problem «Kvant» and indicate the solutions to which problems you are sending. Send solutions to problems in different subjects (mathematics and physics) or from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. In the letter, include an envelope with your home address written on it (you will receive a response in it with the results of checking your decisions). At the beginning of the letter, please write your last name, first name, patronymic, home address; Schoolchildren are also asked to indicate their class and school number.

We regularly publish the names of readers who sent correct solutions. The magazine is holding a competition for the best solutions to the problem from the Problem Book «Kvant». The results of the competition are announced in December. The winners - schoolchildren who sent the most interesting and complete solutions - receive the right to participate immediately in the Resource rounds of the All-Union Schoolchildren Olympiad. The competition jury «Kvant» accepts for consideration only decisions sent no later than the deadline specified in the journal. The deadline for responses to problems in this issue is March 30. For students of the All-Union Correspondence Mathematical School, the correct solution of a problem from the Problem Book is considered a completed optional task (they must indicate their “number” or address, surname VZMSH at the beginning of the letter).

Of course, not all problems placed in the Problem Book are published for the first time. After formation, we usually indicate who offered us this task. Coming up with a new original problem, please, is more difficult than solving someone else’s. If you succeed, send us two copies of the problem and its solution. At the same time, put “New problem in mathematics (in physics)” on the envelope.

We place the most interesting problems of our readers in Problem Book «Kvant» or in other sections of the magazine.

Tasks

M721 — M725; F733 — F737

M721. Each side of the triangle is cut into 3 equal parts. The division points serve as the vertices of two triangles, the intersection of which is a hexagon. Find the area of ​​this hexagon if the area of ​​this triangle is S.

A. Zolotovykh, 10th grade student.
(Moscow, Physics School No. 18 at Moscow State University)

M722. At points $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ located around the circle, the numbers 1, 2, ..., n are placed in a certain order. a) Prove that the sum of $n$ models of differences of numbers is not less than $2n-2$. b) For each number of differences between numbers at least $2n-2$?

A. Razborov
