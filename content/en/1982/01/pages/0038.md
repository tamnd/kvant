⟦folio 36⟧

A. Savin

Pebbles
and chessboard
(memories
about summer holidays)

On a hot July day, Kolya and I went to the beach. Having swum to our heart's content, we lay down on the sand to sunbathe. Next to us, two men were playing chess. Naturally, we immediately began to discuss the position on the board and give hints: I - to one, and Kolya - to the other. But the players were unperturbed and did not react to our hints at all. Desperate to turn this game into a masterpiece of chess art, we began to think about what we should do.

“Shouldn’t we throw some pebbles?” - Kolya asked me. “What is this - toss and catch?” "Not really! - he answered, - Much more interesting! First, let's play this game.

There is a pile of stones. We take turns taking one, two or three stones. The one who takes the last stone wins.”

I decided to play so that I always had an even number of stones, and I lost. In the second game, I began to take stones so that I always had an odd number of stones - and again I lost. I really didn’t want to lose for the third time, and I decided to try to understand the meaning of the game.

And for the first time it turned out that before my last move I had 4 stones. If I took only one stone, Kolya took the other three. If I took two or three stones, he took the remaining two or the only last stone.

How can I avoid ending up in front of four stones again? It is impossible to leave him 5, 6 or 7 stones: having taken 1, 2 or 3 stones, he will again leave me 4 stones. What if I left him 8 stones? Then he will be forced to leave me 5, 6 or 7 stones, and I forced him to make a move in a situation where he has 4 stones. And then it dawned on me!
