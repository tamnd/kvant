1. It is known that when two bodies collide, the maximum loss of kinetic energy of their motion occurs during an absolutely inelastic impact, when the bodies move at the same speeds after the collision. This part of the kinetic energy can be converted into other types of energy, in particular, it can be used to excite one of the steel atoms. Subsequently, when the atom transitions from the excited state to the ground state, a photon can be emitted. The minimum excitation energy $E_{\text{min}}$ atom, being in the ground state, is achieved when an electron moves from the first energy level to the second ($n=1$, $m=2$). For example, the induction energy atoma required to remove an electron from it corresponds to the transition from the first level to the infinite remote one ($n=1$, $m \to \infty$). Therefore, from the problem conditions it follows that the constant $R$ in the formula for radiation frequencies is equal to the induction energy $E_{\text{in}}$ divided by Planck’s constant $h$, and the minimum excitation energy atoma is equal to

$$E_{\text{min}} = hR \left(1 - \frac{1}{2^2}\right) = \frac{3}{4} E_{\text{in}}.$$

Now, from the laws of conservation of energy and momentum, we can obtain the minimum kinetic energy $mv_0^2/2$ of the incident hydrogen atom:

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = 2 \frac{mv^2}{2} + E_{\text{min}},$$

$$mv_0 = 2mv,$$

where

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = 2E_{\text{min}} = \frac{3}{2} E_{\text{in}} = 20, 4 \text{эВ}.$$

When solving this problem, many scientists made a characteristic mistake associated with the incomprehensible physical meaning of the energy of induction of atoma, which they considered the minimum energy of excitation of atoma. Others, having correctly found the minimum value of excitation energy, did not take into account the law of conservation of momentum, considering that all the kinetic energy of the incident atom during a collision turns into excitation energy and is then radiated.

2. The equilibrium condition is the equality of the partial pressures of hydrogen in the first and second vessels. Let $p$ be the initial pressure in the first vessel. It consists of the partial pressure of the gel and hydrogen. Since the masses and temperatures of the gases are the same, the pressure ratio of the gel and hydrogen is inverse with respect to their molar masses. Thus,

$$p_{\text{He}} + p_{\text{H}_2} = p,$$

$$\frac{p_{\text{He}}}{p_{\text{H}_2}} = \frac{\mu_{\text{H}_2}}{\mu_{\text{He}}} = \frac{1}{2},$$

where

$$p_{\text{He}} = \frac{p}{3}, p_{\text{H}_2} = \frac{2p}{3}.$$

For the total pressure in the first vessel to be half the initial pressure, the partial pressure of hydrogen must decrease to a value of $p'_{\text{H}_2} = p/6$. Since the pressure of hydrogen is inversely proportional to the volume it occupies, we obtain

$$\frac{p_{\text{H}_2}}{p'_{\text{H}_2}} = \frac{V_1 + V_2}{V_1}, \text{ и } \frac{V_2}{V_1} = 3.$$

The characteristic error of the multi-hytrix absorbers of the state is that they considered the equality of hydrogen in the second and hydrogen and gel in the first vessels to be the equilibrium condition.

3. The force of interaction between the plates of a capacitor in general cases is determined by the product of the intense field of one of the plates and the charge of the other.

In the absence of a dielectric, the field in the capacitor is $E_1 = U/d$, and the charge on its plates is $q_1 = \varepsilon_0 US/d$, where $d$ is the distance between the plates, $S$ is the area of ​​the plates, $\varepsilon_0$ is the electric constant. Taking into account the fact that the field of one plate is half as much as the field in the capacitor, for the force of interaction between the plates we obtain

$$F_1 = \frac{q_1 E_1}{2} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 SU^2}{2d^2}.$$

After adding the dielectric, the resulting capacitor can be considered as two series connected capacitors, one of which is filled with a dielectric with a food capacity of $\varepsilon$, and the distances between the plates of both are equal to $d/2$. Since the voltage remains constant, changing the capacitance leads to a change in the charge on the plates, which, in turn, changes the field in the capacitor. The capacity of the resulting capacitor is $C_2 = 2\varepsilon_0 S/d$ ($1 + \varepsilon$) (this is obtained from the equality $1/C_2 = d/2 \varepsilon_0 S + d/2 \varepsilon_0 S$); charge on capacitor $q_2 = UC_2 = 2\varepsilon_0 SU/d$ ($1 + \varepsilon$); field $E_2 = q_2/S\varepsilon_0 = 2\varepsilon U/d$ ($1 + \varepsilon$); interaction force

$$F_2 = \frac{Q_2 E_2}{2} = \frac{2\varepsilon_0^2 SU^2}{d^2(1 + \varepsilon)^2}.$$

Relationship of forces

$$\frac{F_2}{F_1} = \frac{4\varepsilon^2}{(1 + \varepsilon)^2} = 2,25.$$
