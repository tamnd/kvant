If during a starquake the star becomes less solid, then its moment of inertia will decrease; This will also reduce the rotation period.

From the definition of the moment of inertia (we gave it on page 6) it follows that if during a starquake the star becomes less solid, its moment of inertia decreases. Since the values ​​of $I/\omega$ remain constant (after all, a starquake occurs as a result of internal “shocks”, without the participation of external forces), a decrease in the moment of inertia leads to an increase in the angular velocity of rotation of the star. This means that the rotation period decreases.

However, after the start of the disruption, when the starquake has already ended, the period continues to decrease for a long time. In one case this time is several days, in another - several years. The constant change in the period can be explained only by assuming that after breaking a part of the star, a state of ordinary matter, it has accelerated its movement, the neutron core continues to rotate at the same speed, and only over a long period of time the speeds are equalized. But this means that the neutron core is in a superfluid state! After all, with normal friction the speeds would equalize in a few seconds.

Now we can proceed to the main topic of our story - the fate of a neutron star, the mass of which is growing. The mass of a star can increase from the fall of celestial bodies on it and due to the flow of matter from neighboring stars of lower mass. An increase in mass leads to an increase in density at the center of the star.

What is a vacuum? Isn't this just emptiness - space in which neutrons, protons, electrons, quanta move?

It turned out that this was not the case. Vacuum, that is, our physical space, is not just a geometric object, but a complex physical system with interesting properties.

First of all, particles, by their presence, change the space around them. An electric field appears around the charged particle, which must be remembered as a kind of vacuum state. In such a modified vacuum, a force appears that acts on other charged particles.

Even geometric properties change around heavy bodies. Thus, in the vicinity of the Sun, the geometry of the vacuum, although slightly, differs from the ενclidian one. The ratio of circumference to radius is not equal to 2π, as the school calculates.
