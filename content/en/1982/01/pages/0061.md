1. {—4}. Solution. This equation is equivalent to the equation
$$\log_2(4-x) = 4 - \log_2(-2-x)$$
$$\log_2(4-x) + \log_2(-2-x) = 4.$$ (1)

Since the identity $\log_a(uv) = \log_a u + \log_a v$ is valid only for $u > 0$ and $v > 0$, when passing from the sum of logarithms on the left side of equation (1) to the logarithm of the product, one must add “conditions” $4-x > 0$, $-2-x > 0$. However, it is enough to add one (any) of these inequalities (why?). Thus, equation (1) is equivalent to the system
$$\log_2[(4-x)(-2-x)] = 4$$
$$4-x > 0.$$

The equation $\log_2[(4-x)(-2-x)] = 4$ is equal to the equation
$$(4-x)(-2-x) = 16$$
$$x^2 - 2x - 24 = 0$$

One of its roots is a solution to the inequality $4-x > 0$, the other is not.

2. $\frac{3}{2}$ cm, $\frac{5}{2}$ cm, $\frac{7}{2}$ cm. Solution. If the length of the shortest side is denoted by $x$, then a side of length $x+2$ will lie opposite the angle $120^\circ$. By the cosine theorem
$$(x+2)^2 = x^2 + (x+1)^2 - 2x(x+1) \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right).$$

This equation has roots $\frac{3}{2}$ and - 1, of which the second cannot be length.
