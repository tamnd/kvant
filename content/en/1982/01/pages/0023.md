Ice cream mystery

When ice cream first appeared, the method of making it was kept as a closely guarded secret. In vain did many European courts try to use snow or ice to freeze a mixture of cream, sugar and fruit juices. The mixture cooled, but did not freeze. I had to resort to a means that we now call industrial spinonage. What did they find out?

“When my grandmother makes ice cream at home,” Walker writes, “she lines the ice cream jug with ice and sprinkles salt on the ice. Why is she pouring salt?

So, it's all about regular salt. Do a number of simple wholesales before responding to Walker. Take snow or crushed ice and gradually add salt to it, stirring the mixture. (It’s nice, but not necessary, for the experience to be accompanied by making ice cream.)

It turns out that the temperature of such a mixture can be lowered to -20°C.

Most often, table salt NaCl is used for cooling. But rarer substances are also suitable: KCl, NaNO₃, etc. The “champion” for lowering the temperature turns out to be CaCl₂. By mixing 42 g of this salt with 100 g of melted ice, you can get a temperature of -55°C!

Which water will freeze faster?

“In cold countries, such as Canada or Iceland, it is well known that hot water, when exposed outside in the cold, freezes more quickly than cold water. This may seem like nonsense to you, but this is by no means an old wives' tale: even Francis Bacon at one time dismissed this fact.

Fill several vessels of various forms with warm and cold water and place them outside the window on a frosty day... And don’t be surprised if in any of the vessels the warm water freezes before the cold, but try to explain why?” (J. Walker).

It is difficult to add anything to this, except, perhaps, some advice: take bottles with narrow necks and wide jars as vessels of various shapes. And one more thing: think about how the temperature of the liquid will change if you lower the pressure above its surface and, thereby, allow the liquid to evaporate more intensely. (You can also check the corresponding experiment, using a regular vacuum cleaner to lower the pressure.)
