⟦folio 40⟧

«Kvant» has been published for twelve years - and for all twelve years the column “Applicant's Workshop” has invariably appeared on its pages. Editorial mail, meetings with students and teachers, and reader questionnaires sent to the editorial office show that this section is of great interest to many young readers of the magazine.

In the “Applicant's Workshop”, readers will find articles devoted to a detailed explanation of the most important and complex questions of programmers of electronic exams in mathematics and physics, analysis of typical problems, mainly taken directly from options for applied university exams, character analysis, especially common mistakes of applicants.

Having become acquainted with the publications of the “Applicant's Workshop”, readers can easily make sure that in order to successfully pass the entrance exams, no additional knowledge is required in comparison with school courses in mathematics and physics. However, you should not think that just reading school textbooks is enough - you need to carefully understand and deeply assimilate the theoretical material, gain solid and lasting skills in solving problems. This can only be achieved through real, hard work, systematically, systematically throughout the rest of the time.

The stats of the “Applicant Practicum” cannot (and do not set a goal) to replace school textbooks. They are written in the expectation that the reader has already mastered school courses in mathematics and physics and would like to get to additional lessons and tasks for independent solution. In addition to the materials that will appear in the “Applicant's Workshop” in the next issues of the magazine, when preparing for entrance exams it would be useful to get acquainted with some articles «Kvant» published in past years. Thematically, a list of such articles is included below; The list shows the year, magazine number and page where the article begins. (It should be borne in mind that in articles published before 1977, sometimes there are terms and designations that are different from those accepted in school now; we hope to carefully study this difficulty.)
