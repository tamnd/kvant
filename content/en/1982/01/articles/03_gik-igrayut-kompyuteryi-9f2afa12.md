COMPUTERS PLAY

On pages «Kvant» we have already talked about the principles embedded in modern computer chess programs and how computers analyze a chess endgame. Here we will talk about two games played by computers in the world chess championships.

Let us remind you that three such championships have already taken place. In 1974, in Stockholm, the Soviet program “Kanssa” became the first world champion. In the second championship, held three years later in Canada, she lost her importance to the American Chess program. The non-training case will occur in the first.type.

"Dachess" - "Kanssa"
White just lengthened check with the queen on a8. Suddenly, “Kanssa” gives up a whole rook - 34...Re8. The commentators were perplexed and embarrassedly explained to the audience that chess programs were still far from perfect and anything could be expected from them. What a general study it was when “Kansa” explained its “call” with the following option: 34...Kg7 35.Qf8+!! Kf:f8 36.Ch6+ and 37.Rs8+ with inevitable mate! Not a single “white chess” player, as the English chess magazine wrote, who was present at the championship discovered this effective queen sacrifice. It is unknown whether Dutch saw this combination or not, but from the purely practical messages in this position, the move 34...Kg7 should have been chosen, since playing without a rook is absolutely hopeless, and not every program (and not every master!) can find the move 35. Qf8+. If White was going to respond to 34...Kg7 to win a piece with 35.g5, then he came forward (35...K:e3 36.gf+ Qf6 37.fe Qg5+ and Qb5 with the decisive move for Black). Thus, "Kanssa" was a victim of its own tactical vigilance. It must be said that the practical messages resulting from cida allow for simple software implementation.
