Schoolchildren living in the general area of ​​operation of the VZMSH and its branches with the MMMF - a department of the VZMSH at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University (see below) choose one of these schools at their own discretion.

Schoolchildren who did not pass the competition at VZMSH or its branch have the opportunity to study in groups “Collective student of VZMSH”. Each such group is a school mathematical circle, working under the guidance of its own mathematics teacher according to the VZMS program. Admission to these groups is carried out until September 20, 1982 in two streams: for those who will begin studying in the 8th grade from September 1982, and for those who will begin to study in the 9th grade.

“Collective student” groups are admitted to VZMSH without introductory work control. To organize a group, an application from the mathematics teacher leading the circle, indicating the list of students, is sufficient. The application must be certified by the school director and stamped. The work of the group leader “Collective student of VZMSH” can pay for schools at the request of VZMSH as elective classes. The application should be sent to VZMSH.

Entrance test

1. Find a four-digit number of the form $a^{19b}$ (with digits in order $a, 1, 9, b$), do by 82.

2. A rectangular coordinate system $XOY$ was drawn on a sheet of paper, the sheet of paper was bent along one coordinate axis, then along the other, and the sheet folded in this way in four was cut with scissors along a broken line with vertices at points (0; 1), (1; 4), (2; 2), (4; 1), (1; 0). Draw an educational “snowflake” when unfolding a sheet of paper and find its area.

3. It is known that $x+y+z=0$ and $xyz\neq 0$. Calculate

$$\frac{x^2}{yz} + \frac{y^2}{xz} + \frac{z^2}{xy}.$$

4. Points $A_1, B_1$ and $C_1$ are taken on sides BC, CA and BA of equilateral right triangle ABC ($\hat{C}=90^\circ$), respectively. At the same time $|BA_1|:|A_1C|=|CB_1|:|B_1A|=|AC_1|:|C_1B|=2:1$. Prove that the segments $C_1C$ and $B_1A_1$ are equal in length and perpendicular.

5. There are three alloys. The first contains 40% copper and 60% nickel, the second - 60% copper and 40% calcium, the third - 60% calcium and 40% nickel. From them it is necessary to make a new alloy weighing 1 kg containing 40% calcium. What is the lowest copper content this new alloy can contain?

6. Is it possible to cut two rectangles from a triangle so that the sum of their areas is equal to two times the area of ​​the triangle?

7. It is known about three numbers that if each

Add one of them with the square of the sum of the other two, then each time you get 1/2. What could these numbers be?

8. The square from the length 1 side is cut into 4 rectangles. What values ​​can the sum of their perimeters take?

9. Is there a natural number $n$ such that the product $1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \dots \cdot n$ of all integers from 1 to $n$ ends with exactly 30 zeros?

10. Ten numbers are written in a row (their averages may be the same). Then under each number we write how many numbers there are in this series that are smaller than it, and thus we get a new series of ten numbers. Can this result in the series: a) 9 00 2 5 3 6 3 6 6, b) 5 6 1 1 4 8 5 8 0 1?

11. The houses of Andrey and Kolya are connected by a straight road. Andrey walks one and a half times faster than Kolya. Each of them rides a bicycle twice as fast as they walk, while Andrey can walk from his house to Kolinoye in 1 hour. At 12 o'clock they both set off from their houses towards each other, and one of them has a bicycle (which is allowed to be left unattended). When, at the earliest, will it be that each of them has already welcomed the other into the house?

12. There are many identical round coins. Is it possible to decompose on a plane: a) 1980, 6) 1981, c) 1982 of them so that each touches the other three? d) Is it possible to lay out a neoctor on a plane (a finite number of identical round coins so that each one touches four others? (In all points a) - d) coins cannot be placed one on top of the other and placed on their edge.)

New admission to the Malyi Mekhmat

For students of rural classes living in the territory of the European part of the RSFSR (except for the Northwestern regions), in Belarus and in Kazakhstan, admission to the Small Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics (MMMF) is being studied - a correspondence mathematical school at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University.

The MMMF program is aimed at deepening knowledge in the most important sections of the school curriculum and develops in schoolchildren the ability to use technologies for studying mathematical

mathematics, compiled under the guidance of faculty professors. This program takes into account the features of entrance exams for the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University.

Classes will begin on September 1, 1982. Training is free. The duration of training is three years. A person successfully studying at the Small Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics may be invited to an oral entrance exam for the purpose of admission to boarding school No. 18 at Moscow State University (see «Kvant», 1979, No. 1). Students who successfully complete all assignments will receive a certificate of completion from MMMF.

Those wishing to enroll in the Malyi Mekhmat must send, no later than April 15, the solution to as many problems as possible in the introductory test of the MIMF, which is published below.

Assignment for the introductory test at the MIMF in 1982
1. Solve inequality
$$\frac{x^2}{x-1} \ge 0.$$
2. Is it possible to arrange the numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., 11, 12 on a circle in such a way that any two adjacent numbers are different from each other?
a) no more than one;
b) no more than two?
3. How many zeros are there in the number 25? = 1 • 2 • 3 • ... • 24 • 25?
4. For 16 lemons you need to plan as many rubles as you can buy lemons for one ruble. How much does one lemon cost?

5. Prove that the sum of the lengths of the diagonals of a convex quadrilateral is always less than the perimeter of this quadrilateral.
6. The seven dwarves decided that each of them would give Snow White at least seven snowdrops for her birthday. When all the flowers were presented, Snow White said that their number did not exceed 50. Hearing this, the smallest of the gnomes immediately declared that he could accurately name the number of snowdrops presented. How many flowers did each dwarf give to Snow White?
7. Find the sum of all coefficients of the polynomial that is obtained after opening the parentheses in the expression
$$(x+2)(x+3)(x-5)(x-1)+4.$$
