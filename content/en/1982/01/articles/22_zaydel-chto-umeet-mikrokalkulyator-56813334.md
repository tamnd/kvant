What does a micro calculator have?

In recent years, the pocket electronic computer (microcalculator) has increasingly penetrated into all branches of human activity, including at school. With the help of a microcalculator, calculations that were previously difficult to dream of are also made possible. Practical microcalculators solve a computational problem at the same speed with which we assign this task to it. However, the capabilities of microcalculators are higher than what follows from the factory instructions, which are generally too brief. To help non-specialists master all the “secrets” of this educational development of the last decade, the book of G. Croyle* was recognized as a solution. The author coped well with the task. Numerous examples and problems, the solutions of which are given in the text, allow the reader to learn how to count on a microcalculator. Having abandoned the feeble attempt of an experienced person to explain the physical principles and mechanism of operation of a pocket calculating machine, the author continues to describe how to most economically carry out certain calculations, which class of microcalculator should be chosen for a specific job of engineering, accounting, scientific or educational calculations. Sufficient attention is paid to checking the accuracy and correct operation of microcalculators.

In the Soviet Union, all the main models of microcalculators are calculated, from the simplest and most practical arithmetic ones, intended mainly for schoolchildren and housewives (for example, BZ-23), to those such as S3-15, and more expensive ones, necessary for an engineer or scientist. Our microcalculators, of course, differ in their notation system, key layout and other details from similar devices from foreign companies. And, although the principles of operation and design of all microcalculators are the same, you should learn how to use them on the models from which you will be working.

Unfortunately, the products followed the simplest path - the book was translated verbatim, retaining in it the “Croiletrons” as the only examples of microcalculators and the English symbols on the keyboard. Meanwhile, a relatively limited number of domestic models allows all of them to be presented in the text, corresponding to the visibility, where necessary, of the management solutions given in the book. It would probably be advisable to prepare a second book, modified in accordance with these wishes, and publish the book in a limited edition in order to include it in addition to the instructions for all microcalculators sold.

It is known that when two bodies collide, the maximum loss of kinetic energy of their motion occurs during an absolutely inelastic impact, when the bodies move at the same speeds after the collision. This part of the kinetic energy can be converted into other types of energy, in particular, it can be used to excite one of the steel atoms. Subsequently, when the atom transitions from the excited state to the ground state, a photon can be emitted. The minimum excitation energy $E_{\text{min}}$ atom, being in the ground state, is achieved when an electron moves from the first energy level to the second ($n=1$, $m=2$). For example, the induction energy atoma required to remove an electron from it corresponds to the transition from the first level to the infinite remote one ($n=1$, $m \to \infty$). Therefore, from the problem conditions it follows that the constant $R$ in the formula for radiation frequencies is equal to the induction energy $E_{\text{in}}$ divided by Planck’s constant $h$, and the minimum excitation energy atoma is equal to

$$E_{\text{min}} = hR \left(1 - \frac{1}{2^2}\right) = \frac{3}{4} E_{\text{in}}.$$

Now, from the laws of conservation of energy and momentum, we can obtain the minimum kinetic energy $mv_0^2/2$ of the incident hydrogen atom:

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = 2 \frac{mv^2}{2} + E_{\text{min}},$$

$$mv_0 = 2mv,$$

where

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = 2E_{\text{min}} = \frac{3}{2} E_{\text{in}} = 20, 4 \text{эВ}.$$

When solving this problem, many scientists made a characteristic mistake associated with the incomprehensible physical meaning of the energy of induction of atoma, which they considered the minimum energy of excitation of atoma. 3. The force of interaction between the plates of a capacitor in general cases is determined by the product of the intense field of one of the plates and the charge of the other.

In the absence of a dielectric, the field in the capacitor is $E_1 = U/d$, and the charge on its plates is $q_1 = \varepsilon_0 US/d$, where $d$ is the distance between the plates, $S$ is the area of ​​the plates, $\varepsilon_0$ is the electric constant. Taking into account the fact that the field of one plate is half as much as the field in the capacitor, for the force of interaction between the plates we obtain

$$F_1 = \frac{q_1 E_1}{2} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 SU^2}{2d^2}.$$
