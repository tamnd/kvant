Thus, in Gelfand’s “stone” game, the novice player’s strategy is very simple: he must take stones so that both piles have an even number of stones, and if each pile has an odd number of stones, then this can be done in two ways: either by taking one stone from each pile, or by moving a stone from one pile to another. “But this is exactly what we shouldn’t do,” I said, “so we will play the stones one by one from one pile to another ad infinitum. In this case, you need to take two stones, then the total number of stones in the piles will decrease all the time and the beginner will be able to triumph, if, of course, he makes the right first move - on the field (6, 6) - and then sticks to our strategy.

Thus, we completely understood the “Gelfand game”, and if so, then it became no longer interesting to play it. Another thing is chess! In this issue we offer you several difficult problems in algebra and integer arithmetic.

1. Prove that the number made up of 1980 by two is divisible by 1982. Hint. Use Fermat's Little Theorem: if p is a prime number and a is not divisible by p, then ap^-1 - 1 is divisible by p («Kvant», 1978, No. 10, p. 7 or 1972, No. 10, p. 2).

S. Manukyan

2*. Prove that if m! / n!(m—n)! is divisible by p^r and p is a prime number, then p^r ≤ m. For which m can equality be achieved?

V. Lev

3. About the polynomial 8x^4 + ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d it is known that for all x from the interval [-1; 1] its modulus values ​​do not exceed unity. Prove that this is the Chebyshev polynomial T_4(x) (see table 1 from the article by N. Vasiliev and A. Zelevinsky on p. 12). Note. Consider the values ​​of this polynomial at x=0, ±1/√2, ±1/2, ±1.

O. Astakhov
