This model is close to implementation in a solid body. In a crystal lattice, atoms are arranged in such a way that in a state of equilibrium, the vector sum of the forces acting on each atom from all the others is equal to zero. When atoms deviate from the equilibrium position, tension and repulsion forces arise, which are similar to elastic forces*).

Let's give some ball, for example the first one on the left, an impulse directed along the chain (push it). Then, as in the example with biliary balls, a wave of elastic deformation will run along the chain, which after some time will reach the right end of the chain. Since the last ball is connected to the chain by a spring, it will not be able to bounce off at all. The stretched spring will force it to return back, and then, due to its inertia, the ball will retract the spring. Now the deformation wave will run from right to left. In this case, they say that the wave was reflected from the end of the chain and began to propagate in the opposite direction. For the same reasons, it will be reflected from the opposite end, etc. These reflected waves are the conditions for our consideration, and in order to get rid of them, we will consider an “infinite” (that is, without ends) chain. It can be implemented by closing a chain of a large number of balls into a ring (Fig. 4). Along such an “infinite” chain, the wave of elastic deformation will move in a circle without reflections until it fades.

Let's deflect one of the balls from its equilibrium position (for example, sweep it clockwise) and release it. Then, under the action of the springs connected to it, the position of the ball in space will periodically change. This movement is called oscillatory.

Vibrations play an important role in nature and technology. The pendulum in the clock performs an oscillatory movement; In household electrical appliances, current and voltage fluctuate; the change of day and night, seasons is also an oscillatory process caused by the movement of the Earth. All-rotating mechanisms cause vibrations in the foundation, which must be taken into account during design.

The simplest type of oscillatory motion is harmonic oscillation, when the displacement of a body from the equilibrium position changes over time according to the law

$$\alpha = \alpha_m \sin(2\pi t/T) = \alpha_m \sin 2\pi vt = \alpha_m \sin \omega t,$$

where in the case of an oscillating chain $\alpha$ is the angular deviation of the ball from the equilibrium position. As you can see, the harmonic oscillation is characterized by two quantities (parameters): the maximum deviation (amplitude) $\alpha_m$ and the oscillation period $T$ (the time interval after which the oscillation is completely repeated). The frequency v is equal to the number of oscillations per unit time, the cyclic frequency $\omega = 2\pi v$ is introduced to simplify the mathematical recording of the oscillatory motion, and the value $\varphi = \omega t$, which determines the position of the ball at a given time $t$, is called the oscillation phase.

Let's give an example. Let the ball perform a complete oscillation in time $T = 4$ s; at the initial moment it is in the equilibrium position, and its maximum deviation is $\alpha_m = 0,1$ rad. Then, if it performs a harmonic oscillation, then the dependence of the deviation on time is given by the formula

$$\alpha = 0,1 \sin(\pi t/2).$$
