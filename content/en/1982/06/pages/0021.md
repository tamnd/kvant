Tasks
M746 — M750; F758 — F762

M746. A paper square is folded in half along some straight line l passing through the center into a (non-convex) pentagon. How to draw straight line l in order to:
a) did the resulting pentagon have the largest area?
b) Did it contain a circle with the largest possible radius (Fig. 1)?

K. Wolfson

M747. a) The sum of n numbers is 0, the sum of their models is a. Prove that the difference between the largest and smallest of them is not less than 2a/n.
b) Inside the convex n-gon $A_1A_2...A_n$, a point O is chosen so that the sum of the vectors $\vec{OA}_1 + \vec{OA}_2 + ... + \vec{OA}_n$ is equal to the zero vector, and the sum of their length is equal to d. Prove that the perimeter of this n-gon is not less than 4d/n.
b) Is it possible to improve this estimate (for some n)?

V. Prasolov

M748. a) Is it possible to place a finite number of parabolas on a plane so that their interior areas cover the entire plane? (We call the inner region of a parabola a convex figure whose boundary is this parabola - see Fig. 2)
b) There are several non-intersecting cones located in space. Prove that they cannot be moved so that they cover the entire space. (We here call a cone an unlimited convex figure obtained by rotating a certain angle around its bisector.)

O. Kuzminykh

M749* a) Prove that if $x_1, x_2, x_3$ are positive numbers, then
$$\frac{x_1}{x_2+x_3} + \frac{x_2}{x_3+x_1} + \frac{x_3}{x_1+x_2} \geq \frac{3}{2};$$
Under what conditions does this inequality turn into equality?
