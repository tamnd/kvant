How to make a good drug?

To prepare the drug you will need two glass plates the size of four tetrad sheets and lycopodium. Lycopodium can be bought at the pharmacy, and it is convenient to use emulsified photographic plastic 9x12 cm in size as plates.

Apply a drop of machine or vegetable oil to the surface of a clean plate (you can use grains of fat, margarine, butter or semolina - whatever comes to hand) and spread it with a thin layer. Then, using a clean cloth, gently wipe the lubricated surface until thoroughly cleaned. The thin layer of fat that remains serves as an adhesive base to hold part of the coating. Small amounts of lycopodium (about the size of a thimble) are poured onto the treated surface, the plate is tilted and, tapping along the edge, powder is added to its base. A wide trace remains on the plate in the form of a fairly dense and uniform layer of lycopodium. Then the inclination of the plate is changed and powder references are added in a different direction. This procedure is repeated until all sheets of paper are removed and excess lycopodium is removed by tapping the bottom edge of the plate on the table. A properly filled plate should be neither too dense nor too transparent. In appearance it resembles frosted glass of medium density.

If the purchase turns out to be unsuccessful, it doesn’t matter. It is easy to sterilize and reproduce again. However, it must be handled carefully.

If you want to save the drug for repeated use, proceed as follows. The dusty plate is placed on the table with the cover facing up, a paper frame 0.5-1 cm wide, cut to the size of the plate, is placed on it, covered with a second (clean) plate of the same dimensions and pecked at the edges with black paper. The resulting device can be successfully used at any time when there is a desire to use a red diffraction pattern or show it to friends.

Diffractionvents - an impressive picture

If you look through a dusty plate at some bright light source of not very large angular dimensions, then you can observe the diffraction vents described above (Fig. 5). In this case, no optical instruments are needed: the role of the object is played by the lens of the eye. The diffraction pattern is created on the retina of the eyes, but is perceived by us in the plane in which the light source is visible.

The source may be the disk of the Moon in a clear night sky, observed from the street or through a room window
