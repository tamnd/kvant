⟦folio 21⟧

We have been publishing Kvant’s contest problems every month from the very first issue of our magazine. The problems are nonstandard ones, but their solution requires no information outside the scope of the USSR secondary school syllabus. The more difficult problems are marked with a star (*). After the statement of the problem, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. It goes without saying that not all these problems are first publications.

The solutions of problems from this issue (in Russian or in English) may be posted no later than August 31, 1982 to the following address: USSR, Moscow, 117071, Moscow.

F761. The circuit is assembled from a battery, two identical amperes and two identical voltmeters (Fig. 6). The amperes $A_1$ and $A_2$ show the correspondence of $I_1=1,1$ mA, $I_2=0,9$ mA; voltmeter $B_2$ shows $U_2=0,25$ Q. What does voltmeter $B_1$ show? What is the battery voltage?

Z. Rafailov

F762. figure 7 shows the voltmeter characteristic of an incandescent lamp, the rated voltage of which is $U_H=220$ V, the rated power is $P_H=100$ W. The lamp will be connected to the AC mains (220 V, 50 Hz) in series

Fig. 7.

with capacitor capacity $C=10$ µF. Determine the current in the lamp and the voltage across it. Assume that during the period of constant voltage the temperature of the filament remains practically unchanged.

A. Zilberman

M746. A paper square is folded in half along a line $l$, passing through its centre, into a (non-convex) nine-sided polygon. How should the line $l$ be chosen in order to:
a) make the area of the polygon maximal?
b)* construct a circle of maximal radius contained in the polygon (see figure Fig. 1)?

K. Vulfson

M747. a) The sum of $n$ numbers is zero, the sum of their absolute values is $a$. Prove that the difference between the largest and smallest number is no less than $2a/n$.
b)* The point $O$ is chosen inside the polygon $A_1A_2...A_n$ so that the sum of vectors $\overrightarrow{OA_1} + \overrightarrow{OA_2} + ... + \overrightarrow{OA_n}$ is the zero vector and the sum of their lengths is $d$. Prove that the polygon’s perimeter is no less than $4d/n$.
c)* Can this estimate be improved (for certain $n$)?

V. Prasolov

M748. a) Can a finite number of parabolas be placed on the plane so that their inner domains cover the entire plane? (The inner domain of a parabola is the convex figure bounded by it — see figure Fig. 2).
b)* Several non-intersecting cones are placed in space. Show that they cannot be moved so as to cover the entire space. (By a cone here we mean the unbounded convex figure obtained by rotating an angle about its bisector).

O. Kuzminykh
