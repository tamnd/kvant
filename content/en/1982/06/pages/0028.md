6) We will also build the sequence $(a_n)$ in batches. The $m$th packet $(m=1, 2, ...)$ will contain 16 numbers:
$$15 \cdot 361^{m-1}, 16 \cdot 361^{m-1}, ..., 30 \cdot 361^{m-1}.$$
Thus, $a_n=361^{m-1}(14+r)$, where $m$ and $r$ are selected from the condition $n=16(m-1)+r$, $m=1, 2, ..., r=1, 2, ..., 16$.
Note that the sum of all numbers in the packs from the first to the $(m-1)$th is equal to
$(15+16+...+30)+(15 \cdot 361+...+30 \cdot 361)+...+
+(15 \cdot 361^{m-2}+...+30 \cdot 361^{m-2})=
=360+360 \cdot 361+...+360 \cdot 361^{m-2}=361^{m-1}-1.$
Let us assume that some number $a$ from the $m$ pack is representable as a sum $b_1+...+b_k$ of several smaller terms $b_1, ..., b_k$ of our sequence. Since $a>361^{m-1}$, among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$ there is at least one from the $m$ pack. Let's consider two cases.
$1^\circ$. Among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$exactly one is from the $m$th pack; we can consider this to be $b_1$. But the equality $a-b_1=b_2+...+b_k$ is impossible, since, on the one hand, $a-b_1$, as the difference of numbers from the $m$-th pack, is not less than $361^{m-1}$, and on the other hand, $b_2+...+b_k$, as the sum of numbers from packs with numbers less than $m$, does not exceed $361^{m-1}-1$.
$2^\circ$. Among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$, more than one is in the $m$-th packet. Then the equality $a=b_1+...+b_k$ is impossible, since the sum of any two different numbers of the $m$th pack is already greater than any third number of this pack.
Thus, none of the numbers $a_n$ can be represented as the sum of other terms of the constructed sequence.
Since $361^{m-1} \leq (1,5)^{16(m-1)}$ and $14+r \leq 10 \cdot (1,5)^r(r=1, ..., 16)$, we get
$$a_n=361^{m-1}(14+r) \leq (1,5)^{16(m-1)} \cdot 10 \cdot (1,5)^r=10 \cdot (1,5)^n$$ — conditions 6) are met.
c), d). Let us construct a sequence $(a_n)$, none of whose terms is equal to the sum of several others, such that for all $n$ there are simultaneously $a_n \leq n^{10}$ and $a_n \leq 100n^{7/2}$.
First let's set $b_m=10^{5m-2}$ for all natural $m \geq 2$, that is, $b_2=10$, $b_3=10000$, $b_4$ equals one followed by 25 zeros, etc.
The sequence $(a_n)$ will again be built “in batches”. Let's take the first pack of states from one number - one. As the $m$th pack for $m \geq 2$, we take an arithmetic progression with the first term $2b_m+1$, the difference $2b_m$ and the number of terms equal to $b_m^2/2$:
$$2b_m+1, 4b_m+1, ..., b_m^3+1.$$
Let us estimate the sum of the numbers of the $m$-th packet
$$\frac{(2b_m+1)+(b_m^3+1)}{2} \cdot \frac{b_m^2}{2} =
= \frac{b_m^5+2b_m^3+2b_m^2}{4} < \frac{b_m^5}{2} < b_m^5-b_m=b_m+1-b_m.$$
Thus, the sum of numbers in packets from 1st to $m$th inclusive is less
$$1+(b_3-b_2)+...+(b_{m+1}-b_m)=b_{m+1}-b_2+1<b_{m+1}.$$
Let us assume that some number $a$ from the $m$-th pack ($m \geq 2$) is representable as the sum of several other terms of the constructed sequence. Since the sum of all terms of the sequence there are several numbers from the $m$-th pack; let's denote them $c_1, ..., c_k$. We denote the remaining terms of our sum by $d_1, ..., d_l$:

$$a=c_1+...+c_k+d_1+...+d_l.$$
