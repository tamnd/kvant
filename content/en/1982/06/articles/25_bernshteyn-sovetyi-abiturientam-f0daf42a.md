⟦folio 53⟧

"Adviсe"
applicants

Admission to a university depends on two events: the first is the choice of an institute, the second is passing the entrance exams.

Choosing an Institute

Here we should look for the golden mean between the possibility of fulfilling a person and the possibility of coincidence. Let's look at an example where they seem to contradict each other.

Your ability to read poetry in the manner of Kachalov and speak in the voice of Papanov clearly points the way to the theatrical stage. And while reading Zhukovsky’s ballad, you imagine the wing of an airplane and the formula of another Zhukovsky. In general, you enjoy adding, subtracting, multiplying, dividing, rooting, logarithms, and calculating percentages.

In this case, we can recommend an institute that is ready to specialize in accounting for cultural and educational institutions.

The choice of a university can be made based on a public opinion poll - on the scale of a family, class, school, etc. Moreover, if you are equally interested in everything, then go to the institute that is recommended by the majority of respondents, and if everything is equally interesting, then do not prove yourself by passing the entrance exams.

Exams

When taking an exam, the situation is fundamentally affected.

1. The examinee and the examiner know the question well. This case is encountered quite rarely, so its consideration is not interesting. The examinee tells the story, the examiner tells the story, and they are happy with themselves and each other.

2. The examinee and the examiner do not know the question well. What the applicant will say in this case is not significant. It’s just important not to deviate from the topic. When answering, you must carefully monitor the examiner's facial expression. The answer should be completed a moment before understanding or, more accurately, interest flashes in his eyes.

When answering, you should not refute well-known truths. For example, there is no need to insist for long that the sum of the angles of a triangle is greater than 180°. After a short discussion, agree with the examiner’s opinion. And think three times before saying that you mean a triangle on a sphere, as the examiner may be interested in spherical geometry.

When answering additional questions, be careful with the phrase: “We don’t have this in the textbook.” The examiner can proofread the textbook and you are at a disadvantage - the examiner can use the textbook, but you cannot.

3. The examinee knows the question well, but the examiner knows it poorly. Listen is encountered so rarely that it still awaits its investigation. The applicant can be recommended to tell only the most part of the day, without going into details, so as not to instill in the examiner the set of dislikes.

4. The examinee knows the question poorly, but the examiner knows it well. The most common occurrence is one that is rare and still awaits research. The applicant can be recommended to tell only the most part of the day, without going into details, so as not to instill in the examiner the set of dislikes.

If your goal is a C, then try to convince the examiner that the question asked is your favorite and you know so much that “it’s a pity for the dear examiner’s precious time.” In addition, it “prevents you from talking about your joy that during the advanced exams you had the opportunity to talk with a specialist on your favorite topic.” It is best to force the examiner to speak on this issue, and in this case you only need to enthusiastically assent.

If you fail to convince the examiner that you are interested in the data, try to demote him. But there is no need to cry - the examiner has never seen anything like this. You can say that your grandfather, grandfather, grandmother, father, mother and two uncles graduated from this institute (here you can show family photos and yours as a child), that the whole family should gather now and leave and give your high marks. The following phrase leads to a good effect: “You can’t give me a bad mark in the very audience in which my grandparents met!”

Getting four in this situation is much more difficult than getting three. If the examiner wants to give you a two or three, ask for an additional question and think for a long time. When the examiner comes to you again with the question “So how?” - raise your hand and say with regret (definitely with regret!): “Okay, put it at four.” Sometimes they put it on.

In the situation under consideration, you can rarely get an A and only on the first exam. Magically, the phrase sounds like this: “I know that I still won’t pass the competition, but I don’t want shame. Let me have at least one peterka.” An attempt at the next exam to convince the examiner that it is not worthwhile to give a mark other than 5, after “Your colleague highly appreciated my knowledge in the previous exam,” does not lead to the desired effect.

Thus, we have considered all possible situations. Your task now is only to determine which particular equation applies to your situation. By the way, if you can quickly and accurately determine the level of knowledge of the examiner, perhaps it would be better for you to become a psychologist or criminologist.

We hope that upon admission to the university you will be guided by two events: the first is the choice of an institute, the second is passing the entrance exams.
