M707. Each of the students in the class studies in no more than two circles, and for any pair of students there is a circle in which they study together. Prove that there is a circle where at least 2/3 of this class attends.

If the whole class is engaged in one of the circles, then everything is proven. Let the entire class not study in any of the circles. Let's consider one of the circles (let's call it circle No. 1) and one of the students involved in this circle (let's call him student A). Since the entire class is not occupied in circle No. 1, there will be a student B who is not engaged in this circle. By condition, there is circle No. 2, where students A and B study together. Since this circle does not cover the entire class, there is student C, who is not included. But student A studies only in circles No. 1 and No. 2, so student C studies together with student A in circle No. 1. Then there should be a circle No. 3, where students B and C study together. Consider any other student D (if there are other students in the class besides students A, B and C). If student D had not studied in either circle No. 1 or circle No. 2, then there would have been no common circle for him with student A. If D had not studied in circles No. 1 and No. 3, then he would not have studied with student C. Finally, if D had not studied in circles No. 2 and No. 3, then he would not have had a common circle with student B. Thus, each student in the class must be in two of the three circles considered. Denoting by $y_1, y_2$ and $y_3$ the number of students in circles No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3, respectively, we obtain $y_1 + y_2 + y_3 = 2y$, where $y$ is the number of all students in the class. It is clear that at least one of the numbers $y_1, y_2, y_3$ must be at least $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 2y$.

F719.*) A small heavy ball of mass m, having a charge q, is given an initial velocity v₀ directed vertically upward. The ball is in a uniform horizontal electric field, the direction of which is equal to E. Neglecting air resistance and the free pressure acceleration depending on height, determine the minimum speed of the ball during its movement.

*) The solution problem F718 is in the article by S. Krotov on p. 30.

The ball is acted upon by two constant forces: the gravity force mg and the force qE from the power field. Therefore, the ball will move with constant acceleration

$$\vec{a} = \vec{g} + \frac{q}{m} \vec{E}.$$

The vector $\vec{a}$ makes an angle α with the horizon, and

$$tg \alpha = \frac{g}{Eq/m} = \frac{mg}{Eq}.$$

The speed of the ball will change all the time both in magnitude and direction. After time t after the start of movement, the speed module

$$v_t = \sqrt{\left(\frac{qE}{m} t\right)^2 + \left(v_0 - gt\right)^2};$$

the vector $\vec{v}_t$ makes an angle β_t with the vertical such that

$$tg \beta_t = qEt/m (v_0 - gt).$$

The speed of the ball will be minimal at that moment of time τ when the projection of acceleration $\vec{a}$ onto the direction of speed $\vec{v}_τ$ will be equal to zero, that is, when the vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{v}_τ$ will be redistributed. The angle that the vector $\vec{v}_τ$ makes with the vertical at this moment will be equal to α. From the condition tg α = tan β_t we find τ:

$$\tau = \frac{m^2 g v_0}{m^2 g^2 + q^2 E^2}.$$

The minimum speed of the ball during movement is equal in magnitude

$$v_{\min} = v_{\tau} = v_0 \frac{qE}{\sqrt{q^2 E^2 + m^2 g^2}}.$$

Let $Q$ be the charge on the surface of the initial (never folded) triangle of area $S$, $A_0$ be the electrostatic energy that this charge possesses. This energy consists of the electrostatic energies of the pairwise interactions of individual charged sections of the triangle.

Let us consider two small sections $m$ and $n$ of the triangle located at a distance $r$ from each other (see Fig. 1). Let $s$ be the area of ​​each plot; the charge of each site is obviously $q = \frac{Q}{S}$ s. Electrostatic energy of interaction of these areas

$$a_0 \sim q^2 / r = Q^2 \frac{s}{Sr}.$$

Let's see how this energy changes after the first addition of the triangle. From figure 2 it is clear that on the folded triangle the sections similar to $m$ and $n$ are sections $m_1$ and $n_1$. The charge on the folded triangle will still be $Q$, the area of ​​the triangle will become $S_1 = S / 2$, the distance between the sites, as is easy to understand, will become $r_1 = r / \sqrt{2}$, and their areas will be $s_1 = s / 2$. Thus, the interaction energy of the sites will become

$$a_1 \sim q^2 / r_1 = Q^2 \frac{s_1}{Sr_1} = a_0 \sqrt{2}.$$

Since the same considerations apply to any pair of pads, the electrostatic energy of the folded triangle will become

$$A_1 = A_0 \sqrt{2}.$$

The change in energy $\Delta A_1 = A_1 - A_0$ is equal to the work $A$ expended in folding the triangle, that is

$$A_1 - A_0 = A_0 (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A$$

When the triangle is folded again, its electrostatic energy will become

$$A_2 = A_1 \sqrt{2}.$$

The change in energy $\Delta A_2 = A_2 - A_1$ is equal to the work $A_x$, which is three areas when added. Thus,

$$A_x = A_2 - A_1 = A (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A_0 \sqrt{2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A \sqrt{2}.$$
