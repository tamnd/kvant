The cube colors the plane

Let us now turn to cubes with multi-colored faces. We will assume that each face of the cube surrounds, in accordance with the color, the grid field with which it comes into contact. The cube can only be used once on each square. Let us now analyze the following problem: can a cube with multi-colored edges be used by a kantoman to color a 4x4 grid so that there are no two unambiguous adjacent squares on it?

The features of the situations under consideration are easiest to identify experimentally, by first considering grids of smaller sizes.

When traversing a 2x2 grid, the first and last squares will always be both adjacent and single-valued. This means that when turning over you cannot make “sharper” turns like RNKL (Fig. 1). But without such “steep” turning points, it is impossible to bypass the 3x3 grid, as well as the 4x4 grid. This means that the required coloring cannot be obtained.
