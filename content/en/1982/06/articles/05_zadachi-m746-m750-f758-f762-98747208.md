Tasks
M746 — M750; F758 — F762

M746. A paper square is folded in half along some straight line l passing through the center into a (non-convex) pentagon. How to draw straight line l in order to:
a) did the resulting pentagon have the largest area?
b) Did it contain a circle with the largest possible radius (Fig. 1)?

K. Wolfson

M747. a) The sum of n numbers is 0, the sum of their models is a. Prove that the difference between the largest and smallest of them is not less than 2a/n.
b) Inside the convex n-gon $A_1A_2...A_n$, a point O is chosen so that the sum of the vectors $\vec{OA}_1 + \vec{OA}_2 + ... + \vec{OA}_n$ is equal to the zero vector, and the sum of their length is equal to d. Prove that the perimeter of this n-gon is not less than 4d/n.
b) Is it possible to improve this estimate (for some n)?

V. Prasolov

M748. a) Is it possible to place a finite number of parabolas on a plane so that their interior areas cover the entire plane? (We call the inner region of a parabola a convex figure whose boundary is this parabola - see Fig. 2)
b) There are several non-intersecting cones located in space. Prove that they cannot be moved so that they cover the entire space. (We here call a cone an unlimited convex figure obtained by rotating a certain angle around its bisector.)

O. Kuzminykh

M749* a) Prove that if $x_1, x_2, x_3$ are positive numbers, then
$$\frac{x_1}{x_2+x_3} + \frac{x_2}{x_3+x_1} + \frac{x_3}{x_1+x_2} \geq \frac{3}{2};$$
Under what conditions does this inequality turn into equality?

$$\frac{x_1}{x_2+x_n} + \frac{x_2}{x_3+x_1} + \ldots + \frac{x_{n-1}}{x_n+x_{n-2}} + \frac{x_n}{x_1+x_{n-1}} \geq 2,$$

M750. Prove that, no matter how you color the cells of an infinite sheet of checkered paper in $N$ colors, there is
a) a rectangle, the vertices of which lie in the centers of cells of the same color (and the sides run parallel to the lines of the grid - along vertical and horizontal straight lines - Fig. 3, a);
b) $l$ horizontal and $m$ vertical lines that intersect at the centers of $lm$ cells of the same color ($l$ and $m$ are any natural numbers - Fig. 3, b);
c) an isosceles right triangle, the vertices of which are the centers of cells of the same color, at $N=2$ (Fig. 3, θ);
d)* the same for $N=3$.

S. Bespamyatnykh

F758. The rigid workpiece is clamped between two parallel guides moving in the horizontal direction at speeds $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ (Fig. 4). At some point in time, the points of contact of the workpiece with the guides lie on a straight line perpendicular to the vectors $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$. Which points of the workpiece have velocities at this moment equal in absolute value to $v_1$ and $v_2$?
S. Krotov

F759. A weightless rod of length $l$ with a small load of mass $m$ at the end of the hinge is fixed at point $A$ (Fig. 5) and is in a strictly vertical position, touching the body of mass $M$. With a little push the system starts to move. At what ratio between $m$ and $M$ will the rod at the moment of “separation” from the body $M$ make an angle $\alpha=\pi/6$ with the horizon? What will be the speed of the body $M$ at this moment? Let's make a claim.

K. Serdeev

F760. A steel ball of mass $m_2=1$ g is deposited into a heat-filling vessel containing $m_1=20$ g of helium at a speed of $v=100$ m/s. Find the change in temperature into the vessel. The impacts of the ball on the walls of the container and the impacts of atoms on the ball are considered to be absolutely elastic.

R. Aleksadrov

M746. A paper square is folded in half along a line $l$, passing through its centre, into a (non-convex) nine-sided polygon. How should the line $l$ be chosen in order to:
a) make the area of the polygon maximal?
b)* construct a circle of maximal radius contained in the polygon (see figure Fig. 1)?

K. Vulfson

M747. a) The sum of $n$ numbers is zero, the sum of their absolute values is $a$. Prove that the difference between the largest and smallest number is no less than $2a/n$.
b)* The point $O$ is chosen inside the polygon $A_1A_2...A_n$ so that the sum of vectors $\overrightarrow{OA_1} + \overrightarrow{OA_2} + ... + \overrightarrow{OA_n}$ is the zero vector and the sum of their lengths is $d$. Prove that the polygon’s perimeter is no less than $4d/n$.
c)* Can this estimate be improved (for certain $n$)?

V. Prasolov

M748. a) Can a finite number of parabolas be placed on the plane so that their inner domains cover the entire plane? (The inner domain of a parabola is the convex figure bounded by it — see figure Fig. 2).
b)* Several non-intersecting cones are placed in space. Show that they cannot be moved so as to cover the entire space. (By a cone here we mean the unbounded convex figure obtained by rotating an angle about its bisector).

O. Kuzminykh
