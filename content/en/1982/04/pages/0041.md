Do I need to look for ODZ?

The traditional type of problems on university entrance exams are equations, inequalities, systems (equations or inequalities).

Numerous manuals for schoolchildren and applicants, when solving problems, often give an unconditional recommendation: “First we look for ODZ!” Apparently, an echo of this recommendation is the artistic recognition “Remember ODZ!”, which hung in one of the Leningrad vocational schools.

First of all, we note that the abbreviation ODZ is not in current school textbooks. The phrase “Areas of Acceptable Values” means “areas of definition.”

The OD of an equation or inequality is, by definition, the intersection of the OD of its left and right sides.

So, is it necessary to start solving an equation (inequality, system) with the Japanese ODZ? Is it necessary, when solving an equation (inequality, system), to look for the ODZ, especially starting the solution with the Japanese ODZ? as a rule, that is, in most of the equations (inequalities, systems) usually proposed in entrance exams, there is no need to start the solution with the Japanese ODZ; Sometimes, in some specially selected or more difficult equations (inequalities, systems), it is useful to start the solution with the Japanese ODZ.

As a rule, there is no need

$$\sqrt{x^2-3x+4}=1-3x. \tag{1}$$

Equation (1) is equivalent to the system

$$\begin{cases} x^2-3x+4=(1-3x)^2 \\ 1-3x\geq 0, \end{cases}$$

which is no longer difficult to solve. (By the way, the inequality $x^2-3x+4\geq 0$, which determines the ODZ of equation (1), follows from the equation included in system (2).) You can also, if desired, move from equation (1) to the derived*) equation $x^2-3x+4=(1-3x)^2$ and check the found roots. (Having solved together equations (1) and system (2) that is equivalent to it, there is, of course, no need to do a check.) This example: goes not to the equivalent, but to the inferential one, and to do the check must be kept in mind in the other examples under consideration.

It is easy to prove that any equation of the form

$$\sqrt{f(x)}=g(x) \tag{3}$$

equivalent to the system

$$\begin{cases} f(x)=[g(x)]^2 \\ g(x)\geq 0. \end{cases}$$

Indeed. If $x_0$ is the root of equation (3), that is, the numerical equation $\sqrt{f(x_0)}=g(x_0)$ is valid, then $f(x_0)=[g(x_0)]^2$ and $g(x_0)\geq 0$; This means that $x_0$ is a solution to system (4). If, conversely, $x_0$ is a solution to system (4), that is, the numerical equation $f(x_0)=[g(x_0)]^2$ and $g(x_0)\geq 0$ are valid, then $\sqrt{f(x_0)}=\sqrt{[g(x_0)]^2}=|g(x_0)|$; since $g(x_0)\geq 0$, $|g(x_0)|=g(x_0)$; hence, $x_0$ is the root of equation (3).

In equation (1), the search for ODZ leads to an unnecessary, but easily performed calculation. However, sometimes insurmountable difficulties arise for applicants who are accustomed to always looking for educational qualifications.
