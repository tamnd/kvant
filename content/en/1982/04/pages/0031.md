M697. Let's call the potbelliness of a rectangle the ratio of its smaller side to the larger one (the potbelliness of a square is 1). Prove that, no matter how you cut the square into rectangles, the sum of their bellies will be at least 1.

We assume that the length of the side of the square is equal to 1. Let $a_k \times b_k$ be the dimensions of the rectangles of the partition, and $a_k \geq b_k$ ($k=1, 2, \dots, n$). Since $a_k \leq 1$ for all $k$,
$$\frac{b_1}{a_1} + \frac{b_2}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{b_n}{a_n} \geq a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \dots + a_n b_n = 1$$
(on the right side of the square).

Note that equality in the written inequality is possible only if all $a_k = 1$ ($k=1, 2, \dots, n$), that is, if the square is divided into strips by segments parallel to its sides.

It is interesting to find out what estimate of the sum of potbellies of the rectangles of the partition can be given for a given rectangle of area 1 and potbellies $k$.
