figure shows the process of solving the “Coiff the Hedgehog” puzzle, which is described in this issue. The hedgehog is made up of eight wooden pentagons, two of whose borders are painted in the color of the head, the other two in yellow. The pentagons are glued together with rubber bands, allowing one half of the hedgehog to be rotated (in different planes) relative to the other. Initially, the hedgehog appears as a blue, slightly flattened octaed, but after several randomly selected turns, some of the iron edges come out and the sharp corners of the pentahedrons begin to stick out like thorns. In the center such a “rubbed” hedgehog is shown, and around it are its other positions; adjacent ones are obtained from each other by rotating by 90° (the direction of rotation is indicated by a black arrow). If we move from the center, following the gray arrows, we will see how the hedgehog’s spines are gradually removed and a “combed” hedgehog is obtained: a blue polyhedron on the left in the middle row.
