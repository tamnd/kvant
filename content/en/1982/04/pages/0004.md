⟦folio 2⟧

Cosmic spring of humanity

For all of us, April was canceled by two outstanding events - the birthday of V.I. Lenin and the first manned flight into space. On April 12, 1961, the Vostok spacecraft, piloted by Yu. A. Gagarin, made the world's first orbital space flight, which lasted 108 minutes. 21 years separate us from this outstanding event in human history, and each of them is canceled out by remarkable successes on the path to space.

Our country has launched more than 1,300 artificial Earth satellites and several dozen manned and automatic spacecraft. The main direction in the development of space research during the All-Misdes years was the creation of orbital scientific stations “Salyut”. According to L.I. Brezhnev, “Soviet science considers the creation of orbital stations with rotating crews as the main path of man into space.”

The orbital scientific station Salyut-6 has been successfully operating in near-Earth space for more than four years. Initially it was assumed that it would be able to function successfully for a year and a half. However, to date this period has been exceeded almost three times. The duration of the main experiments at the Salyut-6 station was constantly increasing - 96, 140, 175, 185 days! The last rekord belongs to cosmonaut Leonid Ivanovich Popov and Valery Viktorovich Ryumin.

At the Salyut-6 station, such complex scientific instruments as the BST-1 submillimeter telephone and the KRT-10 radio telescope with an antenna diameter of 10 m were tested for the first time.

The achievements of astronautics are the property of all people and contribute to the strengthening of peace and the development of progress. Therefore, our country will make a huge contribution to the development of international cooperation in space. Cosmonauts from Czechoslovakia, Poland, the German Democratic Republic, Bulgaria, Hungary, Romania, Vietnam, Cuba and Mongolia worked together with the Soviet cosmonauts on Salyut-6. Preparations for a joint Soviet-French space flight are progressing successfully; Soviet-Indian cooperation in the field of space research is developing.

Soviet space research is paving the way for humans to create specialized orbital stations designed to develop space technology using non-isosomation and deep vacuum, as well as special medical and biological laboratories. Even heavier and longer-lasting orbital stations with advanced scientific equipment for a variety of astrophysical research will appear. The development of civilization will require the creation of orbital space production, powerful power plants, international scientific settlements, illuminating the closest satellite of the Earth - the Moon.

Space awaits you too, our dear readers. The space profession is not easy; it requires deep scientific training and perfect health care. And anyone who would like to master it must prepare for this during his school years.
