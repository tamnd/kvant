F712. A small ball moves at a constant speed $\vec{v}_0$ along a smooth horizontal surface and falls at point $A$ into a cylindrical vertical well of lubbin $H$ and radius $R$. The velocity vector of the ball makes an angle $\alpha$ with the diameter of the well drawn to point $A$ (Fig. 1). Under what correspondence between $\vec{v}_0$, $H$, $R$ and $\alpha$ can the ball, after an elastic collision with the walls and bottom, “get out” of the well?

At figure 1 there is a top view of the trajectory of the ball.

Since the collisions of the ball with the wall and bottom of the well are elastic, the absolute value of the horizontal projection of the ball’s velocity remains unchanged and equal to $v_0$. The horizontal distances between the points at which two successive collisions will occur are equal to $|AA_1| = |A_1A_2| = |A_2A_3| = \dots = 2R$ cos $\alpha$. The time between two successive collisions of the ball with the wall of the well is $t_1 = 2R \cos \alpha/v_0$.

The vertical projection of the ball's velocity does not change when it hits a wall, but when it hits the bottom it changes sign to the opposite. The absolute value of the vertical projection at the first impact with the bottom is $\sqrt{2gH}$; the time of movement from the top of the well to the bottom is $t_2 = \sqrt{2H/g}$.

figure 2 shows the vertical development of the polyhedron $A_1A_2A_3\dots$. The traces of the ball's trajectory on such a scan are paraboles (entire paraboles are traces of the trajectory between successive impacts on the bottom). It is clear that the ball will be able to “choose” from the well if the moment of maximum rise along the parabola coincides with the moment of impact with the wall (that is, at the moment of maximum rise, the ball is at point $A_n$ on the boundary of the well). This means that the tenses $t_1$ and $t_2$ must be related.
