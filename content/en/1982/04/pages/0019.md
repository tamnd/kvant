⟦folio 17⟧

K. Anderson near his installation.

Are traces III and IV with “wrong” curvature? There were approximately the same number of them in photographs as there were electronic ones.

The first proposal, and the weights were natural, was that these were traces of protons. A different sign of the charge is a different sign of the curvature of the trajectory. But a study of photographs led to the conclusion that the ability of positive particles to become ionized is practically the same as that of electrons. If we assume that these are protons, then their speed should be as high as the speed of electrons (see (1)), that is, ~c; the momentum of such protons is 10³ times greater than the momentum of electrons, and therefore, the radii of curvature of their trace trajectories in a magnetic field should be a thousand times greater than that of electrons (see (2)).

There is only one conclusion - not protons.

The second proposal was that the “irregular particles” were electrons, but moving in the opposite direction. Then their trajectory will have the opposite curvature. To test this proposal, Anderson came up with a poignant experiment. He placed a lead plate in the middle of the chamber. Passing through it, the particle loses energy, slows down, and the magnetic field bends its trajectory more strongly. From the photograph (Fig. 3) you can determine the direction of motion of the particle, and knowing that the field is directed “away from us”, you can make sure that it has a positive charge.

And so in 1932 Anderson came to the conclusion that the new particles are nothing more than “positive electrons” with mass $m_e$ and charge $e^+$. At first this proposal was made rather timidly. But it was soon convincingly confirmed in the experiments of Anderson himself, as well as Blackett and Occalini. In addition, these scientists showed that the appearance of positrons in the chamber is due to cosmic radiation leaks.

The most amazing thing in this story was not even the discovery of a new particle, although in physics, especially at that time, this was of paramount importance. The amazing thing was that a particle was discovered that was presented in 1931, the existence of which I did not believe in - this idea was too unteachable.
