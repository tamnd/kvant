Divisible by $q$; of the numbers that are multiples of $q$, exactly $\left[\frac{l}{q^2}\right]$ is divisible by $q^2$; of numbers that are multiples of $q^2$, exactly $\left[\frac{l}{q^3}\right]$ is divisible by $q^3$, etc.; therefore

$$t = \left( \left[\frac{l}{q}\right] - \left[\frac{l}{q^2}\right] \right) + 2 \left( \left[\frac{l}{q^2}\right] - \left[\frac{l}{q^3}\right] \right) + 3 \left( \left[\frac{l}{q^3}\right] - \left[\frac{l}{q^4}\right] \right) + \ldots = \left[\frac{l}{q}\right] + \left[\frac{l}{q^2}\right] + \left[\frac{l}{q^3}\right] + \ldots$$

(obviously, all the terms here, starting with some, are zeros). Therefore, if $s$ is the exponent in which $p$ is included in $\frac{m!}{n!(m-n)!}$, then $s = \sum_k \left( \left[\frac{m}{p^k}\right] - \left[\frac{n}{p^k}\right] - \left[\frac{m-n}{p^k}\right] \right)$, and at $k > \log_p m$ the terms vanish, and at $k \leq \log_p m$ they do not exceed 1, or are integers less than 2:

$$\left[\frac{m}{p^k}\right] - \left[\frac{n}{p^k}\right] - \left[\frac{m-n}{p^k}\right] = \left\{ \frac{n}{p^k} \right\} + \left\{ \frac{m-n}{p^k} \right\} - \left\{ \frac{m}{p^k} \right\} < 2.$$

Due to this, $s$ does not exceed the number of terms, that is, $s \leq \log_p m$. Therefore $r \leq s \leq \log_p m$, that is $p^r \leq m$.

The equality $p^r = m$ is achieved for all $m$ that are powers of a prime number.
