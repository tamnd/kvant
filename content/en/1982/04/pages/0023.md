⟦folio 21⟧

First of all, a humorous poem by M. V. Lomonosov:

Two Astronomers listened together at a feast
And they argued a lot among themselves in the heat.
One repeated: The Earth, spinning, moves around the circle of the Sun;
Another is that the Sun carries all the planets with it:
One was Copernicus, the other was Ptolemy.
Here the cook settled the dispute with his own mouth.

The owner asked: “Do you know the course of the stars?
Tell me, how do you reason about this doubt?”
He gave the following answer: “What is Copernicus right about that?
I will prove the truth, I have never been to the Sun,
Who has seen the simpleton of cooks like this?
Who would turn the hearth around the roaster?

Pushkin, as always, was amazed by the points and subtleties in the famous poem “Movement”. Think again about the content of Pushkin’s masterpiece:

There is no movement, said the bearded sage.
The other fell silent and began to walk in front of him,
He could not have objected more strongly;
Everyone praised the answer to plan.

But, gentlemen, this is a funny case
Another example comes to mind:
After all, every day the sun walks before us,
However, stubborn Galileo was right.

Poztov is naturally struck by the finiteness of the speed of light and the effects associated with it. Fet in his poem “To Faded Stars” writes:

Maybe you are not under those
lights:
The ancient era has extinguished you,—
So even after death I will fly to you
poems,
To the ghosts of the stars, I will be the ghost of a sigh!

Stepan Shipchev plays on the same theme differently in his poem “Star Light”:

Evening star light
Twinkles in the sky.
The gardens were thinking,
And I felt sad.
He's here at my window
Stars are a distant light,

Although he was kind to me
One hundred and forty thousand years
And you have an hour's drive,
How long does it take to get ready?
But no, to negotiate
Come here now.
