Astroomy: science and its creators

“The paths that people penetrate into the fate of celestial phenomena seem to me almost as amazing as these phenomena themselves” - these words, belonging to one of the greatest astronomers Johannes Kepler, serve as an epigraph in D. Herrmann’s book “Discoverers of the Sky” *). The book deals with the history of the accumulation and development of astronomical knowledge; it talks about discoveries and misconceptions, about struggles and struggles of ideas, about the happy and bitter destinies of the creators of science.

Astroomy is one of the oldest, perhaps the most ancient science. Its origins go back to those immemorial times, when a person first asked himself the question: what is it - the starry sky spread above him? And one more thing: where does the sun come from in the morning and where does it go? Why does the moon change its appearance from night to night, gradually turning from a barely noticeable heart into a bright silver disk, and then again becoming damaged, as if it were being devoured by some miracle? Maybe unearthly creatures and unknown forces really are involved in this? And so myths arise, deities are born who demand worship and who must be crushed, or healthy people and a good harvest, a successful hunt and a safe return from a campaign depend on them. In turn, myths give names to many stars, constellations, and nebulae.

This is a naive appeal to the stars, like the attempts of the priests to interpret

The author of the book considers the first and most important step towards the establishment of astronomy as a science to be the systematic compilation of lists of constellations visible at night; also list appeared in Ancient Egypt around 2000 BC. e. Thus, the history of the development of astronomy goes back almost four thousand years. Long centuries of routine, established ideas were replaced by periods of rapid substitution, revolutionary upheavals, and the breaking of accustomed, established views and concepts. This science is still experiencing such a rise today. He is famous for penetrating into the distant depths of the Universe, discovering pulsars, quasars and other exotic objects, and taking astronomical instruments beyond the Earth - to satellites and space stations.

Of course, in the 200 pages of his book, the author could cover the entire 40-century history of astronomy in an exhaustive manner, but he did the maximum possible, painting a logical, coherent picture of improving ideas about the world, showing the relationship of these ideas with the socio-economic development of man. As for the Smichtians, “discoverers of the sky,” the name index to the book contains more than 250 names of scientists who made a significant contribution to astronomy. The list may not be exhaustive, but it is quite complete. And most importantly, the narration is conducted in the style of an unforced story, a conversation with the reader. The book does not even have chapter and section numbers, and the titles themselves are non-standard, catchy, and enticing. For example, in the book “Otgaroni of the World to the Universal Law” there are the following sections: “Researcher from Uraniborg” (about Tycho Brahe), “Brage is looking for a compromise solution”, “Geometric World” (about Kepler’s constructions), “Meeting in Prague”, “On the threshold of celestial mechanics”, “The Heretic” (about Giordano Bruno), “The Conflict of Reason and Power” (about Galilee).

It is very difficult to retell the contents of such a book, so we will limit ourselves to a brief overview of it and only go into more detail on some points.

The opening book, “Stars over Babylon,” talks about the prehistory of astronomy: about the observations of the Sun, Moon and stars in Babylon and Egypt, about ancient calendars and the prophecies of priests, about the first observatories and monumental structures like the “Stone Age computing machine”, which made it possible to predict the movement of the Sun and Moon and even, according to scientists, their eclipses.

In the chapter “A Book with Pictures and a System of Wheels” he talks about the emergence of astronomy as a science, the source of which was systematic observations of the position and movement of stars carried out in Ancient Greece, the grouping of stars in a constellation, the compilation of catalogs of stars and maps of the starry sky, etc. - up to attempts to construct a unified picture of the world. The crown of ancient Greek astronomy is the geocentric system of Ptolemy, according to which in the center of the world there are fixed instruments and one’s own eyes. One can only admire the profundity and wisdom of the ancient scientists - Eudoxus, Aristotle, Hipparchus and the same Ptolemy, who built complex trajectories of the movement of celestial bodies in the form of epiicles, eccentrics, geocentric spheres, etc.
