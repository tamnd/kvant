⟦folio none⟧

figure shows a family of lines drawn by a computer using a parametric equation
$$x = F(t, u), y = G(t, u),$$
where $0 \leq t \leq 1$ and $0 \leq u \leq 1$. When $t$ changes from 0 to 1, the point with coordinates $(x, y) = (F(t, 0), G(t, 0))$ (the end of the pen on the plotter) moves along a certain path, describing the upper curve, and the point with coordinates $(F(t, 1), G(t, 1))$ moves along a different path (the curve with two loops at the bottom). By successively giving the parameter $u$ intermediate values ​​returning from 0 to 1, the computer drew 38 intermediate curves with a thinner pen. In general, figure uses a process of warping one path into another; you just need to imagine that the curves change when the parameter $u$ changes, not in jumps, but continuously. You can read about path deformation in L. Pontryagin’s article “The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra,” published in this issue.

Yu. Kotov
