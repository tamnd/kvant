STALAME RECORDS

Any chess match can end in two outcomes: the victory of one of the guys or a draw. Nitsa, in part, is fixed when there is a stalemate on the board. Here we will look at a series of (artificial) shortest games that end in stalemate.

To checkmate your opponent, you need to “lock” one of his kings in check. Two moves may well be enough for this (1. f4 e6 2. g4 Qh4×). For a pat to occur, all pieces on one side must not be able to move (and their king must not be in check). How many moves must be made to “lock” all the pieces of the same color? It turns out, not so much - you can get stalemate already on the tenth move! Szm Loyd came up with the next record partner about a hundred years ago.

1. e3 a5 2. Qh5 Ra6 3. Q:a5 h5 4. Q:c7 Ra6 5. h4 f6 6. Q:d7+Kpf7 7. Q:b7 Qd3 8. Q:b8 Qh7 9. Q:c8 Kg6 10. Qe6. Pat.

Here, all the remaining black pieces on the board are “walled up,” but if you replace the first 2.5 moves with 1. a3 e6 2. a4 Qh4 3. La3 Q:a4 (and then 4. h4 Q:c2, etc.), then the game will end in a stalemate for White.

This idea can be given a slightly different design.

1. c3 d5 2. Qb3 h5 3. Q:b7 Bf5 4. Q:a7 Ch7 5. Q:b8 Ra6 6. Q:c7 Ra6 7. h4 f6 8. Q:d8+

Kf7 9. Qxd5+ Kg6 10. Qe6. Pat. Here on h7 it is not the black queen that is walled up, but the black bishop.

In the next “battle”, Black also stalemates the enemy on the tenth move, but here it is no longer possible to place the color of the pieces - White sets up a similar stalemate only on the 11th move.

1. h4 e5 2. c4 d5 3. Qb3 dc 4. e4 cb 5 ab Q:h4 6. Ra4 Q:h1 7. g4 C:g4 8. Kf3 C:f3 9. Ka3 C:a3 10. Rb4 C:b4. Pat.

We now demand that not a single piece, either white or black, be captured. In how many moves is a stalemate possible in this case?

It seems that the additional condition will greatly delay the opponent, however, oddly enough, the stalemate turns out to be only two moves later.

1. d4 d6 2. Qd2 e5 3. a4 e4 4. Qf4 f5 5. h3 Ce7 6. Qh2 Ce6 7. Ra3 c5 8. Rg3 Qa5+ 9. Kd2 Ch4 10. f3 Cb3 11. d5 e3 12. c4 f4. Pat.

What about the mutual entangling of queens? The game, which ends in stalemate, lasts 19 moves.

1. e4 d5 2. e5 d4 3. c3 f6 4. Qf3 Kpf7 5. Q:b7 Qd5 6. Kpd1 Q:g2 7. Kpc2 Q:f1 8. Q:c8 Q:g1 9. Q:b8 R:b8 10. Rg1 Rb3 11. Rg6 Ra3 12. Rh6 gh 13. ba Kpg7 14. Kpb2 d3 15. e6 a5 16. h4 a4 17. h5 c5 18. f4 c4 19. f5. Pat white and black.

In the last example, both kings ended up symmetrically tucked in the final position. At the same time, the game was asymmetrical (the enemy’s moves were copied either by white or black), and five pieces disappeared from the board. The following stalemate is unique, in which, firstly, only one knight leaves the board, secondly, both kings again find themselves symmetrically stalemate, and thirdly, what is most surprising, all the moves of White and Black are centrally symmetrical.

1. Kf3 Kc6 2. Kc3 Kf6 3. Kb5 Kg4 4. h3 a6 5. Ka7 Kh2 6. K:h2 K:a7 (first and last spreads) 7. g4 b5 8. Cg2 Cb7 9. e4 d5 10. Kpe2 Kpd7! 11. Qg1 Qb8! (the original placements of the queens and queens were not centered)

It is very difficult to prove mathematically strictly that the given stalemates are the fastest, since this requires going through millions of games. Nevertheless, there is no practical doubt that these records will never be surpassed.

figure shows the process of solving the “Coiff the Hedgehog” puzzle, which is described in this issue. The hedgehog is made up of eight wooden pentagons, two of whose borders are painted in the color of the head, the other two in yellow. The pentagons are glued together with rubber bands, allowing one half of the hedgehog to be rotated (in different planes) relative to the other. Initially, the hedgehog appears as a blue, slightly flattened octaed, but after several randomly selected turns, some of the iron edges come out and the sharp corners of the pentahedrons begin to stick out like thorns. In the center such a “rubbed” hedgehog is shown, and around it are its other positions; adjacent ones are obtained from each other by rotating by 90° (the direction of rotation is indicated by a black arrow). If we move from the center, following the gray arrows, we will see how the hedgehog’s spines are gradually removed and a “combed” hedgehog is obtained: a blue polyhedron on the left in the middle row.
