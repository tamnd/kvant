1 (by decision of I. Chasovskikh). All even integers $\alpha$.
Let $2m$ be an even integer. Then we note that the numbers $\alpha$ and $\alpha + 2m$ satisfy (or do not satisfy) the conditions of the simultaneous problem. Indeed, $\left\lfloor k(\beta + 2m)\right\rfloor - \left\lfloor k\beta\right\rfloor = 2mk$, therefore the difference of the sums from the condition for the numbers $\alpha + 2m$ and $\alpha$ will be equal to $2m(1 + 2 + \ldots + n) = mn(n + 1)$, which is divisible by $n$ (for any natural number $n$).
It remains to find all $\alpha \in [0;2)$ that satisfy the conditions. Obviously $\alpha = 0$ is appropriate.
Let us prove that $\alpha \in (0;1)$ is not suitable.
Receives of the form $\left[\frac{1}{n}; \frac{1}{n - 1}\right)$ for $n = 2, 3, \ldots$ cover the interval (0;1), so there is a natural $n \geq 2$ such that $\frac{1}{n} \leq \alpha < \frac{1}{n - 1}$.
