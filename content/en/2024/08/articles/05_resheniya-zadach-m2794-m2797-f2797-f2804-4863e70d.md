The rules do not have a common point, and at the same time, the environments of the lines containing these rays, no two would be parallel or coincide?

Answer: you can.
The idea of ​​the solution will be as follows: we will find a point $A$ such that on any line passing through $A$ there lies at most one rational point (i.e. a point whose both coordinates are rational). Then, by drawing weighted rays from $A$ to all rational points and removing the initial segment from each ray (from $A$ to the corresponding rational point), we obtain the required set of rays (see figure).

Let $I$ be the intersection point of the segment $AD$ and the arc $BPC$ (Fig. 2). Since $DB = DC$, the inscribed angles $DAB$ and $DAC$ subtended by the arcs $DB$ and $DC$ are also equal. This means that $AD$ is the bisector of angle $BAC$.
According to the trefoil theorem, the center of the inscribed circle in triangle $ABC$ lies on the segment $AD$ and is distant $DB$ from the point $D$. This means that this center coincides with the point $I$. It follows that $CI$ is the bisector of angle $ACB$.
On the other hand, since $AD$ is the perpendicular bisector to $PQ$, then $PI = QI$, and therefore the angles $PCI$ and $QCI$ are equal as supported by equal arcs (in the circle $(BIC)$).
So, $CI$ is both the bisector of the angle $ACB$ and the bisector of the angle $PCQ$. From these two statements, the statement of the problem obviously follows.
Note that the condition of the problem, together with the similar equality of the angles $\angle ABP = \angle CBQ$, means that the points $P$ and $Q$ are isogonally conjugate in the triangle $ABC$. In fact, in this problem we see that the isogonal conjugation takes the circle $(BIC)$ into itself and acts on this circle as a reflection relative to the bisector $AI$.

Then let's look at $f$ as a linear function of the variable $x = a_2$ on the interval $\left[a_1, a_3\right]$. It is enough to check the inequality for $x = a_1$ and $x = a_3$. Next, say in the case of $a_2 = a_3$, we look at $f$ as a linear function of the variable $x = a_2 = a_3$ on the segment $\left[a_1; a_4\right]$, etc. Continuing the reasoning with $a_i$ and then similarly with $b_i$, we can reduce the problem to a situation in which all $a_1, \dots, a_n$ take at most two values ​​$\left(a_1 \text{ и } a_n\right)$ and all $b_1, \dots, b_n$ take at most two values.

Note that with finite accelerations there is always such a “small” time interval after the start of motion, during which the speed of the particle’s removal from the initial position is approximately equal to the initial speed, i.e. we can assume that r ≈ vt. Therefore, to find the speed of the particle, we draw a tangent to the graph of the function at the origin (blue line at figure 2). The magnitude of the speed is numerically equal to the tangent of the angle of inclination of this straight line:

$$v = \frac{9,8 \text{ см}}{14 \text{ мкс}} \approx 7 \text{ km/c}.$$

Let us direct the z axis along the magnetic field induction vector, choosing for simplicity the origin of coordinates so that z = 0 at t = 0. Let us denote v|| the projection of the particle velocity vector onto the z axis, and v_⊥ is the magnitude of the component perpedicular to this axis. Since the Lorentz force acting on a particle does not have a component along the z axis, then

$$z(t) = v||t.$$

In the section perpendicular to the $z$ axis, the particle moves around the circle with a constant speed $v_{\perp}$. The radius of this circle is equal to
$$R = \frac{mv_{\perp}}{qB},$$
where $B$ is the magnitude of the magnetic induction vector, and the time of one revolution is equal to
$$T = \frac{2\pi R}{v_{\perp}} = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}.$$
Thus, the particle trajectory is a helical line with a step $h = v_{\parallel}T$ and a radius $R = v_{\perp}T/(2\pi)$. Choosing the remaining coordinate axes of the maximum in a convenient way, we can write that
$$x(t) = R \cos(2\pi t/T), y(t) = R \sin(2\pi t/T),$$
from where, taking into account that at the moment $t = 0$ the particle is at a point with coordinates $x = R, y = z = 0$, we obtain the formula for the dependence of the model of its movement on time:
$$r(t) = \sqrt{(x(t) - R)^2 + y(t)^2 + z(t)^2} = \sqrt{v_{\parallel}^2 t^2 + 4R^2 \sin^2(\pi t/T)}.$$
Now let's divide the expression for $r(t)$ by $t$:
$$\frac{r(t)}{t} = \sqrt{v_{\parallel}^2 + 4R^2 \left(\frac{\sin(\pi t/T)}{t}\right)^2}.$$
The second term in the radical expression for any $t$ cannot be less than zero, therefore, $r(t)/t \ge v_{\parallel}$. This means that the line going in coordinates $r, t$ from the origin, having common points with the graph given in the conditions and having the smallest slope, is the line $r = |v_{\parallel}| t$. Therefore, in order to determine $v_{\parallel}$, we draw a tangent from the origin to the bottom of the graph (red line at figure 2). The tangent points correspond to situations when the particle is on a straight line passing through the starting point and parallel to the magnetic field line. The tangent of the slope of such a straight line is numerically equal to
$$|v_{\parallel}| = \frac{7,6 \text{ cm}}{40 \text{ мкс}} = 1,9 \text{ km/c}.$$
From here is
$$\cos \alpha = \frac{v_{\parallel}}{v} \approx \pm 0,271, \alpha \approx 74^\circ \text{ или } \alpha \approx 106^\circ.$$
The points of tangency of the lower line are located at equal intervals of time, equal to the time $T$ for the passage of particles of one turn of the helix. To increase accuracy, take the outermost (third) point of contact and get
$$T = 34 \text{ мкс}/3 \approx 11,3 \text{ мкс},$$
$$B = \frac{2\pi m}{qT} = \frac{2 \cdot 3,14 \cdot 10^{-26} \text{ кг}}{1,6 \cdot 10^{-19} \text{ Kл} \cdot 11,3 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{ c}} \approx 35 \text{ мТл}.$$
Between positions $A$ and $B$ time $t_{AB} = 39 \text{ мкс} - 14 \text{ мкс} = 25 \text{ мкс}$ has passed. During this time, the particle mixed along the magnetic field line at a distance
$$\Delta z = |v_{\parallel}| t_{AB} = 4,75 \text{ cm}.$$
In projection onto a plane perpendicular to the magnetic field, the particle moves in a circle with radius
$$R = \frac{v_{\perp} T}{2\pi} \approx 1,21 \text{ cm}.$$
Its displacement in this plane is equal to
$$\Delta l = 2R \sin(\pi t_{AB}/T) \approx 1,50 \text{ cm}.$$
From here we get
$$L_{AB} = \sqrt{\Delta l^2 + \Delta z^2} \approx 5,0 \text{ cm}.$$
