MIXING COLOR

… When I was doing my graduate work at Princeton, I used to go to a cute little restaurant called Daddy's Place. One day, when I was having lunch there, a painter in overalls sat down not far from me. He came down from the second floor where he was painting the room. Somehow we started talking and he started talking about how much you need to know to be a painter. “For example,” he said, “if you had to paint the walls in this restaurant, what color would you choose?” I replied that I didn’t know, to which he said: “The walls need to be painted a dark color to such and such a height, because, you see, people sitting at tables rub their elbows on the walls, so a white wall will not work here. It gets dirty too quickly. But there should be white paint over the dark paint to give the restaurant a clean feel.” Apparently the guy really knew what he was talking about, so I sat with my ears open as he said, “Also, you need to understand colors: know how to mix paints to create different colors. For example, what colors would you mix to make yellow?” I had no idea how you could get yellow by mixing paints. If we are talking about light, then we need to mix green and red, but I knew that he was talking about colors. So I said, "I don't know how to get yellow without yellow paint." “Well,” he said, “if you mix red and white paints, you get yellow.” – Are you sure it won’t turn out pink? “Of course,” he said, “it will turn out yellow.” I believed that he would get yellow because he was a professional painter.
