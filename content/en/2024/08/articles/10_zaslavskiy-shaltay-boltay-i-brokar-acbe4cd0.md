Exercise 1. Prove that for any triangle Brocard points exist, and unique and isogonal conjugations are constructed.

Let us now present the solution to problems 1 and 2.

Solution to Problem 1. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Then $B, O, Q, M$ lie on a circle with diameter $OB$, and $A, H, Q, M$ lie on a circle with diameter $AH$. From here

$$\angle QAH = \angle QMH = \angle QMO = \angle QBO$$

(Fig. 1). We see that $\Delta QAH \sim \Delta QBO$, in other words, $Q$ is the center of the rotational homothety,

which translated $\overrightarrow{AH}$ to $\overrightarrow{BO}$. But from simple angle counting ($\angle OBC = \angle OCB = 90^\circ - \angle ABC = \angle HAB = \angle HBA$) it follows that $\Delta AHB \sim \Delta BOC$. This means that the rotational homothety discussed above also takes $B$ to $C$. Therefore, $\Delta QAB \sim QBC\Delta$, whence $\angle QCB = \angle QBA$ and $\angle QBC = \angle QAB$. Then $\angle QAC = \angle BAC - \angle QAB = \angle CBA - \angle QBC = \angle QBA$, and everything is proven.

Solution to Problem 2. Let $P$ lie on the median of vertex $B$.
