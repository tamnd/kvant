M2796. On the ground is a striped climate polygon, which can be read in its entirety by starting from one of its cells and then moving in two directions - up or to the right. Several such identical strips can be placed inside each other by selling them onto the vector $(-1; 1)$ (Fig. 1). Prove that for any strip consisting of fourreso number of cells, we will find an odd k such that if we combine k of the same strips by inserting them sequentially into each other, then the resulting polygon can be divided along the lines of the seed into two equal parts. (See figure2 for a corresponding example.)

We will assume that the strip is located on an infinite checkered (coordinate) plane. Let's fit our strip P into a checkered rectangle ABCD of size $a \times b$ ($AB = a, BC = b$). There will be $(a + b - 1)$ cells in a strip, so $(a + b)$ is odd. Let Π be bounded on the left and above by a broken line l going from A to C (upward to the right). In parallel, we transfer the rectangle ABCD together with the strip P and the broken line l to the vector $(-a - b, a + b)$; Let us assume that after re-ossing we get a rectangle $A'B'C'D'$, a strip P' inscribed in it and a bounding broken line l' going from $A'$ to $C'$ (Fig. 3).

Let us consider all cells lying entirely in the area bounded by the broken lines l, l' and

Fig. 3

segments $AA'$, $CC'$. Let us show that these cells form the desired checkered polygon M. First, it is clear that M is obtained as a union of $k = a + b$ shifts of the strip P into vectors $(-1; 1), (-2; 2), \dots, (-k; k)$.

It remains to show how the polygon M is divided into two equal checkered polygons. Let $E = CD \cap C'B'$ and $F = AD \cap A'B'$ such that $B'EDF$ is a rectangle. By construction, $CE = C'E = a + b$ means $DE = b$. Likewise, $B'E = a$. We see that E and F lie on the segments $AA'$ and $CC'$, respectively, and the rectangles $B'EDF$ and DCBA are equal. Let's impose the DCBA rectangle on $B'EDF$ (so that the corresponding vertices are aligned: $D - c$ $B'$, $C - c$ $E$, etc.). Let this overlay transform the broken line l into the broken line $l'$. The broken line l'' will divide M into two equal parts. Indeed, the above-considered imposition of DCBA on $B'EDF$ can be obtained by transforming the plane φ, where φ is the sequential application of a shift to the vector $(-a - b, a + b)$ and segments relative to the midline between parallel straight lines $AA'$ and $CC'$ ($\varphi$ – sliding symmetry). It is easy to see that the same transformation φ transforms the corresponding A, C, F, E into F, E, A', C', and the broken line $l''$ into l'. It turns out that φ combines the indicated parts of the polygon M.
