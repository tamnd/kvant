Since the nature of the dependence of the current through the fourth ampere on $I_0$ changes at $I_0 = I_1$, this value corresponds to the fact that the voltage on the diode has reached $U_D$, but the current through the diode is still zero (transition from the horizontal section of the volt-ampere characteristics of the device to the vertical). Let us write down the conditions for the voltage on the diode to be equal to the total voltage on the first and second amperors at $I_0 = I_1$:

$$U_D = I_1r + \frac{I_1}{3}r = \frac{4I_1r}{3}.$$

The situation when the current through the second amperor does not flow, firstly, can only occur when the diode is open. Secondly, if the current through $A_2$ is not flowing, then the voltage at $A_3$ and $A_4$ are equal, which means the currents through them are equal:

$$I_{A3} = I_{A4} = \frac{I_0}{2}.$$

Compliance, the voltage at the first ampere and at the diode must be equal. Since in the case under consideration $I_{A1} = I_{A4}$, then

$$U_D = I_{A1}r, \frac{4I_1r}{3} = \frac{I_0}{2}r, I_0 = \frac{8I_1}{3}.$$

While the diode is closed, i.e. at $I_0 < I_1$, the current through the second amperor is $I_{A2} = I_0/3$. If $I_0 > I_1$, then the voltage on the diode is constant and equal to $U_D = 4I_1r/3$. To construct that part of the desired graph that corresponds to an open diode, you can reason in one of two ways.

First way. The current-voltage characteristic of a diode consists of two straight sections - horizontal and vertical. Therefore, if it is located within only one section (in this case, vertical), the diode can be considered a linear element. The rest of the circuits presented in the conditions also have linear characteristics, i.e. are also linear. Consequently, the dependence of the current strength at the second amperage on $I_0$ at $I_0 > I_1$ is linear. Hence, taking into account that the graph of this dependence must pass through points $(I_1; I_1/3)$ and $(8I_1/3; 0)$, we obtain the graph shown in figure 3.
