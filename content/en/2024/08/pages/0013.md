Fig. 3. Another dependence of the capacitance of a semiconductor structure on voltage at different temperatures

Figure 3 illustrates a more complex case. The abscissa axis shows voltage in volts, the scale is uniform, zero is not on the edge, but closer to the right edge. The ordinate axis is arranged phantasmagorically - firstly, it is discontinuous, but the scale is the same on opposite sides, secondly, zero is outside the scale, thirdly, it shows the capacity, but not itself, but to the power of minus two, i.e. $1 C^{2}$, respectively, the dimension is farad to the power of minus two. Parameter – frequencies at which measurements were made, this is shown by the color and shape of the icon. The parameter $1 C^{2}$ was chosen precisely because in this case the dependence should, according to theory, be straight - and the points actually lie on the straight line. Usually, when, in addition to experimental points, a line is drawn on the picture, this is either a theoretical dependence (with some adjustable parameters) or an empirical dependence, i.e. "drawn through points." Well, we note that the sameness of scales on the ordinate axis below and above the gap at figure is not indicated, this is only a natural hypothesis. Because there is only one division above the gap, but it should be two or three. figure 4 shows a simple and common situation, these are the so-called logarithmic coordinates. Some concentration is plotted on the abscissa axis, judging by the index - electrons, on the ordinate axis - the modulus of some output voltage.

Fig. 4. Dependence of the detector output voltage on the electron concentration in the plasma

The author wanted to show that the logarithms of these quantities are proportional (except for the region of high concentrations), but note that these quantities themselves are not completely proportional (the voltage grows a little slower than the concentration). Figure 5 shows semi-logarithmic coordinates, the x-axis shows time in milliseconds, and the y-axis shows the same concentration, or rather its logarithm. Clearly experimental points are shown, but most likely obtained by two different methods (this is indicated in the text, but it could also be indicated at figure). The results clearly show that the dependence is the same throughout the entire range. Except that at the longest times some features are visible.
