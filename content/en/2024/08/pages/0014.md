Fig. 6. Dependence of alloy resistance on temperature

At figure 6 we see a simple but exotic situation. The abscissa axis shows the temperature, in the form $T^{-} 1^{4}$, and the ordinate axis shows the logarithm of the relative resistance. The author studied the temperature dependence of the resistance of some alloys with additives to the main composition (which is mysteriously designated “MA” on figure) of four rare earth elements, while samarium and terbium reduced the resistance, and neodymium and ytterbium increased it. But the most mysterious thing was (and figure clearly demonstrates this) that both the base alloy and the variants with additives had a completely unusual dependence of the resistivity on temperature - the logarithm of the resistance was proportional to $T^{-} 1^{4}$. On figure 7, as can be seen from the dimensions, time is plotted along the abscissa axis, and specific conductivity is plotted along the ordinate axis. A very complex dependence predicted by the theory is shown - curve 1: a constant, then a sharp decline, then a straight line in logarithmic coordinates. The experimental results - curve 2 - behave very well, with the exception of non-monotonicity at the longest times.
