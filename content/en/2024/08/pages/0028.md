F2803. In a uniform magnetic field in the absence of gravity, the charged part moves. figure 1 shows a graph of the dependence of the modulus of movement of this particle r relative to the point at which this part is located at time t = 0, on the time of its further movement t.

1) Determine the model of the speed v with which the part is moving.
2) Determine the angle α between the particle velocity vector at the moment t = 0 and the magnetic field induction vector.
3) Determine the magnetic field induction if the charge of the particle is q = 1.6·10^{-19} C, and its mass is m = 1.0·10^{-26} kg.
4) Let us find a consideration between two positions of the particle, which correspond to points A and B on the graph.

Fig. 1

A constant magnetic field does not coincide with the work on a charged particle and therefore, if we neglect radiation, the model of the particle’s velocity does not change. This means that the particle velocity is equal to the velocity at t = 0.

Note that with finite accelerations there is always such a “small” time interval after the start of motion, during which the speed of the particle’s removal from the initial position is approximately equal to the initial speed, i.e. we can assume that r ≈ vt. Therefore, to find the speed of the particle, we draw a tangent to the graph of the function at the origin (blue line at figure 2). The magnitude of the speed is numerically equal to the tangent of the angle of inclination of this straight line:

$$v = \frac{9,8 \text{ см}}{14 \text{ мкс}} \approx 7 \text{ km/c}.$$

Let us direct the z axis along the magnetic field induction vector, choosing for simplicity the origin of coordinates so that z = 0 at t = 0. Let us denote v|| the projection of the particle velocity vector onto the z axis, and v_⊥ is the magnitude of the component perpedicular to this axis. Since the Lorentz force acting on a particle does not have a component along the z axis, then

$$z(t) = v||t.$$
