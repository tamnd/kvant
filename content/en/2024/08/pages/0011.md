TO DESIGN A PHYSICAL EXPERIMENT is to change some other parameters at fixed values ​​of some parameters and observe the third group of parameters. For example, at a fixed temperature of the conductor, change the voltage and observe the current. Do you recognize the law? Here’s another experiment: at a fixed ambient temperature, change the voltage and observe the current. Have you noticed the difference? Do you understand that the second experiment is much simpler? How would you proceed if you needed to study resistance at a fixed temperature of the conductor? A comprehensive and detailed answer to this question speaks volumes about your understanding of physics. In each of the three groups of parameters - fixed, changeable, observable - there can be one parameter, but there can also be several. For example, temperature is good for everything. By changing the voltage and observing (remember Ohm's law) the current, you can either stabilize the temperature or consciously change it. Or you can neither stabilize nor change it, but simply measure it. These are three different experiments, and not all of them are simple. Did you notice that we did not specify whether we are talking about the conductor temperature or the ambient temperature? And this greatly changes the situation - it is relatively easy to stabilize the ambient temperature and change it within certain limits, but there can be problems with the temperature of the conductor. What do you think, in what situations is it easy to change the temperature of the conductor, and in what situations is this a very difficult task and it is not clear from which side to approach it?
