In each component $\bar{Y}_i$, people, independently of other components, can perform other transactions as if they were on a cycle of length $n_i$. Indeed, if we denote by $K$ the set of people from $\bar{Y} \setminus \bar{Y}_i$, then, as in the proof of Lemma 3, people from $K$ can always “move” so as not to interfere with the exchanges of friends from $\bar{Y}_i$. This means that if, when performing exchanges, we monitor only component $\bar{Y}_i$, the receiving arrangements of people from component $\bar{Y}_i$ are described by the graph FS($C_{n_i}, Y_i$).

In the case when the graph $\bar{Y}$ is a forest, i.e. all components $\bar{Y}_i$ are trees; from each arrangement of people on cycle $C_n$ one can obtain a standard arrangement by other exchanges. Indeed, we will first obtain the missing arrangement. Further, without violating the absence property, in each component $\bar{Y}_i$, by Lemma 3, people can be rearranged according to increasing numbers (from the point of view of the cycle $C_{n_i}$). Moreover, in the standard arrangement on cycle $C_n$ we also require that the person with the lowest number be the first in the group of people from component $\bar{Y}_i$, this can be achieved by the permutations shown on figure 4.

So, if graph $\bar{Y}$ is a forest, we can always get the standard arrangement. But, unlike Lemma 3, Lemma 4 does not guarantee that all cyclic cdws of the standard arrangement lie in different connected components.

Consider the graph FS($C_{n_i}, Y_i$). Let us mark one arrangement in each connected component of this graph so that as a result, along with each arrangement, all its cyclic cdws are marked. This is possible, since by Lemma 2 cyclic cdws of any arrangement lie in different connected components of the graph FS($C_{n_i}, Y_i$). Among the marked arrangements of people from the composition $\bar{Y}_i$ on the cycle $C_{n_i}$, differing by cyclic cdws, we will call the arrangement in which the first person stands at the first vertex *half-mandarma*. It is clear that there are $\frac{fs(C_n, Y_i)}{n_i}$ half-chandar arrangements.
