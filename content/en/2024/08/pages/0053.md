Problem 2. Collision on a spoke
Two identical small weightless short cylinders can slide without friction along a long fixed horizontal spoke (Fig. 7).

The cylinders are connected to weightless non-growing threads 1 and 2, and the length of thread 1 is $L$ greater than the length of thread 2. A small bead with mass $m_1$ can move along thread 1 without friction, and a small load with mass $m_2$ is attached to the ends of both threads. Initially, the system consisting of cylinders, threads, a bead and a weight is held at rest in such a way that the knitting needle and both threads are in the same vertical plane, the bead and the weight are located on the same horizontal, while the bead is on the same vertical with the cylinder attached to thread 1, and the weight is on the same vertical with the cylinder attached to thread 2. The threads are slightly tensioned. The acceleration due to gravity is $g$. Consider that the dimensions of the cylinders are larger than the dimensions of the bead and the weight. Air compliance can be neglected. The system is released.

1) Determine the velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$ of the bead and the weight, respectively, just before the cylinders collide.

2) Determine the tension forces $T_1$ and $T_2$ of threads 1 and 2, respectively, just before the collision of the cylinders.

A. Vergunov, A. Uymin

Task 3. From the TsPMK archive
In the 2011 TsPMK archive, a problem was discovered that described a rather strange device for experiments with an ideal gas. A closed, thin-walled cylindrical tube of length $L$, installed vertically, is rigidly connected to a fixed cylindrical vessel, the axis of which is also vertical, and the diameter is many times greater than the diameter of the tube (Fig. 8). The upper part of the tube is located at a distance $l$ above the bottom of the vessel. A thin, lightweight, sealed piston can move within the tube without friction. Under the piston there is air, which can be considered an ideal gas. At the top of the tube there are small stops that limit the upward movement of the piston. A small hole was made in the wall of the vessel to fit through it. Using a thin tube, the rutui is very slowly guided through the hole in the vessel, and then the level of rutui in the vessel is just as slowly pushed down to the bottom. Further, cocking $h$ indicates the level of the rutui in the vessel relative to the top of the tube, and $x$ is the distance between the piston and the stops. Consider that during the process of changing the level of rutui in the vessel, the air temperature under the piston and atmospheric pressure will remain constant. Installation parameters: $L = 3,04$ m, $l = 0,04$ m, atmospheric pressure $p_0$ is equal to the hydroelectric pressure of a rutui column with a height of $h_0 = p_0 / (\rho g) = 0,76$ m, where $\rho$ is the rutui density, and $g$ is the acceleration of free fall. The quantities $p_0$, $\rho$ and $g$ are considered unknown. In all points of the problem, provide both analytical expressions and numerical values ​​for the answers. Initially, there is no rutui in the vessel and above the piston, and the air pressure under the piston exceeds atmospheric pressure. In paragraphs 1–3, consider that when the level of rutui in the vessel has reached a maximum value of $h_{\max} = 1,52$ m, then the distance between the piston and the stops is $x_{\max} = 2,28$ m.

1) Distance of the surface level of the rutui in the vessel. At what value of the level $h_1$ of rutui in the vessel did the piston begin to move?

2) Let the level of rutui in the vessel in the process of its increase be $h_1 + \Delta h$, where $0 < \Delta h \ll h_1$. Find the possibility of the value $x_1$ between the piston and the stops.
