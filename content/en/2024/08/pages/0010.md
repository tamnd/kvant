By making friendly exchanges on the graph $C_n$, from any initial arrangement of members we can obtain an arrangement where the units are semi-standard in the sense that the arrangement of members from each connected component $\bar{Y}_i$ along the cycle is cancelable and at the same time
• the first person from component $\bar{Y}_1$ stands at the vertex with some number $k$,
• the “first” person from component $\bar{Y}_2$ (according to our agreement, this person in column $Y$ has number $n_1 + 1$) stands at the vertex with number $n_1 + k$,
• the “first” person from component $\bar{Y}_3$ (according to our agreement, this person in the graph $Y$ has number $n_1 + n_2 + 1$) stands at the vertex with number $n_1 + n_2 + k$, etc.

We will also call such an arrangement of lodes on the graph $C_n$ semi-standard. Semi-standard arrangements obtained from the same initial arrangement can differ only in numbers $k$, i.e. cyclic shifts on $C_n$.

Main theorem. Let the graph $\bar{Y}$ be unconnected, $\bar{Y}_1, \bar{Y}_2, \ldots, \bar{Y}_r$ be its connected components, $v = \text{НОД}(n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_r)$. Then

$$\text{fs}(C_n, Y) = v \prod_{i=1}^{r} \frac{\text{fs}(C_n, Y_i)}{n_i}.$$

(*)

Proof. Let us take an arbitrary arrangement of lodes on the graph $C_n$ and, by exchanges, obtain from it an absent semistandard arrangement. This arrangement is specified by a list in which for each component $Y_i$ a semi-standard arrangement is indicated. Total available

$$\prod_{i=1}^{r} \frac{\text{fs}(C_n, Y_i)}{n_i}$$

such lists. On cycle $C_n$, semistandard arrangements specified by the same list can differ only in cyclic shifts. It follows from Lemma 4 that cyclic shifts of an arbitrary arrangement of $k$ and $l$ positions lie in the same connected component if and only if $k - l$ is divisible by $v$. Thus, the cyclic shifts of one semistandard arrangement correspond to $v$ connection components of the graph FS($C_n, Y$). Track-
