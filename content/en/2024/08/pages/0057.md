Let's look at chameleon number 3: in its column, the first and third cells must be of different colors, which means that the diagonal from its bottom cell can only go to the right. Reasoning in a similar way, we find that for chameleons with numbers 5, 7, ..., 35, the diagonal from the bottom cell goes to the right.
But the same reasoning can be applied to chameleons 35 and 2 - i.e. and from the lower cells of chameleon 2, as well as chameleons 4, 6, ..., 34 diagonals can only go to the right! This means that the entire table is colored in diagonal “lines” (Fig. 3). In this case, the diagonals of neighboring chameleons or chameleons sitting across one must be of different colors: otherwise one of the chameleons will take on the same color twice. This means that the color of the stripes is repeated (first, second, third, first, second, ...), then the final color of the 35th chameleon coincides with the final color of the 2nd - a contradiction.
Comment. From the solution it follows that to satisfy the desired condition, the number of chameleons must be divided by 3; if the initial colors of the chameleons are crossed (red, blue, green, red, blue, green, ...) and each chameleon each time will be the color of the chameleon on the right, then the condition of the task will be fulfilled.
