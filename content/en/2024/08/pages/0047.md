This is the universality and uniqueness of the spectral analysis technique proposed by the great physicist.” Vladimir Georgievich Surdin, Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Senior Researcher $ГАИШ^{3}$ Moscow State University: “Physicists have been working on the theory of radiation and absorption of light by heated bodies since the beginning of the 19th century. And only Gustav Kirchhoff deserves the credit for revealing the subtle and deep meaning hidden behind the original ideas. His seminal work "On the relation between the emissive and absorptive powers of bodies for heat and light" was published in 1860. The basis for this article was a discovery made several months earlier by Gustav Kirchhoff himself, together with Robert Bunsen. They discovered that the Fraunhofer absorption lines in the solar spectrum, first noticed back in 1802–1814 by William Wollaston and Joseph Fraunhofer, coincide with the emission lines of known vapors and gases. This is how spectral analysis was born. Much more important for the development of astrophysics was the discovery by Kirchhoff and Bunsen of emission and absorption lines in the spectra of gaseous bodies. They themselves have already identified some strong lines in the spectrum of the Sun: mainly the lines of calcium and metals. In 1860, Gustav Kirchhoff received the first hint of the true nature of stars when he realized that dark lines in the spectrum of sunlight were caused by various elements absorbing specific wavelengths. Astronomers analyzed similar characteristics in the light of other bright stars and found that they were composed of the same substances found on Earth, and not some mysterious quintessence - a fifth essence, as the ancients believed. By deciphering the optical spectra of stars, we find out the temperature of their surface and determine the luminosity class, which allows us to determine the evolutionary status of the star using the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (“temperature – luminosity”). Systematic measurement of the spectrum makes it possible, based on the Doppler effect, to determine the nature of the spatial motion of the star and its orbital velocity in binary systems. And this is the only way to measure the mass of a star and identify its invisible satellites - exoplanets, white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes. It is impossible to list all the areas of modern astrophysics and the discoveries made in these areas, where spectral studies play a major role. It is a pity that Gustav Kirchhoff and his colleagues did not know what incredible opportunities science gained thanks to their discoveries. The nature and evolution of stars, other planetary systems, the dynamics of galaxies and the Universe as a whole, mysterious dark matter and supermassive black holes, dark energy and many, many other amazing phenomena and objects became known thanks to the spectral analysis of radiation, which was born in small laboratories through the work of several inquisitive enthusiasts in the second half of the 19th century.” The highest appreciation of the scientific discoveries of Gustav Kirchhoff and his contribution to the development of modern science and technology was given in all reports of the Memorial Conference.
