Now it is not difficult to make the transition of induction.
By discarding $a$ and $b$, we obtain that all good pairs not containing $a$ and $b$ remain good; by induction hypothesis, there are no more than $n^2 - 3$.
It remains to estimate the number of good pairs containing $a$ or $b$. These are the pair $(a,b)$, the pairs $(a,c)$ and $(b,c)$ for any perfect child $c$, and at most one of the pairs $(a,c)$ and $(b,c)$ for the remaining children $c$. In total we get no more than $1 + (2n - 2) + 2 = 2n + 1$ pairs; we discover that the number of good pairs does not exceed $(n^2 - 3) + (2n + 1) = (n + 1)^2 - 3$, which is what we needed to prove.

1. Let's cross out all non-integer numbers from the progression (if any). It is clear that after deleting, there remains an infinite killing arithmetic progression consisting of integers. Let its difference be equal to $-d$.
Note that $q^{23} - p^{23}$ is divisible by $d$, so $d$ is not divisible by $p$, otherwise $q^{23}$ would have to be divisible by $p$, which is incorrect. On the other hand, $d$ must be a divisor of $p^{24} - p^{23} = p^{23}(p - 1)$. Since $p$ and $d$ are coprime, $p - 1$ is divisible by $d$. Next, $p^{23} - p$ is divided by $p - 1$ (since it is equal to $p(p - 1)(p^{21} + p^{20} + \ldots + 1)$). Therefore, $p^{23} - p$ is divisible by $d$, and since $p < p^{23}$, we find that $p$ lies in our progression. Likewise, $q$ lies in this progression.
2. $2n - 1$.
Grade. Let's split the square $2n \times 2n$ into $n^2$ squares $2 \times 2$. Among these squares there are no more than $2(n - 1)^2 / 2 = (n - 1)^2$ squares containing at least 2 cells. The remaining squares are $2 \times 2$ at least $n^2 - (n - 1)^2 = 2n - 1$ pieces. From each of them you can cut a three-cell corner.
Example. Let us construct an example by induction on odd $n \ge 1$. With $n = 1$ there are no filled cells, and one corner can be cut out.
For transitions, select an outer “frame” two cells wide into a square. In this frame, fill in all the $8(n - 2)$ cells adjacent to the inner border of the frame (Fig. 11), and in the square inside the frame, fill in the cells by the induction hypothesis. The total number of shaded cells is $2(n - 3)^2 + 8(n - 2) = 2(n - 1)^2$.
