M2797. For the real numbers $0 \leq a_1 \leq a_2 \leq \dots \leq a_n$ and $0 \leq b_1 \leq b_2 \leq \dots \leq b_n$, prove the inequality
$$\left( \frac{a_1}{1 \cdot 2} + \frac{a_2}{2 \cdot 3} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{n(n+1)} \right) \times \left( \frac{b_1}{1 \cdot 2} + \frac{b_2}{2 \cdot 3} + \dots + \frac{b_n}{n(n+1)} \right) \leq \frac{a_1 b_1}{1 \cdot 2} + \frac{a_2 b_2}{2 \cdot 3} + \dots + \frac{a_n b_n}{n(n+1)}$$
This inequality can be proven using Chebyshev’s inequality (see the article “On Chebyshev’s inequality for sums”).
We will also present here the idea of ​​another, more technical solution. Let us denote $f = f\left(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n, b_1, \dots, b_n\right)$ the difference between the left and right sides of the given inequality. Let us fix all $a_i$ and $b_i$ except $a_2$. Then let's look at $f$ as a linear function of the variable $x = a_2$ on the interval $\left[a_1, a_3\right]$. It is enough to check the inequality for $x = a_1$ and $x = a_3$. Next, say in the case of $a_2 = a_3$, we look at $f$ as a linear function of the variable $x = a_2 = a_3$ on the segment $\left[a_1; a_4\right]$, etc. Continuing the reasoning with $a_i$ and then similarly with $b_i$, we can reduce the problem to a situation in which all $a_1, \dots, a_n$ take at most two values ​​$\left(a_1 \text{ и } a_n\right)$ and all $b_1, \dots, b_n$ take at most two values.
A. Antropov
