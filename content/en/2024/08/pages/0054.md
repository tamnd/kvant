Problem 4. Flights of the rod

The experimenter Gluck had at his disposal a fixed non-conducting sphere uniformly charged along the surface with a charge $Q$ of radius $R$ and a very long thin rectilinear non-conducting rod of mass $m$, charged uniformly along the length with a linear charge density $\lambda$. It is known that the charges of the rod and the sphere were of the same name.

In the first series of experiments, Gluck placed a rod on the surface of a sphere in such a way that the rod touched the surface of the sphere with its middle. First, Gluck released the rod from rest, and when the distance between the centers of the sphere and the rod reached an unknown value $l > R$, the rod moved with a speed $u_0 = \sqrt{Q\lambda/(2\pi\varepsilon_0 m)}$ (Fig. 9,a). Then Gluck began to set the rod in motion so that it began translational motion with different values ​​of the initial speed $v$ in the direction perpendicular to the rod and lying in the plane containing the rod and the center of the sphere. Gluck obtained dependences on the initial speed $v$ of the rod of the time $\tau$ elapsed from the moment of start, through which the distance between the centers of the sphere and the rod reached an unknown value

$$\tau_0 = R \sqrt{\frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m}{Q\lambda}}, v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{Q\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m}}.$$

For the second experiment, Gluck made a small hole in the surface of the sphere, placed the end $A$ of the rod inside the sphere so that it was at the center of the sphere (Fig. 11), and released the rod without any initial velocity.

There is no friction between the rod and the hole.

In all experiments, the rods moved translationally. The force of air resistance and gravity can be neglected. The values ​​$s$ and $l$ are many times smaller than the length of the rod.

For the second experiment, determine:

1) time $T_1$ elapsed from the moment of start, after which the end $A$ of the rod ended up on the surface of the sphere, as well as the speed of the rod $v_1$ at this moment in time;

2) speed $v_2$ of the rod at the moment when its end $A$ is at a distance $l$ from the center of the sphere;

3) time $T$ elapsed from the moment of start, after which the end $A$ ended up at a distance $s$ from the center of the sphere.

A. Uymin
