F2816. In a thick plate of non-conductive solid material, a hole of small diameter is drilled perpendicular to the surface, the depth of which is less than the thickness of the plate (Fig. 5). To the bottom of the answer

Fig. 5

One end of the elastic band with a stiffness coefficient of $k$ is attached. The length of the undeformed elastic band is exactly equal to the depth of the response. A bead of mass $m$, charged with a positive charge $q$, is attached to the other end of the elastic band. The plate is located horizontally in a gravity field. The surface of the plate is smooth. A uniform magnetic field is switched on perpendicular to the surface of the plate, the induction vector of which is directed opposite to the acceleration vector of free fall. The magnitude of the magnetic field induction is $B$. By stretching the rubber band, the bead is displaced along the surface of the plate at a distance of $r_0$ from the response axis and released, giving it such a speed perpendicular to the rubber band that the bead moves in a circle.

1) Determine the angular speeds of movement of the bead when rotating it clockwise and counterclockwise (if you look at its movement from above).

The force acting on the bead depends on both its coordinate $\vec{r}$ and its speed $\vec{v}$ in a linear manner. Therefore, if there are two different laws of motion $\vec{r}_1(t)$ and $\vec{r}_2(t)$, which describe some movements of the bead, then equation $\vec{r}(t) = \alpha \vec{r}_1(t) + \beta \vec{r}_2(t)$ for any constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$ correctly describes the law of physical realizable motion of this bead under the influence of the same forces (after all, the speed and acceleration of the bead at any moment of time will be described by the same combinations). For example, if a bead is released from the same initial position as in point 1 without an initial speed, then the law of its motion can be found as a result of the superimposition (superrosicity) of the laws of rotation studied in this point.

3) After what time does it take $\tau$ after releasing the bead to be at a distance $r_0$ from the answer?
