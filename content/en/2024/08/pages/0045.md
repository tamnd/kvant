Gustav Kirchhoff belongs to the galaxy of great scientists of the mid-19th century who, in fact, completed classical physics. This galaxy includes Gustav Kirchhoff himself, Michael Faraday, James Maxwell, Ludwig Boltzmann. But Gustav Kirchhoff stands out especially because his law of the radiative ability of an absolute black body does not fit into classical physics and could not be explained on the basis of the classical theory of radiation. This prompted physicists to ponder the reason for this, resulting in Max Planck's revolutionary quantum theory, which gives us reason to consider Gustav Kirchhoff the forerunner of quantum mechanics.

I consider it necessary to say that with his discoveries Kirchhoff laid the foundation for the direction of modern physics, which are associated with strong radiative processes produced in natural conditions (stars and galaxies) or in technical installations (controlled thermonuclear fusion). I am engaged in mathematical modeling of complex physical and technical systems and am well aware of the important role of the laws of thermal radiation discovered by Gustav Kirchhoff in the study of high-temperature heat and mass transfer. As an example, I will give the problem of laser thermonuclear fusion, in which the heating of a thermonuclear target is carried out using ultra-powerful concentrated laser beams. In this laser thermonuclear plasma, radiative and absorption processes involving

Photons and loaded particles play an extremely important role. The above applies equally to radiative processes in astrophysics, such as radiation from black holes, supernova and solar explosions, and such purely terrestrial processes as laser cutting of metals or heating of spacecraft upon entry into the atmosphere. All these phenomena cannot be described without using the laws of thermal radiation.
