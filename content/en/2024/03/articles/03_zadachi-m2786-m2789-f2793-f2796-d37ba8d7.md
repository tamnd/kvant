PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2786–М2788а, M2789 were offered at the regional stage of the L All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics. The author of problems F2793–F2796 is S. Varlamov.

Problem M2786–M2789, F2793–F2796

M2786. There are 100 white dots in a circle. Anya and Borya take turns painting one not yet painted point red or blue, Anya begins. Anya wants to end up with as many pairs of multi-colored neighboring dots as possible, and Borya wants to end up with as few such pairs as possible. What is the largest number of pairs of multi-colored neighboring dots that Anya can guarantee for herself, regardless of Bori's game? P. Kozhevnikov

M2788. a) A regular triangle T with side 111 is divided by straight lines parallel to its sides into regular triangles with side 1. All vertices of these triangles, except the center of triangle T, are marked. Let's call a set of several marked points linear if all these points lie on the same straight line parallel to side T. How many ways are there to divide all marked points into 111 linear sets? b) The same question if it is additionally required that each linear set be a segment of marked points (i.e., if two different marked points are included in one linear set, then all marked points on the segment between them are too). I. Bogdanov

M2789*. Given a natural number n! 100. Initially, the number 1 is written on the board. Every minute Petya represents the number written on the board as the sum of two unequal positive irreducible fractions, and Vasya leaves only one of these two fractions on the board. Prove that Petya can ensure that the denominator of the remaining fraction does not exceed $2^{n} 50$ after n minutes, regardless of Vasya’s actions. M. Didin

F2795. The ring has a radius R, the thickness of the ring is small compared to its radius, the mass of the ring m is evenly distributed along its perimeter. The ring was untwisted around its axis of symmetry, placed on a rough, friction coefficient equal to P, inclined surface with an angle of inclination D to the horizon, and pushed up. The axis of symmetry of the ring is perpendicular to the inclined surface. The ring is pressed against the surface at all points of its perimeter. At some moment, the center of the ring had zero (relative to the surface) speed and the ring rotated with angular velocity Z. With what acceleration is the center of the ring moving at the indicated moment in time? At what speed does the angular velocity of rotation of the ring decrease at this moment?
