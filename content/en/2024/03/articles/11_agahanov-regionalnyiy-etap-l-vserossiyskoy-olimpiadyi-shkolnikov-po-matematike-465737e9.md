Regional stage of the L All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics

Objectives of the Olympiad

9th grade

1. Oleg has a set of 2024 different checkered rectangles of sizes 1 u 1, 1 u 2, 1 u 3, ..., 1 u 2024 (one rectangle of each size). Can he, by selecting some of the rectangles, form a checkered square of area greater than 1? O. Podlipsky

2. A parabola $y x^{2}$ is drawn on the coordinate plane. For a given number k! 0, we consider trapezoids inscribed in this parabola (i.e., all the vertices of the trapezoid lie on the parabola), whose bases are parallel to the x-axis, and the product of the lengths of the bases is equal to k. Prove that the diagonals of all such trapezoids pass through one point. N. Agakhanov

3. On the island there live knights who always tell the truth, and liars who always lie. To play table tennis, all the inhabitants of the island were divided into two teams A and B, and A had more inhabitants than B. Two players from different teams started the game; After each game, the losing player left the game forever and was replaced by another (not yet playing) member of his team. The team lost, all of whose members left the game. After the tournament, each member of Team A was asked: “Is it true that you lost to a liar in any game?”, and each member of Team B was asked: “Is it true that you beat at least two knights?” All answers turned out to be affirmative. Which team won - A or B? M. Didin

4. All natural numbers from 1 to 1000 are written out once in a row in some order. Prove that it is possible to choose

how many consecutive numbers are written out, the sum of which is greater than 100000, but does not exceed 100500. S. Berlov

5. Given an isosceles triangle ABC (AB BC). On the extensions of the lateral sides AB and BC beyond point B, points D and E are marked, respectively, and on the base of AC, point F is marked, with AC DE and CFE DEF. Prove that ABC 2 DFE. A. Kuznetsov

6. There are 7 different numbers written on the board, the sum of which is 10. Petya multiplied each of them by the sum of the other six and wrote down the 7 resulting products in his notebook. It turned out that there were only four different numbers in the notebook. Find one of the numbers written on the board. I. Bogdanov

7. A point is marked on a circle 1 meter long. Two cockroaches simultaneously ran from it in the same direction at different constant speeds. Every time a fast cockroach caught up with a slow one, the slow one instantly turned around without changing speed. Every time they met face to face, the fast one instantly turned around without changing speed. At what distance from the marked point could their hundredth meeting take place? I. Bogdanov

8. On side BC of acute triangle ABC, points P and Q are chosen so that BP PQ QC. Points X and Y are chosen respectively on the segments AC and AB so that PX A AC and QY A AB. Prove that the intersection point of the medians of triangle ABC is equidistant from the lines XQ and YP. A. Matveev

10. Is there a natural number $n > 10^{100}$ such that the decimal representations of the numbers $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$ differ by rearranging the digits? (In other words, in the decimal notations of the numbers $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$ there should be an equal number of digits 0, an equal number of digits 1, ..., and an equal number of digits 9.)
A. Chironov
10th grade
1. See problem 1 for 9th grade.
2. A parabola $y = x^2$ is drawn on the coordinate plane. For a given number $k > 0$, we consider trapezoids inscribed in this paraboly (i.e. all vertices of the trapezoid lie on the paraboly), whose bases are parallel to the x-axis, and the product of the length of the bases is $k$. Prove that the extensions of the lateral sides of all such trapezoids pass through one point.
N. Agakhanov
3. See problem M2786 “Problem book «Kvant»”.
4. See problem 4 for 9th grade.
5. The diagonals of a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ redistribute and intersect at the point $O$. The centers of the drawn circles of triangles $ABC, BCD, CDA, DAB$ are the vertices of a convex quadrilateral whose perimeter is equal to $P$. Prove that the sum of the radii of the circles of the triangles $AOB, BOC, COD, DOA$ does not exceed $\frac{P}{2}$.
S. Berlov
6. Surrey claims to have found distinct real numbers $x, y, z$ such that $\frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{1}{y^2} + \frac{1}{z^2} + z + 1 = 4$.
Could Serrey's words be right?
P. Kozhevnikov
(based on folklore)
7. Petya claims that he wrote 10 consecutive natural numbers, and it turned out that among all the digits used in these numbers, each digit (from 0 to 9) appears the same number of times. Could Petya's words turn out to be right?
P. Kozhevnikov
8. Given a quadrilateral $ABCD$, in which $\angle A = \angle C = 90^\circ$. It is known that its vertices $A$ and $D$, together with the midpoints of the sides $AB$ and $BC$, lie on the same circle. Prove that the vertices $B$ and $C$, together with the midpoints of the sides $AD$ and $DC$, also lie on the same circle.
A. Kuznetsov
9. Find all triples of (not necessarily distinct) natural numbers $a, b, c$ such that each of the numbers $a + bc, b + ca, c + ab$ is a prime divisor of the number
$\left(a^2 + 1\right)\left(b^2 + 1\right)\left(c^2 + 1\right)$.
A. Chironov, I. Boedanov
10. Each of the 2024 knights is a knight or a knave. Some of them are friends with each other, and the friendship is mutual. Each of them asked about the number of friends, and all the answers turned out to be different numbers from 0 to 2023. It is known that all the knights answered the questions correctly, and all the liars changed the true answer to 1. What was the maximum number of liars among these knights?
Y. Shubin, G. Shubin

$p_i = \left(x_1 - \frac{1}{x_1}\right)\left(x_2 - \frac{1}{x_2}\right) \dots \left(x_i - \frac{1}{x_i}\right)$.
What is the maximum number of natural numbers that can be contained among the numbers $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_{2024}$?
