Yuliy Borisovich Khariton
(on the 120th anniversary of his birth)

L. BELOPUKHOV

Life and destiny
The decision of the Special Committee of the Council of Ministers of the USSR dated March 16, 1946 also contained the following lines:
“Reorganize sector No. 6 of Laboratory No. 2 of the USSR Academy of Sciences into design bureau No. 11 of Laboratory No. 2 of the USSR Academy of Sciences for the development, design and manufacture of atomic bombs.”
And on April 9, 1946, the Resolution of the Council of Ministers of the USSR “Issues of Laboratory No. 2 of the USSR Academy of Sciences” was issued, which repeated the text of the decision of the Special Committee with a slight change - instead of the words atomic bombs, experimental samples of jet engines are listed. According to this decree, several dozen of the first models of atomic and hydrogen bombs were protected in all documents as RDS-1, RDS-2, ..., which was officially read as a special jet engine - model 1, etc. But after the first test, folklore names appeared: jet engine Smalina and Russia do it ourselves. Starting with RDS-6, everyone dealing with this “product” was already convinced that the second decoding was correct and that Kurchatov himself came up with it.
It is unlikely that Julius Borisovich Khariton thought then that this Decree would determine his life and fate for the coming five decades. But in fact, for the previous two years, as the certifying sector of the Institute of Chemical Physics, he was preparing for a new job.
Having assessed the scale of the upcoming activities, Yu.B. Khariton realized that this KB should, in fact, from the very beginning become a large scientific institute, and not just an engineering institution, and that together with the pilot plant it would be a team of many thousands. This means that a whole city with all city services and institutions should appear almost on the spot. To create this city and even to equip and supply scientific and engineering laboratories, a special body is needed, and the head of this body should not be the chief designer, but the initial design bureau, which has all the necessary rules at the state level. And in the decision of the Special Committee a clause appeared:
“Appoint Pavel Mikhailovich Zernov, Deputy Minister of Tank Industry, as the head commander of KB-11 with relief from all other work.”
Together with the head of the First Main Directorate B.L. Vannikkov Khariton and Zernov chose a place for KB-11, which satisfied many conditions. It should be not very far from Moscow, be fairly deserted, but at the same time have transport accessibility and a minimum base for quickly starting work. In a large forest reserve on the border of the Mordovian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic and the Gorky region, near the small monastery village of Sarov (the monastery was closed during Soviet times), an ammunition plant (mortar mines for Katyusha) No. 550 was built at the beginning of the war. Its derivative premises were located in the transferred former monastery buildings. For KB-11 it was not difficult to adapt them to laboratories and workshops. The mortar plant was urgently transferred to another region.

The most important part of Khariton’s work was overseeing the production of bombs at factories No. 1 and No. 2, built on the same territory as KB-11. Back in 1947, Khariton raised the question of building and constructing a special testing ground for full testing of RDS products “charged with fuel,” i.e. real atomic bombs. He participated in choosing the location for this test site and in directing the creation of all the measuring equipment to determine the parameters of the explosion. And, of course, in the preparation of the tests themselves and their implementation. He signed preparation protocols, decisions on testing, and reports on the conduct and results of tests. And he not only signed, but delved deeply and drew conclusions to improve the designs. And although these tests took place very far from Sarov (at the Semipalatinsk and Novaya Zemlya test sites), it was in Sarov that the monument-stele “To Nuclear Weapons Testers” was recently opened. Our team of the best cardiologists was no longer able to start the heart again. Khariton, being nearby, lost his best friend. Until this moment he had never felt hotter.

Did you notice the words “guards”? Yes, several leaders of the Atomic Project had 24-hour security installed. Kurchatov and Khariton initially had three people each, working in shifts. These were senior officials of the State Security Committee in considerable numbers. Officially, they were called assistant secretaries. They must be next to the protected “object” at all secret meetings (in the application room) and follow him even at secret facilities - in Sarov or at the Semipalatinsk training ground. In their memoirs about Khariton, his “secretaries” wrote: “... it was a great happiness for us to work with Julius Borisovich Khariton,” so attentively and caringly did Khariton treat them, always finding an extra opportunity for them to see their families who remained in Moscow. This protection existed for more than 20 years and was only abolished in 1968.

It is curious how a group of young testers, among whom was the author of this article, saw Khariton for the first time at the Semipalatinsk test site. We have already dealt scientifically with Kurchatov and Sakharov, but not yet with Khariton. And one day (in the summer of 1955), during a break in the tests on a Sunday morning on the banks of the Irtysh, we saw a couple on the upper path: the first was frail, of short stature, in an unsightly raincoat, and behind him was a tall representative, walking importantly. “Guys,” said Academician S.A., who was walking with us and who was good to us from work. Khristanovich, “you haven’t seen Khariton yet, he’s coming!” Of course we stared at the second one. “No,” said Sergei Aleksandevich, “this is a guard, Khariton is in front.” I remember we were amazed at his homely, inconspicuous appearance; we did not yet know about his wonderful human traits, about his great modesty.

Monument to Tsar Bomba in Snezhinsk

The Tsar Bomb, developed and constructed by the design bureau of Sarov and Snezhinsk, was tested on October 30, 1961. This was the apogee of the nuclear arms race. In the nuclear weapons museum in Sarov there is a model of the Tsar Bomba, which exactly copies its appearance. And in Snezhinsk there is an original monument - on top of it there is a two-meter diameter painted ball depicting the earth’s globe. There is no inscription, but many knew that the main part of the Tsar Bomba was a two-meter ball of “fuel” - it was lithium. And the coloring of the globe shows the danger for the entire globe.

Social activities and awards. Memory

For almost 30 years after the completion of the first stage of the creation of nuclear weapons (the RDS-37 hydrogen bomb, 1955), Khariton’s work continued, at first glance, rather monotonously. The main thing was to reduce the weight of bombs intended for missiles. Some varied bomb designs for peaceful economic use. There were 114 peaceful underground nuclear explosions in the Soviet Union, and Khariton was directly involved in all of these explosions.

He sometimes found the opportunity to communicate with people he was interested in in an informal setting, when these people had no idea about his work. There are now only a few people left who knew Khariton personally, but there are many memories and evidence of the importance of his personality in the correct decisions and regulations of the Atomic Project. Moreover, the memory of him is preserved in science not only in the field of the use of nuclear energy. In the last twenty years of his life, the former atomic “design bureau” turned under his leadership into a major scientific center of modern physics of world significance. It traditionally develops the work the foundations of which were laid by Yuli Borisovich Khariton - the production and use of super-strong magnetic fields and super-power lasers for thermonuclear fusion, the design of nuclear reactors and pulsed accelerators, the study of the properties of substances at enormous pressures and much more that was associated with the pressures and temperatures that arose during nuclear explosions.

The main parallel is that they were both the first leaders of the first nuclear weapons centers of their countries.
