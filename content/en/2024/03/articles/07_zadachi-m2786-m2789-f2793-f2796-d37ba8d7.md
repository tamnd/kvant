**M2776***. There are n in droves in the country, rated from 1 to n; In each total, only whole non-negative sums of money are possible. A person can only have one shaft at any time. In the exchanger, a person can exchange all the money he has from value i for value j at the rate $\alpha_{ij}$: if he had d unit of value i, in return he receives $\alpha_{ij}$ d unit of value j, and this number is rounded to the nearest integer (a number of the form t - 1/2 is rounded to t for integer t). Moreover, $\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{jk} = \alpha_{ik} u \alpha_{ii} = 1$ (numbers $\alpha_{ij}$ are positive real numbers).
Is it possible in such a country (at least for some n) to find a person who can become rich indefinitely (i.e., so that for any number D a person can, after several exchanges, receive more than D unit of some value)?

At each moment we will calculate the amount of our money S in terms of the first currency (without rounding). Let's put $\beta_i = \alpha_{1i}$; if now we have the i-th currency, then we have $S\beta_i$ units.

**Lemma.** Let S be some number. Then there exists a (not necessarily integer) number T > S such that all numbers of the form $\{T\beta_i\}$ are less than 1/2.
Considering the lemma proven, from the original sum S we choose the number T from the lemma. Let us show that the amount of money cannot exceed T. Indeed, if at some point our amount of money is equal to S ≤ T and we change it to the i-th currency, then we will receive it in an amount equal to the closest amount to the number k $S\beta_j$; since $S\beta_j \leq T\beta_j < \left[T\beta_j\right] + \frac{1}{2}$, this quantity does not exceed $\left[T\beta_j\right]$. Then our amount of money (in terms of the first currency) does not exceed T.
It remains to prove the lemma. There is a very small positive t for which all numbers of the form $\{t\beta_j\}$ are positive and

exchangeable 1/2. Let them all lie in the interval ($\varepsilon; 1/2 - \varepsilon$) (for some positive $\varepsilon$). According to the well-known Kronecker theorem, for a set of numbers $\beta_1, \ldots, \beta_n$ there is an arbitrarily large M such that all numbers of the form $M\beta_j$ are no further than $\varepsilon$ from the integers. Then the number T = M + t is the desired one.

I. Bogdanov

Proof of the lemma. Let us draw parallel support lines through $A$ and $C$ to $P$, and through $B$ and $D$ also (Fig. 2). These four lines form a parallelogram $KLMN$ (we assume that the points $K$ and $L$ lie in the angles $AOB$ and $BOC$, respectively).

Let $T$ be the midpoint of $KL$, so that $OT$ is the midline of the trapezoid (or parallelogram) $AKLC$. Without loss of generality, let the base $AK$ be no greater than the base $CL$, i.e. $AK \leq OT \leq CL$.

If $B$ lies on the segment $TL$, then $S_{ABCD}/2 = S_{ABC} \geq S_{ATC} = S_{AKLC}/2 = S_{KLMN}/4 \geq S_P/4$, whence the required result follows.

Let now $B$ lie on the segment $KT$. Then in the trapezoid $AKTO$ we consider the point $E$ of intersection of the diagonals and the point $F = AB \cap KO$. Since $AK \leq OT$ and $KE/EO = AK/OT$, we have $KF \leq FO$, which means $S_{AKB} \leq S_{AOB}$, hence $S_{AOBK} \leq 2S_{AOB} = S_{ABCD}/2$. But according to our choice of lines, the quadrilateral $AOBK$ covers a quarter of the area $P$. Hence $S_P/4 \leq S_{AOBK} \leq S_{ABCD}/2$. The lemma is proven.

F2782. In the electrical circuit (Fig. 1) all elements are ideal. The capacitance of the capacitor $C = 80 \text{ мкФ}$, the emf of the battery $\varepsilon = 150 \text{ В}$, and the resistance ratio $\frac{R}{R} = 0,25$ are known. Before the key was closed, the capacitor was not charged. What amount of heat is released in the resistor with resistance $R$ after the switch is closed to the moment when the current flowing to the capacitor becomes zero?

Equivalent battery parameters: $\varepsilon_{\text{экв}} = \varepsilon/2$, $r_{\text{экв}} = r/2$. Then the circuit can be represented in the form shown in figure 2. In the stationary state, the charge of the capacitor $q = \frac{C\varepsilon}{2}$, the energy of the electric field Fig. 2

in capacitore $W = \frac{C\varepsilon^2}{8}$. During the charging of a capacitor, the work of electrical forces in the battery

$$A = q\frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \frac{C\varepsilon^2}{4}.$$

The total amount of heat released on the two resistors

$$Q = A - W = \frac{1}{8}C\varepsilon^2.$$

F2783. Planet “Fanmation” is a single-pod sphere. This planet has a magnetic field similar to Earth's. Caused by the magnetic stripes of the planet, the magnetic field induction lines are directed vertically, the field is uniform. To determine the magnitude of induction $B$ in this area, students throw a charged ball with a speed $v_0 = 10 \text{ m/c}$ at an angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$ to the horizon. After some time, the ball returns to the smarma point. During the flight, the projection of the ball onto the horizontal plane made $n = 2$ revolutions. Enter the values ​​$B$ of the magnetic field induction. The ratio of the ball's charge to its mass is $\frac{q}{m} = 2\cdot 10^6 \text{ Кл/кг.}$. Acceleration of gravity $y$ of the planet's surface $g = 8 \text{ m/c}^2$. Keep the air resistance force negligible.

The ball is acted upon by vertical forces $m\vec{g}$ and horizontal magnetic force $q[\vec{v} \times \vec{B}]$. Then in the equation of motion

$$m\frac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t} = m\vec{g} + q[\vec{v} \times \vec{B}]$$

you can separate the variables:

$$m\frac{\Delta\vec{v}_{\text{верт}}}{\Delta t} = m\vec{g}, m\frac{\Delta\vec{v}_{\text{горр}}}{\Delta t} = q[\vec{v}_{\text{горр}} \times \vec{B}].$$

Flight duration

$$T = \frac{2v_0 \sin \alpha}{g},$$

the horizontal projection of the ball moves uniformly around the circle with a period

$$\tau = \frac{2\pi}{q/m}B.$$

According to the conditions, $T = n\tau$, from where

$$B = n\pi \frac{g}{q}v_0 \sin \alpha \approx 5\cdot 10^{-6} \text{Tл.}$$
