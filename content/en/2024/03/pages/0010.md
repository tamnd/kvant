KVANT 2024/№3

Yu.B. Khariton and I.V. Kurchatov at the party congress in the Kremlin (1956) discussed his life position in relation to fundamental political problems. Without touching on the complexity of this issue in this article, we can only note that for Khariton, the security of the Motherland and passion for science always came first. He never took part in political disputes, but he also did not interfere with these disputes. In 1956, at the age of fifty-two, he joined the party. Six times in a row, starting in 1950, Khariton was elected as a deputy of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR. He took his duties as a deputy very seriously. For several convocations he was a deputy in the Michurinsky (in the Tambov region) electoral district (away from foreign correspondents). He achieved complete gasification of the city of Michurinsk and all villages of the Michurinsky district. At sessions of the Supreme Council, it was Khariton who put forward and achieved the adoption of a law on pensions for collective farmers. Meeting regularly with his constituents, he sought solutions to many specific issues that were addressed to him. At the same time, a great difficulty was the non-disclosure of his powerful position in the state. During the election campaigns, he was considered a researcher at the Institute of Chemical Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences. Khariton tried to carry out the work entrusted to him by the state as carefully and actively as everything else he did. Several times he supported

a wave of speeches by scientists regarding the unworthy activities of T.D. Lysenko and a group of philosophers who considered the theory of relativity and quantum mechanics to be incorrect (“bourgeois”) sciences. Yu.B. Khariton (like I.V. Kurchatov) received the highest state prize of the first degree three times. There was no such award officially. But there was a top secret government decree dated March 21, 1946, long before the first test of the atomic bomb, establishing detailed rules for awarding the Atomic Project. There were five degrees of prizes. The first prize is the title of Hero of Socialist Labor, laureate of the Stalin Prize of the first degree, awarding one million rubles, a mansion or large apartment in the city with living space $200 м^{2}$ and a dacha with all the furnishings, a car, exemption from tuition fees for children and free travel for all family members on all types of transport. The second prize is the same as the first, but the monetary reward is less (700 thousand rubles) and there is no city apartment. Accordingly, the monetary reward and the dignity of titles for the third, fourth and fifth prizes were reduced. But free education for children and free travel remained. All workers participating in the Atomic Project received wages one and a half to two times higher than workers in corresponding positions in other branches of industry and science. Khariton transferred all the monetary rewards he received to medical institutions in the Gorky region. After the first test of the bomb on August 29, 1949, by decree of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR with the stamp “not subject to publication,” 36 people received the title of Hero of Socialist Labor, 260 people were awarded the Order of Lenin, 496 people were awarded the Order of the Red Banner of Labor, 52 people were awarded the Order of the Badge of Honor and over 2,000 people were awarded medals. Most of the recipients are employees of the newly born nuclear industry, workers, technicians and engineers.
