Yu.B. Khariton was an extremely modest person. He wore his three Hero Stars only on the most formal occasions. He sometimes found the opportunity to communicate with people he was interested in in an informal setting, when these people had no idea about his work. For example, he had a good friend who had the ability to predict the weather based on folk signs and his own observations. They talked a lot about weather forecasts, and this craftsman was sure that his dear acquaintance Yuliy Borisovich works in agriculture, where weather forecasts are important, and he was only surprised that their young “nephew” always took part in their meetings, as Khariton represented him, who does not have the right to personal meetings with people, does not belong to the “project” (except, of course, relatives and friends). And Khariton’s interest in weather forecasts was explained by the importance of forecasts for setting dates for testing “products.” Official forecasts in those years often turned out to be wrong. By the way, Khariton himself never told his friend about this. One of his guards spoke about him in his memoirs

Here is another example from these memories. When filling out some form (in connection with elections to the Supreme Council), Khariton had to answer a question about his military rank. He knew that some meaning was assigned to him, but he did not know what it was. It is noteworthy that he did not order anyone to clarify the issue, but, considering this matter to be deeply personal, he himself decided on his next visit to Moscow to go to the military registration and enlistment office at his place of residence and find out about his rank. Of course, he had to go with a guard, whom he did not allow to talk about his status. And the guard recounted in his memoirs how Khariton sat near some office and humbly waited for him to deign to receive him

a certain captain. Finally, this captain went to the archives to find out the meaning of “some Yu.B. Khariton,” and they returned, laboring from fear, and said: “You are a colonel general.”

There are now only a few people left who knew Khariton personally, but there are many memories and evidence of the importance of his personality in the correct decisions and regulations of the Atomic Project. Moreover, the memory of him is preserved in science not only in the field of the use of nuclear energy. In the last twenty years of his life, the former atomic “design bureau” turned under his leadership into a major scientific center of modern physics of world significance. It traditionally develops the work the foundations of which were laid by Yuli Borisovich Khariton - the production and use of super-strong magnetic fields and super-power lasers for thermonuclear fusion, the design of nuclear reactors and pulsed accelerators, the study of the properties of substances at enormous pressures and much more that was associated with the pressures and temperatures that arose during nuclear explosions.

Two destinies. Khariton and Oppenheimer

In the book about Khariton mentioned at the beginning of the article (see «Kvant» No. 2) there is a two-story book by David Halloway, a professor of political science at Stanford University in the USA, a historian of science. He devoted several years of his life to the history of the emergence of nuclear weapons and, in particular, in 1994 he published the book “Stalin and the Bomb.” In one of the essays（《In Search of Khariton》） Halloway talked about his meetings and conversations with A.D. Sakharov and Yu.B. Khariton. In 1987, Sakharov told him about Khariton only that “Khariton is the Oppenheimer of the Soviet project.”

Halloway met with Khariton three times. The last meeting in 1992 took place, although this quality is incredible, in Sarov, until recently a top-secret city. But in the new state, in the Russian Federation, a new state system had not yet been born.
