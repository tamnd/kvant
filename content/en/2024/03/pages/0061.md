1. An inexhaustible, non-stretchable thread is thrown through a light block attached to a vertical wall. One end of the thread is attached to a board of mass $M$ lying on an inclined plane with an angle of inclination $\alpha = 30^\circ$, the other end of the thread is attached to a block of mass $m = M/9$, which is located on the board (Fig. 10). The friction coefficient between the block and the board is $\mu_1 = 0,5$, and between the board and the support $\mu_2 = 0,3$. The parts of the thread between the bodies and the block are located in the same vertical plane parallel to the inclined plane. There is no training in the block. Take the free fall acceleration equal to $g = 10$ m/s². Determine the acceleration model $a$ with which the board will move.

2. A homogeneous solid rod $AB$ with mass $m$ can freely rotate around a fixed axis passing through point $O$ perpendicular to the drawing plane (Fig. 11). Point $O$ divides the rod in a ratio of 1:3, counting the left edge. A vertical thread attached to the left edge $A$ of the rod and a fixed support holds the rod in a horizontal position. A ball of mass $M = 6m$ is suspended on two threads attached to the rotation axis $O$ and the right edge $B$ of the rod so that the center of the ball and the attachment points of the threads form a regular triangle. The system is in balance. The thread connecting the ball and the axis of rotation is burned out,

the ball begins to oscillate in the same vertical plane in which it is in a state of equilibrium. Find the ratio of the maximum tension force of the vertical thread $T_{A\max}$ during oscillations to the tension force $T_A$ of this thread in a state of equilibrium (before burning out). Consider the threads as non-fissing and non-stretching; the size of the ball and air resistance can be neglected. Round your numerical answer to the nearest tenth.

3. A mole of an ideal monatomic gas is transferred from some initial state to some final state, first isobarically, then isochorically. In this case, it is necessary to supply a certain amount of heat $Q$ to the gas. The minimum temperature of the gas during these two processes is equal to $T_{\min} = 200$ K. If we transfer the gas from the same initial to the same final state, making it adiabatic and reducing its volume by 10 times, then external forces will have to do work equal in modulus to $A = 40$ kJ. Find the amount of heat $Q$.
