Now we expand the area $\Delta S_i \cos \alpha$ in each cone so that it is at a distance $R$. Then all the resulting areas will approach the sphere with radius $R$, and the flux will be equal to the flux of the gravitational field of mass $M$ through the sphere with radius $R$ ($M = \delta m$, if we consider a small piece of the first initial sphere with radius $r$). Note that the new flow through a sphere of radius $R$ is spherically symmetric. Consequently, our mass can be moved to its center. This means that the entire mass of the sphere can be moved to the center. Thus, the flow of the gravitational field of a spherical symmetrical sphere (ball) with mass $M$ is equal to the flow of a material point with mass $M$, concentrated in the center. But the flow is equal to the product of the magnitude of the force and the area, since in both cases the forces are perpendicular to the surface of the sphere. Therefore, the forces are equal.

3. The solution to this problem can be obtained from the solution to Problem 4.

4. During time $\delta t$, the radius of the ball changes from $R$ to $R + \delta t R'$, but the mass of the substance does not change. Then

$$\rho(t + \delta t) = \frac{\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \rho}{\frac{4}{3} \pi (R + \delta t R')^3} = \frac{R^3}{(R + \delta t R')^3} \rho,$$

or

$$\rho' = \lim_{\delta t \to 0} \frac{\rho(t + \delta t) - \rho(t)}{\delta t} = \lim_{\delta t \to 0} \left( \frac{R^3}{(R + \delta t R')^3} - 1 \right) \frac{\rho}{\delta t} =$$

$$= \lim_{\delta t \to 0} \left( \frac{-3R^2 \delta t R' + \dots}{(R + \delta t R')^3} \right) \frac{\rho}{\delta t} = -3 \frac{R'}{R} \rho = -3H \rho.$$

5. In the case of $n$-dimensional space, Newton's law of universal gravitation looks like this:

$$R'' = -\frac{GM_0}{R^{n-1}}.$$

Both facts (problem 1) are also satisfied, and their proofs are the same. The equation for $\rho'$ (problem 4) will look like

$$\rho' = -nH \rho.$$

And the differential equation for the Hubble constant is transformed to the form

$$H'' + (2 + n)HH' + nH^3 = 0.$$
