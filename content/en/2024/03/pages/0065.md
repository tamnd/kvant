“If equal centripetal forces are directed to individual points of a spherical surface, decreasing in relation to the squares of the distances to these points, then a particle placed inside this surface does not experience attraction from such forces in any direction.
Let $HJKL$ (Fig. 4) be the said spherical surface, $P$ be the partial inside its location; Let us draw through $P$ two straight lines $HK$ and $JL$, enclosing the weights of small arcs $HJ$, $KL$; since the triangles $HPJ$ and $LPK$ are similar, then these weights of the minor arcs will be proportional to the distances $HP$ and $PL$, and the weights of the small parts of the spherical surface adjacent to $HJ$ and $KL$ and limited by straight lines drawn through the point $P$ will be found in relation to the squares lengths $PH$ and $PK$, therefore, the forces of attraction of these small parts of the surface at point $P$ are equal to each other, or these forces are directly proportional to these parts of the surface and are inversely proportional to movement.
2) Newton's proof of this fact covers the next ten pages. We will give a shorter proof here.
Consider a sphere $S$ with radius $r$ centered at point $O$, over which mass $M$ is uniformly distributed, and point $B$ with mass $m$ at a distance $R$ from point $O$. Let's draw a sphere $S_1$ with radius $R$ and center at $O$ through point $B$ (Fig. 5). From spherical symmetry it follows that the force $F$ acting on $B$ from the side $S$ is perpendicular to $S_1$ and the same in magnitude at all points $S_1$. Divide the sphere $S$ into small planes with mass $\delta m$, which can be considered material points. The main idea is to count not just one force $F$, but the “total” force acting on the sphere $S_1$. To do this, we introduce the concept of a vector field and the flow of a vector field through a surface.
A vector field is very simple. If a force is applied at every point in space, then such an object is called a “vector field”.
Why the field is also clear. Very similar to grass, I grow in the field - at every connection point there is travinka. It is also clear that any body of mass $M$ creates a vector field. Let's take the test mass $m$ and place it at a point in space. The test body is affected by its own force at each point - this is the vector field, which we create with a body of mass $M$. This field is called the boundary field. We will, of course, consider fields that change continuously. It is clear that the boundary field is continuous.
The flow of a vector field through a surface is also very clear. This is how many vectors flow through the surfaces. Why does it “leak”? Because a vector field can be represented as a field of velocities of particles moving in space, for example, the velocities of water flow. Let's select a small area of ​​our surface with area $\delta m$ with center at point $x$. We can assume that all particles flowing through these areas have the same speed – $\vec{v}$. It is clear that in time $t$ a volume of liquid $\delta S \cdot v \cdot \cos \alpha \cdot t$ will flow through the platform, where $\alpha$ is the angle between vectors $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{n}$ (normal to the surface). It is clear why it is necessary to consider only the normal component of the velocity - the velocity vector at any point on the surface can be decomposed into a normal component and a component tangent to the surface. It is clear that only the normal component flows through the surface. We will not take into account time, since we have a Stationary problem (or we can assume that we are taking unit time). All that remains is to agree on which normal to consider. If the surface is closed, then it is customary to consider the outer normal. After this, all the numbers obtained for all the small pieces of the surface must be added up. This sum is called the flux of the vector field. Let's not say that we need to move on to the proposal. In physics, all quantities are calculated with some accuracy. And the concept of a limit was precisely born from the understanding that we can calculate physical quantities with increasing accuracy.
