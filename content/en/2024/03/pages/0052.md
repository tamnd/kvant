• Very often, solving a problem leads to a result that could not have been predicted before use. In this case, if we were asked to find a solution to the equation for the Hubble constant, it would not even be clear where to start, because there are no general solution methods. And now we can present these solutions:

$$H = \frac{2}{3} t^{-1},$$

or

$$H = a \frac{\sin \eta}{(1 - \cos \eta)^2}, t + t_0 = \frac{1}{a} (\sin \eta - \eta),$$

or

$$H = a \frac{\sin \eta}{(1 - \cos \eta)^2}, t + t_0 = \frac{1}{a} (\eta - \sin \eta).$$

• The coefficients of the differential equation for the Hubble constant are related to the dimension of our space.
