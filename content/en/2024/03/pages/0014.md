The latest film, simply titled “Oppenheimer,” has just been released in Russia. Oppenheimer spent his last years in a secluded life on the ocean shore of the bay of the small, sparsely inhabited American Caribbean island of St. John. His wife was with him and sometimes his growing daughter came. Once a year several codelegal physicists visited him. True, living conditions were not at all similar to Robinson’s epic. The house he designed himself had one huge room with three walls (on rainy and windy days the fourth wall was replaced by a leather curtain) and a view of the ocean. We had our own generator, all the kitchen appliances, a car for grocery shopping, and the best champagne on order. There was a stunningly beautiful mountain landscape, and an eternally different ocean, and all year round - summer without sweltering heat, and music. Paradise, and that's all. But still there was no peace of mind and, most importantly, health. Oppenheimer smoked all his life, and smoked almost constantly, the result was inoperable throat cancer. Oppenheimer and Khariton were very different as individuals. For example, Khariton never smoked. His family life could serve as an unattainable standard for others. He never had a so-called “personal” life, other than his family life. At the beginning of the article, many enthusiastic words were quoted, testifying not only to the respect, but also to the sincere love of the employees for Khariton. Let us repeat once again the paradoxical words of Academician A.M. Shalnikov: “His only drawback is that he has no shortcomings.” And perhaps this was the main reason for his super-successful fifty years of leadership of a huge scientific team. At first glance, Khariton’s life was not only extremely secret, but also extremely simple. But this is only at first glance.

⟦column⟧

Monument to Yu.B. Khariton near the cottage where he lived for 50 years

He never revealed his soul either to his colleagues or to his relatives and friends; he kept everything important to himself. The last five years of his life coincided with dramatic events in our country. Due to illness, he was no longer able to work closely. And he could not distance himself from thinking about what happened to his country, to the service of which he gave his whole life and all his talent for love of science and people. Let us quote his words, written in 1995 in a letter to American nuclear physicists: “Conscious of my involvement in the remarkable scientific and engineering achievements that led to humanity’s mastery of an almost inexhaustible source of energy, today, in more than a mature age, I am no longer sure that humanity is mature enough to master this energy. I am aware of our involvement in the terrible loss of life, in the monstrous damage caused to the nature of our home - the Earth. Words of repentance will not change anything. God grant that those who come after us find ways, find strength of spirit and determination, striving for the best, and not do the worst.” This is the testament of Yuli Borisovich Khariton to the people of science.
