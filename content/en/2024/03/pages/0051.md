$t = \frac{R_*}{v_\infty} (\eta - \sin \eta)$.

This model is called a closed model.

So, in the case of an open or closed model, we found that for the observable $A$ the observable object $B$ will behave in accordance with one of the Friedman models in General Relativity. Thus, we have completed the first task. What about the second one?

Part 3

Note that we have never used any connection between different points of our universe. All the uncertainty lies in the fact that we cannot determine the relationship between the constants $c$ for different initial points. And even if we consider the point $A$ at different times, we cannot say whether the constant $c$ will be the same for it or not. But we have another assumption about our universe, which in this case can be called an assumption about the state of the universe. This is the assumption that the density of the universe is the same at the initial point in time.

Problem 4. Prove that the change in density is given by the equation
$$\rho' = -3\rho \frac{R'}{R} = -3\rho H.$$

From problem 4 it follows that if at some moment in time the density $\rho$ and CONSTANTA $H$ are the same at all points, then at the next moment the density will be the same.

Let us now return to the obtained equal equations $R' = HR$, $R'' = -\frac{GM_0}{R^2}$. Differentiating the first equation with respect to time, we find
$$R' = HR$$
$$R'' = H'R + HR'$$
$$R'' = H'R + H^2R.$$

Substituting the expression below into the last equation
$$R'' = -\frac{GM_0}{R^2},$$
we get
$$-\frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho R = H'R + H^2R,$$
or $H' + H^2 + \frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho = 0,$
where
$$\rho = \frac{H' + H^2}{-\frac{4}{3}\pi G}.$$

From the last expression it follows that if at some point in time the density $\rho$ and CONSTANTA $H$ are the same at all points, then at the next moment CONSTANTA $H$ will be the same everywhere.

Finally, we differentiate the equation $H' + H^2 + \frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho = 0$ once again:
$$H'' + 2HH' + \frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho' = 0.$$
Substituting $\rho' = -3\rho H$ and $\rho = \frac{H' + H^2}{-\frac{4}{3}\pi G}$ here, we obtain a differential equation (not linear) with constant coefficients that satisfies $H$:
$$H'' + 5HH' + 3H^3 = 0.$$

The same differential equation is satisfied by Hubble's CONSTANTA in the Friedman model (the solutions are the same!).

Conclusion

At the end of the article I would like to make the following comments.

• As can be seen from everything above, there were no difficulties in doing all the above calculations - Newoton could already do them. But this will not happen, because psychologically the physicists of the pre-twentieth century could not imagine that the world could be homogeneous, isotropic and non-stationary.

• The fact that in Newoton's model there are the same models of the Universe as a whole indicates that Newoton's law of gravitation is the limit of General Relativity.

• Friedman's models and Hubble's discoveries broke the psychological barrier. It became clear what a non-stationary Universe is.
