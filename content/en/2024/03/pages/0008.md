KVANT 2024/№3

The difference from the Sarov center is only in one word - technical, not experimental. And one more difference - the Snezhinsky Institute is named after Academician E.I. Zababakhin, while the Sarov Institute has not yet been named after Yu.B. Khariton, despite repeated appeals to the government by scientists and old-timers of Sarov. Regularly, at least twice a month, Khariton took part in meetings of the Scientific and Technical Council under the First Main Directorate of the Council of Ministers of the USSR, which was the headquarters of the entire Atomic Project. And his participation was very active in the discussion of all issues of the project, and not just those related to the “native” KB-11. And for this it was necessary to become familiar with the issues being discussed in advance. Where can I find time for all this? Let us repeat the words of Academician A.M. Shalnikova: “He works as much as normal people cannot work.” Every day from 8 am to 10 pm, sometimes on Sundays, and on Saturdays - always, sometimes until midnight. And definitely on the road. He considered traveling time the most productive time for work, since the means of transportation was not an airplane, but a train (it was not allowed to fly by airplane). Special carriages were equipped for him (as well as for Kurchatov) (with organized hot meals - after all, the journey took up to 10 hours). The allocation of special cars was justified by the creation of working conditions. When Khariton had a traveling companion (for example, I.V. Kurchatov or someone else from the management), he asked not to interfere with his work, and “for the company” he would happily relax for half an hour, starting an interesting conversation with his companion about books or art. One of the trips became very sad. Khariton needed to discuss with Kurchatov the important issue of creating and preparing for testing a bomb with a TNT equivalent of up to one hundred million tons. The penultimate meeting of friends in the Barvikha sanatorium (1950) tests (with a TNT equivalent of 58 megatons) gave rise to the semi-official name of this “product” - the Tsar Bomb. According to the then leadership of the country, such an incredible explosion was needed to intimidate “imperialism.” By his nature, Khariton “like a soldier” carried out the orders of the leadership. But in his heart he was against the creation of such a terrible weapon that could destroy an entire small country. And he knew that Kurchatov was also against it. Khariton and Kurchatov were connected not only by official relations. They became great friends back in the 30s. Therefore, he once again wanted to discuss the problem with Kurchatov before putting his signature on the decision of the scientific and technical council of the Ministry of Medium Engineering to create this bomb. But Kurchatov was near Moscow in a sanatorium after another heart attack. And on February 7, 1960, Khariton arrived at this sanatorium. On a fine winter day, on a bench in the sanatorium garden, they began to discuss the current situation. Carried away by the conversation, Khariton did not immediately notice that Kurchatov had leaned his head on his shoulder. In fact, this was the end - Igor Vasilyevich’s heart stopped.
