He was aware of all the main research and design work down to the smallest detail, knew hundreds of sheets of drawings from memory - all employees were amazed at this knowledge and ability to find optimal solutions to controversial issues. KB-11 developed rapidly. At the same time, work was underway on a plutonium bomb (RDS-1) and a uranium bomb (RDS-2). By the summer of 1949, not only their designs had been developed, but also models of these “special jet engines” had been tested. The tests took place under real conditions of dropping dummy bombs from an airplane from an altitude of 10 kilometers. For these tests, a special airfield was created (in Crimea, north of Evpatoria). The mock-up was the exact design of a bomb, but, of course, without the main nuclear filling, or, as it was written in the documents, “without refueling.” By the way, the bombs themselves were already ready, but the “fuel” was late. 10 kilograms of plutonium were needed, and the capacity of the first plants to produce it was only a few grams per day. The productivity of plants producing pure uranium-235 isotope was also slightly greater. Subsequently, there were many speculative publications on the topic of the independence of Soviet bomb development. In 1984, Khariton himself, in his first interview after the start of declassification of the history of the Atomic Project, said that there were some intelligence materials and, most importantly, very important materials transferred to the USSR by one of the creators of the American bomb, Klaus Fuchs. But Khariton then kept silent about the gigantic research and engineering work that was necessary to bring the American idea to life. And most importantly, the RDS-2, a uranium bomb, was completely developed by KB-11. And all subsequent bomb designs, including hydrogen bombs, were completely independent. I.V. Kurchatov and Yu.B. Khariton are resting in Altai after testing the “hydrogen” RDS-37 (1955) weapon. Ten years later, when the veil of secrecy was greatly reduced, Khariton, in a letter to American physicists, assessed the significance of some American data in a completely different way. It was more important to know not something about the design of the bomb, but about the efforts and achievements that were made in the United States to obtain isotopes of uranium and plutonium. It was the knowledge of these issues that helped, according to I.V. Kurchatov, save at least a year of work on creating a bomb. In 1948, Academician I.E. Tamm was instructed to begin the theoretical development of the idea of ​​a hydrogen bomb. He involved his graduate student A.D. Sakharov in this work, and after he defended his dissertation, Tamm and Sakharov already at KB-11 headed the department for the development of a superbomb. Its first version had the RDS-6 index and was tested in 1953. A.D. Sakharov soon became the main theoretician of the problem (along with Ya.B. Zeldovich) and remained so for 20 years. But Khariton, as the chief designer and chief scientific director of KB-11, was also involved in this problem.
