1. Two artificial satellites move in the same direction around a planet in circular orbits lying in the same plane. Astronauts on the satellites communicate using a laser beam directed from one satellite to another. Periodic satellites find themselves in “blind” spots for some time when the laser beam is blocked by the planet, and communication between the satellites is interrupted. Find the duration t of the satellites’ stay in one of these zones if the radii of the satellites’ orbits are $R_1 = 6,4 \cdot 10^4$ km and $R_2 = 10^5$ km, the radius of the planet $r$ is several thousand kilometers, and the acceleration of free fall on the surface of the planet is $g = 9$ m/s². By refracting the beam in the atmosphere of the planet and the influence of other celestial bodies on the movement of satellites can be avoided.

Note. To simplify calculations, use the approximate formula arcsin $x \approx x$, which is valid for small values ​​of the argument $x$, expressed in radians.

2. A glass tube $l = 1$ m long, hermetically closed at one end, is located vertically from the open end down and is filled with a mixture of air and saturated water vapor. The tube is slowly immersed in water half its length, with the surface of the water in the tube at a depth of $h = 0,45$ m (Fig. 14). Assuming the temperature of the gas mixture in the tube is constant, find the pressure $p_{\text{нас}}$ of saturated water vapor at this temperature. Atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa, water density $\rho_0 = 10^3$ kg/m³. The surface tension of water can be ignored. Take the free fall acceleration equal to $g = 10$ m/s².

3. Two identical metal charged balls with a radius of $r = 2$ cm are located at a large distance from each other. One of the balls is located inside a spherical conducting grounded shell of radius $R$ so that their centers coincide. Through a small insulated hole in this shell, the balls are connected with a thin length of wires. As a result, charges $q_1 = 6 \cdot 10^{-10}$ Cl and $q_2 = 2 \cdot 10^{-10}$ Cl are established on the balls. What is the radius of the shell $R$?

4. On the upper horizontal surface of the liquid layer there is an opaque screen with a small round hole. A flat mirror is placed on the lower boundary of the layer. What will be the radius of the illuminated area on the bottom side of the screen if the hole is illuminated from above with diffuse light? Layer thickness $h = 5$ cm, liquid refractive index $n = 1,5$.

5. The oscillatory circuit consists of a series-connected coil with an inductance of $L = 0,3$ H, a resistor with a resistance of $R = 1$ Ohm and a capacitor of electrical capacity $C = 30$ μF. A weak oscillation damping will occur in the circuit - the energy loss for each subsequent period of oscillation is much less than the energy stored in the circuit at any given time. At some point in time, when the current in the circuit reaches a local maximum value, the direction to the capacitor is $U = 2$ V. What amount of heat $Q$ will be released in the circuit during the subsequent period of oscillation? Take the number $\pi$ equal to 3.14. Express the result in milliinches, rounded to the nearest whole number.
