On August 29, 1949, in a desert area 170 km from Semipalatinsk (now the Kazakh city of Semey), at 6:48 a.m. the explosion control device was turned on and at 7:00 a.m. an explosion occurred on the “RDS-1 product” on a 37-meter tower. It was a plutonium nuclear bomb, its TNT equivalent was 22 kilotons. The machine was switched on for detonation, “pressing a button”, at the direction of Khariton, and the designer of this control machine, Sergei Lvovich Davydov, carried out the operation. Khariton never said what he felt when the explosion occurred. It seems that, like all observers, first of all there came a deep feeling of deliverance from the impending danger of destroying the country with terrible American nuclear weapons. And, of course, a sense of satisfaction and pride in completing incredibly difficult work. Many years later he told his grandson that he was one hundred percent sure of success. Well, maybe it was in keeping with his whole style of work, responsibility, thoroughness and meticulousness. And one more thing. All those present knew that, in addition to the tested RDS-1 bomb, their native KB-11 already had RDS-2 uranium bombs, more powerful and lighter than their American counterparts. Since RDS-1 worked, RDS-2 should also work, and it will be a powerful nuclear shield against the American threat. Perhaps it was then that the catchphrase, remarkable in its brevity and at the same time deep essence, “to recharacterize Oppenheimer,” was born. In this expression, in addition to the similarities, there is also a deep difference in the life and fate of these two great scientists of the past century. First of all, the measured lifespan. If we talk about the length of creative life (starting for both from the age of 16 - the age of entry into university), then for Oppenheimer it is 47 years, and for Khariton - 72. Almost immediately after the anti-human droppings of nuclear bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Oppenheimer first received all-American and then worldwide fame as the “father of the atomic bomb.” Khariton was so secret that for many years (until 1984) his name was not mentioned at all and for another 20 years it was mentioned very rarely. Fame (and even then, in scientific circles, not nationwide) came to him posthumously. This article does not allow us to talk in detail about the main difference between the destinies of Oppenheimer and Khariton. Oppenheimer was not a patriot of his country. For him, the moral question of the ethics of creating atomic and hydrogen bombs was resolved only on a universal scale, in the issue of organizing international control over the creation of nuclear weapons. For some time he was under suspicion of not maintaining secrecy during atomic work. Indeed, the most important information about the American bomb, which accelerated the creation of the Soviet bomb, was received from Klaus Fuchs, one of Oppenheimer's closest colleagues. There were other suspicions related to his pre-war political outlook. As a result of consideration of these issues by the FBI commission, Oppenheimer was deprived of security clearance in 1954 and could only engage in pure physics as director of the Princeton Institute for Advanced Study. Oppenheimer was acquitted in 1963 (during the presidency of J. Kennedy). However, all this caused serious additional trauma to his mental state.
