JULIY BORISOVICH KHARITON

The most important part of Khariton’s work was overseeing the production of bombs at factories No. 1 and No. 2, built on the same territory as KB-11. Back in 1947, Khariton raised the question of building and constructing a special testing ground for full testing of RDS products “charged with fuel,” i.e. real atomic bombs. He participated in choosing the location for this test site and in directing the creation of all the measuring equipment to determine the parameters of the explosion. And, of course, in the preparation of the tests themselves and their implementation. He signed preparation protocols, decisions on testing, and reports on the conduct and results of tests. And he not only signed, but delved deeply and drew conclusions to improve the designs. And although these tests took place very far from Sarov (at the Semipalatinsk and Novaya Zemlya test sites), it was in Sarov that the monument-stele “To Nuclear Weapon Testers” was recently opened. K.I. Shchelkin, Yu.B. Khariton and I.V. Kurchatov on a Sunday walk in Sarov (1954) were no longer enough. And it was decided to create a second center for the design of atomic and hydrogen bombs - KB-1011. Khariton released his closest assistant and first deputy, Kirill Ivanovich Shchelkin, who became the chief designer and scientific director of the new design bureau, and one of the most talented young employees, Evgeniy Ivanovich Zababakhin, to lead this center, who, after Shchelkin’s departure, replaced him in the responsible post of head of KB-1011. There was no enmity between the two design bureaus; their rivalry always turned out to be useful for the cause. And the first “real” hydrogen bomb, RDS-37, with a TNT equivalent of 1.6 megatons, was the joint “brainchild” of Sakharov and Zababakhin, Khariton and Shchelkin. Subsequently, KB-11 specialized in bombs for missiles, and KB-1011 specialized in “products” with other methods of “delivery.” A remote, beautiful area in the Chelyabinsk region was chosen for the deployment of KB-1011. The official name of this design bureau became “Chelyabinsk-70”. They say that once during a meeting the head of the design bureau, looking out the window at the winter landscape, said: “What a beauty! Real Snezhinsk!” Today it is the closed science city of Snezhinsk, which houses the Russian Federal Nuclear Center - the All-Russian Scientific Research Institute of Technical Physics.
