KVANT 2024/№3

Julius Robert Oppenheimer security and there was such confusion that Khariton did not even have anyone to ask permission to invite the American scientist. And he took the risk of doing it himself. Of course, he did not reveal secrets and showed only those laboratories that were not directly related to the design and manufacture of nuclear weapons. He was proud of the “peaceful achievements” that, under his leadership, were made in Sarov for the development of physics as a science. The history of the American atomic project was discussed in lengthy conversations. It turned out that Khariton knew well who Oppenheimer was. “Then I had the idea of ​​comparing these people,” Halloway writes. Moreover, two and a half years later, Khariton himself began his letter with this comparison to the Julius Robert Oppenheimer Memorial Committee, which invited Khariton to take part in events dedicated to the memory of Oppenheimer (ninetieth anniversary of his birth). Khariton regretted that he could not accept this invitation due to his illness, and sent a letter. For the collection dedicated to the memory of Khariton, Halloway wrote the essay “Oppenheimer and Khariton: Parallels of Life.”

⟦column⟧

The main parallel is that they were both the first leaders of the first nuclear weapons centers of their countries. But there are also several coincidences that Khariton himself noted and called them “funny.” This is a coincidence of ages - Oppenheimer is younger by only 2 months and 11 days; coincidence of names - Julius and Julius; an interest in art and literature instilled in childhood in the family (from mothers); almost simultaneous work at Rutherford's Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge. And quite a funny coincidence - not far from Sarov there was the village of Alamasovo (the inhabitants of which had to be relocated to another place). But an analogue of the Sarov KB-11 was a laboratory in the town of Los Alamos, which is quite consonant with the name of the village of Alamasovo. One might add that both almost at the same time defended dissertations related more to chemistry than to physics. Both at the same time (in 1939) became seriously interested in nuclear physics and the interaction of uranium with neutrons and, with a difference of two years, became the actual creators of nuclear weapons design centers in the USA and the USSR. Both of them were present at the first experimental tests of their “products”. On July 16, 1945, in the desert area of ​​New Mexico, several tens of kilometers from the city of Alamagordo, at 5:10 a.m., Oppenheimer turned on the apparatus for checking all electrical systems of the test site and automatically sending a signal to the bomb. At 5.30 there was an explosion. First, there was almost complete silence, illuminated by a flash of light “brighter than a thousand suns” of observers 9 km from the 30-meter-high tower on which the product of Oppenheimer’s laboratory had been installed the day before. It received the code name gadget, which for a long time (in the pre-gadget era) was translated into Russian as a little thing. The TNT equivalent of this plutonium bomb was determined to be 21 kilotons. Oppenheimer himself described his reaction as stunned. Lines from the ancient Indian language he knew well came into his head.
