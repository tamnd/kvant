Let the object be at a distance $a_1$ from the beaker (so the distance from the axis of the beaker to the object is $x = R + a_1$). Since the focal length of a plano-convex "water" lens is $3R$, the distance is $a'_1$ to the image of the object in the lens
$a'_1 = \frac{3Ra_1}{a_1 - 3R}$,
and increasing the image in the first lens
$\Gamma_1 = \left| \frac{3R}{a_1 - 3R} \right|$.

A flat-parella plate of thickness $D$ is inserted between the lenses, which brings objects closer to the second lens at a distance equal to
$\Delta a = D \left( 1 - \frac{1}{n} \right) = \frac{R}{2}$. Thus, the distance from the image in the plane-parallel plate to the second lens
$a_2 = 2R - a'_1 - \Delta a = -\frac{3R(a_1 + 3R)}{2(a_1 - 3R)}$,
and the distance to the image
$a'_2 = \frac{R(a_1 + 3R)}{a_1 - R}$.

From here we find the magnification in the second lens:
$\Gamma_2 = \left| \frac{a'_2}{a_2} \right| = \left| \frac{2(a_1 - 3R)}{3(a_1 - R)} \right|$

and overall increase:
$\Gamma_0 = \left| \frac{2R}{a_1 - R} \right|$.

Considering the values ​​of $a_1$ satisfying the inequality $a_1 > R$, expanding the model and solving the equation, we obtain $a_1 = 2,25R$. Thus, the distance from the axis of the beaker to the wall
$x = R + a_1 = \frac{13}{4}R = 130$ mm.
