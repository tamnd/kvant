In straight lines, the distance between adjacent lattice nodes must be the same - this can be understood from the fact that the entire lattice transforms into itself when rotated around the point $A$ by a right angle. Therefore $AC = AK$ and then the triangle $ABC$ is right-angled.

You can read about the Pick formula, for example, in the article by N. Vasiliev “Around the Pick formula” («Kvant» No. 12, 1974) or in the article by G. Merzon “The Pick formula and thicker ice” («Kvant» No. 9, 2018).

Third solution (proposed by the team of the Nakhimov Naval School, Murmansk). Let's denote $AB = BC = a$, $\angle ABC = \beta$. The area of ​​triangle $ABC$ is equal, on the one hand, to $\frac{1}{2}$, and on the other hand, $\frac{1}{2}a^2\sin\beta$. Let's square and transform:

$$1 = a^4\sin^2\beta = \left(a^2\right)^2 - \left(a^2\cos\beta\right)^2.$$

Since $a$ is the length of the segment with vertices at lattice nodes, then $a^2$ is an integer. The expression $a^2\cos\beta$ is the dot product of the vectors $\overrightarrow{BA}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$, and these vectors have integer coordinates, so their dot product is an integer. Thus, we get that 1 is the difference between the squares of two integers. So these squares are equal to 1 and 0. Thus, $\cos\beta = 0$, so the angle $\beta$ is right, i.e. triangle $ABC$ is right-angled.
