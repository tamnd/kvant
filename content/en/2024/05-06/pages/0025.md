F2790. Water on the International Space Station (MKC) is in a plastic container with thin walls in the shape of a small cube. The packaging was torn, and the water quickly (after hesitating a little) took the shape of a ball. The heat exchange of water with the surrounding influence can be neglected, however, the temperature of the water in the layer ball is higher than the actual temperature of +20°C (in a cube) by $\Delta t = 0,001^\circ$ (at the limit of experimental possibilities, the maximum value can still be changed). How many molecules does this portion of water consist of? Water density $\rho = 1 \text{е}/\text{см}^3$, molar mass $M = 18 \text{е}/\text{моль}$, molar temperament $C = 9R \approx 75 \text{Дж}/(\text{К} \cdot \text{моль}),$ where $R$ is the universal gas constant, tension surface coefficient $\sigma = 0,07 \text{Дж}/\text{m}^2$.

Suppose that the cube was such that its edges had length $L$. Then the volume of the cube is $L^3$, the surface area of ​​the cube is $6L^2$. The volume of the ball $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ is equal to the volume of the cube, and its surface area is $4\pi R^2$. It follows that the difference in surface energies is
$$\sigma L^2 \left(6 - 4\pi \left(\frac{3}{4\pi}\right)^{2/3}\right) = \sigma L^2 \cdot 1,1640.$$
The energy difference between the surface of the water, which was shaped like a cube and now shaped like a ball, causes the water to heat up.
