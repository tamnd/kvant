F2789. The length of the boat is uniform and a flexible rope with a mass of $M = 3,2$ kg is thrown over a monk crossbar with a round cross section and we are in balance. The rope is moved a little and it begins to slide off the crossbar. With what magnitude of force $F$ does the rope act on the crossbar at the moment when the length of the rope $L$ on one side is times greater than on the other? What angle $\alpha$ with the vertical does this force form if the ratio of the diameter $D$ of the crossbar to the length $L$ of the rope is equal to $10^{-2}$?

We assume that the sections of the rope that do not lie on the crossbar are vertical. When one of the ends of the rope is moved $x > 0$ from the equilibrium position, the potential energy loss is equal to $\frac{M}{L} x^2 g$. There is no energy loss. The increase in kinetic energy is equal to that of potential energy:

$$\frac{1}{2} M v^2 = \frac{M}{L} x^2 g.$$

The speed increases in proportion to the coordinate: $v = \sqrt{2 \frac{g}{L}} x$, from where

$$a_x = \sqrt{2 \frac{g}{L}} v = 2 \frac{g}{L} x.$$

At the moment under consideration, at $x = \frac{L}{4}$, the acceleration module of the rope parts is $a = \frac{g}{2}$, the square of the rope speed is $v^2 = \frac{g L}{8}$. Let’s mentally put marks on those points of the rope that are currently at a distance of $D$ from each other and touch the crossbar.
