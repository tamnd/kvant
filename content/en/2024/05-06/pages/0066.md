The work done by external forces is equal to
$$A_{\text{bh}}^{(T)} = 0, 1p_{\text{нас}}V = 0, 1v_0RT,$$
where $v_0 = 1$ mol is the amount of substance in the vessel under the piston. The amount of heat released during the condensation of 0.1 mole of steam is equal to
$$Q_{\text{конд}} = -0, 1v_0ML.$$
On the other side,
$$Q_{\text{конд}} = \Delta U^{(T)} - A_{\text{bh}}^{(T)}.$$
This leads to the second answer:
$$\Delta U^{(T)} = -0, 1v_0ML + A_{\text{bh}}^{(T)} = -0, 1v_0(ML - RT).$$
B. The specific heat of vaporization can be found using the Clapeyron–Clausius equation. Replacing the derivative with the ratio of finite differences, for the gas phase we have
$$L = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta T} \left( \frac{R T^2}{p_{\text{м}}} - \frac{T}{\rho_{\text{ж}}} \right).$$
It is easy to see that the second term is 5 orders of magnitude smaller than the first, so it can be neglected. Calculating the pressure difference for temperatures of 27 °C and 28 °C, we get the answer:
$$L \approx 2, 45 \cdot 10^6 \text{ Дж/кг.}$$

B. Let us transform the first law of thermodynamics using the correspondence $v_0Rdt = pdV + Vdp$:
$$\delta Q = (c_V + R)v_0dT - Vdp.$$
Taking into account the small specific volume, the Clapeyron–Clausis equation has the form
$$\frac{dp}{dT} = \frac{mL}{TV}.$$
Let's substitute $dp$ from this equality into the previous one. After the transformation, we obtain the formula for the molar heat of saturated steam:
$$c = \frac{\delta Q}{v_0dT} = (c_V + R) - \frac{L_{\text{м}}}{T} = c_p - \frac{L_{\text{м}}}{T}.$$
With a small change in temperature $\Delta T$, the supplied amount of heat is approximately equal to
$$Q = v_0 \left( c_p - \frac{L_{\text{м}}}{T} \right) \Delta T.$$
Using the table data, we find that a decrease in volume by 10% corresponds to an increase in temperature by 2 °C. Substituting into the formula for $Q$ the values ​​$\Delta T = 2$ K, $T = 300$ K, $c_p = 4R$ (water vapor is a polyatomic gas), $L \approx 2, 45 \cdot 10^6 \text{ Дж/кг}$ (from the previous paragraph), we obtain
$$Q \approx -228 \text{ Дж.}$$

The change in internal energy is
$$\Delta U^{(нас)} = v_0c_V \Delta T \approx 50 \text{ Дж.}$$

Then
$$A_{\text{bh}}^{(нас)} = \Delta U^{(нас)} - Q \approx 278 \text{ Дж.}$$

D. First, we will make an estimate and try that steam, with an adiabatic decrease in volume, becomes unsaturated, then we will consider it as an ideal gas. We use the adiabatic equation in the form $TV^{\frac{1}{3}} = \text{const}$ and find the temperature when the volume decreases by 10%:
$$T_1 = T_0 (0,9)^{-1/3} \approx 310,7 \text{ К.}$$

Now we can find the change in the internal energy of unsaturated steam:
$$\Delta U^{(Q)} = v_0 \cdot 3R (T_1 - T_0) \approx 266 \text{ Дж}$$

and the work of external forces:
$$A_{\text{bh}}^{(Q)} = \Delta U^{(Q)} \approx 266 \text{ Дж.}$$
