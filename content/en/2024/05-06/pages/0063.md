B1. The number of “pushing” cycles $N$ can be determined by knowing the initial and final amounts of gas in the chamber. During one cycle, air from the pump is pushed into the chamber in the amount of $\Delta v = \frac{p_0V_C}{RT_0}$, therefore
$$N = \frac{(p - p_0)V_0}{RT_0\Delta v} = \frac{(p - p_0)V_0}{p_0V_C}.$$
B2. During an adnabatic ramp from pressure $p_0$ to pressure $p_i$, the piston performs work equal to the change in the internal energy of the air removed in the pump:
$$A_{Q=0} = \frac{5}{2} p_0V_C \left( \frac{T'}{T_0} - 1 \right),$$
where $T'$ is the temperature at the end of the ramp. Using the adnabata equation $Tp^{-2/7}$ = const, the expression for work can be written as
$$A_{Q=0} = \frac{5}{2} p_0V_C \left( \left( \frac{p_i}{p_0}\right)^{2/7} - 1 \right).$$
Further work is done at constant pressure. It is equal
$$A_p = p_iV' = p_iV_C \left( \frac{p_0}{p_i}\right)^{5/7} = p_i^{2/7}p_0^{5/7}V_C.$$
Summing these two works, we get
$$A_i = A_{Q=0} + A_p = \frac{7}{2} p_i^{2/7}p_0^{5/7}V_C - \frac{5}{2} p_0V_C.$$
B3. Due to the work $A_i$, the internal energy of the air that was already in the chamber at the beginning of the $i$th cycle and the portion that is “pushed” into the chamber changes. The change in internal energy is
$$\Delta U_i = \frac{5}{2} p_iV_0 - \frac{5}{2} \left( p_{i-1}V_0 + p_0V_C \right).$$
Equating the change in internal energy and work, taking into account the equality $\Delta p_i = p_i - p_{i-1}$ we have
$$\Delta p_i = \frac{7}{5} p_i^{2/7}p_0^{5/7}V_C/V_0.$$
B4. Integrating the last formula, we obtain
$$\frac{7}{5} \left( p_k^{5/7} - p_0^{5/7} \right) = \frac{7}{5} p_0^{5/7} \frac{V_C}{V_0} k.$$
Having made the transformations, we find
$$p_k = p_0 \left( 1 + \frac{V_C}{V_0} k \right)^{7/5}.$$
B5. We substitute the number of cycles $k = N$ into the last formula for pressure and find
$$p_{\max} = p_0 \left( 1 + \frac{p - p_0}{p_0} \right)^{7/5} = p_0 \left( \frac{p}{p_0} \right)^{7/5} \approx 4,7 \cdot 10^5 \text{ Pa}.$$
From Charles' law we get
$$T_{\max} = T_0 \frac{p_{\max}}{p} \approx 466 \text{ K}.$$
