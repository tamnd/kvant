The Erdős-Lovas conjecture that the lower bound is unimprovable has existed for a long time. However, in 1996, P. Frankl, K. Ota and N. Tokushite, in a joint work, refuted it, proving that $M(n) > (n/2)^n$. This estimate differs from its predecessor by approximately $(e/2)^n$ times, which is a significant weight. So far no one has been able to improve it.
The history of the upper estimates is longer. In 1994, J. Tuza slightly refined the estimate $n^n$, replacing it with $(1-1/e+f(n))n^n$, where $f(n)$ is some function that tends to zero as $n$ increases. In 2011, D. SIGN OF DIVISION ON A GRAPH
Let's show how, using the graph shown in figure, you can check the divisibility of a number by 7. For example, consider the number 3248. We will proceed as follows. Starting from the white top, take 3 steps along the red arrows, then one step along the blue arrow, then 2 steps along the red arrows, then again one step along the blue arrow, then 4 steps along the red ones and so on. If at the end we find ourselves at the white vertex again, then the number is divisible by 7. Try to prove this wonderful fact.
