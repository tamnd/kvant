It's interesting that...
...the ancient Greek scientist Apollonius of Perga anticipated the coordinate method 200 years before the new era. A little later, the concepts were broad and elaborated by Hipparchus of Nicaea, and their exact mathematical concept was proposed by Claudius Ptolemy already in Pveke.
...for the first time in the history of science, using a graphical representation of motion corresponding to the modern coordinate method, the 14th century French scientist Nicholas Oresme established the law of uniformly alternating motion.
...Descartes was the first to identify a point on a line with a number, thereby developing the coordinate method, which opened global connections between algebra and geometry. The very term “coordinates” was coined by the German scientist Gottfried Leibniz in 1692.

...the great Archimedes, considered the founder of the rational theory of centers of gravity, discovered the ability to prove new mathematical facts using their properties, thereby becoming the founder of the so-called barcentric method.

...in the middle of the 19th century, the German scientist August Möbis proposed a new, not similar to Cartesian or polar, coordinate system - barcentric, which turned out to be rich in applications to geometry, chemistry, genetics, statistics, power machines, etc.

...a major contribution to the doctrine of statics was made by Galileo's student Evangelista Torricelli, who developed a formula for the coordinates of the center of gravity of a solid body or a flat figure. Alas, later this formula was not associated with the name of the scientist in any textbook.

...the theory of relative is based on the legitimizing connection between space and time. Therefore, it is natural to consider the fourth coordinate of any event, in addition to the three spatial ones, to be the moment in time when this event occurs. Such a four-dimensional space is called Minkowski space.

...in 2003, two teams of builders, building towards each other across the river between Germany and Switzerland, could not make up half of it. It turned out that the Germans reported the vertical coordinate of the place from the North Sea, and the Ashevtsaritsa from the Mediterranean, although the difference in their levels was 27 centimeters.
