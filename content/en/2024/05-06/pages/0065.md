3. The speed values ​​of both the proton and the electron do not change over time, but the speed vector rotates. If we denote the angle formed by the normal to the interface and the particle velocity vector by $\alpha$, then the penetration depth at the moment $t$
$$x(t) = \int_{0}^{t} v \cos \alpha \cdot dt.$$
At this depth, the magnetic field induction
$$B(x) = \frac{B(L)x(t)}{L},$$
where $B(L)$ is the induction at a penetration depth into the transition section equal to $L$.
Rotation speed of the particle velocity vector
$$\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = \frac{qB(L)}{Lm} x(t),$$ or $$\frac{d^2\alpha}{dt^2} = \frac{qB_Lv}{Lm} \cos \alpha.$$
Since the speed rotation angle is fixed and equal to $\pi$, then the turn time
$$T \sim \sqrt{\frac{Lm}{qvB_L}}.$$
Since the mass of an electron is 1838 times less than the mass of a proton, the electron will need $\sqrt{1838}$ less time to turn around, i.e. $\tau_e \approx 0,023$ p. The depth of penetration of an electron into a region with a magnetic field will be the same factor less.
