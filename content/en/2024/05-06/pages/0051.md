Let the bases of the trapezoid be $a$ and $b$, with $a < b$ (Fig. 7). We will assume that the bases are located horizontally. A trapezoid makes up $b - a$ horizontal stripes. There are $2a + 1$ triangles in the top, and $2b - 1$ in the bottom, each strip has 2 more triangles than the strip above it. It turns out to express $N$, i.e. total number of triangles, you need to find the sum of the arithmetic progression (in steps of 2): $(2a + 1) + (2a + 3) + \dots + (2b - 1)$. This can be done by multiplying the number of terms of the progression by the first and last terms, we get $N = (b - a)(b + a) = b^2 - a^2$. This could be understood differently: if we extend the lateral sides of the trapezoid until they intersect, we find that the trapezoid is obtained from an equilateral triangle with side $b$ by removing an equilateral triangle with side $a$. The number of triangles in an equilateral triangle with side $n$ is equal to $n^2$ (about this, see, for example, the article by E. Bakaev “Drawing the sum of odd numbers” in the magazine “Quaitik” No. 3 for 2015). From here we get $N = b^2 - a^2$.

So, it remains to understand which numbers $N$ can be represented as the difference of the squares of two natural numbers. Note that the numbers $(b - a)$ and $(b + a)$ are of the same parity, so $N = (b - a)(b + a)$ is either odd or divisible by 4.
