Problem 5. Snell's archive
They say that a drawing of an optical system was found in Snell's archive (Fig. 9). Over time, the ink has faded, and the remains of the drawing show only the course of parallel rays through two thin lines and the point $L$, which belongs to the planes of both lines.
1) Restore by constructing the positions of the planes of both lines.
2) Based on the available data, determine the type of each line (collecting or scattering).
3) Find the positions of the optical centers and main foci of the line.
Application. Consider the principles of constructing parallel and perpendicular lines passing through a given point, dividing a segment in half and similar standard geometric procedures known. These geometric constructions do not prove.

A.Vergunov

Problem 1. Tricky gun
The speed at which a projectile leaves a toy gun depends on the angle from vertical at which the shot is fired. The peculiarity of this dependence is that the angular velocity of rotation of the projectile velocity vector immediately after the shot does not depend on the angle at which the shot was fired and is equal to $\omega = 0,5$ rad/s. The cannon is located at the foot of the length of the hill, forming an angle $\varphi = 30^\circ$ with the horizon (Fig. 10). The trajectory of the projectile lies in the plane of the drawing and is such that at a certain moment the projectile falls onto the hill. Air resistance, as well as the dimensions of the gun and projectile, can be neglected. What is the maximum possible flight time of the projectile $t_{\max}$ before falling on the hill?

A.Uymin
