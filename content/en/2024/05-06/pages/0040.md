ki. Remembering her school lessons, she decided to use a sieve for pickling cucumbers. In winter, Baba Yaga made very dense snowballs, which she covered on the outside with a thin crust of ice. With these snowballs she filled a birch tub with a volume of $V = 12$ liters to the brim, the mass of the snowballs turned out to be $m_1 = 4$ kg. She covered all the empty snowballs with table salt, then filled the tub to the top with cold water and put it outside. The next day, she was surprised to find only very salty water in the tub. It is known that $m_2 = 6$ kg of densely compacted snow is placed in the tub, the density of which coincides with the density of snow in snowballs. Consider that while filling the tub, the water seeps between the grains of salt, but the ice crust protects the snowballs from getting wet. Canning the snowballs occurs quickly, and the salt does not have time to react with the snowballs. When salt is dissolved in water, the volumes of water and salt add up. The table salt bought by Baba Yaga was poured into packs measuring $18 \times 10 \times 6$ cm and weighing $m_\pi = 1$ kg. The density of water is $\rho_0 = 1000 \text{ кг}/\text{m}^3$, the density of crystalline table salt is $\rho_c = 2150 \text{ кг}/\text{m}^3$. The mass and volume of the ice crust of the snowball can be neglected.
1) Determine the bulk density $\rho_{\text{нас}}$ of table salt.
2) What mass $m_c$ of salt was poured into the tub?
3) What volume $V_B$ does salt water occupy?
4) What is the mass $M$ of salt water in the tub?
5) What is the density $\rho_K$ of salt water in the tub?

N. Bogoslovsky
