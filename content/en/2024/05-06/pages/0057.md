7. 98!.
Example. Let the numbers $\sqrt{2}, 1, 2, \dots, 98$ be written on the cards. Then in the unsuccessful pile, card $\sqrt{2}$ should be on top, and the remaining cards can be placed in any of the 98! ways. In this case, any sum of numbers on several vertices of cards will have the form $\sqrt{2} + n$, where $n$ is a natural number, i.e. will be irrational.
Grade. Let's break all the stacks of cards into 98! groups, in each of which one stack is obtained from another by cyclic permutation (i.e., by moving several tops of cards from the wizz stack). Each group consists of 99 stacks. Let us prove that each group contains at least one unsuccessful pile.
Let's assume that a group without unsuccessful piles has found it. Let's select one of the piles and arrange the cards from it in a circle in the same order in which they lie in the pile: $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{99}$. Then all stacks from the group will look like $a_i, a_{i+1}, \dots, a_{99}, a_1, \dots, a_{i-1}$ for $i$ from 1 to 99.
Let's start going from card $a_1$ clockwise until the sum of the numbers $a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_j$ on the passed cards becomes rational. Next, we’ll start going from $a_{j+1}$ until the sum of the numbers on the passed cards becomes rational. Such a moment will occur because the corresponding stack starting with $a_{j+1}$ is not unlucky. Let's perform the described operation 100 times. According to the Dirichlet principle, he will find a card $a_t$ with which the sum begins at least twice. Let's leave only the steps between the first and second report from the card $a_t$ (including the first, but not including the second).
Let's look at the sum $S$ of numbers passed (which number is counted as many times as it is passed). On the one hand, $S$ is rational, since we took segments of cards with a rational sum. On the other hand, we started with card $a_t$ and ended with card $a_{t-1}$, i.e. walked several full circles. It follows from this that the sum of all the numbers completed will be equal to $nS'$, where $n$ is the number of laps completed, and $S'$ is the sum of the numbers on all cards. However, $nS'$ is irrational by condition, which means $nS'$ is also irrational. Contradiction.
Thus, each group has at least one unsuccessful pile. Then the total number of unsuccessful stacks is at least $99! \cdot \frac{1}{99} = 98!$.
Comment. There are other examples. For example, you can take cards $1 + \sqrt{2}, 2 + \sqrt{2}, \dots, 50 + \sqrt{2}, 1 - \sqrt{2}, 2 - \sqrt{2}, \dots, 49 - \sqrt{2}$. Let's call the first 50 cards positive and the rest negative. In an unlucky pile consisting of these cards, the first card must be positive, and for any $k$ among the first $k$ cards there must be more positive cards than negative ones. The number of unsuccessful stacks in this case will be 98!.
