And isn’t there a better definition that follows from the famous “Tuorig test”, formed by him in the article “Computing Machinery and Mind”, published in 1950 in the Oxford philosophical journal Mind: “a machine can be considered intelligent if it is able to carry on a conversation with one person who cannot distinguish it from another person”? Option: “a machine can be considered artificial intelligence if you can talk to it like a person, or even better.” Further, better, apparently, means that the machine will not scold you or call you a fool, but will simply politely suggest that you think again.

What can artificial intelligence do? In principle, a lot. For example:
• fish research and data mining;
• management of financial flows both on the scale of companies and on the scale of the state;
• making financial and economic decisions in the city in a shorter period of time than is done by humans;
• building a credit risk model;
• industrial robotization;
• robotization of scientific research;
• creation of new structural materials;
• complete machine medical diagnostics;
• transition to free transport;
• transition from games between people to games between people and machines;
• machine art (painting and music);
• the ability to conveniently communicate between a person and a machine (smartphones and laptops).

In this impressive list, unfortunately, only two positions have been mastered so far. However, one of them is a gaming one. According to competent experts, there are no longer games in which a person could defeat a machine. The first game to fall in battle with the machine was chess. In 1997, in his third match with the Deep Blue machine, then world champion Garry Kasparov lost a six-game match with a score of 3.5:2.5. And after this the ethicist could no longer beat the artificial intelligence at chess.

A similar situation arose in the ancient game of “go”, widespread in the eastern countries, grand chess. Unlike chess, the board for this game is lined with 361 lines (19 by 19), one player has 181 black, the other 180 white “stones”, the stones are placed in turn at the points of intersection of the lines. The goal of the game is to surround the board’s territory with your stones as quickly as possible, while the enemy’s stones that are surrounded are eliminated from the game. The game is much more abstract than chess, and has a very deep strategy.
