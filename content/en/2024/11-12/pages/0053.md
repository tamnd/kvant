5. 10.
Since the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is enclosed in a circle, the sum of its opposite angles is $180^\circ$, which implies that $\angle EDO = 180^\circ - \angle ADO = \angle ABC$ (Fig. 7). The center of the hanging circle lies at the intersection of the bisectors of the triangle, so $\angle DEO = \angle BEO$, and the point $O$ lies on the bisector of the angle $B$. Let the bisector $EO$ of angle $AEB$ intersect $AB$ at point $M$. Triangles $DEO$ and $BEM$ are similar at two angles, from where
$$\frac{ED}{BE} = \frac{DO}{BM} = \frac{EO}{EM}.$$
Applying the property of equal fractions, we get
$$\frac{ED + DO}{BE + BM} = \frac{EO}{EM},$$
follow,
$$ED + DO = \frac{(BE + BM) \cdot EO}{EM}.$$
It is known that the bisector of a triangle divides the opposite side in proportion to the adjacent sides. Applying this property to the triangle $BEM$ and the bisector $BO$, we obtain $\frac{BE}{BM} = \frac{EO}{OM}$, whence $\frac{EO}{EM} = \frac{BE}{BM + BE}$. We have the final
$$ED + DO = (BE + BM) \cdot \frac{EO}{EM} = \frac{(BE + BM) \cdot \frac{BE}{BM + BE}}{BE} = BE.$$
Thus, the sum $ED + DO$ can take on only one value: 10.
6. 17.
Let's formulate the problem in graph language. There is a tree that has four vertices with degrees 3, 4, 5 and 7, and the remaining vertices are hanging (degree 1). You need to find the number of vertices in this tree.
Let $x$ be the number of highest vertices. Let's find the number of edges. On the one hand, it is equal to half the sum of the degrees of the vertices, i.e. $\frac{3 + 4 + 5 + 7 + x}{2} = \frac{19 + x}{2}$. On the other hand, the number of edges in a tree is 1 less than the number of vertices. Since there are $x + 4$ vertices, there are $x + 3$ edges.
We obtain the equation $\frac{19 + x}{2} = x + 3$, from which we find that $x = 13$. This means that the tree has $x + 4 = 17$ vertices.
