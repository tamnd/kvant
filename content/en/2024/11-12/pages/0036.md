71 knots
K.KOKHAS

- Of course, works of art do not age,” explained colleague Sprud. - But I don’t like meaningless fitifloshki. I would like an antique item to be functional, so that it is not just ancient, but at the same time - gurgle - how can I say it, high-tech in its class.
- “Understood,” said Gargoyles. - I think I can help you. Do you know what the Egyptian triangle is?
- I hear it once, gurgle!
- An absolutely brilliant instrument, although it seems a little rustic. Used in the construction of the Egyptian pyramids!
- “What are you saying,” colleague Sprud became interested.
- This is a rope tied into a ring with 12 knots tied on it. With its help, right angles were measured. This is amazing! To measure a right angle, you take the first, fourth and eighth knots and pull them apart to form a triangle. Then the angle at the vertex of which there is a four knot is a straight angle!
- Curious, curious,” muttered colleague Sprud, looking at the drawing (Fig.1).
- But few people know, continued Gargoyles, that they have a much more advanced version of this instrument. This...
ancient Sumerian triangle!
The gargoyle took from the desk drawer a beautiful, rather shabby rope, on which a lot of knots were tied.
- “There is little left of the ancient Sumerians,” Gargoyles said with a hint of regret. - True masterpieces are too fragile. This is just a later imitation. But time cannot erase the intellectual aura of this subject! Look, there are not 12 knots here, but significantly more - 71. At that time, they had not yet gotten around to identifying this rope in the ring. To construct a right angle ABC, we must count nodes from the first node A: we will denote the 21st node as B, and the 42nd as C (Fig.2). The last one, i.e. 71st knot, compatible with knot A. Of course, counting ten knots is quite troublesome,” Gargoyle improvised, “but I assure you, it’s worth it.” This isn't a crude 3, 4, 5 hack. In fact, it's 5 times more accurate! You see: by pulling nodes A, B, C, we get a triangle from sides 20, 21 and 29, and it is rectangular!
- I'll take it! - Sprud’s colleague finally pecked.
