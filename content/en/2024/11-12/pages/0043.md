But here's what's interesting. On the speedometer and on the steelyard we see the number zero. There is also a zero on the thermometer at figure 4. But on the barometer and on the medical thermometer (Fig. 6) there is no zero. Why? Moreover, on the scale of the aneroid barometer we see the words storm, rain, wind, variable, clear, dry, dry

Fig. 6. Medical thermometer

⟦column⟧

What does the abbreviation “v.” mean here? Why are explanatory words written on the barometer scale? There are no words on the scales of other devices. Let's try to answer these questions. Atmospheric pressure is a variable quantity. It depends on the place on earth and on time (and also on altitude, of course). For example, on December 4, 1954, a record atmospheric pressure was recorded in Moscow - 767 mm Hg. Art. And on December 4, 2022, this record was broken. Another example - on November 23, 1970, the pressure was 720 mm Hg. Art. and it was considered a record for that day. But on November 23, 2023, the pressure dropped (i.e. decreased) to 719.7 mmHg. Art. As we can see, the range of atmospheric pressure does not exceed 60 mm Hg. Art. This is less than 10% of the normal pressure of 745–755 mmHg. Art. As a result, an ordinary (not weather-dependent) person does not feel fluctuations in atmospheric pressure, and therefore the numbers on the barometer scale do not tell him anything. That is why explanatory words are written on the barometer! And those numerical pressure readings that are not observed in nature are not written at all. Likewise, a medical thermometer should not read above 42 degrees. At high temperatures, blood proteins coagulate, which is deadly. The other extreme is low temperature. Values ​​below 32 degrees are incompatible with human life.
