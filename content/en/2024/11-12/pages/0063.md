3. Let us first consider the state of the circuit when the switch is in position 1. It is clear that in this case the potential of the outer sphere is $\varphi_3 = 0$ and the potential of the inner sphere is $\varphi_1 = -\varepsilon$. Consequently, the charges of these spheres satisfy the equations

$$q_1 + q_3 = 0, \frac{q_1}{4\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{q_3}{12\varepsilon_0 a} = -\varepsilon,$$

where

$$q_1 = -6\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon, q_3 = +6\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon \approx 0,367 \text{ нКл}.$$

In this state, the potential of the sphere relative to infinity is negative, so that after the switch, a charge $q'_2$ flows into it from the “ground”. The sum of the charges of the inner and outer sphere remains equal to zero, but to maintain the potential difference, the charge $\Delta q$ moves between them. Thus, the new charges of the sphere are determined by the equations

$$q'_1 + q'_3 = 0,$$

$$\frac{q'_1}{8\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{q'_2}{8\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{q'_3}{12\varepsilon_0 a} = 0,$$

$$\frac{q'_1 + q'_2 + q'_3}{12\varepsilon_0 a} - \left( \frac{q'_1}{4\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{q'_2}{8\varepsilon_0 a} + \frac{q'_3}{12\varepsilon_0 a} \right) = \varepsilon,$$

where

$$q'_1 = -\frac{72}{11} \varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon \approx -0,400 \text{ нКл},$$

$$q'_2 = +\frac{24}{11} \varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon \approx +0,133 \text{ нКл},$$

$$q'_3 = +\frac{72}{11} \varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon \approx +0,400 \text{ нКл}.$$

Note that $\Delta q = q'_3 - q_3 = \frac{6}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon$, i.e. the source after the switch does work

$$A = \varepsilon\Delta q = \frac{6}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon^2.$$

To analyze the change in the energy of capacitors, it is easier to imagine a system of conducting bodies consisting of three spheres and an “earth”, which we can also consider spheres, but of infinite radius, as a connection of three spherical capacitors: formed by the inner and middle spheres, formed by the middle and outer spheres and formed by the outer sphere and “earth”.

Using the formula $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 \frac{r_1 r_2}{r_2 - r_1}$ for the capacitance of a spherical capacitor with plate radii $r_1$ and $r_2$, we determine the capacitance of the capacitors:

$$C_{12} = 8\pi\varepsilon_0 a, C_{23} = 24\pi\varepsilon_0 a, C_{34} = 12\pi\varepsilon_0 a.$$

Switch Do $q_{12} = q_{23} = 6\pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon$, $q_{34} = 0$ and the energy of this capacitor system

$$W = \frac{q_{12}^2}{2C_{12}} + \frac{q_{23}^2}{2C_{23}} = 3\pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon^2.$$

After switch $q'_1 = \frac{72}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon, q'_2 = \frac{48}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon,$

$$q'_3 = \frac{24}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon$$ and the energy of the capacitor system

$$W' = \frac{q'_1^2}{2C_{12}} + \frac{q'_2^2}{2C_{23}} + \frac{q'_3^2}{2C_{23}} = \frac{36}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon^2.$$

From the energy balance equation found in the switch circuit:

$$Q = A - \Delta W = \frac{3}{11} \pi\varepsilon_0 a\varepsilon^2 \approx 0,334 \text{ нДж}.$$
