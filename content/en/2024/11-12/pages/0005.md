UNEXPECTED DECISION OF THE NOBEL COMMITTEE

And one more thing. Alfred Nobel's will clearly stated that prizes should be awarded for achievements that benefit humanity, and, according to the text of the will, not sometime in the future, but already in the year the prize was awarded. And work in computer science in this sense has a great advantage over physical research. For example, for work on experimental laser instruments for creating attosecond light pulses, which were awarded last year’s Nobel Prize in Physics, it is impossible to even roughly imagine the timing of their use in any devices that improve people’s living conditions. And the results of work on artificial intelligence are here, in everyone’s hands, or on their desks, or in their pockets. Over the past couple of decades, research on artificial intelligence and its applications has become so numerous that the Nobel Committee on Physics, having decided to turn to this branch of science, was in great difficulty - there were too many good and already implemented works. And when choosing the laureates, he chose as a criterion the connection of works in computer science with physics and, from a large number of nominees, he singled out those who used specific physical ideas in their works. Both laureates were physicists in their first works.
