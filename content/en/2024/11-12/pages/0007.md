Geoffrey Hinton is a British-Canadian scientist, computer scientist, and cognitive psychologist. He was born in 1947 into a family of prominent Londoners. His grandmother's uncle, his great-great-uncle, so to speak, George Everest was a geographer and chief surveyor of India. It was he who “discovered” the Himalayan peaks and determined their height. In his honor, the highest land peak on earth, Chomolungma, is also called Everest. Jeffrey's father is an entomologist, his mother is a writer. The cousin is a nuclear physicist, one of the very few female scientists who participated in the American Manhattan Project (the creation of the atomic bomb). The boy's parents encouraged his interest in mathematics and natural sciences. He remembered his mother’s order: “Be an academic or a failure.” He became an academician. Hinton received his bachelor's degree from the University of Cambridge, but not in mathematics or physics, but in experimental psychology. At the age of 31, he became a doctor of science at the University of Edinburgh - defended his dissertation on artificial intelligence. And since then, for 45 years now, that’s all he’s been doing. He worked at different universities, but eventually settled in Canada, becoming a professor at the computer department.
