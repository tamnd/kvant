UNEXPECTED DECISION OF THE NOBEL COMMITTEE

But all this is for now – plans, projects, outlines. What's the matter? What is the reason why Russia lags behind the leading countries of the world in this matter? There are several reasons. The main thing is the lack of well-trained information technology specialists and the lack of supercomputers, without which work in this direction cannot be carried out. But, perhaps, the main reason is the skeptical attitude of more than 50% (according to social research) of the Russian business community towards artificial intelligence, as a fashionable novelty that will never replace decision-making by a person, not a machine. It should be recognized that this mistrust has deep roots and is not unique to Russia. Indeed, if we consider artificial intelligence not as an assistant, but as a full partner in human life, then a number of questions immediately arise. One of them is a decrease in the number of jobs in the productive and service sectors. Fewer and fewer people will be needed to work physically (i.e. with their hands). Where to earn a living? A certain number of workers will be needed to deploy work on artificial intelligence. But some people will simply not be capable of such work, where it is no longer skillful and hardworking hands that are important, but developed brains. The next problem is education. Education should become such that it develops the brain and the ability to learn. Currently this is happening in individual schools, with individual teachers. But for the most part, the entire system of mass school education does not teach independent thinking, but rather weans it. And to some extent, the university system too. And this is not only a Russian problem. This is a worldwide problem. You need to think about this now and take some steps. Thus, at the sociological faculty of Moscow State University named after M.V. Lomonosov one of the topics of bachelor's graduation works is devoted to

the problem of changing the educational system in the era of the introduction of artificial intelligence. A start has been made. But there is another global issue related to the mass implementation of artificial intelligence. In March 2023, Elon Musk led the organization of obtaining signatures for an open letter to the governments of all countries. The letter is entitled “Suspend giant experiments with artificial intelligence.” In addition to a specific proposal to stop work on creating more powerful artificial intelligence systems than GPT-4 for six months, the letter talks about the risks of artificial intelligence, such as “obsolescence of humanity” and “loss of control over society as a whole.” The letter was signed by several thousand computer scientists and artificial intelligence, including both future Nobel laureates in physics in 2024. After the prize was awarded, John Hopfield said: "As a physicist, I get very nervous about things that are out of my control." And Geoffrey Hinton left his post as vice president of Google “in order to be able to speak freely about the risks of artificial intelligence.” John Hopfield compared modern AI developments to the discovery of uranium fission in 1938. Until recently, it seemed that history had put an end to the risks of this discovery and that ultimately it brought humanity the benefit of environmentally friendly energy production. But now the scales of history have tipped again on this issue. It turned out that “control over society as a whole” does not exist. Or maybe it's not so scary. And even just one fact of a sharp increase in the quality of human communication after the creation and implementation of the GPT-4 system in the scales of history will become more important than the risks of artificial intelligence. I'd like to think so.
