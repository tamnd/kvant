5. The first number is higher.
To compare two numbers, consider their difference
$$\Delta = \left( 5 - 4 \sin \frac{3\pi}{14} \right) - \left( 4 \cos \frac{\pi}{7} - 5 \sin \frac{\pi}{14} \right).$$
Denoting $-\frac{\pi}{14} = x$, we can rewrite
$$\Delta = 4 \sin 3x + 5 - 4 \cos 2x - 5 \sin x.$$
Using the formula for the sine of a triple angle and the cosine of a double angle, this difference can be reduced to the form
$$4 \sin 3x + 5 - 4 \cos 2x - 5 \sin x = -16 \sin^3 x + 8 \sin^2 x + 7 \sin x + 1.$$
Let us make the change $\sin x = t$ and prove that the function $f(t) = -16t^3 + 8t^2 + 7t + 1$ is non-negative on the interval $[-1;1]$. Indeed, $f(-1) > 0$, $f(1) = 0$, and the derivative $f'(t) = -48t^2 + 16t + 7$ is equal to zero at points $t = \frac{7}{12}$ and $t = -\frac{1}{4}$, with $t = \frac{7}{12}$ being the maximum point and $t = -\frac{1}{4}$ being the minimum point at which the value $f\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = 0$ is taken. Since the value of $\sin \frac{-\pi}{14}$ is different from 1 and from $-\frac{1}{4}$, we get that $\Delta > 0$, so the first number is greater than the second.

If the pyramid is a tetrahedron, then it is enough to select any 4 points that do not lie in the same plane. This can be done in $C_{12}^4 - C_{7}^4 = 495 - 35 = 460$ ways (the case when all 4 points lie in the $\alpha$ plane is subtracted). If the base of the pyramid contains a polygon with at least 4 vertices, then this base lies in the $\alpha$ plane. The number of ways to select such a polygon is equal to the number of ways to select any subset of given points of the plane $\alpha$, consisting of at least four elements, i.e. $C_{7}^4 + C_{7}^5 + C_{7}^6 + C_{7}^7 = 35 + 21 + 7 + 1 = 64$. The vertex can be chosen in 5 ways, so the number of such pyramids is $5 \cdot 64 = 320$.
As a result, we get 460 + 320 = 780 ways.
7. $\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$.
Let the side of the base of the pyramid be $a$, the side edge be $b$, and $\angle SAB = \alpha$ (Fig. 15). Wire the height of the isosceles triangle $ABS$ to the base $AB$, we find that $\cos \alpha = \frac{AB}{2AS} = \frac{a}{2b}$. From here
$$\sin^2 \alpha = 1 - \left( \frac{a}{2b} \right)^2 = \frac{4b^2 - a^2}{4b^2},$$
and $\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2b} \sqrt{4b^2 - a^2}$.
Let us project the primes $AD$ and $SF$ onto the plane $SAB$ parallel to the straight line $AD$ (Fig. 16). Since the segment $XY$ is parallel to the projection plane, the length of its projection $X'Y'$ is equal to the length of the segment $XY$. The problem was reduced to finding the distance $\rho$ from point $Y'$ to line $SF'$, where $F'$ is the projection of point $F$. Pulachai
$$\rho = \frac{2S_{F'Y'S}}{F'S};$$
$F'S^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos(180^\circ - \alpha) = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab \cdot \frac{a}{2b} = 2a^2 + b^2;$
$2S_{F'Y'S} = ab \sin \alpha = \frac{a}{2} \sqrt{4b^2 - a^2}$.
So, $\rho = \frac{a}{2} \sqrt{\frac{4b^2 - a^2}{2a^2 + b^2}}$. Substituting $a = 2$, $b = 4$ here, we find that $\rho = \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$.
