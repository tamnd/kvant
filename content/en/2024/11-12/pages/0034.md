KVANT 2024/No. 11–12

COMPETITION NAMED AFTER A.P. SAVINA

We continue our next competition to solve mathematical problems. The tasks are designed mainly for students starting from grades 8–9, and we advise younger students to try their hand at the magazine competition «Kvant» (see website kvantik.com). Send solutions to problems you can solve to: savin.contest@gmail.com. In addition to your first and last name, indicate the city, school and class in which you study. We welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by teams (in this case, one work is sent with a list of participants). You can participate starting from any tour. The winners will receive diplomas from the magazine «Kvant» and prizes. Assignments, solutions and results are published on sites.google.com/view/savin-contest We wish you success!

9. From a checkered tablecloth with side square 1, cut out a rectangle with sides parallel to the sides of the squares, as in figure. The total area of ​​the white part of the rectangle is 10. Find its perimeter. G. Karavaev

10. Is it possible to represent the number 202420242024202420242024 as the sum of six natural numbers, each of which is written a) only with the numbers 0 and 4; b) only in numbers 4? E. Bakaev

11. Misha watched “What? Where? When?" and wrote out the score, starting from 0:0 until the end of the game (one point is played in each round; the game ends when spectators or experts score 6 points). If the spectators had more points, Misha made a note with a blue pen, if the experts had more points, with a red pen, and if there was a draw, with a green pen. Could it be that there were equal numbers of red, blue and green entries? M. Evdokimov

12. Is it possible to cover the entire surface of some convex polyhedron in one layer with one regular decagon and several squares? E. Bakaev

13. There are 50 light bulbs in the room. Each light bulb has two switches connected to it, each either on or off. The light comes on only when both switches are connected

to it, included. At first there were 15 light bulbs in the room, and when all 100 switches were turned on, 24 light bulbs began to light. How many switches now need to be switched for all the lights to light up? G. Karavaev

14. a) Given a triangle with area 4. Mark a point in it and connect it to the vertices, resulting in three triangles. If their areas are equal to 1, 1, 2, then we call such a point good. Consider a triangle with vertices at three good points. 1) Prove that it is similar to the original triangle. 2) Find its area. b) Given a triangle with area 6. Mark a point in it and connect it to the vertices, resulting in three triangles. If their areas are 1, 2, 3, then we call such a point good. Find the area of ​​a convex hexagon with its vertices at six good points. E. Bakaev

15. Fedya laid out 16 lottery tickets in front of him and is selling them for 1000 rubles each. Two of them are winning - the buyer will receive the cost of the ticket and another 1000 rubles in winnings. Fedya is kind today, so for 100 rubles you can ask him any question that allows the answer “yes” or “no,” and he will answer it honestly. Is it possible, by asking several of these questions, to be guaranteed to earn a) 1200 rubles; b) 1300 rubles? F.Ivlev

16. Several points are marked on the line. It is known that for any marked points A, B, C such that B on the segment AC, $AB BC d^{3}$ holds. What is the largest number of points that can be marked? E. Bakaev
