Geoffrey Hinton significantly improved the Hopfield neural network by introducing a distribution of the activities of each synapse, depending on the input data, according to a probabilistic law similar to the physical law of energy distribution of microparticles. Since this physical law was first formulated by Ludwig Boltzmann, the Hinton neural network received the name “Boltzmann machine”. It was precisely this connection between Hinton’s neural network and physics that the Nobel Committee had in mind when awarding him the physics prize. The Boltzmann machine itself, without human intervention, could evaluate and structure the input data. And besides this, in all of his neural networks, Hinton uses the backpropagation algorithm as the most important element of machine learning and self-learning. Hinton has said more than once that this idea was not invented by him, but by other researchers back in the 70s. But it was Hinton and his colleagues who embodied this idea into specific, “deep” neural networks. His famous, most cited article was published in 1986 and was called “Learning representation by back-propagating errors.” There are many different Russian translations of this name, but it is clear that the article was about the backpropagation algorithm. Hinton and his collaborators, participating in the pan-Canadian project “Artificial Intelligence, Robotics and Society,” created scientific programs that he called “Neural Learning and Adaptive Perception” and “Learning in Machines and Brains.” He continues to work actively in this direction and has received the unofficial title of “godfather of artificial intelligence.” And official recognition in the computer world was secured by awarding him (together with two other scientists) the Alan Turing Award in 2018 “for conceptual and engineering breakthroughs that have made

Artificial intelligence and its risks

The international term for artificial intelligence sounds a little different. International (English) name – AI. Artificial Intelligence. Without delving into the subtleties of translation, we will only note that in explanatory dictionaries, both electronic and “paper” ones, there is disagreement in the interpretation of the terms intellectuality, intellect, intelligence, intellectual. Intelligence is most often understood as reason, the ability to reason. But here are a few definitions for the use of the Russian term artificial intelligence, or AI (from Wikipedia): AI is a scientific direction within which the problems of hardware or software modeling of those types of human activity that are traditionally considered intellectual are posed and solved; AI is a direction in computer science and information technology, the task of which is to recreate intelligent reasoning and actions using computer systems and other devices; AI is the ability of a system to correctly interpret external data, learn from such data, and use the acquired knowledge to achieve specific goals and solve problems using flexible adaptation. These and similar definitions reveal the content of artificial intelligence.
