John Hopfield

York and in 1958 became a Doctor of Philosophy in physics. The topic of Hopfield's dissertation was the quantum mechanical theory of the contribution of excitons to the complex dielectric constant of crystals. (An exciton is a quasiparticle, which is an electronic excitation that propagates through a crystal without transfer of charge or mass. For semiconductor crystals, it is, in fact, a complex of a conduction electron and a hole.) He continued his work in solid state physics at Bell Laboratories, working on the optical properties of semiconductors. But then I became interested in biology, biophysics and biochemistry and began teaching not physics, but biology and chemistry. In 1974, at the University of California, Hopfield presented an interesting and important paper, “The Mechanism of Error Correction in Biochemical Reactions,” to explain the fidelity of DNA replication. And since 1980, his main interest was the theoretical study of the functioning of the brain and its neural network. Hopfield devoted more than 40 years of his life to these works. He applied cybernetic methods to analyze the functioning of the brain. In 1982, his article “Neural networks and physical systems with emerging collective computing capabilities” appeared. In this

Hopfield's work theoretically modeled the neural network of the brain as a physical system in which the elements interacted with each other so that the total energy of interaction tended to a minimum value. As an example, Hopfield gives a collective of ferromagnetic atoms, in which the atoms influence each other so that their spins “line up” in parallel. Then the interaction energy will be minimal. The elements of any system can theoretically be endowed with some interaction activity, similar to energy. The network of such elements has a formal similarity with the neural network of the brain, in which axons, these processes of neurons, intersect at synapses, where signal transmission - information - occurs. This theoretical model consisted of algorithms and programs for constructing the structure (“architecture”) of a data array that is entered into a computer. The interaction of this data can be thought of as a network. Therefore, this theoretical model is called the “Hopfield network”. And it is possible to make training programs that will force the machine to find the minimum value of the general characteristic of the activity of interaction of node elements of networks. Hopfield's ideas gave impetus to the development of different versions of data processing algorithms ("artificial neural networks") for various specific cases of obtaining results. In these algorithms, the use of the system state, obtained in this problem, for subsequent processing, proposed by Hopfield, became significant, i.e. machine learning and machine self-learning.
