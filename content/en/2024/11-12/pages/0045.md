11th grade
Option 1

1 (3 points). Find all real values ​​of $x$, for each of which there exists a geometric progression consisting of real numbers such that its seventh term is $\sqrt{(25x-9)(x-6)}$, its nine term is $x+3$, and its fifteenth term is $\sqrt{\frac{25x-9}{(x-6)^3}}$.

2 (4 points). Solve the equation system
$$\begin{cases} \sqrt{x+5} - \sqrt{1-x-4z} + 4 = 2\sqrt{y-4x-x^2+z}, \\ |y+4|+4|y-5| = \sqrt{81-z^2}. \end{cases}$$
3 (5 points). Find all values ​​of the parameter $p$ for which the equation
$$p\cos 3x + 3(p+4)\cos x = 6\cos 2x + 10$$
has at least one solution. Solve this equation for all such $p$.

4 (5 points). Two circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ intersect at points $A$ and $B$, and their common tangent with $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ has common points $C$ and $D$, respectively, and the point $B$ is located closer to the line $CD$ than the point $A$. Ray CB intersects $\omega_2$ at points $B$ and $E$. Find the ratio $ED:CD$ if the diagonal $AD$ of the quadrilateral $ACDE$ divides the segment $CE$ in the ratio $2:5$, counting from the vertex $C$.

5 (4 points). Given a checkered rectangle $100 \times 400$. In how many ways can 8 cells of this rectangle be shaded so that the shaded set has at least one of the following symmetrical centers of the rectangle, relative to any of the two “middle lines” of the rectangle, let’s call the segment connecting its two opposite sides. Answer in the form of an expression containing no more than three terms (they may include factors, biomial coefficients).

6 (4 points). Find all triples of integers $(a;b;c)$ also that:
• $a < b$,
• the number $b-a$ is not a multiple of 3,
• the number $(a-c)(b-c)$ is the square of some prime number,
• equality $a^2+b=710$ holds.

7 (6 points). At the base of the sign lies an equilateral triangle of area 1. The areas of its lateral faces are equal to 3, 3 and 2. Find the volume of the sign.

Option 2

1 (3 points). The angles of a convex polygon form an arithmetic progression having difference $2^\circ$ and starting at angle $143^\circ$. What is the maximum number of vertices such a polygon can have?

2 (4 points). The integers $x, y, z$ satisfy the equality $x\ln 16+y\ln 8+z\ln 24=\ln 6$. Find the smallest possible value of the expression $x^2+y^2+z^2$.

3 (4 points). From the set $M$, consisting of seven consecutive natural numbers, sixes of pairwise different numbers are selected so that the sum of the numbers in each of the sixes is a prime number. Let $p$ and $q$ be two of such sums. Find the set $M$ if $p^2-q^2=792$.

4 (5 points). The diagonals $BD$ and $AC$ of the trapezoid $ABCD$ intersect at the point $M$, and the relation of the bases is $AD:BC=1:2$. Points $I_1$ and $I_2$ are the centers of circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, placed in triangles $BMC$ and $AMD$, respectively. A straight line passing through point $M$ intersects $\omega_1$ at points $X$ and $Y$, and $\omega_2$ at points $Z$ and $W$ ($X$ and $Z$ is close to $M$). Find the radius of the circle $\omega_1$ if $I_1I_2=13/2$ and $MZ\cdot MY=5$.

5 (5 points). Which is greater: $5-4\sin \frac{3\pi}{14}$ or $4\cos \frac{\pi}{7}-5\sin \frac{\pi}{14}$?

6 (4 points). Given 12 points: 7 of them lie on the same circle in the $\alpha$ plane, and the remaining 5 are located in the non-plane $\alpha$.
