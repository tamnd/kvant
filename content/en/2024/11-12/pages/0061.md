$L$ decreases at a constant rate.
By adding up the changes in distance and time during the chase, we find the required time:

$$L_0 = \left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right) t, \quad t = \frac{L_0}{v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha} = 50 \text{ с.}$$

It is clear that the distance between the participants in the chase changes according to the law $L(t) = L_0 - \left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right) t$.
During time $dt$, Wallace's velocity vector rotates (along with the direction towards Gromit) by

$$d\varphi = \omega dt = \frac{v_2 \sin \alpha \cdot dt}{L(t)},$$

where

$$\omega(t) = \frac{v_2 \sin \alpha}{L_0 - \left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right) t}.$$

We found Wallace's law of change in angular velocity, but it is clear that Gromit's is exactly the same. It is convenient to associate the angle of rotation with a change in distance. Since $dL = -\left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right) dt$, then

$$d\varphi = -\frac{v_2 \sin \alpha}{v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha} \frac{dL}{L}, \quad \varphi = \frac{v_2 \sin \alpha}{v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha} \ln \frac{L_0}{L}.$$

Thus, taking into account the equality of speed models, the angle of rotation of the speed vectors of the common participants in the chase at the moment when the distance between them decreases by 4 times will be

$$\varphi_4 = \frac{\sin \alpha}{1 - \cos \alpha} \ln 4 = \sqrt{3} \ln 4 \text{ рад} \approx 2,401 \text{ рад} \approx 137,6^\circ.$$

In what follows we will denote $\frac{v_2 \sin \alpha}{v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha} = k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Then $L(\varphi) = L_0 e^{-k\varphi}$. By the end of the chase, Wallace and Gromit should be practically at the same point, so we will continue to monitor only Wallace’s movement. Let's align the origin of coordinates with the point of its start, choose the direction towards Gromit as the direction of the $y$ axis, directing the $x$ axis perpendicular to it in the direction of Gromit’s displacement. Since Wallace's velocity is constant, the projections of his velocities

$$v_x = \frac{dx}{dt} = v_1 \sin \varphi, \quad v_y = \frac{dy}{dt} = v_1 \cos \varphi.$$

Then

$$\frac{dx}{d\varphi} = \frac{v_1}{\omega} \sin \varphi = \frac{v_1 L}{v_2 \sin \alpha} \sin \varphi = \frac{v_1 L_0}{v_2 \sin \alpha} e^{-k\varphi} \sin \varphi,$$

$$\frac{dy}{d\varphi} = \frac{v_1}{\omega} \cos \varphi = \frac{v_1 L}{v_2 \sin \alpha} \cos \varphi = \frac{v_1 L_0}{v_2 \sin \alpha} e^{-k\varphi} \cos \varphi.$$

The total rotation angle for the entire chase is very large (at $L \rightarrow 0$ $\varphi \rightarrow \infty$), so Wallace's (and Gromit's) final coordinates can be found using the math hint in the conditions. We get

$$x_k = \frac{v_1 L_0}{v_2 \sin \alpha} \frac{1}{1 + k^2} = \frac{v_1 \left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right)^2}{v_2 \sin \alpha \left(v_1^2 + v_2^2 - 2v_1 v_2 \cos \alpha\right)} L_0 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} L_0,$$

$$y_k = \frac{v_1 L_0}{v_2 \sin \alpha} \frac{k}{1 + k^2} = \frac{v_1 \left(v_1 - v_2 \cos \alpha\right)}{v_1^2 + v_2^2 - 2v_1 v_2 \cos \alpha} L_0 = \frac{1}{2} L_0.$$

This means that by the end of the chase, Wallace will be at a distance from his old point (origin of coordinates)

$$r_y = \sqrt{x_k^2 + y_k^2} = L_0 = 100 \text{ м}.$$

Gromit's side point has coordinates $(0; L_0)$, so his movement pattern during the chase is exactly the same:

$$r_\Gamma = \sqrt{x_k^2 + \left(y_k - L_0\right)^2} = L_0 = 100 \text{ м}.$$

To avoid the picture laid out by Wallace's pebbles, it is enough to analyze the condensation qualitatively. Since the angular velocity of the monotonic race remains constant, the radius of curvature of its trajectory $R(t) = \frac{v}{\omega(t)}$ decreases monotically. Therefore, it is clear that the trajectory has the form of a twisting spiral (Fig. 19), emerging from the origin along the $y$ axis and “winding” onto the end point $(x_k; y_k)$.
