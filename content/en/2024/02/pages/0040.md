cities (TG), All-Olympiad (VO), Moscow Mathematical Olympiad (MMO), Leningrad Mathematical Olympiad (LMO), Leningrad selections (LO), Tournament named after M.V. Lomonosov (TL). Some of the problems are original.

Visualization of the cycle

1 (TL, 1989). At a mathematics competition at the MIMINO Institute, 20 problems were proposed. 20 schoolchildren came to the closing ceremony. Each of them solved two problems, and it turned out that among those who came, two schoolchildren solved each problem. Prove that it is possible to organize the analysis of problems in such a way that each student tells one of the problems he has solved and all the problems are analyzed.

2 (MMO, 1966). a*) In how many different ways can 22 squares be marked on an 11×11 checkered board so that on each vertical and on each horizontal two squares are marked? (Two methods are considered the same if one can be obtained from the other by rearranging the rows and columns.)

6) The same question about the 5x5 table, but you can’t rearrange rows and columns.

3 (TG, 1985/86). 20 football teams are holding a championship. On the first day, all teams played one game. On the second day, all teams also played one game. Prove that after the second day you can also indicate 10 teams, that no two of them played each other.

4 (TG, 1986/87). Some cities only allow paired apartment exchanges. Prove that any complex exchange of apartments can be completed in two days.

5 (MMO, 1970). City X has 1000 cottages, each with one person living in it. One fine day, everyone moves from their home to some other one. After the move, each house still has exactly one tenant. Prove that it is possible to paint all the cottages in three colors so that for each owner the color of the new house is different from the color of the old one.

6* (LO, 1990). A 100-volume collection of Leo Tolstoy’s works stands in disarray on a shelf. You are allowed to take any two volumes with numbers of different parities and swap them. In what minimum number of such permutations can all volumes be completed in order?

7. Each key only fits its own piggy bank. The keys to 30 piggy banks were moved around and each one was placed in a piggy bank. What is the probability that after two have been hacked, all piggy banks will be opened?

8 (MMO, 1963). The plant produces rattles in the form of a ring with 3 red and 7 blue balls attached to it. How many different rattles can be produced? (Two rattles are the same if one is formed from the other by moving the balls around the ring.)

9. a) In how many ways can you color $n$ with different colors a circle divided into $p$ sectors, where $p$ is a prime number? What if $p$ is a compound? (Each sector turns out to be one paint; it is not necessary to use all colors.)

6) Prove that $n^p - n$ when $p$ is prime is always divisible by $p$.

10 (MMO, 1962). An irregular $n$-gon is inscribed in a circle, which, when the circle is rotated by a non-zero angle, will coincide with itself. Prove that $n$ is a composite number.

11. There are $n > 2$ straight lines on the plane, no 2 are parallel and no 3 intersect at the same point. It is known that it is possible to rotate the plane around a certain point $O$ by a certain angle less than $180^\circ$, so that each of the drawn lines coincides with some other drawn straight line. At what $n$ is this possible?

12 (TG). Several pieces are placed on the chessboard. In one move, the chip moves to an adjacent (vertical or horizontal) field. After several moves, it turned out that each chip raised once on all fields and returned to the original field. Prove that there was a moment when none of the pieces were on their original square.

The idea of ​​a cyclic group

13 (A. Kovaldiki). Is it possible to come up with a sequence of rotations of the faces of a Rubik's cube, repeating which you can obtain all possible arrangements of cups?

14. Each next member of the sequence is the sum of the squares of the previous digits. Prove that the sequence is periodic and that it will probably contain either 1 or 89. How do sequences behave in which the next term is, respectively, the square of the sum or the sum of the squares of the previous digits?

15. The numbers $x_0$ and $x_1$ are non-zero. With all $n$ $x_{n+1} = kx_n - x_{n-1}$. For what $k$ can this sequence have a period?

16. It is known that for all $n$ $a_{n+1} = a_n + b_n$, $b_{n+1} = b_n + c_n$, $c_{n+1} = c_n + d_n$, $d_{n+1} = d_n + a_n$.

Prove that if all these sequences are periodic, then they are zero.

17*. Let us denote by $S(y)$ the sum of the digits of the number $y$. Prove that there exist infinite numbers $n$ such that $S\left(2^n\right) > S\left(2^{n+1}\right).$
