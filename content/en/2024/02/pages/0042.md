KVAN T 2024/№2

37* (A. Kanel). Given an infinite word z (in the Russian alphabet), u and v are its various subwords. Prove that it is possible to find words s and t such that svt is a subword of z and that in the word z there are arbitrarily long periodic subwords (the length of the period is not greater than max u, v) or arbitrarily long subwords without occurrences of sut. 38 (TG, 1987 88). A sequence of words consisting of the letters “A” and “B” is considered. The first word is “A”, the second is “B”, the kth word is obtained by adding the k 1st word to the k 2nd word on the right (so the beginning of the sequence looks like “A”, “B”, “AB”, “BAB”, “ABBAB”, ...). Can a “periodic” word occur in the sequence, i.e. a word consisting of several (at least two) identical pieces, following each other, and only from them? 39*. Is there an infinite sequence of three letters such that no combination of letters will be repeated twice in a row? 40**. a) In the language of the MBU-MBA tribe there are only 4 sounds: M, B, U, Y. Synonyms are any two words UXXV and UXV, where U, X, V are arbitrary sequences of sounds. Prove that the number of tribe concepts is finite. b) What if only the words UXXXV and UXV are synonyms?

Billiards

41*. a) A tangent is drawn to the square from an external point. The point is reflected relative to the point of contact. Another tangent is drawn through it and the procedure is repeated. Can a point escape to infinity? b) From a point outside the regular pentagon that does not lie on the continuation of the sides, a tangent is drawn to it and the point is symmetrically reflected relative to the point of tangency. The same thing happens with the new point, only another tangent is selected, etc. If we go to the continuation side, the process will stop. Prove that there are infinitely many points of period 10. c) What can be said in the general case? 42 (MMO, 1965). In a rectangular billiard of size p u 2 q, where p and q are odd integers, there are pockets in each corner and in the middle of each side of length 2q. A ball is released from a corner at an angle of 45° to the side. Prove that the ball will definitely fall into one of the middle pockets. 43* (LMO). Professor Smith stands in a square room with mirrored walls. He wants to arrange several students in the hall so that from his place he cannot see his own reflection. Will the professor be able to do this? 44. Identical balls are strung on a ring and collide absolutely elastically. Prove that their arrangement repeats periodically.

Miscellaneous tasks

⟦column⟧

45*. Each city has an odd number of roads. A black or white flag is raised in the central square of the city. Every morning in each city the color of the flag changes if there are more neighbors with a flag of a different color. Prove that the color of each flag either stabilizes or blinks with a period of 2.46* (from the book “Math Aquarium”). There is an unlimited number of black and white dice. We need to build a solid parallelepiped-shaped tower out of them so that each black cube borders on an even number of white cubes, and each white cube borders on an odd number of black cubes. For any given lower layer of cubes, can such a tower (of finite height) be built? 47* (p.b), c) – A. Kanel). An endless strip of checkered paper in both directions consists of black and white cells. Every second, a cell with an even number of black neighbors becomes white, and a cell with an odd number of black neighbors becomes black. Prove that: a) if after $2^{n}$ seconds the original coloring is repeated, then it is periodic with a period of 3 $2^{n}$; b) the original coloring repeats periodically if and only if it itself is periodic (periodicity in time is equivalent to periodicity in space). c) What can be said about a strip of arbitrary width? Or about the entire checkered plane? 48 (A. Kanel, B. Ufnarovsky). Given a rectangle with an aspect ratio of 1991. A square is cut from it, and the same procedure is performed with the remaining rectangle. Prove that the sequence of side ratios of the constructed rectangles is periodic (by solving this problem, you can prove that each quadratic irrationality can be decomposed into a periodic continued fraction, possibly with a preperiod). The converse statement is also true: a number that can be decomposed into a periodic continued fraction is quadratic irrationality (see the book by A.Ya. Khinchin “Continued Fractions”). 49*. The complete works of an unknown classic stand in disarray on the bookshelf. The librarian selects a volume at random that is out of place and puts it in its place (in this case, some of the volumes are moved). Can he always put the volumes in order in a finite number of operations?
