As Rivest wrote: “The problem of factorization was at that time a little-known art form. There was little literature on this subject. It was difficult to get good estimates of the time that the already proposed algorithms would require."
Noted mathematician and popularizer of mathematics Martin Gardner published an article about RSA in his regular column in Scientific American. The article was titled "A New Type of Cipher That Would Take Millions of Years to Break." This article contains the phrase: “Rivest and his associates have no proof that no one in the future will quickly find an algorithm that can factor such a large number as the name used $r$, or break the cipher by another method that they do not agree on. They consider both possibilities of the scales to be separate.”
As a challenge, Scientific American readers created RSA with a challenge that asked them to decipher the message shown at figure:

So, the RSA method has been found to be as difficult as the decomposition into prime factors of a number that is the product of two primes. In this regard, methods began to be developed for decomposing numbers into prime factors. The breakthrough in the problem of factoring composite numbers came not only from the development of faster computers, but also from theoretical advances. One of the mathematicians who was attracted to this topic was K. Pomerantz.

Quadratic Pomerantz solution

Factoring numbers has fascinated Pomerantz since his school teacher asked him to factor the number 8051, giving him five minutes to do the task. Although Pomerantz was good at mental math, he decided to first find a quick path to the solution rather than just checking one number after another. “I spent a couple of minutes looking for a clever way, but I was worried that I was spending too much time. Then, belatedly, I started looking for divisors, but I never got the hang of it.”

Eventually Pomerantz learned about the conditions his teacher had in mind. Until 1977, the fastest method of factorization was Fermat's factorization method and its generalizations. Fermat's method is based on finding integers $x$ and $y$ that satisfy the $x^2 - y^2 = r$ correspondence, leading to factorization

$$r = (x - y)(x + y).$$

Using Fermat's method, Pomerantz takes just a few seconds to decompose 8051 into 83 and 97 on demand.

Based on Fermat's method, Pomerantz developed a new method that outperformed other factorization methods for over 10 years. This new discovery, called the quadratic solution, has very serious implications for the evolving world of Internet security. It was thanks to this method that the RSA-129 problem was solved.
