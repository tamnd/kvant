⟦folio 6⟧

KVANT 2024/№2

Seven-year-old Julius with his sisters Lida and Anya and their teacher Rosalia Loor. The ill mother went to Germany for treatment in 1910, where she soon married a famous psychologist, a student of Freud. In 1932 they emigrated to Palestine and lived a long, calm and prosperous life there. But the children, Julius and his half-sisters, were not abandoned. The father was lucky to find a bonne for the children (with compulsory German language training). She came from the Estonian university city of Dorpat (Tartu), was well educated and taught children not only the German language, but also much more. Her name was Rosalia Loor, and the children called her Rollie. In his autobiography, Yu.B. Khariton will write: “Later, this simple, good and kind woman replaced our mother.” Julius could not study at the gymnasium (the three percent prevented

This is a norm limiting classical gymnasium education for Jews). Rosalia found a capable student who not only prepared Yuli to enter a real school, but also helped the capable young man graduate in five years instead of seven. Yuli's favorite subjects were mathematics, physics and chemistry. In 1917, 13-year-old Julius had to begin working life at the same time as studying. At first he worked in a library archive, but then became an apprentice mechanic and electrician in railway telegraph workshops. Such early maturation of the young man helped the family survive in the difficult circumstances of the ensuing devastation. In addition, in 1918, the rule came into effect that higher education should be provided only to those who had work experience. Khariton set himself the goal of entering the Polytechnic Institute, and in 1921 he easily became a student in the electromechanical department of this institute. In the second semester, he got into the lecture stream of several faculties, where lectures on physics were given by Abram Fedorovich Ioffe, who had already become a leading Russian physicist. And this became a turning point in Khariton’s entire life. Ioffe's lectures combined the most modern atomic physics with classical thermodynamic concepts. The course of lectures was called “Kinetic Theory of Gases”. The young man was shocked by the very possibility of explaining thermodynamic laws by penetrating into the deep essence of matter. In the middle of the semester (at the age of 17), Yuliy decides to become a physicist and moves to the physics and mechanics department of the institute. And he immediately makes such progress in laboratory and practical studies that he is invited to work in his newly created laboratory by Nikolai Nikolaevich Semenov, the future Nobel laureate in chemistry. After 4 years, Khariton defended his thesis in the specialty “experimental physics” and received the qualification “engineer physicist”. These two names, introduced by A.F. Ioffe, are still used in philosophy.
