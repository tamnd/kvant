First of all, let's study the remainders of the Fibonacci numbers when divided by 1990. We would like to find a remainder equal to zero. How to look for it? That's the task. However, let's look at the past. (The Presidents problem showed the usefulness of studying history.) Note that the sequence can be “continued back in time” using the formula u n 2 u n u n 1.

Then we get that $u_{0} 0$. Wonderful! Zero is divided by 1990. It remains to establish the periodicity of the remainders, starting from the zero term. Note: we do not need to find a member of the Fibonacci sequence with a zero remainder - it is enough to establish its existence. It is not difficult to understand that the remainder of one Fibonacci number cannot be said about the remainder of the next one. This is where the problem about presidents comes to the rescue: after all, in order to predict the outcome of an election, it is not enough to know the party affiliation of the previous president - the results of the last 10 elections are needed. That is why we called the partisanship of the last 10 presidents “pre-election state.” Now it is clear that as the state of the term $u_{n}$ we need to take a pair of remainders from division by 1990 terms $u_{n} 1$ and $u_{n} 2$. The two previous remainders already determine the next one. Here is the key to the solution: firstly, the "state" determines the remainder of the number, and secondly, each "state" determines the next state. Now everything is clear: there is a finite number of states (estimate how many), they will begin to repeat, which means the remainder of the Fibonacci numbers will repeat. However, it’s too early to rejoice. Why does the period start with a zero term? Here the idea of ​​“reverse motion” will help us: the state of each remainder determines the state of not only the next one, but also the previous one (the formula $u_{n} 2 u_{n} u_{n} 1 ),$ convinces us of this, and this implies the absence of a preperiod.
