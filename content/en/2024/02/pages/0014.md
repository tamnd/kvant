But, perhaps, the main role was played by the scientific competence of Georgy Nikolaevich Flerov, an employee of the Kurchatov Nuclear Laboratory. He carefully studied all the foreign scientific journals that were still in the university library before the war and even in the first months of the war. And he discovered that by the end of 1940, articles on nuclear physics and any information about work in this area of ​​physics had disappeared from all scientific journals. It became clear to nuclear physicist Flerov that this could not be caused by the cessation of “uranium work,” but, on the contrary, by their deployment and complete secrecy. The disappearance of articles by prominent physicists testified to their participation in these works and possible achievements. And Flerov began regularly sending letters about this to the Presidium of the Academy of Sciences. But the letters remained unanswered. Flerov began to raise the status of the recipients of his letters - to the Council of People's Commissars, to the People's Commissariat of Defense of the USSR, to the Central Committee of the CPSU. And finally he realized that the main authority in the country was the State Defense Committee (GKO), led by I.V. Stalin. The representative of this committee for science was S.V. Kaftanov, chairman of the All-Union Committee for Higher Education Affairs. He receives a letter from G.N. Flerov addressed to Stalin. But at the same time, he is given a notebook with calculations on the chain reaction of uranium fission, found on a German officer killed at the front, who was a nuclear physicist before mobilization. S.V. Kaftanov, together with A.F. Ioffe, report this to Stalin and show Flerov’s letter. Here are excerpts from this letter. “... 10 months have already passed since the beginning of the war. After listening to Kaftanov’s explanations and touched by the despair in Flerov’s lines, Stalin made a decision. Despite the difficult military situation in the southern direction, on September 28, 1942, the State Defense Committee considered the issue and adopted Resolution No. 2352ss (“ss” meant top secret) “Organization of work on uranium.” The first paragraph of the Resolution obligated the USSR Academy of Sciences to organize a special laboratory for work “on uranium”. But only four months later the Academy of Sciences responded to this Resolution. The main reason for such a long delay was the question of the head of this laboratory and the scientific direction of the entire uranium problem. S.V. Kaftanov, on behalf of the State Defense Committee, nominated A.F. Ioffe. But the seventy-year-old “chief physicist,” citing age and ill health, nominates instead the 38-year-old head of the LPTI Atomic Nucleus Laboratory, Igor Vasilievich Kurchatov, who has distinguished himself in many areas of physics. In January 1943, Kurchatov’s candidacy for the post of head of the new Secret Laboratory No. 2 was approved, and in the order of the State Defense Committee dated February 16, 1943, the name of Igor Vasilyevich Kurchatov was first mentioned as the scientific leader of the entire problem of “using atomic energy for military and peaceful purposes.” Secret laboratory No. 2 was immediately created not in Kazan, but in Moscow. At first, there were only 10 employees, among them G.N. Flerov and K.I. Shchelkin, recalled from the front, Yu.B. Khariton’s closest collaborator in detonation work. A few years later, Laboratory No. 2 began to be called the Laboratory of Measuring Instruments of the USSR Academy of Sciences (LIPAN), then - the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I.V. Kurchatov. Nowadays this is a huge scientific institution - the National Research Center “Kurchatov Institute”.
