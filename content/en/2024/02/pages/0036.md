Residents of the Hawaiian Islands are proud that they have had a presidential form of government since time immemorial. Every 4 years, either a Republican or a Democrat is elected president. Local sociologists have discovered a strict law that determines the party affiliation of the next president. Although this law is secret, information has leaked to the press that the partisanship of the next president is completely determined by the partisanship of the previous ten. Hawaii has been ruled by a Republican for the last three terms. Prove that there will always be periods of three Republicans in a row. Solution. It is enough for us to show that the partisanship of presidents recurs periodically. Let's call the “pre-election state” or simply “state” the sequence of parties to which the last 10 presidents belonged. Each state determines the partisanship of the next president, and therefore the next pre-election state. There is a finite number of states (no more than $2^{10} ),$ therefore, according to the Dirichlet principle, one of them will be repeated. The repetition of one state will cause the repetition of the following states, and from this moment the states will begin to repeat periodically, which means the sequence of ruling parties will be periodic. But since the presidential form of government in Hawaii has existed since time immemorial, the repetition of two states has already occurred, and the states have been repeating for a long time. As you can see, in all the analyzed problems the same thing is present reasoning,

establishing periodicity. Let us formulate it in general terms. Reasoning 1. If something can only be in a finite number of states and the state at a given moment in time uniquely determines the state at the next moment in time, then, starting from a certain moment, the states will begin to repeat periodically. However, in the problem about presidents, it was necessary to check that at the moment we are in a period - only then can we say that there will always be moments of rule by three Republicans in a row. For this it took “time immemorial.” This is an important point: reasoning 1 only states periodicity, starting from a certain place, but it is not at all necessary that the first state be repeated. (This can already be seen in the example of Problem 1. Another example is conditionally periodic fractions.) If the period does not begin immediately, then the sequence of states preceding the period is called the pre-period. To prove that the initial state will ever occur, we must also show that there is no pre-period. To do this, a slightly different idea is often used than in the problem of presidents - the idea of ​​\u200b\u200b“reversing”. Reasoning 2. If the number of states is finite and each state uniquely determines both the subsequent state and the previous one, then there is no pre-period in the sequence of states. This simple reasoning is not immediately clear. Let's try to explain it. At some point a period will appear (reasoning 1). Let's choose a state, for example, from the third period. It uniquely identifies the previous state. In the second period, this state will be repeated, and the repetition will cause further repetition (reasoning 1 in the “reverse move”). The period will be “transported into the past,” which means that all states are periodically repeated. Here is a typical example (see A.
