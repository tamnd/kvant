We will consider the rocket and the fuel remaining in it at time $t$ to be a system of variable composition. The direction of the axis $x$ coincides with the direction of the speed of the rocket $v(t)$. During the movement, the relative speed of gas flow from the rocket nozzle remains constant, therefore, the mass fuel consumption $\mu$ also does not change with time. For the projection of the momentum flux we have
$$\Pi_x = \mu(v - u),$$
where does the equation of motion come from (external forces do not act, $p(t) - \mu$ rocket momentum)
$$\frac{dp}{dt} = \mu(u - v).$$
(9)
From the analysis of this equation it follows that the rocket reaches its maximum momentum at that moment of time, let’s denote it $t_1$, when its speed $v(t_1)$ becomes equal to the relative speed of the outflowing gases $u$. In order to find the mass of the rocket at moment $t_1$, one should obtain the Meshersky equation from equation (9) and design it. The derivative of the mass of the system with respect to time is equal to $-\mu$, therefore correspondence (9) leads to the equality
$$m \frac{dv}{dt} - \mu v = \mu(u - v),$$
from which the differential equation follows
$$\frac{dv}{u} = -\frac{dm}{m}.$$
Interpreting this equation, we obtain the well-known dependence of the mass of the system on velocity:
$$m(v) = m_0 e^{-\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)}.$$
(10)
From this formula it follows that by the moment of time $t_1$, when the speed $v$ becomes equal to $u$, the mass of the rocket with the fuel remaining in it decreases by $e$ times compared to the mass $m_0$. Therefore, the required maximum impulse is equal to
$$p_{\max} = \frac{m_0 u}{e}.$$
(11)
Note that the resulting answer does not depend on the fuel mass flow rate $\mu$. It would seem that it follows that the mass of fuel $m_0 \left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right)$, which shows the rocket during the time it takes to achieve maximum momentum, can be ejected from a resting rocket almost instantly, and its acquired momentum can be calculated on the basis of the law of conservation of momentum using the formula
$$p'_{\max} = \frac{m_0 u}{e}(e - 1).$$
(12)
Why do correspondences (11) and (12) give different answers? Which one should be considered correct?
Answer (11) is correct. The fact is that in the laboratory system of absence, in which the rocket initially dug, the speed of the gases leaving the rocket cannot be considered equal to $u$, even if the gases are ejected by the rocket in a very short time. At any moment during the acceleration process, the gas velocity relative to the laboratory system is $w(t) = u - v(t)$. Therefore, on the right side of formula (12), instead of the speed $u$, the average speed of gases relative to the laboratory system during the acceleration time $\langle w \rangle_{t_1}$ should become. It is not possible to calculate this average speed without interpreting Meshersky’s equation, and interpreting this equation gives the answer (11).
