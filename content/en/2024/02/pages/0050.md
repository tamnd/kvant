Problem 7. A homogeneous chain is suspended at one end by a thread such that the other end touches the table surface. I burned it out. Determine the dependence of the pressure force of the chain on the length of the part that has not yet fallen. The impact of the links on such an inelastic chain, the mass of the chain is $M$, its length is $L$.

Just as in the case of the cobra problem, we first give a solution based on the theorem on the motion of the center of mass, and then consider the equation of motion of a variable state system.

Let the axis $x$ be directed vertically upward and its zero at the floor level. Then at the moment when the coordinate of the upper end of the chain is equal to $x$, the coordinate of the center of mass of the chain is determined by the formula

$$x_c = \frac{x^2}{2L},$$

which is identical to the corresponding formula in the cobra task. Differentiating twice, we obtain the relation for acceleration

center of mass:

$$a_c = x_c'' = \frac{x'^2}{L} + \frac{x \cdot x''}{L}.$$

Next, you should understand, by choosing an adequate physical model, how the coordinate $x$ of the upper end of the chain changes. Nothing better than the model proposed when considering the problem of a chain sliding off a table comes to mind. So, we will assume that the chain is a set of identical point masses connected by incredible and inextensible threads of the same length. Then the top point of the chain moves only under the influence of gravity with the acceleration of free fall $g$, therefore, the equality is valid

$$x = L - \frac{gt^2}{2}, x' = -gt = -\sqrt{2g(L-x)}.$$

Substituting these equalities into the relation for the acceleration of the center of mass, we obtain an expression for the reaction force:

$$N = Mg + Ma_c = 3Mg \left( 1 - \frac{x}{L} \right).$$

Now let's find the reaction force in another way. Let us consider as a variable state system that part of the chain that is already on the table. The dependence of its mass on the coordinate $x$ of the upper end of the chain is given by the formula $m(x) = \frac{M(L-x)}{L}$.

The derivative of the momentum of this part of the chain, very indo, is equal to zero, and the projection of the momentum flux onto the $x$ axis is equal to $\Pi_x = \frac{M}{L} x'^2$. As a result, we have the equation of motion

$$0 = N - \frac{Mg(L-x)}{L} - \frac{M}{L} x'^2$$

and we arrive at the already known answer for $N$:

$$N = 3Mg \left( 1 - \frac{x}{L} \right).$$
