Childhood and youth

Yu.B. Khariton was born on February 27, 1904 in St. Petersburg. The surname Khariton is of Greek origin, but these possibilities of the roots of Greek origin remained unknown to him.

Father, Boris Iosifovich Khariton, had a legal education, but early began to engage in literary publishing, working in the editorial offices of cadet newspapers. In 1918, he organized the “House of Writers” in Petrograd, a club for communication (and some material support) of the capital’s intelligentsia. In the fall of 1922, the leadership of the “House of Writers” was arrested and expelled from Russia along with other cultural figures, representatives of the “ideologically alien propetarutiu” intelligentsia. The infamous “philosophical steamer” landed the exiles in Germany, but B.I. Khariton eventually settled in Riga, where he began publishing the emigrant newspaper Segodnya. In 1940, in accordance with the agreement with Hermann, Latvia became one of the republics of the USSR. Soon B.I. Khariton was arrested and sentenced to 7 years in the camps. A year later he died either in the camp or on the way to the camp.

Very young Julius

Yu.B. Khariton's mother, Mirra Lvovna Burovskaya, was a professional actress. She played both at the Moscow Art Theater and at the Maly Theater. Not very tall, thin, with delicate features, she was ideally suited to the role of young girls and even girls (she had great success in the role of Mytyl in the romantic “The Blue Bird” by Maeterlinck). Therefore, she necessarily participated in tours of Moscow theaters. On one of her trips she met Boris Khariton in St. Petersburg. The wedding took place. Julius is born. Mirra had two daughters from a previous marriage. The new family now has three children. The half-sisters loved their little brother very much, and he loved them. But still, a strong family and a comfortable childhood did not work out. Mother went on tour, performances in the evenings, rehearsals in the afternoon. Nevertheless, she managed to instill in little Julius an interest in art, reading and music. He remained with this interest throughout his life.
