In 1977, American mathematicians R.L.Rivest, A.Shamir, L.Adleman (R.L.Rivest, A.Shamir, L.Adleman) were able to come up with such a function, which became the basis of the RSA cryptosystem, named after the first letters of the founder’s last names. RSA is a public key cryptographic algorithm based on the computational complexity of the problem of factorization (factorization) of large numbers that are the product of two primes.

Let's describe how encryption works in the RSA system.

Description of the RSA cryptosystem

We will assume that the source text is translated in a standard way into an integer $m$. Let's choose two sufficiently large prime numbers $p$ and $q$ and calculate the product $r = pq$. Then we choose a number $e < r$, which must be coprime with $\varphi(r)$. Here $\varphi(r) - \varphi(\text{йлера от } r)$, denoting the number of natural numbers less than or equal to $r$ and coprime to it. Since $p$ and $q$ are prime numbers, then $\varphi(r) = (p-1)(q-1)$.

The numbers $e$ and $r$ are the public key, i.e. become publicly available for use in the encryption algorithm.

Then the number $d$ is found from the condition:

$$de \equiv 1(\bmod \varphi(r)),$$

(*)

those. the reciprocal of $e$ mod $\varphi(r)$. This is done using the Euclidean algorithm and can be calculated quickly.

The two prime factors $p$ and $q$ and the number $d$ are the private encryption key.

The number $m < r$ is encrypted with the following function:

$$E_e(m) = m^e(\bmod r) = c,$$

those. To encrypt, you need to raise $m$ to the power $e$ and calculate the remainder when divided by $r$.

Encryption can be done very efficiently even for huge values ​​of $r$: it requires less than seconds of computer time.

The inverse algorithm used for encryption is to raise the number of the ciphertext to the power $d$ mod $r$. The decryption of the message $c$ is carried out by the function

$$D_d(c) = c^d(\bmod r).$$

Decryption also takes less than seconds of computer time.

The correct operation of the decryption method follows from the equalities

$$D_d(c) = D_d(E_e(m)) = m^{ed}(\bmod r) = m^{k\varphi(r)}m(\bmod r) = m(\bmod r) = m$$

for some natural $k$. Here the required equality follows from (*), and the fourth – from Euler’s theorem. (For numbers $m$ not coprime to $r$, it is necessary to separate the modulo checks $p$ and $q$.)

All the information on number theory necessary to understand the RSA algorithm is presented in more detail in the book [4].

The question arises, how reliable is the RSA system?

To decode the message, you need to know the private key $d$. Currently, the only effective way to find $d$ is based on applying the Euclidean algorithm to $e$ and $\varphi(r)$, and to do this you need to know the prime factorization of $r$.

Number factorization problem

There are no known fast algorithms for solving the do sins factorization problem. If it is found, it will most likely be classified.

Rivesta estimates that if the best known algorithm and the fastest modern computer of the time had been used, the required runtime would have been about 40 quadrillion years! It is this practical ability to factor the product of two large prime numbers that makes the RSA encryption system possible.

Rivest soon realized that he had made an arithmetical error in estimating the time. However, given the methods available at that time for decomposing numbers into prime numbers, it would take thousands of years to solve this problem.
