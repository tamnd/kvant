Childhood and youth

Yu.B. Khariton was born on February 27, 1904 in St. Petersburg. The surname Khariton is of Greek origin, but these possibilities of the roots of Greek origin remained unknown to him.

Father, Boris Iosifovich Khariton, had a legal education, but early began to engage in literary publishing, working in the editorial offices of cadet newspapers. In 1918, he organized the “House of Writers” in Petrograd, a club for communication (and some material support) of the capital’s intelligentsia. In the fall of 1922, the leadership of the “House of Writers” was arrested and expelled from Russia along with other cultural figures, representatives of the “ideologically alien propetarutiu” intelligentsia. The infamous “philosophical steamer” landed the exiles in Germany, but B.I. Khariton eventually settled in Riga, where he began publishing the emigrant newspaper Segodnya. In 1940, in accordance with the agreement with Hermann, Latvia became one of the republics of the USSR. Soon B.I. Khariton was arrested and sentenced to 7 years in the camps. A year later he died either in the camp or on the way to the camp.

Very young Julius

Yu.B. Khariton's mother, Mirra Lvovna Burovskaya, was a professional actress. She played both at the Moscow Art Theater and at the Maly Theater. Not very tall, thin, with delicate features, she was ideally suited to the role of young girls and even girls (she had great success in the role of Mytyl in the romantic “The Blue Bird” by Maeterlinck). Therefore, she necessarily participated in tours of Moscow theaters. On one of her trips she met Boris Khariton in St. Petersburg. The wedding took place. Julius is born. Mirra had two daughters from a previous marriage. The new family now has three children. The half-sisters loved their little brother very much, and he loved them. But still, a strong family and a comfortable childhood did not work out. Mother went on tour, performances in the evenings, rehearsals in the afternoon. Nevertheless, she managed to instill in little Julius an interest in art, reading and music. He remained with this interest throughout his life. Beginning of scientific activity

Khariton’s diploma work, like his work in a scientific laboratory, was related to physics and chemistry. But, graduating from college, Khariton was not only engaged in science. In 1925, the “Physics Problem” was published, created by him together with his friends A.F. Walter and V.N. Kondratiev. This problem book became the first Soviet visa problem book in physics and was used in all technical universities of the country for almost 30 years. But its authors were still students when they created it.

This problem made Khariton’s name known to all physicists in the country. And the publication of his first scientific works brought his name to the international physical and chemical arena. A truly remarkable early sunrise! After only a year of work, he was recommended by A.F. Ioffe, P.L. Kapi-

Tsei and N.N. Semenov to undergo a scientific internship at the Cambridge Laboratory of E. Rutherford. There he is engaged in precise laboratory studies of the interaction of alpha particles with matter. But the laboratory as a whole is switching to research in the field of nuclear physics. And Khariton is not only aware of all the work being done in the laboratory, he takes part in discussions and discussions. However, his “nuclear” time has not yet come. In 1928, the 24-year-old scientist became a Doctor of Physiophy (physics) at the University of Cambridge, defending a thesis on his experimental work with alpha particles.

Khariton used his summer breaks from work to travel around England on a motorcycle and automatic, and to travel around Europe with its museums and ancient architecture. He becomes an avid and skilled photographer.

Returning to his homeland in 1928, Khariton remained in Berlin to witness physical assault marches inoculating Berelin residents with slogans about the chosenness of the German nation and anti-Semitic appeals. On the pages of newspapers there was a fight of physicalist demagogues with the slogans of socialists and communists. Familiar Berelin physicists and the intelligent circle of his materials assured that all this was just politicking and buffoonery, that no one would take the physicists seriously and they would soon be forgotten about them.

But history decided differently. After 4 years, Hitler became Reich Chancellor, and 10 years later Germany started a peaceful war. Khariton foresaw this turn of events. Returning to the Physico-Technical Institute, he decided to direct his scientific work towards strengthening the defense capability of his native country. This decision was another milestone that determined his life's destiny. The field of science that combined physics and chemistry in his solution was the science of explosions and detonation. Nuclear physics

When Khariton’s laboratory (together with the Institute of Chemical Physics) separated from the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology, he himself regularly attended a seminar on nuclear physics, which Igor Vasilievich Kurchatov, head of the newly created laboratory of the atomic nucleus, began to teach at LPTI. It was 1932, a turning point in the physics of the microworld. The neutron was discovered, a proton-neutron model of the atomic nucleus was created, and work on artificial radioactivity began. Enrico Fermin in Italy, Federic and Irene Jollu-Curie in France have been working for several years on the interaction of neutrons with atomic nuclei

personal elements, including uranium nuclei. The awarding of Nobel Prizes to these scientists (Jollus-Curie in chemistry in 1935 and Fermin in physics in 1938) was recognition of the importance of these works both for the development of the theory of the atomic nucleus and for practical purposes - the obtained so-called local atoms.

In 1934, the German physicist and discoverer of the element rhenium, Ida Nodak, analyzing the results of the experiments of Jollu-Curie and Fermin, made the assumption that the nuclei of heavy elements, when hit by a neutron, can split into two or three parts having very high energy. Since she studied physical chemistry and electrochemistry, she published her reports in the journal of applied chemistry. But physicists did not read chemical journals, and chemists did not care about atomic nuclei. Therefore, Nodak's hypothesis passed independently. The German physicist and radiochemist Otto Hahn, director of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute of Chemistry, especially protested against this hypothesis.

But four years have passed and everything has changed. Otto Hahn and his assistant Fritz Strassmann, repeating Fermin with training using slow neutrons from uranium, proved the fact of the division of nuclei into two parts and the emergence of chemical elements in the middle of the periodic table from uranium nuclei. They dated the article about this discovery as December 17, 1938. Just two weeks later, on January 4, 1939, it was published in one of the most famous scientific journals - in the journal Natural Sciences, which was read by both chemists and physicists.

By this time, the talented author physicist and radiochemist Lise Meitner had been working with Otto Hahn for almost 30 years. The shift cipher is sometimes called a Caesar cipher because Julius Caesar used such a cipher with $b = 3$.

A shift cipher is an example of a private key cryptosystem. Such cryptosystems differ in that the same key is used for both encryption and decryption, therefore such cryptosystems are called symmetric.

With symmetric encryption, both the sender and the recipient must know the key. However, the problem arises of how to tell each other the secret key so that he does not recognize the construction. This problem was solved using asymmetric cryptography (public key encryption) methods.

Public key cryptography
The difference between public key cryptography and classical cryptography is that when using keys, it is difficult to obtain the decryption key from the encryption key. This means that the encryption key does not need to be kept secret. You can tell the whole world what encryption key to use in order to encrypt a message, while keeping the decryption key secret. As a result, there is no need to first establish a communication channel between the sender and the recipient in order to securely transfer the secret key that is needed to encrypt information.

Public key cryptography began with the work of American cryptographers W. Diffie and M. Hellan in 1976.

In a public key encryption scheme, there are two keys - the public key, denoted by the letter $e$, and the secret key, denoted by the letter $d$. The encryption function $E_e$ uses the public key, and the decryption $D_d$ uses the secret key.

Decrypting a message without knowing the secret key is practically impossible, i.e. the function $f$ is irreversible from the point of view of practical computation. Such easily computable functions, for which the inverse function is difficult to calculate unless it has some additional information, are called one-way functions with a secret.

The following examples can be given to demonstrate what public key encryption is (see [3]).

Let there be a larger telephone directory in which there are surnames for all letters of the alphabet, including “ь”, “ъ”, “ы”. Each surname is assigned to a thousand telephone numbers. Let's assume that there are no computers to work with this directory. We will take a letter of plaintext and encrypt this letter with some telephone number, referring to a surname starting with this letter. From plaintext consisting of letters, you get ciphertext consisting of telephone numbers.

Costs a certain amount of success, but it is still possible to restore the original plaintext. To do this, you need to find a given phone number, see what surname it corresponds to, restore the letter and do this with all the letters.

You can also pre-compile another telephone directory that does not contain telephone numbers. Then the decryption procedure will take less time. A telephone directory in which there is no data by last name is the discovery key used in encryption. The telephone directory, which is missing numbers, is the private key that will be used by the one who decrypts the text.

The main question in public key encryption is: how to come up with a suitable one-way function?

It may seem surprising, but, despite all the variety of the most ingenious methods of encryption and decryption, which throughout have been developed by the most sophisticated minds, the mathematical description of any of them is based on only one mathematical concept - the concept of a one-way function.

In 1977, American mathematicians R.L.Rivest, A.Shamir, L.Adleman (R.L.Rivest, A.Shamir, L.Adleman) were able to come up with such a function, which became the basis of the RSA cryptosystem, named after the first letters of the founder’s last names. RSA is a public key cryptographic algorithm based on the computational complexity of the problem of factorization (factorization) of large numbers that are the product of two primes.

Let's describe how encryption works in the RSA system.

Description of the RSA cryptosystem

We will assume that the source text is translated in a standard way into an integer $m$. Let's choose two sufficiently large prime numbers $p$ and $q$ and calculate the product $r = pq$. Then we choose a number $e < r$, which must be coprime with $\varphi(r)$. Here $\varphi(r) - \varphi(\text{йлера от } r)$, denoting the number of natural numbers less than or equal to $r$ and coprime to it. Since $p$ and $q$ are prime numbers, then $\varphi(r) = (p-1)(q-1)$.

The numbers $e$ and $r$ are the public key, i.e. become publicly available for use in the encryption algorithm.

Then the number $d$ is found from the condition:

$$de \equiv 1(\bmod \varphi(r)),$$

(*)

those. the reciprocal of $e$ mod $\varphi(r)$. This is done using the Euclidean algorithm and can be calculated quickly.

The two prime factors $p$ and $q$ and the number $d$ are the private encryption key.

The number $m < r$ is encrypted with the following function:

$$E_e(m) = m^e(\bmod r) = c,$$

those. To encrypt, you need to raise $m$ to the power $e$ and calculate the remainder when divided by $r$.

Encryption can be done very efficiently even for huge values ​​of $r$: it requires less than seconds of computer time.

The inverse algorithm used for encryption is to raise the number of the ciphertext to the power $d$ mod $r$. The decryption of the message $c$ is carried out by the function

$$D_d(c) = c^d(\bmod r).$$

Decryption also takes less than seconds of computer time.

The correct operation of the decryption method follows from the equalities

$$D_d(c) = D_d(E_e(m)) = m^{ed}(\bmod r) = m^{k\varphi(r)}m(\bmod r) = m(\bmod r) = m$$

for some natural $k$. Here the required equality follows from (*), and the fourth – from Euler’s theorem. (For numbers $m$ not coprime to $r$, it is necessary to separate the modulo checks $p$ and $q$.)

All the information on number theory necessary to understand the RSA algorithm is presented in more detail in the book [4].

The question arises, how reliable is the RSA system?

To decode the message, you need to know the private key $d$. Currently, the only effective way to find $d$ is based on applying the Euclidean algorithm to $e$ and $\varphi(r)$, and to do this you need to know the prime factorization of $r$.

Number factorization problem

There are no known fast algorithms for solving the do sins factorization problem. If it is found, it will most likely be classified.

It is this practical ability to factor the product of two large prime numbers that makes the RSA encryption system possible.

Quadratic Pomerantz solution

Factoring numbers has fascinated Pomerantz since his school teacher asked him to factor the number 8051, giving him five minutes to do the task. Although Pomerantz was good at mental math, he decided to first find a quick path to the solution rather than just checking one number after another. “I spent a couple of minutes looking for a clever way, but I was worried that I was spending too much time. Eventually Pomerantz learned about the conditions his teacher had in mind. Until 1977, the fastest method of factorization was Fermat's factorization method and its generalizations. Fermat's method is based on finding integers $x$ and $y$ that satisfy the $x^2 - y^2 = r$ correspondence, leading to factorization

$$r = (x - y)(x + y).$$

Using Fermat's method, Pomerantz takes just a few seconds to decompose 8051 into 83 and 97 on demand.

Based on Fermat's method, Pomerantz developed a new method that outperformed other factorization methods for over 10 years. This new discovery, called the quadratic solution, has very serious implications for the evolving world of Internet security. It was thanks to this method that the RSA-129 problem was solved.

The solution to RSA-129

The RSA-129 hacking project started on September 3, 1993 under the leadership of mathematicians A. Lenstra and D. Atkins. They united about 600 people in 24 countries, 1600 personal computers. Coordination is carried out via the Internet and was one of the first such projects.

For breaking RSA-129, the cutoff $B = 16333609$ was chosen and the factor base size was $\pi(B) = 524339$. Based on experience with many other factorizations, it was concluded that it is necessary to collect more than 8 million numbers such that $y = x^2 (\bmod r)$. By March 21, 1994, approximately 8.25 million numbers had been collected. This involved about 220 days of procession.

Then we moved on to the matrix stage of searching for linearly dependent vectors. Let us assume that it was necessary to solve a system of 524,339 linear equations with 8.25 million unknowns.

205 dependencies were found. This process took 45 hours. Odana from these dependencies and to exclude $r$.
