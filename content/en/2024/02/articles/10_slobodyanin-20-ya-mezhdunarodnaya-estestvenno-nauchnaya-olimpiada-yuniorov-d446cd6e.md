20th International Junior Science Olympiad

Schoolchildren whose age does not exceed 15 years take part in this Olympiad. The selection of candidates for the Russian team is made from among the participants in the regional stages of the All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics or chemistry, who have scored more than 30% of the maximum points. In June, 30–35 of the most successful schoolchildren are invited to a training camp at MIPT, where training is carried out according to the Olympiad program and experimental skills are developed. Portuguese words

We notice that all the words in the right column begin with $pl$, and Portuguese words fall into three classes in this regard:
1) plátano, plebe (class “pl”);
2) praino, prancha (class "pr");
3) chegar, chão, cheio (class “ch”).
It is obvious that words of the class "pr" and class "ch" at some point in the history of the Portuguese language were formed from the class "pl".

Words of class "pl" were not changed. This can only be explained by the fact that they appeared in the language after both changes had been made. Consequently, the class "pl" consists of later borrowings.

We note that in the class “pr” (as well as in the class “pl”) the original sound $n$ is preserved, while in the class “ch” the sound $n$ disappears (cf. chão and planum, cheio and plenum). This means that words of the “pr” class appeared in the language after the process of dropping $n$ was completed (otherwise $n$ would have dropped out in these words as well). Thus, the class “ch” is the most ancient, and the class “pr” refers to early borrowings.
