M2799. Given a natural number n. Ilya came up with a couple of different polynomials of degree n (with real coefficients). Sasha may have conceived a couple of different polynomials of degree n. Lenya knows n; his goal is to find out whether Ila and Sasha have the same pairs of polynomials. Lenya chooses a set of k real numbers $x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_k$ and reports these numbers. In response, Ilya fills out a 2×k table: for each i = 1, 2, ..., k, he writes in two cells of the i-th column a pair of numbers $P(x_i)$, $Q(x_i)$ (in any of the two possible orders), where P and Q are the polynomials he conceived. The table is filled in similarly by Sashi. At what smallest k can Lenya (look at the tables) surely add the goal?

Answer: 2n + 1.

Let us show that for k = 2n (and even more so for k < 2n) Lenya will not be able to uniquely determine the pair P, Q. Let him call the set $x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_{2n}$. Let's put

$$A = (x - x_1)(x - x_2) \dots (x - x_n),$$
$$B = (x - x_{n+1})(x - x_{n+2}) \dots (x - x_{2n}),$$

so $A(x_i) = 0$ for i = 1, 2, ..., n and $B(x_i) = 0$ for i = n + 1, n + 2, ..., 2n. Then if Ilya wished for $P_1 = A + 2B$ and $Q_1 = -A - 2B$, then in the i-th column of the table there will be numbers $\pm 2B(x_i)$ for i = 1, 2, ..., n and numbers $\pm A(x_i)$ for i = n + 1, n + 2, ..., 2n. But the same table is suitable for the pair $P_2 = A - 2B$ and $Q_2 = -A + 2B$; Sashi could have guessed it.

On the other hand, we will show that for k = 2n + 1 the Ilya table can satisfy at most one pair of polynomials P, Q. Assume the opposite, let there also be two pairs: $P_1$, $Q_1$ and $P_2$, $Q_2$. Then $P_2$ coincides with $P_1$ or $Q_1$ at least for n + 1 different values ​​of the argument, say, with $P_1$. Therefore, $P_1$ and $P_2$ are the same polynomials (since their difference is a polynomial of degree at most n, having at least n + 1 different roots). From the table in this case we find that the values ​​of $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ coincide at 2n + 1 points, and then $Q_1 = Q_2$.
