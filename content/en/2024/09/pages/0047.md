A. $v_{cp} = \frac{4v_1v_2v_3v_4}{v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4}$;

IN. $v_{cp} = \frac{4v_1v_2v_3v_4}{v_1v_2v_3 + v_1v_2v_4 + v_1v_3v_4}$;

C. $v_{cp} = \frac{4v_1v_2v_3v_4}{v_1v_2v_3 + v_1v_2v_4 + v_1v_3v_4 + v_2v_3v_4}$;

D. $v_{cp} = \frac{v_1v_2v_3v_4}{v_1v_2v_3 + v_1v_2v_4 + v_1v_3v_4 + v_2v_3v_4}$.

As a rule, answers A and B are usually discarded first. Obviously, answer A fails the dimensional test. In fact, the left side of this formula has the dimension of speed (m/s), and the right side has the dimension of the cube of speed (m/s)³. What is striking about answer B is its asymmetry. Note that the denominator of this fraction is $v_1$ occurs 3 times, and the values $v_2$, $v_3$ And $v_4$ - only 2 times. But in the conditions of the problem, all speeds are equal, so none of them should stand out in the answer. Let us generalize this observation: if there is some symmetry in the conditions of the task, then a certain symmetry must inevitably appear in the answer.¹ In case B, this is not observed, and this answer cannot be correct.

Thus, we are left with only two last answers, each of which successfully passed the test for dimensionality and symmetry. Let us now consider such a special case when the answer is very iden. Let the driver have the same speed in all sections $v_1 = v_2 = v_3 = v_4 = v$, which in this case is obviously equal to the average speed. Let's notice all the speeds in the answers C and D to the same and equal v. Ultimately correct $v_{cp} = v$ for answer C and the counterintuitive expression $v_{cp} = v/4$ for answer D.

E. $v_{cp} = \frac{v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4}{4}$.

And yet, let's try to check the analysis and make sure that answer E cannot be correct. Again, a special case will help us: let it remain constant in the first three sections of the pot: $v_1 = v_2 = v_3 = v$, and in the last one it is very small: $v_4 \rightarrow 0$. What should be the average speed of the motorcyclist along the entire journey? It is obvious that due to the low speed $v_4$ the time $t$ of movement becomes very large (in the limit of one tends towards infinity: $t \rightarrow \infty$). This means that from the definition of average speed ($v_{cp} = s/t$) it should be, like $v_4$, also very small. Please note that at $v_4 \rightarrow 0$ in the case of the correct answer C, the average speed also turns out to be very small. But answer E gives an implausible result in this case: $v_{cp} = 3v/4$. (It is clear that for the analysis it was possible to assume low speed in any other section.)
