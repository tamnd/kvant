13. At $N = 15$.
Grade. For $N \leq 14$, we construct a graph in which the vertices correspond to the fugitives, and the edges connect two fugitives if they can swim together. Note that the resulting graph is bipartite. Indeed, figure 9 shows the graph for $N = 14$, and for smaller $N$ its subgraph is obtained.
In addition, all fugitives whose number is an exact square fell into one share. Consequently, each voyage must involve a fugitive from this share, so that at no time can more than one of them be on the right bank. However, at $N > 1$ there is more than one person in this share, so everyone will not be able to cross to the right bank.

Algorithm. For $N = 15$ we will show how it is possible to transport two fugitives with numbers $x$ and $y$ to the right bank, the sum of which is an exact square. Let's depict the algorithm in the form of a diagram in which the arrows show the direction of movement: $(1, 15) \rightarrow, 1 \leftarrow, (6, 10) \rightarrow, (10, 15) \leftarrow, (1, 3) \rightarrow, (3, 6) \leftarrow, (x, y) \rightarrow, 1 \leftarrow$.
This way you can successively transport the pair $(2, 14), (4, 12), (5, 11), (7, 9), (3, 13)$ to the right bank. After this, fugitives with numbers 1, 6, 8, 10, 15 will remain on the left bank, who can cross to the right bank in the following way:
$(1, 8) \rightarrow, 1 \leftarrow, (6, 10) \rightarrow, (3, 6) \leftarrow, (1, 15) \rightarrow, (10, 15) \leftarrow, (6, 10) \rightarrow, 1 \leftarrow, (1, 3) \rightarrow, 1 \leftarrow, (1, 15) \rightarrow$.
14. Angles $PA_1A$ and $PBA$ are inscribed in circle $\Omega$ and rest on one arc (Fig. 10). By virtue of symmetry, angle $PBA$ is half of angle $PBQ$, which is central to circle $\omega_2$ and subtended by arc $PQ$. And the angle $PA_1Q$ is inscribed in the same circle and rests on an arc that complements it to the full circle. From the above it follows that
$$\angle PA_1A = \angle PBA = \frac{1}{2} \angle PBQ = 180^\circ - \angle PA_1Q$$
Posibly, the points $A, A_1$ and $Q$ lie on the same straight line. Similarly, points $B, B_1$ and $Q$ lie on the same line.
Let us extend straight lines $QA$ and $QB$ until they intersect with circles $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ at points $C$ and $D$, respectively. Since $QC$ and $BB_2$ are the diameters of circles $\omega_1$ and $\Omega$, then $\angle QB_1C = \angle BB_1B_2 = 90^\circ$, i.e. point $C$ lies on line $B_1B_2$. Similarly, point $D$ lies on line $A_1A_2$.
The angle $QKC$ is right as if inscribed in the circle $\omega_1$ and based on its diameter. Right triangles $QA_1K$ and $QKC$ are similar because they share an acute angle. So, $\frac{QA_1}{QK} = \frac{QK}{QC}$, whence $QK^2 = QA_1 \cdot QC$. Likewise, $QM^2 = QB_1 \cdot QD$. Since right triangles $QA_1D$ and $QB_1C$ are also similar, then $\frac{QA_1}{QB_1} = \frac{QD}{QC}$.
Therefore, $QA_1 \cdot QC = QB_1 \cdot QD$ and $QK = QM$. It remains to note that the points $L$ and $N$ are symmetrical to the points $K$ and $M$ relative to the lines $QC$ and $QD$, respectively. Therefore, the points $K, L, M, N$ are equidistant from the point $Q$, and therefore lie on the same circle with the center at this point.
15. Note that
$$\frac{1}{(k-1)!+k!} = \frac{1}{(k-1)!(k+1)} = \frac{k}{(k+1)!} = \frac{1}{k!} - \frac{1}{(k+1)!},$$
poseable
$$\frac{1}{(k-1)!+k!} + \frac{1}{k!+(k+1)!} + \dots + \frac{1}{(n-1)!+n!} = \frac{1}{k!} - \frac{1}{k+1} + \frac{1}{(k+2)!} + \dots + \frac{1}{n!} - \frac{1}{(n+1)!} = \frac{1}{k!} - \frac{1}{(n+1)!} < \frac{1}{k!}.$$
Now we rearrange the terms on the left side of the inequality from the condition:
$$1! \cdot \left( \frac{1}{0!+1!} + \frac{1}{1!+2!} + \dots + \frac{1}{(n-1)!+n!} \right) +$$
$$+ 2! \cdot \left( \frac{1}{1!+2!} + \frac{1}{2!+3!} + \dots + \frac{1}{(n-1)!+n!} \right) + \dots$$
$$+ n! \cdot \frac{1}{(n-1)!+n!} < 1! \cdot \frac{1}{1!} + 2! \cdot \frac{1}{2!} + \dots + n! \cdot \frac{1}{n!} = n.$$
