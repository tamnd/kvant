About the nature of electric current
in metal conductors
L. BELOPUKHOV

From an early age we hear and remember the expressions “electric current”, “current flow through wires”. From school physics it is firmly established that electric current is the ordered direction of movement of electric charges.

The concept of “electric current” arose a long time ago, more than 200 years ago. In 1800, at a meeting of the Royal Society of London, the Italian physicist, 55-year-old professor at the University of Pavia, Alexandro Volleta (1745–1827), presented a report on the results of his research on electricity. He also demonstrated his invention, which had already received the name “volmov so.” For that time, this prototype of electric batteries was the most powerful source of current. In his report, Alexandro Volleta first used the terms “electric current, electromotive force, electrical capacity, electrical voltage.” And for two hundred years now, these terms have remained unchanged for electrical phenomena.

Volta was an uncharged scientist. He became interested in physical descriptions at the age of 13, and at the age of 34 he became a university professor, having worked in this position for more than 40 years. He communicated with many European physicists. With his knowledge and understanding of electrical phenomena, he charmed Napoleon, who awarded him the Legion of Honor and gave him the titles of count and senator. Italy considers him its national hero. And the unit of electrical voltage and electric motion of force in 1861 received the name volm.

Today, the legacy of the voltaic pillar is batteries and accumulators, which work on the same principle, but are much smaller in size. They are used as direct current sources in all devices to make life easier and better (gadgets) - from electric flashlights to sophisticated smartphones. But it is not easy to figure out how electric current is distributed in them - this is the field of electrochemistry. Therefore, we will teach from the most common case - electric current in conductors (metallax).

Georg Simon Ohm (1789–1854) and other physicists quickly established that electric current in metals can be detected and changed by its action, primarily thermal. Electric current produces heat, heats the wire, which means it experiences resistance to its movement like mechanical friction. Ohm called this phenomenon in conductors electrical resistance and, based on experiments, established his significant law - the magnitude (strength) of the current in the conductor I is directly proportional to the voltage U at the ends of the conductor: I ~ U. He called the proportionality coefficient the conductivity of the conductor, and its inverse value - the resistance of the conductor (R).

For the simplest case (one-dimensional problem), Ohm established the dependence of resistance on the geometry and properties of the conductor: R = ρl/S, where l is the length of the conductor, S is its cross-sectional area, aρ is a value characterizing the property of the conductor material, called the specific resistance of the substance.
