Cools are frequencies (wavelengths) of electromagnetic radiation from atomic electrons. For the phenomenon of electric current, this is the current strength and the resistivity of the substance. The most convenient way for this turned out to be the use of wave quantum mechanics. In this version of quantum mechanics, the wave characteristic of a microparticle is the so-called psi-function of coordinates and time, which mathematically has the form of a wave equation and is a solution to some wave differential equation (with a second partial derivative with respect to coordinates and a first derivative with respect to time). This equation was formulated by Erwin Schrödinger in 1926. Why does the psi function have a wave character? The fact is that back in 1923, Louis de Broglie suggested that not only the photon has a dual, particle-wave representation, but also every microobject, in particular the electron. As a particle (corpuscle), it $при_{r}$ in motion is characterized by momentum p and the associated energy E. But the electron can exhibit wave properties (interference, diffraction), characterized by wavelength $O_{Бр} h p$, where h is Planck’s constant. This wavelength is called the de Broglie wavelength of the microparticle or de Broglie wavelength. Have you noticed that this formula (de Broglie's postulate) is an inversion of Einstein's postulate for the photon momentum p h O? In de Broglie's formula for the momentum of a microparticle, a relativistic expression for the dependence on speed should be used: $p mv 1 v^{2} c^{2}$. It is obvious that the wave properties of microparticles should manifest themselves most clearly in the lightest particles - electrons. It was postulated (by Schrödinger, Heisenberg, Bohr) that the psi function is expressed mathematically by the wave equation, in which wavelength and frequency are determined by momentum and energy according to de Broglie's postulate. And in principle there is no visual “understanding” of the de Broglie wave for a microparticle, just as there is no visual trajectory of an electron in an atom. The creators of matrix quantum mechanics (Max Born, Werner Heisenberg, Niels Bohr) showed that this form of quantum mechanics gives exactly the same experimental results as the Schrödinger equation in wave quantum mechanics. And it was these scientists who defined the psi function as a probabilistic characteristic of the behavior of a microparticle and as a quantity that oscillates in a de Broglie wave. Once again, it should be emphasized that there is no clarity in this statement; this is only one of the mathematical formulations of the probabilistic nature of the behavior of a microparticle. The use of the concept of a de Broglie wave has become convenient for many problems in quantum mechanics, in particular for explaining electric current in metal crystals. There are several quantum theories of this phenomenon, differing in varying mathematical complexity and varying degrees of agreement with experimental data. In one of the least complex theories, the behavior of an electron in a metal is described by the Schrödinger equation, in which the effect on the electron is determined by the sum of the electric field of the ions of the crystal lattice, periodically repeating in space, and the external electric field applied to the conductor from a current source. This takes into account the vibrational motion of these ions, which obeys the statistical Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. The result of this oscillatory motion can be considered as a chaotically changing inhomogeneous distribution of ions in the crystal, only on average representing a certain crystalline system.
