$$(y_1 - x_1)(y_2 - x_2) \geq x_1x_2$$ and under this condition the sum $y_1 + y_2$ is the minimum possibility. Without loss of generality then $y_1 - x_1 \geq x_1$. Take the $x_1$th white segment $W$ from the left and the $(y_2 - x_2)$th black segment $B$ from the left. Some of them have the right end to the left.
1) Let the right end $W$ be to the left (or the ends coincide). Then the right $x_2$ black segments do not intersect the left $x_1$ white segments. Contradiction.
2) Let the right end $B$ be to the left. Let's discard all the white segments to the left of $W$ (including it) and all the black segments to the left of $B$ (including it). The remaining white segments (at least $x_1$ of them) do not intersect with the discarded $y_2 - x_2$ black ones; from here it already follows that $y_2 - x_2 < x_2$.
Let's put $x'_1 = x_1$, $y'_1 = y_1 - x_1 \geq x'_1$, $x'_2 = x_2 - (y_2 - x_2)$ and $y'_2 = y_2 - (y_2 - x_2) = x_2 \geq x'_2$; then there are $y'_1$ white and $y'_2$ black segments left. Consider any $x'_1$ remaining white and $x'_2$ remaining black segments. If there are no intersecting ones among them, then adding to them all the discarded black segments, we obtain a set of $x_1 = x'_1$ white and $x_2 = x'_2 + (y_2 - x_2)$ black segments of the original set, among which there are no intersecting ones; this is impossible. This means that the remaining set satisfies the conditions (for the new numbers $x'_1, y'_1, x'_2$ and $y'_2$), while it contains fewer segments than the original one, therefore

$$0 < x'_1x'_2 - (y'_1 - x'_1)(y'_2 - x'_2) =$$
$$= x_1(2x_2 - y_2) - (y_1 - 2x_1)(y_2 - x_2) =$$
$$= x_1x_2 - (y_1 - x_1)(y_2 - x_2) \leq 0.$$
