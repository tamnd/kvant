In his book Mathematical Puzzles and Fun, Martin Gardner
proposed the following problem: divide twelve pentominoes into three groups of
four elements in each, then find a figure with an area of ​​20 squares,
which can be formed from four pentominoes of each group.
It turns out that there is a symmetrical 20-cell figure, which is
solving the problem. By superimposing three such figures on top of each other, we will collect “three-
layer cake."
To make the task a puzzle, we connect sets of three with screws
pentomino figures according to the pattern indicated at figure.

We get four game elements of the puzzle:

From these elements it is necessary to fold a symmetrical three-layer figure.
Because pentominoes can rotate on screws, they create a lot of
different combinations of relative positions, which significantly complicates
puzzle. When assembling it, you not only need to find a pentomino for each figure
their place in the “pie”, but also manage to hook them onto each other.
