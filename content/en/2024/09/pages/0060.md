6) We will paint the sides of the triangles red, yellow and green. We cover one of the lattice nodes with a ceptr, and the grid lines passing through it are axes. Let's color the axes in different colors. They divide the plane into six parts. Let's consider one of them, limited by the red and yellow axes. The segments inside it, parallel to the green axis, are colored green. We will divide the remaining segments into “zigzags”, enclosed between two green segments, and color these “zigzags” alternately red and yellow (Fig. 4). Similarly, color the sides of the triangles inside other colors.
Consider an arbitrary node inside the same part. Red and yellow “zigzags” pass through it; along them you can reach both the red and yellow axis. And along the green segment passing through this node, you can reach one of the neighboring axes and go to the green “zigzag”, which is then added to the green axis. Further along the axis on which it is located you can reach the center. Similar reasoning is applied to nodes located on the axes. Therefore, between every two lattice nodes there are paths of all three colors.

5. They can.
Of the heel of cards that the spectator gives to the assistant, at least two will be of the same suit. The assistant will lay them out in ascending order of merit, and it is them that the magician will guess. There are $4 \cdot \frac{13 \cdot 12}{2} = 312$ such pairs; the magician and his assistant can check them in advance. All that remains is to figure out how to use the remaining three cards to convey to the magician a number from 1 to 312. They can also check all 52 cards in advance. The card that the assistant opens first can occupy any of the five possible places in the row, the second can occupy any of the four, and the third can occupy any of the remaining three. Moreover, the order of their numbers relative to each other can be any of 3! = 6. Thus, the assistant can, through his actions, tell the magician any number from 1 to $5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 = 360$ - this is enough to guess one of 312 possible pairs.
