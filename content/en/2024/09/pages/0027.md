We will assume that the average cross-section inside the bottle is much larger than the cross-section of the hole in the neck. In this case, the water that gets into the bottle further falls to the bottom or into the water already accumulated in the bottle and loses mechanical energy, but this part of its “life” is no longer of interest to us.
If the speed of water flow at the outlet of the tap is $v_0$, then at the entrance to the neck of the bottle the speed of movement becomes greater and it is equal to $\sqrt{v_0^2 + 2gH}$. And since water consumption is maintained, the cross-sectional diameter of the water scabs in the neck $D_1$ will decrease compared to the diameter of the scabs at the outlet of the tap $D_0$. This condition corresponds to the relation
$$D_0^2 v_0 = D_1^2 \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2gH}.$$
(1)
If the value $D_1$ is close to the value $D$, then in this case
$$\sqrt{v_0^2 + 2gH} \approx v_0 \left(\frac{D_0}{D}\right)^2,$$
(2)
and the cross section through which air leaves the bottle will be equal to
$$\frac{D(D - D_1)\pi}{2}.$$
In this case, you can calculate the speed $u$ of the air leaving the bottle (assuming that its density differs little from the density of the air outside the bottle), and also estimate the difference in pressure $\Delta p$ of the air inside and outside:
$$\frac{u\pi D(D - D_1)}{2} = \frac{v_0\pi D_0^2}{4},$$
or $u = \frac{v_0 D_0^2}{2D^2 - 2DD_1},$
$$\Delta p = \frac{\rho_{\text{возд}} u^2}{2} = \frac{\rho_{\text{возд}}}{8} \left(\frac{v_0 D_0^2}{D^2 - DD_1}\right)^2.$$
This pressure difference should be such as not to slow down the flow of water at the entrance to the neck of the bottle, i.e. inequality must be satisfied
$$\rho_{\text{возд}} \frac{v_0^2 + 2gH}{2} > \frac{\rho_{\text{возд}}}{8} \left(\frac{v_0 D_0^2}{D^2 - DD_1}\right)^2.$$
Let us substitute relation (2) into this inequality:
$$\rho_{\text{возд}} \frac{v_0^2 (D_0 - D)^4}{2} > \frac{\rho_{\text{возд}}}{8} \frac{v_0 D_0^2}{(D^2 - DD_1)^2},$$
$$\rho_{\text{возд}} \left(\frac{1}{D}\right)^4 > \frac{\rho_{\text{возд}}}{4} \frac{1}{(D^2 - DD_1)^2}.$$
If we assume that the ratio of water density to air density is approximately 900, then in this case the resulting inequality can be written as follows:
$$60 \left(1 - \frac{D_1}{D}\right) > 1, \text{ т.е. } D - D_1 > \frac{D}{60}.$$
Note that the resulting relationship corresponds to the assumption that the difference between the diameter of the hole in the pot and the diameter of the scabs of water in the pot is much less than the diameter of the scab. If we assume that $D_1 = (59/60) D$, then in this case the scab of water in the pot is slowed down, hitting the wide part of the bottle behind the pot, losing a significant part of the vertical component of the speed. In principle, there is nothing unusual about this. Something similar happens if, for example, a scab of water falls on a horizontal surface of a stationary stone that has dimensions larger than the cross-section of the scab of water.
We return to relation (1) and substitute the diameter of the scab in the pot $D_1 = (59/60) D$:
$$D_0^2 v_0 = (59/60)^2 \cdot (0,8)^2 \cdot D_0^2 \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2g \cdot 50D_0},$$
$$v_0 = 0,6188 \sqrt{v_0^2 + 10 \text{ m}^2/\text{c}^2},$$
$$v_0^2 \cdot 2,6112 = v_0^2 + 10 \text{ m}^2/\text{c}^2},$$
$$v_0^2 \cdot 1,6112 = 10 \text{ m}^2/\text{c}^2},$$
$$v_0 \approx 2,49 \text{ m}/\text{c}.$$
The dynamic pressure of water at the inlet to the pot is equal to
$$\rho_{\text{возд}} \frac{v_0^2 + 10 \text{ m}^2/\text{c}^2}{2} \approx 8,2 \text{ кПa}.$$
This is much less than atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa. The water consumption in this case is equal to
$$\frac{\pi D_0^2}{4} \cdot v_0 \approx 0,195 \text{ л}/\text{c}.$$
and the bottle filling time is
$$\frac{100}{0,195} \text{ c} \approx 511 \text{ c} \approx 8,5 \text{ мин}.$$
