SCARLET CHILDREN REALLY LOVE picture books. Remember the proverb – it’s better to see once than to hear a hundred times? It's just about book illustrations. Schemes, diagrams figure, i.e. graphical representations of information are also present in textbooks. For example, a physics textbook for grade 7 shows a combination of movable and stationary blocks (Fig. 1). The fixed block serves to change the direction of the force (simply speaking, for convenience), the movable block allows you to lift the load with a force that is half the weight of the load. This combination of blocks is called a chain hoist. To see a model of a particular machine or design, you can go to the Polytechnic Museum. In Moscow, such a museum stores more than 200 thousand objects. Many cities have entertaining science museums. There is such a museum in the city of Nalchik - this is the Einstein Museum of Entertaining Sciences. All of its exhibits are skillfully made and are pleasing to the eye. Let's get to know some of them. At figure 2 on the left, two identical weights connected by a thread are in equilibrium. In the center figure the mass of the left weight is doubled

All weights on this figure are motionless, static. This model demonstrates the phenomenon of equilibrium and belongs to the branch of mechanics called statics. The exhibit on figure 3 relates to dynamics and illustrates the law of conservation of energy. Two identical coils are mounted on a horizontal cylindrical axis on the left and right. If we start rotating the axle clockwise, both tapes will begin to wind onto the reels, and the axle will rise up. In the upper position, the potential energy reserve of this mechanical system will be greatest. If you then release the axle from your hands, the tapes will begin to unwind from the reels. The central disk will begin to rotate counterclockwise while moving downward. Potential energy will be converted into kinetic energy of the rotating disk. Eventually all potential energy will turn into kinetic energy. Next, the disk at the lowest point will continue to rotate around its axis and, winding the tape around the axis, will begin to rise upward. The process of moving the disk up and down will continue periodically. Eventually, as a result of the dissipation of energy, the movement will, of course, stop, and we will again see the static picture shown in figure.
