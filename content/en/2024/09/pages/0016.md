Let us prove the correctness of entering three-dimensional space. Proposition 3. An arbitrary tiling of a regular hexagon with rhombuses can be represented as a projection of some orthogonal surface. Proof. In order to build the required structure from cubes, we assign spatial coordinates x, y, z to all the vertices of the rhombuses, where x, y, z are integers ranging from 0 to n. Let's assign the coordinates 0,0,n to the top vertex of the hexagon. Then we will sequentially move to neighboring vertices.

If this procedure does not lead to a contradiction, then using the spatial coordinates of the vertices we will construct a three-dimensional surface and the corresponding arrangement of cubes. A contradiction can arise if, having reached the same point along different paths, we must assign different coordinates to it. The cycle can be successively reduced to a point, reducing its interior by one diamond at each step.
