Remark 2. The spatial analogue of a zonogon is a zonodrone - a convex centrally symmetric polygon, all of whose faces are also centrally symmetric. figure 6 shows an example of a zonodrome - a truncated octadron.

Zonogons and Zonodras first appeared in the works of the Russian crystallographer Evgraf Stepanovich Fedorov [1]. There, for the first time, the main idea was outlined, which we will use many times in the future - to build chains of parallelograms touching parallel sides.

Fedorov called zonogons pair-siders, and zonodra - pair-boundaries. We will stick to more advanced terminology. In the book [2], another name is used for zonogons – parallel-sided 2n-gons.

We will look at several solutions to this problem, each of which is interesting and useful in its own way.

First solution to problem 1: exit into space.2 If you color the diamonds in three colors depending on their orientation, then in the mosaics you can see a set of individual cups folded into an n×n×n box. Each cube is positioned to be as close as possible to some fixed corner of the box. For us, this will be the further corner at which the floor and two walls meet, let's call them left and right. We will call the surface formed by the free faces of the cups the orthogonal surface (Fig.8).
