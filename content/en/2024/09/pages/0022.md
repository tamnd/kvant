PROBLEM "QUANTUM"
Tasks
in mathematics and physics
We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are taken over by the star. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.
Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant».
Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses.
Problem M2810 is intended for the 62nd Ural Tournament of Young Mathematicians. Problem M2811 - at the May training assemblies of candidates for the team for the International Mathematical Olympiad. Problem M2812 – at the Kurchatov Olympics.
Problems M2810-M2813, F2817-F2820
M2810. Given a natural number $n \geq 2$. In how many ways can the cells of the square $n \times n$ be colored in four colors so that any two cells that have a common side or vertex are covered in different colors?
I. Efremov
M2811. The sequence of natural numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{100}$ is such that $a_1 = 1$ and for every natural number $n = 1, 2, \dots, 100$
the number $(a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n)\left(\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n}\right)$ is an integer. What is the maximum value that $a_{100}$ can take?
M. Turevsky
M2812. On the coordinate plane, at some points with integer coordinates there is a pebble (there is a finite number of pebbles). You are allowed to make the following move: choose a pair of pebbles, take some vector $\vec{a}$ with integer coordinates and then move one of the selected pebbles to the vector $\vec{a}$, and the other to the opposite vector $-\vec{a}$; In this case, it is prohibited for more than one pebble to be at one point. Is it always possible to achieve a situation in a few moves in which all the pebbles lie on the same straight line?
K. Ivanov
M2813*. The quadrilateral $ABCD$ is circumscribed around a circle with center $I$ (Fig. 1).
The perpendicular midpoints of the cutoffs $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at the point $P$. The circumcircles of triangles $APC$ and $BPD$ intersect at points $P$ and $Q$. Prove that $I$ lies on the line $PQ$.
Tran Kuang Hung
