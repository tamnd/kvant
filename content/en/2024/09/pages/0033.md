When moving in a circular motion, its tip draws various curves on the surface of the sand. And here is a model of an arched bridge built without a single nail (Fig. 8). According to this principle, in former times, when there were no reinforced concrete floors, they made semicircular windows in houses and temple vaults. Let's look at horizontally located rails, which (unlike ordinary ones!) are not parallel (Fig. 9). Each wheel is mounted on a cylindrical axle. If you look closely at the axles, you will notice that the radii of the axles are not constant: the axis of the large wheel becomes thinner towards the ends, and the axis of the smaller wheel, on the contrary, thickens. Under the action of rolling force, the large left wheel rolls from left to right, the smaller right wheel rolls from right to left. The point is that as each wheel moves along the rails, its center of mass actually moves downward. This shift is almost imperceptible to the eye, since the change in diameter

This exhibit makes a very strong impression. According to Mathematical Studies, figure 10 depicts the most fascinating exhibit. The inclined segment is attached to a vertical axis using two joints and can rotate around this axis. In the vertical plane there is a curvilinear section in the form of a hyperbola. When rotated, the segment passes through the cut. The point is that this segment is part of the rectilinear generatrix of the hyperboloid of revolution.
