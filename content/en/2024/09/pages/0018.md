Second solution to problem 1: de Bruijn chains. Let ABCDEF be the given hexagon and n the length of its sides. Since the hexagon is partitioned into $6n^{2}$ regular triangles of side 1, the total number of rhombuses in the partition must be $3n^{2}$, and it suffices to check that the partition consists of $n^{2}$ rhombuses of each orientation. We will consider rhombuses of a fixed orientation, for example, those whose sides are parallel to the segments AB and CD. For each such rhombus one can construct a de Bruijn chain, i.e. a sequence of diamonds that starts on side AB, ends on side ED, and in which every two adjacent diamonds have a common side parallel to AB (Fig.16). There will be n such chains in total (according to the number of unit segments on the side).
