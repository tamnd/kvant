There is something wrong with the classical electron theory of conduction. Why is it studied in both school and university physics? The merit of this theory is that with its help Ohm's law and the Joule-Lenz law, the concept of conductor resistance and its temperature dependence were qualitatively explained. How is Ohm's law derived in classical electron theory? This law expresses the connection between the cause - the potential difference of the electric field and the effect - the movement of free electrons. In classical mechanics, this relationship is determined by Newton's law. The motion of an electron under the influence of field forces is approximately considered uniformly accelerated in the direction opposite the field strength vector (due to the negative charge of the electron). Taking the initial speed of this movement as zero immediately after the collision of a free electron with an ion, the maximum drift speed before the next collision can be expressed as follows: $v_{макс} a W$, where W is the average time between two collisions, and the acceleration is equal to a eE m (e and m are the charge and mass of the electron, E is the strength of the electric field acting on the electron).
