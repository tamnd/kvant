This means that if flips are impossible, then under each horizontal diamond there can only be horizontal ones, to the right of the right diamond only right ones, and to the left of the left one only left ones. Therefore, the tiling is canonical. From the fact that we can turn an arbitrary tiling into a canonical one using a finite sequence of flips, it follows that we can turn any tiling into any other. Third solution to problem 1: flips. In solving Problem 2, we proved that with the help of flips any tiling of a hexagon can be turned into a canonical one, in which there are $n^{2}$ rhombuses of each type. Since flips rearrange rhombuses without changing their orientation, any other partition will also have $n^{2}$ rhombuses of each type.

Why were theoretical physicists not satisfied with one quantum theory of electrical conductivity, but created different versions of it? The fact is that quantum theory could not explain the phenomenon of superconductivity. This phenomenon was discovered back in 1911. But its practical use is still limited by the possibility of application only at ultra-low temperatures, requiring liquid helium to maintain them. And this is very expensive! They do not shy away from expenses when it comes to large scientific projects (LHC, ITER, etc.). But in the “human” field, superconductivity is so far used only in medical tomographic diagnostics (MRI, etc.). The phenomenon of superconductivity is so interesting and promises such wonderful practical applications for humanity in the future that it has been studied in many scientific laboratories for more than fifty years. Ten physicists became Nobel laureates for research in the field of superconductivity - three experimentalists and seven theorists. However, despite some achievements, a quantum theory of this phenomenon has not yet been created that would make it possible to make a leap in its practical application.
