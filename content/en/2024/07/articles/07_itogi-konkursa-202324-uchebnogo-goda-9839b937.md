$L \cdot 2\pi x E_{(x)} = \frac{L\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, opening $E_{(x)} = \frac{\sigma}{2\pi x\varepsilon_0}$.

The initial distance from the ball with the charge to the electrically charged rod is $\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$, and at the moment when the speed of the ball is maximum, the distance between it and the rod will be $4aN + \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$.

These two distances correspond to the potential difference $U$ of the electric field created by the rod. This difference is equal to
$$U = \frac{\sigma \ln \left(2^{5/2}N + 1\right)}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}.$$
The speed of the ball is found from the equation
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = qU$$

$v = \sqrt{\frac{q\sigma \ln \left(2^{5/2}N + 1\right)}{\pi\varepsilon_0 m}}.$

At the moment of reaching maximum speed, the ball moves in a circle with radius $R = 4Na$. Its acceleration at this moment is $v^2/R$. The ball is subject to an electric field (in the direction from the charged rod) and is pulled by a thread (in the opposite direction). The sum of these forces provides the ball with acceleration. Hence the force $F$ of thread tension at this moment is found:

$$F = qE + \frac{mv^2}{R} = qE + \frac{2qU}{R} = \frac{q\sigma}{2\pi a\varepsilon_0} \frac{1}{4 + 1/\sqrt{2}} + \frac{q\sigma}{4\pi Na\varepsilon_0} \ln \left(2^{5/2}N + 1\right).$$
