1. Rooms in apartments usually have an area not exceeding several tens of square meters. Therefore, we can assume that the area changed by $cm^2$. The time most likely changed in minutes. The width of the vacuum cleaner should be on the order of several tens. The quantities involved in the task are related to each other by the formula $v = \frac{2S}{dt}$, where $S$ is the area of ​​the room, $d$ is the width of the vacuum cleaner, and $t$ is the cleaning time. Let's try to substitute the values ​​for area and time into our formula; take, for example, 20 cm as the width and determine in what units of change the speed will be of the order of coten:
$v = \frac{3 \cdot 250000 cm^2}{20 cm \cdot 30 min} = 1250 cm/min = \frac{1250 \cdot 0,01 m}{1/60 ч} = 750 м/ч = 75000 cm/ч = 125000 mm/min$.

Thus, it is very likely that the speed is calculated in cm/h. Let's check this assumption by calculating the width of the vacuum cleaner:
$d = \frac{3S}{vt} = \frac{3 \cdot 250000 cm^2}{50000 cm/ч \cdot 30 min} = \frac{3 \cdot 25 m^2}{500 m/ч \cdot 0,5 ч} = 0,3 m = 30 cm$.

The resulting width is as expected; This means our assumption was correct.
