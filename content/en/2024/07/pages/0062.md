2. Let $V$ be the volume of the vessel, $v_0$ the initial amount of substance in the vessel, and $\Delta v$ the amount of substance moving into the vessel from the atmosphere. Let us write down the equations of state of the gas in the vessel before and after injection:

$$p_0V = v_0RT_0, \quad p_1V = (v_0 + \Delta v)RT_1.$$

Let the air pressure inside the pump be equal to $p_B$, and $dV_B$ be the volume occupied by a portion of air in the colibe substance $dV$ inside the pump. When pumping a specified portion of air into a vessel, the pump performs mechanical work $\delta A_H = p_BdV_B$. But from the Mendeleev–Clapeyron equation $p_BdV_B = RT_0dV$, therefore $\delta A_H = RT_0dV$. Thus, the total mechanical work of the pump is

$$A_H = \Delta vRT_0.$$

Let us write down the first law of thermodynamics for a system consisting of a gas initially located in a vessel and pumped into it by a pump:

$$A_H + U_0 + U_1 = U_K.$$

Here $U_0 = v_0C_VT_0$ is the initial internal energy of the air initially located in the vessel, $U_1 = \Delta vC_VT_0$ is the sum of the internal energies of the pumped air at the stage of pumping into the vessel, and $U_K = (v_0 + \Delta v)C_VT_1$ is the final internal energy of the system under consideration. The molar heat of air at constant volume is $C_V = 5R/2$. From here

$$\Delta vRT_0 + \frac{5v_0RT_0}{2} + \frac{5\Delta vRT_0}{2} = \frac{5(v_0 + \Delta v)RT_1}{2}.$$

Thus,

$$\frac{T_1}{T_0} = \frac{5v_0 + 7\Delta v}{5(v_0 + \Delta v)} = \frac{p_1}{p_0}\frac{v_0}{(v_0 + \Delta v)}, \frac{\Delta v}{v_0} = \frac{5}{7}\left(\frac{p_1}{p_0} - 1\right).$$

3. Let $\vec{T}$ be the force acting on the thread at the point of its attachment, equal in magnitude to the tension force of the thread at a given point. Then, since the system is in equilibrium,

$$\vec{T} + m\vec{g} + \lambda L\vec{E} = 0.$$

Let us introduce the coordinate system $xy$, where the $x$ axis is directed horizontally to the right, and the $y$ axis is directed vertically upward. Then from the equilibrium condition

$$E_y = \frac{mg}{\lambda L}, E_x = \frac{T}{\lambda L}.$$

Let us connect the tension forces of the thread $T_0 = mg$ at the point of attachment of the load and $T$ at the point of attachment of the thread. Let us consider an infinitesimal element of a thread of length $dl$. Its equilibrium condition is written as follows:

$$\left(\vec{T} + d\vec{T}\right) - \vec{T} + \vec{E}\lambda dl = 0.$$

Proceria on the direction of the tangent to the thread, we get

$$dT + \lambda E_\parallel dl = 0.$$

Let the tangent to the thread form an angle $\varphi$ with the vertical. Then

$$E_\parallel = E\cos \varphi - E\sin \varphi,$$

where

$$dT + E_y\lambda dl\cos \varphi - E_x\lambda dl\sin \varphi = 0.$$

But $dl\cos \varphi = dy$ and $dl\sin \varphi = -dx$, so

$$dT + E_y\lambda dy + E_x\lambda dx = 0.$$

Summing up, we get

$$T - T_0 = T - mg = -E_y\lambda(y - y_0) - E_x\lambda(x - x_0).$$

Since $y - y_0 = H$ and $x - x_0 = -s$, we have

$$T - mg = \lambda\left(E_xs - E_yH\right).$$

No since $T = \lambda LE_x$, then

$$\lambda LE_x - mg = \lambda\left(sE_x - HE_y\right) = \lambda sE_x - \lambda H\frac{mg}{\lambda L},$$

$$E_x = \frac{mg}{\lambda L}\frac{L - H}{L - s}.$$

For the directionality of the power plant field we obtain

$$E = \sqrt{E_x^2 + E_y^2} = \frac{mg}{\lambda L}\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{L - H}{L - s}\right)^2}.$$
