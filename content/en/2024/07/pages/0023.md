Consider an equilateral triangle with side k, divide it into regular triangles with side 1 and mark all the vertices of these triangles; We call the resulting construction a k-triangle. In what follows, by straight lines we will always mean straight lines parallel to the sides of this triangle and passing through at least one marked point. a) Lemma. Let A be a marked point in a k-triangle. Then there is a unique way to draw k lines such that all marked points, except perhaps A, are covered by these lines. Namely, for each side of the k-triangle, we need to draw all the lines parallel to it and lying between this side and the point A (including the side itself, but excluding the line containing A Fig. 1 (Fig. 1). Proof. Induction on k. The base for k 1 is easy to check: we need to draw a line containing the two remaining points, except A. To proceed, consider the side of the k-triangle on which A does not lie. If the line containing this side is not drawn, then all k + 1 marked points on this line must be covered by different lines; this is impossible, since there are k lines. So, this line is drawn. Eliminating it and the points of the k-triangle lying on it, we obtain a k 1 -triangle in which k 1 lines are drawn with the same conditions.
