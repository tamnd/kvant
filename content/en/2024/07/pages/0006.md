This correspondence is called the length reduction efficiency. It shows that the longitudinal dimensions of bodies relative to stationary reporting systems are reduced in comparison with the length measured in movable ones.

Both are effective: time substitutions and length contractions predicted by Albert Einstein have been confirmed many times in various physical experiments and phenomena. These same ones showed a fairly transformed Lorentz to describe the functional connections between spatial coordinates and time.

For low speeds, when velocities relative to motion are observed much less than the speed of light, the Lorentz transformation transforms into the classical Galilean transformation:

$$x = x' + Vt', \quad t = t'.$$

Galileo's transformations show that both spatial segments and time intervals in classical physics remain unchanged, i.e. invariant when passing from one inertial observer to another. The consequence of this is the absolute nature of time, according to which it flows equally at all points of the Universe.

An important consequence of the Lorentz transformation is the constancy of the following quantity in inertial reporting systems:

$$c^2 \left( t_2^2 - t_1^2 \right) - \left( x_2^2 - x_1^2 \right) =$$

$$= c^2 \left( \left( t_2' \right)^2 - \left( t_1' \right)^2 \right) - \left( \left( x_2' \right)^2 - \left( x_1' \right)^2 \right).$$

Generalization of this correspondence to the case of three spatial coordinates $(x, y, z)$ allows Herman Minkowski to conceive of a four-dimensional space-time, in which each of the variables has the meaning of a coordinate of a four-dimensional space.

Relativistic law of velocity addition. The relative theory is usually called relativistic theory. This name is associated with the English word relativity - relative. Let's use this term and we are talking about the law of addition of speed. This law connects the velocities of a point in two inertial reporting frames. Having specified the increments for the variables $(x, t)$ and $(x', t')$ using equation (1) and dividing these increments by each other, we obtain

$$v = \frac{v' + V}{1 + \frac{v' V}{c^2}}.$$

where $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ and $v' = \frac{\Delta x'}{\Delta t'}$ are the speed of a point in a stationary and moving reporting system, $V$ is the speed of a moving system relative to a stationary one.

Expression (3) is the relativistic law of velocity addition. At low speeds, when $V \ll c$, it turns into the classical law of speed addition:

$$v = v' + V.$$

It is easy to see that this expression satisfies Einstein's second postulate. To do this, it is enough to equate $v'$ and $V$ to the speed of light, resulting in $v = c$.

Relativistic energy and momentum. Lorentz transformations not only provided a new understanding of space-time relations, but also fundamentally changed the definition of such concepts as mechanical energy and momentum of a freely moving body. According to the relative theory, the relativistic energy $E$ and momentum $p$ of a free material particle are equal to the corresponding

$$E = \frac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}, \quad p = \frac{mv}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}.$$

where $m$ is the particle mass. The value $E_0 = mc^2$ is called rest energy. It is easy to verify that it is of gigantic magnitude even for small rest masses. Floor-
