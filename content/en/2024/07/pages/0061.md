After filling the second vertical section from the left with water, the water will begin to flow into the third elbow, fill the jumper below between the third and fourth and “cut off” the air in the third elbow from the atmospheric air. An air “plug” is formed, the pressure $p$ inside which will increase as the fourth elbow is filled. Let us write down the equilibrium conditions (pressure equality) for the first and second vertical sections of the tube (Fig. 23):
$$p_0 + \rho g H = p + \rho g l,$$
for the third and fourth:
$$p_0 + \rho g l = p + \rho g (l - x).$$
From here we get
$$H - l = x.$$
Since the air inside the tube began to skim away from the volume corresponding to one vertical section, then, according to the Boyle-Mariotte law
$$p_0 l S = px S.$$
From the last two equalities we find
$$p = \frac{p_0 l}{H - l}.$$
We substitute this into the equilibrium conditions for the first two sections of the tube and get
$$p_0 + \rho g H = \frac{p_0 l}{H - l} + \rho g l,$$
or
$$\rho g (H - l)^2 + p_0 (H - l) - p_0 l = 0.$$
Denoting $p_0 / g = l_0$ for convenience, we solve the quadratic equation, leaving only the positive root that has a physical meaning:
$$H = l + \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{l_0^2 + 4l_0 l} - l_0 \right).$$

The volume of water pours can be determined by the total length of the pipe filled with water:
$$V = S (H + 3l - x) = 4Sl.$$

1. Let $v_0$ be the speed of docks $a$ and $b$ before colliding with docks $c$, and $m$ be the mass of each dock. Since there is no friction between docks $a$ and $b$, and docks $b$ and $c$ are fastened upon impact, then immediately after the impact the speed of docks $a$ is equal to $v_0$, and the speed of docks $b$ and $c$ is $v_0 / 2$ (from the law of conservation of momentum). Let us introduce the axis $x$ in the direction of velocity $v_0$. Since the horizontal surface is smooth, the center of mass of the system moves at a constant speed $v_{\text{цм}} = 2v_0 / 3$. At the moment of impact, the center of mass of the system is ahead of the center of the dock $a$ by amounts $L / 3$, and by the time the dock $a$ is entirely on the dock $c$, the center of mass of the system lags behind the center of the dock by the same amount $L / 3$. Then to move dock $a$ we get
$$\Delta x = \Delta x_{\text{цм}} + \frac{2L}{3} = \frac{2(v_0 \tau + L)}{3},$$
where $\tau$ is the time of movement of dock $a$ relative to docks $b$ and $c$. Next, we will combine docks $b$ and $c$ into one body of mass $2m$ and characterize it with index 2. We will characterize board $a$ with index 1. Let $\vec{F}$ be the friction force acting on the first body from the second, and $x$ be their relative displacement after the impact. Note that friction occurs only in the area where docks $a$ and $c$ overlap. The magnitude of the sliding friction force is directly proportional to the force of the normal reaction of the rough part of the support surface, therefore $F_x = -\mu mgx / L$. Let's write Newton's second law for each of the bodies:
$$m\vec{a}_1 = \vec{F}, \ 2m\vec{a}_2 = -\vec{F},$$
where
$$\vec{a}_1 - \vec{a}_2 = \vec{a}_{\text{отн}} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m} + \frac{\vec{F}}{2m} = \frac{3\vec{F}}{2m}.$$
Then we obtain the equation of motion for the variable $x$:
$$x'' = -\frac{3\mu gx}{2L}.$$
This is the equation of harmonic oscillations with cyclic frequency $\omega = \sqrt{3\mu g / (2L)}$. Its general solution has the form
$$x(t) = A \sin (\omega t + \varphi_0).$$
Let us determine $A$ and $\varphi_0$ from the initial conditions:
$$x(0) = A \sin \varphi_0 = 0, \quad \varphi_0 = 0,$$
$$x'(0) = \frac{v_0}{2} = \omega A \cos \varphi_0, \quad \text{или} \quad A = \frac{v_0}{2\omega}.$$
