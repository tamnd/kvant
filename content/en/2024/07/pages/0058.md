1. The elevator covers the required distance of $n$ floors with a height of $l_0$ each in the minimum possible time $t$, if accelerated by acceleration $a_0$ to a speed $v_0$, then moves at a constant speed $v_0$ for time $t - \frac{2v_0}{a_0}$ and decelerates to a complete stop in time $\frac{v_0}{a_0}$.
Let's draw the corresponding graph of the elevator speed versus time (Fig. 18).
The area under it is proportional to the distance traveled:
$$n l_0 = \frac{t + \left(t - \frac{2v_0}{a_0}\right)}{2} v_0,$$
whence $t(n) = \frac{n l_0}{v_0} + \frac{v_0}{a_0}$.
However, this formula is valid only for $t - \frac{2v_0}{a_0} > 0$, i.e. when the elevator manages to reach maximum speed $v_0$. This condition can be rewritten as $n > \frac{v_0^2}{a_0 l_0}$. If the elevator does not have time to reach the maximum speed $v_0$, then the following strategy for the dependence of speed on time becomes optimal in time: it is necessary to accelerate the watering time with acceleration $a_0$ and slow down half the time with the same acceleration module.
A graph of speed versus time is presented at figure 19. In this case, the distance traveled
$$n l_0 = \frac{a_0 t}{2} t,$$
whence $t(n) = 2 \sqrt{\frac{n l_0}{a_0}}$.
By condition, both points belong to the case $t(n) = \frac{n l_0}{v_0} + \frac{v_0}{a_0}$, therefore $t_2 = \frac{2 l_0}{v_0} + \frac{v_0}{a_0},$
$t_4 = \frac{4 l_0}{v_0} + \frac{v_0}{a_0}$. Then
$$t_3 = \frac{3 l_0}{v_0} + \frac{v_0}{a_0} = \frac{t_2 + t_4}{2} = 6,5 \text{ с.}$$
From the above calculations it follows that when lifting one floor, the elevator does not reach the maximum possible speed. So
$$t_1 = 2 \sqrt{\frac{l_0}{a_0}} = \sqrt{2 \left(t_4 - t_2\right) \left(2 t_2 - t_4\right)} = 2 \sqrt{3} \text{ с} \approx 3,5 \text{ с.}$$
3. Note that the thread is not taut before the ice is placed in the vessel. This means that the center of mass of the “lever + vessel” system is located horizontally at the level of the support, which makes it possible to exclude the corresponding terms in the moment equation.
The graph has 4 sections. At the first, very indo, the ice heats up. At the second stage, the ice melts and water begins to flow from the ice into the vessel, evenly distributed along its bottom. However, this section ends before the ice has time to completely grow - at the moment the ice flake detaches from the day, i.e. at the end of the lost section, the remaining ice burst into flames. By the way, the beginning of this section also does not necessarily coincide with the moment of the beginning of melting, because water can form in some depressions on the ice and the position of the center of mass of the ice can remain unchanged for some time. In general, the behavior in the second section is almost impossible to predict, since the process highly depends on the shape of the piece of ice, as well as on how exactly the body will be brought to it. In the third section, first the melted ice floating on the surface of the water is put into the vessel, and then the water is heated to boiling point. In the fourth section, the water has already reached boiling point and evaporates. The change in the tension force of the string at the beginning is associated with the redistribution of the weight of the contents as it heats up, and at the end - with a change in the mass of the contents. The mass of ice can be found using the third segment of the graph from the rule of moments $(x - \text{dлина} \frac{1}{8} \text{ рычага})$:
$$T_3 \cdot 2x = mg \cdot 3x, T_3 = 3 \text{ Н}, m = 0,2 \text{ кг.}$$
The heating power can be easily calculated using four segments, knowing the mass evaporated over a known period of time. Good points on the graph: $(1600 \text{ с; } 3 \text{ Н})$ and $(3200 \text{ с; } 1,8 \text{ Н})$. From these we obtain $\Delta T = 1,2 \text{ Н}, \Delta m = \frac{2 \Delta T}{3g} = 0,08 \text{ кг}, \Delta t = 1600 \text{ с.}$ Hence
$$P = \frac{L \Delta m}{\Delta t} = 115 \text{ Вт.}$$
Knowing the power, it is easy to calculate the initial temperature of the ice:
$$t_0 = \frac{cm \Delta t_{\text{BOДЫ}} + \lambda m - P \tau_3}{c_{\text{лъда}} m} \approx -81 \text{ °C.}$$
