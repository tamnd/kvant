Are TRAJECTORIES so familiar to you?

It would seem that the concept of trajectory, introduced at the very first steps in mastering a physics course, could be simpler? However, even when describing various mechanical movements it “multiplies”, breaking the framework of the simple first set of straight lines and circles. Inevitably we have to turn to parabolas, spirals, cyclones when it is necessary to display the shape of the orbits of natural and artificial satellites, planets, comets and astrudes. And what are the trajectories of molecules in liquids and gases, charged parts in electric and magnetic fields?...

Yes, this, at first glance, a simple concept will accompany us for a long time when studying physics.

Questions and tasks

1. A black board is moving along a smooth horizontal table with speed v. What shape should chalk be left to this board, thrown horizontally with a speed u perpendicular to the direction of movement of the board, if: a) the friction between the chalk and/docker is negligible; b) is the training great?

2. What line does the midpoint of the segment between two pedestrians walking uniformly along parallel roads describe?

3. Two automations simultaneously start moving at the same speeds from the intersection of two mutually perpendicular roads. What trajectory will the second automatic period follow? And the first relative to the second?

4. Can the directions of the instantaneous velocity and acceleration of a matter point coincide when it moves along a curved path?

5. A wheel of radius R rolls without slipping along a horizontal road with a speed v₀. What trajectory does the point on the wheel rim follow? What is the radius of curvature at its vertex?

6. Two particles interacting with each other form a closed system, the center of mass of which ends. At some point in time, the position of the part and the trajectory of the part whose mass is m₁ are known. How to construct the trajectory of the second particle if its mass is m₂ = $\frac{1}{2} m₁$?
