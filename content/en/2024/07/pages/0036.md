It seemed that all that was left was to construct one of the vertices of the original triangle at $\Omega$ and the job was done. But the construction did not work out. So, let’s show how, starting from the triangle $HIO$, you can construct circles $\Omega$ and $\omega$ with a circumference and a ruler. We have the centers of these circles. All that remains is to construct their radii. To do this, we will use formulas that express the lengths of the sides of the triangle $HIO$ through $R$, $r$ and $p$ (see [8]). Let's get the system

$$\begin{cases} OH^2 = 9R^2 + 8Rr + 2r^2 - 2p^2, \\ IH^2 = 4R^2 + 4Rr + 3r^2 - p^2, \\ OI^2 = R^2 - 2Rr, \end{cases}$$

where

$$R^2 = \frac{OI^4}{2\left(OI^2 + IH^2\right) - OH^2},$$

$$r = \frac{R^2 - OI^2}{2R},$$

$F(x) = (x - a)(x - b)(x - c) = 0,$

where $a$ is the length of the tangent segment drawn from the vertex $A$ of the triangle to the point of tangency, and $b$ and $c$ are defined similarly (Fig. 4). In [8] it is shown that the triangle equation takes the form

$$F(x) = x^3 - px^2 + \left(r^2 + 4Rr\right)x - r^2p = 0.$$
