In order for a photon starship to be an effective means of interplanetary travel, a constantly operating source of photons of gigantic power is required. Such a source of light pressure can only be the annihilation of matter and antimatter. For other physical mechanisms, the output of light energy and the created light pressure are orders of magnitude lower for the same fuel mass consumption. As noted at the beginning of the article, obtaining antimatter in large quantities is an impossible task. To do this, you will have to spend more energy than you can get in the form of light. All this makes photon rocket projects pure fantasy. Much more realistic are the launches of miniature sailing starships to the stars using powerful light sources located on the ground or in space. Such miniature space sailboats are planned to be sent to nearby stars as part of the Breakthrough Starshot project. The sailboat itself (Fig.4) will consist of a StarChip nanoprobe weighing 1 g and an ultra-light sail with a mass of 1 g and an area of ​​$16 м^{2}$. A 100 GW laser complex will be used to accelerate StarChip. Laser beams concentrated on the sail should, according to scientists, provide an acceleration of the sailboat of 10,000 g and accelerate it to a speed of 15% to 20% of the speed of light in five minutes.
