MATHEMATICAL CIRCLE

"An easy solution to a difficult geometry problem"

K. KOZERENKO, A. GERASIMOVA, A. LUZGINA

WE put QUOTES IN THE TITLE OF THE ARTICLE because it is a quote. This was the title of L. Euler’s article [1], which he published in 1767. In it, Euler posed the question of reconstructing a triangle from the point of intersection of altitudes H, the point of intersection of medians M, the point of intersection of bisectors I and the center of the circumcircle O (Fig. 1). The fact that the point M lies on the segment HO appeared in this article (see [2]). Fig. 1 215 years passed, and in 1982, the American mathematician William Wernick published a list of 139 problems [3], in which he tried to systematize the problems of reconstructing a triangle from some of its three points using a compass and a ruler. Among these problems, Euler's problem was mentioned at number 80 as hitherto unsolved. In 1996, Myers announced the solution of another 29 problems from Wernick’s list [4], among which was problem $№80.^{1}$ As an example, Myers gave the solution

one of 29 tasks. This solution used the following theorem (see [7], chapter 5). Theorem. Let a segment of length 1 be given. Then, if a cubic equation with rational coefficients has no rational roots and has a positive (real) root a, then a segment of length a cannot be constructed using a compass and ruler. The proof of this theorem is not simple and uses algebraic apparatus that goes far beyond the scope of the school curriculum, so we do not present it (however, we advise the reader to understand it). In this article, based on the same theorem, we will provide a proof that the answer to problem 80 from Wernick’s list is negative, i.e. that there is no algorithm for reconstructing a triangle using a compass and a ruler from points H, I and O. But, if a compass and a ruler are missing, then a natural question arises: is there a tool that, adding to the compass and ruler, can solve the problem? We will answer this question as well. To begin with, we note (and this is simply amazing!) that with a compass and a ruler, starting from the triangle HIO, you can restore many elements of the triangle: construct the circumcircle:, the incircle Z, the circle of nine points H (Euler’s circle) and even find the semi-perimeter p of the desired triangle (Fig. 2). When we discovered this, we thought that was almost it!
