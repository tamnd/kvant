**Weierstrass' Theorema.** Let $f(x) - \text{непрерывная на отрезке } [a; b]$ be a function. Then there are points $x_1$ and $x_2$ from this segment and for all points $x \in [a; b]$ the inequality $f(x_1) \leq f(x) \leq f(x_2)$ is true.

**Problem 5.** Let a closed convex curve $\gamma$ be given on the plane. Then there are three points $A_1, A_2$ and $A_3$ on it, and $A_1A_2 = A_1A_3$ and chords $A_1A_2$ and $A_1A_3$ cut off an area equal to the area of ​​the function bounded by the curve $\gamma$ (Fig. 10).

**Solution.** Let us define the curve $\gamma$ of the parametrically continuous function $\gamma : [0; T] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$. In this case, this function takes values ​​not on a straight line, but on a plane. Again, its continuity can be intuitively understood within the framework of the second definition. Namely, a small change in the variable $t$ corresponds to a small change in the point $\gamma(t)$ on the plane.

For an arbitrary point $A_1$ on the curve, two chords can be drawn that divide the figure into three parts equal in area. Let us denote the ends of the chords by $A_2$ and $A_3$. And the bypass path $A_1A_2A_3$ is done clockwise. The length of the chord $A_1A_2$ is denoted $l(t)$, the length of the chord $A_1A_3$ is denoted $r(t)$. Our task is to prove that the difference $l(t) - r(t)$ can be equal to zero for some $t$.

Let $t_0$ be the value of the parameter $t$ such that the length of the chord $l(t_0)$ is minimal. And the path of this $t_0$ corresponds to the point $A_1$. We shift the point $A_1$ to the point $A_2$, the path of the new point $A_1$ corresponds to the parameter $t_1$. Then notice that the chord $l(t)$ becomes the chord $r(t)$. In addition, $l(t_0) - r(t_0)$, due to minimality, $l(t_0)$ is less than zero, which means $l(t_1) - r(t_1)$ is greater than zero. And it remains to refer to the Bolzano-Cauchy theorem.
