The linear dependence of the resistivity of copper on temperature, known as $R$ of the heating element, can be represented as $R(T) = R_0(1 + \alpha(T - T_0))$, where $R_0$ is the resistance of the heating element at a temperature of $T_0$, $\alpha$ is the temperature coefficient of resistance. Let the water temperature change from $T_1$ to $T_2$ in time $\tau$, then, according to the law of conservation of energy and the Joule-Lenz law, the correspondence $mc(T_2 - T_1) = \frac{U^2}{R_{\text{cp}}}\tau$ will be valid, where $c$ is the specific heat of water, $m$ is the mass of water, $U$ is the source voltage,
$$R_{\text{cp}} = \frac{R(T_1) + R(T_2)}{2} = R_0\left(1 + \frac{\alpha}{2}(T_1 + T_2)\right).$$

Here $T_0 = 0^\circ\text{C}$. Let $\tau_1$ be the time of heating water from $T$ to $T + \Delta T$, and $\tau_2$ be the time of heating water from $T + \Delta T$ to $T + 2\Delta T$. Then
$$\tau_1 = \frac{mcR_0\Delta T}{U^2}\left(1 + \frac{\alpha}{2}(2T + \Delta T)\right),$$
$$\tau_2 = \frac{mcR_0\Delta T}{U^2}\left(1 + \frac{\alpha}{2}(2T + 3\Delta T)\right).$$

According to the conditions of the problem we have
$$\frac{\tau_2}{\tau_1} = \frac{1 + \frac{\alpha}{2}(2T + 3\Delta T)}{1 + \frac{\alpha}{2}(2T + \Delta T)} = 1 + \frac{p}{100}.$$
From these equalities we find
$$\Delta T = \left(\frac{1}{\alpha} + T\right)\frac{2p}{200 - p}.$$
