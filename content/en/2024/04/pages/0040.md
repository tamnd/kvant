2. Both rest and sliding (9 points)

When studying the friction force as part of a school physics course, they usually say that the maximum value of the static friction force is equal to the sliding friction force. In fact, in order to bring a body out of a state of rest, it is necessary to apply a force somewhat greater than the sliding friction force. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about two coefficients: the static friction coefficient $\mu_{\pi}$ and the sliding friction coefficient $\mu_{\mathrm{c}} \left( \mu_{\mathrm{c}} < \mu_{\pi} \right)$. The static friction force satisfies the correspondence $F_{\mathrm{tp.пок}} \leq \mu_{\pi} N$. After transition to sliding, another coefficient is used to determine the friction force:
$F_{\mathrm{tp.ck}} = \mu_{\mathrm{c}} N$.

A. For a wooden block of mass $m$, located on an extended wooden horizontal surface, the coefficients of static and sliding friction are equal to $\mu_{\pi} = 0,5$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{c}} = 0,4$, respectively. Periodic changes in the horizontal force begin to act on the block, the projection of which on the horizontal axis $x$ in the first half of the period is equal to $F_{x}^{(1)} = 1,001 \mu_{\pi} mg$, and in the second half of the period changes sign and becomes equal to $F_{x}^{(2)} = -1,001 \mu_{\pi} mg$. Draw graphical dependences of the speed of the bar on time for two periods longer after the start of the process. The period of change of force is $T = 4$ s, the acceleration of free fall is $g = 10 \mathrm{m/c}^{2}$.
