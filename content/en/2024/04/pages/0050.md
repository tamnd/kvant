$$a(t) = \frac{\mu_\pi mg}{t_0} - \mu_c g.$$

3. Let us denote by $\Delta T(h) = (t_k - t(h))$ the temperature difference between the drop and the environment at height $h$. Let $\Delta t$ be replaced by a small drop mass $\Delta m$ in a short time, then the relation is valid

$$\lambda \Delta m = \alpha \Delta T(h) S \Delta t,$$

or, if the drop speed is $v$,

$$\frac{\lambda v \Delta m}{\alpha S} = \Delta T(h) \Delta h.$$

When summing up the expressions on the right side of this formula, a value is obtained that is numerically equal to the area of ​​the figure limited by the temperature profile graph, the abscissa and ordinate axes. This area does not depend on the size of the drop and the speed of its fall. Thus, the total mass that turns into ice during the fall is proportional to its surface area and inversely proportional to the speed of the fall:

$$m \sim \frac{S}{v} \sim \frac{R^2}{v}.$$
