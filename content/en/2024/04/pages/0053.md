3. Team $A$ won.
Let there be at least one knight $r$ in $B$. Then $r$ won against at least double knights from $A$, let $s$ be one of them. Since $s$ is a knight, he correctly answered the question asked to him, i.e. he lost to a liar. But from the rules it follows that each player passes $s$ no more than once, and both the knight $r$ and the knave pass. This contradiction shows that $B$ consists only of liars. Let's assume that $A$ consists of only knights. In this case, each of them beats some liar from team $B$, but each liar in $B$ beat no more than one knight from $A$, since he lied when answering the question. Consequently, different knights from $A$ correspond to different knaves from $B$, therefore there are no fewer knights in $B$ than in $A$; contradiction.
Thus, there is at least one liar in team $A$; let's denote one of them by $l$. Then $l$ lied, i.e. it cannot be passed by any liar from $B$ – and therefore not by any player from $B$. This means that $l$ either won all his games, or it was not his turn. In either of these cases, team $A$ won.

5. Let us denote by $O$ the midpoint of the other $DBE$ circles circumscribed about the triangle $DBE$ (Fig.11). The line $BO$ is the outer bisecting tris in triangle $DBE$, and therefore in triangle $ABC$. But the triangle $ABC$ is equilateral, so $BO \parallel AC$.
Note further that $\angle EOD = \angle EBD = \angle ABC$. Thus, in equilateral triangles $EOD$ and $ABC$ the angles at the vertices and also the bases are equal, therefore the triangles themselves are equal. Hence, firstly, $BA = BC = OE = OD$. Secondly, the distance from point $O$ to line $DE$ is equal to the distance from point $B$ to $AC$, and the latter is equal to the distance from $O$ to $AC$ (since $BO \parallel AC$). This means that the point $O$ lies on the bisector of the angle between the lines $DE$ and $AC$.
From the condition $\angle DEF = \angle CFE$ it follows that this bisector is the perpendicular bisector to the segment $EF$. Thus, $OF = OE = OD$. In other words, point $O$ is the center of a circle circumscribed about triangle $DFE$. Therefore, $2\angle DFE = \angle DOE = \angle ABC$, which is what was required.
