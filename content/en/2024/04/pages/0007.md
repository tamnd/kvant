Many mathematical problems arise in connection with the figure of the right-handed knight. Let's look at some of them. It follows from the definition that if a right-handed knight can move from square $(i, j)$ to square $(k, l)$, then it can also make the reverse transition from square $(k, l)$ to square $(i, j)$. It is also easy to notice that the knight's tracks are located periodically along the rows, columns and diagonals of the docks with an interval of 5 cells. Therefore, the following statements hold.

**Proposition 2.** The number of different paths of a right-handed knight on a board of size $n \times n$, $n \geq 5$ is equal to five. Moreover, each path passes exactly once through every consecutive 5 cells in the rows, only and diagonals of docks.

Let us now consider the question of the number of tracks of a right-handed horse on a given path.

**Statement 3.** The number of tracks of a right-handed knight on any path on a board of size $n \times n$, $n \geq 5$ is equal to $\left\lfloor n^2/5 \right\rfloor$ or $\left\lfloor n^2/5 \right\rfloor$ (here $\lfloor u \rfloor$ denote, respectively, the circle of visas and up to the nearest integer).

**Proof.** Let a board of size $n \times n$, $n \geq 5$ be given. Let's assume that the right-handed knight starts from square $(1, 1)$. Then, taking into account the periodicity, it will leave $\left\lfloor n/5 \right\rfloor$ traces in the first line. And on the board there are $\left\lfloor n/5 \right\rfloor$ lines with the same arrangement of traces. The first trace of the trace in the second row will be in the fourth column. Therefore, in the second line the knight will leave only $\left\lfloor (n-3)/5 \right\rfloor$ tracks, and on the board there are $\left\lfloor (n-1)/5 \right\rfloor$ lines with the same arrangement of tracks. Likewise, the third line will leave $\left\lfloor (n-1)/5 \right\rfloor$ knight tracks, and there are $\left\lfloor (n-2)/5 \right\rfloor$ similar lines on the board. In the fourth line, the number of traces is $\left\lfloor (n-4)/5 \right\rfloor$ and there are $\left\lfloor (n-3)/5 \right\rfloor$ similar lines on the board. Finally, the number of traces in the fifth line is $\left\lfloor (n-2)/5 \right\rfloor$, and the number of similar lines on the board is $\left\lfloor (n-4)/5 \right\rfloor$. All subsequent lines will coincide with one of the first heels in terms of the location of the tracks in them. Thus, the total number of knight tracks on the board in this case is equal to the following value:

$$S_1 = \left\lfloor \frac{n}{5} \right\rfloor^2 + \left\lfloor \frac{n-3}{5} \right\rfloor \left\lfloor \frac{n-1}{5} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n-1}{5} \right\rfloor \left\lfloor \frac{n-2}{5} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n-2}{5} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{n}{5} \right\rfloor^2 + \left(\left\lfloor \frac{n-1}{5} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n-4}{5} \right\rfloor\right)\left(\left\lfloor \frac{n-2}{5} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{n-3}{5} \right\rfloor\right).$$

Substituting $n$ together for successive values ​​of $n = 5k, 5k + 1, \dots, 5k + 4$, we find that $S_1$ is equal to $\left\lfloor n^2/5 \right\rfloor$ or $\left\lfloor n^2/5 \right\rfloor$ depending on $n$.

We get a similar result if the knight starts from cells $(2, 1), (3, 1), (4, 1)$ and $(5, 1)$. We leave verification of these facts to the readers as an exercise. By Statement 2, this is sufficient to complete the proof.

Of course, the same results are also true for a left-handed knight.

In this section, we will prove our main result concerning the number of holes and dominoes in a minimal rigid packing of arbitrary docks of size $n \times n$, $n \geq 2$, and also present a general method for constructing minimal rigid packings.

Let us denote by $D_{\min}(n)$ the minimum number of dominiques, and by $H_{\max}(n)$ the maximum number of holes in rigid laying on a board of size $n \times n$. It is obvious that these quantities are related to each other as follows:
