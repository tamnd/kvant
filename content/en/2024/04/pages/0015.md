Is there a lot of “garbage” generated when the brain works?

The brain is another example of a human system that is seemingly isolated from the rest of the organization (from infection or medications we sometimes take). Nerve cells are the life span of an organization and require special care and protection. If you look closely, the brain actually has several barberovas protections. The first is from external influences; it is a durable scoop box about 7 mm thick. The second one also protects against injury; it is the liquid in which the brain floats; it acts as a damper during sudden impacts. And finally, the third, most mysterious, blood-to-cephalic barrier (BBB) ​​- between the network of blood vessels and brain tissue. The specification barrier is selective: through it, oxygen and nutrients enter the brain, and carbon dioxide and products of brain activity are removed. At the same time, a healthy BBB of the brain must be impermeable to bacteria, viruses, medications, and tumor cells.

But what about brain waste?
After all, 20–25% of the total energy received by the organization is spent for the functioning of the brain.

How are waste products removed from the brain area? For a long time (more than hundreds of years) it was believed that there were no lymphatic vessels in the brain. Recently, however, thin fluid-filled channels have been discovered between brain cells. This is how the brain is cleared of waste. This system, a kind of analogue of the lymphatic system, received a similar name - lymphatic.

But when does this happen? And this has recently been answered: during sleep. It turns out that the main function of the brain during sleep is to remove harmful substances that have accumulated during the day. And only secondarily, during sleep, the brain processes information and replenishes the day. This is why the body needs to spend a third of its life sleeping. Moreover, it turned out that brain cells decrease in size during sleep. This increases the distance between cells and facilitates the removal of waste.

That’s how important sleep at night and movement during the day are!
