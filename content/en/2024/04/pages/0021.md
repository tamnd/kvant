The rigid structure, indicated in figure by the values ​​of the powerful masses at the ends and the length of the sections of the imperfect rods, is held in place without movement, and the adopted length of the imperfect and inextensible rope is attached at the upper end to the grinding and we occupy a vertical position. The structure is released without giving it movement. With what accelerations will the loads move immediately after release?
Let us introduce a coordinate system with the cutoff origin at the point of attachment of the rope to the junction of the horizontal and vertical rods. Let one of the horizontal axes $x$ be directed along the horizontal rod from the first load to the second, the second horizontal axis $z$ is perpendicular to the horizontal rod, and the axis $y$ is directed downwards along the vertical rod. Along the $z$ axis, the weights and points of the rods do not move at all. And since it is indicated that the rope is long and at the initial moment is located vertically, this means that the load $M_3$ (low) and the places where the rope is attached to the rods at the initial moment can only move along the horizontal axis $x$. At the initial moment, all external forces acting on the system (gravity forces and the tension force of the rope) are directed vertically, which means that the center of mass of the motion system at the initial moment is in the vertical direction. As a result, we can assume that for the initial moment of motion there is a stationary center of rotation of the system, relative to which it begins to rotate with some angular acceleration, let’s denote it $\beta$. This instantaneous center of rotation is located on a vertical rod below the attachment point at a distance from the beginning of the cutoff equal to the vertical coordinate of the center of mass of the system
$$y_{\text{цм}} = \frac{M_3 L_3}{M_1 + M_2 + M_3}.$$
Relative to this instantaneous center of rotation, the center of mass of the system is at the same horizontal level with coordinate $x$ equal to
$$x_{\text{цм}} = \frac{-L_1 M_1 + L_2 M_2}{M_1 + M_2 + M_3}.$$
The moment of all external forces relative to the instantaneous center of rotation is equal to
$$\left( M_1 + M_2 + M_3 \right) g x_{\text{цм}} = \left( -L_1 M_1 + L_2 M_2 \right) g.$$
The moment of inertia of all system loads relative to the instantaneous center of rotation is equal to
$$J = M_1 \left( L_1^2 + y_{\text{цм}}^2 \right) + M_2 \left( L_2^2 + y_{\text{цм}}^2 \right) + M_3 \left( L_3 - y_{\text{цм}} \right)^2.$$
Instantaneous angular acceleration is equal relative to the moment of forces to the moment of inertia of the system:
$$\beta = \frac{\left( -L_1 M_1 + L_2 M_2 \right) g}{J}.$$
The linear acceleration of each of the loads is equal in magnitude to the product of the angular acceleration $\beta$ and the distance $s$ from this load to the instantaneous center of rotation and is directed perpendicular to the segment connecting the instantaneous center of rotation with this load.
