1. At the stadium (8 points)
Three small bodies are initially at rest at three equidistant points belonging to a circle 3 m long (Fig. 1). At some point, they simultaneously begin to move in one direction around a circle with constant speeds $v_1 = 4,0$ m/s, $v_2 = 3,6$ m/s, $v_3 = 3,0$ m/s, respectively. In this case, the bodies do not collide, passing each other mismo. A column is the smallest arc of a circle containing three bodies. For example, in the initial position the length of the column is 2 m. Find the minimum length of the column during the movement of the body.

2. Good, flat (6 points)
The cities $A$ and $B$ are connected by a road, which consists of two sections: “good” (with recently made repairs, where cars drive fast) and “bad” (with old broken asphalt, on which cars drive slowly). At some point in time, a car leaves city $A$ for city $B$, then cars continue to leave every 30 seconds. On a “good” section of the road all cars drive at the same high speed, and on a “bad” section - at the same low speed. If we calculate the arithmetic average of the speed of all automatic cars on the road at some moment, when the first car has already arrived in the city $B$, and the last one has not yet left the city $A$, then we get a value of $u_1 = 70$ km/h. If we calculate the arithmetic average of the speed of only those automatic vehicles on the road that have already traveled half the distance between cities, we will get a value of $u_2 = 60$ km/h. Find the automatic speed at "good"

3. Echo sounder on an ice floe (8 points)
figure 2 shows the profile of a section of a resting ice floe (side view). The length of the side of the cage corresponds to 30 cm. On the flat horizontal surface of the ice floe, measurements are taken using a special echo sounder, placing the echo sounder at points with different coordinates along the $x$ axis. The echo sounder emits vertical downward sound signals, and after some time registers the signal reflected from the day. It can be assumed that after reflection from the day, the signal propagates vertically upward. The graph presented at figure 3 shows the dependence $t(x)$, where $t$ is the time elapsed from the moment the sound pulse is emitted by the echo sounder until the moment of reception of the signal reflected from the day, and $x$ is the coordinate of the echo sounder. Plot a graph of the distance from the top surface of the ice floe to the day versus coordinate $x$. Consider that the average speeds of sound propagation in ice and water are 3000 m/s and 1500 m/s, respectively.
