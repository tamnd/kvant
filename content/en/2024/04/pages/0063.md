Taking into account the weightlessness of the threads, as well as the symmetry of the system, we can conclude that the tension forces of the threads on which the ball $M$ is suspended are equal to each other in absolute value (Fig. 23,a): $|\vec{T}_O| = |\vec{T}_{O1}| = |\vec{T}_B| = |\vec{T}_{B1}| = T_1$. Having written Newton's second law for the ball in projection onto the axis $y$, we obtain $Mg = 2T_1 \cos \alpha$, we open the moment about the axis, the passage through the point $O$ will take the form $T_A \frac{L}{4} = mg \frac{L}{4} + T_1 \frac{3L}{4} \sin 2\alpha$, or $T_A = mg + 3Mg \sin 2\alpha$, where $L$ is the length of the rod $AB$.

After burning out the thread, the ball will move along a circular arc with the center at point $B$ and radius $\frac{3L}{4}$. The tension force of the thread attached to the ball will be maximum when it passes point $E$, when the thread takes a vertical position (Fig. 23, b). The speed $v$ of the ball at point $E$ can be found from the law of conservation of total mechanical energy:
$$\frac{Mv^2}{2} = Mg \frac{3L}{4} (1 - \cos \alpha),$$
whence $v = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2} gL(1 - \cos \alpha)}$.

The value of $T_2 = T_{B2}$ can be obtained from Newton's second law, written in projection onto

$$\text{osc } y: \frac{Mv^2}{3L/4} = T_2 - Mg, \text{откуда}$$

$$T_2 = \frac{4M}{3L} \frac{3}{2} gL (1 - \cos \alpha) + Mg = Mg (3 - 2 \cos \alpha).$$

The moment of the tension force $T_2$ relative to the axis $O$ in this case will also be maximum. Therefore, the tension force of the thread attached to the point $A$ of the rod will also be maximum, which can be found from the equation for moments of force, relative to the axis point: $T_{Amax} \frac{L}{4} = mg \frac{L}{4} + T_2 \frac{3L}{4}$. Taking into account the above expression for $T_2$ we have $T_{Amax} = mg + 3Mg (3 - 2 \cos \alpha)$. Thus, the required relation is equal to
$$\frac{T_{Amax}}{T_A} = \frac{mg + 3Mg (3 - 2 \cos \alpha)}{mg + 3Mg \sin \alpha} = \frac{1 + 18 (3 - \sqrt{3})}{10} \approx 2.4.$$

3. From the conditions of the problem it is clear that the gas is initially isobarically compressed. Here it gives off an amount of heat. The gas is then heated isochorically where it receives a quantity of heat. Using the first law of thermodynamics, we find the amount of heat given off by the gas in an isobaric process:
$$Q_p = \Delta U_p + A_p = \frac{5}{2} vR (T_{min} - T_H),$$
where $v$ is the amount of gas substance, $T_H$ is the initial temperature of the gas. This amount of heat is a negative value. Now let’s find the amount of heat received by the gas in an isochoric process:
$$Q_V = \Delta U_V = \frac{3}{2} vR (T_K - T_{min}),$$
where $T_K$ is the final gas temperature. The total amount of heat supplied is equal to the external forces during the advabatic process.
$$A_{ad} = A = \frac{3}{2} vR (T_K - T_H).$$

Using the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation for two gas states we obtain
$$p_H V_H = vRT_H, p_H V_K = vRT_{min},$$ from where $\frac{V_H}{V_K} = \frac{T_H}{T_{min}}$.

Combining the written equations and taking into account that $\frac{V_H}{V_K} = 10$, we find
$$Q = A - 9vRT_{min} = 10 \text{ кДж.}$$
