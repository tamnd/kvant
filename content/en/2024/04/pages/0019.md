M2779. Prove that for every natural $k$ there is a natural $n$ such that

$$\text{НОК}(1,2,3,\dots,n) = \text{НОК}(1,2,3,\dots,n,n+1,\dots,n+k).$$

If from the series of the first $n (n \geq 2)$ natural numbers 1, 2, ..., $n$ we leave only powers of prime numbers (including the first power, i.e. the prime numbers themselves), and cross out the remaining numbers, then the LCM of the numbers remains the same, i.e. will be equal to LCM(1,2,3,...,n). Indeed, if we cross out the number $n = p_1^{\alpha_1} p_2^{\alpha_2} \dots p_k^{\alpha_k}$, where $k \geq 2$ (and $p_i$ are various primes included in the expansion with exponents $\alpha_i \geq 1$), then the LCM of the remaining numbers will be divisible by $n$, since the number $p_1^{\alpha_1}, p_2^{\alpha_2}, \dots, p_k^{\alpha_k}$ is in the remainder series.

This means that the equality

$$\text{НОК}(1,2,3,\dots,n) = \text{НОК}(1,2,3,\dots,n,n+1,\dots,n+k),$$

It is sufficient (and in fact necessary) that the numbers $n+1, n+2, \dots, n+k$ have no powers of prime numbers (including the first power).

This can be achieved as follows. Take $2k$ distinct primes $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_{2k}$. According to the Chinese remainder theorem, one can choose a natural number $n$ that simultaneously satisfies the following conditions: $n \equiv -1 (\bmod p_1 p_{k+1})$, $n \equiv -2 (\bmod p_2 p_{k+2})$, ..., $n \equiv -k (\bmod p_k p_{2k})$.

Then $n+i$ is divisible by $p_i p_{i+k}$ for every $i=1, 2, \dots, k$ and is therefore not a power of a prime number. This completes the solution to the problem.

Note that the same reasoning can be used to prove a stronger statement about the existence of $n$, for which none of the numbers $n+1, n+2, \dots, n+k$ is a prime number or an exact power (greater than 1).

In conclusion, we will say that the function $f(n) = \text{НОK}(1,2,3,\dots,n)$ plays an important role in number theory. It is clear that $f(n+1)$ is divisible by $f(n)$, and it is easy to establish that the part $\frac{f(n+1)}{f(n)}$ can only equal 1 or a prime number. The function $g(n) = \ln f(n)$ is known as the Chebyshev function. It is known to have linear growth, so $g(n) = n(1 + \alpha(n))$, where $\lim_{n \to \infty} \alpha(n) = 0$.

M2780. The chromatic number of a graph is the minimum number of vertices such that any two vertices connected by an edge are covered in different colors. Given a natural number $n \geq 3$ and a graph $G$ with a chromatic number $\chi(G) = n$, which has more than $n$ vertices. Prove that in $G$ there are two vertex-disjoint subgraphs $G_1$ and $G_2$ such that $\chi(G_1) + \chi(G_2) \geq n+1$.

A clique is a set of vertices in which any pair of vertices is connected by an edge. Let us choose the maximal clique $K$ in $G$, i.e. a clique consisting of the maximum number of vertices, and its vertices $A_1, \dots, A_k$ are empty. It is clear that $\chi(K) = k$. It follows from the condition that $2 \leq k \leq n$ and that $K$ do not coincide with $G$.
