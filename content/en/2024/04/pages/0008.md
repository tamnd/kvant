KVANT · 2024 / No. 4

Theorem. The maximum number of holes in a rigid installation is given by the following equation:

$$H_{\max}(n) = \begin{cases} \left\lfloor \frac{(n-2)^2}{5} \right\rfloor, & n \equiv 0, 2, 4 (\bmod 5); \\ \left\lfloor \frac{(n-2)^2}{5} \right\rfloor, & n \equiv 1, 3 (\bmod 5). \end{cases}$$

Proof. We have already considered minimal rigid layings of boards of size $n \times n$, $2 \leq n \leq 6$ and we can directly verify that this theorem holds for them. In the following, for convenience, we will consider a board of size $(n+2) \times (n+2)$, assuming that $n \geq 5$. The core of such a board has a size of $n \times n$. Let's place holes in the core in accordance with the following connections along a certain path of the right-handed knight. Carrying out such mowing along the entire border of the board, we will leave only the cells in the corners of the board unmoiled. It is easy to see that there are only 5 possible locations for the holes in the corners of the board, up to rotation by $\pi k/2$, $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. All of them are depicted on figure 12 (the dotted line shows the border of the board core). In all cases except 6), the squares of the board that remain unpatched can be completely tiled with Dominics.

Let $n = 5k$. Then it is easy to see that if we start placing holes on the board, starting from square $(2,2)$, then the corners of the hole will correspond to types a), b), d), e) (Fig. 13). Since the type of angles is uniquely determined by any of these angles, we will have a similar situation if we start to move holes, starting from cells $(4,2), (5,2)$ and $(6,2)$.
