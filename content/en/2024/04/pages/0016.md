PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2790–M2792 was offered at the XLV Tournament of Cities. Problem F2797–F2800 were offered at the 85th Moscow Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics.

Problem M2790–M2793, F2797–F2800

M2790. Prove that among the vertices of any convex hentagon one can find three that form an obtuse triangle, none of whose sides coincide with the sides of the hentagon. A.Yuran

M2791. Each cell of the N u N table contains a number. Let's call a cell C good if one of the cells adjacent to C on the side has a number 1 more than in C, and some other cell adjacent to C on the side has a number 3 more than in C. What is the greatest possible number of good cells? A. Chebotarev

M2792. There are 9 vertical columns in a row. In some places, horizontal sticks are inserted between adjacent posts; no two are at the same height. The beetle crawls from bottom to top; meeting a stick, he crawls along it to the next post and continues to crawl up. It is known that if a beetle starts at the bottom of the first (leftmost) column, then it will finish its path at the ninth (rightmost) column. Is it always possible to remove one stick so that the beetle at the end of the path ends up at the top of the fifth column?
