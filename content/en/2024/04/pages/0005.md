Taking into account the above, we can reformulate this problem into two problems of coating and packaging [2, 3]:

Place on a board size $n \times n$ squares as many $3 \times 3$-pieces as possible, formed by four $2 \times 1$-DOMINO tiles and one $1 \times 1$-hole (see Fig. 3); in this case, the intersection of two pieces along the same space is allowed, and all independent squares of the board given by the pieces must be completely filled with DOMINO.

The part of the board consisting of all its cells with the exception of the cells located along the edges is called the core. Thus, a board of size $2 \times 2$ does not have a core, and the core of a board of size $3 \times 3$ consists of one central cell. From the above it follows that when laying rigidly, holes can only be in the core of the board.

It is easy to see that the relative positions of two holes located at figure 4, accurate to a rotation of 90 degrees, are impossible with rigid installation. It follows that any area of ​​the board that is a square of size $2 \times 2$ contains no more than one hole, and any area of ​​the board that is a square of size $3 \times 3$ contains no more than two holes when laid rigidly.
