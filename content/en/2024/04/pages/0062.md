5. At the initial moment of time, the voltage between points $A$ and $B$ is zero. Therefore, the currents flowing through the circuit elements, including fuses, are obviously less than $I_\Pi$. Since the resistance of the fuses is negligible, no current flows through resistances $R_1$ and $R_2$ connected in parallel to $\Pi_1$. A circuit equivalent to the original one is presented in figure 21,a. The total resistance of section $AB$ is equal to $R/3$. In this case, the current $I$ flowing through the fuse $\Pi_1$ will be determined by the expression $I = \frac{U_{AB}}{R/3} = \frac{3U(t)}{R} = \frac{3at}{R}$. The distribution of currents in the system is shown in figure 21, b. Current $I$ reaches the nominal value at $\Pi_1$ at time
$t_1 = \frac{I_\Pi R}{3a} = 4$ min.

In this case, fuse $\Pi_1$ burns out, and the equivalent circuit takes the form shown in figure 21,c. The total resistance $R^*_{AB}$ of section $AB$ in this case will be equal to $R^*_{AB} = \frac{R}{2} + \frac{R}{3} = \frac{5R}{6}$. The current flowing through the resistance is equal to $I^* = \frac{U_{AB}}{R^*_{AB}} = \frac{6a(\tau + t_1)}{5R}$, where $\tau$ is the time missing from the moment of burnout $\Pi_1$. Due to the symmetry of the circuit, $I_{AD} = I_{AC} = \frac{I^*}{2}$, $I_{DB} = \frac{2I^*}{3}$, $I_{CB} = \frac{I^*}{3}$. Then the current $I_{CD}$ flowing through fuse $\Pi_2$ will be equal to $I_{CD} = I_{AC} - I_{CB} = \frac{I^*}{6} = \frac{a(\tau + t_1)}{5R}$. The current through $\Pi_2$ becomes the nominal value after a time $\tau$ after burnout $\Pi_1$, equal to
$\tau = \frac{5I_\Pi R}{a} - t_1 = \frac{14I_\Pi R}{3a} = 56$ min.

The total operating time of fuse $\Pi_2$ is
$t_2 = \tau + t_1 = 60$ min.
