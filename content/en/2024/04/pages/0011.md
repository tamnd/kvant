These organic substances are formed in leaves during photosynthesis, while different wavelengths of light incident on the leaf lead to the formation of carbohydrates - the red part of the spectrum and amino acids and proteins - the blue part of the spectrum. And the movement of photosynthesis products itself occurs through the vessels of the phloem. The ascending and descending flows are separated by a layer of cambium - cells that divide throughout the life of the tree. The cambium gives life to both xylem and phloem cells. The division of cambium cells, which stops only in winter, forms annual rings, which are visible on the cut of the trunk (see Fig. 1). The cambium and the adjacent layers of xylem and phloem are the most active parts of the tree trunk. Together with leaves and root hairs, they form a single organism that ensures the growth and development of the tree. As you move away from the cambium into the trunk, the conducting properties of the xylem are gradually lost, and it turns into a strong core. And when removed to the outer part, the phloem turns into bark, which primarily performs protective functions. As a tree trunk thickens over time, the bark on a growing tree trunk should gradually crack. The characteristics of a tree as an open system are continuous growth (and thickening of the trunk) throughout its life, annual shedding of leaves (needles) and renewal of root hairs. Some of the waste products of the tree accumulate in its core. A kind of closure of such an open vascular system of trees occurs at several levels and, thereby, connects the elements of the tree into a single organism. The first is at the nutrient level. Aboveground organs (trunk, branches, leaves, buds) need minerals and water from the soil, which are absorbed by the roots. And the roots need

The second is with the help of hormones. This shows some similarities with the animal world, although the number of phytohormones is smaller and their functions in plants are simpler. Phytohormones are involved in the process of growth and growth movements of the plant, under stress, in the phenomena of flowering, fruit development, shedding leaves... There are several hormones that activate plant growth. The two main phytohormones of growth are synthesized in the roots, this is cytokinin, and in developing buds and leaves, this is auxin. Cytokinin moves along the xylem from the roots to the top of the tree, and auxin, on the contrary, moves from the tips of the shoots along the phloem down to the roots of the tree. These hormones and their interaction actually bind the tree into a single organism, allowing further development of both the upper and lower parts of the tree. In this case, the presence of both hormones and the balance between them is important. Thus, cytokinin causes cell division only in the presence of auxin. Another growth hormone, gibberellin, is produced both in leaves and roots and is transported through the vessels of both xylem and phloem. Its role is to lengthen the stems. The cambium is located between the xylem, through which cytokinins move up the stem, and the phloem, through which auxins move down. This leads to the fact that with an excess of cytokinin, the predominant formation of phloem occurs, and with an excess of auxins, xylem occurs. There are several phytohormones that, on the contrary, suppress growth. This is important, for example, in the fall, when their accumulation puts the kidney cells into a state of rest. And in the spring, a decrease in their content awakens bud growth. When plants are stressed (as a result of dehydration, cold, etc.), the action of such hormones inhibits metabolic processes and protects cells from death. In autumn, these hormones lead to leaf fall and fruit drop.
