For the thread to break, it is necessary that the tension force $T_1$ reaches its maximum value, which is equal to $T_1 = 0,6 M g$. Using both equalities, we find that this will happen when the ball moves along the groove from its center to a distance $d = \frac{0,2 M - m}{2 m} l$. The motion of the ball is uniformly accelerated by acceleration $a = g \sin \alpha \approx g \alpha$, and it travels this distance in time
$$\tau = \sqrt{\frac{2 d}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{0,2 M - m}{mg \alpha}} l.$$

3. According to the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation, $p_1 n V = \frac{m}{M} R T_1$. After the balloon rises to a height $h$ and loses part of the gas with mass $\Delta m$, hydrogen with mass $(m - \Delta m)$ remains in the balloon shell, for which equation $p_2 V = \frac{(m - \Delta m)}{M} R T_2$ is valid. From the natural gas law it follows that $\frac{p_1 n V M}{R T_1} = \frac{p_2 V M}{R T_2} + \Delta m$. From here we obtain the final gas temperature:
$$T_2 = \frac{p_2 V T_1 M}{n p_1 V M - \Delta m R T_1}.$$ The change in temperature with height is $T_2 - T_1 = \tau h$. Finally we get
$$h = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{\tau} = \frac{1}{\tau} \left( T_1 - \frac{p_2 V T_1 M}{n p_1 V M - \Delta m R T_1} \right).$$

4. In addition to the force of gravity and the tension force of the thread, a charged bead is subject to a vertical upward force equal to $q E$ from the Earth's electron field. As this force rises to the top point of the trajectory, it does positive work on the bead equal to $-2 q E l$. In accordance with the law of conservation of mechanical energy, we can write $\frac{m v_0^2}{2} - 2 q E l = \frac{m v^2}{2} + 2 m g l$. The minimum initial speed of the bead provides the conditions under which, at the moment when the bead reaches the most point of its trajectory, the thread tension momentarily becomes zero. In this case, centripetal acceleration provides the sum of only two forces: $m g$ and $q E$. The equation of motion for this point will look like this: $m \frac{v^2}{l} = m g + q E$. Expressing the speed from the second equation and substituting it into the first, we get
$$v_0 = \sqrt{5 \left( g + \frac{q E}{m} \right) l}.$$

A ray of light will stop passing through the feature if, when falling on the second face of the feature $AC$ (Fig. 18), total internal reflection is observed, i.e. the angles of incidence on this face will be interrupted by the value determined by the equality $\sin \gamma = 1/n$. For the first facet of recognition, we write down the law of light refraction: $\frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \beta} = n$. The angle $\beta$ is determined from the correspondence for the angles of the triangle $ABC$: the sum of the interior angles of a given triangle is $\varphi + \pi/2 + \beta + (\pi/2 - \gamma) = \pi$, hence $\beta = \gamma - \varphi$. Substituting $\beta$ into the equality $\sin \alpha = n \sin \beta$ and using the trionometric formula for the sine of the angle difference (replacing $\sin \gamma = 1/n$ in it) we obtain $\sin \alpha = n \sin (\gamma - \varphi) = \cos \varphi - \sin \varphi \cdot \sqrt{n^2 - 1}$. From here
$$\alpha = \arcsin \left( \cos \varphi - \sin \varphi \cdot \sqrt{n^2 - 1} \right).$$
