Gas exchange occurs in the alveoli (thin-walled bubbles covered with a network of capillaries): oxygen enters the blood from the air, and carbon dioxide is removed from the blood. Since the oxygen pressure in the air filling the alveoli is greater than in the venous blood, oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the venous blood and turns it into arterial blood. The diameter of the alveoli is about 300 microns, their number is hundreds of millions, and the total area of ​​the interaction area (air chamber) is 100 m². At deep DEX, the total surface of the alveoli due to their stretching can increase to 250 m² (!) (two orders of magnitude greater than the human surface area). During gas exchange in the exhaled air, the oxygen content decreases from 21 to 16%, and carbon dioxide increases from 0.03 to 4%.

We present the mechanism of blood movement through the circulatory system; a kind of pump is the contractions of the heart. In the case of lymph, the situation is different: there is no “pump”, the movement of lymph occurs due to gravity and is quite slow. When the muscles surrounding the lymphatic vessels contract, the lymph is pushed in one direction. Special valves (Fig. 6) prevent it from moving in the opposite direction. As a result, it is the mobility of the person himself that serves as the moment for lymph. More movement means better removal of harmful substances. Less - harmful substances accumulate in the body.

Let's compare the speed of blood movement through the circulatory system and lymph through the lymphatic system and the volumes pumped through both systems. About 6 liters of blood passes through the heart per minute. At the same time, 15 milliliters are filtered through the walls of the blood capillaries into tissue fluid. Of these, only 3 milliliters of liquid enter the lymphatic vessels per minute, and up to 4 liters per day. The difference in volumes dug up per unit of time is more than three orders of magnitude.
