The speed of fall is determined from the equality of the resistance force and the force of gravity:
$$Mg = \beta Sv^2, \quad v^2 \sim \frac{\rho V}{S} \sim R.$$
Taking into account that the mass of a drop is proportional to $R^3$, we obtain for the fraction of freezing mass the correspondence
$$n = \frac{m}{M} \sim \frac{R^{3/2}}{R^3} \sim R^{-3/2}.$$
Thus, the fraction of the mass of a large drop that turns into ice is equal to
$$n = \frac{1}{4} \cdot (2,25)^{-3/2} = \frac{2}{27} \approx 7,4\%.$$
4. A. Let us denote by $V(I)$ the dependence of the voltage on the tunnel diode on the current flowing through it. Then the current in the circuit under consideration is determined by the equation $U_0 - IR = V(I)$, the number of solutions of which depends on the value of the parameter $R$. A graphical method for solving this equation is to determine the coordinates of the point (or points) of intersection of the current-voltage characteristic and straight line $U = U_0 - IR$. On the graph presented in figure 8, the blue lines show a straight line corresponding to a resistance value at which the circuit current is determined ambiguous (the equation has three solutions). Thin solid black lines show straight lines corresponding to such resistance values ​​$R_1$ and $R_2$ that the equation has two solutions. It is easy to see that the original resistance values ​​at which the current in the circuit is determined uniquely are given by the inequalities
$$R < R_1, \quad R > R_2.$$
Now all that remains is to determine the values ​​of $R_1$ and $R_2$. The value of $R_1$ is determined from the equation
$$u = 6u - 4iR_1.$$
A similar equation can be written to determine the value of $R_2$:
$$4u = 6u - iR_2.$$
Solving these equations, we get the answers:
$$R > \frac{2u}{i}, \quad R < \frac{5u}{4i}.$$
B. A parallel connection of a tunnel diode and a resistor can provide a constant current in this circuit (in some voltage ranges) if the current-voltage characteristic of this parallel connection has a horizontal section, which is realized at the value of the resistance of the ballast resistor
$$R_0 = \frac{U_2 - U_1}{I_1 - I_2} = \frac{600}{9} \text{ Ом} \approx 66,7 \text{ Ом}.$$
Now you can easily calculate the constant value of the current through the load:
$$I = I_1 + \frac{U_1}{R_0} = 13 \text{ мА}.$$
Let us denote the minimum input voltage $U_{\min} = 3,0 \text{ В}$, then the value of the current $I$ through the load is realized at the following values ​​of load resistance:
$$\frac{U_{\min} - U_2}{I} \leq R \leq \frac{U_{\min} - U_1}{I},$$
or
$$170 \text{ Ом} \leq R \leq 215 \text{ Ом}.$$
Using similar reasoning, one can calculate the range of possible load resistance values ​​for the maximum input voltage $U_{\max} = 3,4 \text{ В}$. The result is a double inequality
$$201 \text{ Ом} \leq R \leq 246 \text{ Ом}.$$
The intersection of these ranges gives the answer to the problem question:
$$201 \text{ Ом} \leq R \leq 215 \text{ Ом}.$$
