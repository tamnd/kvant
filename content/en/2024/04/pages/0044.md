B. What amount of heat $Q$ will be released in the circuit over a very long time after the key is moved to position 2?
4. Inflate the tire (15 points)
The problem considers a physical model that describes some features of the process of inflating the chamber of the pedal wheel. The chamber volume $V_0$ is considered unchanged. The initial air pressure in the chamber is $p_0$ at ambient temperature $T_0$. After pumping air into the chamber, pressure $p$ must be created at the same temperature $T_0$. Air is considered an ideal diatomic gas ($c_V = 2,5R$).
A. At this point the chamber is connected through a nipple to a vessel of very large volume $V_1$ ($V_1 \gg V_0$), which contains air at a temperature $T_0$ and such a pressure $p_{\text{max}}$ ($p_{\text{max}} > p$) that after filling the chamber and cooling it to a temperature $T_0$, the air pressure in the chamber becomes equal to $p$. The ippel lets air into the chamber until the pressure in it equals the pressure in the vessel, after which the ippel closes and does not release air back. A portion of air from a vessel is pushed into the chamber very quickly, so we can assume that the “pushing” process is carried out without heat exchange between this portion and the air remaining in the vessel, as well as between the air in the chamber and the environment. As a result, the temperature and air pressure in the chamber increases to values ​​$p_{\text{max}}$ and $T_{\text{max}}$. Some time after this, the air temperature in the chamber is compared with the ambient temperature $T_0$, the pressure in the chamber becomes equal to $p$. Assuming the known values ​​of $T_0 = 300$ K, $p = 3 \cdot 10^5$ Pa and $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa, determine $p_{\text{max}}$ and $T_{\text{max}}$.
B. Now imagines that the chamber is inflated with air using a hand pump. At the beginning of each cycle, the pump cylinder with volume $V_{\text{ц}}$ ($V_{\text{ц}} \ll V_0$) is filled with air at atmospheric pressure $p_0$ and temperature $T_0$. Let at the beginning of the $i$th cycle the pressure in the chamber be equal to $p_{i-1}$. On the $i$th cycle, the air in the cylinder is first adinabaticly reduced by the piston from pressure $p_0$ to pressure $p_i$, and then “pushed” through the nipple at constant pressure $p_i$ without heat exchange, as in point A of the problem. After this, the inlet valve opens, through which air from the environment enters the pump cylinder at a pressure of $p_0$, the piston moves to its original position, the cylinder is filled with atmospheric air at a pressure of $p_0$. This ends the cycle. Naturally, you will have to perform quite a few ($N \gg 1$) such cycles of “pushing” air into the chamber so that after the temperature has been established at $T_0$, the pressure in the chamber becomes equal to $p$. Since the chamber is inflated very quickly, the heat exchange between the air in the chamber and the environment begins after the pump has finished operating.
B1) How many cycles $N$ are needed to inflate the chamber? Parameters $p_0, p, V_{\text{ц}}$ and $V_0$ are considered known.
B2) What work $A_i$ is performed by the piston on the $i$-th cycle? Answers above reading $p_0, p_i$ and $V_{\text{ц}}$.
B3) Determine the change in pressure in the chamber at the $i$th cycle $\Delta p_i$, considering $p_0, p_i, V_{\text{ц}}$ and $V_0$ known.
B4) In order to find the pressure in the chamber after the end of the $k$th cycle, we use the following assumption. The relationship obtained in the previous paragraph can be represented in the form $\frac{\Delta p_i}{f(p_i)} = \alpha$, where $f(p_i)$ is some function $p_i$, $\alpha$ is a coefficient that does not depend on $p_i$ ($\alpha$ and $f(p_i)$ you know if you did point B3). Let's sum these expressions from $i = 1$ to $i = k$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{\Delta p_i}{f(p_i)} = \alpha k.$$
Summation can be approximately replaced by interlining:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{\Delta p_i}{f(p_i)} \approx \int_{p_0}^{p_k} \frac{dp}{f(p)}.$$
Determine the pressure $p_k$ in the chamber after the $k$ cycle. Parameters $p_0, V_{\text{ц}}, V_0$ and $k$ are considered known.
B5) Calculate $p_{\text{max}}$ and $T_{\text{max}}$, considering the known values ​​of the parameters $T_0 = 300$ K, $p = 3 \cdot 10^5$ Pa and $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa.
Note. The value of a certain interiorization from the degree of a function gives the formula
$$\int_{a_1}^{a_2} \frac{dx}{x^k} = \frac{1}{(k-1)} \left( \frac{1}{a_1^{k-1}} - \frac{1}{a_2^{k-1}} \right).$$
