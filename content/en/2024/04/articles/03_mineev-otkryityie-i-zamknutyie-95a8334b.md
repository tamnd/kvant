These organic substances are formed in leaves during photosynthesis, while different wavelengths of light incident on the leaf lead to the formation of carbohydrates - the red part of the spectrum and amino acids and proteins - the blue part of the spectrum. And the movement of photosynthesis products itself occurs through the vessels of the phloem. The ascending and descending flows are separated by a layer of cambium - cells that divide throughout the life of the tree. The cambium gives life to both xylem and phloem cells. The division of cambium cells, which stops only in winter, forms annual rings, which are visible on the cut of the trunk (see Fig. 1). The cambium and the adjacent layers of xylem and phloem are the most active parts of the tree trunk. Together with leaves and root hairs, they form a single organism that ensures the growth and development of the tree. As you move away from the cambium into the trunk, the conducting properties of the xylem are gradually lost, and it turns into a strong core. And when removed to the outer part, the phloem turns into bark, which primarily performs protective functions. As a tree trunk thickens over time, the bark on a growing tree trunk should gradually crack. The characteristics of a tree as an open system are continuous growth (and thickening of the trunk) throughout its life, annual shedding of leaves (needles) and renewal of root hairs. Some of the waste products of a tree accumulate in its core. A kind of closure of such an open vascular system of trees occurs at several levels and, thereby, connects the elements of the tree into a single organism. The first is at the nutrient level. Aboveground organs (trunk, branches, leaves, buds) need minerals and water from the soil, which are absorbed by the roots. And the roots need

products of photosynthesis occurring in leaves. If this exchange is insufficient, then growth slows down, branches and leaves shrink and decrease in size, the number of fruits... The second is with the help of hormones. This shows some similarities with the animal world, although the number of phytohormones is smaller and their functions in plants are simpler. Phytohormones are involved in the process of growth and growth movements of the plant, under stress, in the phenomena of flowering, fruit development, shedding leaves... There are several hormones that activate plant growth. The two main phytohormones of growth are synthesized in the roots, this is cytokinin, and in developing buds and leaves, this is auxin. Cytokinin moves along the xylem from the roots to the top of the tree, and auxin, on the contrary, moves from the tips of the shoots along the phloem down to the roots of the tree. These hormones and their interaction actually bind the tree into a single organism, allowing further development of both the upper and lower parts of the tree. In this case, the presence of both hormones and the balance between them is important. Thus, cytokinin causes cell division only in the presence of auxin. Another growth hormone, gibberellin, is produced both in leaves and roots and is transported through the vessels of both xylem and phloem. Its role is to lengthen the stems. The cambium is located between the xylem, through which cytokinins move up the stem, and the phloem, through which auxins move down. This leads to the fact that with an excess of cytokinin, the predominant formation of phloem occurs, and with an excess of auxins, xylem occurs. There are several phytohormones that, on the contrary, suppress growth. This is important, for example, in the fall, when their accumulation puts the kidney cells into a state of rest. And in the spring, a decrease in their content awakens bud growth. When plants are stressed (as a result of dehydration, cold, etc.), the action of such hormones inhibits metabolic processes and protects cells from death. In autumn, these hormones lead to leaf fall and fruit drop.

An example of a closed circulatory system in animals and humans

The circulatory system of animals is considered closed and consists of two circles of blood circulation: one circle closes the heart and lungs, the other – the heart and internal organs (Fig. 3). At the same time, the idea of ​​complete closure of the circulatory system is inaccurate. Its functioning requires interaction with several open systems: respiratory, digestive and lymphatic.

The respiratory system brings oxygen into the lungs and removes carbon dioxide. This process takes place in the alveoli of the lungs, produced by the capillaries of the circulatory system.

The digestive system is the main supplier of “building materials” to the body.

The role of the lymphatic system is less known. We present the mechanism of blood movement through the circulatory system; a kind of pump is the contractions of the heart. In the case of lymph, the situation is different: there is no “pump”, the movement of lymph occurs due to gravity and is quite slow. When the muscles surrounding the lymphatic vessels contract, the lymph is pushed in one direction. Special valves (Fig. 6) prevent it from moving in the opposite direction. As a result, it is the mobility of the person himself that serves as the moment for lymph. More movement means better removal of harmful substances. Less - harmful substances accumulate in the body.

Is there a lot of “garbage” generated when the brain works?

The brain is another example of a human system that is seemingly isolated from the rest of the organization (from infection or medications we sometimes take). Nerve cells are the life span of an organization and require special care and protection. If you look closely, the brain actually has several barberovas protections. The first is from external influences; it is a durable scoop box about 7 mm thick. The second one also protects against injury; it is the liquid in which the brain floats; it acts as a damper during sudden impacts. And finally, the third, most mysterious, blood-to-cephalic barrier (BBB) ​​- between the network of blood vessels and brain tissue. The specification barrier is selective: through it, oxygen and nutrients enter the brain, and carbon dioxide and products of brain activity are removed. At the same time, a healthy BBB of the brain must be impermeable to bacteria, viruses, medications, and tumor cells.

But what about brain waste?
After all, 20–25% of the total energy received by the organization is spent for the functioning of the brain.

How are waste products removed from the brain area? For a long time (more than hundreds of years) it was believed that there were no lymphatic vessels in the brain. Recently, however, thin fluid-filled channels have been discovered between brain cells. This is how the brain is cleared of waste. This system, a kind of analogue of the lymphatic system, received a similar name - lymphatic.

But when does this happen? And this has recently been answered: during sleep. It turns out that the main function of the brain during sleep is to remove harmful substances that have accumulated during the day. And only secondarily, during sleep, the brain processes information and replenishes the day. This is why the body needs to spend a third of its life sleeping. Moreover, it turned out that brain cells decrease in size during sleep. This increases the distance between cells and facilitates the removal of waste.
