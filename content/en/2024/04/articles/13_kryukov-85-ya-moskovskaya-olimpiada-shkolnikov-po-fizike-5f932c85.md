2. Both rest and sliding (9 points)

When studying the friction force as part of a school physics course, they usually say that the maximum value of the static friction force is equal to the sliding friction force. In fact, in order to bring a body out of a state of rest, it is necessary to apply a force somewhat greater than the sliding friction force. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about two coefficients: the static friction coefficient $\mu_{\pi}$ and the sliding friction coefficient $\mu_{\mathrm{c}} \left( \mu_{\mathrm{c}} < \mu_{\pi} \right)$. The static friction force satisfies the correspondence $F_{\mathrm{tp.пок}} \leq \mu_{\pi} N$. After transition to sliding, another coefficient is used to determine the friction force:
$F_{\mathrm{tp.ck}} = \mu_{\mathrm{c}} N$.

A. For a wooden block of mass $m$, located on an extended wooden horizontal surface, the coefficients of static and sliding friction are equal to $\mu_{\pi} = 0,5$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{c}} = 0,4$, respectively. Periodic changes in the horizontal force begin to act on the block, the projection of which on the horizontal axis $x$ in the first half of the period is equal to $F_{x}^{(1)} = 1,001 \mu_{\pi} mg$, and in the second half of the period changes sign and becomes equal to $F_{x}^{(2)} = -1,001 \mu_{\pi} mg$. The period of change of force is $T = 4$ s, the acceleration of free fall is $g = 10 \mathrm{m/c}^{2}$.

Each angle of a polygon has values ​​of either 90° - a of them are empty, or 270° - we will call them large and there are b of them empty, and a + b = 100. Let us write down the property of the sum of the angles of a polygon:

$$90^\circ a + 270^\circ b = 180^\circ (100 - 2);$$
$$(180^\circ - 90^\circ)a + (180^\circ + 90^\circ)b = 180^\circ (100 - 2);$$
$$(-90^\circ)a + (90^\circ)b = 180^\circ (-2);$$
$a - b = 4.$

Since a + b = 100, we find that there are only b = 48 large angles. Let's take any large angle. Let's extend one of its sides inside until it intersects again with the border of the polygon. Thus we get a cut that divides this large angle into two angles of 180° and 90°. In this case, the polygon was divided into two polygons, in which any two adjacent sides are perpedicular and the total number of large angles in them is at least one less than in the polygon before the cut. By poset, repeating this procedure as long as possible (no more than 48 times), we will obtain polygons with peredicular sides without large angles, i.e. rectilinear. Since at first there was one polygon and it was divided into two parts no more than 48 times, the result was no more than 49 rectangles.

3. After the redistribution of charges, the potentials of the balls will be equalized: $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2$. Since the shell is conductive and connected to practically infinite sources of charges, a charge is induced on its inner surface, equal in model to the charge of the ball inside and opposite in sign.
