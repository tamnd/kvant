How do charges know how to distribute them?
If the charges lie on a plane, then it is no longer clear how they should interact. Because in a vacuum and air - please, through metal - in no case, and they lie on the border! But we don’t have a plane at all, but a ball, any line connecting the charges passes through the metal and the charges should not interact. And this is not the end of the problem.

You know the formula for the relationship between field strength and charge density on the surface. Question: What is the pressure (ratio of force to area) on the surface?
If you simply multiply the field strength and charge density, you get an answer that is twice the correct one. The “charge on the surface” model failed us for the second time. What happens if we assume that the charge on the surface of the metal is in a thin, but still not zero-thick layer, at least partially protruding into vacuum or air? If you think about it, electrons, even without an excess charge, already rise above the level of atomic nuclei by approximately an international distance... Doesn’t such a model solve both problems?

The psychological reason for writing this article was admiring the articles of I.F. Ginzburg “Unsolved problems of fundamental physics” (Uspekhi Fizicheskikh Nauk, 2009, No. 5, p. 525).

29. Which is greater: $1 \cdot 22 + 2 \cdot 21 + 3 \cdot 20 + \dots + 22 \cdot 1$ or $2^2 + 4^2 + 6^2 + \dots + 22^2$? Explain your answer.

A. Zaslavsky

30. Badgers, squirrels, beans and chipmunks celebrated the New Year. At first, all the animals, except badgers, danced in a circle, and then everyone danced in a circle, except for the squirrels. In each round dance, no two identical animals stood side by side. What is the smallest number of beavers that could be present at the festival if there were 50 more squirrels than badgers?

G. Karavaev

31. Several segments are drawn inside the square ABCD, as in figure. The marked angles are equal. Prove that BN = DM.

K. Knon

32. Given a convex $n$-gon, divided into triangles by disjoint diagonals. Petya wants to sterilize some diagonals in order to obtain a partition of the $n$-gon into polygons, among which there can be more $m$-gons ($m > 3$). What is the smallest number of $m$-gons that can be guaranteed?

a) Solve the problem for $m = 4$.
b) Solve the problem in the general case.

E. Bakaev
