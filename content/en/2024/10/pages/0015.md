$k$ regular decagons with side 1 and rhombuses with side 1. Find the most possible value of $k$.

12 (USAMO-2022). Let $b \geq 2$ and $w \geq 2$ be integers, and $n = b + w$. Given 2$b$ identical black rods and 2$w$ identical white rods, the length of each of which is 1. These rods form a regular 2$n$-gon so that the parallel sides have the same color. After this, the convex 2$b$-gon $B$ is formed by parallel transfer of black rods, and the convex 2$w$-gon $W$ is formed in a similar way from white rods. figure 37 shows an example of polygons $B$ and $W$ in the case where $b = 3$ and $w = 2$. Prove that the difference between the areas $B$ and $W$ depends only on the numbers $b$ and $w$, and not on how exactly the black and white segments are chosen on the sides of the 2$n$-gon.

13 (Yu. Bazlov, St. Petersburg Olympiad, 2018). A regular hexagon is divided into equal rhombuses with sides parallel to the sides of the hexagon. On three sides of the hexagon, among which there are no adjacent ones, directions were set in the order of traversing the hexagon counterclockwise. Then an arrow was placed on each side of the rhombus, directed in the same way as the side of the hexagon parallel to this side. Prove that there is no closed path following the arrows.

14 (A. Ustinov, A. Yuran, Moscow Mathematical Olympiad, 2024). The plane is divided into parts by several straight lines, among which there are non-parallel ones (Fig.38). Those parts whose border consists of two rays were painted over. After this, another straight line was drawn. Prove that, regardless of the position of the new line, there are shaded points on both sides of it.

15 (G.S.M. Coxeter, [7]). Prove the following two theorems in affine three-dimensional geometry.

a) If all the faces of a convex polyhedron are parallelograms, then their number is equal to the product of two consecutive numbers.

6) If each face of a convex polyhedron has a center of symmetry, then the weight of the polyhedron also has a center of symmetry.

16. There are three boxes in a row, each containing $n$ balls. The first box contains balls placed with the number 3, the second with the number 2, and the third with the number 1. In one move, you are allowed to change a pair of balls in adjacent boxes. What is the minimum number of moves required to sort the balls: the first box should contain balls with the number 1, the second with the number 2, and the third with the number 3?

17. Prove that a double set of $n = 2^m + 1$ balls ($m \geq 1$) can be ordered in $n(2n - 1)/3$ rearrangements.
