Remark 6. The estimate proved in Proposition 9 shows that a double set of 5 balls cannot be sorted in less than 15 balls. Paper [3] proposed an algorithm that sorts a double set of $n$ balls in $\left\lfloor 2n^2/3 \right\rfloor$ balls. If $n = 2^k + 1$, then this algorithm works optimally and requires only $n(2n-1)/3$ balls. In addition, the authors of this article performed computer experiments and found that for $n \leq 20$, with the exception of the case of $n = 6$, sorting a double set of $n$ balls can indeed be done in $\left\lfloor n(2n-1)/3 \right\rfloor$ shifts. In this case, sorting occurs, generally speaking, no longer in the same way as it was in the case of $n = 5$. If $n = 6$, then you need one more shift than required by the lower estimate (23 instead of 22). There are also upper and lower bounds for the case when there are $k$ balls of each type, see [4].

For further study of the issues discussed, we recommend readers the article [5, 6] and the following assignment.
