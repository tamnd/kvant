Thus, there are shaded points on both sides of the straight line OY. 15. b) Let us divide each face of this polyhedron into parallelograms. Each edge of the polyhedron defines a certain set of parallel sides and thereby a certain set of parallelograms, which we call a zone. Each parallelogram defines two zones running in different directions along the surface of the polyhedron. These two zones must intersect again along another parallelogram, which we will call paired with respect to the first. The main and paired parallelograms are equal and located in parallel planes, since their sides are pairwise equal and parallel. Moreover, the plane of any parallelogram cannot be parallel to the plane of more than one parallelogram, since otherwise the polyhedron would not be convex. The remaining parallelograms from the partition of the same face F will have paired parallelograms lying in the same plane, which means that the paired parallelograms will add up to a face equal and parallel to F (paired to F). Every two faces will have a center of symmetry, and for adjacent faces these centers will coincide, because they must be the centers of symmetry of a common edge. Since, moving from a face to an adjacent face, we can go along all the faces of a given polyhedron, the center of symmetry will be common to all faces, which means it will be the center of the entire polyhedron. 16.
