By the way, in Yakutin they also use this method of “freezing” the soil at depth around the foundation concrete piles. Steel pipe piles are sunk into the ground next to the foundation. Inside a hollow pile-pipe with strong walls, hermetically sealed at the top and at the bottom, is poured, for example, kerosene, which does not harden even at temperatures below −50 ° C and whose density depends linearly on temperature. This is in contrast to water, whose density has an extreme at +4 °C. Therefore, the kerosene located in the upper part of the pipe piles cools greatly in winter and sinks to the lower part of the volume provided to it. There it is heated by the soil surrounding the pile, which freezes, ensuring the strength of adhesion of the piles to the surrounding soil. In the summer, the kerosene located in the upper part of the pipe warms up, but its thermal conductivity is very low, and convection of the liquid between the lower part of the volume and the upper part does not occur.
