Problem 12. A coin $M$ lying on a horizontal surface is moved with the help of a light non-straightening thread fixed in the urine $A$ and thrown over a stationary block $B$, as shown in figure 17 (top view). The free end of the thread is kneaded very slowly, so as the coin moves, the reaction force from the thread acting on it is compensated by the force of the coin. Consider the consideration $AB$ to be much larger than the radius of the block.

1) What trajectory does the coin follow?
2) At what distance from urine $A$ will the coin be when the section of thread between urine $A$ and block $B$ ($AB = d = 1,0$ m) straightens?
3) Estimate the work of the force acting on the coin during the movement of the coin being at the vertex $C$ of the rectangular coin $ABC$, and $AC = AB$. The mass of the coin is $m = 7,7$ g, the coefficient of exchange between the coin and the floor is $\mu = 0,3$.

1) The force of the thread acting on the coin is directed along the bisector of the angle $AMC$, the vertex of which is the coin, since the thread is weightless. The force of the exchange, compensating the force of the exchange thread, is directed opposite to the speed of the coin. This means that the speed of the coin during its movement is also directed along the bisector of the angle $AMC$. The coin's velocity vector has identical components on the sides of the angle $AM$ and $MC$. Consequently, sections of the thread $AM$ and $MC$ are shortened by the same amounts at the same time, i.e. $MC - AM = const$.

Posously, the coin moves along a hyperbola whose $A$ and $C$ are foci.

2) Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be the distances from the coin to the point $A$ and $B$, respectively, at the final time of its movement. Then, on the one hand, $x_1 + x_2 = d$, and on the other hand, from the definition of a hyperbola it follows that $\sqrt{2}d - d = x_2 - x_1$. From here we find the distance $x_1$:

$$x_1 = \left( 1 - \frac{1 - \sqrt{2}}{2} \right) d \approx 0,293 \text{ м}.$$

$$R = \frac{x_1^2 + d^2}{2x_1} \approx 1,85 \text{ м},$$

$$l_{\max} = R \arcsin \frac{d}{R} \approx 1,06 \text{ м}.$$
