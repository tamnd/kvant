Note that the decomposition of a permutation into a maximal short product of transpositions may not be unique. For example,

$$\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\
1 & 2 & 3 & 4
\end{pmatrix} = (43)(42)(32)(14)(13)(12) = (23)(13)(12)(41)(42)(43).$$

The process of decomposing a given permutation into a product of transpositions can be represented as a permutation graph and divided into levels. At each level he has exactly one point of intersection of paths connecting numbers and their images. We assign each such point a label equal to the number of the corresponding transposition in the decomposition of the permutation (transpositions are numbered from right to left). We will call such graphs interchangeable permutations. figure 28 shows two labeled permutations constructed using expansions (2).

Permutations and zonogons

Let a convex $2n$-gon be divided into parallelograms such that the sides of the parallelograms are equal and the sides of the polygon are parallel. Let us fix two opposite vertices of the polygon and mark them with their north and south poles ($N$ and $S$). The interiorum of the sides of a given $2n$-gon is numbered from 1 to $n$, moving counterclockwise from the north pole to the south. We will also assign the number $k$ to all segments parallel to the $k$ side. In particular, opposite sides of a polygon will have

Let us consider a broken line running along the sides of the polygon along the left border from $N$ to $S$, the links of which are the segments $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{n-1}, a_n$ (Fig. 29). We will successively transform it into a broken line $\tilde{a}_n, \tilde{a}_{n-1}, \dots, \tilde{a}_2, \tilde{a}_1$, going from $N$ to $S$ clockwise along the right border of the polygon. The ends of the broken line remain fixed at the poles. At each step, two adjacent links, which are the sides of the parallelogram of the partition, will be replaced by two other links - two other sides of the same parallelogram. The segment $a_k$ will be located on one of the segments marked with the number $k$. It is argued that this can always be done.
