Gromit's ideas and Wallace's developments

1. Catching up with the rules (20 points)
Wallace and Gromit fell in love with the game of treaties on a large flat surface, the rules of which were invented by Gromit. One participant proposed the curve along which he wanted to run, and the other had to invent the rules of the chase that would provide the first with such movement. They then ran in accordance with the proposed rules, with the first one marking the trajectory of his movement with pebbles. After the end of the chase, the participants climbed the building next to the empty one and admired the resulting curve. In one of the variants, the rules proposed by Gromit were as follows: in the starting position, the participants were at a distance of $L_0 = 100$ m and at the same time began to run at an equal speed of $v_1 = v_2 = 4$ m/s. Wallace always ran strictly in the direction of Gromit, and Gromit ran in a direction that always made the same angle $\alpha = 60^\circ$ with the direction away from Wallace.

With an error of no more than a second, determine the time it will take Wallace to catch up with Gromit. Find the law of change in the angular velocity of rotation of the chase participants. By what angle should the velocity vectors of the participants in the chase be rotated from its beginning to the moment of time when the distance between them decreases to $L = 25$ m? At what distances from their starting point are Wallace and Gromit at the end of the chase (when calculating these distances, the sizes of the participants in the chase can be neglected)? Roughly draw the curve that the pebbles left by Wallace form.

Note:
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-t} \cos \omega t \cdot dt = \frac{1}{1 + \omega^2},$$
$$\int_0^\infty e^{-t} \sin \omega t \cdot dt = \frac{\omega}{1 + \omega^2}.$$

2. Experiment on an unknown planet (20 points)
One day, while scanning the sky in search of interesting information, Gromit came across a program that talked about an experiment carried out by unknown scientists. Fortunately, all the numerical data in the work were expressed in terms of known constants (the charge and mass of the electron, the speed of light and Planck’s constant), so Gromit was able to convert them into “earthly” units. According to the information received, these scientists create a practically uniform magnetic field in a vacuum chamber, peredicular to the gravity field, which can be considered uniform. In this chamber, highly spherical nanoparticles with a removed charge of $\frac{q}{m} = +157,08$ C/kg were produced in the direction of a horizontal axis perpedicular to both fields. The magnitude of the projection of the initial velocity onto this axis was changed, and for each of its values, the height of the initial point above the horizontal “floor” of the chamber was selected so that the frequency (if possible) fell on the “floor”, and its flight range was the maximum possible. Based on the data obtained, Gromit plotted the dependence of the maximum possible flight range $L_{\max}$ on the value $v_0$ (Fig. 1). He especially noted that the break point on the graph corresponded to $v_0 \approx +39,64$ m/s. Having become acquainted with the graph, Wallace noted that it would be interesting to know what the acceleration of free fall is on the planet of experimenters.
