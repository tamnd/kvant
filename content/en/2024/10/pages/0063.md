The coordinate $x$ and the speed of the body $v$ depend on time according to the laws $x(t) = v_0 t - \frac{at^2}{2}$ and $v(t) = v_0 - at$, respectively.
At time $\tau$ – the moment the puck stops – can record
$$x(\tau) = v_0 \tau - \frac{a \tau^2}{2} = l, \quad v(\tau) = v_0 - a \tau = 0.$$
Solving this system of equations, we find the initial speed of the puck: $v_0 = \frac{2l}{\tau}$ and the acceleration with which it moved: $a = \frac{2l}{\tau^2}$. On the other hand, uses Newton's second law in projection onto the $x$ axis, namely the equation
$$-ma = -mg \sin \alpha - \mu mg \cos \alpha,$$
we get
$$a = g \sin \alpha + \mu g \cos \alpha.$$
Equating the two expressions for acceleration $a$, we find the friction coefficient:
$$\mu = \frac{1}{\cos \alpha} \left( \frac{2l}{g \tau^2} - \sin \alpha \right)$$
and we will finally get
$$v_1 = 2 \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha - \frac{l^2}{\tau^2}} = 4 \sqrt{6} \text{ m/c} \approx 10 \text{ m/c}.$$
2. Let's direct the $x$ axis of the coordinate system down along the inclined plane, connecting the origin of coordinates with the coordinate of the lower edge of the board in the equilibrium position. The equilibrium position is characterized by the initial slope of the spring $x_H$, i.e. $(M + m)g \sin \alpha = kx_H$. According to the condition, the board with the cube is moved down along the inclined plane, releasing the spring, and released at zero speed. If after this the board with the cube oscillates, during which the cube does not slide along the board, then these oscillations will be harmonic, since the board and the cube will move as one. The equation of motion of a board with a cube in projection onto the $x$ axis has the form
$$(M + m)x'' = (M + m)g \sin \alpha - k(x_H + x),$$
or $(M + m)x'' + kx = 0.$
It follows that the board with the cube performs harmonic vibrations with a circular frequency $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M + m}}$. During oscillations between the board and the cube, it acts with equation $mx'' = mg \sin \alpha - F_{\tau p}(t)$. The friction force in this equation is the restoring force. It depends on time and changes sign when the direction of movement of the board changes. Maximum force value

friction takes place in the lowest position of the board:
$$|F_{\tau p}^{\max}| = mg \sin \alpha + m |x_{\max}''|.$$
Here $|x_{\max}''|$ is the maximum acceleration of the board with the cube, equal to $\omega_0^2 A$, where $A$ is the desired maximum displacement of the board (oscillation amplitude).
At the same time, the friction force between the cube and the board, if the cube does not slide along the board, does not exceed the value of the sliding friction force, i.e. $|F_{\tau p}^{\max}| \leq \mu mg \cos \alpha$. Thus, the following inequality must hold:
$$mg \sin \alpha + m \omega_0^2 A \leq \mu mg \cos \alpha.$$
This inequality makes it possible to find the maximum displacement of the board at which the cube will not slide along the board during oscillations that arise in the system:
$$A = \frac{(m + M)g}{k} (\mu \cos \alpha - \sin \alpha) \approx 0,15M.$$

1. According to the conditions of the problem, $V_2 = nV_1$, and $V_3 = kV_2 = nkV_1$. Using the process equation 1-2, we get $p_2 = np_1$, and since $pV = vRT$, then $T_2 = n^2T_1$. Similarly, using process equation 2-3, we get $T_3 = kT_2 = n^2kT_1$. The efficiency of a heat engine cycle is $\eta = \frac{A}{Q^+}$, where $A$ is the work done by the gas per cycle, and $Q^+$ is the amount of heat received by the gas per cycle. For this cycle, using the correspondences obtained above, we have
$$A = \frac{1}{2}(p_2 - p_1)(V_3 - V_2) = \frac{1}{2}p_1V_1n(n - 1)(k - 1),$$
$$Q^+ = Q_{12} + Q_{23} = \Delta U_{12} + A_{12} + Q_{23}.$$
Here the change in the internal energy of the gas in section 1-2 is equal to
$$\Delta U_{12} = C_V(T_2 - T_1) = \frac{3}{2}vRT_1(n^2 - 1),$$
The kolic heat received by the gas in section 2-3 is equal to
$$Q_{23} = C_p(T_3 - T_2) = \frac{5}{2}vRT_1n^2(k - 1).$$
Finally we get
$$Q^+ = 2vRT_1(n^2 - 1) + \frac{5}{2}vRT_1n^2(k - 1),$$
$\eta = \frac{n(n - 1)(k - 1)}{4(n^2 - 1) + 5n^2(k - 1)} = \frac{1}{13} \approx 0,077.$
