Problem 8. A heavy bead is worn on nimь, which is attached to two mocks of a horizontal stitch (Fig. 12). The bead is moved to a position where the komor called nimь and shmania form a right triangle with an angle α and a release. Determine the acceleration vector at the initial time.

During the movement, the sum of the distances from the bead to the suspension points will remain constant, since the thread is inextensible. This means that the movement will occur along an elliptical arc with foci at the suspension points. The normal acceleration component is proportional to the square of the velocity, therefore the acceleration of the bead at the initial moment has only the tangential component $a_\tau$, i.e. it is directed tangentially to the ellipse. Let us denote the suspension points $F_1$ and $F_2$ (Fig. 13). Let $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ be the angles between the normal to the ellipse at the point where the bead is located and the thread segments. According to the optical property of the ellipse, $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2$. Let us introduce the notation $\varphi = \varphi_1 = \varphi_2$. Since the thread is smooth and inextensible, its tension force $T$ is the same along the entire length of the thread, which means that the projections of the tension forces of the thread segments on the tangent direction ($T\sin\varphi$) are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Therefore, acceleration is created by the tangential component of gravity. So the acceleration model is

$$a = a_\tau = g\sin\varphi = g\sin \frac{\pi/2 - \alpha}{2} = g\sin \left( \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\alpha}{2} \right).$$

The acceleration vector is directed at an angle $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\alpha}{2}$ to the horizon.

Problem 9. If you hang a picture upside down, attached to one star, there is a high probability that it will become warped over time. Why is her position unstable? Is it possible to somehow balance the picture without tying it to a star and without using two stars at some distance from each other?

Let us consider a rectangular picture of a thread 2c, the center of mass of which is located at the intersection of the diagonals, and the length of the thread is 2a (Fig. 14). In a position of balance,

when the picture is hung vertically, the suspension point is at a distance $b = \sqrt{a^2 - c^2}$ above the horizontal line passing through the ends of the thread. The center of mass of the painting is located at a distance $h = b + d$ below the suspension point. If the thread slides along the nail, then the suspension point lies on an ellipse with semi-major axis $a$ and semi-minor axis $b$. The center of mass of the painting is still located directly below the suspension point at a distance of $h$ from it. The system is in an unstable state while the center of mass decreases as the thread slides along the nail, i.e. if $h > b + d$. The equation of the ellipse is as follows:

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1.$$
