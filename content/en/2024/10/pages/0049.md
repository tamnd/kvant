3. Such different loans (17 points)

One of Wallace's devices used an air capacitor of three concentric conducting spheres with radii $a$, $2a$ and $3a$ (Fig. 2). The outer and inner (using a thin insulated wire threaded through small holes in the outer and middle spheres) spheres were connected to a source with EMF $\varepsilon$ and the outer sphere could be grounded using a switch, which for this purpose had to be moved to position 1. “What if we create the possibility of grounding the middle sphere as well?” - Gromit asked. After thinking, Wallace added pin 2 to the switch, connected to the middle sphere with a thin insulated wire without contact with the outer one. In one experiment, the switch was left in position 1 for a long time with the middle sphere uncharged, and then Wallace moved it to position 2.

What charge did the outer sphere have before the switch? What were the charges of all three spheres after the switch? How much heat was released in the circuit after switching?

Known are $a = 11$ cm, $\varepsilon = 20$ V, electrical constant $\varepsilon_0 \approx 8,85 \cdot 10^{-12} \text{Ф/m}$.

4. Ultrashort pulse (18 points)

To study the range of electric states in complex biomarkov molecules, Wallace needed to record the duration of processes in the range of hundreds of attoseconds. “To do this, it is necessary to learn how to generate electromagnetic pulses of even shorter duration,” he reasoned in the presence of Gromit. After thinking, he continued: “Any impulse in which the dependence of a changing quantity on time is described by the function $f(t)$ can be represented as a sum of harmonic oscillations with different frequencies (harmonics). But in this sum it is necessary to take into account not only the amplitudes of the harmonics, but also their phase shifts, and it is more convenient to do this using the method of complete amplitudes, within which the decomposition of the signal into harmonics is written in the form of an integral $f(t) = \int a(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}d\omega$, where integration is carried out over all frequency values ​​in the signal spectrum.” “It turns out that to create short pulses you need a set of harmonics with a spectrum in the form of a wide, almost uniform comb,” suggested Gromit. Note that due to the structure of his vocal cords, Gromit could not speak humanly, but he was a perfect telepath.

Check if Gromit's clue is correct. To do this, study a signal with such a comb spectrum consisting of $N$ identical teeth. In the first tooth, the amplitude of harmonics $a(\omega) = A$ in the frequency integral is from $\frac{\pi}{T}$ to $\frac{2\pi}{T}$, in the second - from $\frac{3\pi}{T}$ to $\frac{4\pi}{T}$ and so on (Fig. 3).
