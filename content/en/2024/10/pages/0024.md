Landdu on exam

At the very beginning of the 1950s, Landdu had to take exams from one group at the Physics and Technology Institute. Apparently, at that moment he had no desire to do this, and he decided to radically use the standard procedure. Entering the classroom, where students were already waiting for him, he said without preamble:
- Raise your hands those who want to get a “C” on the exam.
Several hands went up.
- “Come here,” Landdu told them and immediately gave everyone a “C” in their record books. When the “lucky ones” left the audience, he turned to the rest:
- Now raise your hands, those who want to get a "four".
Again several people raised their hands. The situation repeated itself, and another group of students left with the coveted grades in their transcripts.
- Well, - said Landdu, looking around at the handful of people who remained, - so you, though, will appreciate “five”?
The students looked down modestly.

- “There’s nothing you can do,” the academician smiled, “let’s get your notes.”
And he quickly gave everyone excellent grades. The entire exam took five minutes.
This amazing story had a funny continuation. In the early 1970s, the young Doctor of Science G., later an academician, decided to revive the “Landdu method” of taking exams at the Physics and Technology Institute. Starting the exam in one of the groups, he addressed the students in the same way:
- Raise your hands if you want to get three.
And then, to his horror... the whole group raised their hands. In great confusion, G. ran to the training unit.
