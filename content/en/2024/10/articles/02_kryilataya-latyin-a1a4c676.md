Is “permafrost” eternal?
S. VARLAMOV

IT IS VERY HOT INSIDE THE EARTH - according to physicists, in the center of the Earth the temperature reaches approximately 6000 degrees, and in what Celsius or Kelvin temperature scale this value is given, of course, does not matter, since the difference between the scales (273°) is small in comparison with the value itself. According to measurement results obtained while drilling to great depths, the temperature increase is approximately α = 3 degrees for every 100 meters. This corresponds to the fact that when studying the Earth, there is a flow of thermal energy to its surface, and in different places at a depth of about several hundred meters, the value of this flow fluctuates within $W = 0,03 - 0,05$ W/m². The magnitude of the flow depends on both the location (geographical coordinates) and the composition of the rocks, but practically does not depend on the average temperature on the surface.

Indeed, if at a depth of 10 km the temperature is 300 °C, and in different places on the Earth’s surface (for example, in Russia) the average temperature varies from +10 °C to -10 °C, then the temperature gradient, which is determined by the magnitude of the heat flow, differs in these places by only 3%. Based on the values ​​of the flow and temperature gradient, it is possible to estimate the thermal conductivity of the rocks that make up the upper part of the Earth:

$$\lambda = \frac{W}{\alpha} = 1,0 - 1,7 \text{ Вт}/(\text{К} \cdot \text{м}).$$

We will operate with the average values ​​applied $W = 0,04$ W/m², $\lambda = 1,5$ W/(K m²), $C = 2$ MJ/(K m³), ​​and consider several specific issues.

If the average temperature in the Moscow, Tula, and Ryazan regions is $t_0 = +8$ °C, then what is the temperature underground in these places at a depth of $h = 10$ m? Answer:

$$t_{10} = t_0 + h \frac{W}{\lambda} = +8,26 \text{ °C}.$$

If the average temperature in Yakutini is $t_0 = -8$ °C, then at what depth will the temperature be 0 °C? In other words, what is the thickness of the $H$ layer of “permafrost”? From Eq.

$$H \frac{W}{\lambda} - 8 \text{ °C} = 0$$

we get $H = 300$ m.

If average stable temperatures in the Moscow, Tula, Ryazan regions range from +20.6 °C in summer to -7.4 °C in winter, then what is the average depth $L$ of soil freezing during the winter period? We assume that from an average temperature of +8 °C to an average value between -7.4 °C and 0 °C the soil will release warm power

$$\frac{S \lambda(8 + 7,4) \text{ °C}}{2L} = \frac{SC \lambda(8 + 7,4/2) \text{ °C}}{t},$$

where $S$ is the area, $t$ ≈ 2 months is the duration of the cold period. From here there is $L \approx 1,3$ m.
