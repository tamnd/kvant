IT'S CURIOUS THAT...

To find the equation of the tangent to the polynomial $P(x)$ at some point $x_0$, you can go the direct route: find an arbitrary one at $x_0$, it will give the slope. Then write the equation of the straight line passing through the point $(x_0,P(x_0))$ with the resulting slope.

But it can be built differently. Divide $P(x)$ by $(x-x_0)^2$ with remainder:

$$P(x) = (x-x_0)^2Q(x)+R(x).$$

Then $y = R(x)$ is the tangent equation!

Let us prove this simple and unexpected fact.

Note that the straight line $y = R(x)$ passes through the desired point. Besides,

$$P'(x) = 2(x-x_0)Q(x)+(x-x_0)^2Q'(x)+R'(x),$$

Means,

$$P'(x_0) = R'(x_0).$$

But $R(x)$ is a polynomial of the first degree, its arbitrary is the coefficient of $x$. This means that straight line $y = R(x)$ has an angular coefficient that coincides with arbitrary $P(x)$ at point $x_0$, therefore, it is a tangent.
