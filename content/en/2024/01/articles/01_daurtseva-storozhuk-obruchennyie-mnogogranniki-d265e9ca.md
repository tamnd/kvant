Wedded polyhedra

N. DAURTSEVA, K. STOROZHUK

Part one: practice

He who loves practice will achieve knowledge in art. Albrecht Durer

The book of the Polish mathematician Hugo Steinhaus “Mathematical Kaleidoscope” describes an interesting model of the dodecahedron [1]. The model is a development of two halves of a dodecahedron; they can be cut out of thick cardboard. To demonstrate, you need to put these two halves together and tighten them with an elastic stretchable “hoop” - an elastic band. If you toss this structure, the hoop, shortening, will pull the model into a three-dimensional body. The hoop cannot become any shorter, so it remains on the surface. This model is interestingly and clearly written about in the “Models” section of the “Mathematical Etudes” website [2]. The line along which the hoop is adjacent after folding is $является^{геодезической.}$ You can read about geodesic lines on polyhedra on pages «Kvant» in the article [3] and in the “Etudes” section of the “Mathematical Etudes” website [4]. Other regular polyhedra also have closed geodesics (green lines on figure 1), this suggests that for them it is possible to come up with models similar to the Steinhaus model.
