M2763. The intended number $k \ge 2$ is given. Prove that the intended number with the theme sum of the digits gives all possible remainders when divided by $k$.

We assume that $k \ge 2$. Let us show that both numbers with an even and numbers with an odd sum of digits have the required property. By $S(n)$ we denote the sum of the digits of the number $n$. Now we can assume that $k$ is not divisible by 10 (otherwise it will realize all remainders modulo $k/10$ in both parities of the sums of digits, and then add the last digit to them in all ways).
If the assumption is that the number in $k$ is nine, then in 11$k$ there is no longer one. Let us denote by $l$ that of the numbers $k$ and 11$k$ whose penultimate digit is not 9. Let $c$ be the last digit of $l$, and $t$ the number of digits in $l$.
If $S(l)$ is odd, then any remainder $a$ is realized as $a$ and $\bar{a}$, these numbers have sums of digits of different parities.
If $S(l)$ is even, then for a given remainder $a$ we choose a $(t+1)$-digit number $M$, starting with the digit 10-$c$ and giving a remainder $a$ when divided by $k$ (there is such a thing!). Then the sum $M + 10^t l$ will have exactly one carry, so the numbers $M$ and $M + 10^t l$ have different parities of sums of digits and both give a remainder $a$ when divided by $k$.
Thus, our statement is proved and the problem of solution is proved.

Prove that the lines PF, QE and AO intersect at one point.

The spelling should be $\angle FPB = \angle FMB$, which means $\angle FPE = \angle FMC$. On the other hand, $\angle FEP = \angle FCM$ (BCEF written), hence the similarity of $EPF \sim CMF$ and therefore $\frac{PE}{MC} = \frac{EF}{CF}$.

Likewise, $\frac{FQ}{BN} = \frac{EF}{BE}$. This means, since $MC = BN$, we have $\frac{PE}{BE} = \frac{QF}{CF}$ (i.e. points P and Q divide the heights BE and CF in the same ratio). Taking into account $\frac{BE}{CF} = \frac{AE}{AF}$ we get $\frac{PE}{FQ} = \frac{AE}{AF}$, therefore triangles AEP and AFQ are similar. We obtain $\angle FAQ = \angle EAP$, in other words, the lines AP and AQ are symmetrical with respect to the bicsectriciary angle BAC.

Now the solution can be completed using the isogonal theorem (see, for example, the article by A. Kulikov and D. Prokopenko “The isogonal theorem” in «Kvant» No. 4, 5 for 2018) for the four-sided EPFQ and the center A: pairs of straight lines AE and AF, AP and AQ are isogonal in the angle BAC (i.e. symmetric with respect to bicsectrics of angle BAC). This means that the lines AH and AG, where H = PE $\cap$ QF and G = PF $\cap$ QE, are also isogonal. But H is the orthocenter of triangle ABC, and, as is known (and easy to calculate in angles), lines AH and AO are isogonal with respect to angle BAC. It turns out that the lines AO and AG coincide, which completes the solution.

From the solution it is clear that the problem can be generalized: the bases of the heights E and F can be replaced by any points E and F on the sides AC and AB such that points B, C, E, F lie on the same circle, and point O by a point isogonally conjugate to the point PE $\cap$ QF.
