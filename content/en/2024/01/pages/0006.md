Due to the weak ordering of stars in the Galaxy, the trajectories of starships will have the form of broken lines, characteristic of the movement of Brownian particles. In accordance with the law of random walk, starships will fly further and further from the Sun, and the square of their distance from the Sun should be proportional to the time of exploration of the Galaxy by humanity. The authors of this article decided to test a similar Brownian model of human settlement in the Galaxy using a map of the distribution of stars in our Galaxy, obtained based on observations of the European Space Agency's optical space telescope Gaia (Global Astrometric Interferometer for Astrophysics). The main task of the telescope is to compile a detailed map of the distribution of stars in our Galaxy. Currently, Gaia data has compiled the most detailed 3D map of the Milky Way, containing information on the exact positions and movements of 1.8 billion stars. The Gaia star catalog provides the following information about each star: i id is the number of the star in the catalog; i D – right ascension, the angle in the plane of the celestial equator between the circle of star declination and the direction to the vernal equinox (this is one of the coordinates of the second equatorial system, which is used to describe the position of objects on the celestial sphere); i G – declination, the angle between the direction to the star and the plane of the celestial equator (this is another coordinate of the second equatorial system); i parallax – change in the apparent position of a star relative to a distant background depending on the position of the observer (measured in arcseconds, and the smaller the parallax, the farther away the star).
