1) Let us write down the first law of thermodynamics for the substance under the piston:
$$\delta Q = dU + \delta A = dU + pdV = dU + d(pV) - Vdp.$$
Next, we will use the expression for the internal energy of an ideal gas and the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation:
$$U = vC_V T, pV = vRT.$$

Since the substance in the vessel remains entirely in the gaseous phase, then
$$d(pV) = vRdT.$$
Then we get
$$\delta Q = vC_V dT + vRdT - Vdp = vC_p dT - Vdp,$$
where $C_p = C_V + R = 4R$ is the molar heat capacity of an ideal polyatomic gas at constant pressure.
For the heat capacity of an ideal gas, expressed through the parameters $(p,T)$, we have
$$C = \frac{\delta Q}{dT} = vC_p - V \frac{dp}{dT} = vC_p - \frac{vRT}{p} \frac{dp}{dT} = v\left(C_p - R \frac{\varepsilon_p}{\varepsilon_T}\right).$$
Then the molar heat capacity of saturated steam is
$$C_{\text{нас}} = C_p - \alpha R = R(4 - \alpha) = -93,9 \text{ Дж/(моль·К).}$$
