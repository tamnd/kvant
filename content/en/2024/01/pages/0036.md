$\left( \lambda \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}; 0 \right)$, where $\lambda = \cos \beta$. The point $R$ lies on the segment $B'Z$, so the vector $\overrightarrow{PR}$ is equal to $t\overrightarrow{PB'} + (1-t)\overrightarrow{PZ}$ for some $t$, and the point $R$ has coordinates $\left( t\lambda \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}; \frac{1-t}{2} \right)$.

Angle $RON$ is equal to $30^\circ$, so its tangent $\frac{RN}{ON}$ is equal to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. At the same time $ON = OP + PN = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6} + t\lambda \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Hence $\frac{1-t}{2\left( t\lambda \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6} \right)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, i.e. $\lambda = \frac{2-3t}{3t}$. Next, $H = 2TR = 2PN \mathrm{tg}\beta = 2\lambda \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda^2}} - 1 = t\sqrt{3(1-\lambda^2)}$. Substituting the expression $\lambda$ through $t$, we get $H^2 = \frac{12t-4}{3}$. But at the same time, $L = 12NR = 6(1-t)$ means $H^2 = \frac{8-2L}{3}$.

In the initial position, the minimum height is $H = 0$, and $L = 4$. When $L$ is shortened to 3, the height of the finished octahedron is equal to the distance between the edges of $\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}$.

For the model of tehahedron, cube, dodecahedron, depicted on figure 1, the dependence $H^2(L)$ has the form of a square trinomial $H^2 = aL^2 + bL + c$. The formula derivation scheme is also the same, but the calculations are more cumbersome.

Let's put together in a table the formulas for a model with an edge of length 1. In the case of a cube, we used the size $d$ of a triangular area (the general formula is too long).

| Model | $H^2(L)$ | $L_{\max}$ | $L_{\min}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tegahedron | $\frac{1}{8}L^2 - \frac{3}{2}L + 3$ | $6 - 2\sqrt{3} \approx 2,5$ | 2 |
| Octahedron | $\frac{-2}{3}L + \frac{8}{3}$ | 4 | 3 |
| Cube | $\frac{1}{18}L^2 - \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}L + 8$ | $6(\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}) \approx 6,2$ | $3\sqrt{2} \approx 4,2$ |
| Dodecahedron | $\frac{1}{5\sqrt{5}}L^2 + \frac{2}{5}L + \left(4 + \frac{8}{\sqrt{5}}\right)$ | $5 + 3\sqrt{5} = 11,7$ | $\frac{5 + 5\sqrt{2}}{2} = 8,1$ |

figure 7 shows graphs of the dependence $H(L)$, provided that the length of the hoop $L_{\min}$ is equal to 1. The yellow curve corresponds to the tegahedron, the blue curve to the octahedron, the green curve to the cube, and the red curve to the dodecahedron. It is interesting that the $H(L)$ dependence curve for a tegahedron lies on a hyperbola, for an octahedron - on a parabola, and for a cube and dodecahedron - on an ellipse!
