Analysis. For the estimation calculation we choose
the simplest linear law of density change (see example 3). Let us calculate the mass of a ball with radius $r$ inside the Earth, the center of which coincides with the center of the Earth, $r < R$:
$$m(r) = \int \rho(r) dV = \int (\rho_0 - \alpha r) \cdot 4\pi r^2 dr = 4\pi r^3 \left( \frac{\rho_0}{3} - \frac{\alpha}{4} r \right) + C,$$
$$m(0) = 0 = C,$$
$$g(r) = G \frac{m(r)}{r^2} = \frac{4\pi G}{3} \left( \rho_0 r - \frac{3\alpha r^2}{4} \right).$$
Since $g_R = G \frac{M}{R^2} = \frac{4\pi GR \rho_{cp}}{3}$, we get
$$g(r) = g_R \left( \frac{\rho_0}{\rho_{cp}} \frac{r}{R} - \frac{3}{4} \frac{\rho_0 - \rho}{\rho_{cp}} \left( \frac{r}{R} \right)^2 \right).$$
This formula is a marine model of the direction of the gravitational field inside the Earth. By exploring this function using the Mathcad 15 application package, you can obtain the graphs presented on figure 7. The green line corresponds to the first theoretical model, the red curve to the second theoretical model, and the blue squares to experimental data on the acceleration of gravity inside the Earth. Note that the physical characteristics of the second theoretical model of the direction of the gravitational field inside the Earth are closer to the experimental results.

Example 6. Plot a graph of the Earth's gravitational field potential as a function of the distance to the center of the planet. The earth can be considered a homogeneous sphere.

Analysis. A body of mass $m$ is acted upon by a force $F(r) = -GmMr^3/R^3$ at $r < R$ (see example 4). This force is directed towards the center of the Earth. We use the correspondence $F = -\frac{dU}{dr}$, where $U$ is the potential energy of the body in the Earth’s gravitational field. Hence $U(r) = -\int Fdr + C$, or $U(r) = \int GmMrdr/R^3 + C$. The constant $C$ is found from the boundary condition $U(R) = -GmM/R$. After substitution we get $C = -3GmM/(2R)$, therefore,
$$U(r) = G \frac{mM}{R} \left( \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{r}{R} \right)^2 - \frac{3}{2} \right).$$
Since the potential of the gravitational field is numerically equal to the potential energy of a body of unit mass, we write the potential inside the Earth in this form:
$$\varphi(r) = G \frac{M}{R} \left( \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{r}{R} \right)^2 - \frac{3}{2} \right).$$
It is known that at $r \ge R$ the potential outside the Earth is equal to $\varphi(r) = -G \frac{M}{r}$. The dependences $\varphi(r)$ inside and outside the Earth are shown in figure 8. At the surface of the Earth, the potential of the gravitational field is $\varphi(R) = -G \frac{M}{R}$, and at the center of the Earth it is $\varphi(0) = -3G \frac{M}{2R}$.
