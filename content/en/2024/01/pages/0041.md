Example 4. Plot a graph of the acceleration of gravity $g(r)$ (or the direction of the gravitational field) in the Earth’s gravitational field as a function of the distance to the center of the planet. Consider the density of the Earth to be constant.

Analysis. It has already been said that a homogeneous ball outside itself creates such a gravitational field as if all its mass were concentrated in the center. Thus, outside the Earth, the acceleration changes according to the law $g(r) = G \frac{M}{r^2}$. Let's place a unit test mass at point $A$ at a distance $r < R$ from the center (Fig. 5). As noted in the first example,

Fig. 5. Interaction between a unit test mass and a ball of radius $r$

the force of interaction between this mass and the surrounding spherical layer is zero. Consequently, the test mass interacts only with the ball, the mass of which is equal to $m(r) = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho = M r^3 / R^3$, where $\rho$ is the density, $M$ is the mass, $R$ is the radius of the Earth, and the acceleration at point $A$ is

$$g(r) = G \frac{m(r)}{r^2} = G \frac{M r}{R^3} = g_R \frac{r}{R},$$

where $r \leq R$ and $g_R = G \frac{M}{R^2} \approx 9,81 \text{ м/c}^2$ are the acceleration of gravity on the Earth’s surface. Thus, inside the Earth, acceleration changes in direct proportion to the distance from the center of the planet. And outside the Earth

$$g(r) = G \frac{m(R)}{r^2} = g_R \frac{R^2}{r^2},$$ where $r \geq R$.

Graphs of these interactions are presented in figure 6.

Fig. 6. Graphs of the acceleration of gravity inside (red line) and outside the Earth (blue line)

The formula $g(r) = g_R \frac{r}{R}$ for determining the acceleration of gravity inside the Earth under conditions when the density of the planet is considered constant can be called the *first theoretical model* of a directed field inside the Earth. Based on experimental data (example 3), we come to the conclusion that the theoretical model, which was considered in example 4, does not correspond. The density of the Earth is not a constant value, it increases depending on the depth, which will be taken into account in the next theoretical model.
