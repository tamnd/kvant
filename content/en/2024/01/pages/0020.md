The bisects of the angles NBC and MCB intersect the corresponding segments DE and DF at points K and L. Prove that the lines RK and QL intersect at $\omega$.

Uses M2615 as a lemma (see «Kvant» No. 8 of 2020):

In triangle ABC, the inscribed circle touches sides CA and AB at points $B_1$ and $C_1$ respectively. An arbitrary point D is selected on side AB. Point $L$ is the center of the incircle of triangle BCD. Let the bisector of angle ACD intersect the line $B_1C_1$ at point M. Then $\angle CML = 90^\circ$.

Let $I$ and $J$ be the centers of circles $\Omega$ and $\omega$. It follows from the lemma that $\angle JKB = 90^\circ$ (Fig. 2). Further, since $JR \perp AB$, the four points B, R, J, K lie on the same circle (with diameter BJ). From here

$$\angle JRK = \angle JBK = \angle JBN + \angle NBK =$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \angle ABN + \frac{1}{2} \angle NBC = \frac{1}{2} \angle ABC.$$

Not then

$$\angle BRK = 90^\circ - \angle JBK =$$

$$= 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2} \angle ABC = \angle BFD,$$

It turns out that $RK \parallel FD$. It follows that the preparation with the center $A$, which transfers $\Omega$ to $\omega$, transfers the line FD to the straight line RK (since the preparation transfers F to R, and the line transfers to a line parallel to it). This means that the second intersection point of RK and $\omega$ is the image of point D with the above preparation. Repeating all the previous reasoning, we understand that straight line QL also passes through the same point. This completes the decision.
