F2776. On the flat surface of a plano-convex lens made of glass with a refractive index of $n = 1,5$ and located in the air, we project a parallel beam of rays along the normal. Determine the shape of the convex surface of the lens if the flat one has the shape of a circle with a radius of $R = 0,5$ m, and all the rays refracted by the lens passed through one point $F$, located at a distance of $f = 1$ m from the nearest point $O$ of the lens. As an answer, indicate molcin $H$ lenses

(maximum distance from a point on a convex surface to a flat one).

In order for all incident rays to intersect at one point $F$ after passing through the lens, they must have the same optical path lengths. Let us introduce the coordinate system $Oxy$ shown in figure and write

equality of the optical path length of an arbitrary ray pre-polished on the convex surface of the lens at a point with coordinates $(x; y)$, and the optical path length of the ray passing along the symmetry axis of the lens:

$$n \cdot (H - y) + 1 \cdot \sqrt{x^2 + (y + f)^2} = n \cdot H + 1 \cdot f,$$

whence after simplification we obtain the quadratic equation

$$(n^2 - 1)y + 2f(n - 1)y - x^2 = 0.$$

Its positive (meaningful) solution has the form

$$y = \frac{f}{n + 1}\left(\sqrt{1 + \frac{(n + 1)x^2}{(n - 1)f^2}} - 1\right).$$

The desired value $H$ is the value $y$ at point $x = R$, therefore

$$H = \frac{f}{n + 1}\left(\sqrt{1 + \frac{(n + 1)R^2}{(n - 1)f^2}} - 1\right) = 0,2 \text{ м.}$$

Why is it common to use spherical lenses, even though our solution implies that for an ideal lens the surfaces should be non-spherical? Why do we deliberately not use the terms “focus”, “focal length” and “optical power” in the text of the problem?
