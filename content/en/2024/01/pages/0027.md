F2773. A weightless spring has a large number of turns, but initially only $N \gg 1$ turns can be deformed, and the rest are fastened together. The load on this spring performs free oscillations with a period of $T_0$ and an amplitude of $A_0$. Starting from a certain moment, each time the load passes through the equilibrium position with the free dal, one of the fastened coils of the spring passes (the length of the spring in the undeformed state does not change). Determine the amplitude $A$ of oscillations of the load after increasing the number of free turns by $\Delta N \gg 1$. Estimate (probisimally) the time $t$ spent from the moment of release of the first of the initially fastened turns to the moment of completion of oscillations with amplitude $A$.

All changes in the number of free turns and, accordingly, the stiffness of the spring will take place in the equilibrium position, when the spring is not deformed, and the length of the spring remains the same, therefore the coordinate of the equilibrium position and the energy of the system are conserved. Let $k$ be the rigidity of one turn, then the equality of energy in the extreme positions during the initial oscillations and after the release of $\Delta N$ turns has the form
$$\frac{k}{N} \frac{A_0^2}{2} = \frac{k}{N + \Delta N} \frac{A^2}{2},
$$
from where we find the desired amplitude:
$$A = A_0 \sqrt{1 + \frac{\Delta N}{N}}.$$
Let $m$ be the mass of the load, and the periods of oscillation before the release of the turns and after the release of $n$ turns are equal
$$T_0 = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{mN}{k}} \text{ и } T_n = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m(N+n)}{k}}.$$
From here we get the correspondence
$$T_n = T_0 \sqrt{1 + \frac{n}{N}}.$$
By the desired moment of time $t$, the load will make half a oscillation with each number of turns from $(N+1)$ to $(N+\Delta N)$ inclusive:
$$t = \frac{1}{2} \left( T_1 + T_2 + \dots + T_{\Delta N} \right) = \frac{T_0}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{\Delta N} \sqrt{1 + \frac{n}{N}}.$$
Based on the conditions $N \gg 1$ and $\Delta N \gg 1$ this expression can be passed:
$$t \approx \frac{T_0}{2} \int_0^{\Delta N} \sqrt{1 + \frac{n}{N}} dn = \frac{T_0}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{n}{N} \right)^{3/2} \int_0^{\Delta N} =$$
$$= \frac{T_0}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta N}{N} \right)^{3/2} - 1.$$
Pay attention to the difference between the limits of the sum and the integral: it is necessary to correctly account for the areas of the extreme columns. It would definitely be a little higher with symmetrical correctness, i.e. when choosing limits from 0.5 to $\Delta N + 0,5$, but in the conditions of the problem this is not important.
A. Vlasov
