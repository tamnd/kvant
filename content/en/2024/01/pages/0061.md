Let us show that the original system has a solution $(x_0; y_0; z_0)$ such that $x_0 y_0 z_0 = -\sqrt{7}$. (In other words, we found above that if a system has a solution, then for these solutions either $xyz = \sqrt{7}$ or $xyz = -\sqrt{7}$. It may turn out that the system has no solution at all, or that for any solution of the system the product $xyz$ is equal to $\sqrt{7}$.)
Let's take, for example, $y_0 = -\sqrt{6} \sqrt{7}$ and leave only the first two equations of the system (the third equation is their consequence). She takes on the appearance
$$\begin{cases} x^3 + \sqrt{7} = \frac{7}{z^3} + \sqrt{7}, \\ -\sqrt{7} - z^3 = \frac{7}{x^3} - \frac{7}{z^3} \Leftrightarrow \begin{cases} x^3 = \frac{7}{z^3}, \\ 2z^6 + \sqrt{7}z^3 - 7 = 0. \end{cases}$$
This system has a solution $z_0 = \sqrt[6]{\frac{7}{4}}, x_0 = \sqrt[6]{28}$.
So, for a triple of numbers $\left( \sqrt[6]{28}; -\sqrt[6]{7}; \sqrt[6]{7} \right)$, the expression $xyz$ reaches its minimum value $\sqrt{7}$.
7. a) $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}, 6) \frac{\sqrt{3}}{5 + \sqrt{3}}$.
a) Suppose that the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is convex and $E$ is the intersection point of its diagonals. Then $BD = EB + ED = \sqrt{AB^2 - \frac{AC^2}{4}} + \sqrt{AD^2 - \frac{AC^2}{4}} = 2 + 1 = 3$ means $DA + DB = 3 + \sqrt{2} > 2 + \sqrt{5} = SA + SB$. However, $SA > DA$ and $SB > DB$ (SDA and SDB are right triangles with a right angle at the vertex $D$), i.e. $SA + SB > DA + DB$ – contradiction. Consequently, the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is non-convex (Fig. 9), and since the triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ are equilateral with a common base $AC$ and $AD < AB$, then the point $D$ lies inside the triangle $ABC$.
Next we have $BD = EB - ED = 2 - 1 = 1$, whence
$$SA + SB = \sqrt{SD^2 + AD^2} + \sqrt{SD^2 + DB^2} = \sqrt{SD^2 + 2} + \sqrt{SD^2 + 1} = 2 + \sqrt{5},$$
therefore $SD = \sqrt{3}, SB = 2, SA = SC = \sqrt{5}$. Then $S_{ABCD} = S_{ABD} + S_{CBD} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AC \cdot BD = 1$, and as a result we find the volume of parameters $V_{SACBD} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot S_{ABCD} \cdot SD = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
6) There is a unique such ball, and due to the symmetry of parametials with respect to the plane $SBD$, the center of the ball lies in this plane. Since the ball touches the faces of the parametials, its center $O$ lies inside the triangle $SBD$, and it touches the segments $SD$ and $BD$. It follows that the point $O$ lies on the bissertrix $DK$ of the triangle $SBD$.
Let $L$ be the base of the perpendicular from the point $K$ to the segment $BD$. Then from the similarity of triangles $BKL$ and $BSD$ we have
$$KL = SD \cdot \frac{BK}{BS} = SD \cdot \frac{BD}{BD + SD} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} + 1}.$$
This means that the distance from the point $O$ to the line $BD$ (i.e. the radius of the ball) is $KL \cdot \frac{OD}{KD} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{1 + \sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{OD}{KD}$.
On the other hand, we can find the radius of the ball as the distance from the point $O$ to the plane $SBC$. To do this, we first find the length of the height $DM$ in the triangular paramid $SBCD$. The volume of this paramid is $V_{SBCD} = \frac{1}{2} V_{SABCD} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}$. FROM $SB = BC = \sqrt{5}$ and $SA = 2$ we get $S_{SBC} = 2$. Then $V_{SBCD} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot DM \cdot S_{SBC}$, whence $DM = \frac{3V_{SBCD}}{S_{SBC}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$. This means that the distance from the point to the plane $SBC$ is equal to
$$DM \cdot \frac{OK}{KD} = DM \left( 1 - \frac{OD}{KD} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \left( 1 - \frac{OD}{KD} \right).$$
Now let’s equate the two obtained expressions for the radius of the ball:
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{1 + \sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{OD}{KD} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \left( 1 - \frac{OD}{KD} \right) \Rightarrow \frac{OD}{KD} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{5 + \sqrt{3}}.$$
In total, the radius of the ball is equal to
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} + 1} \cdot \frac{OD}{KD} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{5 + \sqrt{3}}.$$
