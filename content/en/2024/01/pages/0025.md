3) It also follows from the previous solution that with an increase in volume without condensation, the temperature in the vessel should decrease. Then the steam in the vessel will remain saturated and at the same time condense, since otherwise the vapor pressure in the vessel becomes the pressure of saturated vapor at the same temperature, which is impossible. Let us differentiate the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation taking into account the change in the gaseous amount of the substance in the vessel:

$$pV = vRT, \varepsilon_p + \varepsilon_V = \varepsilon_v + \varepsilon_T,$$
$$\varepsilon_T (\alpha - 1) + \varepsilon_V = \varepsilon_v,$$

since in the process under consideration $\varepsilon_p = \alpha\varepsilon_T$. From the first law of thermodynamics:

$$\delta Q_{\text{нас}} + Ldm = 0,$$

where $Q_{\text{нас}}$ is the amount of heat received by non-condensing saturated steam, and $dm = Mdv$ is the change in the mass of water vapor. Since the state of non-condensing water vapor in coordinates $(p,T)$ is described by the phase equilibrium curve, its heat capacity is equal to the heat capacity $C_{\text{нас}}$ obtained by solving the first point, therefore for the amount of heat $\delta Q_{\text{нас}}$ we have

$$\delta Q_{\text{нас}} = C_{\text{нас}} dT.$$

Returning to the first law of thermodynamics for the system, we obtain

$$vC_{\text{нас}} dT + LMdv = 0, \varepsilon_v = -\frac{C_{\text{нас}} T\varepsilon_T}{ML},$$

where
$$\varepsilon_T (\alpha - 1) + \varepsilon_V = -\frac{C_{\text{нас}} T\varepsilon_T}{ML}.$$

We finally find

$$\Delta T = -T_0 \frac{\beta}{\alpha - 1 + \frac{(C_p - \alpha R)T_0}{ML}} = -T_0 \frac{\beta}{\alpha - 1 + \frac{(4 - \alpha)R}{ML}} \approx -1,2 \text{ K}.$$
