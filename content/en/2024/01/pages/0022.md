For the moments of pressure forces relative to the lower edge, we find that the center of gravity of the liquid layer drops by $\frac{a}{3}$ (Fig. 2.6):

$$2k\Delta x\delta x \geq \frac{\rho a^2\delta x}{2} g \frac{a}{3},$$ or $k\Delta x \geq \frac{\rho ga^3}{12}$.

In a filled aquarium, a water pressure force $F_\pi = \frac{1}{2}\rho ga^3$ acts on the side wall. To solve, it is necessary to determine the point of application of this force.

Since the horizontal water pressure at the same level is the same, it is obvious that this point will be located on the middle vertical. Vertically, the pressure is distributed linearly from zero at the top to $\rho ga$ at the bottom. It is known that the center of gravity of a triangle is located at the point of intersection of its medians. Using the vertical pressure distribution in our case with the height distribution of a mass of strips of the same width into which an equilateral triangle is mentally cut parallel to the base, we can state that the point of application of force is located at a distance of $a/3$ from the bottom of the vessel.

A rigorous mathematical calculation of forces and moments of forces can be carried out using integration. Let us define pressure as a function of depth $y$ from the surface of the liquid: $p(y) = \rho gy$. Total water pressure force:

$$F_\pi = \int_0^a p(y)ady = \frac{\rho ga^3}{2}$$.

Moment of force relative to the upper bolt:

$$M_B = \int_0^a yp(y)ady = \frac{\rho ga^4}{3}$$.

Moment of force relative to the lower clamp:

$$M_H = \int_0^a (a-y)p(y)ady = \frac{\rho ga^4}{6}$$.

Conditions for the absence of leaks can be written as inequalities:

$$3k\Delta x \geq \frac{\rho ga^3}{2}$$ (condition on the direction of the resulting force),

$$k\Delta x \cdot a \geq \frac{\rho ga^4}{3}$$ (no rotation around the top edge),

$$2k\Delta x \cdot a \geq \frac{\rho ga^4}{6}$$ (no rotation around the bottom edge).

Obviously, the inequality $k\Delta x \geq \frac{\rho ga^3}{3}$ is the most stringent, from which we obtain

$$\Delta x_{\min} = \frac{\rho ga^3}{3k}$$.

2) Note that since the springs compress equally, the total force with which they will press on the wall of the aquarium is 2 times the force of water pressure. If the springs are placed in places, the conditions for no leakage will take the following form:

$$3k\Delta x \geq \frac{\rho ga^3}{2}$$ (condition on the direction of the resulting force),

$$2k\Delta x \cdot a \geq \frac{1}{3}\rho ga^3$$ (no rotation around the top edge),

$$k\Delta x \cdot a \geq \frac{1}{6}\rho ga^3$$ (no rotation around the bottom edge).

From here we get

$$\Delta x_{\min} = \frac{\rho ga^3}{6k}$$.

In this case, the total force with which the springs will press on the wall of the aquarium exactly coincides with the force of water pressure.
