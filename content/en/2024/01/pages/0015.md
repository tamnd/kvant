From these numbers, the sum of the selected numbers was calculated and all the resulting sums were calculated into a line in non-increasing order. The first number in the line is equal to S.
Enter the smallest possible value of the second number in the string.

ANSWER: $\frac{49S}{50}$.

Let $x_1 \ge x_2 \ge \dots \ge x_m > 0 > x_{m+1} \ge x_{m+2} \ge \dots \ge x_n - \text{dанные числа. По условию } x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_m = S = -(x_{m+1} + x_{m+2} + \dots + x_n)$.
If $m \ge n/2 = 50$, then $x_m$ does not exceed the arithmetic mean of the numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m$, hence $x_m \leq \frac{S}{m} \leq \frac{S}{50}$. Then we estimate the sum $S'$, the next largest after $S$, as follows:
$$S' \ge x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_{m-1} = S - x_m \ge S - \frac{S}{50} = \frac{49S}{50}.$$
Otherwise $m \leq n/2$, then $n - m \ge n/2$ and, therefore, $x_{m+1}$ is not less than the arithmetic mean of the numbers $x_{m+1}, \dots, x_n$, hence $x_{m+1} \ge -\frac{S}{n-m} \ge -\frac{S}{50}$. Then we evaluate $S'$ like this: $S' \ge x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_m + x_{m+1} = S + x_{m+1} \ge S - \frac{S}{50} = \frac{49S}{50}$.
It remains to give an example in which it is enough to know the data $\frac{S}{50}$, and the remaining 50 are equal to $-\frac{S}{50}$.
A. Golovanov

M2763. The intended number $k \ge 2$ is given. Prove that the intended number with the theme sum of the digits gives all possible remainders when divided by $k$.

We assume that $k \ge 2$. Let us show that both numbers with an even and numbers with an odd sum of digits have the required property. By $S(n)$ we denote the sum of the digits of the number $n$. Now we can assume that $k$ is not divisible by 10 (otherwise it will realize all remainders modulo $k/10$ in both parities of the sums of digits, and then add the last digit to them in all ways).
If the assumption is that the number in $k$ is nine, then in 11$k$ there is no longer one. Let us denote by $l$ that of the numbers $k$ and 11$k$ whose penultimate digit is not 9. Let $c$ be the last digit of $l$, and $t$ the number of digits in $l$.
If $S(l)$ is odd, then any remainder $a$ is realized as $a$ and $\bar{a}$, these numbers have sums of digits of different parities.
If $S(l)$ is even, then for a given remainder $a$ we choose a $(t+1)$-digit number $M$, starting with the digit 10-$c$ and giving a remainder $a$ when divided by $k$ (there is such a thing!). Then the sum $M + 10^t l$ will have exactly one carry, so the numbers $M$ and $M + 10^t l$ have different parities of sums of digits and both give a remainder $a$ when divided by $k$.
Thus, our statement is proved and the problem of solution is proved.
You can estimate how large the numbers in the given constructions are, and understand that in the conditions of the problem it is enough to have natural numbers with an even sum of digits and not exceeding $200k^2$.
