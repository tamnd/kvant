In the partial case, when $F = 0$, there is no acceleration in the system $K$, that is, $a = 0$, therefore

$$a' = -a_0.$$

Thus, a moving observable will discover that all free bodies in his system have an acceleration equal in absolute value to the acceleration of the reporting system itself, but directed in the opposite direction. We can say that in this system there is a field of forces that acts on bodies with forces $F_n$, called inertial forces.

So, from the point of view of the moving observable equation of motion of a body, in the general case it should be written as follows (Fig. 2):

$$m a' = F + F_n.$$ (2)

Comparing (1) and (2), it is easy to see that

$$F_n = -m a_0.$$

That is, in the case of translational motion of a non-pepper reporting system, the force of inertia depends on the acceleration of this system, which is easy to change experimentally. Let us also note once again that the force of inertia (like the force of gravity) is proportional to the mass of the body.

Potential energy of a body in this field. You can do this for the field of inertial forces, using analogies with the gravity field of the Earth. The similarity of these fields lies, first of all, in the fact that all free bodies in the accelerated reporting system move with the same accelerations, independent of their masses.

It is known that near the Earth's surface (where the free pressure acceleration is constant) a body with mass $m$ has potential energy

$$\Pi = mgx,$$

where $x$ is the height of the body above the Earth’s surface (Fig. 3). Note, by the way, that the quantity $\varphi = gx$ is called the gravity field potential. When moving a body from a height $x_1$ to a height $x_2 < x_1$ the field does work

$$A = mg (x_1 - x_2) = -(\Pi_2 - \Pi_1),$$

that is, work equal to the potential energy of the body. This work goes to increase the kinetic energy of the body: by how much the potential energy has decreased, the kinetic energy has increased by the same amount, so the sum of the kinetic and potential energy remains constant. Therefore the total energy

$$E = \frac{mv^2}{2} + mgx$$

remains unchanged and is maintained during movement.
