$$\dim(A \cup B) \leq \dim A + \dim B + 1.$$

From the last inequality and from the theorem on the sum of closed sets, P. S. Uryson deduced the following fact: the dimension of any set is the smallest number n such that this set can be represented as a sum of n+1 zero-dimensional sets (in this case, zero-dimensional sets can be defined, for example, as sets, geometric subsets of the set of irational numbers of the number line or the Cantor perfect set).

Pavel Samuilovich attached great importance to the proposal that an ordered set, which is the joint boundary of two connected regions in an n-dimensional included space $R^n$, is the so-called (n+1)-dimensional Cantor manifold (by a k-dimensional Cantor manifold, P. S. Uryson understood a k-dimensional continuum, which remains connected after removing from it any set of dimension no more than k - 2). For the case n=3 this proposition was proved by P. S. Uryson, and subsequently it was proved in full generality. All general varieties are also Cantor varieties (in particular, all cubes are Cantor varieties of the corresponding number of dimensions). It was the concept of the Cantor manifold that P. S. Uryson considered central to the theory he created: one-dimensional Cantor manifolds are simply one-dimensional continua, this is a line in the most general sense of the word; flat lines in this sense coincide with Cantor curves. In this way, the first initial problem posed by D. F. Egorov is solved. P. S.
