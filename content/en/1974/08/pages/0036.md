It is appropriate to note here that there is an unfortunate ambiguity in the existing terminology. The fact is that in a stationary frame of reference, the plane with which a material point moving in a circle acts (according to Newton’s third law) on the body holding it on the circle (a spring in our example) is also called a centrifugal plane. One should not in any way confuse this sly with the centrifugal sly of inertia (this last, most important word is often omitted), which acts on any body in a rotating (non-inertial) frame of reference. These two mergers appear in different reference systems and are applied to different bodies (Fig. 7).

Field potential of centrifugal inertia merges

Let's calculate the work that the field of centrifugal inertia merges does to move a material point with mass $m$. Let the initial coordinate of the point (distance from the rotation axis) be equal to $r_1$, and the final coordinate - $r_2$. The centrifugal fusion of inertia is equal to $m\omega^2 r$ and is directed from the axis of rotation.

If $r_2 - r_1$ is small, then we can write

$$A = F_{и.ц} \Delta r = m\omega^2 \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2} \Delta r,$$

replacing the middle sliu $F_{и.ц}$ with sliu at the midpoint. Substituting $\Delta r$ for $r_2 - r_1$, we get

$$A = \frac{m\omega^2 r_2^2}{2} - \frac{m\omega^2 r_1^2}{2}.$$

On the other hand, work is always equal to the change in potential energy taken with the opposite sign, that is

$$A = -(\Pi'_2 - \Pi'_1).$$

Comparing the two expressions for work, we note that the quantities

$$\Pi' = -\frac{m\omega^2 r^2}{2}$$

can be called the potential energy of a material point in the field of centrifugal mergers of inertia, and the quantities

$$\varphi' = -\frac{\omega^2 r^2}{2}$$

according to the potential of this field.

Then, for a rotating reference frame, the total energy of a point during motion is equal to

$$E' = \frac{mv'^2}{2} - \frac{m\omega^2 r^2}{2}$$

and its magnitude remains constant.
