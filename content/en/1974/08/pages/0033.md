If we consider the movement of a body in non-inertial reporting systems, then we cannot forget that in addition to the usual forces of interaction, inertial forces also act on bodies. To write down the equation of motion of a body, you must be able to determine all the forces. Body forces depend on the relative position of the body and on their relative velocities. The inertial forces caused by the acceleration of the movement of the reporting system should obviously also depend on the nature of the movement of the reporting system itself.

Let's consider two cases:

1) a system of reporting relative to a stationary inertial system of translational motion with some constant acceleration;
2) The reporting system rotates around a fixed axis at a constant angular velocity.

In the first case, only one force will arise, which is called the force of inertia. In the second case, we need to talk about two forces. The first depends only on the coordinates, that is, on the distances to the axis of rotation; These forces are called centering by inertial force. The second depends on the speed of movement of the body relative to the rotating reporting system, but does not depend on the coordinates; it is called the Corniss force in the part of the French mechanic Corpolis (1792-1843). We will not talk about Corniss force in this article.

In order to obtain specific expressions for inertial forces during translational motion and for centering inertial forces, it is enough to compare the movements of the body in the inertial and non-inertial reporting systems.

Forward movement of the reporting system

Let the reporting system $K'$ move uniformly accelerated with acceleration $a_0$ relative to the inertial system $K$. Let us consider a body that is acted upon by a force $F$ from another body, giving it an acceleration $a$ relative to the system $K$. The acceleration of this body in the system $K'$ is denoted by $a'$. Let us describe the movements of the selected body from the point of view of two observers: one is associated with the system $K'$ (we will call it a moving observer), the other is in the system $K$ (we will call it a stationary observer).

For a stationary observer (in an inertial reporting system), the body participates in two movements (Fig. 1): with acceleration $a'$ relative to system $K'$ and with acceleration $a_0$ relative to system $K$, therefore its total acceleration

$$a = a' + a_0.$$

Let us write down the equation of motion of the body:

$$m a = F,$$

or

$$m (a' + a_0) = F.$$

The last equality can be rewritten in another way:

$$m a' = F - m a_0.$$ (1)
