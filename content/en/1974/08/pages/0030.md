Listen on the bus
Let's start with an ordinary incident. The bus braked sharply at a traffic light, passengers were pulled forward. Someone stepped on the person standing forward and, of course, wriggled around. From the point of view of the rules of conduct, everything is in order. But for a physicist, the end of the story must seem incongruous. The passenger, of course, is not to blame: it’s probably the arrogant driver who is to blame - he should be interested in the science of behavior, but let’s just ask ourselves, what, strictly speaking, is the driver’s fault? How was the driver able to push the passengers? After all, he sits far away and cannot even reach them. We can say that the force (of course, not from the driver, but from the motor or, perhaps, from the brakes) was transmitted through the floor. This answer also cannot be considered satisfactory: after all, the horas remain standing in place - the passenger seems to be pulled, rather, by the head...

If he is not deliberately trying to confuse the matter, then everything is explained very simply. The driver braked the bus; the passengers' legs (due to the high static friction of the soles on the floor) also slowed down, and the torso and head continued to move at the same speed, obeying the first law. Newton's law of inertia. If there were no friction, for example, all the passengers stood on perfect rollers (which you can’t do for the sake of science!), then they would not immediately feel any impact, and one after another they would hit the wall of the driver’s cabin that suddenly stopped in front of them.

Such a description will be given by any reasonable observer who is in an inertial coordinate system, for example, this scene is observed while standing on the sidewalk.

But it may happen that another person wants to analyze the same story, say, the driver of the bus, who was sitting tightly in his cross.
