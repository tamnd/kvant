⟦folio 44⟧

Kvant's problem

Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than October 1, 1974 to the address: 117071, Moscow, V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka Publishing House, magazine «Kvant». After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: “Problem «Kvant», M276, M277” or “... F288”.
Solutions to problems in each of the subjects [mathematics and physics], as well as new problems, should be written in separate envelopes. Please also send assignments from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking your decisions). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics or “... a new problem in mathematics”). After creating a task, we promise to indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. The most difficult edematous asterisks.
In this issue, as in the previous one, “Problem «Kvant» is composed of problems from the last All-Union Olympiad and their generalizations. We will place a complete set of Olympiad problems and a detailed story about the final type in issue No. 10 for this year.

Tasks

M276—M280; F288—F292

M276. Given a square ABCD. Points P and Q lie on sides AB and BC, respectively, with BP = BQ. Let H be the base of the perpendicular dropped from point B onto the segment PC. Prove that angle DHQ is a right angle. (10 grades)

N. V. Vasiliev

M277. Several red and several blue dots are specified. Some of them are connected by segments. Let's call a point “special” if more than half of the points connected to it have a color different from its color. If there is at least one special point, then any special point is selected and repainted in a different color. Prove that after a finite number of steps there will be no singular point left. (10 grades)

A. M. Steinberg

M278. a) Each side of a convex hexagon has length greater than 1. Does it always contain a diagonal of length greater than 2?

6) In a convex hexagon ABCDEF, the diagonal lengths AD, BE and CF are greater than 2. Does it always have a side of length greater than 1? (8 grades)

N. Kh. Adakhanov

M279. On n cards laid out in a circle are written numbers, each of which is equal to +1 or -1. In what smallest number of questions can one determine for sure the product of all n numbers, if in one question one can find out

a) product of numbers on any three cards?

6) product of numbers on any three cards lying in a row? (n is a natural number greater than 3). (9 grades)

Yu. I. Ionin
