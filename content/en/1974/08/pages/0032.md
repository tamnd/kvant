In all these cases, the interaction of bodies will occur without unrepresented contact. The concept of a physical field of forces was introduced to describe this type of interaction. This concept became firmly established in physics and in the 20th century completely eliminated the concept of contact forces. Only at short distances is it sometimes more practical to talk about the contact of bodies, about a push or impact.

In order to preserve Newton's third law, they talk about the inverse effect of the body on the field. Thus, for example, the interaction of two electrical charges distant from each other is related to the interaction of charge and field at the location of the charge.

The incident on the bus can also be described using the concept of a field of forces. In a braking bus, from the driver’s point of view, a field of inertial forces arises that acts on passengers. But it should be emphasized that this field arose due to the fact that the motion reporting system is accelerated, and it can be “destroyed” by the transition to an inertial coordinate system.

Thus, accelerated in a moving bus (we will further say - in the coordinate system) a field of inertial forces arises, remarkable, first of all, in that it creates equal accelerations for all bodies in the system, regardless of their mass. These accelerations are equal in absolute value to the acceleration of the coordinate system and are directed in the opposite direction.

Only one force in nature - the force of gravity - has the same properties. For example, in the Earth's gravity field, all bodies fall freely with the same acceleration relative to the inertial frame of reference. This analogy actually has a deep meaning - it reflects the linear principle of equivalence *), the fundamental meaning of which is revealed in the theory of relativity.

In the meantime, we can formulate an important conclusion: like gravitational forces, inertial forces are proportional to the mass of the body. This is a consequence of the equality of the acceleration of all free bodies in a non-perial coordinate system.

What are the types of inertial forces?

The principle of Galileo states that in any moving inertial frame of reference the same laws of mechanics apply as in a frame at rest**). Otherwise, they say that no experiments inside the system can be observed to know whether it moves uniformly and rectilinearly or ends. In particular, the material point, on which no bodies act in such a system, will either end or move without acceleration. To this we must add that the point should not be in either an electric or magnetic field and should not be acted upon from the outside by any massive body. It is reasonable to apply the Galilean principle, so to speak, in the opposite direction to establish whether the reporting system is inertial or not. A measure of this, very much, should be the degree of accuracy of the implementation of Newton's laws in this system.

*) See, for example, the article by D. Borodin “Gravitational mass”, «Kvant», 1973, No. 2.

Let us note, by the way, that useful Earth can also be “destroyed” by moving to another coordinate system, for example, to a spaceship. The phenomenon of irrevocability (when the force of gravity is “balanced” by the force of inertia) reflects the flow of gravitational forces with inertial forces.

**) The Einstein principle goes even further, asserting that all laws of physics (not just mechanical ones) remain the same. In this article we will talk only about classical mechanical phenomena.
