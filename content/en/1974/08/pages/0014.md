During these days, excitement grew in the ocean, attracting Pavel Samulovich and forcing him to swim with special enthusiasm. By August 17, this excitement turned into a real storm, and the bathing, undertaken that day at the beginning of the sixth hour in the afternoon, turned out to be fatal for him: grabbed by a huge wave, Pavel Samulovich was thrown against a rock. He was pulled out of the water only after forty minutes, and attempts to bring him back to life remained unsuccessful.

P.S. Urson was buried there, on the plaque in Ba.

The entire scientific activity of P. S. Urson lasted for about four years, and during these few years he left an indelible mark on science. The theory of dimension, metrization theorems, the famous “Urson's Lemma” on the existence of many continuous functions in normal spaces, the inclusion theorem in Hilbert space - these and other results of P. S. Urson represent a huge contribution to science, not only not forgotten in the half century since his death, but which has proven its ability in numerous further studies.

The remaining independent scientific works of P. S. Urson, sometimes available only in sketches and even only in oral statements, were extensively prepared for publication and published in full under the activity of Browser, who, in particular, read the proofs of all the works of P. S. Urson. Their first publications were reports in German, reports in French, a complete Russian translation amounted to two volumes, published under the title “Transactions on topology and other areas of mathematics” in 1951. Now the main topological theorems proved by P. S. Urson are included in all topology textbooks.

It is difficult, and even useless, to talk about the directions in which the mathematical work of P. S. Urson would have developed if his life had not been cut short so prematurely and so brutally. But there is no doubt that in his person mathematical science has lost a scientist of the highest caliber, with universal mathematical talent, with interests covering the whole of mathematics, with curiosity that extended to the most diverse areas of human knowledge. The general talent of his personality, objectively imprinted in his scientific heritage, was perceived by people in its most varied manifestations: in his lectures, amazing in its insight and imagery of thoughts, in his ability and love to work, in his strong and deep reactions to everything significant that arose around him in the life of human society, in the acuity with which he perceived nature and art (mainly music), and in many other things that make the image of Paul Samulovich is not only alive and independent, but also unique in his creative power and in his human charm for all the people who had to meet him on the path of life.
