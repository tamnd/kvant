All free bodies “fall” with acceleration $a_0$, equal to the acceleration of the motion of the system itself, then by analogy the expression for the potential energy of a body with mass $m$ in the field of inertial forces can be written as follows:

$$\Pi' = ma_0x'$$

where $x'$ is the coordinate of the body in the $K'$ system. It is reasonable to call the quantity $\varphi' = a_0x'$ the potential of the inertia force field.

Then the total energy of the body to the accelerated reporting system is equal to

$$E' = \frac{mv'^2}{2} + ma_0x'$$

where $v'$ is the speed of the body. Moreover, the value of the total energy remains constant all the time.

Centrifugal force of inertia

Let us now consider a reporting system that rotates with a constant angular velocity $\omega$ around a fixed axis. Such a system could be, for example, a rotating disk. We have already said that in the case of developing a reporting system, it is necessary to take into account two forces of inertia - centrifugal and rooted. Let us limit ourselves to considering only the centrifugal force of inertia. This force depends on the coordinate of the body and does not depend on whether it is at rest or moving relative to the grown reporting system, therefore, for simplicity, we will assume that the body is motionless.

Let there be a body on the main rotating disk, which is held on the disk by a spring at a distance $r$ from the rotation axis. Let us describe his behavior from the point of view of the stationary and mobile observable.

A stationary observer (associated with an inertial reporting system) will say that since a body moves in a circle, it has an acceleration called centripetal and equal in magnitude

$$a_\mu = \omega^2 r.$$

This acceleration of the body is imparted by the elastic force of the stretched spring $F_y$, since no other bodies in the horizontal plane act on the body (Fig. 5). According to Newton's second law

$$m a_\mu = F_y.$$

From the point of view of the moving observable (associated with the rotating inertial system of the report), the body will end, therefore, the sum of all forces acting on the body is equal to zero. Therefore, this observable must say that there is a force that balances the elastic force (Fig. 6). This is the centrifugal inertial force $F_{и.ц}$, equal to

$$F_{и.ц} = -F_y = -m a_\mu.$$

The minus sign indicates that this force is directed opposite to the centripetal acceleration, that is, along the radius from the axis of rotation, and is equal in magnitude to $F_{и.ц} = m\omega^2 r$.
