August 17, 1974 marks the fiftieth anniversary of the death of one of the most talented Soviet mathematicians P. S. Urson.

Pavel Samuilovich Urson was born on February 3, 1898 in Odessk. His great abilities manifested themselves in his childhood: scientists came easily to him and his broad curiosity affected both the natural sciences (mainly physics and chemistry), and mathematics, as well as languages ​​and literature. Having together graduated from the Moscow part of P.N. Popova’s gynimania in 1915, in the same year he entered Moscow University at the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, and intended to become a physicist. P. P. Lazarev’s attention was drawn to his outstanding abilities, which contained a proposal for a transformed police-bioprokatic nature that arose when P. S. Urson entered the university. Under the leadership of P.P. Lazarev, Pavel Samuilovich’s first scientific work was done, it was called “On the radiation of the Coolidge tube”, it was experimental in nature and was sent in 1915.

However, the scientific interests of P. S. Urson began to lean more and more towards mathematics. The decisive influence in this regard were the lectures given at Moscow University by D. F. Egorov and N. N. Luzin, whose student P. S. Urson always considered himself to be.
