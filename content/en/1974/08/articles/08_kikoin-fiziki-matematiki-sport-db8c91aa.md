"Goes to the top

on the heels..."

These simple verses were contained in one of the greetings addressed to Academician Igor Evgenievich Tamm on the day of his seventieth birthday. The peaks spoken of in this spirit are not the allegorical peaks of science, but peaks in the literal sense of the word - the peaks of high mountains. Physicists around the world, Nobel Prize winner in physics, Academician I. E. Tamm, are known as the author of many outstanding scientific works on the theory of nuclear forces, the theory of special states of electrons on the surface of a crystal (“Tamm levels”), the theory of radiation of fast electrons in matter, the theory of plasma in a magnetic field and many others. But climbers in our country also knew I. E. Tamm as an enthusiast of alpine sports. I. E. Tamm was one of the first climbers to the peaks of Altai, and one of the peaks in this region bears his name (Tamm Peak).

Climbers also know many other scientists - physicists and mathematicians - who love mountains and climbing. Famous Soviet mathematician, corresponding member

Academy of Sciences of the USSR Boris Nikolaevich Delone not only made many climbs to the peaks himself, but was also the organizer of the first mountaineering camp in our country. This he laid the foundation for the system of training and sports improvement of climbers, which made accomplished climbers the strongest in the world.

Another mathematician, academician Aleksadr Danilovich Aleksadrov, is also known not only as the author of outstanding works on geometry, but also as a master of sports of the USSR in mountaineering, who made a number of difficult ascents on

Caucasus and other regions of the country. An excellent alpine skier, he, being the rector of Leningrad State University, more than once won excellence in slanting from his young students - university students.

Many other physicists and mathematicians both in our country and abroad, including the most famous of them, are passionate about alpine sports. Such luminaries of physics as Niels Bohr and Werner Heisenberg have climbed to the tops of mountains more than once. Enrico Fermi loved to make mountain hikes, on foot and even by bicycle. The famous physicist John Tyndall (the phenomenon of light scattering in turbid media is called the Tyndall effect) was the first to climb one of the most difficult mountaineering peaks - Mattergorin.

But mountaineering is not the only sport that has fascinated physicists and mathematics. At first glance, the increase in sports scientists seems strange. Many people believe that successful scientific work requires that all the scientist’s time be spent on it. Where can you find time to travel through the mountains? But it’s not for nothing that the eastern latter says that finishing belongs to labor, like night to day. Rest is necessary for everyone who works. But to separate means to open up from what one is doing during labor. But it is very difficult for a scientist to open up to science. And the best way to discover it is through sports. Moreover, one that requires great and prolonged physical activity and is associated with strong desires. This is exactly the kind of mountaineering: moving upward, with a heavy backpack, in conditions of constant danger, in the best way makes you forget about what the brain has been busy with all year... It is possible that this is why many physicists and mathematicians prefer mountaineering. But, as we have seen, other sports are not alien to them.

Sports, any kind of sport, is the best recreation for those involved in science. And for those who are still learning it!
