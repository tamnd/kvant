Physicists - members of the USSR Academy of Sciences on stamps

Among the almost 4,500 stamps issued in our country after the Great October Revolution, six stamps are dedicated to physicists - members of the USSR Academy of Sciences. The scientists depicted on them were prominent physicists and major organizations of Soviet science.

The first of these stamps was issued in 1956. It depicts a portrait of academician Alexei Nikolaevich Krylov. Academician Krylov was an outstanding mathematician, mechanic and specialist in mathematical physics. He was also the initiator of the creation of the Physics and Mathematics Institute of the Academy of Sciences and its first director. This institute was subsequently transformed into the P. N. Lebedev Physical Institute and the V. A. Steklov Mathematical Institute. In 1963, on the centenary of the birth of A.N. Krylov, a second stamp with his portrait was issued.

In the same year, a stamp was issued with a portrait of academician Igor Vasilyevich Kruchatov. It was released in connection with the sixtieth anniversary of his birth. Academician I.V. Krutatov created and led a large team of Soviet scientists who successfully solved the problems of using atomic energy. On his initiative, the world's first nuclear power plant was built and the Institute of Atomic Energy was created, which bears the name of this remarkable scientist.

In 1961, on the seventieth anniversary of the birth of Academician Sergei Ivanovich Vavilov, a stamp with his portrait was issued. S.I. Vavilov was distinguished as a specialist in physical optics. Along with research activities, he carried out enormous scientific and organizational work. For many years, S.I. Vavilov was the director of the P.N. Lebedev Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences and the editor-in-chief of the Great Soviet Encyclopedia. From 1945 to 1951 he was Chairman of the USSR Academy of Sciences. He was also one of the founders of the All-Union Society “Znanie” and its first representative.

In 1974, two more postage stamps were published with portraits of physicists - members of the USSR Academy of Sciences. One of them depicts the vice-president of the USSR Academy of Sciences, academician Mikhal Dmitrievich Millonshikov, and the other - academician Lev Andreevich Artsimovich. Both of them were members of the editorial collection of the magazine «Kvant».

A student of Academician A. N. Kolmogorov, Academician M. D. Millonshikov was one of I. V. Kruchatov’s closest collaborators. He made major contributions to a number of areas of modern hydrodynamics and, in particular, to the study of the hydrodynamic properties of hot plasmas. The activities of M.D. Millonshikov as vice-president of the USSR Academy of Sciences left a deep imprint on the development of Soviet science.

Academician L.A. Artsimovich was also one of the closest employees of the USSR Academy of Sciences. His main areas of research interest were physics and physical electronics. After the death of I.V. Kruchatov, L.A. Artsimovich headed the reporting research in the field of hot plasma physics and controlled thermodermic reactions. For many years, he supervised the work of the largest group of scientists of the Academy of Sciences, assigned to the Department of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, and later to the Department of General Physics and Astronomy, being the academician-secretary of these departments.

V. A. Rudov
