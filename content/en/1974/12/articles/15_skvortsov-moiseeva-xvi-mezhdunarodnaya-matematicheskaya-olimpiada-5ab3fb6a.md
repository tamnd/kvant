International School Olympiads

XVI International Mathematical Olympiad
V. A. Skovortsov, Z. I. Moiseeva

Since 1959, international mathematics olympiads for schoolchildren (IMO) have been regularly held. These Olympiads quickly gained great international prestige.
If teams from 7 countries (Bulgaria, Hungary, East Germany, Poland, Romania, USSR and Czechoslovakia) took part in the 1st Olympiad, then teams from 18 countries (Austria, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, East Germany, Democratic Republic of Vietnam, Cuba, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, Finland, France, etc.) took part in the 16th Olympiad. Czechoslovakia, Sweden and Yugoslavia).
Starting from the XI IMO, our magazine regularly publishes detailed information about the progress of the Olympiads and analysis of the problems proposed at them.

The number of countries participating in the International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren is growing every year. In the XVI Olympiad, held in July 1974 in the city of Erfurt (GDR), 18 countries already participated. This year's Olympiad participants were joined by fairly strong teams from the Democratic Republic of Vietnam and the United States of America, which made the struggle for place recognition even more intense and interesting.
As in past years, the team of each country consisted of 8 students, with the exception of this year the teams of Cuba (of 7 people) and the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (of 5 people).

On each of the two days, the Olympiad participants were asked to solve 3 problems in 4 hours. These problems were selected by an international jury from among the problems previously proposed by the participating countries.

Tasks of the first day of improvement

1. There are three cards, each of which has one of the integers written on it. These numbers p, q, r satisfy the condition 0 < p < q < r.

Three players A, B and C play a game, one round of which consists of the following: cards are moved and divided among the players one at a time; then each player receives a number of balls equal to the number written on the card he received; then the cards are collected, and the balls remain with the players. The game continues for N rounds, N ≥ 2. At the end of the game, player A has 20 balls,

for $B - 10$ balls, for $C - 9$ balls. It is known that in the last round player $B$ received $r$ balls. It is required to determine which player received $q$ marbles in the first round (USA; 5 points were given for a complete solution to this problem).

2. In triangle $ABC$, the angles at vertices $A, B$ and $C$ are equal to $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$, respectively. Prove that the inequality

$$\sin \alpha \cdot \sin \beta \leq \sin^2 \frac{\gamma}{2}$$

is a necessary and sufficiently conditional condition for finding a point $D$ on the segment $AB$ such that the value $CD$ is the geometric mean of the values ​​$AD$ and $BD$ (Finland; 6 points).

3. Prove that for any natural number $n$ the number

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} C_{2n+1}^{2k+1} 2^{3k}$$

not divisible by 5 (Romania; 8 points).

Quests for the second day of perfection

4. Consider partitions of the chessboard $8 \times 8$ into $p$ mutually disjoint rectangles satisfying the following conditions:

1) each rectangle consists of a certain number of cells and contains as many white cells as black ones;
2) if $a_i$ is the number of white cells in the $i$-th rectangle, then $a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_p$.

Find the maximum value $p$ for which such a partition is possible, and determine for this value $p$ all sequences $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_p$ for which such a partition can be realized (Bulgaria; 6 points).

5. Find set of sum values

$$S = \frac{a}{a+b+d} + \frac{b}{a+b+c} + \frac{c}{b+c+d} + \frac{d}{a+c+d},$$

where $a, b, c, d$ are arbitrary positive real numbers (Netherlands; 7 points).

6. Let $P$ be a polynomial with non-constant integer coefficients, and let $n(P)$ be the number of all distinct integers $k$ for which $[P(k)]^2 = 1$. Prove that

$$n(P) - \deg(P) \leq 2,$$

where $\deg(P)$ denotes the degree of the polynomial $P$ (Sweden; 8 points).
