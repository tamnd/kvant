VII International Physics Olympiad
G. S. Tarasok

In July 1974, the VII International Physics Olympiad among schoolchildren took place in Warsaw. Teams from Bulgaria, Hungary, East Germany, Poland, Romania, USSR, Czechoslovakia, and Germany took part in the Olympiad. The German team participated in the Olympics for the first time.

The USSR team included winners of the All-Union Physics Olympiad, who showed good results at the training camp. This:

Kurchanov Anatoly - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 21 in Zhitomir, Ukrainian SSR;

Masich Sergey - 10th grade student of physics and mathematics boarding school No. 165 in Novosibirsk;

Nanniy Oleg - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 37 in Chisinau, Moldova;

Alexey Rudnev - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 762, Moscow;

Evgeniy Falkin is a 10th grade student at physics and mathematics boarding school No. 165 in Novosibirsk.

On July 9, a theoretical round was held, in which participants had to solve 3 problems. The maximum number of points in type I was received by: Jerzy Tarasok, Jaroslav Deminet (Poland) and Geza Meshchena (Hungary). This task was completed by Jerzy Tarasok, who received the maximum possible number of points. Among the participants of the Soviet team, Anatoly Kurchanov had the best result in the theoretical type, and Evgeniy Falkin had the best result in the experimental type.

On July 13, the closing ceremony of the Olympiad took place, at which the results were summed up. Of the 40 participants in the Olympiad, 18 were among the winners. Our team was the only one at the Olympiad, all of whose members were loaded with diplomas and valuable gifts.

The acquaintance with the historical and cultural monuments of Poland began with a visit to the Sundial Museum in Jedrzeow, then there was an interesting rafting trip along the animal river Dunajec, and a visit to the salt mines in Wieliczek. In Krakow, students from the USSR visited the V.I. Lenin Museum, went on an excursion to the Wawel Royal Castle, and the Institute of Nuclear Research. Below are the tasks of the theoretical and experimental rounds.

Problem 1 (mechanics, atomic physics)

A stationary hydrogen atom, which is in the ground energy state, collides with the same hydrogen atom, moving at a speed of $v$. Use the Bohr model and knowing that the energy of induction of a hydrogen atom is $E_H$, and the mass of the atom is $m_H$, determine the limiting speed $v_0$, below which the collided atoms are elastic.

After reaching speed $v_0$, collisions between atoms can become inelastic, causing radiation. Determine the percentage of the difference between the radiation frequencies observed in the direction coinciding with the initial speed of the incident atom, and in the opposite direction, to the arithmetic mean of these frequencies.

$$E_H = 13,6 \text{ эв} = 2,18 \cdot 10^{-18} \text{ дж};$$

$$m_H = 1,67 \cdot 10^{-27} \text{ кц.}$$

Problem 2 (optics)

A narrow beam of light falls on a plane-parallel plate (see figure) at point $A$ (with coordinate $x = 0$) perpendicular to the plate. The refractive index of the plate changes according to the formula $$n_x = \frac{n_A}{1 - \frac{x}{R}}$$

where $n_A$ and $R$ are constants.

The beam leaves the plate at point $B$ at an angle $\alpha$ to the initial direction. 1) Determine the refractive index $n_B$ at point $B$ at which the beam leaves the plate. 2) Determine the coordinate $x_B$ of point $B$. 3) Determine the thickness of the plate $d$.

$$n_A = 1,2; \quad R = 13 \text{ см}; \alpha = 30^\circ.$$
