⟦folio 44⟧

Tasks

M296—M300; F308—F312

M296. The table $n \times n$ contains $n^2$ numbers whose sum is non-negative. Prove that it is possible to rearrange the columns of the table so that the sum of $n$ numbers along the diagonal, looking from the lower left corner to the upper right corner, is non-negative.

A. Tupanov

M297. There are 12 points given on the plane, which are the vertices of four squares $A_1B_1A_2C_1$, $A_2C_2A_3B_2$, $A_3B_3A_4C_3$ and $A_4C_4A_1B_4$ (the vertices of each square are listed clockwise). Prove that $B_1B_2B_3B_4$ and $C_1C_2C_3C_4$ are congruent parallelograms, one of which is obtained from the other by rotating by $90^\circ$ (these parallelograms can be degenerate: the four vertices of each of them in this case lie on the same straight line).

J. P. Kuptsov

M298. Let us write down all irreducible fractions $\frac{p}{q}$, where $0 < p < q \leq m$, in ascending order ($m$ is the given natural number). For example, with $m = 5$ the sequence $\frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{5}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{3}{5}, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{3}{4}, \frac{4}{5}$ will be obtained.

Prove that for any two adjacent fractions $\frac{p}{q} < \frac{r}{s}$ in such a sequence the equality $qr - ps = 1$ holds.

M299. For what $n$ can a regular $n$-gon be placed on a sheet of paper in a line so that all the vertices lie on the lines? (Lines are parallel straight lines located at equal distances from each other.)

H. B. Vasiliev

⟦folio 45⟧

M300*. The alphabet consists of three letters: a, b, c. Let us use a word to create a sequence of any length consisting of these letters. When forming words, certain letter combinations (of two or more letters) are considered prohibited. It is known that in the list of prohibited letter combinations all words have different lengths.

Prove that there are words of any length that do not contain forbidden letter combinations.

A. M. Stepin

F308. The photometer is a sheet of paper with a thick heel. This sheet is placed between two precise light sources. The luminous intensity of one source is 20 sv, the luminous intensity of another source needs to be determined. To do this, first select the distances $l_1$ and $l_2$ sources from the sheet so that the spot does not stand out against the background of the paper when viewed from the side of the first source. It turns out that $l_2 = 2l_1$. Then the distances $l_1'$ and $l_2'$ are selected so that the spot does not stand out against the background of the paper when viewed from the side of the second source. It turns out that $l_1' = l_2'$. Determine the luminous intensity of the second source.

The sheet is placed perpendicular to the line connecting the sources.

Clue. Each section of paper can be characterized by its transmittance and light reflectance.

F309. The snake poured straight into a stationary narrow pipe lying on a plane. The second half of the snake wriggles arbitrarily along the plane. Considering the snake to be a thin uniform cord of length $l$, find in which area the center of gravity of the entire snake can be.

V. D. Gorbunova

F310. The ends of the arms are fixed. How, without freeing the ends of the arm, can you deform it so that the arm after such treatment

sought to a) shrink? b) leave?

F311. A homogeneous magnetic field changes in absolute value with a constant speed $k(B=kt)$. There is a piece of copper whose density is $d$ and resistivity $ρ$. The mass of the piece is $m$. From this piece you can draw a homogeneous wire, from which, in turn, you can make a closed loop. What is the maximum possible current in this circuit?

V. D. Kerzhantsev

F312. The radnus hoop $R$, rolling at a speed $v$ on a horizontal surface, impinges absolutely inelastically on the height step $h(h < R)$. What speed will the hoop have when it “runs up” the step? At what minimum speed can the hoop “climb” the step?

There is no telling.

M. Gerke, D. Sakharov, A. Cesis (students of Vinnichka p. ii. No. 17)
