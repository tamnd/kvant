BALL TOUCHING THE EDGES OF A POLYHEDON

In recent years, university entrance exams often offer problems that involve a ball touching all the edges of a polyhedron. This article is related to this topic.

Let the ball touch all the edges of a certain polyhedron. Then the following statements are true (prove them yourself):

(1) each face of the polyhedron intersects the surface of the ball along a circle tangent to the edges of the polyhedron, that is, along a circle inscribed in the face; thus, the faces of the polyhedron will also be polygons in which a circle can be inscribed;

(2) the base of the perpendicular, lowered from the center of the ball to any face of the polyhedron, is the center of the circle inscribed in this face;

(3) the perpendiculars, restored to the planes of the faces at the centers of the inscribed circles, intersect at one point equal to the one formed from all the edges of the polyhedron - in the center of the ball;

(4) the segment of the perpendicular dropped from the center of the ball to the edge of the polyhedron is equal to the radius of the ball.

Now let's consider some types of polytopes for which the indicated ball exists.

Task 4 (NSU). Find the volume of a part of a regular metrahedron with an edge of length a, enclosed between two spheres: one sphere touching all the faces of the metrahedron, the other touching all the edges.

Let us denote the required volume by $v$, then $v = v_1 - 4v_2 - v_3$, where $v_1$ is the volume of a ball of radius $R$ touching all edges of the metrahedron, $v_2$ is the volume of a spherical segment cut off by one face of the metrahedron from this ball, $v_3$ is the volume of a ball of radius $r$ removed in metrahedron.

Note that if a ball is inscribed in a cube, then it touches all the edges of the regular metrahedron formed by the diagonals of the faces of the cube (Fig. 6). If the diagonal of a cube's face is $a$, then its edge is $\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$, and the radius of a ball thrown into the cube is equal to half of this edge, $R = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{a\sqrt{2}}{4}$.

A ball touching the edges of a regular truncated pyramid

If a ball touches all the edges of a regular truncated pyramid, then, as follows from statement (1) - (4) (see p. 55), the center of this ball lies at the height of the pyramid connecting the centers $O, O_1$ of the bases, and the ball touches the edges of the bases of the pyramid at their midpoints $M, M_1, \dots$ (Fig. 8), and $OO_1M_1M$ is a trapezoid, $OO_1 \perp OM$, and the perpendicular, lowered from the center $K$ of the ball onto the side face, hits the point $P$ - the middle of the apothem $MM_1$. This construction and Theorem 2 (we also apply to a truncated pyramid) underlie the solution of problems involving a ball touching all edges of a truncated pyramid.

Exercises

1. (NSU). The ball touches all the edges of the cube. Find the surface area of ​​the cube lying inside the cube if the edge of the cube is 1 cm.
2. (NSU). The ball touches all the edges of the cube. Find the volume of a sphere and cube object if the edge of the cube has length $a$.
3. In a regular quadrangular pyramid, a side edge forms an angle equal to $45^\circ$ with the plane of the base. Where is the center of the ball touching all the edges?
4. (NSU). A ball of radius $R$ touches each edge of a regular tetrahedron. Find the volume of their object.
5. (MSU, mechanism, 1966). A ball of radius $r$ touches all edges of a triangular pyramid. The center of the ball lies inside the pyramid at its height at a distance $r\sqrt{3}$ from the vertex. Prove that the pyramid is correct. Find the height of the pyramid.
6. (MIPT, 1963). The side of the base of a regular triangular pyramid is equal to $a$, the side edge of the pyramid is equal to $b$. Find the radius of the sphere touching all the edges of the pyramid.
7. The lateral edge of a regular $n$-gonal pyramid forms an angle $a$ with the base plane. At what distance from the plane of the base is the center of the ball touching all the edges of the pyramid, if the side of its base is $a$?
