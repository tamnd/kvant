This article uses some simple logical operations: negation, conjecture, disjunction, implication. Many of you know about them from school - some from elective mathematics courses, and some from the most ordinary ones. Yes, and in «Kvant» I talk about them - see, for example, «Kvant» No. 4 for 1971.

For those who do not know the definitions of logical operations and do not have other numbers «Kvant», we will provide here only the minimum information that is needed to read and understand the article by A. Priyatel.

Recall that a statement is a sentence about which it makes sense to say that it is true or false.

New statements can be formed from various statements using logical operations. The question of the truth of the resulting statements depends, of course, on what (true or false) the original statements were, and, of course, on the logical operation itself. Namely:

1) The negation of a true statement is false, and the negation of a false statement is true. If we denote the original statement by the letter A, and the negation operation by the sign “—” (A reads “not A”), then our definition can be given in the following table:

| A | Ā |
| :--- | :--- |
| And | l |
| l | And |

(i - abbreviated “truth”, l - “false”).

2) The conjunction of two true statements A and B (A ∧ B) is read as “A and B”) is true, and if at least one of them is false, then the conjunction is false:

| A | B | A ∧ B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| And | And | And |
| And | l | l |
| l | And | l |
| l | l | l |

3) A disjunction of two statements (the notation A ∨ B reads “A or B”) is true if at least one of them is true, and false if both are false:

| A | B | A ∨ B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| And | And | And |
| And | l | l |
| l | And | l |
| l | l | l |

4) Finally, the implication of propositions A and B (A ⇒ B) reads “A bleibt B,” “From A follows B,” or “If A then B”) is false if the consequent of A is true and the conclusion of B is false; in all other cases the implication is true:
