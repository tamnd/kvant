VII International Physics Olympiad
G. S. Tarasok

In July 1974, the VII International Physics Olympiad among schoolchildren took place in Warsaw. Teams from Bulgaria, Hungary, East Germany, Poland, Romania, USSR, Czechoslovakia, and Germany took part in the Olympiad. The German team participated in the Olympics for the first time.

The USSR team included winners of the All-Union Physics Olympiad, who showed good results at the training camp. This:

Kurchanov Anatoly - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 21 in Zhitomir, Ukrainian SSR;

Masich Sergey - 10th grade student of physics and mathematics boarding school No. 165 in Novosibirsk;

Nanniy Oleg - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 37 in Chisinau, Moldova;

Alexey Rudnev - 10th grade student of secondary school No. 762, Moscow;

Evgeniy Falkin is a 10th grade student at physics and mathematics boarding school No. 165 in Novosibirsk.

On July 9, a theoretical round was held, in which participants had to solve 3 problems. The maximum number of points in type I was received by: Jerzy Tarasok, Jaroslav Deminet (Poland) and Geza Meshchena (Hungary). On July 11, on experimental.type, all participants were offered one task. The best experimental

This task was completed by Jerzy Tarasok, who received the maximum possible number of points. Among the participants of the Soviet team, Anatoly Kurchanov had the best result in the theoretical type, and Evgeniy Falkin had the best result in the experimental type.

On July 13, the closing ceremony of the Olympiad took place, at which the results were summed up. Of the 40 participants in the Olympiad, 18 were among the winners. Our team was the only one at the Olympiad, all of whose members were loaded with diplomas and valuable gifts.

1st prize
received: Jerzy Tarasok, Jaroslav Deminet (Poland), Geza Meshchena (Hungary).

I wasn’t at the Olympics.

Honorable mentions
received: Sergey Masits, Olee Nanniy, Evgeny Falkin (USSR), Maria Tseika, Mihai Gantsiu-Petziu (Romania), Ulf Brustel, Hans-Geore Martin (GDR).

Various excursions were organized for the Olympiad participants. The acquaintance with the historical and cultural monuments of Poland began with a visit to the Sundial Museum in Jedrzeow, then there was an interesting rafting trip along the animal river Dunajec, and a visit to the salt mines in Wieliczek. In Krakow, students from the USSR visited the V.I. Lenin Museum, went on an excursion to the Wawel Royal Castle, and the Institute of Nuclear Research. On July 18, all Olympiad participants visited the former concentration camp in Auschwitz. Members of the USSR team laid flowers on the mass graves
