There is another important case when moving charges do not emit. Let us consider a direct current flowing along some curved circuit. It is well known from experiment and theory that such a current creates a constant magnetic field, but does not radiate. But every current is the movement of charged particles. Each of these particles, as we know, creates a radiation field. Why don't these charges taken together radiate? It turns out that the radiation fields created by different particles cancel each other out with such precision that the result is a purely static field. We can assume that in this case the charged particles move with the same constant average speed, and there is no radiation.

Using Maxwell's equation, one can establish a general fact: if the location of currents and charges in space does not depend on time, then there is no radiation.

Temporarily ignoring the quantum nature of atomic particles, we will describe the simplest atom in the language of classical physics - a hydrogen atom, consisting of one proton and one electron.

The electron rotates around the proton in a circle of radius $r_B^*$ (Fig. 2), so that the force causing centripetal acceleration, $mv^2/r_B$, is equal to the Coulomb force of attraction $e^2/r_B^2$ (m is the mass of the electron):

$$\frac{mv^2}{r_B} = \frac{e^2}{r_B^2}.$$

From here we can express the speed of rotation of the electron

$$v^2 = \frac{e^2}{mr_B}.$$ (2)

The greater the speed of an electron, the smaller the distance between it and the proton.

The total energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom is the sum of its kinetic energy $E_{kин} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{e^2}{2r_B}$ and pseudospatial energy $E_{пот} = -e^2/r_B$:

$$E = \frac{e^2}{2r_B} - \frac{e^2}{r_B} = -\frac{e^2}{2r_B}.$$ (3)

Classical physics cannot explain the fact that all hydrogen atoms are the same size. For now we will simply accept, considering this an experimental factor, that all hydrogen atoms have $r_B \approx 10^{-10} m$.

The centripetal acceleration of an electron when moving in a circle is equal to

$$a = \frac{v^2}{r_B} = \frac{e^2}{mr_B^2}.$$
