⟦folio 69⟧

Fibonacci number sequence *)

$$\Pi_{12} = (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, \dots)$$

is also given by the recurrence relation $a_{k+1} = a_k + a_{k-1}$ for $k > 2$ and the initial conditions $a_1 = a_2 = 1$.

We looked at four ways to define a numerical sequence: analytical, tabular, graphical and recurrent. Naturally, there is one more method - pure (verbal description). For example, the words “sequence of squares” (or, more precisely, “sequence of squares of natural numbers”) is a verbal description of the sequence

$$\Pi_6 = (1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \dots, k^2, \dots).$$

The analytical method is the most important; this method can most concisely and clearly indicate the law of sequence construction. However, there are also sequences for which an analytical task is generally impossible (for example, a sequence of prime numbers) or very cumbersome.

So, we now know all the main ways to set a sequence. As already mentioned, number sequences play an important role not only in mathematics, but also in science and technology. Particularly often the application is in the form of sequences: arithmetic and geometric progressions. You will study them in 8th grade.

*) Leonardo Fibonacci is an Italian mathematician of the 13th century. Fibonacci numbers were first encountered when solving the ancient problem of rabbit reproduction. Subsequently, Fibonacci numbers were encountered in a number of practical and theoretical problems. This is described in more detail in the brochure by N. N. Vorobyov “Fibonacci Numbers” (Moscow, “Nauka”, 1969), designed for high school students. (Approx. translation)
