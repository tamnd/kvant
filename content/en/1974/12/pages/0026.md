⟦folio 24⟧

$$s = \frac{4\pi F^2 \lg^2 \frac{\alpha}{2}}{4}, \quad \alpha = \gamma \text{угловой размер Солнца (см. рис.ок)}.$$
Taking the angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$, we get
$$s = \frac{\pi F^2}{4 \cdot (110)^2}.$$
(since $2tg 15^\circ \approx \frac{1}{110}$). The energy flux incident on a unit surface of the image of the Sun in a lens is $\frac{S}{s}$ times greater than without a lens. From the Stefan-Boltzmann law it follows that the steady-state temperature $T$ at the focus will be $\left(\frac{S}{s}\right)^{1/4}$ times greater than the temperature $T_0$ to which the Sun can heat objects without a lens. Then the relationship between the plane of the lens (or mirror) and the temperature at its focus can be written as follows:
$$S = \frac{\pi F^2}{4 \cdot (110)^2} \frac{T^4}{T_0^4}.$$

From the literature *) it is known that in order to ignite, for example, paper, a temperature of about $250^\circ$ C is required, that is, about $520^\circ$ K, and for dry wood it is necessary to increase this temperature to $500-700^\circ$ C, that is, for example, up to $800-1000^\circ$ K.

An experiment conducted by the French naturalist Buffon showed that it was indeed possible to light a piece of dry, resin-impregnated wood from a distance of 158 feet (about 47 meters). Buffon's apparatus was a structure of 168 mirrors, each 48 square inches (approximately $310 \text{ cm}^2$) in area, mounted on a common grama. Obviously, it is easier to light wood impregnated with resin than wood that has not been impregnated. Therefore, we will assume that wood impregnated with resin ignites at the lower of the given temperatures, that is, at $800^\circ$ K. Then, according to formula (1), putting $T_0 \approx 60^\circ$ C $\approx 330^\circ$ K, we obtain $S = 4,9 \text{ m}^2$, and the total area of ​​the mirror in Buffon’s experiment was equal to $5,2 \text{ m}^2$. Thus, Buffon's experiments do not contradict our theory.

Let's try to calculate the area of ​​the mirror that Archimedes might have come across. We will assume that the ships were made of the same materials as Buffon's tablet. The distance from the mirror to the ships, according to legend, was equal to the distance of a crossbow shot, that is, about 400 meters *). In this case $S \approx 350 \text{ m}^2$. But Archimedes didn’t just have to set the ships on fire, he had to set them on fire quickly, since the ships could move and, unlike the biofon tablet, they would not wait to be set on fire. Calculating the time it takes wood to heat up to the ignition temperature and weight is cumbersome and complicated, since it is necessary to take into account, for example, the thermal conductivity and thermal conductivity of wood. Therefore, use the experimental data given in Lawson’s book “The Atomic Bomb and Fires**). According to these data, in order to light double boards in 20 seconds, illumination is required 70 times greater than the illumination of objects by direct sunlight in the summer. To create such illumination, the mirror area must be increased to $700 \text{ m}^2$. The dimensions of the mirror themselves are not too large - the ancient Greeks built larger structures. However, the sun's rays reflected from various parts of the mirror must converge at one point, which requires careful manufacturing of such a granular mirror. But that's not all.

*) See, for example, the book by R. Bradbury “Fahrenheit 451”.
**) D. Lawson “Atomic bomb and fires.”
