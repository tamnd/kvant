World Championship
During the IFIP competition, the 1st World Championship among chess programs took place in Stockholm.

The Soviet Union was represented by the Kansa program, created at the Institute of Management Problems by G. M. Adelson-Velsky, V. L. Arlazarov, the author of the article, and others.

The parties took place for five consecutive evenings in the concert hall of the Birger Jarl Hotel. Six games were being played at the same time, and the attention of a large audience (200-300 people) moved from one fight to another. In order for the audience not to get bored, the director of the tournament, international master D. Levy, commented on the games all the time, and this commentary was immediately translated into Swedish.

On the stage there were two small computers, six terminals and eleven telephones that connected the hall with different cities in Europe. One such telephone connected the hall with the Institute of Management Problems in Moscow, where Kansa worked.

The tournament was full of surprises and oddities.

In the first game against the author’s program “Franz”, after move 30, “Kanssa” suddenly began to think for a long time, and if she had not put checkmate on move 34, she would probably have run out of time. This is explained by the fact that on the 30th move of “Kanssa” it was midnight in Moscow, and “Kanssa”, without understanding the clock, decided that she had more than 24 hours to think about it. Perhaps it was precisely this slowness that allowed her to find a mating combination.

Surprises continued in the second.type in the game against Tech-2. According to the tour rules, in case of technical problems, you could take a half-hour break to correct them. The author of Tech, tired of the protracted game of the 1st round (it lasted until 3 am), simply overslept the beginning of the round and was exactly half an hour late. This was read to him as a break due to technical problems, and then during the game he had no right to leave his watch on.

The inaccuracy of the author of "Tech" affected the game - he made the wrong move that the machine told him, and noticed it too late. The move was reversed, but this was only for “Kansu” and led her to a mistake. In the end, I had to enter this position into the machine again and replay the game.

Technical errors of our opponents have become an unkind tradition. In the third.type, after Kansa’s victory was declared in the game “Kanssa” with “Chaos”, the authors of “Chaos” announced that they had made a mistake when entering “Kanssa’s” penultimate move into their machine. They asked permission to replay the game from this move, but they didn’t expect that we would accept this offer, especially after Levi said that if Kansa’s machine breaks down during replay, then there will be no process.
