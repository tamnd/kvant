Theorem 1. If at some moment $t_0$ the speed of movement $v_0$ is less than $V$, then during the entire further movement there will be $v < V$. If $v_0 > V$, then it will always be $v > V$.

Let us prove that over time the difference $v - V$ becomes any pre-selected arbitrarily small value $h$. To do this, consider the moment in time

$$t_1 = t_0 + \frac{(v_0 - V)m}{S(V + h) - F}.$$

During the time from $t_0$ to $t_1$, the speed decreased from the value $v_0$ to a value greater than $V$, that is, it decreased by less than the value $h$.

$$v_0 - V = \frac{S(V + h) - F}{m}(t_1 - t_0).$$

It follows that in some time intervals $t^*$ the increasing acceleration was less than $\frac{1}{m}[S(V + h) - F]$. (If this were not so, then over the period of time under consideration the speed would decrease by no less than amounts (***).)

So, let at moment $t^*$ we have

$$|a^*| < \frac{S(V + h) - F}{m}.$$
