⟦folio 2⟧

A material point, on which no forces act, moves rectilinearly and uniformly in the inertial system of the report.

However, it is not possible to “isolate” from the influence of forces. In our earthly conditions, a material point is acted upon by the force of the Earth’s straightening (gravity), the friction force, the force of air resistance, and others. In addition, many devices created by man (a car, an electric train, a rocket) are always subject to forces. In a word, there is no way to cope with this.

Yes, but why can a car move evenly? The reader, of course, will say that there is nothing surprising in this: all the forces acting on the car are balanced (that is, their equilibrium value is zero), and this is equivalent to the fact that no forces act on the car at all - so it moves uniformly.

And yet there is something amazing in the movement of a car. The driver is driving at a speed of 60 km/h. But then he looked at his watch (“But I’m afraid!”) and, after a few seconds of acceleration, drove off at a speed of 80 km/h. And again the movement is uniform, that is, again all forces are balanced. Who balances the forces? Chauffeur? And how does he manage to balance them at a speed of 60 km/h, and at a speed of 80 km/h, and at other speeds? Isn't this amazing? But if you ask the driver about this, he will be even more surprised than you: it doesn’t even occur to him that something needs to be “balanced”!

Let's try to understand this issue using mathematics. Note that gravity need not be considered at all: on a horizontal section, this force is balanced by the reaction force from the road. In addition, we will assume that there is no wind, we will neglect the force of Lunya’s erection, and so on. Then only the driving force $F$ will remain, associated with the action of movement and with the friction of the driving wheels on the road (we will consider this force constant in the considered section of movement), and with the resistance force (Fig. 1).
