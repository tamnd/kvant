And perhaps it is silent because the limiting value of the speed (that is, $V$) is stable: no matter in which direction the speed deviates from $V$, it will certainly approach $V$.

The stability of the value $V$ can be clearly demonstrated by constructing a phase diagram (or, as they also say, a “phase portrait”) of equation (*) (see Fig. 4). Let's mark the point $V$ on the number axis. If at some moments the actual speed $v$ turns out to be less than $V$ (point $v_1$ on the diagram), then from (*) it is clear that the acceleration $a$ will be positive, that is, the speed will increase. This is indicated on the diagram by an arrow pointing to the right. If at some moments the speed is greater than neck $V$ (point $v_2$ on the diagram), then the acceleration $a$ is negative, that is, the speed begins to kill (the arrow is directed to the left).

The phase diagram shows that the velocity has "nowhere to go" except to approach the value $V$. This is reminiscent of the behavior of a ball in a hole (Fig. 5): wherever it is, it is “pulled” to the lowest point - to a stable equilibrium position. By analogy, the point $V$ on the phase diagram under consideration is also called a stable equilibrium position.
