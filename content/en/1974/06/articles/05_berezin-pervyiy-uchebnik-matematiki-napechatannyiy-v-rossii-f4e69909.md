The first mathematics textbook published in Russia

The first Russian mathematics textbook was the book “Arithmetic” by Leonty Filippovich Magnitsky, published in 1703 in Moscow. During the receipt of the book, this served as the only textbook. Future astronomers, surveyors, learned captains and admirals of the Russian Navy studied it at the Moscow Navigation School and numerical or argumentative schools. The numerical averages of L.F. Magnitsky himself in the Navigation School were the explorer of Kamchatka and the Kuril Islands S.I. Chelyuskin, surveyors F.F. Luzhin and I.M. Evrenov, who compiled the first geographical maps of Kamchatka, vice admiral - hydrograph and potatoes, explorer of the Caspian Sea, the Chukchi Peninsula and the Sea of Okhotsk F.I. Soimonov, admiral N.F. Golovin, Admiral General M.M. Golitsin, Captain-Commander A.I. Chirkov and multigeography.

Mikhail Vasilievich Lomonsov is self-taught in “Arithmetic” by L. F. Magnitsky. He, like L.F. Magnitsky before him, studies at the Slavic-Greek-Latin Academy, but they did not master mathematics there.

M.V. Lomonsov calls “Arithmetic” by L.F. Magnitsky “the gates of learning” and, according to the testimony of some biographers of M.V. Lomonsov, he even learned it by heart.

“Arithmetic” by L. F. Magnitsky contains approximately 600 pages of text. Of these, more than 40 pages are separated. And this was natural - since the book is not only a textbook, but also a semi-character introduction to mathematics. She explains why mathematics is needed and what role it plays in life.

A talented book written by a mathematician-teacher, self-taught, peasant son Leonty Magnitsky secured his name.
