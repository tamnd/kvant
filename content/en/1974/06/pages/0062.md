To the article “On the strength of chess pieces”
1. $P_{Kp}(n) = \frac{4(n-1)(2n-1)}{n^2},$
$P_{\Phi}(n) = \frac{2(n-1)(5n-1)}{3n},$
$P_{\lambda}(n) = 2(n-1),$ $P_{C}(n) = \frac{2(n-1)(2n-1)}{3n},$
$P_{K}(n) = \frac{8(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2},$
$P_{\Pi}(n) = \frac{(n-1)(3n-4)}{n(n-2)}.$

It is easy to verify that for $n=8$ these formulas yield the mobility of the figures found in the article. When $n$ is even, the white-squared and dark-squared bishops have the same mobility; if $n$ is even, the bishop of the color which has more fields is more mobile (put on these mobility elements yourself). Dividing $P_x(n)$ by $P_\pi(n)$ gives the figure strength $x$.

2. In order to find the mobility of the figure $x$ at $n \rightarrow \infty$, it is necessary to calculate the corresponding limit. For pieces with limited movement on the board (king, knight, pawn) we get:

$$P_{Kp}(\infty) = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} P_{Kp}(n) =$$
$$= \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{4(n-1)(2n-1)}{n^2} =$$
$$= 8 \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{2n}\right) = 8,$$

similar to $P_{K}(\infty) = 8$, $P_{\Pi}(\infty) = 3$. Of course, these values ​​are easy to predict if you look closely at figure 1, ​​5, 6 articles: with increasing $n$ the proportion of fields from whom

If the number of moves of a piece differs from the set value, it tends to zero.

The movements of the queen, rook and bishop are linear in nature, and, very/no, their mobility at $n \rightarrow \infty$ increases without limit. However, for these figures one can obtain asymptotic estimates:

$$P_{\Phi}(n) \approx \frac{10}{3} n,$$
$$P_{\lambda}(n) \approx 2n,$$
$$P_{C}(n) \approx \frac{4}{3} n.$$

From the last formulas it follows that the forces of distant figures at large values ​​of $n$ are approximately as follows:

$$F_{\Phi}(n): F_{\lambda}(n): F_{C}(n) \approx 5: 3: 2.$$

3. The equation $F_{\lambda}(n) = F_{C}(n) + F_{K}(n)$ has two roots: $n=8$ and $n=3$.

The equation $F_{Kp}(n) = F_{C}(n)$ has one root $n=6$.

4. $\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(3n-1)}{6}.$

5. $\frac{1}{12}n(n-2)^2(2n^3-12n^2+23n-10)$

for four $n$ and $\frac{1}{12}(n-1)(n-3)(2n^4-12n^3+25n^2-14n+1)$ for odd $n$. Note that it has still not been possible to solve these problems for four queens.

6. From problem 2 it follows that two rooks are stronger.

8. The mobility of an end on an infinite board is equal to
1 if $a=b=0$;
4 if $a=b \neq 0$ or one of the numbers $a, b$ is zero and the other is not;
8 in all other cases.
