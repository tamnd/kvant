Made from kinetic and potential energy. Let us write the expression for the potential energy of the deformation:

$$E_{\pi} = \frac{kx^2}{2} = \frac{kx_0^2}{2} \cos^2 \omega t = \frac{kx_0^2}{4} (1 + \cos 2\omega t).$$

From this it is clear that the potential energy also changes over time according to the harmonic law, but the frequency of its oscillations is 2 times greater than the frequency of oscillations of the pendulum mixture.

Kinetic energy can be represented as the difference between the total energy, equal to $E = \frac{kx_0^2}{2}$, and the potential energy:

$$E_k = E - E_{\pi} = \frac{kx_0^2}{4} (1 - \cos 2\omega t).$$

Consequently, kinetic energy changes out of phase compared to potential energy. Graphically this is shown in Fig. 4, where $T'$ is the period of energy oscillations, and $T$ is the period of mixing oscillations.

We see that when considering oscillations, we can talk not only about oscillations of mixing, speed, acceleration, but also about oscillations of kinetic and potential energy. Now let's look at several problems and make sure that to solve them it is enough to find out the nature of the restoring force, write down the corresponding expression for it, and also use the law of conservation of energy.

Task 2. We place a weight of mass $m$ on the scales hanging on a spring from a height $h$ and remain on the scale (Fig. 5, a). The coefficient of simplifying the spring is $k$. The masses of the spring and cup are small compared to the mass of the load. Determine the amplitude and period of free oscillations of the cup with the load.

The system will indeed oscillate, since it is a spring pendulum located vertically. But, in addition to the elastic forces of the spring, the force of gravity also acts on the cup with the load. What is her role? This force turns out to change the equilibrium position of the system, shifting it down by values ​​$\Delta x$ (Fig. 5, b).

Let's consider two coordinate systems: $OX$ and $O'X'$. Let us write down the law of pendulum motion in the first system:

$$ma = -kx + mg.$$
But
$$x = x' + \Delta x.$$

The value $\Delta x$ can be found from the condition of equality of the forces of gravity and elasticity in the equilibrium position: $mg = k\Delta x$. That's why

$$ma = -kx' - k \frac{mg}{k} + mg,$$

that is

$$ma = -kx'.$$

Consequently, the acceleration of the system is reported only by elastic forces.

The oscillation period is

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}.$$

We will find the amplitude of the oscillations based on the law of conservation of energy.
