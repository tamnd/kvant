HOW TO MEASURE A MOLECULE?
N. A. RODINA

Our basic knowledge of molecules can be expressed in very simple words:
1. All bodies are made up of tiny particles - atoms and molecules.
2. Atoms and molecules are in a state of continuous movement. This movement is eternal, never stopping under any circumstances.
3. Molecules of all bodies interact with each other. In different substances they interact differently, depending on the type of molecules and the distances between them.
These three provisions are called the basic provisions of the molecular-kinetic theory.
In science, perhaps, there are few areas of knowledge that have such a rapid and fruitful history of development as knowledge about molecules. Mention of the smallest particles of matter is found in manuscripts dating back to the 12th century BC. This means that this knowledge has been created over the course of 32 centuries, that is, 3200 years! Many great scientists contributed their work to the creation of molecular kinetic theory. In science, the structure of molecules and their behavior have been studied sufficiently fully to even rearrange molecules and create substances with a predetermined property. Artificial rubies, rubber, plastics, medicines and vitamins have been created. You've probably read about the difficulty with which these substances were added in their natural form in the past: rubies were added from underground, rubber from the sap of tropical trees, plastics were not known at all.

So, the molecules have been well studied, and they continue to be studied in our time. Moreover, the main directions of development of modern physics are associated with the application of knowledge about the structure of matter. This should be kept in mind by anyone who plans to study physics in the future.

Our story about molecules will be devoted to determining the sizes of molecules.

Can you imagine how big we are? Is it possible, for example, to show with your fingers at least the distance between air molecules, which is approximately 10 times greater than the diameter of the molecule itself?

The sizes of molecules have been determined in many experiments. Let's describe one of them; This experiment was tested about 60 years ago by the English scientist Robert Rayleigh.

A drop of olive oil was placed in a cleanly washed large vessel containing water and placed on its surface. The drop spread over the surface of the water and formed a round film. Gradually the area of ​​the film increased, but then the spreading stopped and the area stopped changing.
