Problem 4. A hydrometer of mass m is a ball filled with two and a cylindrical tube with a cross section S. It is placed in zinc with density $\rho$ (Fig. 7). The hydrometer adjusts the temperature slightly deeper than is necessary for equilibrium, and then releases it. The least free oscillations of a hydrometer.

In the equilibrium position, the gravity layer is balanced by the buoyant force. If the hydrometer is immersed deeper into the liquid, the buoyant layer becomes stronger than the forces of gravity, and an equilibrium force appears, directed upward. Having passed the equilibrium position by inertia, the hydrometer turns out to be immersed in the liquid less than is necessary for equilibrium, and an equilibrium force appears, directed downward. Thus, the change in the buoyant force acts as a restoring force:

$$\Delta F_{\text{выт}} = -\rho g \Delta V = -\rho g Sx$$

(the minus sign indicates that the change in buoyancy force is opposite to the change in the volume of the immersed part of the hydrometer). Therefore, in this case $k = \rho g S$, so

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{\rho g S}}.$$

Problem 5. A mathematical magnet of length l is mounted on a cart that rolls without friction from an inclined plane with an angle of inclination $\alpha$. The most important equilibrium of a magnet and the period of its oscillations.

Since the magnet is on a cart rolling down an inclined plane with acceleration $a = g \sin \alpha$, its equilibrium position will be the position in which the magnet moves relative to the plane with the same acceleration $a$ as the cart.

Two forces act on the ball: the force of gravity $mg$ and the tension force of the thread $F_H$. The balance of these forces in the equilibrium position should impart acceleration $a$ to the ball:

$$F_p = ma = mg \sin \alpha.$$

From figure 8 it is clear that this is only possible if the magnet thread is perpendicular to the inclined plane.

So, the component of gravity along the plane, equal to $mg \sin \alpha$, should provide the magnet with accelerated movement along the inclined plane. The tension force, always perpendicular to the trajectory of the ball relative to the cart, also cannot act as a restoring force. Therefore, one layer remains - the gravity component, perpendicular to the plane and equal to $mg \cos \alpha$. We can say that the magnet oscillates as if in a different gravitational field with the acceleration of free fall $g' = g \cos \alpha$.
