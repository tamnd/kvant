Task 1. We perform a harmonic oscillation of a spring magnet with a cyclic frequency $\omega$ and amplitude $x_0$. Find the maximum values ​​of acceleration and speed of the magnet, as well as the magnitude of its mechanical energy.

According to Newton's second law for harmonic oscillations

$$a = -\omega^2 x.$$

Since the displacement $x$ changes according to the harmonic law, and $\omega^2$ is a constant value, then the acceleration of the magnet $a$ also changes according to the harmonic law, and its amplitude

$$a_0 = \omega^2 x_0.$$

The minus sign in the equation of motion indicates that the acceleration oscillations are opposite in phase to the mixing oscillations.

Since the displacement of the magnet and its acceleration change with time according to the harmonic law, it is natural to assume that the speed of the magnet changes according to the same law. The structure of the calculation actually confirms these assumptions. The amplitude of velocity fluctuations can be found using the law of conservation of energy.

The mechanical energy of the magnet $E$ consists of the kinetic energy of the ball $E_K = mv^2/2$ and the potential energy of elastic deformation of the arm $E_\pi = kx^2/2$.

Fig. 4.

When passing through the equilibrium position, when the weightless arm is undeformed and the ball speed $v_0$ is maximum, the total energy

$$E = E_K \max = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

At the moment of the greatest distance of the ball from the equilibrium position, when its speed is zero and the deformation of the arm is maximum, the energy

$$E = E_\pi \max = \frac{kx^2}{2}.$$

The total energy of the magnet does not change over time (we are considering undamped free oscillations), therefore

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{kx^2}{2},$$

where

$$v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} x_0 = \omega x_0.$$

The velocity oscillation is determined by the mixing oscillation phase at the fourth period, that is, at $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
