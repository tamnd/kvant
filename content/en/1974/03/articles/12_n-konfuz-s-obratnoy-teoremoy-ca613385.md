Confusion with the “Inverse Theorem”

It is not often, not at all often, that writers resort to mathematical terminology. And a work of art with a “mathematical title” is generally a rarity. Therefore, A. Zharenov’s poem “The Inverse Theorem” (magazine “Seeker”, 1972, No. 2) could not but attract natural interest.

Let's say right away that the story does not specifically talk about mathematics as such. It tells about the life of Viktor Nazarov, who once dishonestly took over a large sum of children. Actually, money was the whole and meaning of his life. However, after this, his life, instead of becoming, as he hoped, happy and cloudless, was filled with continuous nightmares and constant fear of retribution. Nazarov’s enrichment became known to criminal elements, and a real hunt was organized for the “underground millionaire,” which ended in his murder. In parallel with the description of Nazarov’s life, the detective line of the story unfolds - an investigation into this idea.

This is how the author describes Nazarov’s difficult thoughts, the creation of which is constantly pressed by the idea he perfected:

“The face of a school mathematics teacher is in the fog: “Children, we call such reasoning a theorem... If in a theorem the conclusion is made a condition, and the condition a conclusion, then the first will be direct, and the second the inverse... Children, if the direct theorem is true, this does not mean that its inverse is also true...”
