On the first page of the cover there is a composition on the themes of the articles by A. P. Savin “Project Plane” and E. Ya. Gik “Project Chess” (pp. 9-19).

Against the background of a perspective image of a chessboard, a model of the projective plane is drawn - a circle, the diametrically opposite points of which are considered glued together. Six multi-colored areas on this circle border each other in pairs, so six colors are needed to correctly color the resulting map. Figure in the middle region refers to Desargues’ theorem, one of the most beautiful theorems of projective geometry (see also p. 36).

On the second page of the cover you see a paper ring cut to length. This ring is glued together from a twisted strip of paper. If you twist the strip half a turn before gluing, then after gluing you will get a Mebnus sheet; on the given figure the strip is twisted by one and a half turns.

It can be seen that after cutting along the length, the ring does not break into two pieces. It turns out that it is possible to make such a ring that, by cutting it along its length, we get a chain of two or even three rings. This is described in the article by V. A. Kordemsky “Topological experiments with your own hands” (pp. 73-75).
