SPATIAL MAZE

Everyone knows labyrinths whose corridors lie on the same plane. But in nature there are also spatial labyrinths, the corridors of which are located at different levels. These are caves. Cave explorers - spelologists - have a well-developed spatial imagination. They are relatively easy to navigate in the intricacies of transitions, turns, ascents, descents and dead ends. We too will become spelologists for some time.

Here is a floor plan of a spatial labyrinth. The floor numbers are placed with the corresponding numbers, the number 6 indicates the roof. All plans are oriented in the same way (the direction “south - north” is the same for them). Black lines indicate impenetrable partitions, white lines indicate holes in the floor; you can move through the corridor from one floor to another (up through a hole in the ceiling). You need to enter the hole in the roof and walk along the passages to exit through the hole in the floor of the first floor. You can also move in the opposite direction, entering from the underground floor of the first floor and exiting onto the roof.

The labyrinth can be made from sheets of transparent plexiglass, and a ball can be passed through it for real tennis. In this case, the width of the corridors and the height of the floors should be 40-45 mm (do not forget to take into account the thickness of the plexiglass!). figure shows the floor of the third floor with internal partitions installed on it and holes cut in the floor (the outer walls are made common to the entire labyrinth). When superimposing floors, you need to be careful: do not confuse them with each other and do not change the orientation. The exit hole in the roof will be lined with some kind of paint to confuse it with the exit in the floor of the first floor. After launching the ball, you can turn the labyrinth over to make work easier.

Your labyrinth will be interesting and useful entertainment for friends.
