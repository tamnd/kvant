⟦folio 34⟧

Quantum problem solver

Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than May 1, 1974 to the address: 117071, Moscow, V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka Publishing House, magazine «Kvant». After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: Problem «Kvant», M251, M252 “or”... F263”. Solutions to problems in each subject (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be sent in separate envelopes. Send problems from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking your decisions). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Quantum problem”, “new problem in physics” or “...new problem in mathematics”).

After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. The most difficult ones are marked with asterisks.

Tasks

M251-M255; F263-F267

M251. Given $n$ figs of several colors, and the physes of each color are no more than $\frac{n}{2}$. Prove that they can be placed on a circle so that no two physics of the same color stand next to each other.

F. G. Shleifer

M252. a) On the plane there is a regular smudge-head on the side $a$. It is allowed to “roll” along the plane, turning over (symmetrically reflecting) relative to any side. Prove that for any point $M$ of the plane and any $\varepsilon > 0$ it is possible to roll the scourer into such a position that its center will be located from the point $M$ at a distance less than $\varepsilon$ (Fig. 1).

6) Solve a similar problem for a regular pentagon.

c *) For which regular $n$-gons is a similar statement true?

G. A. Galperin

M253. Three points are given on the plane, which correspond to the inscribed, circumscribed and one of the outer circles of the triangle. How can you reconstruct the triangle using this data?

(Recall that the outer circle of a triangle is a circle tangent to one of the sides of the triangle and extended by the other two sides.)

B. V. Martynov
