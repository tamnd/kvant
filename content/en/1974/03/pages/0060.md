Adding the first two equations of system (16) and taking into account that $(F_{12}\Delta s_1) + (F_{21}\Delta s_2)$ is the change in the interaction energy of system I, taken with a minus sign, and $(F_{13}\Delta s_1) + (F_{23}\Delta s_2)$ is the work of external forces, we obtain

$$\Delta (K_1 + K_2) + \Delta I_1 = A_{\text{внешш}}.$$ (17)

A familiar result: the change in the mechanical energy of the system is equal to the work of external forces.

According to the third equation of the system (16)

$$\Delta K_3 = A'.$$ (18)

where $A'$ is the work of forces of system I on system II.

Adding equations (17) and (18), we have

$$\Delta (K_1 + K_2) + \Delta I_1 + \Delta K_3 = \Delta E_1 + \Delta E_{II} = A_{\text{внешш}} + A'.$$ (19)

Here $E_1 = K_1 + K_2 + \Pi_1$ is the total energy of the first system, and $E_{II} = K_3$ is the energy of the second system. From this it is clear that

$$A_{\text{внешш}} = -A'.$$

If

$$\Delta E_1 = -\Delta E_{II},$$ (20)

that is, if the decrease in energy of the first system is equal to the increase in the energy of the second (or vice versa).

Potential energy of interaction of the system with external bodies

The condition for equality of work (equality (14)) can be written differently if we introduce the concept of potential energy of interaction of the systems under consideration. Let us rewrite equation (19) in expanded form, substituting the expressions for work $A_{\text{внешш}}$ and $A'$:

$$\Delta E_1 + \Delta E_{II} = (F_{13}\Delta s_1) + (F_{23}\Delta s_2) + (F_{31}\Delta s_3) + (F_{32}\Delta s_3).$$ (21)

Let the forces of interaction of bodies of system I with system II be conservative. Then $(F_{13}\Delta s_1) + (F_{31}\Delta s_3) = -\Delta I_{13},$ and $(F_{23}\Delta s_2) + (F_{32}\Delta s_3) = -\Delta I_{23},$ where $I_{13}$ and $I_{23}$ are the potential energies of interaction of bodies 1-3 and 2-3. Sum

$$\Pi_{13} + \Pi_{23} = \Pi_{I-II}$$ (22)

represents the potential energy of interaction of system I - II. It can be called the potential energy of interaction of system I with external body 3.

Now we write equality (22) as follows:

$$\Delta E_1 + \Delta E_{II} = -\Delta I_{I-II}.$$ (23)

This means $\Delta E_1 = -\Delta E_{II}$ and, therefore, $A_{\text{внешш}} = -A'$ in the case when $\Delta I_{I-II} = 0$, that is, when the potential energy of interaction of the system does not change.

When the motor energy of their interaction (the energy of the extended drive belt) remains unchanged. If there is a stone on the Earth, the potential energy of interaction changes. Therefore, in the first case $A_{\text{внешш}} = -A'$, and in the second this equality does not hold.

The role of friction forces

Speed-dependent forces in mechanics are friction forces. Like all forces, friction forces change the kinetic energy of a system. Moreover, although friction forces can perform positive work, the total work of friction forces of the internal system is always negative, and they reduce the kinetic energy of the system.
