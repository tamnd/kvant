One of the most remarkable properties of project geometry is the “principle of duality”: on the project plane, what theorem remains valid if in it all the “mochika” layer is replaced with the “straight line” layer, the “straight line” layer with the “mochika” layer and interchanges the expressions: “we lie on...” and “we pass through...”. An example of such duality can be the above properties of the design plane.

Design plane models

We obtained the projective plane by introducing "points at infinity" and "line at infinity". Is it possible to see what came out of this? We saw the “line at infinity” (without one point) when we projected the projective plane Q onto the plane P. But at the same time, other points “went to infinity” - the line l₂. I would like to get a model of the design plane in which all the points were “visible”.

One such model has already been almost built. Namely, let us fix a point O in space and call the straight lines passing through the point O “points,” and the planes passing through the point O “straight lines.” Look again at figure 6-8. Each point of the projective plane actually corresponds to a single “point” of the model - a straight line passing through the point and point O, and to each straight line - a plane passing through the point and point O.
