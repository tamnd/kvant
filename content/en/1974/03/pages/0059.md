Nevertheless, it is very often stated that if forces external to the system being dissipated do work $A_{\text{внеш}}$, then the forces applied from the system to external bodies do the same external work $A'$, which has the opposite sign:

$$A_{\text{внеш}} = -A'.$$ (14)

But this will only happen in the case when the dispersing system and the external bodies make the same movements. And this is not always the case. According to Newton's third law, forces must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, but displacements do not have to be equal.

As an example, let's look at two simple systems: the Earth and a stone falling on it *). Then the gravitational forces for both the Earth and the stone will be considered external forces. It’s a different matter if, for example, a stone is lifted by hand (Fig. 8). Then the work of external force $F_1$ applied to the stone is exactly equal in magnitude and opposite in sign to the work of force $F_2$ applied to the hand from the side of the stone. In the same way, the work performed by the engine, connected by transmission with the machine, is equal in magnitude and opposite in sign to the work performed by the machine on the engine.

Let us find out in more detail, from the point of view of the law of conservation of energy, the conditions under which equality (14) is satisfied. Let there be an open system I of two bodies. They are acted upon by external forces from the third body (system II). Systems I and II are generally closed (Fig. 9).

According to Newton's second law

$$m_1 a_1 = F_{12} + F_{13},$$
$$m_2 a_2 = F_{21} + F_{23},$$
$$m_3 a_3 = F_{31} + F_{32}.$$ (15)

The forces $F_{12}$ and $F_{21}$ are internal to system I, and the forces $F_{13}$ and $F_{23}$ are external. System II consists of one body, and all forces acting on it are external.

Let the first body move to $\Delta s_1$, the second to $\Delta s_2$, and the third to $\Delta s_3$ in a short time. Let us multiply equations (15) by $\Delta s_1$, $\Delta s_2$ and $\Delta s_3$, respectively. Then, taking into account that $m_i(a_i \Delta s_i) = \Delta K_i$, we get

$$\Delta K_1 = (F_{12} \Delta s_1) + (F_{13} \Delta s_1),$$
$$\Delta K_2 = (F_{21} \Delta s_2) + (F_{23} \Delta s_2),$$
$$\Delta K_3 = (F_{31} \Delta s_3) + (F_{32} \Delta s_3).$$ (16)
