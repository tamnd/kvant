Law of conservation of energy in a closed system

In a closed system in which conservative forces act, the work done by these forces is $A = -\Delta \Pi$. On the other hand, in all cases $A = \Delta K$. This means that when work is done, an increase in kinetic energy is accompanied by a decrease in potential energy and vice versa:

$$\Delta K = -\Delta \Pi.$$

This implies the law of conservation of total mechanical energy for a closed system of bodies:

$$E = K + \Pi = \text{const.} \quad (10)$$

This is all quite simple and misconceptions are rare. The situation is different if external forces act on the system.

Change in the energy of the system under the influence of external forces

Nibaulier propagates two misunderstandings here.

Firstly, it is not always clear that external forces directly change only the kinetic energy of the bodies of the system, but not the potential energy of interaction of these bodies. A change in the potential energy of a system is always determined by the work of interaction forces (internal forces). Of course, external forces change the location of the bodies of the system (since their kinetic energy changes), and due to this the working of internal forces changes, which means the potential energy of the system changes. But if there were no conservation forces acting in the system, then the potential energy would not change.

Indeed, empty, an external force $F$ acts on the system consisting of stone and Earth. This force could be, for example, the tension of a rope tied to a stone. Then, according to Newton's second law,

$$m a = F + mg. \quad (11)$$

Let the stone move vertically upward by $\Delta s$ over a certain period of time. Multiplying equation (11) glory and on the right by $\Delta s$, we obtain

$$m(a \Delta s) = (F \Delta s) + m(g \Delta s),$$
or

$$m(a \Delta s) - m(g \Delta s) = (F \Delta s). \quad (12)$$

The first term on the left is the change in kinetic energy. Indeed, in this case

$$m(a \Delta s) = ma \Delta s = ma \frac{v_2^2 - v_1^2}{2a} = \frac{mv_2^2}{2} - \frac{mv_1^2}{2} = \Delta K.$$

The second term on the left is the change in potential energy $\Delta \Pi$. Note that the change in potential energy will occur due to the work of the interaction forces between the Earth and the stone. And the right part is the work of an external force, which can be called external work $A_{\text{внешш}}$. Therefore, equality (12) can be written as follows:

$$\Delta K + \Delta \Pi = A_{\text{внешш}}, \quad (13)$$

that is, the change in the mechanical energy of the system is equal to the work of the external force.
