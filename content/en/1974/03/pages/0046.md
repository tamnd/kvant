From figure 11, 6: DF=NC=MC-MN. But MC=AB, and MN=BM tg γ=AB tg α tan γ. Posmot DF=AB—AB tg α tan γ=AB (1−tg α tan γ) and

$$\frac{AB}{DF} = \frac{1}{1−tg α tg γ}.$$

Angles α and γ are not independent: γ = β−α, where β−γ is the angle of refraction of the rays. From the law of light refraction sin β = n sin α, that is β = arcsin (n sin α). Since the angle α is small, and the values ​​of n are usually of the order of 1, we can assume that β = arcsin (nα) = n α. Then

$$\gamma = nα−α = α(n−1).$$

Hence,

$$\frac{AB}{DF} = \frac{1}{1−tg α tg γ} = \frac{1}{1−αγ} = \frac{1}{1−(n−1)α^2}$$

And

$$E = E_0 \frac{AB}{DF} = E_0 \frac{1}{1−(n−1)α^2}.$$

Thus, in the area DE the illumination increased by

$$\frac{E}{E_0} = \frac{1}{1−(n−1)α^2}$$ times,

and in section EF - in

$$\frac{E_0+E}{E_0} = 1 + \frac{1}{1−(n−1)α^2}$$ times.

F221. A dock lies on a stationary circular cylinder of radius R, as shown in figure 12, a. The thickness of the dock is h. At what ratio between h and R will we establish equilibrium? The friction between the dock and the cylinder is large.

To find out what the equilibrium of the dock is, you need to rotate it relative to the cylinder by a small angle α and see what will be the farthest one.

Since the friction between the dock and the cylinder is high, when the dock is turned it does not slide along the cylinder, but the height of its center also changes. In this case, the equilibrium position of the dock will be stable if, when turning, the center of gravity of the dock rises. Therefore, in order to solve the problem, you need to find the position of the center of gravity of the dock in a horizontal position, then find the position of the center of gravity of the dock, rotate it through a small angle α, and write down the condition that in this new position the center of gravity of the dock is higher than in the previous one.

We will count all heights from a horizontal plane passing through the middle of the cylinder.

When the dock is horizontal, its center of gravity - point C - is at a height (see Fig. 12, 6)

$$H_1 = OC = R + \frac{h}{2},$$

and in the new position - at the top

$$H_2 = C'M.$$

Let us connect the height of H₂ with R, h and α, for which we will make some additional constructions. Let us lower a perpendicular from point C' to the side of the dock, resting on the cylinder, that is, on the base of the dock, until it intersects with this side at point B'. Then from point O we draw a radius to the point of tangency A and extend it to a straight line passing through points C' and parallel to the base of the dock; we obtain point D. From point D we lower the perpendicular DF ​​on the horizontal plane and the perpendicular DE to the straight line C'M.

Height

$$H_2 = C'M = C'E + EM.$$
