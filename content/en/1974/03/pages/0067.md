⟦folio 65⟧

The author seems to be successful in “Combinatorics” addressed to schoolchildren by N. Ya. Vilenkin *); However, some students may be put off by the sheer size of this book (328 pages). The informative brochure by N. I. Ezhov, A. V. Skorokhod and M. I. Yadrenko has only 83 pages of small format.

Introductory § 1 is devoted to a discussion of the content of combinatorics and the formulation of the “basic rule,” which in our literature is often called the product rule or the multiplication rule. The next two sections are related to finite sets and operations on them, as well as the number of a subset of a finite set; this material is to some extent applied in the second half of the book to § 7, which explains the concept of the right (or Cartesian) product $A \times B$ of finite sets $A$ and $B$ (that is, the set of pairs $(a, b)$ of elements from $A$ and from $B$). §§4-6 are connected by an ordered set. Then, in the language of finite sets, some specific basic concepts of combinatorics and realistic problems are presented, while in the book of V. A. Vilensky, on the contrary, general concepts are in the foreground. His book consists of two chapters: “Power” and “Attitude”; the reader can get some idea of ​​its focus from A. N. Kolmogorov’s articles “What is a function” and “What is a graph of a function” (Kvant, 1970, Nos. 1, 2). At the same time, the author explains the important concepts of sets and operations on sets, implication (following) and equivalence (equivalence), predicates and quantifiers, relations and functions using quite meaningful examples (also close to secondary school students!). For example, throughout the book the concept of a set of solutions to an equation (or inequality, system of equations, system of inequalities) is used. An essential component of V. A. Vilensky’s book is also its numerous tasks and exercises.

In general, it seems to me that the interesting books of N. I. Ezhov, A. V. Skorokhod, M. I. Yadrenko and V. A. Vilensky would be useful to translate into Russian.
