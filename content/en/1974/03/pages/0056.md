Fig. 3. The change in the potential energy of interaction of bodies 1 and 2 is determined by the work of forces $F_{12}$ and $F_{21}$ on displacements $\Delta s_1$ and $\Delta s_2$.

$$A = (F_{12}\Delta s_1) + (F_{21}\Delta s_2) = -[\Pi(r_2) - \Pi(r_1)] = -\Delta \Pi,$$

where $F_{12}$ is the force acting on body 1 from body 2, and $F_{21}$ is the force acting on body 2 from body 1 (Fig. 3).

Interaction forces between bodies, which depend only on the distance between the bodies, are conservative forces. The mentioned statement is a consequence of the fact that the work of conservative forces is determined only by the initial and final positions of bodies and does not depend on the shape of their trajectory.

The change in potential energy is easily calculated if the dependence of the forces on the distance between the interactions of the bodies is known.

Zero potential energy level

According to equation (3), the work of interaction forces determines not the potential energy itself, but its change.

For gravity forces near the Earth's surface

$$\Delta \Pi_T = mgh_2 - mgh_1,$$

where $h_1$ and $h_2$ are the heights of the body above the Earth in the initial and final states.

Change in potential energy of a deformation spring

$$\Delta \Pi_y = \frac{k(\Delta l)^2}{2} - \frac{k(\Delta l_1)^2}{2},$$

where $k$ is the elasticity coefficient, and $\Delta l_1$ and $\Delta l_2$ are the initial and final deformation of the spring.

Since work determines the change in potential energy, and not the energy itself, the change in energy in mechanics has a physical meaning. Therefore, one can arbitrarily choose the state of the system in which its potential energy is considered equal to zero. This state corresponds to a zero level of potential energy. The choice of the zero level is made in different ways and is dictated solely by one’s own convenience, that is, the simplicity of writing the equation expressing the law of conservation of energy.

Obviously, as a state with zero energy, choose the state of the system at which P is minimal. Then the potential energy is positive.

A spring has minimum potential energy in the absence of deformation, while a stone has minimum potential energy when it lies on the surface of the Earth. Therefore, in the first case (Fig. 4)

$$\Pi_y = \frac{k(\Delta l)^2}{2},$$

and in the second case (Fig. 5)

$$\Pi_T = mgh.$$

But any constant value $C$ can be added to these expressions, and this will not change anything as a result of applying the law of conservation of energy. It can be considered that

$$\Pi_y = \frac{k(\Delta l)^2}{2} + C, \quad \Pi_T = mgh + C.$$

If in the second case we put $C = -mgh_0$, then this will mean that the zero energy level will be at a height of $h_0$ above the Earth.
