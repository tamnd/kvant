⟦folio 5⟧

It is cooled from the inside due to the energy released during the passage of current, and is cooled from the surface due to contact with the cold walls of the gas-discharge device or with the surrounding layers of ordinary gas. The plasma produced by intense gas discharges can be many times hotter than the metal, glass or neutral gas that surrounds it. In addition, such a plasma is thermally nonequilibrium in one more respect. It consists of a mixture of several components, unequally heated. However, of these components are electrons, another are positive ions and a third are neutral atoms. They are also evenly mixed together, like oxygen and nitrogen in the atmosphere.

In contrast to the general gas mixture, all parts of which, unequally depending on their belonging to one or another component, have the same average kinetic energy of random thermal motion, the average kinetic energy of electrons, electrons and neutral atoms of gas discharge plasma is different. Electrons, as a rule, have higher energies than ions, and the kinetic energy of ions can exceed the energy of neutral atoms and molecules. Pozny can say that plasma is a mixture of components with different temperatures. As is known, the average value of the kinetic energy $\bar{E}$ of a part of the gas participating in the random thermal movement is related to the temperature $T$ by the following simple formula:

$$\bar{E} = \frac{3}{2} k T,$$

where $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant.

Due to the difference in the average kinetic energy of electrons, electrons and neutral parts in plasma, together with one general temperature, three different temperatures should be distinguished - electronic $T_3$, intern $T_и$ and atomic $T_a$. Usually $T_3 \gg T_и > T_a$. The very large difference between $T_3$ and $T_и$, characteristic of most forms of gas discharge, is due to the enormous difference in the mass of the electrons of the ions. External sources of electrical energy, with the help of which a gas discharge is created and maintained, transfer energy directly to plasma electrons, since it is light electrons that carry current. They gain their energy by colliding with fast-moving electrons. However, in each individual collision, due to the large difference in mass, the light electron transfers only a small part of its kinetic energy to it, bouncing off it like a ping-pong ball hitting a massive steel ball.

A simple analysis based on the application of the law of conservation of energy and the law of conservation of momentum shows that if a body of small mass $m_1$ collides elastically with a body of many times greater mass $m_2$, then the relative proportion of kinetic energy that a light body is able to transfer to a heavy one cannot exceed $\frac{4m_1}{m_2}$ (try to prove this yourself). The ratio of the mass of an electron to its mass is equal to $1:1840 A$, where $A$ is the atomic weight of the substance that belongs to it. Consequently, the most relative value of the energy transferred in one collision is only about $2 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \frac{1}{A}$.

An electron must experience a lot of collisions with electrons in order to completely give up the excess energy it has.

Since parallel to the processes in which energy is exchanged between electrons and ions, there is a process of acquiring energy by electrons from electrical sources.
