At one time I acquired the collection “Mathematics in the Modern World” (M., Mir, 1967). While reading it, I encountered unexpected difficulties. In the “section” of pages 113-144, the order of the pages turned out to be uneven. After reading p. 113, everyone expected to see the collection of the same sheet with. 114, but sees - p. 118. Next should be p. 115, and in my copy it turned out to be p. 119 and so on. In other words, the order of pages in this section can be represented by the following sequence:
(113, 118), (119, 116), (117, 114), (115, 120), (121, 126), (127, 124), (125, 122), (123, 128), (129, 134), (135, 132), (133, 130), (131, 136), (137, 142), (143, 140), (141, 138), (139, 144).

Having created such a “page layout” for a selected section of the book, I no longer experienced the previous difficulties in moving from the page I had read to the next one in order.

This episode with the unusual copy of the book could have been left without any criticism. However, a completely natural question will arise: how was this order of pages in the book formed, what was the printer’s error?

We will express some messages on this issue, presenting its complete solution to the reader.

The imprint of the book states “Paper... 70 × 108¹/₁₆ = 6.5 bum. l." This conditional phrase means the following:

The book is sewn from 6.5 terdy, each full terdy is formed by folding a sheet of 70 × 108 cm format four times in half (half terdy is made from a sheet of 70 × 54 cm format, a full terdy contains 16 book sheets with a format of 17.5 × 27 cm.

To avoid mistakes when assembling terms into a book, they are needed. In our book it turns out to be number 3. The non-educational term is number 5.

Let's agree on the following procedure for typographic production of a complete terdy: we type separately all 32 of its pages along with their serial numbers, form them into a pair of “matrices” of 16 pages each, make impressions from both matrices on both sides of the sheet, form the terd by successively folding the paper sheet in half.
