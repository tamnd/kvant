Enough. We need to prove that if sequences $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of length $3n$ satisfy conditions (1), then these sequences are equivalent. It is more convenient to prove a more general statement: if sequences $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of length $k$ satisfy conditions (1), then these sequences $\alpha$ and $\beta$ simply coincide.

2) Let us assume that in the case where $k = p$, the statement has already been proven. Let us prove it for $k = p + 1$. So, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be sequences of length $p + 1$ and let correspondence (1) hold. Let, further, the sequence $\alpha$ consist of the digits $\{a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{p+1}\}$, and the sequence $\beta$ - of the digits $\{b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{p+1}\}$.

a) if $a_1 = b_1$, then consider the sequences $\alpha' = \{a_2, a_3, \dots, a_{p+1}\}$ and $\beta' = \{b_2, b_3, \dots, b_{p+1}\}$. It is clear that the sequences $\alpha'$ and $\beta'$ satisfy conditions (1). And since the lengths of these sequences are equal to $p$, we can use the inductive hypothesis. Thus, the sequences $\alpha'$ and $\beta'$ are equivalent, that is, the sequence $\alpha'$ can be translated into the sequence $\beta'$ by several permutations. The same permutations take $\alpha$ to $\beta$. This means that the sequences $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are equivalent.

6) Let $a_1 \neq b_1$, and let $a_1 = 1, a_2 = 2$ for definition. Since $a_1 = 1$, then $|\alpha_1| > 0$. Then by virtue of (1) and $|\beta_1| > 0$. Therefore, there is a number $q$ such that $b_{3q+1} = 1$. In the sequence $\beta$ we place adjacent points of the numbers $b_{3q+1}, b_{3q+2}, b_{3q+3}$ and $b_{3(q-1)+1}, b_{3(q-1)+2}, b_{3(q-1)+3}$. As a result of this rearrangement, the point of the digits in $b_{3q+1}, b_{3q+2}, b_{3q+3}$ will move 3 digits to the left. Then we place this triple with the adjacent triple of digits $b_{3(q-2)+1}, b_{3(q-2)+2}, b_{3(q-2)+3}$ and so on, after $q$ permutations we obtain the sequence $\beta''$, the first digit of which will be $b_{3q+1}$, that is, 1. The sequences $\beta$ and $\beta''$ are equivalent and therefore, as proven above, satisfy conditions (1). This means that the sequences $\alpha$ and $\beta''$ satisfy conditions (1). But the first elements of these sequences coincide. Thus, case 6) has been reduced to the already discussed case a). The theorem has been proven.
