⟦folio 13⟧

Joe accepts Bate's plan

“Everything is fine, I like the operation plan, Bate,” Joe said, pacing the hotel room and washing down each puff of smoke with a good sip of whiskey. Old Bate sat in an armchair by the miserable fireplace, habitually feeling the handle of a pistol under his arm.

- Of course! - Bate walked through his false teeth. “It’s not for nothing that half of all the shats have been chasing me for fifteen years.” It’s unlikely that I would have paid such a price if only I could wield a cassette tape. New science is my strong point. Remember, Joe, it was I who first introduced helicopters when the banks were limited. How do I...

- Wait! — the first Joe boasting colleague. “I value you, that’s why I work with you.” And this creative new idea - to communalize three warehouses with textiles in one night - is also magnificent. But the drivers...

- These are the iron guys! - Bate exclaimed. - You can rely on them! No pharaoh can catch such people!

“I trust this guy, Bate.” But the price! 10 dollars per ton-mile on trucks - but for that price I’m ready to haul it by hand! We will go broke even if everything burns out.

And Joe showed in cheap hotel style how he was ready to move the loads. The chair creaked pitifully: it was in these hotels that Joe liked to discuss difficult operations, where there was less chance of sniffing at a hidden police microphone.

“But don’t forget, Joe, what the guys are risking... And besides, besides... I made sure to light them less.” No, no, don’t cheat - it won’t work with that. The point is completely different: I apply the scientific method.

Joe looked at Bate with respect (after all, he had once graduated from college), but still objected:

- Listen, Bate. Fuck me, I'm turning on more money, thousands of dollars. I want to be privy to the heart of the matter. Only you are simpler, you know, I’m more of a master key...

“Okay, Joe,” Bate nodded seriously, realizing that he must strain all his teaching abilities, otherwise the matter will not work out, and he will run aground.

- How many buyers steal textiles?

- Four. The first two are sixty tons each, and the other two are forty tons each.

— Do you remember how many are in the warehouses?

“Are you laughing, Bate?” I remember these numbers even in my dreams: 75, 75 and 50.
