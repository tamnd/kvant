Let’s fantasize about more specific topics: we’ll ask which astroid people will fly to in the near future. First of all, this is the astroid Eros (see Fig. p. 4). It approaches Emla at a distance of 2.3·10⁷ km (only the small astroid Icarus comes closer to us, it approaches at 5.7·10⁵ km). Such a “meeting” between Eros and Emley will take place in June 1975.

Long before the flight, astronauts and engineers must know well what speeds and accelerations are allowed on this astroid, so that some part or even an astronaut does not fly into space. The length of Eros is 35 km, its diameter is 10 km. Eros rotates around its short axis, perpendicular to the direction in which it is elongated, with a period of 5 hours.

For numerical estimates, two simplified models of Eros can be accepted: a) a cylinder, b) a cigar-shaped body. For these two models, one can estimate, firstly, the gravitational force $F_g$, which is different at different points of the surface and has the smallest value at the ends of the astroid, and secondly, one can find the centripetal acceleration from the known angular velocity of rotation. High centripetal acceleration at the ends of the “cigar”. Table 1 shows the results of such a calculation at the ends of the Eros and Geographos astroids.

| Estimated value | Eros | Sigraa | Geographos |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rotation period (h) | 5,2 | 5,2 | 5,2 |
| Length (km) | 35 | 31 | 31 |
| Diameter (km) | 10 | 0,7 | 0,7 |
| Gravity acceleration ($\times 10^3$ m/s²) | 6,64 | 0,414 | 0,121 |
| Centripetal acceleration during daily rotation ($\times 10^3$ m/s²) | 1,92 | 0,173 | 0,173 |
| Relative gravitational acceleration (surface measurement) ($\times 10^3$ m/s²) | 4,72 | 0,241 | -0,052 |

As we see in the example of Geographos, the astroid can rotate so quickly (calculated with a siger-shaped shape) that the gravitational force is too small to provide the necessary centripetal acceleration for bodies lying on the surface of the astroid. Therefore, stones and other objects will fly away from the surface of the astroid into the surrounding space. The weight of the bodies turns out to be negative *).
