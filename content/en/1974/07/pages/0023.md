HOW PHYSICISTS DETERMINE THE CURVATURE OF A PARABOLA

Let us consider the movement under the influence of gravity of a body (material point of mass $m$), which at the initial moment is imparted a horizontal speed equal to $v_0$. Let's choose a reporting system associated with the Earth. With respect to this reporting system, the body participates in two movements - horizontal (along the $x$ axis) and vertical (along the $y$ axis). Equation of body motion horizontally

$$x = v_0 t,$$
(1)

vertically -

$$y = \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

The total motion of the body occurs along a curve, which is easy to determine by establishing the relationships between the coordinates $x$ and $y$ of various points of the body’s trajectory. To do this, from equations (1) and (2) we express $y$ through $x$. We get

$$y = \frac{gt^2}{2} = \frac{gx^2}{2v_0^2}.$$

Equation (3) expresses the relationship between the coordinates $x$ and $y$ of all points of the curved trajectory described by the thrown body.

It is known that a stone thrown horizontally from the roof of a tall building or a high volume moves in a curve called a parabola. The trajectory of the stone during flight is curved, deviating from straight horizontal movement. The degree of curvature may be greater or less depending on the initial speed. Is it possible to somehow estimate the degree of curvature of the trajectory? To do this, use the understandable curvature of the curve.
