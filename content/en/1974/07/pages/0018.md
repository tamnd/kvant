A little theory

What allows you to save 1,400 dollars on transportation costs? Let's follow the actions of clever gangsters. First, Bate found a feasible transportation plan. The method that he used is called the minimum element method and it is clear why: in it, transportation is always placed on routes with minimum tariffs, and if there are two routes with the same tariff, then preference, naturally, should be given to the one for which the possible transportation is greater.

Once they had a feasible plan, Bate and Joe began trying to improve it in a distributed manner. This is perhaps the simplest, although not the fastest, way to improve your transportation plan. But before presenting this method in general form, we formulate a strictly transortine linear programming problem.

Let there be $m$ suppliers (warehouses) and $n$ consumers, $a_i$ the capacity of the $i$ warehouse, and $b_j$ the need of the $j$ consumer. Let $x_{ij}$ be transportation from the $i$th supplier to the $j$th consumer. Only such transportation plans are acceptable for which $*$

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n} x_{ij} = a_i \quad (i=1, 2, \dots, m),$$

$$\sum_{i=1}^{m} x_{ij} = b_j \quad (j=1, 2, \dots, n),$$

that is, everything that is there is transported from each warehouse to each consumer.

*) We are considering the so-called “closed” model of the transport problem, for which $\sum a_i = \sum b_j$, that is, the sum of the capacity (warehouses) is equal to the sum of the needs.
