⟦folio 68⟧

Together all the time. But I don’t know why.

“These are real friends,” said Pythogoras, “we can separate them below.” I call them friendly, and here's why: the sum of the proper divisors of each of them is equal to the other. Here are the proper divisors of Number 220: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 11, 20, 22, 44, 55, 110; their sum is 284: 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 10 + 11 + 20 + 22 + 44 + 55 + 110 = 284; the proper divisors of 284 are Numbers 1, 2, 4, 71, 142; their sum is 220: 1 + 2 + 4 + 71 + 142 = 220. When someone asked me the other day what a friend is, I told him “a friend is my other self,” and gave the examples of the numbers 220 and 284. You are probably wondering if there are other pairs of friendly numbers. Yes, I know of three other couples like this, they are at the end of the hall; but these are very large numbers.

“And I also noticed that the Numbers somehow strangely divided into pairs when the dancing began. For example, 11 and 13, 17 and 19, 29 and 31. But 5 danced either with the 3rd or with the 7th. “What’s the matter?” asked Geometry.

“Oh, it’s very simple,” Pythogoras smiled, “only pairs of simple Twin Numbers danced.” You know that if the numbers p and p + 2 are prime, then they are called twins. These are the numbers that came together. As for Number 5, it is the twin of both Number 3 and Number 7, so it danced with one or the other.

- Oh, how could I not notice this? After all, I know Prime Numbers very well,” Geometry said with regret.

“It’s okay, estera,” Arithmetic consoled her, “don’t worry.” Let's better thank our dear Pythogoras for another moment.

The members thanked Pythogoras, and he, in turn, assured them that he would continue to faithfully serve Arithmetic, Geometry, Philosophy and Music, dear to his heart.
