Guests came to Princess Arithmetic - her sister Geometry and cousins ​​Philosophy and Music. The princess was delighted with this visit - the sisters had been close friends since childhood.

In honor of the guests, a ball was held, to which, by order of the princess, all Natural Numbers were invited. They walked in an endless stream into the majestic Hall of Arithmetic.

Here comes Odin. He is proud of the honor that has fallen to him - being the first to enter the hall and greet the princess and her guests. Behind him come Two, Three, CheMyre, etc. Why don’t they look at each other!

The dancing has begun. Almost all Numbers took part in the holiday. . .

The hostess was having fun too. Suddenly she noticed that Muzka was familiar with something - she was looking in surprise at one number and then at another, looking at the groups in which they had united. . .

- What's the matter, Millia? Why aren't you having fun, look at the variety! - Arithmetic turned to her.

- Oh, dear! It is this diversity that makes me think. Your courtiers have taught you to behave this way, I don’t understand anything!

- Why does this surprise you? After all, they are all different, differing from each other not only in appearance and position in society, but also in character. Would you like me to tell you something about them? - asked the princess, tenderly kissing her sister.

- I will be very grateful to you. I think that your story will also interest Geometry and Philosophy. Isn't that right, my dears? - Muzka turned to Geometry and Philosophy, who also closely followed the Numbers.

“Of course, of course,” they opened, “we apparently can’t figure it out on our own.”

“Well, I guess I’ll satisfy your curiosity,” said Arithmetic. - However, wait, here’s what I came up with: let’s ask Pythogoras to do this with me: after all, he knows all the secrets of the Numerical World.

The friends knew Pythogoras very well - he visited them more than once. All four approached him.

“Wise Pythogor,” the princess turned to him, “our dear guests are interested in my courtiers.” I was going to tell them something about this, as they say, habituated World, but I changed my mind - after all, you will do it much better than me. Please do not deny us the process; I will be happy to listen to you - after all, you will probably have some news.

- Glad to serve you, dear princess. “I am at your service,” Pythogor said, bowing, and began his story.

There is nothing interesting in the world of Numbers! Each Number is unique and contains a number of substitutable properties. Take Number One for example. Of course, you noticed how proudly he entered the hall. Our One has the right to this - he is the first in the series of Natural Numbers and has undeclared properties: every Natural Number is divisible by him, and he is divisible only by himself - and this is the only number that has only one divisor. It is noteworthy that when dividing and multiplying by One, other numbers do not change. Everyone respects and appreciates him. He personifies equality, while Two is the beginning of inequality. Three has remarkable properties: in addition to the fact that Three is a pre-city number, it is the first odd prime number. But this is still not enough. Three is the only number equal to the sum of the previous Numbers: 1 + 2 = 3. What and Five have interesting properties. But the properties of Number Six are even more substitutable. Look how important he walks around the countryside, feeling his superiority.

- What is it? - asked Philosophy.

— The fact is that this number is equal to the sum of its own divisors: its divisors are 1, 2, 3, and their sum is 1 + 2 + 3 = 6. This is perfection! That’s what I call him, the Perfect Number**).

- Amazing! Are there really no other numbers like this? - asked Geometry.

“There are,” answered Pythagoras, smiling, “but there are very few of them.” This is natural: after all, perfection is beauty, and beauty, unfortunately, is not encountered very often. I know four Perfect ones

“Of course, of course, the research of our respected Pythogoras will continue,” said Arithmetic and, slyly narrowing his eyes, asked the sisters: “aren’t you surprised by the behavior of the pair of Numbers 220 and 284?”

“These are real friends,” said Pythogoras, “we can separate them below.” I call them friendly, and here's why: the sum of the proper divisors of each of them is equal to the other. Here are the proper divisors of Number 220: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 11, 20, 22, 44, 55, 110; their sum is 284: 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 10 + 11 + 20 + 22 + 44 + 55 + 110 = 284; the proper divisors of 284 are Numbers 1, 2, 4, 71, 142; their sum is 220: 1 + 2 + 4 + 71 + 142 = 220. When someone asked me the other day what a friend is, I told him “a friend is my other self,” and gave the examples of the numbers 220 and 284. You are probably wondering if there are other pairs of friendly numbers. Yes, I know of three other couples like this, they are at the end of the hall; but these are very large numbers.

“And I also noticed that the Numbers somehow strangely divided into pairs when the dancing began. For example, 11 and 13, 17 and 19, 29 and 31. But 5 danced either with the 3rd or with the 7th. “What’s the matter?” asked Geometry.

“Oh, it’s very simple,” Pythogoras smiled, “only pairs of simple Twin Numbers danced.” You know that if the numbers p and p + 2 are prime, then they are called twins. These are the numbers that came together. As for Number 5, it is the twin of both Number 3 and Number 7, so it danced with one or the other.

- Oh, how could I not notice this? After all, I know Prime Numbers very well,” Geometry said with regret.

“It’s okay, estera,” Arithmetic consoled her, “don’t worry.” Let's better thank our dear Pythogoras for another moment.

The members thanked Pythogoras, and he, in turn, assured them that he would continue to faithfully serve Arithmetic, Geometry, Philosophy and Music, dear to his heart.
