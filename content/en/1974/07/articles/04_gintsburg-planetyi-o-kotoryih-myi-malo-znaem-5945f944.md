Introduction

In addition to the large planets - Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto - smaller planets - the astroid - also revolve around the Sun. The first such planet, named Ceres, was discovered in 1801. Its diameter is 740 km. Then there were the discovered Pallas - 490 km, Juno - 200 km, Vesta - 400 km. Almost all astroid orbits are located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter (see Fig.). Currently, the orbits for 1,700 astroid have been determined. In modern telescopes, about 50,000 astroid are visible. At first, the named astroid was taken from Greek mythology, then they were called by their names. There are astroids Vera, Olga, Valeria, Lyudmila, Tatyana. There is the astroid Zoya, named after the Kosmodemyanskaya Zone.

In recent years, astrophysicists have been talking about the importance of studying the substance of astronauts and speaking about them from astronauts.

The invasion of the Earth, especially the earth's crust, has undergone numerous transformations (melting, volcanic processes and others), which have changed its original appearance. The moon has its own special history, which has not yet been solved.

There is a belief that the invasion of astroids, on the contrary, did not melt and retained the peculiarities of past eras. Then you can use it to learn about the initial stages of the development of the solar system 4 - 5 billion years ago - the era of planetary revolution.

Astroids can also be found in practical applications - the force of gravity on these small planets is negligible, they could serve as take-off planes for large spaceships of the future.

But there are also such planets, sometimes very large, ten times larger than Earth, which cannot be distinguished even with the best telescope. These are planets of other worlds, they revolve not around the Sun, but around other luminaries - distant stars. The distances to the nearest stars - several light years - are thousands of times greater than the distance from Earth to the most distant planet in our solar system - Pluto. Light travels 300,000 km per second, in a year this amounts to 9.46·10¹² km, and to Pluto it is “only” 5.9·10⁹ km. This article will tell you how to identify these planets and determine their parameters.

Asteroids

The idea of ​​flying to the astroids and the conditions on these small planets was developed in detail by the pioneer of astronautics K. E. Tsiolkovsky 50 years ago. Tsiolkovsky was attracted by the previous autumn's low force of gravity, which facilitated the landing and take-off of a spacecraft and the movement of a person on the surface of the astroid. Let us take, like Tsiolkovsky, as an example an astroid of 6 km, which is about 2000 times smaller than the Earth’s astroid. There are several thousand such planets in the astroid order.

Let's see what the gravity is on such an astroid. Gravity acceleration $g = \gamma M/R^2$, where $M = \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3$ is the mass of the planet, $\rho$ is the density, $R$ is the radius, $\gamma = 6,68 \cdot 10^{-11} m^3/кгс^2$ is the universal gravitational constant. Or $g = \frac{4\pi}{3}\gamma\rho R$. Just as Tsiolkovsky did, they use an astroid equal to $\rho = 5,3 \times 10^3$ kg/m³, that is, almost equal to the average density of the Earth ($\rho_3 = 5,5 \times 10^3$ kg/m³). This value depends somewhat on the existing core, like the Earth, and their average density is about $3 \cdot 10^3$ kg/m³. But for rough estimates this.res is significant. Acceleration $g$ is proportional to the radius $R$, which means that on the astroid the acceleration of gravity is approximately 2000 times less than on Earth: $g = 4,9 \cdot 10^{-3} m/c^2$.

Planets of other stars

Planets of other stars are not visible in telescopes. Nevertheless, these planets were discovered.

The star is clearly visible, and the motion of the star is influenced by its gravity by its invisible satellite - the planet. Planets are detected by star deviations from linear motion. External forces on the star system - the planet - do not act (other stars are far away), so the center of mass moves clearly in a straight line and, moreover, evenly. The experimental difficulty of detecting planets is shown by the example of Barnard's star.

There was a star near our Sun that was very suitable for its planets. This is Barnard's Star, named after the amateur astronomer who discovered it. This star is faint, but it moves at a fairly high speed - 10.3″ per year (1/360 degree). Its distance from Earth is 5.9 light years. Closer to it is only a double star in the constellation Centaurus.
