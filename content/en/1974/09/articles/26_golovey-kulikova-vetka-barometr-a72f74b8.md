⟦folio 75⟧

Weather conditions play a huge role in human life, so it is very important to know in advance about all expected weather changes.

Now in all countries there are meteorological stations that monitor the state of the atmosphere (the amount of precipitation, wind force and air pressure at various altitudes are measured) and make weather forecasts. When making an accurate forecast for a given area, it is necessary to take into account a large number of different factors affecting the state of the atmosphere of the entire Earth. This complex problem has not yet been fully resolved. This method of determining the weather (it is called synoptic) allows for some inaccuracies in the forecasts. Biophysical observations of nature can reduce these uncertainties.

Data on changes in the behavior of animals and plants before a change in weather has been published and used in backgammon to predict various atmospheric phenomena. For example, it was noticed that if seagulls fly far out to sea early in the morning, there will be no strong wind until the evening. And, conversely, a massive return of birds to the shore from the sea precedes the approach of a storm. Another example is that long before the storm begins, jellyfish rush to hide in a safe place in advance. It turns out that the signal for this is infrasounds with a frequency of 3-13 frequencies, which arose from the tremors of air waves. Intense infrasound oscillations that form wave crests above the sea surface in strong winds as a result of vortex processes propagate faster than the storm front. Jellyfish perceive these vibrations. As a result of studying this phenomenon, a device was designed that allows one to determine the direction of the storm and its strength long before it begins (about 15 hours).

As a result of numerous observations, it has been established that plants are extremely sensitive to air pressure, temperature and humidity. For example, coniferous trees usually lower their branches in front of jams and raise them up before clear weather. Spruce has this feature to a large extent; Observed tourists can determine the upcoming weather based on the condition of its roof.

The ability to respond to weather changes is retained in a non-living tree. This property can be used to make a homemade barometer. To do this, remove a dry or branch from a piece of trunk. The dynamism of the branch at the base should be 10-15 mm, length 30-50 cm. Clear the resulting suokok from the bark and attach it to the wall by the rest of the trunk so that the branch is in approximately the same position as on the tree. Now all that remains is to make a scale for the absence of the position of the end of the branch, and the barometer will be ready. Cut out a sector from paper or cardboard with a radius equal to the length of the branch and a rotation angle of 90°. For ease of absence, mark divisions every 5 mm on the circumference of the sector. The spruce branch will act as an arrow, the position of which depends on the state of the atmosphere. By making several observations depending on the weather conditions, one can make a forecast with this homemade barometer.

We recommend that you not only observe, but also make daily notes, noting the position of the branch and the weather condition in the table. Such a table will help you make more accurate weather forecasts and its strength long before it begins (about 15 hours in advance).
