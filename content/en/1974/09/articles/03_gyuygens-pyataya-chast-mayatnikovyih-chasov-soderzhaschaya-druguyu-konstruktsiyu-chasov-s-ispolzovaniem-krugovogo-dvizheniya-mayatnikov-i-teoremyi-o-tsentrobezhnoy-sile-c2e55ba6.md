Fifth part
"Pendulum Clock", containing a different clock design using the circular motion of pendulums, and the theorem on centering force
H. Huyens

...I had the intention of producing a description of this clock together with the theories relating to circular motion and to the centering force, as I want to call it. But on this subject I have more material than time to expound it at the present moment. But in order for those interested in this issue to quickly become acquainted with a new, by no means useless discovery, so that any accident does not prevent publication, I, contrary to my initial assumption, also added this part to the previous ones. It briefly describes the design of the new watch and then follows theorems on centering force; their proof is postponed to a later time.

CONSTRUCTION OF THE SECOND WATCH
I did not consider it necessary to describe here the distribution of the wheels of the internal mechanism; This device can easily be made into watches in various versions. It will be sufficient to describe that part of the clock which regulates its course in a determined manner.
The next figure will depict this part of the clock.

The DH axis should be imagined as vertical, capable of rotating in two bearings. In A, a plate is attached to the axis, having a certain width and curved along the curve AB, which is the line BAE obtained in 紊 and a parabola EF is formed. BCF is a thread attached to the curve AB, the end of which describes a paraboly.

A group F is attached to the thread. If the axis DH rotates, then the thread BCF, extended into a straight line, will entail a group F, which will describe horizontal circles. These circles will be larger or smaller, depending on the greater or lesser force with which they act on the axis of the wheel rotating the drum K. But all these circles will lie on a parabolic conid and that is why the duration of one revolution will always be the same, as follows from the fact that I will announce this movement later. If the revolution is to be completed in half a second, then the parameter of the parabola EF must be $4^{1/2}$ inch of wash fugue, that is, it must be equal to half the length of the pendulum, of which each oscillation is $1/2$ seconds long. From the parameter of the parabola, the parameter of the resulting parabola is determined; it is equal to $27/16$ of the first parameter; The segment AE is also determined, which is equal to half the length of the parabola EF. If the revolution must be completed per second, then the length must be taken four times larger, both the parameters and the length AE.

THEOREMS ABOUT CENTRIGAL FORCE CAUSED BY CIRCULAR MOTION*)

If two identical bodies describe unequal circles at the same time, then their central forces are related as the lengths of the circles or as the diameters.

If two identical bodies move at the same speed along the circumference of different circles, then their central forces are inversely proportional to the diameters.
