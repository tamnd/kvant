Scientific conference for students of specialized boarding schools

For the second year in a row, studying at the Tbilisi Secondary Physics and Mathematics Boarding School named after. V. M. Komarova holds a scientific conference during the spring holidays. This year, students from secondary school No. 7 in Batumi, Yerevan Physics and Mathematics Boarding School, specialized boarding school of Physics and Mathematics in Kyiv, Physics and Mathematics Boarding School No. 18 in Moscow, Physics and Mathematics Boarding School No. 165 in Novosibirsk and secondary school No. 52 in Riggi. Students from several schools in Tbilisi also took part in the conference. Essentially, it was a gathering of students from physics and mathematics intern schools.

The grand opening of the conference took place on March 26 in the premises of the Computing Center of the Georgian USSR Academy of Sciences. On the same day, the mathematics and physics sections began working. Schoolchildren heard a number of reports on mathematics and physics. From March 26 to March 30, the focused work of the conference took place.

Matematika
Although most of the reports were abstract in nature, they were listened to with great interest by the conference participants. Let us note the neo-octodes from these reports.

K. Tatishvili (Tbilisi, 9th grade) spoke about the outstanding Georgian scientist, one of the founders of Tbilisi State University, a remarkable mathematician and patriot of his country, Andree Mikhailovitsa Razmadze (1890-1929), who created the modern Georgian mathematical school.

E. Bekaya (Batumi, 10th grade) spoke about the life of the Great Russian mathematician Pafnutya Lvovich Chebyshev (1821-1894) and about his solution to the following problem: to find a polynomial $T(n)$ of the nth degree with a coefficient of 1 at the leading term, which least of all deviates from zero on the segment $[1, -1]$, that is, such a polynomial $p(x)$ for which $|p(x)|$ is smallest compared to $|x| \leq 1$

all other polynomials of the same degree with coefficient 1 for the leading term. These polynomials have the form

$$T_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} \cos(n \arccos x).$$

Now they are called Chebyshev polynomials. To write them down explicitly, you can use the formula

$$T_{n+1}(x) = 2x T_n(x) - T_{n-1}(x)$$

and the first two Chebyshev polynomials:

$$T_1(x) = x, T_2(x) = 2x^2 - 1.$$

Try to prove formula (2) based on formula (1).

The report by G. Tod (Tbilisi, 9th grade) was devoted to construction with compasses and a ruler, as well as solving the problem of “doubling the cube” using special devices invented by Plato, Menech, Eratothenes and other ancient scientists.

M. Torond Zhadze (Tbilisi, 9th grade) spoke about Peano curves (this report was published in Kvanita, 1974, No. 8).

The report by M. Drachinsky (Tbilisi, 10th grade) talked about integration methods. Integration is estimating the value of functions at some point if the values ​​of this function at several ($n$) points are known.

The desired value is taken to be equal to the value at the same point of the polynomial (Lagrange polynomial), which at the given $n$ points takes the same values ​​as the function under consideration. The speaker talked about the Lagrange polynomial and Newton's interpolation polynomial, which coincides with the Lagrange polynomial, but is constructed (from finite differences) much faster.

In the report of A. Timoshin (Rigga, 9th grade) “Some methods of proving inequalities,” Cauchy’s inequality was proved

$$\frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n} \geq \sqrt[n]{x_1 x_2 \dots x_n}$$

(all $x_i \geq 0$) and Bernoulli's inequality

$$1 + a^n > 1 + na$$

($a \neq 0, a > -1, n \geq 2$).

Gedalin (Tbilisi, 8th grade) read the report “On one minimal property of a triangle.” In this report, two proofs of the following fact were given: among all the triangles inscribed in a given acute triangle, the triangle with the most perimeter is the one whose vertex is the base of the altitudes of the given triangle.

V. Kharlanov (Kyiv, 10th grade) read the report “Some problems about trees in graph theory.” A tree is a connected graph without cycles (closed paths) and loops (graphs have already been discussed in our journal - see «Kvant», 1973, No. 8, p. 55). Vladimir proved several theorems, for example: in a tree, every two vertices connected by a letter are one chain, the number of trees built on n vertices is equal to $n^{n-2}$ and others, and then posed several problems. Try, for example, to determine

1) how many trees with m edges can be built on n vertices;

2) How many graphs without cycles with m edges can be built on n vertices.

Physics

The reports on physics were distinguished by great diversity both in the essence of the issues considered and in the form of the work done.

The topics of work discussed by the conference participants go far beyond the scope of a school physics course. Only the middle theoretical training, the help of scientific supervisors (they, as a rule, were researchers from universities and physics research institutes), the opportunity to conduct experiments in scientific laboratories and, of course, a huge interest in physics allowed the guys to achieve success.

An interesting report was made by P.I. Vanov (Novosibirsk, 10th grade) “Tunable dye lasers,” which talked about the design of a liquid laser with smooth frequency tuning, created by a group of students in the laboratory of the Novosibirsk Institute of Nuclear Physics.

In the laboratory of technical measurements of the scientific and technical society of students in the city of Knev, for many years, research has been carried out to create electrical devices that can be used in school physics experiments. V. Drachev (Novosibirsk, 9th grade) in his report “Fish-Eye Camera” spoke about a device he made for taking photographs with a viewing angle close to 180°.

One should not think that at the conference, schoolchildren served or read reports from morning to evening. The hosts organize very interesting expertise for the guests in Tbilisi and its surroundings - in Jvari, Mtskheta and Sagurami, as well as in the house-museum of I. G. Chavchavadze.

On March 29, a meeting was held between the conference participants and the editors of our magazine. Deputy editor-in-chief M. L. Smolyansky spoke to the schoolchildren. He spoke about the work of the editors, about the magazine’s plans for 1974-1975. Schoolchildren took an active part in the meeting. They expressed their opinions about the journal, about individual articles, and talked about which areas of mathematics and physics they were especially interested in. Then the editorial staff answered numerous questions from schoolchildren.

On the morning of March 30, a “blitzpolympada” lesson in mathematics was held. Here are some of the problems proposed in this competition.

1. Divide the clock face into three clocks by two faces so that the sums of the numbers in each clock are the same.

2. What's more: ate or ate?

3. Is it possible to use five bronze coins (no. 1, 3 and 5 kopecks) to deduct the amount of 16 kopecks?

4. There are 70 balls in a box: 20 red, 20 yellow, 20 green and 10 black. How many balls must be subtracted from the box (in the dark!) so that there are at least 10 one-ball balls?

The closing ceremony of the conference took place on the afternoon of March 30. The guests thanked the hosts for their hospitality, and the hosts invited everyone present to the next conference.

What can I say in conclusion?

Our magazine has already written about the following schools with a physical and mathematical bias (see «Kvant», 1972, No. 2, 5). These rallies have been held regularly since 1971. It is gratifying that now students of physics and mathematics boarding schools have organized their own scientific conferences, which are very interesting and meaningful.

We are confident that in the near future, conferences of physics and mathematics boarding schools will gain credibility and will be held not only in Tbilisi, but also, say, in Novosibirsk, Pira and other cities of our country. We wish them further success.
