Lucky tickets

1. Let's call a bus ticket “lucky” if the sum of the digits of its six-digit number is written in the last two digits, taken in the same order. For example, lucky tickets with numbers 728124, 032419. How many lucky tickets are there in the first million, that is, from numbers 000,000 to 999,999?

V

If a body moves along the circumference of a circle with the same speed that it would travel, freely falling from a height of 1/4 of the diameter of the circle, then we experience a central force equal to its weight, that is, it pulls the thread with which it is attached to the center with the same force as if it were suspended from the thread.

[If the height is $H = R/2$, then for the final speed during free fall we have: $v = \sqrt{2gH} = \sqrt{Rg}$, and for the indicated central force we have:

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{R} = \frac{mRg}{R} = mg.$$]

VI

If a body runs through various horizontal circles, which all lie on the curved surface of a parabolic end (parabolon) with a vertical axis, then the time of revolution is always the same, whether the circles are larger or smaller, and this time of revolution is twice the duration of the oscillation of a pendulum, the length of which is equal to half the revolution parameter of the parabola.

2. We write the six-digit bus ticket number $N$ in the form $N = 1000A + B$, where $A$ and $B$ are three-digit numbers.

Let's call a ticket with number $N$ “twice lucky” if the sum of the digits of the number $A$ is equal to the sum of the digits of the number $B$ and the product of the digits of the number $A$ is equal to the product of the digits of the number $B$. Are there non-trivialable double lucky tickets? We will call three-lucky tickets such double-lucky tickets in which in the number $N$ the number $B$ is obtained from $A$ by rearranging the digits, as well as tickets in which there are zeros in the number entry.
