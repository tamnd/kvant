Series too small

We will discuss the mathematical part of the series “Library of the Physics and Mathematics School” published by the mass production company “Nuka” (editor: Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. M. Gelfand).

To date, 8 books “Library of the Physics and Mathematics School” have been published (editor: Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. M. Gelfand).

To date, 8 books “Library of the Physics and Mathematics School” have been published (editor: Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. M. Gelfand).

Turning to the contents of the individual books in the series, starting with the easiest-to-solve brochures 1* and 3*: “Mathematical Problems” and “Modern Mathematical Problems.” The very names of these books fully reveal their character: these are simply collections of (rather heterogeneous in topics and methods of solution) problems offered at various competitions in mass specialized “mathematical” schools; at the end of the brochures there are collected solutions (or instructions for solving) all the problems of the “main part” of the books without exception. In addition, each of the books ends with a section “Additional problems”, the solutions to which are not indicated.

The high level of the task and the careful preparation of answers and instructions is guaranteed by the very list of authors, including well-known mathematicians who have been working in “mathematical” schools for many years. To solve most of the problems in issues 1* and 3* of the “Library”, they are carried out quite significantly in the scope of the mathematics course of the first eight years of high school. In terms of the nature of the problems, they are in many ways close to those published in the “Problem Book «Kvant»”, being, however, on average somewhat simpler. The second one seems to me to be the more successful of these two brochures, partly this is explained by the subtitle “Arithmetic and Algebra” of issue 3*, or in general the geometric part of the collection 1* seems to be the most complex.

In this regard, we can only hope that the compiler of the “additional” series of “Libraries” to lovers of geometry LINK sees some “debt” in the continuation of issue 3* and the soon to be published issue 4*: N. B. Vasiliev, S. A. Molchanov, A. L. Rosenal, A. P. Savin, Mathematical modern (geometry).

Let us now move on to the “most basic” part of the series, represented by issues 1, 2 and 5. Despite the fact that here, of course, problems and exercises constitute an essential component of the text (but how can one learn mathematics without problems?), yet the main part of these three issues are educational materials of a theoretical nature. The connection between these three books (especially the first two of them) with the mathematical school existing at Moscow University is obvious: these issues set out the ZMS course, including information of a theoretical nature and “control tasks” offered to students to check the degree of mastery of the material (these tasks formed the basis of the brochures accompanying each section, exercises for independent solution).

Written at a high scientific and methodological level, devoted to the most important issues of a general mathematical nature, these brochures enjoy well-deserved popularity and, undoubtedly, have a significant impact on mathematics-intensive schoolchildren. Named because the author evaluates these brochures quite highly, even we are not talking about their indisputable merits, but about the fact that the author is not completely satisfied with them.

First of all, I am not satisfied with the number of brochures.
