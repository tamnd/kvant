We were talking about the next task. There is a physical mathematician - a figure suspended at one point and oscillating. Its reduced length is the length of a mathematician having the same period of oscillation. Let us draw a segment of this length through the center of gravity of the matrix, starting at the suspension point. Then the other end of the segment is called the swing center. (Mersenne began by trying to answer the question of how a sword should be used in order for the blow to be most effective. If we consider the sword to be a segment that the “warrior” holds by the end A, then it turns out that the center of swing is $\frac{2}{3}$ length away from A.

Mersenne believed that the optimal point coincides with the center of swing and is far from the center of gravity.

Mersenne's opponent Baldi believed that it was necessary to be the place closest to the center of gravity.) In the problems proposed by Mersenne to Huygens, we were talking about the centers of swing of circular sectors, segments, isosceles triangles, suspended in different ways. Huygens wrote: “...at that time I did not find what would allow me to begin calculations, and, as it were, turned back at the very threshold and refrained from any research. The reason for a new set of experiments for the adjustable matrices of our watches, equipped, in addition to the lower constant weight, with a second movable weight, as stated in the description of the watch.

Based on this, I started the research again, this time with the best one. From this I not only had the pleasure that I had found something that so many people had been looking for and understood the laws of nature that apply to this case, but I also received a certain benefit that made me take up this issue in general, namely, I found an easy and convenient way to adjust the clock. “I found this rule in 1664,” Huygens noted in Mersenne’s letter stating the problem.
