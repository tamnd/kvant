F252. A kanam is wound on the cylindrical one. We hit one of the ends of the rope with a force of $F$. In order for the kanam not to slide along the pole, when one strand of the rope is wound tightly around it, the second rope needs to be held at the end of the rope, if $n$ of its strands are wound on it? How will the force $f$ change if we take such an input larger than the radius? (The strength of $f$ does not depend on the strength of the rope.)

The force $f$ can depend only on the diameter of the pole $d$, the coefficient of friction $\mu$ between the pole and the rope and on the magnitude of the force $F$. However, from the dimension messages it can be concluded that

$$f \sim F.$$

Indeed, it is impossible to select a combination of $F$, $\mu$ and $d$ such that it has the dimension of force.

Thus,

$$f = kF,$$

where the proportionality coefficient $k$ is related to the coefficient of friction between the rope and the pillar.

From this formula it immediately follows that for any change in the radius, the force $f$ remains the same.

In order to answer the first question of the problem, consider the second spool of the rope. For him, the role of force $F$ is played by force $f$, therefore

$$f_2 = kf,$$

where $f_2$ is the force that must be applied to the free end of the rope when two winks are wound around it. For the third circuit

$$f_3 = kf_2 = k^2f$$

and so on.

For the $n$th turn we get

$$f_n = kf_{n-1} = k^2f_{n-2} = \dots = k^{n-1}f.$$

From the first equality we find the coefficient $k$:

$$k = \frac{f}{F}.$$

Hence

$$f_n = k^{n-1}f = \left(\frac{f}{F}\right)^{n-1}f.$$
