Crushed drop

Analogy is born at the crossroads of memory and reflection and sometimes links together images and events that are very distantly related. An unexpected analogy, even a separate or superficial one, born at the right time, can help break the deadlock and illuminate the path to a solution.

Once upon a time, in the late 40s, I participated in experimental work. Its goal was to determine the physical characteristics of a substance that had not previously been studied. Previously, this substance simply did not exist in its pure form; chemists obtained it at the cost of great effort.

At first glance, the problem is not at all new, and it should be solved by marveling at the paths trodden by many researchers who studied the physical characteristics of other substances. Our task, however, was complicated by the fact that we could only experiment with microsopp grains. Each grain weighed about one millionth of a gram, and its size was several ten microns. We were very limited in the number of grains - chemists added them with difficulty.

The group I worked with had to determine the melting point and surface tension of a substance in the liquid phase.

In a conventional “macrosopsis” experiment, changing the melting temperature is easy and simple: a thermometer is immersed in the sample and monitored as its readings change as the sample is heated. the temperature gradually increases. When it reaches a certain value, its growth will stop due to the fact that the heat flowing to the sample begins to be spent not on heating, but on the melting process.
