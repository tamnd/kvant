The treatment term is the concept of a cellulum number, and the species difference is the property of being divisible by 2 (without a remainder).

In the case of a theorem, the naming of the parts is different; it is best considered using the example of a theorem with a one-way arrow (see (9) or (10)). The first part is the explanatory part of the theorem. The second and third parts (coming immediately before and after the ⇒ sign) are called the corresponding and conclusion of the theorem. The explanatory part describes its circumstances that must take place in order for the theorem to be formed, and the notations that are used in its formulation. In theorems (9), (10) (as well as in each of theorems (2)—(8)), the explanatory part indicates that an arbitrary integer is considered, the vertices of which are designated by the letters A, B, C, D. The theorem as a whole states that if (with the notation described in the explanatory part) the conditions of the theorem are satisfied, then its conclusion is valid.

Note that to obtain the converse theorem, it is necessary to keep the same explanatory part, and place the conditions and conclusion in places (see (9) and (10)). Note also that in theorems expressing necessary and sufficient conditions (for example, (2)–(8)), the conditions and conclude are equivalent, that is, they can be swapped without changing the meaning of the theorem.

Using these theorems as an example, it is easy to see how important it is to correctly formulate the explanatory part - especially when moving on to the converse theorem. Let us take as an example theorem (13), which we write in the form of two theorems, direct and converse:

$$\forall \square ABCD) (ABCD — ромб) \Rightarrow (AC \perp BD);$$
