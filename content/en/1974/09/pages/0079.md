16. Let $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots = S$. The series obtained after rearranging the terms $a_n$ will be denoted by $b_1 + b_2 + b_3 + \dots$, and its partial sums by $T_n$.

Let everything be $a_n \geq 0$ first. Then the theorem is obvious: $T_n \leq S$, whence lim $T_n$ exists and is equal to $T \leq S$. But the series $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots$ is in turn obtained by permuting from $b_1 + b_2 + b_3 + \dots$, so $S \leq T$, that is, $S = T$. The theorem is clearer if everything is $a_n \leq 0$.

18. According to A17a, 6, for the series $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots$ to converge, it is necessary and sufficient to satisfy the following condition: for any $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a $k$ such that for any $n > k$ and any $p$ $|a_{n+1} + \dots + a_{n+p}| < \varepsilon$.

For $p = 1$ we obtain from this that lim $a_n = 0$. The last condition is necessary, but, of course, not sufficient for the convergence of the series (for example, it is satisfied for the harmonic series $1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n} + \dots$, and yet this series diverges).
