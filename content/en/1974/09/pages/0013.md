2. Let there be some bracing surface (for Huygen, a parabolide is the bracing surface of the parabola $py = x^2$ around the axis $y$, Fig. 2). A heavy material point of sources is braced along a horizontal section (circle), if the force equal to all and centering is directed normal to the surface (perpendicular to the tangent plane), and then the formula for the final matrix is ​​applied here. In this case, $\alpha$ is the angle of the normal with the axis, $l$ is the length of the normal segment between the axis and the surface, $u$ is the projection of this segment onto the axis. Here the transition from a finite matrix to the movement of a tension point is to some extent analogous to Halley’s transition from a mathematical matrix to the movement of a tension point along a circular iron. Next, Huygens replaces that the parabola $py = x^2$ has magnitudes $u$ (projection of the normal segment onto the axis)

does not depend on the position of the point and is equal to $\frac{p}{2}$. From this he concludes that the period of bracing of the traction point along any horizontal sections of the parabolic is the same:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{p}{2g}}.$$

This follows from (1) and provides a new way to obtain ischronic oscillations, which was so important when constructing clocks. If you hang the final matrix so that, regardless of the angle $\alpha$ of the inclination of the thread to the axis, its end moves along the surface of the parabola obtained from bracing the parabola $py = x^2$, then the bracing period will not depend on $\alpha$. In other words, it is necessary to make sure that when $\alpha$ changes, the length $l$ changes, ensuring the constancy of the projection $u$ on the axis. Huygens came up with an extremely ingenious method of suspension. He proposed making a plate according to the formula of a ubiquitous parabola $y^2 = ax^3 + b$, enclosing the end of the thread at a certain point, and then, it turns out, we can choose $a, b$ and the length of the thread in such a way that no matter how we stretch the thread, winding part of it on the plate, the other end will be on the parabola. The secret of this ingenious method of suspension is based on the same mathematical considerations as the method of suspension of the cyclondal matrix.
