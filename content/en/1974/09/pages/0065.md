M. Gedalin (Tbilisi, 8th grade) read the report “On one minimal property of a triangle.” In this report, two proofs of the following fact were given: among all the triangles inscribed in a given acute triangle, the triangle with the most perimeter is the one whose vertex is the base of the altitudes of the given triangle.

V. Kharlanov (Kyiv, 10th grade) read the report “Some problems about trees in graph theory.” A tree is a connected graph without cycles (closed paths) and loops (graphs have already been discussed in our journal - see «Kvant», 1973, No. 8, p. 55). Vladimir proved several theorems, for example: in a tree, every two vertices connected by a letter are one chain, the number of trees built on n vertices is equal to $n^{n-2}$ and others, and then posed several problems. Try, for example, to determine

1) how many trees with m edges can be built on n vertices;

2) How many graphs without cycles with m edges can be built on n vertices.

The report by V. Fedorov (Moscow, 8th grade) “Systems of countable sets” was devoted mainly to solving the following three problems: does a non-supported set A exist of a subset of a countable set Q such that

1) For all $x$, $y \in A$ the set $x \cap y$ is finite if $x \neq y$?

2) For all $x$, $y \in A$ the set $x \Delta y$ is finite ($x \Delta y = (x \cup y) \setminus (x \cap y)$?

3) For all $x$, $y \in A$ the set $x \subset y$ is what $y \subset x$?

A. Voronov (Novosibirsk, 10th grade) in his report “Some information about factorial rings” described an algorithm for decomposing a polynomial over a ring of integers into non-conductible polynomials. The head of the section corrected his report, but the schoolchildren understood almost nothing, which is not surprising - understanding this report requires knowledge of the basics of algebra.

The same can be said about the report by A. And again (Moscow, 10th grade) “On the expansion of the polynomial $x^3 + y^3 + z^3$ in a ring satisfying the condition of equivalence of both left and right inversion.”

Physics

The reports on physics were distinguished by great diversity both in the essence of the issues considered and in the form of the work done.

The topics of work discussed by the conference participants go far beyond the scope of a school physics course. Only the middle theoretical training, the help of scientific supervisors (they, as a rule, were researchers from universities and physics research institutes), the opportunity to conduct experiments in scientific laboratories and, of course, a huge interest in physics allowed the guys to achieve success.
