⟦folio 8⟧

Sketch of a clock with a cyclondal pendulum made by Huygens.

this title does not fully reflect its content. The actual design of the pendulum clock is described only in the first of five parts.

Huygens considered his main achievement to be the invention of the cyclondal pendulum. Firstly, Huygens proved that if the end of a pendulum moves along a cyclon*) (and not along a circle, as in the case of a mathematical pendulum), then its oscillations are isochronous. Secondly, he came up with a very ingenious way of suspending a pendulum, ensuring the movement of its end along a cyclonde: he placed the suspension between two plates (sheaves), bent along a cyclonde, onto which part of the thread is wound when the pendulum is deflected. The largest part of the book (the second and third parts) is related to the Cyklondal pendulum, where the mechanical and mathematical statements necessary to justify the isochronism of the Cyklondal pendulum and the rules for its suspension are proven. Huygens writes: “To carry out these proofs, it was necessary to strengthen and, where necessary, additional the teaching of the great Galileo about the fall of bodies. The most desirable fruit, as if the great pinnacle of this teaching, is the discovered multiproperty of Cyklonda.”

The fourth part of the book “On the center of swing” begins: “When I was still almost a boy (he was not 17 years old - S.G.), the most learned man Mersenne asked me and many others the task of determining the center of swing. From the letters that Mersenne wrote to me, as well as from the recently published topics of Descartes, concluding a response to Mersenne’s letters on this subject, I conclude that this task was in use at that time.”

*) The cycle is described by a fixed point on the boundary of a circle that rolls without sliding in a straight line. Mathematicians in the 17th century discovered many remarkable properties that this curve possesses.
