Scientific conference for students of specialized boarding schools

For the second year in a row, studying at the Tbilisi Secondary Physics and Mathematics Boarding School named after. V. M. Komarova holds a scientific conference during the spring holidays. This year, students from secondary school No. 7 in Batumi, Yerevan Physics and Mathematics Boarding School, specialized boarding school of Physics and Mathematics in Kyiv, Physics and Mathematics Boarding School No. 18 in Moscow, Physics and Mathematics Boarding School No. 165 in Novosibirsk and secondary school No. 52 in Riggi. Students from several schools in Tbilisi also took part in the conference. Essentially, it was a gathering of students from physics and mathematics intern schools.

The grand opening of the conference took place on March 26 in the premises of the Computing Center of the Georgian USSR Academy of Sciences. On the same day, the mathematics and physics sections began working. Schoolchildren heard a number of reports on mathematics and physics. From March 26 to March 30, the focused work of the conference took place.

Matematika
Although most of the reports were abstract in nature, they were listened to with great interest by the conference participants. Let us note the neo-octodes from these reports.

K. Tatishvili (Tbilisi, 9th grade) spoke about the outstanding Georgian scientist, one of the founders of Tbilisi State University, a remarkable mathematician and patriot of his country, Andree Mikhailovitsa Razmadze (1890-1929), who created the modern Georgian mathematical school.

E. Bekaya (Batumi, 10th grade) spoke about the life of the Great Russian mathematician Pafnutya Lvovich Chebyshev (1821-1894) and about his solution to the following problem: to find a polynomial $T(n)$ of the nth degree with a coefficient of 1 at the leading term, which least of all deviates from zero on the segment $[1, -1]$, that is, such a polynomial $p(x)$ for which $|p(x)|$ is smallest compared to $|x| \leq 1$

all other polynomials of the same degree with coefficient 1 for the leading term. These polynomials have the form

$$T_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} \cos(n \arccos x).$$

Now they are called Chebyshev polynomials. To write them down explicitly, you can use the formula

$$T_{n+1}(x) = 2x T_n(x) - T_{n-1}(x)$$

and the first two Chebyshev polynomials:

$$T_1(x) = x, T_2(x) = 2x^2 - 1.$$

Try to prove formula (2) based on formula (1).

The report by G. Tod (Tbilisi, 9th grade) was devoted to construction with compasses and a ruler, as well as solving the problem of “doubling the cube” using special devices invented by Plato, Menech, Eratothenes and other ancient scientists.

M. Torond Zhadze (Tbilisi, 9th grade) spoke about Peano curves (this report was published in Kvanita, 1974, No. 8).

The report by M. Drachinsky (Tbilisi, 10th grade) talked about integration methods. Integration is estimating the value of functions at some point if the values ​​of this function at several ($n$) points are known.

The desired value is taken to be equal to the value at the same point of the polynomial (Lagrange polynomial), which at the given $n$ points takes the same values ​​as the function under consideration. The speaker talked about the Lagrange polynomial and Newton's interpolation polynomial, which coincides with the Lagrange polynomial, but is constructed (from finite differences) much faster.

In the report of A. Timoshin (Rigga, 9th grade) “Some methods of proving inequalities,” Cauchy’s inequality was proved

$$\frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n} \geq \sqrt[n]{x_1 x_2 \dots x_n}$$

(all $x_i \geq 0$) and Bernoulli's inequality

$$1 + a^n > 1 + na$$

($a \neq 0, a > -1, n \geq 2$).
