To facilitate understanding, let us make two preliminary remarks.

1. Whatever problem Heugens was working on, he always thought about possible applications of obtaining results to watches. And in this case he wanted to use the Konec matrix. This is the name of a thread with a group that rotates around an axis passing through the suspension point (Fig. 1). Let $l$ be the length of the thread, $\alpha$ the angle of the thread with the vertical, $R$ the distance from the load to the axis. If the magnet moves in a circle and the angle $\alpha$ remains constant, then $\frac{mv^2}{R} = mgtg\alpha$. Hence $v = \sqrt{gRtg\alpha}$. For the period—temporary turnover—we obtain (since $T = 2\pi R/v$):

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{R}{g}}ctg\alpha = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l\cos\alpha}{g}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{u}{g}}.$$ (1)

Here $u = l\cos\alpha$ is the length of the projection of the thread onto the magnet axis.

There are numerous discussions of the formula for the finite matrix period in the Heuguens text. The movement of the finite matrix is ​​compared with two movements that had been thoroughly studied by that time: with a free pad and oscillations of a simple, or mathematical, matrix (Geugens calls its oscillations lateral, in contrast to the circular oscillations of the finite matrix).
