SPRING DROPS

Many physical phenomena are associated with spring drops. For example, we can consider the pattern of formation of a “droplet” shape, changing in perfection and beauty, prepared from a melting icicle. I can’t explain why, but the shape of the swelling drop seems to me the height of geometric perfection. Only the sphere can compare with it in beauty and logical pattern of form. You can consider the reflections of the sun living on the surface of a drop that swells at the tip of an icicle. The glare fluctuates in the rhythm of the breathing of a swelling drop. You can consider the spring zone, which, according to the poet, accompanies the flight of an icicle from winter to spring. The sound of a drop sounds in many poetic and musical lines, and, of course, one should talk about the acoustics of a drop hitting the surface of water or ice, covered with a layer of water. The drop breaks into small fragments, and each of the fragments adds sound to the spring ringing.
