A.K. KIKOIN
FREE FALL OF BODIES
ON
ROTATING EARTH

Sometimes you can come across a statement that, due to the fact that the Earth rotates around its axis, the softness force acting on a body differs in different places on the Earth’s surface. They also say that body weight varies in different places on Earth. Both this and other connections are due to the fact that in different places on the rotating Earth the acceleration of the free fall of the body is different. Is all this true? In this article we will try to answer this question.

1. Mechanical motion, as we know, is relative. This means that if we use different reporting systems, then relative to these systems the movement of the same body turns out to be different. If, for example, we associate a coordinate system with an airplane flying at a constant speed in the horizontal direction, then in this coordinate system the cargo dropped (or rather, fallen) from the airplane moves down along a vertical straight line (Fig. 1). But in a coordinate system associated with some point on the Earth’s surface, for example, with point O (see Fig. 2), the same load moves along a curve called a parabola. Consequently, the trajectories of movement are different. The speeds of movement are also different. If we conventionally consider the coordinate system associated with the Earth to be stationary, then with the speed of movement of the aircraft (and the associated coordinate system) relative to the Earth equal to $v_c$, the speed $v$ of the load relative to the Earth at any time is equal to

$$v = v_c + v_r,$$
