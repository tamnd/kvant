Methodological manual for the section “Inequalities”?

Currently, many publishing houses in our country publish literature on mathematics intended for those entering higher education institutions. These books and brochures pursue mainly two goals - purely informational and methodological.

From a book published by a particular institute, an applicant learns the options for written work offered to those entering this institute and becomes familiar with solutions to typical problems. This helps him more correctly guide the choice of his future specialty, and judge the level of requirements for applicants. In addition to these reference materials, such books, as a rule, also have sections that cover issues that are most difficult for applicants. These sections are essentially methodological guides to the most important sections of the entrance exam program.

And if the only requirement for reference materials can be, please, only the correctness of the answers or solutions, then the methodological sections must satisfy much more high requirements.

Unfortunately, not all publishing houses treat books intended for applicants with due responsibility. In Europe, the methodological manuals produced by immi suffer from server-side deficiencies. Meanwhile, it is absolutely clear that an unsuccessful benefit may turn out to be at least useless.

Recently, the Saratov Economic Institute published a small book intended to examine inequalities, and this material, as the author indicates in the preface, allows us to recommend it “as a methodological guide to the section “Inequalities”.” Meanwhile, familiarization with the book shows that in no case can one agree with this opinion of the author - there are so many sloppy formulations, unreasonable recommendations and outright errors in it.

There is a well-known “classic misunderstanding: many applicants believe that before solving an equation, one must find its range of acceptable values. However, one can easily give examples where the preliminary equality is unnecessary from a logical point of view and, in addition, introduces the values ​​$\frac{3x-1}{2-x} \geq 0$ and then $\frac{3x-1}{2-x} > 1$, which is at least uneconomical. It is very strange that even in those cases when the author uses the equivalence of equalities, he still looks for ODZ without explaining why (see pp. 47-49).
