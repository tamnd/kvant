Mihanl Vasilyevich Lomonosov (1711-1765)… In all areas of science and “art” that were dealt with by the St. Petersburg Academy in the 18th century (with the exception of mathematics), Lomonosov was undoubtedly the most substituted and most original representative. For the Russian state, Lomonosov became a questionable proof of the talents, inclinations and skills of the Russian people in the field of science and culture... It is known that Lomonosov considered chemistry to be his main “profession”, but in chemistry Lomonosov was primarily a great physicist-chemist. At the same time, in the field of pure physics, Lomonosov showed himself with amazing breadth and originality. Deserving Lomonosov in physics begins with creating the necessary conditions for its development in Russia...

In the second edition [translated by M.V. Lomonosov] of “Wolfan Experimental Physics”, in 1760, Lomonosov included “Additions to Experimental Physics”, containing brief references to some results in the field of physics obtained by himself. This is, first of all, his kinetic theory of heat, or, as he says, “my system of thermal motion,” one of the substituted representatives of the kinetic theory; further, his original and, despite the fallacy, witty and profound “new theory of colors”, in which for the first time a concrete attempt is made to connect the doctrine of light and matter; finally, his theory of atmospheric electricity...

The publication of Lomonosov's scientific manuscripts, as well as the study of his forgotten printed scientific materials, revealed Lomonosov to us as a substitute optician, inventor and designer of new telescopes, microsops, photometers, periscopes, who built them in a laboratory-workshop together with assistant craftsmen...

Many of Lomonosov's notes and aphorisms concerning the laws of conservation of motion and matter, the theory of atoms and ether, the question of the correspondence of heavy and inert mass...

Franz Epinius (1724-1802) appeared at the Academy as a representative of a new generation and a significant new direction in physics. For this generation, the Vyotonic method of solving problems of accepting and repelling forces becomes indisputable, although until recently for Bülfingar, Euler, Lomonosov it was completive. Stating from the very beginning in his famous treatise “An Experience in the Theory of Electricity and Magnetism” (Petersburg, 1755): “I consider it an undoubted axiom that a body cannot act where it does not exist,” Epinius actually uses the Vyotonian scheme in his theory of electrostatic interactions and induction. The name of Epinius is also associated with other important physical results. He was responsible for the discovery of the pyroelectric properties of tourmaline, the implementation of the first achromatic microsop *), built in St. Petersburg and for a long time stored in the physics room of the Academy of Sciences...

A quick look at the activities of [non-born] academic physicists gives a fairly clear picture of the state and development of physics at the St. Petersburg Academy in the 18th century. In terms of quality, this physics ranked one...

[*] An achromatic microsopion has a complex lens in which the images of objects formed by light waves of different lengths practically coincide and therefore do not distort.]
