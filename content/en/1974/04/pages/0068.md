COSMONAUTICS DAY

April 12, 1961 will forever remain in the memory of all people as the day of an outstanding victory of science and technology. On this day, man made his first orbital space flight around the globe. The first cosmonaut was a Soviet man, communist Yuri Alekseevich Gagarin.

Thirteen years separate us from this event. And every year brings new achievements in the field of exploration and use of outer space. In 1973, in the Soviet Union, in accordance with the space research program, two spacecraft were launched: Soyuz-12 with cosmonauts Vasily Grigorievich Lazarev and Oleg Grigorievich Makarov and Soyuz-13 with cosmonauts Pyotr Ilyich Klimko and Valentin Vitalievich Lebedev. During these flights, complete inspections and tests of the conditions of the onboard systems of the Soyuz spacecraft were carried out and spectroscopy of individual sections of the earth's surface was carried out in order to obtain data for solving national problems. Cosmonauts Klimkom and Lebedev recorded ultraviolet study of stars using the Orion-2 telescope system installed on board Soyuz-13. This study is inaccessible to terrestrial telescopes, since it is absorbed by the atmosphere.

Successfully completed preparations for the joint Soviet-American space flight planned for 1975.

American astronauts conducted a series of experiments with the orbital space station Skylab. The Pioneer space probe passed near Saturn and transmitted valuable information about this mysterious planet.
