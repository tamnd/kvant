Johann Leitmann (1667-1736) ... took the department of mechanics and optics at the Academy, and he can rightfully be called the father of optical engineering and precision mechanics in Russia. He was known to Peter I as the author of books on watches and glass grinding, and Peter sought to invite him to Russia even before the founding of the Academy. Arriving in St. Petersburg in 1726, he soon set about organizing work on precision mechanics and optics... There are sufficient reasons to think that special attention and success in the practical fields of optics in St. Petersburg in the 18th century in the works of Lomonosov, Kulibin, Epinus, Euler and others were largely determined by the work of Academician Leitman.

The great Leonhard Euler (1707-1783) ... was at the St. Petersburg Academy first a professor of physiology, then physics and, finally, mathematics ... Together with Peter I and Lomonosy, Euler became the good genius of our Academy, determining its glory, its strength, its productivity. Euler's physics, of course, fades in the rays of his mathematical glory, but in itself it is very impressive and deserves much more attention than it has received so far. Despite the fatal errors of Euler's wave optics, it was she who prepared Fresnel optics. Based on the excellent Russian translation of the “Letters” made by Euler’s student Academician Rumovsky, many generations of Russian people studied physics.
