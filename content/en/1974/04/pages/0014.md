ESSAY
DEVELOPMENTS OF PHYSICS
AT THE ACADEMY OF SCIENCES

S. I. Vavilov

The outstanding Soviet physicist Academician Sergei Ivanovich Vavilov, who was Chairman of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1945 to 1951, paid a lot of attention to issues of the history of physics. Among his works there is an article entitled “Essay on the development of physics in the Academy of Sciences over 220 years.” It was published in 1945, for the anniversary of the Academy, in “Essays on the History of the Academy of Sciences,” published by the Publishing House of the USSR Academy of Sciences. In this article, S.I. Vavilov characterized the contribution of Academy members to the development of Russian physics. Minor editorial additions to the article are given in square brackets.

“... when gathering the Academy in Russia, where there were still no universities or even secondary schools, Peter undoubtedly understood that it could not be copies of the Paris Academy of Sciences and the Royal Society of London. The Russian Academy could not be the pinnacle of the scientific paradise, as in France and England, due to the absence of this paradise itself. The task of the new Academy was, first of all, to create it. For this reason, an Academy of a very special kind arose on the banks of the Neva. Along with the scientific meetings at which... Bernoulli, Euler and others made reports on the most pressing issues of European science, all academicians were engaged in university and hypnastic teaching and, above all, were professors. In addition, academicians were entrusted with more difficult work on the foreign study of Russia, its nature, history, peoples, language; Academicians were constantly involved in technical expertise and organization of production. This must be kept in mind when considering the first period of physics at the Academy until the very end of the 18th century. The number of people involved in the development of physics in the Academy of Sciences throughout the 18th century is very small; These are academicians who officially occupy the department of physics or actually worked in this field, a small technical staff of mechanics, optics and individual inventors, like the famous Kulibin.

For a long time, until recently, the scientific life of the Academy was entirely determined by the personnel of its members. Therefore, naturally, our essay is based on the commented list of members of the Academy...

Daniel Bernoulli (1700-1782) ... in his famous treatise on hydrodynamics (which he wrote in St. Petersburg) and a long series of memoirs on mechanics and acoustics, showed himself to be one of the most notable representatives of mathematical physics. He spent 8 years in St. Petersburg (1725-1733), but until the end of his life he maintained a technical connection with the Academy...
