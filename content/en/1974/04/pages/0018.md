Vasily Vladimirovich Petrov (1761-1834)...
In 1795, V.V. Petrov became an extraordinary professor at the newly formed Medical-Surgical Academy. Here he assembled a model physics office for his own, where his main experimental work was carried out. They are collected in three books:
1) “Collected physical and chemical new experiments and observations”, 1801;
2) “News about Galvania - Voltaic experiments”, 1803; 3) “New Electrical Experiments”, 1804. In these volumes we have a very large amount of experimental material... on elucidating the nature of various conditions of luminescence*), on significant experiments with the voltage arc, discoveries by Petrov, and on electrostatics. Unlike Lomonosov, Petrov was not at all inclined to broad generalizations; he was an empiricist who, however, constructed his experiments in a very rational and thoughtful manner. Among Russian scientists and foreigners working in Russia at that time, V.V. Petrov was undoubtedly a major phenomenon...

[*] Lominescence is a special type of glow. This is how lights and rotten lights, clock numbers and compass hands glow, covered with a special substance - lominoforum.]

E. H. Lenz

Emilius Khristanovich Lenz (1804-1865) ... firmly inscribed his name in the history of electromagnetism along with Ohm and Joule and became the founder of a branch of Russian physics that focused on electromagnetism and the development of precision changes in this area.

[Among Lenz’s numerous scientific works, two are especially famous: “On determining the direction of galvanic currents excited by electrodynamic induction” and “On the laws of heat release by galvanic current.” In the first of them, he established a rule determining the direction of induced currents (the famous Lenz rule), in the second he discovered the law of the thermal action of current (Joule-Lenz law).]
