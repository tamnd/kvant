Euler introduced a series of understandable concepts into science, which became the main object of its study for many decades. Thus, the consideration of the zeta function, famous in number theory, goes back to Euler, with the help of which the properties of the distribution of prime numbers in the series of positive integers are still studied.

By the time of Euler, several problems had been studied in mathematics in which it was necessary to find the maximum or minimum of a certain quantity depending on the choice of a certain line. Here are classic examples of this city: among all the flat lines of length l, the least is mine, which covers the maximum area. Among all the curves connecting points A and B (the curves are assumed to be flat, located in a vertical plane, point A is located above point B), the most is my, along which between the points, moving under the influence of force, from point A we get to B as soon as possible. The initial velocity is assumed to be zero. This is the famous brachistochrone problem, posed by the famous Swiss mathematician Johann Bernoulli and simultaneously solved by three famous mathematicians of that time - Newton, Jacob Bernoulli and L'Hopital.

Euler grasped the importance of this type of problem and created a general method for solving them - expressed calculus, which is fully used in science today.

Euler had an exceptional influence on the system of overcoming mathematics from its very beginnings to its peaks known at that time. He wrote textbooks on arithmetic, elementary algebra, an introduction to mathematical analysis and analytical geometry, and created a system for presenting trigonometry, which has been proven to us in almost unchanged form. He introduced the now popular notation for trigonometric functions. He also owns the designations for the numbers e, π, i = √-1.

Many of L. Euler's students eventually became academicians. S. E. Guryev (1764-1813) deserves special mention, who, a quarter of a century before the French mathematician O. Cauchy, drew attention to the need for consistency in the presentation of mathematical analysis. The question of the foundation of mathematics, including mathematical analysis, was the subject of his main works. Guryev’s main path in this direction should be considered the book “Experience in Improving the Elements of Geometry” (SP6., 1798). I will quote the words of Guryev, which he called the treatise: “Reading the mathematical discoveries of modern times and turning to the principles that are confirmed by experience, I always imagine a huge significance, constantly elevating on weak foundations, always reduced to the end of the decline of sowing an extraordinary vastness of the most useful knowledge to the human race.” Let us note that the issues of substantiating mathematics, building its solid foundation, have occupied a prominent place in mathematical research since the 19th century, and our science can be proud of the fact that one of the founders of this movement was our co-author.
