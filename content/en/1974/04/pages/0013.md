⟦folio 11⟧

A. A. Markov

After 1901, Lyapunov devoted all his energy to solving Chebyshev’s problem on the equilibrium of living rotating masses. He was dissatisfied with his initial results, since in them he explored only first approximations. According to his opinion, the solution to a natural problem requires a complete and rigorous solution, since otherwise one can come to erroneous conclusions. Lyapunov's contemporary, the French mathematician A. Poincaré, was working on the same problem, but he was content with only the first approximation. As a result, they came to opposite conclusions, which led to a long-term scientific dispute. The astronomer Darwin (son of the famous natural scientist Charles Darwin) came up with a cosmogonical theory based on the approximate results of Lyapunov. In the end, the dispute ended with the complete victory of Lyapunov’s solvers.

The name of A. A. Markov is associated with major achievements in the development of the theory of differential equations, the theory of continued fractions and their application to questions in the theory of functions, the theory of quadratic forms and the theory of probability. Particularly important results were obtained by A. A. Markov in probability theory, where he is deservedly considered one of the founders of the modern state of this science. He introduced a new object of study in probability theory, called “Markov objectives”. We are talking about a sequence of random events, the probability of occurrence or non-occurrence of which depends on whether or not the corresponding event appeared in the previous place. For Markov's purposes, the author himself obtained a number of global results. Using Markov chains, patterns of alternation between vowels and consonants were examined using the examples of two classic works of Russian literature - “Eugene Onegin” by A. S. Pushkin and “The Childhood Years of Barrov’s Grandson” by S. T. Aksakov.

A. A. Markov does not reach the period when the concept of a chain and Markov process became central not only in probability theory, but also in physics, the theory of automatic control, various applications of probability theory in biological and production problems, and in information theory.

Of great importance for the development of domestic social thinking are the numerous speeches of A. A. Markov against the arbitrariness of the tsarist regime and the church. He did not go through the mismo of the events of 1905, the differences between L.N. Tolstoy and the church, and the refusal of the tsarist government to approve the election of Maxim Gorky to the Academy.
