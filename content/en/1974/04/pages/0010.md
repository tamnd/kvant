The second half of the century passed in the Academy of Sciences and in the mathematical life of the entire country under the enormous influence of the ideas and results of the remarkable mathematician P. L. Chebyshev (*) (1821-1894). Under his influence, the St. Petersburg mathematical school, excellent in composition and strength of the results obtained, grew up. She worked with great success in a number of areas of mathematics and primarily in function theory, mathematical analysis, number theory and probability theory. This school was closely connected with the demands of practice, and Chebyshev himself did not say that practice is the starting point for mathematical theories and at the same time their scientific and social value is tested on it.

The scientific work of P. L. Chebyshev began in his early days as a student at Moscow University. For his work “Exploring the Roots of Control,” the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics awarded him a silver medal in 1841. In 1847, Chebyshev moved to St. Petersburg, and his entire subsequent life was connected with St. Petersburg University, as well as with the Academy of Sciences, where he was involved in preparing for the publication of L. Euler's manuscripts on number theory. The work was completed extremely quickly - within two years, two volumes of L. Euler's works on number theory, equipped with detailed indexes, were published. These two volumes included many unpublished results, stored in manuscripts and in notes.
