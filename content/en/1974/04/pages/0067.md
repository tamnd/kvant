V. I. LENIN -
GREATEST
SCIENTIST OF THE XX CENTURY

On April 22, 1974, the Soviet people celebrate the 104th anniversary of the birth of V.I. Lenin, the general successor of the revolutionary teachings of Marcas and Engels, the creator of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union, the founder of the world's first socialist state, joining the working people of the whole world. Lenin was not only a great statesman and political figure, but also a revolutionary in science, who opened a new stage in the development of Marxism, enriching all the components of Marxism - philosophy, political economy and scientific communism.

V.I. Lenin gave a deep and philosophical analysis of the radical restructuring of physical science at the beginning of the 20th century.

He completed this most complex work in his book “Materialism and Empriocriticism,” published in 1909.

V.I. Lenin wrote in his book that atoms and electrons are extremely complex, and we have never learned everything about them, but we will continue to find new properties in them, previously unknown to science. He also wrote that physics in the first years of the 20th century gives birth to a dialect, that is, it confirms with its research the main provisions of dialectical materialism. He expressed creative confidence that physics will continue to develop along the same path. Both of these general predictions came true completely.

From the first days of the existence of Soviet power, V.I. Lenin paid great attention to the development of science. The young Soviet government sought to provide every possible assistance to scientists and scientific organizations in those difficult days. They established text contact with the Academy of Sciences. V.I. Lenin closely followed the work of I.P. Pavlov, K.A. Timirzev, I.V. Michurin, I.M. Gubkin, G.M. Krzhinzhanovsky and other Russian scientists. He sought to direct all the efforts of our country's scientists to solve the most important problems of building socialization.

Nowadays, the number of states that have embarked on the socialist path of development is constantly increasing. Such countries now exist not only in Europe, but also in Asia, Africa and Latin America. And in every such country, Lenin’s name is surrounded by deep love and respect. This is confirmed in the issue of postage stamps dedicated to Vladimir Ilyich. We include here some of the stamps issued by countries that have recently freed themselves from colonial rule.

V. Rudov
