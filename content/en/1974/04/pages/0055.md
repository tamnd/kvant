The book systematically uses, in addition to the well-known concept of ODZ, another, not at all pleasant, term “region of admissible solutions to an inequality,” which the author does not define. This significantly complicates an accurate understanding of the text, especially since, say, at the end of the solution to example 6 (p. 54) this term has one meaning, and when solving example 2 (p. 51) - another.

Some of the book's theoretical positions and recommendations are either unclear or simply incorrect. For example, listing the main properties of inequality, the author formulates the following: “if $a > b$, then $1/a < 1/b$” (p. 35); us. 39 it is stated that the inequalities $ax^2 + bx + c > 0$, $ax^2 + bx + c < 0$ are “equivalent, since by multiplying by $-1$ they are reduced to each other” and so on. Us. 49-50 the author proves the theorem: “the inequality $f(x)/\varphi(x) \ge 0$ is not equivalent to the inequality $f(x)\varphi(x) \ge 0$.” But, first of all, the theorem in this formulation is not valid (one should say “generally speaking, it is not equivalent”). And secondly, the non-negative theorem for constructing an infinite inequality $f(x)\varphi(x) \ge 0$ in its ODZ is equivalent to the inequality $f(x)\varphi(x) \ge 0$.

Special mention should be made of those cited on p. 68 as a sample of reasoning about one exponential inequality. Having obtained his solution $x > 0$ as a result of the necessary transformations, the author further writes: “Check. We substitute any value $x > 0$, for example, $x = 1$ ...” and, after carrying out calculations, comes to the correct numerical inequality. It remains, however, not clear what meaning and what significance the author attaches to this test.

When considering the inequality $\sqrt{f(x)} < g(x)$, applicants often carry out the following erroneous reasoning: since $\sqrt{f(x)} \ge 0$ is by definition a root, and $g(x)$ is greater than $\sqrt{f(x)}$, then therefore (?), $g(x) > 0$. The author also admits a similar logical defect, when solving the inequality $|x-a| < b$, he writes: “the number $b$ is positive, since it is greater than the non-negative number $|x-a|$” (p. 38), as will be, for example, the inequality $|x-3| < -1$ does not exist.

Although the author gives six examples in the paragraph on irrational inequalities (pp. 50-54), the reader is unlikely to be able to learn how to solve such inequalities correctly: in examples 1 and 3 there is actually no need to solve the inequalities, in examples 2 and 4 the inequalities are simply not solved, in the solution of example 5 there is more involved in mere calculations and a logical error is made, and in example 6 it is very unclear and imprecise statements that it is difficult to understand its weights. When considering trigonometric inequalities, simple calculations are scrupulously reproduced, but no instructions are given on the most difficult question for applicants - how to solve simple trigonometric inequalities.
