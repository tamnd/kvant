M205. 24 students solved 25 problems. The teacher has a table $24 \times 25$, which records who solved what problems. It turned out that at least one student solved each problem. Prove that

a) you can mark several problems with a “tick”, such that each of the students solved a number (in particular, perhaps zero) from the marked problems;

b) you can mark several problems with a “+”, and some of the rest with a “-” sign, and assign an integer number of points to each problem, so that each student scores equal points for the problems marked with “+” and “-”.

Both problems a) and b) are easy to solve using the theorem on systems of linear equations. First, let us formulate problem 6) in a suitable way.

Let the number of students be $m$ and the number of tasks be $n$. Let us define the numbers $a_{ij}$ ($1 \leq i \leq m, 1 \leq j \leq n$) as follows: $a_{ij}$ is equal to 1 if the $i$th student solved the $j$th problem, and 0 if not. $$a_{11}x_1 + a_{12}x_2 + \dots + a_{1n}x_n = 0,$$

$$a_{21}x_1 + a_{22}x_2 + \dots + a_{2n}x_n = 0,$$

$$a_{i1}x_1 + a_{i2}x_2 + \dots + a_{in}x_n = 0,$$

$$a_{m1}x_1 + a_{m2}x_2 + \dots + a_{mn}x_n = 0$$

or, in short, for every $i$ from 1 to $m$

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}x_j = 0.$$

F213. The maximum permissible speed of an automatic vehicle on a slippery road when passing a turn of radius $R$ is $V_{\max}$. At the turn, the road is inclined at an angle of $\alpha$ to the horizontal. What is the minimum speed at which an automatic transmission must travel to negotiate a turn?

Let us consider the forces acting on the machine in the first case (Fig. 10, a). This is the gravitational force mg, the normal reaction force from the road N and the friction force $F_{\tau p} = kN$ (the friction coefficient of the wheel on the road). Since the machine moves at the maximum permissible speed, the friction force prevents the machine from “skidding” (moving up the road) and is directed down the inclined plane of the road.

In the second case, the friction force $F'_{\tau p} = kN'$ (N' is the reaction force of the road in this case) prevents automatic sliding under the influence of gravity down the road plane and is therefore directed upward (Fig. 10, b).

Let us write down the equations of motion of the automaton for the first and second cases. To do this, we project all the forces on the vertical and horizontal axes.

Since the machine does not move in the vertical direction, then

$$\frac{mv^2_{\min}}{R} = mg \frac{\sin \alpha - k \cos \alpha}{\cos \alpha + k \sin \alpha}.$$

From here

$$v_{\min} = \sqrt{Rg \frac{\sin \alpha - k \cos \alpha}{\cos \alpha + k \sin \alpha}}.$$

In order to find $k$, we use equations (1) and (3). From equation (1) we express $N$, substitute it into equation (3) and get

$$\frac{\sin \alpha + k \cos \alpha}{\cos \alpha - k \sin \alpha} = \frac{v^2_{\max}}{Rg}.$$

And

$$k = \frac{v^2_{\max} - Rg \sin \alpha}{v^2_{\max} \cos \alpha + Rg}.$$

Then

$$v_{\min} = \sqrt{Rg \frac{v^2_{\max} (\mathrm{tg}^2 \alpha - 1) + 2Rg \sin \alpha}{Rg (1 - \mathrm{tg}^2 \alpha) + 2v^2_{\max} \sin \alpha}}.$$

F216. By standing up and squatting at the right moments, the boy on the swing sharply increases the amplitude of his swings. Explain why this works.

A swing with a boy is a magnet. The free oscillations of the magnet are damped due to the presence of resistance forces: the energy of the oscillations of the magnet turns into heat. In order to make the oscillation undamped, these losses must be compensated in some way.

Using a special mechanism, a magnet can periodically supply energy from the outside. If the energy imparted to the magnet, for example, over a period, is greater than the energy lost during the same time, then the amplitude of the oscillations will increase. At the same time, however, energy losses will also increase. Therefore, when the energy coming from outside is equal to the energy being lost, the amplitude of the oscillations will stop changing. Such oscillations are called self-oscillations. An example of a mechanical self-oscillating system is a magnet in a watch.

Another way to increase the amplitude of oscillations is to change the parameters of the system itself. This is exactly the method discussed in this problem.

Let us first consider the oscillations of the simplest oscillatory system - a mathematical magnet. If the magnet oscillates for some random reason, then you can swing it, that is, increase the amplitude of the oscillations, as follows: whenever the magnet passes the equilibrium position, we will raise it, and when the magnet is deflected to the maximum, we will lower it. Thus, we periodically (with a frequency twice the frequency of the magnet's oscillation) change the length of the pendulum thread - a parameter of the system itself. Therefore, this method of occurrence of oscillations is called parametric occurrence.

When we lift a magnet, we do work on it and increase its potential energy. By lowering the magnet, we provide the opportunity for the force of gravity to do work, which reduces the energy of the magnet. But the whole point is that more energy comes from outside than is consumed. Indeed, we lift the magnet by the thread to a height of $h$, thereby decreasing the length of the thread by $h$, we do work

$$A_1 \approx F_{H_1} h = \left( mg + m \frac{v^2}{R} \right) h,$$

since the force of tension of the thread $F_{H_1}$ in the equilibrium position not only balances the force with gravity, but also imparts a center-building acceleration to the magnet. When, at the point of maximum deviation from the equilibrium position, we allow the magnet to fall, that is, we increase the length of the thread to the first initial value $R$, the force of gravity does the work

$$A_2 = mgh \cos \alpha,$$

since the vertical movement of the magnet is $h \cos \alpha$.

Thus, energy enters the system in one half-cycle

$$E = A_1 - A_2 > 0.$$

As a result of this, the amplitude of the oscillations will increase (of course, if this incoming energy generates more heat due to the action of resistance forces).

Now let's return to the problem with the swing. From the above it is clear that in order to swing the swing, the boy must squat whenever the swing is at its maximum deviation, and stand up when passing the equilibrium position. And in this case, the length of the magnet thread seems to change (if the length of the thread is considered to be the distance from the point of suspension to the position of the center of the boy’s weights), and the role of the pulling force of the thread is played by the reaction force from the legs - $N$. In the equilibrium position $N = mg + m \frac{v^2}{R}$ and

$$A_1 \approx \left( mg + m \frac{v^2}{R} \right) h,$$

where $h$ is the height of the suspension of the center by the boy’s weights. When the swing is maximally deflected and the boy squats, the force of the weights does the work

$$A_2 = mgh \cos \alpha.$$

Thus, during the period of the system, energy is received

$$E = 2 \left( A_1 - A_2 \right) \approx 2h \left[ m \frac{v^2}{R} + mg (1 - \cos \alpha) \right].$$

This value is positive, so the energy and, consequently, the amplitude of the oscillations will increase.

F217. An electron beam tube with accelerating voltage $U$ is placed in a uniform magnetic field with induction $B$ directed along the axis of the tube. A small spilled spot is observed on the screen. If you change the induction values, you can replace that for some values ​​of $B_0, 2B_0, 3B_0, \dots$ the electronic asma formula is proper to the point. The explanation for this is a taco phenomenon. How can you use this experiment to determine the ratio of an electron's charge to its mass?

Electrons are ejected from the tube's electron gun with a small angular velocity spread and enter a magnetic field. In this case, in the direction of the axis, the electrons have identical velocity components, which, according to the law of conservation of energy, are equal

$$v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2eU}{m}}$$

and the various components, the redistribution axes, are much smaller than $v_1$.

For velocity components parallel to the tube axis, the magnetic field below is uniform and linear. The beginning of the components redistributing the axis leads to a curvature of the electron trajectory. Indeed, the Lorentz force $F = evB$ acts on an electron in a magnetic field by redistributing its field lines,

where $e$ is the charge of the electron, $v$ is its speed, in this case equal to the redistribution of the electron component. This force imparts centripetal acceleration to the electron.

$$a_u = \frac{v^2}{R}$$

where $R$ is the radius of the circle along which the electron will move. According to Newton's second law

$$m \frac{v^2}{R} = evB$$

$$R = \frac{mv}{eB}$$

and the period of electron rotation in a circle

$$T = \frac{2\pi R}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{eB}$$.

We obtained a very important result: whatever the components of the electron velocity, the redistributing axes of the tube, all electrons make one revolution in the same time. If the electron velocity components parallel to the tube axis were all equal to zero, then the electron trajectories would be circles of different radii passing through one point - the point at which the electrons leave the gun (Fig. 13). Moreover, all electrons would arrive at this point simultaneously.

Since the electrons also participate in uniform rectilinear motion along the axis of the tube, the resulting trajectories of their movements will be helical lines. Moreover, these lines will intersect at each screw pitch (Fig. 14), since the velocity components parallel to the axis are the same for all electrons. At the points of intersection of the trajectory and the electron formula, the spot on the screen will be observed at the point if the distance $L$ from the electron gun to the screen is a multiple of the helical line step $h = v_1T$, that is

$$L = nv_1T = \frac{n}{B} 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2mU}{e}}.$$

Moreover, at $B = B_0$ $n = 1$, at $B = 2B_0$ $n = 2$, etc.

Using such an experiment, it is possible to determine the ratio of the charge of an electron to its mass. Indeed, from the last formula

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{8\pi^2 U}{L^2 B_0^2}$$

Having measured $U$, $L$ and $B_0$, we find the ratio $\frac{e}{m}$.
