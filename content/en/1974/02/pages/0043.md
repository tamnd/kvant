Now let's discuss the question of whether we have studied all the triangles described around A and similar to B. So far we have considered all triangles of this type, obtained from B by rotation, homotein and, perhaps, also by symmetry about the straight line. These are all possible triangles X that are similar to B “vertex numbering-preserving”, that is, such that $\Rightarrow X_1 = \Rightarrow B_1$, $\Rightarrow X_2 = \Rightarrow B_2$, $\Rightarrow X_3 = \Rightarrow B_3$. So, if it is required under conditions of just such similarity (that is, to assume that $\Rightarrow M_1 = \Rightarrow B_1$, $\Rightarrow M_2 = B_2$, $\Rightarrow M_3 = \Rightarrow B_3$), then the solution can be considered complete: the entire set of admissible triangles X is exhausted by the two families S and $S'$ that we studied.

If we allow similarities with “renumbering of vertices” (that is, such that $\Rightarrow X_1 = \Rightarrow B_i$, $\Rightarrow X_2 = \Rightarrow B_j$, $\Rightarrow X_3 = \Rightarrow B_k$, where $(i, j, k)$ is any permutation of the numbers 1, 2, 3), then we get not two, but 12 families $S_{ijk}$, and $S'_{ijk}$. The vertices of triangles from these families fill 18 circles*). On figure 9, a or 9, b, you can select any triple of straight lines, pairwise non-parallel and passing (one at a time) through the points $A_1, A_2, A_3$ - designate their corresponding $x_1, x_2, x_3$ - and you will get one of the representatives of the family $S_{ijk}$ or $S'_{ijk}$ (in this case $x_1||b_i, x_2||b_j, x_3||b_k$). It turns out that from these 12 families it is possible to indicate the one in which the triangle is larger than in all the others. To generate the results, we label the vertices of triangles A and B so that $\Rightarrow A_1 \leqslant \Rightarrow A_2 \leqslant \Rightarrow A_3$ and $\Rightarrow B_1 \geqslant \Rightarrow B_2 \geqslant \Rightarrow B_3$ (now we can afford this), and position triangle B so that the orientations of A and B coincide. Then the largest triangle belongs to the family $S_{123}$. This statement was proven by participants in a mathematical circle from the city of Kirovsk under the leadership of N.D.
