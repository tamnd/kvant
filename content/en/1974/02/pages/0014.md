⟦folio 12⟧

The “seed” field required to “start the dynamo” could be created by various physical mechanisms. However, one of the simplest explanations of the connection is that a slight magnetization is inherent in any rotating body. The assumption about the existence of such magnetization was expressed by P. N. Lebedev. His experiments attempted to detect the magnetization created by a rotating disk. Although the technical experiment of that time did not make it possible to reliably detect the occurrence of magnetization, the experiments were subsequently repeated by other scientists using more advanced installations. In particular, the magnetization of a rod that occurs when the rod rotates around its longitudinal axis was experimentally measured. Estimates show that the field induction that occurs when the Earth rotates is negligibly small, on the order of $10^{-11}$ of the Earth's field induction. However, the table, having arisen, could strengthen the small field due to the action of the “geomagnetic dynamo”, as described above. The source of energy necessary to create the field could be the rotation of the Earth.

This theory cannot be considered final, and the question of the origin of the Earth's magnetic field largely remains open. Since this problem is related to the peculiarities of the state of matter near the center of the Earth, an explanation of the Earth's magnetism would be an important result for both geology and astronomy. A solution to this problem, it is believed, would help to connect the fact of the absence of a diopole magnetic field on the Moon with the internal structure of this planet.
