F214. In the circuit shown at figure 11, $R_1 = 10$ com, $R_2 = R_3 = 5$ com, and terminals 1-2 an alternating voltage $U = 127$ V is applied. Diodes can be considered ideal - in one direction of motion their resistance is infinitely small, in the other - infinitely high. Find how much power is released at resistance $R_1$.

Since the diodes are ideal, we can assume that during the period of voltage change at the terminals, sections of the circuit $AB$ and $CD$ are short-circuited, and the other period is open. This means that in the first case, this circuit is equivalent to the circuit shown in figure 12, $a$, and in the second case, the circuit shown in figure 12, $b$.

The amount of heat released at the resistance $R_1$ during the first irrigation period is equal to

$$Q_1 = \frac{U^2}{R_1} \frac{T}{2}$$

($T$ is the period of voltage change in the circuit, $U = 127$ is the effective voltage value).

During the second irrigation period, current flows through resistance $R_1$

$$I = \frac{U}{R_1 + R_2 + R_3}$$

and an amount of heat is released on it

$$Q_2 = I^2 R_1 \frac{T}{2} = \frac{U^2}{(R_1 + R_2 + R_3)^2} R_1 \frac{T}{2}.$$

During the period $T$, an amount of heat is released at resistance $R_1$

$$Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = \frac{T}{2} U^2 \left[ \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{R_1}{(R_1 + R_2 + R_3)^2} \right].$$

This means that on average (over the period) power is released at resistance $R_1$

$$P = \frac{Q}{T} = \frac{U^2}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{R_1}{(R_1 + R_2 + R_3)^2} \right] \approx 1 \text{ вт.}$$
