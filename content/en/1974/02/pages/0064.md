1. The magnitude of the photocurrent is proportional to the luminous flux incident on the photocell. When the light source is a laser and a glass plate is placed in the path parallel to the light path, the photocurrent decreases because part of the light flux is reflected from the surface of the plate and does not reach the photocell. Of course, the same effect of “loss” of light flux occurs for a light bulb. However, there is another effect associated with the refraction of rays incident on the plate at a certain angle, which leads to an increase in the luminous flux, and hence the amount of photooutflow. The plate plays the role of a focusing device that sends additional light flux to the photocell without first hitting it. In this case, very much, this effect exceeds the effect of losses.
