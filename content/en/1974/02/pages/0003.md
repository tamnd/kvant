V. A. Leshkovtsev. First flight to Venus (3rd cover page)

Mixture (p. 12, 60)

On the first page of the cover you see an old marine room. This gilded crown chamber is believed to have been made in 1790 and may have been in the captain's cabin of a British Royal Navy ship. The compass was hung on chains and attached with crossed rings to the ceiling of the cabin. This ensured the horizontal position of the magnetic needle during any motion. It was necessary to look at the room with blue eyes. This room is now kept in the National Maritime Museum in Greenwich (Great Britain).

You can read about why the magnetic needle shows the direction light-south, whether it is always set in this direction, and when and why the needle deviates in the article by A. B. Schwartzburg, “The Earth’s Magnetic Field,” published in this issue of our magazine.
