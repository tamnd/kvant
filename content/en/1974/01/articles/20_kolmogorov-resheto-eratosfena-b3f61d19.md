⟦folio 77⟧

Solved Eratovfena

This beautiful form of the Eratovfen sieve *) is taken from the book “Mathematical Leisure” by M. Gardner (Publishing House “Mir”, 1972). All numbers that are not crossed out (red or blue) are prime, except for the number 121. Explain why!

Problem 1. To get a list of prime numbers less than 1000, you need to “weed out” the numbers that are divisible by 2, 3, 5, 7, 11... What prime number can you keep?

How will the answer change for the case of compiling a table of prime numbers less than 10,000?

Pairs of prime numbers “twins**” are canceled with an exclamation mark in the table. There are nine of them in our table.

It is known that there are infinitely many prime numbers. But no one knows whether there are finitely or infinitely many pairs of twins.

Problem 2. The first two pairs of twins (3, 5) and (5,7) have a common element (5). "Consider" between the second and third pair of twins (11, 13) is equal to
11 — 7 = 4.

Consider between the third and fourth (17, 19)
17 — 13 = 4,
between fourth and fifth (29, 31)
29 — 19 = 10.

Let us prove that the given consideration between neighboring pairs of twins will never be less than complex.

A. N. Kolmogorov

*) See «Kvant», 1973, No. 4, p. 71.
**) See «Kvant», 1973, No. 5, p. 56.
