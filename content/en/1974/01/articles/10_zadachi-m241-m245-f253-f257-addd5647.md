We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. Along with relatively easy problems, there are also problems that are not easy for specialists - mathematicians and physicists - to solve. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. If you can’t quickly cope with a task, don’t give up, try to return to it again - in a day, in a week. If a problem interests you, then do not put it off even after a solution has been found—think about how to write down the solution most rationally and convincingly, how you can generalize the problem, clarify or clarify its result, what related problems it allows you to study.

In the second part of the section "Problem Book", Kvanta "" we post the solution to the problems (about six months after the publication of the conditions) taking into account the letters sent by readers. Essentially, the solution to each problem is a small note, and we advise those who did not receive «Kvant» in the process or did not solve the problems with it. In odd numbers «Kvant» (No. 1, 3, ...) we give the names readers who send us correct solutions to problems. Schoolchildren who regularly send interesting and useful solutions throughout the year receive the right to participate in three All-Union Olympiads in mathematics or physics along with the winners of regional Olympids. In addition, the editors establish several special awards for the winners of our ongoing problem-solving competition (we will announce them later).

Of course, not all of our problems are published for the first time. After formation, we usually indicate who proposed the task to us. Coming up with a new original problem - making a small independent “discovery”, unfortunately, is even more difficult than solving a ready-made one. If this bothers you, send us the problem along with the solution. Write how your problem arose: have you encountered similar problems or solution ideas, what general phenomena or mathematical theorems is associated with your new result. We will publish more beautiful and interesting problems in the “Problem Book” or other sections «Kvant».

Our address: 117071, Moscow V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, editorial office of the magazine «Kvant»

On the envelope after the address, write the solution to which problem you are sending (for example: “problem book M242, M245” or “F257” or “new problem in mathematics (physics)”). Send solutions to problems for each subject (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems for each subject in separate envelopes. Formulations of new tasks (as well as any other materials intended for publication in the journal) must be sent in two copies. We receive a lot of letters from readers and try to thank all our regular correspondents. We kindly ask you to draw up solutions carefully in order to facilitate the work of the editors and consultants of the “Problem Book” section, Kvanta." At the beginning of the letter, be sure to write your last name, first name, patronymic, home address, as well as the class and school in which you are a teacher (and your “number” if you are a student of VZMS). Enter the letter with your address written on it (for a response after checking the solution). We can take into account letters from readers only if they are written in Russian and sent no later than a month and a half after the publication of the corresponding issue.

The deadline for sending answers to problems in this issue is March 1, 1974.

M241. Prove that $3^{1974} + 5^{1974}$ is divisible by 13.

M242. Let $A_i H_i$ be the height and $A_i M_i$ the median drawn from the vertex $A_i$ of the acute triangle $A_1 A_2 A_3$ ($i = 1, 2, 3$). Prove that one of the three products
$|H_1 M_1| \cdot |A_2 A_3|, |H_2 M_2| \cdot |A_3 A_1|, |H_3 M_3| \cdot |A_1 A_2|$
is equal to the sum of the other two $^*$). Is this statement true for right and obtuse triangles?
S. Salnikov, 10th grade student (2. Mary)

M244. Given two sets of $n$ real numbers:
$a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ and $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n$. Prove that if at least one of two conditions is satisfied:
a) from $a_i < a_j$ it follows that $b_i \leq b_j$;
b) $^*$ from $a_i < a < a_j$, where $a = \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n}{n}$, it follows that $b_i \leq b_j$;
then the inequality is true
$n (a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \dots + a_n b_n) \geq \geq (a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n) (b_1 + b_2 + \dots + b_n)$.
A. Gritsoryan, 10th grade student (Baku)

M245. It is proposed to construct $N$ points on the plane so that all pairwise distances between them are equal to a predetermined number:
for each two points $M_i, M_j$ it is known what the distance $|M_i M_j| = r_{ij}$ should be ($i$ and $j$ are any numbers from 1 to $N$).
a) Is it possible to construct if the distances $r_{ij}$ are given in such a way that any $N$ points can be constructed?
b) Is it enough to require that all $N$ points can be constructed?
b) What will change if you build points not on a plane, but in space? What then is the smallest $K$ for which it is possible to construct any $K$ points from the given $N$ points and ensure the construction of all $N$ points?

M. L. Gerver

Using the superposition principle, find the electric field strength at the center of the ball and at points at a distance r from the center of the ball (r less than the radius of the ball).

Dielectric molecules can be represented as dumbbells of length l with charges +q and -q at the ends. The number of molecules per unit volume is n.

F255. When photographing the Moon from the Earth with a camera whose lens has a focal length F, an image of the Moon in the form of a disk of radius R is placed on the photographic plastic. Then, using the same apparatus, a sharp image of the Moon is obtained. It has radius R1. When photographing the Moon for the second time, at what distance did the camera lens move relative to the photographic lens?

F256. A bullet hits the center of a square, freely suspended dock. If the speed of the bullet is v > v0, then it pierces the dock right through. At what speed will the dock move if the speed of the bullet is 2 v0? nv0? At what bullet speed will the dock speed be maximum?

The mass of the bullet is m, the mass of the dock is M, and the resistance is considered independent of the speed.

Given from the poem by L. Euler “On the impact of bullets when shooting at a dock,” 1770.
