⟦folio 12⟧

Bone into a solid city state is more difficult than ordinary liquids.

Helium therefore remains liquid down to the lowest temperatures. Near absolute zero, its solidification follows the laws of quantum physics. According to these laws, the idea that atoms completely stop at absolute zero usually turns out to be incorrect.

Further development of low temperature physics led to the discovery of another remarkable property of liquid gel. At a temperature of $T=2,18^\circ$ K it goes into a state of superfluidity, discovered and carefully studied by Academician P. L. Kapitsa *).

Having obtained liquid helium in 1908 and achieved record low temperatures, Kamerlingh Onnes dramatically changed the direction of his research. Having seen that the temperature region near absolute zero is a whole world of special physical phenomena, he begins a consistent study of the various properties of matter at low temperatures. “Of all the areas of physics,” Onnes wrote, “the only questions that approach us are the expectation of solutions from measurements at helium temperatures.”

New research led Onnes in 1911 to the discovery of the phenomenon of superconductivity **).

Almost half a century passed from the discovery of this phenomenon before its nature became clear. This is a unique case of a scientific problem in our time. However, even today, when the theory of superconductivity has already been created, the properties of superconductors continue to cause surprise and admiration for many physicists.

*) See article: A.F. Andreev, Superfluidity of liquid gel, «Kvant», 1973, No. 10.
**) See article: V.Z. Kresin, The nature of superconductivity, «Kvant», 1973, No. 11

"What do you do when you dent a shooting star?" - asked the examiner.

“You need to look at your watch, notice the time, determine the direction of movement, estimate the approximate length of the luminous trajectory, determine from which constellation the star appeared; Having done all this, one can approximately calculate the trajectory of a falling star.” ...the examiner was waiting for this answer. Tasks

1. If you add up the digits of a two-digit number, you get a number that is 5 times smaller than the one you are looking for. Find this two-digit number.
2. If in a three-digit number you add up all the possible numbers formed from the digits of this number, you will get a number that is twice as large as the one you are looking for. Find this three-digit number.
3. If in the original number you add up all possible numbers formed from the digits of this number, you will get a number equal to the desired one. Find all such three-digit numbers.
