The pressure of unsaturated steam at temperature $t_2 = 100^\circ \text{ C}$ is found from its mass from the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation:
$$p_2 = \frac{m_B R T_2}{\mu V} = \frac{10^{-3} \cdot 8,3 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 373}{18 \cdot 5 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 3,4 \cdot 10^4 (\text{н/м}^2).$$
When connecting a second vessel, the volume occupied by steam will double. Consequently, the minimum mass of water required to saturate this volume will double. However, it is still less than the mass of the poured water. Therefore, the steam will be saturated, and its pressure
$$p_3 = 2,3 \cdot 10^3 \text{ н/м}^2.$$
Note that when solving the problem, the volume occupied by water in the vessel was not taken into account. This can be done because the volume of water is small compared to the volume of the vessel.

Humidity
The presence of water vapor in the air can be described quantitatively using two characteristics - absolute and relative air humidity.
Absolute air humidity, as is known, is called the density of water vapor $\rho = m/V$, and relative humidity is the percentage ratio of the density of water vapor to the density of saturated vapor at the same temperature, that is, $f = \frac{\rho}{\rho_H} \cdot 100\%$.
