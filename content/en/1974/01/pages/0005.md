At the same time, many mathematicians and physicists of the older generation (alas, I belong to them) pay great attention to their sports activities. And this despite the fact that in our school years, sports education is in its infancy. At that time, one could only dream of children's sports schools, as well as schools of physics and mathematics, large universities, mathematical Olympiads and a magazine like «Kvant».

During the years of study of people of the next generation, the amount of knowledge imparted at school was significantly less. Classes in groups were organized according to the so-called laborator-team method: everyone learns lessons according to their abilities and desires, and one person is responsible for the entire team - the most successful student in a given subject.

Several decades have passed, significantly changing the conditions of our life; made a qualitative leap in education, especially in the field of exact sciences. The increased flow of information has increased psychological stress, and school activities have become more focused. New living and studying conditions required youth (and even adults) to have a certain psychological and physical stability. I am convinced, based on many years of observations and my own experience, that such stability is especially necessary for those who study mathematics and physics. In fact, both those who are still just solving difficult problems and those who are already thinking about complex problems are well aware of the special mental effort required for such activity. People who know the specifics of service classes in mathematics, physics, and their applications know the joy of discovery (one of the amazing joys of life, not familiar to everyone), and hard work, and the bitterness of refusing some entertainment, and the established tension, and the established period of intense work. Norbert Wiener wrote: “Intensive research work changes to the limit. If a scientist loses the opportunity to devote himself as fully as he devotes himself to his work, this will immediately affect the quality of his articles.”*).

I have many friends who are mathematicians and physicists, and I have noticed in all of them a desire for “mental relaxation.” However, different people find release in different ways. Some are fond of bridge (isn't this a game for mathematicians?), others are into chess (the art of chess is worthy of the wise!), and others are into occasional walks. And, alas, very little - physical education. I am deeply convinced that neither bridge, nor chess, nor other games, although they appealed to me, but require mental effort, do not bring real relaxation. In addition, service classes in mathematics and chess at the same time are apparently impossible. Examples of this can be used at the time of life, a gap from mathematics, in which I previously worked actively.

Some of the mathematicians and physicists I knew well said that strong tobacco and drinks bring clarity of thought and some creative ideas. I did not argue with this point of view, but suggested trying another means - going for a run, for example; regularly engage in last-minute physical exercise
