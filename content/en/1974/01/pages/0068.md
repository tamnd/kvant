Exercises

1. Water is poured into a wide communicating vessel at a temperature of $T = 293^\circ \text{ K}$. One of the vessels is tightly closed. Determine the difference in water levels in the vessels if the saturated vapor pressure at this temperature is $p = 2 \cdot 10^3 \text{ h/m}^2$. You can change the vapor pressure of water in an open vessel.

2. A heavy pan, upside down, floats in the water. At a water temperature of $T_1 = 293^\circ \text{ K}$, air occupies only 1/15 of the volume of the pan, and when the water is heated to $T_2 = 373^\circ \text{ K}$, it fills 2/3 of its volume. Determine the mass of the pan $m$, considering it a thin-walled cylinder with a bottom area $S = 10^{-2} \text{ m}^2$. Atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5 \text{ h/m}^2$. Neglect the saturated vapor pressure of water at the initial temperature. The air temperature in the pan is considered equal to the water temperature.

3. A wide vessel contains $m = 0,5 \text{ kg}$ of water at a temperature of $T = 273^\circ \text{ K}$. The air from the vessel was quickly pumped out. At the same time, part of the water froze due to intense evaporation. Determine the mass of frozen water, assuming that the system is teloposed. The specific heat of vaporization of water is $L = 2,3 \cdot 10^6 \text{ дж/кг}$, and the specific heat of melting of ice is $\lambda = 3,3 \cdot 10^5 \text{ дж/кг}$.

4. Two vessels with water were placed under a glass cover. The water levels in the vessels are different. Explain how the height of the levels will change over time.

5. Explain why raw water boils faster than boiled water. Will raw water boil in a pan floating in boiling boiled water?
