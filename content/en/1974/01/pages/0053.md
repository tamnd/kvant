F212. From the sliding ocean along the circular slope of the glacier at a depth of 1 km, a sheet of ice breaks off - an iceberg (its height is less than 1 km). What part of the iceberg can melt when floating? The temperatures of ice and water are $0^\circ\text{C}$.

First, let's find how much heat is released when the iceberg floats. It is equal to the change in potential energy of the ice-water system.

When ice floats, the potential energy increases by
$$\rho_{\text{л}} V_{\text{л}} gh$$
($\rho_{\text{л}}$ is the density of ice, $V_{\text{л}}$ is the volume of the ice floe, $h$ is the height of the rise of the ice floe; we neglect the change in ice volume due to melting) and decreases simultaneously by the amounts
$$\rho_{\text{в}} V_{\text{в}} gh$$
($\rho_{\text{в}}$ is the density of water), since when the ice floats up, it corresponds to the volume of water that falls down from the surface. Change in energy of the ice system during heat release:
$$Q = V_{\text{л}} (\rho_{\text{в}} - \rho_{\text{л}}) gh.$$
We will assume that all this heat went to melting the ice (we neglect heat-conducting water and the movement of its layers, that is, kinetic energy when the ice rises):
$$Q = \Delta m_{\text{л}} \lambda$$
($\lambda$ is the specific heat of melting of ice);
from here
$$\Delta m_{\text{л}} = \frac{V_{\text{л}} (\rho_{\text{в}} - \rho_{\text{л}}) gh}{\lambda}$$

or
$$\frac{\Delta m_{\text{л}}}{M_{\text{л}}} = \frac{\Delta m_{\text{л}}}{V_{\text{л}} \rho_{\text{л}}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{в}} - \rho_{\text{л}}}{\rho_{\text{л}}} \frac{gh}{\lambda}.$$
Substituting here $\rho_{\text{л}} = 0,9 \cdot 10^3 \text{ кel/м}^3$, $\rho_{\text{в}} = 10^3 \text{ кel/м}^3$, $g = 10 \text{ m/c}^2$, $h = 10^3 \text{ m}$ and $\lambda = 334000 \text{ дж/кг}$, we find
$$\frac{\Delta m_{\text{л}}}{M_{\text{л}}} \approx \frac{1}{300}.$$
I. Sh. Slobodetsky
