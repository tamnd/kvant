Task 6. Determine what part of the specific temperature of water vaporization goes to increase the internal energy of the system at $T = 373^\circ\text{K}$. $L = 2,3 \cdot 10^6$ j/kg.

From the law of conservation of energy we have

$$L = \Delta w + A,$$

where $\Delta w$ is the change in internal energy during the evaporation of 1 kcal, $A$ is the work of distribution. Since the distribution is isobaric, then

$$A = p_H (v_H - v_0),$$

where $v_H = \frac{RT}{p_H \mu}$ is the specific volume of saturated steam, and $v_0$ is the specific volume of water, which can be neglected in comparison with $v_H$ (see problem 1). Thus

$$\frac{\Delta w}{L} = \frac{L - A}{L} = 1 - \frac{RT}{\mu L} = 1 - \frac{8,3 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 373}{18 \cdot 2 \cdot 10^6} \approx 0,9.$$

That is, 90% of the supplied heat is spent on overcoming the forces of interaction between molecules during the evaporation of water at $373^\circ\text{K}$.

*) Let us recall that specific volume is the reciprocal of density, that is, the volume of a unit mass of a substance.

Problem 7. A long cylindrical vessel with a non-volatile piston with an area of ​​$S = 0,01$ m² contains $m_B = 0,01$ of water at a temperature of $T_0 = 273^\circ\text{K}$. The piston fits tightly to the walls of the vessel, but moves without pretense and at the initial moments is at the surface of the water (Fig. 4, a). Water is boiled, and the capacity of boiling $N = 1$ kkm is constant, and less water through the walls of the vessel and the piston can be neglected. Draw a graph of the height of piston lift $h$ versus time $t$. Atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5$ n/m².

Until the pressure of saturated water vapor becomes equal to normal atmospheric pressure (this will happen at a temperature of $T_1 = 373^\circ\text{K}$), the piston will be at the surface of the water, rising only due to its thermal distribution. The operating time for heating $t_1$, required for heating water to temperature $T_1$, can be easily found:

$$t_1 = \frac{cm_B (T_1 - T_0)}{N} = \frac{4 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0,01 \cdot 100}{10^3} = 4 \text{ (с)}$$

(here $c = 4 \cdot 10^3 \text{ дж/кг} \cdot \text{эрад}$ is the specific temperature of water). The corresponding section on the graph (see Fig. 5) is shown as a straight line without a slope:

$$h = 0 \text{ при } 0 < t < t_1,$$

since the coefficient of thermal distribution of water is negligible.
