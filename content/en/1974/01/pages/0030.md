Both during etching and polishing, an insignificant amount of metal is removed from the surface and the geometric dimensions of the formed part practically do not change. This is explained by the fact that at the current density used in such processes, the rate of metal dissolution is very low. If the current density is increased hundreds of times, then the rate of aode dissolution of the metal will also increase. then the amount of metal removed can be consternimo with the amount of metal removed during cutting. This allows the use of electrolysis for metal processing.

At figure 2 the general scheme of electrochemical processing of metals is presented. Just as with etching and polishing, the part being formed is connected to the positive pole of the current source, and the electrode-tool is connected to the negative pole. But, unlike conventional electrolytic baths, where the distance between the electrodes is 150÷200 mm, here this distance is reduced to 0.05÷0.5 mm, and through this narrow gap between the electrodes the pump continuously dissolves the electrolyte. The non-working surfaces of the cathode are covered with insulating materials.
