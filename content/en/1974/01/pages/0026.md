A certain potential difference is established between the electrode immersed in the electrolyte and the electrolyte. This may occur as a result of the fact that metals have the ability to “transition” into the solution in the form of ions. In this case, the electrode is charged negatively, and the electrolyte is charged positively. A layer of positive ions is formed along the surface of the electrode. (Note that this potential difference does not cause a current through the hemigel - this is the name given to the separate electrode with the corresponding electrolyte. The ions are in a state of dianic equilibrium with the electrode.) Another option is also possible, when the metal is charged positively and the electrolyte - negatively. This occurs in cases where the electrode lowered into the electrolyte does not “dissolve”, but electrolyte ions are located on its surface.

It is impossible to directly measure the stress in a semi-reinforced material. However, you can choose one “permanent” half-belt and, by connecting different half-belts to it, change the potential difference between the electrodes. The “permanent” or, as it is called, the normal electrode is assigned zero potential, and then we can assume that the second electrode has a fully defined potential - the normal potential ($\varphi_{\min}$).

The table below is compiled from the results obtained by this method. A common dam plate is chosen as a normal electrode, and the corresponding electrolyte is a homogeneous*) hydrogen solution. The normal potential determines the energy required to reduce 1 mole of a given substance from ions.

Since it is usually not the potential of an individual electrode that is of interest, but the voltage between a pair of electrodes, it is clear that the choice of a normal electrode can be arbitrary. It is important that with a selected pair of electrodes for the electrolysis of each electrolyte substance, it is necessary to determine the minimum potential.

The electrode potentials of metals in the table are written in descending order. Please note that the sequence of metals (including hydrogen) K, Na, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Ag, Au is nothing more than the activity series of metals known from chemistry. In this row, each metal can be pressed out of the solution by the sollyl metal standing to its right. For example, possible reactions

$$\text{Zn} + \text{Cu}^{++} \rightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{Zn}^{++},$$

$$2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}^{+} \rightarrow 2\text{Na}^{+} + \text{H}_{2}.$$

This example shows how physics provides a quantitative description of empiric substitutions in chemistry for regularities. Reactions proceed in the direction in which energy is released, and the threshold voltage values ​​are directly proportional to the amount of this energy.
