⟦folio 34⟧

Mathematics can be done in different ways. You can only take a book and read it in a row: definitions, theorems, proofs. Or you can act more actively: read the definitions and statements of the theorems, put the book aside and come up with proofs yourself. The first method makes it possible to move quickly at first. But only the second method allows you to read the mathematical theory, remember it for a long time and learn to apply it.

Only by solving already known problems - by discovering something new for yourself - can you approach something new for others.

It is precisely this kind of active work that the reader is intended for in the notes that are published in the “Mathematics Circle” section. Generally, these notes are structured like a math circle activity: the reader is presented with several related tasks. Some of them are solved in the text, while others - both easy and more difficult - are left for independent solution.

As a rule, our math club is available to schoolchildren in grades 8–9, and some in grades 7 as well.
