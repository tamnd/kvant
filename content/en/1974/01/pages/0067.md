At a temperature $T_1 = 373^\circ$ K, the saturated steam pressure becomes equal to the external pressure, and the piston begins to rise above the water surface (see Fig. 4, 6) (saturated water vapor is formed under the piston). Since the power of the heater is constant, the mass of steam $m = \frac{N(t-t_1)}{L}$ (where $L = 2,3 \cdot 10^6 \partial x/\partial z$ is the specific heat of vaporization of water) is proportional to the operating time of the heater after reaching temperature $T_1$. From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation, the volume of steam $V = \frac{R T_1 m}{p_0 \mu} = \frac{R T_1 N}{p_0 \mu L}(t-t_1)$ is also proportional to this time (since $T_1$ and $p_0$ are constant). For the piston lift height $h$ we finally have

$$h = \frac{V}{S} = \frac{R T_1 N}{p_0 \mu L}(t-t_1)$$ at $t_1 < t < t_2$,

where $t_2 = t_1 + \frac{m_B L}{N} = 4 + \frac{0,01 \cdot 2,3 \cdot 10^6}{10^3} = 27(c)$

- the point in time when all the water in the vessel has evaporated. On the graph this section is depicted as a straight line with a slope angle $\alpha$, and

$$tg \alpha = \frac{R T_1 N}{p_0 \mu LS} = \frac{8,3 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 373 \cdot 10^3}{10^5 \cdot 18 \cdot 2 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0,01} \approx 0,08 (m/c).$$

After this, the temperature of the steam will rise and it will no longer be saturated. The change in steam temperature occurs according to the law: $\Delta T = \frac{N(t-t_2)}{c_p m}$, where $c_p = 2 \cdot 10^3 \frac{\partial x/\partial z}{\kappa \cdot \varepsilon \rho \alpha} -$ is the specific heat of water vapor during isobaric heating. Corresponding changes in volume from the periodic equation—

$$Kлапейрона: \Delta V = \frac{m R \Delta T}{\mu p_0} = \frac{NR(t-t_2)}{mc_p p_0}.$$

For the piston lift height we have

$$h = h_1 + \frac{NR}{\mu c_p p_0 S}(t-t_2)$$ at $t > t_2$,

where $h_1 = \frac{m_p RT_1}{\mu p_0 S} = \frac{0,01 \cdot 8,3 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 373}{18 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0,01} \approx 1,7 (m)$ is the height of the piston lift at the moment of completion of water evaporation. On the graph, this section is depicted as a straight line with an angle of inclination $\beta$, and

$$tg \beta = \frac{NR}{\mu c_p p_0 S} = \frac{10^3 \cdot 8,3 \cdot 10^3}{18 \cdot 2 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0,01} \approx 0,2 (m/c).$$

When solving the last problem, the question arises: will water boil in a vessel at $T_1 = 373^\circ$ K? As is well known, for boiling it is necessary that gas bubbles at the bottom of the vessel begin to grow due to the evaporation of the liquid inside them. Strictly speaking, this occurs when the saturated vapor pressure slightly exceeds external atmospheric pressure, namely, by the sum of the excess pressure values

$$p_1 = \frac{2 \sigma}{r},$$ where $\sigma$ is the coefficient of surface tension of the liquid, and $r$ is the radius of the bubble, and hydrostatic pressure $p_2 = \rho g h$, where $\rho$ is the density, and $h$ is the height of the liquid in the vessel. Therefore, boiling of a liquid at normal external pressure begins at a slightly higher temperature than $373^\circ$ K$^*$), and in the problem under consideration there will be no liquid. Obviously, the rule is that replacement overheating of the liquid does not occur, since the additional pressure is small compared to the external pressure. In order to overheat a liquid, it and the walls of the vessel must be thoroughly cleared of gases.
