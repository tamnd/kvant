Metallic sodium is formed. If $U > 3,94$ $\theta$, then sodium may be partially formed, but once formed, it will quickly react with water.

The fact that the threshold directions of electrolysis differ for different metals is used to separate metals. Imagine that you have a copper salt with small admixtures of silver and gold. How to extract precious metals? Let us carry out electrolysis of copper salt CuSO$_4$, using this crude copper as an anode. In this case, reactions are possible at the anode: release of oxygen, dissolution (that is, formation of ions) of copper and dissolution of silver and gold. Metallic copper will be deposited on the caote. If the potential difference of the electrodes is taken to be small enough (less than 0.80$\theta$—0.34$\theta$ = 0.46$\theta$) so that the dissolution reactions of silver and gold and electrolysis of water cannot take place, then the anode is destroyed, they copper Cu$^{++}$ goes into solution, depositing on the caot, and silver and gold, insoluble at this potential difference will remain on anode.
