By definition, the efficiency of a cycle is equal to $\eta = \frac{A}{Q_1}$, where $A$ is the work done per cycle, and $Q_1$ is the amount of heat received by the gas from the outside. The work done per cycle is numerically equal to the area bounded by the cycle contour in diagram $(p, V)$. In this case, this is the area of ​​the triangle $ABC$ (Fig. 11):

$$A = \frac{1}{2} (p_B - p_C)(V_C - V_B) = \frac{1}{4} p_C V_C.$$

Now let's find the amount of heat received by the gas when heated. According to the first law of thermodynamics

$$Q = \Delta U + A,$$
