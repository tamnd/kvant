With further expansion (at section $KC$), heat is separated from the gas.

Thus, the amount of heat received by the gas per cycle is equal to

$$Q_1 = Q'_1 + Q''_1 = \frac{3}{4} p_C V_C + \frac{49}{64} p_C V_C = \frac{97}{64} p_C V_C.$$

Now you can find the efficiency of the cycle:

$$\eta = \frac{\frac{1}{4} p_C V_C}{\frac{97}{64} p_C V_C} \approx 0.165, \eta = 16.5\%.$$

The maximum theoretical efficiency of the cycle is equal to

$$\eta_{\max} = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1},$$

where $T_1$ is the maximum and $T_2$ is the minimum gas temperature.

From figure 12 it is clear that the gas temperature is minimum at point $A$, that is

$$T_2 = T_A = \frac{\mu p_A V_A}{mR} = \frac{\mu p_C V_C}{2mR}.$$

The state of the gas in which its temperature is maximum corresponds to the point $M$ on the segment $BC$, in which the isotherm touches the segment $BC$. From the symmetry of the cycle graph it follows that this point is in the middle of the segment $BC$, so $V_M = \frac{3}{4} V_C^*$.

At the same time $pV = (pV)_{\max} = \frac{9}{8} p_C V_C$. The gas temperature at point $M$ is

$$T_1 = \frac{9\mu}{8mR} p_C V_C.$$

$$\left( \frac{dT(V)}{dV} = \frac{\mu}{mR} 3p_C - 4\frac{p_C}{V_C} V \right) = 0.$$

Hence $V_M = \frac{3}{4} V_C$.
