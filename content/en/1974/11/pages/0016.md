Over the past century, Mars' Great Opposition dates have been set between July 23 and September 24, spanning a two-month period with the perihelion date of August 23 following the day. This very rare confrontation took place in 1924.

The intervals between two oppositions have only two values: 5447 days (15 years without one month) or 6259 days (17 years and 50 days). 15-year intervals were almost twice as common. It can be shown that there is a particular pattern: after two 15-year intervals there is one 17-year interval, then again two 15-year intervals, etc. But this is not always the case. As can be seen from the table, after such a 47-year cycle, the data for the Great Opposition of Mars is almost two weeks old (compare, for example, 1909 and 1956) and may exit the “resolved” period (see Fig. 1). Then the Great will no longer be the confrontation that occurs 47 years after this one, but the next one. So, it would seem that the next Great Confrontation after 1971 should occur 15 years later, in 1986 (after 47 years of the confrontation in 1939). But the opposition of 1986 will come on July 10 and will no longer be Great (although quite favorable for observers of the southern hemisphere and tropical pole). The next Great Opposition of Mars will not occur until September 29, 1988.

Thus, the boundaries of the dates of the Great Controversies are somewhat wider than they were in the past century. They are concluded between July 15 and October 2. Typically, if the Great Opposition occurs at the end of July or early August, it is followed (after 2 years and 75-80 days) by also a very favorable opposition, when Mars is not much further from the Earth than during the Great. An example of such a “post-death” confrontation is the confrontation on October 25, 1973. For example, if the Great Confrontation occurs in September, it is preceded by a very favorable “pre-great” confrontation in late July or early July. An example is the “pre-great” opposition of Mars on June 24, 1954 or July 6, 1907.

Perhaps, generally speaking, as the onset of two oppositions on July 15 and October 2 at equal distances from the perihelion. Then both of them can be considered Great.
