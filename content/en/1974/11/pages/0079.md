Problem 6. Intersect the lateral sides of an equilateral triangle with a line parallel to the given one, so that the line segment enclosed between the lateral sides is equal to the sum of the segments adjacent to the base cut off by this line.

Having solved this problem, we will find that we have thereby found a fifth way to solve problem 1. This method is as follows. We construct an equilateral triangle $A_1BC_1$ (Fig. 4), whose angle $B$ is the given one and $|A_1B| = |BC_1| = p$. We draw a straight line $MN$ so that $BMN = \hat{A}$ (to the second given angle of the Iz triangle). Next we build $\hat{BA}_1O = \frac{1}{2}BMN$ and $\hat{BC}_1O = \frac{1}{2}BNM$ and through the point $O$ we draw $(AC) \parallel (MN)$. Triangle $ABC$ is skewed.

To find the sixth method, assume that the solution problem, the triangle $ABC$, is skewed (Fig. 5). Let's set aside $|AC_1| = 2p$ and draw $(B_1C_1) \parallel [BC]$ and $(BB_1) \parallel (AC_1)$. It is easy to verify that $|BB_1| = |CC_1| = 2p - |AC| = |AB| + |BC|$. Then we mark a point $O$ such that $[BO] \cong [AB]$, then $[OB_1] \cong [B_1C_1]$ and therefore $\hat{BA}O = \hat{AOB}$, and $\hat{B}_1OC_1 = B_1C_1O$. Considering that $(BB_1) \parallel (AC_1)$, we come to the conclusion: $[AO]$ and $[C_1O]$ are bicsectrices of the angles $BAC_1$ and $B_1C_1A$.

All these arguments lead to the following constructions: $|AC_1| = 2p$, $\hat{BAC_1} = \hat{A}$ and $B_1C_1A = \hat{C}$ ($A$ and $C$ are the given angles of the Izsky triangle). Next we draw bicsectrices $AO$ and $C_1O$ of angles $\hat{BAC_1}$ and $B_1C_1A$ and through their intersection point $(O)$ we draw $(BB_1) \parallel (AC_1)$ and, finally, $[BC] \parallel (B_1C_1)$. The triangle $ABC$ thus formed is skewed.

Exercises

1. Divide the corner whose vertex is inaccessible in half.
2. A straight line is drawn through a point and an inaccessible vertex of a given angle.
3. Divide this segment into three equal parts.
4. If the median in a triangle is equal to half the side to which it is drawn, then this triangle is a right triangle.
5. Prove that in any triangle the bases of the altitudes, the midpoints of the sides and the midpoints of the segments connecting the orthocenter with the vertices of the triangle lie on the same circle, the center of which is the midpoint of the segment connecting the center of the circumcircle with the orthocenter of the triangle. (This circle is called the ninth point circle or Euler circle.)
