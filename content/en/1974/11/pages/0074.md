They were awarded the prize “for a series of works on the experimental detection and study of spatial parity nonconservation in nuclear electromagnetic transitions.” Let's try to figure out what is hidden behind this complex scientific formulation.

Physics claims that all its studied phenomena and processes are caused by four different types of forces - gravitational, electromagnetic, nuclear and the so-called forces of weak interaction. The latter are responsible for processes occurring taking into account protons or neutrons on the one hand and electrons or positrons on the other. The action of these forces manifests itself, for example, during radioactive decay. Until relatively recently, physicists believed that any of these four types of interaction is carried out with the preservation of spatial geometry. In fact, the electrostatic field created by a precise electric charge is the same in all directions. The gravitational field created by an exact mass is also symmetrical. However, it has recently been shown that in phenomena where weak interactions occur, this spatial symmetry is violated. The reason for the violation of physics is not yet clear, but this fact has been established in indisputable experiments.

Soon after this discovery, some theoretical physicists put forward the idea that weak interactions are more general than previously thought and are involved in the interaction of nuclear particles (protons and neutrons). Until recently, it was believed that such particles interact with each other only with the help of nuclear forces. If the proposal put forward about the presence of weak interactions between nuclear particles were confirmed, this would mean the existence of our ideas about the nature of such particles. However, experimental verification of the proposed hypothesis seemed practically impossible.
