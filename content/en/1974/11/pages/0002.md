The “automatic machine” for playing chess plays with Napoleon” (Fig. A. Unehovsky).

This “machine gun,” created by the Hungarian inventor and mechanic V. Kempelem in 1769, was a thoroughly hoax, although it testified to Kempelem’s great design abilities. Strong magnets were inserted into the bases of the chess pieces. They attracted iron balls located under the dock, and thus signaled to the chess player sitting in the “machine” about the opponent’s moves. The “Turk” sitting at the dock was controlled using a complex system of levers.

The meeting with Napoleon took place in 1809; one of the best Hungarian chess players of that time, Allgaier, was sitting in the “automatic machine”.

The machine was “trained” to truly play chess only in the 60s of our century. You can read about how advanced electronic computers play chess in this issue (see p. 17).
