Fisheye camera
V. Drachev,
A. Mazur

In the 9th issue of our magazine for 1974, we talked about the scientific conference of students of physics and mathematics integrative schools, which takes place in March of this year in Tbilisi. At this conference, V. Drachev presented a report on the design and operation of a device for obtaining photographs with a viewing angle close to 180°. This device was designed by a group of students from integrative school No. 165 in Novosibirsk. This article explains how to make such a camera yourself.

Let's imagine a swimmer who dives under the water without a mask. What picture will open to him if he looks up through the calm surface of a pond?

Provided that the body of water is relatively small, the swimmer will see the following. On the dark ceiling (the surface of the water) there is a bright round window, in which the entire sky is “sandwiched” from horizon to horizon. All objects surrounding the pond stand at the edges of this window, with their “tops” directed towards its center. Moreover, it will be possible to see even part of the coastal day.

All this can be easily explained on the basis of the phenomenon of total internal reflection of light. It is known that when moving from an optically denser to an optically less dense medium, there is a limiting angle of incidence (angle $\alpha_0$ by figure 1), starting from which light will not escape from the first medium to the second. In this case the light is said to experience total internal reflection. For example, for the transition water - air $\alpha_0 = 49^\circ$.

Now let’s imagine that all the rays go in the reverse order, that is, from air to water. Then, if the useful angle from which light will not exit from the first medium to the second is $98^\circ$ (that is, two limiting angles $\alpha_0$), then the viewing angle in the air will be $180^\circ$. The field of view becomes much wider.

This is exactly how fish see, for example (Fig. 2). True, objects placed at the edges of the air hemisphere turn out to be highly distorted and, of course, displaced from their position.
