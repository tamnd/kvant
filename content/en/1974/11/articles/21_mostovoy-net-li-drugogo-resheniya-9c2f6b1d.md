IS THERE NO OTHER SOLUTION?
A.I. Mostovoy

Many school problems can be solved in different ways. Japanese solutions like this are an interesting and useful activity. Everyone can be convinced of this.
This note talks about how you can find different ways to solve problems.

... it is more useful to solve one problem in several ways than to solve several problems in one way.

We will discuss different ways to solve the following problem.

Task 1. Construct a triangle using its two angles and a perimeter of 2 r.

The general solution to this problem looks like this. Assume that the solution problem is that triangle ABC is exclusive (Fig. 1). On the continuation of AC from points A and C, set aside the segments $[AA_1] \cong [AB]$, $[CC_1] \cong [BC]$ and connect point B with points A$_1$ and C$_1$. The resulting triangle $A_1BC_1$ can be constructed using the side $A_1C_1$, equal to the perimeter 2 p of the exclusive triangle, and two adjacent angles $\hat{A}_1$ and $\hat{C}_1$, equal to the corresponding halves of the angles $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{C}$. It remains to construct the auxiliary triangle $A_1BC_1$ and the perpendicular bisectors to the sides $A_1B$ and $BC_1$ (or equal angles $\widehat{ABA}_1 = \hat{A}_1$ and $\widehat{CBC}_1 = \hat{C}_1$). As a result, we obtain the required triangle ABC.

But now, having solved the problem in this way, let’s think about it again. Let us construct a certain triangle $A_1BC_1$ (Fig. 2) from two given angles. Then, as in the first solution, we straighten the perimeter of the constructed triangle: $[A_1D_1] \cong [A_1B]$, $[C_1E_1] \cong [C_1B]$. After this, we will draw the rays $[BD_1]$, $[BA_1]$, $[BC_1]$, $[BE_1]$ and set aside $|D_1F| = 2$ r. Next we will carry out $[FE] \parallel [BD_1]$ and $(DE) \parallel [D_1F)$. The triangle ABC (Fig. 2) thus formed is exclusive. This is easy to prove (here and further)
