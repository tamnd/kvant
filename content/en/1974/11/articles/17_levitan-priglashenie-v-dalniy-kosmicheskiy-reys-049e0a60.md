Invitation to deep space flight

The book by Yu. N. Efremov “In the Depths of the Universe” *), inviting us to the most isolated areas of boundless space, makes us think about why people need such unusual journeys. Remember the argument between the characters in the movie “Nine Days of One Year”? Some of them argued that a person does not need to dream of reaching beyond the boundaries of the solar system, and even more so, beyond the boundaries of the galaxy. Another chanted: “We are for the Galaxy!” After reading the book by Yu. N. Efremov, you will feel like participants in this dispute and will find many convincing arguments indicating, in particular, that the parties to the first point of view could be not only “for the Galaxy”, but also “for the Metagalaxy”.

There is no such thing as easy space travel. The good book we recommend you read is also not an easy one. The author decided to write it so that it would be “more accessible to non-specialists and so that some of its pages would be of interest to the astronomer.” And he successfully completed this task.

The book by Yu. N. Efremov is curious, first of all, because its author managed, within the framework of the two most important ideas of his work, to outline in many details and details a grand picture of understanding the world of stars and galaxies. What are these ideas?

The first idea: all the history of understanding the Universe has been inextricably linked with the problem of spreading distances to celestial bodies and their systems. By proving this, the author almost absolutizes the concept of “distance to a celestial body”, turning it into a kind of physical characteristic of celestial bodies. It sounds interesting: “mass of the Sun”, “temperature of the star”, “diameter of the Galaxy”, etc. But in Efremov’s book you will find “distance of the Sun”, “distances of nearby stars”, “distances of star clusters”, “distances of galaxies”. Physicists have the right to demand that the reporting system be accurately indicated to them, either there is no “distance of the Sun”, but there is “distance

The Sun from the Earth”, “the distance of nearby stars from the Sun”, etc. But do not rush to be indignant, or Efremov’s book uses a perfect definition of the reporting system: in all cases we are talking about distances from the Earth (or the Sun) to all other celestial bodies. Such “geocentrism” (or helocentrism), of course, is completely justified: without knowing the distances to celestial bodies, we cannot get a correct idea of ​​the picture of the world, understand our place in the world, or know the nature of celestial bodies.

《Give me the exact distances to the celestial bodies, and I will tell you where you find and what is on the celestial bodies》—this is the meaning of the plot of Efremov’s book. But the exact distances were not obtained suddenly and not immediately. There are many examples of erroneous calculations and associated misconceptions. Many of the “exact distances” are still the subject of heated debate and painful doubts today. Therefore, the story of distances turns into a fascinating drama of ideas, which gave rise to our present-day ideas about the Universe.

The second idea: the accumulation of collective data about the Universe and, above all, data related to distances to the galaxy, instilling in our 20th century the most important qualitative result. The author of the book evaluates this result as a revolution comparable to the Copernican revolution. Copernicus indicates the place of the Earth in the solar system. The successes of extragalactic astronomy are of interest that goes far beyond the scope of “pure” astronomy. The main century of this history is covered in ten books by Yu. N. Efremov.

After reading this book, you will become acquainted with the most ingenious methods for solving multi-chastronic problems, gain a variety of information about our and other galaxies, various types of stars, pulsarchs and quasarchs, and gain a new understanding of much of what you have heard about many times before.

The book by Yu. N. Efremov is written in a fascinating way. Both the content of the book and the format of the story captivate me. The description of the chilling expanses of the cosmos according to the telltale man of Imor (who is no stranger to astronomers!) is full of figurative comparisons, additional epigraphs and, what is especially important, is imbued with the author’s own passion - an accomplice in a number of the most important astronomical studies and discoveries. This passion should be conveyed to the reader. After all, passion always meets us. This could be an encounter with a mystery, nature, a person, a book...
