Task 4. Can a seven-barrel be cut into convex six-barrels?

Solved and e. Suppose that I manage to cut some hexagon into convex hexagons. Let's denote the number of those vertices of the six-walls that lie inside the original semi-wall by $m$, and the number of remaining vertices (that is, lying on the border of the seven-wall) by $m'$. As arcs connecting the vertices, we choose straight segments of the sides of the polygons that satisfy the following condition: the segment must connect two vertices and not pass through the remaining vertices. Let us denote by $n$ the number of such arcs and by $l$ the number of areas into which these arcs divide the plane (the number $l$ is one greater than the number of six-sided arcs). It is clear that any two vertices are connected by a chain of arcs. By virtue of Euler's theorem

$$m + m' - n + l = 2.$$

Since the outer region is bordered by $m'$ arcs, and each of the others is bordered by at least six arcs, then

$$2n \geq 6(l - 1) + m'.$$

From some vertices on the border of the semi-wall there are only two arcs. Let us denote the number of such vertices by $a$. Every other vertex has at least three arcs, so

$$3m + 3(m' - a) + 2a \leq 2n.$$
Hence, by virtue of equality (3)
$$n \leq 3l + a - 6.$$
Comparing this inequality and inequality (4), we get

$$2a - m' \geq 6.$$

Since on the boundary of the seven-wall there are at least two vertices from which arcs emerge leading inside the seven-wall, then $m' - a \geq 2$. From this inequality and inequality (5) it follows that $a \geq 8$.

On the other hand, since the semicircle is cut into convex polygons, then every vertex from which arcs emerge is a vertex of the semicircular, and then $a \leq 7$. Thus, a semi-wall can not be cut into convex six-walls.
