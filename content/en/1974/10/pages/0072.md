The relationship between the number of the member $k$ (natural number) and the member itself (real number) can also be depicted in the form of a table (see Tables 1, 2). This method of specifying a sequence is called a tabular method, but it can only uniquely specify a finite sequence.

Many sequences can be called a so-called “recurrence relation” (recurrence formula). In the recurrent (inductive) method of specifying the $k$th term of the sequence is expressed in terms of the previous or several previous ones. The recurrence relation must be supplemented with the initial conditions. So, for example, the sequence $\Pi_8$ can be specified by the recurrent formula $a_k = -2a_{k-1}$ with $k > 1$ and initial conditions $a_1 = -2$, that is, the initial (first) term of the sequence is indicated directly, and each subsequent term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by $-2$.
