Problem 1 (10 grades)

Images of objects distant from the window (and therefore from the lens) are quite sharp in the photograph. Consequently, the lens was aimed at infinity and the image was obtained at the focal plane of the lens.

Fig. 8.

Judging by the photograph, the main optical axis of the lens was horizontal. If this were not so, the width $d$ of the window frame in the photo would be different at the top and bottom of the window (Fig. 7). Let’s determine at what height above the window sill the lens was installed *).

All lines that lie in a horizontal plane passing through the main optical axis of the lens must be horizontal in the photo. Let's continue the lines of the horizontal sashes of the window frame until they intersect. (Since in nature these lines are parallel, in the image plane they will intersect at one point.) Let us draw a horizontal plane through this point. The level at which this plane intersects the window frame indicates the height $H$ of the lens above the window sill. To determine the numerical value of the quantity $H$, it is necessary to have some “scale” size in the photograph. Let's choose the handle of the window frame as the “measuring unit”. We will assume that its actual size is $h \approx 10$ cm. In the photo the handle size is $h' \approx 1$ cm. In the photo, height $H' \approx 4,5$ cm = 4.5 $h'$. Therefore, in reality the height of the lens above the window sill

$$H = 4,5h \approx 45 \text{ cm}.$$
