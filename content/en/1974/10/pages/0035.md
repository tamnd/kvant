Problem 2 (MIPT, 1966). The edge of a regular tetrahedron ABCD is equal to a. A sphere is built on edge AB, as on a diameter. Find the radius of a ball circumscribed by a trihedral angle of a tetrahedron with its vertex at point A and tangent to the constructed sphere.

Solution. Let $O$ be the midpoint, $AB$ be the center of the given sphere, $O_1$ be the center of the desired ball. It is easy to report that $O_1$ lies at the height AM of the tetrahedron ABCD (Fig. 2).

Let us denote by $x$ the radius of the ball with center at point $O_1$. Since this ball is inscribed in a trihedral angle A, it follows that $AO_1 = 3x$. In fact, for any ball circumscribed by a trihedral angle A, the ratio of the distance from its center to the vertex A to the radius is a constant. Consider a ball described in a regular tetrahedron ABCD; its center, as is known, coincides with the center of the circumscribed sphere; the ratio of the radii of the circumscribed and circumscribed spheres for a regular tetrahedron is easily calculated and equals 3.

Let's depict $\triangle AMB$ on a separate drawing ($\times M$ - straight line) (Fig. 3). Consider $\triangle AOO_1$.

Fig. 2.

We have

$$AO_1 = 3x, \quad AO = \frac{a}{2},$$

$$O_1O = \frac{a}{2} \pm x.$$

The “+” sign corresponds to the subsequent external contact of the balls with centers at points O and O₁, the “-” sign to the subsequent internal contact. (At figure 3 the case of an internal tangency is depicted. In the case of an external tangency, the point $O_1$ must be on the continuation of AM beyond the point M.)

From the right triangle AMB we find cos $\angle O_1AO$, and then, by writing the cosine theorem for $\triangle OAO_1$, we find x.

Answer: $\frac{a}{8} \left( \sqrt{6} \pm 1 \right)$.
