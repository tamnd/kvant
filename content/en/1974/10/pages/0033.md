F258. A ring made of monk wire breaks if it is charged with charge Q. The diameter of the ring and the diameter of the wire are increased by 3 times. At what charge will this new ring break?

Under the influence of electric repulsion forces, the ring is deformed, and at any place in the ring the resulting elastic force is equal to the force of the electrical interaction of the selected elementary section with the rest of the ring: $F_{эл} = F_{упр}$.

The force of interaction between two exact charges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ is at a distance of $r$ from each other, according to Coulomb's law it is equal to $\frac{Q_1 Q_2}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 r^2}$. This is apparently the only combination of $Q_1, Q_2$ and $r$ that has a force dimension. Therefore, the electric force in the ring must be proportional to $\frac{Q^2}{R^2}$ (R is the radius of the ring):

$$F_{эл} = \alpha \frac{Q^2}{R^2},$$

where $\alpha$ is the proportionality coefficient.

At the moment of rupture, the elastic force per unit cross-sectional area of ​​the wiring is equal to the limiting force $\sigma$, which depends only on the materials of the wiring, therefore

$$F_{упр} = \sigma S$$

(S is the cross-sectional area of ​​the wiring).

Thus,

$$\alpha \frac{Q^2}{R^2} = \sigma S. \tag{1}$$

If a ring having a second larger radius and a second larger wire diameter breaks at charge $Q_1$, then in this case

$$\alpha \frac{Q_1^2}{(3R)^2} = \sigma S_1.$$

Since $S \sim d^2$ and $S_1 \sim d_1^2$ (d and $d_1$ are the diameters of the wiring), then $S_1 = 9S$, and therefore

$$\alpha \frac{Q_1^2}{9R^2} = 9\sigma S. \tag{2}$$

Then from equalities (1) and (2) we obtain $Q_1 = 9Q$.

F259. A thin-walled cylinder is rolled along a horizontal plane by acceleration a. Bar A, the dimensions of which are small compared to the radius of the cylinder, skolcite along the inner surface of the cylinder mak, which remains constant between the radius vector of point A and the vertical. This is true if the coefficient of friction of the block on the surface of the cylinder is equal to k.

In the coordinate system associated with the horizontal plane, three forces act on the block: the gravitational force mg, the normal reaction force N from the cylinder and the friction force $F_{тр}$ (Fig. 27). The resultant of these three forces is directed horizontally and imparts acceleration a to the block. Pozny, projected all the forces on the vertical and horizontal axes, we can write:

$$- mg + N \cos \alpha + F_{тр} \sin \alpha = 0,$$

$$N \sin \alpha - F_{тр} \cos \alpha = ma,$$

where $F_{тр} = kN$. Eliminating N from these equations, we obtain

$$\cos \alpha + k \sin \alpha = \frac{g}{a} (\sin \alpha - k \cos \alpha).$$

Let's divide both sides of the equation by cos $\alpha$:

$$1 + k \mathrm{tg} \alpha = \frac{g}{a} (\mathrm{tg} \alpha - k).$$

From here

$$\mathrm{tg} \alpha = \frac{a + gk}{g - ak},$$

$$\alpha = \mathrm{arctg} \frac{a + gk}{g - ak}.$$
