Now let's find the direction of the field at some point $A$ inside the ball. Let us denote the distance from this point to the center of the positive charged ball $r_1$, and to the center of the negative charged ball $r_2$. The field direction vector at point $A$ consists of three vectors (Fig. 21):

$$E, E'_1 \text{ и } E'_2,$$ where $E'_1 = \frac{qnr_1}{3\varepsilon_0}$ is the field of a positive charged ball, $E'_2 = \frac{qnr_2}{3\varepsilon_0}$ is the field of a negative charged ball.

Let us first find the sum $E'$ of the vectors $E'_1$ and $E'_2$ Consider the triangle $AO_1O_2$ and

ABC. Angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle O_1AO_2$ are equal, and the following follows:

$$\frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{O_1A}{O_2A},$$

because

$$\frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{E'_1}{E'_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{O_1A}{O_2A}.$$

From the similarity of the triangles it follows that the vector $E'$ is parallel to the line $O_1O_2$.

The magnitude of the vector $E'$ can be found from the correspondence

$$\frac{E'}{E'_1} = \frac{O_1O_2}{O_1A},$$

where

$$E' = E'_1 \frac{O_1O_2}{O_1A} = \frac{qnl}{3\varepsilon_0}.$$

Then the final direction of the field at point $A$ is equal to

$$E_A = E - E' = E - \frac{qnl}{3\varepsilon_0}.$$

As you can see, the direction of the field at point $A$ does not depend on either $r_1$ or $r_2$, that is, it does not depend on the position of point $A$ inside the ball. This means that the field inside a completely polarized ball is uniform.
