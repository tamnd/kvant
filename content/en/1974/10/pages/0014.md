⟦folio 12⟧

This pulse hits the Kr crystal and excites its molecules. The crystal is selected so that its excited molecules emit light at a frequency twice that of the incident light. This new part corresponds to the green light. Such an effect is possible only with a very high power of the incident pulse. Having reflected from two auxiliary flat mirrors placed at an angle of 45°, the green pulse passes through a vessel with water C. A little milk is mixed into the water to enhance the scattering of green light in all directions, in part, in the direction of the camera with an open plane, passing through the beam. Then the light goes through the cell K with carbon disulfide and through Polaroid 2. Moreover, this Polaroid is oriented so that the vibrations transmitted by it are perpendicular to the vibrations transmitted by the first Polaroid. Thus, no light passes through both polaroids at all. But carbon disulfide has a remarkable ability, discovered at the end of the last century by the physicist Kerr: if a strong electric field is created in it, perpendicular to the direction of the propagating light, then the polarization of the light changes. Obviously this auxiliary field is created between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor placed in carbon disulfide and connected to a high voltage source. But in the experiment of photographing light, the field is created by a powerful infrared beam that passes through the P plate and also hits the Kerr lens. Light with changed polarization can pass through the second polaroid and enter the FA camera. A light filter F is placed between the camera and the Foto camera, which transmits only green light (infrared light also passes through the camera - it is partially absorbed by the filter F). It is not difficult to arrange the instruments so that the green and infrared pulses arrive at the Kerr tube simultaneously. Thus, the infrared pulse “opens” the optical shutter (using a combination of two Polaroids and a Kerr lens) for a negligibly short time of 10⁻¹¹ s (the lifetime of the infrared pulse).

Thanks to this, it was possible to photograph the green impulse, which appeared to the photographer in the form of a slightly blurred spot inside a vessel with water. This replacement photograph represents figure on p. 10. To obtain it, the installation shown in figure 2 was used.

The dimensions of the green pulse image in the field do not correspond to the preliminary size calculation. The red block that appears to the photographer is a trace of an infrared pulse that also hit the Foto camera.
