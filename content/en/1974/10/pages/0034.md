Drawing in stereometric problems
I. F. Sharytsin

When checking written work submitted to calculation training assignments, the following is often discovered: in pure geometric tasks, a drawing is accompanied quite well, and what is depicted in the draft can only be called a drawing conditionally. And one can only wonder how, with the help of such a “drawing”, the applicants still managed to solve the problem. It is no secret that a good drawing can provide significant assistance in solving geometric problems, especially problems in stereometry. Of course, learning to make such drawings as the artists «Kvant» do is hardly possible for an ordinary school student. However, if you pay attention to the quality of the drawing when solving each geometric problem, then in the end you will learn to do it quite well. Make it a rule not to start solving a problem until you have done a good drawing. Do not use paper, make your drawing large, draw invisible lines with dotted lines, do not draw auxiliary lines until you are sure of their necessity. Very often, stereometric problems will be reduced to one or several problems in planometrics. Make a separate drawing for each task. Balls, as a rule, should not be depicted; It is enough to indicate their center, points of contact with a straight line, plane or other balls. The fact that two balls are touching means that the distance between their centers is equal to the sum of the radii if the touch is external, and the same distance is equal to the difference of the radii if the touch is internal.

Let's move on to examples.

Problem 1 (MSU, Faculty of Chemistry, 1968). Inside the sphere of space there are four balls of radius r. Which of these balls touches the other three and the surface of the sphere. Determine the radius of the sphere.

Solution. Let A, B, C and D be the centers of balls of radius r, and O be the center of the sphere (Fig. 1). From the fact that the balls touch each other in pairs externally, it follows that ABCD is a regular tetrahedron with edge 2r. All balls touch internally with center O, therefore the distances AO, BO, CO, DO are equal to each other and equal to R - r, where R is the desired radius of the sphere.
