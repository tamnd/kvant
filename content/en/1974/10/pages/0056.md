We write the total energy of the system during this period in the form
$$W'_1 = W'_B + E'_1A + U'_1A + E'_13$$
(for analogy, we wrote the term $E_{1A}$, although $E'_{1A} = 0$). Machine $A$ changed its speed in this system by values ​​$v$. At the same time, the Earth's speed became $v'_3$. From the law of conservation of momentum
$$Mv'_3 + mv = -Mv$$
we'll find
$$|v'_3| = \left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right)|v|$$
The total energy of the system during this period is equal to
$$W'_2 = W'_B + E'_2A + (U'_1A - \Delta U') + E'_23$$.
Let's write down the law of conservation of energy:
$$W'_2 - W'_1 = E'_2A - E'_1A - \Delta U' + E'_13 = 0$$
or
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{M}{2} \left[ \left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right)v \right]^2 - \frac{Mv^2}{2} - \Delta U' = 0$$
Thus,
$$\Delta U' = \Delta E' = \frac{3m}{2}v^2 + \frac{m^2}{2M}v^2 = \Delta U$$.
The mass of burned fuel in both systems is the same, the change in internal energy, and therefore the amount of heat released during combustion, is also the same. This means that the specific heat of combustion of the fuel is the same for the observed one, located in two distant systems.
So, there is no low paradox. The work done due to the energy released during fuel combustion goes to increase the mechanical energy of the system as a whole. Thus, the conclusion made in the conditions of the problem about the change in energy is incorrect.
The error lies in the fact that the conditions of the system are not closed, and the conclusion was made on the basis of the law of conservation of energy in the form in which it is applicable for closed systems.
Speaking about the change in the speed of the Earth, we are approaching the solution of a “mathematical” problem. Of course, the change in the Earth's momentum is negligible. Some of the energy is “carried away” as a result of heating the Earth under the wheels, pebbles, sand, etc. flying out from under the wheels.
