8th grade, task 4. Let's prove that $n = k$.

Let's assume that $n > k$. Then

$$n^n > k^n > k^k;$$

but the number $n^n$ has $k$ digits, and therefore must be less than the number $k^k$, which has $n$ digits. Likewise, there cannot be $n < k$, and therefore $n = k$. It remains to be seen when the number $n^n$ will have $n$ digits. Note that $n$ cannot be more than ten: if $n > 10$, then $n^n > 10^n$, and $10^n$ has $n + 1$ digits; if $n < 10$, then $n^n < 10^n$ and therefore $n^n$ has no more than $n$ digits. Make sure the values ​​$n = 1$, $n = 8$ and $n = 9$ are appropriate.
