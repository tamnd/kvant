Problem 5 (the formulation of this problem is a re-phasing of the problem given in 1973 at the Olympiad at MIPT). The axes of three equal, pairwise touching cylindrical surfaces are mutually perpendicular. The radius of the smallest ball touching all three surfaces if the radius of each of them is equal to $r$.

Solution. Consider the cube $ABCDA_1B_1C_1D_1$ with edge $2r$. We can assume that edge $AA_1$ lies on the axis of one cylinder, edge $DC$ on the axis of another, and, finally, edge $B_1C_1$ on the axis of the third (Fig. 7).

Let us prove that the center of the desired ball coincides with the center $O$ of the cube.

Indeed, consider three cylinders, concentric and passing through the point $O$. Then $O$ is the only point lying inside or on the surface of all these three cylinders, and therefore, for any point other than $O$, the distance to one of the three lines $AA_1$, $DC$ and $B_1C_1$ is greater than the distances from $O$ to these lines.

Answer: $r(\sqrt{2} - 1)$.

Solution. Consider the triangular paramid $SABC$ (Fig. 8), which has an equilateral base $\Delta ABC$ ($AC = CB$) with an angle $ACB$ equal to $\frac{2\pi}{n}$, $SC$ perpendicular to the plane $ABC$ and a projection $C$ onto the plane $SAB$ coincides with the center of the circle inscribed in triangle $ABS$. Consider now a cone whose vertex is at $C$ and whose base circle is the circle inscribed at $\Delta SAB$. It is easy to see that this cone can be considered as the desired one. In fact, if we place $n$ paramidas equal to $SABC$ paramides next to each other, so that their vertices $S$ coincide, then the cones inscribed in these paramidas will form a system that satisfies the conditions of the problem.
