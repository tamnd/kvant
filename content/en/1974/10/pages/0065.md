To the article “Drawing in stereometric problems”

1. Answer: $\frac{1}{3}(1 + 2\sqrt{2})^3 \pi R^3$.

Note. Draw a section of the cone with a plane passing through the height of the cone and the center of one of the balls lying on the base.

2. Answer:
$$\frac{1}{3}r\left(6 + \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{27 + 12\sqrt{3}}\right).$$

Note. Express the side of the base of the prism in terms of $r$ and find the radius of the 4th ball. The height of the prism is the sum of $r$, the radius of the 4th ball and the height of the tetrahedron, the vertices of which are the centers of the balls.

3. Answer: $\frac{2}{5}r\left(8\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{37}\right)$.

Note. Draw a cross section of the prism with a plane passing through the height and centers of both spheres.

4. Answer: $1 : \text{tg}^2\alpha : 1$.

Note. Let $A_1$ and $B_1$ be the second points of contact of the balls with the edges of the angle, $M$ and $N$ the bases of the perpendiculars dropped from the points of contact to the edge. All elements of the prism $AA_1MBB_1N$ are determined from the condition. To get the answer, use the theorem on the product of segments secant to a ball.

5. Answer: $\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{96}a^3$.

Note. Prove that the point $O$ lies on $AC$.

6. Answer: $\frac{a}{6}$.

Note. Unroll the section with a plane passing through the center of the ball, the vertex of one of the prisms and the perpendicular plane of the given triangle.
