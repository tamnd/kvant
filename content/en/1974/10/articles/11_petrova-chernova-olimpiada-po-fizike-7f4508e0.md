Task 2 (10 grades)

Let us consider an event in two reporting systems - stationary and moving, which we associate with the machine $B$. We will assume that the movement occurs without sliding and there are no forces.

In the initial period ($v_A = v_B = v$), the total impulse of the Earth-machine system in the stationary reporting system is equal to

$$P_1 = 2mv$$

(for convenience, we will assume that the masses of the cars are the same). During this period, the total mechanical energy of the system is

$$E_1 = 2m \frac{v^2}{2} = mv^2.$$

The total energy of the system consists of the kinetic energy of machines $A$ and $B$ in the internal energy of the fuel:

$$W_1 = E_1 + U_1.$$

Since the energy “attributable” to the machine $B$ does not change over time, we will denote it $W_B (W_B = \text{const})$, and the energy “attributed” to the machine $A$ will be represented as $W_1A = E_1A + U_1A$. Thus, at the first stage of motion ($v_A = v_B = v$) the energy of the system is equal to

$$W_1 = W_B + E_1A + U_1A.$$

We will assume that all the energy released during fuel storage went to increase the automation speed $A$.

Since our system is closed, the total impulse must be conserved. The impulse of the machine $B$ $P_1B = \text{const}$, and the impulse of the machine $A$ changes by values ​​$m \Delta v_A = mv$ (we neglect the change in the mass of the machine as a result of removing part of the fuel). Consequently, the Earth’s impulse changes by the same amount, that is, the Earth’s speed increases, which can be determined from the correspondence $|Mv_2| = |mv|$:

$$|v_3| = \frac{m}{M}|v|.$$

(At the same time $v_3 = -\frac{m}{M}v$; we consider that the wheels rotate without slipping.) The mechanical energy of the system became equal

$$E_2 = \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{m(2v)^2}{2} + \frac{M\left(\frac{m}{M}v\right)^2}{2}.$$

The increase in mechanical energy will occur due to a change in internal energy (as a result of fuel storage). Let us now write down the total energy of the system:

$$W_2 = W_B + E_2A + E_3 + (U_1A - \Delta U).$$

According to the law of conservation of energy, $W_2 = W_1$, that is, $E_2A - E_1A + E_3 - \Delta U = 0$, or

$$\frac{3}{2}mv^2 + \frac{m^2}{2M}v^2 - \Delta U = 0.$$

Thus,

$$\Delta U = \Delta E = \frac{3}{2}mv^2 + \frac{m^2}{2M}v^2.$$

Let us now consider an event in a mobile system associated with machine $B$. Let us carry out all the above reasoning, preserving the assumptions. Initial impulse of the system

$$P'_1 = -Mv$$

(since the Earth relative to the machine $B$ moves at a speed $-v$). Mechanical energy in the initial period is equal to the kinetic energy of the movement of the Earth relative to the machine $B$):

$$E'_1 = \frac{Mv^2}{2}.$$

We write the total energy of the system during this period in the form
$$W'_1 = W'_B + E'_1A + U'_1A + E'_13$$
(for analogy, we wrote the term $E_{1A}$, although $E'_{1A} = 0$). Machine $A$ changed its speed in this system by values ​​$v$. At the same time, the Earth's speed became $v'_3$. From the law of conservation of momentum
$$Mv'_3 + mv = -Mv$$
we'll find
$$|v'_3| = \left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right)|v|$$
The total energy of the system during this period is equal to
$$W'_2 = W'_B + E'_2A + (U'_1A - \Delta U') + E'_23$$.
Let's write down the law of conservation of energy:
$$W'_2 - W'_1 = E'_2A - E'_1A - \Delta U' + E'_13 = 0$$
or
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{M}{2} \left[ \left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right)v \right]^2 - \frac{Mv^2}{2} - \Delta U' = 0$$
Thus,
$$\Delta U' = \Delta E' = \frac{3m}{2}v^2 + \frac{m^2}{2M}v^2 = \Delta U$$.
The mass of burned fuel in both systems is the same, the change in internal energy, and therefore the amount of heat released during combustion, is also the same. This means that the specific heat of combustion of the fuel is the same for the observed one, located in two distant systems.
So, there is no low paradox. The work done due to the energy released during fuel combustion goes to increase the mechanical energy of the system as a whole. Thus, the conclusion made in the conditions of the problem about the change in energy is incorrect.
The error lies in the fact that the conditions of the system are not closed, and the conclusion was made on the basis of the law of conservation of energy in the form in which it is applicable for closed systems.
Speaking about the change in the speed of the Earth, we are approaching the solution of a “mathematical” problem. Of course, the change in the Earth's momentum is negligible. Some of the energy is “carried away” as a result of heating the Earth under the wheels, pebbles, sand, etc. flying out from under the wheels.
