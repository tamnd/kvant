Olympiad in mathematics

From April 11 to April 17, 1974, the final stage of the VIII All-Russian Mathematics Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in the capital of the Armenian Republic, Eurasia.

Having started in December 1973, the Olympiad successfully reached its finish line. The schoolchildren had to do a lot of work during this time. They participated in modern ones in schools, districts, regions or resource areas. And now the schoolchildren, having successfully completed three stages, sat down at their desks again to take part in the fourth, most important stage of the All-Olympiad.

In addition to the winners of the region and Resurikan Olympiads, the winners of the VII All-Russian Olympiad were also allowed to participate in the final.type. In total, the following people took part in the final.type: 8 classes - 169 people, 9 classes - 212 people and 10 classes - 220 people.

On April 11, in the building of the Eurasian University, the grand opening of the final.type VIII All-Olympiad took place. The Minister of Education of Armenia S. T. Akhumyan, the Chairman of the Olympiad jury, Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences S. Mergelyan and others greeted the schoolchildren. A greeting from the Minister of Education of the USSR M.A. Prokofiev was also read:

“Dear guys! Today, in the cities of Donetsk, Gorky and Eurasia, the best representatives of the region, co-operative and autonomous resources of our country, meet - young physicists, chemists and mathematicians who have shown interest in science, the ability to work hard, have original thoughts and work independently with a book.

These qualities will help you cope with the questions posed. It is known that the strongest win. But above all, participation in this Olympiad allows you to hope that it will enrich you and lead to the achievement of the goal that you set for yourself.

I wish you more success in mastering knowledge! Let your passion for science and your creative attitude to work never weaken!”

On April 12 and 14, the 1st and 2nd rounds of the final type Olympiad took place. In total, schoolchildren from each class were given 7 problems. It took 4 hours to solve each type of problem.

This year, along with the typical “Olympic” problems, equally accessible to schoolchildren in grades 8, 9 and 10, the solution of which requires only wit and the ability to reason logically, problems were also given in which the solution required some technical difficulties.

8th grade
First day

1. The cards have numbers written on them, each of which is equal to +1 or -1. It is allowed, indicating three cards, to ask: “What is the product of the numbers on these cards?” (The numbers themselves do not tell us.) Each minimum number of such questions must be asked in order to find out the product of the numbers on all cards, if the number of cards is equal to: a) 30; b) 31; b) 32?

In each case, prove that it is impossible to get by with fewer questions.

2. Among the numbers of the form 36ᵉ -5ᵉ, where k and l are natural numbers, find the smallest in terms of abscile value. Prove that the number found is the smallest real number.

3. a) Each side of a convex hexagon has a length greater than 1. Is there a diagonal of length greater than 2 in it?

Second day

4. Find all natural numbers n and k such that nᵉ has k digits and kᵉ has n digits.

5. On legs CA and CB of equilateral right triangle ABC, points D and E are chosen respectively so that CD = CE. The extensions of the perpendiculars dropped from points D and C to line AE intersect the hypotenuse AB at points K and L, respectively. Prove that KL = LB.

6. On an 8x8 chessboard, two people play a game of cat and mouse. The first has one chip - a mouse, the second has several chips - cats. All chips move the same way: to the right, to the left, up or down one cell. If the mouse ends up on the edge of the board, then in its next move it jumps off the board; If a mouse and a mouse land on the same square, then the mouse eats the mouse.

The players take turns, with the second one replaying all the skates with his own move (different skates can be moved in different directions). The mouse starts. She tries to jump off the board, and the horses try to eat her before that.

a) Let there be only two skates. The mouse is already placed on some square not on the edge. Is it possible to place a skate on the edge of the board so that they can eat the mouse?

b) Let there be three skates, but the move has gone wrong: for the first time she makes two moves in a row. Prove that the mouse can escape from the skate, whatever the initial placement of the tiles.

7. Prove that the numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., 32 can be arranged in such an order that for no two numbers they are equal.

8th grade, task 4. Let's prove that $n = k$.

Let's assume that $n > k$. Then

$$n^n > k^n > k^k;$$

but the number $n^n$ has $k$ digits, and therefore must be less than the number $k^k$, which has $n$ digits. Likewise, there cannot be $n < k$, and therefore $n = k$. It remains to be seen when the number $n^n$ will have $n$ digits. Note that $n$ cannot be more than ten: if $n > 10$, then $n^n > 10^n$, and $10^n$ has $n + 1$ digits; if $n < 10$, then $n^n < 10^n$ and therefore $n^n$ has no more than $n$ digits. Make sure the values ​​$n = 1$, $n = 8$ and $n = 9$ are appropriate.

$$A = 10^2 a + 10 b + c$$
where $a, b$ and $c$ are the digits of the number $A$. They need to find $A$ such that $3 A = (10^2 + 10 + 1)(a + b + c)$.
From here
$$37 (a + b + c) = 10^2 a + 10 b + c.$$
Hence,
$$63 a = 27 b + 36 c$$
or
$$7 a = 3 b + 4 c.$$
Let's assume that $b \geq c$. If $7 a = 3 b + 4 c$, then $7 (a - c) = 3 (b - c)$. From here
$$b - c = a - c = 0$$
or
$$b - c = 7, a - c = 3.$$
If $b < c$, then $7 (a - b) = 4 (c - b)$, whence
$$c - b = 7, a - b = 4.$$
(Remember that $a, b$ and $c$ are numbers). We finally obtain the following solutions: numbers $A$, in which all digits are the same (there are nine such numbers); the numbers 370, 481, 592 and the numbers 407, 518, 629 - fifteen numbers in total.
