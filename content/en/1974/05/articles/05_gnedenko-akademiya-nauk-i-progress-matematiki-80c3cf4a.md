The Great October Socialist Revolution of 1917 significantly changed the role of the Academy of Sciences in the life of the country, the form of its work and the development of its activities.

The Academy of Sciences not only began to grow numerically, but, most importantly, it significantly changed its scientific face: it became closer to the vital needs of the country, to the needs of its economy, industry, and culture. The largest research institutions have appeared within the Academy of Sciences, in which, under the leadership of original scientists, research is carried out on the most important problems of science, and issues of special interest for the development of the country are also considered. The necessary expenses were borne by the state. Science became an important part of the life of Soviet society, and the Academy of Sciences took on the responsible function of the leader of Soviet science; as we usually say, it became the headquarters of Soviet science.

But such a restructuring could not be carried out on its own; it required enormous organizational services and the constant attention of party and government leaders. History has preserved in the memoirs of contemporaries and in documents the memory of the heronic days of revolucion and civil war, when the country bled to death and strained all its services to withstand the desperate struggle against the counter-revolution, the interventionist

tami, hunger, devastation. But even in these days, V.I. Lenin and his comrades cared about the scientific and technical progress of the country, about its reaching the forefront in all fields of knowledge. Already in 1918, Lenin not only took measures to preserve the best representatives of Russian ancient revolutionary science, but also to attract them to the side of the Soviet government, to cooperate with the new social system, and to actively solve the scientific problems that faced the country. A characteristic episode directly related to these events is given by M. Gorky in his memoirs about Lenin *).

“I remember I visited him with three members of the Academy of Sciences. There was a conversation about the need to reorganize one of the highest scientific institutions in St. Petersburg. Having seen off the scientists, Lenin said with satisfaction:

He named one of the biggest names in Russian science, and a day later he told me on the phone:

Let us now tell you about another wonderful member of our Academy.

Alexey Nikolaevich Krylov (1863-1945) lived a full and fulfilling life. Mathematics, mechanics, shipbuilding - in each of these areas he combined the talent of a scientist and the energy of an organizer, a high culture of theoretical research and a deep understanding of the role of practical applications.

A. N. Krylov carried out his first scientific work in the compass section of the Main Hydrographic Directorate. These works were related to the study/deviation of magnetic compasses - deviations of the compass needle from the direction of the Earth's magnetic stripe, caused by the influence of magnetized bodies (steel hull of a ship) or electromagnetic fields of ship electrical and radio installations.

For a year, A. N. Krylov worked at a shipbuilding plant. Then he entered the Naval Academy, which he graduated in three years (in 1890), and after that he spent almost 50 years there. At the Academy, he supervised mathematics classes, and from 1892 he also began lecturing on ship theory.

The training courses created by A. N. Krylov constituted a true class of naval science, and his methods for calculating the basic characteristics of a ship and below are the foundation of the theory of the unsinkability of a ship. In this area, A. N. Krylov followed the ideas of the remarkable Russian philologist and scientist Admiral S. O. Makarov.

With the organization of the Mathematical Institute named after. Steklav, the nature of mathematical research in the Academy of Sciences changed dramatically. The time of great lone mathematicians has passed, and the period of large teams working on problems has come that require not only the development of large ideas, but also the systematic work of many scientists on various aspects of one problem. What has been said does not mean at all that the talent of an individual scientist ceased to play a role in the development of science, but it ceased to be the only driving force. More and more, the role has become organized by the collective, whose thoughts and aspirations are directed in one direction, towards certain decisive questions of science.

The influence of the Academy of Sciences extended to the calculation of meaning as they became widely applied to modern research. Scientists working in universities were included in special commissions created by the Academy of Sciences to solve specific scientific problems.

Academic institutions are currently committed to creating important congresses in areas of science, as well as important scientific meetings. The editorial boards of major scientific journals are also concentrated in the Academy of Sciences. From time to time, the Academy of Sciences publishes notes on problems that summarize the results of research to identify the problem and outline prospects for its further development. In academic institutions, capable scientists are engaged exclusively in scientific research. The surrounding team, passionate about scientific research, stimulates research into the most difficult questions of science.

Communication with graduate students and the organization of scientific seminars, access to which is open to everyone, provides constant connections with young people striving for scientific research and who are at the primary stage of forming creative interests and scientific ideals.

The issue of preparing a scientific degree and the wider involvement of young people in scientific research arose with particular urgency in the late twenties, when the country recovered from the devastation caused by the first world and civil wars, and moved on to the development of industry and economic recovery. As a result, already in 1928, and then in November 1929, the Central Committee of the All-Union Communist Party (6) adopted resolutions on expanding and improving the training of research personnel both for the return of assessments of the network of universities and for the Academy of Sciences. The university alone could not cope with this enormous task, and therefore it was completely natural that the Academy of Sciences was involved in its solution. Postgraduate studies at the Academy were organized in 1929, and admissions were announced in the same year. There were 365 applications submitted. The special commission for admission to graduate school, which included leading scientists, accepted only 69 people and accepted all of them into the candidate's graduate school. The next year, 97 people were already accepted (one of them into doctoral studies). In 1931, the Academy already accepted 207 people, and 7 candidates were accepted into doctoral studies.

It must be said that the organization of postgraduate studies at the institutes of the Academy brought substitutive results. Many of its students have grown into leading representatives of science today and have made a significant contribution to the progress of not only their chosen areas of scientific research, but also to the development of many areas of practice. As an example, I dared to give myself the following fact: as a graduate student at the Mathematical Institute. V. A. Steklov in 1934-1937 was M. V. Keldysh, who obtained first-degree results in the theory of functions, the theory of differential equations and aerodynamics. They were taught pages for aviation to go through the phenomena of flutter (oscillations such as the position of a flag in the wind) and entering a spin, and also indicated methods to combat them. These scientific results were widely used by our industry during the Great Patriotic War. Currently, M. V. Keldysh is the chairman of the Academy of Sciences and shows a deep understanding of not only mathematics and mechanics, but also other sciences.

The Great Patriotic War and the subsequent period of restoration of the national economy showed the importance of mathematical methods. The mathematicians of the Academy of Sciences, along with the entire Soviet people, made their significant contribution to the cause of victory. Here we participate in the calculation of new designs, and the compilation of bombing tables, and problems of quality control of industrial products, and assistance in the search for minerals using new methods. The Motherland highly appreciated this work of Soviet mathematicians, including members of the Academy of Sciences.

In subsequent times, mathematicians of the Academy of Sciences took an active part in solving the most important problems related to the restoration of the national economy and strengthening the country's defense. The mastery of atomic energy, the conquest of space, the creation of modern computers and the development of programming methods, solving problems of optimal process control - all these works contain the enormous work of Soviet mathematicians.

Along with this, there is no systematic development of new mathematical research methods, solving problems of mathematical science itself, or constructing its theory.

In recent years, they found out that one institute named after. Glass is no longer enough for the full-blooded progress of Soviet mathematics.

In the depths of this institute, new large scientific institutions grew up - the Leningrad Branch of the Mathematical Institute (LOMI), the Institute of Applied Mathematics, the Computing Center of the Academy of Sciences, the Ural Mathematical Institute. But perhaps the largest event in the development of the Academy of Sciences should be considered the processing of its Siberian branch. The initiator of the organization of this department and its permanent leader is a prominent representative of the theory of functions of complex variables and fluid mechanics - Academician M. A. Lavrentyev.

Representatives of this department participate both in the development of purely applied problems and in solving the most difficult theoretical problems of mathematics. Telery begins a new stage in the development of the Academy - the creation of the Far Eastern Scientific Center.

Mathematicians, who worked and worked at the Academy during the Soviet period of its existence, can look back with pride on the path they have traveled. They made an invaluable contribution to the development of theoretical mathematics and its applications in literally all areas of knowledge. As a result of their works, many areas of mathematical science have significantly developed, a number of them have significantly changed their content, and new directions of research have emerged.

The Academy is strong not only because it employs many outstanding scientists, but also because of its constant connections with universities and other educational institutions of the country. Academic institutes are churning out scientific youth every day.

At 250 years old, the Academy of Sciences is still young, it takes on the most difficult scientific problems and successfully solves them, it constantly accepts teaching in solving pressing problems facing our country, it itself does not remain unchanged, but grows and develops in the same way as our entire country develops.
