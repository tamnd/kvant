Collector's Corner

On stamps -
Academy of Sciences of the USSR

Our country has repeatedly issued stamps dedicated to the USSR Academy of Sciences, its institutes and institutions. The first two such stamps were issued in 1925 in connection with the celebration of the 200th anniversary of the Academy.

These stamps featured a portrait of the first Russian academician, an outstanding scientist of our homeland, M.V. Lomonosov, against the backdrop of the main building of the Academy of Sciences in Leningrad. We will reproduce one of these brands at the top of our selection.

In 1945, in connection with the celebration of the 220th anniversary of the Academy, a series of two stamps was issued. One of them depicts the building of the Presidium of the Academy of Sciences in Moscow, on Leninsky Prospekt. We see the same building on the right side of the recently issued stamp dedicated to the 250th anniversary of the Academy of Sciences.

In 1940, in a series of three stamps dedicated to M.V. Lomonosov, a stamp was issued with an image of the oldest building that belonged to the Academy of Sciences in Leningrad - the famous Petrovskaya Kunstkamera, which for a long time housed a physics office and an astronomical observatory.

The building of the kunstamera is also reproduced on the 1974 anniversary stamp (left side) and on three other stamps shown here. Two of them are dedicated to M.V. Lomonosov. The third stamp was issued in 1957 in connection with the 250th anniversary of the birth of one of the founders of the Academy of Sciences, the outstanding mathematician and mechanic Leonhard Euler.

In 1839, the Academy was enriched with the Pulkovo Observatory, which is now the Main astronomical observatory of our country. It was founded by the Russian astronomer V. Ya. Struve. We see the main building of this observatory on the two stamps presented here. The first of them came out in 1954 for the opening of the observatory, restored from ruins after

The Great Patriotic War, during which the front line of the Geronic defense of Leningrad passed on its territory. The same stamp reproduces portraits of three major Russian astronomers - academicians V. Ya. Struve (center), F. A. Bredikhin (left) and A. A. Belopolsky (right). The second stamp was issued two years later, in 1956. It is dedicated to the 125th anniversary of the birth of Academician F.A. Bredikhin, an outstanding comet researcher.
