⟦folio 28⟧

UC in St. Petersburg, due to the lack of its own scientists, it was decided to attract foreign specialists; The prospect of working in a new large center for science and education was very tempting for many. Among the first to receive an invitation were several mathematicians - a prominent follower of Leibniz, J. Herma, who taught at the University of Frankfurt-on-Oder, as well as young Nikolai and Daniel Bernoulli, friends of Euler. In the autumn of 1725, all three Basel residents were already in St. Petersburg. In his autobography, Euler says that he “was filled with an inexpressible desire to go with him to St. Petersburg in 1725”*). Friends ruled out inviting Euler to the vacant position.

In St. Petersburg there were the most favorable conditions for calculating Euler's genius: material security, the opportunity to engage in any activity, the presence of a daily magazine for publishing works. The largest group of specialists in the field of mathematical sciences in the world at that time worked here, which included, in addition to the mentioned J. Hermann and D. Bernoulli (his brother Nicholas died in 1726), the versatile H. Goldbach, with whom Euler shared a common interest in number theory and other issues, the author of works on trigonometry F. H. Mayer, astronomer and geographer J. N. Del i l, mathematician and physicist G. W. Kraft and others. Since that time, the St. Petersburg Academy has become one of the main centers of mathematics in the world.
