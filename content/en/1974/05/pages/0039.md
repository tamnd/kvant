Thus, Kurchatov’s work immediately fell in line with the development of two pressing problems of contemporary solid state physics: the physics of dielectrics and the physics of magnetism. At this time, Igor Vasilyevich energetically “puzzled” (one of his any expressions) theoretically from Leningrad (Ya. I. Frenkel) and from Kharkov (L. D. Landdu), who were engaged in theoretical magnetism. This cycle of Kurchatov’s works ends with the publication of the famous monograph “Ferroelectricity”, published simultaneously in France.

Since the 30s, as is known, the rapid development of nuclear physics began, which was accompanied by a cascade of major discoveries. This is the discovery of the neutron, positron, artificial radioactivity, etc. Kurchatov will decisively switch his laboratory to work in this promising area of ​​physics.

At this time, at the Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute (LPTI), where Kurchatov worked, there was practically no “culture” of atomic nucleus physics, except for the small laboratory of D. V. Skobeltsyn, which mainly dealt with cosmic rays.

Kurchatov and his group had to start everything practically from scratch. At this time, Igor Vasilyevich spent whole days in the library and studied literature. The “literary” period lasted relatively short. Very soon, experimental work on nuclear physics began in Kurchatov’s laboratory. Quite quickly, the main direction of his interest was determined: artificial radioactivity during irradiation with neutrons. Then one could see a typical picture: Igor Vasilyevich rushes from one end of the corridor to the other with an irradiated image in his hand to study the next short-lived nucleus.

At that time, there was not a single operating cycltoran in the country, and only at the Radium Institute the construction of the first cycltoran with a vertical arrangement was completed.
