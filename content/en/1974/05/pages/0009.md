The USSR Academy of Sciences determines the development of modern physics and ensures the training of scientific workers of the highest qualifications.

To a small extent, it is completely impossible to talk about the achievements of the Academy of Sciences in the field of physics over the years of improvement. Detailed scientific works have already been written about this. In addition, large works in their scientific content are extremely complex and go far beyond the scope of school concepts.

We will discuss here only some of the fundamental research of outstanding modern physicists - members of the USSR Academy of Sciences. At the same time, we are talking about works whose authors are no longer among us. As for the living authors of fundamental physical research below, we hope that they themselves will discuss the properties of the achievement on the pages of our journal.

Despite the large number of directions into which modern physics has been divided, its main sections will remain the physics of the creative body, optics and radio electronics, the physics of the atomic nucleus and elementary particles. In each of these areas, modern physicists have carried out research that has become classic.

We will teach our story from the physics of the created body.

A huge contribution to this extremely important area of ​​physics was made by the work of Academician Abram Fedorovich Yuffe and his students.

Most created bodies have a critical structure. When studying their properties, physicists always

Academician A.F. Yuffe (1880-1960).

strive to use samples that are as close as possible to ideal critical structures and in terms of the degree of chemical purity. This is fully justified, because only with perfect images can unambiguous research results be obtained.

For a long time, physics was dominated by the idea that real critical bodies differ little from ideal ones. The generally accepted theory of critical bodies, developed by Max Born, was based on the idea of ​​an ideal crystal, where every atom is in its place, and any structural disturbances (impurities, internal defects) are completely absent. This theory described many properties of critical bodies (electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, etc.). But as soon as it comes to determining tensile strength, there is a huge discrepancy between theoretical ideas and examinations.
