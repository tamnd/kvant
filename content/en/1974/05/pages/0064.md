$$LK = \frac{RM \cdot PK}{PM} = a \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{2}} < \frac{a}{2}.$$

This means that the edge PR of the tetrahedron (and therefore other edges: PS, QR and QS) intersects the cube.

To calculate the volume, it is convenient to depict a given body as a tetrahedron with medial angles. To determine what part of the volume of the tetrahedron is each of the middle pyramids, it is convenient to use the following statement (prove it yourself), which turns out to be useful quite often.

Given two pyramids SABC and SA$_1$B$_1$C$_1$, and A$_1$ lies on SA; B$_1$ on SB; C$_1$ to SC. Then the ratio of the volumes of these pyramids is equal to the ratio of the products of the lengths of the edges emerging from the vertex S, namely

$$\frac{V_{SA_1B_1C_1}}{V_{SABC}} = \frac{SA_1 \cdot SB_1 \cdot SC_1}{SA \cdot SB \cdot SC}.$$

Now it is easy to find the volumes of the pyramids “cut off” from the vertices P and Q. Each of them is $\frac{PK}{PQ} \cdot \frac{PL}{PR} \cdot \frac{PN}{PS} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{2}} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{2(3 + 2\sqrt{2})}$

part of the volume of the tetrahedron, and the volumes of the pyramids “cut off” from the vertices R and S are $1/16$ the volume of the tetrahedron (check it out!). The volume of a tetrahedron is $\frac{b^3 \sqrt{2}}{12} = \frac{2a^3 \sqrt{2}}{3}$. This means that the volume of the volume of the cube and tetrahedron will be equal to $\frac{2a^3 \sqrt{2}}{3} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{3 + 2\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{8} \right) = \frac{a^3 \sqrt{2}}{12} (16\sqrt{2} - 17)$.
