⟦folio 9⟧

A.F. Ioffe's method became one of the main methods for studying the mechanism of deformation of crystalline bodies.

Continuing these studies, A.F. Ioffe established that plastic deformation occurs in a crystal not intermittently, as all physicists had thought until then, but in an abrupt manner. Under non-permanent load, the deformation occurs in jumps, repeating at regular intervals and even accompanied by faint clicks, reminiscent of a clock ticking.

With these, as well as some other works, A. F. Ioffe laid the foundation for modern ideas about the mechanism of strength and plasticity of real solids. He gave physicists a profound idea about the need to study various defects in the crystal lattice, which extremely strongly influence many of the properties of solids.

Another area of ​​solid-state physics, in which Academician A.F. Ioffe also made a huge contribution together with his students, is semiconductor physics. Today it is difficult to imagine the development of technology without semiconductors. But more than forty years ago, when A.F. Ioffe began a systematic study of the properties of semiconductors, many physicists were critical of this endeavor. At that time, it seemed that only metals and dielectrics were materials worthy of serious physical research. Conductors and insulators are important and necessary technical things, but semiconductors, although they include a large number of natural compounds, are a useless and unpromising material. But A.F. Ioffe brilliantly presented the enormous revolutionary role that semiconductors are already playing in technology today. “It can be suggested,” he said a little later, “that the progress of technology in much smaller quantities makes it possible to become one of the main methods for studying the mechanism of deformation of crystalline bodies.

At first, it was necessary to develop methods for obtaining sufficiently pure semiconductors and methods for determining their basic physical characteristics: the concentration of current carriers, the type of conductivity (electronic or hole), mobility of electrons and holes, etc. Many of these methods, first created by A.F. Ioffe and his students, subsequently became classified.

Ioffe's school carried out a whole series of Püner studies on the electrical, galvanomagnetic, thermoelectric and photoelectric properties of various types of semiconductors.

One of the most important results obtained by A.F. Ioffe and his colleagues was the discovery of the enormous influence of impurities on the electrical properties of semiconductors. A.F. Ioffe showed that the application only changes the conductivity of semiconductors over a wide range, but can even change the current storage device, transforming an electronic semiconductor into a hole one and vice versa. Moreover, the role of application can be played not only by foreign atoms, but also by the semiconductor’s own atoms in case of their excess or deficiency. For example, an excess of atoms in a PbS semiconductor makes this semiconductor an electronic one, and an excess of sulfur atoms makes it a hole semiconductor.

A.F. Ioffe was the first to formulate and experimentally substantiate modern ideas about the mechanism of the rectifying action of semiconductors. He showed that as a result of contact of two semiconductors with different current carriers - electron and hole - a blocking layer is formed (in modern terminology, a “p-n junction”).
