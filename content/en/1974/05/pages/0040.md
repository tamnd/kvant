Mysovsky, and begins a whole series of physical work with him. Mysovsky has been studying natural radioactivity for a long time. Igor Vasilievich took over almost all the management of the work on setting up the cyclotron, and the cyclotron was launched quite quickly.

Work in Kurchatov's laboratory was carried out with maximum intensity. For the chemical identification of artificial nuclei, he attracted his brother Boris Vasilyevich Kurchatov, and very soon radiomic studies were established with indicator (that is, negligible) amounts of substances. In a word, during his years of work in nuclear physics, Kurchatov’s laboratory had, as they say, world-class access.

Soon after the start of work, a monograph by I.V. Kurchatov was published entitled “Disintegration of the Atomic Nucleus” (1935). In this regard, I cannot help but recall the tragicomic event at the institute, one of the heroes of which was the author of these lines. In the early 30s, I needed to use α-particles in aluminum powder, which served as a source of positrons (this was shortly after the Jollu-Curie spouses discovered artificial radioactivity). For this purpose, I used a radio station, which was covered with a thin mica window. The irradiated sample was placed on the CLUDE. To avoid the danger of radion leakage, the role was stored in another room, which was invariably locked. However, by some chance the room was not closed, someone entered it and, apparently by accident, the mica window of the role fell through. The counters in all the institute's laboratories immediately started singing. Got up

panic. Measures were immediately taken: all windows were open, all ventilation was turned on. A few hours later the “accident” was cleared and the meters returned to normal. One could have calmed down on this if it had not been for the introduction of one of the employees, who decided to report this case to the director of the institute, Abram Fedorovich Ioffe. A.F. Ioffe had a highly developed wealth of radioactive radiation. The incident greatly upset him, and he appointed a commission to investigate, find out the causes and determine the consequences. Kurchatov was appointed chairman of the commission. Calculations showed that even if the long-lived decay product of radon - for the sake of E with a decay period of 29 years - from its entire amplitude had settled on the surface of the institute building, the radioactivity would have been an order of magnitude less than the natural background. The commission drew up a corresponding act, which reassured A.F. Ioffe. However, over the course of several months, this event was remembered more than once, and it was reflected in one of the regular skits.

On the monograph given to me, Igor Vasilyevich wrote the following: “To dear Isaac, in fond memory. The book has many flaws. You will notice them yourself; I will point out only one thing that may elude you: nothing has been written about the roles for α-particles and their insidious properties. 20/IV 1935 I.V. Kurchatov.” Apparently, this is an event of the right time in his memory.

Work on nuclear physics was carried out continuously until the war. True, starting from 1937, I could no longer follow him, because I left for Sverdlovsk and practically did not meet with Igor Vasilyeviy. And only at the end of 1942 Kurchatov unexpectedly appeared in Sverdlovsk, came to my laboratory and inquired about what I was doing.
