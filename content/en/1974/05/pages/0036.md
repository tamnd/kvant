⟦folio 34⟧

Euler's 1769 work "On Trajectories" contains brilliant reports on obtaining, using functions of the complex variable from the equation of two mutually orthogonal families of curves on a surface (that is, lines such as meridians and parallels on a sphere), an infinite number of other mutually orthogonal families. This work turned out to be very important in the history of mathematics. In the next work of 1771 “On chalks, the surface of which can be turned into a plane,” Euler proves the significant theorem that any surface that can be obtained only by bending the plane, but not stretching it and not skimming (like a sheet of paper that bends, but hardly stretches), if it is not finite and not cylindrical (that is, it is not obtained by moving a generatrix of a straight line passing constantly through one point or parallel itself), represents a perfect tangent to some spatial curve (its cusp edge).

Equally remarkable is Euler's work on the potato project.

In conclusion of the description of Euler’s geometric works, we fully agree with the statement of the German mathematician W. Kommerya: “The fame and merits of Gauss will not be built if we point out that next to the ideas and methods that Gauss so brilliantly used in the “Disquisitiones generales” (though often only in a special form or only incompletely formulated), there is already Euler, for example, the spherical mapping (when the map of the surface is put in correspondence, a piece of a sphere of radius 1, consisting of all points at which the radius of this sphere is parallel to the normals to the surface at the points of this piece), defining the surface in parametric form, the coincidence of linear elements as a condition for superposition during bending, the study of geodesic lines (that is, the shortest lines on a surface between two of its points) using the angle that they form with the curves of a certain family on the surface, and others.”

One can imagine how revealing Euler's work on the curvature of surfaces and developable surfaces would have been for mathematicians of that era. The works in which Euler studies surface mappings that preserve similarity in the small (mapping conformals), based on the theory of functions of a complex variable, should seem downright transcendental. And the work on polyhedrals began a completely new part of geometry and, in its principles and depth, stood alongside the discoveries of Euclid.

About Euler's work on analysis

There are so many of these works, and they are so comprehensive, that we do not decide to give their descriptions here. Let's just add that he simplified and completed the entire large departments of the analysis of infinitesimals, interpretation of functions, the theory of series, differential equations, which had already been started before him, so that they acquired approximately the form that remains to a large extent with him and to this day. Euler, in addition, began a whole new chapter of analysis - the calculus of variations. This initiative of his was soon picked up by Lagrange and a new science emerged.

Euler's influence on the development of mathematics in Russia

Practically all creative working mathematicians in Russia experienced the fruitful influence of Euler. The style and direction of Euler's work had a certain influence on the entire further development of mathematics.
