Alexey Nikolaevich Krylov was a Teacher in the true and high sense of the word. He created a school of shipbuilders, from which many remarkable scientists emerged: V. L. Pozdonin, P. F. Papkovich, Yu. A. Shimansky and others.

A. N. Krylov was also a remarkable inventor; he built the first machine in Russia for integrating differential equations, and a number of important ship and artillery instruments.

And, of course, A. N. Krylov is remembered by us as a wonderful citizen, public figure, and patriot. Even in the expensive years, he began to apply the achievements of science to those in need of the country's defense: from 1900, A. N. Krylov was in charge of the Experimental Pool for testing ship models; in 1908-1910, he was the Chief Inspector of the shipyard and chairman of the Marine Technical Committee; in subsequent years, he advised at the Metal, Putilov, Obukhov and other plants. His work unfolded on a new scale after the Great October Socialist Revolution, a year before which A. N. Krylov was elected a full member of the Academy of Sciences. In 1919, A. N. Krylov became the head of the Maritime Academy. In 1921-1927, A. N. Krylov, as part of a commission to renew scientific ties, was in a foreign team, carrying out an important task for the Soviet government: he supervised the construction of ships ordered for the Soviet fleet. Upon Alexey's return, Nikolaevich continued to lead the Maritime Academy, actively participating in solving the most important technical issues of military and civilian access. During the Great Patriotic War, this multifaceted activity of A. N. Krylov acquired particular importance.

In 1943, for outstanding achievements in the development of Soviet science and defense affairs, Alexei Nikolaevich Krylov was awarded the title of Hero of Socialist Labor, and he was awarded the Order of Lenin three times. Nowadays the Naval Academy in Leningrad bears the name of A. N. Krylov.

With the organization of the Mathematical Institute named after. Steklav, the nature of mathematical research in the Academy of Sciences changed dramatically. The time of great lone mathematicians has passed, and the period of large teams working on problems has come that require not only the development of large ideas, but also the systematic work of many scientists on various aspects of one problem. What has been said does not mean at all that the talent of an individual scientist ceased to play a role in the development of science, but it ceased to be the only driving force. More and more, the role has become organized by the collective, whose thoughts and aspirations are directed in one direction, towards certain decisive questions of science.

The influence of the Academy of Sciences extended to the calculation of meaning as they became widely applied to modern research. Scientists working in universities were included in special commissions created by the Academy of Sciences to solve specific scientific problems.
