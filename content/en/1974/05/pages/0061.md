Problem 2. Given two equal regular triangular paramidas of volume $V$, located symmetrically relative to the point $O$. Find the volume of the common part of these two paramidas if the point $O$ lies at the height of one of them and divides this height in a relative ratio of 2:1, counting from the paramid.

In this task, it is extremely important, although quite difficult, to make a correct, good, well-founded drawing. Let $SABC$ be the first paramid. The base $A_1B_1C_1$ of the second paramid intersects the height $SH_1$ at the point $H$, dividing it in a relative ratio of 1:2 (counting from the vertex $S$), and at the intersection with the paramid $SABC$ gives a triangle $MNP$, similar to the triangle $ABC$, $MN = \frac{1}{3} AB$.

From here it is easy to deduce (check!) that the triangle $MNP$ is entirely inside the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$. Similarly, inside the base $ABC$ there will be a triangle $M_1N_1P_1$, which is a section of the second paramid by the plane of the triangle $ABC$.

Now we can depict a body that is a common part of the two paramidas under consideration (Fig. 4), and the position, for example, of the point $L$ is as follows: we need to connect the point $S$ with the middle of the edge $BC$, the intersection of this line with the straight line $S_1A_1$ gives the point $L$ (for proof, consider the section of the paramids by the plane $SA_1S_1$).
