L.D. Landdu theoretically predicted that free electrons (conduction electrons) in a metal should create an additional magnetic moment in an external magnetic field, directed in the direction opposite to the direction of the external magnetic field, that is, a diamagnetic moment. This is the so-called Landdu diamagnetism.

We do not stop here with the classic work of L. D. Landdu on the theory of superfluidity of a liquid body. It is discussed in detail in the article by A.F. Andreev («Kvant», 1973, No. 10).

Moving on to work on optics, we will focus only on the replacement research of academicians Dmitry Sergeevich Rozhdestvensky, Leonid Isaakovich Mandelstam and Sergei Ivanovich Vavilov.

Academician D.S. Rozhdestvensky developed a report on the technological preparation of optical glass, thanks to which already in 1925 the Soviet Union completely abandoned the purchase of such glass in other countries. He made very important contributions to the study of resonant absorption of light by Parmichel metals and the development of spectral analysis. He significantly improved the microsop theory.

For a long time, physics could not give the correct answer to such a seemingly simple question: “Why is the sky blue?” Even Newton, who devoted many years of hard work to this problem, was never able to solve it. Another English physicist, Rayleigh, was the first to create a satisfactory theory of light scattering in the atmosphere. Assuming that the light scattered by the molecules was not affected, there was a good agreement with the observational results.

It would seem that everything is clear, the problem is completely solved. And only one physicist, Academician L.I. Mandelstam, did not agree with this interpretation. He proved that the true cause of the blue color of the sky is density fluctuations (random changes in air density relative to the average density) occurring under the influence of thermal motion. L.I. Mandelstam showed that Rayleigh's formula is correct, and the physical essence of the light scattering picture has been improved. This work was published back in 1907. It is one of the first works on the theory of fluctuations, that is, microsoptic deviations of a particular physical quantity from the average value.
