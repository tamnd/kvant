The idea of ​​​​creating an office scientifically justified itself, and after two years V. A. Steklov decided to submit a note to the bureau of the physics and mathematics department with a proposal and a statement justifying the need to organize a specialized scientific institution at the Academy of Sciences - the Institute of Physics and Mathematics. In this note he wrote: “Not one of the natural sciences, if it is not a matter of collecting raw material, but of actual creativity, can do without mathematics, the mother of all sciences. As for physics, placed ahead of all other sciences, as follows in the project of the Lomonosv Institute, at present mathematics and physics have merged into one whole to such an extent that it is sometimes difficult to separate where mathematics ends and physics begins" *).

V. A. Steklov’s proposal was unanimously supported both in the physics and mathematics department and in the Predium of the Academy of Sciences. As a result, in the same 1921, in response to the “ripening need in science to technically connect the physical sciences with the purely mathematical,” the Institute of Physics and Mathematics of the Russian Academy of Sciences was founded. V. A. Steklov was elected director of the institute. The Institute was organized on the basis of a Mathematical Office, a Physics Laboratorium and a Seismic Network.

The Institute of Physics and Mathematics existed until 1934, and then it was divided into two independent institutions - the Mathematical Institute. V. A. Steklov *)
