It hardly needs mentioning that after the discovery of uranium fission, this was the most burning question that interested all physicists. At the 1940 nuclear conference in Moscow, the weight of uranium fission was discussed with the active participation of Kurchatov. On his initiative, a note from the government was drawn up, which pointed out the importance of this problem and the need to organize extensive research in this area.

At the beginning of 1943, the organization of work on the practical use of the phenomenon of uranium fission was formalized by a government act. Joint activities with I.V. Kurchatov began in a new port, in a new role. It is clear that a more pressing physical problem could not be imagined at that time. Of course, the problem of protecting ships from magnetic mines, which was then dealt with by Igor Vasilievich, was also very relevant. It is known that the work of A.P. Aleksadrov and I.V. Kurchatov in this area saved the lives of many thousands of sailors. But the problema of uranium did not tolerate any differences. Thus began the directed epic of solving the practical problem of creating atomic weapons. At first, only a few people were involved in direct work. Soon Igor Vasilievich attracted major theorists (Ya. V. Zeldovich, I. Ya. Pomeranchuk, etc.). The sphere of influence was divided quite quickly. Problems not directly related to nuclear physics were entrusted to me:

as Kurchatov put it, “you are a specialist in studies” (he jokingly called all work not related to the core, study physics). A. I. Alikhanov himself and A. I. Alikhanov deal with issues of nuclear physics.

A hectic organizational period began, when it was necessary to gather people, obtain premises and equipment. We were temporarily provided with premises in Pyzhevsky Lane and at the Institute of Inorganic Chemistry on Kaluzhskaya Street. And again Igor Vasilyevich could be seen running with irradiated targets from one end of the corridor to the other. It seemed that we were back at LPTI. Along with this work, Kurchatov performed enormous organizational work. We hung out on Pyzhevsky until late at night.

It was once said that it was necessary to prepare reports on the work program, indicating deadlines for the practical solution of problems. We set about compiling such a report - each in our own way. And one evening they introduced me to the government. Each one also reported on his part. Each report contained a clause indicating the time frame for obtaining practical results. As you know, these deadlines were met.

At this time, Igor Vasilyevich organized work not only on the creation of the institute. Theorists and experimenters mutually trained in the basics of future nuclear technology. The main problems associated with the practical task that had been posed were collectively discussed. Everyone, especially Kurchatov, felt great about the team. More worrying was the question of whether Nazi Germany would overtake us. There was no certainty that Germany had managed to avoid occupying the uranium gap.
