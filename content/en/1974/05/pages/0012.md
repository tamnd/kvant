The analogy between electric and magnetic fields has been known for a long time. There is also a well-known formula analogy between magnetic and dielectric constants. But for a long time, among dielectrics, a substance with an anomalously high dielectric constant, similar to the anomalous magnetic permeability of ferromagnetic materials, was not discovered. Only in the twenties were several such substances found, including Rochelle salt. Their research was carried out by academician Igor Vasilyevich Kurchatov and his colleagues. Within a short time, their work laid the foundation for a new field of dielectric physics called ferroelectric. It turned out that the dependence of the electric moment of the Rochelle cols on the external electric field is the same as the dependence of the magnetized ferromagnet on the magnetic field: the same histeresis loop is observed. The Curie point (there were two of them) and the phenomena accompanying the transition through this point were studied. In a word, the electrical version of the long history of the study of ferromagnets was reproduced.

In the history of Soviet physics, the name of Academician I.V. Kurchatov is inextricably linked with the solution to the great problem of creating domestic atomic and thermonuclear weapons and the development of nuclear energy.

You will find a story about this research in the article by Academician I.K. Kikon, “He Wasted a Happy Life,” published in the same issue of our magazine.

In modern physics of the creative body, theoretical physics plays an important role with its complex mathematical apparatus. The crystalline body, which was previously viewed as a system consisting of vibrating atoms and molecules, is now viewed as a volume filled with background gas. Phonons are called quanta of the field of a bulb lattice - by analogy with phonomes, quanta of an electromagnetic field. The phonon, like the photon, behaves in many respects as if it were a particle; therefore they are often called quasiparticles. The honor of introducing the concept of “phonon” into science belongs to academician Igor Evgenievich Tamm, who in 1930 used the concept of “elasticity quantum” when considering the issue of light scattering in crystals.
