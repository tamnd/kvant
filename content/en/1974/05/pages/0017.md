Over the past decades, people of non-existence have been widely used in various fields of science and technology. Based on it, new methods for chemical and varietal analysis of various substances have been developed - the so-called logistic analysis. In the development of these practical applications, the logistics industry belongs to S. I. Vavilov. But the work on the creation of logistical light sources, which opened a new stage in the history of lighting technology, is especially important.

The main source of light these days, overlay light bulbs, is very important. The efficiency of these lamps does not exceed 8%. A large 90% of the energy is lost or creating invisible infrared radiation. The spectral composition of visible light from these lamps differs significantly from the composition of sunlight, to which the human eye is most adapted. The temperature of the tungsten filament in the lamp was 2200-2300°C. To obtain light close to sunlight and increase light output, it would have to be raised to 6000°C. However, long before this, the light bulb filament will be scattered.

The disadvantages of electronic overlay lamps allow scientists to look for new, more economical and convenient light sources. Logistics lamps turned out to be such sources. Logistic substances are light transformers. They can transform one type of light into another, for example, invisible ultraviolet rays, that is, useless in lighting technology, into visible ones, or homogeneous light into wide spectral bands of the most varied states.

S.I. Vavilov was the first to propose powerful ultraviolet radiation from mercury lamps to produce visible light using logistic substances. Following this path, he created logistics lamps and fluorescent lamps.

The main part of Vavilov's logistic lamps is a gas-discharge tube filled with ruthium vapor at low pressure. Electronic passages through the tube during discharge excite ultraviolet radiation from mercury. If a discharge is observed through the walls of the tube, you will notice that its interior glows with a faint blue light. The main share of mercury radiation is concentrated in the ultraviolet region. To convert it into visible light, a layer of “light transformer”—crystalline logistics powder—is applied to the inner wall of the tube. By using various powders, one can obtain frontal light. Of greatest practical interest are powders whose candlelight is close to the daytime scattering of sunlight (for example, daylight under a cloudy sky). The efficiency and average lamp life are significantly longer than usual.

Let us now move on to work in the field of atomic nucleus physics.

Atomic nuclei include charged particles - protons, brought together to a distance of the order of 10⁻¹³ cm. It would seem that they should immediately collapse under the influence of the enormous power-plant repulsion of protons from each other. But we know that this does not happen. Only very heavy nuclei of elements at the end of the periodic table are unstable, and this leads to radioactive decay. What gives such incredible strength to atomic nuclei? Atomic nuclei are significant only because powerful nuclear forces act between all the particles contained in them.
