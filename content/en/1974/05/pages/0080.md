4. We will carry out the proof by contradiction. Let us assume that all the balls are removed and placed into $N$ boxes such that each box contains no more than three red balls and no two boxes contain the same number of balls. Let us number the boxes in increasing order of the number of balls in them and denote by $n_i$ the number of balls (white and red in total) in the $i$-th box.
Indefinitely, from what has been said it follows that the following relation is valid:
$n_1 \geqslant 1, n_2 \geqslant n_1 + 1, n_3 \geqslant n_2 + 1, \dots, n_N \geqslant n_{N-1} + 1,$ from which we conclude that the estimates $n_1 \geqslant 1, n_2 \geqslant 2, \dots, n_N \geqslant N$ hold.
Adding all these inequalities and remembering that $n_1 + \dots + n_N = 5100$, we get
$5100 \geqslant \frac{N(N+1)}{2},$

whence $N \leqslant 100$. But, on the other hand, $N \geqslant 100$, since under this condition it is possible to sort 300 red balls without placing more than three red balls in each box.
