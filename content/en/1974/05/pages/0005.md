These ideas of the present and the consistent were put into practice. The Soviet government immediately recognized the need to support the Academy of Sciences. On April 9, 1918, the Secretary of the Council of People's Commissars N.P. Gorbunov, on behalf of V.I. Lenin, informed the leadership of the Academy that “The Council of People's Commissars considers it extremely desirable to have the opportunity to broadly develop the scientific enterprises of the Academy and invites the Academy to bring to the attention of the Council about the existing assumptions of experimental research and experimental work. And all this was done in the most difficult time for the young Soviet republic, during the civil war, when the situation at the fronts was desperate, and devastation paralyzed economic life in the country.

At the same time, V.I. Lenin compiled a “Sketch of a plan for scientific and technical work” - the first step towards long-term planning for the development of Soviet science. In it V.I. Lenin put forward the idea of ​​a great economic development of the country with the direct teaching of science. This document begins with the words: “Aka-

demi sciences, which has begun a systematic study and examination of the natural derivative forces of Russia, should immediately be given an order from the Supreme Council of the National Economy...”

New tasks required new forms of organization of science, primarily the creation of a large number of scientific institutes that united the rest of the country's scientists and prepared new scientific personnel from talented youth. Even during the life of V.I. Lenin, the Institute of Physics and Mathematics, the Institute of Physiochemical Analysis, and the Institute for the Study of Platinum and Other Precious Metals were organized at the Academy of Sciences. At the same time, the Physicotechnical Institute, now named after its founder A.F. Ioffe, the State Optical Institute named after D.S. Rozhdestvensky, the Radium Institute named after V.G. Khlopin, the Central Agrohydropinmic Institute named after N.E. Zhukovsky, the State Institute of Applied Chemistry, the Institute for the Study of the North and a number of other large scientific centers were organized under other departmental departments. Subsequently, the activities of these institutes were closely connected with the Academy of Sciences.

V.I. Lenin closely followed the work of the Academy of Sciences on the study of the Kursk magnetic field and the natural resources of the Kola poles. They provided great assistance to the great Russian physiologist Academician I.P. Pavlov and the outstanding mechanic, corresponding member of the Academy of Sciences N.E. Zhukovsky. He attracted the greatest scientists, future academicians G. M. Krzhinzhanovsky, G. O. Graftya, V. F. Mitkovch, and the future corresponding member of the Academy of Sciences M. A. Chatelain to participate in the development of the plan for the electrification of Russia (the GOELRO plan).
