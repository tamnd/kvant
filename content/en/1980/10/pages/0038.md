F607. The plane flies on a closed route ABC. Points A, B and C lie at the vertices of a regular triangle. In which case will the time spent on the flight be less: if the wind blows in the direction of vector AB or if the wind blows in the direction of vector BA?

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

Let $l$ denote the side length of triangle ABC. If the speed of the vector is $u$ and it blows in the direction $\overrightarrow{AB}$, then along the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ the plane flies at a speed $u + v$ (where $v$ is the aircraft speed module) and will take $A \rightarrow B$ time to fly
$$t_1 = \frac{l}{u + v} = \frac{l}{v(1 + d)},$$ where $d = \frac{u}{v}$.

Along vector $\overrightarrow{BC}$ the plane flies at speed $\overrightarrow{v}_1 = \overrightarrow{v} + \overrightarrow{u}$ (Fig. 1). Since the vector $\overrightarrow{v}_1$ is directed along the vector $\overrightarrow{BC}$, the modulus of the vector $\overrightarrow{v}_1$ is equal to the sum of the projections of the vectors $\overrightarrow{v}$ and $\overrightarrow{u}$ onto $\overrightarrow{BC}$, and the sum of the projections of these vectors onto the axis perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{BC}$ is zero:
$$v_1 = v \cos \beta - u \sin \alpha,$$
$$v \sin \beta - u \cos \alpha = 0.$$
From here, taking into account that $\alpha = 30^\circ$, we get
$$v_1 = v \sqrt{1 - \frac{u^2}{v^2} \cos^2 \alpha} - u \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2}v \sqrt{4 - 3d^2} - \frac{1}{2}d.$$
The same p. module will be the speed on the CA section. Therefore, the plane will take time to fly $B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$
$$t_2 = \frac{2l}{v_1} = \frac{4l}{v \sqrt{4 - 3d^2} - dv}.$$
The total time $t$ of flight $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$ is $t_1 + t_2$.
If the wind blows along vector $\overrightarrow{BA}$, then flight $A \rightarrow B$ is completed in time
$$t'_1 = \frac{l}{v - u} = \frac{l}{v(1 - d)}.$$
The speed in sections BC and CA in this case (Fig. 2) will be equal in magnitude
$$v'_1 = \sqrt{v^2 - \frac{3}{4}u^2} + \frac{1}{2}u = v \sqrt{1 - \frac{3}{4}d^2} + \frac{1}{2}dv,$$
and the flight $B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$ will take time
$$t'_2 = \frac{2l}{v'_1} = \frac{4l}{v \sqrt{4 - 3d^2} + dv}.$$
The total time $t'$ of flight $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A$ is equal in this case to $t'_1 + t'_2$. The time ratio of $t'$ and $t$ is
$$\frac{t'}{t} = \frac{1}{1 + d} + \frac{4}{\sqrt{4 - 3d^2} + d}.$$
Carrying out algebraic transformations, we obtain
$$\frac{t'}{t} = 1.$$
