All-Russian Olympiad for SCHOOLCHILDREN

From March 24 to 27, 1980, the final stage of the VI All-Russian Olympiad in Mathematics and Physics took place in Kalininka, Kursk, Ufa and Irkutsk.

To hold the All-Russian Olympiad, an Organizing Committee was created under the Ministry of Education of the RSFSR, which, as last year, was headed by the famous Soviet mathematician Academician V. S. Vladimirov.

The All-Russian Olympiad is held in four stages: the first stage - school Olympiads; the second - district (city); third - republican (ASSR), regional and regional; fourth - zonal Olympiads.

In the fourth stage, one team each takes part (from three schoolchildren from eighth to tenth grade) from the Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, territories, region, specialized boarding schools at Moscow, Leningrad and Novosibirsk universities. (Teams from Moscow and Leningrad do not participate in the fourth stage.) The teams are made up of the winners of the third stage. In addition to them, the winners of the final stages of the previous All-Union and All-Russian Olympiads, who received diplomas of I and II degrees, as well as the winners of the magazine competition «Kvant», participate in the zonal Olympiads.

This year, the fourth stage competition task in mathematics consisted of 5 problems, the solution of which took 4 hours. In addition, all participants were given a programmed task drawn up by the methodological commission of the USSR Ministry of Education. The task included 10 questions on the school curriculum, each of which was accompanied by a set of 5 answers (see «Kvant», 1980, No. 9, p. 47). The students had to choose the correct answer.

At this stage, the most prominent were students from specialized boarding schools No. 45 at Leningrad State University, No. 18 at Moscow State University, No. 165 at NSU, representatives of schools in the Bashkir and Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republics, Krasnoyarsk and Primorsky Territories, Belgorod, Volgodsk, Irkutsk, Kemerovo, Moscow, Perm and Ulyanovsk regions.

The competition task of the fourth stage in physics included 4 theoretical problems (4 hours were given to solve them) and 2 experimental problems (3 hours were given to complete them). All the proposed tasks turned out to be feasible for the students. At least 10 participants completed each task.

Below is the assignment for the final stage in mathematics and physics and the names of the winners of the All-Russian Olympiad.

Matematika

8th grade

1. They decided to seat the group of tourists in buses so that each bus had the same number of passengers. At first, 22 people were seated in each bus, but it turned out that it was not possible to seat one tourist. When one bus left, all the tourists boarded the remaining buses equally. Find out how many initial buses there were and how many tourists were in the group, if it is known that each bus can accommodate no more than 32 people.

2. Let us place 2n points on the segment AB symmetrically relative to the middle of the segment. Some n of them are colored, the rest are blue. Prove that the sum of the distances from all beautiful points to point A is equal to the sum of the distances from all beautiful points to point B.

3. ABCDEH is a regular hexagon, M is the midpoint of the segment CD, K is the midpoint of the segment DE, O is the intersection point of the segments AM and BK. Prove that the areas of triangle ABO and quadrilateral MDKO are equal, and find the angle between lines AM and BK.

4. How many different numbers appear in the sequence

$$\left[ \frac{1^2}{1980} \right], \left[ \frac{2^2}{1980} \right], \left[ \frac{3^2}{1980} \right], \dots, \left[ \frac{1980^2}{1980} \right],$$

where $x$ is the integer part of the number $x$?

5. From an arbitrary point M circumscribed about triangle ABC, circles are drawn perpendicular MH and MK to lines AB and AC, respectively (points H and K lie on lines AB and AC, respectively). For each point M, the length of the segment HK will be the greatest?

9th grade

1. Is it possible to write all natural numbers from 1 to 30 in a table that has 5 rows and 6 columns, so that:

a) were the sums of the numbers in each column the same?

b) were the sums of the numbers in each column the same?

2. List all natural numbers $n$ for which the number $2^8 + 2^{11} + 2^n$ is the square of a natural number.

3. Each vertex of a convex $(2n+1)$-gon is colored in one of three colors
