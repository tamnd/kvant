Among the numerous speeches there was also a greeting from a representative from Kharkov, who warmly thanked Abram Fedorovich for his efforts to create a new physics and technology institute in the capital of Ukraine (at that time Kharkov was the capital of the Ukrainian SSR). The core of this institute was a group of LPTI employees led by Ivanov Vasilyevich Obremov and Alexander Ilch Leipunsky. This was the beginning of the implementation of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe’s idea of ​​the need to create scientific physical centers in large industrial regions of the Soviet Union.

Abram Fedorovich repeatedly explained to us the need for science in the USSR. “It is impossible,” he said, “for science to concentrate in Moscow and Leningrad. Soviet physics is obliged to exert its influence on the development of the country’s industry.” Following the Ukrainian Physico-Technical Institute (UPTI), there were organizations of physical institutes in Dnepropetrovsk, Tomsk and Sverdlovsk. Groups of highly qualified physicists from among the LPTI employees - Ioffe's students - were sent to all these institutes.

The address that the LPTI staff offered to Abram Fedorovich was denied that this address was signed not only by a huge number of his direct students, but also by students of the students of his first students, who by that time had managed to become quite good physicists. This was not said for the sake of rhetoric, but reflected the actual state of affairs. Looking ahead, I can say that even during the life of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe, among his students there were at least 15 academicians and about 30 corresponding members of the USSR Academy of Sciences. I don’t know if there is anyone else from the teachings of the world who can boast of the best results in their own country.

Abram Fedorovich was indeed not only an outstanding scientist, but also an excellent educator. This is explained, first of all, by the fact that he had the secret of selecting talented young employees who quickly became major scientists. In addition, Abram Fedorovich managed to create a working atmosphere at the institute in which each employee felt involved in a huge, important matter, created the need for his work, and felt responsible for it. People worked enthusiastically, not wanting strength. At the institute it was possible to find employees for work at any time of the day or night. And this was the normal style of work of the institute.

Our passion for experimental work was confirmed by Abram Fedorovich who reproached us for spending a lot of time doing experiments and spending too little time reading scientific literature. He even entrusted his great friend Yakov Ilch Frenkel, head of the department of theoretical physics at the institute, with organizing a seminar on modern theoretical physics specifically for experimenters.

But let's return to the celebration of the 10th anniversary of the institute. Abram Fedorovich loved and highly valued a good joke. That is why, at the end of the ceremonial eating, we staged what is now called a skit party. More precisely, it was a play that we wrote specifically for the anniversary and was called “10 years that shocked physicists.” We rehearsed this play in secret, so only the participants knew about it. The play consisted of 10 acts, consistently reflecting each year in the life of the institute from the day of its creation. We, the participants, really wanted to know the reaction of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe, who did it in the front row. The play was a great success: the critics, including Abram Fedorovich, laughed almost continuously and rewarded the actors with stormy performances.
