Electron

One of the constituent parts of the atom, the negatively charged electron, was discovered back in the 19th century. In 1897 it was possible, albeit roughly, to measure the charge of an electron; later its mass was measured and even later its other properties were discovered - rotational and magnetic moments.

When illuminated, especially with ultraviolet light, a negatively charged body gradually loses its charge, and a neutral one becomes positively charged. This phenomenon, called the photoelectric effect or, in short, photoelectric effect, was studied by Stoletov back in the 19th century. The nature of the charges emitted by the body during the photoelectric effect - electrons - is especially clearly visible in the experiment I conducted in 1912, which was a modification of previously used devices.

Small charged metal dust o fell into the space between two parallel horizontal plates A and B (figure 1). If the particle turns out to be negatively charged, then it can be kept from falling by charging the top plate positively and the bottom plate negatively.

A positively charged dust particle is held by a field of the opposite sign. It is possible to select such a potential difference $U_1$ between the plates so that the electric force just balances the force of gravity. Then the speck hangs motionless for many hours.

The electric force that acts on the charge $e_1$ of the dust is directed upward and is equal to $e_1 \frac{U_1}{d}$, where $d$ is the distance between the plates $A$ and $B$. Let us denote the force of gravity acting on the dust particles by $P$. The condition for the equilibrium of dust in the space between the plates is the equality of gravitational force and electrical force:

$$P = e_1 \frac{U_1}{d}.$$

In this state, I illuminated the pollen with a light source that caused the photoelectric effect. After some time, the previously motionless dust began to fall. Since the gravity $P$ could not change due to illumination, $U_1$ and $d$ did not change, then, consequently, the charge $e_1$ decreased to some value $e_2$.

By increasing the potential difference $U_1$ to a new value $U_2$, it was possible to stop the dust again; in this case the equality $e_2 \frac{U_2}{d} = P$ must be satisfied.

By charging and discharging the dust again, this process could be repeated hundreds of times, each time at the corresponding potentials $U_1, U_2, U_3, \dots$. It turned out that the various charges $e_1, e_2, e_3, \dots$ that the dust had were precisely expressed by the following relationship:

$$e_1:e_2:e_3:... = \frac{1}{U_1}:\frac{1}{U_2}:\frac{1}{U_3}:... = 1:2:3:...$$
