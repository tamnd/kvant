In particular, Abram Fedorovich was criticized for the fact that he developed research in nuclear physics at the institute, which, according to the speakers, did not promise practical applications even in the distant future. According to the same reports, he was also criticized for the development of work in the field of semiconductor physics. Now it’s clear to everyone how wrong Abram Fedorovich’s critics were, how ridiculous their argument was. The current generation must pay tribute to the scientific disgrace of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe, which allowed him to promptly formulate and pose such pressing problems as the physics of the atomic nucleus and the physics of semiconductors - the foundations of the modern scientific and technological revolution.

It was not easy for Abram Fedorovich to leave the post of director of the LFTI in 1950, which he founded and which he led for more than 30 years. Moreover, this departure was left rather matter-of-factly. Shortly before this event, we celebrated Abram Fedorovich’s seventies. The ceremonial establishment of production in the assembly hall of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Leningrad was deliberately modest organizationally. Of the hundreds of greetings and addresses sent to the hero of the day from all over the country and from abroad, only three were read: from the Presidium of the USSR Academy of Sciences, from employees of the institute and the party district. The latter itself was limited to the scientific report of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe “On the problems of semiconductors.”

In the evening, Abram Fedorovich invited his closest students to his home. At dinner, Abram Fedorovich told us that, despite everything and even his age, he does not look back at the past and is optimistic about the future. He shows us honorary diplomas awarded to him by various organizations, including foreign scientific institutions and universities. Among them were diplomas of an Honorary Member of the English Physical Society (1944), the University of Paris (Sorbonne, 1946), the University of Bucharest (1947), the University of Graz (Austria, 1949), etc.

I especially liked the diploma of an Honorary Member of the Chinese Society of Physics (1949), designed in the form of a scroll of white silk wide ribbon, on which the text of the diploma was written neoglili-famine (of course, with a Russian translation attached to it).

What Abram Fedorovich said that he looked into the future with confidence and optimism was not a “red word”. Indeed, soon after leaving the post of director of LPTI, he, with his characteristic energy, threw himself into organizing the Institute of Semiconductors of the USSR Academy of Sciences. In this he was greatly supported by the Presidium of the Academy of Sciences and its President Serey Ivanovich Vavilov. Thus, the scientific work of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe was not interrupted for a single day.

In 1955, on the eve of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe’s 75th birthday, a group of academic physicists, his students, sent a letter to the government with a request to award A. F. Ioffe the title of Hero of Social Labor. To justify our request, we listed the greatest scientific achievements of Abram Fedorovich and especially noted his exceptional role in training highly qualified scientific personnel who ensured a quick and timely solution to the problem. In particular, we pointed out that among Abram Fedorovich’s students there are already 15 academicians and about 30 corresponding members of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

To celebrate the anniversary, the Academy created an organizing committee, and I was honored to become its representative. Naturally, as a representative, I was very concerned about the government’s reaction to the aforementioned letter asking for the award of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe.
