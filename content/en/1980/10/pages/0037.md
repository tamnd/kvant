F605. A wire fuse blows if the direction across it is 10 V. In what direction will the fuse blow if its length l is increased by n times, and its diameter d by k times.

If the direction U to the fuse (and the current flowing through it) is such that the temperature of the conductor is equal to T (for example, the melting point), an amount of heat is released in the conductor

$$Q_1 = \frac{U^2}{R} = \frac{U^2}{\rho \frac{l}{s}} = \frac{\pi U^2 d^2}{4 \rho l}$$

(R - resistance, q - resistivity of the conductor, s = πd²/4 - cross-sectional area). The amount of heat released to the environment

$$Q_2 = aS(T - T_{cp}) = a\pi dl(T - T_{cp})$$

(S = πdl is the surface area of ​​the conductor, T_{cp} is the ambient temperature and α is the proportionality coefficient). At thermal equilibrium, Q₁ = Q₂, that is

$$\frac{\pi U^2 d^2}{4 \rho l} = a\pi dl(T - T_{cp})$$

From here we find the direction U on the conductor at which it is heated to temperature T:

$$U = 2l \sqrt{\frac{\alpha Q(T - T_{cp})}{d}}$$

With conductor length l₁ = nl and conductor diameter d₁ = kd, the temperature of the conductor is equal to T in the direction

$$U_1 = 2ln \sqrt{\frac{\alpha Q(T - T_{cp})}{kd}}$$

Thus,

$$U_1 = U \frac{n}{\sqrt{k}}.$$

I. Slobodetsky

F606. The puck slides without initial speed along an inclined plane with angle α. The friction coefficient between the washer and the surface of the inclined plane changes with the distance l from the top according to the law μ = kl (k = const). At what distance from the top should the stop be placed so that after a single-zone elastic collision with the stop, the puck stops as high as possible?

Let at point A, located at a distance l₀ from the top of the inclined plane, the friction force acting on the washer be equal to (see Fig.) |F̅τ| = m |g| sin α, where m is the mass of the washer. Since at point A the friction coefficient is μₐ = kl₀, at this point |F̅τ| = μₐ |N| = kl₀ m |g| cos α, that is

$$m |g| sin α = kl₀ m |g| cos α,$$

where

$$l_0 = tg \alpha/k.$$

It is easy to show that the maximum possible height of the puck rising after one impact on the stop is the height h₀ corresponding to point A. In order for the puck to stop at point A after the impact, it is necessary that when rising after the impact the puck had a speed of zero at point A.

Let's find at what distance l from the top of the inclined plane the stop must be installed so that the puck stops at point A after the impact. Let's write the energy comparison law (see Fig.):

$$mgH = mgh_0 + F_{cp.1}l + F_{cp.2}(l - l_0).$$
