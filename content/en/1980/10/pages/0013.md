Solvay Congress Committee (from left to right): N. Bohr, A. Einstein, T. de Donder, O. Richardson, P. Langevin, P. Debye, A. F. Ioffe, B. Cabrera. 1931

The play actually had many funny and poignant moments. So, for example, two departments that financially belong to our institute were depicted in the form of kings sitting on thrones with the inscriptions “Tsar-Narkompros” and “Tsar-Sovnarkom”. For added importance, they were dressed in the doctor's robes of Abram Fedorovich himself. The actors portraying the management of the institute tried their best to please the rulers, showing various tricks, such as turning water into blood, iron into a snake, etc. For their zeal, they were rewarded - they received a bag of coins. The play ended with couplets performed by the actors sitting in a rocket heading into space to meet the Academic Council. Each verse ended with a refrain

"Higher and higher and higher
We're flying on a rocket into the air.
We will fill it with physical education soon
The entire stellar, transcendental world."

Inspired by the success of this play, we introduced into the tradition the organization of such evenings twice a year: on the May and October holidays. And they invariably enjoyed success with the institute’s staff. Abram Fedorovich Ioffe himself often took part in the pieh.

I remember one of these evenings, at which one rather significant event in the life of Soviet scientists was celebrated. I mean the decree issued in 1933 on the introduction of academic degrees and titles. To us, a young researcher, it seemed to contradict the two constructions of socialism, since the resolution provided for material benefits for a scientific degree or title, and not for the work directly performed.
