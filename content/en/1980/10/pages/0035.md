⟦folio 33⟧

M601. Prove that in any triangle ABC, the midpoint of side BC lies on the segment connecting the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle ABC with the point of the circle circumscribed about this triangle, diametrically opposite to vertex A, and bisects this segment.

Let H be the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle ABC, A' be the point of the circumscribed circle, diametrically opposite the vertex A (Fig. 1). Let's connect point A' with vertices B and C. Triangles ABA' and ACA' are rectangular (the angles at vertices B and C are based on the diameter), and therefore [A'B] || [CH] and [A'C] || [BH]. This means that the quadrilateral BA'CH is a parallelogram. Its diagonals [BC] and [A'H] at the intersection point are divided in half, from which the statement of the problem follows.
