M603. Solve the system of equations
$$\begin{cases} x + \frac{3x-y}{x^2+y^2} = 3, \\ y - \frac{x+3y}{x^2+y^2} = 0. \end{cases}$$

Let's multiply the first equation of the system by $y$, the second by $x$ and add these two equations. We get
$$2xy + \frac{(3x-y)y-(x+3y)x}{x^2+y^2} = 3y$$
or
$$2xy - 1 = 3y.$$
Hence $y \neq 0$. That's why
$$x = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2y}.$$
Let us substitute the resulting correspondence into the second equation of the original system; We get
$$y \left[ \left( \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2y} \right)^2 + y^2 \right] - \left( \frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2y} \right) - 3y = 0$$
or
$$4y^4 - 3y^2 - 1 = 0,$$
whence $y^2 = 1$, that is $y_1 = 1, y_2 = -1, x_1 = 2, x_2 = 1.$

L. Kuptsov

F604. Under conditions of weightlessness, a liquid placed in a glass cylindrical vessel with a base radius of $R_1$ took the shape shown in figure 1. The free surface of the liquid had the shape of a sphere with a radius of $R_0$. The same liquid, placed in a glass spherical vessel of radius $R_2$, took the shape shown in figure 2. The free surface of the liquid was flat. Determine the height of the liquid level in a spherical vessel. What shape will the liquid have in a spherical vessel if the radius of the vessel is greater than $R_2$?

The contact angle, which is formed at the point of contact of three media by the surfaces of these media (tangent to these surfaces at a given point), is determined by the merging interactions between the molecules in these media and does not depend on the force of gravity. Therefore, the contact angle must be the same in zero gravity and on Earth, and it is this that determines the shape of the surface of the liquid in a spherical vessel at the points of contact of the three media (in our case, liquid, solid and gas).

Fig. 2.

From figure 1 and 2 it is clear that
$$\cos \alpha = \frac{R_1}{R_0} \text{ и } \cos \alpha = \frac{h}{R_2}.$$
From here we find $h = R_1R_2/R_0$. Therefore, the height of the liquid level in a spherical vessel is equal to
$$H = h + R_2 = \frac{R_2}{R_0} (R_1 + R_0).$$
In vessels with radii $R < R_2$ and $R > R_2$ the liquid levels will be higher and lower, respectively, than in a vessel with radius $R_2$. In any case, at the point of contact of the three media, the contact angle is equal to $\alpha$.
