I especially remember the meeting at which the work of Dayton Miller was discussed, who repeated Michelsan’s significant experiment and allegedly obtained the exact opposite result, which contradicted the special theory of relativity. It must be said that in those days there were still disputes about the validity of Einstein’s special theory of relativity. I, and not only me, but all those who are now sixty, remember very well the exhaustive debates on this topic. Both foreign and Soviet physicists were opponents of this theory. Naturally, they seized on Miler's work as further "proof" of the failure of the theory of relativity. And I remember well the speech of Abram Fedorovich Ioffe at the meeting, where, having made a similar analysis of Miler’s experimental setup, he subjected it to careful criticism and proved the inconsistency of the conclusions made by the author. Subsequently, Abram Fedorovich repeatedly defended Einstein's theory of relativity from the attacks of its opponents.

But let's return to the congress of physicists. On the second or third day, the entire congress, which was approximately 150 people, was transported by special train to Nizhny Novgorod. Several meetings were held at Nizhny Novgorod University. Then the congress participants were placed on the ship, and the visas began sailing along the Volga. The work of the congress did not stop for a single day. The meetings took place both on the ship and at stops. In Kazan and Saratov, meetings took place within the walls of universities. And all the time we felt that our beloved Abram Fedorovich Ioffe was the soul of the congress. We saw what kind of author he used not only among his Soviet colleagues, but also among foreign physicists.

During the voyage, stops were made not only for business messages, but also simply for individuals in very picturesque places on the Volga. Here, on the section, we observed Abram Fedorovich in a completely different environment. We were disgusted to see him organizing outdoor games; for example, composing in a race in which he himself took part. It would not be amiss to note that Abram Fedorovich was a good runner and quite often beat younger opponents.

After the end of the official part of the congress, its participants traveled to the Caucasus. On the very first day of the journey, Joffe, Paul, Debye and other equally famous scientists came to visit our student campus. As I remember now, Abram Fedorovich offered us a game that consisted of the following. The participants of the game, sitting in a circle, began to take turns naming the famous scientists. The first participant names one surname, the second calls the already named surname and adds another one, and so on. The first person to leave the game is the one who forgets at least one named surname. Thus, the number of players continuously decreases until one winner remains, with the best memory. Among the well-known names, of course, were the names of sites in the physicists' game; The funny thing is that they were among the first to drop out of the game, because they forgot to say their own names.
