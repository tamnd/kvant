True, we were somewhat embarrassed by a certain “sloppiness” of the physical installations themselves. It seemed to us that they were not assembled beautifully and elegantly enough, but we did not dare to make critical replacements in this regard.

Finally, Abram Fedorovich led us to the laboratory, where there were installations on the tables, looked at with greater care, where complete order reigned. In these laboratories we did not meet a single living soul, which surprised us a lot. Abram Fedorovich saw the surprise on our faces and hastened to explain what he saw in a slightly writhing volume: “You are not surprised by the cleanliness and order in these laboratories. The fact is that no one is working here now - the employees are on vacation.” After these words, we realized that during intensive scientific work, people pay more attention to existing affairs, and not to external beauty and grace. I will note right away that at that time all physicists, regardless of their level, were engaged in unspecified experimental work, that is, installing the installation, assembling circuits, starting with Academician Yuffe himself and ending with the student who had just entered the laboratory. Any experimental physicist at that time had to be a jack of all trades: a mechanic, a glassblower, an electronics engineer, and a carpenter.

It goes without saying that after this experiment it seemed almost unattainable to all of us to become one of the institute’s employees.

...I will remember 1928 for the rest of my life. This year, the sixth in a row, the All-Russian Contest of Physicists took place. The organizer of the meal was Academician Yuffe.

Typically, the participants in the meetings were already mature physicists who had to make financial contributions, which were relatively significant, at least from our student point of view. Therefore, it is natural that the students did not get eaten, but... But this meal was special!

Firstly, the government allocated quite a significant amount for organizing the meal. Secondly, the world's leading physicists were invited to the meal, and many of them accepted the invitation. There are approximately 20 foreign delegates from Germany, France, England, USA, Holland, Poland, Czechoslovakia; Among those who came there were also world-famous physicists such as Debye, Brillouin, Dirac, Lewis, Paul, Born... So this meal was practically international. Thirdly, the program carried out by the eatery was unusual: the eatery will open in Moscow, but the main part of its work will be carried out on a specially chartered ship, which will sail down the Volga from the city of Nizhny Novgorod (now Gorky). It is clear that among the students there was only talk about this miracle food. We didn’t even dare to dream about beating on it.
