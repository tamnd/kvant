A useful endeavor

According to the Decree of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR, since 1969, at the calculation of educational institutions, preparatory departments have been opened for working and rural youth, demobilized soldiers of the Soviet Army. The very fact of the existence of preparatory departments suggests that their services have certain problems in knowledge; Therefore, the primary task of the preparatory departments is to eliminate these problems, with the help of the service to recapture lost knowledge. Many services graduated from high schools before the introduction of new programs, therefore, preparatory departments must quickly master the new material necessary for further successful training in universities.

To successfully solve these problems, preparatory departments must have a wide arsenal of high-quality teaching aids, specially written for their service.

Unfortunately, until now, most of the textbooks that are used by preparatory departments, in particular in mathematics, are either self-education manuals or university preparation manuals written according to the old program.

The radical changes that school mathematics programs have undergone are well known, and it is natural that the lack of full-fledged textbooks for mathematics courses has a negative impact on the work of preparatory departments.

In 1979, the publishing house "HIGH SCHOOL" published a textbook by A. Mordkovich*) in mathematics, specially written for preparatory departments.

This book consists of 11 chapters. The first chapter, called “Real Numbers,” is introductory. In addition to mathematics associated with real numbers, it contains elements of set theory and elements of mathematical logic. Here we are talking about the simplest transformations of entire heterononal expressions, about linear equations with one and two variables, about linear inequalities with one variable. The main title of this chapter is to introduce the reader to the basic ideas of algebra at the simplest level, to give him material for practical studies, without waiting for a more detailed and thorough development of the theory, which is done in the following chapters.

The second chapter is devoted to transformations of algebraic expressions (integer, fractional, irrational), and the fourth chapter is the solution of algebraic equations (integer, fractional, irrational) and systems of such equations. The third chapter is about functions. The concept of function in the book is based on the concept of correspondence between a set. The basic properties of functions are determined (parity, periodicity, monotonicity), and then specific functions are studied (linear, power, trigonometrice). The difficult concept of an inverse function is clearly and accurately introduced and, on its basis, inverse trigonometric functions and a power function with any different exponent are studied. This chapter discusses in detail various transformations of graphs, and ends the chapter with a paragraph on graphing solving equations, inequalities, and systems of equations.

The fifth chapter is called “Basic formulas of trigonometry and their applications.” It is constructed as follows: the addition theorem is formulated, and then from the formula

$$\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin \alpha \cos \beta + \cos \alpha \sin \beta, \cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos \alpha \cos \beta - \sin \alpha \sin \beta$$

All other trigonometry formulas are displayed. The resulting formulas are used to transform expressions containing trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions, to prove trigonometric points and solve trigonometric equations.

The sixth chapter is devoted to inequalities. Here, the method of inverses for solving heteronomous inequalities with one variable is presented, systems and sets of inequalities are introduced, and inequalities and systems of inequalities with two variables are considered. A separate paragraph is devoted to presenting methods for proving inequalities.

The seventh chapter is called “Sequences.” It contains the method of inverses for solving multivariate inequalities with one variable; systems and sets of inequalities are introduced; inequalities and systems of inequalities with two variables are considered. A separate paragraph is devoted to the method of mathematical induction.

The eighth chapter contains a presentation of issues related to the concepts of a continuous function at a point and the limit of a function.
