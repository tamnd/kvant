Repetition of the experiment of Michelson and Morley

All the above conclusions of the special relative theory are based on the fact that the speed of light in a vacuum does not depend either on the speed of movement, the source, or on the speed of movement observed. It is said to be a fundamental constant.

This important fact was first tested experimentally by the American physicist Michels in 1881, and in 1886 the experiment was carried out again, this time together with Morley, also an American scientist. Since then, the experiment of Michelson and Morley, repeated many times, has gone down in the history of physics as one of the most important experiments*).

Michelson and Morley discovered that the speed of light propagation along the direction of the Earth's motion around the Sun and perpendicular to this direction is the same. More precisely, from the experiments it could be concluded that the ratio of the “longitudinal” speed of light $v_{11}$ (according to the movement of the Earth) to the “transverse” speed $v_{\perp}$ (perpendular to the movement of the Earth) differs from

$v_{11} = 1 \pm 10^{-5}$.

Recently, two physicists - Brillet from France and Hall from the USA - built a new installation to test the experiment of Michelson and Morley, in which the light source is a laser. They managed to significantly increase the accuracy of the experiment and obtained that

$$\frac{v_{11}}{v_{\perp}} = 1 \pm 10^{-15}.$$

To date, this experiment represents the most accurate test of the basic construction of the special theory of relative surface.
