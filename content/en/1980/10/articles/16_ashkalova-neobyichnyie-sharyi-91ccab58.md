Three reports at the Batumi festival

For active participation in the Festival of Young Mathematicians, a number of schools were awarded a subscription to «Kvant» for 1981 (see «Kvant» No. 9). Among them is Tbilisi school No. 96. This school is not a mathematics school, but an educational secondary school. A mathematics museum has been created at the school and is open. math club, schoolchildren publish a math magazine. The reports of the eighth-graders of this school (team leaders - mathematics teacher L. A. Shteingari and O. E. Shamanlov) were very well and carefully prepared. We bring to your attention a brief summary of these reports sent by L.A. Shteingari.

Cutting shapes into congruent pieces

We will say that the figure $F$ is cut into parts $A$ and $B$ if $A \cup B = F$ and $A \cap B = \emptyset$ (cutting into three parts, into four parts, etc. is defined similarly).

At figure 1 the line is cut into two congruent parts (A is the union of “red” segments without left endpoints, B is the union of “blue” segments without left endpoints). Prima can be easily cut into any number of congruent pieces.

A ray (both with and without a beginning), a circle, a sine curve, a plane and an angle can be cut into two congruent parts.

But the segment cannot be cut into two congruent parts (assume the opposite; then $A$ is mapped onto $B$ by central symmetry or parallel translation; using Weierstrass’s theorem on the existence of a limit, one can arrive at the contradiction).

It is also impossible to cut a circle and a square into two congruent parts.
