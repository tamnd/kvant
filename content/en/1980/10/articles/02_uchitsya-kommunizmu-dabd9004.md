Considering communism!

This happened on October 2, 1920. In Moscow, in the building where the Lenin Komsomol Theater is now located, the Third Congress of the Russian Communist Youth League was held. 602 delegates from all over Russia took part in it. On this day, Lenin addressed them with his significant speech “Tasks of Youth Unions.”

The Civil War has not yet ended. Devastation and hunger reigned supreme throughout the entire territory of the country conquered from the enemies. And Lenin spoke about communism, about how to build a future communist society, in the name of which severe sacrifices were made.

This speech is not very long, and you all have read it. But have each of you, our dear young readers, tried to think about its content, to comprehend the depth of Lenin’s thoughts? There are probably those among you who “got through” this speech, just as he “got through” mismo many sections of the general school course. And this speech must be studied, and studied thoughtfully, leisurely, and not repeatedly. After all, in it Lenin brought to the youth his most precious and bright thoughts - thoughts about an amazing and beautiful society that human history had never known before.

Lenin did not embellish or conceal anything. The path to communism is difficult for any country, even a very highly developed one. And it is a hundred times more difficult for an industrially backward, undeveloped country, the majority of whose population does not know either the alphabet or the multiplication table. And this is exactly what Russia was like in 1920.

The construction of communism is associated with a deep qualitative restructuring of the entire economy and the consciousness of all members of society. An abundance of material goods, an abundance of free time from work are possible only on the basis of extremely electronic, complex automated production, based on the greatest achievements of science and technology. And only a comprehensively educated, harmonious physical and spiritually developed person who continuously improves himself can manage such production.

It would seem that for Russia in 1920, communism is an incredibly distant future. But Lenin spoke about it as the most important thing in the lives of those who did in the hall where the congress took place. He spoke of it as a historically inevitable reality.

Revolutions are not limited to destruction and recognition; they never destroy everything that was conquered by mankind in the past. Revolutions only take into account what has lost its right to exist at a given point in time, what prevents society from moving forward, what slows down its development. And having taken this into account, they open the way to something new, immeasurably more progressive. A revolution is not only a gigantic destruction, but also an even more gigantic construction one.

In 1920, it seemed to many in Russia that, by destroying the old social system, it was necessary to throw into the dustbin of history the entire history of science, art and education and together create a small continuation of science, art and education in all its parts. Remember the futurists’ slogan: “Let’s throw Pushkin, Dostoevsky, Tolstoy off the ship of modernity!”? But Lenin clearly knew that the future is born from the past, and therefore, after the overthrow of the old social system, it is necessary to continue the gigantic work of critically reviewing all past human achievements.

As you know, Marxism united in itself all the best, what to this

time was created in German classical philosophy, English political economy and French utopianism. But in order to accomplish this gigantic work, Marx had to deeply master all these ideas and theories.

Vladimir Ilyich Lenin was also an outstanding scientist. Then he understood perfectly well that the workers and peasants of our country were faced with an incredibly difficult task - to transfer the entire past experience of man, to reflect from it everything that is still progressive, to develop and assimilate all this in relation to new historical conditions, new tasks of society. It would not be difficult to completely destroy the old bourgeois education system. But, as Lenin said, “to be able to distinguish what was necessary in the old school and useful to us, ... to be able to extract from it what is necessary for communism” - this task was much more difficult. Only those who had mastered this science and culture well, studied this art and the educational system could be able to extract what was necessary for communism in the past in science and culture, art and education. Hence the main task set by Ilyich in his speech - to study, study and study!

But you can learn in different ways. You can simply accumulate new knowledge in your memory, turning it into a kind of encyclopedia. Lenin was a simple opponent of such “dead knowledge”, bookish universality. It was in this speech that he uttered the famous phrase: “You can become a communist only when you enrich your memory with the knowledge of all the riches that a person has produced.” Knowledge of all riches, that is, the basic funcatal laws of nature and society, is, according to Lenin, the path to a fully developed person.

But these riches should not lie like a dead weight in human memory. They must become a means of everyday struggle to build a communist society. Building this society means every day, every hour, solving some small, modest, but specific and useful tasks. And Lenin gives examples of such tasks that he follows every day: to study ourselves and teach others - to help eliminate illiteracy; create community gardens to help fight hunger; Organize cleanliness in a village or in your neighborhood to help in the fight against an epidemic disease. “The Union of Communist Youth must be a shock group that provides assistance in all work, shows its initiative, its initiative.” A person gains a lifetime of constant experience in practical activities. Without borba you won’t become borscht. The one who builds communism is the one who builds, and does not sit back and wait for everything to be done without his participation, but he will come to the ready.

In his remarkable speech, Lenin gave a clear answer to all the main questions facing the country and its young generation: what is a communist, how to become a communist, what and how to study, how to create communist morality, how to learn to place the interests of society above your own interests. This was the complete program of the Komsomol for the entire future. And everything that it contains is true today. Of course, many of the specific problems cited by Lenin as examples have already been solved. But communism has not yet been built. And every student should become an active participant in this construction. And this, as in the previous year, means deep mastery of the basics of scientific knowledge, active participation in social work, every day at school, at home, on the street, solving specific problems related to the formation of a new person and the construction of a new society. And today the main task of all Soviet youth is to teach communism!
