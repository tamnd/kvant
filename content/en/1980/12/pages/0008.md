If, having done work against the forces of interaction between the pointer and the magnetic field, the pointer is deflected at a certain angle and released, then under the influence of these forces it will return to its original position after several oscillations. This equilibrium position is stable because it corresponds to the minimum value of the arrow's potential energy - in any other position its potential energy is greater by the amount of work spent on changing the initial orientation.

According to quantum concepts, atoms placed in an external magnetic field experience only two orientations of their own magnetic fields: either “along the field” (like magnetic needles in the Earth’s magnetic field) or “against the field.” If the orientation “along the field” corresponds to the energy atoma $E_1$, then the orientation “against the field” will correspond to a slightly larger value of the energy atoma $E_2$ (just as the energy of a magnetic needle taking “against the field” is greater than the energy of a magnetic needle spontaneously oriented “along the field” by the amount of work spent on this reorientation).

In other words, placing the target atom in an external magnetic field causes the main energy level to expand into two sublevels with energies $E_1$ and $E_2$, with $E_2 > E_1$ (figure 6).

Transitions of atoms $E_2 \rightarrow E_1$ (from sublevel $E_2$ to sublevel $E_1$) are accompanied by radiation, and transitions $E_1 \rightarrow E_2$ are accompanied by absorption of waves with frequency $v_0 = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{h} = \frac{\Delta E}{h}$. The energy difference $E_2 - E_1$ is not large, and the frequency $v_0$ lies in the radio wave range.

The value $\Delta E$ depends very little on external influences. Therefore, radiation with frequency $v_0 = \frac{\Delta E}{h}$ can be used as a reference. But to implement this in practice, it is necessary to “sort” the atoms by energy.

Due to the fact that an electric field has no effect on neutral atoms, and a uniform magnetic field can only cause the orientation of the magnetic fields of atoms, “sorting” of atoms by energy is done using an inexpensive magnetic field. As can be seen from figure, atoms oriented “along the field” will be drawn into the region of greater induction, and atoms oriented “against the field” will mix in the direction of less induction. The mixing is caused by the inequality of forces acting on the “northern” and “southern” parts of the atoms ($|\vec{f}_1| > |\vec{f}_2|$).
