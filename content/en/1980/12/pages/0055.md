Ten-class student Mikhail Epiketov (Alma-Ata, 1st degree diploma) developed the SONAR system for debugging programs on EC computers. To speed up debugging, many languages ​​and programming systems (for example, OS EC, ALGOL - BURROWS) provide the ability to monitor the meaning of various names, calls to procedures, etc., but in the DOS EC operating system the programmer must organize debugging himself. To do this, you can put a print statement after each assignment in the program and use the printout to see how to use the program, but the size of the program then doubles, and when you throw out additional print statements, new errors are often “introduced” into the program. The SONAR system allows the programmer, with the help of one or two operators, to organize debugging printing for the entire program or specified sections of it and also simply “turn off” this printing after finishing debugging.

Numerical machines built on large inter- nal circuits have become widespread in a variety of areas in recent years. Microprocessors control the research apparatus of spacecraft and the largest rolling mills, determine the exposure during photography, regulate the operation of automatic engines and pocket calculators. Of course, the quality of their work depends on the management of the immi program. It is often necessary to understand such a program, understand the underlying algorithm, and perhaps make the necessary changes. But understanding an unfamiliar program written in machine commands is not at all easy: to do this, you need to replace the commands with their non-monic notations, operate with comments, separate commands from data, break the program into separate blocks and develop connections between them, etc. All this work is performed by the kit created by Sergey.

In the second section, Olga Khoroshevskaya (Novosibirsk, 9th grade) received a first degree diploma, who spoke about optimizing the structure of a computer network connecting several computer centers. Having built a detailed model of the network operation in the ALGOL-GDR language and successfully applied stochistic optimization (Page's method), Olya showed that of the several options for organizing communication between computer centers, the most outstanding one is the ring structure.
