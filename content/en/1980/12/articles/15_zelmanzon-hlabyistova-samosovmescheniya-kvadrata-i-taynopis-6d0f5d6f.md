How is encryption done?

Let's take an “empty” square $8 \times 8$ (Fig. 4) and a lattice of the same size - a square in which some cells are cut out (an example of a lattice is at figure 2). Let's put a lattice on the square. In the cells located under the “windows” we will write the beginning of the message that we want to encrypt. Then we apply some self-alignment to the lattice square. If the lattice is made “well”, the cells of the square, being under the “windows” for the first time, will close and new cells will appear under the “windows”. Let's write in them the continuation of the encrypted message. Then we put the lattice in its original position and apply another self-alignment to it. And so on.

When “decrypting” we put a lattice on the “filled” square (for example, on figure 1) and apply the same sequential self-alignment that was used during encryption, we read the encrypted message “piece by piece”.

Each specific lattice is made for a certain specific set of (ordered!) self-alignments. For example, the lattice on figure 2, which we used in the lesson, is made for the set $R^{270^\circ} = R^{-90^\circ}$ (after all, we turned the lattice clockwise), $R^{180^\circ} = R^{-180^\circ}, R^{90^\circ} = R^{-270^\circ}$.

In order to use a lattice during encryption or decryption, you must, of course, know “its” set of self-alignments.
