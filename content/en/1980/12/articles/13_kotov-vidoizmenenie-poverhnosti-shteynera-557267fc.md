Modification of the Steiner surface

The cover of the first issue of «Kvant» for this year featured a computer-generated drawing of a Steiner surface. In a note on this subject, readers were asked to draw and describe surfaces obtained from the Steiner surface by replacing the zero on the right side of its equation by ±0.001, that is, the surface

$$x^2y^2 + x^2z^2 + y^2z^2 + xyz = \pm 0,001.$$

Ten-classicist Oleg Morozov from Chelyabinsk sent us his drawings (see figure 1 and 2) and also a description of the surfaces: Surface $x^2y^2 + x^2z^2 + y^2z^2 + xyz = 0,001$ is two-sided, open, since it has “outgrowths” near the coordinate axes that go to infinity. Sections of the surface by coordinate planes are Zigrov (12 branches in total). The “nadins” on the surface do not touch each other and the coordinate axes (...).

The surface $x^2y^2 + x^2z^2 + y^2z^2 + xyz = -0,001$ consists of four congestive closed non-intersecting parts, which also do not intersect any coordinate plane. The “nadins” on it seem to intersect each other and disappear at the intersection points.

Fig. 3.

The editors contacted the author of the note, Yu. Kotov, asking him to comment on this letter. Yuri Vladimirovich, in turn, turned to his colleague, the EC-1022 computer, with a request to draw the required surfaces. As a result, the computer presented two drawings (figure 3 and 4), and the author of the note responded to our reader like this:

Dear Oleg, you basically correctly imagined the surfaces in question and depicted them quite convincingly, noting their basic geometric properties. In our opinion, if we consider the time-varying surface $x^2y^2 + x^2z^2 + y^2z^2 + xyz = T$, here $T$ is a parameter, the moment of transition “through zero”, in which the Steiner surface appears, is interesting. At this moment, the endless “outgrowths” “disappear” and the surface breaks up into four layers.

But we need to make some clarification: the four parts into which the surface at $T < 0$ is located are the main ones, they do not have the edges depicted in Vaschy figure. There are no edges separating the “nadins” from the rest of the surface for $T > 0$. The cross-section of the “outgrowths” approaches when sufficiently distant from the origin of coordinates.

Fig. 4.

Yu. Kotov
