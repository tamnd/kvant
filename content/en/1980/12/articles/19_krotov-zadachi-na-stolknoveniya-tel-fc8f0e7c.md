tel

Quite often, during entrance exams, applicants are offered problems that involve the collision of bodies (sometimes a collision is called an impact, or impact, or dispersion). Generally we are talking about the collision of two bodies; if the colliding bodies are larger, their interaction can be represented as a set of pairwise collisions.

Before solving any problem in physics, and in particular the collision problem, you need to understand which phenomena considered in the problem play the main role, and which phenomena can be neglected. In accordance with this, it is necessary to select an idealized picture and establish for it the applicability of the corresponding physical laws.

The process of collision of bodies can be divided into three stages: the first stage - the bodies collide with each other at constant speeds and at some point collide with each other; the second stage (the collision itself) - interaction forces appear between the bodies, which act for a certain period of time; third stage - bodies spread out at new speeds that do not change over time. The general formulation of the collision problem assumes that given the initial velocities of bodies, their final velocities are determined (or vice versa). In this case, the stage of the collision itself is not actually considered, but only its nature is discussed: the impact is elastic or inelastic, central or non-central, etc.

As an example, consider an elastic blow. Let a small body of arbitrary shape collide with another body. When they come into contact, the bodies begin to deform. The resulting elastic deformations are transmitted from one part of the body to another at the speed of sound (that is, quite quickly), and different parts of the body receive different speeds. Finally, the deformation pulses reach the opposite boundaries of the bodies, are reflected from them, and the bodies bounce off each other like compressed springs. What happens to the initial reserve of mechanical energy of bodies?

Due to the uneven speed of movement of different parts of the body, vibrations will occur; they take away a certain energy, which, when the vibrations decay, turns into heat. In addition, any oscillating body becomes a source of sound waves, which also carry away some of the energy. If the impact is non-central, that is, if the relative speed of the colliding bodies does not pass along the line of the centers of the bodies, the bodies begin to rotate. The energy required for this is also drawn from the kinetic energy of the translational motion of the body. Rotation can also be caused by frictional forces. However, if the ratio of losses to the initial supply of mechanical energy is small, all irreversible transformations of mechanical energy into other types of energy are neglected and the impact is considered completely elastic. The idealized picture of such an impact is as follows: the kinetic energy of the translational motion of the colliding bodies is partially converted into the potential energy of elastic deformations; potential energy grows until the speeds of movement of both bodies are equal, and then it turns into kinetic energy of propagation of the body. Exercises

1. On the path of a body of mass $m$, sliding along a smooth table with a speed $\vec{v}$, there is a stationary unfixed slide with mass $M$ and height $H$ (the profile of the slide is chosen at figure 6). Determine the speeds of the body and the slide after the body leaves the slide. The body moves without leaving the slide; there is no friction between the body and the slide.

2. At what angle $\beta$ do two identical smooth balls fly apart after an absolutely elastic impact? Before the impact, one of the balls was buried, and the other was moving at a speed $\vec{v}$, directed at an angle $\alpha$ to the line of the centers of the balls.

3. A ring with radius $R$ and a small ball inside the ring can move along a smooth table surface. At some point the ball elastically collides with the ring. Before the impact, the ring is buried, and the ball moves at a speed $\vec{v}$, which makes an angle $\alpha$ with the radius drawn to the point of impact. Find time until the next strike.

4. Two smooth identical balls of mass $M$ each accumulate on a smooth horizontal surface, touching each other. The third ball, also of radius, but with mass $m$ moves along the surface with a speed $\vec{v}$ passing through the point of contact of the stationary balls. Determine the speed of the balls after an absolutely elastic impact.
