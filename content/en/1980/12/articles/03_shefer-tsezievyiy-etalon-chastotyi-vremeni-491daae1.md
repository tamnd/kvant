Any scientific research - both in the field of the microworld and in space - all derived processes - from the manufacture of paper clips to the construction of an atomic icebreaker - are ultimately based on changes. Advances in technology and expansion of scientific research require increasingly sophisticated methods of change. For example, with the advent of supersonic aviation, airport control services have to deal with millionths of a second. The study of atomic and nuclear processes requires changes in time intervals thousands of times less than billions of fractions of a second.

The measurement of physical quantities is essentially a property of their comparison with the corresponding stages. Therefore, the accuracy of a physical experiment depends on the accuracy with which the basic units of measurement are established.

First stage of time

Unlike length and mass measurements, which can be made repeatedly, a time interval process measurement cannot be repeated and time cannot be changed by non-predictive comparison with a stage. Time can be judged by changes in the world around us, for example, by the movements made by a body moving rectilinearly and uniformly. The measurement of time can also be based on a periodic process, for example, a uniform expression, a pendulum swing.

Due to the fact that the movement of bodies does not occur in empty space and forces are always acting on them, absolutely uniform movements and ideally strict periodic processes do not exist in nature. Hence the inevitable inaccuracies in any method of changing time.

The Earth-Sun system was adopted as a sample “clock” from which all clocks on the globe are checked. The reference points for reporting time intervals are taken to be the moments in time when the Sun is in a certain position relative to the earthly observer. The first time step was chosen to be the daily expression Earth. The time interval between two successive upper positions of the center of the solar disk (culminations) is called the true solar day. However, the length of these days varies throughout the year (the movement of the Earth around the Sun is uneven). Therefore, to report time, it was decided to use the average solar day, the long-term state of which was agreed to be considered equal to the average duration of the true solar day for the year. The unit of time - a second - is taken to be 1/86400 hours of an average solar day. To store time in the intervals between astronomical observation and reproduction of time units - seconds - pendulum clocks are used. In the best examples of particularly accurate watches, the change in rate during the day is ~0.001 seconds, which corresponded to a relative error of $\frac{\Delta t}{t} \approx 10^{-8}$.

Quartz watch

The development of radio electronics makes it possible to create more accurate time keepers - quartz watches, which use elastic oscillations of quartz instead of a pendulum.

When a quartz plate, cut in a certain way from a crystal, is deformed, opposite charges arise on opposite faces. The magnitude and sign of the charge on the face depends, respectively, on the magnitude and direction of the deformation (figure 1). This phenomenon, called the direct piezoelectric effect, is reversible: if a potential difference is applied to a piezoquartz plate, then the plastic, depending on the polarity of the voltage, will either collapse or expand. Such a plate, connected to an alternating voltage source, performs forced oscillations with a frequency equal to the frequency of electrical oscillations. When the frequency v of electrical oscillations coincides with the natural frequency v₀ of oscillations of the plate, the amplitude A of its elastic oscillations will be greatest (figure 2). The natural frequency of the plate, determined by its size and shape, weakly depends on temperature and external influences.

From the point of view of radio engineering, a piezoquartz plate is equivalent to a general resonant circuit containing inductance and em-capacity, but differs from it in a very sharp resonance. This property of piezoelectric quartz has long been used in radio engineering to stabilize the frequency of generators in radio transmitters. The same property of piezoquartz—high constancy of its own frequency of oscillation—was used to create astronomical quartz clocks. The main component of such a clock is the electric generator.

Comprehensive stage

Observations of a group of pendulum clocks and the first quartz clocks in 1937 showed that the length of the day changes abruptly on average per year by 0.002 s and at the same time gradually increases by 0.0016 s per 100 years *). This means that the maximum accuracy of determining the second using a clock “the Earth rotating around its axis” does not exceed $\frac{2 \cdot 10^{-3}}{86400} \approx 0,2 \cdot 10^{-7}$.

In 1956, the International Bureau of Weights and Measures decided to move to a more stable standard of time - the rotation of the Earth around the Sun. As this, use the duration of the tropical year - the period of time between two successive passages of the center of the Sun through the point of spring equilibrium. Since the length of the tropical year is not strictly constant due to the disturbance exerted by the planets on the Earth's movement, one of them was chosen - 1900.

Why are atomic clocks more accurate than terrestrial and quartz clocks?

Unlike the macrocosm, where there are not even two completely identical bodies, in the microcosm all parts of the same type, for example electrons, are indistinguishable. Atoms, consisting of the same number of particle elements, are also completely identical. Molecules that are the same combinations of the same number of the same atoms also turn out to be perfect.

Identical atoms or molecules have identical and exclusively constant sets of natural frequencies of vibrations; they can emit or absorb electromagnetic waves of the same and strictly defined lengths, depending only on the structure of a given type of particle. It is important that these generators of electromagnetic vibrations are not subject to wear and tear.

This is why it was so tempting to use atomic systems to standardize frequency (time) and length. Since the behavior of microparticles is determined by the laws of quantum mechanics, etons created on the basis of atomic systems are called quantum.

The first quantum ethon was the ethon of length. It turned out that the international standard of length - a meter - contains 1650763.73 wavelengths of radiation that arose during the transition between two specific states of the krypton atom ($^{86}$Kr).

It would seem that the standardization of frequency and the establishment of a unit of time should be carried out along the same orange line of krypton-86, which is used to establish a unit of length. However, it is more convenient to use radio frequency monitoring, since this makes it possible to combine a frequency etapon with a radio circuit that controls the progress of a quartz clock or allows comparative frequencies with the eton. Taking these circumstances into account, the International Conference of Weights and Measures in 1967 decided to abandon the natural ethon of time “the Earth revolving around the Sun” and move on to an artificial creation and a quantum ethon not related to astroνομobservables, which uses one of the wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation from cesium atoms, which lies in the radio range ($\lambda \approx 3,27 \cdot 10^{-2}$ m). In order to obtain the principle of the operation of a quantum clock, we will have to consider how atoms behave in a magnetic field.

Atom and magnetic needle

In addition to the electric field, electrons and atomic nuclei create magnetic fields in the surrounding space. The magnetic field induction vector atoms is the geometric sum of the magnetic induction vectors of the fields of its constituent particles. For some atoms, the resulting induction vector is zero, but many atoms, including cesium atoms, have a magnetic field. Such atoms are roughly similar to magnetic arrows.

In the absence of an external magnetic field, the induction vectors of magnetic fields of cesium atoms, as well as those of magnetic needles sufficiently distant from each other, are oriented randomly. In the language of quantum mechanics, this means that at the same energy level there are atoms with different directions of magnetic fields (figure 5).

If, having done work against the forces of interaction between the pointer and the magnetic field, the pointer is deflected at a certain angle and released, then under the influence of these forces it will return to its original position after several oscillations. According to quantum concepts, atoms placed in an external magnetic field experience only two orientations of their own magnetic fields: either “along the field” (like magnetic needles in the Earth’s magnetic field) or “against the field.” If the orientation “along the field” corresponds to the energy atoma $E_1$, then the orientation “against the field” will correspond to a slightly larger value of the energy atoma $E_2$ (just as the energy of a magnetic needle taking “against the field” is greater than the energy of a magnetic needle spontaneously oriented “along the field” by the amount of work spent on this reorientation).

In other words, placing the target atom in an external magnetic field causes the main energy level to expand into two sublevels with energies $E_1$ and $E_2$, with $E_2 > E_1$ (figure 6).

Transitions of atoms $E_2 \rightarrow E_1$ (from sublevel $E_2$ to sublevel $E_1$) are accompanied by radiation, and transitions $E_1 \rightarrow E_2$ are accompanied by absorption of waves with frequency $v_0 = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{h} = \frac{\Delta E}{h}$. The energy difference $E_2 - E_1$ is not large, and the frequency $v_0$ lies in the radio wave range.

Heating through a narrow slit (1), in which metallic cesium evaporates, cesium atoms fly out in the form of a flat beam and enter the region of a low-grade magnetic field $B_1$ created by a magnet (2) of a special shape. For simplicity, figure shows only the trajectories of atoms along the edges of this beam. The movement of atoms occurs inside a vacuum tube, not shown in figure.

In a low-grade magnetic field $B_1$, cesium atoms are oriented “along the field” and “against the field”, taking energy values ​​$E_1$ and $E_2$, respectively. But in addition to orientation, atoms are separated by energy. Some of the atoms at the upper edge of the beam, oriented “along the field” and having energy $E_1$, are drawn into the region of higher induction and do not participate in the operation of the device. Another part of the atoms of the upper edge of the beam, oriented “against the field” and having energies $E_2$, are pushed out of the region with higher induction, and their trajectories are curved downward. In the lower part of the beam, atoms with energies $E_2$ (orientation “against the field”) are deflected to the side less than the induction and leave the game, and atoms oriented “along the field” (with energies $E_1$) are deflected towards the axis of the device.

Cesium atoms sorted by energies $E_1$ and $E_2$ enter the resonator (3), where a uniform magnetic field $B$ (magnet (4)) is created, in which the atoms move molinearly and maintain energy splitting. An electromagnetic field is also created in the resonator area, the frequency of which can smoothly change. If this frequency is exactly equal to the frequency $v_0 = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{h}$, then part of the atoms with energies $E_1$, having absorbed a quantum of energy $hv_0 = E_2 - E_1$, will go into the state $E_2$ and take an orientation “against the field”, and part of the atoms with energies $E_2$ of another beam under the influence of a quantum $hv_0$ as a result of stimulated emission will go into state $E_1$, changing the orientation “against the field” to the orientation “along the field”*).

Behind the resonator there is a region of low-grade magnetic field $B_2$ (magnet (5)), similar to field $B_1$. In this field, atoms are sorted by energy, as a result of which comprehensive interactions with the electromagnetic field appear; atoms with energies $E_2$ and $E_1$ are deflected towards the beam axis and fall on the applicator. Atoms with energies $E_1$, which have not absorbed a quantum of the electromagnetic field $hv_0$, and atoms with energies $E_2$, which do not experience stimulated emission, are deflected by the low-grade magnetic field $B_2$ away from the axis of the device and do not reach the applicator. Thus, if the frequency of the electromagnetic field is not equal to the frequency $v_0 = \frac{\Delta E}{h}$, then there is no interaction of the electromagnetic field with cesium atoms. In this case, the atoms do not enter the application.
