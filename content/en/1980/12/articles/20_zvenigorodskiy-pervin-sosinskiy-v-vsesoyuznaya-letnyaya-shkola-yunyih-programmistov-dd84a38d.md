summer school
young programmers

From July 25 to August 10, 1980, the V All-Russian Summer School for Young Programmers was held in the Novosibirsk Academic Town, organized by the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences, the editorial office of the magazine «Kvant», Novosibirsk State University and the USSR Ministry of Education. For the first time, the selection of students was made through the Correspondence School of Programming, the materials of which are published on the pages of our magazine. The best students of 3SPS, as well as the winners of the Programming Olympiad («Kvant», 1979, No. 3), were invited to the Summer School. In this way, all 145 students of the Summer School were selected. They came from all over the Union: from Kaliningrad to Sakhalin, from Norilsk to Tashkent; there were guys from big cities and from rural areas. Mostly, these were schoolchildren in grades 6-9, but there were also quite a few students in grades 4-5. How this necessary school took place is described in this article.

Scientific conference for schoolchildren

Please, the most interesting events of the Summer School were the conferences that took place on July 28 and August 8. At the first, young program students spoke about the work carried out during the school year, at the second - directly at the Summer School.

The reports of the first conference were divided into five sections: system programming, modeling and optimization on a computer, the use of computers in the educational process and applied data processing problems, process control, gaming and sports programs. The meeting of each section was conducted by a scientist or specialist who is well acquainted with the specifics of using computers in accordance with the assessment of the area: projector of the Novosibirsk State University, Doctor of Technical Sciences, prof. N. G. Zagoriyko, senior researcher Research Institute of Computers (Minsk) M. V. Khodosh, senior researcher Computing Center of the Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences V. E. Malyshkin, employee of the Leningrad Institute of Aviation Instrumentation N. N. Brovin, senior researcher Institute of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR L. N. Ivaenko.

Word to system programmers

According to the general opinion, the first section (system programming) turned out to be the strongest: three out of five reports in this section were awarded first degree diplomas.

Ninth-grader Vitaly Tsikoza (Novosibirsk, first-degree diploma) spoke about the new version of the Shpa graphic system he developed, called Shpa-5. The Summer School students really liked the DUGAT procedure included in Speech-5, which allows them to draw a circular arc through three given points: now there is no need to tinker with a certain radius and direction of the arc. Ten-class student Mikhail Epiketov (Alma-Ata, 1st degree diploma) developed the SONAR system for debugging programs on EC computers. To speed up debugging, many languages ​​and programming systems (for example, OS EC, ALGOL - BURROWS) provide the ability to monitor the meaning of various names, calls to procedures, etc., but in the DOS EC operating system the programmer must organize debugging himself. To do this, you can put a print statement after each assignment in the program and use the printout to see how to use the program, but the size of the program then doubles, and when you throw out additional print statements, new errors are often “introduced” into the program. The SONAR system allows the programmer, with the help of one or two operators, to organize debugging printing for the entire program or specified sections of it and also simply “turn off” this printing after finishing debugging.

Nine-classic student Serey Kotov (Novosibirsk, 1st degree diploma), at the request of the Laboratory of Experimental Information of the Computing Center of the Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences, developed a kit for analyzing the program of the INTEL-8080 microprocessor. Numerical machines built on large inter- nal circuits have become widespread in a variety of areas in recent years. Microprocessors control the research apparatus of spacecraft and the largest rolling mills, determine the exposure during photography, regulate the operation of automatic engines and pocket calculators. Of course, the quality of their work depends on the management of the immi program. It is often necessary to understand such a program, understand the underlying algorithm, and perhaps make the necessary changes. But understanding an unfamiliar program written in machine commands is not at all easy: to do this, you need to replace the commands with their non-monic notations, operate with comments, separate commands from data, break the program into separate blocks and develop connections between them, etc. All this work is performed by the kit created by Sergey.

Diplomas of II degree and III degree were awarded to the reports of Mikhail Simbirskoe (Kharkov, 9th grade) on working with numbers of non-standard length and Svetlanna Uropina (Novosibirsk, 5th grade) on her program for lexical analysis of texts in various programming languages.

Programs simulate and help in production

In the second section, Olga Khoroshevskaya (Novosibirsk, 9th grade) received a first degree diploma, who spoke about optimizing the structure of a computer network connecting several computer centers. Having built a detailed model of the network operation in the ALGOL-GDR language and successfully applied stochistic optimization (Page's method), Olya showed that of the several options for organizing communication between computer centers, the most outstanding one is the ring structure. By general opinion, Olin's report was the best in terms of use and overheads (performed by a computer on the Sword - see Fig. 2).

The report of *Serdeya Gavrilenko* (Novosibirsk, 5th grade), which was awarded a II degree diploma, aroused great interest in the third section. Serezhina’s “Question-Answer” program allows you to send data recorded in a certain way from any subject area into a machine, and then ask the computer about this data, receiving, as the speaker put it, “answers at the level of meaning.”

The first degree diploma in the fourth section was awarded to co-authors *Mikhail Dun* and *Andrey Kadal* (Kharkov, 9th grade) for two reports: “Modeling of technological processes for manufacturing products from thermosetting polymers” and “Automation of the design and manufacture of central roller cam mechanisms.” These works have already been implemented in production: they were awarded a special prize as developments with the most practical significance.

There was still a lot of interesting work. We will only note the reports of *Pavel Zemtsov* (Novosibirsk, 6th grade), who in the language “Simulya - I” carried out the work of the production workshop, *Igor Mezentsev* (Angarsk, 9th grade) on the registration system “Trulyashchik”, *Nikolai Verba* (Kharkov, 6th grade), who found all 92 solutions to the “problem about 8” on the computer queens" (see «Kvant», 1977, No. 12, p. 22), *Victoria Volkova* (Novosibirsk, 5th grade) about the application of Euler’s method to solving the equation of motion of an aircraft and, finally, familiar to our readers (as the developers of Shpagi-I) *Asia Salikhova*, who spoke about promising work on modeling random walks on crystal lattice.

What have we learned

At the first conference, the reports of schoolchildren from Novosibirsk and Kharkov, where their school programmers have long worked, stood out at a high level. And although there are interesting works from Estonia (Newsskaya S.H.), Angarsk, Almaty, Moscow, Tuymazy, Kuibyshev, the majority of students

ZShP - before the Summer School there was no opportunity for such joint work with a computer.

A different picture was observed at the final conference. Here everyone was on equal terms. Each team was given large portions of terminal time to launch and debug a program on a chosen topic. The development and debugging of these programs served as one of the test assignments, and the most interesting works were presented at the final conference.

The leaders of the delegates (teachers and computer application specialists) were amazed at what interesting and complex programs the schoolchildren, who had first used a computer for the first time, were able to create and debug in two weeks. Without being able to talk about all the interesting works, we will name only a few of them.

Three teams received a 1st degree diploma for the work “Examiner’s Ultimate in Chemistry” (speaker - M. Epiktegov), “School Journal” (speaker - S. Brodetskaya) and “Task on the oxidation reaction” (speaker - E. Kornilin). Of the interesting works that received II degree diplomas, we note the following three: “Robot Housewife” (N. Navarova), “Decipherer” (E. Zabokritsky), “Chess Judge” (L. Nekhamkin).

According to the general opinion of the participants, guests and leaders of the V Summer School, it was definitely a great experience. We are pleased to note here that, in addition to excellent conditions, organizational experience and the traditional hotel of Akademgorodok, this was facilitated by the fairly robust and high level of students achieved in the correspondence school programming classes of our magazine.
