Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology

Below are published materials from the written entrance exams of 1979. Five hours were allotted for work in both mathematics and physics.

Mathematica

Option 1
1. Solve the equation
$$\sqrt{2-x} + \frac{4}{\sqrt{2-x}+3} = 2.$$
2. Solve inequality
$$\frac{x-1}{\log_3(9-3^x)} \leq 1.$$
3. Find all solutions to the equation
$$\sin(2x - \frac{\pi}{4}) = \sqrt{2}\sin^2x,$$
satisfying inequality
$$\log_{\cos^23}(1 + \sin(7x+5)) < \sin 8x.$$
4. The extensions of the altitudes AM and CN of an acute triangle ABC intersect its circumcircle at points P and Q. Find the radius of the circumcircle if $|AC| = a, |PQ| = \frac{6}{5}a.$
5. In terrada ABCD, two angles at edges AB and BD are right, $\widehat{ACD} = a$. The top of the cone coincides with one of the tops of the terraer, the circle of the base of the cone is inscribed in one of the boundaries. Find the angle in the axial section of the cone.
6. Point N is located on the bank of a river, the width of which is 1 km, and the speed of the current is 1 km/hour. Point M is located at least 3 km downstream on the other bank. A fisherman leaves point M and walks along the shore towards N at a speed of 4 km/hour. In due course, a carrier sails from point N on a boat, crosses the river, and, having caught the fisherman, transports him to point N. The boat moved back and forth in a straight line, and the direction of movement was chosen so that the shortest possible time elapsed from departure to return, equal to $\frac{9}{8}$ hours. The speed of a boat in still water is 4 km/hour. Find the distance (downstream) between points M and N.

Option 2
1. Solve inequality
$$0,4^x - 2,5^{x+1} > 1,5.$$
2. Solve system of equations
$$\begin{cases} \log_2y = \log_4(xy - 2), \\ \log_9x^2 + \log_3(x-y) = 1. \end{cases}$$
3. A circle of radius R drawn through the vertices A, B and C of the straight trapezoid $ABCD$ ($\widehat{A} = \widehat{B} = \frac{\pi}{2}$) intersects the segments AD and CD respectively at points M and N so that $|AM|:|AD| = |CN|:|CD| = 1:3$. Find the area of ​​the trapezoid.
4. Solve the equation
$$\sin x + \sin^2x + \cos^3x = 0.$$
5. Two trains leave points A and B towards each other at the same time. Each of them moves at first uniformly accelerated (the initial speeds of the train are zero), and then, when they reach a certain speed, uniformly. The speed ratio of the uniform motion of the train is $\frac{4}{3}$. At the moment of meeting, the trains had equal speeds, and arrived at points B and A approximately simultaneously. Find the ratio of the train accelerations.
6. The base of the right paralleleped $ABCDA_1B_1C_1D_1$ is the square ABCD. Find the most possible angle between straight line BD$_1$ and plane BDC$_1$.

Physics

Option 1
1. Three identical smooth billiard balls of radius R are located on a smooth horizontal plane, as shown in figure 1. Ball 1 is given a speed $\vec{v}_0$, it hits ball 2 first, then ball 3 and is abandoned. Determine the distance r between the centers of balls 2 and 3 and the velocities that balls 2 and 3 will acquire after the impact. Collisions are elastic.
2. The cylinder under the piston contains n = 0.5 moles of air at a temperature T$_0$ = 300 K. How many times will the volume of the gas increase when the amount of heat Q = 13.2 kJ is imparted to it? The molar temperature of air at constant pressure is C$_p$ = 29.1 J/(mol K).
3. Connect parallel-connected coils with inductance L and resistance R through a key K and a source with a constant emf E and internal resistance r (Fig. 2). At the initial moment of time, switch K is open and there is no current in the circuit. What charge Q will flow through resistance R after switch K is closed? Neglect the ohmic resistance of the coil.

Option 2

1. As a result of the interaction of action nuclei and tritium, a gel nucleus and a neutron are formed: $^2\text{H} + ^3\text{H} = ^4\text{He} + n$. This releases significant energy. What part does the neutron carry with it? The kinetic energies of action and tritium before the reaction can be neglected in comparison with the released energy.

2. Laser tubes with a volume of $V_0 = 60$ cm$^3$ must fill a mixture of gel and neon in a molar ratio of 5:1 at a total pressure of $p_0 = 6$ mmHg. Art. There are points for these gases, each volume is $V_C = 2$ liters. Pressure in the gel balloon $p_1 = 50$ mmHg. Art., neon - $p_2 = 200$ mm Hg. Art. How many tubes can be filled?

3. A charged capacitor with capacity $C$ is connected through a switch $K$ to two parallel connecting coils with inductances $L_1$ and $L_2$ (Fig. 4). At the initial moment of time the key is open. If you close the key $K$, it will consume current through the coils. It is known that the maximum current flowing through coils with inductance $L_1$ turns out to be equal to $I_1$. Find the initial charge on the capacitor. Neglect the ohmic resistance of the coils.

4. A thin ray of light (Fig. 5) falls on a glass wedge perpendicular to its face. Glass assumption indicator $n = 1,41$, angle $\alpha = 10^\circ$. How many bright spots will be visible on a screen placed behind the wedge?

Option 3

1. At what length of day would bodies at the Earth's equator weigh half as much as those at the pole? The radius of the Earth is $R = 6400$ km.

2. An ampoule with a radioactive drug $^{24}\text{Na}$ (half-life $\tau_{1/2} = 15$ h) is cooled by an air flow. At the beginning of the air experiment, the cooling air will heat up by $\Delta t_0 = 2^\circ\text{C}$. After what time will the cooling air heat up by $\Delta t_1 = 1,8^\circ\text{C}$?

3. The stationary charged part (proton) is located between the plates of a flat capacitor placed in a vacuum. The direction $U_c(t)$ of a direct new shape (Fig. 6) is applied to the capacitor. Determine the amplitude of the direction $U_0$ and the energy of the particle $E$ at the moment of impact on the capacitor plate, if it is known that during the time $\tau = 10^{-6}$ s the particle has traveled a distance $3/8L$, where $L$ is the distance between the capacitor plates, $L = 5$ cm. The mass of the proton is $m_p = 1,67 \cdot 10^{-24}$ g, its charge is $e = 1,6 \cdot 10^{-19}$ Cl.

4. In a thin-walled glass ball with a diameter of $2R = 4$ cm, two images of a candle flame are visible, caused by reflection from the near and further walls of the ball. Image dimensions refer to 19/21. Determine the distance between the center of the ball and the candle.
