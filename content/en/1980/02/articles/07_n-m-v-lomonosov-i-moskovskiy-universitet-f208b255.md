M. V. Lomonosov
and Moscow University

The creation of Moscow University is inextricably linked with the name of the great estate granted by the state to the Russian scientist-nugget M.V. Lomonosov (1711-1765).

A letter from M.V. Lomonosov dates back to 1748, where he formulates his opinion on university regulations and; in part, indicates that “the university must necessarily have a third faculty: law, medicine and philosophy (I leave the theological to the synodal schools), in which masters, licenses and doctors would be produced.” In 1754, M.V. Lomonosov drew up a project for organizing Moscow University and two gymnasiums attached to it. Finally, in January 1755, the energetic services of M.V. Lomonosov increased rapidly: a decree on the establishment of Moscow University was published. It is symbolic that the building that initially housed the first Russian university stood on Red Square, next to the Kremlin - on the spot where the Historical Museum stands today.

In 1940, by Decree of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR, Moscow State University was named after its founder. In 1961, in connection with the 250th anniversary of the birth of M.V. Lomonosov, a commemorative tabletop bronze medal was issued, which, in part, depicts the outline of the Main building of Moscow State University on the Lenin Hills (you can see this medal in the picture).
