Let $|BC| = a, |CC_1| = b$. Let's place the origin of coordinates at point $C$, and direct the axes $x, y, z$ along vectors $\overrightarrow{CB}, \overrightarrow{CD}, \overrightarrow{CC_1}$. We have $\overrightarrow{BD_1} = (-a; a; b)$. The plane ($BDC_1$) is given by the equation $\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{a} + \frac{z}{b} = 1$. Then the vector $\overrightarrow{n}$, perpendicular to this plane, has coordinates $\left( \frac{1}{a}; \frac{1}{a}; \frac{1}{b} \right)$. Hence,

$$\sin \alpha = \cos \left( \overrightarrow{n}, \overrightarrow{BD_1} \right) = \frac{\overrightarrow{n} \cdot \overrightarrow{BD_1}}{\left| \overrightarrow{n} \right| \cdot \left| \overrightarrow{BD_1} \right|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{a^2} + \frac{1}{b^2}} \cdot \sqrt{2a^2 + b^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \left[ \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^2 \right] + 5}}.$$

It is easy to check that $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^2 \geq 2$, and $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^2 = 2$ only for $a = b$ (recall that $a > 0, b > 0$). Therefore, $\sin \alpha < \frac{1}{3}$ at $a \neq b$ and $\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{3}$ at $a = b$.

So, $a = \arcsin \frac{1}{3}$ at $a = b$; flat sin increases by $\left[ 0; \frac{\pi}{2} \right]$, at $a \neq b$ we have $\alpha < \arcsin \frac{1}{3}$. This means that $\arcsin \frac{1}{3}$ is the most possible value for $\alpha$.
