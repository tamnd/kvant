Mathematics
at Moscow State University

January 25, 1980 marked 225 years since the signing of the decree on the establishment of Moscow University. At the university, two imazias were created: one for the children of the commoners, the other for the children of commoners. The grand opening of the university took place on April 26, 1755. But this was only the opening of immazii. Classes with students began only in the summer of 1755, when 30 young people seconded for spiritual seminars gathered at the university (there were no other prepared applicants for university studies in the country at that time).

Moscow University was opened thanks to the vigorous activity of M.V. Lomonosov, which added to the widespread spread of education in Russia, not associated with class restrictions. However, at that time the government of the serfdom empire categorically denied the peasantry the opportunity to receive higher education. Lomonosov argued that “at the university, the student who has learned more is more respectable: and whose son he is, there is no need for that.”

At first, the university consisted of three faculties: philosophy, law and medicine - and, formally, two gymnasiums. In fact, there was only one gymnasium. Only classes were held separately for Dvoyayan children and children of commoners. Association was allowed only in the last, so-called “rector’s” class. This last class provided additional preparation for entering the university, and classes lasted the same year.

All students must take classes at the Faculty of Philosophy, which is a free general education student for transfer to the Faculty of Medicine and Law. Classes at the Faculty of Philosophy lasted three years, and at the Faculty of Medicine and Law lasted Thursday of the year. Thus, studying at the university lasted 7 years. The medical and law faculties were called “highest” faculties at that time.
