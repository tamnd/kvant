D. M. Perevoshikov

investigators in the fields of mathematics, biology, physics, chemistry, mechanics, external ballistics and artillery.

The Patriotic War of 1812 had a hard impact on Moscow University: Napoleonic artillery plundered and burned the collections, library, and buildings. Many students and teachers left to defend the country. Classes at the university stopped. It took a long time to restore an accessible level of teaching, and further progress in the mathematical life of Moscow University dates back to the twenties. This progress is largely associated with the change of Professor Dmitry Matveevich Perevoshikov (1790-1880), the author of a number of textbooks on mathematics and the thirteen-volume “Manual Mathematical Encyclopedia”, which served as a real book for mathematicians, engineers, artillerymen, natural astronomical observatories, and then from 1830 to 1851 he was its director, combining this work with the work of the dean, professor, rector. He owns more than seventy scientific (mathematical and astronomical), pedagogical and popular science works.

Among Perevoshikov’s direct students, Nikolai Efimovich Zernov (1804-1862) should be mentioned first. A significant process of mathematical education and its renewal is associated with his name. In particular, it was Zernov who, after his teacher, first began teaching a course on probability theory at the university. The topic of Zernov’s master’s thesis, “On the daily and annual motion of the Earth,” was suggested to him by his teacher, and the topic of his doctoral dissertation, “A Discourse on the Interration of an Equation with Partial Differentials” (1837), was chosen by him himself. It is interesting to note that this was the first doctoral dissertation in mathematics defended at Moscow University.

The events of December 1825 greatly affected the life of the university. Suffice it to say that among the university students there were at least sixty Decembrists and a third of them were students and former students of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. Full surveillance was established over the university, and together with the program installation of 1804, a new so-called censorship ustav was introduced. According to this charter, the university was prohibited from publishing scientific works on natural sciences, philosophy and medicine. Only textbooks were allowed to be printed (of course, after the corresponding doctorate).

The famous work “The Past and Thoughts” by A. I. Germen, a graduate of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University, contains memoirs about professors, classmates and the self-importance of university life in 1829-1833. Germen highly appreciated the role of Moscow University in the development of Russian culture:

“In the history of Russian education and in the life of the last two generations, Moscow University and Tsarskoye Rural Face play a significant role.

Moscow University grew in importance along with Moscow after 1812; demoted by Emperor Peter from the tsarist capitals, Moscow was promoted by Napoleon (either willingly or unwillingly) to the capital of the Russian people... In it, the university is increasingly becoming the center of Russian education.”
