Now we can formulate a rule, on the basis of which all the analyzed problems were solved: in order to find out in what respect the plane π (respectively, the line l) divides a segment that is not parallel to it, we should consider the projection along the plane π or along some known line lying in the plane π (respectively, along the line l).

As an example, the application of this rule in plane trilogy will be proven.

Menelaus's theorem. If line l intersects sides AB, BC and CA of triangle ABC or their extension at points K, L, M respectively, then
$$\frac{AK}{KB} \cdot \frac{BL}{LC} \cdot \frac{CM}{MA} = -1^*$$.

Proof (Fig. 5). Let's project a triangle parallel to line l onto some line intersecting it. Then
$$\frac{AK}{KB} \cdot \frac{BL}{LC} \cdot \frac{CM}{MA} = \frac{A'P}{PB'} \cdot \frac{B'P}{PC'} \cdot \frac{C'P}{PA'} = (-1)^3 = -1.$$
(Note that the converse theorem is also true; it can be proven by contradiction.)

Let us now analyze a problem in which not only the ratios of the lengths of parallel segments are important, as was above, but also these lengths themselves, as well as the angles between the segments. Unlike previous problems, both the direction of the projection and the plane on which we are projecting play a role here.

Solution (Fig. 6). Let us project the recognition along the diagonal B$_1$D onto the plane of the face ABB$_1$A$_1$. It is easy to see that the result will be the drawing shown in figure 6, 6. It is clear that any segment parallel to the plane ABB$_1$A$_1$ is projected into a segment of the same length (!). Therefore, the segments under consideration will be congested with their projections [DX], where X ∈ [AD$_1$]. Obviously, the shortest of them is the perpendicular DH dropped from point D to (AD$_1$). Triangle DHD$_1$, like ΔABD$_1$, is rectangular and isosceles, therefore |DH| $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ |DD$_1| = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ a (this is the answer to the second question). The length of the segment DM can be found from the right triangle MPD, in which |DP| = $\frac{1}{3}$ |DD$_1| = \frac{a}{3}$, |MP| = $\frac{1}{3}$ |AB| = $\frac{2a}{3}$. Thus,

$$|DM| = \frac{a\sqrt{5}}{3}$$
