The ideas of the theory had a significant influence on the development of the theory of functions of a complex variable. Prominent representatives of this direction of research at Moscow University, which received significant applications in aerodynamics and hydrodynamics, were V. V. Golubev (1884-1954), I. I. Great progress was made by scientists at Moscow University in the theory of differential equations - both general and particular arbitrary. The works of V.V. Stepanov (1889-1950), who developed the strong scale in the qualitative theory of differential equations, I.G. Petrovsky (1901-1973) and A.N. Tikhonov, who from different positions developed the theory of differential equations in particular arbitrary computational orders, are considered classic.

Significant successes have been achieved by university mathematicians in the field of number theory. A. Ya. Khinchin (1894-1959) obtained a number of functional results in the field of metric number theory; L.G. Shirelman (1905-1938) proved in a very elementary way a substitute fact on the way to solving Goldbach's problem: every integer can be represented as the sum of a limited number of simple terms. A. O. Gelfond (1906-1968) developed a substitute research method that allowed him to prove the following conjecture, expressed at the beginning of the century by D. Hilbert: any number of the form $a^\beta$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are algebraic numbers, and $\alpha \neq 0$ and $\alpha \neq 1$, and $\beta$ irrational, is transcendental.

Beginning in 1923, the circle of interests of Moscow mathematicians included the theory of probability. A. Ya. Khinchin was a pioneer in this field. Two years later, A. N. Kolmogorov became interested in questions of probability theory. From now on

This is how the Moscow school of probability theory arose, which transformed the face of this science. Currently, graduates of Moscow University have created strong theoretical schools outside Moscow State University: in the German Democratic Republic, Bulgaria and some allied resource countries.

Through the efforts of O. Yu. Schmidt (1891-1956) and A. G. Kursh (1908-1971), a community school of modern algebra was created at the university and far beyond its borders, which had and is having a strong influence on the development of practically all areas of algebraic research.
