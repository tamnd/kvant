Demagnetization of ships during the Great Patriotic War

On June 24, 1941, at 2:41 a.m., the destroyer Gnevny was struck by a mine at the mouth of the Gulf of Finland. At 4 hours 21 minutes, the cruiser Maxim Gorky was blown up by a mine in the same area, but reached Tallinn under its own power. It was assumed that the mines were non-contact (magnetic). Already in the first days of the war, the enemy created a server mine threat of escape from Soviet naval bases and on the main sea routes of communication, using a total of 1060 anchor strike mines and up to 160 données non-contact mines. The situation was created by a threat. What about our fleet? Was he prepared for the possibility of a mine blockade or was it just an accident and completely unexpected?

Among the many tasks of defense importance, demagnetization of ships occupied an important place. In a short article, it is impossible to cover in detail the entire epic of demagnetization and list all the participants, since in addition to scientists, many military sailors and shipbuilders were involved in solving this problem.

For a magnetic mine to explode, it does not require direct contact with the ship's hull. Explode due to the impact of the ship's magnetic field on it. The idea of ​​making such a fuse is so simple that it could have arisen immediately after the invention of the compass and the appearance of stable ships. Everyone knows that the compass needle is deflected if an iron object is brought to it. The deflection of such an arrow placed inside a mine can be used to close the fuse contact. With an appropriate increase in sensitivity, the mine explosion will occur at the moment when the ship is above it, which means that its most unprotected part, the bottom, will be affected. Further more ships, as a rule, perish when such mines explode underneath them. In addition, magnetic mines lying on the bottom are not supplied by the usual trawling method, designed to cut and detonate anchor mines. The installation of magnetic mines can be carried out not only from ships, but also from aircraft - on the open sea, in bays, harbors, and on the fairway.

These advantages of magnetic mines over conventional anchor contact mines for the basis of naval specialists were predicted in advance by their use in the upcoming war, especially since our sailors had to get acquainted for the first time with the magnetic mines of the English invaders back in September 1919 on the Northern Dvina. Even then, several of our ships were blown up while the English were loading magnetic mines. However, military sailors quickly cleared the fairway of these still comparatively primitive mines.
