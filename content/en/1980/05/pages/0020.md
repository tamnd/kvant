What Logic Is and Isn't

Initially, “logic” was understood to be the same as “laws of thinking.” Logicians studied this science in the hope that they could find better ways of thinking and more reliable ways of avoiding mistakes than those their ancestors knew. They hoped that they could teach this art to all people. However, experience showed that these were unrealistic places.

Translation from English by L. Savina of an article from Mathematics Magazine, v. 50 (1977), no. 1, p. 5-11.

A normal, healthy person knows all the "laws of thought" that have ever been invented, and there is nothing that logicians can teach him about thinking and how to avoid mistakes. This does not mean that a person knows how he thinks, and it does not mean that he never makes mistakes. The same applies to the organs of movement with which all normal, healthy people are born. I don't know how I want to, but I do it. Sometimes I stumble. The laws of walking might be of interest to physiologists and physicists, but all I want is to continue walking.
