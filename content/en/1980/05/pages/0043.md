Point C, for any position of the rod, is at a distance $l/2$ from the vertex O of the right angle (the median drawn from the vertex of the right angle is equal to half the hypotenuse). Therefore, point C moves along a circle of radius $l/2$ centered at point O. The speed $v_c$ of point C is directed tangent to the trajectory and is perpendicular to OC at any moment.

Since the rod is inextensible, the projections of the velocities of points C and B onto the direction of the rod are equal to (Fig. 2).

$$v \cos \alpha = v_c \cos \beta = v_c \cos \left( 2\alpha - \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = v_c \sin 2\alpha.$$

Because

$$v_c = \frac{v \cos \alpha}{\sin 2\alpha} = \frac{v}{2 \sin \alpha}.$$

The projection of the acceleration $\vec{a}_c$ of point C onto the radius OC = $l/2$ must be equal to $\frac{v_c^2}{l/2} = \frac{v^2}{2l \sin^2 \alpha}$. What is the direction of vector $\vec{a}_c$? Note that the projection of the velocity $\vec{v}_c$ of point C on the horizontal axis is equal to $v_c \cos (\alpha - \beta) = v_c \cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha \right) = \frac{v}{2}$. This quantity does not depend on α and is therefore constant. This means that the vector $\vec{a}_c$ is directed vertically.

Thus,

$$a_c \cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - a \right) = \frac{v^2}{2l \sin^2 \alpha},$$

where

$$a_c = \frac{v^2}{2l \sin^3 \alpha}.$$

After closing the key K1, the direction on the capacitor becomes equal to E. In this case, the charge of the capacitor is equal to

$$q_1 = C \varepsilon.$$

This charge passes through the galvanometer when switch K1 is closed. Hence,

$$\alpha = kq_1 = kC \varepsilon,$$

where $k = \text{const}$ is the proportionality coefficient.

After closing the switch K2, the direction across the capacitor becomes equal to the resistor across the resistor, that is, $U = IR$, where I is the current flowing through the resistor. Since the resistor is connected after the pressure with the source, $I = \varepsilon / (R + r)$. Therefore, $U = \varepsilon R / (R + r)$. The charge of the capacitor after closing the key K2 becomes equal

$$q_2 = CU = C \varepsilon \frac{R}{R + r}.$$

Consequently, a charge passes through the galvanometer after closing key K2

$$q = q_1 - q_2 = C \varepsilon \frac{r}{R + r}$$
