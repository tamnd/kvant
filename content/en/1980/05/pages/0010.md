⟦folio 8⟧

Yes, these minesweepers went out on a combat mission.

The forecasts of the LPTI and navy specialists were justified: the very first action of the fascist German command in the naval theaters of war after the treacherous attack on the Soviet Union was an attempt to block our ships in their bases and tie up their military operations with massive laying of magnetic mines. The Nazis had high hopes for the effectiveness of these new naval weapons and were confident that Soviet sailors and specialists would not be able to quickly find ways and means to protect ships.

It was in these difficult conditions of the beginning of the war that the enormous work that was done in the pre-war years by scientists, sailors and shipbuilding specialists began to tell. In the very first days, a number of ships of the Baltic Fleet were burned and time was closed on the deck along the sides.

A group of LPTI scientists involved in the demagnetization of ships. From left to right A. R. Regel, Yu. S. Lazurkin, I. V. Kurchatov. The photo was taken in Poti in December 1941.

The battleship "Marat" is the first of the largest ships of the USSR Navy, equipped with a demagnetization system.
