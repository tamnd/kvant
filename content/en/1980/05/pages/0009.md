⟦folio 7⟧

President of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Academician A.P. Alexandrov.

The first changes in the magnetic fields of ships and experiments to compensate for them were carried out by LPTI employees in 1937 in the dry dock of Kronstadt on the destroyers Yakov Sverdlov and Artem, and then on the Leningrad Lyceum. The results were very positive. In May 1938, in the Orange seaport, a temporary demagnetizing winding was applied to the Dozorny ship, and by changing the field under the ship, the optimal current in it was selected. Then the ship made more passes with the winding turned off and on over disarmed non-contact magnetic mines installed at different depths. It was recorded that the mines were reliably set off when a ship passed over them with the winding turned off and did not react at all when the optimal current was turned off in the winding.

Thus, the task of mine protection of a small ship was successfully solved. It was time to move on to the experience of demagnetizing large ships. In October 1938, the LINKOR “Marat” was allocated for experiments. And on this largest ship of our Navy, with the help of a temporary demagnetizing winding, it was possible to reduce the magnetic field tenfold in an unprecedented proximity to the keel.

To obtain all the necessary data about the ship's field for designing a protection system, it was necessary to change the magnetic induction values ​​of this field at a large number of points at different depths. To do this, an aluminum rod with a magnetometer attached to it on a special trolley was lowered under the ship *). The rod was moved under the ship and installed at different depths. For each position of the rod, the magnetic induction was changed at several points under the keel and outside the sides of the ship. The LFTI group learned how to quickly perform this labor-intensive work, which requires good debugging of the instrument and device, so as not to delay the ships any longer in the roadstead.

Thus, by the beginning of the Great Patriotic War, reliable methods of protecting our ships from enemy magnetic mines had been created.

The first morning of the war found A.P. Alexandrov’s group aboard the LINKOR “Marat”. Already at 4 o'clock in the morning, a combat alert was announced on it: enemy planes appeared from the Finnish coast. Having checked the compliance of the calculated ampere-turns in the sections of the windings and made sure that the direction of the currents in them was correct, the commission commissioned LINKora protection devices, went ashore in Kronstadt and immediately received the command’s order to promptly equip several minesweepers with mine protection systems. Without limiting this, the group of A.P. Alexandrov mounted two electromagnetic trawls from the available cable, and already on June 27, 1941

*) Magnetometer is a device designed to measure magnetic field induction.
