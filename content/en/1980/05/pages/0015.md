His fellow second-year mechanic Vasya Zhirov was a machine gunner in the infantry. But he did not die behind a machine gun. Komsomol mekhanites fought and died behind enemy lines, who were selected in the first days of the war as the most reliable and resilient: two Vasilys - Podolsky and Vlasenko, Volodya Ignatovich and Vanya Archimenko. They were in tens. The junior political instructor of the motorized airborne battalion, spirant Kazimir Agayev (Komsomol member since 1929), was landed with the tenth near Kursk in the winter of 1942; In February of the same year, Kazimir Agayev died a heroic death in stubborn unequal fighting behind enemy lines.
