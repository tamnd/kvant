⟦folio 44⟧

From the pages of an old textbook

One day in the library I came across a strange book. This was the twenty-first edition of the “Textbook of Physics”, recommended for study in gymnasiums, secondary schools and other secondary educational institutions. The author of the textbook is a famous teacher, a fellow student of D. I. Mendeleev at the Pedagogical Institute, Konstantin Dmitrievich Kraevich.

This textbook has served for decades and has always been very popular. Thus, its twelfth edition was awarded the “full prize of Emperor Peter the Great.” However, the textbook can still be used today. It only lacks a section on the physics of the 20th century and the style of presentation is somewhat different from the modern one.

Here is a description of several experiments, taken from the textbook by K. D. Kraevich. If you wish, you can test these experiments at home.

About torture 1

“If a body a (for example, a copper weight under construction) heated to a certain temperature (for example, up to 50°C) is placed in a depression made in a piece of ice A and covered with another piece of ice B, then the body will cool, yielding its heat to the ice and turning it into water (Fig. 1). After some time, the body will assume the temperature of the melted water and ice, that is, 0°C, and further melting will stop. The water can be collected with a sponge, and the amount of water formed is determined by shaking; it can serve as a measure of the heat that the body has lost when cooled from 50 to 0°C, and which must be communicated to it in order to heat it from 0 to 50°C.”

The ice calorimeter has a number of advantages over other calorimeters. The main one is the virtual complete absence of heat dissipation into the environment.
