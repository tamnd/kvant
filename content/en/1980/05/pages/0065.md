12. Let us assume that the sequence $(x_n)$ converges and has a limit of $a \leqslant 0$. Let us use the definition of the limit of a sequence at $\varepsilon=1/2$; then there is a natural number $N$ such that for all $n>N$ the inequality
$$|\sin n-a|<1/2$$
Let us show that this is not true: there is a natural number $k$ such that $k>N$ and at the same time
$$\sin k-a>1/2$$
Solving the inequality $\sin x>1/2$, it is easy to verify that it is true, in particular, on the interval
$$\left[ \frac{\pi}{6}+2N\pi; \frac{5\pi}{6}+2N\pi \right]$$
Since the length of this interval is $2\pi/3>1$, it contains at least one integer; let's denote it $k$. So the distance from the origin to the left end of this interval is $\frac{\pi}{6}+2N\pi>N$, obviously $k>N$. Thus, there is a natural number $k>N$ such that $\sin k>1/2$, and then
$$\sin k-a>\frac{1}{2}-a>\frac{1}{2}$$
(because $a \leqslant 0$). The case $a \geqslant 0$ is treated similarly.
