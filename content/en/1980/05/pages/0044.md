F581. A man stands in front of a mirror with one eye closed. He closes the image of his closed eye in the mirror by pecking a piece of paper onto the mirror. What will a person see if he opens his closed eye and closes his open one?

On figure, red lines show the path of rays coming from the left eye to the right, and blue lines - from the right eye to the left. Thanks to these rays, the image of one eye is visible to the other eye. Since the eyes are located symmetrically, it is clear that when a person sticks a piece of paper onto area A of the mirror, he will always see the piece of paper in the place of the image of his closed eye.

F582. A body floats in water so that 2/3 of its volume is immersed in water. What part of the volume of the body will be under water if a vessel with water is mixed with acceleration $\vec{a}$ in the vertical direction?

Two forces act on the body: the gravity force $M\vec{g}$ (M is the mass of the body) and the force $\vec{F}$, which is equal to all fluid pressure forces acting on small areas of the body surface (Fig. 1). According to Newton's second law

$$\vec{F} + M\vec{g} = M\vec{a}.$$

In order to find $\vec{F}$, we will use the following technique. Let us select a volume $V_1$ in the liquid, equal to the volume of the part of the body immersed in the liquid and having the shape of the immersed part of the body (Fig. 2). This isolated volume is acted upon by the rest of the liquid by exactly the same force $\vec{F}$ as on the body. And since the allocated volume of liquid together with the vessel moves upward with acceleration $\vec{a}$, then

$$\vec{F} + m\vec{g} = m\vec{a},$$

where $m$ is the mass of the allocated volume of liquid. That's why

$$M(\vec{a} - \vec{g}) = m(\vec{a} - \vec{g}) \Rightarrow M = m.$$

But $M = \varrho_T V$, and $m = \varrho V_1$, where $V$ is the volume of the body, $\varrho_T$ is the density of the body and $\varrho$ is the density of the liquid. Hence,

$$\varrho_T V = \varrho V_1.$$

From here

$$\frac{V_1}{V} = \frac{\varrho_T}{\varrho}.$$

This ratio, as we see, does not depend on the acceleration $\vec{a}$ of the vessel and, therefore, is the same for any acceleration. Therefore, no matter with what acceleration $\vec{a}$ the vessel moves, the volume of the immersed part of the body will be 2/3 of the total volume of the body.

I. Slovodetsky
