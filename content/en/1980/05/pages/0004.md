The victory that saved the world

35 years have passed since that unforgettable day when our people first celebrated Victory Day over the fascist invaders. The path to this victory was difficult. Before attacking our country, the Nazis captured all of Western Europe and subjugated European industry. All of Europe fed the fascist troops and supplied them with the most modern weapons. It seems that in all the land there is no such force that could stop fascism, redirect its armies to domination over the world. History has refuted these predictions. The heroic Soviet people destroyed the monstrous fascist military machine and forever freed man from the fascist discourse. No other nation in the world at that time could have solved this incredibly difficult historical task.

Imagine for a moment that fascism has won and a fascist “new order” has been established throughout the world. The target people were completely exterminated. The peoples of our country would be turned into workers, deprived of the most basic human rights. Each of you, our young readers, must firmly remember what you owe to those who died in this war. This gives you the opportunity to study normally, freely choose a profession and lead an independent life. Without this great victory, each of you would have been working 10-12 hours a day doing hard work in fascist labor camps, living from hand to mouth, without the slightest hope for normal nutrition, education and personal happiness. It’s hard for you to imagine, but your fathers and grandfathers had to face this country’s opportunity face to face. Remember those who gave their lives for this victory and be proud of them! Remember and respect those who survived this country's war!

Schoolchildren in our country have long been patronizing the graves of fallen soldiers of the Great Patriotic War, looking for those who died, conducting trips to battle sites, and organizing school museums of combat classes. This is a fair tribute to those who defended your right to a free and happy life. Each of you must participate in this work.

When you watch films about the Great Patriotic War, read books about its heroes, many of you think that heroism was needed only in those days, and you wish that you were born too late. Of course, the war made extremely severe demands on every resident of our country; it required mass heroism, and even children showed it. But even then, heroes were not only those who burned in a tank, rammed an enemy plane or, with their rescued breasts, covered a machine-gun embrasure. There was no less heroism in the lives of those who, in the terrible cold in the wastelands of Siberian cities, restored evacuated factories, armed, clothed, fed our soldiers, or those who resisted the fascists in temporarily occupied territories. The following years were also heroic, when our people, who had lost more than 20 million people, regained control of the war economy.
