About the benefits
rolling balls

When throwing pebbles into the water, look at the grains, or those formed; otherwise such throwing will be empty fun...
Kozma Prutkov

We invite you to continue this experience.

Take a lump of platen and place it on the table. Cover the lump with a plate and roll it out under pressure. The plate movements should be circular with a relatively small radius. Make sure that all points on the plate describe circles of equal radius at the same height above the table.

Try to predict what shape the plate will take.

First, the top and bottom surfaces are equal: in the reporting system associated with the plate, the table performs the same rotation. Consequently, the resulting body must consist of two identical halves.

Secondly, the lump carried by the plate rolls around a certain point onto the table. It is natural to assume that the plate will take a shape that allows it to roll freely. This requirement is satisfied, in particular, by a finite surface.

And indeed, the plate lump turns into two cones, symmetrical to the relative obshezoniya.

Let us denote by α the positive angle at the vertex of the cone's cross-section, by r the radius of the cone's mastery, and by h their height (see figure). Let the volume of the plate lump be equal to V, and the radius of rotation of the plate be equal to R. Let us find the relationship between the indicated quantities. Since

$$h = \frac{R}{2\cos\alpha}, \quad r = h \mathrm{tg}\alpha \quad V = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^2 h,$$

we get

$$\frac{12}{\pi} \frac{V}{R^3} = \frac{\sin^2\alpha}{\cos^5\alpha}.$$

In other words, if you want to obtain a cone with a given angle α, the quantities V and R must be related to each other in a complete definition of correspondence.

So, at α = 45°

$$\frac{V}{R^3} = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}}.$$

If the initial plate lump has the shape of a ball with radius q, then

$$V = \frac{4}{3} \pi q^3 \quad \frac{\varphi^3}{R^3} = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}.$$

From here

$$\frac{R}{\varphi} = 2^{\frac{5}{6}} \approx 2,$$

that is, R ≈ 2φ is the radius of rotation of the plate when the diameter of the plate ball is approximately equal.

We also note that it is impossible to obtain regular cones with an angle α significantly less than 45°. It's not hard to explain why. The smaller the angle α, the greater the length of the cone generatrix (with a constant plate volume), which means the larger the surface the pressure should be applied to. In such conditions, it turns out to be practically impossible to form the apex sections of the cone, since the main pressure comes to the sections near the cone zone. In addition, a small angle α corresponds to large values ​​of R, at which incorrect hand movements can greatly distort the shape of the best body.
