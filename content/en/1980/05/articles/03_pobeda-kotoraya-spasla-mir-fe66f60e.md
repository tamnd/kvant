The victory that saved the world

35 years have passed since that unforgettable day when our people first celebrated Victory Day over the fascist invaders. The path to this victory was difficult. Before attacking our country, the Nazis captured all of Western Europe and subjugated European industry. All of Europe fed the fascist troops and supplied them with the most modern weapons. It seems that in all the land there is no such force that could stop fascism, redirect its armies to domination over the world. History has refuted these predictions. The heroic Soviet people destroyed the monstrous fascist military machine and forever freed man from the fascist discourse. No other nation in the world at that time could have solved this incredibly difficult historical task.

Imagine for a moment that fascism has won and a fascist “new order” has been established throughout the world. The target people were completely exterminated. The peoples of our country would be turned into workers, deprived of the most basic human rights. Each of you, our young readers, must firmly remember what you owe to those who died in this war. This gives you the opportunity to study normally, freely choose a profession and lead an independent life. Without this great victory, each of you would have been working 10-12 hours a day doing hard work in fascist labor camps, living from hand to mouth, without the slightest hope for normal nutrition, education and personal happiness. It’s hard for you to imagine, but your fathers and grandfathers had to face this country’s opportunity face to face. Remember those who gave their lives for this victory and be proud of them! Remember and respect those who survived this country's war!

Schoolchildren in our country have long been patronizing the graves of fallen soldiers of the Great Patriotic War, looking for those who died, conducting trips to battle sites, and organizing school museums of combat classes. This is a fair tribute to those who defended your right to a free and happy life. Each of you must participate in this work.

When you watch films about the Great Patriotic War, read books about its heroes, many of you think that heroism was needed only in those days, and you wish that you were born too late. Of course, the war made extremely severe demands on every resident of our country; it required mass heroism, and even children showed it. But even then, heroes were not only those who burned in a tank, rammed an enemy plane or, with their rescued breasts, covered a machine-gun embrasure. There was no less heroism in the lives of those who, in the terrible cold in the wastelands of Siberian cities, restored evacuated factories, armed, clothed, fed our soldiers, or those who resisted the fascists in temporarily occupied territories. The following years were also heroic, when our people, who had lost more than 20 million people, regained control of the war economy. This heroism of the difficult everyday life of your parents is beautifully told in a wonderful

We still need geronism today: the development of communist society and the formation of a new man will require many geronic achievements from our people. Representativeness, corruption, theft of state (and therefore people's!) property, bribery and some other negative phenomena of our life have not yet been eliminated. And we need conscious, active, irreconcilable fighters against all such shortcomings, fighters for the bright ideals of communism, and not for personal momentary material well-being.

Our country would be much richer now if it weren’t for the war, if we weren’t prevented from building a new life. In preparing for this war, our people consciously sacrificed their well-being. But in life there is a harsh nonarchy of values, and we had no other choice. In essence, we are only just beginning to live in a relatively favorable international situation for our country, associated with detente and the transition to the path of peaceful coexistence.

The scientific and technological revolution has brought to life weapons of mass accounting, threatening all life on our planet. But man has lived 35 years without a new world war, and no matter how hard the opponents of détente try and try, the forces of peace are growing and strengthening every day. At the head of these forces is the great Soviet Union, which consistently pursues Lenin’s peace-loving foreign policy. The whole world recognizes the enormous contribution made to the struggle for peace by the head of our state, Leonid Ilyich Brezhnev. We not only saved man from the nightmare of the physical “world order,” but also saved him from a new world war.

Our science also made a significant contribution to this great victory of the Soviet people. Many scientists immediately took up arms in our troops, partisan detachments and militia units. And every research center, every university has its own MEMORIAL PLATE with the names of fallen heroes. But the contribution of science to our victory is much broader. Thanks to the enormous work of our scientists before the war and during the war, Soviet military equipment was completely updated. The army received new aircraft (fighters, attack aircraft, dive bombers), rocket-propelled mortars ("kataoshi"), which terrified the Nazi soldiers, and T-34, "IS" and "KB" tanks, which were superior to similar German weapons.

Soviet scientists tried in every possible way to bring our victory closer. We will tell here only about some of the works of our physicists, mathematicians and mechanics.

To solve defense scientific and technical issues in Leningrad, besieged by physical troops, a special group of scientists was created, headed by the director of the Leningrad Physical Technical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences, academician Abram Fedorovich Yuffe. On instructions from the Leningrad City Party Committee, this institute created a dewey and effective incendiary mixture that destroyed hundreds of enemy tanks, and developed new counter-investigations. Simple and easy-to-process semiconductor thermal electronic generators, designed by A.F. Yuffe and powered by common kerosene lamps, were widely used by partisan radio operators. LPTI employee Yuri Borisovich Kobzarev (now an academician) created the world's first radar installation.

A group of scientists from the same institute, led by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Pavel Pavlovich Kobeko, helped ensure the reliability of the ice road across Lake Ladoga, the only one connecting Leningrad with the “Main Land”, which in those years was called the “Road of Life”.
