In total, more than 300 people from the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University went into the active army, half did not return. Komsomol members of the faculty are searching for traces and military biographies of their fallen comrades. More than fifty people returned to Mekhanit from various fronts of the Patriotic War. Currently, the faculty employs 33 participants of the Great Patriotic War; 15 of them are professors, doctors of science (about the same number of veterans work at the Research Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University).

Flutter - this word terrified test pilots and aircraft designers in the pre-war years. But mathematicians came to grips with this mysterious phenomenon that caused the destruction of planes in the air. After the mathematical theory of flutter was developed by our faculty professor M.V. Keldysh, the phenomenon of this phenomenon disappeared, and during the war there were no cases of aircraft destruction due to flutter.

From the first days of the war, faculty scientists were most actively involved in scientific research on defense topics. The team of the Department of Probability Theory under the leadership of Academician A. N. Kolmogorov conducted extensive research on the development of the theory of artillery shooting and the creation of a shooting table. In connection with the timing, the problem of the most advantageous dispersion of projectiles when firing over areas was solved. Professor S.V. Bakhalov, who was an artilleryman during the Civil War, developed a theory of artillery fire control devices. In organizing the defense of Moscow from enemy air raids, Professor N.A. Glagolev provided all possible assistance from the air defense troops, who solved the problem of the optimal placement of anti-aircraft batteries in the Moscow Military District.

The problem of the stability of the shape of the air pollution balloon, as well as the strength of the pollution cables, which is important for air defense, was solved by Professor Kh. A. Rakhmatulny; He also developed the theory of parachutes and the aerodynamics of squeaking bodies.

A difficult and urgent problem for our aviation was the bombardment of enemy troops from low altitudes at low aircraft speeds. Many prominent scientists of the faculty took part in solving this problem. Academician N. E. Kochin gave a practical solution to the problem of the theory of aircraft flight at low altitude, and in 1942 the staff of the Department of Probability Theory created tables for combinations from low altitudes at low aircraft speeds.

The whole world knows the “Road of Life” along the ice of Lake Ladoga and its role in the defense of Leningrad and the salvation of many Leningraders. A professor from the Department of Control Theory of Technical Features of Artillery took part in the creation of this road, which was carried out at the Department of Control Theory under the leadership of Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Ilyushin from the first days of the war. Despite the fact that during the enemy raids on Moscow the mechanic's laboratories were badly damaged, laboratory work at the control department had a break of only about two months (at the end of 1941).
