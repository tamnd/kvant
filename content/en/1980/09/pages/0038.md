M596. Given is a five-pointed prism with bases $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5$ and $B_1B_2B_3B_4B_5$. All edges of the bases and all segments $A_iB_j$ ($i, j = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$) are surrounded by either red or blue so that in every triangle with vertices at the vertices of a prism whose sides are surrounded there are two sides of different colors. M598. Given a plane $P$, a point $P$ on this plane and a point $Q$ outside the plane $P$. Find all points $R$ in the plane $P$ for which the ratio $(|PQ| + |PR|)/|QR|$ is maximum.

Fig. 1.

First we prove that all the edges of the lower base are surrounded by the same color. Indeed, let him find two edges of the lower base surrounded by different colors, then on the lower base he will find two adjacent edges surrounded by different colors (Fig. 1). We can think of these as edges $A_1A_2$ and $A_2A_3$, with the first edge surrounded by red and the second edge surrounded by blue. From the heel of segments $A_2B_k(k = 1, 2, \dots, 5)$ there are at least three segments of the same color, we will assume that this color is red. Of the three vertices on the upper base in which these segments lead, he will find two neighboring vertices. We denote them by $B_i$ and $B_j$. Obviously, edge $B_iB_j$ must be blue, otherwise in triangle $A_2B_iB_j$ all edges would be colored red (see Fig. 1). The segment $A_1B_i$ must also be blue, otherwise all sides of the triangle $A_1A_2B_i$ were colored red. The segment $A_1B_j$ must also be blue so that all sides of the triangle $A_1A_2B_j$ are not red. But in this case, all sides of the triangle $A_1B_iB_j$ will be blue. We got a contradiction.

Similarly, it is proved that the edges of the upper base are colored the same.

Now suppose that the edges of the lower base are colored red and the edges of the upper base are colored blue. Then no more than two blue segments emerge from any vertex of the lower base (otherwise, two blue segments coming from one vertex of the lower base will end at neighboring vertices of the upper base, forming a triangle with the “blue” segment connecting them - see Fig. 2). Thus, of the segments $A_iB_j$ there are at most $2 \cdot 5 = 10$ blue. Similar reasoning carried out with other bases shows that there are also no more than ten red segments. But the total number of segments $A_iB_j$, as it is not difficult to count, is 25, and we found that there are no more than twenty of them. Contradiction. Thus, we proved that all the edges of the common bases of the prism are painted the same color.

Note that the statement of the problem for any prism with an odd number of vertices at the base is not true for a prism with an even number of vertices at the base (give an example).

Let us denote by $x$ the distance from the point $P$ to the point $R$ and put $\alpha = \sqrt{QPR}$ (Fig. 1). Then the relation specified in the given relation is written as follows:

$$\frac{x + |PQ|}{\sqrt{x^2 + |PQ|^2 - 2x|PQ| \cos \alpha}}.$$

Note that when $x$ is fixed, this ratio will be maximum when $\cos \alpha$ is maximum, and this will happen when the plane $PQR$ is perpendicular to the plane $P$ (Fig. 2). Therefore, we can assume that the plane $PQR$ is perpendicular to the plane $P$, and $\angle QPR$ is the angle between the straight line $PQ$ and the plane $P$.
