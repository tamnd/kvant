The geometric chapter contains an image of Euclid's Elements with detailed proofs that do not coincide with Euclid's proofs. Ibn Sina even gives a proof of the V postulate of Euclid (parallel axions), based on the assumption of the existence of equidistant straight lines (equivalent to the postulate being proved!).

In the geometric part of the Book of Healing, Ibn Sina supplements the axions and postulates of Euclid with the axioms of the existence of a point, line and surface and the axiom of the possibility of choosing a point on a line or surface. Ibn Sina systematically applies arithmetic terminology to geometric quantities; for example, he talks about multiplying a line, which the ancient Greeks did not do at all; Ibn Sina's terminology played a significant role in expanding the concept of number (which in ancient times meant only natural numbers). to what we call a positive real number.

As we see, Ibn Sina was mainly interested in problems of mathematics, bordering on logic, of which he was a great sign (in a number of works Ibn Sina developed the logical teachings of Aristotle).

Here are some more examples of Ibn Sina’s arithmetic results contained in the “Book of Healing”:

1. If a number is given which, when divided by 9, leaves a remainder of 1 or 8, then the square of that number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 1. If a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 2 or 7, then the square of that number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 4. If a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 4 or 5, then its square divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 4. the remainder is 7. Finally, if a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 3.6 or 0, then its square divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 0.

2. If a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 1.4 or 7, then its cube divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 1. If a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 2.5 or 8, then its cube divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 8. And if a number divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 3.6 or 0, then its cube divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 8. the remainder is 0.

3. If successive odd numbers are placed in a square table, then the sum of the numbers on each diagonal will be equal to the cube (dimension) of the side; the sum of the numbers filling the square is equal to the square-square (dimension) of the side of the square.

For example, for a square table of size 4x4 we get

$$1 + 11 + 21 + 31 = 7 + 13 + 19 + 25 = 4^3$$ and $$1 + 3 + 5 + ... + 29 + 31 = 4^4.$$
