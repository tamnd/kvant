⟦folio 42⟧

I. Nikolskaya

“It’s not true that...” - how to understand this?

Petya decided to develop his willpower and told Katya about it.
“I’ll start by saying,” he confided to his sister, “that I’ve made a plan for the holidays and will fulfill it in all respects.”
On the eve of the holidays, Petya solemnly handed Katya a piece of paper, on which was neatly written:
During the holidays, I will definitely read at least one thick book, go to a museum or a lecture, and, if there is no thaw, I spend all my evenings at the skating rink.
Having hung the obligation over the bed, Petya resolutely set to work from the first day of the holidays: he began reading “Records of a Hunter” and brought “Three Musicians” from the library.
The next day he went to the Zoological Museum to see the baby mammoth Dima. Petya spent five evenings in a row at the skating rink, but then it became completely cold (+5°!) and the skating rink had to be cancelled. By the end of the holidays, Petya finished reading “Notes of a Hunter” and read half of “Three Musiquettes.” In general, Petya was pleased with himself, but Katya managed to ruin his mood.
“By the way, you weren’t at the skating rink yesterday or the day before,” she remarked sarcastically.
- I had a good reason! - Petya was indignant.
“Well, besides, you weren’t at the lecture,” Katya reminded.
Petya really couldn’t get enough of going to the lecture, but even here he found something to object to: at the Zoological Museum, he listened to a guide talking about mammoths.
“But you were going to a museum or a lecture, not a museum and a lecture at once,” Katya continued, “and you didn’t finish reading The Three Musicians.”
Realizing that he couldn’t argue with Katya, Petya shook his hand and went to his friend Mitya, a very sensible person who was also interested in mathematical logic.
Mitya wanted to console her upset friend.
“We’ll see who’s right, you or Katya,” he said. “What do you know?”
