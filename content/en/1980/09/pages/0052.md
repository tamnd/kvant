⟦folio 50⟧

As always, with the September issue of our magazine, the new academic year begins in the “Applicant Practicum”. In this section, the editors of «Kvant» will systematically publish materials addressed, first of all, to those who are preparing for university entrance exams.

Readers will find in the articles of the “Applicant's Workshop” a detailed explanation of the most difficult topics and individual theoretical issues of school courses in mathematics and physics, analysis of the most common errors of applicants, methodological recommendations, analysis of solutions to typical problems from the options of applied exams. The pages of this section will also contain samples of options for written exams and problems for oral exams in mathematics and physics, which were offered in 1980 at various universities in our country.

The stats of the “Applicant's Workshop” will be useful not only to those who are going to enter the mathematical or physics departments of universities and institutes, but also to applicants to technical schools. Today's tenth-graders, especially those who live in rural and working-class settlements, far from scientific and pedagogical centers, will have the opportunity to learn what the application exam is and what requirements are imposed on applicants. By the way, much of the materials in the “Applicant’s Workshop” will be available to ninth-graders, so all high school students can read this section.

Future applicants, of course, are interested in the question of how best to organize preparation for the application exams. First of all, you need to carefully familiarize yourself with the “Entrance Examination Programs for Applicants.” It lists in detail the basic mathematical and physical concepts that applicants must master, theorems and statements that must be able to be proven, and facts that must be known. All this information is contained in school textbooks - they should be studied first. To successfully pass entrance exams, no additional knowledge is required compared to school courses in mathematics and physics. However, one should not think that simply reading school textbooks again is enough! It is necessary to carefully understand and deeply assimilate the theoretical material, gain creative and lasting skills in solving the problem. The key to success in exams is systematic and regular independent work during the entire time remaining before the exams. Mathematics and physics cannot be subtracted in one day or in one week - only systematic literals take the next step in the exam task and the questions are simple and easy.

A few words about one more issue - about solvers. It is no secret that some applicants (and even more often, their parents) hope to use solvers before exams to quickly and without much difficulty repeat the necessary theoretical material and become familiar with methods for solving typical problems. This is deeply forgotten! No amount of decisiveness can replace the main thing - genuine interest in the chosen specialty, the internal need to learn, hard and real work.

Editorial board of the magazine «Kvant», 1980, No. 1, p. 46-47.
