What's next?
Having learned to play NIS, you may have already asked yourself the question: “Couldn’t the simple and effective discrete methods that led to complete success in the case of NIS be extended to other games?” There have been attempts to apply such methods of the real, but they have not yielded results even for the close “relative” of the NIS - the pinnacle of Chenon’s switching cerae. (This game is very similar to PIP, it is also played on a graph with two visible vertices $s$ and $t$, only the vertices, rather than the edges, are painted in it; in this case, player $C$ tries to show the vertices in such a way that from $s$ to $t$ one can walk along the vertices of their color, and player $P$ - place him.) Moreover, in 1976 Iven and Tarjan. proved that finding a winning strategy for the vertex of the Chenon switching cerus is no less simple than solving non-classical combinator problems, for which, as many mathematicians believe, there are no effective solution algorithms at all. There is still a long way to go before such problems are finally resolved.
