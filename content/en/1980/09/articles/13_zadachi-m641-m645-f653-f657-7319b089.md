Tasks

M641—M645; F653—F657

M641. Given a regular hexagon ABCDEF with center O. Points M and N are the midpoints of side CD and DE. Lines AM and BN intersect at point l. Prove that:
a) triangle ABL and quadrilateral DMLN have equal areas;
b) ALO=OLN=60°;
b) OLD=90°.

E. Gotman

M642. Prove that every natural number can be represented as $a_0+2a_1+2^2a_2+\ldots+2^n a_n$, where each of the numbers $a_k$ is 0, -1 or 1, and $a_k \cdot a_{k+1}=0$ for all $0 \leq k \leq n-1$, and such a representation is unique.

I. Zhuk

M643. Cards with numbers 1, 2, ..., 32 are stacked in order. It is allowed to remove an excess number of cards and place them between some of the remaining ones or under them, without changing the order of both, but otherwise arbitrarily.

This operation is called moving. Prove that in 5 movements it is possible
a) rearrange the cards in reverse order;
b) arrange the cards in any order;
b) prove that not every card order can be obtained in 4 moves.

V. Turchaninov

M644. a) Prove that there exists a convex 1980-gon with sides of lengths 1, 2, ..., 1980, all of whose angles are equal in magnitude.
b) Does such a 1981-gon exist?

G. Gurevich

M645. There are three corridors in the basement (figure 1; $|OA|=|OB|=|OC|=l$), all exits from which are closed. Inspector Warnicke and a representative are there. Warnicke replaces the representative if the distance between them does not exceed $r$. He knows that the representative's maximum speed is half his own maximum speed. At the initial moment, the inspector is at point O and does not see the representative. How

Varika must act to ensure that he catches the representative if a) $r=l/3$, b) $r=l/4$, c) $*r>l/5$, d) $*r>l/7$. Neglect the width of the corridors and the sizes of people. (Varika must come up with such a plan of action that, even if the representative knows about it in advance, he still cannot escape.)

V. Drinfeld. V. Sokolov

F653. Some elements $Z$, connected to a battery with EMF §=10 V and internal resistance $r=10$ kOhm, connected to an external current source as shown in figure 2. The current-voltage characteristic of such a circuit is shown in figure 3. Construct the current-voltage characteristic of element $Z$.

V. Mozhaev

F654. The mass stand $M$ stands on a smooth table. A mass ball $m$ is attached to the tripod on a light thread of length $l$. The thread is deflected at a small angle $\alpha$ and released (Fig. 4). Draw a graph of the speed of the shativ versus time. The table ball with the base of the frame is absolutely elastic.

A. Zilberman

F655. Currently, solenoids with superconducting windings are used. Such solenoids can create a magnetic field for a long time without consuming energy. However, if for some reason a section of the solenoid winding loses its superconducting properties, an accident will occur. In this area, the current will release a large amount of heat and an explosion will occur. Come up with the simplest device to eliminate such accidents (do not try to come up with any circuits with a relay that opens the circuit - they will not help).

G. Myakishev

F656. The rectangular dump truck body is filled with sand. The height of the body is $h=1$ m, its width is $d=3$ m, its length is $l=6$ m. What force acts on the rear side of the dump truck during uniform automatic movement and when it moves with acceleration $a=3$ m/s$^2$? Sand density $\rho=1,5 \cdot 10^3$ kg/m$^3$, friction coefficient between sand grains $\mu=0,6$.

I. Slovodetsky

F657. The radii of curvature of two identical soap bubbles collapsing together are equal to $R$. After the partition between the bubbles burst, one bubble of radius $R_1$ was formed. Find atmospheric pressure. The surface tension coefficient of soap solution is $\sigma$.
