“It’s not true that...” - how to understand this?

Petya decided to develop his willpower and told Katya about it.
“I’ll start by saying,” he confided to his sister, “that I’ve made a plan for the holidays and will fulfill it in all respects.”
On the eve of the holidays, Petya solemnly handed Katya a piece of paper, on which was neatly written:
During the holidays, I will definitely read at least one thick book, go to a museum or a lecture, and, if there is no thaw, I spend all my evenings at the skating rink.
Having hung the obligation over the bed, Petya resolutely set to work from the first day of the holidays: he began reading “Records of a Hunter” and brought “Three Musicians” from the library.
The next day he went to the Zoological Museum to see the baby mammoth Dima. Petya spent five evenings in a row at the skating rink, but then it became completely cold (+5°!) and the skating rink had to be cancelled. By the end of the holidays, Petya finished reading “Notes of a Hunter” and read half of “Three Musiquettes.” In general, Petya was pleased with himself, but Katya managed to ruin his mood.
“By the way, you weren’t at the skating rink yesterday or the day before,” she remarked sarcastically.
- I had a good reason! - Petya was indignant.
“Well, besides, you weren’t at the lecture,” Katya reminded.
Petya really couldn’t get enough of going to the lecture, but even here he found something to object to: at the Zoological Museum, he listened to a guide talking about mammoths.
“But you were going to a museum or a lecture, not a museum and a lecture at once,” Katya continued, “and you didn’t finish reading The Three Musicians.”
Realizing that he couldn’t argue with Katya, Petya shook his hand and went to his friend Mitya, a very sensible person who was also interested in mathematical logic.
Mitya wanted to console her upset friend.
“We’ll see who’s right, you or Katya,” he said. “What do you know?”

In what case is the negation of a statement true, and in what case is it false? Let's start with something simple. Let's say you claim that "Chapaev" is the middle one in our club, and Katya, as always, argues, assuring that "Chapaev" the middle one is not played in our club. If your statement is true, then Katino is false, and if you are wrong, then Katya is right.

The negation of a given sentence $A$ is a sentence that states that $A$ is not true; thus, if $A$ is true, its negation is false; if $A$ is false, its negation is true. The negation of a sentence $A$ is sometimes indicated by $\overline{A}$ or $\overline{A}$ (Fig. 1).

Constructing its negation for any sentence is as easy as shelling pears: just put in front of it the words false, that. For example, the negation of the proposal “Chapaev” the middle one in our club will be

“It reminded me that a sentence and its negation cannot be both true and false at the same time,” Mitya noted.

After thinking, Petya said:

- Yes, and the sentences All the excellent students in our class are sports students and All the excellent students in our class are not sports students are both false: Katya is an excellent student and a figure skater, but you are an excellent student, but you don’t play sports. Unfortunately, some excellent students in our class are not athletes.

“That’s it,” Mitya processed, “you just formulated the negation of the sentence. All the excellent students in our class are sportsmen: Some excellent students in our class are not sportsmen.” This sentence has the same meaning as the sentence It is not true that all the excellent students in our class are athletes. Generality is the negation of the proposition All have the property $P$ is the proposition Some x do not have the property $P$.

Symbolically it is written like this:

$$\forall x P(x) \Leftrightarrow \exists x P(x).$$

The sign $\forall$ means everything (any, any, any), and the sign $\exists$ means neotorious (exists, exists, exists).

Let, for example, $x$ mean a lake, and $P$ the property of being freshwater; then entry (1) will mean “It is not true that all lakes are freshwater and some lakes are not freshwater.”

Interestingly, the negation of a sentence having the form $\exists x P(x)$ is constructed in a similar way:

$$\forall x P(x) \Leftrightarrow \forall x P(x).$$
