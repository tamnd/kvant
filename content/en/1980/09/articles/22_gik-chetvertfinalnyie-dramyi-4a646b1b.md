Quarterfinal dramas
Our page today is related to the results of the quarterfinal matches for the World Championship. Before their start, the most interesting was the duel in Alim-Ata between M. Tal and L. Polugavevsky. The host of the page was at the end of the match in the capital of Kazakhstan and immediately after it ended he asked Lev Polugavevsky to explain the principle of his confident victory with a score of 5.5:2.5. That's what the grandmaster said.
— Recently, due to a number of his successes, Tal’s growth has increased greatly. I think this atmosphere played a negative role for him. And when I found out that his coaching group needed to include two Bakuvites - G. Kasparov, who recently became the youngest grandmaster in the world, the greatest icon of modern openings, and my former coach V. Bagirov, it became clear to me that Talya wanted to bet on the opening. I took this into account when preparing for the match.
Polugavevsky's words are successfully illustrated by the fourth game of the match.
M. Talya - L. Polugavevsky Sicilian defense
1. e4 c5 2. Kf3 d6 3. d4 cd 4. K:d4 Kf6 5. Kc3 a6 6. Cg5 e6 7. f4 b5.
This option was developed in detail by Polugavevsky and bears his name. The grandmaster even wrote a book about him called “The Birth of a Variant.” It is curious that the foreword to it belongs to Mihanl Tal. 8. e5 de 9. fe Qc7 10. ef. In the second game of the match, Talya sacrificed a bishop - 10. C:b5+, however, Black repelled the attack and won. 10.e5 + 11. Ce2 F:g5 12. Qd3 Q:f6 13. Rf1 Qe5 14. Rd1 Ra7 15. Kf3 Qc7 16. Kg5 f5 17. Qd4 h5.
This position is well known to specialists. The next move, found by Tal during home preparation, could have stunned another partner...18. L:f5!? Sacrifice of an entire rook!
In fact, the next decision was a shabby terraca, opened it on the laid down page and handed it to me. At the very top of it was inscribed - 18. R: f5, then densely, in small letters, a whole cascade of options ending somewhere around the 30th move. The analyzes were included in the notebook several years ago... Yes, indeed, it’s not worth experimenting with Polugavevsky in the opening...
Next in the game was 18...ef 19. Kd5 Qd7 20. Qh4 Ce7 21. Kpf1 C:g5 22. C:h5+ Kpf8 23. Q:g5 R:h5 24. Q:h5 Qf7 25. Qh8+ Qg8 26. Qh4 Kpf7 27. Qh5+g6 28. Qh4 Qg7 29. Qd8 Ce6 30. Qxb8 Rd7 31. c4 bc. The influence of the time limit. See for yourself that 31...Q:b2 led to a decisive crossing for Black. 32. Nc3 R:d1+33. K:d1 Qd4 34. Nc3 Qd3+35. Kpf2 Qd4+36. Kpf1 Qd3+. Draw.
In the semi-finals, L. Polugavevsky meets V. Korchnoi, who defeated the world extrempon T. Petrosn with a score of 5.5:3.5.
The tension continued with the match between B. Spassky and L. Portsch. Ten games turned out to be unworthy - the opponents achieved one victory each and eight games ended in peace. According to the position, the pre-option in this case was given to the participant who won more games with black. Happiness turned out to be on the side of Hungarian chess.
(A strange message was published in some newspapers. They said that in a tied outcome of the match, the winner is the one who has more victories!?) This is the position in which, as it turned out, the fate of the match was decided.

Spassky played here 24. Bc3? and after 24...Ke3 25. Qh4 C:c3 26. bc K:f1 27. R:f1 Cf5 28. d4 R:f6 29. gf Q:f6 30. Q:f6 + R:f6 was simply left without a pawn and after eight moves was forced to surrender. Instead of 24. Bc3, 24. Cd5 deserved attention. The position of the queen on h3 is uncomfortable, but on 24...Ke3 it can be sacrificed - 25. Q:e3!? B:e3 26. C:e3, and then Ce3-d2-c3. Analyze this opportunity yourself.
L. Portsch's opponent in the semifinals was R. Hübner, who beat grandmaster A. Adoryan with a minimum score of 5.5:4.5. The penultimate game of the match turned out dramatically for Hungarian chess.

After 65...R:h3 Black won the game and equalized the score. However, they played 65...Rlc5, hoping to move into an easily won pawn endgame. This was followed by a fantastic answer 66. K:h4!!, and Hübner managed to save the game, since the captured rooks 66...R:g5 led to stalemate!

It is easy to report that $a + b + c$ cannot equal 5, 6 and 9 (the cubes of these numbers end in 5, 6 and 9 respectively). It remains to check the cases $a + b + c = 7$ and $a + b + c = 8$.
