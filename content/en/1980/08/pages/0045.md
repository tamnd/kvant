EMF of the battery, if the external current should not change by more than 50 mA?

$U = 26 - 13I$. The point of intersection of the load straight line with the current-voltage characteristic of the barrett determines the current flowing through the circuit: $I_0 = 1000$ mA. According to the problem conditions, the permissible current limit values ​​are 950 mA and 1050 mA. Let's plot straight lines $I_1 = 950$ mA and $I_2 = 1050$ mA on the graph. They cross the current-voltage characteristic of the barrett. Let us draw load lines through the intersection points. (Since the slope of the load line depends only on the resistance $R$, which, according to the conditions of the problem, remains unchanged, all load lines are parallel.) These load lines intersect the axis $U$ at points $U_1 = \varepsilon_1 = 20$ V and $U_2 = \varepsilon_2 = 32$ V. Thus, when the emf of the battery changes in the range from $\varepsilon_1 = 20$ V to $\varepsilon_2 = 32$ V, the current in the circuit will be change in the range from $I_1 = 950$ mA to $I_2 = 1050$ mA, that is, it will change unchanged with an accuracy of ±5%.

F596. The time interval between two consecutive occultations of Jupter's satellite Io varies throughout the year from a minimum value of 42 and 28 minutes 21 s to a maximum value of 42 and 28 minutes 51 s. Receiving these data, determine the speed of light, assuming that the Earth moves around the Sun in a circular orbit with a radius of 150 ml. Km. The radius of Jupter's orbit is much larger than the radius of the Earth's orbit, and the speed of Jupter is much less than the speed of the Earth; we can assume that during the year the relative position of the Earth and Jupter practically does not change and changes in each duration of Io's orbital period are associated only with a change in the direction of the Earth's speed. The greatest deviations of each revolution of Io from the true one will be observed when the Earth's speed is directed along a straight line connecting the Earth and Jupter.

The minimum time interval $t_1 = 42$ and 28 min 21 s between two successive eclipses of Io is observed at a time when the Earth is moving towards Jupter, and the maximum $t_2 = 42$ and 28 min 51 s is when the Earth is moving away from Jupter. In the first case, during the time $t$, corresponding to the true period of revolution of Io, the Earth will approach Jupter at a distance of $l = v_3t$ ($v_3$ is the speed of the Earth), and the observed period of revolution of Io will be less than the true one for a time

$$t - t_1 = v_3t / c,$$

where $c$ is the speed of light. Considering that $t = (t_1 + t_2) / 2, t - t_1 = (t_2 - t_1) / 2$ and $v_3 = 2\pi R / T$ ($R = 150$ ml. km is the radius of the Earth’s orbit, $T = 365$ days is the Earth’s orbital period), from the last equality we obtain

$$t_2 - t_1 = \frac{2\pi R}{T} (t_2 + t_1) / c.$$

where

$$c = \frac{2\pi R}{T} \frac{t_2 + t_1}{t_2 - t_1} \approx 3,03 \cdot 10^5 \text{ км/c.}$$
