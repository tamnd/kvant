F598. A vessel of depth $H$ is filled with liquid, the density of which varies linearly from $\varrho_0$ at the surface to $Q$ at the bottom of the vessel. Two small balls of equal volume $V$, connected by a thin, weightless, unstretched thread of length $l$, are placed into the vessel. The density of one ball is $\varrho_1$, the density of the others is $\varrho_2$. After some time, the balls are installed as shown in figure. Find the tension force of the thread.

The speed of an electron leaving the cathode with energy $E$ is equal to $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{2E/m}$, where $m$ is the mass of the electron. In a uniform magnetic field with an inductance $\vec{B} \perp \vec{v}$, the electron moves along a circle of radius $R$ (see Fig.). The centripetal acceleration $|\vec{a}| = v^2/R$ is imparted to the electron by the Lorentz force $|\vec{F}| = e|\vec{B}||\vec{v}|$ (e is the charge of the electron), that is

$$\frac{mv^2}{R} = e|\vec{B}||\vec{v}|,$$

or $$\frac{2E}{R} = e|\vec{B}| \sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}.$$

From here

$$R = \frac{\sqrt{2Em}}{e|\vec{B}|}.$$

So in the anode circuit it is absent when electrons do not reach the anode, that is, at $R \leq r/2$ (see Fig.), or

$$\frac{r}{2} \geq \frac{\sqrt{2Em}}{e|\vec{B}|}.$$

This condition is satisfied when

$$|\vec{B}| \geq \frac{2\sqrt{2Em}}{re}.$$

At $|\vec{B}| = 2\sqrt{2Em}/re$ the current in the circuit stops.

I. Slovodechkiy

The forces acting on each ball are the gravitational force $\vec{m}\vec{g}$, the thread tension force $\vec{T}$ and the Archimedes force $|\vec{F}| = \varrho_x gV$, where $\varrho_x$ is the density of the liquid at the depth $x$ at which the ball is located (see Fig.). Since the density of a liquid is linear as the depth of the vessel increases, at any depth $x$ the density of the liquid is $\varrho_x = \varrho_0 + \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} x$.

Let us write down the equilibrium conditions for each ball (in projections onto the $X$ axis):

$$m_1 g + T - F_1 = \varrho_1 gV + T - \left(\varrho_0 + \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} x_1\right) gV = 0,$$

$$m_2 g - T - F_2 = \varrho_2 gV - T - \left(\varrho_0 + \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} x_2\right) gV = 0,$$

where $x_1, x_2$ is the immersion depth of the balls. Solving these equations together, we find

$$T = \frac{Vg}{2} \left(\varrho_2 - \varrho_1 - \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} l\right).$$

The configuration indicated at figure is possible with $T \geq 0$, $x_1 > 0$, $x_2 < H$. If we imagine that the balls are not connected to each other, but $\varrho_0 < \varrho_1 < \varrho$ and $\varrho_0 < \varrho_2 < \varrho$, then the first ball is installed at a depth where the density of the liquid is $\varrho_1 = \varrho_0 + \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} x_1$;

The second ball will behave similarly:

$$\varrho_2 = \varrho_0 + \frac{\varrho - \varrho_0}{H} x_2.$$

The distance between the balls in this case will be equal to

$$x_2 - x_1 = \frac{(\varrho_2 - \varrho_1)H}{\varrho - \varrho_0}.$$

If the length of the thread $l$ is greater than $x_2 - x_1$, then the thread will not be tensioned.
