⟦folio 47⟧

Quantum
for younger students

Quests

1. a) Find a three-digit number equal to the cube of the sum of its digits.
6) Find a four-digit number equal to the fourth power of the sum of its digits.
c) Is there a five-digit number equal to the fifth power of the sum of its digits?

2. One day I was returning from a Sunday trip out of town. It was very stuffy in the train carriage. I went out into the vestibule, but it wasn’t the best there either. But then the train slowed down, and a fresh breeze blew from the ventilation necks. The train stopped, the breeze died down, reappeared during the next braking, and so on at all stations. Where did he come from? Which vestibule of the varon was I in—front or back? I will also note that the door from the vestibule to the carriage and all the windows in the carriage were open.

3. Is it possible to issue coins in denominations of 5, 20 and 50 kopecks to make 5 rubles?

4. Seven diplomats are sitting at a round table. They must have one conversation with each other. Two diplomats will only talk if they are nearby. After each Diplomat concludes negotiations with its neighbors, the Diplomat stands up and takes new positions to continue conversations.

Is it possible to organize a meeting of diplomats so that with each new placement at the table, each of them has new neighbors?

5. Divide the round clock face into three parts so that the sum of the numbers in each part is equal to 17.

These tasks were suggested to us by F. Bartenev,
G. Karaev, A. Savin, Y. Temratsev,
