How do dew droplets form?

As you, of course, know, air is a mixture of various gases. First of all, these are nitrogen and oxygen. There is also water vapor in the air. True, their contribution is not great: the density of water vapor is hundreds of times less than the density of air. But their presence determines the so-called air humidity. Human well-being, the growth and development of plants, the operation of many precision instruments, and the like are highly dependent on air humidity.

It turns out that the amount of water vapor in the air cannot be arbitrary. There is a maximum mass of water that can be evaporated inside 1 m³ of air at a given temperature. With further addition, condensation of water vapor occurs and water droplets are formed. The higher the temperature, the more moisture the air can hold.
