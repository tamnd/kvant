⟦folio 36⟧

Let us first consider the main case when the arc $AB$ of a circle with center $O_i$ does not touch the circle $\Gamma$. Let us draw a circle $K$ of radius $R/2$ (Fig. 6) through the points $O$ and $A$; it intersects the circle with center $O_i$ at points $A_i$ and $C$. Let us denote by $l$ the length of the arc of the circle $K$, enclosed between the points $A$ and $C$ and tangent to the circle $\Gamma$. It is not difficult to prove that $\overrightarrow{AC} < l$. On the other hand, $l = R \cdot \varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the radian measure of the angle $AOC$; hence $\overrightarrow{AC} < R\varphi$. Thus, if we managed to arc $AB$ into smaller arcs, through the ends of which and the point $O$ we can check a circle of radius no greater than $R/2$, then we will thereby prove the inequality $\overrightarrow{AB} < R\alpha$. Let us describe the method of such partitioning.

Consider the circle $K'$, symmetrical to the circle $K$ relative to the line $OC$; let it intersect the arc $AB$ at the point $A_1$, and the point $A'$ be symmetrian to the point $A$. Recall that the arc $AB$ was chosen so that $|AO| > |A_1O|$; hence $|A'O| > |A_1O|$, whence $\overrightarrow{COA_1} > \overrightarrow{COA'} = \varphi$. Next, we will symmetrically reflect the circle $K'$ relative to the straight line $OA_1$, etc. Since with such a division of the arc $AB$ small arcs $\overrightarrow{AC}, \overrightarrow{CA_1}$, ... are visible from the point $O$ at an angle not less than $\varphi$, through a finite number of reflections the point $B$ falls inside the reflected circle (at figure 6 this image circle is a point).

Then, as the last circle, you need to take a circle of radius less than $R/2$, passing through the points $O$, $B$ and the last resulting point of the partition.

The required partition has been constructed.

It remains to consider the case when the arc $AB$ inside the circle touches the circle $\Gamma$. Our goal is to prove the inequality $\overrightarrow{AB} < R\alpha$. Let us assume the opposite: let $\overrightarrow{AB} > R\alpha$. Then there is $\varepsilon > 0$ such that $\overrightarrow{AB} > \varepsilon + R\alpha = \left( R + \frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha} \right)\alpha$. But if the radius of the outer circle is slightly increased, for example by $\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}$, then there will be no contact with the arc $AB$, and therefore $\overrightarrow{AB} < \left( R + \frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha} \right)\alpha$. We have come to disagreement. Therefore, $\overrightarrow{AB} < R\alpha$ in this case as well.

Note that the length of the boundary of the union of our circles coincides with the length of the circle $\Gamma$ only in the case when all the inner circles pass through the center $O$ of the circle $\Gamma$ and touch it (Fig. 7).

S. Kazakov
