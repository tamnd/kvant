A few questions about astronomy
(see «Kvant» No. 5, p. 42)

1. At any point on the equator, the length of the day is always equal to the length of the night.
2. There is a change of seasons at the equator. Although the Sun is always above the horizon for exactly 12 hours, its maximum height above the horizon varies during that day.
3. At the equator, twice a year - on the days of the spring (March 21) and autumn (September 23) equinoxes - the Sun is at its zenith at noon. Thus, the hottest days at the equator occur in spring and autumn.
While in the middle latitudes there is one seasonal cycle per year, at the equator there are two cycles.
4. No, not intermediate. At the equator, on the day of the summer solstice (as well as on the day of the winter solstice), the height of the culmination of the Sun is lower compared to other days of the year.
