⟦folio 40⟧

Let us write the equation of motion of the beam in projections onto the $X$ axis:

$$ma = F_1 - F_2 = -\frac{2\mu mg}{L} x.$$

From this equation it follows that at the initial moment the beam moves according to the harmonic law:

$$x(t) = \frac{L}{2} \cos 2\pi v t,$$

Where

$$v = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{2\mu g}{L}}.$$

(The amplitude of the beam’s vibrations is equal to the initial coordinate of its center of mass.)

The velocity modulus $\vec{u}$ of the beam increases with time according to the law

$$u(t) = \pi v L \sin 2\pi v t$$

and at some instants of time $t_1$ becomes equal to the linear speed $v$ of the right cylinder. Since $v = \omega r$,

$$\omega r = \pi v L \sin 2\pi v t_1.$$

From here we find $t_1$:

$$t_1 = \frac{1}{2\pi v} \arcsin \frac{\omega r}{\pi v L}.$$

Since at the moment of time $t = t_1$ the slippage between the right cylinder and the beam will disappear, the friction force $\vec{F}_2$ from this moment will be the static friction force.

The static friction force compensates for forces directed parallel to the plane of possible sliding. Consequently, starting from time $t_1$, the beam will move at a constant speed $v = \omega r$. This movement will continue until the static friction force reaches its maximum possible value, equal to the sliding friction force. This will happen at time $t_2$, when the coordinate of the center of mass of the beam is equal to zero and the friction force $\vec{F}_1(t_2)$ becomes equal to the maximum possible static friction force $\vec{F}_2(t_2)$ between the beam and the small cylinder. The time $t_2$ can be determined from the correspondence

$$\omega r(t_2 - t_1) = x(t_1) \equiv \frac{L}{2} \cos 2\pi v t_1;$$

from here

$$t_2 = t_1 + \frac{1}{2\omega r} \cos 2\pi v t_1.$$

Starting from the moment of time $t_2$, the projection onto the axis $X$ of the mixed center of mass of the beam changes in time according to the law

$$x(t) = -\frac{\omega r}{2\pi v} \sin (2\pi v(t - t_2));$$

the projection of the beam velocity changes in time as

$$\dot{x}(t) = -\omega r \cos (2\pi v(t - t_2))$$

(movement with non-zero initial speed and zero initial displacement).

Since $r < R$, the maximum speed of the beam in the established process is less than the linear speed of rotation of the surface of the large cylinder and does not exceed the linear speed of rotation of the surface of the smaller cylinder ($u_{\max} = \omega r$). Therefore, starting from time $t_2$, uniform motion of the beam is impossible. The beam will perform a harmonic oscillation with an amplitude of $A = \frac{\omega r}{2\pi v}$ and a period of $T = \frac{1}{v}$.
