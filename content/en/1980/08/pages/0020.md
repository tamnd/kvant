⟦folio 18⟧

Poem by Lobachevsky

In a letter sent from Stockholm in the fall of 1890, Sofia Kovalevskaya writes: “... you cannot be a mathematician without at the same time being in the soul.” And indeed, as the history of science shows, since the time of the Pyragoreans, mathematics has become more fascinating and even tried to write.

S. Kovalevskaya's interest in literature is well known. But did the great Russian geometer N. I. Lobachevsky write poetry?

Referring to a poetic message to Lobachevsky, composed by his wife’s brother N. E. Velikopolsky, L. B. Modzalevsky writes*): “From this message it is clear that N. I. Lobachevsky himself was engaged in versification in his young years. But

*)《Materials for the biography of N. I. Lobachevsky》（M., AN, 1948), p. 165.

his poetic experiments have not reached us.”

“But maybe they can be found?” — I thought, and a long search began. And here in front of me is a thin book, the 17th issue of the literary magazine “The Volga Ant,” published in 1834. Reading:

Volga flood near Kazan

The queen flows solemnly to the distant waters of the vast Caspian Sea, You turn towards Kazan on the evidence of the search. And here in front of me is a thin book, the 17th issue of the literary magazine “The Volga Ant,” published in 1834. Reading:

This poem about our Volga begins with such sincere lines. I look at the signature: “Ave. L...sky."

If in this mysterious abbreviation “L...sky” the surname of the great geometer is actually hidden, then why “Pr.” and not “N.” or "H. AND."?

It is known that Lobachevsky signed his publications in different ways. In particular, two of his essays: “Decreasing the degree in a binomial equation when an exponent without units is divided by 8” and “On the disappearance of trionometric lines” - published in separate editions by the printing houses of Kazan University in 1834, that is, just when the seventeenth issue of “The Volga Ant” was published, signed “Pr. Lobachevsky." This means that the author of the poem “The Volga Flood at Kazan” would like to know. Nikolai Ivanovich himself, one might say, tells us to find out the protected name “Pr. L...sky" - Professor Lobachevsky! But why did he, the rector of Kazan University and a famous mathematician, suddenly - precisely in 1834 - “risk” publishing his poem?

Is this connected with Pushkin’s arrival in Kazan in September 1833? Pushkin, who said: “Inspiration is needed in pozzie, as in geometry”?
