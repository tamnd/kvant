From March 24 to March 28, 1980, the Festival of Young Physicists was held at the Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences. By publishing a story about this holiday, the editors hope that similar holidays will be organized in various regions of our country.
The high scientific level of the Moscow Festival of Young Physicists was ensured by the active training of FIAN scientists. I would like scientists from other institutes, and above all Komsomol members, to take an active part in organizing such holidays.

V. Kaslin. A. Starodub

FROM the greeting of the team of school No. 2

Participants in scientific seminars, which took place in the column hall of the Physical Institute named after. During the spring school holidays, P. N. Lebedev of the USSR Academy of Sciences (FIAN) looked with interest at the seemingly strange paintings hung on the walls and columns of the hall. The hero of all the pictures was a cute rain bear named Puf. He courageously got out of the incredible situations in which he found himself, violating certain laws of physics. He was helped by his wonderful ingenuity, resourcefulness, self-control and, of course, a sense of humor.

Over the course of five days, from March 24 to March 28, children from the heel of Moscow schools (No. 2, 18, 57, 91, 179) and from school No. 82 in the village of Chernogolovka near Moscow came to visit the cheerful Puf - students of the Pra-
Festival of young physicists, dedicated to the 110th anniversary of the birth of V. I. Lenin. The first letters of the name of this holiday gave the name to the funny lion cub.

The idea of ​​holding the Young Physicists Festival was born at FIAN a long time ago. It was based on many years of work carried out by the institute’s staff at sponsored school No. 2, as well as the first work experiences of students from school No. 179 in the Optical Laboratory of the institute. The purpose of the holiday is to encourage schoolchildren to engage in scientific activities and help them choose a future profession. In addition to the FIAN, the All-Union Society “Znanie” and the Commission of the USSR Academy of Sciences for Work with Youth took part in organizing and holding the Holiday.

The holiday began on Monday, March 24, with a meeting with leading scientists from FIAN.

The history of this oldest research institute in our country, inextricably linked with the history of the formation of Russian, and then Soviet, school of physics, was vividly presented to the children during a speech by the wonderful director of the institute, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences A. V. Vinogradov.

One of the most interesting messages for the children on the first day of the Holiday was the speech of Lenin and Nobel Prize laureate Academician A. M. Prozhorov. Aleksandr Mikhailovich spoke about many scientific areas, including problems of communication and information. But central to his speech was a particularly important talk about the problem of a scientific degree, about the different paths of young people into science. The guys literally bombarded Alexander Mikhailovich with a stream of questions about his personal path into science, about his point of view on the problem of training a theoretical physicist and an experimental physicist.
