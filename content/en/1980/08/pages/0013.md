Exercise
our readers

1. Prove that $0 \leq a \leq b$ satisfies the inequality
$$\ln \frac{b}{a} \leq \frac{b^2-a^2}{2ab}.$$
V. Yasinsky

2. Prove that if the non-octorian term of the sequence 1, 31, 331, 3331, ... is divisible by 541, then it is also divisible by 19.
I. Suev

3. Prove that if
$$x = a_1 + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n} + \frac{1}{a_n - y}$$
That
$$y = a_n + \frac{1}{a_n - 1} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_2 + x}$$
M. Gerver

4. Let $a, b, c$ be the lengths of the sides of the triangle $ABC$, $a_1, b_1, c_1$ be the corresponding lengths of the sides of the triangle

$A_1B_1C_1$ with vertices at the points of tangency of the circle inscribed in triangle $ABC$ (see figure). Prove that
$$\frac{a^2}{a_1^2} + \frac{b^2}{b_1^2} + \frac{c^2}{c_1^2} \geq 12.$$
V. Melnik

5. A cylinder is built on the base of a right circular cone. Prove that the intersection of this cylinder with a plane tangent to the cone produces an ellipse whose area is equal to the lateral surface of the cone.
B. Aleynikkov

6. In an equilateral triangle, the bissertis angle at the base is twice the length of the bissertis angle at the apex. Find the angles of this triangle.
S. Sefibekov

7. Prove that the center of the inscribed circle of the triangle $ABC$, the intersection point of its medians and the intersection point of the segments connecting the vertices of the triangle $ABC$ with the points of tangency of its sides with the out-circle (an out-circle is a circle tangent to a side of the triangle and the extension of its two other sides) are on the same straight line.
U. Alla

8. Prove that in every triangle
$$m_a^2 = \left(\frac{b+c}{2}\right)^2 - bc \left(\sin \frac{\widehat{A}}{2}\right)^2,$$
where $m_a$ is the length of the median drawn to the side opposite the angle $A$, $b$ and $c$ are the lengths of the other two sides of the triangle, $\widehat{A}$ are the values ​​of the angle $A$.
F. Kadirov

9. The set $M$ contains a finite number $n > 2$ of points of the plane that do not lie on the same straight line, and if the non-octorian three points of this set form a triangle, then the point of intersection of the altitudes of this triangle also belongs to the multi-point

gesture $M$. What values ​​can $n$ take?
R. Ushakov

10. We will call a function $F$, which defines on the set of all integers and takes non-negative integer values, modern, if for any integers $m, n$ the correspondence holds
$$F(mn) = F(m)F(n),$$
$$F(m+n) \leq 1980 [F(m) + F(n)]$$
and, in addition, $F(1980) = 0$.
Find all the modern features.
B. Katz

11. Find the maximum number of ways in which one triangle can be inscribed into another (the vertices of the inscribed triangle are on different sides of the described one).

A. Serdyuk

12. Without the help of proof
a) $\cos 42^\circ < \frac{3}{4}$;
b) $\tg 70^\circ > \frac{19}{7}$.
S. Mayzus

13. In expressions like
$$\sqrt{A \pm \sqrt{B}}$$ you can often get rid of the double root by using the formula
$$\sqrt{1 \pm 2 \cos \alpha \sin \alpha} = |\cos \alpha \pm \sin \alpha|.$$
For example:
$$\sqrt{13 \pm \sqrt{48}} = \sqrt{13} \sqrt{1 \pm 2 \sqrt{\frac{12}{13}} \sqrt{\frac{1}{13}}} = \sqrt{13} \left(\sqrt{\frac{12}{13}} \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}\right) = \sqrt{12} \pm 1.$$
Here are some examples to help you solve it yourself:
1) $\sqrt{2 \pm \sqrt{3}}$;
2) $\sqrt{11 \pm \sqrt{21}}$;
3) $\sqrt{9 \pm 4 \sqrt{2}}$.
V. Olkhov
