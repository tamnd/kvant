First decision. Let K, L, M, N be the midpoints of the side AB, BC, CD and AD of the inscribed quadripartite ABCD (Fig. 1), [AC] ⊥ [BD]. Let us prove that |ON| = |BL| and |OM| = |BK|.

Indeed, |KL| = |MN| and [KL] || [MN] as midlines in triangles ABC and ACD with common base AC. Therefore BKL = BAC = BDC (inscribed angles based on the same arc). Further, [OM] ⊥ [CD], and [MN] ⊥ [BD] (since [MN] || [AC]), so BDC = OMN. So BKL = OMN. In addition, KBL = 180° – MDN = MON. Thus, triangles BKL and MON are congested (on a side and two angles), whence |ON| = |BL|, |OM| = |BK|.

Equalities |OK| = |DM| and |OL| = |DN| follows from the concircleness of the triangles OKL and DMN.

Second solution. Let us draw a chord AE perpendicular to AD (Fig. 2). Arc AE is loaded onto arc BC (since ADE = BAC), so |AE| = |BC|. Since EAD = 90°, ED is the diameter and O ∈ [ED]. Since [ON] is the midline in right triangle ADE, |ON| = |AE|/2 = |BC|/2, as stated.

Let us give an example of partitioning space into pairwise skew lines.

Let the line l0 be vertical (the Oz axis is at figure 1). Through each point T of the horizontal plane α (Oxy), different from O, we draw a straight line TM, which is perpendicular to the segment OT and inclined to the plane α at an angle arctan 1/|OT|.

Thus, as r = |OT| from 0 to +∞ the straight line TM will gradually tilt from the vertical position l0 to the horizontal. For a fixed r, the straight lines TM rotate around the l0 axis, forming a single-strip einerbolude Γr (Fig. 2). For each r > 0, a hyperbolide with a “neck” of radius r, increasingly wider, is obtained.

The straight lines TM, constructed for all points T ∈ α, different from O, and the straight line l0 form the required partition. Let's prove it.

First, let us prove that no two of the drawn lines are parallel. Indeed, if two straight lines belong to different families Γr1 and Γr2, r1 ≠ r2, then the angles φ1 and φ2 of their inclination to the horizontal plane α are different. If two lines belong to the same family Γr, then their projections either onto the plane α or onto the vertical plane will be very indo not parallel.

(Note that on each hyperbolide Γr we can choose one of two families of linear generators: we can direct the vector TM′ from the point T (Fig. 1) clockwise or counterclockwise. We direct it the same way each time.)

Secondly, you need to prove that exactly one of the straight lines passes through each point in space. This is almost obvious “from the continuity messages,” but for a rigorous proof it is better to use the coordinate method.

Let P(x; y; z) be a point of the line TM on figure 1; P′(x; y) its projection onto the plane α. Then |TP| = r|z|, therefore |OP′|² = r²(1 + z²), that is

x² + y² = r²(1 + z²).
