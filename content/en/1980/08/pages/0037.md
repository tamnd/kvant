6) Let the centers $O_1, O_2, \dots, O_n$ of all n circles lie on the same diameter of the outer circle $\Gamma$. Let us denote by $R$ the radius of the circle $\Gamma$, and by $r_i$ the radius of the circle with center $O_i$.

If the “inner” circles do not intersect (touching is allowed), then the length of the boundaries of their union is equal to $\sum_{i=1}^{n} 2\pi r_i = \pi \sum_{i=1}^{n} 2r_i \leq 2\pi R$, which is what we needed to prove.

For the general case the following lemma follows:
Let the circles with centers $O_1, O_2, O'_1$ be located as shown in figure 1. Then

$$\widehat{AB} + \widehat{BM} > \widehat{AM}$$

(by $\widehat{AB}$ we denoted the length of the circles $AB$).

Proof of the lemma. Let us construct an auxiliary circle with center $O'_1$ as shown in figure 2. It is not difficult to prove that $\widehat{BM} + \widehat{MC} > \widehat{BC}$. Next, $\widehat{AB} + \widehat{BC} = \widehat{AMC}$. Adding these two relations, we obtain $\widehat{AB} + \widehat{BM} > \widehat{AM}$, as required.

We apply the lemma and immediately obtain a proof of the statement for the case of two circles (see figure 3).

Let now we have n circles with centers $O_1, O_2, \dots, O_n$ on the same circle diameter $\Gamma$.

Let us denote by $\Gamma_1$ the boundaries of their union (in what follows we will call $\Gamma_1$ a contour). Let us distance the sequence of contours $\Gamma_1, \Gamma_2, \Gamma_3, \dots, \Gamma_n$, where contour $\Gamma_{i+1}$ is obtained from contour $\Gamma_i$ by replacing the circle with center $O_i$ by the circle with center $O'_i$ on the same diameter of the circle $\Gamma$, touching the boundaries of the circle with center $O_i$ from the inside, and the circle with center $O_{i+1}$ from the outside (on figure 4 shows how the original black outline $\Gamma_1$ turns into a blue outline $\Gamma_2$; figure 5 shows the red outline $\Gamma_5$, and the original circles are marked with a black dotted line).

Let $L_i$ be the length of contour $\Gamma_i$. Based on Lemma $L_i \leq L_{i+1}$, and equality is achieved if and only if the circle with center $O'_i$ coincides with the circle with center $O_i$. The last contour $\Gamma_n$ will be the boundary of the union, just like this and all k circles, and therefore, according to the remark made at the beginning of the solution, its length is $L_n \leq 2\pi R$. Therefore, $L_1 \leq L_2 \leq \dots \leq L_n \leq 2\pi R$, which is what was required.

C) Let us now move on to the case when all circles contain the center O of the outer circle $\Gamma$.

Obviously, the contour can be divided into arcs of internal circles that have the following property: when moving along the muzzle $AB$ from $A$ to $B$ the distance to the center O of the circle $\Gamma$ decreases. Since all circles contain the center of the circle $\Gamma$, any of its radii intersects the contour once.

Therefore, to solve the problem, it is enough to prove that $\widehat{AB} \leq Ra$, where $a$ is the radian measure of the angle $AOB$; then the length of the contour, which is the sum of the lengths of the arcs, will be no more than 2$\pi R$.
