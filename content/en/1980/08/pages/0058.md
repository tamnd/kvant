It turns out that when the drop dries, a convex salt ring is formed, inside which small salt crystals are distributed in an even layer. Since the guys were unable to find an explanation for this effect in the literature, they put forward three hypotheses and carried out experiments that were supposed to confirm or refute the hypotheses put forward.

Some reports were abstract in nature. In them, the guys touched on the most modern and interesting areas of physics, for example, the control of thermonuclear fusion, holosrania, and the effects of abnormally low friction.

Like every holiday, the Festival of Young Physicists ended with the presentation of prizes and gifts. The authors of the reports recognized as the best by the journal received proofs of the works of leading scientists. Among them are reprints of the Nobel lectures of academicians N. G. Basov and A. M. Prokhorov with their authors. A. Kushnerov, author of the report “Crystal Formation. Growing methods. Application" and A. Akhmetov, M. Matveev and V. Pokrovsky, author of the report "Some observations on the drying of solution drops." (The topic of these reports is very close to the “Quant” Laboratory.) The schools participating in the Festival were loaded with certificates from the Komsomol Committee of the Lebedev Physical Institute and a subscription journal “Quant” for 1981. The all-encompassing society “Knowledge” donated the collections “Science and Man” to the school team leaders. Special challenge prizes - a store of resistances from which the famous Russian physicist N.A. Umov worked, and the glass lion cub Pyuf, a work of art from Fianov glass makers - were given to the teams of school No. 2 and No. 57, which took 1st and 2nd places in the Young Physicists Tournament.
