⟦folio 20⟧

A. Dozorov

Physics without instruments

It's summer, time for a fun school holiday. Textbooks are put aside, there is no need to pore over homework. How nice it is to jump from a slope into a river, or wander through the forest, or just lie down in the grass and look up at the sky, without thinking about anything. But look carefully around you, and you will see a lot of interesting things in the most seemingly attractive and familiar things.

Here you are standing near a pond or a large forest meadow. There are almost certainly water strider beetles running back and forth across the water surface. Take your time, stop and watch how the bug crushes the surface layer of liquid underneath, leaning on it. He apparently “knows” physics.

On a summer evening, in thick grass or in the forest, fireflies can be seen on tree stumps. These living lanterns produce chemicals in their organization that, when oxidized in air, emit light. Firefly “created” a source of light in his organization.

The cuttlefish pushes liquid out of its organization and moves according to the laws of jet motion. Well done too.

And there are many curious things in inanimate nature. We offer you several experiments that you can conduct on your own or with your friends. Don't be upset if you can't explain everything to them. The new school year will come, and much of what we saw in the summer will find its explanation.

So, let's get to it.

1. Agree, not many residents of the middle zone, where it is not so hot in summer, have seen a mirage. If you want to see him, be patient and do the following. First, select a suitable, level wall facing the Sun during the day. Wait for calm, sunny weather. Place yourself with a friend at opposite ends of the wall, next to her. Look carefully along it, and you will suddenly see...
reflection of your friend.

We warn you once again that the experiment requires a lot of patience. Any good experiment, as a rule, does not work out right away.

2. If you keep your eye on the Moon, one day you will definitely see one, and if you’re lucky, two rainbow rings around it.
