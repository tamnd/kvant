Foil task. If the foil from the object is placed on a hard surface, always in one direction, then it always curls upward, towards this direction. Why?

"Match" task. As you know, ointment has no effect on a common match. But if you light a match and let her decide, then a strong ointment will attract her. Explain this phenomenon.

All participants in the finals, for the most part, successfully completed the proposed tasks. The main ideas of the solution were discussed during physiology and aroused great interest not only among the participants, but also among the critics. We hope that some of the guys continued to work on the tasks after the end of the Tournament.

According to the final results, team No. 2 (captain - A. Odintsov) took first place in the Tournament. She was awarded the challenge prize of the Tournament - a resistance store from the physical laboratory of the famous Russian physicist N.A. Umov with his author. Second place was taken by team school No. 57 (captain - D. Filippov). She received the second, also challengeable, prize - the glass lion cub Pluf, made by skilled Fan glassblowers. Third place was won by team school No. 179 (captain - S. Shishkov).

Let us note that for the first time the Tournament of Young Physicists was held last year, in 1979. Then it was the first experience. The current Tournament was distinguished by a wider composition of its participants and significantly better organization. This became possible thanks to the help of the Lebedev Physical Institute, the All-Union Society “Znanie”, the Commission for Work with Youth of the USSR Academy of Sciences, physics teachers, students of the Physics Faculty of Moscow State University, MIPT students and many others.

For the third time, students from Omsk and the Omsk region took part in optimization in physics and mathematics, organized jointly by the Moscow Physical Institute (MIPT) and the Omsk Polytechnic Institute (OmPI). This optimization was born from the common slogan for institutes: “Getting to know the student starting from school.”

Schoolchildren's competitions were held according to the rules of student competitions. Thirteen problems in mathematics and eight problems in physics were proposed for solution. The tasks are non-standard, of varying degrees of difficulty, designed for ninth grade students. Of course, it is impossible to solve all the tasks in four hours, and this was not required. To win, it was enough to solve several problems, the completeness of the solution and the number of participants who solved the problem. Thanks to this system, it was possible, having completed only two tasks, to identify those who, trying to declare the immensity, took on everything at once, without bringing everything to completion.

As a result, first place among mathematicians was taken by Evveny Brusnetsov (school No. 88, 10th grade) and Igor Titov (school No. 109, 9th grade).

The best physicists were Olets Derzhekov (school No. 66, 10th grade), Mikhail Markin (school No. 88, 9th grade) and Vadim Maslov (school No. 11, 8th grade).

After the end of the Olympiad, a problem analysis took place, and even later, an interesting conversation with students from MIPT and Ompi, where schoolchildren received answers to many questions that interested them, and became acquainted with the advertising gases “For Science” and “Polytechnic”. The winners of the Olympiad received awards - certificates and diplomas - and recommendations for entering a university.
