Among the tasks of the second type were, for example, the following:

Foil task. If the foil from the object is placed on a hard surface, always in one direction, then it always curls upward, towards this direction. Why?

"Match" task. As you know, ointment has no effect on a common match. But if you light a match and let her decide, then a strong ointment will attract her. Explain this phenomenon.

Problem "pV-diagram". Some amount of oxygen is transferred from state 1 to state 2, as shown in figure (pressure and volume are in relative units). What can be determined about changes in gas parameters?

All participants in the finals, for the most part, successfully completed the proposed tasks. The main ideas of the solution were discussed during physiology and aroused great interest not only among the participants, but also among the critics. We hope that some of the guys continued to work on the tasks after the end of the Tournament.

According to the final results, team No. 2 (captain - A. Odintsov) took first place in the Tournament. She was awarded the challenge prize of the Tournament - a resistance store from the physical laboratory of the famous Russian physicist N.A. Umov with his author. Second place was taken by team school No. 57 (captain - D. Filippov). She received the second, also challengeable, prize - the glass lion cub Pluf, made by skilled Fan glassblowers. Third place was won by team school No. 179 (captain - S. Shishkov).

Let us note that for the first time the Tournament of Young Physicists was held last year, in 1979. Then it was the first experience. The current Tournament was distinguished by a wider composition of its participants and significantly better organization. This became possible thanks to the help of the Lebedev Physical Institute, the All-Union Society “Znanie”, the Commission for Work with Youth of the USSR Academy of Sciences, physics teachers, students of the Physics Faculty of Moscow State University, MIPT students and many others.

For the third time, students from Omsk and the Omsk region took part in optimization in physics and mathematics, organized jointly by the Moscow Physical Institute (MIPT) and the Omsk Polytechnic Institute (OmPI). This optimization was born from the common slogan for institutes: “Getting to know the student starting from school.”

Schoolchildren's competitions were held according to the rules of student competitions. Thirteen problems in mathematics and eight problems in physics were proposed for solution. The tasks are non-standard, of varying degrees of difficulty, designed for ninth grade students. Of course, it is impossible to solve all the tasks in four hours, and this was not required. To win, it was enough to solve several problems, the completeness of the solution and the number of participants who solved the problem. Thanks to this system, it was possible, having completed only two tasks, to identify those who, trying to declare the immensity, took on everything at once, without bringing everything to completion.

As a result, first place among mathematicians was taken by Evveny Brusnetsov (school No. 88, 10th grade) and Igor Titov (school No. 109, 9th grade).

The best physicists were Olets Derzhekov (school No. 66, 10th grade), Mikhail Markin (school No. 88, 9th grade) and Vadim Maslov (school No. 11, 8th grade).

After the end of the Olympiad, a problem analysis took place, and even later, an interesting conversation with students from MIPT and Ompi, where schoolchildren received answers to many questions that interested them, and became acquainted with the advertising gases “For Science” and “Polytechnic”. The winners of the Olympiad received awards - certificates and diplomas - and recommendations for entering a university.

Below are the problems in mathematics and physics proposed at the Olympiad.

Matematika

1. Find a five-digit number that, when multiplied by 9, forms a five-digit number written in the same digits as the original, but in reverse order. (2 okka)

2. Given triangle ABC; l_A, l_B, l_C are the lengths of its bisectors, and l_A < 1, l_B < 1, l_C < 1. Prove that the area of ​​triangle ABC is less than 1. (3 okkas)

3. Two steamships sail across the sea in fixed directions at constant speeds. At 12:00 the distance between the ships was 5 miles, at 12:00

4. There are three points $A, B, C$ on a line, and the point $C$ lies between the points $A$ and $B$. Find the set of point $O$ such that the bicsectrix of angle $AOB$ passes through point $C$. (4 points)

5. Find the sum of fractions of the form $\frac{1}{(n+1)^{m+1}}$ for all natural numbers $m$ and $n$. (5 points)

6. Solve the system
$$\begin{cases} 2\cos \frac{10\pi}{x} + 4\cos \pi x + 1 = 0, \\ 2\cos \frac{5\pi}{x} + 2\sin \pi x - 2\cos \pi x - 1 = 0. \end{cases}$$

7. Solve the equation
$$tg^4 x + tg^4 y + 2 \text{ctg}^2 x \cdot \text{ctg}^2 y = 3 + \sin (x+y).$$ (4 points)

8. Prove that there is no natural number that, after crossing out the first digit from the left, decreases by 35 times. (3 points)

9. Is it possible to cut a seventeenth triangle into fourteen triangles? (2 points)

10. Does the equation $b^2x^2 + (b^2+c^2 - a^2)x + c^2 = 0$, where $a, b, c$ is the side length of some triangle, have real roots? (2 points)

11. It is known that for any real $x_1$ and $x_2$
$$|f(x_1) - f(x_2)| \leq \frac{1}{2}|x_1 - x_2|.$$
Prove that the equation $f(x) = x$ has at most one root. (3 points)

12. For all $b > a > 0$ and any $x$ the value of the polynomial $P(x) = a + b\cos x + \frac{b}{2\sqrt{2}}\cos 2x$ is non-negative. Identify the many values ​​that the fraction $\frac{b+a}{b-a}$ can take. (4 points)

13. Astronauts on an alien planet found the following inscription: %! &+!0++!!! They found out that this is how an inhabitant of the planet performed a neo-ordinary operation on natural numbers (operations can be “+”, “-”, “×”, “:”, “weigh to a power”, “extract the root]). It is also known that the planet uses a neo-ordinary positional system for writing numbers, but the basis of the number system and the order in which the digits are listed are unknown. Assume that the operation sign and the equal sign are single-valued symbols. Determine what calculation could have been performed. (5 points)

Physics

1. On the stripe of some planet without an atomosphere the packet starts. In a short time, the thrusters impart to the package a speed $\vec{v}$, directed at an angle $45^\circ$ to the horizon. According to the model, this speed is greater than the first cosmic speed, but less than the second cosmic speed for this planet. In the future, the divgater does not work. Will the package return to the planet? Explain. (2 points)

2. The body slides without friction up a slope of 1:50 with an initial speed of 1 m/s. Find the path traveled by the body in 7 s. Take $g = 10$ m/s². (4 points)

3. A hydraulic station was built on the river. It turned out that 100 km upstream, neither the speed of the current nor the water level changed. A similar situation occurs 100 km downstream. Where did the energy come from for the operation of hydrolestration? (1 point)

4. A weight of mass $m$ (Fig. 1) lies on a weightless spring attached to the table with rigidity $k$. The same load falls on him from a height $H$ and floats towards him. What must the object $H$ do for the sticky weights to jump up, breaking away from the pressure? (4 points)

5. In the pumped-out sealed state, the volume $V = 10$ dm³ is open and the cone containing $m = 10$ g of water is located. The vessel is heated at a temperature of $t = 100^\circ C$. How much water will evaporate? (4 points)

6. Two identical metal balls with positive charges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ differ by force $\vec{F}$. Will the force of interaction change (according to the model) if one of the balls changes the sign of the charge without changing the magnitude of the charge and the distance between the balls? Explain. (2 points)

7. Point charges $q$ and $Q$ are located at distances 2R and 3R from the center of an uncharged metal sphere with radius $R$ (Fig. 2). The potential of some point $A$ of the sphere turned out to be equal to zero. Considering $q$ extracted, find $Q$. (5 points)

8. Construct an image of a circle in the curved mirror with the center at the focus of the mirror (Fig. 3). Assume that the radius of the circle $r$ is quite small compared to the radius of the mirror $R$. (4 points)
