⟦folio 45⟧

Letter
to the editor

In «Kvant» No. 10 of 1978 the decision problem M478 was printed. Here are its terms:

In the volleyball tournament, two teams played one match each.

a) Prove that if for any two teams there is a third one that matches these two, then the number of teams is at least seven.

b) Construct an example of such a tournament consisting of seven teams.

b) Prove that if for any three teams there is one that is correct for these three, then the number of teams is at least 15.

The solution stated that if (for a given m) for any m teams there is a team that defeats them, then the number of teams is not less than 2$^{m+1}$ - 1 (this is proven by induction on m), and the question was asked for which m this estimate does not work, that is, for which m such a tournament of 2$^{m+1}$ - 1 teams exists.

After the publication of the decision problem M478, the editor «Kvant» received a letter from Hungary from Professor L. Cirmas. He reported that similar problems about turipra were considered by the general mathematicians G. and E. Sekereshmi back in 1965. They proved that if for any m teams there is a team that matches them, then the number of teams is at least 2$^{n}(k+1)$ - 1.

Applying the last statement to n=m-1, k=m+1, we get that the number of teams participating in the tournament is not less than 2$^{m+1}(m+2)$ - 1.

In particular, for m>2 there cannot be 2$^{m+1}$ - 1 commands in such a turipre, which gives an imprinted answer to the question posed in the turipre M478.

In 1963, the famous Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdős proved a theorem from which it follows that a tournament of 2$^{m}m^{2}$ teams, in which for any m teams there is a team that wins against them, exists for m≥2. Thus, for the minimum number f(m) of teams in such a tournament with m≥2, the estimate 2$^{m-1}(m+2)$ – 1≤f(m)≤2$^{m}m^{2}$ is valid.

It is difficult to significantly improve her score, but maybe one of the readers «Kvant» managed to do it?

In conclusion, I would like to offer reader «Kvant» one more task:

Let k be a natural number, 4k+3 a prime number. Let's assign 4k+3 to the command from 1 to 4k+3.

a) Show that there is a tournament of these teams in which the team with number m if and only then beats the team with number n, m≠n, when the equation

m — n=l^2 — r (4k+3)

has a solution in integers l and r.

b) Prove that in this tour for any two teams there are at least k teams that are equal to these two.

b) Check that for k=4, according to the turipre 19 commands for any three, there is a fourth that is consistent with these three.

This example, constructed by G. and E. Sekereshmi, shows that their estimate is not better for m=3.

Ask - we answer

“Recently, quite by accident, I saw the vibrations of a scab against the background of a television screen. When the scab was located peredicularly to the line forming the image of the screen (that is, vertically), the portion of the scab falling on the screen image had the shape of a four-force, which was drawn from above the view. By holding the scab with different strength, I received different “egraphics”; and not only sinus curves, but also curves of complex shape, most likely being graphs of a given scab vibration.

I ask you to oblige this is.”

(from a letter from reader A. Perevezentsev).

We think the answer to this question will be of interest to the reader «Kvant». The consultant of the physics department of our magazine A. Volodin answers.

The human eye has the ability to “remember” an image for a short time - about 0.1 s. When an object is illuminated by a flash of light that lasts less than 0.05 s, a person creates a visual image in the form of an instant photograph of the object. You could observe such “photography” during an evening or night thunderstorm. Every observer during an evening or night thunderstorm.

When illuminating a sounding, and therefore oscillating, scab with a flash of light shorter than 0.01 s, the scab will appear curved in the shape of a sinus. (Illumination with ordinary continuous light creates a fuzzy, blurry picture of a oscillating scab.) Telerr imagines that short flashes of light follow one after another, repeating periodically every 0.02-0.1 s. In this case, a series of “instant photographs” are obtained in the human visual apparatus, merging with one another. As a result, the illusion of a continuous process is created. If the frequency of flashes coincides with the frequency of the oscillating scab or is a multiple of it, then the scab is illuminated every time in the same position and looks like a motionless sinus.

If it is necessary to differentiate frequencies during the period of time between flashes, the scab will take a position close to the original, but not coinciding with it. In this case, an impressive slow vibration of the scab is created.

Lighting in the form of a series of short, periodic, repeating flashes is called strokosoppic. Such lighting is used to study periodic processes.

In the experiment described by A. Perevezentsev, the source of strokosoppic illumination was a television screen. The image on the screen is “drawn” by an electron beam line by line (625 lines in total), and when the entire screen is projected, the beam is extinguished, returns to its original place and begins to draw the next frame of the image. Every month every 0.04 s. Thus, the TV screen can be considered a source of strososcopic lighting with a flash repetition rate of 25 Hz.

The fact that when the scab is positioned horizontally and vertically, the observed patterns differ is a consequence of the heterogeneity of the illumination source. In fact, the horizontal scab is viewed against the background of a separate line. Vertical - against the background of all lines. In the first case, the entire scab is illuminated by a “flash” lasting ~6·10⁻⁴ s (this is the time of the bright glow of the line). Against the background of one scab, a certain phase of the oscillating scab is observed. Note that the picture will be clear only if the line and the scab are strictly parallel. In the second case, areas of the scab are illuminated by successive flash lines, and different phases of scab oscillation are observed.

The movie screen and “daylight” lamps also have the property of a strokosoppic light source.”
