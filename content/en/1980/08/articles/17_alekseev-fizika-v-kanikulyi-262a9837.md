PHYSICS ON VACATION

The summer school holidays are coming to an end. Of course, each of you is familiar with the feeling of excitement before the start of a new school year: is there anything waiting for us ahead?

Maybe you are already “tired” of separating and should you start studying as soon as possible? Don't rush to open your textbooks. Observe the scales and phenomena around you. You will see a lot of interesting and useful things. Probably, not all of you will be able to oblige yourself to us. Don't be upset. Try to remember well what you don’t understand, so that later, in the new school year, you can find out everything.

How do dew droplets form?

As you, of course, know, air is a mixture of various gases. First of all, these are nitrogen and oxygen. There is also water vapor in the air. True, their contribution is not great: the density of water vapor is hundreds of times less than the density of air. But their presence determines the so-called air humidity. Human well-being, the growth and development of plants, the operation of many precision instruments, and the like are highly dependent on air humidity.

It turns out that the amount of water vapor in the air cannot be arbitrary. There is a maximum mass of water that can be evaporated inside 1 m³ of air at a given temperature. With further addition, condensation of water vapor occurs and water droplets are formed. The higher the temperature, the more moisture the air can hold.

Imagine that during the day the amount of water in the air reached its maximum value. Night has fallen. The air temperature has dropped,

The maximum mass of water in the air has decreased.
Obviously, excess water vapor forms water droplets - dew or fog.
Now do some independent observations.
First, do an experiment that does not require much time.
Take a glass jar and bring its neck to the toe of the boiling kettle. Place the jar on the table upside down. After some time, the air in it will cool and abundant dew will fall.
While the air in the jar is cooling, look at the spout of the boiling kettle. There is no visible "steam" directly at its edge. Why? Steam, in the correct meaning of the word, cannot be seen at all, because it is a pronounced medium, like most other gases. What is commonly called "steam" is fog. At some distance from the spout of the boiling kettle it can actually be seen.
A slightly longer experiment can be performed outdoors. During the day, place the jar on the ground upside down and lightly cover the neck with soil. At night the air will become colder, dew will appear on the walls of the jar, as you can see early in the morning.

"Kubar" and "Io-no"
Very interesting experiments can be done with rotating themes.
Toy waters and windmills came to us from ancient times. Even a top does not age for many centuries.
In ancient paintings you can see a boy with a whip and a top, holding a beetroot. The whip is bent around the top several times. Obviously, this is how the top spun.
However, you can also start the top in the usual way, that is, with your hand, and then use a whip to whip up the top so that its rotation lasts as long as possible. They say that in our country this game can be found in some places even now. And they call her “Kubar”.
Ancient China is usually considered the birthplace of the game “Io-no”. Try to do it.
Take a metal reel, for example from a children's construction set. Dynamir should be about 5 cm, the diameter of the axle should be about 0.5 cm, the length of the axle should be 1-2 cm. Tie a strong thread the length of your leg tightly to the axle.
The game is as follows. Wind the thread around the spindle, take the end of the thread in your hand and release the spool. When the thread is completely unwound, hold it lightly - the spool begins to rotate again. The task is to keep the coil rotating all the time. Having learned this, you

Rotating chain
An ordinary chain will help you see the unexpected effect when rotating.
Take a chain about 20 cm long. Carefully connect its outer links. Tie a thread 30 cm long to one of the links. Take the other end of the thread in your hand.
Making circular movements of a small radius with your hand in a horizontal plane, make the chain rotate. After some time, it will rise, straighten out and take the shape of a circle lying in a horizontal plane. An unexpected result, isn't it?

Who's faster?
Place a not very smooth board on the table and lift one end of it a little. Place two identical cylinders on the board side by side, but one is solid and the other is hollow. Which one do you think will scan the board faster? Teler release the cylinders and check yourself.
Change the cylinders, take solid large and small, large solid and small hollow, cylinders from different materials, and so on.

Why does a rotating cylinder rise?
Make a cylinder with a diameter of about 4 cm and a length of 10 cm from thick paper. Wrap it several times with nylon tape, place it on the edge of the table and hold it by the free end of the tape. The tape will remain in your hands, and the cylinder, rotating, will fly forward in the direction of the jerk. What will happen to him next?
There are two options: the cylinder will either fly up or fly under the table. Check how the final result depends on the position of the free end of the tape during the jerk (the free end can be above or below the cylinder axis).
Note that this experiment demonstrates the emergence of the so-called lifting force necessary to influence aeronautics.
