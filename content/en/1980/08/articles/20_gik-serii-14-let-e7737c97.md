⟦folio 52⟧

Analyzing related problems, he led students to independently deduce the required rule, formula, or theorem.

Unfortunately, this method is almost never used in modern teaching. Therefore, it is interesting to see the appearance of a small methodological manual by A. Ya. Halmaizer “Combinatorics and Newton’s Biome” (Moscow, “Prosveshcheniye”, 1980), presented by the “method of expedient problems”. This book begins with a task about rearranging volleyball players on the court.

The coach of the volleyball team decided to change the location of the churches.

“We’ll start the next meeting differently,” he announced after the next procedure. “You, Serdy, will stand on serve, Volodya – on the fourth number, in attack, and you, Nikolai...”
“What if we’re passing through again?” asked the captain.
“Then I’ll rearrange it again,” the coach answered coolly. “Until we try all the possible arrangements...”

How can you calculate how many ways you can arrange volleyball players into 6 possible places?

Combinatorial tasks often arise in a wide variety of human activity conditions. An understanding of these problems and the ability to understand them are necessary for physicists and biologists, economists and engineers, linguists and sports judges.

The book contains information about such concepts of combinatorics as permutations, placements, combinations and permutations with repetitions.

The content of these concepts is revealed through analysis of the tasks involved, such as the following:

A dot in Morse code represents the letter E, type - the letter T. Friendly letters and permutation signs are two, three,..., and some even six different scenes - dots or type. And how many scenes can be used to convey any of hundreds of characters (letters, numbers, signs)?

In the Braille alphabet (for the people of the world), each symbol - letter or number - is represented by six kokam; some of them have bulges. By touching these signs with their fingers, the blind can “read” the text. How many different characters can there be in Braille?

The book does not contain traditional theorems with proofs or formulas with conclusions. When analyzing a practical problem, the author makes generalizations, uses analogies, and the reader independently comes to the desired conclusion.

The book combines the presence of important mathematical information and accessible presentation. Every curious high school student can easily read it. Teachers will find interesting material in it for extracurricular and club activities. The style of presentation should be recognized as scales for textbooks on mathematics.

All that remains is to regret the small volume of the book and express the hope that “Enlightenment” will continue to publish such literature, which is necessary for both students and teachers.

I. Brovikov
Series - 14 years
Interesting books on mathematics are not always easy to acquire. At the same time, there is a special series of brochures “Matematika, kibernetka”, where you can subscribe by mail for a year in advance and, therefore, will not have to worry about purchasing them in the future. This series has been published by the publishing house “Znanie” since 1967.

The authors of the brochures are the largest Soviet and foreign scientists, prominent experts in their field and often talented popularizers.

The service's topics are varied. Much attention is paid to the biographies of current and past mathematicians. Here are some of the names that have already been associated with the previous brochures: the substitute American mathematician G. Weilb, the mathematical genius of India Ramanujan, the French classic of mathematics D'Alembert, the famous Russian scientists A. Lyapnov, P. Chebyshev, V. Steklov.

Some brochures touch on issues of mathematics and its history, talk about the process of mathematical calculus, about mathematical education and science, and about teaching mathematics in secondary and high schools. In 1973, a brochure was published, useful for schoolchildren - N. Rozov, E. Glagova, Zh. Rabot “Legitimate mathematical school at Moscow State University.”

A number of works related to applied issues of mathematics. To be convinced of this, their simple names are enough: “Matematika in biology”, “Cybernetwork and medical diagnostics”, “Cybernetwork and psychotria”, “Probability theory in natural sciences”, “Economics and mathematics”, “Matematika and art”, “Matematika and epidemiology”.

Of course, there are many brochures dedicated to mathematics itself, and, as a rule, the most pressing problems and achievements of science are touched upon. So, already in the very first brochure, published in January 1967, I talk about the World Mathematical Congress of Mathematicians, held in the fall of 1966 in Moscow.

Of course, in one short note it is impossible to dwell on all the brochures in the series - after all, their number has long exceeded one and a half hundred. For those wishing to get acquainted with serial power in more detail, we recommend brochure No. 10 for 1979 - A. Ya. Marguli with “Series “Matematika, kibernetka”” for 12 years.”
Most of the brochures in the “Matematika, knibernetka” series are written in an engaging, lively language; the best books were awarded prizes at major popular science literature competitions. There is no doubt that many of the brochures, both those already published and those published in the future, will be read to you with great interest and use. In our opinion, the series of books “Matematika, kibernetka” deserves to take the risk once and subscribe to it.

E. Gik
