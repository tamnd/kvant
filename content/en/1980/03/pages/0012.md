Cold light

The disadvantages of the incandescent lamp as a source of visible light have forced scientists to look for new light sources that are more economical in the sense of converting electrical energy into light and light, closer to the sun in color properties. Thus, the task was to both increase light output and increase light quality.

S.I. Vavilov had the idea of ​​using the cold glow phenomenon called lominescence for these purposes (see, for example, Physics-10, pp. 210-212). If the Sun or an incandescent lamp represent heat sources in which radiation occurs due to heating to a high temperature, then the glow of the lominophore (lominescent body) is, as a rule, not associated with its heating.

We will be interested in the so-called photolominophores, the glow of which is caused by irradiation with light from an external source. Photolominophores convert radiation of one wavelength into radiation of another wavelength. Many dyes (for example, rhodimine orange dye) exhibit bright laminescence under the influence of a daylight candle.

The modern doctrine of literal lominescence owes much to S.I. Vavilov. In particular, S.I. Vavilov showed that lominophores - as a radiation energy converter - can have a very high efficiency. This property is very important for the practical use of lominophores.
