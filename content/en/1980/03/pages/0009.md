5. Applying displacements to problem solving

Problem 4. Two circles with centers $O_1, O_2$ intersect at points $A$ and $B$. Prove that the line $O_1O_2$ redistributes the segment $AB$ and divides it in half.

Solution. Let us denote by $l_1, l_2$ the semicircles lying on one side of the straight line $O_1O_2$, by $l'_1, l'_2$ the semicircles lying on the other side (Fig. 9), and by $s$ the symmetry with respect to $(O_1O_2)$. Then $l'_1 = s(l_1), l'_2 = s(l_2)$. Since $s(l_1 \cap l_2) = s(l_1) \cap s(l_2) = l'_1 \cap l'_2$, we get $s(A) = B$, that is, the points $A$ and $B$ are symmetrical with respect to $(O_1O_2)$. This is where the required statement follows.

In this solution we took advantage of the fact that for any movement $f$ and any figures $M, N$ the correspondence $f(M \cap N) = f(M) \cap f(N)$ is valid. Prove it yourself.

Problem 5. On the sides $AB$ and $BC$ of an arbitrary triangle $ABC$ squares $ABMN$ and $BCPQ$ are constructed outside it. Point $K$ is the middle of segment $MQ$. Prove that the segment $BK$ redistributed the base $AC$ and twice its crown.

Solution. Let $L$ be the midpoint of the segment $AC$ (Fig. 10). It is necessary to prove that the segments $BK$ and $CL$ redistributed the circle and are congruent. To do this, it is most convenient to establish that the first segment is obtained from the second by rotating by $\pm 90^\circ$ around a certain point. So, we would like to find a point, a rotation by $90^\circ$ or $-90^\circ$ around which transforms the point $C$ into $B$, and the point $L$ into $K$. But it is clear that turning $r$ by $-90^\circ$ around the center of the square $BCPQ$ takes the point $C$ to $B$. This means that it remains to establish that $r(L) = K$.

We have $r(C) = B, r(B) = Q$; let's put $r(A) = D$. Then $r([BA]) = [QD]$, that is, the segments $AB$ and $DQ$ have been redistributed and are congruent. This means that the segments $BM$ and $QD$ are parallel and congruent, that is, $BMDQ$ are parallel. Therefore, the point $K$ (the midpoint of the diagonal $MQ$) is also the midpoint of the segment $BD$. Since $r([CA]) = [BD]$, we get $r(L) = K$. So, $r(C) = B, r(L) = K$, that is, $r([CL]) = [BK]$, and therefore the segments $CL$ and $BK$ have been redistributed and are congruent.
