Let us denote by $t$ the parallel translation that takes the point $A$ to $C$. Then $t(A)=C$, $t(B)=D$. So, we have two movements $t, s_2$, each of which takes the points $A, B$ to $C, D$ respectively. Since $g=s_2^\circ s_1$ also maps $A, B$ to $C, D$, $g$ matches either $t$ or $s_2$. Solution. Let $r$ be a rotation with center $O$ through an angle $\alpha$ (Fig. 8), and $t$ be a parallel translation. Let's put $A=t(O)$. Next, we draw straight lines $l_1, l_2$ and perpedicular lines ($OA$), the first of which passes through the point $O$, and the second through the middle of the segment $OA$. Symmetry with respect to straight lines $l_1, l_2$ is denoted by $s_1, s_2$. Then problem 2 implies $s_2^\circ s_1=t$. Next, by $l$ we denote the straight line passing through the point $O$, from which $l_1$ we obtain a rotation through the angle $\frac{1}{2}\alpha$, and by $s$ we denote symmetry with respect to the straight line $l$. Then from Problem 1 it follows $s_1^\circ s=r$. This means $t^\circ r=(s_2^\circ s_1)\circ(s_1^\circ s)=s_2(s_1^\circ s_1)\circ s=s_2^\circ s$, from which it follows (by virtue of Problem 1) that $t^\circ r$ is a rotation through the angle $\alpha$ around the point $O_1$ at which the lines $l_2$ and $l$ intersect.

Note that in solving we took advantage, firstly, of the associativity of the operation of composition of displacements (that is, the ability to arbitrarily place parentheses; this property is valid not only for displacements, but also for any mappings) and, secondly, the fact that for the main symmetry $s_1$ the figurative mapping coincides with $s_1$, and therefore $s_1^\circ s_1$ is also 逮real display.
