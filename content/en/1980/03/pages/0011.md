And light and heat

We are so used to electric lighting that sometimes we don’t appreciate it as much as we should and forget its history.

The first to appear was the incandescent electric lamp.

"TAKES OUT

- this is what Mayakovsky wrote in his poem “Burning Hair”.

The poet is right when he points out that “both light and heat come from a bottle,” but, as befits a poet, he exaggerates when he compares an incandescent lamp to the Sun. There are very important differences between incandescent light and sunlight. First of all, the city's incandescent light is the same as sunlight. Moreover, in the incandescent lamp study the "heat" of the city is stronger in relation to the "light" than in the study of the Sun. Both differences are caused by the same reason: the temperature of the filament (“hair”) of an incandescent lamp (3000°C) is approximately two times lower than the temperature of the surface of the Sun (6000°C).

Let's move from position to numbers, and we come across an interesting paradox. An incandescent lamp is an almost ideal converter of electrical energy into radiation energy (with an efficiency close to unity) and at the same time an incandescent lamp is a very uneconomical light source. How is this possible?
