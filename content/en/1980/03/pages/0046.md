⟦folio 44⟧

Moscow State University
them. M. V. Lomonosova

Mathematics in humanities faculties

Written exam

A high school graduate should be able to combine knowledge of various branches of mathematics when solving problems, that is, approach solving creative problems. The practice of entrance exams in mathematics at humanities faculties shows that applicants are not yet coping with this well enough.

Below is a preliminary analysis of one of the options for the department of political economy of the Faculty of Economics, indicating typical errors and some statistics: the proportion of applicants (as a percentage of the total number of those who passed the exam) who solved this problem completely and did not solve it at all. Other faculties are also represented by each - one option, but only with answers and brief instructions.

Department of Political Economy, Faculty of Economics

1. Solve the equation
$$\sqrt{37}-48 \text{ctg} x=8 \text{ctg} x-5.$$

Solution. Let us denote ctg x by z and solve the auxiliary equation
$$\sqrt{37}-48z=8z-5z.$$

Let's square both of its parts (in this case, the construction of roots may appear). Solving the resulting quadratic equation
$$16z^2-8z-3=0,$$
let's find $z_1=3/4$, $z_2=-1/4$. The root $z_2$ is a construction, since $8z_2-5<0$, and $\sqrt{37}-48z_2>0$ (the arithmetic meaning of the root!). The value $z_1$, as verified, satisfies the equation. Solving the equation cot x=3/4, we find the answer: $x=arctg 3/4+\pi n$ ($n\in\mathbb{Z}$).

The main mistake in solving this problem is acquiring the constructed roots (lack of verification) with an unequal transformation (squaring both sides of the equation). 44% of applicants solved this problem completely, 8% did not solve it at all.

2. In equilateral triangle ABC ($|AB|=|BC|=8$), point E divides side AB in a ratio of 3:1 (counting from vertex B). Find the desired vectors $\vec{CE}$ and $\vec{CA}$ if $|CA|=12$.

Solution (Fig. 1). From the definition of the scalar product it follows that
$$\cos \alpha = \frac{\vec{CE} \cdot \vec{CA}}{|\vec{CE}| \cdot |\vec{CA}|},$$
where $\alpha$ is the angle between vectors $\vec{CE}$ and $\vec{CA}$. Let us introduce the coordinate system as indicated in figure 1; by the property of the altitude of an equilateral triangle $|OA|=|OC|$. From $\Delta OBC$
$$|OB|=\sqrt{|BC|^2-|OC|^2}=2\sqrt{7}.$$
Since $\vec{AE}=1/4\vec{AB}$, we have $\vec{CE}=\vec{CA}+1/4\vec{AB}$. In the Oxy coordinate system: $\vec{CA}=(-12; 0)$, $\vec{AB}=(6; 2\sqrt{7})$, $\vec{CE}=(-21/2; 2\sqrt{7/4})$. Substituting into the expression for cos $\alpha$, we find
$$\cos \alpha=\frac{(-12)(-21/2)}{12 \cdot \sqrt{(21/2)^2+7/4}}=\frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}.$$
Answer. The angle between vectors $\vec{CA}$ and $\vec{CE}$ is equal to $\arccos \frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}$.

The practical students below did not use the coordinate method here; finding the angle $\alpha$, as a rule, arbitrary using traditional planimetric methods, the expression $\alpha=\pm\arccos \frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}+2\pi n$ ($n\in\mathbb{Z}$) was proposed as an answer, which indicates a mechanical application of formulas, without understanding the meaning of the problem. 27% of applicants solved the problem completely, 34% did not solve it at all.
