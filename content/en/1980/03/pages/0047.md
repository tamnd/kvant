4. Find the most and most values ​​of the function $y = |x^2+2x-3| + \frac{3}{2}\ln x$ on the interval [1/2; 4].

Solution. First of all, we note that one of the roots of the square trilinen $x^2+2x-3$, namely $x=1$, belongs to the segment [1/2; 4]. When the notation is valid

$$y = \begin{cases} -x^2-2x+3+3/2\ln x, & x \in [1/2; 1]; \\ x^2+2x-3+3/2\ln x, & x \in [1; 4]. \end{cases}$$

The derivative function $y(x)$ can be written as

$$y' = \begin{cases} -2x-2+\frac{3}{2x}, & x \in [1/2; 1]; \\ 2x+2+\frac{3}{2x}, & x \in [1; 4]. \end{cases}$$

(Note the parentheses: at the point $x=1$ the derivative is undefined). Since $\frac{-4x^2-4x+3}{2x} < 0$ for $x \in [1/2; 1]$ (this follows from considering the roots of the square trilinen $-4x^2-4x+3$), on the interval [1/2; 1] function $y(x)$ kills. Likewise for $x \in [1; 4]$ $y'(x) > 0$. This means (see “Algebra and the beginnings of analysis” 9) p. 54) that on the interval [1; 4] function $y(x)$ increases. Moreover, as is easy to see, the function $y$ is non-continuous under $x=1$. This means that the smallest value of the function is [1/2; 4] is achieved at $x=1$ and is equal to $y(1)=0$, and the smallest value can only be achieved at the ends of the segment [1/2; 4].

But $y(4) = 21+3\ln 2 > y\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{7}{4} - \frac{3}{2}\ln 2$.

Answer. The smallest value of the function is 0, the largest is 21+3\ln 2.

Typical errors: many applicants do not notice the critical point $x=1$ (where the derivative $y'(x)$ does not exist), others assume the maximum of the function at $x=1/2$ ($y'(1/2)=0$) for the largest value (without using the functions at the other end of the segment). Almost no applicant attempted to draw a graph of this function, which would have saved many from giving incorrect answers. 17% of applicants coped with this task, 38% did not solve it at all.

5. Solve the equation

$$\log_3x+7(9+12x+4x^2) + \log_2x+3(6x^2+23x+21) = 0.$$

Solution. First of all, note that $9+12x+4x^2 = (2x+3)^2$ and $6x^2+23x+21 = (2x+3)(3x+7)$. If we denote $\log_3x+7(2x+3)$ by $z$, the original equation can be rewritten as $2z+1+\frac{1}{z}=4$, multiplying by $z$ to find $2z^2-3z+1=0$, revealing $z_1=1/2$, $z_2=1$. We have $z_1=1/2 \Rightarrow \sqrt{3x+7}=2x+3$, $z_2=1 \Rightarrow 3x+7=2x+3$.

By squaring the first equation, we arrive at a quadratic equation, the roots of which are $x_1=1/4$, $x_2=-2$. The root of the second equation is $x_3=-4$. During our transformations, the set of roots may expand, so we need to check; it shows that the values ​​of $x_2=2$ and $x_3=-4$ are constructions.

Answer. {-1/4}.

Typical mistakes: many people got confused when trying to reduce logarithms to a common base, incorrectly converted logarithmic expressions, neglected the construction of roots and then did not check. 39% of applicants solved this problem correctly. The “solutions” of 47% of applicants were considered completely erroneous.
