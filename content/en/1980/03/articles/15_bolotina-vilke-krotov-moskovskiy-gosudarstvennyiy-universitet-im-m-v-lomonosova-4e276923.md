Mathematics in humanities faculties

Written exam

A high school graduate should be able to combine knowledge of various branches of mathematics when solving problems, that is, approach solving creative problems. The practice of entrance exams in mathematics at humanities faculties shows that applicants are not yet coping with this well enough.

Below is a preliminary analysis of one of the options for the department of political economy of the Faculty of Economics, indicating typical errors and some statistics: the proportion of applicants (as a percentage of the total number of those who passed the exam) who solved this problem completely and did not solve it at all. Other faculties are also represented by each - one option, but only with answers and brief instructions.

Department of Political Economy, Faculty of Economics

1. Solve the equation
$$\sqrt{37}-48 \text{ctg} x=8 \text{ctg} x-5.$$

Solution. Let us denote ctg x by z and solve the auxiliary equation
$$\sqrt{37}-48z=8z-5z.$$

Let's square both of its parts (in this case, the construction of roots may appear). Solving the resulting quadratic equation
$$16z^2-8z-3=0,$$
let's find $z_1=3/4$, $z_2=-1/4$. The root $z_2$ is a construction, since $8z_2-5<0$, and $\sqrt{37}-48z_2>0$ (the arithmetic meaning of the root!). The value $z_1$, as verified, satisfies the equation. Solving the equation cot x=3/4, we find the answer: $x=arctg 3/4+\pi n$ ($n\in\mathbb{Z}$).

The main mistake in solving this problem is acquiring the constructed roots (lack of verification) with an unequal transformation (squaring both sides of the equation). 44% of applicants solved this problem completely, 8% did not solve it at all.

2. In equilateral triangle ABC ($|AB|=|BC|=8$), point E divides side AB in a ratio of 3:1 (counting from vertex B). Find the desired vectors $\vec{CE}$ and $\vec{CA}$ if $|CA|=12$.

Solution (Fig. 1). From the definition of the scalar product it follows that
$$\cos \alpha = \frac{\vec{CE} \cdot \vec{CA}}{|\vec{CE}| \cdot |\vec{CA}|},$$
where $\alpha$ is the angle between vectors $\vec{CE}$ and $\vec{CA}$. Let us introduce the coordinate system as indicated in figure 1; by the property of the altitude of an equilateral triangle $|OA|=|OC|$. From $\Delta OBC$
$$|OB|=\sqrt{|BC|^2-|OC|^2}=2\sqrt{7}.$$
Since $\vec{AE}=1/4\vec{AB}$, we have $\vec{CE}=\vec{CA}+1/4\vec{AB}$. In the Oxy coordinate system: $\vec{CA}=(-12; 0)$, $\vec{AB}=(6; 2\sqrt{7})$, $\vec{CE}=(-21/2; 2\sqrt{7/4})$. Substituting into the expression for cos $\alpha$, we find
$$\cos \alpha=\frac{(-12)(-21/2)}{12 \cdot \sqrt{(21/2)^2+7/4}}=\frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}.$$
Answer. The angle between vectors $\vec{CA}$ and $\vec{CE}$ is equal to $\arccos \frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}$.

The practical students below did not use the coordinate method here; finding the angle $\alpha$, as a rule, arbitrary using traditional planimetric methods, the expression $\alpha=\pm\arccos \frac{3\sqrt{7}}{8}+2\pi n$ ($n\in\mathbb{Z}$) was proposed as an answer, which indicates a mechanical application of formulas, without understanding the meaning of the problem. 27% of applicants solved the problem completely, 34% did not solve it at all.

3. From a vessel filled to the brim with pure lcipherin, 2 liters of lciferin were poured, and 2 liters of water were added to the remaining lciferin. After moving, 2 liters of the mixture were poured again and 2 liters of water were added. Finally, again, after moving, 2 liters of the mixture were poured and 2 liters of water were added. As a result of these operations, the volume of water in the vessel became 3 liters greater than the volume of lciferin left in it. How many liters of lcipherin and water ended up in the vessel as a result of the operations performed?

Solution. If we denote the volume of the vessel by $V$ liters, then after the first operation (replacing two liters of glycerin with water), glycerin will occupy $\frac{V-2}{V}$ part of the vessel. Pouring 2 liters of the mixture, we get $(V-2)\frac{V-2}{V}$ liters of glycerin in the vessel and after adding water, glycerin will occupy $\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^2$ part of the vessel. After the third operation, glycerin will occupy $\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^3$ part of the vessel, and the amount of glycerin in the vessel will be equal to $V\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^3$ liters. According to the conditions of the problem, there is 3 liters more water in the vessel, that is, $V\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^3 + 3$. Adding these quantities, we come to the equation $2V\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^3 + 3 = V$, open $V^3 - 9V^2 + 24V - 16 = 0$. The left side of this equation is easy to factor; the equation will take the form

$$(V-1)(V-4)^2 = 0.$$

It follows that $V=4$ l, since $V=1$ l does not fit the meaning of the problem; This means that the volume of glycerin is $V\left(\frac{V-2}{V}\right)^3 = 0,5$ l.

Answer. The volume of glycerin is 0.5 l, the volume of water is 3.5 l. This problem turned out to be the most difficult of the five problems offered to the students. Although, as you can see, it is solved very simply, only 7% of students solved it, and 80% did not know what to do with it.

4. Find the most and most values ​​of the function $y = |x^2+2x-3| + \frac{3}{2}\ln x$ on the interval [1/2; 4].

Solution. First of all, we note that one of the roots of the square trilinen $x^2+2x-3$, namely $x=1$, belongs to the segment [1/2; 4]. When the notation is valid

$$y = \begin{cases} -x^2-2x+3+3/2\ln x, & x \in [1/2; 1]; \\ x^2+2x-3+3/2\ln x, & x \in [1; 4]. \end{cases}$$

The derivative function $y(x)$ can be written as

$$y' = \begin{cases} -2x-2+\frac{3}{2x}, & x \in [1/2; 1]; \\ 2x+2+\frac{3}{2x}, & x \in [1; 4]. \end{cases}$$

(Note the parentheses: at the point $x=1$ the derivative is undefined). Since $\frac{-4x^2-4x+3}{2x} < 0$ for $x \in [1/2; 1]$ (this follows from considering the roots of the square trilinen $-4x^2-4x+3$), on the interval [1/2; 1] function $y(x)$ kills. Likewise for $x \in [1; 4]$ $y'(x) > 0$. This means (see “Algebra and the beginnings of analysis” 9) p. 54) that on the interval [1; 4] function $y(x)$ increases. Moreover, as is easy to see, the function $y$ is non-continuous under $x=1$. This means that the smallest value of the function is [1/2; 4] is achieved at $x=1$ and is equal to $y(1)=0$, and the smallest value can only be achieved at the ends of the segment [1/2; 4].

But $y(4) = 21+3\ln 2 > y\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{7}{4} - \frac{3}{2}\ln 2$.

Answer. The smallest value of the function is 0, the largest is 21+3\ln 2.

Typical errors: many applicants do not notice the critical point $x=1$ (where the derivative $y'(x)$ does not exist), others assume the maximum of the function at $x=1/2$ ($y'(1/2)=0$) for the largest value (without using the functions at the other end of the segment). Almost no applicant attempted to draw a graph of this function, which would have saved many from giving incorrect answers. 17% of applicants coped with this task, 38% did not solve it at all.

5. Solve the equation

$$\log_3x+7(9+12x+4x^2) + \log_2x+3(6x^2+23x+21) = 0.$$

Solution. First of all, note that $9+12x+4x^2 = (2x+3)^2$ and $6x^2+23x+21 = (2x+3)(3x+7)$. If we denote $\log_3x+7(2x+3)$ by $z$, the original equation can be rewritten as $2z+1+\frac{1}{z}=4$, multiplying by $z$ to find $2z^2-3z+1=0$, revealing $z_1=1/2$, $z_2=1$. We have $z_1=1/2 \Rightarrow \sqrt{3x+7}=2x+3$, $z_2=1 \Rightarrow 3x+7=2x+3$.

By squaring the first equation, we arrive at a quadratic equation, the roots of which are $x_1=1/4$, $x_2=-2$. The root of the second equation is $x_3=-4$. During our transformations, the set of roots may expand, so we need to check; it shows that the values ​​of $x_2=2$ and $x_3=-4$ are constructions.

Answer. {-1/4}.

Typical mistakes: many people got confused when trying to reduce logarithms to a common base, incorrectly converted logarithmic expressions, neglected the construction of roots and then did not check. 39% of applicants solved this problem correctly. The “solutions” of 47% of applicants were considered completely erroneous.

Note that only 6% of applicants solved the entire option without errors.
