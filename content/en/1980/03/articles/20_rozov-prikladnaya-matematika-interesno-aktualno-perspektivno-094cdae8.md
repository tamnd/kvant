Applied mathematics: interest, relevance, perspective!

The book by A. N. Tikhonov and D. P. Kostomarov “Stories about Applied Mathematics” (M., “Nauka”, 1979) reveals the content of the work of an applied mathematician, that professional, creative activity that presents problems to machine calculation and makes this calculation possible. The authors themselves see the purpose of their book as “to tell a wide range of readers and, above all, young people in a popular form, about applied mathematics, ideas, methods, and research difficulties associated with the application of mathematical methods and computer technology to the study of the laws of nature and their use in the practical activities of people.”

Now the young reader will receive information about the specialty “applied mathematics”, as they say, first-hand. After all, one of the authors of the book, Academician A. N. Tikhonov, director of the Institute of Applied Mathematics of the USSR Academy of Sciences, was the initiator of the creation of the Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics at Moscow University and is its permanent dean, and the other author, D. P. Kostomarov, is a professor of this faculty, a prominent specialist in computational mathematics and mathematical physics. Unfortunately, it is not often that authors of this kind consider it necessary and, most importantly, find the time to write a book addressed to schoolchildren.

A book like this has been needed for a long time. At least since the beginning of the seventies, when various universities in our country opened departments of applied

(computational) mathematics. Indeed, for a young mathematics lover choosing his path after school, a specific, interested and competent account of modern science is very important.

...It cannot be said that not enough has been written about electronic computers for a wide range of readers. There are quite a large number of popular science books and brochures that talk about the principles of the internal structure of computers, their widespread use in various fields of science and technology, what practical important results they “produced,” and solutions to numerous economic problems. Newspapers and magazines often publish materials about computers, but, as a rule, these materials only say in the most general phrases what computers actually provide, how much they can do.

In all these stories about computers, the mathematician’s activities are left behind the scenes; it is not explained how to actually “get” the computer to solve the desired problem. That is why many boys and girls (and not only them) have the impression that a computer is capable (or not tomorrow will be able) to cope with any task on its own, and the job of a specialty in applied mathematics is only to state in machine language（《program》） the conditions of the problem and press the “Start” button.

What, first of all, is applied mathematics? The authors of the book will answer this way: modern applied mathematics “includes a range of issues related to the use of mathematical methods and computer technology... in a wide variety of human fields.”

And this circle of questions is spreading - before our eyes, a process is rapidly taking place, which is called the mathematical science and arbitrariness. TODAY it is impossible to fully understand the laws of both inanimate and living nature, to ensure the progress of technology, to manage the derivatives and development of the national economy without the thorough and systematic involvement of mathematical apparatus and electronic computers.

It is absolutely clear that it is impossible to directly apply mathematical methods to the study of a real object (natural phenomena, machine operation, technical process, drawing up an economic plan). First, the problem of interest to us must be given a mathematical form - to describe the most essential features and properties of the object in the language of mathematical concepts and correspondences. That is why the first basic element of applied mathematics is, as they say, the formalization of the object under study, the construction of an appropriate mathematical model. The construction of such a model requires a combination of global knowledge about a real object with a high mathematical culture. That is why an applied mathematician must have not only comprehensive mathematical training, but also truly develop in the field of knowledge or creativity in which he is engaged.

The book provides several specific examples of constructing mathematical models (using mathematical concepts and facts accessible to schoolchildren). The reader has the opportunity to become familiar with various aspects of this work, in particular with the problem of calculating the degree of correspondence of a model to a real object. However, from the problem (about the movement of a body that is reported at a natural speed at an angle to the Earth’s surface) - the authors return to it several times - it makes it possible to tell how the process of changing the requirements of practice, deepening human knowledge entails a consistent refinement of the mathematical model, which describes the real process more deeply and comprehensively.

But let’s assume that the mathematical model has already been built. Then we will face a typically mathematical

a task which, roughly speaking, consists in finding the answer we are interested in using known initial data and certain correspondences. Ponsk method of solving a formulated mathematical problem is a human matter. If this method is unknown, then it is naive to think that a machine can find it.

What is new, then, in applied mathematics compared to classical mathematics? After all, classical mathematics (sometimes very unsuccessfully trying to call it “pure”) arose from the practical needs of people, dealt with mathematical models, and solved mathematical problems.

In the past, when solving problems, the main method was to obtain the necessary consequences from given premises using the laws of formal logic, and the main goal was to introduce into the derivation an “exact formula” that clearly connects the initial quantities with the given ones. Meanwhile, in most cases it is impossible to find an explicit formula in most cases; an exact answer based on various principles is impossible in principle. Then it is natural to try to indicate the correct execution of an infinite sequence of computational operations that allows one to obtain an approximate solution with required accuracy in a finite number of steps. However, this approach is used very rarely - not because mathematicians considered calculations an unworthy occupation, but because they are extremely labor-intensive and even practically impracticable on existing computing environments.

The creation of modern computers has radically changed the situation: now there is a real opportunity to carry out an exhaustive analysis of wide classes of mathematical problems, not only by forming logical conclusions, but also on the basis of the actual implementation of the calculation. The second main element of applied mathematics is precisely the development of algorithms - precise descriptions of sequences of computational operations that make it possible to obtain approximate solutions to problems using a computer. Having selected several fairly typical problems, the authors trace the entire path from the preliminary understanding of the problem and the emergence of the identity that forms the basis of the numerical method, to the final formation of the algorithm, its justification and comparison with other algorithms for solving the same problem. This material is of undoubted interest for equine logical mathematics, since it contains the structure and simultaneous execution for understanding the presentation of important mathematical issues. Pay special attention to the chapter containing the introduction of a certain interval and a description of numerical interpolation algorithms.

The authors of the book convincingly show that problems of applied mathematics are complex and server-based mathematical problems that require broad knowledge in a wide variety of areas of theoretical mathematics and reduce all the capabilities of computer technology.

A small but clearly written chapter is devoted to the third basic element of applied mathematics—electronic computers. Here we discuss the history of computer technology (but without describing the technical side of the matter), the problems of human-machine communication, and the most diverse applications of computers.

Electronic computers are one of the most significant creations of science and technology of the 20th century, opening up enormous program capabilities. However, computers themselves cannot solve unambiguous problems; they do not work without the direction of human influence. Therefore, today applied mathematics in the machine is actively attracting the interest of talented young people - those who in a few years will be able to turn the possibility of a program into the reality of life. The wonderful book by A. N. Tikhovov and D. P. Kostomarov serves precisely this lofty goal.

N. Rozov
