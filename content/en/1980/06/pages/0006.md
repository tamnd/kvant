Let's return to the question of the length of day and night. Both of these phenomena lengthen the day and shorten the night: the Sun rises earlier in the morning and sets later in the evening than the “geometric” sunrise and sunset, determined by the moments when the center of the Sun crosses the horizon. The advance of sunrise and the delay of sunset are determined by the time required to change the height of the Sun at the horizon by 16′+35′=51′. This time, in turn, depends on the geographic latitude of the place.

The celestial equator intersects the horizon at an angle of 90°-f. When f = 0, that is, at the earth's equator, the celestial equator is located vertically. There, the Sun, in its apparent daily movement caused by the rotation of the Earth, rises in the morning and descends in the evening perpendicular to the horizon, traveling 51′ in 3.4 minutes (based on the calculation that a complete revolution of the Earth by 360° occurs in 24 hours).

In Moscow, whose latitude is close to 56°, the celestial equator is inclined to the horizon at an angle of 90°—56°=34°. Here the Sun rises at sunrise and descends obliquely at sunset, so its height changes slowly. In a straight line which is close to 56°, the celestial equator is inclined to the horizon at an angle of 90°—56°=34°.

It is gratifying to note that we are in the light season, when the day was longer than the night, longer than the dark. If we take a non-historic year of 365 days and assume that the day is equal to the night of March 18 and September 25, then the interval between these dates is 191 days, and from September 25 to March 18 there are 174 years, that is, the light season is 17 days longer than the dark season. This difference consists of ten days associated with the phenomena described above, and seven days, which are caused by the uneven movement of the Earth in its orbit. The Earth moves faster near perihelion, which it passes annually in early January, and slower near aphel, which occurs in early July. If you count the number of days between astronomical equivalences, then from March 21 to September 23 there will be 186 of them, and from September 23 to March 21 there are a year - a total of 179. But the first interval occurs in spring and summer in the northern hemisphere and in autumn and winter in the southern hemisphere. Therefore, we, the inhabitants of Sverma, are in a more favorable position, since we have a long and long cold and dark season, in contrast to the inhabitants of the southern hemisphere, where autumn and winter are longer and on average cold.
