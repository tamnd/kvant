⟦folio 57⟧

Consulting: world chess champion, international grandmaster A. Karpov.
The page will be introduced by E. Gik, Master of Sports of the USSR in chess, Candidate of Technical Sciences.
In the previous issue of our “Chess Page” we introduced you to the meaning of the opening combination, in which, already on the sixth move, they sacrificed the queen and declared checkmate to the non-spinner king in two moves (“Legal’s checkmate”). Effective queen sacrifices always make a strong impression and remain in the memory of chess players for a long time. Today we will show you a few more combinations in which the queen is sacrificed. All of them rightfully belong to the pearls of chess creativity.
The first combination belongs to the general American chess player of the last century, his unofficial world champion Paul Morphy.

L. Paulsen - P. Morphy.

This position was encountered in his game with L. Paulsen from the tournament in New York in 1857 - the first tournament in which P. Morphy played. Here followed: 1...Q:f3!!
2. gf Rg6+ 3. Kph1 Ch3
4. Rd1 (4. Rg1 is followed by mate in three moves, Black is defenseless and after 4. Qd3 f5
5. Qc4+ Npf8!) 4...Cg2+
5. Kpg1 C:f3+ 6. Kpf1 Cg2+

7. Kpg1 Ch3+ (Morphy chooses the easiest path to victory, but Black could announce a formed mate in four moves: 7...Ce4+
8. Kpf1 Cf5! 9. Qe2 Ch3+
10. Kpe1 Rg1×) 8. Kph1 C:f2 9. Qf1 C:f1 10. R:f1 Re2
11. Ra1 Rh6 12. d4 Ce3. White gave up.
The next queen sacrifice belongs to another great combination master, M. Tal. These two parties are separated by more than 110 years!

1. K. Torre - Um. Lasker. White starts and wins.

M. Tal - A. Suetin.

1. Q:e5! de 2. ef+, and Black resigned. 2...Kpf8 is followed by 3. Ch6×, and 2...Kpd7 is solved by 3. Cf5++ Kpc6
4. Ce4+ Kd5 5. C:d5+Kpd7
6. C:a8+ Kpe7 7. Cg5+ Kpf8
8. Ch6+ Kpe7 9. f8Q+.

The most interesting combination in the following diagram occurred exactly 70 years ago in the game of two outstanding grandmasters Reti and Tartakover.

2. Adams - K. Torre. White starts and wins.

Both of them contain geometric motifs. The parts were played by the talented turn-of-the-century mechanical master K. Torre, and in one case he was the author of the combination, and in the other, a co-author.
