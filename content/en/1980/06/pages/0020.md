⟦folio 18⟧

Tasks

M626—M630; F638—F642

M626. Each side of a convex quadrilateral is divided into 8 equal parts. Matching division points on opposite sides are connected to each other, and the resulting cells are colored in a checkerboard pattern. Prove that the sum of the areas of the cells is equal to the sum of the areas of the white cells.

V. Proizvolov

M627. Each cell of an infinite sheet of checkered paper contains a natural number.
a) Let each of these numbers appear exactly once. (Give examples of such arrangement of numbers!)
Prove that for any given m there are two neighboring (sharing a common side) cells whose number difference is at least m.
6) * Let each number n ∈ N occur exactly n times (that is, 1 - once, 2 - twice, etc.).
Indicate the most number k such that there are sure to be two neighboring cells in which the difference in numbers is not less than k. (Give an example of such an arrangement in which the difference in numbers in any pair of neighboring cells is no more than k+1.)

A. Tolyvo

M628. A triangle is constructed on a sphere, one “side” of which has dimensions of 120°. Prove that the “median” dropped to this “side” is divided by each of the other two “medians” into two equal parts. (“Medians” and “sides” are other great circles.)

A. Yaguyants

M629. a) Prove that the number 2^{2n-1}-9n^2+21n—14 is divisible by 27 for any natural number n.
b) Prove that if the numbers a+b and a^2+b are divisible by m, then a^n+b are divisible by m for any n (a, b and m are some natural numbers).
b) * Prove that if f(n)=a^n+b_0+b_1n+...+b_kn^k is divisible by m for n=1, n=2, ..., n=k+1, n=k+2, then f(n) is divisible by m for any n (a, b_0, b_1, ..., b_k, m are some natural numbers).

T. Malikov
