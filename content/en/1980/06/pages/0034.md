Another example: can a 6x6x6 cube be made from 27 1x2x4 "creeps"?

Let's assume that a 6x6x6 cube is made up of 27 "blocks" of size 2x2x2, colored blue and white and arranged in a "checkerboard pattern" (Fig. 7); let there be 14 blue “blocks” and 13 white “blocks”. Now note that no matter how we arrange the 1x2x4 “kripich” inside a large cube, half of the unit cups that make it up (1x1x1) will be blue and half will be white. Therefore, if a cube of size 6×6×6 could be folded from 27 1×2×4 bricks, then if this cube was “chessboarded” in 2×2×2 “blocks”, there would be as many white single cups in it as there were blue ones. In fact, we have 108 white single cups and 112 blue ones. Therefore, a 6x6x6 cube from 27 1x2x4 creeps cannot be folded.

“Any map on a plane can be colored in no more than four colors so that any two neighboring countries are colored in different colors” (countries are considered neighboring if they have a common section of borders); countries that have only a few common points are not considered neighboring). This problem was first mentioned in a letter to Hamilton by the English mathematician De Morgan (1852). Then it was formed from the rostrum of the British Geographical Society by the outstanding English mathematician Arthur Calley (1878). Since then, the problem of four colors has plagued scholars and mathematicians. For almost a hundred years it seemed impenetrable. As a result of many years of painstaking work, it was proven that any map containing no more than 41 countries can be colored in four colors in the required manner; but this, of course, did not solve all the problems. Only quite recently, in September 1976, in the journal of the American Mathematical Society “Bulletin of American Mathematical Society” a message appeared from the American mathematicians Apel and Haken, who proved the significance of this hypothesis (it is remarkable that the proof “took part”, and a very significant one, in the computer).

At the same time, at the end of the 19th century it was established that any map on a plane can be correctly colored with five colors (“correctly” means so that no two neighboring countries are painted in the same color). Try to find your own proof of this fact. But first, experiment.
