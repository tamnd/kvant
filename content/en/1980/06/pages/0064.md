2. Let's write down the initial and final pressure values ​​in different parts of the tube. Initial pressures: $p_{AB} = 2 \rho gh = p, p_{CD} = \rho gh = p/2, p_{EF} = 0$. Final pressures: $p_{F} = \rho gh + p'_{CD} = p/2 + p'_{CD}, p'_{CD} = \rho gh + p'_{AB} = p/2 + p'_{AB}$. According to the Boyle-Mariotte law, $p_{AB}h = p'_{AB}x$ and $p_{CD}h = p'_{CD}(3h - x)$, where $x$ and $3h - x$ are the final lengths of sections $AB$ and $CD$, respectively. As a result, we obtain the equation $6p^2 - 12pp_F + 5p^2 = 0$, from which $p_F = p(1 + 1/\sqrt{6})$ (the second coefficient - with a minus sign in brackets - is discarded, since according to the meaning of the problem, the pressure must be notified).

3. According to Faraday's law, the voltage on the coil is $U_L = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$. Since the current in the circuit increases in direct proportion to time, $U_L$ does not change with time and at any moment $t$ $U_L = L \frac{I}{t}$. Consequently, the voltage on the capacitor, equal to the voltage on the coil, also remains constant: $\frac{Q_0}{C_0} = \frac{Q_0 - q}{C}$, where $Q_0$ is the initial charge on the capacitor, $q$ is the charge leaving the capacitor plates during time $t$, $C$ is the capacitance of the capacitor at the moment $t$. From the equality $L \frac{I}{t} = \frac{Q_0}{C_0}$ we find $I:I = \frac{Q_0}{LC_0} t$. Then the charge leaving the capacitor is $q = I_{cp} t = \frac{I}{2} t = \frac{Q_0}{2LC_0} t^2$. From plotting the voltage across the capacitor we find $C$:

$$C = C_0 \frac{Q_0 - q}{Q_0} = C_0 \left( 1 - \frac{t^2}{2LC_0} \right).$$
