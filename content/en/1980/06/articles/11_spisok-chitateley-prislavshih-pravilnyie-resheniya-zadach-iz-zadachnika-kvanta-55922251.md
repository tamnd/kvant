⟦folio 28⟧

How is this force directed? A technique for determining that the magnetic field is directed towards us. When the rod moves upward, the emf generated due to the action of the Lorentz force on free electrons is directed from left to right. While the rod is below the equilibrium position (acceleration is directed upward), the direction of the current in the rod coincides with the direction of the emf, and the capacitor is charged. In this case, the force $F$ is directed downward, that is, opposite to the acceleration $x$ of the rod. When the rod is above the equilibrium position, the direction of the emf remains the same, but the speed of the rod, and therefore the emf, decreases. In this case, the capacitor is discharged, the current in the rod changes direction, and therefore the force $F$ also changes direction - now it is directed upward, that is, again opposite to the acceleration. Having considered the downward movement of the rod, we will be convinced that in this case the direction of the force $F$ is opposite to the acceleration of the rod. Thus,

$$F = -CB^2l^2x.$$

Let us now write the equation of motion of the rod:

$$Mx = Mg - k(x + x_0) - CB^2l^2x.$$

Considering that $Mg = kx_0$ (see (*)), we finally have

$$Mx + CB^2l^2x = -kx.$$

Such an equation, as is known, describes harmonic wheels with a frequency

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M + CB^2l^2}}.$$

The amplitude $A$ of the wheel is equal to the initial deflection of the rod, and at any point in time

$$x(t) = A \cos \left( \sqrt{\frac{k}{M + CB^2l^2}} t \right).$$

(The role of the capacitor, as we see, coincides with an increase in inertia.)
