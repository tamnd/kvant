"Lenin
and now
more alive than all the living -
our knowledge
strength
and weapons."

The life and work of V.I. Lenin serve for all Soviet people as a vivid example of a courageous struggle for the highest and brightest human ideals. His scientific heritage is an inexhaustible source of inspiring ideas. Hence the lively, genuine attention to every event associated with the name of Vladimir Ilchts, the natural desire to penetrate into the day of every Leninist statement.

It is especially interesting and important for readers of Kvanat to get acquainted with some of V. I. Lenin’s thoughts regarding mathematics and physics, and to learn in more detail about the facts of his biography related to these sciences.

Matematika and physics were used the most in the Ulyanov family. First of all, this was explained by the fact that V.I. Lenin’s father, Ilya Nikolaevich Ulyanov, studied at the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Kazan University and then taught mathematics at the gymnasium for many years. With respect and warmth, I. I. Ulyanov recalled the sovereign’s award to the Russian mathematician Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky, who during his studies was the rector of Kazan University. Lobachevsky treated the young student with consideration and even recommended that he be recruited to work at the meteorological station - a task, at that time, complex and responsible. According to Anna Ilnichna Ulyanova-Elizarova, senior secretary of V.I. Lenin, “an almost reverent attitude towards science” distinguished Ilya Nikolaevich all his life and from childhood throughout everyone in the Ulyanov family.

Ilya Nikolaevich Ulyanov devoted a lot of time to working with children and, in particular, constantly tried to explain the lessons of physics and mathematics more deeply to them, introduced them to astronomical and physical instruments, and carried out interesting experiments. In the Ulyanovs' house they were fond of composing and solving witty logical problems, mathematical puzzles, and playing chess, which certainly contributed to the development of sharp thinking in children. Volodya Ulyanov was also greatly influenced by his older brother Alexander, who from childhood showed extraordinary mathematical abilities and studied at the natural sciences department of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of St. Petersburg University.

At the gymnasium, Volodya Ulyanov was taught excellently. One of his comrades later recalled: “We often could not make out everything that was given. This is where Vladimir Ilchts came to the rescue. He loved to do mathematics...” In addition to compulsory mathematics classes, Volodya Ulyanov took an active part in the gymnasium mathematics circle, where he solved difficult problems and even composed them himself. In June 1887, he brilliantly completed his final exams (including three exams in mathematics) and was awarded - one of the entire graduating class - a gold medal (in 1921, Lenin donated this medal to the fund for helping those going to die).

In the memoirs of V.I. Lenin there is numerous evidence that he showed great interest in mathematics. For example, in the summer of 1887, he met and had a long conversation with G.N. Shebuev, who lectured on mathematics at Kazan University. More than once Shebuev enthusiastically assured that Vladimir Ilchts should definitely enroll in the Faculty of Mathematics, that he was “definitely mathematical”
