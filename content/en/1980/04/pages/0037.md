F573. Estimate how much water can be taken into account in a sieve with square cells measuring $2 \times 2$ mm. The diameter D of the sieve is 20 cm. The sieve threads are not wetted with water.

The liquid between the sieve threads will form a mesh. The radius of this change cannot be greater than half the distance between the threads, that is, $r_{\max} = 1$ mm. Therefore, the maximum pressure that can be in the liquid above Menascus exceeds atmospheric pressure by values ​​of $\Delta p = \frac{2\sigma}{r}$ (σ is the surface tension of water). This formula is valid for a round menska, that is, for a sieve with round cells. However, for estimation, we can ignore the complex menisk formula on a square grid and use this formula.

So, $\Delta p = \frac{2\sigma}{r}$. On the other hand, $\Delta p$ is equal to the hydroxotic column of liquid $h$, drunk in a sieve:
$$\frac{2\sigma}{r} = \rho gh.$$
From here we find $h = \frac{2\sigma}{\rho g r}$. The mass of the liquid in the sieve is equal to the product of its volume $V$ and density $\rho$, that is
$$m = V \rho = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} h \rho = \frac{\pi D^2 \sigma}{2gr} \approx 0,458 \text{ кг}.$$

F574. Two balls with masses $m_1=0,2$ kts and $m_2=0,3$ kts fly towards each other at a speed of $v=20$ m/s each. A graph of the force of interaction of the balls during a collision versus time is shown in Fig. 1. How much heat was released when the balls collided?

The change in momentum of each ball is equal to the momentum of the force acting on this ball. The impulse of the force $\vec{F}$ acting on the ball during the time $\Delta t$ is equal to $|\vec{F}| \Delta t$. Consequently, the total impulse of the force acting on the ball during the collision is equal in absolute value to the area of ​​the figure under the graph of the dependence $|\vec{F}|(t)$. From figure 1 we find the impulse of force $|p_c|$:

$$|p_c| \approx 10^5 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 54 \text{ H} \cdot \text{c} = 5,4 \text{ H} \cdot \text{c}$$

(there are approximately 54 squares under the dependence graph $|\vec{F}|(t)$; the area of ​​each square is $10^5 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{ H} \cdot \text{c}$).

For projections of impulses onto the $X$ (Fig. 2) axis, we can write:
$$p_1 = m_1 v - |p_c|,$$
$$p_2 = -m_2 v + |p_c|.$$

Here $\vec{p}_1$, $\vec{p}_2$ balls after collision; $m_1 v$, $m_2 v$ — mods of ball impulses before collision. From (1) and (2) we find the speeds of the balls after the collision:
$$v_1 = \frac{p_1}{m_1} v - \frac{|p_c|}{m_1} = -7 \text{ м/c},$$
$$v_2 = \frac{p_2}{m_2} = -v + \frac{|p_c|}{m_2} = -2 \text{ м/c}.$$
