Speaking about the contribution that V.I. Lenin made to the cause of public education, one cannot help but dwell on his attitude towards the people's teacher. This topic has been close to Vladimir Ilch since childhood. V.I. Lenin’s father, I.I. Ulyanov, devoted more than 50 years of his life to the cause of public education, and his attitude towards the people’s teacher could not but affect the views of Vladimir Ilchch. V.I. Lenin understood that it was impossible to create a new school without winning the teaching staff to the side of the Soviet government. In November 1917, the Union of International Teachers was created. Lenin approved and supported him in every possible way. At the First All-Russian Congress of International Teachers, he said that “the task of the new pedagogy is to connect teaching activities with the task of the socialist organization of society**).

Vladimir Ilch will address this issue more than once. “We must place the people's teacher at a height that he would never stand and cannot become in Burguz society. This is a truth that does not require proof. We must move towards this state of affairs systematically, steadily, by truly working on his spiritual ascent, and on his comprehensive preparation for his actual high rank, and most importantly, most importantly and most importantly, on raising his financial position,” writes V. I. Lenin in the article “Pages from the Diary” *) (January 1923).

There is no issue related to public education that Vladimir Ilch does not worry about. On August 16, 1921, V.I. Lenin signed the decree of the Council of People's Commissars “On the procedure for publishing textbooks.” He ensures that the country's libraries are supplied with the latest scientific and technical foreign literature.
