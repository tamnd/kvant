F576. The plane moves uniformly along straight line OB at speed $v > c$ (at the speed of sound). The person being observed monitors the flight from point A. At the moment when the plane is at point O, a special device installed on the plane emits two short sound pulses: first a pulse with a small amplitude, then a pulse with a larger amplitude. The time interval between pulses is equal to m. Under what conditions does the observable register first a pulse with a larger amplitude, and then with a small one? Distance OA is equal to L, angle AOB is equal to $\theta$.

Let the plane move from point O to point D in time t. Let us plot on the segment AO a segment AC such that $|AC| = |AD|$. If by the time the second pulse (with a large amplitude) is emitted, the first pulse (with a small amplitude) has time to “travel” the distance $|OC|$ and ends up at point C, then both pulses will be registered at point A simultaneously. If the distance “traversed” by the first impulse during time t is less than $|OC|$, then the second impulse will be registered at point A equal to that of the first. Thus, we are interested in the conditions under which energy is carried out

$$c\tau < |OC|.$$

Since the time t is small, we can assume that the angles at which the observed person sees the plane at points O and D are practically the same, that is, the angle $\phi$ (see Fig.ok) is very small. Therefore, we can assume that $\vec{ACD} = \vec{ADC} = 90^\circ$. Then $|OC| = |OD| \cos \theta = v\tau \cos \theta$.

Let's substitute this expression for $|OC|$ into (*):

$$c\tau < v\tau \cos \theta.$$

From here we find that the second pulse will be registered at point A equal to the first one if

$$v > c/\cos \theta.$$

This inequality means that the speed of the aircraft must in all cases be greater than the speed of sound. Then, as our consideration shows, it may turn out that the plane is approaching the observed one, and the observed one will hear the plane moving away.

F577. When the temperature is $t_0 = -20^\circ C$, the battery operates to maintain the room temperature $t_1 = 16^\circ C$. When, in addition to the battery, a power supply of $P_1 = 1$ kW was turned on, the temperature in the room was set at $t_2 = 22^\circ C$. Determine the thermal power of the battery.

When.mechanie. Heat transfer from one body to another is proportional to the temperature difference of these bodies ($\Delta P = k\Delta t, k = const$).

At the set temperature in the room, the thermal energy released by the devices is equal to the heat losses (heat loss losses). Therefore, the thermal power of the battery is equal to

$$P = k(t_1 - t_0).$$

With power on and battery running

$$P + P_1 = k(t_2 - t_0).$$

From (1) and (2) we find the warm power of the battery:

$$P = P_1 \frac{t_1 - t_0}{t_2 - t_1} = 6 \text{ кВт}.$$
