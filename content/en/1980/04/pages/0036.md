⟦folio 34⟧

M565. Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be distinct positive numbers. Let us denote by $b_k$ the arithmetic mean of the real products of $k$ given numbers ($k=1, 2, \dots, n$):

$$b_1 = \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n}{n},$$
$$b_2 = \frac{a_1 a_2 + a_1 a_3 + \dots + a_{n-1} a_n}{n(n-1)},$$
$$b_n = a_1 a_2 \dots a_n.$$

Prove the inequalities:

a) $b_1 \geq \sqrt{b_2}$,
b) $b_k^2 \geq b_{k-1} b_{k+1}$ ($k=2, \dots, n-1$);
b) $\sqrt[k]{b_k} \geq k+1 \sqrt{b_{k+1}}$ ($k=2, \dots, n-1$).

Let $s_1 = nb_1, s_2 = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} b_2, \dots,$
$\dots, s_k = C_k^k b_k, \dots, s_n = b_n^*$.

Consider the polynomial

$$P(x) = x^n - s_1 x^{n-1} + \dots + (-1)^{n-1} s_{n-1} x + (-1)^n s_n.$$ (1)

Since $P(x) = (x - a_1)(x - a_2) \cdot \dots \cdot (x - a_n),$ (2)
$P(x)$ has $n$ distinct positive roots $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$.
a) Differentiate it $n-2$ times, and then divide it by $n(n-1) \dots 4 \cdot 3$. We get a quadratic trinomial

$$x^2 - \frac{2}{n} s_1 x + \frac{2}{n(n-1)} s_2,$$ (3)

having different positive roots (between two roots of a polynomial there is always the root of its derivative - see figure). This means that the polynomial (3) has a positive discriminant $D = \frac{s_1^2}{n^2} - \frac{2s_2}{n(n-1)}$, which opens $\frac{s_1}{n} > \sqrt{\frac{s_2}{n(n-1)}}$, that is, $b_1 > \sqrt{b_2}$.

6) Let us now differentiate the polynomial $P(x)$ ($n-k-1$) times ($k=2, \dots, n-1$). We obtain the polynomial $n(n-1) \cdot \dots \cdot (k+2) x^{k+1} - (n-1) \cdot \dots \cdot (k+1) s_1 x^k + \dots + (-1)^{k-1} s_{k-1} \times \frac{(n-k+1)!}{2} x^2 + (-1)^k s_k (n-k)! x + (-1)^{k+1} s_{k+1} (n-k-1)!,$ (4)

having $k+1$ different positive roots. Therefore the polynomial $(-1)^{k+1} (n-k-1)! s_{k+1} y^{k+1} + (-1)^k (n-k)! s_k y^k + (-1)^k \frac{(n-k+1)!}{2} s_{k-1} y^{k-1} + \dots$

... + 1, $(4')$ obtained from the polynomial (4) by replacing $y = \frac{1}{x}$, also has $k+1$ different positive roots. Let us now differentiate the polynomial $(4')$ $k-1$ times. We obtain the quadratic trinomial $(-1)^{k+1} (n-k-1)! \frac{(k+1)!}{2} s_{k+1} y^2 + (-1)^k (n-k)! k! s_k y + (-1)^k \frac{(n-k+1)!}{2} (k-1)! s_{k-1},$ (5)

having two different positive roots. Let's divide it by $(-1)^{k-1} (n-k-1)! (k-1)!$; we get a trinomial

$$\frac{k(k+1)}{2} s_{k+1} y^2 - k(n-k) s_k y + \frac{(n-k+1)(n-k)}{2} s_{k-1}.$$

Its discriminant is positive, so

$$k(n-k) s_k^2 - (k+1)(n-k+1) s_{k-1} s_{k+1} > 0$$

or

$$\frac{s_k^2}{(C_k^n)^2} - \frac{(k+1)(n-k+1)}{k(n-k)} \frac{s_{k-1} s_{k+1}}{(C_k^n)^2} > 0,$$

that is

$$\left( \frac{s_k}{C_k^n} \right)^2 - \frac{s_{k-1}}{C_k^n} \cdot \frac{s_{k+1}}{C_k^n} > 0$$ (6)

(since)

$$\frac{n-k}{k+1} C_k^n = C_n^{k+1} \text{ и } \frac{k}{n-k+1} C_k^n = C_n^{k-1}$$

Thus,

$$b_k^2 > b_{k-1} b_{k+1} (k=2, \dots, n-1).$$

Inequality 6) is called Newton's inequality. c) We will prove c) by induction. The basis of induction ($k=1$) is inequality a). Let us now assume that the
*) Expressions $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ are called elementary (basic) symmetric functions of $n$ variables $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$, and the fact that they with alternating signs serve as coefficients of a polynomial with roots $a_1, \dots, a_n$ is Vieta’s theorem.
