⟦folio 61⟧

Consulting: world chess champion, international grandmaster A. Karpov. Conducted by the USSR chess strategist, Candidate of Technical Sciences E. Gik.
Among the many problems of dov and non-dimensional positions on the chessboard, the so-called symbolic or, in other words, pictorial problems are of particular interest. The initial or final arrangement of figures in such problems or the course of the solution itself is represented by some kind of figure, symbol, number or letter. Typically symbolic tasks are associated with prominent personalities, anniversaries or important events.

In this place, as you know, Cosmonautics Day will be celebrated in our country. And it was on April 12, 1964 that problems 1-3 of the international master in chess composition E. Poghosyants were published for the first time. The author connected them to Yuri Gagarin, the first person on Earth to fly into space. The initial arrangement of the pieces in each of the three-moves depicts the letter “G”. We invite you to solve these symbolic problems. It is curious that only 4 figures are used in them.

In our February page, we have already offered you one problem - a multi-move game in which the white queen, after lengthy and tedious maneuvers, announces checkmate to the non-hitting king on the 14th move. Such problems in chess composition are called turns. Non-training task 4 also appears to be an enemy. The board here is generally square, but the dimensions are

4. J. Mortens. Checkmate in 21 moves.

Problem 5, due to the famous chess composer G. Dawson, is very interesting. It can also be called pictorial, only the figure here is formed not by the figures, but by the board itself.

Tasks 1-3, as we have seen, contained only four figures. However, the minimum material that allows you to create a chess work consists of three pieces (with two kings on the board there is simply nothing to give a mat). However, one of the most record-breaking in this sense is problem 6. It is solved very simply, but keep in mind that this is the strangest three-figure problem - it was invented 135 years ago!
