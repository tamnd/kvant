⟦folio 33⟧

M564. For which points M of side BC of triangle ABC is the following statement true: $\triangle MPQ \sim \triangle ABC$, if points P and Q are:
a) the centers of the circle described respectively around the triangles ABM and ACM;
b) the points of intersection of their medians;
c) the points of intersection of their heights?

a) Let us show that the point M on side BC can be chosen arbitrarily.

Triangles MPQ and APQ are congruent ($|AP|=|MP|$, $|AQ|=|MQ|$, Fig. 1). Let us prove the similarity of triangles APQ and ABC. Let K, L, O be the midpoints of AB, AC and AM respectively. Without loss of generality, we can assume that $\overset{\frown}{AMB} < \pi/2$ (case [AM] $\perp$ [BC]) is trivial).

A circle can be described around each of the quadrilaterals AOLQ and AKPO ($\overset{\frown}{AOQ} = \overset{\frown}{ALQ} = \overset{\frown}{AOP} = \overset{\frown}{AKP} = \pi/2$ - see Fig. 1). Therefore $\overset{\frown}{LAQ} = \overset{\frown}{LOQ}$ and $\overset{\frown}{POK} = \overset{\frown}{PAK}$. But $\overset{\frown}{LOQ} = \overset{\frown}{POK}$ are like vertical angles. Hence $\overset{\frown}{LAQ} = \overset{\frown}{PAK}$ and

$\frac{|AL|}{|AQ|} = \frac{|AK|}{|AP|}$. Therefore, the composition of a rotation by an angle $\overset{\frown}{PAK}$ with a center at point A and prepared with a coefficient $k = \frac{|AK|}{|AP|}$ with the same center A transforms triangle APQ into a similar triangle AKL. So, $\triangle APQ \sim \triangle ABC$.

6) A homothety with coefficient $\frac{3}{2}$ and center at the point M takes $\triangle PQM$ to its similar $\triangle KLM$ (Fig. 2). Let us assume that $\triangle KLM$ is similar to $\triangle AKL$. Let's look at three cases:

1°. $\alpha = \delta$ (see Fig. 2). Then $(AL) \parallel (KM)$, so $|MB| = |MC|$, that is, $M$ is the middle [BC].

2°. $\alpha = \gamma$. Then $(AM) \perp (KL)$, so $M$ is the projection of vertex A to side BC.

3°. $\alpha = \widehat{A}$. Then either $\beta = \gamma$ and $|MB| = |MC|$ or $\beta = \delta$ and $M$ are the projection of vertex A (on [BC]).

It is easy to see that the converse is also true: if M is the midpoint of side BC or the projection onto [BC] of the vertex A, then the triangles MPQ and ABC are similar.

c) In this case, as in case a), point M on side BC can be chosen arbitrarily. Indeed (Fig. 3), $\overset{\frown}{MPQ} = \overset{\frown}{ABC}$, $\overset{\frown}{MQC} = \overset{\frown}{ACB}$ (as angles with mutually perpendicular sides); means $\triangle MPQ \sim \triangle ABC$.

Unfortunately, when publishing the conditions of this problem, the typesetter re-used the signs of similarity and congruence (he studied under the old program); We apologize to the reader who this typo allowed to understand the conditions.
