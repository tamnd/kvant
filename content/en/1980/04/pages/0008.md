Lenin and school

“The tasks of youth in general and communist youth unions and all other organizations in particular could be expressed in one word: “The task is to learn.” These words, spoken by V.I. Lenin at the III All-Russian Congress of the Russian Communist Youth Union on October 2, 1920, are key to understanding the attitude of the young Soviet state, led by V.I. Lenin, to the cause of education. Thoughts about the need to educate the masses were expressed by Vladimir Ilch long before the victory of the revolution... The end of the century has passed. Massive addresses and announcements incapacitate Marxist circles. And in distant Siberian exile, a young revolutionary, who is not yet working, writes about the impossibility of building a new society without radically changing the old school, sketches out a plan for creating a new education system.

Time has passed. The Great October Socialist Revolution triumphed in the country. Power passed into the hands of the people. The Geronic years of struggle and creation have arrived. On all fronts, the authorities are vigorously fighting the interventionists and White Guard gangs. There was devastation and hunger everywhere, there was not enough fuel and electricity. But even in this difficult time for the country, V.I. Lenin considered it necessary, as the first priority task, to raise the issue of creating a new system of young education.
