3. It is well known that the education of a scientific materialist worldview is impossible without familiarizing students with the history of science and students understanding the main stages of the development of science. It was from these messages that the concept of the simplest differential equations was introduced into the new mathematics program. Only three equations are studied in detail: uniformly accelerated motion

$$y'' = a,$$

harmonic vibrations

$$y'' = -k^2 y$$

and exponential growth or decline

$$y' = ky.$$

Let me remind you that for Newton there were two main problems of analysis: 1) finding their arbitrary functions, 2) finding these functions themselves by correspondence between functions and their arbitrary ones. The second problem is the problem of interposing differential equations. Newton considered the problem of finding antiderivatives as a simple special case.

The entire ideology of the mathematical natural from Newton to Laplace is inextricably linked with the concept of a differential equation. Laplace set out the general principle of determinism based on the fact that the basic laws of nature are expressed in the form of differential equations, and their interpretation allows one to truly imagine the future. Internalization of the corresponding differential equations, as is known, is a scale problem that can be solved only by numerical methods. But it is not difficult to give a clear idea of ​​the problem. Without a fairly specific understanding of this stage of development of the mathematical natural, it is impossible to understand further stages, the emergence of statistical physics, quantum physics.
