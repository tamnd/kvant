⟦folio 30⟧

Tasks

M616 — M620; F628 — F632

M616. Is it possible to divide the numbers 1, 2, ..., 30 into groups
a) five numbers each,
b) six numbers each
so that the sums of numbers in all groups are the same?
b) For what n and k can the numbers 1, 2, ..., nk be divided into n groups of k numbers each so that the sums of the numbers in all groups are the same?

S. Berkolaiko

M617. Inside the triangle there are circles α, β, γ, δ of the same radius so that each of the circles α, β, γ touches two sides of the triangle and circle δ (Fig. 1). Prove that the center of the circle δ belongs to the line passing through the centers of the inscribed circle of the given triangle and the circumcircle.

V. Yagubyants

M618. a) Prove that there are infinitely many natural numbers n such that n! is divisible by n²+1.
b) Prove that for any α>0 there are infinitely many natural numbers n such that [αn]! is divisible by n²+1. (Here m!=1·2·...·m; [x] is the integer part of the number x.)

A. Sivatsky, 10th grade student

M619. Prove that if the equality |CD|=|AD|+|BC| is satisfied for a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, then the bisectors of its angles A and B intersect on the side CD.

I. Sharytsin

M620. Let x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ be real numbers such that x₁²+x₂²+...+xₙ²=1. Prove that the sum of the models of 2ᵐ numbers
±x₁±x₂±...±xₙ
(so with all possible combinations of the signs “+” and “—”) does not exceed 2ᵐ.

J. Kasakovskikh
