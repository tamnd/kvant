Great scientist and thinker

110 years separate us from the birthday of Vladimir Ilyich Lenin - the inspirer and leader of a turning point in world history - the Great October Socialist Revolution, the creator of the first state of workers and peasants.

Socialism and communism are built on a scientific basis. And V.I. Lenin made a huge contribution to this science. He deeply and comprehensively developed the question of the role of science in the construction of a socialist and communist society.

Lenin paid great attention to the development of young Soviet science. On his instructions, in the first years of Soviet power, many scientific centers were created, which later gained worldwide fame. Among them, we mention the Physico-Technical, Optical, Radium and Physico-Mathematical Institutes established in Petrograd, the Central Aerodynamic Institute in Moscow, and a special radio laboratory in Nizhny Novgorod (Gorky).

The greatness of a genius is characterized by the ability to foresee the future development of events. Lenin possessed this ability to the fullest. Thus, a scientific analysis of the development of events in Russia in 1917 allowed Lenin to determine with almost mathematical accuracy the moment of the socialist revolution. His famous words: “Delay in restoration is like death” were not just a phrase, but a precise formulation, the result of a deep analysis of a specific political situation.

The works of brilliant scientists are also characterized by the fact that over time they not only do not reflect their significance, but, on the contrary, serve as the basis for the further development of the ideas contained in these works. This was the case with the works of Newton, Maxwell, and Einstein. This applies even more to the works of Lenin. And although we are separated from

It is especially interesting and important for readers of Kvanat to get acquainted with some of V. I. Lenin’s thoughts regarding mathematics and physics, and to learn in more detail about the facts of his biography related to these sciences.

Matematika and physics were used the most in the Ulyanov family. First of all, this was explained by the fact that V.I. Lenin’s father, Ilya Nikolaevich Ulyanov, studied at the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Kazan University and then taught mathematics at the gymnasium for many years. With respect and warmth, I. I. Ulyanov recalled the sovereign’s award to the Russian mathematician Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky, who during his studies was the rector of Kazan University. Lobachevsky treated the young student with consideration and even recommended that he be recruited to work at the meteorological station - a task, at that time, complex and responsible. According to Anna Ilnichna Ulyanova-Elizarova, senior secretary of V.I. Lenin, “an almost reverent attitude towards science” distinguished Ilya Nikolaevich all his life and from childhood throughout everyone in the Ulyanov family.

Ilya Nikolaevich Ulyanov devoted a lot of time to working with children and, in particular, constantly tried to explain the lessons of physics and mathematics more deeply to them, introduced them to astronomical and physical instruments, and carried out interesting experiments. In the Ulyanovs' house they were fond of composing and solving witty logical problems, mathematical puzzles, and playing chess, which certainly contributed to the development of sharp thinking in children. Volodya Ulyanov was also greatly influenced by his older brother Alexander, who from childhood showed extraordinary mathematical abilities and studied at the natural sciences department of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of St. Petersburg University.

At the gymnasium, Volodya Ulyanov was taught excellently. One of his comrades later recalled: “We often could not make out everything that was given. This is where Vladimir Ilchts came to the rescue. He loved to do mathematics...” In addition to compulsory mathematics classes, Volodya Ulyanov took an active part in the gymnasium mathematics circle, where he solved difficult problems and even composed them himself. In June 1887, he brilliantly completed his final exams (including three exams in mathematics) and was awarded - one of the entire graduating class - a gold medal (in 1921, Lenin donated this medal to the fund for helping those going to die).

In the memoirs of V.I. Lenin there is numerous evidence that he showed great interest in mathematics. For example, in the summer of 1887, he met and had a long conversation with G.N. Shebuev, who lectured on mathematics at Kazan University. More than once Shebuev enthusiastically assured that Vladimir Ilchts should definitely enroll in the Faculty of Mathematics, that he was “definitely mathematical”

Having chosen the path of a professional revolutionary, V.I. Lenin understood perfectly well that the main theoretical task of Russian Marxists - an in-depth analysis of the social system of Russia and identifying the role of various classes in the revolution - can only be solved on the creative basis of a painstaking study of facts and an accurate analysis of figures characterizing Russian reality. Therefore, simultaneously with an in-depth study of the ideas of Marxism, he began intensively studying statistical science, which uses mathematical methods to quantitatively express the patterns of socio-economic processes.

V.I. Lenin was the largest representative of economic and social statistics. A new stage in the theory and practice of economic statistics and its application to sociology is associated with his name. He masterfully knew how to use statistical data and constantly emphasized the need for their systematic use for in-depth study of social phenomena. He put forward a number of fundamental provisions on the techniques and methods of statistical data processing, on the accuracy of digital analysis, on the correspondence between the concrete and the abstract in statistics. The number of descriptions of the characteristics of mass socio-economic phenomena producing in society, V. I. Lenin always considered in the early years the idea that numbers, statistical material should clearly reveal the real state of things in economic and political life, in the class struggle, should serve as convincing evidence of one or another specific conclusion.

Lenin first introduced into social statistics the concept of the distribution function, which underlies physical statistics. This allows him, in his work “The Development of Capitalism in Russia,” to refute the populists’ idea of ​​a special path of development for Russia and scientifically substantiate the assertion that it, like many other countries, is following the capitalist path. Lenin successfully used the same method in his work “Imperialism as the Highest Stage of Capitalism,” where he predicted the inevitability of the collapse of the world colonial system. This genius foresight is being realized before our eyes.

The fundamental thoughts of V.I. Lenin on methodological issues of statistics have not lost their significance in our time, although the mathematical apparatus of statistics today is much more developed than it was at the turn of the 20th century. Excellent examples of criticism by V.I. Lenin of subjectivism, hatred, physicalization of numbers fully apply to those bourgeois researchers who, hiding behind local numbers, deliberately distort actual facts, form the use of statistical information, build mathematical models that ignore connections, causality and sources of changes in society.

Among the huge number of Lenin's works, there is not a single one that examines a specific physical phenomenon. Nevertheless, the influence of V.I. Lenin on the development of physics is difficult to overestimate. Essentially, Lenin laid the scientific foundations of physiology and the methodology of this science in his landmark work “Materialism and Empiricritism.”

At the end of the 19th - beginning of the 20th centuries, the focus of attention of natural scientists and philosophers was on a large number of experimental facts that did not fit into the attractive framework of physical concepts (the discovery of radioactivity and RENTEN rays, the creation of the theory of relativity and the discovery of the dependence of the mass of electrons arising from this theory).

A number of prominent scientists of that time argued that these experimental facts reflect the objectivity of the external world, or at least the objectivity of our knowledge about the world around us and its phenomena. In creating “Materialism and Empiricriticism,” Lenin set himself the goal of showing the fallacy of such views, revealing the social and epistemological roots of physical idealism, pointing out the right way out of the crisis in physics, and demonstrating the validity of the dialectical-materialistic approach in physics and mathematics.

V.I. Lenin saw the crisis of physics “in the absence... of a direct, decisive and irrevocable recognition of the objective value” of physical theories. He saw one of the principles of idealism in physics in the successful connection of physics (and the natural world in general) with mathematics. Possessing an extremely speculative, abstract character, mathematics creates the prerequisites for the emergence of viewing mathematical abstractions as existing independently, in complete isolation from real action, independently of it. This is where the idealistic solution to the basic question of physiology arose by those scientists for whom “the fact that these physicists limit their theory to a system of equations is a refutation of materialism; equations and that’s it, no matter, no objective realization, only symbols... “Matter disappears,” only equations remain.”

And in our day, during the period of the rapid process of mathematization of knowledge, Lenin’s instruction is of particular importance that the question separating materialism from various idealistic trends is not what “functional correspondence” can mathematically describe the causes of connections and other categories of various sciences, “... but whether the source of our position of these connections is an objective law of nature or a property of our mind.” Man undoubtedly creates patterns that exist in nature objectively, outside of our consciousness.

The philosophical ideas of V.I. Lenin have a beneficial effect on all modern science; they have become a strong ideological foundation of modern physics. This is another confirmation of V.I. Lenin’s brilliant vision, made seventy years ago, that “Modern physics lies in childbirth. It gives birth to dialectical materialism.” Now it can be argued that the birth of the state is exactly in the form that Lenin foresaw.

Soviet scientists sacredly respect the precepts of V. I. Lenin. Speaking at a ceremonial meeting dedicated to the 250th anniversary of the USSR Academy of Sciences, General Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee L. I. Brezhnev said: “A Soviet scientist, if, of course, he is a truly Soviet scientist, in all his scientific activities proceeds from the scientific worldview of Marximal-Lennism, is an active fighter for the cause of communism, against any forces of reaction and obscurantism.” There is no doubt that those young readers of our magazine who will eventually come to greater science, will truly justify the high importance of the Soviet scientist, and will successfully serve their people, our Motherland.
