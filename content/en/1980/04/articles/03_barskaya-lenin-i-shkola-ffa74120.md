⟦folio 7⟧

V.I. Lenin and N.K. Krupskaya are middle peasants of the village of Kashino on the opening day of the rural electric station.

The creation of a commissariat of education was marked, and on November 9 of the same year the Decree of the State Commission for Public Education was adopted. 15 departments of public education were created. Among them are departments for the introduction of universal literacy, out-of-school education, school medicine and hygiene, school construction, technical schools and political education. Lenin writes: “Our cause in the school field is the same struggle for the overthrow of the bourgeoisie: we openly declare that school is outside of life, outside of politics - it is a lie and philistinism.”*).

Under the leadership of Vladimir Ilchch, employees of the Education Commissariat carried out a lot of work in the construction of a new school. Lenin repeatedly pointed out the importance and complexity of this work, and everyone tried to warn his comrades against mistakes and excesses. Vladimir Ilchch constantly spoke about the need for skillful use of the cultural heritage of the old system.

During these years, Lenin’s provisions on the school were organizationally enshrined in the “Regulations on the Unified Labor School of the RSFSR” dated September 30, 1918.

Article 1. All schools of the Russian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic, which are under the jurisdiction of the People's Commissariat for Education, are given the name “Unified Labor School”.

Article 2. The unified school is divided into 2 levels: 1st - for children from 8 to 13 years old and 2nd - from 13 to 17 years old.

Article 3. School education for levels 1 and 2 is free.

Article 5. In schools of the 1st and 2nd levels, joint education is introduced.

Article 6. Teaching in schools of any kind of believer and performing religious rites in schools is not allowed.

* V. I. Lenin. PSS, vol. 36, p. 77.

Speaking about the contribution that V.I. Lenin made to the cause of public education, one cannot help but dwell on his attitude towards the people's teacher. This topic has been close to Vladimir Ilch since childhood. V.I. Lenin’s father, I.I. Ulyanov, devoted more than 50 years of his life to the cause of public education, and his attitude towards the people’s teacher could not but affect the views of Vladimir Ilchch. V.I. Lenin understood that it was impossible to create a new school without winning the teaching staff to the side of the Soviet government. In November 1917, the Union of International Teachers was created. Lenin approved and supported him in every possible way. At the First All-Russian Congress of International Teachers, he said that “the task of the new pedagogy is to connect teaching activities with the task of the socialist organization of society**).

Vladimir Ilch will address this issue more than once. “We must place the people's teacher at a height that he would never stand and cannot become in Burguz society. This is a truth that does not require proof. We must move towards this state of affairs systematically, steadily, by truly working on his spiritual ascent, and on his comprehensive preparation for his actual high rank, and most importantly, most importantly and most importantly, on raising his financial position,” writes V. I. Lenin in the article “Pages from the Diary” *) (January 1923).

There is no issue related to public education that Vladimir Ilch does not worry about. On August 16, 1921, V.I. Lenin signed the decree of the Council of People's Commissars “On the procedure for publishing textbooks.” He ensures that the country's libraries are supplied with the latest scientific and technical foreign literature.

V.I. Lenin signed the decree “On admission to higher educational institutions of the RSFSR” and “Resolution of the Council of People’s Commissars on preferential admission to higher educational institutions of representatives of the proletariat and the poorest peasantry.” The next step towards attracting workers to higher education institutions was the “Resolution of the Council of People’s Commissars on workers’ faculties,” adopted on September 17, 1920.

Workers' faculties played a huge role in the transformation of higher education, filling the VSU classrooms with workers and peasants.

All measures taken to restructure the education system were aimed at educating young specialists who would enthusiastically take on the restoration of the national economy and economy and would achieve success in socialist science, literature and technology.
