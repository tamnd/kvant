Lenin and physics

The comparison of the name V.I. Lenin and physics is neither accidental nor artificial. The great theoretician and practitioner of socialism, Lenin, just like Marx and Engels, could not get past the mismo of physics. Among other natural sciences, physics occupies a central place - the position of the headquarters, receiving information about the period and sending its results-directives back to the period. An abstraction in essence, but based on experience and observation, physics, always in its generality, serves as a natural scientific basis for simultaneous philosophy and technology. This was the case in the time of Achimedes, Newton, Maxwell, and this is absolutely obvious in the new physics.

The struggle for the physics of dialectical materialism, continued by Lenin, always behind Marx and Engels, inevitably had to develop on the material of the stages of the new physics. On the other hand, as the greatest political figure who created a socialist state, V.I. Lenin had to meet physics as the main technology.

The fundamental technical transformation of the country required, first of all, the strengthening of the scientific, physical Fundamenta, and, of course, it was no coincidence that at the very beginning of the revolution,

Until his time, apparently, documents had not yet been processed that specifically outlined the role of V. I. Lenin in the development of Soviet physics. Much more is known regarding Lenin's views on physics in connection with the analysis of basic Marxist physics. The book “Materialism and Empirio-Criticism,” published back in 1909 in Moscow, throughout its considerable length concerns physics and physicists, and its penultimate part, “The Newest Revolution in Natural Science and Physicist Idealism,” is specifically devoted to the physics of the early 20th century.

The era of the publication of Materialism and Empirio-Criticism was exclusively directed towards physics throughout the world. During these years, “new physics” was created based on unexpected facts of radioactivity, on the phenomena of gas discharges and on the amazing properties of light. The first versions of the theory of atoms were constructed, the concept of the electron was clarified, and there were disputes about the existence of a positive electron. At this time, the principle of relative was formed, weaned by its unusualness of many of the most daring, and the theory of quantum arose, met with hostility from almost all sides, starting from its creator - M. Planck. This was the era of the actual collapse of classical physics, when its seemingly forever indestructible foundations began to shake - Newton’s scheme of absolute space and time, the constancy of mass and the continuity of action.

The fact that the mass of the electron depends on the speed seemed completely crushing for the “mother” of classical physics... A few years after the publication of Lenin’s book, in 1911, in a speech at the second management congress, Professor N.A. Umov characterized the state of affairs as follows: “The subsequent development of physics is a process against Materials, which ended in its expulsion. But next to such negative activity there is the work of forming an electromagnetic symbol: it must be capable of depicting the properties of the materials of the world, its atomic structure, inertia, radiation and absorption of energy”*).

In this speech there were exclamations at which the arrows of “Materialism and Empirio-criticism” had been aimed several years earlier. “Isn’t it time to banish materials?... Matter has disappeared, its developments have been replaced by systems of related electrical inductives, and before us is drawn together the familiar materials, a profoundly different electromagnetic one**). There is no doubt that for N.A. Umov, as a physicist, the electromagnetic world was an objective world, that the “disappearance of materials” meant only the disappearance of Newtonian constant mass. But this is not how these luzungs were perceived by physicists and physicists who practice natural science. “Disappearance of materials》 was considered experimental. Evidence of the collapse of materials."

There were new facts, however, and their meaning now remains unclear. Given this state of affairs, at a certain stage of development, the only possible way to create a physical theory is a mathematical description of the found laws and further extrapolation of the obtained mathematical forms up to new experience confirming the extrapolation.

Combining experimental data and mathematical forms and relying on the classical scheme, a modern physicist builds his theory. But on this inevitable path it is extremely easy to get carried away on the wrong trail.

“The great success of naturalgonium,” writes Lenin, “approaching such homogeneous and simple elements of matter, the laws of motion of which allow mathematical processing, gives rise to the oblivion of the matter of mathematics. “Matter disappears”, only equations remain. At a new stage of development and, supposedly, in a new way, the true Kantian idea is obtained: reason is prescribed by the laws of nature”*).
