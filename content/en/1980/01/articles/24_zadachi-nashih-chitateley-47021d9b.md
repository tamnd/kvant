⟦folio 55⟧

1->X; 0->C;
WHILE X<1000::1/X+C->C;
X+1->X ALL;

After completing the first two instructions, the table of names will look like this:

| Name | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| x | 1 |
| c | 0 |

Now the cyclic prescription begins to work. First of all, the conditions are checked
$$X<1000$$

The value of X, as can be seen from the table, is equal to 1, so the condition is true and all requirements up to the keyword BCE will be fulfilled. After this, the table becomes like this:

| Name | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| x | 2 |
| c | 1 |

The next step checks the conditions again. It is still true, so the very instructions are fulfilled once again.

2. Prove that the varithmetic progression is 1, 14, 27, 40, ...
there are infinitely many terms written by just twos. Find the general appearance of these members.

D. Nyamsurzh

3. Prove that in the sequence
31, 331, 3331, 33331, ...
there are infinitely many terms divisible by 31, and none divisible by 13.

I. Suev

4. Find the area of ​​rhombus ABCD if the radius

the circumcircles of triangles ABC and ABD are equal to R and r, respectively.

I. Korshak

5. In some kingdoms 3n! cities and n types of transport (n!=1·2·...·n; read: “en-factorial”). Any two cities are connected by one type of transport. Prove that there is always a city from which you can visit at least two cities and return back without changing your mode of transport.

V. Guba
