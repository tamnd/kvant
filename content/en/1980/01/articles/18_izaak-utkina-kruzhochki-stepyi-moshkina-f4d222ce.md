Circles
Moshkin's steppes

After an elective in mathematics, seventh-grader Styopa Moshkin went to see his classmate Oleg. He decided to take advantage of the Steppe parish.

- Let me copy off the geometry, otherwise I don’t understand anything.

- Do you think that after this you will understand her better? - Styopa unexpectedly objected, deciding this time to show consciousness.

- Well, how do you figure out this geometry? - Oleg exclaimed. - In algebra, wherever you go, keep counting until you get the answer. And in the geometry of movements you can’t see or make jokes, and now there are some circles, these, what’s their name, compositions...

— During the elective, we were just analyzing compositions of movements.

And you know, with him it’s just like in algebra: we count, we count, and we get the answer. So great! Let me tell you!

- Why do I need your elective? You would better tell me how to solve homework.

The guys didn’t argue for long: Styopa’s enthusiasm turned out to be stronger than Oleg’s. Soon, sheets of paper for the draft, pens, and a blank double-marked notebook with a square appeared on the table. At first, Styopa tried to imitate Maria Vasilyevna, but quickly fell into a more excited speech:

“You understand, a circle is kind of like multiplication in algebra, an operation between letters,” Styopa began. “Only the letters do not represent numbers, but permutations.” And just like in algebra, it doesn’t matter how you put parentheses.

Styopa wrote in a clean notebook

$$R \circ (Q \circ P) = (R \circ Q) \circ P.$$  (1)

- This is called the law of associativity. And the same movement $E$ is when all the points remain in place, like units们

$$P \circ E = E \circ P = P.$$  (2)

And each movement has a figurative $P$, $P^{-1}$ is indicated; for him
$$P \circ P^{-1} = P^{-1} \circ P = E.$$ (3)

“Everything is clear,” said Oleg, “but why didn’t you mention the simplest property?”

And Oleg wrote $Q \circ P = P \circ Q$ on the draft.

“Oh no, this is not always true for moving,” said Styopa. In pure terradi the following entry appeared with figure:
Example. $S_a \circ S_b \neq S_b \circ S_a$.

In the example, Oleg quickly figured it out (and you?); Styopa wrote two more lines in a blank note:
$$P = Q \Leftrightarrow T \circ P = T \circ Q,$$
$$P = Q \Leftrightarrow P \circ T = Q \circ T,$$
providing them with the following comment:
— Like in algebra: both sides of an equality can be “multiplied” by the same displacement, and can also be reduced. Only on the same side. Like this: $P \circ T = T \circ Q \Rightarrow P = Q$ cannot be done!
- Why? - Oleg asked, but immediately answered himself. - If $T \circ Q \neq Q \circ T$, as in the example, then it turns out to be nonsense.

Then Styopa decided to check what movements Oleg still knows. When he squeezed the words “central symmetry” out of Oleg, the guys remembered the designation $Z_Q$ (for symmetry with center $Q$), and Styopa wrote $Z_Q \circ Z_Q = ?$ Oleg, after thinking a little, erased the question mark, and the following entry appeared in the terrada:
$$Z_Q \circ Z_Q = E.$$ (5)

Here Styopa proposed a more complex question: $Z_P \circ Z_Q = ?$ When the guys figured it out in the draft, Oleg carefully wrote down:
Composition of two central symmetries - transfer:
$$Z_P \circ Z_Q = 2Q \vec{P}.$$ (6)

Under figure

Styopa added proof:
$$\overrightarrow{A A}' = \overrightarrow{A Q} + \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{P A}' = \overrightarrow{Q A}' + \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{A' P} = \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{Q P} = 2Q \vec{P}.$$
You probably guessed Styopa’s next question: $Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = ?$ With Styopa’s hint, Oleg solved this problem; he wrote the result like this:
The composition of three central symmetries is also a central symmetry:
$$Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_S,$$ (7)
ede $S$ is such a point that
$$\overrightarrow{PS} = \overrightarrow{QR}.$$ (8)

Proof.
$$Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = E \circ Z_S = Z_S.$$
“You and I will continue to put numbers under the signs = and => so as not to forget where we got what,” said Styopa.

Then Oleg remembered that, in addition to the central one, there is also a central symmetry: $S_a$; in the terrace he immediately wrote down:
$$S_a \circ S_a = E,$$ (9)
after which the guys began to think about the question $S_b \circ S_a = ?$ Having remembered the elective, Styopa suggested doing this first in the case of $a \perp b$. The following entry appeared in the terrada:

Composition of axial symmetries with redistribution of axes - central symmetry:

$$S_b \circ S_a = S_a \circ S_b = Z_P,$$

if $a \perp b$ and $P = a \cap b$.

Instead of proof they made figure.

And for the case $a \parallel b$ the guys wrote:

Composition of two axial symmetries with parallel axes - parallel translation:

$$S_b \circ S_a = 2AB,$$

if $a \parallel b$, $A \in a$, $B \in b$, $(AB) \perp a$.

Proof. $S_b \circ S_a$ $\overset{(2)}{=} S_b \circ \circ (E \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(9)}{=} S_b \circ ((S_c \circ S_c) \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(1)}{=} (S_b \circ \circ S_c) \circ (S_c \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(10)}{=} Z_B \circ Z_A$ $\overset{(6)}{=} 2AB$, where $(AB) = c$.

“You see,” Styopa triumphed, “we count, we count, we get the answer!” And our answers - equalities - are good because they can be read in both directions. The last one, for example, can be read like this:

Any parallel transfer can be represented as a composition of two axial symmetries, the axes of which are perpendicular to the direction of transfer and are absent from each other by half of its length.

The guys returned to the problem "$S_b \circ S_a = ?$" in the general case when $a$ intersects $b$. New entries have appeared, and instead of proof - figure:

Composition of two axial symmetries with intersecting axes - rotation:

$$S_b \circ S_a = R_P^{2\alpha},$$

ede $P = a \cap b$ and $a - \text{yeol}^*$ between $a$ and $b$.

Styopa announced that this correspondence can also be “read in the other direction”: the rotation can be represented as a composition of two axial symmetries, the axes of which intersect at the center of the rotation and the uelel between them is equal to half the uelel of the rotation.

Then the guys took turn $(R_P^\alpha)$; The following entries appeared in the trash:

$$R_P^\alpha \circ R_P^{-\alpha} = E,$$

$$R_P^\alpha \circ R_P^\beta = R_P^{\alpha + \beta}.$$

Composition of two turns - turn:

$$R_Q^\beta \circ R_P^\alpha = R_T^{\alpha + \beta},$$

if** $T$ is the point of intersection of lines $c = R_P^{\alpha/2}(a)$ and $b = R_Q^{\beta/2}(c)$, then $c = (PQ)$.

Proof. $R_Q^\beta \circ R_P^\alpha$ $\overset{(1,2)}{=} (S_b \circ S_c) \circ (S_c \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(1)}{=} S_b \circ (S_c \circ S_c) \circ S_a$ $\overset{(9)}{=} S_b \circ S_a = R_T^{2(\alpha/2 + \beta/2)} = R_T^{\alpha + \beta}$.

*) The guys forgot to write that the smallest angle between $a$ and $b$ is taken.

**) The guys started to demand $\alpha > 0$, $\beta > 0$ and $\alpha + \beta < 2\pi$.
