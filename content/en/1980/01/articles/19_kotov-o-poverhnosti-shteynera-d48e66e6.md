The first page of the cover shows a computer-generated, artist-created image of a Steiner surface defined by a 4th degree equation
$$x^2y^2 + y^2z^2 + z^2x^2 + xyz = 0.$$
(*)

As always happens, an equation that is characteristic in appearance appears interesting, and so does a characteristic geometric image.

The cover shows sections of the surface of a family of planes perpendicular to one of the coordinate axes (Ox). These sections resemble “figure eights”, which are notified and turn into something like “bows” (the invisible parts of the computer sections are not shown).
In Figures 1 and 2 the surface is shown from two angles.

It intersects the coordinate axes x, y, z at 6 “particular” points with coordinate values ​​1/2 and - 1/2. On the integrals between singular points, along each coordinate axis, the surface intersects itself (Fig. 1).
In the neighborhood of the origin, it is close to the theme of intersecting coordinate planes, which is clearly visible (with the center at the origin) and touches four of its faces along four circles. Visible circles are shown on figure 1 in red. Each two of these circles have a common point - one of the six individuals.

The Steiner surface can be considered closed (if we exclude the coordinate axes that belong to it from consideration); it bounds four identical bodies “joined” by edges (segments of coordinate axes). If we assume that, moving along any side of the surface, one can “pass through” another cavity intersecting it, then with such a movement it is easy to get to the opposite side; our surface, like a Möbius strip or a Klein bottle, is one-sided.

In conclusion, the question is: what kind of surface will you get if you put the numbers +0.001, -0.001 instead of zero on the right side of the equation (*)? If this question commas its figure, we will ask the computer to draw these surfaces more accurately.
