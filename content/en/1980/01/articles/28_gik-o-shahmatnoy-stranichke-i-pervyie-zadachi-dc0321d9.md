Chess page

I congratulate the team members of "Kvant" on the reports of "Chess Page". Between the accounting shaft in the unit and the back mathematics, office and ...
chess.

If you are interested in mathematics and physics, then most likely you are also a fan of chess, love to play it, and willingly solve problems and problems. That is why, starting from this issue, a permanent “Chess page” opens in «Kvant». We hope that reading it will be a pleasant and useful release for you after solving a problem in mathematics and physics. On this page you will find various chess problems, etiodes, game endings, chess puzzles. Of course, taking into account the readership, we will strive to ensure that the proposed positions contain some kind of mathematical element, a geometric idea. Here you will also get acquainted with effective combinations from games of masters, and receive brief information about the events taking place in the chess world. The page will be managed by Evgeniy Gik, and will be advised by the world chess champion, international grandmaster, editor-in-chief of the Chess Review 64 magazine Anatoly Karpov.

It was no coincidence that we turned to A. Karpov with a request to advise the “Chess Page” in our magazine. At one time, he graduated from the mathematical scale in Tula with a gold medal, was recognized by the regional mathematical Olympiad, but... later he chose his specialty not in mathematics, but in chess. It cannot be said that Anatoly completely abandoned mathematics. He graduated with honors from the Faculty of Economics of Leningrad State University, and as you know, economics and mathematics are connected technologies.

Evgeniy Gik is a master of sports of the USSR in chess, candidate of technical sciences, and our regular author. Many people probably read his book “Matematika on the Chessboard” with satisfaction.

Tasks

Position No. 1 arose in one of the games at the 1968 Moscow State University championship. The white players were played by the then first-year mekhanite student, young master Anatoly Karpov, and the black by mekhanite graduate Evgeniy Gik. Black, having two extra sands, was quite optimistic. However, White found an effective “geometric” continuation, which immediately decided the fate of the game in their tracks (as a result, the winner became the champion of Moscow State University, and the loser took second place). This original opens with a collection of selected games for the champions, published in 1978.

Theses #2 are a classic example illustrating the non-educational geometric properties of a chessboard. Paradoxically, White manages to leave behind the seemingly unstoppable enemy pawn.

Problem #3 comes from the eminent chess composer Samuel Loyd, who is also one of the greatest masters in the genre of mathematical problems and puzzles, the author of the popular game "15".
(In «Kvant» various games and problems, embellished by S. Loyd, were previously published; you can familiarize yourself with his chess-mathematical problems in No. 6, 1976.) Although this problem is purely chess, it also contains some geometric motifs.
