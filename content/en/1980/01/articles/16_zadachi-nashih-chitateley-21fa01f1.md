⟦folio 40⟧

F562. The impotence capacitors $C_1 = 2.00$ µF and $C_2 = 3.00$ µF are connected in series and connected to a battery with an emf $E = 120$ V. the middle point of which is grounded. The wire connecting the capacitors can be grounded using key K. Find the charges $q_1, q_2$ and $q_3$ that will pass through sections I-I, II-II and III-III after closing the key in the directions indicated at figure.

Before the switch is closed, the charges $q$ of both capacitors are the same (since the capacitors are connected in series) and equal

$$q = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$$

After closing the key, the directions on the capacitors will become equal to $\frac{1}{2} E$, and the charges of the capacitors will be equal

$$q' = \frac{1}{2} C_1 E \quad \text{and} \quad q'' = \frac{1}{2} C_2 E.$$

Consequently, charges will pass through sections I-I and II-II in given directions

$$q_1 = q' - q = \frac{C_1 (C_1 - C_2)}{2 (C_1 + C_2)} E = -2.4 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ KJ}.$$

And

$$q_2 = q - q'' = \frac{C_2 (C_1 - C_2)}{2 (C_1 + C_2)} E = -3.6 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ KJ}.$$

The total charge of the capacitor plates connected to each other before the switch was closed was equal to zero, and after the switch was closed it became equal to $q'' - q'$. Such a charge is very/no, and more advanced than the III-III section, that is

$$q_3 = q'' - q' = \frac{1}{2} (C_2 - C_1) E = 6 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ KJ}.$$

I. Slobodetsky

Exercise
our readers

Using the Cauchne-Bunyakovsky inequality

$$(A_1^2 + A_2^2 + \dots + A_N^2) \times \times (B_1^2 + B_2^2 + \dots + B_N^2) \geq (A_1 B_1 + A_2 B_2 + \dots + A_N B_N)^2$$

(for its proof, see V. Levin’s article “Parabolas and Inequalities” - «Kvant»,

1976, no. 4; for $N = 2$ and $N = 3$ the geometric meaning of this inequality is very clear: the product of the lengths of two vectors is not less than their scalar product), solve the following problems:

1. Find the smallest values ​​of the expressions
   a) $(x + y)^2 + (x + a)^2 + (y + b)^2$, where $a$ and $b$ are parameters;
   b) $(x + y + z)^2 + (x + a)^2 + (y + b)^2 + (z + c)^2$, where $a, b, c$ are parameters;

b) $(x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n)^2 + (x_1 + a_1)^2 + (x_2 + a_2)^2 + \dots + (x_n + a_n)^2$, where $a_i$ are parameters.

At what values ​​are these values ​​achieved?

2. Prove that the square of any real root of a polynomial

$$P(x) = x^n + a_n x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0$$

variable sums of the squares of all its coefficients.

L. Puzach
