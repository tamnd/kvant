Self-oscillations in air flow

There are several ways to generate undamped oscillations of elastic bodies. However, from them - with the help of a built-in air flow. We offer you a simple experiment to verify this with your own eyes.

Cut out a strip of thick paper 2-3 cm wide and 50-80 cm long. Wrap the ends of the strip around two pencils several times and grab them with your hands. Loosely place the strip at a distance of 20-50 cm in front of the switched on fan so that the plane of the strip is horizontal. You will see that under the influence of the air flow the strip begins to oscillate, and its oscillations will be irregular and non-periodic.

Now gradually tighten the strip (there is no need to apply too large numbers). At certain tensions, rather strong periodic oscillations will occur and a standing wave will be established on the strip (see figure). The oscillations that appear will continue as long as you can maintain the experimental conditions unchanged. Obviously, this is an auto-reading process.

Let's try to understand the reason for the formation of auto-oscillations. The air flow flowing around the strip causes the appearance of air vortices, which periodically tear off the strip. At the moments of vortex separation, the resistance of the strip to the air flow changes. Since the vhrini come off at regular intervals, we can say that a force is periodically exerted on the paper strip by the air flow. As a result, the strip self-oscillates.

Why did the vibrations become noticeable only at a certain tension on the paper strip? The fact is that the strip is an elastic body capable of performing its own vibrations. By changing the tension of the strip, we thereby changed the frequency of its own vibrations. When the frequency of natural oscillations coincides with the frequency of self-oscillations, a kind of resonance occurs and the amplitude of self-oscillations increases sharply.

Thus, in this dough experiment, auto-resonant and resonant phenomena are intertwined.

What undesirable effects these phenomena can lead to are discussed in the third issue of the magazine for 1972 in the article by L. Aslamazov “Why do the wires hum.”
