Hero of our time

A traveler crossing a mountain range in the direction of the wind - from leeward to windward - will immediately notice a change in the weather. Rising to the top, he finds himself in clouds, otherwise he will be caught by rain or snowfall; beyond the pass it is cloudless and the wind is calm and dry. This type of wind is called a foehn. Why does this sudden change in weather occur? How is air “heated”? To understand this issue, let's see what happens if a high mountain range is in the path of the wet wind.

“Having collided” with a mountain, the air flow begins to “climb” the slope. In this case, any visible mass of air passes into an area of ​​​​lower pressure. There is an increase in the volume occupied by this mass. As a first approximation, we can neglect the heat exchange between individual air masses and consider the expansion process to be adnabate. In this case, the work is done due to the internal energy of the air. This means that the air temperature is understood.
