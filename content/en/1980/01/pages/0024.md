Consider a point $A$ located at a distance of 1 m from the origin $O$. Let's estimate how long it takes from the beginning of the body's movement to reach this point. At point $A$ the speed is 1 m/s. Let's take the point $A_1$, which lies halfway between the beginning $O$ and $A$. On segment $A_1A$ the instantaneous speed will be less than 1 m/s, and for a segment 1/2 m long it will take more than 1/2 s. Let us now take the point $A_2$ - in the middle between $O$ and $A_1$. On the segment $A_1A_2$ the instantaneous speed will be less than 1/2 m/s (all points located from $O$ are less than 1/2 m away), and the segment $A_1A_2$ with a length of 1/4 m will again take more than 1/2 s. You have, of course, already proven, as we will argue further: point $A_3$ is the middle of the segment $OA_2$, into a segment $A_2A_3$ 1/8 m long at a speed less than 1/4 m/s, again it will take more than 1/2 s, etc. The division process can be continued indefinitely, and we can collect any number of segments, the passage of which takes more 1/2 s, never add up to $O$. This means that a body from $O$ cannot get to $A$ at all!

We proposed that $A$ is 1 m away from $O$. But in a similar way it is shown that the body from $O$ cannot reach any point at all. This is the remarkable reasoning with which classical mechanics began!
