⟦folio 31⟧

Exercise

M601—M605; F613—F617

M601. Prove that in any triangle $ABC$ the midpoint of side $BC$ lies on the segment connecting the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle $ABC$ with the point of the circle circumscribed about this triangle, diametrically opposite to the vertex $A$, and bisects this segment.

G. Groshev, 10th grade student

M602. In Pascal's triangle, the beginning of which is depicted at figure 1 (each “inner” number is equal to the sum of the two sides above it), in the next line there are three numbers in a row, forming an arithmetic progression.
a) Which next line contains the same event?
b) Prove that it will occur in an infinite number of lines, and indicate all the numbers of such lines.

A. Avramov

Fig. 1. In the $n$th line, the $k$th number in order is equal to
$$C_n^k = \frac{n \cdot (n-1) \cdot \dots \cdot (n-k+1)}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot k}.$$

Fig. 2.

M603*. Solve the system of equations
$$x + \frac{3x - y}{x^2 + y^2} = 3, \quad y - \frac{x + 3y}{x^2 + y^2} = 0.$$
L. Kuptsov

M604. a) Andrey, Victor, Sergey, swimming underwater, simultaneously emerged at points $A_0, B_0, C_0$ and immediately dived again, and Andrey decided to swim a third of the way to Victor in a minute, Victor - a third of the way to Sergey, Sergey - a third of the way to Andrey. A minute later they emerged again (points $A_1, B_1, C_1$ - see Fig. 2) and decided to repeat this maneuver - already in half a minute; then - in a quarter of a minute, etc.
Where and when will they meet?
b) Inside a sphere of radius 1 km there are a million points numbered from 1 to a million. Every second, simultaneously, each point moves to the next one by number at $1/3$ distance to this point; the last point moves towards the first in the same way. Prove that after some time all the points will converge inside a certain sphere of radius 1 mm.

F. Shleifer

M605. On the plane there are marks $2n+1$ of different points. Let us number them with the numbers 1,2,...,2n+1 and the distance of the next transformation of the $R$ plane: first, symmetry is made relative to the first point, then - relative to the second, etc. - up to the $(2n+1)$th point.
a) Prove that this transformation $R$ has a single “fixed point” (a point that maps into itself).
