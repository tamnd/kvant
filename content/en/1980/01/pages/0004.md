V. Leshkovtsev

Physics
at Moscow State University

It is not without reason that Moscow State University (MSU) bears the name of the great Russian scientist Mikhail Vasilyevich Lomonosov. The university was created 225 years ago on his advice and design. “My main reason,” Lomonosov wrote about this project, “is that the plan should serve for all future years.” There was this hope - the university arose and soon became one of the main charms of Russian education and science. Above is the building of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University on the Lenin Hills.

Lomonosov did not take any active part in the activities of Moscow University. He had no students in the field of physics, so he practically could not have any influence on the development of physics at Moscow State University.

Moscow University was formed by decree of Empress Elizabeth Petrovna on January 12, 1755. At first there were only three faculties: physics, law and medicine; In the last course I took a physics course. Only in 1804 was the Faculty of Physical and Mathematical Sciences formed. Life itself (the development of production, the needs of the army) began to make demands on the training of specialists in these fields of science.

Even Peter I, striving for the development of domestic science, pointed out the need not only to teach science, but also to “produce it.” But while foreign scientists were invited to the Academy of Sciences (Euler, Bernoulli, Lenz and others), there was nothing of the kind for Moscow University.
