This means that $l(t_1 + t_2) = l(t_1)l(t_2)$. This is how the function indicator first appeared in science.

We have $l(x) = e^x$, where $e = l(1)$, that is, this is the distance from 0 at the moment $t = 1$. Using the fact that $e$ is the distance from 0 at time $t = 1$, and the fact that $v = l$, it is not difficult to show that $e > 2$ (prove it!). Actually $e = 2,71828$...; $e$ became known as the Napier number. By considering the movements occurring according to the law $v(t) = ks(t)$, it is possible to obtain exponential functions with other bases.

For any positive $a$ the time $t$ for which $s(t) = a$, Napier called the (natural) logarithm $a$ (denoted $\ln a$). Due to the above $\ln ab = \ln a + \ln b$. Napier compiled tables of logarithms for twenty years, and in 1614 “Description of an amazing table of logarithms” was published, the foreword to which contained excuses for inevitable errors and ended with the words: “Nothing was perfect at first.”

Napier's discovery is remarkable not only because he created tables of logarithms, but also because he showed that new functions can emerge from the study of motion. Beginning with these works of Galileo and Napier, mechanics became a constructed source of new functions and curves for mathematics.
