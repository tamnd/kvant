⟦folio 44⟧

Composition of axial symmetries with redistribution of axes - central symmetry:

$$S_b \circ S_a = S_a \circ S_b = Z_P,$$

if $a \perp b$ and $P = a \cap b$.

Instead of proof they made figure.

And for the case $a \parallel b$ the guys wrote:

Composition of two axial symmetries with parallel axes - parallel translation:

$$S_b \circ S_a = 2AB,$$

if $a \parallel b$, $A \in a$, $B \in b$, $(AB) \perp a$.

Proof. $S_b \circ S_a$ $\overset{(2)}{=} S_b \circ \circ (E \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(9)}{=} S_b \circ ((S_c \circ S_c) \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(1)}{=} (S_b \circ \circ S_c) \circ (S_c \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(10)}{=} Z_B \circ Z_A$ $\overset{(6)}{=} 2AB$, where $(AB) = c$.

“You see,” Styopa triumphed, “we count, we count, we get the answer!” And our answers - equalities - are good because they can be read in both directions. The last one, for example, can be read like this:

Any parallel transfer can be represented as a composition of two axial symmetries, the axes of which are perpendicular to the direction of transfer and are absent from each other by half of its length.

The guys returned to the problem "$S_b \circ S_a = ?$" in the general case when $a$ intersects $b$. New entries have appeared, and instead of proof - figure:

Composition of two axial symmetries with intersecting axes - rotation:

$$S_b \circ S_a = R_P^{2\alpha},$$

ede $P = a \cap b$ and $a - \text{yeol}^*$ between $a$ and $b$.

Styopa announced that this correspondence can also be “read in the other direction”: the rotation can be represented as a composition of two axial symmetries, the axes of which intersect at the center of the rotation and the uelel between them is equal to half the uelel of the rotation.

Then the guys took turn $(R_P^\alpha)$; The following entries appeared in the trash:

$$R_P^\alpha \circ R_P^{-\alpha} = E,$$

$$R_P^\alpha \circ R_P^\beta = R_P^{\alpha + \beta}.$$

Composition of two turns - turn:

$$R_Q^\beta \circ R_P^\alpha = R_T^{\alpha + \beta},$$

if** $T$ is the point of intersection of lines $c = R_P^{\alpha/2}(a)$ and $b = R_Q^{\beta/2}(c)$, then $c = (PQ)$.

Proof. $R_Q^\beta \circ R_P^\alpha$ $\overset{(1,2)}{=} (S_b \circ S_c) \circ (S_c \circ S_a)$ $\overset{(1)}{=} S_b \circ (S_c \circ S_c) \circ S_a$ $\overset{(9)}{=} S_b \circ S_a = R_T^{2(\alpha/2 + \beta/2)} = R_T^{\alpha + \beta}$.

*) The guys forgot to write that the smallest angle between $a$ and $b$ is taken.

**) The guys started to demand $\alpha > 0$, $\beta > 0$ and $\alpha + \beta < 2\pi$.
