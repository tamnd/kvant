And each movement has a figurative $P$, $P^{-1}$ is indicated; for him
$$P \circ P^{-1} = P^{-1} \circ P = E.$$ (3)

“Everything is clear,” said Oleg, “but why didn’t you mention the simplest property?”

And Oleg wrote $Q \circ P = P \circ Q$ on the draft.

“Oh no, this is not always true for moving,” said Styopa. In pure terradi the following entry appeared with figure:
Example. $S_a \circ S_b \neq S_b \circ S_a$.

In the example, Oleg quickly figured it out (and you?); Styopa wrote two more lines in a blank note:
$$P = Q \Leftrightarrow T \circ P = T \circ Q,$$
$$P = Q \Leftrightarrow P \circ T = Q \circ T,$$
providing them with the following comment:
— Like in algebra: both sides of an equality can be “multiplied” by the same displacement, and can also be reduced. Only on the same side. Like this: $P \circ T = T \circ Q \Rightarrow P = Q$ cannot be done!
- Why? - Oleg asked, but immediately answered himself. - If $T \circ Q \neq Q \circ T$, as in the example, then it turns out to be nonsense.

Then Styopa decided to check what movements Oleg still knows. When he squeezed the words “central symmetry” out of Oleg, the guys remembered the designation $Z_Q$ (for symmetry with center $Q$), and Styopa wrote $Z_Q \circ Z_Q = ?$ Oleg, after thinking a little, erased the question mark, and the following entry appeared in the terrada:
$$Z_Q \circ Z_Q = E.$$ (5)

Here Styopa proposed a more complex question: $Z_P \circ Z_Q = ?$ When the guys figured it out in the draft, Oleg carefully wrote down:
Composition of two central symmetries - transfer:
$$Z_P \circ Z_Q = 2Q \vec{P}.$$ (6)

Under figure

Styopa added proof:
$$\overrightarrow{A A}' = \overrightarrow{A Q} + \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{P A}' = \overrightarrow{Q A}' + \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{A' P} = \overrightarrow{Q P} + \overrightarrow{Q P} = 2Q \vec{P}.$$
You probably guessed Styopa’s next question: $Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = ?$ With Styopa’s hint, Oleg solved this problem; he wrote the result like this:
The composition of three central symmetries is also a central symmetry:
$$Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_S,$$ (7)
ede $S$ is such a point that
$$\overrightarrow{PS} = \overrightarrow{QR}.$$ (8)

Proof.
$$Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = Z_P \circ Z_Q \circ Z_R = E \circ Z_S = Z_S.$$
“You and I will continue to put numbers under the signs = and => so as not to forget where we got what,” said Styopa.
