9. Whirlwind in a cup of coffee

Gently swirl the spoon in the cup of hot coffee until the coffee begins to swirl evenly. Now carefully pour cold milk into the center of the cup in thin shavings. You will see a small vortex forming there. You may also notice a small indentation in the middle. If you jelly hot milk into coffee, there will be no whirlwind. Why does a vortex appear in the first case and not in the second?

10. “Interaction” of sinking objects

When several objects are simultaneously immersed in a viscous liquid, such as an oil or sugar solution, a great deal of interaction can occur between them.

Let's give examples.

1) Lower the two cylinders (Fig. 7) one after the other into the viscous liquid. At certain values ​​of fluid viscosity, cylinder sizes and speeds, the second cylinder can catch up with the first and go around it so that they are located at the same level. Then they will begin to rotate and, plunging, will disperse in different directions.

2) Drop one disk into the liquid, followed by two more. You will see that the latter will catch up with the former and all three will form a stable butterfly configuration (Fig. 8).

3) Now throw several balls (three - six) into the viscous liquid in a pile. They will diverge in such a way that they end up at the vertices of a horizontal regular polygon, which will increase in size as it descends.

How can this be explained?
