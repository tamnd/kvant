1. Determination of the efficiency of an inclined plane

Task 1. Suggest a method for measuring the efficiency of an inclined plane using a dianamometer and a ruler.

Task 2. Experimentally study the dependence of the efficiency of an inclined plane on its height and draw a graph of this dependence.

Task 3. Compare your experimental dependence of the efficiency of an inclined plane on its height with the theoretical one.

Given: inclined plane, block, diamometer, measuring ruler, graph paper.

In this problem, the efficiency η is determined by the formula

$$\eta = \frac{mgh}{|F|l}$$

A diamometer is used to measure the force model $\vec{F}$, with which it is necessary to uniformly pull a block of mass $m$ along an inclined plane of length $l$ at different heights of the plane $h$.

To determine the theoretical dependence of efficiency on height by distance figure 1. For $|F|$ and $\eta$ we obtain the corresponding

$$|\vec{F}| = mg(\mu \cos \alpha + \sin \alpha),$$

$$\eta = \frac{1}{\mu \text{ctg} \alpha + 1} = \frac{1}{\mu \sqrt{l^2/h^2} - 1} + 1.$$

The coefficient of friction $\mu$ can be found by measuring $|\vec{F}|$ at $h = 0$: $\mu = |\vec{F}|/mg$. figure 2 shows a characteristic of the dependence of the efficiency of an inclined plane on its height ($l = 0,5$ m, $\mu = 0,3$).

2. Determination of liquid density

Determine the density of this liquid.

Given: a vessel with an unknown liquid, a vessel with water, a measuring ruler, two metal bars, a lever.

Moreover. The density of water is assumed to be equal to $\rho_B = 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$.

Three measurements will be taken to establish the balance of the lever, when one block is alternately in the air, in water, in an unknown liquid, and the other block is always in the air. In this case, it is reasonable to leave the length of the lever with the first group unchanged, and the length of the lever with the second group is corresponding.
