How many options?

Ah, those problems that begin with the words “In how many ways can you...”! Teachers know what kind of confusion schoolchildren sometimes offer as a solution to such problems. Currently, this topic (combinatorics) remains only an elective. The proposed material was developed during an elective course in mathematics for grade IX, but, in our opinion, it will be interesting and useful not only for its students. In the first part of the article published below, problems solved using the “product rule” will be developed; in the second part (which we will publish in the next issue) the solution methods will be sorted out.

Search scheme

Let's imagine that we are in the dining room and decide to choose a three-course lunch. We look at the menu and see in the list of first courses, soup and shea, in the list of second courses - goulash, cutlets, pancakes and fish, and finally, for the third we are offered fruit juice or tea. All the possibilities presented to us can be conveniently represented by the following diagram (see figure).

This diagram shows all the lunch options. When the lines of the diagram correspond to the fact that the choice of lunch is carried out in three steps. In the first step, we choose the first dish (the three options available to us are indicated by the letters B, S, W). Regardless of the decision we made in the first step, we have four options for choosing the second dish. In the diagram, this independence is expressed in the fact that from the letters of the first line there are equal numbers of arrows in the second line (namely, four arrows each). Regardless of what dishes we chose in the first two steps, we have two options for choosing the third dish, and in the diagram, from each letter of the second line there are two arrows leading to the third line. Each lunch option corresponds to a path in the diagram that follows the arrows from the top line to the bottom. So, for example, let С→K→M correspond to the lunch “soup - cutlets - fruit drink”, and let Ш→K→Ч correspond to the lunch “shi - cutlets - tea”.

Using the diagram, it is easy to calculate the number of all possible options for choosing lunch - it is equal to the number of paths from the top line to the bottom or, which is the same, the number of letters in the bottom line. Since in the first line there are 3 bkuva, in the second evening there are more - 3 4, and in the third there are twice as many as in the second - 3 4 2 = 24, there are 24 possible lunch options in total.
