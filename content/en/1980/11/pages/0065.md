2. According to the rules of the game, any pair of ships must be at least one square apart from each other. Let's surround each ship with each half-field width (Fig. 3); the resulting rectangle is called an extended ship. Let's find the area of ​​the seven expanded ships that we need to sink. The area of ​​the expanded catarrh is 4 cells (2×2), the area of ​​the espressor is 6 cells (3×2), the cresser is 8 cells (4×2). The total area is 36 cells. The area of ​​the expanded board is also 36 squares, that is, the expanded ships completely cover it. It follows from this that the corner areas of the 5x5 board are necessarily occupied by ships (otherwise the corner area of ​​the expanded board “disappears”). Now it's easy to iterate through all possible expanded ships. There are only five of them (Fig. 4, a) - e); we do not take into account rotations and reflections of the board).

The analysis allows us to effectively complete the game. First, we will fire four shots at the corners of our 5x5 board. As we know, they all achieve their goals. If at the same time three Cathars are replenished (Fig. 4, a), then the location of the remaining ships is determined uniquely. Let only one Qatar be sunk. Having oriented the board so that this katar stands on the a1 square, fire the fifth and sixth shots on the a3 and b1 squares. The results of these two shots determine which of the cases - “b” or “c” - takes place. If shots in the corners lead to the sinking of two katars, then, oriented the board so that these katars stand on the vertical a, fire the fifth and sixth shots again on the fields a3 and b1. Based on the results of these shots, you will honor which of the cases - "e" or "d" - was chosen by the enemy.
