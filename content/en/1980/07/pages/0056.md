Knight's move

The knight is the most unteachable and amazing chess piece, and it is no coincidence that the expression “knight’s move” has long become a popular phrase. “The despicable jester of the chessboard” - this is what the famous inventor of mathematical and chess puzzles G. Dudeney called the horse. And Grandmaster S. Tartakover, weakened by his wit, once remarked that “the entire chess game is one disguised knight move.”

On our chess pages we will first encounter various tasks and combinations on the chessboard, the main participant of which is the knight. However, one of the oldest chess combinations is called “stolen checkmate”. The final chord in such a combination always belongs to the horse. An interesting example of a stale mat is one of Blata's problems (see «Kvant», 1980, No. 5). Let us now present an episode from P. Morphy's game.

Morphy - Brain

Everything is ready for the combination, all that remains is to remove the queen from the e7 square.

1. e5! Qg5 2. h4! Qg4 3. Qa3+ Kg8 4. Ke7+ Kpf8 5. Kg6+ Kg8 6. Qf8+! Rf:f8 7. Ke7×. The knight declares checkmate, and all the retreat fields for the enemy king are occupied by their own pieces and pawns.
