⟦folio none⟧

A chess competition opens in this issue of the magazine. You will be offered various chess problems, ethos, combinations and puzzles. We will strive to ensure that the tasks and positions of the competition serve as a complement and illustration to the materials of the “Chess Page”, published in the same issue of the magazine.
This year the chess competition «Kvant» will consist of six rounds.
The results of the competition will be announced in May 1981. The winners of the competition will be awarded chess and mathematical literature authored by world chess champion Anastoly Karpov.
Readers of the magazine who bring their own interests to problems with chess-mathematical additions will receive additional points.
The solution to the competition tasks should be sent in one copy, in a separate envelope, to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka 21/16, «Kvant».
The envelope must be marked “Chess competition No. 7-80”.
The deadline for sending responses to the competition tasks of this issue is August 31, 1980.

MAIN CHARACTER-HORSE

Problem 1 is a position from A. Karpov’s game, played by him and Zsoldos in 1973 in Hungary. The effective blow carried out here by the future world champion immediately decided the outcome of the fight.
Find him.
Problem 2, compiled almost a hundred years ago, in 1885, belongs to the famous German problem solver W. Schinkman. In No. 2 for 1980, we cited one interesting Shinkman puzzle related to the rearrangement of pieces on the board. This problem is a real chess piece, so to speak, without “tricks”.
Problem 3 of V. Korolkova is not entirely simple. Checkmate in one move here can be
bet both a white and a black knight. The task is to find out whose turn it is. If white and black have made equal numbers of moves, then white checkmate. If White made a move more, then, obviously, his king gets checkmated. You will have to make the appropriate calculations.
The problem of a knight traveling across all squares of a chessboard is a classic one in both mathematics and chess. The community of literature is associated with it (see, for example, «Kvant», 1971, No. 9). In problem 4, which we invite you to solve, the board is quite unusual; Before you go around her, think about whether this can be done.

1. White starts and wins.
2. Checkmate in 3 moves.
3. Checkmate in 1 move.
4. Is it possible for a knight to go around all the squares of this board by visiting each of them once?
