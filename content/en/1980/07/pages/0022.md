Let us now consider the process of colliding two eggs. We will assume that the eggs are exactly the same - both in size and shape, and their shells are “designed” for the same maximum load $T$. The eggs collide with different ends - one blunt, the other sharp.

According to Newton's 111th law, the forces acting during the collision from one egg to another are equal in absolute magnitude and directed in opposite directions. Let us imagine the forces $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ as the sum of forces $\vec{f}_1, \vec{f}_1'$ and $\vec{f}_2, \vec{f}_2'$, respectively directed along tangents to the contacting surfaces of the eggs. The absolute magnitude of the force $\vec{f}$ depends on the angle between the tangents: $f = \frac{F}{2} \cos \frac{\alpha}{2}$. Thus, the greater the angle between the tangents, the greater the “tearing” forces generated during the collision. So, in fact, it is more advantageous to level by holding the egg with the sharp end towards the enemy. There is another reason for this strategy: in the egg there is an “air sac” at the blunt end, and because of this the blunt end is less durable (try to explain this fact).
