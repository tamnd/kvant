Fabrikant figuratively said, “interference translates the phase correspondences of light waves into the language of intensities, which makes it possible to record them on a photographic plate.”
Interference analysis allowed Gabor to prove that the amplitude and phase of a monochromatic wave scattered by an object can be detected photographically if this wave integrates with a reference monochromatic wave of the same particle. A photographic plate with an interference pattern recorded on it is called a hologram. Literally translated from Greek, this means “full recording” - a recording of the spatial structure of the wave reflected by an object.
A light wave scattered by any object can be considered as a set of secondary waves, the sources of which are individual points of this object. Consequently, in order to form a hologram of a complex object, it is necessary to understand in detail how a process hologram is obtained - a hologram of a point.

At the points of intersection of solid circles and dashed lines, as well as dashed circles and solid lines, the conditions for interference minima are met:
$$\Delta \varphi = \pm \pi, \pm 3\pi, \pm 5\pi \dots \Rightarrow$$
$$\Rightarrow X_p^2 = X_1^2 + X_2^2 - 2X_1X_2 = (X_1 - X_2)^2 \Rightarrow$$
$$\Rightarrow X_p = X_1 - X_2.$$

The blue lines on figure 3 connecting these points are the set of points for which the minimum conditions are satisfied.
