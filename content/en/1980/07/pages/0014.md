The learned expression for the radii of the interference pattern rings coincides with the expression for the radii of the rings of the so-called Fresnel zone plate (zone lattice), well known in physics for more than 150 years*).

So, the simplest geographer - the geographer of a point - is a Fresnel zone plate. (An enlarged photo of the zone plate is presented on figure 4.) The only difference between the geography of the point and the classical zone plate is the smooth transition from dark to light rings.

Reconstruction of a spherical wave using the simplest geographer - a zone plate

To reconstruct an image using geography, Gabor proposed illuminating the geography with a reference wave.

*) You will become acquainted with the properties of zone plates by reading the article by O. Kabardin and N. Schaefer, published in «Kvant» No. 1 for 1979.

Let's consider the process of restoring the image of a point using a zone plate. Let us illuminate the plate with the same plane wave (the same length) as the reference one, with the help of which the geography of the point was obtained. In this case, we will position the plate so that it is parallel to the front of the plane wave (figure 5).

Any small section of the zone plate can be considered as a diffraction grating. Select a narrow strip on the plate. It is a lattice, the period of which is not constant - it is equal to $d_k = \Delta r_k = r_{k+1} - r_k$ and, as can be seen from expression (*), decreases with distance from the center of the plate to the periphery. Let's consider a small section of the lattice. Since $r_k$ changes slowly with increasing $k$, we can assume that in a small section of the grating its period is constant and equal to approximately constant value $d = \Delta r_k$ - the distance between two adjacent light stripes (that is, the radial distance between two adjacent rings).
