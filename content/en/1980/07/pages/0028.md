**M577. What is the smallest number of pieces that need to be placed on the squares of a chessboard with dimensions**

a) $8 \times 8$ cells.
b) $n \times n$ cells so that on every straight line passing through the center of an arbitrary field and parallel to some side or diagonal of the board, there is at least one piece? (Chips are placed in the centers of the fields.)

Answer: $2n$ chips when $n$ is even (in particular, 16 when $n=8$), and $2n+1$ when $n$ is odd.

One of the possible placements of chips is shown in Figures 1 and 2. Let us prove that a smaller number of chips can be done without cost.

Let's start with the more difficult case of odd $n$ (Fig. 2). Obviously, on each of the red segments, parallel to the diagonals and located in a non-gonal square, there should be a chip; these segments $2n-2$. There should also be a chip on each of the blue segments. We will show that for this you will have to add at least three more chips; Indeed, in order to “cover” all four sides of the blue square with chips, they would have to be placed at the opposite vertices of this square, but then the diagonal of the square would remain free.

The case of even $n$ is now very clear: here we simply specify $2n$ of disjoint segments parallel to the diagonals (Fig. 1).

We invite readers to find out how many different arrangements there are of the smallest number of chips that satisfy the conditions (for different $n$), and generalize the problem to the case of rectangular boards of size $m \times n$. It would also be interesting to study various spatial analogues of this problem (the questions that arise here are apparently very difficult; see the article “Arrangement of cubes” - «Kvant», 1972, No. 4).
