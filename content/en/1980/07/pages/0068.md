Like many works of the Dutch artist Escher, this engraving most of all generates the imagination of mathematicians and physicists. Looking at it, we immediately want to ask: how did the artist “see” the spherical convex pieces of a fairy-tale city? A physicist who loves optics will probably offer this answer - the author looked at his city through a glass plate, flat parallel at the edges and thick in the center (like a lens, but not spherical, but with variable curvature). A geometer may think that the geometry of space, before the artist’s eyes, “went crazy” and was turning from Euclidean at the edges to spherical in the center. And a TOPOLOGIST mathematician will probably say that the picture was drawn correctly on a thin rubber film, and the cunning author approached it from behind and began to inflate it like a soap bubble. What do you think of this page engraving by Escher?
