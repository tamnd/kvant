Conclusion

In conclusion of the article, I would like to answer the following question: why was holography - in principle a simple method of Fotoeplicity of an interference pattern formed by two coherent points - created only very recently? Indeed, interference and diffraction were well studied back in the 19th century; photography was invented in the mid-19th century; so Gabor did not need knowledge of any new achievements of science to open the holographic. However, it took a deep understanding of the phenomena of interference and diffraction and, of course, an inventive mind to combine all the known elements of holography into one bright idea. The example of the creation of holography has been well used, that in our morphologie new and valuable ideas and discoveries are born on the basis of old, well-known concepts.

In addition, it must be emphasized that along with the principles of difficulties in the development of holography, there are also purely technical ones. Further, in 1947, Gabor's idea was still premature, since sources of monochromatic corrected light were needed to produce high-quality holograms. Such sources—lasers—appeared only in 1960. To create high-quality holograms, high-resolution photographic materials are required. As we have seen, when obtaining volumetric holograms, the distances between parabolic mirrors inside the emulsion layer turn out to be on the order of the wavelength. It is this value that should determine the resolution of the emulsion. For visible light $\lambda \sim 10^{-6}$ m; this means that on one millimeter of photoplastic material $\sim 10^3$ lines should be resolved (“see” separated). Such materials have been created only in recent years. A large number of technical difficulties are currently facing the creators of holographic cinema and television. But a lot has already been done to overcome these difficulties.
