1980 is the year of the XXII Olympic Games in Moscow. It is not surprising that not only Muscovites, but also residents of all cities and villages of our country are already accustomed to seeing the Olympics emblem on the pages of newspapers and magazines, on posters and souvenirs - five interlocking rings, symbolizing the five inhabited continents of the globe.

Let's take a closer look at this laconic emblem (Fig. 1): three rings on top, two on the bottom - that seems to be all. But look at figure 2. Here again there are five interlocking rings - three on top, two on bottom, but something is wrong. What exactly?

These rings are linked in a more complex way - the figure they form resembles part of the mail of an ancient warrior, while the rings of the Olympic emblem are linked in a common chain.

Of course, five rings can be linked and not only in the same way as in Figures 1 and 2.

The first engagement can be easily obtained from the rings of the Olympic emblem by interlocking the end rings of the chain, and the second can be obtained by interlocking a third with each of them, then with each of the three the fourth, and finally, with each of the four - the fifth ring.

Is it possible to link five rings so that each one is aligned with the others? Try, however, I must warn you that all your attempts will be unsuccessful. Why? Here's why.

Let us assume that such a coupling has been achieved. Let's tie a label with the number 1 to some ring; This ring is also coupled with the tray rings and is not cohesively coupled with one. Let's attach a label with number 2 to this ring. In turn, the ring with number 2 must be linked to all other rings, except for the ring with number 1. Let's take one of the rings that have not yet been proven and attach a label with number 3 to it - as we know, it is linked to rings No. 1 and No. 2, so it must be linked to one of the not yet proven rings. propoved rings (to which we give number 4) and not linked to the last ring (to which we give number 5). Let's look at rings No. 4 and No. 5. If they are interlocked, then ring No. 4 is interlocked with all four remaining rings, and we assumed that each ring is interlocked with three others. This means that rings No. 4 and No. 5 are not interlocked, but in this case, ring No. 5 will not be interlocked with two rings: No. 3 and No. 4, but only interlocked with two, which again contradicts the assumption. Thus, our assumption about the possibility of engaging the heels of the rings so that each was engaged in alignment with the tray turned out to be incorrect.

And on figure 5 the perfect amazing clutch of three rings is depicted. No two of them are linked to each other, but try to sing them - you won’t succeed. However, if you cut any of these rings, then all the rings turn out to be Borromean rings. (The three holey planes on the first page of the cover of this issue of the magazine are linked in the same way. Look closely - no two of them are linked, and it is impossible to separate them.)
