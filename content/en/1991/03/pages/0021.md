They have a thin membrane that can withstand a pressure difference of $\Delta p=1000$ Pa. In the left part of the vessel (it makes up 1/3 of the volume of the vessel) the heater is turned on. Due to the thermal conductivity of the membrane, heat flows to the right side of the vessel. It is known that with a temperature difference of $\Delta T=1$ K, an amount of thermal conductivity $\Delta Q=0,2$ J flows in one second. At what maximum power of the heater does the membrane remain intact during prolonged heating?

R. Aleksadrov

F1280. In a box with three pins $A$, $B$ and $B$ there is an unknown circuit consisting of resistors. Using an ohmmeter, the resistances between the various terminals are changed: $R_{AB}=10$ Ohm, $R_{BB}=20$ Ohm, $R_{AB}=30$ Ohm. A battery with a voltage of $U=1,5$ V is connected to points $A$ and $B$, and an ammeter with a resistance of $r=5$ Ohm is connected between $B$ and $B$. What will it show?

A. Sashin

F1281. The circuit shown in figure is assembled from a heel of identical capacitors and a coil. Find the maximum current through the coils after connecting a battery with voltage $U_0$. Also find the maximum voltage across capacitors connected in parallel. The resistance of the wires is considered small.

A. Zilberman

F1282. A cube with an edge of $a=10$ cm and a mass of $M=1$ kg is suspended externally with a rigidity of $k=400$ N/m so that its base is parallel to the ground. A flow of control balls is directed from below onto the cube, with a speed of $v_0=20$ m/s at the height of the first initial position of the base of the cube. The cube begins to oscillate, moving progressively. Find the period of these oscillations. Estimate the time during which the amplitude of the oscillations will decrease by 2 times. The mass of each ball is $m=1$ g, the concentration of balls in the flow is $n=1000$ m$^{-3}$, neglect the distances between the balls.

I. Margin

M1246. Prove that in any arithmetic progression whose members are natural numbers there are two numbers with the same sum of digits.

Let $a$ be the first term and $d$ the difference of the progression. We can, of course, assume that $d>0$. Then all terms of a progression of the form $a+10^n d$, where $n$ is large enough (so $10^n>a$), have the same sum of digits, equal to the sum of all digits $a$ and $d$.

S. Genkin
