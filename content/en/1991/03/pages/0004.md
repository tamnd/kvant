COMPUTING
NO COMPUTING

Academician A. MIGDAL

If mathematics is the art of avoiding calculations, then theoretical physics is the art of calculating without mathematics.

The statement given as an epigraph arose as a result of disputes between physicists and mathematicians and needs clarification. Further, the simplest calculations in physics cannot be done without mathematics. However, at the first, most important stage of the work of a theoretical physicist, when the physical picture of the phenomenon is established, mathematics recedes into the background and plays only an auxiliary role. But before talking about the connection between physics and mathematics, you should know what theoretical physics is.

I. HOW DO PHYSICISTS WORK?

Experimenters and theorists

There are two types of physicists - experimenters and theorists, and these two professions are almost never combined in one person. Experimental physicists study the correspondence between physical quantities, or, more solemnly speaking, discover the laws of nature, use experimental setups, that is, produce changes in physical quantities using devices. To do this, you need to deeply understand the phenomena being studied (in order to know what and how to change). Theoretical physicists study nature using only paper and pencil, that is, inferential correspondences between observable quantities, relying on previously found experimental and theoretical laws of nature. The reason for the separation of these two professions is not only that each of them requires its own special knowledge: knowledge of measurement methods in one case and mastery of mathematical apparatus in the other. The main reason is that these professions correspond to different characteristics of thinking and different forms of intuition. Intuition, i.e. the ability to subconsciously find the right path, plays a crucial role, especially in the first stages of work. Unfortunately, the gift of intuition does not arise immediately, but as a result of hard work, as a reward for solving many scientific problems.

Since theoretical physics deals with more abstract concepts than experimental physics, theoretical physics requires a more abstract form of intuition, sometimes close to the intuition of mathematics. In the past century, when physics was not yet so specialized, many physicists combined both professions. Thus, Maxwell, who theoretically obtained equations that describe all electromagnetic phenomena, was also involved in experiments. Hertz, who discovered experimental electromagnetic waves, was also a good theoretician. And yet, in each it is powerfully indicated which of the professions is the main one: for Maxwell it is theoretical physics, and for Hertz it is experimental physics.
