Our system is an oscillator; the role of the oscillator coordinate is played by the charge $Q$, the potential energy of the oscillator is the energy of the capacitor, and the kinetic energy is the energy of the magnetic field of the coil. From the energy recording it is clear that the rigidity of this oscillator is $1/C$, and the role of mass is played by inductance $L$. Can immediately write down the vibration frequency: $\omega = \sqrt{1/LC}$.

Such an oscillator is called an electric oscillator circuit.

How to guess the solution?

Let us give an example of how the neoctoral features of a solution are awakened before the apparatus for an exact solution of the problem is built, before the equations on the basis of which the problem will be solved are found. This is backwards and an example of a more complex dimensional analysis than in the case of an oscillator.

One of the most difficult and unresolved problems in theoretical physics is the connection between gravitational and electromagnetic phenomena.

If such a connection exists, then as a result of solving some not yet found equations, a dimensionless number will be obtained*), giving a correspondence between the gravitational constant $\gamma$ and quantities characterizing electromagnetic phenomena, such as the speed of light $c$, the charge of the electron $e$ and its mass $m$.

If quantum phenomena are significant, then Planck’s constant $h$ may also enter into the problem. Knowing the dimensions of the quantity $\gamma, c, e, m, h$, it is easy to verify that from these quantities only two independent dimensionless combinations can be made:

$$\alpha = e^2/\hbar c; \xi = \hbar c/\gamma m^2$$

(recall that $\hbar = h/2\pi$).

The first of these combinations characterizes the interaction of an electron with an electromagnetic field (dimensionless combinations).

Dimensionless numbers that arise as a result of a physical problem, as we have already said, are of the order of several units or fractions of one. III. ULTIMATE SIMPLIFICATION

The main idea of ​​quantum mechanics Let's try to show with another example how, by simplifying the problem to the limit, we can determine the main features of the phenomenon. This example will explain what a qualitative approach to a problem is.

According to quantum mechanics, the energy of an electron in an atom can only take discrete values.

Possible values ​​of electron energies in a hydrogen atom are given by the expression*

$$E_n = - \frac{me^4}{\hbar^2} \frac{1}{2n^2}.$$

The differences in the values ​​of $E_n$ for two different $n (n=1, 2, 3, \dots)$ are determined with greater accuracy in the frequencies of the spectral lines observed in the experiment.

The basic idea of ​​quantum mechanics is that each part (in this case, the electron) is characterized by a wave process.

*) Physicists, as a rule, do not use the SI system. (And ater.)
