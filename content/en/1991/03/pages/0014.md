1. The number of such generalized mean trees formed by systems of closed rivers on the projective plane at the intersection with an infinitely distant highway is the well-known Catalan numbers 1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42,...

The Catalan number $c(n)$ was simply defined as the number of ways to distribute brackets in a product of $n$ components. For example, $c(3) = 2$. The two ways to distribute parentheses are $(ab)c$ and $(bc)c$. In the product of four components, brackets can be arranged in five ways:

$((ab)c)d, (ab)cd, (a(bc))d,$
$a((bc)d), a(b(cd))$.

Pozny $c(4) = 5$, etc.

Catalan numbers have the remarkable property of appearing unexpectedly in a variety of problems. More information about this can be found in the article by M. Gardner “Catalan Numbers” («Kvant», 1978, No. 7, p. 20).
