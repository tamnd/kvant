New word - gradient. In the formulas $F_x = -\Delta E_p / \Delta x$, $F_y = -\Delta E_p / \Delta y$ and $F_z = -\Delta E_p / \Delta z$ it follows that the quantities $\Delta E_p / \Delta x$, $\Delta E_p / \Delta y$ and $\Delta E_p / \Delta z$ must also be projections of some vector, although the potential energy $E_p$ is a scalar quantity. What kind of vector is this?

Let's carry out the following purely mathematical operation. Let us mentally direct along the three coordinate axes $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ three vectors, modulo equal to units (they are called unit vectors); let's denote them $\vec{i}$, $\vec{j}$ and $\vec{k}$ (Fig. 3). By multiplying the projections of the vector $\vec{F}$ by the corresponding unit vectors, we obtain three vector quantities $-F_x \vec{i}$, $F \vec{j}$ and $F_z \vec{k}$, they are called components of the vector $\vec{F}$ along the coordinate axes. The vector $\vec{F}$ is expressed through its components as follows:

$$\vec{F} = F_x \vec{i} + F_y \vec{j} + F_z \vec{k}.$$

No can also be written like this:

$$\vec{F} = -\left( \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta x} \vec{i} + \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta y} \vec{j} + \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta z} \vec{k} \right).$$

The quantity in parentheses on the right side of the equation is a vector. It is called the potential energy gradient and is denoted by the symbol grad, so

$$\vec{F} = -\text{grad} E_p.$$ (5)

This is the connection between two measures of interaction - force and potential energy: force is equal to the potential energy gradient taken with the opposite sign.

How is this country vector directed? From formula (5) it is clear that it is directed towards increasing potential energy. If, for example, we are talking about gravity, then the potential energy increases as the body rises. The vector grad $E_p$ is directed there, vertically upward.

You can, of course, say it differently: the force of the project is directed towards a decrease in potential energy. In the general case, the potential energy gradient vector always indicates the direction of the most intense change in this quantity.

So... We have found a connection between two measures of interaction for the case when it manifests itself in the form of gravitation and elasticity. But there is also a mechanical interaction that manifests itself in the form of friction. For him, the only measure, the only characteristic is strength (friction force). There is no potential energy associated with this force because the friction force does not depend on the coordinates, while potential energy is a function of the coordinates.

Thus, in mechanics both measures of interaction are used - force and potential energy, but force is useful in all cases, but potential energy is not.

Let us note in conclusion that in quantum mechanics, which describes the phenomena of the microworld, the measure of interaction is exclusively potential energy. Forces are not included in the equation of quantum mechanics.

A. Kikopin
