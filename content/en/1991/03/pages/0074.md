10. According to the law of conservation of energy, mechanical work is equal to the difference between the energy consumed by the electric motor from the battery and the thermal energy lost in its winding:
$$A = \mathcal{E}I_1t - I_1^2rt.$$
We will also find the winding resistance $r$ from the law of conservation of energy, but written for the case of complete braking of the armature:
$$0 = \mathcal{E}\dot{I}_2t - I_2^2rt.$$
Thus,
$$A = \mathcal{E}I_1(1 - I_1/I_2)t = 8 \text{ Дж.}$$

11. From the formula for the dependence of mass on speed
$$m = m_0/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$$
we get
$$v/c = \sqrt{(1-(m_0/m)^2) = 0,6 = 60 \%}.$$
12. From the first nuclear reaction
$$x + \frac{27}{13}\text{Al} \rightarrow \frac{27}{12}\text{Mg} + y$$
it follows that the first unknown part has no electric charge. So this is a neutron:
$$x = \frac{1}{0}n.$$
Then from the first reaction we obtain that

$$v/c = \sqrt{(1-(m_0/m)^2) = 0,6 = 60 \%}.$$
13. Let us write down the laws of conservation of energy and momentum for the closed system “l-mezon - two photons” (see Fig. 5):
$$E = E_1 + E_2$$
$$P = P_1 \cos \alpha + P_2 \cos \beta$$
$$0 = P_1 \sin \alpha - P_2 \sin \beta.$$
Considering that $E = mc^2$, $P = mv$, $P_1 = E_1/c$ and $P_2 = E_2/c$, we find the required speed $v$ medon (mass $m$):
$$v = c \sin (\alpha + \beta)/(\sin \alpha + \sin \beta) \approx 2,2 \cdot 10^8 \text{ м/c.}$$
14. Let us denote the change in the internal energy of the hydrogen atom by $\Delta E$. Then for the wavelength of a photon that an atom can emit, returning it to an unexcited state, will be equal to
$$\lambda = hc/\Delta E.$$
Now he will use the laws of conservation of momentum and energy for the process of collision of a hydrogen atom with a proton:
$$mv_0 = mv_1 + Mv$$
$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = \frac{mv_1^2}{2} + \frac{Mv^2}{2} + \Delta E.$$
Since the mass of the proton $m$ is practically equal to the mass of the hydrogen atom $M$, we obtain
$$\Delta E = mv_1(v_0 - v_1),$$
And
$$\lambda = hc/(mv_1(v_0 - v_1)) \approx 1,3 \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ м.}$$
15. The energy released during a given nuclear reaction is equal to the difference between the binding energy of the gel nucleus and the total binding energy of the deuterium and tritium nuclei:
$$E = (2m_p + 2m_n - m_{\text{He}})c^2 - (2m_p + 3m_n - m_{\text{H}} - m_{\text{He}})c^2 = (m_{\text{H}} + m_{\text{H}} - m_{\text{He}} - m_n)c^2 = (2,01355 + 3,01550 - 4,00159 - 1,00866) \times 1,67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 9 \cdot 10^{16}/1,6 \cdot 10^{-13} \text{ МэВ} \approx 17,7 \text{ МэВ.}$$
