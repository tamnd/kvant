$$\Delta E_p = m g h_2 - m g h_1 = - m g (h_1 - h_2).$$
The coordinate $x$ of the body changes by the amounts
$$\Delta x = x_2 - x_1 = - (x_1 - x_2).$$
From figure 1
$$\frac{h_1 - h_2}{x_1 - x_2} = \sin \alpha.$$
Hence,
$$\Delta E_p = - m g (x_1 - x_2) \sin \alpha = m g \Delta x \sin \alpha.$$ (2)
Comparing expressions (2) and (1), we obtain
$$F_x = - \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta x}.$$
This means that the projection of the force vector $\vec{F}$ onto the axis $X$ is equal to the change in potential energy taken with the opposite sign per unit displacement along the axis $X$. An inclined plane is different from a flat one in that for each unit of path along it there is a greater change in potential energy than along a flat one. A round mountain slope is more difficult to climb because each step along it involves a greater change in potential energy than on a gentle slope.
The reader can easily see for himself that for the projection of the force vector onto the $Y$ axis, a similar formula is obtained: $F_y = - \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta y}$. And if, when the body moves, the coordinate $z$ also changes (the body moves not along a plane, but in space), then for the projection $F_z$ one can obtain a similar formula: $F_z = - \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta z}$.

Elastic interaction. Let us now consider the case when a body moves under the action of an elastic force, in the direction of a spring. The upper part of figure 2 shows an undeformed spring with a body attached to it. Let us direct the coordinate axis $X$ along the axis of the spring, and the origin of the connection coordinates with the right end of the undeformed spring.
Now let the spring grow so that the coordinate of its end becomes $x_1$. An elastic force equal to $-kx_1$ acts on the body. Let us assume that under the influence of this force the body moved to position $x_2$, where the elastic force became equal to $-kx_2$. But we will assume that the body is acted upon by a constant force equal to the average value $-k \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}$, so the projection of the force on the axis $X$ is equal to
$$F_x = - k \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}.$$ (3)
What is the potential energy? In the initial position it is equal to $k x_1^2/2$, and in the final position $-k x_2^2/2$. This means that the change in potential energy
$$\Delta F_p = \frac{k}{2} (x_2^2 - x_1^2) = - \frac{k}{2} (x_1 + x_2) (x_1 - x_2).$$ (4)
Comparing equalities (4) and (3), we find, as in the case of gravity,
$$F_x = \frac{\Delta E_p}{x_1 - x_2} = - \frac{\Delta E_p}{\Delta x}.$$
And here the change in potential energy per unit of displacement along the coordinate axis is equal to the projection of the force on this axis. “Strong” spring (with high stiffness $k$)
