As another example, consider the problem of finding the external oscillation period of a small weight. First of all, let's find out what quantities can be included in the expression for the period. Since the forces acting on the bob are gravity and elasticity, it is natural to assume that the period of oscillation depends on the root of free fall $g$ and the mass $m$ of the bob and on the stiffness of the spring $k$. Of course, such quantities as temperature and air humidity will not be included in the problem if we focus on the damping of oscillations. (To simplify the problem, you need to know what you can do with it!) From the values ​​$g$, $m$ and $k$ you can make only one combination that has the dimension of time - $\sqrt{m/k}$. Therefore, the period of $T$ is equal to

$$T = C \sqrt{m/k}.$$

Acceleration $g$ was not included in the response. The dimensionless constante $C$ cannot be found from the dimensional messages; we can only say that it is neither very large nor very small—it is of the order of unity. Indeed, the value of $C$ must be found from the solution of the equation of motion of the weight that we have not written down. And dimensionless coefficients arising from the solution of the equation encountered in physics, as a rule, turn out to be of the order of unity*). An exact calculation gives the value 2π for $C$.

Thus, without calculations, using only dimensional analysis, we found the approximate value of the period of oscillation of the external weight.
