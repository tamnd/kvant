About glaciers, pressure cookers and Carnot's theorem

If you ask a schoolchild why skates glide well on ice on a frosty day, you will most likely hear a simple and very simple answer: “The skate rubs against the ice, a thin water lubricant forms between them, which ensures gliding.” However, to a student more skilled in physics, such an answer will seem too simple and therefore uninteresting. “No,” he will say, “the point here is not friction, but the pressure of the skate on the ice.
