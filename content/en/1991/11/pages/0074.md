8. Since the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on its temperature, the change in internal energy is the same for all three processes. From the first law of thermodynamics it follows that the amount of heat supplied to the gas will be greater, the greater the work done by the gas. In the isochoric process 0-1, the work is zero, and in the other two it is positive, so the least amount of heat is supplied in the first process. To compare the work in the second and third processes, let us note that the work of a gas in an isobaric process
$$A = p\Delta V = vR\Delta T$$
depends only on temperature changes. Therefore, we can replace the isobaric process 0–3 with the isobaric process 4–2 (Fig. 2), where points 4 and 0 lie on the same isotherm. After such a replacement, it becomes obvious that the work in the isobaric process is greater than in the 0-2 process (the area under the segment 4-2 is greater than under the segment 0-2). So, the greatest amount of heat is supplied more in the isobaric process 0-3.

10. The efficiency of an ideal Carnot machine, in which the heating temperature is $T_1$ and the refrigerator temperature is $T_2$, is equal to $(T_1 - T_2)/T_1$. In both cases, the numerator increases (by 1 K), and the value in the first case (when $T_1$ increases) increases, in the second (when $T_2$ decreases) remains unchanged. This means that the efficiency will increase more in the second case.

11. When the mirror is rotated by an angle $\alpha$, the normal to the mirror will rotate by the same angle. Since the incident beam does not change its direction, the angle of incidence will also change by $\alpha$. The reflection angle is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal one; therefore, the reflected beam will rotate through an angle of 2$\alpha$.
