J. Jeans himself was extremely inspired by the result of his research and, above all, by its clarity and simplicity. He wrote: “... it is clear to us why all the stars have very similar weight; this is because they are all formed by the same process. They probably look like factory products made by the same machine.” Knowing today that stars differ in mass by thousands of times, and conditions in the interstellar medium differ even more, we have the right to call Jeans’ optimism premature. Yes, he himself understood that only the first milestone had been passed and, foreseeing the imminent difficulties that the theory of the formation of stars and galaxies would face, he warned: “With the current state of our knowledge, any attempt to discuss final solutions on the main problems of cosmogony would be nothing more than pure ORMATism.” Over the past century, we have learned a lot about the formation of stars and, most importantly, we have become convinced that the theory of gravitational instability of J. Jeans has stood the test of time.

Aerospace Correspondence School
The correspondence aerospace school (ZAKSH), organized by the All-Union Youth Aerospace Society (VAKO) "Soyuz" and the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT), is conducting a competitive enrollment of students in grades 10 and 11 for the 1992-93 academic year.
ZAKSH manuals were prepared by MIPT employees and researchers and are recognized to help everyone who is going to enter universities, especially aerospace. The school program covers the following sections of science and technology:
— Movement of spacecraft
— Rocketry
— Movement in the atmosphere
— Exploring the Earth from space
— Space Research
— Advanced cosmonautics.
During the year, ZAKS students receive:
- six school tasks, including

theoretical material, a list of references for further acquaintance with the areas of aerospace physics and problems for independent solution;
— “Space chronicle of ZAKSH”, a state from the diary of the most significant events in cosmonautics of the current year and information materials on the history and current state of space research.
The full course of study at ZAKSH is 2 years. Students entering 11th grade have the opportunity to graduate from school using a shortened program.
Competitive enrollment at the school is carried out with the aim of enrolling students in a free form of education. Those who do not pass the competition can become students through a paid form of education. In addition, the school distributes the ZAKSH Space Chronicle of the current year to those interested for a small fee. All necessary information on this issue will be provided subject to the admission committee receiving a decision on the admissions task.
