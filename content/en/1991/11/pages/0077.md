7. Let us prove the statement of the problem by indication. For $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ it is obvious. Let $n > 2$ and let for boards of size $m \times m$, where $m < n$, the statement has already been proven. Let us take any set of minors on the board $n \times n$ that satisfies the conditions of the problem. Let's consider pairs of cells that are symmetrical relative to the main diagonal. There are $(n^2 - n)/2$ such pairs. If in each pair at least one cell is covered, then the total number of covered cells is at least $n + (n^2 - n)/2 > n^2/2$, and the statement is true. Let cells $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$ not be covered. By crossing out the $i$th and $j$th horizontal lines and the $i$th and $j$th vertical lines from the board. The board $(n - 2) \times (n - 2)$ will remain. The intersection of each of the minors with this/dockere is a semipermeter minor of at least $n - 2$. It is true that each minor has at most two rows, or two columns, or one row and one column crossed out, since otherwise it would cover one of the cells $(i, j)$ or $(j, i)$. Thus, the diagonal of the board remaining after crossing out $(n - 2) \times (n - 2)$ is covered with minors of a half-permeter no less than $n - 2$, and, therefore, by the assumption of indication, at least half of the non-crossed out cells are covered with minors. Let's now look at the crossed out cells. There are $4n - 4$ in total. The semipermeter of the minor covering cell $(i, i)$ is at least $n$, so it covers at least $n - 1$ cells in the $i$ row and column. Likewise, a minor covering cell $(j, j)$ covers at least $n - 1$ cells in the $j$th row and column. Since cells $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$ are not covered, at least $2(n - 1)$ cells are covered in these four rows and columns, i.e. at least half of the crossed out cells. So, the minors from the set under consideration cover at least half of the squares of the board $n \times n$. The statement has been proven.
