XXV All-round
Olympiad in Physics

A. ZILBERMAN

The final stage of the 1991 All-Out Physical Olympiad was held from April 13 to 23 in the city of Tashkent.

At the theoretical.type, as usual, the active classicists were offered four problems for four hours, and the rest were given five problems for five hours. Here are the conditions of these problems (some of them are published in “Problem «Kvant»”).

Theoretical tour

9th grade

1. At the left edge of the cart, length $L=0,2$ m and mass $M=1$ kg, lies a cube with mass $m=0,3$ kg (Fig. 1). The cube is only given a horizontal speed of $v_0=1$ m/s to the right. Assuming that the cart is motionless at the initial moment, determine at what distance from the left edge of the cart the cube will be located after it stops slipping relative to the cart. The friction coefficient of the cube about to the trolley is $\mu=0,1$. The impacts of the cube on the walls are considered to be absolutely elastic. The cart moves across the table without friction.

2. $M=0,3$ kg of water is poured into a saucepan and a thermometer and a boiler are lowered into it, the power of which can be changed. It turned out that with a power of $P_1=50$ W, water heats up no higher than $t_1=+50$ °C, and with a power of $P_2=100$ W - no higher than $t_2=+65$ °C. How long does it take for this portion of water to heat up from room temperature $t_0=+20$ °C to $t_3=+70$ °C with a heating power of $P_3=200$ W? Estimate the maximum temperature at this power. Specific heat capacity of water $c=4200$ J/(kg deg).

3. The flashlight bulb is connected to a voltage source using long wires. With a wire length of $l_1=10$ m, the current through the light bulb turns out to be equal to $I_1=0,17$ A, with $l_2=20$ m - $I_2=0,13$ A. What will be the current through the light bulb with a wire length of $l_3=40$ m? What will this current become if the light bulb is connected to a source? The internal resistance of the source is negligible. The dependence of the point of a light bulb on its voltage is shown in figure 2.

4. A circle with a radius of $R=10$ m is drawn on the horizontal surface of the ice. In the center of the circle there is a zazz, and the wolf, as you already agreed, is on the circle (Fig. 3). The hare moves in a straight line at a constant speed $v_0=2$ m/s. The wolf must move in a circle so that the distance between him and the hare remains equal to the beginning at all times. To what point on the circle can the wolf reach without breaking the rules of the game? Ice friction coefficient $\mu=0,05$. The wolf moves strictly in a circle, without jumping.

10th grade

1. See problem 4 for 9th grade.

2. A box of length $L=0,2$ m (Fig. 4) lies on a rough inclined plane with friction coefficient $\mu=0,8$, forming an angle $\alpha=30°$ with the horizon. A weight is glued to its upper edge, the mass of which is equal to the mass of the box. There is no friction inside the box. At some points the load comes loose and begins to slide down. How far will the box move? Consider the impacts of the load on the walls of the box to be absolutely elastic.

3. In the vessel under the heating piston there is a certain amount of liquid nitrogen and its vapor at a temperature of $T_1=78$ K - point 1 on the p, V-diagram on figure 5. The piston is slowly moved back, increasing the volume of the vessel at a constant temperature - section 1-2-3 on the diagram. At point 3, the pressure in the vessel becomes $p_1=686$ mm pt. Art. The piston is fixed, and the vessel is cooled to a temperature of $T_2=76$ K, the pressure decreases.
