This photograph clearly shows that dense dark clouds are released less than once from the rarefied gaseous medium that fills the entire small space, which glows under the influence of hot stars embedded in it.

heated gas has elasticity: it is this property of the gas that allows sound waves to propagate in it. Therefore, Jeans will conclude that gravitational rolling can begin only if the gravitational force defeats the force of gas pressure.

To understand under what conditions this occurs, let us make several simple physical estimates. Let's consider what processes occur during a small random rolling of a certain volume of gas with a characteristic size $\lambda$ and density $\rho$. On the one hand, the force of gravity tends to continue this decline. If there were no gas pressure at all, then all the matter in this object would fall towards its center during free fall

$$t_{\pi} \sim (G\rho)^{-1/2},$$

which, as we see, does not depend on the size of the disturbances.

This formula can be understood using Kepler's third law. The fall of any particle to the center of the object occurs under the influence of the force of attraction to the mass $M \sim \rho a^3$, where $a$ is the initial distance from the particle to the center. The fall time represents half the period of movement along a very narrow ellipse, at one of the foci of which the center of the object is located. But, according to Kepler's third law, the period of motion along an ellipse with a major axis $a$ is equal to the period of motion around a mass $M$ in a circular orbit with a radius $a/2$. The period of such circular motion can be easily calculated from Newton's second law.

On the other hand, in the absence of gravity, the force of gas pressure would cause the object to expand in “dynamic time,” which can be estimated as follows:

$$t_{\pi} \sim \frac{\lambda}{v_{3B}},$$

where $v_{3B}$ is the speed of sound in the gas, which is equal in order of magnitude to the speed of gas molecules:

$$v_{3B} \sim \sqrt{\frac{RT}{\mu}}.$$

It is clear that if $t_{\pi} \ll t_{\pi}$, then the gas pressure will have time to redistribute the substance in such a way as to prevent its further gravitational decline. On the contrary, if $t_{\pi} \ll t_{\pi}$ then the gravitational roll-off will occur faster than the gas pressure forces are reduced. Let's find the ratio of $t_{\pi}$ and $t_{\pi}$:

$$\frac{t_{\pi}}{t_{\pi}} \sim \frac{\lambda}{(G\rho)^{-1/2}v_{3B}} = \frac{\lambda}{(RT/G\rho\mu)^{1/2}}.$$
