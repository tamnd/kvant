Selected school problems in physics

1. Before the thread burns out, the accelerations of the loads are zero, and the tension force of the thread is $3mg$. At the first moment, before the loads have time to shift, the distances, and therefore the elasticity forces of the springs, retain their previous values, therefore the accelerations of the two lower loads under the regime are equal to zero. The resulting force acting on the upper load immediately after the thread burns out is equal to $3mg$, therefore, its acceleration is directed downward and equal to $3g$.

2. Let us denote the larger mass by $m_1$, and the smaller mass by $m_2$. Let's write down the equations of motion for the block and loads

$$F-2T=0,$$
$$m_1g-T=m_1a,$$
$$T-m_2g=m_2a,$$

where $a$ is the acceleration of the loads, $T$ is the tension of the thread, $F$ is the force acting on the block from the axis. Considering that by condition $F=m_1g$, we exclude $T$ and $a$ from the equation and find

$$m_1=3m_2.$$

3. The acceleration of the entire rope is $a=F/m$ (where $m$ is the mass of the rope). The section of rope enclosed between the selected section and the free end is subject to only the tension force $F_H$ in this section. Considering that the mass of this segment is $m_1=m(l-x)/l$, we obtain

$$F_H-m_1a=F(l-x)/l.$$

4. At a small angle of inclination, the block is at rest, and for the static friction force we obtain from Newton’s second law

$$F_{трп}=mg\sin \alpha.$$

There is no sliding under condition $F_{тр}<\mu N=\mu mg\cos \alpha$, or under condition $tg \alpha<\mu$. If $\alpha>arctg \mu$, the block will slide off the doxie and

$$F_{трск}=\mu N=\mu mg\cos \alpha.$$

Therefore, the graph of dependence $F_{тр}(\alpha)$ consists of two sections (Fig. 1).

5. A motorcyclist moving at a speed $v$ is subject to the force of gravity $mg$, the normal reaction force $\vec{N}$ and the static friction force $\vec{F}_{тр}$. The equations of motion in simple terms on the vertical axis and on the horizontal axis directed towards the center of the circle have the form

$$F_{тр}-mg=0,$$
$$N=mv^2/R.$$

In order for the wheels not to slip on the surface of the cylinder, the following conditions must be met:

$$F_{тр} \leq \mu N.$$

Finally we get

$$v \leq \sqrt{gR/\mu} \approx 14 \text{ m/c}.$$

6. The number of tellots in the first and second layers can be found using the formulas

$$Q_1=c_Bm\Delta t, Q_2=rm,$$

where $\Delta t=|t_2-t_1|, c_B$ is the specific heat, and $r$ is the specific heat of vaporization of water. Equating each number of tellots to the potential energy of the beam $Mgh$, we find

$$h_1=8,6 \text{ м}, h_2=57,7 \text{ м}.$$

This simple example allows you to clearly feel the scale of energy “involved” in the world around us.
