1. Answer: no.
If the last digit $\alpha$ of $a_{n-1}$ is not more than 5, then

$$10a_n = a_{n-1} - \alpha \leqslant a_{n-1}.$$

Otherwise, the last digit of the number $a_n = 10a_{n-1} - a_{n-1}$ does not exceed 5 and $10a_{n+1} \leqslant a_n = 9a_{n-1}$. So, for any natural $n$ either $a_n < a_{n-1}$ or $a_{n+1} < a_{n-1}$. Therefore, if the sequence $(a_n)$ were infinite, then among its values ​​one could distinguish an infinite decreasing sequence of natural numbers.

4. Answer: a) yes; 6) no.

a) In a regular triangle $ABC$ with center $O$ of the incircle and any point $P$ on the circle, the vectors $\overrightarrow{PA}, \overrightarrow{PB}, \overrightarrow{PC}$ and $\overrightarrow{PO}$ form the required quadruple, unless the vector $\overrightarrow{PO} = \vec{r}$ is collinear with the others (Fig. 14). Indeed, denoting $\overrightarrow{OA} = \vec{a}, \overrightarrow{OB} = \vec{b}, \overrightarrow{OC} = \vec{c}$, we have, for example:

$$\overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{PB} \cdot \overrightarrow{PC} + \overrightarrow{PO} = (\vec{a} + \vec{b} + 2\vec{r}) \cdot (\vec{c} + 2\vec{r}) = (2\vec{r} - \vec{c})(2\vec{r} + \vec{c}) = 4|\vec{r}|^2 - |\vec{c}|^2 = 0.$$
