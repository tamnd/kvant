Substituting the numerical data ($p_H$ at 17 °C is 0.02 · 10⁵ Pa), we find that in 1 s, 0.16 g of water comes from saturated steam per 1 cm² of surface. If we assume that evaporation occurs at the same rate, then the water level in the vessel should drop by 1 cm every 6 seconds!

It is clear that this does not at all resemble the real picture. We have suffered a very honorable defeat, and now it is necessary to understand its principles. Discussion of some of them.

The first reason lies in the experiment itself. The real process of evaporation is significantly replaced due to the fact that the air in the room is not dry, but moist. Moreover, humidity itself of about 60-80% would lead to a replacement of the evaporation process by only 3-4 times. But if no air flow is created near the surface of the water (for example, by turning on the Ventilator), then the humidity indefinitely near the surface turns out to be quite close to 100% and the evaporation process is replaced many more times. Our estimate of the evaporation rate refers to an ideal servant, when the return flow of molecules to the surface is completely absent.

It turns out, however, that even in this ideal case we are mistaken by about 30 times, but this error is associated with the calculation itself. The fact is that only 3-4% of the molecules adjacent to the surface are captured by this surface (“attached”) and end up inside the liquid. The remaining molecules are reflected back.

Let's sum it up. If an absolutely dry air flow picked up all the emitted molecules, the surface of the water in the vessel would drop by 1 cm not in 6 seconds, but in 3 minutes. But still much faster than one might expect. Isn't it true?

How to emit radiovolts?

Before emitting radiovolts - electromagnetic oscillations determine frequencies, you need to obtain these oscillations. This can be done using a continuous oscillation generator. But how to ensure that the presence of oscillations can be recognized far from the generator is the topic of this note.

Let's formulate the problem more specifically: what needs to be connected to the output of the continuous oscillation generator for an electromagnetic wave to run from it? This question is not simple, it can be easily guessed that the elements for the role of a radiating antenna are not suitable. Here, for example, is a resistor. If we connect it to the output of the generator, then all the energy that it receives from the generator will be completely converted into heat. A capacitor is also not suitable for the role of an antenna - the average power it receives from the generator is exactly zero (the phase shift between voltage and current is equal to four periods). This means that he simply has nothing to emit - after all, when emitting electromagnetic waves, energy must be transmitted from the source in all directions. The same is true for the inductor.

Thus, in order for the power to be optimized from the generator, the phase shift between current and voltage must be changed - it should not be the fourth period. This can be achieved, for example, by connecting a capacitor and resistor in series. However, nothing useful will happen for us: now the loads (capacitor and resistor) are optimized by the energy generator, but all this energy is completely converted into heat.
