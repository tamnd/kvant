XXV All-round
Olympiad in mathematics

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
D. MITKIN,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. REZNICHENKO

This year, the final stage of the All-Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics was held in Smolensk from April 17 to 24. Teams from all resource cities (with the exception of Georgia), teams from the cities of Moscow, Leningrad, and physics and mathematics schools at the universities of Leningrad, Novosibirsk, Kyiv, Evreana, and Alma-Ata came to the Olympiad. In addition, students from specialized training centers at Moscow State University and the Leningrad Pioneer Palace, students from gymnasium No. 1 in Riga, secondary school No. 57 in Moscow and the team from Smolensk took part in the Olympiad. In total, 175 schoolchildren completed their studies: 58 ninth-graders, 61 tenth-graders and 56 senior-classics. For the first time, guests from abroad participated in the final stage of the All-Russian Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren: 6 British school students from Belgium. Observers from Bulgaria, Spain, China and Greece were also invited.

As in previous years, the final stage of the Olympics was held in two rounds, in each of which it was proposed to solve 4 problems. It took 5 hours to complete one type of task. The assessments of complete solutions to problems in points, from which the jury was based when determining the winners, are given in Table 1 (points were assigned taking into account the difficulty of the problem and in such a way that the total number of points for all problems in.type for each class was equal to 30). Tables 2-4 show data on the number of corresponding 9-, 10- and 11-class students who solved the problem completely (+) or with some shortcomings (±), as well as data on the number of works in which the solution contained gross errors (≠) or was completely absent (-).

The winners of the final stage of the All-Olympiad were loaded with diplomas, certificates, special prizes and memorable gifts. In addition, the organizing committee of the Olympics and George identified three schoolchildren, one from each class, to award them scholarships from the Philanthropy background. The best results at the Olympiad were achieved by: in the 9th grade - Kauks Valdis (gymnasium No. 1 in Riga), in the 10th grade - Ambayis Andris (secondary school No. 12 in Daggavpils), in the 11th grade - Perlin Aleksandr (specialized educational and methodological center at the Leningrad Palace of Pioneers). Students from Belgium completed 9th grade assignments. The greatest success of these is adding Mountain Arthur. He received a third degree diploma.

Based on the results of a now-traditional survey of schoolchildren conducted by members of the Jorri among the Olympada participants, the best competitive problems in each class were determined. In the 9th grade, problems 7, 8 and 4 were recognized as the best, taking the corresponding first, second and third places. The first place in the 10th grade was taken by problem 4, the second - fourth place, only slightly inferior to problems 6, 7 and 8. In the 11th grade, problem 7 was recognized as the best, the second and third places were shared by problems 6 and 8.

TASKS

First day
9th grade
1. Solve for number purposes system of equations
$$\begin{cases} xz - 2yt = 3, \\ xt + yz = 1. \end{cases}$$

Yu. Nesterenko
2*. There are $n$ numbers written on the board. You are allowed to erase any two numbers, say $a$ and $b$, and write one number $(a+b)/4$ together. This operation is repeated $n-1$ times, leaving only one number on the board. Prove that if $n$ units were initially written on the board, then as a result of all operations the number on the board remains at least $1/n$.

V. Berlov
3. Four lines are drawn on the plane so that any two of them intersect and no three pass through the same point. On each of these three points of intersection with the rest, two adjacent segments are identified. A total of eight segments are formed. Can the lengths of these segments be equal?
a) number 1; 2; 3; 4; 5; 6; 7; 8?
b) different natural numbers?

A. Verzins
4*. A lottery ticket is a card with 50 empty cells in a row. Each lottery participant writes numbers from 1 to 50 in all cells of the ticket without repetition. The lottery organizers will fill out their standard card using the same rules. A ticket that has the same number written in at least one cell as the one written in the cell of the standard card is considered a winner. What number of tickets must a lottery participant fill out in order for the ticket to win, regardless of how the standard card is filled out?

A. Verzins

10th grade
1. Prove that for non-negative numbers $a, b, c$ the following inequality holds:
$$\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{3} \geq a \sqrt{bc} + b \sqrt{ca} + c \sqrt{ab}.$$
D. Tereshin

2. Are there triangles whose lengths
a) all three medians,
b) two medians
is an integer number of times less than the length of the side to which these medians are drawn?

N. Adakhanov
3. The natural numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., $n$ are written on the board, where $n \geq 3$. In one move, you are allowed to replace any two numbers $p$ and $q$ with numbers $p+q$ and $|p-q|$. After several moves, it turned out that all the numbers written on the board were equal to $k$. What are the possible meanings of $k$?

B. Merkulov
4. The figure at figure is cut along the sides of the cells into several polygons, none of which contains a square of size $2 \times 2$ cells. What is the name of the possible number of such polygons?

A. Anjans

11th grade
1. Given a natural number $a_0$, the numbers $a_n$ are constructed sequentially with $n=1, 2, ...$ according to the following rule: if the last digit of the number $a_{n-1}$ does not exceed 5, then it is discarded, and the numbers $a_n$ are obtained (perhaps as a result there will be nothing left - then the construction ends), otherwise - $a_n=9a_{n-1}$. Is it possible for the sequence $(a_n)$ to be infinite?

A. Azamov, S. Konyazin

2*. The numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$ satisfy the equalities
$$\alpha^3 - 3\alpha^2 + 5\alpha = 1,$$
$$\beta^3 - 3\beta^2 + 5\beta = 5.$$
Find $\alpha + \beta$.

B. Kukushkin

3. Points A, B, C, D and E are chosen on the sphere so that the segments AB and CD intersect at point F, and points A, C and F are equidistant from point E. Prove that lines BD and EF are perepredicular.

V. Chinik, I. Serdeeev

4. Does it exist on a plane?
a) a set of four pairwise non-clinear vectors, in which the sum of any two vectors is predetermined by the sum of the other two;
b) a set of 91 non-zero vectors in which the sum of any 19 vectors is predetermined by the sum of the rest?

D. Fomin

Second day

9th grade

5. a) Find two natural numbers x and y such that xy+x and xy+y are the squares of different natural numbers.

6) Is it possible to find such x and y in the range from 988 to 1991?

A. Anjans

6. On the sides AB, BC, CD, DA of the rectangle ABCD, respectively, points K, L, M, N are taken, different from the vertices. It is known that KL || MN and KM ⊥ NL. Prove that the intersection point of the segments KM and LN lies on the diagonal BD of the rectangle.

D. Tereshin

7*. The investigator came up with a plan for interrogating the witness that would guarantee disclosure of the presentation. He is going to ask questions that essentially only have yes or no answers (which question is asked may depend on the answers to previous questions).

The investigator believes that all answers will be correct; he calculated that no more than 91 questions would have to be asked in any answer option.

Show that the investigator can create a plan with no more than 105 questions to ensure that the presentation is revealed even if one question is answered incorrectly.

A. Anzhans, I. Solove, V. Slitinsky

8. Given a square measuring 5x5 cells. One cell contains a “-” sign, the rest contain a “+” sign. In one move, it is allowed to change the signs to the opposite simultaneously in all cells of any square with boundaries along the grid lines, consisting of more than one cell. In which cells can a minus be located so that in a few moves all the signs can become flat at the same time?

A. Grinichevich

10th grade

5. Point D is the center of the circle circumscribed about the missing triangle ABC. A circle passing through points A, B and D intersects sides AC and BC at points M and N respectively. Prove that the circumcircles of triangles ABD and MNC are equal.

V. Chinik

6*. You can get a new polygon from a polygon using the following operation: cut it along a segment into 2 parts, turn one of the parts over and attach it to the other part along the cut lines, if the parts do not have common points except the cut point. Is it possible to obtain a triangle from a square using several such operations?

I. Voronovich

7. Naozem minor $k \times l$ daughter $n \times n$ intersection of some $k$ horizontals with $l$ verticals and its semiperimeter is the number $k+l$. Several minors, each half-perimeter no less than $n$, together cover the entire main diagonal of the daughter $n \times n$. Prove that they cover at least half of the daughter's cells.

D. Flaass

8. Let $a_1, \dots, a_{100}, b_1, \dots, b_{100}$ be distinct real numbers. Table 100×100 of filling in numbers according to the following rule: in the cell at the intersection of the $i$th row and $j$th column the number $a_i + b_j$ is written. It is known that the product of numbers in any column is equal to 1. Prove that the product of numbers in any row is equal to -1.

D. Fomin

11th grade

5. Points K and N are taken on sides AB and AD of square ABCD, respectively, so that AK·AN=2BK·DN.

Segments CK and CN intersect diagonal BD at points L and M. Prove that points K, L, M, N and A lie on the same circle.

D. Tereshin

6. There are 100 states on Mars that are at war with each other. To maintain peace, the decision was made to form several military blocs so that each bloc had no more than 50 states and any two states were together in at least one bloc.

a) What is the smallest number of blocks that can be bypassed?

6) The same question with the additional requirement that any bloc should include a total of no more than 80 states.

D. Flaass

7*. Given 2n different numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n, b_1, b_2, \dots, b_n$. Table $n \times n$ is filled in according to the following rule: in the cell located at the intersection of the $i$th row and $j$th column, the number $a_i + b_j$ is written. Prove that if the product of numbers in all columns is the same, then so is the product in all rows.

D. Fomin

8. What is the largest value the expression can take?

$$|y_1 - y_2| + \dots + |y_{1990} - y_{1991}|,$$

if $y_k = \frac{x_1 + \dots + x_k}{k}$ is at $k = 1, \dots, 1991$, and the numbers $x_1, \dots, x_{1991}$ satisfy the conditions

$$|x_1 - x_2| + \dots + |x_{1990} - x_{1991}| = 1991?$$

A. Kachurovsky
