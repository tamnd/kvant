XXV All-round
Olympiad in Physics

A. ZILBERMAN

The final stage of the 1991 All-Out Physical Olympiad was held from April 13 to 23 in the city of Tashkent.

At the theoretical.type, as usual, the active classicists were offered four problems for four hours, and the rest were given five problems for five hours. Here are the conditions of these problems (some of them are published in “Problem «Kvant»”).

Theoretical tour

9th grade

1. At the left edge of the cart, length $L=0,2$ m and mass $M=1$ kg, lies a cube with mass $m=0,3$ kg (Fig. 1). The cube is only given a horizontal speed of $v_0=1$ m/s to the right. Assuming that the cart is motionless at the initial moment, determine at what distance from the left edge of the cart the cube will be located after it stops slipping relative to the cart. The friction coefficient of the cube about to the trolley is $\mu=0,1$. The impacts of the cube on the walls are considered to be absolutely elastic. The cart moves across the table without friction.

2. $M=0,3$ kg of water is poured into a saucepan and a thermometer and a boiler are lowered into it, the power of which can be changed. It turned out that with a power of $P_1=50$ W, water heats up no higher than $t_1=+50$ °C, and with a power of $P_2=100$ W - no higher than $t_2=+65$ °C. How long does it take for this portion of water to heat up from room temperature $t_0=+20$ °C to $t_3=+70$ °C with a heating power of $P_3=200$ W? Estimate the maximum temperature at this power. Specific heat capacity of water $c=4200$ J/(kg deg).

3. The flashlight bulb is connected to a voltage source using long wires. With a wire length of $l_1=10$ m, the current through the light bulb turns out to be equal to $I_1=0,17$ A, with $l_2=20$ m - $I_2=0,13$ A. What will be the current through the light bulb with a wire length of $l_3=40$ m? What will this current become if the light bulb is connected to a source? The internal resistance of the source is negligible. The dependence of the point of a light bulb on its voltage is shown in figure 2.

4. A circle with a radius of $R=10$ m is drawn on the horizontal surface of the ice. In the center of the circle there is a zazz, and the wolf, as you already agreed, is on the circle (Fig. 3). The hare moves in a straight line at a constant speed $v_0=2$ m/s. The wolf must move in a circle so that the distance between him and the hare remains equal to the beginning at all times. To what point on the circle can the wolf reach without breaking the rules of the game? Ice friction coefficient $\mu=0,05$. The wolf moves strictly in a circle, without jumping.

10th grade

1. See problem 4 for 9th grade.

2. A box of length $L=0,2$ m (Fig. 4) lies on a rough inclined plane with friction coefficient $\mu=0,8$, forming an angle $\alpha=30°$ with the horizon. A weight is glued to its upper edge, the mass of which is equal to the mass of the box. There is no friction inside the box. At some points the load comes loose and begins to slide down. How far will the box move? Consider the impacts of the load on the walls of the box to be absolutely elastic.

3. In the vessel under the heating piston there is a certain amount of liquid nitrogen and its vapor at a temperature of $T_1=78$ K - point 1 on the p, V-diagram on figure 5. The piston is slowly moved back, increasing the volume of the vessel at a constant temperature - section 1-2-3 on the diagram. At point 3, the pressure in the vessel becomes $p_1=686$ mm pt. Art. The piston is fixed, and the vessel is cooled to a temperature of $T_2=76$ K, the pressure decreases.

4. The length of the chain of resistors contains a very large number of links (Fig. 6). The correspondence of resistors in the first link is $R$ and $R$, in the second - $2R$ and $2R$, in the third - $4R$ and $4R$, and so on - in each subsequent link it is 2 times greater than in the previous one. How many times will the current change when the capacitor capacitance increases to $C'=1000$ µF? At this frequency, the galvanometer needle practically does not reach the switch.

11th grade

1. On figure 8 there is a photograph of two flat-panel plates, making some angle $α$ and a private angle between themselves, immersed in water. The horizontal size of the plates is 12 cm. Based on the shape of the interface between the liquid and air, determine the angle $α$. Consider wetting complete.

2. The efficiency of a heat engine in the 1-2-3-1 cycle, consisting of production 1-2, isochore 2-3 and adiabatic 3-1, is equal to $\eta_1$ (Fig. 9). In the cycle 1-3-4-1, consisting of adiabatic 1-3, isochore 3-4 and isochore 4-1, the efficiency is $\eta_2$. What is the efficiency of a heat engine operating in the 1-2-3-4-1 cycle? The working substance is an ideal monatomic gas.

3. It is known that when an uncharged conducting sphere is introduced into a homogeneous electric field with intensity $E_0$, the field strength near point $A$ turns out to be equal to $3E_0$, and the field strength near point $B$ becomes equal to zero (Fig. 10). Determine the field strength near point $B$. Find the total charge induced on the VAB hemisphere. The radius of the sphere is $R$.

In general, the level of difficulty of theoretical problems was approximately the same as in previous years. None of the problems remained unsolved.

The tasks of the experimental tour were prepared, according to established tradition, by the hosts of the Olympiad - Tashkent University scientists and school teachers. They had to offer a lot of difficulties (everyone who tried to buy, for example, a battery in April, understands well what we are talking about), but in the end everything worked out.

Let us briefly describe the tasks of the experimental round.

In ninth grade, the first task was in optics. It was necessary to determine the refractive index of an unknown liquid (a solution of salt in water, slightly tinted, but the areas did not know this), using a light bulb on a stand, a battery, a thin-walled cylindrical Sta-

a clear glass string, black paper, an elastic band for fastening, a ruler and graph paper. The authors of the problem proposed a beautiful solution - wrapping a glass of black paper and leaving a thin vertical slit, illuminate it with a lamp and observe from above. In this case, two bows are clearly visible - one in the air, it is not refracted, and the other in the water. Next - a little geometry and trigonometry, and the answer is ready. The participants came up with many ways to solve the problem, some of which were very beautiful - for example, using a glass of water as a cylindrical line and positioning this line, the source and the screen so that the line was in the middle, while the geometry turned out to be very simple.

The second task was about training - it was necessary to change the coefficient of the third small piece of plywood on a horizontal surface (an album with a paper cover). Two wooden rulers were also given. There was a rather clear hint: a hole was made on a piece of plywood with a drill, where it was convenient to push the corner of the ruler and only the plywood, tilting the ruler at different angles. It was quite easy to find the angle at which the plywood would no longer slide (jamming) and change it using a second ruler. However, almost no one did this, although many solutions were proposed online, including some quite interesting ones.

The first task for the tenth grade was completed as standard - it was necessary to change the loose matrix, namely sand (more precisely, as it was specially noted in the conditions, the density of the matrix from which the sand is composed). Equipment: wide-necked bottle, test tube, water, thread, graph paper. The simplicity of this task turned out to be deceptive - it is not so easy to find the optimal method that eliminates errors associated with the thickness of the test tube walls (it is difficult to change

The second task is a “black box” with two outputs containing the source. It was necessary to find the EMF and internal resistance of the source using a voltmeter (its resistance was more comparable to the internal resistance of the source) and a potentiometer with a resistance of 1 kOhm. It was necessary to figure out how to make two (or better yet, three) known resistors from this potentiometer. One way is to set the potentiometer slider approximately in the middle and use a voltmeter to select a more precise position, turning on the “halves” one by one. Now we have a resistor with a resistance of 500 Ohms and another one with a resistance of 250 Ohms, if we connect the “halves” in parallel. Then it’s quite simple - you need to create several circuits and calculate the unknown EMF and internal resistance of the source (you could take more circuits than needed for the calculation and check if there is, say, a diode in the “black box”). However, the arithmetic turned out to be not so simple - the non-literate participants did everything correctly, but they were respectful in the calculations and did not add up to the answer -

4.5 V and 1 kOhm (flat battery, resistor).

In the first problem for the eleventh grade - optical - it was necessary to change the refractive index of glycerol and an unknown substance (pieces of irregularly shaped fused quartz). Equipment: a cylindrical lamp with parallel ends, a light bulb on a stand, a battery, water, glycerin, graph paper. The idea is clear - “drown” the pieces in a mixture of water and glycerin so that they are not visible. In this case, we can assume that the optical properties of the mixture are the same as those of the pieces, and the rest is clear.

In the second problem, it was necessary to find the temperature of the filament of an overlay light bulb at a given current. The temperature coefficient of tungsten was determined, and it was necessary to change the resistances at room temperature and at the operating point. The difficulty is that even a small current noticeably heats up the filament, so the filament had to extrapolate the characteristics of the filament in the region of low currents.
