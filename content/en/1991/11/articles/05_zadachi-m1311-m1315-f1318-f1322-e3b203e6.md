M1311 — M1315, F1318 — F1322

M1311. A triangle has integer side lengths $x, y, z$, and it is known that the length of one of its heights is equal to the sum of the lengths of the other two heights. Prove that $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ is the square of an integer.

D. Fomin

M1312. The fields of the doceki $n \times n$ are painted in three colors - blue, white, red, and it is known that next to each blue cell there is a white one (bordering on the side), next to the white one there is a red one and next to the red one there is a blue one. Prove for the number $k$ of cells of one say, red - the color of the estimate: a) $k \leqslant 2n^2/3$; b) $k \geqslant n^2/11$

F. Nazarov

M1313. Find 8 natural numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_8$ such that

$$\sqrt{a_1} - \sqrt{a_2} - \sqrt{a_3} + \dots + \sqrt{a_8} - \sqrt{a_9} = 2.$$

L. Kurlyandchik

M1314. The diagonals of the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ intersect at the point $M$, and $P$ and $Q$ are the centers of the hanging circles of triangles $ABM$ and $CDM$. Prove that

$$FD + CD < 4PQ.$$

F. Nazarov

M1315. Integers are placed on a circle. You are allowed to erase any even number, and write down their sum together with the two numbers adjacent to it (due to this, the number of numbers is reduced by two). Such operations are carried out as long as possible, i.e. until there is not a single even number left, or one or two numbers remain. Prove that the number of remaining numbers depends only on the initial arrangement, but not on the order of action.

D. Fomin

F1318. A bicycle wheel released from a height of $H=1$ m (Fig. 1) jumps to a height of $h=0,8$ m. Let us spin the wheel to a speed of $n=2$ rps and release it from the same height. At what angle to the vertical will it bounce off the floor? What if the rotation speed doubles? Another 2 times? Friction coefficient on horizontal surface $\mu=0,7$, wheel radius $R=0,5$ m.

O. Kubinsky

F1319. A steel string with a diameter of $d=1$ mm and a length of $l=1$ m was stretched and its ends were fixed at the same height. A mass of $M=1$ kg was attached to the middle of the string. Find the "sag" in the strings. What does it become if the temperature is reduced by $\Delta T=1$ K? Young's modulus for steel $E=2 \cdot 10^{11}$ N/m$^2$, coefficient of linear expansion $\alpha=1,2 \cdot 10^{-5}$ K$^{-1}$. The distance between the suspension points does not change.

Z. Rafailov

F1320. In the circuit shown in figure 2, the battery is ideal, and the resistors - except $R_x$ and $R_y$ - have a resistance of 10 ohms. Finding between points $A$ and $B$ is 4 V, and between $B$ and $C$ is 2 V. Find the values ​​of $R_x$ and $R_y$.

F1321. In a “black box” with three terminals (Fig.3) there is a circuit consisting of a $R=100$ Ohm resistor, an inductor and a capacitor. When changing on alternating current ($\omega=1000$ with$^{-1}$), resistance values ​​of $R_{AB}=75$ Ohm and $R_{BC}=125$ Ohm were determined. Can the inductance of the coil be equal to 1 H? Justify your answer with diagrams and calculations. At this frequency, consider the coils and capacitor to be ideal.

A. Zilberman

F1322. The space corpus flies from earth to Mars along the Hohmann-Zander orbit (the perihelion of this orbit is in the orbit of the earth, and the afein is in the orbit of Mars). Find the time of this flight, as well as the minimum time during which the astronauts will have to wait on Mars for the moment of departure on the return journey along the same orbit. The period of revolution of the earth around the Sun is $T_3=365,25$ days, Mars - $T_M=687$ days. Consider the orbits of the planets to be circular and lying in the same plane.

M. Gavrilov
