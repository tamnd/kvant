Recently, mathematical techniques and models have become increasingly used as tools in various fields of biology. The synthesis of the two sciences turned out to be not only interesting, but also a very useful approach for describing and studying biological processes (see, for example, the article by M. Berkinbit and E. Glagolova “Mathematics in Living Organisms” in the February 1990 issue of Kvanata). Therefore, we invite everyone who is interested in mathematics and biology (perhaps more of one of these sciences, and a little bit of the other) to test themselves.

Schoolchildren, students of technical and vocational schools can take part in the Olympiad. The winners will be determined separately for each class (with a common set of tasks).

As a rule, the proposed tasks require checking a fairly in-depth analysis of the described systems, so it is not frustrating if you are not able to complete the task in solving some of them. In principle, you can become an Olympic winner by solving only a few problems well.

The work must be completed in Russian in a notebook. Start solving each problem on a new sheet of paper. On the cover of your notebook, answer the following questionnaire:

1. Last name, first name, patronymic.
2. Class number, school (other educational completion).

Send your work to the address: 119823, Moscow, GSP, Moscow State University, VZMSH, Biological and Mathematical Olympada. The deadline for submitting works is no later than February 1, 1992.

The winners will be awarded prizes, and their names and analysis of solutions to problems will be published in Kvanita.
We wish you success!

2. Currently, about a million species of insects are known. The Union of Professional Insect Enthusiasts has compiled descriptions of all these species, consisting of 40 sentences each. It has been established that it takes approximately 3 minutes to read, comprehend each sentence and check whether the characteristic indicated in it is present in a given individual insect. How should a good identification device be designed to minimize the average time taken to identify an unknown insect? What will this time be equal to?
