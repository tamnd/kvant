GUIDE TO
FANTASTIC CHESS

The game of chess has been created for many centuries, its rules have changed several times. There are quite a few national varieties of chess known. The most ancient game is considered to be chaturanga, which came from India and then turned into shatranj among the Arabs and shatranj among the Persians. They still play Japanese chess (shogi), Chinese (tsonki), Korean (tyankein), Armenian (tama), Mongolian (shatar), etc.

Unusual, fabulous chess games are distinguished from real chess by an unknown board, unknown pieces or unknown rules of the game. Of course, it is possible that two or even all three “undeclarations” may be present at once.

The easiest way to get a chess game is to increase the size of the board. Many mathematical and entertaining games and problems have been invented on rectangular or square boards and even on a bacon board.

In real chess games, the record belongs to a 16x12 board. Such maximums were once proposed by the world champion X. P. Capablanca in order to overcome what seemed to him the inevitable “death of a draw” in chess. The game is played with a captured piece set, and the initial move of a pawn is possible immediately on the fourth square. To win, it is enough to mate the opponent's queen. Capablanca's match with the Hungarian grandmaster Marosi in maximachmaty, which took place in 1929, ended in victory for the author of the game with a score of 3:1. Partini lasted more than a hundred moves and lasted for ten hours. Capablanca’s invention was not distributed; the promise of “nobody’s deaths” turned out to be exaggerated.

Let us also mention the 12x12 board for playing belik chess, the cradle of which was India. Each player had 12 pawns and pieces with exotic names - crocodile, giraffe, lion, unicorn.

The famous conqueror Tamerlane, a passionate chess lover, also considered the size of an ordinary board insufficient. For samples of chess of his personal system, a special 11x10 board was made - pieces of the same type (general, camel, knight, etc.) were located on it in three rows.

Many games were invented by chess composers and storytellers. (Often these games were once described on pages «Kvant», we will provide the corresponding links.)

In chess on parallel boards (1985, No. 8), the game is played simultaneously on two boards located parallel to one another. The moves on each board are normal, but pieces can move in space from one board to another.

By geometric or topological transformations of a standard board it is not difficult to build boards of the most fantastic shapes. You can play on a cylindrical board (1989, no. 9, 10), toridal, conical and even on a Mobius strip.

Projective chess is played on a projective board (1974, no. 3). The rules of the game are based on the properties of straight lines in projective geometry. In particular, any family of parallel prizes intersects at the “infinity” point.

There are a huge variety of fairy-tale figures. Let us recall only some of the games with the participation of such figures: circus, lattice board, Frankfurt chess, Maschesky (1989, No. 9, 10).

In the game before the first chess, the one who announces check first will win. In the game of chess without checks, a check is prohibited - the first of them must also be checkmate. For example, in a game of chess with checks, check is obligatory - if it is possible, then it must be declared (by any means). In two-move chess, each move of white and black consists of two regular moves (after the first “half-move”) the king can be in check). In chess giveaways, the winner is the one who is the first to effectively use all the special cards, each of which gives a disadvantage that changes the normal course of the match. There are 72 cards in the deck, and the partners always have five in their hands. Here are the instructions they may contain.

“Make a move, then swap the positions of your opponent’s bishop and knight.”
“If three corners of the board are occupied, then place any of your pieces in the fourth corner—following this instruction replaces the usual move.”
“Make a move, then swap the places of your king and the opponent’s king - provided that they do not fall into check.”
Among the cards there are repeating ones, as well as canceling the opponent’s actions.

The one whose turn it is to walk chooses any of the cards and reveals it. The goal of the game is to mate the non-spinner king, but achieving it can happen in the most necessary way. The game may have a card, after presenting which the enemy king will instantly be captured. The deck of cards can be replenished using your own imagination. In France the game is becoming quite popular.
