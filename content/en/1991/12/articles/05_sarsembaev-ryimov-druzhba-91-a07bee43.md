Friendship - 91

In Poland, in the city of Legnica, from June 10 to 15, 1991, the International mathematical horizontal bar for schoolchildren “Friendship - 91” was held. It was attended by teams from several cities in Poland, as well as teams from Stara Zagora (Bulgaria), Almaty (Kazakhstan) and Yerevan (Armenia).

The horizontal bar competition took place over two days. Students in grades 9-10 were offered 3 problems per day, students in grades 5-8 were asked to solve the individual competition on the first day, and the team competition on the second day.

The winners of the competition were loaded with diplomas, recognition and medals. One of the gold medals was received by Arzik Petrosyan from Yerevan. The team championship was won by schoolchildren from Stara Zagora. The competition was very interesting. Exciting excursions around the cities of Poland were organized for the participants.

Here are some of the problems offered at the competition.

1. Solve for number purposes the equation:

$$2 = 10 - 1, 21 = 101 - 10, 1001 = 1001 - 0$$

(all numbers are written in the ternary system).

12. Prove that the Fibonaccian number system satisfies all the conditions of the previous problem. Find the sum of numbers in this system:

a) $100...00 + 100...00$;

b) $10101...01 + 1$;

c) $10101...01 + 100...00$.

15. Is it possible to divide all natural numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, ... into two increasing sequences $a_1, a_2, a_3, ...$ and $b_1, b_2, b_3, ...$ such that $b_k - a_k = k$ for any $k=1, 2, 3, ...$?

After some thought, you will, of course, agree on how to choose such sequences not in the decimal number system, but in the Fibonacci number system. Try to discover and prove it.

$$a) \frac{m^2 + n^2}{3} = 1991,$$

2. In triangle $ABC$, sides $AC$ and $BC$ are equal. Show that if the point $M$ is the midpoint of the segment $AB$, and the point $T$ is an arbitrary point inside the segment $BC$, then the circles drawn in the triangle $ATC$, $ATM$ and $MTB$ are tangent to the same straight line.

3. Prove the inequality for any real numbers $a$ and $b$:

$$(a+b)^4 \leq 8(a^4+b^4).$$

Azamat Sarsebaev and Alias ​​Rymov, 11th grade students of the Russian Federal School, Almaty
