We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with a star. After formulating the problem, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than February 15, 1992 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 12-91” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1316” or “F1323”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of the decision verification).

Please send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your school number and the class in which you will study.

Problems M1317 - M1320 were proposed at this year's International Mathematical Olympiad.

Tasks

M1316. Prove that an arithmetic progression of different natural numbers, none of which contains the digit $c$ in its decimal notation, consists of a) for $c \neq 0$ no more than 72 numbers,
b) for $c = 0$ of no more than 80 numbers.
Are these assessments available?

S. Tarasov

M1317. Prove for any triangle $ABC$ the inequality

$$\frac{1}{4} < \frac{IA \cdot IB \cdot IC}{l_A \cdot l_B \cdot l_C} \leq \frac{8}{27},$$

where $I$ is the center of the incircle, $l_A, l_B, l_C$ are the lengths of the bisectors of triangle $ABC$.

A. Skopenkov

M1318. Given a connected graph with $n$ edges. Prove that its edges can be labeled with numbers from 1 to $n$ so that for each vertex from which at least two edges emerge, the numbers on these edges do not have a common divisor greater than 1. (A graph is a system of points, some pairs of which are connected by edges. A graph is called connected if along its edges one can pass from any vertex to any other.)

M1319. Given a triangle $ABC$ and a point $M$ inside it. Prove that at least one of the angles $MAB, MBC, MCA$ is less than or equal to 30 degrees.

M1320. For an arbitrary given number $\alpha > 1$, construct an infinite bounded sequence $x_1, x_2, \dots$ such that for any $m, n (m \neq n)$ the inequality

$$|\alpha_m - x_n| \| m - n|^\alpha \geq 1.$$

F1323. The body was slightly shifted from the position of unstable equilibrium, and it moved. In this case, the speed of removal from the starting point returns according to the law $v(x) = A\sqrt{x}$, where $x$ is the distance to the starting point, $A$ is a constant coefficient. After what time will the body be at a distance $L$?

Z. Rafailov

F1324. They poured some water into the pressure cooker, sealed it tightly and put it on the fire. By the time all the water had evaporated, the temperature of the pan was 115 °C, and the pressure inside was 3 atmospheres. What fraction of the volume was initially occupied by water? Initial temperature 20 °C.
A. Sheronov
