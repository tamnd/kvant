III International
Olympiad on information
Candidate of Technical Sciences
V. KIRYUKHIN

This year, the III International Olympiad of Information (MOI) was held in Greece from May 19 to 24. Despite the unusually early timing of the Olympiad, representatives from 22 countries in Europe, Asia, Africa and Latin America gathered in the small town of Anavyssos on the Aegean coast near Athens to determine the strongest schoolchildren in the field of information. Representatives of two more countries - Iran and South Korea - were present as observers.

74 schoolchildren took part in the Olympiad, 5 of them outside the competition. The USSR team, based on the results of performance at the All-Union Olympiads and based on the results of participation in winter and spring training camps, included: Sergei Gershtein - a graduate of the University of Yekaterinburg, Akton Sukhanov - a graduate of the school. 470 St. Petersburg, Denis Uvarov - graduate of the northern school. 11 Novokuznets.

Since the organizers expected a significant number of countries to participate in the Olympiad, and there was every reason for this (the number of participating countries in the II MOI compared to the I MOI increased from 13 to 24), the number of all teams was reduced from 4 to 3 people. Team leaders V. Kirikhin and A. Denisenko, after a thorough analysis of the level of training of all candidates for the USSR national team, including Denis Kim (n. sh. 10, Temirtuu), Valery Lepekhin (n. sh. 132, Volgograd) and Valery Khamen (n. sh. 30, Grodno), left their choice on the three above-mentioned guys.

Greece is the birthplace of the Olympic movement. Any celebration here takes place in the best traditions established in ancient times. III MOI was no exception. The organizing committee of the Olympiad, headed by its chairman X. Killas, as well as all services led by the chairman of the III MOI projector of the University of Athens, Professor G. Philokipros, did everything possible to ensure that the winners were determined in a pure fight and each of the participants gained as many impressions as possible about the unique beauty of this amazing country.

Like last year, III MOI was held in two rounds. Both tours offered the use of personal computers. Participants were provided with personal computers IBM PC/XT and one of the following integrated programming environments: TURBO PASCAL Ver. 5.5, QUICK BASIC Ver. 4, GWBASIC, MICROSOFT C Ver. 5, TURBO C++, LCN LOGO Ver. 2.0, IBM LOGO, LOGOWRITER, FORTRAN 77 Ver. 4+.

Most worked their programs within the TURBO PASCAL Ver. system. 5.5.

The scientific committee of the Olympiad submitted 4 problems to the first trial for consideration by the international jury, and 3 to the second challenge. Before each trial, one problem was chosen by vote. Let us present them (both of these tasks were proposed by representatives of Greece).

Round 1 problem

Number the positions in a 5x5 matrix (table) as follows. If number i (1≤i<25) corresponds to a position matrix with coordinates (x, y), then number i+1 can correspond to a position with coordinates (z, w), calculated according to one of the following rules:

1) (z, w)=(x±3, y);
2) (z, w)=(x, y±3);
3) (z, w)=(x±2, y±2).

As always, Chinese schoolchildren performed very strongly at this okappada. They have two gold and one silver medals in their collection. The representatives of Czechoslovakia were slightly inferior to them, whose successful performance was a surprise to everyone. Moreover, their participant Igor Mali became the absolute winner of the III MOI, receiving an IBM PC personal computer as a reward. The USSR national team was in fifth place in the team competition, behind the teams of Hungary and Yugoslavia. The winners of the previous okappadomanda Bulgaria and the traditionally strong team of the now united Germany took 9th and 8th places.

In general, the USSR national team performed well in this okappada wave, and the results reflect the state of affairs in the field of school information in our country. As the analysis of the works showed, the algorithmic training of all Soviet schoolchildren is not inferior to the winners, and maybe even superior. However, their technical training does not meet the increased requirements that allow them to win in information science optimization. This is due to a “computer hunger” that we cannot identify even among our talented children - almost all of the team members had free access to personal computers, but for our schoolchildren this remains a problem. In addition, our long-standing illness is affecting us, associated with the desire to distinguish the theoretical part and the practical part in informatics, and with an emphasis on the first. An indicator of this is the optimization carried out at all levels using informatics, where the algorithmic side of solving estimation problems is sometimes even higher than the implementation of the algorithm on a computer, and this is motivated by the fact that optimizations are carried out using informatics, and not based on typing speed.

It is impossible not to mention one more fact that was noted by the organizers of this okappada. Among the winners, there are increasingly young participants. Now 15 years old, the winners were never surprised. Unfortunately, even for okappada in information science of the all-basic level, participants in grades 7-9 are a rarity. There is no doubt that a lot depends on the earlier involvement of schoolchildren in informatics, and in order not to lose our leading positions, we need to pay closer attention to this. Otherwise, a lag is inevitable.

In addition to the USSR national team, Belarus was represented as an independent team at the III International Olympic Games as a member of the UN and UNESCO. Results of the performance of Belarusian schoolchildren: Vladimir Svidersky - 125 points (bronze medal), Vladimir Beliy - 96 points, Vladimir Kabak - 87 points.

At the closing of the MYI, the representative of Germany, on behalf of his government, invited those present to the 1992 Informatics Optimization in Bonn.
