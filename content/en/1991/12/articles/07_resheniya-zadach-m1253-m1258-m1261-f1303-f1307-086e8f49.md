M1258. With $n > 3$ numbers arranged around a circle, you can perform the following operation: replace the trifecta of consecutive numbers $x, y, z$ with the trifecta $x + y, -y, z + y$ (in exactly that order).

a) Is it possible, using such operations, to obtain from a set of 20 numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., 9, 10, -1, -2, -3, ..., -9, -10 the set 10, 9, ..., 3, 2, 1, -10, -9, ..., -3, -2, -1?

b) Prove that for any set of $n$ numbers on the circle whose sum is positive, one can obtain one and only one set of $n$ non-negative numbers.

We will assume that the numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_{n-1}, a_n$ are enclosed in a cycle:

$$a_{n+1} = a_1, a_{n+2} = a_2, ...$$

Note that the transformation specified in the conditions, when repeated (in the same place), returns us to the original sequence. This suggests that there is some kind of permutation of two numbers hidden behind it - we will now see that this is exactly the case.

Consider the sequence $B$ of partial sums:

$$b_1 = c + a_1,$$
$$b_2 = c + a_1 + a_2,$$
$$b_3 = c + a_1 + a_2 + a_3,$$
$$b_3 = c + a_1 + a_2 + a_3,$$
$$b_n = c + a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_{n-1} + a_n,$$
$$b_{n+1} = c + a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_{n-1} + a_n + a_{n+1},$$

and let's see how these numbers change with the indicated replacement. (Here $c$ is an arbitrarily chosen constant that does not play a role: we do not distinguish two sequences that differ in the choice of $c$ - only the differences $b_k - b_{k-1} = a_k$ are important for us.) If $x = a_{k-1}, y = a_k, z = a_{k+1}$ changes to $x + y, -y, z + y$, then $b_{k-2} = d, b_{k-1} = d + x, b_k = d + x + y, b_{k+1} = d + x + y + z$ (where $d$ is a non-negative constant) changes to

$$d, d + x + y, d + x, d + x + y + z.$$

We see that in this case $b_k$ and $b_{k-1}$ simply change places, and the rest $b_i$ do not change. Let's consider two cases.

F1304. The efficiency of a heat engine in the cycle 1-2-3-1 (see Fig.ok), consisting of production 1-2, ishore 2-3 and adiabat 3-1, is equal to $\eta_1$. In the cycle 1-3-4-1, consisting of adiabats 1-3, 3-4 and isochors 4-1 are made, the efficiency is $\eta_2$. What is the efficiency of a heat engine operating in the 1-2-3-4-1 cycle? The working substance is an ideal monatomic gas.

In the 1-2-3-1 cycle, heat is consumed in section 1-2 and separated in section 2-3. That's why

$$\eta_1 = \frac{Q_{12} - Q_{23}}{Q_{12}},$$

where $Q_{23}$ is the amount of heat given off by the gas in section 2-3.

In the 1-3-4-1 cycle, heat is consumed in section 4-1 and separated in section 3-4. Similar to the previous equality,

$$\eta_2 = \frac{Q_{41} - Q_{34}}{Q_{41}}.$$

It is easy to see that $Q_{23}$ given out is equal to $Q_{41}$ consumed - in both cases, the entire amount of heat corresponds to the change in internal energy, and the temperature differences are the same: $T_2 - T_3 = T_1 - T_4$. So,

$$Q_{23} = Q_{41} = Q.$$

Now we can write down an expression for the efficiency of the “larger” cycle:

$$\eta = \frac{A}{Q_{\text{потр}}} = \frac{A_{12} - A_{34}}{Q_{12} + Q_{41}} = \frac{Q_{12} - Q_{34}}{Q_{12} + Q_{41}}.$$

The amount of heat $Q_{12}$ and $Q_{34}$ are simply expressed in terms of $Q$:

F1305. In the circuit at figure 1, the battery voltage is $U_0 = 10$ V, the capacitance of the capacitor is $C = 1$ uF, the resistance of the zalbano-meter is $R = 1$ kOhm. Ten times per second, the capacitor is disconnected from the circuit and immediately connected back, reversing its terminals. What current does the zalbano meter show? How many times does the current change when the capacitance of the capacitor increases to 1000 μF? With such a frequency of switching, the zalbano meter needle practically does not move.

For given values ​​of $R$ and $C$, in a time of $\tau = 0,1$ s, the capacitor has time to almost completely recharge each time - its charge at the end of each interval is equal in magnitude

$$q = CU_0.$$

This means that during the time $\tau$ a charge flows through the zalbano-meter

$$q_G = q - (-q) = 2q = 2CU_0,$$

and the average current through the zalbano-meter (this is what the magnetoelectric device shows) turns out to be equal to

$$I_{cp} = \frac{q_G}{\tau} = \frac{2CU_0}{\tau} = 2 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{A}.$$

With a large capacitor value $C' = 1000$ μF, the process looks different - during time $\tau$ the voltage $U$ on the capacitor changes, but only very slightly:

$$\Delta U \approx \frac{I\tau}{C'} = \frac{U_0\tau}{C'R} = 1\text{B} \ll U_0.$$

It is clear that the voltage on the capacitor fluctuates around zero and changes almost linearly (Fig. 2). Therefore, the average current in this case is equal to

$$I'_{cp} = I = \frac{U_0}{R} = 10^{-2} \text{A},$$

and the current ratio is

$$\frac{I'_{cp}}{I_{cp}} = \frac{10^{-2} \text{A}}{2 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{A}} = 50.$$

F1306. There are known experiments in which disks made of non-magnetic materials are applied in an inexpensive magnetic field between the stripes of an electromagnet with practically no acceleration (see figure). In one of the experiments, four disks of the same size were examined - made of silver, platinum, zinc and an unknown metal. A disk made of silver moves in the back of an electromagnet with a certain constant speed v at a current in the winding $I_1=0,41$ A. A disk made of platinum moves at the same speed with a current in the winding $I_2=1,39$ A. With karamta, will disks made of zinc move at the same speed? What metal is the fourth disk made of if it moves at the same speed at a current of $I_3=0,29$ A? Consider that in a given range of currents, the field in the back of the electromagnet is proportional to the magnitude of the current. Take the necessary data on materials from the attached table.

The braking force leading to the fall of the non-accelerating disk arises from the interaction of Foucault induction currents in the disk with currents in the electromagnet winding. With the same sizes of all disks, we can assume that this force

$$F \sim I_{\text{ннд}} I_{\text{обм}} \sim \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\text{ннд}}}{\rho} I_{\text{обм}}.$$

Neglecting distortions of the total field due to Foucault currents, for the induced emf we can write

$$\mathcal{E}_{\text{ннд}} \sim \frac{\Delta B}{\Delta h} v \sim I_{\text{обм}} v,$$

where $\Delta B/\Delta h$ is the rate of change of magnetic field induction with height.

So,

$$F \sim \frac{I_{\text{обм}} v}{\rho} I_{\text{обм}} = \frac{I_{\text{обм}}^2 v}{\rho}.$$

For steady motion the equality is true

$$F - mg = 0.$$

From here, introducing the density of materials $d$, we obtain the correspondence

$$\frac{I_{\text{обм}}^2 v}{\rho d} = \text{const}$$

(if you use the same units of change of the same values ​​for all materials, then you don’t have to worry about dimensionality). These tasks allow you to check the validity of this correspondence:
