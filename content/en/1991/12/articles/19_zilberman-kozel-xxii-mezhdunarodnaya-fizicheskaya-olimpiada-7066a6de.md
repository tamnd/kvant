Problem 3

To study in detail the properties of individual atoms, it is necessary to obtain these atoms in a state of almost rest in a small part of space for a certain time. For these purposes, a method has recently begun to be used, called the method of cooling atoms using a laser. Let's look at its basics.

$^{23}$Na atoms, which arise during the evaporation of sodium at a temperature $T=10^3$ K, are formed into a narrow beam using a collimator and directed into a vacuum chamber (Fig. 3). Over the entire cross-section, the beam of atoms is illuminated by a counter-propagating beam of light from a powerful laser. The laser frequency is chosen such that resonant absorption of the phonate occurs by atoms whose speed is equal to $\vec{v}_0$ and the number of times they are in the ground state. Absorbing light, the atom moves to the first excited level with energies $E$ and width $\Gamma$ (Fig. 4), and its speed changes by a certain amount $\Delta v_1=v_1-v_0$. Then, as a result of spontaneous emission, the atom emits light and returns to the ground state, its speed changes by values ​​of $\Delta v'=v'_1-v_1$ and deviates by an angle $\varphi$ from the original direction (Fig. 5).

This order of acts of absorption and radiation is repeated many times until the speed of the atoms changes by values ​​$\Delta v$ and the resonant absorption of light by the particle $v$ becomes impossible. In the future, it is necessary to change the laser frequency in order to maintain resonant absorption at the new speed and continue to decelerate the atoms until some of them reduce their speed to a very small value.

Especially in distant countries, where it is very hot in the morning, during the day and in the evening, and the local pastry chef is located in the hotel where the team leaders live, the food is sometimes, although tasteful, but unfamiliar, the rivals from team $N$ look great and carry out complex calculations without a single mistake, and the wingspan of the local cockroach reaches, according to rumors, half a meter. This year's experimental round included only one task (according to the MFO rules, there can be either one task or two). This task was not difficult - a “black box” for direct current measurements, but it required many measurements and careful analysis of the results obtained. We will not talk about this task in detail, we will only say that in the “box” there was a resistor (corresponding to about 0.5 kOhm), a silicon diode and a high-capacity capacitor (about 150 μF), and for measurements two avometers, a pair of resistors of various values ​​and a direct current source with a potentiometer at the output were provided.

Such a task should not have caused serious difficulties for our participants (in any case, when preparing for the MFO, the guys coped with very similar tasks easily), however, not everything turned out to be so simple. The hosts of the Olympics did not have enough equipment and equipment for not everyone at once, and the experiment was organized in two shifts (there is nothing particularly bad about this, sometimes we had to do the same at the All-Union Olympics). In accordance with the alphabet, our team ended up in the second shift, and the guys were asked to go on an excursion before lunch. However, one of ours was “lucky” - in the morning he had a temperature of 38° (Celsius), and he did not go on the excursion for completely legal reasons. Two more refused to eat a full meal and, in violation of the rules, preferred to simply rest before the tour. But the two largest participants could not refuse the organizers and spent several hours in the Botanical Garden - they “flew” on the experimental type, receiving only bronze medals as a result.

As a result, three of our team received gold medals. This is Timur Shutenko - he has the absolute best result in the theoretical type and in total, Sergei Bashinsky and Alexander Lyapin. Two received bronze medals - Sergei Dobrovolsky (he was 1/4 point short of silver) and Vintsas Tamoshnas.

The guys from China performed brilliantly this year - all five received gold medals. They became the first in the unofficial team competition, taking reps for last year, when our team beat the Chinese. (To be fair, it should be said that the Chinese beat us quite strongly.) Our participants came second, strongly ahead of the other teams.

It is known that the economic situation of Cuba's resource workers is not great now, but the Olympics were held very well. The host of the Olympiad is extremely conscientious in the preparation of tasks; the work was checked at a high level. Everything was organized clearly, including the cultural program for the Olympiad participants and members of the International Jury. I especially want to note the very friendly atmosphere that reigned at the Olympics. Further, rather anticipated discussions took place in an atmosphere of mutual understanding and striving for agreement.
