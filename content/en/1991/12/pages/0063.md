By what value $\Delta v_1$ will the speed of such an atom change after the first absorption of a photon?
2) In what speed interval $\Delta v_0$ are there atoms that can absorb a photon with the frequency calculated in step 1)?
3) What is the maximum angle $\varphi$ that an atom can scatter through after a single photon is emitted?
4) At what maximum decrease in speed $\Delta v$ can a photon of the same frequency $v$ still be absorbed?
5) Estimate the number of absorption events - radiation, after which the atom, moving along the beam axis, will slow down from $v_0$ to almost zero speed.
6) Estimate the duration of the process of emission and absorption of a photon by an atom. How far will the atom fly during this time?

It must be said that the selection of problems of a theoretical type turned out to be quite satisfactory, although the problems could have been somewhat more complex. The first problem, relating to the theory of rotational motion of a rigid body, is quite traditional. It goes somewhat beyond the scope of our school curriculum, but for those who are familiar with physics as an elective course, it should not cause any particular difficulties.

The second problem seems more complex; relating to the theory of relative velocities, this problem can be formulated more simply: an electric current flows along the circuit described in the conditions, and in the reporting system associated with this circuit, in addition to the electric field, there is also a magnetic field. The process, on Optimide, the problem had to be solved in the original version. Formally, it does not go beyond the scope of our school curriculum.

The third problem is related to an interesting physical phenomenon - cooling by light (in the magazine «Kvant» No. 5 for 1990, an article by I. Vorobyov is related to this phenomenon). The solution to this problem is also quite accessible to our readers.
