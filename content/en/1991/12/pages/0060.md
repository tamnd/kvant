XXXII International Mathematical Olympiad

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. VAVILOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. FOMIN

The basis of the emblem of the XXXII International Mathematical Olympiad is the national flag of Sweden and the third generation of the Koch curve, named after the Swedish woman mathematician who discovered it. The construction of the Koch curve in this case begins with an equilateral triangle, which is called the seed. The seed is the zero generation of the Koch curve. Now we will divide each of its sides into three equal parts and apply a regular triangle to each of the middle segments on the outer side. The result is a non-convex dodecagon - this is the first generation of the Koch curve. Each side will do the same; We'll get the second generation. The curve depicted on the Olympics emblem is the third generation of the curve. This process can be continued further; The “limit” curve is called the fractal Koch curve.

At the Olympiad, overshadowed by this red curve, our country was represented by six schoolchildren: Malinni-

Kova Evgenia (St. Petersburg, no. 239), Perlin Alexander (St. Petersburg, no. 239), Ambayis Andris (Duagavpils, no. 12), Temkin Mikhail (Moscow, no. 57), Zhukhovitsky Vsevolod (St. Petersburg, no. 239), Nekrashevich Vladimir (Kyiv region, village of Krutyshe Gorby). All team members were well prepared and were in excellent sports shape: the team won four gold and two silver medals (see table) - an excellent result! Moreover, this year the team established a unique regime: it scored the most (in percentage terms) number of points in the entire history of the USSR’s participation in international mathematical Olympiads.
