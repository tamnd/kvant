It is very close to the Varian number system, adopted in the ancient civilization of the Mayans who lived in Central America. The creation of this number system dates back to the beginning of our era. If the Varian number system had a combined deca-sexadesimal character, then the Mayan system was penta-twenty. The first 19 numbers were written with a combination of a dash, denoting a five, and a dot (units):

But the main role was played by the two-digit number system. When writing a number, the “digits” were signed one below the other, with the eldest being the top digit. The adjective “distorted” means this: the third - after 1 and 20 - the key number in the Mayan system was not $20 \times 20=20^2$, but $18 \cdot 20=360$; Next came $18 \cdot 20^2, 18 \cdot 20^3, 18 \cdot 20^4$. A creature and a special designation for zero that resembles a half-closed eye:

Here are some examples of writing numbers in the Mayan system:

$$(1 \cdot 20+0=20, 19 \cdot 360+13 \cdot 20+13=7113, 10 \cdot 360+7=3607).$$

The main difference between the recording of numbers in the Varian numbering system and in the Mayan system from the Primal numbering lies precisely in the “positional” principle of these systems: while among the Primlians the letter I always means one, and V - five, regardless of where these letters are located, among the ancient Babylonians and among the Mayan Indians the meaning of the “number” depends on the place it occupies, or “position”. That is why such systems for recording numbers (which includes the commonly used “decimal system” created in India in the 8th-9th centuries or a little earlier) are called positional.
