At first glance, there is no connection between the games "nim" and "shokoladaka". Let's take a closer look at the chocolate bar shown at figure. It consists of 6×8=48 slices. It is divided by 5 vertical and 7 horizontal straight lines, with one vertical line passing to the left of the displaced segment, 4 to the right of it, 5 horizontal lines passing above the displaced segment and 2 below.

Let us now arrange four stones: in the first there is 1 stone, in the second - 4, in the third - 5 and in the fourth - 2. We ask the players after each move in the game with a chocolate bar to take stones from the piles, and if, say, they broke off a strip of 3 pieces on top, then we take 3 stones from the third pile, if they broke off a strip of one piece on the right, then we take 1 stone from the second pile, etc. Note that then, game in "shokoladaku", players will also play "nim", and the one who wins in "shokoladaku" also wins in "nim". But we have already learned to play “nim”, and it is natural for us to place the order of moves: first make a move in the game “nim”, and then break the chocolate accordingly.

So, playing “shokoladaku” is simply playing “neem” on even piles of stones. And, as I say, the question is settled.
