Thus, we come to the somewhat paradoxical conclusion that every superluminal *none-river* speed is essentially a manifestation of the *longitudinal* Doppler effect. The possibility of such an unusual manifestation was predicted back in 1966 by the English astronomer Martin Rees, but for a long time this prediction did not arouse much interest.

But at the beginning of 1971, a message appeared from an employee of the Massachust Technical Institute, Irwin Shapiro, which struck astronomers like a thunderclap. Shapiro observed radio emission from the quasar 3 C 279 to measure the effect of the general relative theory - the deflection of radio waves in the gravitational field of the Sun. In doing so, he used a highly advanced device - an ultra-high-resolution radio interface, which allows him to distinguish individual details of the quasar.

From Shapiro's observations, which by that time were unprecedented in their accuracy, it followed that the individual components of the quasar 3 C 279 were flying away at a speed approximately 10 times the speed of light!

To date, such “superluminal” expansion has already been observed in at least several quasars (see photo on the cover of the magazine).

Of course, the vast majority of physicists and astronomers are convinced that what we are seeing here is not real superluminal motion of bodies, but most likely just a manifestation of the effect of a relativistic cannonball. But it's amazing that such a cannonball is actually realized on a cosmic scale! After all, it is one thing when we are talking about the relativistic velocities of individual elements of a part, and quite another thing to also observe the velocities of clumps of matter with a mass perhaps a million times greater than the mass of the Sun! What forces were able to accelerate a gigantic mass of matter to such a speed? It is possible that the path to new unexpected discoveries begins with this question.
