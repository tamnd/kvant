In Poland, in the city of Legnica, from June 10 to 15, 1991, the International mathematical horizontal bar for schoolchildren “Friendship - 91” was held. It was attended by teams from several cities in Poland, as well as teams from Stara Zagora (Bulgaria), Almaty (Kazakhstan) and Yerevan (Armenia).

The horizontal bar competition took place over two days. Students in grades 9-10 were offered 3 problems per day, students in grades 5-8 were given individual results on the first day, and team results on the second day.

The winners of the competition were loaded with diplomas, recognition and medals. One of the gold medals was received by Arzik Petrosyan from Yerevan. The team championship was won by schoolchildren from Stara Zagora. The competition was very interesting. Exciting excursions around the cities of Poland were organized for their participants.

Here are some of the problems offered at the competition.

1. Solve for number purposes the equation:

$$2 = 10 - 1, 21 = 101 - 10, 1001 = 1001 - 0$$

(all numbers are written in the ternary system).

12. Prove that the condition “$q_{n+1}$ is divisible by $q_n$ for all $n=0, 1, 2, 3, ...$ is necessary for any notation to make sense in the number system with basis (1)

$$N = \langle a_n a_{n-1} \dots a_1 a_0 \rangle,$$

Where

$$a_0 < \frac{q_1}{q_0}, a_1 < \frac{q_2}{q_1}, a_2 < \frac{q_3}{q_2}$$, etc.

13. What condition must the numbers of the basic sequence $q_1, q_2, q_3, ...$ satisfy so that only two digits 0 and 1 appear in the notation of numbers? What other condition should be imposed on these numbers $q_1, q_2, q_3, ...$ so that no two ones come in a row?

14. Sequence of numbers

$$q_0 = 1, q_1 = 2, q_2 = 3,$$

$$q_3 = 5, q_4 = 8, q_5 = 13, ...$$

Where

$$q_{n+1} = q_n + q_{n-1},$$

called the Fibonacci sequence. Prove that the Fibonaccian number system satisfies all the conditions of the previous problem. Find the sum of numbers in this system:

a) $100...00 + 100...00$;

b) $10101...01 + 1$;

c) $10101...01 + 100...00$.

15. Is it possible to divide all natural numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, ... into two increasing sequences $a_1, a_2, a_3, ...$ and $b_1, b_2, b_3, ...$ such that $b_k - a_k = k$ for any $k=1, 2, 3, ...$?

After some thought, you will, of course, agree on how to choose such sequences (this can be done in the only way)

$$a_1 b_1 a_2 b_2 a_3 b_3 a_4 b_4 a_5 b_5 a_6 ...$$

But catching a pattern in the processing of $(a_k, b_k)$ pairs is not so easy. How can you find out, for example, which of the two sequences $a_k$ or $b_k$ 100 belongs to, what number it corresponds to, what number will be with it (without writing out all the previous $a_k$ and $b_k$)?

It turns out that in order to form this pattern, you need to write these sequences not in the decimal number system, but in the Fib number system. Try to discover and prove it.
