at the Olympics. Especially in distant countries, where it is very hot in the morning, during the day and in the evening, and the local pastry chef is located in the hotel where the team leaders live, the food is sometimes, although tasteful, but unfamiliar, the rivals from team $N$ look great and carry out complex calculations without a single mistake, and the wingspan of the local cockroach reaches, according to rumors, half a meter. And although our team consists of repeated winners of the All-Union Physics Olympiads, who did not pass the selection process and trained on the server for three weeks before the trip (however, the guys also had time to leave), even they sometimes made mistakes, and sometimes in quite simple situations.

This year's experimental round included only one task (according to the MFO rules, there can be either one task or two). This task was not difficult - a “black box” for direct current measurements, but it required many measurements and careful analysis of the results obtained. We will not talk about this task in detail, we will only say that in the “box” there was a resistor (corresponding to about 0.5 kOhm), a silicon diode and a high-capacity capacitor (about 150 μF), and for measurements two avometers, a pair of resistors of various values ​​and a direct current source with a potentiometer at the output were provided.

Such a task should not have caused serious difficulties for our participants (in any case, when preparing for the MFO, the guys coped with very similar tasks easily), however, not everything turned out to be so simple. The hosts of the Olympics did not have enough equipment and equipment for not everyone at once, and the experiment was organized in two shifts (there is nothing particularly bad about this, sometimes we had to do the same at the All-Union Olympics). In accordance with the alphabet, our team ended up in the second shift, and the guys were asked to go on an excursion before lunch. However, one of ours was “lucky” - in the morning he had a temperature of 38° (Celsius), and he did not go on the excursion for completely legal reasons. Two more refused to eat a full meal and, in violation of the rules, preferred to simply rest before the tour. But the two largest participants could not refuse the organizers and spent several hours in the Botanical Garden - they “flew” on the experimental type, receiving only bronze medals as a result.

As a result, three of our team received gold medals. This is Timur Shutenko - he has the absolute best result in the theoretical type and in total, Sergei Bashinsky and Alexander Lyapin. Two received bronze medals - Sergei Dobrovolsky (he was 1/4 point short of silver) and Vintsas Tamoshnas.

The guys from China performed brilliantly this year - all five received gold medals. They became the first in the unofficial team competition, taking reps for last year, when our team beat the Chinese. (To be fair, it should be said that the Chinese beat us quite strongly.) Our participants came second, strongly ahead of the other teams.

It is known that the economic situation of Cuba's resource workers is not great now, but the Olympics were held very well. The host of the Olympiad is extremely conscientious in the preparation of tasks; the work was checked at a high level. Everything was organized clearly, including the cultural program for the Olympiad participants and members of the International Jury. I especially want to note the very friendly atmosphere that reigned at the Olympics. Further, rather anticipated discussions took place in an atmosphere of mutual understanding and striving for agreement.

The next, XXIII International Physics Olympiad will be held in Finland. We wish success to its participants!
