Problem 3. A circle of radius R is drawn on a horizontal plane. In the center of the circle, a glass cone with a base radius stands vertically, resting its apex on its center

$R$, raster area 60° and refractive index n. The circle is viewed from a great distance along the axis of the cone. What is the apparent radius of the circle?

This problem, like some other problems in geometric optics, admits variable solutions. The reason is that the image of any point is created by an infinite number of rays, and therefore the nature of the image of an object of finite dimensions significantly depends on the relative position of the extreme rays.

The difference between this task and the previous ones is that there is a danger of “losing” one of two equally natural answers. To find them, we use the principle of ray generation.

Let's mentally send the beam vertically down at the very edge of the base of the cone (Fig. 2). Obviously, the ray, after refraction on the side surface, will deviate towards the center of the circle, so the apparent radius of the circle will be equal to $R' = R$, and even “with a margin” (points located further from the center than A do not provide an image). Everything would be fine, but we forgot about total internal reflection. Indeed, if $n > 2/\sqrt{3}$, then the outermost ray will not pass through the side surface. And all the other rays too.
