F1305. In the circuit at figure 1, the battery voltage is $U_0 = 10$ V, the capacitance of the capacitor is $C = 1$ uF, the resistance of the zalbano-meter is $R = 1$ kOhm. Ten times per second, the capacitor is disconnected from the circuit and immediately connected back, reversing its terminals. What current does the zalbano meter show? How many times does the current change when the capacitance of the capacitor increases to 1000 μF? With such a frequency of switching, the zalbano meter needle practically does not move.

For given values ​​of $R$ and $C$, in a time of $\tau = 0,1$ s, the capacitor has time to almost completely recharge each time - its charge at the end of each interval is equal in magnitude

$$q = CU_0.$$

This means that during the time $\tau$ a charge flows through the zalbano-meter

$$q_G = q - (-q) = 2q = 2CU_0,$$

and the average current through the zalbano-meter (this is what the magnetoelectric device shows) turns out to be equal to

$$I_{cp} = \frac{q_G}{\tau} = \frac{2CU_0}{\tau} = 2 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{A}.$$

With a large capacitor value $C' = 1000$ μF, the process looks different - during time $\tau$ the voltage $U$ on the capacitor changes, but only very slightly:

$$\Delta U \approx \frac{I\tau}{C'} = \frac{U_0\tau}{C'R} = 1\text{B} \ll U_0.$$

It is clear that the voltage on the capacitor fluctuates around zero and changes almost linearly (Fig. 2). Therefore, the average current in this case is equal to

$$I'_{cp} = I = \frac{U_0}{R} = 10^{-2} \text{A},$$

and the current ratio is

$$\frac{I'_{cp}}{I_{cp}} = \frac{10^{-2} \text{A}}{2 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{A}} = 50.$$

3. Rafailov
