Index 70465

The toy shown at figure was called a two-dimensional Rubik's cube in Japan. So far, this puzzle exists only on a computer screen, since no one has yet managed to come up with a mechanism that would turn any horizontal or vertical row of three squares in a panel of nine squares.
The puzzle requires from an arbitrary initial state to obtain a single-color coloring of each side of the panel in a specified number of moves.

There are $2^9 - 2 = 510$ different colored panel states. Computer calculations showed that among them there are 192 states, of which the puzzle can be solved in 6 moves (these are the states farthest from “correct”). At the bottom of figure is the solution for one such original square puzzle. At home, this puzzle is easiest to do using common children’s cubes, and the rows are turned over “by hand.”
