Today, art historians and museum workers have a variety of physical methods at their disposal to identify fakes that do not correspond to the period for which they are “designed.” When, for example, the surface of an art product is illuminated with ultraviolet rays, it begins to fluoresce. Photographing such a luminous surface, especially with a microcoppa, reveals many details that are not visible to the naked eye. And infrared rays allow you to “see” what is hidden under the paint layer. The same can be said about X-ray photography. X-ray reveals a portrait of the Spanish King Charles V under Tishchan's "Judith".

Scientists also have at their disposal the so-called activation analysis. It consists in activating the nuclei of metal atoms with the help of quick-release agents. Then releasing the received energy, the nuclei form a specific spectrum, which can be used to distinguish, for example, lead from titanium. It turns out that in the old days artists primed the cold with lead white, but today they use titanium for this. It is known that titanium white began to be produced in the 20s of our capital.

Now the largest museums in the world, including the British Museum, the Louvre, the New York Metropolitan, as well as the Peabody Museum at Harvard, near Easton in the USA, are acquiring compact proton-ion accelerators. An accelerator, built in the USA and costing the French Ministry of Culture 1.6 million dollars, is installed in the Louvre dungeon. It is used to identify works of art, as well as in solving problems of their preservation.

Thus, the Peabody Museum discovered a “modern” bone plate, the carving on which depicted a mamount. This record appeared in the museum in 1889 and created a real sensation at the time, since it “testified” that in the territory of what is now the United States, some of these possible relatives of elephants were found. Now everything fell into place: mamounts were not found in the New World, as zoologists and paleontologists had long claimed.

One should not think that physical methods are used in art only to expose fakes. No, with their help “rehabilitation” is also carried out. We are talking, in part, about the new method of PIRE, or proton-individualized x-ray emission.

What is the essence of this method?

If the protons accelerated by accelerators are focused into a thin beam with a diameter of 0.5-1 mm (beam energy up to 4.5 MeV) and directed onto the surface, for example, a manuscript, then the protons begin to excite the atoms of the metals that make up the paint or ink. You don’t have to worry about preserving ancient documents: the radiation energy does not exceed the energy of a 100-watt light bulb; the book is illuminated from a distance of half a meter!

Excited atoms emit X-rays, which are recorded by sensitive detectors. The signals from the detectors are then sent to a computer for analysis.
