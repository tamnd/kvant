Problem 9. In triangle $ABC$ angle $B$ is equal to $120^\circ$. On the side $AC$ the point $M$ is taken, and on the line $AB$ the point $K$ is taken so that $BM$ is the bisector of the angle $ABC$, and $CK$ is the bisector of the angle mixed with $ACB$. Line segment $MK$ intersects side $BC$ at point $P$. Prove that $\angle APM = 30^\circ$.

Solution. This is a two-step reasoning. For triangle $BMC$, the segments $BK$ and $CK$ are the bisectors of the external angles $B$ and $C$ (Fig. 6). Therefore, $MP$ is the bisector of angle $BMC$, and $P$ is the intersection point of the bisectors of the angles external to angles $B$ and $M$ of triangle $ABM$. This means that $AP$ is the bisector of angle $BAC$. Finally we get

$$\angle APM = \angle PMC - \angle PAM = \frac{1}{2} (\angle BMC - \angle BAM) = 30^\circ.$$
