Using the PIRE method, a 42-line Bible printed by the Gutenberg printing house, located in the Harvard University library, was recently “read.”

They found out that Gutenberg used large amounts of lead and copper for his printing ink. Gutenberg's printing assistants mixed new batches of paint literally every day. The Bible consisted of several notebooks. Up to six notebooks were printed simultaneously every day. At first Biblud was 40 lines, but then they switched to 42 lines. Hyphenations were not allowed - only two word hyphenations were allowed for the entire text! After the first sheet was printed on both sides, they began printing the 2nd and 120th, etc. This made it possible to work on six notebooks at the same time, although it required very precise adherence to the “technology” revealed using the PIRE method. A specialist from Louvart also interned in the laboratory where the examination of the Gutenberg Bible was carried out. The museum wants to examine Gutenberg's 36-line Bible, which is printed identically to the 42-line version and is kept in the Bibliothèque Nationale de France.

With the help of PIRE, it was possible to “rehabilitate” the famous “map of Vinland”. Vinland - “Land of Grapes” - the Normans called in their sagas a distant land that lay beyond Greenland, rich in wild grapes. It was allegedly discovered by the legendary Eric the Red, who is considered today the discoverer of America.

The map of Vinland entered scientific circulation in the early 60s of our century. Scientists were amazed by the amazing image of Greenland, not much different from the modern one. It was believed that such precision cartography was simply impossible to add in the days of the brave Vikings.

In 1974, the card was declared a fake based on analyzes conducted by a team of scientists at the University of Chicago. Analyzes showed a high content of titanium descriptions in the ink that was used to draw lines on the map.

And now PIRE has proven that at least a third of the lines, and even more, are written on the map with ink in which the titanium content does not exceed the “norm”, that is, the minimum that can be considered a natural impurity. In other lines, titanium was completely absent!

Does this mean that the card's authenticity has been proven? No, it was simply shown that titanium descriptions were not used for the ink, i.e. the ink is not modern. But someone could have made ink using a medieval recipe. So far, physics cannot prove the complete authenticity of the card. Now, if only she would allow us to build a time machine...

Everyone, of course, remembers Mark Twain’s great book “A Yankee in King Arthur’s Court” and the Knights of the Round Table. For many years, in the large cathedral in the English city of Winchester, on the wall there has been a huge - large six meters in diameter - round table, or rather its table top. Tourists from different countries admired its multi-colored sectors and the large red and yellow flower in the very center. Unfortunately, today the admirer of antiquity and noble knights will be disappointed: radiocarbon analysis has shown that the tree from which the tabletop was made grew in England only half a thousand years later than the time when the legendary King Arthur lived and worked...

So physics helps us today to solve the eternal mysteries of exciting art. Life is short, art is eternal, the ancients said.
