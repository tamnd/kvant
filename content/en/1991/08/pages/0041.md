Let's bring this paper to one of the bubbles. Look! It stretches out and turns from a ball into an egg.
If we bring the paper even closer, our bubble is removed from the table and flows upward like a balloon.
Now, by bringing the paper alternately to one or the other bubble, we will make them dance a funny electric dance.

Experienced Ørsted
In all physics classrooms, with the help of expensive and complex instruments, the famous experiments of the Danish scientist Oersted, who discovered the relationship between electricity and magnetism, are repeated. These devices are a galvanosop and an electrical element.
We will also try to prove that

The wire through which an electric current passes, being brought close to a magnetized needle, causes this needle to move out of its equilibrium position.
We will build our utensils from a large glass full of water and a jar for jam, filled with half-linted water, salted with a pinch of table cols; from a teaspoon, a fork, a piece of coke, broken into pea-sized pieces, a needle, a small magnet and a spoon of zinc 20 cm long and 2 cm wide.

Let's start with the compass. Let's rub the needle with a magnet, water it on the needle all the time in one direction. Let the needle float in a large glass, either by lubricating it with fat, or by sticking it into a piece of paper cut out like a human figure. We know that one of the ends of the needle, say the one closest to the figure’s feet, will immediately turn towards the top.

Then he will start setting up the galvanosop. This is a device that must itself report the appearance of current. Place a teaspoon on the edge of the glass, directly above the magnetized needle, in the same direction. A compass and a teaspoon - that's all the galvanosop is. Isn't it true that everything is very simple so far?

What remains is the electrical element device. Wrap the pieces of coke in tripa and tie them with thread, first stick the handle of a fork into the coke. Let's immerse this coke pie in salt water and get a positive band of the element. We will place the teeth of the fork on one of the ends of our spoon. Place one end of the zinc plate on the other end of the spoon, immersing the other end in salt water so that it does not touch the coke pie. This end will be the negative end of our element.

An electric current will immediately be generated and the game will be thrown out of its equilibrium position; it will return to this position as soon as we remove the zinc plate from the salt water.
