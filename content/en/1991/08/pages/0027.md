Another of the many possible ways to construct a trapezoid is based on the idea presented in the article “Regular polytopes and rotations” («Kvant» No. 10, 1989). We construct a square ABCD, draw circles through its vertices C and D with the center at point O - the middle of side AB (Fig. 3). Let us construct a perpendicular to AB from point O, its segment x (it is equal to $\sqrt{5}-2$) between the square and another circle constructed from point O backwards and forwards along segment AB. The resulting points Q and P are the projections of the vertices M and N of the trapezoid onto the base AB.

V. Senderov

M1272. Prove that for any n positive numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$ whose sum is equal to 1, the inequality holds
$(1/a_1^2-1)(1/a_2^2-1)...(1/a_n^2-1) \geqslant (n^2-1)^n$.

If we refer two numbers u, v (0<u<v<1) so that their sum remains constant, then the product $uv=(u+v)^2/4-(u-v)^2/4$ increases and the product $(1/u^2-1)(1/v^2-1)=(1-u^2-v^2+u^2v^2)/u^2v^2=(1-u+v^2)/u^2v^2+2uv+1$ decreases. Of n numbers with sum 1, among which not all are equal to 1/n, there is a number u less than 1/n and a number v greater than 1/n (a similar message played a key role in the “shortest proof of Cauchy’s theorem”, see the article by Yu. P. Solovyov in «Kvant» No. 3 for this year). We will refer u and v, preserving the sum, until one of them becomes equal to 1/n; in this case, the product $P=(1/a_1^2-1)\times...\times(1/a_n^2-1)$ will decrease. If in the resulting set not all numbers are equal to 1/n, you can repeat this procedure with another pair of numbers and do this until all numbers are equal to 1/n; in this case P will become equal to $(n^2-1)^n$. Thus, the original product P was no less than this value.

L. Kurlyandchik
