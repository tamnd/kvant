Of course, I did not claim that these were diamonds, just as I did not pass off my gold coins as gold, and I traded them as jewelry, only of a special variety. They became a specialty of my company and competed even with Austrian rock crystal and rhinestones.

I could find hundreds of ways to use the hardened golovrovami, and I told the guys that it was time for them to patent the process, and so be it. In the meantime, nothing else should be made.

Nice guys, just not serious. You see, they’ve already put all this on. And they were not at all interested in whether the business brought money.

Just then Christmas was approaching - the busiest time for us - and I was so busy that I asked the guys about the patent only after the New Year. They looked at each other, then at me and sighed well.

- We decided not to take out a patent, dad.

Yeah! Nobility leapt up, I thought. They will publish the formula in some scientific journal and present their discovery to a person. And some LOB will add a trifle and take a patent in his name.

- Why did you decide so? - I asked patiently.

“It’s too dangerous,” they say in unison. And then Leo started explaining about the conservation of energy, and Larry started explaining about the atomic bomb, and they became more and more frequent, so that my head started spinning from these “e equals gm squared” and “perberation effects when waves are superimposed.” Well, I interrupted them:

“I can’t explain it any simpler,” Leo said, and Larry added: “We’d better show you.”

A lot of snow had fallen the day before, and the yard was covered in snowdrifts. Larry went down to the basement and brought back a bag of dimes that were still there in the barrels. And he also brought a blowgun. Then he put a dime on the snowdrift, and on that dime he put another one. And he took the pebble and threw it onto the coins. When the pebble hit them, they flashed, as always, and disappeared.

- So what? - I asked. We always knew that they were fragile. And I warned all clients about this.

“Take a better look, dad,” says Leo and points to where the coins were. The snow had melted there, and there was a hole about an inch and a half in the crossbar and a little less than an inch deep. But I still couldn’t understand what he was getting at.

The guys took me behind the house, to a large snowdrift where we cleared snow from the roof. This snowdrift was almost the height of a man. Leo took ten coins and carefully pressed them like a sausage into the snow at chest height. Then he took us four steps to the fence and stepped out with a blowgun. Here, for a second, it was like a blizzard raged. And when the air cleared, I looked - there was nothing left of the snowdrift, and it smelled like after a thunderstorm.

Then it dawned on me. I grabbed Leo's hand and shouted:

- Yes, this is wonderful! Who needs all this trinkets? You can clear an entire city or highway of drifts in one hour.

But the guys just shook their heads.

- No, dad. You yourself are a peaceful person and you raised us to be the same. Don't you understand what this could lead to?

Then Leo began to explain, but only remained silent and listened.

- After all, in this way it is possible to produce a weapon for destroying hydrogen bomb structures. To avoid a snowdrift, ten coins were enough.
