Most often, in problems of this type, a certain geometric structure is “constructed,” the parts of which are taken to be certain figures and their elements.

An illustration here can be provided by the stereometric “monster” problems encountered in exams at such challenges as MIPT, Mekhanite and VMC MSU and some others. I won’t give examples of such tasks, especially since you don’t have to go after them. It is enough to recall the stereometric problems from the 1990 versions, published in the journal «Kvant», No. 1 and 2 for 1990 and 1991.

The condition of a geometric design almost certainly leads to a multi-year problem, turning it into a kind of “shelf” problem (many shelves and each with its own tasks) or a “matryoshka” problem (several tasks, one inside the other).

However, problems of this kind do not necessarily have to be based on a complex geometric structure. Here is a simple example made up of two (or three) “shelf” tasks:

Problem 5. Design a convex quadrilateral and divide it into four triangles. Prove that the product of the areas of two opposite triangles is equal to the product of the areas of the other two triangles.

Problem 6. Prove that of all the quadrilaterals inscribed in a given circle, the square has the most density.

Solve problems 5 and 6 using them and construct the next problem.

Problem 7. A quadrilateral ABCD is inscribed in a single circle; the diedynamico intersect at point M. Find the area of ​​this quadrilateral if it is known that the product of the areas of triangles ABM and CDM is equal to 1/4.

Solution. To solve it, it is enough to note (Fig. 4) that $S_1S_3 = S_2S_4 = 1/4$ (problem 5), and also that $S = S_1 + S_3 + S_2 + S_4 \geq 2\sqrt{S_1S_3} + 2\sqrt{S_2S_4} = 2$ (inequality about the arithmetic mean).

In addition (problem 6) $S_{ABCD} \leq 2$, since the area of ​​a square inscribed in a unit circle is 2. From all this it follows that $ABCD$ is a square and its area is 2.
