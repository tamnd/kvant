AGAINST FRAUDERS

Candidate of Biological Sciences
I. LALAYANTS,
Candidate of Biological Sciences
A. MILOVANOVA

The famous Shroud of Turin turned out to be a fake! The shroud, the burial shroud that was said to encase the body of Jesus Christ, is more than a millennium “younger” than Christ himself.

In 1989, the discrepancy in time was proven strictly scientifically. Therefore, John Paul II fell, who owed the results of the research, said: “Publish.” And two weeks later, sad facts for the church were published in the leading international scientific journal “Nature”.

First of all, let's fill in what the essence of the matter is. It is known that in Turin, in northern Italy, for many centuries, a certain canvas 4.3 meters long and 1.1 meters wide has been kept in a special central ark. On the canvas there is a “print” of the body of a bearded man, in whom they see the image of Christ. At one time, computer modeling was carried out, which makes it possible to “reveal” three-dimensional estimates of the face and body of a person lying with his arms extended along his body. Photographs of this image in the “negative” went around the whole world.

It must be said that the Vatican itself, starting from the 14th century, questioned the authenticity (authenticity) of the shrouds (from the word “plash”). It first appeared around 1350 in the premises of a certain Jofey de Charny, a French knight-godmother, whose count's premises were not far from Paris. Legend has it that in 1203, the godmothers, just before the capture of Constantinople, saw shrouds in its environs. But the count took the secret of finding the shroud with him to the grave.

Having placed de Charny in the church, where the canvas was kept, a stream of pilgrims and pilgrims poured in, wanting to receive communion of the relics. They were not confused by the question: where has the shrine been located for almost half a millennium and why has it not worn out over such a long time?

A little later - already during the Renaissance - many people (especially artists) began to pay attention to the fact that Christ had a beard on the shroud. The fact is that all ancient Roman sources show us him... beardless! This is how he is depicted on the walls of the catcombs of Rome, on the mozanke of the Ravenna Cathedral of the late 5th century, in a painting of the crucifixion carved from ivory around 420.

But the most interesting is the Roman mosaic, accidentally discovered during excavations in the English house of Hinton St. Mary in 1963. This house is located in the county of Dorset, which lies west of London. The beardless Christ is depicted in the center of the filled mosaic, surrounded by the four evangelists - Matthew, Luke, Mark and John. To prevent Christ from being confused with someone else, the artist placed the famous Greek “Chi Po” above his head - the initial letters of the word “Christ”.
