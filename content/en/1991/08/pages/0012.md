The first group burial of the Kroman people in the Czech region of Moravia was dated. The age of discovery is 28 thousand years!
And not far from Moravia, in the author’s Galgenberg, scientists unearthed a figurine of a woman, which an ancient artist carved from steatite, or soapstone. The age of the figurine is 27 thousand years.

Today, the resolution of radiocarbon analysis has increased dramatically due to the use of computers and its “cooperation” with other more subtle and accurate physical methods. We are talking about mass spectrometry, which allows you to “directly” count the number of $^{14}_{6}$C atoms. In addition to increasing the accuracy of the analysis, the new method allows you to dramatically reduce the volume and weight of the sample, which is used to determine age.

The method involves separating the $^{14}_{6}$С atom from the rest of the carbon mass in the sample. To do this, all carbon atoms—both stable natural and radioactive—are ionized, after which they are accelerated by an accelerator to an energy of approximately 10 MeV (10 million electrons). Next, all the atoms will be “driven” through a magnetic field, in which the lighter atoms $^{12}_{6}$С and $^{13}_{6}$С are deflected to a greater extent than the heavier ones $^{14}_{6}$С. All that remains is to count the number of $^{14}_{6}$С atoms at the finish line (only these atoms will be there).

The mass spectrometry method allows not only to reduce the sample mass required for analysis by 1000 times, but also to push the dating back further. The fact is that the number of radioactive decays of $^{14}_{6}$С using the old method was determined as the number of scinitation, or flashes of molecules of a fluorescent substance, which were combined by photomultipliers and counted by a computer. With this fairly crude method, at least 10 thousand $^{14}_{6}$C atoms were required, contained in 1-5 g of pure carbon (correspondingly in 25-1000 g of sample). For a mass spectrometer, only 0.5-5 mg of carbon is required!

The requirements for carbon radioactivity have also been sharply reduced. In samples older than 37-40 thousand years, the radioactivity did not differ from the background, which exists due to cosmic radiation and modern radioactive contamination. Today, as already mentioned, it is not radioactivity as such that is determined, but only the number of $^{14}_{6}$C atoms, which is much easier and more accurate.
