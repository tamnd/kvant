"Paris, January 1, 1890
To my son Jean

My dear little Jean!
Among the experiments described in this book, there are simple ones that will be fun for parents and children gathered at the table in the evening...
All these experiments, both simple and complex, do not require any equipment: our laboratory, as you know, consists of kitchen utensils, corks, matches and all sorts of other substances that we always have at hand...

This is how Tom Titus’s book “Scientific Entertainment” begins (translation from French by M. Gershenzon, M.-L., Children's Literature Publishing House, 1937), excerpts from which we present to your attention today.

From old experiences

INTERESTING CASES OF EQUILIBRIUM

Two forks and a nickel

Fold two forks so that the teeth of one rest on the teeth of the other; insert the nickel into the slot between the middle teeth of the forks. Now, after several unsuccessful attempts, you will, of course, manage to place this food with the edge of a nickel on the edge of the glass, so that the nickel only touches the outside of the glass. Here our food supply is balanced.

To do this, you just need to put another, fifteenth, match on top of all the matches, in the hollow between them.

All 28!

If the table is perfectly horizontal and firmly on the floor, you can line up all twenty-eight dominoes like this on figure.

First, place three domino bones standing up - it is easier to build such a fragile structure on them than on one bone. Then, when everything is built, you will carefully remove the two outermost bones, which served as supports, and place them on the top of your fragile building.
