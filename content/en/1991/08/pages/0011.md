The carbon absorbed during photosynthesis goes into the construction of huge biomolecules of cellulose, or fiber, which is part of grass, wood, flax, cotton, etc. Lean animals feed on grass, from which they get horns and hooves, leather, from which in ancient times they did everything. And the bones and tusks of elephants have been valued by bone carvers since ancient times as an excellent material for crafts. And if you determine the amount of $^{14}_{6}$С remaining, then you - with a certain measure of error - will determine when this or that item was made.

It's easy to say "determine" when we don't know how much $^{14}_{6}$C was in the ancient sample. Yes, in most cases this is not known to students, but there are some “reference” points. The same Libby determined the amount of radioactive carbon in ancient Egyptian objects, the time of manufacture of which was precisely known from historical documents and other sources (which made it possible to calculate the personal initial amount of $^{14}_{6}$С). In addition, archaeologists and antipolgogi were able to accurately determine the age by the depth of occurrence. Naturally, the older the site, the deeper you have to dig. Thus, it gradually became clear how much radioactive carbon a sample of a given time could contain. Without studying, they talk about the date with an accuracy of ±(30-80) years.
