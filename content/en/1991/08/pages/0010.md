Currently, the shroud is the property of the Vatican (with the conditions of its eternal storage in the Touris Cathedral). Since 1931, when the first “negative” photographs were taken, scientific research into the shrouds began. After the war, in 1951, a special Guild of Holy Shrouds was even organized, on the initiative of which a study of the canvas by a group of international experts was carried out in 1978. Accurate dating of the canvas was necessary.

What is the essence of the method?

Everyone knows that our atmosphere is constantly bombarded by particles of very high energies. Getting into the nuclei of atoms, they knock out neutrons - particles that do not carry a charge and are equal in mass to protons with a charge of +1.

The number of protons in an atomic nucleus is equal to the atomic number of the element in the periodic table. Thus, the nitrogen nucleus, which is in 7th place in the table, contains 7 protons and 7 neutrons (nucleus $^{14}$N: 7 is the atomic number, 14 is the mass number, i.e. the total number of protons and neutrons).

As already mentioned, cosmic rays knock out neutrons from nuclei, which have a fairly high energy. Since the neutron has no charge, it quite easily penetrates the nitrogen nucleus, knocking out a proton from it. This creates a new nucleus, which contains 8 neutrons and only 6 protons.

The new nucleus has a charge of 6 and a mass number of 14, i.e. it is located in a carbon cage.

Due to the unbalanced number of protons and neutrons, the nucleus of the carbon isotope $^{14}$C turns out to be unstable (radioactive): as a result of β-decay (the emission of an electron and an antineutrino), it again turns into $^{14}$N.

Radioactive carbon decays very slowly: half of the nuclei decay in about $T_{1/2} = 5600$ years (this time is called the half-life). One would think that in $2T_{1/2} = 11200$ years all radioactive nuclei would decay. But this, of course, is not true.

Over the next $T_{1/2} = 5600$ years, half of the remaining nuclei decay (i.e., a quarter of their initial number will remain), over the next - half of this quarter, etc. In complete form, you can write the law of decrease in the number of radioactive nuclei as follows (the law of radioactive decay):

$$N(t) = N_0 \cdot 2^{-t/T_{1/2}}.$$
