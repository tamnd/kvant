Convex

The word “convex” is used quite often in life, just like the word “concave”. For example, there are flat, convex and concave mirrors. However, the everyday and mathematical understanding of convexity are somewhat different. In mathematics, all (without exception!) geometric figures are divided into two classes: convex and non-convex. The mathematical understanding of convexity in everyday language can be expressed as follows: convex figures are figures that do not have concavities. Here's the exact definition:

A set is called convex if, along with any two points A and B, it also contains a segment connecting them. The empty set is also considered convex.

If a set has a concavity (“hole”), then the segment connecting its edges does not completely belong to the set, so such a set is non-convex.
