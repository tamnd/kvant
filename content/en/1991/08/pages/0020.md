Cone is a step
from small to large

An experienced schoolchild more than once saw a beam of light from a separated projector. Therefore, he had already agreed that the mathematical definition of a cone is:

A cone (infinite) is a set consisting of points of rays emanating from the same point, called the vertex of the cone.

The definition itself suggests how to construct examples of cones: you need to take rays coming from the point $O$ and passing through some set $M$. (They say that such a cone generates the set $M$.) If as $M$ we take a circle in space that does not contain $O$, then we get a circular cone, and if it’s a polygon, then we get a polyhedral one. If the rays are replaced by segments, then we get a “school” circular cone and a paramid, respectively. What happens if $O$ lies inside the circle? What if on its boundary circle?
