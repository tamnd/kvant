M1296. You can get a new polygon from a polygon using the following operation: by cutting it along a segment into 2 parts, turn one of the parts over and attach it to the other part along the line of the cut, if the parts have no common points except the cut points. Is it possible to obtain a triangle from a square using several such operations?

I. Voronovich

M1297. The numbers α and β satisfy the equalities
$$\alpha^3 - 3\alpha^2 + 5\alpha = 1,$$
$$\beta - 3\beta^2 + 5\beta = 5.$$
Find α+β.

V. Kukushkin

M1298. A lottery ticket is a card with 50 empty cells in a row. Each participant in the lottery writes numbers from 1 to 50 in all cells without repetition. The lottery organizers will fill out their standard card using the same rules. The winner is the ticket that has the same number written in at least one cell as is written in the same cell of the standard card. What is the smallest number of tickets that the player needs to fill out in order to have a winning ticket, regardless of how the standard card is filled out?

A. Berzinysh

M1299. There are n numbers written on the board. The degree of any two of them is allowed, say a and b, and together they can be written as one number (a+b)/4. This operation is repeated n-1 times, and as a result, one number remains on the board. Prove that if n units were initially written on the board, then as a result of all operations on the board there remains a number no less than 1/n.

V. Berlov

M1300. Consequently, he came up with a plan for interrogating the witness, guaranteeing the disclosure of the presentation. He is going to ask questions to which only the answers will be “yes” or “no” (which question will be asked may depend on the answers to previous ones). Therefore, all answers will be correct; he calculated that no more than 91 questions would have to be asked in any answer option.

Show that it is therefore possible to create a plan with no more than 105 questions that guarantees the discovery of the view even if one question may be answered incorrectly (but it may be that all the answers are correct).

When tagging: If you only get plans with more than 105 questions, write the best one.

A. Anzhans, I. Solove, V. Slitinsky

F1303. A circle with a radius of $R=10$ m is drawn on a horizontal ice surface. In the center of the circle there is a hare, and a wolf - as you have already agreed on the circle. The hare can move in a straight line with a constant speed $v_0=2$ m/s, as shown in figure 1. The wolf must move in a circle so that the distance between him and the hare remains equal to the origin at all times. Can a wolf reach every point on the circle without breaking the rules of the game? Friction coefficient on ice $k=0,05$. The wolf moves strictly in a circle, without jumping.

A. Zilberman

F1304. The efficiency of a heat engine in the cycle 1 - 2 - 3 - 1 (Fig. 2), consisting of production 1 - 2, ishore 2 - 3 and adiabatic 3 - 1, is equal to $\eta_1$. In the cycle 1 - 3 - 4 - 1, consisting of adiabatic 1 - 3, isotherm 3 - 4 and isochor 4 - 1, the efficiency is $\eta_2$. What is the efficiency of a heat engine operating in the cycle 1 - 2 - 3 - 4 - 1? The working substance is an ideal monatomic gas.

A. Sheropov

F1305. In the circuit at figure 3, the direction of the battery is $U_0=10$ V, the capacitance of the capacitor is $C=1$ uF, the resistance of the galvanometer is $R=1$ kOhm. Ten times a second, the capacitor is disconnected from the circuit and immediately connected back, reversing its terminals. What current does the galvanometer show? How many times will the current change when the capacitance of the capacitor is increased to 1000 μF? With such a switching frequency, the galvanometer needle practically does not reach.

Z. Rafailov

F1306. There are known experiments in which disks made of non-magnetic materials fall in a non-uniform magnetic field between the strips of an electromagnet with practically no acceleration (Fig. 4).
In one of the experiments, four disks of the same size were examined - made of silver, platinum, zinc and an unknown metal. A disk made of silver falls in the back of the electromagnet with a certain constant speed $v$ when also in winding $I_1=0,41$ A. A disk made of platinum falls with the same speed when also in winding $I_2=1,39$ A.
At what current will the zinc disk fall at the same speed? What material is the four disk made of if it fell with the same speed at also $I_3=0,29$ A? Consider that in a given range of currents, the field in the back of the electromagnet is proportional to the magnitude of the current. Take the necessary materials from the attached table (see p. 24).

S. Kozel

F1307. A quantum of electromagnetic radiation is scattered by a resting electr (the so-called Compton effect). In this case, the scattering quantum changes frequency, and the electron receives a recoil momentum $p$. Determine at what angles relative to the direction of incident radiation an electron with a given momentum can move. Consider that the speed of an electron is significantly less than the speed of light.

Yu. Samarsky
