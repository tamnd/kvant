Stearic motor

To make this motor, we do not need steam, electricity, rolled air, or gasoline. For this we only need... a candle.

Heated two pins and stuck their heads into the candle on both sides, in the middle, perpendicular to the wick. This will be the axis of our engine; Place the candle with the ends of the pins on the edges of two glasses and get better balance. If you now cut the candle at both ends, a drop of stearin will fall into one of the plates placed under the ends of the candle. The balance will be disrupted, the other end of the candle will overflow and fall; at the same time, a few drops of stearin will drain from it, and it will become lighter than the first end; it will rise up, the first end will drop, drop a drop, it will become lighter, and... our motor begins to work with all its might; gradually the candle's vibrations will increase more and more.

soap bubbles and films

Candle, go out!

Very large and beautiful bubbles can be blown from a glass or tin funnel.

Huge bubbles, up to 30 cm in diameter!

The funnel should be lowered into a wide vessel in order to thoroughly moisten its edges in the soap solution.

Carefully, holding the funnel vertically, lift it and blow, taking breaks, each time pinching the narrow end of the funnel with your finger. Otherwise, the tension force of the soap film will expel the air from the ball. And the film removes this air with considerable force. This is very easy to verify. Bring the narrow end of the funnel to the hot candle and say:

- Candle, go out!

The flame will fade, fade, then go out.

Soap film always strives to take such a shape that its surface is as small as possible.

Trying to achieve a lamp glass of a finite shape, one in which one end is narrower than the other. Moisten the entire inside of the glass with soapy water and let the water drain. Now lower the wide end of the glass into the water, holding it vertically. Carefully remove the glass from the solution. The soap film covered the hole. Now hold the glass horizontally and you will find that your film will move out of place and run towards the narrow end of the glass.

If you dip the glass into the solution over and over again, the films will run one after another, as if trying to catch up with each other.

Ball in a barrel
Make a ring of wire on the bottle and twist the ends of the wire to form a handle. Make another ring of the same diameter; moisten the rings with the soapy solution that we used for previous experiments. Then blow a bubble between these two rings (like we have on the left side of the picture). Carefully expanding the rings, stretch this ball into a cylinder; stop as soon as the surface of the cylinder begins to take a concave shape.

Now ask someone to moisten a tube in soapy solution and insert its end into the cylinder through the film, which is tightened with the upper ring. Let your friend very, very carefully blow out the second one inside the first bubble.

Our cylinder will gradually split apart and turn into a barrel.

When the second bubble approaches the walls of the “barrel”, you need to lightly separate it from the tube, then carefully pull the tube out of the “barrel”.

So our ball hung between the lid and the bottom. If we now expand the rings, our ball turns into an egg. And if you bring the rings closer together, the “barrel” will develop to the sides, the ball will touch the bottom, and both bubbles will end up in the same bubble.

Touch-me-not ball
I just told you how to blow a soap bubble - a ball in a cylinder bubble. When you look at this small ball, it seems to you that it is touching the film of the cylinder. But in reality this is not the case! The ball does not touch the cylinder! Here is an experience that will help us verify this. Ask a friend to hold the wire rings so that the cylindrical soap bubble takes a horizontal position. If the distance between the rings does not exceed three times their diameter, our cylinder will retain its shape in this position.

Experienced Ørsted
In all physics classrooms, with the help of expensive and complex instruments, the famous experiments of the Danish scientist Oersted, who discovered the relationship between electricity and magnetism, are repeated. These devices are a galvanosop and an electrical element.
We will also try to prove that

The wire through which an electric current passes, being brought close to a magnetized needle, causes this needle to move out of its equilibrium position.
We will build our utensils from a large glass full of water and a jar for jam, filled with half-linted water, salted with a pinch of table cols; from a teaspoon, a fork, a piece of coke, broken into pea-sized pieces, a needle, a small magnet and a spoon of zinc 20 cm long and 2 cm wide.

Let's start with the compass. Let's rub the needle with a magnet, water it on the needle all the time in one direction. Let the needle float in a large glass, either by lubricating it with fat, or by sticking it into a piece of paper cut out like a human figure. We know that one of the ends of the needle, say the one closest to the figure’s feet, will immediately turn towards the top.

Then he will start setting up the galvanosop. This is a device that must itself report the appearance of current. Place a teaspoon on the edge of the glass, directly above the magnetized needle, in the same direction. A compass and a teaspoon - that's all the galvanosop is. Isn't it true that everything is very simple so far?

What remains is the electrical element device. Wrap the pieces of coke in tripa and tie them with thread, first stick the handle of a fork into the coke. Let's immerse this coke pie in salt water and get a positive band of the element. We will place the teeth of the fork on one of the ends of our spoon. Place one end of the zinc plate on the other end of the spoon, immersing the other end in salt water so that it does not touch the coke pie. This end will be the negative end of our element.

An electric current will immediately be generated and the game will be thrown out of its equilibrium position; it will return to this position as soon as we remove the zinc plate from the salt water.
