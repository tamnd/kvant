The famous Shroud of Turin turned out to be a fake! The shroud, the burial shroud that was said to encase the body of Jesus Christ, is more than a millennium “younger” than Christ himself.

In 1989, the discrepancy in time was proven strictly scientifically. Therefore, John Paul II fell, who owed the results of the research, said: “Publish.” And two weeks later, sad facts for the church were published in the leading international scientific journal “Nature”.

First of all, let's fill in what the essence of the matter is. It is known that in Turin, in northern Italy, for many centuries, a certain canvas 4.3 meters long and 1.1 meters wide has been kept in a special central ark. On the canvas there is a “print” of the body of a bearded man, in whom they see the image of Christ. At one time, computer modeling was carried out, which makes it possible to “reveal” three-dimensional estimates of the face and body of a person lying with his arms extended along his body. Photographs of this image in the “negative” went around the whole world.

It must be said that the Vatican itself, starting from the 14th century, questioned the authenticity (authenticity) of the shrouds (from the word “plash”). It first appeared around 1350 in the premises of a certain Jofey de Charny, a French knight-godmother, whose count's premises were not far from Paris. Legend has it that in 1203, the godmothers, just before the capture of Constantinople, saw shrouds in its environs. But the count took the secret of finding the shroud with him to the grave.

Having placed de Charny in the church, where the canvas was kept, a stream of pilgrims and pilgrims poured in, wanting to receive communion of the relics. They were not confused by the question: where has the shrine been located for almost half a millennium and why has it not worn out over such a long time?

A little later - already during the Renaissance - many people (especially artists) began to pay attention to the fact that Christ had a beard on the shroud. The fact is that all ancient Roman sources show us him... beardless! This is how he is depicted on the walls of the catcombs of Rome, on the mozanke of the Ravenna Cathedral of the late 5th century, in a painting of the crucifixion carved from ivory around 420.

But the most interesting is the Roman mosaic, accidentally discovered during excavations in the English house of Hinton St. Mary in 1963. This house is located in the county of Dorset, which lies west of London. The beardless Christ is depicted in the center of the filled mosaic, surrounded by the four evangelists - Matthew, Luke, Mark and John. To prevent Christ from being confused with someone else, the artist placed the famous Greek “Chi Po” above his head - the initial letters of the word “Christ”.

What is the essence of the method?

Everyone knows that our atmosphere is constantly bombarded by particles of very high energies. Getting into the nuclei of atoms, they knock out neutrons - particles that do not carry a charge and are equal in mass to protons with a charge of +1.

The number of protons in an atomic nucleus is equal to the atomic number of the element in the periodic table. Thus, the nitrogen nucleus, which is in 7th place in the table, contains 7 protons and 7 neutrons (nucleus $^{14}$N: 7 is the atomic number, 14 is the mass number, i.e. the total number of protons and neutrons).

As already mentioned, cosmic rays knock out neutrons from nuclei, which have a fairly high energy. Since the neutron has no charge, it quite easily penetrates the nitrogen nucleus, knocking out a proton from it. This creates a new nucleus, which contains 8 neutrons and only 6 protons.

The new nucleus has a charge of 6 and a mass number of 14, i.e. it is located in a carbon cage.

Due to the unbalanced number of protons and neutrons, the nucleus of the carbon isotope $^{14}$C turns out to be unstable (radioactive): as a result of β-decay (the emission of an electron and an antineutrino), it again turns into $^{14}$N.

Radioactive carbon decays very slowly: half of the nuclei decay in about $T_{1/2} = 5600$ years (this time is called the half-life). One would think that in $2T_{1/2} = 11200$ years all radioactive nuclei would decay. But this, of course, is not true.

Over the next $T_{1/2} = 5600$ years, half of the remaining nuclei decay (i.e., a quarter of their initial number will remain), over the next - half of this quarter, etc. In complete form, you can write the law of decrease in the number of radioactive nuclei as follows (the law of radioactive decay):

$$N(t) = N_0 \cdot 2^{-t/T_{1/2}}.$$

Today, the resolution of radiocarbon analysis has increased dramatically due to the use of computers and its “cooperation” with other more subtle and accurate physical methods. We are talking about mass spectrometry, which allows you to “directly” count the number of $^{14}_{6}$C atoms. In addition to increasing the accuracy of the analysis, the new method allows you to dramatically reduce the volume and weight of the sample, which is used to determine age.

The method involves separating the $^{14}_{6}$С atom from the rest of the carbon mass in the sample. To do this, all carbon atoms—both stable natural and radioactive—are ionized, after which they are accelerated by an accelerator to an energy of approximately 10 MeV (10 million electrons). Next, all the atoms will be “driven” through a magnetic field, in which the lighter atoms $^{12}_{6}$С and $^{13}_{6}$С are deflected to a greater extent than the heavier ones $^{14}_{6}$С. All that remains is to count the number of $^{14}_{6}$С atoms at the finish line (only these atoms will be there).

The mass spectrometry method allows not only to reduce the sample mass required for analysis by 1000 times, but also to push the dating back further. The fact is that the number of radioactive decays of $^{14}_{6}$С using the old method was determined as the number of scinitation, or flashes of molecules of a fluorescent substance, which were combined by photomultipliers and counted by a computer. With this fairly crude method, at least 10 thousand $^{14}_{6}$C atoms were required, contained in 1-5 g of pure carbon (correspondingly in 25-1000 g of sample). For a mass spectrometer, only 0.5-5 mg of carbon is required!

The requirements for carbon radioactivity have also been sharply reduced. In samples older than 37-40 thousand years, the radioactivity did not differ from the background, which exists due to cosmic radiation and modern radioactive contamination. Today, as already mentioned, it is not radioactivity as such that is determined, but only the number of $^{14}_{6}$C atoms, which is much easier and more accurate. Today, art historians and museum workers have a variety of physical methods at their disposal to identify fakes that do not correspond to the period for which they are “designed.” When, for example, the surface of an art product is illuminated with ultraviolet rays, it begins to fluoresce. Photographing such a luminous surface, especially with a microcoppa, reveals many details that are not visible to the naked eye. And infrared rays allow you to “see” what is hidden under the paint layer. The same can be said about X-ray photography. X-ray reveals a portrait of the Spanish King Charles V under Tishchan's "Judith".

Scientists also have at their disposal the so-called activation analysis. It consists in activating the nuclei of metal atoms with the help of quick-release agents. Then releasing the received energy, the nuclei form a specific spectrum, which can be used to distinguish, for example, lead from titanium. It turns out that in the old days artists primed the cold with lead white, but today they use titanium for this. It is known that titanium white began to be produced in the 20s of our capital.

But the famous paint “Prussian blue” appeared at the end of the 18th century. Now the largest museums in the world, including the British Museum, the Louvre, the New York Metropolitan, as well as the Peabody Museum at Harvard, near Easton in the USA, are acquiring compact proton-ion accelerators. An accelerator, built in the USA and costing the French Ministry of Culture 1.6 million dollars, is installed in the Louvre dungeon. It is used to identify works of art, as well as in solving problems of their preservation.

Thus, the Peabody Museum discovered a “modern” bone plate, the carving on which depicted a mamount. This record appeared in the museum in 1889 and created a real sensation at the time, since it “testified” that in the territory of what is now the United States, some of these possible relatives of elephants were found. Now everything fell into place: mamounts were not found in the New World, as zoologists and paleontologists had long claimed.

One should not think that physical methods are used in art only to expose fakes. No, with their help “rehabilitation” is also carried out. We are talking, in part, about the new method of PIRE, or proton-individualized x-ray emission.

What is the essence of this method?

If the protons accelerated by accelerators are focused into a thin beam with a diameter of 0.5-1 mm (beam energy up to 4.5 MeV) and directed onto the surface, for example, a manuscript, then the protons begin to excite the atoms of the metals that make up the paint or ink. You don’t have to worry about preserving ancient documents: the radiation energy does not exceed the energy of a 100-watt light bulb; the book is illuminated from a distance of half a meter!

Excited atoms emit X-rays, which are recorded by sensitive detectors. The signals from the detectors are then sent to a computer for analysis.

Using the PIRE method, a 42-line Bible printed by the Gutenberg printing house, located in the Harvard University library, was recently “read.”

They found out that Gutenberg used large amounts of lead and copper for his printing ink. Gutenberg's printing assistants mixed new batches of paint literally every day. The Bible consisted of several notebooks. Up to six notebooks were printed simultaneously every day. At first Biblud was 40 lines, but then they switched to 42 lines. Hyphenations were not allowed - only two word hyphenations were allowed for the entire text! After the first sheet was printed on both sides, they began printing the 2nd and 120th, etc. This made it possible to work on six notebooks at the same time, although it required very precise adherence to the “technology” revealed using the PIRE method. A specialist from Louvart also interned in the laboratory where the examination of the Gutenberg Bible was carried out. The museum wants to examine Gutenberg's 36-line Bible, which is printed identically to the 42-line version and is kept in the Bibliothèque Nationale de France.

With the help of PIRE, it was possible to “rehabilitate” the famous “map of Vinland”. Vinland - “Land of Grapes” - the Normans called in their sagas a distant land that lay beyond Greenland, rich in wild grapes. It was allegedly discovered by the legendary Eric the Red, who is considered today the discoverer of America.

The map of Vinland entered scientific circulation in the early 60s of our century. Scientists were amazed by the amazing image of Greenland, not much different from the modern one. It was believed that such precision cartography was simply impossible to add in the days of the brave Vikings.

In 1974, the card was declared a fake based on analyzes conducted by a team of scientists at the University of Chicago. Analyzes showed a high content of titanium descriptions in the ink that was used to draw lines on the map.

And now PIRE has proven that at least a third of the lines, and even more, are written on the map with ink in which the titanium content does not exceed the “norm”, that is, the minimum that can be considered a natural impurity. In other lines, titanium was completely absent!

Does this mean that the card's authenticity has been proven? No, it was simply shown that titanium descriptions were not used for the ink, i.e. the ink is not modern. But someone could have made ink using a medieval recipe. So far, physics cannot prove the complete authenticity of the card. Now, if only she would allow us to build a time machine...
