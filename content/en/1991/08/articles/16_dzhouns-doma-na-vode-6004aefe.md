Houses on the water
D. Jones

There is a long history of popish architecture and irrational urban planning on the THOUGHT that houses should be made movable, so that in case of redevelopment there is no need to destroy old buildings. To move houses, it would be most convenient to use air cushion sights, but since the pressure exerted by buildings on the supporting surface is 0.02-2 atm, an air cushion is unlikely to provide the required lifting force. In addition, moving the building would be accompanied by unimaginable noise. Therefore, Daedalus intends to use air and water together, the density of which is 1000 times higher. A water-supported platform could generate significant lift with relatively little water consumption. Unfortunately, the water will flood the entire street unless the flow is somehow diverted. Daedalus proposes equipping the platform with a water-draining peg that collects water and returns it to the system. Daedalus designs buildings equipped with tanks, pumps and everything necessary to turn them into self-propelled structures in a matter of minutes. Such a system can also be equipped in many important buildings erected on shallow or “floating” foundations.

Clapping loudly, these urban super-water striders will glide from place to place, obeying the whims of the architectural model: a high-rise building will give way to a multi-row view, and cottages and pavilions will cluster at the foot of the giants. Factories will travel around the country in search of skilled workers or government subsidies; Empty multi-storey offices will crawl into central London, where the demand for them is enormous, and old offices with their staff will leave their homes, yielding to the demands of the redevelopment commission. Gothic gutters and country villas will be located separately or interspersed, communicating with current government policies (if they are simply left alone, then over time they will naturally come to an equilibrium). City designers will be able not only to learn from their mistakes, but also to correct them. Only city mapmakers and utility workers would probably curse the new Utopia.

(New Scientist, February 3, 1972)
