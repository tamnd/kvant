Ozone, volcanoes and...
rockets

Doctor of Technical Sciences
V. BURDAKOV

Among the many economic problems common in our time, the ozone problem occupies a special place. If there is no ozone in the earth’s atmosphere, then solar hard UV radiation (wavelength $\lambda<300$ nm) will destroy the phytoplankton of the oceans and seas, up to 70% of the oxygen from carbon dioxide, increasingly produced by living organisms, industry and transport, returns to our earth’s atmosphere. Forests and plants return up to 30% oxygen to the atmosphere and are also rich in ultraviolet radiation. Finally, people, of whom there are now more than 5.3 billion on Earth and most of whom love to bask in the sun, are increasingly putting themselves in danger. Tanning is provided by soft UV radiation ($\lambda>300$ nm), called erythemal. It is very useful because it produces vitamin D in the body. When combined with hard (bactericidal) radiation, erythema rays can cause not only painful burns, but also more dangerous consequences. Doctors and biologists believe that a decrease in the concentration of stratospheric ozone by just 1% leads to an increase in skin cancer by 6% and increases susceptibility to other diseases, especially skin, pulmonary and allergic ones.

A variety of measurement methods show that the amount of stratospheric ozone in the earth's atmosphere tends to gradually decrease! It’s good that there are compensating processes - ozone, disappearing at its usual highs, begins to be produced closer to the troposphere (Fig. 1), where the air density is significant.

But environmental specialists sounded the alarm - after the polar rainfall (i.e. in October), an “ozone hole” began to appear over Antarctica - a sharp decrease in ozone concentration in the entire atmospheric column. Moreover, signs of “ozone holes” began to be detected in the Northern Hemisphere, but not at the pole, but approximately at the latitude of Leningrad.

New phenomena, as always, give rise to a lot of hypotheses. There was no shortage of them, not this time. They blamed everything for the “ozone holes”: the earth’s magnetic field, freons (chlorinated hydrocarbons, used as a heat transfer agent in refrigerators), and even the international “ether” flowing around the Earth from its North Pole.

Nibaulier seems to have a realistic version that connects the phenomenon with the existence of stable outlets in the earth’s atmosphere: one large one in the Antarctic region and three smaller ones in the North Pole region. While ozone existed at “its” heights, everything was fine, but when the maximum of its concentration dropped into the zone of more intense winds (Fig. 2), atmospheric polar outlets began to block the ozone’s path to the Earth’s poles.

There are other equally dangerous reasons for the disappearance of ozone, for example, volcanoes and... rockets. Volcanoes deliver ozone-depleting substances to a height of up to 12 km, from where they rise into the stratosphere due to air turbulence. Rockets go directly into the ozone layer.

Currently, the largest number of launches of the package are carried out by the military. Short- and medium-range military packages, as well as many strategic packages, have solid propellant propulsion systems, the emission products of which contain large quantities of azrosols, chlorine and its compounds, as well as nitrogen oxides, i.e., the main ozone destroyers. Unfortunately, solid fuel boosters are increasingly used in astronautics (USA, China, France, Japan). This is completely unacceptable, especially considering the tendency towards an increase in the volume of space transport and an increase in the power of package carriers. Further, promising space vehicles that do not have solid rocket boosters and operate on the cleanest fuel vapors: hydrogen with oxygen and hydrocarbon fuel with oxygen, also require the most careful economic analysis, since the duration of their stay in the cold layer is significantly longer than that of ballistic carriers (Fig. 3).

figure 4 shows the dependence of the total amount of chlorine compounds delivered to the ozonosphere by just one space transport system - the Space Shuttle). This also takes into account the break in launches due to the Challenger accident (a). The same figure shows a graph of periodic and systematic deviations of ozone concentration in the collapsed position (b). Periodic deviations caused by solar activity, and systemic deviations are causes that most likely have an antipologenic nature. From a comparison of the two graphs, it can be seen that the decrease in ozone concentration correlates with the regular launches of the Shuttle. At the same time, in April 1982, there was a powerful eruption of the mechanical volcano El Chichon, which released a large amount of azrozole, chlorides and nitrogen oxides into the earth's atmosphere. This natural event was used by some specialists as the basis for explaining the causes of “ozone holes” in Antarctica (c). But the “ozone hole” gradually disappeared, and then appeared again - after the resumption of Shuttle launches, when there were no longer large volcanic eruptions. Doesn't this prove the Shuttle's involvement in the ozone hole? Be that as it may, one thing is clear: natural and antipologenic processes must be studied together, since only in this case will it be possible to properly coordinate measures to protect the environment. And the sooner this is done by the international community, the more effective the protection of the measure will be.

The anthropogenic contribution to our environmental disaster is steadily increasing. This can be seen at least in the example of the destruction of the ozone layer (Fig. 5). Further, the numerous tests of nuclear weapons in the atmosphere that were carried out in the 60s, from the point of view of destruction of the ozone layer, are nothing compared to the current situation. An even more difficult picture awaits people in the future world if scientific and technological progress is not brought under the control of information and environmental services.
