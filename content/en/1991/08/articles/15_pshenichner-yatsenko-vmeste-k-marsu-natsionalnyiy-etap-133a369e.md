national stage of the international competition “Together to Mars!” completed.*) From June 17 to 21, the final was held in Moscow, the winners of the composition were determined and awards were given. Before reporting on the most interesting works, we will tell you how the competitive selection took place.

In total, the Organizing Committee received about 350 works (not as many as we expected!). Depending on the topic, they were examined at the Moscow Aviation Institute, Moscow State Technical University. N. E. Bauman, at the State Astronomical Institute named after. P.K. Sternberg. Each work submitted for consideration was accompanied by a scorecard in which three specialists entered their grades. As a result, the Organizing Committee was presented with lists of authors recommended for participation in the final part of the competition. After discussing all the candidates at a joint meeting of the jury and the Organizing Committee, which was held under the presentation of Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences M. Ya. Marov, taking into account the experts, the list of participants in the final was approved.

The financiers gathered near Moscow at the Olymppets Center for International Cooperation. Here new challenges awaited them. On the first day, all final participants defended their projects in sections. 19 of the best were identified, who the next day made presentations to experts at the Moscow Aviation Institute and Moscow State Technical University. On the third day in the evening, the general jury, headed by the representative of the Scientific and Technical Council of the USSR Space Science Federation, Professor V.P. Senkevich, conducted an interview with the authors of the most protected stage: Ira Litvyakova from Vitebsk, Sergey Stenyuk from Donetsk and Maxim Shtanev from Kharkov. Their work was sent to the Planetary Society of the USA. Now, based on an analysis of all submitted works, the international jury will determine 20 participants in the final stage in Washington.

Participants in the finals in Moscow were loaded with diplomas; the authors of the nine best works were presented with valuable gifts and honorary diplomas from the USSR Space Federation. The financiers met with scientists and designers of space technology,

The result of such activity could be a multi-variant posture project for the exploration and development of Mars.

A few words about the works that have received recognition from the site.

A simple and clear model was presented by Ira Litvyakova. When flying to Mars, a situation may arise when a comet of interest for scientific research eats up not far from the ship. In such a case, it is necessary to have on board the ship an automatic device capable of merging with a comet and landing on the surface of its nucleus. According to Ira’s calculations, a very low speed is needed to take off from the surface of the nucleus of a non-sick comet. This speed can be imparted to the device by a compressed spring. The work provides a list of comets passing pergels in 1994-2000, and analyzes the probability of encountering a comet. To continue this work, it is necessary to more closely analyze the trajectories of comets and the possibility of merging with it a probe sent from an international ship.

The work of Pavel Zaychuk (Krasnoirsk) showed that approximate calculation of trajectories can be carried out not only by specialists. Pavel gives a calculation of various flight options to Mars and comes to the conclusion that it is better to assemble the Mars expeditionary complex in a high near-Earth orbit.

With this choice of launch and return points, the required amount of fuel on the interplanetary ship decreases. All the difficulties involved in rebuilding the systems that ensure the assembly and maintenance of the spacecraft in orbit.

One of the server difficulties when working in high Earth orbit is the need to be protected from radiation fields. When flying along an interplanetary trajectory, there is less radiation, but it also creates a server problem due to the long flight duration.

Maxim Shtangev from Kharkov dedicated his work to anti-radiation protection of the ship. He proposes to create a magnetic field around the ship, similar to the Earth’s magnetic field, which will reliably protect us from cosmic radiation. The magnetic coil that created the field is proposed to be made from a high-temperature superconductor. Since the magnetic field does not do work on a charged particle moving in it, the energy required to maintain the current in the coil is theoretically zero. It is enough to create a powerful current in the coil when the ship starts. To obtain the required current, Maxim proposes to use the packet motor “in combination” as a magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) generator. Such an MHD generator is capable of generating a huge direct current for a short time. The few seconds during which modern MHD generators operate are enough to start the superconducting circuit. The paper presents two options for radiation protection for specific spacecraft projects proposed for flight to Mars. The first option is for a torus-shaped spacecraft. A special layer of superconducting material must be applied to the surface of the torus housing. After the current is sewn into the circuit around the ship, a magnetic field appears in the shape of an apple. The second, more economical, protection option is an anti-radiation magnetic umbrella. It is a flat torid coil of sufficiently large area and is suitable for protecting a cylindrical ship.

Alexander Kostyrya (Dnipropetrovsk region) chose the Moon as a possible base for the launch and return of the interplanetary spacecraft to low-Earth orbit. He doesn't dwell on ballistics or technical issues. Its topic is providing food product expertise. The problems of space plant growing and poultry farming are considered in detail. By the way, the idea has been expressed that plants and animals grown on the Moon should cope better with the conditions of interplanetary flight than those grown on Earth. The issue of growing Japanese quails in space is considered in particular detail (this unpretentious poultry has long been the object of experiments in orbit). The current installation for their cultivation, created by Alexander’s circle mates - members of the “Coeval” club of SPTU No. 76 of the Mezhevsky district of the Dnepropetrovsk region, is described.

Models of the proposed devices were presented by many participants in the competition. Although the competition was based on an idea, not a model, such “voluminous illustrations” aroused constant interest. Ideas successfully illustrated by models include the work of Grigory Shifrin (Dnepropetrovsk). Shifrin proposes to make a take-off and landing model of the Martian ship in the form of a discus with a large area of ​​the glue and aerodynamic rudders. Due to the small moments of inertia, the diskette is easily controllable, has a critical angle of attack of about 45°, and even with such tilts it does not fall into a tailspin, but can safely glide without using the engines to a pre-selected site.

Most of the work submitted to the competition was devoted to a compiler description of the Mars expedition as a whole. Among them, the project of Sergei Stepiok (Donetsk) stands out for its detailed elaboration. Crew - 4 people, duration of the expedition - 520 days with a 20-day stay on the surface of Mars, propulsion - electric propulsion. For these initial conditions, all the main components of the ship are described in detail in the five-page report.

In general, the totality of the competition works allows us to see the features of the future Mars expedition, which makes it possible to hope for the creation of a general, carefully developed project in several versions.

The competition is over, the gifts have been given, but the work can continue. The organizing committee and the jury are interested to know if there are any readers «Kvant» who want to take part.
