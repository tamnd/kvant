A program that prevents computer crashes

A representative of one of the large software development companies announced the creation of a special program that allows almost zero computer downtime caused by failures; uses a very simple method and is a development of a long-known approach. The company's OREMA program (from the Latin "omemus" - "let's pray") sends out prayers at certain intervals for the safety of memory contents, the normal functioning of disks and tape drives, as well as other most frequently failing equipment.

Being literary at its core, OREMA uses standard prayers and prayers stored on external media in the form of a program implemented in Latin, Fortran and other languages. The service is held regularly, three times a day, automatically; the operator’s participation is reduced only to installing the tapes and entering ritual commands from the remote control such as “From now on and forever and ever” or “Amen”.

Prayers in Latin can first address peripheral equipment and then, through internal routines, again address the central process, while prayers in Fortran and other languages ​​address directly the central process.

The supplied prayer software covers all currently known causes of possible failures. In addition, a special function is provided that allows you to modify the OREMA package and store the additional prayer entered after the last “Amen” block. To close institutions, a special package of hidden prayers is issued.

During trial runs on some types of computers, it was possible to reduce equipment downtime by an average of 98.2%. The manufacturer's representative considered it his duty to emphasize that OREMA currently only prevents failures caused by equipment failures. Active work will be carried out to create a more advanced version - the “Do not sin!” program, which allows you to eliminate operator errors and other human stupidity.

W. Minkler
