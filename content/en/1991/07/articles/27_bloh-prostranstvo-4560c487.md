Space
Werner Heisenberg once made a very telling remark. One day while walking we started talking about space. I had just read Weil's book Space, Time, Materials, and under its influence I proudly declared that space is simply a field of linear operations.
“Nonsense,” Heisenberg replied, “there is space in the head and birds fly through it.”
Felix Vloch
(Physics Today, 1976, 29 (12) 27)

Proof by infection
Professor Nernst loved to pepper his lectures with funny stories, anecdotes and jokes. We students really liked it, and I remember that even his assistant, replacing the professor at lectures, said: “Here Professor Nernst tells such and such a story.”
Discussing the first and second laws of thermodynamics, Nernst explained: “As you remember, the first fundamental law of thermodynamics was formed and theoretically substantiated by three physicists - Robert Mayer, James Pressot Joule and Hermann Von Helmholtz; the second law of thermodynamics was formed by two great physicists - Rudolf Clausius and William Thomson; I discovered the third law on my own. This proves that there cannot be a fourth fundamental law of thermodynamics.”
Edgar V. Kucher
(From the book “More Random Walks in Science”, 1982, London, Institute of Physics)

Paulie in Paradise
Wolfgang Pauli was weakened by his ability to instantly penetrate into the essence of the theoretical constructions of his colleagues and find errors and ambiguities in them. Surely God was impatiently waiting for him in paradise, where Pauli arrived in 1958.
“I believe,” said the Lord, “there is a lot in physics that remains incomprehensible to you, and you would like to know the truth.”
“Yes, creator,” answered Pauli. “To be honest, I have been insanely endowed with the endless merits of my colleagues.” For example, I have been worried all my life about why the mass of a proton is 1836.11 times greater than the mass of an electron, and they have the same charges. Where does such a ridiculous number come from? Surely there is some explanation for this. However, all theories devoted to this problem are not worth a penny.
“Well, here’s the explanation given on the appendix.”

“The familiar language of quantum mechanics of the 20th century,” said the Lord and handed Pauli a sheaf of written sheets.
He greedily ran through what he had written, looked at the first page, then at the fourth, and returned the packet with a sigh:
- Oh my God, that’s not it again!
(From the book “Treasury of Humor” by A. Asimov, London, Woburn Press, 1971)
