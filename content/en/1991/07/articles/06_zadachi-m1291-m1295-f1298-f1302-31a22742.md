M1291 — M1295, F1298 — F1302

M1291. Prove that a) in a regular 12-gon, 6) in a regular 54-gon, find 4 diagonals that do not pass through the center of the polygon and intersect at one point.

S. Tokarev

M1292. A council of 2,000 detats decided to approve a state budget containing 200 items of expenditure. Each detail prepared its own draft budget, in which it indicated for each stat the maximum permissible, in its opinion, amount of expenses, so that the total amount of expenses does not exceed a given value S. For each stat, the council approves the most significant expenditure, which agreed to allocate at least k detats. For each smallest k, can it be guaranteed that the total amount of approved expenses will not exceed S?

I. Serdeeev

M1293. There are two non-intersecting circles in this angle. Triangle ABC is located between the circles so that its vertices lie on the sides of the angle, and equal sides AB and AC touch the corresponding circles. Prove that the sum of the radii of the circles is equal to the height of the triangle dropped from vertex A.

I. Sharitsin

M1294. A cube measuring 10x10x10 is made up of 500 black and 500 white cups in a checkerboard pattern (cubes adjacent to each other have different colors). 100 cups were taken from this cube in such a way that each of the 300 measuring 1x1x10, parallel to some edge of the cube, was missing exactly one cube. Prove that the number of black cups drawn is divisible by 4.

A. Spivak

M1295. On a rectangular screen of size $m \times n$, placed on single cells, more than $(m-1)(n-1)$ cells glow. If in any square $2 \times 2$ three cells are not lit, then after a while the fourth one will go out too. Prove that, nevertheless, at least one cell will always glow on the screen.

A. Chasovskikh

F1298. A heavy ball was thrown at speed $v_0$. Considering the resistance force proportional to the speed of the ball, find the length of time after which the ball will fall to the point from where it was thrown. At speed $v_0$ the resistance force is $F = a v_0 \gg m g$ (here $a$ is the proportionality coefficient, $m$ is the mass of the ball).

A. Andrianov

F1299. A black ball with a radius of $r=1$ mm is suspended on a thin thread $l=1$ m long. The entire system is placed in an evacuated glass tube with argon at a pressure of $p_0=0,1$ Pa. The ball is illuminated with a horizontal beam of light, the energy flux density of which is equal to $w_0=100$ J/$\mathrm{m}^2$ s. Estimate the deviation of the ball from its equilibrium position under the influence of light. Neglect the thermal conductivity of the ball. Consider that an absolute black body, heated to an absolute temperature of $T$, emits energy equal to $\sigma T^4$ per unit surface area per unit time, where $\sigma=5,67 \cdot 10^{-8}$ W/$\mathrm{m}^2$·K$^4$. The gas temperature in the tube is constant and equal to $T_0=293$ K. The molar mass of argon is $M=0,04$ kg/mol, the density of the ball is $\rho=1$ g/cm$^3$.

A. Semenov

F1300. It is known that when an uncharged conducting sphere is introduced into a uniform electric field of intense $E_0$, the field strength near the point $A$ (see figure) turns out to be equal to $3E_0$, and near the point $B$ - zero. Determine the field strength near point $B$. Find the total charge induced on the hemisphere $BAB$. The radius of the sphere is $R$.

V. Mozhaev

F1301. Three identical windings are symmetrically located on the torid core, forming a transformer. One of the windings was connected to an alternating voltage source, the second was left open, and a voltmeter was connected to the third. It turned out that the voltmeter in this case shows the source voltage. What will it show if the second winding is short-circuited? Consider the resistance of the windings to be negligible, the voltmeter and the source to be ideal, and the magnetic permeability of the core to be unchanged at different values ​​of magnetic induction.

A. Andrianov

F1302. A body of mass $M$ leaning against the wall is subjected to a force perpendicular to the wall that varies according to the harmonic law $F=F_0\cos \omega t$. A weightless spring is placed between the body and the wall. What should its rigidity be for the amplitude of the force acting on the wall to differ from $F_0$ by a factor of 3?

I. Vorobiev
