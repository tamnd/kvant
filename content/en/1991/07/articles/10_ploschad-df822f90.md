A master tiler is a person who, in the process of his work, is constantly measuring areas. Having covered the wall with tiles, he can easily determine the area of ​​the wall (in slabs) by counting the number of tiles laid. But in fact, he always solves the inverse problem: first he measures the area of ​​the wall, and then calculates the required number of tiles.
The “tile maker’s method” also turns out to be useful when calculating the areas of complex figures. Let's put a square grid on the paper and put it on the figures. Then its area will be no less than the number of grid squares that lie entirely inside the figure, and no more than the number of cells that have common points with this figure. This grid is called a palette.
