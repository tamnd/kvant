Suppose that among the numbers $r_1, r_2, ..., r_n$ there are only $m$ different. Note that only one $k$ can satisfy the equality $r_{k+1} = r_k$ (when $a_{k+1} = n$). For all other $k$ from 1 to $n-1$ the differences $r_{k+1} - r_k$ are distinct numbers. But from $m$ different numbers one can make only $m(m-1)$ different ordered pairs and thus no more than $m(m-1)$ different differences. And there should be at least $n-2$. Thus, $m(m-1) \geq n-2$. If $m$ were less than $\sqrt{n}$, then the result would be (for $n \geq 4$) a contradiction: $n - \sqrt{n} > n-2$. For $n=3$ it is easy to verify by an undefined check that among the residue tracks $r_1, r_2, r_3$ there are always at least two different ones. Thus, the statement is proven.

F1278. A projectile flying vertically exploded at maximum height. Projectile fragments fall to the ground during a period of time t. Find the speed of the fragments at the moment of explosion.

The speed of a projectile launched upward at the top point is zero. We will assume that the sedges fly in all directions with the same speed v. Then the maximum difference in the falling time - it corresponds to the time t of the sedges falling onto the ground - will be for the sedges flying vertically. A fragment flying upward after an explosion with a speed v, after a time 2v/g ends up at the point of explosion, and its speed will be equal to v and directed downward. This means that the time difference is exactly equal to 2v/g=t, whence
$$v = gt/2.$$
Note that if the speeds of the fragments are different, then nothing definite can be said.

F1282. A cube with an edge of $a = 10$ cm and a mass of $M = 1$ kg is suspended on a spring of stiffness $k = 400$ N/m so that its base is parallel to the ground. A flow of elastic balls is directed from below onto the cube, with a speed of $v_0 = 20$ m/s at the height of the first initial position of the base of the cube. The cube begins to oscillate, moving progressively. Find the period of these oscillations. Estimate the time during which the vibration amplitude will decrease by a factor of 2.

The mass of the cube is large compared to the mass of the balls; This means that after absolute control, the speed of the ball relative to the cube remains practically unchanged. The momentum transferred to the cube when the ball hits (see figure) is equal to

$$P = 2m(v_0 - v).$$

Number of beats in time $\Delta t$ yes

$$N = (v_0 - v)a^2n\Delta t.$$

The force acting on the cube from the flow of balls is equal to

$$F = \frac{NP}{\Delta t} = 2m(v_0 - v)^2a^2n \approx 2ma^2nv_0^2 - 4ma^2nv_0v.$$

ka, the second is the resistance force, proportional to the speed $v$. The cube is also subject to elastic force from the spring and gravity. For the displacement $x$ relative to the equilibrium position, we obtain the equation

$$Mx'' + 4x'v_0mna^2 + kx = 0.$$

This is the equation of damped oscillations. General methods for solving such equations are known, but in our case everything is quite simple - the attenuation can be considered small (we will do so, and then check the validity of this assumption). So the period of oscillation is equal to

$$T \approx 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{M}{k}} \approx 0,31 \text{ с.}$$

During one period it can be considered (with slight attenuation)

$$x = x_m \cos \left( \frac{2\pi}{T} t + \varphi \right).$$

The work of resistance forces for one period in this case can be calculated as follows. For a short period of time $\Delta t$ the work done by force $F = \alpha v$ is equal to

$$\Delta A = F\Delta x = \alpha vv\Delta t = \alpha v^2\Delta t.$$

The work per period can be found by knowing the dependence of the square of the speed on time:

$$\Delta A = \alpha (v_m \cos \omega t)^2 \Delta t,$$

$$A = \alpha \left( (v_m \cos \omega t)^2 \right)_{\text{cp}} T = \alpha \frac{v_m^2}{2} T.$$

In this case, the maximum energy of the system is

$$W = \frac{Mv_m^2}{2},$$

and its relative change is

$$\frac{\Delta W}{W} = \frac{A}{W} = \frac{\alpha v_m^2 T/2}{Mv_m^2/2} = \frac{\alpha T}{M} \approx 0,24.$$

When the amplitude decreases by a factor of 2, the energy decreases by a factor of 4. Then the required number of periods can be determined as follows:

$$\left( 1 - \frac{\Delta W}{W} \right)^K = \frac{1}{4} \Rightarrow K = 5,$$

and the decay time turns out to be equal

$$\tau \approx KT \approx 1,5 \text{ с.}$$
