Suppose that among the numbers $r_1, r_2, ..., r_n$ there are only $m$ different. Note that only one $k$ can satisfy the equality $r_{k+1} = r_k$ (when $a_{k+1} = n$). For all other $k$ from 1 to $n-1$ the differences $r_{k+1} - r_k$ are distinct numbers. But from $m$ different numbers one can make only $m(m-1)$ different ordered pairs and thus no more than $m(m-1)$ different differences. And there should be at least $n-2$. Thus, $m(m-1) \geq n-2$. If $m$ were less than $\sqrt{n}$, then the result would be (for $n \geq 4$) a contradiction: $n - \sqrt{n} > n-2$. For $n=3$ it is easy to verify by an undefined check that among the residue tracks $r_1, r_2, r_3$ there are always at least two different ones. Thus, the statement is proven.
