About bioelectrets

At the beginning of the article, we said that electrets do not occur in nature. However, this is not entirely true. It turns out that a living human or animal organism uses the electretium effect. Research by scientists has shown that bioelectrets exist. The inner walls of blood vessels carry an associated negative charge. The potential created by bioelectrity is called desatopotential by doctors. Blood elements - red blood cells, platelets, leukocytes - are also negatively charged. Perhaps this explains the fact that blood, despite its enormous molecular weight, easily passes through the thinnest capillaries.

As soon as the disatoppotential disappears (due to illness, damage to a vessel), the blood clots and a blood clot forms. This is how the body's defense works. (The soluble filrinogen in the blood becomes insoluble, forms many threads in which the blood form elements become entangled, the wound heals, and a plug is formed that prevents further blood from flowing out of the vessel.)

On the other hand, the formation of blood clots inside the vessels often leads to a serious disease - thrombophlebitis, the vein becomes clogged with a blood clot, the vessels swell and swollen, clearly diseased veins are visible under the skin.

Artificial vessels—prosthetic blood vessels—have been made for a long time (Fig. 1). They are woven like stockings and made corrugated. In the case of serbez disease, the affected vessels are replaced with prostheses.

But since the artificial vessel is a foreign body and does not carry disatoppotential, blood clots form on its walls.

It turns out that with the help of electrets it is possible to promote disatoppotential. At the Institute of Transplantology of Artificial Organs and Tissues, a series of experiments was carried out in the body (as doctors say, in vitro), and then on experimental dogs (in vivo).

Instead of a vessel, in one case there was a tube sewn in, on the inner side of which the desatopotential was modeled with a negative electretin charge, in the second case a tube without a charge, in the third case with a positive charge. A tube (artificial vessel) with a positive charge became completely clogged with a blood clot after three days; without a charge, it did not clog for ten days. In a tube with a simulated disatoppotential, a thrombus did not form.
