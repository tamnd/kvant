We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than September 15, 1991 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 7-91” and the numbers of the problem whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1291” or “F1298”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solution).

Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the Skoda number and the class in which you are a teacher.

Tasks

M1291 — M1295, F1298 — F1302

M1291. Prove that a) in a regular 12-gon, 6) in a regular 54-gon, find 4 diagonals that do not pass through the center of the polygon and intersect at one point.

S. Tokarev

M1292. A council of 2,000 detats decided to approve a state budget containing 200 items of expenditure. Each detail prepared its own draft budget, in which it indicated for each stat the maximum permissible, in its opinion, amount of expenses, so that the total amount of expenses does not exceed a given value S. For each stat, the council approves the most significant expenditure, which agreed to allocate at least k detats. For each smallest k, can it be guaranteed that the total amount of approved expenses will not exceed S?

I. Serdeeev

M1293. There are two non-intersecting circles in this angle. Triangle ABC is located between the circles so that its vertices lie on the sides of the angle, and equal sides AB and AC touch the corresponding circles. Prove that the sum of the radii of the circles is equal to the height of the triangle dropped from vertex A.

I. Sharitsin

M1294. A cube measuring 10x10x10 is made up of 500 black and 500 white cups in a checkerboard pattern (cubes adjacent to each other have different colors). 100 cups were taken from this cube in such a way that each of the 300 measuring 1x1x10, parallel to some edge of the cube, was missing exactly one cube. Prove that the number of black cups drawn is divisible by 4.

A. Spivak

M1295. On a rectangular screen of size $m \times n$, placed on single cells, more than $(m-1)(n-1)$ cells glow. If in any square $2 \times 2$ three cells are not lit, then after a while the fourth one will go out too. Prove that, nevertheless, at least one cell will always glow on the screen.

A. Chasovskikh

F1298. A heavy ball was thrown at speed $v_0$. Considering the resistance force proportional to the speed of the ball, find the length of time after which the ball will fall to the point from where it was thrown. At speed $v_0$ the resistance force is $F = a v_0 \gg m g$ (here $a$ is the proportionality coefficient, $m$ is the mass of the ball).

A. Andrianov
