STRAIGHT OF A RING...

V. CHVANOV

What is an analogy?

In science there are many ways to pose and solve problems. Some of them have a fairly studied area of ​​application - any section of a given science or even a type of problem. Others - such as analysis, synthesis, generalization - are universal and can be used with equal success in various fields of science.

One such general method of meaning is analogy. Its essence is in comparing similar properties in objects that are different in nature. Thus, if it is known that object A has a set of some properties a, b, c, and object B has a set of its properties a* and b*, then we can assume that B also has property c*.

Reasoning by analogy cannot do without agreement and intuition. This is the weakness of this research method, but this is also its strength. It is thanks to this feature that analogy allows you to transfer ideas from one area of ​​​​knowledge to others, seemingly completely unrelated to it. This often leads to the solution of long-standing problems that had not been resolved until then, or to the emergence of new ones.

In algebra, success can lead to a transition to another number system, in geometry - a transition from the plane to space. Less obvious is the analogy between a straight line and a circle. This article illustrates it.

Ring ruler

The most interesting problem is one that has not yet been solved by anyone. It provides an opportunity to truly experience the work of independent creativity. Many mathematicians create collections of unsolved problems according to their taste. The model began with the following tasks: 1.

Task 1. For what k can the circle of the diapyna n=k²-k+1 (n, k are integers) be divided by k points into k duegs so that for any m=1, 2, ..., k²-k the dueg of the diapyna m can be found?

Bydem call a circle that satisfies the conditions of the problem an (n, k)-universal ring. A special case of this problem was published in «Kvant» No. 4 for 1979 (problem M558): “There are k>1 black sectors in a circle, the angle of each of which is less than 180°/(k²-k+1). Prove that the circle can be rotated around the center O so that all the black sectors go into the white part of the circle.”

The simplicity of the formulation seemed to hint at the simplicity of the solution. However, after reviewing the known results (some of them are depicted in figure 1), I realized that the task was not easy.

But while looking through old issues «Kvant», I came across problem M399 («Kvant» No. 8, 1976):

Task 2. On a segment of distance 7 you can put five points so that for any m=1, 2, ..., 7 you find two of these five points at a distance m.

Try to find out what is the maximum number k(n) of points that need to be placed on a segment of distance n, so that for any m=1, 2, ..., n, two of these five points are found at a distance m.

a) Solve this problem for the first few values ​​of n (we know k(n) for n≤13).
