The phone rang, but I didn’t pick up the phone. What if it's from the big city? Or Bernard? Or maybe the police. I can easily imagine how I will explain to him. Genetron will completely refuse to publish my story. Bernard will generally say that he knows nothing.

I had incredible tension throughout my whole body, my manhood was shrinking into a tight knot from tension and... I don’t even know what to call this feeling. The feeling that comes after...

Carrying out gencide?
A completely wild thought. I couldn't believe that I had killed hundreds of triplons of intelligent beings with my own hands. Destroyed an entire galaxy... A ridiculous accusation. But it wasn't funny to me at all.

Goraddo found it easier to believe that I had killed a man, my friend. Smoke, a melted lamp frame, a socket spreading like a puddle of plastic, a burnt wire...

Virgil!
I threw him a tanning lamp that was turned on. I'm still nauseous. Dreams, cities, violence against Gail (what, I wonder, happened to his former girlfriend, Kandis?). Water flowing into a pipe. Galaxies scattered around us. Endless horror... But at the same time - enormous potential for beauty. A new form of life, simbus, transformation.

Did I kill them all? For a moment I panicked. Tomorrow, I thought, I’ll go there and clean out the apartment. I'll think of something. I didn't even remember Bernard.

When Gail came home, I was sleeping on the couch. I got up feeling very bad, and she immediately noticed it.

- Are you sick? - Gail asked worriedly, sitting down on the edge.

I shook my head.

Gail put her hand on my forehead.
- Edward, you have a fever. Very tall.

I dragged myself to the bathroom and looked at myself in the mirror. Gale stopped behind me.

- What is this? she asked.

The collar of my shirt covered my entire neck with white lines. Like on the highway. Apparently, they entered my body a long time ago, several days ago.

“Wet palms...” I muttered.

It's surprising that this didn't occur to me earlier.

Obviously we almost died. At first I still tried to fight, but literally after a few minutes I weakened and insisted that I could no longer move. Gail found herself in the same state an hour later.

I was lying on the couch in the living room, all wet with sweat. Gail is on the couch. Her face became white, like talc, her eyes closed - like a corpse in a balsamist’s laboratory. For some time it seemed to me that she really died. And even in that helpless, sick state, I was still angry at myself and felt guilty for my weakness, for my failure to think in time about all the possible consequences. But soon there was no strength left for this either. I couldn't even blink; so I closed my eyes and just waited.

In the arms and legs, the rhythm of some activity was clearly wrong. With each pump of blood, a sound arose inside me, similar to the sound of an orchestra of thousands of musicians playing different integral fragments of several symbols. Music that sounded in the blood... Gradually the sound became sharper, but at the same time more harmonious: the accumulation of active waves subsided, dividing into separate harmonic signals.

These signals seemed to grow into me, into the rhythm of my own heart.

First they subjugated our immune responses. The war - and it really was a war, the like of which had never been known on Earth, a war with the trillions participating in the comparisons - lasted about two days.

By the time I found the strength to reach the kitchen faucet, they were already working on my brain, trying to crack the codes and find the god hiding in the protoplasm. I drank and drank until I felt nauseous, then I drank more, in slow sips, and took the glass of water to Gail. She pressed it to her chapped lips and began to drink greedily. Her eyes turned red, yellowish dirty crumbs fluttered around. But now the skin has returned to some semblance of a normal shade. A few minutes later we were already done at the kitchen table and languidly chewing pshsha.

- What the hell happened to us? - she asked first thing.

I didn’t have the strength to oblige, and I just shook my head. Then he peeled the orange and divided it into two.

“We need to call a doctor,” she said.

But I knew we wouldn't do it. I had already begun to receive messages from them, from which it became clear that the fear of freedom that had arisen among us was false.
