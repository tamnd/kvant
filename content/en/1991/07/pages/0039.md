ST ELM'S FIRE
AT YOUR HOME

A. PANTSULAYA

If you like books about sea voyages, adventures, caravels, brigs and pirates, then you’ve probably read about how sometimes strange luminous points appear on ships’ yards, shrouds, bowsprits and even sailboats - the lights of Saint Elmo. Mystery has long shrouded these lights. No one even really knew whether they promised grief or good luck. One of the last conquistadors, Nikolai Gumilev, decorated the ship “Flying Dutchman” with them:

"But a sign of sadness and misfortune,
The lights of Saint Elmo glow,
Dotted with its board and gear.”

François Rabelais, the biographer of the cheerful fat giants Gagrantua and Pantagruel, looked more optimistically at the famous lights. During a storm, the travelers' ship was close to destruction, and only the appearance of the lights of Saint Elmo instilled confidence in the sailors and, ultimately, helped them escape.

In our time, the heyday of science and technology, you can easily get the lights of St. Elmo without going on a sea voyage, without throwing in your lot with pyramids and giants, and without even leaving your apartment. What is needed for this? It turns out - a piezoelectric sparkler for a gas stove. Lighters come in different models, but they all have common elements - a key and an electrode isolated from the body (the second electrode is the body).

But then you picked up the sparkler and pressed the key - a spark leaked between the electrode and the body with a characteristic three-dimensional sound. You have received a so-called spark discharge. You've probably seen more than once a giant natural spark - lightning, flashing between two clouds or between a cloud and the ground. Let's return, however, to the zipper.

It is known that in order for an electric spark to leak into the air, the direction between the corresponding electrodes must be very large - on the order of several keys. Where does this direction appear in the book? After all, there is no battery and it is not connected to the mains. It turns out that the whole point is in an interesting and important physical phenomenon - the piezoelectric effect (remember - the spark plug is piezoelectric). What is the essence of this phenomenon?

Let's imagine a crystal with the cell structure shown in figure 1. The shaded circles are its positive ions, the luminous ones are its negative ones. Although there are positive charges on face A and negative charges on face B, in general the crystal and its surface are electrically neutral. Let's place our crystal between the plates and compress them. Positive he 1 and negative he 2 will be pressed inside the cell, causing the protruding charges (on plane A - positive, and on plane B - negative) to decrease. In other words, a negative charge will appear on plane A, and an equally positive charge will appear on plane B, i.e. a potential difference will appear. When the crystal is stripped, the whole picture will change to the opposite - onns 1 and 2 will be pushed out of the cell, onto a flat surface.
