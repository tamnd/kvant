6) Get scores for $k(n)$ for any $n$. Estimates $(\sqrt{8n+1} + 1)/2 \leq k(n) \leq \sqrt{4n+5} - 1$ are known. Try to prove these inequalities and, if you can, find more accurate estimates.

Problems 1 and 2 are similar, as if they were closely related. The only difference is that in one case a circle is considered, and in the other a segment. The idea of ​​transferring everything that is known about task 2 to task 1 will immediately arise.

For brevity, we will call a segment of length $n$, placed as required in the conditions of Problem 2, a universal ruler of length $n$.

In the article by A. Savin “From a school problem to a problem” («Kvant», No. 12, 1976), I outline the solution to this problem. The upper bound - $k(n) \leq \sqrt{4n+5} - 1$ - was obtained there from the following construction of a universal ruler of arbitrary length $n$:

$$\frac{1, 1, ..., 1}{m \text{ раз}}, \frac{m+1, m+1, ..., m+1}{l \text{ раз}}.$$

If $n < m+l(m+1)$, then the last segment is taken correspondingly shorter than $(m+1)$, while the ruler remains universal.

Soon I managed to build successful ruler designs for some $n$.

$$L_1 = \begin{cases} 1, 2, 2, 5, \\ 3, 1, 2 \end{cases}$$

$$L_2 = 1, 2, \begin{cases} 2, 2, 7, 7, \\ 5, 5, 1, 2, 2 \end{cases}$$

$$L_3 = 1, 2, 2, 2, \begin{cases} 2, 9, 9, 9, \\ 7, 7, 7, 1, 2, 2, 2 \end{cases}$$

$$L_l = 1, \begin{cases} 2, ..., 2, \\ (l+1) \text{ раз} \end{cases}$$

$$2l+1, ..., 2l+1, 1, \begin{cases} 2, ..., 2, \\ l \text{ раз} \end{cases}$$

The segment enclosed in brackets can be repeated several times in a row, and the ruler of the new length will still be universal.

Universal sequence and universal ruler

I was unable to extend the results obtained to Problem 1. But there was another problem that turned out to be close to the previous two:

Problem 3 (M400, «Kvant» No. 8, 1976): We call the sequence of natural numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_k$ parts of terms from which we can obtain any sequence of $n$ numbers in which each of the numbers 1, 2, ..., $n$ appears once. For an arbitrary $n$, the shortest $(n, k)$-universal sequence is specified.

An example of a (4.12)-universal sequence is: 1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, 3, 1, 4, 2, 1, 3.

The solution given in the journal suggests a way to construct a certain construction of a universal sequence that gives the following estimates for the number $k(n)$:

$$(n(n+1))/2 + n - 2 \leq k(n) \leq n^2 - 2n + 4.$$
