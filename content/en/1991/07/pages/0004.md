AND ASTROPHYSICS ESPECIALLY
IMPORTANT AND INTERESTING?

Academician V. GINZBURG

Last year, an article by Soviet physicist Academician Vitaly Lazarevich Ginzburg was published in the May issue of the journal Physics Today, published by the American Physical Society. The article was called “What problems in physics and astrophysics are now particularly important and interesting?” We hope that this article turns out to be useful and interesting to you, our reader, especially schoolchildren. That's why we bring it to your attention (with minor changes). Don't be alarmed if some terms and expressions are not clear to you now. The "portrait" of modern physics, written by a famous scientist, remained in the memory and - who knows? — may influence the choice of your scientific direction.

Probably, many people paid attention to the narrowness of the horizon of the majority of even capable young physicists. Such a physicist may know the intricacies of some difficult and complex area, say, quantum field theory. But you will not get an answer to questions like what is the nature of superconductivity or ferroelectricity, what is the structure of a neutron star, or how one can hope to detect gravitational waves. Meanwhile, to have an idea about all this and much more, you don’t need to spend a lot of time and effort. In addition, is there any harm in proving that breadth of views and information are not only natural (after all, physics is so interesting!), but also very important for success in work, i.e. even pure pragmatists should strive for this breadth and information. There is no place here to discuss the principles of the noted situation and general ways of change. I will only say that to achieve the latter goal, I organized and gave special additional lectures for students, and in 1971 I published an article [1]*, which listed about 20 problems that seemed especially “hot” to me at that time. In relation to each question (problem), brief explanations were shared (the essence of the matter, unclear points, etc.) and literature was provided. Since I was counting mainly on the younger generation, I explained in some detail what is already quite obvious to a mature person. Namely, the present emphasized the conventionality and subjectivity of any list of “the most important and interesting problems” and the undoubtedly impossibility of focusing only on such problems. At the same time, I am convinced that the purpose of a number of problems and issues is indeed highlighted according to many principles - due to their potential importance for technology, special mystery, etc., etc. In addition, it is impossible to embrace everything, and for the purposes of education and broadening one’s horizons, it is simply inevitable to highlight some limited range of issues.

How young physicists responded to my article [1] and its subsequent revised edition, I still don’t know. But the collectivists wrote the article in general without enthusiasm. I will not list the conditions of the reproaches. And among the unsatisfied, there was undoubtedly one like this: the list of problems does not include the one that the critic is dealing with, “hence it is obvious” that the list is bad.
