It can be seen that $n$ will be largest when $(m - (k + 1)/2)^2$ is smallest. Therefore, for odd $k$ we put $m = (k + 1)/2$, and for even $m = k/2$ or $m = (k + 2)/2$. So, $n \geqslant (k + 1)^2/4 - 1/4 = (k^2 + 2k)/4$. Expressing $k$ in terms of $n$, we get
$$k \leqslant \sqrt{4n + 1} - 1.$$
Now let's mark the non-diagonal cells that remain without removal for now. There are $2(n - 1)$ of them, so
$$p(n) \leqslant 2(n - 1) + \sqrt{4n + 1} - 1.$$
or
$$p(n) \leqslant 2n + \sqrt{4n + 1} - 3.$$
For some values ​​of $n$ this estimate can be further improved. Remember the universal ruler of problem 2. Let's mark the main diagonal of the large square using the construction described on page 20. We will take the intervals between the cells as follows:

$$0,1,1,4,(7), 2,0,1$$
$$0,1,1,1,6,6,(11),4,4,0,1,1$$
$$C,1,1,1,1,8,8,8,(15),6,6,6,0,1,1,1$$

When marking the main diagonal of a square using this table, the number of offset cells $k$ is 2 greater than the number of commas (taking into account repetitions of the longest interval) in the selected row of the table. At figure 8 $k = 10$, the number of repetitions of the maximum interval is $m = 2$. Its length is $k - m - 1$. The total length of the two intervals adjacent to this series is $k - m - 2$ and is repeated $(k - m - 4)/4$ times. Then there are unit and zero (when two adjacent cells are offset) intervals with a total length of $(k - m - 2)/2$. Then the length of the diagonal
$$n = m(k - m - 1) + (k - m - 2)\times(k - m - 4)/4 + (k - m - 2)/2 + k,$$
or
$$n = \frac{1}{4}(2mk - 3m^2 + k^2 + 4).$$
