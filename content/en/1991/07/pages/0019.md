However, this example can be improved.

Build on figure 13. Taking the lower base of the hexagon equal to 1/4 of the side, we find that the maximum diameter is $\frac{\sqrt{11570}}{256} = 0,4201$... Let us now try to find such a base of the hexagon such that the maximum diameter is the smallest among the partitions of this type. (As before, we will add that the diameters of all the partition sets are equal.) So, let $d$ be the largest diameter. For ease of calculation, let's take the side length to be 1.

Then $x^2 + y^2 = d^2 = EF^2,$ $(1 - 2x)^2 + (1 - y)^2 = d^2 = AG^2 = HL^2$ (Fig. 14). (We will not take into account $b = (4x - 1)$, but, as it turns out later, this distance in the optimal case is less than $d$.) First, we have $y = \frac{3}{2} x^2 - 2x - 1$. Therefore $d^2 = \frac{9}{4} x^4 - 6x^3 + 8x^2 - 4x + 1$ (check!). Let us find the value $x$ at which $d^2$, and therefore $d$, is minimal. Equating the derivative to zero and solving the resulting cubic equation, we find that the desired value of $x$ is equal to 0.384... Now, remembering that in fact the length of the side of the square is equal to $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, we find that the maximum diameter of such a partition is 0.4200...
