The problem of obtaining a super-dust atmosphere is important for industrial micro-forest production, where one speck of dust a fraction of a micron in size can damage a very large or large integrated circuit (VIC or VIC). Therefore, huge amounts of money are spent on creating clean rooms. The air is filtered through a multi-stage air purification system, which occupies entire floors (the so-called technology floors).

And the requirements for cleanliness are increasing all the time. If previously it was possible to work at classistin 1000 (1000 dust particles per cubic foot, or 30 dust particles per liter) (compare, in rural areas, where we say: “Oh, what clean air!”, There are about 100,000 dust particles per liter), then now the number of dust particles should not exceed 3 per liter (class 100) or even 0.3 (class 10). And dust particles should not be larger than 0.3 microns.

Can a multi-stage air purification system cope with such a task? In principle, yes, but for a very short period of time. The fact is that the purified air quickly becomes dusty again, since in the workshop there are working mechanisms, and most importantly, people who “generate” dust (a person “generates” one and a half million dust particles per hour). Employees are passed through vestibules where dust particles are blown off, dressed in the cleanest clothes made from special fabrics, and sometimes even in spacesuits, like astronauts, but this does not solve the problem.

What is the solution? One option is to capture dust at the places where it is generated using local electret dust collectors and resistors. Such influence, purified in technological floors, will pollute the hot water more slowly.

A clean atmosphere isn't just needed in the electronics industry. Radioactive dust at coal mines and nuclear power plants is very harmful. Absolutely clean air is needed during surgical operations, in optical laboratories, and on spaceships. Electret local dust collectors without external power sources will help solve these important problems.

Electret injectors have many other uses. By spraying agricultural crops with charged water, they increase their productivity and reduce the consumption of dangerous pesticides. By atomizing fuel in engines, they reduce fuel consumption, increase engine power, and reduce the toxicity of exhaust gases...

The reader probably has a question: why are magnets known to everyone, but electrets are talked about only in a narrow circle of specialists? I think that, firstly, electrets have been studied relatively recently, and therefore even in the specialized literature there are relatively few publications about electrets; secondly, in the school textbook and even in textbooks for colleges, practically nothing is said about electrets.

And the prospects for electrets are enormous.
