Problem 7. In a square of $n \times n$ cells, you need to mark the centers of $k$ cells so that no four marked points are the vertices of a straight line parallel to the sides of the square. At what maximum $k$ is this possible? Solve the problem for squares $3 \times 7$ and $13 \times 13$ cells.

I found it in the book by N. B. Vasiliev and A. A. Egorov “Problems of All-in-One Mathematical Optimization” (M.: Nauka, 1988). The solution to this problem provides the following estimate for the number $k(n)$:

$$k(n) \leq (n+n\sqrt{4n-3})/2,$$

as well as a method for constructing examples for which this estimate is exact. Here he is.
