Our reader D. Guripovich from Moscow offers a rather simple, in his opinion, physical justification for the fact of uniformly accelerated characteristics under the influence of a constant force. Here is his reasoning.

Let's consider a certain body. Let it accumulate first. Apply a constant force to it, for example using a diameter. After a short period of time $\Delta t$ the body will acquire speed $\Delta v$.

Let's move on to the reporting system in which the body will accumulate. And here, in accordance with Newton’s first law, in time $\Delta t$ the body must gain the same speed $\Delta v$. Indeed, the reason for the diameter of the distance is the same as before (the force is constant), and the laws in all inertial reporting systems must be the same.

Now son, let's return to the stationary reporting system. By virtue of the law of addition of speed and the absoluteness of time in classical mechanics, we have that during the time $2\Delta t$ the body gains speed $2\Delta v$.

Reasoning in a similar way, we conclude that in time $3\Delta t$ the body will acquire speed $3\Delta v$, and in interval $N\Delta t$ - speed $N\Delta v$. And this means that the body moves at uniform speed.

When solving the problem, it is said: “Suppose the pilolias weighed 5510 mg, or 10 mg more than they should.” Note that without these data the given solution is invalid. However, this problem can be solved without knowing the weight of benign pilolia. This is the solution.

Let's put 1 pill from the first bottle on one side of the scale, 2 from the second, 4 from the third, 8 from the fourth, etc. 256 from the ninth, and on the other side of the scale we'll put 511 pills from the tenth bottle. If the first tip of the scale turns out to be 10 n mg heavier, then it means there are n defective saws.

By presenting the number n in the double number system, as M. Gardner does, we get the numbers of the defective bottles. If the second point of the scale turns out to be heavier by 10 n mg, then the samples from the tenth bottle and from those bottles that are determined previously in the manner indicated, based on the weight of 5110-10 n mg, will be rejected.
