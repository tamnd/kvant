Algebraic closedness of the set of complex numbers
We have constructed the set of complex numbers $C$ as a distribution of the set of real numbers in which any quadratic equation is solvable. At first glance, it may seem that for an equation of higher degrees to be solvable, the set $C$ will need to be expanded over and over again. It turns out that no more extensions are needed. If we take the equation

$$a_0 x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + a_2 x^{n-2} + \dots + a_{n-1} x + a_n = 0$$

any degree to which all coefficients $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ count arbitrary complex numbers, then all its roots belong to the set $C$, and therefore, to solve equation (5) no new numbers not included in $C$ are required.

This property is called the algebraic closedness of the set of complex numbers. It was first formulated by the Dutch mathematician Albert Girard back in 1629, and a rigorous proof was obtained only in 1799 by Carl Friedrich Gauss.
