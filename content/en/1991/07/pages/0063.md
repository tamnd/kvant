ROUNDING IN THE BLOOD
(fantasy story)
G. BIR

- Looks like I slit my wrists, doesn't it? Don't worry. Everything is fine. Genetron takes me back. Bernard just called. — Virgil pointed to the diverter device with intercom installed in the bathroom.

I sat down on the toilet lid and immediately noticed that on the very edge of the shelf above the sink there was a sun reflector with numerous fluorescent light bulbs. However, the wire was pulled out of the socket.

- Do you really want this? - I asked, dropping my shoulders.

“Sorry, yes,” said Virgil. “They will be able to take care of me better than anyone else.” So I decided to clean myself up and head out tonight. Verjid will pick me up in his limousine. Class! From now on, everything will be top class.

The pink-colored water looked strange: it didn’t look like dissolved soap at all.

- What is that in your water - foamy shampoo? - I asked, but after seconds of the agreement myself - and I suddenly felt sad: these crazy events were so obvious and inevitable.

“No,” Virgil replied.

I already knew this.

“No,” he repeated, “these are visions through the skin.” They didn’t tell me everything, but I thought that now they began to send out scouts, pioneers. Astronauts.

He looked at me carefully, and in his gaze I did not notice even a shadow of hesitation, rather just curiosity: how, supposedly, I would react to this. The confirmation of my agreement in his words made me sag internally, as if I was preparing for a blow. I had not thought about this possibility at all before, apparently because I was busy with other aspects of the problem.

— Is this the first time this has happened? - I asked.

“Yes,” he answered and laughed. “I was wondering whether to release these little devils into the sewer system.” Let them know what our world really is like.

- Then they spread all over the world!
- That's for sure.
- How do you feel now?
- Now it’s not bad at all... There must be billions of them here. — Another splash of the hand. -What do you think? Maybe we should release them?

Quickly, almost without thinking, I knelt down by the bathtub. My fingers themselves found the wire from the tanning lamp and stuck the plug into the socket. Virgil remained the same as a boy when he supplied electricity to doorknobs, made punch that turned urine blue, and played a thousand other stupid jokes. He has not grown up, has not matured to understand that his genius is quite enough to really change the world, but at the same time he must also have a huge sense of responsibility.

Virgil reached out to the plug plugging the drain.

- You know, Edward, I...

He never finished. I grabbed the lamp, threw it into the bathtub and immediately jumped back because the water literally exploded into a cloud of steam and sparks. Virgil screamed, held on convulsively - then everything froze. Only the lamp continued to flicker, and a thin shaving of smoke rose from his hair.

I lifted the toilet lid and was immediately stopped. Then I held my nose and walked into the living room. My legs suddenly refused to support me - and I collapsed on the sofa.

About an hour later, rummaging around in the kitchen, I found a box of bleach, grease, and a bottle of whiskey. He returned to the bathroom and, carefully turning his gaze away from Virgil, first poured whiskey into the bathroom, then rubbing alcohol, then poured out bleach. The bleach immediately began to sink into the water, and I went out, closing the door tightly behind me.
