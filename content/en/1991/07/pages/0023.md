Binary relatives of the universal ruler

They turned out to be two variants of problem M574 («Kvant», No. 7, 1979):

Problem 5. A finite sequence a₁, a₂, ..., aₙ of numbers 0 and 1 must satisfy the following condition: for any integer k from 1 to n-1, the sum C(k)=a₁aₙ+a₂aₙ+aₙ-kaₙ is an odd number. Prove that such a sequence exists for unburned n<1000.

Problem 6 (M574, ring version). Indicate the values of n for which there exists a sequence a₁, a₂, ..., aₙ of numbers 0 and 1 located around a circle such that for any k(1≤k≤(n-1)) the sum of the pairwise products of terms spaced at k, i.e. C(k)=a₁aₙ+a₂aₙ+...+aₙ-kaₙ, odd.

Problem 5 is completely solved. Its solution («Kvant» No. 6, 1980) not only indicates all values ​​of n for which there exists an excluding double sequence (let's call it an n-sequence for brevity), but also gives several ways to construct it.

I will give (without proof) one of them.

Let A=a₁, a₂, ..., aₙ be some sequence that satisfies the conditions of the problem (for example, A=1101). Then the sequence B=A 00...0 A 00...0 A=b₁, ..., b₇n-3, consisting of three sets of A and two sets of zeros between them, also satisfies it. In our case, B=1101 000 1101 000000000 1101.
