Many readers of the magazine are well aware of the “Hanoi Bath” puzzle, which consists of a community children's paramid and two more rods. It is allowed to transfer the paramidka disk from one side to the other, observing the following rules: you can only place a smaller disk on top of a larger one. It is required to move all the disks from the first rod to the third, provided that they were in descending order of size. It is known that such a rearrangement can be done in 2$^n$—1 moves, where $n$ is the number of disks on the first rod.
Now on sale there is a puzzle stage in which the rods are replaced by
holes, disks - squares, and the size of the disk is indicated by the number on the square.
Teacher V. Pinaev suggested changing the rules a little: the initially empty disks are on the first rod not in descending order, but in some random order. Is it possible in this case to assemble the disks on the third rod according to the rules of transfer?
At figure one of the options for placing disks on the first rod in the initial position is given.
Read about this and other varieties of “Hanoi Bunny” in one of the next issues of the magazine.
