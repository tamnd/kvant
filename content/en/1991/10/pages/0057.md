Walk through all points of the image. Having constructed images $A'$ and $B'$ (using non-refracting rays $AO$ and $BO$), we can claim that all points of the image lie between them (Fig. 12). An example of such a point $C'$ is the image of the point $C$.

Task 5. Restore the position of lines and its main focuses from the known positions of predmeta $AB$ and its image $A'B'$ (Fig. 13).

After refraction in the face, the ray $AB$ (emitted by all the “luminous” predmeta points) must pass through all the image points $A'B'$. Therefore, it is obvious that the point $L$ of intersection of lines $AB$ and $A'B'$ (Fig. 14) lies on the face. Non-refracting rays pass through the optical center lines $O$, i.e. straight lines connecting the “luminous” point and its image. This means that the optical center of lines is at the intersection of lines $AA'$ and $BB'$. The main optical axis is perpedicular to the “plane” of thin lines. Constructing tricks can be done in the same way as in task 3. Do it yourself.

In this example, the lines turned out to be scattering. Come up with an arrangement of segments $AB$ and $A'B'$ yourself so that you get a collecting line.

 Constructing the predmeta image in the collecting face becomes somewhat more complex if the object "crosses" the focal plane. In this case, the predmeta image is “torn”: part of the image is the actual “luminous” points behind the lens, the other part is minimal images of the predmeta point in front of the lens.

Task 6. Construct an image of an oblique arrow $AB$ passing through the main focus of the collecting line (Fig. 15).

As in previous problems, the image of the arrow lies on the straight line containing the ray resulting from the ray $AB$ after refraction in the face (Fig. 16). In this case, this straight line is parallel to the main optical axis lines. Using the rays $AO$ and $BO$, we find the positions of the points $A'$ and $B'$.
