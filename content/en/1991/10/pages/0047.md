you were conducted by high school graduate P. Berlov. The equipment used is very simple and similar experiments can be replicated at home, all you need is a jar of water, black ink and a high wattage light bulb.

The first step is to create an “atmosphere” (or “ocean”) in which we will observe convective currents. Take a clear jar or aquarium, preferably larger (the shape doesn’t matter). Place a cavity at the bottom of the jar into which the ink will then be poured. If the bottom of the vessel is flat, then such a cavity can serve as a ring attached to the bottom with plasticine; in extreme cases, the edges of the cavity can be made of plasticine. The height of the cavity should not be more than 2-3 mm, since a higher height will lead to significant ink consumption. The success of the experiment largely depends on how to install the cavity. Even a slight tilt can cause ink to spread throughout the vessel.

Now fill the jar with water and let it sit for a while.

The next step is to fill the cavity at the bottom with ink. It is better to take black ink, which clearly shows convective movements and dissolves well in water. It is clear that you cannot first pour ink and then water, since the ink will immediately mix with water. It is best to pour ink at a temperature lower than the water temperature. In this case, the density of the ink is greater than the density of water and the ink spreads well along the bottom of the cavity. So first cool the ink, then draw it with a squeegee or rubber bulb through the contail tube, wipe off the remaining ink and, carefully bringing the tube to the bottom, slowly pour the ink into the cavity.
