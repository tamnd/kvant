(fantasy story)

J.D. PEARCE

You, of course, know basically everything about Homer Green. So, there is no need for me to talk about it. I myself knew a lot, but nevertheless, when I had the opportunity, dressed in the old style, to get into this extraordinary house and meet Green, I experienced a strange feeling.

The house itself, perhaps, cannot be called so unusual - no more than its images. Sandwiched between other buildings of the 20th century, it is believed to be well preserved and is not visible against the background of the ancient houses surrounding it. But despite the preliminary psychological preparation, when I entered, stepped on the carpet, saw the chairs, the shared fleece fabric, and smoking accessories, heard (and saw) a primitive radio receiver (although I knew that it played old recordings) and, finally, the most amazing thing, was able to look at the fire kindled in the stone, I was overcome by a feeling of unreality.

Green was sitting in his usual place, in the shelter, by the fire. A dog lay at his feet. I could not forget that he, apparently, is one of the most valuable people on Earth. But the feeling of the unreality of what was happening, inspired by the surrounding environment, still dominated many, and Green himself also seemed unreal to me. I felt a sharp bitterness towards him.

The feeling of unreality did not disappear later when I introduced myself. How many people have promoted here? Of course, this could have been known in advance from reports.

The story is reprinted from the collection of science fiction stories “Practical Invention” (M: Mir, 1974).

“I’m Carew, from the Institute,” I said. “You and I have never met, but I was told that you would be glad to see me.”

Green stood up and extended his hand to me. I readily shook it, although this gesture was unusual for me.

“Yes, I’m glad to see you,” said Green. “I took a little nap.” This whole procedure causes something like a slight shock. After a while I decided to change my clothes a little. I hope that my drug will last forever. Please sit down,” he added.

We settled down by the stones. The dog, which had stood up when I appeared, lay down again and pressed itself to the feet of its owner.

- You would probably like to check my reactions? - Green asked.

“No, there’s no rush, we can do it later,” I answered. “It’s so cozy here.”

It was easy to distract Green. He relaxed and began to look into the fire.

I will not detail the content of our short conversation. It is reproduced in my dissertation “Some Aspects of the Twentieth Century” (see Appendix A) and, as we know, the spring was short. I was very lucky that I received permission to meet with Green.
