M1284. Based on the AC of equilateral triangle ABC, point D is chosen so that the circle inscribed in triangle ABD has the same radius as the circle tangent to the extended segments BC and BD and the segment CD (external to triangle BCD). Prove that this radius is equal to 1/4 of the height of the triangle dropped to the side.

Taking advantage of the fact that two tangents drawn from one point to a circle are equal, we denote them as shown in figure 1 (here it is also used that the tangents drawn from point D to each of two equal circles form equal angles, so they are all equal). Equating two tangents drawn from vertex B of the triangle to the far circle, and taking into account that AB=BC=x+y, we get:

$$y+2z=y+x+u,$$

where

$$x+u=2z.$$

Now the solution can be completed by a routine calculation using the area of ​​the triangle through the half-perimeter p and the radius r of the inscribed circle, S=rp, as well as through the radius $r_a$ of the out-circle tangent to side a ($S=(p-a)r_a$): if h is the height dropped to the side of the triangle ABC, and r is the radius of equal circles, then the area $S_{ABC}$ is

$$h \cdot \frac{x+u}{2} = S_{ABD} + S_{DBC} =$$

$$= r(x+y+z) + r(y+z + \frac{x+u}{2} -z-u) =$$

$$= r(x+y + \frac{x+u}{2} + y + \frac{x+u}{2} -u) = r(2x+2u).$$

Hence r=h/4.

However, this can also be proven using equality (*), purely geometric. A blue quadrilateral with vertex C (see figure) can be attached to the pink sector so that one of its sides u lies on the base AC of the triangle, and the other - the plane x+u=2z - is tucked along its midline, which, thereby, touches the inscribed circle in the triangle ABD (one of the naked radii coincides with the red one, perpendicular to the base, and the other is a continuation of the red radius, perpendicular side side, the conditions (*) shows that C will fall into the middle of the base). It follows that the diameter of the circle 2r is equal to half the height h.

I. Sharyzin, N. Vasiliev
