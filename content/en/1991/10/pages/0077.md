5. It is clear that a number divisible by the sum of the other two must be greater than each of them. Therefore, there are only 4 fundamentally different ways to choose the dividend and two numbers that add up to the divisor. Let's look at them in order:
1) $ABBB$ and $(ABB + ABB)$, i.e. $1000A + 111B$ is divisible by $200A + 22B$. If you subtract a divisor multiplied by 5 from the dividend, then the difference equal to $B$ must also be divided by the divisor, and this is only possible if $B = 0$. In this case, $A$ can take any non-zero value.
2) $ABBB$ and $(ABB + ABB)$, i.e. $1000A + 111B$ is divisible by $110A + 12B$. If you subtract the divisor multiplied by 9 from the dividend, then the difference equal to $10A + 3B$ must also be divisible by the divisor. But $0 < 10A + 3B < 110A + 12B$, therefore not an integer impossible.
3) $ABBB$ and $(ABB + ABB)$, i.e. $1000A + 111B$ is divisible by $20A + 2B$. If you subtract the divisor multiplied by 50 from the dividend, then the difference equal to $11B$ must also be divisible by the divisor. Firstly, this is possible with $B = 0$ (then $A$ is any non-zero digit). If $B \neq 0$, then the equality must be fulfilled: $11B = k(20A + 2B)$ ($k$ - natural). Hence $(11 - 2k)B = 20kA$. Here $A \neq 0$, because otherwise it would be $B = 0$. Therefore, since $A$ and $B$ are natural, and $A/B \geq 1/9$, then $(11 - 2k)/20k \geq 1/9$, whence $k \leq 2$. Thus, $k = 1$ or 2. If $k = 1$, then $9B = 20A$, and $B$ is divisible by 20, which is impossible. If $k = 2$, then $9B = 40A$, and $B$ is divisible by 40, which is also impossible.
4) $ABB$ and $(AB + AB)$, i.e. $100A + 11B$ is divisible by $20A + 2B$. If you subtract a divisor multiplied by 5 from the dividend, then the difference equal to $B$ must also be divided by the divisor, and this is only possible if $B = 0$. In this case, $A$ can take any non-zero value.
Thus, if one of the numbers is divisible by the sum of the other two, then $B = 0$ and $A \neq 0$, i.e. always $A > B$, and, therefore, “rubbish” is greater than “rubbish”.
