But light is an electromagnetic wave, and it is characterized by more than just intensity. The oscillation frequency is important, different frequencies are perceived by the eye as different colors, and a photo camera is able to record this difference (color photography). But the wave has one more characteristic - phase. You came across this clear idea when studying interference - the result of the interference of two waves is determined by the difference in their phases. A coherent wave reflected from different points of an object arrives at the place of observation with different shapes, i.e., the phase of the reflected wave does not contain information about the volumetric shape of the object. But... on photographic plastic, which records only the intensity of light, the phase of the wave is hopelessly lost. Not a single light recorder is capable of recording the phase of a light wave: oscillations occur with such a high frequency (millions of times higher than the estimated frequency of radio waves) that both in the photomulsion and in the human eye they inevitably average out over a very large number of oscillations, and hence the loss of phase.
