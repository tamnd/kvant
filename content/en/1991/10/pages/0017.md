Psychology believes that the ability to memorize and remember peaks around the age of 25, and then gradually begins to weaken.

Here we come to another very important point. Memorizing information in the brain cannot even by a stretch be compared with domain magnetization reversal or laser burning methods. This process in a person does not coincide with strict (or dry) logical, absolutely precise, dispassionate. When memorizing, a person removes something from an image, and adds something, compares it with something previously seen, looks for analogies in completely different areas, etc. Generally speaking, this is a creative process; memorizing information is always combined with its simultaneous processing.

Next. Recording on an optical disc is carried out sequentially bit by bit; it is called “bit-by-bit”. And the brain continues approximately the same when a person, for example, reads a book. But other situations are more typical... You walked into the next room, from which the cotton was stripping, and instantly saw a smoking color TV, assessed the distance from it to the window signs, recorded the frightened eyes of a junior, and much more. The brain has the ability to parallelly input information, and this input is carried out in a conveyor belt, synchronously with the flow of time or, as it is nice to say in technology, on a real time scale. And the optical memory (and the magnetic one too), no matter how quickly the disk rotates, would perceive this picture (mark by mark, without missing anything) when it would hardly be needed... And one more thing. Recording on disks is carried out primarily using a digital method; those analog forms of perception of drawings, curves, histograms that are accessible to a laser beam can not be considered truly analog. The transfer of half-tones, color shades, two-dimensional or even more three-dimensional pictures requires a huge, sometimes fantastic amount of digital information. And analogue perception, inherent in the brain, is not as accurate as digital perception, and can be done with very little money.

But not only in how information is remembered, but also in what is obtained in the end, biomemory is fundamentally different from artificial one. The laser beam places the data, a specific bit, at a specific point on the disk, each bit has its own “address”, nothing gets mixed up or lost. This is the subject of the city's one thing - to maintain perfect order in the common scorched points. But is this already ideal? What if a scratch distorts the disc, or even if a piece of the disc breaks off? Everything that was there will be lost forever. That’s right, you say, after all, a catastrophe has occurred, how can it be foreseen and, moreover, overcome?

No, it’s wrong, it will be returned by a highly experienced brain that has seen different things over millions of years of evolution. Disasters are as natural as the normal course of life. And if you don’t adapt to disasters and don’t develop a protective reaction against them, then it really will be a disaster. The brain attracts a large group of cells, not necessarily close to each other, to remember even the simplest information. The recording seems to be smeared throughout the entire memory capacity. If part of the “brain memory” is damaged, the memory weakens, but the previously recorded picture is completely preserved.
