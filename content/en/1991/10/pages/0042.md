We must accept as one of the most general laws of heat that “all bodies freely communicating with each other and not subject to unequal external influences acquire the same temperature, as shown by a thermometer.”
J. Black

Are you familiar with the temperature?

We develop an intuitive idea of ​​temperature from the first days of our lives. However, the tasks facing science require increasingly accurate interpretations of what we perceive with our senses. Thus, an important stage in the development of the doctrine of thermal phenomena was the identification of differences between the concepts of “quantity of heat” and “temperature”. The first person to clearly formulate the idea of ​​the need for their development was the Scottish scientist J. Black.

The history of the creation and use of a device for measuring temperature - thermometers - is interesting and informative. Serodnya knows liquid and gas thermometers, semiconductor and optical ones. And the variety of external temperatures nowadays in science is great: they distinguish between electron and ion temperatures, brightness and color, noise and antenna, etc.

But don't be embarrassed! In this issue of “Kaleidoscope” we talk about the most common, one might say, “school” temperature.

Questions and tasks
1. A long-dried towel lies on a hot heating radiator. Are the radiator and the towel equally hot when assessed by touch? Are their temperatures the same?
2. Will the room temperature drop if you open the door of a running refrigerator?
3. Water is boiling in a saucepan and pasta is being cooked in it. Does the water in the pasta tubes boil?
4. What should you do to avoid hot tea: immediately throw sugar into it and then wait five minutes, or remove the sugar and dissolve it?
5. Add kerosene to a flask with a narrow neck and mark its level in the neck. If you lower the flask into hot water, then at first the level of kerosene will drop, but later it will begin to rise. Why?

6. A straight segment was drawn on a disk cut from a copper plate. Will it remain straight if the disc is heated?
7. How can you use a medical thermometer to change a person’s body temperature if the ambient temperature is 42 °C?
8. Is it possible to use a room thermometer in Antarctica?
9. There are two unsold thermometers. How to determine which one is hotter?
10. What will the thermometer that a cosmonaut take with him when going into outer space show?
11. Is it possible, without resorting to tables, to say whether the critical temperature of water is above or below room temperature?
