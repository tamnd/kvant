7. Let us first prove the following lemma.
Lemma. Let $P'$ and $Q'$ be the bases of the perpendiculars dropped from the points $P$ and $Q$ to the line $l$. Then $PQ \geq P'Q'$, and equality occurs only in the case when the lines $PQ$ and $l$ are parallel.
Proof. It is obvious that the segments $P'P$ and $Q'Q$ lie in planes perpendicular to the line $l$. These planes are parallel, the distance between them is $P'Q'$. Consequently, the distance between points $P$ and $Q$ is not less than $P'Q'$, and $PQ = P'Q'$ only if the segment is $PQ \parallel l$. The lemma is proven.
Let's turn to the construction problem. Let us assume the opposite: let the point $T_3$ be different from $T_0$ (Fig. 8). This is possible only in the case when the lines $a, b$ and $c$ are parallel.
