Now it seems natural that there is a difference in the processing and reading of information of comparable types of memory. For machine memories, address sampling is typical - information is found exactly at the address prescribed by the program. The brain is also capable of this, but it can extract what it wants in a completely different way. We are talking about associative sampling, in which the selection is carried out according to certain qualitative characteristics, drawing on a large array of information.

In the short story by K. Capek “What the Pozt Saw,” the police inspector, investigating the collision of a machine gun with an old woman, uses the testimony of a very absent-minded poet, who was present at the time and, who gives it, wrote a poem, which is called hot on the trail. It also contained the following lines: “Oh the swan’s neck, about the chest, about the drum and these sticks.” The intruder was discovered precisely based on the “indications” of the post - the license plate of the machine was - of course, you already agreed - 235. Of course, associative sampling can lead to inaccuracies or errors when reading, but it sharply speeds up the search, which is especially important with a large memory capacity. As before, it’s good to combine both types of ponska, and the brain can do it. However, we will try to be objective - no matter how perfect a person’s brain is, “even on the Sun there are spots.” It’s good, of course, that by forgetting, the brain protects itself from complete wear and tear. But what if he scores something that he would like to keep? Or, on the contrary, a friend will spit out something from his depths that during the lesson you suddenly begin to cooperate with barely angry laughter. It is difficult for the brain to fill in monotonous, useless digital data - how many telephone numbers, for example, do you remember? And in the simplest digital storage facility thousands of them can be stored...

The working temperature of the brain is our constant 36.6, already at around 37 we set the red line. What if we allow fluctuations of ±3 °C typical for technical requirements? It’s good if it’s +3 °C - a fiery imagination will somehow cling to the outline of reality. What if -3 °C? It’s probably better not to imagine this... Very minor changes in pressure, humidity, even the Earth’s magnetic field, not to mention vibrations, accelerations, shocks - all this can be categorically contraindicated. Of course, it would be nice to straighten all this out, but it comes to accepting the world as it is.

And yet, huge capacity, reliable storage, parallel-serial input-output of analog and digital information, address and associative Japanese, information processing at all stages of recording, storage, reading - this “calling card” of the human brain cannot but cause natural artificial memory.

It is clear that disk drives - optical or magnetic - no matter how they are improved, they will never be able to come closer to the ideal. We need some new idea, a new principle, a new physical effect.

It turns out that there is a suitable effect, this effect is optical, and it was discovered back in 1947 by the English scientist of Hungarian origin D. Gabor. This is a method of recording light flows, which is significantly different from photography and significantly exceeds its capabilities. Until 1960, until the laser was invented, which made it possible to produce beams of coherent light, voting was “dormant” (Gabor’s expression from the speech on the occasion of his being awarded the Nobel Prize in 1971), but already in 1961 the first laser voting was obtained.
