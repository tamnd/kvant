Naimplicu

In the sixth issue of Quat, the solution problem F1277 was published, which shows how to find the position of the center of mass of a wire semicircle using energy messages. The same result is quite simply obtained by another physical method - using the dynamic rotational movement.

First, let’s take a whole wire stab of mass $m$ and spin it around the axis $O$ to an angular velocity $\omega$. Let's find the tension force of the wire $F$ by writing Newton's law for a small element of the wire corresponding to the central angle $\alpha$:

$$F\alpha = \frac{m\alpha}{2\pi}\omega^2R \Rightarrow F = \frac{m\omega^2R}{2\pi},$$

where $R$ is the radius of the ring.

Now let's write down Newton's law for a semicircle. Note that its center of mass moves in a circle of radius $l_\pi$ ($l_\pi$ is the desired distance from the center of mass of the wire to its center), so provol

$$2F = \frac{m}{2}\omega^2l_\pi.$$

From here, substituting the expression for $F$, we find

$$l_\pi = \frac{2R}{\pi}.$$
