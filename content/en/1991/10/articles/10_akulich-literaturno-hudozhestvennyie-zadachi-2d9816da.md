LITERARY-
ARTISTIC TASKS
I. AKULICH

traveled 525 versts. The distance between the cities is 175 versts. How many miles does each person travel per day?

L. Cassil.
Condititis and Schwambrania. Book two, chapter “The Problem with Travelers.”

A very nice task. It could even be used in entrance exams for current visas. Solve it. If you can’t, look at the answer at the end of the issue.

So, the reader is convinced that the first of the problems considered has a completely strict and unique solution. But this is not always the case.

Most often, certain adjustments come to the detected problem. Here's an example.

Problem 3. At three stations: Vorobyovo, Grachevo and Drozdovo there were an equal number of employees. At the Drozdovo station there were 6 times fewer Komsomol members than at the other two combined, and at the Vorobyovo station there were 12 more party members than at the Grachevo station. But at this last one there were 6 more non-Party people than at the first two. How many employees were there at each station?

Problem 4. The sum of the first three terms of the geometric progression is 28, the denominator is $^9/2$, the third term is $^3/2$ times the denominator. Find the fourth term.

G. Velkh, L. Panteleev.
Republic, Shkid.
Chapter “Skid will fall in love.”

Shkidets Sparrow failed to cope with this task. And no wonder: its conditions contain contradictions. To bring it to a decidable form, two refinements will have to be made. First, we will assume that one of the three given numbers is specified incorrectly. The second clarification is better: the third term of opposition is equal to the amount in rubles that the author of this article recently burned at the market for potatoes.

Now the problem has become solvable. So find the fourth term.

Task 5. This is what the Martian drum sounds like:
“Trash-rubbish-rubbish-rubbish,
Rubbish-rubbish-rubbish,
Rubbish, rubbish
Rubbish, rubbish, rubbish."
Kurt Wopnegut.
Sirens of Titan.
Chapter four.

Well, the sound is pleasant, but where is the task? Some kind of rubbish... But let’s imagine that “rubbish” and “rubbish” are some numbers, and “rubbish” is not equal to “rubbish”. Then each read line is represented as an encrypted record of some number. Let one of these numbers be divided by the sum of the other two. Now tell me: what is more - “rubbish” or “rubbish”?

Here, for convenience, it makes sense to designate: “rubbish” = A, “rubbish” = B. Then we are dealing with five numbers: $\overline{ABBB}=1000A+111B$, $\overline{ABB}=100A+11B$ (2 pcs.) and $\overline{AB}=10A+B$ (2 pcs.). True, it is unclear which of them is divisible by the sum of the other two (and which ones), but this difficulty is assumed. The point is... no, decide for yourself.

And in conclusion - perhaps the most amazing task.

Task 6. There is a four-story house, there are windows all over each floor, two servant windows and two chimneys on the roof, and two apartments on each floor. In what year did the doorman's grandmother die?

J. Hasek.
Similarities of the good soldier, Schweik.
Part one, chapter III.

This task seems akin to the succession: there is an elderberry in the garden, and there is a guy in Kyiv. But let’s take into account that Schweik set his task in 1914, and add the conditions: the year of the grandmother’s death is equal to the product of the total number of windows in this house by the number of pipes and the age (in 1914) of one of the apartments, who was personally present at the funeral. Then the solution clearly emerges.

That's it. And in conclusion, three suggestions for readers:

1) Changes and additions were made to all tasks (except the first) compared to their original form. Try introducing other changes of your own - perhaps they turn out to be much better and more interesting. The requirement is the same: that the solution be unique.

2) When reading fiction, you may encounter other literary and artistic tasks. Try to make them look comfortable.

3) And finally, share your findings with the editors of the magazine «Kvant». The best of them will be published.
