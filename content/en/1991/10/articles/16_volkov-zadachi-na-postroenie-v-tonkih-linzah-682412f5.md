Tasks
to build
in thin lenses

V. VOLKOV

In oral entrance exams, there are often tasks involving facial expressions. The reason for this is convenience for the examiner: the task is easy to formulate, you don’t need to write anything down, just draw a picture. Experience shows that even well-prepared applicants are often baffled by such tasks, especially unconventional ones that are not covered in the textbook. I decided to help applicants and, starting with simple ones and ending with quite non-standard ones, analyze several tasks for constructing a face.

Before you start reading the article, pick up a textbook and review the basic definitions: optical center, main and secondary optical axes, focal length, focal plane. Figures 1-3 show three main cases of constructing an image of an object point lying in the main optical axis; Each formation uses three main beams.

Before moving on to the analysis of problems, we will make one important remark. For convenience and clarity, the drawings for the article use rays coming at various, including large angles to the main optical axis. However, we must remember that in fact we are talking only about narrow rays: it is these rays that form a clear image.

Problem 2. Given the path of the ray AL after refraction in a thin lens - ray LA'. Optical center of the lens O (Fig. 5). By construction, determine the position of the main foci of the lens.

Let us draw ray KO, parallel to AL, through the optical center of the lens (Fig. 6). It will intersect the refracted ray at point D of the rear focal plane. Having restored the focal plane, we find point F - the main back focus. Let's draw a ray MO, a parallel ray LA'. It will pass through the same point E in the front focal plane as the beam AL. By restoring the focal plane, we find the position of the front focus.

Task 3. Based on the position of the object and its image (parallel arrows AB and A'B' (Fig. 7)) restores the position of the lens and its main foci.

The optical center of the lens O is located at the intersection of the non-refractive rays AA' and BB' (Fig. 8). The plane of the lens is parallel to the arrows. The main optical axis is perpendicular to them. Next, drawing rays parallel to the main optical axis (Fig. 9) through points A and A', we find the position of the main foci of the lens.

Task 5. Restore the position of lines and its main focuses from the known positions of predmeta $AB$ and its image $A'B'$ (Fig. 13).

After refraction in the face, the ray $AB$ (emitted by all the “luminous” predmeta points) must pass through all the image points $A'B'$. This means that the optical center of lines is at the intersection of lines $AA'$ and $BB'$. The main optical axis is perpedicular to the “plane” of thin lines. Task 7. An optical system consists of two collecting lenses with a common optical axis and focal lengths $F_1$ and $F_2$. Distance between lenses $l=F_1+F_2$. Determine the lateral magnification of this system.

Consider the magnification of the arrow $AB$, perpendicular to the main optical axis of the system. Let us draw a ray from point $A$ parallel to the main optical axis of the lenses. After refraction in the first lens, it will pass through the focus of the first lens, and therefore through the focus of the second. Consequently, after refraction in the second lens, the beam will again go parallel to the main optical axis. The image $A'$ of point $A$ will lie on this ray $L_2M$ (or its continuation; Fig. 17), and point $B'$, very indo, lies on the optical axis (another ray $AF_1$ is used to construct the image). The image size $A'B'$ is equal to the distance between the straight line $L_2M$ and the main optical axis, i.e., the length of the segment $O_2L_2$. Triangles $L_1O_1F$ and $L_2O_2F$ are similar with a similarity coefficient of $F_1/F_2$. Hence,

$$A'B'=O_2L_2=O_1L_1\frac{F_2}{F_1}=AB\frac{F_2}{F_1},$$

i.e., the magnification is equal to $\Gamma=F_2/F_1$ and does not depend on the position of the object.

Task 8. The diffuser of a lens with a focal length $F=11$ cm is located in a half-line lying to the right of $B'$ (to do this, construct images of some points belonging to these segments).

After refraction in the diverging lens, the ray, parallel to the main optical axis, will travel in such a way that its continuation will pass through the focus of the lens. After the segment from the mirror, the ray will go exactly in the opposite direction and after the second refraction in the lens it will be parallel to the axis if the ray incident on the mirror is perpendicular to its surface, that is, it goes along the radius of the mirror. This means that the focus of the lens must coincide with the center of curvature of the mirror, so

$$R=F+d=17\text{ см.}$$
