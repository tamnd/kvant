M1282. Prove that there are no two (other than parallelograms) trapezoids such that the sides of each of them are respectively equal to the base of the other.

In any trapezoid, the difference between the sides is less than the difference between the bases. Indeed, if $E$ is a point on the larger base $CD$ of the trapezoid $ABCD$ such that
$$ABCE — параллелограмм (см. рисунок), то
CD-AB=DE>|AD-AE|.$$
(The last inequality—the fact that each side of a triangle is greater than the difference of the other two—follows from the “triangle inequality,” which states that each side is less than the other two.)
This implies the statement of the problem.

To

But these six inequalities are incompatible: they imply

Further, at the first step, only one connection can be transformed from “<” to “>”: after all, here either $a < b < c < d < p < q$ or $p < q < a < b < c < d$, and in this case only one of the three inequalities is satisfied

F1294. One side of a thin metal plate is illuminated by the Sun. At an air temperature of $T_0$, the illuminated side has a temperature of $T_1$, and the opposite side has a temperature of $T_2$. What will the temperature be if a plate of double thickness is taken?

Heat is radiated from each side of the plate. The total power output is

$$W = a(T_1 - T_0) + a(T_2 - T_0)$$

(a is the proportionality coefficient). The plate receives the same power from the Sun. For a plate twice as thick we obtain

$$W' = a(T_3 - T_0) + a(T_4 - T_0).$$

In thermal equilibrium, the heat flow propagates from the illuminated side to the opposite side so that through any cross section of the plate the heat flow is the same and equal to the flow carried away by air from the unlit side, i.e.

$$a(T_2 - T_0) = k \frac{T_1 - T_2}{d}$$

(k is the proportionality coefficient, d is the thickness of the plate). For a double thickness plate -

$$a(T_4 - T_0) = k \frac{T_3 - T_4}{2d}.$$

After simple transformations we find

$$T_3 = T_0 + (T_1 + T_2 - 2T_0)(2T_1 - T_2 - T_0)/(2(T_1 - T_0))$$,

$$T_4 = T_0 + (T_2 - T_0)(T_1 + T_2 - 2T_0)/(2(T_1 - T_0)).$$

E. Ponomarev

F1296. The LC circuit will oscillate. Determine the total amount of heat that will be released in the resistor.

The coils are connected in parallel, therefore the voltages that change the points in the coils after closing the switch are the same, and the changes in the currents in the coils are inversely proportional to the values ​​of the coil inductances:

$$\frac{I - I_x}{I_x} = \frac{2L}{L} = 2,$$

where $I_x$ is the current in each coil by the time the oscillations in the circuit end (Fig. 2).

From the law of conservation of energy it follows that

$$\frac{CU^2}{2} + \frac{LI^2}{2} = \frac{2L + L}{2} I_x^2 + Q,$$

where $Q$ is the required amount of heat released by the resistor.

F1297. An accurate light source moves parallel to the main optical axis of the collecting line with focal length $F$. At what distance from the line does he find himself at the moment when the speed of his image in his face is equal in magnitude to the speed of the source? Distance from the main optical axis of the line to the source $H=F/4$.

Denoting the distance from the source to the line as $d$, and the distance from the line to the image as $f$, we write the line formula:

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}.$$

In a very short period of time $\Delta t$, the distance from the source to the line will decrease by $\Delta d=v_0\Delta t$, and from the line to the image will increase by $\Delta f=u\cos \alpha \times \Delta t$. Then (see figure)

$$\frac{1}{d-v_0\Delta t} + \frac{1}{f+u\cos \alpha \cdot \Delta t} = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f},$$

or

$$\frac{v_0\Delta t}{d^2} = \frac{u\cos \alpha \cdot \Delta t}{f^2}.$$

The image speed $u$ is equal to the source speed $v_0$ at $f_1=d_1\sqrt{\cos \alpha}$. Considering that $\cos \alpha=F/\sqrt{F^2+H^2}$, we get

$$\frac{1}{d_1} + \frac{1}{d_1\sqrt{\cos \alpha}} = \frac{1}{F},$$

$$d_1=F\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos \alpha}}\right)=F\left(1+\frac{4}{1+H^2/F^2}\right)=F\left(1+\frac{2}{4\sqrt{17}}\right).$$

A. Zilberman
