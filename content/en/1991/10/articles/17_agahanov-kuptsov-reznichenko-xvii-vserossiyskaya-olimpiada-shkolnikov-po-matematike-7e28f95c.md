ХVII All-Russian
Olympiad Schoolchildren

This year, the final stage of the All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics and physics took place in Petrozavodsk, Belgorod, Ufa and Novosibirsk.

Mathematics

As usual, the compound was carried out over two days, each day the participants were offered 4 tasks. After each problem is formed, the number of points awarded for its complete solution is indicated in parentheses.

9th grade

First day

1. Give the locus of the vertices of the parabolas having equations $y = -x^2 + bx + c$ and tangent to the parabolas $y = x^2$. (6)

2. Let $AB$ be half-possible with center $O$ and take point $C$, different from $A$, $B$ and $O$. From point $C$, rays are drawn at equal angles $k$ $AB$, intersecting the half-way at points $D$ and $E$. At point $D$ a perpendicular is drawn to ray $DC$, which intersects the semi-period for the second time at point $K$. Prove that if $K \neq E$, then $KE$ and $AB$ are parallel. (8)

3. Three different positive numbers are given. Prove that they can be denoted by $a$, $b$ and $c$ such that the inequality

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} > \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{a}.$$ (10)

4. On the leftmost cell of the strip $1 \times 100$, divided into cells $1 \times 1$, there is a counter. Two players alternately move the fish, and in one move the chip is allowed to move 1, 10 or 11 squares to the right. The one who cannot make a move pings. Who wins when ruling the game: the player who starts the game or his partner? (12)

Second day

5. Are there two integers whose sum of cubes is 1991? (6)

6. On the diagonal $AC$ of the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ the points $K$ and $M$ are taken, on the diagonal $BD$ the points $P$ and $T$ are taken so that $AK = MC = \frac{1}{4} AC$, $BP = TD = \frac{1}{4} BD$. Prove that the line passing through the midpoints of the segments $AD$ and $BC$ also passes through the midpoints of the segments $PM$ and $KT$. (8)

7. The wooden board $n \times n$ is placed on the squares $1 \times 1$. Two players alternately make cuts with their foreheads according to the following rules: from the edge of the board or from any grid node to which a cut has already been made, a cut of one length is made along the grid line. The one who breaks through is the one after which the board falls apart. Who wins when ruling the game: the player who starts the game or his partner? (10)

8. Each of the numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ is greater than 1 and $|a_{k+1} - a_k| < 1$ is greater than $1 \leq k \leq n-1$. Prove that the sum $\frac{a_1}{a_2} + \frac{a_2}{a_3} + \dots + \frac{a_{n-1}}{a_n} + \frac{a_n}{a_1}$ is less than $2n-1$. (12)

10th grade

First day

1. Find out all the natural numbers $p$ and $q$, also that the equation $x^2 - pqx + p + q = 0$ has integer roots. (6)

2. The segments connecting point $K$ with vertices $A$ and $D$ of rectangle $ABCD$ intersect side $BC$. The perpendiculars dropped from $B$ and $C$ onto the lines $DK$ and $AK$ respectively intersect at the point $M$. Prove that if $M \neq K$, then $MK$ and $AD$ are perpendicular. (8)

3. The polygon-shaped city is divided into square polygons by streets. At the vertices of the polygons there is an area. Each corner connects two squares and does not pass through other squares. One-way traffic has been introduced on each face so that: a) you can arrive at each square; b) you can leave each square; b) the weight of the city can be driven around along its borders.

Prove that there is a square in the city that can be driven around along the streets that border it. (10)

4. A table with 6 columns and $n$ rows, $n \geq 2$, is filled with angles and units so that all rows are distinct and, along with any two rows $(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_6)$ and $(b_1, b_2, \dots, b_6)$, it also contains the row $(a_1b_1, a_2b_2, \dots, a_6b_6)$. Prove that some of its columns contain at least half zero. (12)

Second day

5. At each vertex of the cube sits a moxa. At the same time, all the flies mount and land again in some order, one at each vertex. Prove that you will find three flies whose initial and final positions will be located at the vertices of equal triangles. (5)

6. Given a straight line with dimensions $11 \times 12$. Prove that this straight line:

a) it is possible to tile a non-overlapping straight line with dimensions $1 \times 6$ and $1 \times 7$, the total number of which is twenty; (1)

b) it is impossible to pave nineteen straight lines of this size. (8)

7. Solve the system of equations:

$$\begin{cases} 5x(1 + \frac{1}{x^2} + y^2) = 12, \\ 5y(1 - \frac{1}{x^2} + y^2) = 4. \end{cases}$$ (10)
