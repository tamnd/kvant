Final type assignment LIV of the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad

8th grade
1. Prove that if $a > b > c$, then
$$a^2 (b - c) + b^2 (c - a) + c^2 (a - b) > 0.$$
I. Serdeeev

2. Using these points $A$ and $B$ on the plane, it is required to construct a point $C$, symmetrical to the point $A$ relative to $B$. Is it possible to do this, using only a compass of constant distance $r$, if $a) AB < 2r$, $6) AB \geq 2r$?

I. Serdeeev

3. To guard the facility around the clock, it is necessary to establish a guard at the post in two shifts: day and night. The duty officer can work a day or night shift, or for 24 hours in a row. In the first case, he is provided with at least 1 day, in the second - at least 1.5 days, in the third - at least 2.5 days. What is the smallest number of juras needed under these conditions?

I. Serdeeev

4. There are six identical-looking weights weighing 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 g, respectively. The weights were inscribed with “1 g”, “2 g”, “3 g”, “4 g”, “5 g” and “6 g”. How can you check the correctness of all six inscriptions by two weighings on a cup scale without other weights?

S. Tokarev

5. There is an established air connection between other countries so that any two cities from different countries are connected by exactly one flight and only in one direction, and from each city you can fly somewhere. Prove that there are four cities $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ that can be visited by flying directly from $A$ to $B$, from $B$ to $C$, from $C$ to $D$, from $D$ to $A$.

M. Grinichuk

9th grade
1. Solve the equation
$$1 + x + x^2 (1 + x + \dots + x^{10}) = (1 + x + \dots + x^6)^2.$$
A. Checkmarks

2. The magician laid out a deck of a) 36, 6) 54 cards into several piles and on all the cards in each pile wrote a number equal to the number of cards in this pile. Then he moved the cards in a special way, again arranged them into piles and wrote on each card to the right of the first number the second, equal number of cards in the new pile. Could a magician ensure that there were no identical pairs of numbers written on the cards, but for each pair $(m, n)$ it was possible to find a pair $(n, m)$?

A. Skopenkov

3. Prove that in the regular 12-gon $A_1 A_2 \dots A_{12}$ the diagonals $A_1 A_5$, $A_2 A_6$, $A_3 A_8$ and $A_4 A_{11}$ intersect at one point.

S. Tokarev

4. The graph of the function $y = \frac{1}{x}$ at $x > 0$ is given, and the coordinate axes are erased. How can axes be restored using a compass and ruler, even if their directions are not known in advance?

I. Serdeeev

5. In the cells of the table $15 \times 15$ non-zero numbers are arranged so that each of them is equal to the product of all the numbers in neighboring cells (we call cells that have a common side “neighboring”). Prove that all numbers in the table are positive.

S. Tokarev

10th grade
1. The function $f(x)$ for each value $x \in (-\infty, \infty)$ satisfies the equality
$$f(x) + (x + \frac{1}{2}) \cdot f(1 - x) = 1.$$
a) Find $f(0)$ and $f(1)$.
6) Find all such functions $f(x)$.
V. Kukushkin

2. How many $n$ of 16 identical billiard balls can be placed in space so that each ball touches exactly three or more? List all possible values ​​of $n$.

A. Spivak

3. Two non-intersecting circles are inscribed in this angle. Triangle $ABC$ is located between the circles so that its vertices lie on the sides of the angle, and equal sides $AB$ and $AC$ touch the corresponding circles. Prove that the sum of the radii of the circles is equal to the height of the triangle dropped from the vertex $A$.

I. Sharitsin

4. A cube of size $10 \times 10 \times 10$ is composed of 500 black and 500 white cups in a checkerboard pattern (cubes adjacent to each other have different colors). 100 cups were taken from this cube so that in each of the 300 rows of size $1 \times 1 \times 10$, parallel to some edge of the cube, exactly one cube was missing. Prove that the number of black cups drawn is divisible by 4.

A. Spivak
