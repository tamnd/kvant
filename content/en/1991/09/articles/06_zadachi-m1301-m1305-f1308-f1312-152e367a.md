We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The tasks published in it are standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with asterisks. After creating the task, we promise to indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all problems are published for the first time.
Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than November 15, 1991 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in advanced subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 9—91” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1301” or “F1308”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope).
Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).
At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.
Problems M1305 and F1308—F1311 were proposed at the final stage of the XXV Vesezny Olympiad in mathematics and physics.

M1301. Is a tetrahedron necessarily regular if a) five dihedral angles are equal to each other; b) eight plane angles are equal to each other?
Is the paramid ABCD necessarily regular if its base ABC is a regular triangle and the three plane angles at vertex D are equal to each other?
V. Senderov

M1302. Prove that the product of the polynomial $(x+1)^{n-1}$ and any polynomial (non-zero) has at least $n$ non-zero coefficients.
G. Karpakh, A. Savin

M1303. Find all infinite sequences of natural numbers $q_1, q_2, ..., q_n$, ... also that (for all $n > 3$)
$$q_n = (q_{n-3} + q_{n-1}) / q_{n-2}.$$
O. Aliev

M1304. Let $I$ be the center of the inscribed circle of triangle ABC, $R$ the radius of the circumscribed circle around it. Prove that
$$R^3 \geqslant IA \cdot IB \cdot IC.$$
A. Solovyev

M1305. Given $2n$ different numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n, b_1, b_2, ..., b_n$. Table $n \times n$ is filled out according to the following rule: in the cell located at the intersection of the $i$th row and $j$th column, the number $a_i + b_j$ is written. Prove that if the product of numbers in all columns is the same, then so is the product in all rows.
D. Fomin

F1308. At the left edge of the cart, length $L=0,2$ m and mass $M=1$ kg, lies a cube with mass $m=0,3$ kg (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1.

The cube is only given a horizontal speed of $v_0=1$ m/s to the right. Assuming that the cart is motionless at the initial moment, determine at what distance from the left edge of the cart the cube will be located after its sliding relative to the cart stops. The friction coefficient of the cube about to the cart is $\mu=0,1$. The impacts of the cube on the walls are considered to be absolutely elastic. The cart moves across the table without friction.
A. Zilberman
