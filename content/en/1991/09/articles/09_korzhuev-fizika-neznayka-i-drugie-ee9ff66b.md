PHYSICS,
Neznayka and others

A. KORZHUEV

You have probably come across descriptions of various physical phenomena in works of fiction more than once. In this article we want to offer you several such descriptions from children's books and ask you to figure out what physical phenomena we are talking about. In order to solve the proposed “tasks,” you will have to look at the described natural phenomena from the point of view of what you already know from physics.

1. Here, for example, is this “task” from M. Prishvin’s story “The Golden Ray”:

“...The silence in the forest was such that during the day not a single shaving of influence moved. Realizing immediately that it was impossible to find Antipyts by following the tracks, having made a circle with his head held high, the Grass fell on the tobacco stream of influence and little by little, losing the air trail, then meeting him again, finally adding to the owner.”

What physical phenomenon are we talking about?

2. And here is the “task” from A. Serafimovich’s book “Forest Life”:

“Hastily and educatedly, the boy lowered his finger and, raising it, began to slowly turn it. From the side where the breeze was inexorably blowing, a feeling of coldness was felt in my finger.”

Explain why.

3. And now we give an excerpt from the well-known story by V. Kataev “The Lonely Sail of Beleget”:

“At first you might think that the stars are motionless. But no. If you look closely, you can see that the whole heavenly one is slowly turning. One star goes down for the dachas. Others, new ones, are emerging from the sea.”

Why do stars behave so “strangely”?

4. Another excerpt from the same story:

“...Gavrik stood up, stretched out his arms, rolled up his pants and, yawning, entered the water ankle-deep. Has he gone crazy, or what? Your feet are already chilled to the point of blue, and then there’s the flattery of the sea, the very sight of which gives you chills.

However, the boy knew well what he was doing. The water only looked cold. In fact, it was very warm, hotter than the impact. The boy simply warmed his feet in it.”

What causes such interesting properties of water? Why does it have an impact? What time do you think - in the evening or in the morning - did what is written about in the passage take place?

“Gavrik’s palms burned pleasantly. The oars, lowered into the green water, seemed broken.”
Try to explain this phenomenon too.

6. And here is the flight to the Moon, described by the children's writer N. Nosov in the book “Dunno on the Moon”:
“In the portholes, the sky was still black with stars, with the brightly sparkling disk of the Sun and the silvery, connected Moon above. The Sun was the same size as it is usually visible from the Earth, but the Moon had already become twice as large...”
Why did this happen, since on Earth the apparent sizes of the Moon and the Sun are almost the same?

7. An interesting description of another phenomenon in this book:
“It seemed like it was day, but at the same time it was night. This has never happened on Earth before. When the Sun is visible on Earth, the stars are not visible, and, conversely, when there are stars, the Sun is not.”
Think and try to find out whether this is science fiction and fiction or could it really be so?

8. And the third excerpt from N. Nosov’s book “Dunno on the Moon”. It describes mysterious phenomena that occur in conditions of weightlessness:
“The tongue-and-groove plugged the spout of the kettle with his finger, but the water immediately began to bubble out from under the lid. This bubble grew larger and larger, and finally broke away from the lid and, shaking as if it were made of liquid jelly, floated through the air...
...—We have some kind of miracle here! — Shpuntik said confusedly. “The bubble is coming out of the teapot.”
“A bubble is popping up—that’s not a miracle,” Znayka answered.
He approached the kettle and looked sternly at the bubble blowing out of the kettle's spout...
...—There is no miracle here, but a completely understandable scientific phenomenon. You all know that water is heated by stirring. The lower layers of water in the kettle, when heated over a fire or electric power supply, become lighter and float upward, and cold water from the upper layers is released in their place. In the kettle there is, how can I put it, a water cycle. But such a cycle will occur if the water has weight. If there is no weight, that’s how it is now...”

We invite you to think about what will happen if there is no weight, and continue Znayka’s explanation. And we suggest you think about whether the fire will burn in zero gravity.

And if you want to test yourself, look at the end of the issue (on p. 73).
