Let M and N be two squares of given n. We will say that they are located horizontally (vertically) and write M-N (correspondence, M|N) if they are intersected by an integral (vertical) line; By condition, for any pair of M and N squares, exactly one of these relations is always satisfied.

Let further X and Y be the leftmost and rightmost squares (adjacent to the sides of P). Of course, X-Y. To determine this, we assume that Y lies not lower than X.

There is at most one square Z such that Z|X. (It must be horizontally absent from X by no more than 1, so Z—Y; so the center of Z must lie in the rectangle shaded at figure 1.)

Let's consider two cases.
1) There is no such Z, i.e. Z - X for any Z. Consider the uppermost square T; let t be a straight line along its lower side. For all squares V below t, the conditions V|T and X—T must be satisfied, so their centers lie in a rectangle with horizontal side 2, shaded by figure 2. It is clear that there are at most 2 such squares (the center of at most one square lies on the left and right half of the rectangle). Thus, straight line t is the desired one.
2) There is a (unique) square Z such that Z|X. It lies above X (since Z—Y). Now let the straight line t pass along the upper side of X. Above t is located only Z. If the integral square N is located below t, then Z|N (since Z—N is false) and X—N, so that the center of N is in the rectangle shaded at figure 3. There are no more than 1 such squares N, and the straight line t is the desired one.
