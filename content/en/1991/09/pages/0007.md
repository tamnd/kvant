Anytime long. Sometimes the center of the ball is aligned with the center line of the scab, and the ball rotates around a certain horizontal axis, like a small turbine.

As the water increases to the right, the ball sinks deeper into the water, stops spinning, and its center is established on the center line of the scab, still making noticeable oscillations (Fig. 3, b, c). And finally, if you open the tap even more, the ball will completely disappear under the water (Fig. 3, f).

The immersion of a light hollow ball occurs mainly due to hydrodynamic resistance - the force acting on it from the flow. This force can be easily changed by controlling the depth of the ball's immersion.

Let’s choose to the right so that the ball is immersed in the water to a certain depth, say the value of the ball’s radius, and we are in equilibrium. It is convenient to record such a dive by eye. In an equilibrium position, the Archimedes force $F_A$ balances the gravitational force of the ball $mg$ and the resistance force $F_c$:

$$mg + F_c = F_A.$$

This equation is $m = \rho_m 4/3\pi R^3$, where $R$ is the radius of the ball, and $\rho_m$ is its average density. The radius of a qing pong ball is $R \approx 1,9$ cm, its mass is $m \approx 2,5$ g, i.e. $\rho_m \approx 0,09$ g/cm$^3$, which is approximately 11 times less than the density of water $\rho_b = 1$ g/cm$^3$ (the bottom of the ball floats, almost without sinking). The Archimedes force acting on a half-immersed ball is $F_A = 4/3\pi R^3 \rho_b/2$. For $F_c$, Newton’s formula (2) is used, substituting $S = \pi r^2$ there, where $r$ is the radius of the scab. We get

$$\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \rho_m g + C_x \pi r^2 \frac{\rho_b v^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \rho_b g,$$

from which we express the coefficient of hydrodynamic pressure $C_x$:

$$C_x = \frac{4}{3} \left( \frac{R}{r} \right)^2 \frac{gR}{v^2} \left( 1 - 2 \frac{\rho_m}{\rho_b} \right).$$

To find the value $C_x$ from here, you also need to know the values ​​$r$ and $v$, which are not so easy to change directly.
