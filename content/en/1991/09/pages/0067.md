I stopped working, partly because I was determined to buy glass, and partly because I had already heard many times about the properties of slow glass - and still could not understand it properly. A physicist friend of mine once advised me, for clarity, to imagine a sheet of slow glass as a hologram, which does not require a laser beam to recreate visual information and in which each photon of massive light passed through a spiral tube lying outside the capture radius of any of the glass atoms. This, in my opinion, pearl of inconvenience not only did not explain anything to me, but also convinced me even more strongly that a person as little inclined towards technology as I am should be interested not in causes, but only in results.

The most important result, in the eyes of the average person, was that light took longer to pass through a sheet of slow glass. The new sheets were always jet black because not a single ray of light had yet passed through them. But when such glass was placed, for example, near a forest lake, this lake appeared in it. And if the glass was then inserted into the window of a city apartment somewhere in an industrial area, then within a year this window seemed to offer a view of a forest lake. And it was not just a realistic, but a motionless picture - no, ripples ran across the water, glittering in the sun, animals randomly came to drink, birds flew across the sky, day replaced day, one season replaced another. And a year later, the beauty detained in the subatomic channels was exhausted, and a familiar gray street appeared within the framework.
