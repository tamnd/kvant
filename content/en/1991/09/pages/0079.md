Test with ropes. Do you think you can complete the task by hanging from one rope and swinging from it like Tarzan on your face? You are wrong for two reasons: firstly, the rope is not read enough to distinguish an adult, and secondly, even if you did not break the rope under your weight, you still would not be able to reach the other rope. Nevertheless, the picture provides a clue to the right decision.

By tying the scissors to one rope, you can swing them like a pendant. By pulling the other rope to the pendant and waiting for the scissors to swing towards you, you can squeeze them and tie both ropes.

Solving this problem requires two necessary ideas. It is necessary to prove, firstly, that the ropes should be swung and that, secondly, the scissors can be used as a pendulum load, that is, “for other purposes.” The difficulties that people have when using various devices and objects for other purposes are psychologically called the special term “functional limitation.” Having heard about the lower ones, we only think about how to cut the rope for them, which, of course, cannot help in solving the test. Carpet test. Since the bottle cannot be touched by anything, the test can be solved by someone who agrees that the carpet is already touching the bottle and can somehow be used to push it, for example onto the floor.

The guess turns out to be correct. Indeed, start rolling the carpet and, when the roll reaches the bottle, carefully holding it by the ends, push the bottle off the carpet with it, without touching it with anything other than rolling it into a carpet roll.
