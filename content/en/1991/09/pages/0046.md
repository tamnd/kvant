Another interesting series of problems is related to the conditions for the equilaterality of a tetrahedron. For example, a trethedron (an arbitrary triangular paramide) is called isohedral if all its faces are triangles equal to each other. A whole series of conditions are known that are necessary and sufficient for a tetrahedron to be equilateral. Without striving for a perfection, let alone a record, I will form the following multi-point problem.

Task 15. Which of the following conditions are necessary and sufficient for the trihedron $ABCD$ to be isohedral:

a) Opposite edges are equal in pairs.

b) The perimeters of all faces are equal to each other.

c) The sums of plane angles at three vertices are equal to $180^\circ$.

2) Equalities are satisfied

$$\angle BAD = \angle BCD = \angle ABC = \angle ADC.$$

3) Equalities are satisfied

$$\angle BAC = \angle BDC,$$
$$\angle ABD = \angle ACD,$$
$$\angle BAD = \angle BCD.$$

e) All faces have equal circumradii.

g) All faces have equal circumradii.

h) All faces have equal areas.

u) The segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges are pairwise perepilcular.

k) The center of the circumscribed sphere coincides with the center of mass of the trihedron.

l) The center of the circumscribed sphere coincides with the center of mass of the trihedron.

m) The centers of the circumscribed and circumscribed spheres coincide.

n) The sum of the cosines of two right angles is -2.

o) On the sphere circumscribed about the trihedron there are the centers of four balls, each of which touches one face at an internal point and the planes of the other three faces.
