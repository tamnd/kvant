Let $AA_1$ and $BB_1$ be the altitudes of the triangle, $AA_0$ and $BB_0$ the medians. The condition implies the similarity of triangles $AA_0A_1$ and $BB_0B_1$. There are two possible locations of points $A_1$, $A_0$, $B_1$, $B_0$ on the sides of triangle ABC, shown in Fig. 1, a and 1, b. In the first case, points $A$, $B$, $A_0$ and $B_0$ are located on the same circle. From this type of parallelism $A_0B_0$ and $AB$ it follows that the trapezoid $AB_0A_0B$ is equilateral, and therefore the equality $AC=BC$. In the second case, points $C$, $M$, $A_0$ and $B_0$ appear on the same circle. In the inscribed quadrilateral $CA_0MB_0$ the diagonal $A_0B_0$ is half $AB$ and bisects the diagonal CM. The diagonal of CM is $\frac{2}{3} m_c$ and is divided by the diagonal $A_0B_0$ in the ratio of 3:1. From the equality of products of diagonal segments we find $m_c^2 = 3AB^2$. Thus, the required locus of points consists of the median perpendicular to the segment $AB$ and a circle with center in the middle $AB$ and radius $AB\sqrt{3}$.

In point 6) the point is also located either on the median perpendicular to $AB$, or on the arc of a circle, from the point of which $AB$ is visible at an angle $60^\circ$.

In general, when varying the conditions of a problem, interesting series can arise. I will give an example of two such series (without trying to complete them).

There is a well-known classical theorem stating that from the equality of the two internal bisectors of a triangle it follows that it is equilateral (the Steiner-Lemus theorem). In itself, the statement of this theorem is true and acceptable, if not for the difficulties that one encounters in its proof, in contrast to related trivial theorems concerning medians or heights. Already here you can see the bad rights of the bisectors. But it is fully manifested in the next task.

Problem 14. Will triangle ABC be equilateral if it has:
a) the bisectors of the external angles of a triangle are equal, and $K$ is their point of intersection?
b) the segments $KA_1$ and $KB_1$ are equal, where $AA_1$ and $BB_1$ are the bisectors of the interior angles of the triangle, and $K$ is their intersection point?
c) does the equality $C_1A_1 = C_1B_1$ hold?
d) is the circle passing through the points $A_1$, $B_1$ and $C_1$ tangent to side $AB$ of the given triangle?

In all points the answer is negative. The most non-trivial thing happens in points d) and e).
