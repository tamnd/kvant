The densities of water and ice are equal to the correspondence $Q_e=1000 \text{ кт}/\text{м}^3$ and $Q_\pi=920 \text{ кт}/\text{м}^3$, the specific heat capacity of water is $c=4200 \text{ Дж}/(\text{кг} \cdot \text{К})$, the specific heat of melting of ice is $\lambda=330 \text{ кДж}/\text{кг}$.

Let us write down expressions for the change in volume of the “water-ice” system and for its initial volume ($S$ is the area of ​​the vessel):

$$\frac{m}{\rho_\pi} - \frac{m}{\rho_B} = bS, \quad \frac{M}{\rho_\pi} + \frac{M}{\rho_B} = HS,$$

where

$$\frac{m}{M} = \frac{b}{H} \frac{\rho_B - \rho_\pi}{\rho_B + \rho_\pi}.$$

Finally we get

$$t_B = \frac{\lambda b}{cH} \frac{\rho_B - \rho_\pi}{\rho_B + \rho_\pi} \approx 37,7 \text{ °C}.$$

F1290. Capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ (Fig. 1) are connected between two parallel buses. The wiring of the jumper length $L$ with capacitor $C_3$ concerns the busbars. A uniform magnetic field of induction B is directed perendicular to the bus plane. The bridge moves at a constant speed v. Find the charge on capacitor $C_3$.

In a moving conductor, an induced emf $E = BLv$ arises, the polarity of which is easy to determine using Lenz’s rule. This leads to a redistribution of charges on the capacitors.

Let us use the equivalent circuit shown in figure 2 (we choose the signs of the charges arbitrarily), and we obtain the system of equation

$$\begin{cases} U_{AB} = \frac{q_1}{C_1} = \frac{q_2}{C_2} = E - \frac{q_3}{C_3}, \\ q_1 + q_2 - q_3 = 0. \end{cases}$$

Solving the system for charge $q_3$, we find

$$q_3 = \frac{E}{1/C_1 + C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3} = \frac{BLv}{1/C_1 + C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3}.$$
