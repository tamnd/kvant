8. Let us leave the words to Enike: “...If there is no weight, as it is now, then the lower layer of water, having heated up, will not become lighter and will not rise up, and the rest will be down and will heat up until it turns into steam. This steam, expanding from heating, begins to rise to the cold water above it, as a result of which it bubbles higher out of the kettle...
It follows from this that it is necessary to boil water under zero-gravity conditions... in a state in which the lid closes tightly and does not allow either water or steam to pass through.”
Now it is easy to understand that fire will not burn in weightlessness, or hot air will not rise up and there will be no flow of oxygen.

6. Let us write down the equilibrium conditions of the piston in the initial and final states:

$$p_1 S - mg - p_0 S = 0,$$
$$p_2 S - mg - p_0 S - F = 0,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the piston, $p_0$ is atmospheric pressure, $F$ is the force applied to the piston. Subtracting the first equation from the second, we get

$$F = p_2 S - p_1 S$$

(the pressure force returns the present to cancel the $F$ force). We find pressure $p_2$ from the equation of the isothermal process

$$p_1 V_1 = p_2 (V_1 - S \Delta h).$$

We get

$$F = \frac{S \Delta h}{V_1 - S \Delta h} p_1 S = 6 \text{ H.}$$
