STRANGE EMPEROR
AND THE STRANGE COMMANDER
(mathematics - psychological research
with two fairy tales in simple time)

I. AKULICH

The commander Terence, getting ready for a well-deserved rest, came to the emperor and asked to pay him 5 million brass (brass is a copper coin weighing 5 g). The emperor, however, was stingy and decided to deceive the commander. He told him: “I don’t want you to be content with such a poor reward. You will go to the treasury and on the first day you will take out a coin worth 1 breaststroke, the next day a coin worth 2 breaststroke, then 4, 8, 16 and so on, each time doubling the amount of money. I will order that appropriate coins be made for you every day. And as long as you have the strength to deliver them from there without anyone else’s help, they are yours. But as soon as the next coin turns out to be too much for you, stop, and this is the end of our agreement.” Terence was very happy. He imagined a huge number of coins, one larger than the other, which he would take out of the treasury.

What was the result? Terence's enrichment lasted only 18 days, because the last coin weighed about 655 kg (he also rolled it with great difficulty, using a spear as a lever), and the next one would have been completely unliftable. The total amount earned by Terence was only 262,143 breaststroke, i.e. almost 20 times less than desired. The emperor triumphed, and Terence suffered immensely.

This story can be read in detail from Ya. I. Perelman in his “Life Mathematics”, as well as in the 8th issue of the magazine «Kvant» for 1989.

Leap into the distant past

More than a century ago, Lord Kelvin said in a lecture on the Baltic Sea: “Of all the two hundred billion men, women and children who ever walked on the wet sand with the correspondences of the world before the meeting of the British Association at Aberdeen in 1885, how many are there who, when asked, “Did the sand shrink under your foot?” would you answer anything other than “Yes”?” Indeed, as O. Reynolds showed at the mentioned meeting of the British Association, the sand under the foot does not move away, but expands, a matter of common sense.

But let's not deviate too much from our topic. It’s better to ask a similar question about the imperial award:

“Among the millions of readers of Ya. I. Perelman’s book and tens of thousands of subscribers «Kvant», how many are there who noticed that both the emperor and the commander in this story behaved at least strangely and completely illogically?”

What is this strangeness and illogicality? We'll see now.

First, let's try to estimate some of the values ​​that we will need. From the story it is clear that a coin weighing 655 kg was almost the limit of Terence’s physical capabilities: a little more - and he would not even be able to pull it off. Let’s estimate this “a little” at 45 kg, i.e. we will assume that the largest coin that can be strengthened by Terence has a mass of 700 kg (which corresponds to a denomination of 140,000 brass). estimate how many wheat grains it will take to put one grain on the first square of the chessboard, 4 on the second, etc., doubling the number of grains each time. Having received a naive answer: “Half a bag,” the author rejoiced and began to convince his acquaintance that he was not just mistaken, but very mistaken, that in fact the number of grains is of the order of a bucket, returning from cell to cell like an avalanche; gave a number and the result using a geometric progression and... received a skeptical grin in response: “What are you talking about, what trillions of tons are there? If it says half a bag, it means half a bag!” A complete fiasco! And it is not surprising, since the human mind refuses to accept too gigantic, “unearthly” numbers.

From this leap into the past we will make this choice. People can be roughly divided into two groups: one, no matter how incredible the result turns out to be, trusts calculations and strict logic, others rely on common sense and do not believe anything that contradicts it. Both are quite normal and natural.

Which of the two groups should Terence belong to? On the one hand, he was very happy, because he immediately realized to what great values ​​the denominations of the coins would increase, even if he started with only one breaststroke (Ya. I. Perelman said so: “I imagined many coins, one larger than the other”). Therefore, we confidently place the brave commander in the first group. But how could he not understand in this case that since the value of a coin is proportional to its weight, then the weight will also increase like an avalanche? Just missed it? No, this could not be: the emperor specifically drew Terence’s attention to the fact that he was allowed to take coins as long as he could lift them himself, without built help. It turns out that Terence both understood and did not understand that he was dealing with an avalanche and what it would lead to. There is no other way to describe such behavior than for countries.

The reader, apparently, is convinced that this story has a double meaning. But it can also be deepened further. Let's say that $k$ may not be integer values. At what $k$ will Terence receive the greatest reward? It is not difficult to understand that this will happen at such $k$, when on the last, 10,000th day the coin will weigh exactly 700 kg, i.e., amount to 140,000 breaststroke, which will happen at $k = \sqrt[999]{140 000} \approx 1,0012$.

Then Terence’s total income for more than 25 years of farming is: $S = \frac{k^{10000} - 1}{k - 1} \approx 120$ million breaststroke! This is many times more than he asked the emperor. I will rise, not the avalanche that thunders, but the one that crawls. Thus, one could advise Terence to respond to the emperor’s seemingly tempting offer:

“Sovereign! This reward is too generous for me. Moreover, a rapid decrease in the treasury can cause greater damage to you and the entire state. Therefore, I cannot agree to such a rapid increase in the value of coins. But to answer your proposal at all would be stupid on my part. I ask one thing: let the value of coins increase, but not so quickly. I will be quite satisfied if each coin is heavier than the previous one by only 12 hundredths of a percent.” (Note: Most likely they didn’t know the percentages then. But I think Terence could have explained his wish without them).

Trying is not torture! Lo and behold, the emperor would have swallowed the bait without noticing the hook, which would ultimately have resulted in ruin for the empire.

Here, however, a difficulty arises: the denomination of the coins will not be expressed in whole numbers, which, perhaps, was not allowed in those days.
