In the everyday sense of the word, we hope so. And in the physical?

The concept of “work” should have been on the periphery of exact science. But the need to apply theoretical results and methods to given applied ones, in the theory of machines and mechanisms, forced us to comprehend it and include it in the ranks of strict physical concepts. And Helmholch used the physical concept of “work” when formulating the law of conservation of energy.
Of particular interest is the consideration of work performed in various physical fields - gravitational, electric, magnetic. The general approach to the emergence of the given here allows us to become better acquainted with the concept of field. So, the keys to working with this issue of “Kaleidoscon” are “work in the fields.”

Questions and tasks
1. Is the same amount of work needed to be done to uniformly lift a vertical body upward to a height H and uniformly move the same body along a horizontal path to a distance equal to H?
2. How much work will be done if a force of 30 N lifts a load weighing 2 kg to a height of 5 m?
3. Will the work done by the escalator motor change if a passenger standing on a uniformly upward moving escalator staircase begins to climb it evenly?
4. A body falls freely from a certain height. Is the same amount of work done by gravity in successive equal intervals of time?
5. Attached to two nails located at the same height are the ends of a chain of length l and the ends of two hinges of interconnected rods, the total length of which is also equal to l. Whose center of gravity is lower - the chain or the rods?
6. The planet moves along an ellipse, at one of the foci of which the Colon is located. At what point of the trajectory will the plan speed be maximum and at what point will it be minimum?
