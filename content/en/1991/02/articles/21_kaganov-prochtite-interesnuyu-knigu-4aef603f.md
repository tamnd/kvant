Read an interesting book

Not far from my house is the “Akademniki” kniosk. Having passed through it, I leave and look through the exhibited publications. How many interesting popular science books are being published! Unfortunately, it is not possible to read everything. We have to strictly select. But even with the most stringent selection of books at home, purchased but unread books accumulate. “Nothing,” I reassure myself, “I’ll free you and oblige you to read it.” And indeed: a book bought a long time ago about something interesting, but familiar only to the casual, comes to hand in more or less free time and serves as the main reading for several days. As a result, without great conditions for use by a “specialist” in an area far from his specialization. Of course, you cannot become a real specialist by reading only popular science books. But you can learn a lot of interesting things, especially if the book was written by a specialist without quotes!

Among popular science books, there are those that I buy without hesitation - what a new book “Libraries «Kvant». Sometimes, it’s true, the author gives me a book. I read almost everything, whether purchased or given as a gift. And I must say, I get a lot from reading these books.

Physics (let’s be honest, I admit that in the “Library «Kvant» I read books on physics, on mathematics - very rarely) has become such a huge, such a diverse science that it is impossible to know it all professionally. And get acquainted with distant areas of accounting. And books from the “Library «Kvant»” can help.

Sometimes, looking at the book window, I feel sad: the eye notices “old” books with the famous Mobius strip on the cover. Why did they feast? I know most of them - they are interesting books. What kind of help they could provide to the student, the university lecturer, with what satisfaction the schoolchildren would read them if someone advised him to read these books. “They printed too much,” a critical reader will say. What does "a lot" mean? It seems that the circulation of one book from the “Library «Kvant»” did not exceed the number of schools in our country. But every book is a wonderful guide for grain work. I highly recommend: the path in each school will be “Library «Kvant». I think that if my advice is followed, the level of knowledge in physics and mathematics among our school graduates will noticeably increase. And most importantly, a certain number of capable children will not waste their abilities - their encounter with physics and mathematics is...

* * *

The book from the “Library «Kvant»”, which will be discussed - “Physics in the World of Polymers”, was given to me by its authors - Doctors of Physical and Mathematical Sciences Alexander Yulievich Grosberg and Alexey Removich Khokhlov. We are “related”: they, like me, are students of academician Ilya Mikhailovich Lifshts. And it is its teacher. The book contains a follow-up article, “Physics of Polymers and Biophysics in the Works of Ilya Mikhailovich Lifshtz.” In it they talk not only about the content side of the scientific work of I.M. Lifshts, but also about Lifshts the lecturer, Lifshts the teacher, Lifshts the person. “... We consider it our duty to remember here Ilya Mikhailovich Lifshts (1917-1982), with whom we were lucky enough to study and who was not only the author of the multi-gene or specific scientific results mentioned in this book, but also created around himself an inspiring atmosphere of ardent interest in science.” True, this quote is not from the afterword, but from the preface. And she ends with the words: “The reader will now judge our attempt to recreate this atmosphere in the book.”

In my opinion, the attempt was deleted...

* * *

There are receptions of different styles. The goal is to present the contents of the book in such detail and clearly that after the decision you don’t have to read the book at all. And if you read, then only to familiarize yourself with the details omitted by the author of the decision. Sometimes, when a book is difficult to reach, such slaughter is very important. But I want you, dear reader, to gain insight into the world of polymers. Therefore, this style is inappropriate here. Moreover, the style of devastating massacre is inappropriate - I like the book. It is written interestingly about interesting things.

A polymer is a molecule consisting of a huge number of units. Artificial polymers contain from hundreds to tens of thousands of units, and in natural polymers the number of units reaches a billion (10^9) and even ten billion. This fact alone makes the polymer a unique object. For example, the behavior of one molecule can be described in terms and methods of statistical physics, rather than mechanics, and the predictions obtained along this path turn out to be reliable. Phase transitions can occur in one polymer molecule - and this would seem to be a characteristic feature of a macrosoppech object...

For the authors, polymer is the first and foremost physical object, the properties of which can be explained if you understand how polymers and their individual units move and interact with each other. Characteristics
