Matematika 9—11

Unexpected
inverse problem

It is known that a theorem converse to a given theorem is a theorem whose condition is the conclusion of the given theorem, and the conclusion is the condition of the given theorem. We propose six problems in the form of “direct” theorems. All of them are very simple and are known to primary schoolchildren. Prove the converse of these “direct” theorems on your own. You will see that all the proofs of inverse theorems are much more difficult than the proofs of “direct” theorems.

Direct theorems

1. In a trapezoid, the segment connecting the midpoints of the bases divides it into two equal figures.

2. In a parallelogram, the sum of the distances between the midpoints of opposite sides is equal to its half-perimeter.

3. If the point $H$ is the orthocenter (the point of intersection of the heights) of the acute triangle $ABC$, then $\angle HBA = \angle HCA, \angle HAB = \angle HCB$.

4. If point $O$ is the center of a circle circumscribed about triangle $ABC$, then $\angle OAB = \angle OBA = \angle OBC = \angle OCB = \angle OCA = \angle OAC$.

5. In an equilateral triangle, the center of the circle inscribed in the triangle lies on the Euler straight line. (Euler's straight line is a straight line that contains the orthocenter, the point of intersection of the medians and the center of the circumcircle of the triangle.)

6. In an equilateral triangle, the bisketrices of the angles at the base are equal.

Directions for proving converse theorems

1. In the quadrilateral $ABCD$, the point $M$ is the midpoint of $AD$, the point $N$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that $S_{ABN} = S_{DCN}$. Then $AD \parallel BC$, i.e. $ABCD$ is a trapezoid.

2. Prove that the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a parallelogram (Fig. 1). Let the points $M, N, E$ be the midpoints of the segments $AB, DC$ and $AC$, respectively. Then $AD \parallel EN$ and $EN = \frac{1}{2} AD, BC \parallel ME$ and $ME = \frac{1}{2} BC$. Since $MN \leq ME + EN$, then $MN \leq \frac{AD + BC}{2}$.
