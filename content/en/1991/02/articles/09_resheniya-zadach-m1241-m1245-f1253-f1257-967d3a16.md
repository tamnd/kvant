M1241. There are 1990 piles consisting of corresponding 1, 2, 3, ..., 1990 stones. In one step you can select the same number of stones from any set of piles. In what minimum number of steps can all the stones be selected?

Answer: 11. When solving this problem, it is convenient to assume that we have 1990 boxes containing 1, 2, ..., 1990 stones. After each throwing of stones, we will divide the boxes into groups containing the same number of stones. Let there be $n$ groups of boxes at some points (some of them may already be empty). The next throwing out of stones is done like this: we select several boxes belonging to some $k$ groups and throw out the same number of stones from them. As a result, boxes that belonged to different groups before discarding will still contain different numbers of stones, and boxes from which nothing is thrown away will remain in the same groups where they were before. Thus, if at the beginning there were only $n$ groups of boxes, then after the next ejection the number of groups will be no less than $\max \{n, n-k\} \geqslant \frac{1}{2} n$. So, at each step the number of groups of boxes decreases by no more than 2 times.

If at the beginning there were 1990 different groups of boxes, then at the next step there will be no less than 995, then 498, 249, 125, 63, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. The last group may already consist of empty boxes. So, the total number of throws is at least 11.

In 11 steps you can select all the stones if you do this: first, from all boxes containing at least 996 stones, select 995 stones each. Next

M1244. In the Senate, consisting of 30 senators, every two are friends or at enmity, and each is at enmity with 6 others. Find the total number of senatorial tracks in which either all three are pairwise friends, or all three are at enmity with each other.

ANSWER: 1990.
Let $x$ be the number of senatorial tracks that satisfy the conditions of the problem, $y$ be the number of remaining tracks. Then $x + y = \frac{30 \cdot 29 \cdot 28}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3} = 4060$. If each of the senators writes down all the tracks in which he is included and in each of which two other senators are simultaneously either his friends or enemies, a list of $\frac{23 \cdot 22}{2} + \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2} = 268$ tracks will be obtained. All such lists will indicate $30 \cdot 268 = 8040$ tracks. But each commission of the type we are interested in will be indicated in three lists, and each other three – in one. Therefore $3x + y = 8040$. Solving the system $x + y = 4060, 3x + y = 8040$, we obtain $x = 1990$.

F1253. A rectangular vessel with water is placed on two supports spaced at a distance of $L$ from each other. Above the vessel, on a crossbar, a piece of lead with mass $M$ is suspended on a thread at a distance $l$ from the center of the vessel (see figure). The reaction forces of the supports are equal to $N_1$ and $N_2$. The thread is lengthened so that the lead is browned into the water. What will be the reaction forces of the supports after this? The density of lead in the groove is greater than the density of water.

When the weight is in the water, the tension force of the thread will become less by the amount of the buoyancy force - the Archimedes force

$$F_A = Mg/n.$$

At the same time, the water level in the vessel rises, and the force of water pressure on the bottom will increase by the same amount (Newton’s third law). For a symmetrical vessel, the point of application of this additional force is in the middle of the day of the vessel.

Obviously, the sum of the support reaction forces remains the same:

$$N'_1 + N'_2 = N_1 + N_2.$$

Pozny, if the force $N'_1$ acting from the side of the left support becomes greater by magnitude $f$ than before, then the force $N'_2$ will also be greater by the same magnitude.

From the equality to zero of the total moment of additional forces acting on the system:

$$2fL/2 = F_A l$$

we get

$$f = F_A l/L = Mgl/(nL).$$

Then finally

$$N'_1 = N_1 + f = N_1 + Mgl/(nL),$$

$$N'_2 = N_2 - f = N_2 - Mgl/(nL).$$

F1255. The surface of the lifeless planet is covered with a thick layer of frozen carbon dioxide. It is proposed to create an atmosphere of pure oxygen on it, breaking down the carbon dioxide into carbon and oxygen. How long will it take to do this if $10^6$ moles decompose in 1 second? It is necessary to obtain a pressure of $p=0,2$ atm. Consider that a temperature of $T=200$ K will be established at the surface, at which the evaporation of carbon dioxide can be avoided. The mass of the planet is $M=7,5 \times 10^{22}$ kel (approximately equal to the mass of Lunya), the radius is $R=1750$ km.

If the thickness of the atmosphere is small compared to the radius of the planet (this will definitely need to be checked), then for the mass of oxygen we can write:

$$m = 4\pi R^2 p/g.$$

We find the acceleration of free pressure $g$ on the surface of the planet from the law of universal gravitation:

$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2}.$$

Then

$$m = 4\pi R^4 p/(GM) \approx 5 \cdot 10^{17} \text{ кг.}$$

This is approximately $1,5 \cdot 10^{19}$ moles.

Each molecule of carbon dioxide produces one molecule of oxygen during decomposition, which means the process will take $\approx 1,5 \cdot 10^{13}$ from $\approx 500,000$ years. Not so much...

To roughly estimate the thickness of the atmosphere, let’s find the oxygen density near the surface of the planet:

$$\rho = pM/(RT) \approx 0,4 \text{ кг/m}^3.$$

At this density, the thickness of the atmosphere

$$h = m/(4\pi R^2 \rho) \approx 30 \text{ км.}$$

In fact, the thickness is several times greater (density decreases with height), however, this value is many times less than the radius of the planet.

According to the law of conservation of energy,

$$4A\mu mg = \frac{mv_0^2}{2},$$

$$\mu mg = ka,$$

where $\mu$ is the friction coefficient, $k$ is the spring stiffness. From here we get (taking into account that $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$)

$$\mu g = \frac{k}{m} a = \frac{4\pi^2}{T_0^2} a.$$

Finally

$$A = \frac{v_0^2}{8\mu g} = \frac{v_0^2T_0^2}{32\pi^2a}.$$

$$x = -a - (A + 2a) \cos \omega_0 t,$$ where $\omega_0 = 2\pi/T_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$.

$$v = x' = (A + 2a)\omega_0 \sin \omega_0 t.$$
