A battery with a voltage of $U_0=1,5$ V is connected between one end of the coil and turn number 300, counting from this end. Which points will flow along both parts of the coil after a time of $t=0,1$ s after connection? The battery is considered ideal, magnetic flux dissipation is pretenbreich.

in this part of the coil is equal to 1.5 V. Then the total emf is equal to 10 V, and the current flows through the resistor according to the magnitude

$I=10$ V/200 Ohm=0.05 A.

This current appears immediately after connecting the cable - in an abrupt manner (in other words, the rise time of this current from zero to the calculated value is determined by “electrostatics” - inter-bar capacitors, however, this time can be made very small; in any case, a detailed analysis is very complicated, and we will not deal with it).

Is there a violation of the well-known rule that the current through the coils cannot change abruptly? In our case, everything is in order - after all, in fact, the total magnetic flux should not change abruptly, but it is determined by the currents in both parts of the coil. Consequently, in a smaller part of the coil there will be a current surge immediately after switching on. Let's do the calculation. To ensure that there is no jump in the magnetic flux immediately after switching on, the fields of both parts of the coil must compensate each other. The fields of all turns in the core add up, which means there is a correspondence between the currents.

$n I_{n0}=(N-1)I$, and $I_{n0}=U_0(N-n)N/(n^2R)=17/60$ A,

where $I_{n0}$ is the initial current in the smaller part of the coil.

The induced emf in a small part of the coil is equal to $U_0$, and the current in it increases according to a linear law. The inductance of the part of the coil in which the current changes is easy to find: there is no flux dissipation in the core, the fields of all turns add up, therefore, the inductance is proportional to the square of the number of turns. Then

$L_n=Ln^2/N^2=0,125$ Gn.

According to Faraday's law (we will only take into account the changing part of the magnetic flux),

$-L_n \frac{dI_n}{dt}=U_0$.

Pozny finally

$I_n=I_{n0}+U_0 t/L_n=1,62$ A.
