I) no two black vertices were adjacent;
II) the number of black vertices was greater than the number of white ones.
Then the polyhedral M cannot be inscribed in the sphere.
Before moving on to the proof of the theorem, let us make some remarks. Let us have a fixed sphere and some other angle whose edge intersects this sphere. Take the point of intersection of the edge with the sphere and draw a tangent plane (Fig. 3) through it. A dihedral angle cuts a linear angle in this plane. We will call this angle the linear angle of the dihedral angle relative to a given sphere, or simply the relative angle of the dihedral angle. Obviously, the relative angle does not depend on the choice of one of the two points of intersection of the edge with the sphere. If the edge touches the sphere, then it is convenient to set the relative angle values ​​equal to zero.
Let us now consider a convex polyhedral angle, all of whose edges intersect a given sphere. The relative angles of its dihedral angles will be called the relative angles of a given polyhedral angle. Let a polygonal angle have $n$ faces, and its vertex lies on the sphere. Then the sum of its relative angles is equal to $\pi(n-2)$. In fact, let us draw a tangent plane through the vertex of a polyhedral angle, and then draw a plane parallel to it, a secant and a sphere, and all the edges of the polyhedral angle (this is possible, since all the edges intersect the sphere). A polyhedral angle cuts out a polygon in this last plane, the angles of which are equal to the relative angles, which follows from the theorem on angles with pairwise parallel sides (Fig. 4).

Let's move on to the proof of the theorem itself. Let us assume that the polygon $M$ hangs into a sphere. Let us denote the relative angle of a dihedral angle with edge $P_i$ by $\gamma_i$. Let $n_k$ be the number of edges converging at vertex $B_k$.

From what was proved above it follows that the sum of the relative angles at the vertex $B_k$ is equal to $\pi(n_k-2)$. Let $\beta_i = \pi - \gamma_i$, angle $\beta_i$ be an external relative angle, then $\gamma_i = \pi - \beta_i$. If the sum of internal relative angles is $\pi(n_k-2)$, then the sum of external relative angles is $2\pi$. So, if at vertex $B_k$ the edges $P_i, P_j, \dots, P_l$ are combined, then $\beta_i + \beta_j + \dots + \beta_l = 2\pi$.

Let's write out similar equalities for each vertex of the polyhedron, so let's multiply the equalities corresponding to the black vertices by -1 and add them all. There are more black vertices, therefore, there will be a negative number on the right side. The distance is the amount costing the left side. If the $i$th edge goes from a black vertex to a white one, then the number $\beta_i$ enters the left side once with a “+” sign and once with a “-” sign, for a total of 0; if - from white vertex to white, then $\beta_i$ enters with “+” both times.
