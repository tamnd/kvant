Now let us discuss the case when, when the load oscillates, it is regularly hit with a hammer. figure 4 shows a graph of the displacement $x$ of the load versus time $t$ (from the problem conditions it is obvious that the change $\Delta T$ in the period of steady oscillations is quite small compared to the period $T_0$ itself). As can be seen from the graph, the deflections of the load in both directions are the same relative to the point with coordinate $+a$, $A$ is the desired oscillation amplitude. According to the law of conservation of energy,

$$4A\mu mg = \frac{mv_0^2}{2},$$

and

$$\mu mg = ka,$$

where $\mu$ is the friction coefficient, $k$ is the spring stiffness. From here we get (taking into account that $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$)

$$\mu g = \frac{k}{m} a = \frac{4\pi^2}{T_0^2} a.$$

Finally

$$A = \frac{v_0^2}{8\mu g} = \frac{v_0^2T_0^2}{32\pi^2a}.$$

Let's consider the section of the graph $MNK$ (see Fig. 4). Movement here occurs according to the law

$$x = -a - (A + 2a) \cos \omega_0 t,$$ where $\omega_0 = 2\pi/T_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$.

Load speed

$$v = x' = (A + 2a)\omega_0 \sin \omega_0 t.$$

In steady state, section $MN$ is absent. The speed at point $N$ is equal to the initial speed $v_0$ after hitting with a hammer:

$$v_0 = (A + 2a)\omega_0 \sin \omega_0 \Delta T \approx A\omega_0^2\Delta T,$$

where

$$\Delta T \approx \frac{v_0}{A\omega_0^2} = \frac{32\pi^2a}{v_0^2T_0^2} \frac{v_0T_0^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{8a}{v_0}.$$

About the approximate solution. The condition $A \gg 2a$ means

$$\frac{v_0^2T_0^2}{32\pi^2a} \gg 2a,$$

where

$$v_0T_0 \gg 8\pi a.$$ (*)

The condition $\Delta T \ll T_0$ means

$$\frac{A\omega_0^2}{v_0} \ll T_0,$$ or $v_0T_0 \gg 16\pi a.$ (**)
