And then there comes a moment in the life of a drop when its size becomes equal, and then less than the length of the free space of air molecules (between contacts with each other) $l \approx 10^{-7}$ m. This means that the drop is no longer in a continuous, but a rarefied environment, and the laws of life of a drop change. How long does a drop have to live?

Now approximately $1/6n \langle u \rangle$ molecules fall per unit surface per unit time. Air ($n - \text{плотность молекул}, \langle u \rangle = \sqrt{8RT/\pi M}$ - their average thermal speed). Each molecule brings energy of the order of $3/2kT$ (more precisely $5/2kT$, if we take into account that the airiness is from diatomic molecules; but this is not important for estimating the order of magnitude). $$4\pi r^2 \frac{1}{6} \rho \langle u \rangle \frac{5RT}{2M}.$$
