Fur standing on end

In the July 1990 issue «Kvant» we offered readers the problem “Drying Fur.” Let us repeat its conditions:

In the Pushcha industry, the problem of drying fur after washing arises. Fur inserts stick together, forming unsightly “icicles”. This spoils the appearance of the fur. It is necessary to find a way to separate the fur strands during the drying process.

Judging by the open readers, this problem interested them no less than the exotic task of saving the ancient Greek article (see «Kvant» No. 7, 1990). However, as before, many things went the traditional way: if it is necessary to perform some action, then a special device must be constructed. Therefore, the editor has received many letters suggesting various kinds of cunning tricks. They, according to the authors, will separate the stuck together fibers of the fur, thereby allowing the tellum of influence to pass inside the fur.

It must be said that the majority of the solutions sent are clearly uninventive and imperfect. The concept of an ideal solution or ideal final result (abbreviated IFR) is one of the main concepts of the theory of solving inventive problems (abbreviated SRI3). It was created by our outstanding compatriot G.S. Altshuller. Currently, TRI3 is successfully used all over the world.

So let's get back to the task. Let’s imagine the ideal result: the fur fibers will rearrange themselves and fuse together, now air flows freely inside the fur. Drying is carried out quickly, the appearance of the fur is undesirable. The correct formulation of the ideal solution often makes the answer very powerful. The main thing is not to be afraid of the seeming impossibility of implementing a decision. In mathematical problems, readers are probably accustomed to labeling the desired quantity as x. Similarly, we need to find the required x-element that allows us to carry out the required action.

The guys who proposed calculations between during drying believed that the x-element was the teeth of the comb. Too pronunciation! In this case, for some drying, you have to pay for the design of the entire calculation machine. A much more beautiful solution was sent by Oleg Grebennikov from Ufa. He writes: “Fur fibers stick together due to the surface tension of water. Make the fur non-wettable, then the need for drying will be eliminated, and the body can be shaken off.” Perhaps ximists should think about these solutions, but we, without delving into its analysis, will populate the x-element from the field of physics.

Let us formulate the problem as follows: what physical field can “melt” fur fibers in different directions? The answer is very interesting. Probably all readers have seen the school experience: when charging an electric sultan, its petals spread out to the sides. However, not everyone was able to use knowledge of physics to solve a technical problem. The power of psychological inertia is too great.

But the participants of the “On the Inventor” circle of the Sepychevskaya school in the Vereshaginsky district of the Perm region (the head of the circle is A.I. Kolobov) found a purely physical x-element. We present their solutions to the problem in full.
