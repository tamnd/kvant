Epilogue,
or What they kept silent about in Tragedini and who needs it

Well, of course, we haven’t discussed everything. For example, as already mentioned, one could consider the so-called processes of molecular transfer of mass (diffusion), momentum (viscosity) and energy (conductivity). This would require knowledge of the corresponding coefficients and would make it possible to describe the section of droplet motion from point $D$ to point $S$, beyond which the free-molecular flow regime begins. Further, the influence of oxygen present in the air, a 《alien》 gas from the point of view of the substance of the nitrogen drop, has not been discussed; one can expect that this oxygen, like any substance, will tend to diffuse to where it does not exist, that is, inside the drop; in any case, it somehow interferes with the evaporating nitrogen molecules. It is also not taken into account that the shape of the drop is not necessarily spherical; it fluctuates during movement (this can be seen from figure 2, $\theta$). Further, we believe that the temperature of the drop throughout its entire life remains equal to the temperature of saturated vapor at atmospheric pressure above a flat surface of liquid nitrogen, but this may not be entirely true.

And finally, who needs this drop with its tragedy?

The behavior of individual drops and entire clouds of them needs to be known in many cases. For example, if you need to make visible (visualize) the flow pattern around a model of a future aircraft, add droplets of liquid nitrogen to the flow and place them in the right place - as a result of the scattering of light on the “fog” consisting of these droplets, as well as some of the water and carbon dioxide condensed in their cold traces, all the dependencies of the air around the model will become visible. And in the so-called cryogenic wind tunnels, liquid nitrogen is specially injected into the flow to cool it, and it is important to know how quickly nitrogen droplets with different initial sizes evaporate. And in many processes of chemical technology, purification of industrial gases and in other cases, it is necessary to know the initial behavior of an individual drop (of any substance) with a changing mass.

It is useful for us that this little tragedy of a nitrogen drop allows us to discuss some processes and show how experiment and theoretical reasoning complement each other.
