M1242. On two sides AB and BC, a regular 2n-gon is taken at points K and N so that angle KEN, where E is the vertex opposite B, is equal to π/2n. Prove that NE is the bisector of angle KNC.

On rays BA and BC we also plot points A' and C' that AA' = AB, CC' = BC. Points A', B and C' are the vertices of a regular 2n-gon whose center coincides with point E (a regular hexagon is shown at figure). When rotated through an angle $\frac{\pi}{n}$ around point E, point A' goes to B, and point B goes to C'. In this case, the point K goes to the neoctor point K' on the segment CC'. Since EK = EK', $\angle KEN = \angle NEK' = \frac{\pi}{2n}$, triangles KNE and K'NE are congruent. But this also means that $\angle KNE = \angle K'NE = \angle CNE$.

N. Adakhanov, N. Nechvetaev, D. Tereshin, D. Fomin

M1243. a) The equation $x^2 + x + = 0$ is written on the board. The first of the two cerratzs names any three numbers, the second arranges them according to his choice together with asterisks. Can the first ensure that the resulting equation has different rational roots, or can the second always accommodate it?
b) The equation $x^3 + x^2 + x + = 0$ is written on the board. The first of the two cerratzs names any three numbers, the second puts it in place of any of the asterisks; then the first one calls out another number, the second one puts it in place of one of the two remaining asterisks; finally, the first one puts any number in place of the last one a) Answer: can. The first player wins if he names pairwise distinct integers a, b, c whose sum is 0 (for example, 1, -3 and 2). Then the equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has roots $x_1 = 1$, $x_2 = \frac{c}{a} \neq 1$.
