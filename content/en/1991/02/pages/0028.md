We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than April 15, 1991 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant».
Solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 2 - 91” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1266” or “F1273”. In the column “...sender’s address” please write your first and last name legibly. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision verification in this envelope).
The conditions of each original problem proposed for publication are assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).
At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.

M1266. Inside a circle of radius 1990 centered at the origin, there are 555 points with integer coordinates, no three of which lie on the same straight line. Prove that there are two triangles of equal area with vertices at these points.
K. Kokhas

M1267. Let $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$ be some permutation of the numbers 1, 2, ..., n; $r_k$ is the remainder when dividing the number $a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_n$ by $n$ ($k=1, 2, ..., n$). Prove that among the numbers $r_1, r_2, ..., r_n$ at least $\sqrt{n}$ are distinct.
L. Kurlyandchik

M1268. An arbitrary point $X$ is taken inside the triangle $ABC$. Lines $AX, BX, CX$ intersect sides $BC, CA$ and $AB$ at points $A_1, B_1, C_1$. Let $AB_1 \cdot AC_1 \cdot BC_1 \cdot BA_1 \cdot CA_1 \cdot CB_1 = \Pi$. Prove that the area $S$ of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ is equal to $S = \sqrt{\Pi}/2R$, where $R$ is the radius of the circumscribed circle around triangle $ABC$.
A. Girchich

M1269. On the data plane there is a triangle $ABC$. Line $p$ is parallel to line $AB$ and located at a distance $AC$ from it so that there are no interior points of triangle $ABC$ inside the strip formed by these two lines ($p$ and $AB$). Line $q$ is parallel to line $AC$ and located at a distance $AB$ from it so that there are no interior points of triangle $ABC$ inside the strip formed by these two lines ($q$ and $AC$). Lines $p$ and $q$ intersect at point $L$. Prove that the line $AL$ passes through the midpoint $BC$.
Y. Koval

M1270. Prove that if the last decimal digit of the number $m$ is 5, then $12^m + 9^m + 8^m + 6^m$ is divisible by 1991.
N. Vasiliev

F1273. A rope of length $l$ is fixed at one of its ends to the upper spheres of radius $R$ (Fig. 1). At some points the rope is released. Find the acceleration of the rope immediately after this. There is no friction.
A. Vyshko

F1274. In a heat-conducting state, divided in half by a partition, steam is close to saturation; on the left side - at a temperature of +20 °C, and on the right - at +50 °C. The septum is removed. What pressure will be established in the vessel? What pressure will it have if the contents are heated to +50 °C? cool to +20 °C? Initially there is no water in the vessel. Take the data necessary for the calculation into tables.
M. Yaskevich
