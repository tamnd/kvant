Act 3. Inevitable braking

As the drop accelerates, the air resistance becomes lower and lower. How to describe this power?

How many times have they told the world that the force of air resistance moves

body moving relative to it (aerodynamic force) is proportional to the density of the air, the square of the size of the body (or its cross-sectional area) and the square of the speed:

$$F_a = -C_a\rho r^2 v^2.$$

This statement can be obtained, for example, from dimension messages. Here the minus sign takes into account that the force is directed in the direction opposite to the movement, and the dimensionless coefficient $C_a$, generally speaking, depends on the speed, but this dependence is weak and can be obtained from experiments or other more complex theoretical reports.

You can also use reasoning. The flow of air mass inside a cylinder with a cross section equal to the cross section $\pi r^2$ of a spherical drop is equal to $\rho v \pi r^2 (\kappa r/c)$; but a unit of mass carries momentum (specific impulse) $mv/m = v$, so the total flux of momentum in that cylinder is $\rho v \pi r^2 v = \rho v^2 \pi r^2(H)$. Of course, the line of gas current when flowing around a drop is somehow bent - so some kind of factor $C_a$ appears, taking into account everything that is not taken into account in these considerations.

So, taking into account this aerodynamic force, the equation of motion of a drop (Newton’s second law) can be written as

$$m \frac{dv}{dt} = mg - \rho \pi r^2 v^2 C_a.$$

And if we divide both parts by the mass of the drop $m = 4/3 \pi r^3 \rho_0$, we get

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = g - \frac{3}{4} C_a \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} \frac{v^2}{r},$$

i.e., the acceleration of the drop consists of two parts: the acceleration of the gravitational force $g$ and the negative acceleration (braking) associated with the aerodynamic force.
