Drawing in
stereometric
tasks

I. SHARYGIN

When solving stereometric problems, the role of the drawing increases significantly. The drawing not only acts as a valid element of the solution, but also a large part is included in the solution method and provides the keys to solving the problem. Some types of such tasks will be discussed in this article.

First of all, we note that spatial bodies can be divided into two groups: “good”, convenient for depiction, and “bad”. The first group includes triangular ones (primarily regular ones), parallelepipeds, triangular and quadrangular parameters. Ko the second - $n$-gonal ($n > 4$) and parameters, truncated parameters and round bodies, especially the sphere, are recognized.

One of the most frustrating techniques is that in a given “inconvenient” construction a “good” polyhedron is isolated as a key element. This example gives

Problem 1. Find the two-dimensional angle between adjacent lateral edges of a right hexagon if the plane angle at the vertex is $\alpha$.

Solution. Here it is convenient to consider a triangular parameter - a kind of 1/6 part of the given one - the vertices of which are the ends of the height and one side of the base of the original parameter (Fig. 1). In this parameter, $OB=OA$, $\angle BOA=60^\circ$, $SO$ are perpendicular to the plane $AOB$, $\angle ASB=\alpha$. The dihedral angle between planes $ASB$ and $OBS$ (or $OAS$) is equal to half the desired angle (required angle $\varphi$). Let us drop from $A$ perpendiculares $AD$ and $AK$ to $BS$ and $OB$. Then $\angle KDA=\varphi/2$ (prove it). Putting $OA=OB=R$, we have

$$AK=R\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, AB=R, AD=\frac{R}{\cos \alpha/2},$$

$$\sin \frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{AK}{AD}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos \frac{\alpha}{2},$$

$$\varphi=2\arcsin\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos \frac{\alpha}{2}\right).$$

This very simple task is purely illustrative.
