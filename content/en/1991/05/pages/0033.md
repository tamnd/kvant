In exactly the same way, let’s write Ohm’s law for the AfCdA circuit:

$$I_1(t) = -\frac{(2\pi - 3\sqrt{3})R^2}{2r} \frac{\Delta B(t)}{\Delta t}.$$

Substituting the value $I_1(t)$ into the previous equation, we obtain the equality

$$I_2(t) = -\frac{(10\pi + 3\sqrt{3})R}{10r} \frac{\backslash B(t)}{\backslash t}.$$

According to Ampere's law, each element of the ring $\Delta l$ with a current $I(t)$ will be acted upon by a magnetic field of a force equal in magnitude to $\Delta F = I(t)\Delta l \cdot B(t)$ and directed along the radius of the ring. Due to the mirror symmetry of these forces relative to the horizontal axis passing through the centers of the ring, the resulting force acting on each stake in the vertical direction is zero. The lack of symmetry of forces relative to the vertical axis passing through the center of the left ring ($I_1(t) \neq I_2(t)$), on the contrary, leads to the appearance of a resultant force acting along the horizontal direction. Obviously, it is equal to the difference in forces acting along the arc AfC and symmetrically to the arc on the opposite side of the ring:

$$F = F_2 - F_1 = I_2(t)l_{AC}B(t) - I_1(t)l_{AC}B(t),$$

where $l_{AC}$ is the length of the chord on which the arc AfC ($l_{AC} = 2R\sin \alpha/2 = R$) rests. The final expression for the force $F$ will be

$$F = -\frac{9\sqrt{3}R^3}{5r}B(t) \frac{\Delta B(t)}{\Delta t}.$$

The action of this force during a short period of time $\Delta t$ leads to a change in the momentum of the ring:

$$m\Delta v = F\Delta t = -\frac{9\sqrt{3}R^3}{5r}B(t)\Delta B(t) = -\frac{9\sqrt{3}R^3}{10r}\Delta(B^2(t)),$$

and, therefore, the stake will gain speed

$$v = \frac{9\sqrt{3}R^3}{10mr}B_0^2.$$
