Fig. 3. Summation of oscillations of continuously changing frequencies: a) first pulse; b) second impulse; c) the result of addition.

Instead of simple signals of a constructed frequency, animals emit complex ones. They smoothly change the period (frequency) of the oscillations that fill each pulse. The superposition of such active waves leads to the fact that at the place of reception they are summed with different frequencies. What does the addition of such waves lead to?

Battles
1. Prove that a number of the form $2^{k-1} \cdot (2^k - 1)$, where $2^k - 1$ is a prime number, is perfect.
2. Let us denote by $\sigma(n)$, where $n$ is a natural number, the sum of all divisors of the number $n$. Prove that if the numbers $n_1$ and $n_2$ are coprime, then $\sigma(n_1 \cdot n_2) = \sigma(n_1) \cdot \sigma(n_2)$.
3. Let $n$ be an even perfect number. Then $\sigma(n) = 2n$. Represent $n$ as $2^{k-1}b$, where $b \geq 2$ and $b$ are odd, and prove that $b = (2^k - 1)c$. Prove further that $c = 1$ and $2^k - 1$ are prime numbers.
