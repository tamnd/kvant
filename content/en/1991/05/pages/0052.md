In the next task, the main problem (as in task 4) is to find the desired graphical interpretation.

Problem 6. Two balls of radius 1 and 3 touch each other externally. Through the point M, located at a distance of 3 from the center of the changing ball, two straight lines are drawn, tangent to the common balls. Find the angle between the tangents if you know that one of them forms an angle of 45° with a straight line passing through the center of the balls.

Solution. Let us denote the centers of the balls by $O_1$ and $O_2$, respectively, and by $A$ and $B$ the points of tangency with them of the tangent that forms an angle of 45° with the straight line $O_1O_2$. And now this construction is sufficient to the triangular prism $O_1ACDBO_2$ (Fig. 7). Since edge $AB$ is perpendicular to edges $AO_1$ and $BO_2$, then this prism is straight. In the rectangle $O_1CO_2D$ the diagonal $O_1O_2$ forms an angle of 45° with the edge $O_1D$, therefore this rectangle is a square. Its diagonal is $O_1O_2=4$, which means the side of this square is $2\sqrt{2}$. The bases of our prism are rectangular triangles with right angles at the vertices $O_1$ and $D$ ($O_1C^2=9-1=AC^2-AO_1^2$). Point $M$, by condition, is located at a distance of 3 from $O_1$ on straight line $AB$. But $O_1B=3$. Thus, two cases arise: 1) $M$ coincides with $B$; 2) $M$ symmetrine $B$ relative to the point $A$.

Next, we note that the second tangent of the symmetrine $AB$ is relative to the plane $MO_1O_2$. Therefore, the angle between the tangents is equal to twice the angle between one of them and the plane $MO_1O_2$ (or complements this angle to $180^\circ$).

Consider the first case: $M$ coincides with $B$. We need to find the angle between the straight line $BA$ and the plane $BO_1O_2$. This angle is equal to the angle between $DO_1$ and the plane $BO_1O_2$. Consider the triangular paramid $BDO_1O_2$. Its volume is $\frac{1}{6}BD \cdot DO_2 \cdot DO_1=\frac{4}{3}$. Let us now take the triangle $BO_1O_2$ as the base: $BO_2=3$, $BO_1=3$, $O_1O_2=4$. The area of ​​$BO_1O_2$ is equal to $2\sqrt{5}$. If $h$ is the height dropped from $D$ to $BO_1O_2$, then $\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{5}h=\frac{4}{3}$. From this equation we find $h=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$. Thus, the sinua between $DO_1$ and the plane $BO_1O_2$ is equal to $\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$. This means $\frac{\alpha}{2}=\arcsin \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$ (or arctg $\frac{1}{3}$), where $\alpha$ is the desired angle.

The second case is considered independently. However, we can report that in the second case the answer is the same as in the first. This follows from the fact that $AO_1$ is the common perpendicular between $AB$ and $O_1O_2$, and the points $M$ corresponding to each case are symmetric about $A$.
