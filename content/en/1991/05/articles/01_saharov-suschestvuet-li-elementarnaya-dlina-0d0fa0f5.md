DOES IT EXIST
ELEMENTARY LENGTH?

A. SAKHAROV

Scientists all over the world are expecting very important things from particle physics.
results of practical and general philosophical significance, perhaps even clarification of the basic concepts of
space, time and principles.
Such changes in fundamental principles are not to be expected in all
other branches of physics in which atomic particles (electrons, photo-
tones, nuclei) can be considered as given. In biophysics, molecular and crystal physics, optics and most other branches of physics, the basic principles of quantum mechanics, statistical physics and relative theory provide a solid, reliably proven basis for the theoretical description and explanation of observed phenomena, for new predictions, discoveries and practical applications (like the transistor, laser, electroliminescence, parametric resonance, Mössbauer effect, holography, etc.).

And we are confident that any phenomenon in these industries can receive an exhaustive description based on known principles, perhaps, however, with the use of more powerful mathematical tools and new experimental data (as has been the case, for example, in recent years for the phenomena of superfluidity and superconductivity).

But as soon as physicists try to explain the nature of mass, pollution and other properties of the elementary particles themselves, their mutual transformations and interactions, it is printed that in this area of ​​physics the currently known basic principles are not enough. Particles with high penetrating power were discovered in cosmic rays, and it was assumed that these were electrons that had high energy and therefore “do not obey” quantum electrodynamics. But it soon turned out that these were just particles with a mass 200 times greater (they were called μ-mesons), and it was this “trivial” circumstance that explained their penetrating ability. There are currently no known phenomena that could be interpreted as a clear violation of modern theory. Let us dwell on this important circumstance in more detail.

In modern physics, 4 types of interactions are known:

1. “Strong” interactions, a typical example of which is nuclear forces.
2. Electromagnetic interactions.
3. “Weak” interactions responsible for the β-decay process.
4. Gravitational interactions.

The most complete quantitative theory and the most complete experimental data exist for electromagnetic interactions, so this is where the best field is to try to detect deviations from the current theory. So far, all such attempts have yielded negative results. We will describe some of them, since in such an important issue even a non-attractive result seems very important; analysis of the accuracy of experiments provides an assessment of the possible extent of the validity of modern ideas. At the same time, this question has various connections with other branches of modern physics and is of independent interest.

Today, the most studied of the electromagnetic properties of particle elements is the magnetic moment. According to the assumption made in 1925 by Uhlenbeck and Gaudsmith, the electron is like a small wolf - it has a mechanical torque numerically equal to 1/2 (in units of $\hbar$), and, in addition, has a magnetic dipole moment equal to $e/2m$. This assumption has received numerous confirmations from spectroscopy and from the study of magnetic phenomena. Subsequently, the outstanding English physicist Dirac showed that the assumption of Uhlenbeck and Gaudsmith is compatible with the description of the electron as an exact charged particle, subordinating the theory of relativity and quantum theory to the equation.

However, in the 30s it turned out that the magnetic moment of a proton is 2.9 times greater than $e/2m_p$, where $m_p$ is the proton mass. Moreover, the Soviet theorists Tamm and Altshüler appeared, and the American scientist Alvarets discovered in the experiment the presence of a magnetic moment in a neutron, which is electrically neutral and, according to the above formula, should be devoid of a magnetic moment. Nowadays it is customary to call the value $\mu_0 = e/2m$ normal magnetic moment, and everything beyond this is anomalous. The anomalous moment of the proton and neutron is due, according to modern concepts, to their internal structure, but there is no theory of this effect, as, indeed, in general there is no theory of strongly interacting particles.
