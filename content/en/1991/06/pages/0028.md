F1273. A rope of length $l$ is fixed at one of its ends at the vertex of a sphere of radius $R$ (Fig. 1). At some point the rope is released. Find the acceleration of the rope immediately after this. Trenz is missing.

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

The only force that accelerates a rope is the force of gravity. This means that we need to find its component along the sphere.

Divide the rope into small sections of length $\Delta l$ (Fig. 2). Let the angle between the radius drawn to one of such sections from the center of the sphere and vertically be equal to $\varphi$. Then the component of the gravitational force along the sphere for this section is equal to

$$\Delta F = \Delta mg \sin \varphi = \frac{M}{l} \Delta lg \sin \varphi,$$

where $M$ is the length of the rope. But from figure it is clear that $\Delta l \sin \varphi = \Delta h,$

where $\Delta h$ is the difference in heights between the ends of this section, therefore,

$$\Delta F = \frac{M}{l} g \Delta h.$$

The required total accelerating force is

$$F = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Delta F_i = \frac{M}{l} g \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Delta h_i = \frac{M}{l} g H.$$

If the length of the rope is equal, for example, to four circumferences, i.e. $l = \pi R/2$, $H = R$, and the acceleration of the rope at the initial moment is equal to

$$a = \frac{F}{M} = \frac{2}{\pi} g.$$
