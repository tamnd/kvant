Let us recall the law of light reflection - the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection - we can form this property as follows: every ray emerging from one focus of the ellipse, after reflection, falls into the second focus. This property is called the optical property of the ellipse. It is also observed in nature: there are caves with ellipsoidal vaults, in which two places are found that are quite distant from each other, but the voice of a person standing in one of these places is heard in the other as if the speaker was standing nearby. In some palaces and castles, the architecture designed halls with such an effect.

Since the optical property of the ellipse will be the main one in our reasoning, we present its proof, especially since it is quite short and simple.

Let $l$ be a line tangent to the ellipse at point $M$, and let the segments $F_1M$ and $F_2M$ form unequal angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Fig. 3) with the line $l$. Let's construct the point $F'_1$, symmetrically to the point $F_1$ relative to the straight line $l$. The point $N$ of intersection of the straight line $l$ with the segment $F'_1F_2$ is located outside the ellipse (due to its convexity), and therefore the sum of its distances to the foci is greater than that of the point $M$ lying on the ellipse. But from symmetry it follows that $F_1M = F'_1M$ and $F_1N = F'_1N$. Therefore, $F_2M + MF_1 = F_2M + F'_1M$, which is less than $F_2N + NF_1 = F_2N + NF'_1$, i.e. $F_2F'_1 > F_2M + MF'_1$. But the segments $F_2F'_1$, $F_2M$ and $MF'_1$ are sides of the triangle $F_2MF'_1$, and in it the sum of two sides are large triangles, i.e. the inverse correspondence $F_2F'_1 < F_2M + MF'_1$ is satisfied. Thus, the assumption that the angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are different led us to a contradiction. Therefore, these angles are equal (point $N$ coincides with point $M$).

Let us also draw a straight line passing through the middle of the segment $F_1F_2$ perpendicular to it.
