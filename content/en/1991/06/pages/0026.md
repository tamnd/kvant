M1263. Inside the circle there are two more circles touching the outer circle at points A and B, respectively, and intersecting each other. Prove that if one of the intersection points lies on the segment AB, then the sum of the radii between the circles is equal to the radius of the larger one. Is the opposite true?

Let $O, O_1, O_2$ be the centers of these circles; $r, r_1, r_2$, respectively, are their radii (see figure), with $O_1$ lying on OA, and $O_2$ on OB. If the point C of the intersection of circles $O_1$ and $O_2$ lies on AB, then the isosceles triangles OAB, O_1AC and O_2BC are similar to each other (they have equal base angles). From here it obviously follows that $OO_1CO_2$ is a parallelogram, and therefore $r = OA = OO_1 + O_1A = O_2C + O_1A = r_2 + r_1$.

The converse statement is also true: if $r = r_1 + r_2$, then the segment AB passes through the intersection point of the circles $O_1$ and $O_2$. To prove it, we construct a parallelogram $OO_1C'O'_2$ whose vertices $C'$ and $O'_2$ lie on the segments AB and OB. Triangles $O_1AC'$ and $O'_2BC'$ are similar to isosceles triangle OAB, so

$$O_1C' = O_1A = r_1,$$

which means $C'$ lies on the circle $O_1$, and

$$O'_2B = O'_2C' = OO_1 = OA - O_1A = r_2,$$

whence it follows that $O'_2 = O_2$ and that $C'$ also lies on the circle $O_2$.

V. Dubrovsky

M1264*. On an infinite white sheet of checkered paper, the square $2 \times 2$ of the cell must be painted black. Is it possible for several operators to do this?

Answer: no. We will prove this by using an idea that has often been encountered in solving similar problems. Color the checkered plane in two colors (say, red and blue) so that any square $3 \times 3$ and $4 \times 4$ cells contain an even number of red cells, and some square $2 \times 2$ contains an odd number of them.
