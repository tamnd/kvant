frontal figure. Look externally at figure and you see that when rotated, the potential energy of the straight jumper does not change, and the change in potential energy is associated with the movement of its small section of length $\Delta l$ from the right end to the left:

$$\Delta E_p = \Delta mg \Delta l = \frac{m}{2R + \pi R} \Delta l g \Delta l = \frac{mgR}{2 + \pi} \varphi^2. \tag{2}$$

From expressions (1) and (2) we obtain

$$l_\pi = \frac{2R}{2 + \pi}.$$

(Try to calculate the position of the center of mass of the semi-jumper yourself; in this case $l_\pi = 2R/\pi$.)

Let's continue our calculations, and again use energy messages. Note that if the energy of the oscillatory system can be reduced to the form

$$E = E_p + E_k = \frac{Ax^2}{2} + \frac{Bx'^2}{2},$$

where $x$ is a parameter describing the deviation of the system from the equilibrium position, and $x'$ is the time derivative of $x$, then the period of oscillation of the system is

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{B}{A}}.$$

In our case $x = \varphi$. From expression (2) we immediately obtain

$$A = \frac{2mgR}{2 + \pi}.$$

To determine the value of $B$, let's calculate the kinetic energy of our figure. The contribution of half-ness is quite easy to find, since the speed of any of its points is equal to $v = \omega R = \varphi'R$:

$$E_{k1} = \frac{m_1 v^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{m \pi R}{2R + \pi R} (\varphi'R)^2 = \frac{\pi m R^2}{2 + \pi} \frac{\varphi'^2}{2}.$$

The jumper is more complicated - its different points have different speeds. Let us give the answer expressed in terms of the moment of inertia of the rod $I$ (we hope that most of our readers are familiar with this concept):

$$E_{k2} = I \frac{\varphi'^2}{2} = \frac{m_2 (2R)^2}{12} \frac{\varphi'^2}{2} = \frac{m2R}{2R + \pi R} \frac{R^2}{3} \frac{\varphi'^2}{2} = \frac{2mR^2}{3(2 + \pi)} \frac{\varphi'^2}{2}.$$
