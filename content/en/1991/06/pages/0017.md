The charge distribution can be clearly seen if you sprinkle the surface of the electret with a powder that is used when copying text or documents - xerox powder. Where the charge is greater, more powder is deposited, and the surface will be black. Therefore, the method of developing a charge was called the xerographic method.

Work to obtain as uniform a charge distribution as possible continued. Having noticed that rough electrodes give a more uniform distribution, we decided to use a very fine mesh as an electrode - its cells are visible only under the microsomes.

The charge distribution over the surface was studied under microsomes. It turned out that the areas of “uniform” distribution (in 摄影yah - completely black areas) are densely located black oval spots. At the Institute of Crystallogy of the USSR Academy of Sciences in the laboratory of surface phenomena under the leadership of Professor G.I. Distler, using a very subtle decoration method, they looked at the charge topography of the electret surface under an electron microsom. Small charge spots again disintegrated into clusters of precise defects of almost circular shape. Scientists put it this way: “The existence of clusters of precise defects is a regularity, not an exception...”

In other words, the uneven distribution of charge over surfaces is already established at the molecular level and is natural.

And then a seemingly strange question arose: what is called “uniform” distribution? And how can we estimate the “uneven” distribution?

We say: “The earth is round.” Is it round? There are mountains on Earth, huge dominions. We say: “A polished surface is smooth.” And under the microsome the surface is rough. Depending on the height of the roughness, 14 classes of surface cleanliness are distinguished. The terms “uniform”, “round”, “smooth” are relative.
