Fig. 1. Formation of electret.

Due to the inflow of charges from the outside and the slow drift of unbound charges present in the dielectric, as well as the chaotic tell motion, a slow depolarization of the electret occurs. The lifetime of an electret largely depends on the conductivity of the dielectric. The lower the conductivity, the longer the relaxation time, the more stable the electret. For this reason, ferroelectric dipoles are quickly screened by free charges. Ferroelectric electrets have a short lifetime.

Theoretically, all dielectrics have electret properties, but the lifetime of most of them is very short - seconds or, at best, hours. And only some dielectrics (ceramics made from calcium titanium, films of fluoroplastic, polycarbonate, lavsan, as well as polymethyl methacrylate plexiglass) retain an electreton state for a long time - months, years and even ten years. *)
