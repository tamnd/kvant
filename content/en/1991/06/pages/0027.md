M1265*. a) Prove that among 21 pairwise distances between 7 different points of the plane, the same number is the same among 15 pairwise distances between 6 different points of the plane?

Fig. 1. Fig. 2.

Answer: 6) 9. An example is given in figure 2.

Let us prove statement a). Let the distribution $a$ for points $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_7$ of the plane be repeated at most $k$ times. Let us denote by $n_i$ the number of lengths $a$ emerging from the point $A_i$. Without loss of generality, one can assume that $n_1 \geq n_2 \geq \dots \geq n_7$.

Note that $n_1 + n_2 \leq 9$. Indeed, otherwise, on a circle with centers $A_1$ and $A_2$ and radius $a$ of 7 points, one will find at least $n_1 + (n_2 - 2) \geq 8$ points, which is impossible.

It is clear that from the point $A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4$ the distance between any two points is not equal to $a$. Let $A_i A_j \neq a$ ($i \neq j$). In this case, on a circle with centers $A_i, A_j$ and radius $a$ will find at most $n_i + n_j - 2$ points, since points $A_i$ and $A_j$ are not on the indicated circle, which means $n_i + n_j - 2 + 2 \leq 7$. Therefore $n_3 + n_4 \leq n_i + n_j \leq 7$, hence we have $n_4 \leq 3$, thereby $n_7 \leq n_6 \leq n_5 \leq 3$.

Because
$$k = \frac{1}{2} \left( (n_1 + n_2) + (n_3 + n_4) + n_5 + n_6 + n_7 \right) \leq \frac{1}{2} (9 + 7 + 3 + 3 + 3),$$

whence $k \leq 12$.

The example is very iden (Fig. 1) in a regular hexagon from the side $a$, rasstorie $a$ for the vertices and center is repeated 12 times. So, $k = 12$.
