Fig. 5. Layout of charge spots on the surface of the electret.

The electret series is in principle discrete. Charge inhomogeneities begin at the atomic level. The electric fields of discrete charges are nonuniform, and if the charges are arranged regularly, as in a crystal lattice, then the fields are periodic. If you move away from the surface, the periodicity smoothes out. The fields are homogenized, and quite quickly the field becomes homogeneous. Like in a television image: if we look at it closely, we see a system of dots of different brightness, and the product can see the smoothly changing color of the image.

The size of a thin near-surface layer (TS-layers), within which the discreteness of the charge and the inhomogeneity of the field is located, is a quantitative criterion understood as “uniform” and “inhomogeneous” distribution.

Thus, for charge elements arranged regularly (crystal lattice plane), the TP of layers is only 3A (1A = 10⁻¹⁰ m). If we examine the electret from a distance greater than 3A, then we can assume that the charges are distributed evenly and the field is uniform.

The model did not contradict the previously accepted flat capacitor approximation. Indeed, we can assume that the field outside the thin surface layer is zero, as for a flat capacitor.

The proposed model includes the parallel-plate capacitor approximation as a special case.

The electret was “willingly” polarized in the form of a system of round charge spots. Calculations have shown that, for round charge spots of TP layers, the value is on the order of three spot radii.

Near-surface fields can be very strong and can be used for practical purposes. By adjusting the size and pitch of the spots, you can form the required field values ​​(Fig. 5). The electret shown in figure is called an artificial discrete charge structure (IDS) electret.

From the model of an electret with a discrete near-surface charge, as if from a horn of an image, fundamentally new possibilities for the use of near-surface fields of electrets began to pour out.
