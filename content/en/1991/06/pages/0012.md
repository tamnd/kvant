DIELECTRIC
ANALOGUE MAGNETS

Doctor of Technical Sciences
G. EFASHKIN

Magnets in the form of pieces of iron ore are often found in nature and have been known since ancient times. When the connection between magnetic and electrical phenomena was discovered, the question arose: is it possible to obtain an electrical analogue of a magnet - a dielectric, which would create an electric field? This assumption was made by the English physicist O. Hevisyad in 1892. He also proposed a name for the analogue of a magnet - “electret”.

Electrets are not found in nature in their natural state. Is it possible to obtain electrium artificially, how are artificial magnets obtained? Magnetization of a magnetic material (magnet) is carried out by placing it in an external magnetic field. You can try to obtain electriet in a similar way, by placing the dielectric in an external electric field.

The matter turned out to be more complicated. Only in 1922, thirty years after Hevisyad's theoretical assumption, Mototoro Eguchi in Japan managed to obtain the first electrets. Eguchi placed a mixture of palm resin and rosin (Carnauba wax) between two metal electrodes, melted the mixture, turned on the direction and, without removing the direction, cooled it. The result was a dielectric plate that created an external electric field - an electret.

Soon after this, the first electrite microphone was created. The design of the microphone was extremely simple. The electret was placed between two electric ones, one of which was pressed tightly, and the second, which played the role of a membrane, was placed with a small gap. The microphone worked without a power source. This is an arbitrary impression of a miracle. In fact, when the membrane vibrated, the electric field between the electret changed, and a current was generated in the external circuit. The electret directly converts sound energy into electrical current.

Why are such simple microphones not widely used? As the designers say, carnauba wax was a low-tech material - it melted at a low temperature and was fragile.

But the idea was good. Therefore, starting with the search for the technology of this electretonic material. In the 50s, at the Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences, G. I. Skanavi and A. N. Gubkin obtained durable ceramics with good electrical properties - calcium titanate - and designed a microphone on a ceramic electret.

In the 60s, electrical efficiency was discovered in thin polymer films of lavsan, fluoroplastic, and polycarbonate. The practical application of electrets began to develop rapidly.

In parallel with the search for new electrical materials, work has been carried out on the theory of the electrical state of dielectrics. The Soviet scientist Professor A.N. Gubkin created the phenomenological theory of electrites, which is now recognized throughout the world.
