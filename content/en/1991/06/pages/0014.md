running across the field. Since there are a lot of molecular dipoles, when they are oriented, the “flat” and “minus” of neighboring dipoles inside the dielectric compensate each other. Only the surface charges will remain uncompensated. The dielectric becomes polarized. Charges of one sign appear on one surface, and charges of the opposite sign appear on the other. Free charges in the external field move (drift) and are also separated.

The orientation of dipfields and the drift of free charges occur in time. If the process occurs quickly, in a very short period of time (millionths of a second), the polarization is called instantaneous. Inter-polarization (polarization time is calculated in minutes, hours, days and even years) is called relaxation. To characterize the polarization process, the concept of relaxation times is introduced.

Relaxation polarization can be thought of as if the dielectric dipoles are slowly rotating in a viscous liquid. The relaxation time depends very much on temperature, and if the dielectric is heated, the strength of the “liquid” decreases, and the dipoles turn significantly faster.

The sign of the charge surface when the difields are oriented is opposite to the sign of the polarizing electrode. The pluses of the dipoles are attracted to the minus of the electrode. Therefore, surface charges are also called heterocharges (from the Greek word heteros - other).

Charges whose sign is the same as the sign of the electrode can “flow” (inject) from the electrode onto the surface of the dielectric. Such charges are called heterocharges (from the word homos - identical).

With relaxation polarization, if the external field is turned off, the warm motion does not immediately scatter the molecules. Depolarization of the dielectric is also slow. The dielectric remains filled for some time. Residual polarization occurs.

Let's place the electret material between two electrodes pressed to the material and apply a voltage $U_0$ to them; then a field $E_0 = U_0 / d$ will arise between the electrodes. In the field, relaxation polarization of dipoles occurs and a surface heterocharge is formed, the density of which is $\sigma_{\text{retero}}$ (Fig. 1). On figure difields are depicted in the form of elongated rectangles. Homocharges are injected from the electrodes onto the surface of the electret material. They are depicted in circles on figure. The density of homocharges is $\sigma_{\text{гомо}}$. The densities of heterocharges are not the same. The total charge density is $\sigma_{\text{сум}} = \sigma_{\text{retero}} - \sigma_{\text{гомо}}$.

Turn off the voltage, remove the external field, remove the electrodes. We got the electret. When the electret material was polarized, the charges separated, positive charges appeared on one surface, and negative charges appeared on the other. By analogy with magnets, the “north” and “south” poles were formed.

Let's consider a free (without electrodes) electret. The electric field of the electret is created by the difference between homo and heterocharges. Some heterocharges are overlapped by homocharges. Let's imagine that there is only excess charge (Fig. 2) on the surface. The picture is familiar. After all, this is a flat-plate capacitor. The difference is that in a flat capacitor the charge is on the metal plates and it is free, that is, it can easily drain from the metal. And the electret charge is related: However, the fields created by a flat capacitor and an electret obey the same laws.

In this case, they say that the electret is considered in the approximation of a flat capacitor. It is known that the strip capacitor is concentrated inside the capacitor. There is an external margin only at the edges. Therefore, in this approximation, the electret has only an internal field. If you calculate the internal field of the electret, it turns out to be very strong. When realizing the charge density $\sigma = 10^{-4} \text{ KJ/m}^2 E = 10^7 \text{ B/m}$.
