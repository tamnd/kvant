Theo will familiarize ourselves with the temperature established in the state through $T_3$ and write the heat balance equation:

$$\rho_1 \frac{V}{2} (T_3 - T_1) = \rho_2 \frac{V}{2} (T_2 - T_3),$$

where

$$T_3 = \frac{\rho_1 T_1 + \rho_2 T_2}{\rho_1 + \rho_2} = 318 \text{ K}, t_3 = 45^\circ \text{C}.$$

Now it is easy to find the steady-state pressure:

$$p_3 = mRT / (MV) = (\rho_1 + \rho_2)RT / M = p_2 T_3 / (2T_2) = 6150 \text{ Pa}.$$

You can, just in case, make sure that the resulting pressure is less than the saturated vapor pressure for a temperature of +45°C.

If the steam temperature is up to +50°C, then the pressure will increase slightly and amount to

$$p_4 = p_3 T_2 / T_3 = 6250 \text{ Pa}.$$

If the steam is cooled to +20°C, then it becomes and its pressure is (see table)

$$p_5 = 2380 \text{ Pa}.$$

F1275. A point particle having mass $m$ and charge $Q$ is placed at a distance $L$ from an infinite conducting plane and released. How long does it take the particle to reach the plane? There is also no strength. (Hint: You can use the mirror image method.)
