F1285. A physicist of mass 80 kg stands on a square wooden raft of size $2 \times 2 \times 0.3$ m, made of wood with a density of 800 kg/$\text{m}^3$. How far from the center of the raft must it move so that the edge of the raft dips into the water?

F1286. Two beads of equal mass $M$, carrying charges of opposite signs $Q$ and - $Q$ (Fig. 2), are mounted on a non-conducting rod bent at a right angle. At the initial moment, the beads are motionless and are located at distances $d$ and $2d$ from the vertex of the angle. Let's let them go. Where is the “far” bead at the moment when the “near” one reaches the top of the corner? Find the speeds of the beads at the moment when the distance between them is $d$.

F1287. Not far from the included transformer, a closed coil of copper wire was placed. The current in it turned out to be out of phase by π/4 relative to the current in the transformer winding. How much will the power dissipated in the coil change if it is made not of copper, but of nichrome, keeping all dimensions unchanged? The resistivity of nichrome is 65 times higher than that of copper. The current amplitude in the transformer winding is assumed to be the same in general cases.

A. Zilberman

Problem solutions
M1251 — M1255, F1263 — F1267

Answer: the required point $M$ lies on the bissertrix of the angle, and $AM=AP=AQ$ (A is the vertex of the angle; Fig. 1) - these conditions allow us to construct $M$ in a very special way.

We will start with arbitrary points $M$, $P$ and $Q$, for which the sum $PM+MQ$ is given, and in several steps we will move them so that we get the location indicated in the answer, and the area $APMQ$ will increase at each step (more precisely, not decrease).

1°. Let's replace the quadrilateral $APMQ$ and make it convex (if it is initially small).

To do this, replace $M$ with the point $M'$, symmetrically $M$ relative to the middle of the segment $PQ$. It is clear that $M'$ lies inside the angle (and even inside the parallelogram $APA'Q$; Fig. 2), $PM+MQ=PM'+M'Q$, and the area of ​​the quadrilateral increases.

2°. Fix $\Delta PQM$ and move it inside the corner so that $AP=AQ$.

It is more convenient to divide not the triangle, but the vertex of the angle $A$, preserving its values, i.e. along the arc of the circle $PAQ$ (Fig. 3). Area $\Delta APQ$ becomes most important.
