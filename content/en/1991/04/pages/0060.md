Astronomical Research Base on Mars, 1992 (K. Erike).

move forward 35 years, i.e. until 2001. The general prognosis of the development of science by the famous scientist and science fiction writer A. Clark also dates back to this time. And although today’s article is devoted to the exploration of outer space, we believe that for the sake of the picture it would be more correct to acquaint the reader first with A. Clark’s general pronunciation (see table on p. 57).

And here is how the symposum participants imagined the milestone of two thousand.

K. Eriche: “Today, at the end of 2000, international flights along the routes from Mercury to Saturn are carried out by portable rafted aircraft... The heaviest and most advanced automatic probes have reached the planet Pluto and are paving the way to the general and unexplored areas located beyond this planet and in international space...

our helionauts... beat in various areas of the solar system, from the sun-scorched coasts of the planet Mercury to the icy cliffs of Titan, a satellite of Saturn...

...Three years have passed since the extraction and processing of metal ore on Mercury was organized. On Mars, work has just begun to implement a long-term program of introducing culture specially created for Martian conditions into the applied areas of the northern and southern hemispheres of the planet...

...Not only significant technical achievements in practically all areas of space technology, but mainly progress in the development of pulsed nuclear and thermonuclear engines have finally provided the enormous energy capabilities necessary for international flights.

...thanks to them (power plants - V.N.) long-term flights within the solar system became economically possible..."
