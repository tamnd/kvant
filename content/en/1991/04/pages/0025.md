The densities of satellites should be treated simply as the concentrations of excited and unexcited molecules. A complex formula was used, substituting the experimental data into which I obtained a value of $h$ one and a half times less than generally accepted. The result disheartened me. After some time, the work of the French physicist Dora appeared, who performed the same experiments, but used the correct formula for the intensity of the satellites and obtained the value $h$, which is in good agreement with the generally accepted one. After processing according to the correct formula, my data also gave an excellent agreement, but I felt that there was no point in publishing them, despite the proposal of my teachers. The matter was limited to a reference in one of the articles by Landsberg and Mandelstam, published in 1930.

It is curious that the incorrect formula that I used is associated with the proposal about the significant role in Raman scattering of acts of stimulated emission of photons, harmful in physics by Einstein *). Ten years later, in 1940, I pointed out the possibility of experimentally detecting stimulated emission of photons. In 1951, together with Butaeva and Vudinsky, I described in an author’s application a method of amplifying light based on the use of these acts. We have received the appropriate copyright certificate. As is known, this method of light amplification is used in lasers. Unfortunately, I did not explicitly indicate a very important property - the concentration of emitted and incident radiation. It is this property of stimulated emission acts that allows the creation of optical laser generators.

*) With stimulated emission, the incident photo does not change its energy, but only gives a “signal” for the molecule (atom) to emit an additional photon.

On March 3, 1991, at the age of 84, the remarkable scientist and teacher, laureate of the USSR State Prize, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, professor, full member of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR Valentin Aleksadrovich Fabrikant died.

During his long creative life, Valentin Aleksadrovich managed to do a lot - make wonderful discoveries (he is the founder of work on parametric amplification of light, which laid the foundation for the creation of lasers), educate a galaxy of students, write wonderful books.

V. A. Fabrikant was also at the origins of the creation of our magazine. Being a permanent member of the editorial collection, he was a great friend of readers and editors. When Valentin Aleksadrovich came to the editorial office, his delicacy, charm and intelligence attracted those around him, created around him that atmosphere of the aristocracy of spirit that we associate today with university life at the beginning of the century.

Until his last days, Valentin Aleksadrovich did not excel in his scientific, pedagogical and educational activities. And now the sad news of his death found the editors preparing for publication of his article - the one that lies in front of you. In this article, V. A. Fabrikant turns to the data of his youth. His work is a striking example of a scientist’s critical attitude to the results of his work, an attitude that is inherent in talent and is the driving force of progress.
