ABOUT CIPHERS

E. IGNATIEV

The need for a method of writing that would hide the meaning of what is written from the constructed eye and would make it accessible only to the slightly connected has existed among people since ancient times. This is where the art of secret writing arose, which today has grown almost to the size of an entire science - kritgorafini. Herodotus also mentions secret writing and even gives examples of such letters that are understandable only to the addressee. According to Plutarch, the Spartans used special mechanical devices for writing and reading secret messages. To record religious secrets, the priests used special writing that was incomprehensible to the unbound.

Julius Caesar had his own system of secret writing, with the help of which he wrote down his secrets; it was based on replacing one letter with another - a technique still used in our time.

In the Middle Ages, many outstanding minds worked on the invention and development of critographic systems - such as the philosopher Bacon of Verlyamsky, the mathematician Viet, the historian Hugo Grotius and others.

But critography reached its highest development only in modern times, with the development of diplomatic relations and complex trade turnover, requiring the strictest secrecy. In our day, hundreds and thousands of so-called encrypted, i.e. secretly written, telegrams circulate around the world.
