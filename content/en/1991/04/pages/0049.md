How can one obtain such a picture in a home laboratory? It turns out there are two ways to do this. In the first, which we have already discussed in essence, the prism is covered with a triangular plywood cover and pressed against the edge of the desk with a clamp so that the hypotenuse face of the prism protrudes a few millimeters beyond the edge of the table. The light source can be a film projection lamp (for example, a K-12-30 lamp from a children's filter), and the observation screen can be a pillowcase thrown over the back of a chair. The picture is observed in reflected rays according to the scheme shown in figure 5.

In the second method, an unsecured device is simply placed on a desk, and the device layer is examined directly with the eye in diffused daylight in reflected or transmitted rays (according to the diagrams in Figures 6.6 and 6.a). This method is simpler and more accessible, and it also makes it possible to simultaneously observe and compare the picture in transmitted and reflected light. We will discuss this in more detail.

A piece of white paper or Vatan paper BB is placed on the table, two faceted cardans are placed on it, placing them parallel to each other at a distance of 4-5 cm, these “rails” are covered with photoplastic Pl 9x12 cm, washed from the emulsion, a prism P is placed on the plate, after carefully wiping the contacting surface, and the facets of the prism facing the window are covered a CN overlay made of black paper, cut to the size of this edge, with a side-bend 2-3 cm wide.

To observe the picture in reflected rays, you need to replace the white background with black and vice versa: place a piece of black paper on the table and directly on it - a glass plastic with a prism placed on it. A translucent PN overlay, for example made from tissue paper, can be applied to the grana facing the window.

Of particular interest is the simultaneous observation and comparison of both patterns - both in transmitted and reflected rays. For this purpose, the arrangement of the device is used according to figure 6, a, but under the glass plastic on one side (on top of one of the cardans) a sheet of black paper is inserted approximately half the width of the plate, and the prism is positioned so that half of its hypotenuse face is above the black lining.
