K. Erike considered the main national space goals for the period from 1970 to 1985 (in 1966):
1. Exploration of the Solar System by automatic Hondas and creation of a scientific and technical base for future manned flights to Venus and Mars.
2. Organization of a permanent scientific laboratory on the Moon.
3. Creation of functional (application-purpose) space stations on near-Earth sites, incl. Stationary orbits.
V. Purdy: “...let me list the main events in the field of space exploration...
1. Habitated space stations in Earth orbit, 1975–1980.
2. Manned flight with the migo Venus probody, 1980–1985.
3. Inhabited observatory on the Moon, 1985-1990.
4. Manned flight with the Mars migo body, 1985–1990.
5. Observatories in various parts of the Solar System (automatic), 1980-1990.
6. Human flight to Mars, 1990–2000.
...Reusable cargo transport facilities will be developed, but will not be used until the year 2000 or later, since the volume of traffic will not yet be sufficient to justify the examination of such vehicles.”
H. Purdy: “It is unlikely that tourist trips to the Moon will become popular in 2001 due to the lack of comfort, risk and the too high cost of such trips...
...The journey begins its lunar expedition in a building that will be used for modern airfields for supersonic transport. The passenger cross in an aerospace aircraft will be located inside a pressurized capsule designed to rescue the passenger in the event of an accident... The takeoff will be horizontal, like modern aircraft.”
