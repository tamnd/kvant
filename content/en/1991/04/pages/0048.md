Let us reflect the beam $2_n$ in the direction of the beam $2_o$, using a glass plate as a reflector. This could be photographic plastic washed from the emulsion, or a piece of window glass, or black glass, or a plate of optical glass, or a second prism, or something else. Let's bring the reflector to the sign. Then, in the semi-dark area $B'O'$ of the screen, the beam $2_n$ will overlap the beam $2_o$, and the illumination of the screen will noticeably increase. This can be verified directly by moving the reflector up or down along the reflector, or by using a reflector of a smaller size than the reflector, or by tilting the reflector, forming a gap in the form of an air wedge (Fig. 2).

After making sure that there are overlapping beams, we again press the reflector to the target. However, no interface picture will emerge yet. Maybe the air gap is too thick? Let's try to reduce it. To this end, we clean the mating edge from dirt and dust, for example, by breathing on the edge and then thoroughly wiping the foggy surfaces with a dry, clean cloth. Let's apply the reflector to the mark again and with light circular movements and gentle pressure we will rub it against the edge $AD$.
