When the friction of the body against the surface bogies occurs μ. How far will the body move relative to the cart?

In this problem, we will apply the law of change in the mechanical energy of the system together with the law of conservation of momentum.

The speed of the body $v_1$ at the base of the inclined plane will be found from the law of conservation of mechanical energy:

$$\frac{m v_1^2}{2} = m g h \Rightarrow v_1 = \sqrt{2 g h},$$

and the final speed of the joint motion of the body and the cart $v_2$ will be calculated using the law of conservation of momentum:

$$m v_1 = (m + M) v_2 \Rightarrow v_2 = v_1 \frac{m}{m + M}.$$

The change in the energy of the “body-cart” system is the work of the friction force acting between them:

$$\frac{(m + M) v_2^2}{2} - \frac{m v_1^2}{2} = - F_{\text{тр}} L,$$

where $L$ is the distance traveled by the body along the cart. Considering that $F_{\text{тр}} = \mu N = \mu mg$, we get

$$L = h \frac{M}{\mu (m + M)}.$$

The question arises: why do we calculate the work of the friction force as if the cart were ending and the body was moving along it at a distance of $L$? Doesn't the job depend on the reporting system? The answer is this. Of course, the work done by any force applied to a given body depends on the reporting system. For example, if some force $\vec{F}$ accelerated a body of mass $m$ from a state of rest to a speed $\vec{v}$, then the work of this force is positive and equal to the change in kinetic energy and therefore should not depend on the reporting system?

From the point of view of energy balance, human work should not depend on the reporting system. However, human work on the stone clearly differs between different reporting systems. How so?
