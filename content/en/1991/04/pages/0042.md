...the quantum of action was to play a fundamental role in physics... its appearance brought about something completely new, an unprecedented contract, which seemed to require a transformation of the most basic of our physical thinking...
Max Planck

The energy of a beam of light emerging from a certain point is not distributed continuously in an ever-increasing volume, but adds up from a finite number of weekly energy quanta localized in space, posited or arising only as a whole.
Albert Einstein

Is it so familiar to you?

QUANTUM?

It’s unusual to inquire about this from the page of a single-issue magazine. However, this physical character, the leader of the 20th century, caused such a revolution in minds, forced to change the already seemingly unshakable ideas that it is still difficult for many, who are becoming aware of the quantum nature of light for the first time, to reconcile with new outlandish concepts. Outstanding scientists had to go through this difficult “elevation of consciousness” at the beginning of the century, and the hypothesis of quantum photons gathered a brilliant constellation of names under its banner. Explaining various effects, turning experimental data with confirmations and new ideas, the corpuscular hypothesis turned into a quantum theory of light. Thus physics reincorporated the concept proposed by Newton 200 years earlier. This theory achieved its final victory in the late twenties after the creation of quantum mechanics and its unification with the wave theory of light.
We invite you to think about the necessity of both quantum and wave properties of light, and not only it.

Questions and tasks

1. What is the energy of light incident on a substance converted into during photoefficiency?

2. Two electric neutral plates are illuminated, one metal, the other semiconductor. Will the plates remain neutral when photoelectric effects occur?

3. Where—on photographic paper or film—can chemical processes produce generated energies?

4. Is it possible to take photographs in perfect darkness?

5. Are the light pressures produced on a perfectly white and perfectly black surface different?

6. Can light hold loads?

7. If a comet is visible in the sky in the evening, in which direction is its tail pointed?

8. When a positron annihilates with an electron, two γ quanta are formed. In what case will the energies of these quanta be equal and they will move in opposite directions?

9. Can a photon with decent energy turn into an electron-positron pair?

10. Is a free electron capable of absorbing a light quantum?

11. By studying the monochromatic light flux, you can find out the mass of its constituent photons. What characteristic of light needs to be determined experimentally for this?

12. Photon and electron have the same kinetic energy. Which one has the longer wavelength?
