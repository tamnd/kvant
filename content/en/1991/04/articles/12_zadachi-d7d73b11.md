Tasks

1. Four cats - Vaska, Fluff, Basilio and Leopold - were hunting mice. Fluff and Leopold produced as many mice together as Basilio and Vaska. Vaska produced more mice than Basilio, but Vaska and Leopold produced fewer mice than Fluff and Basilio. How many mice did each cat produce if Fluff produced 3 mice?

2. Solve the arithmetic puzzle shown at figure. Same letters correspond to the same numbers, different letters correspond to different numbers.

3. In an equal trapezoid, we drew diagonals and heights from the vertices of the upper base (see figure). Prove that the sum of the areas of the black triangles is equal to the area of ​​the red pentagon.

4. Three bus routes pass by the house. Their numbers are three-digit numbers, and they are all squares. Moreover, they are written with the same numbers. What are the bus numbers?

5. There are 15 pieces placed on a chessboard so that there is at least one piece in each horizontal and each vertical row. Prove that it is possible to remove one piece from the board so that the remaining pieces again satisfy the same requirement: in each horizontal row and in each vertical row there is one piece.

These tasks were proposed to us by S. Lyashenko, A. Alekseev, I. Nadel, A. Jafarov and V. Proizvolov.
