Length

Length is one of the first geometric concepts that humans encounter. The first measures of length were washed away by natural ones and therefore were preserved to this day. Indeed, in the newspapers you can read the following phrases: “The hut is located at a distance of two days’ march from the village,” “A crack about a palm’s width crossed a stone slab.”
But how convenient were the original length measures - cubit, vershok (shi-different). Thus, three Russian cubits made up two Persian ones, and Persian ones received the name arshin in Rus' (from

rina of the palm at the level of the fingers), sahen (the distance between the ends of the fingers of the hands spread to the sides) - after all, they are always with you, they were so inaccurate; For different people, these units are different. The state is coming to introduce standards of length - standard units of measurement. But in different countries these units turn out to be still medieval measures.

The concept of the length of a segment played a huge role in the development of mathematics. After all, what is the length of a segment? This is a number that indicates how many times the selected unit of length fits on this segment. If this stage does not fit an integer number of times, then it comes to enter a fractional length in exact accordance with the Euclidean algorithm.

Even the ancient Greeks knew that the diagonal of a square is incommensurable on its side, that is, it cannot be expressed through its side in the form of an ordinary fraction. As a result, irrational numbers appeared, so algebra and geometry were connected through the concept of length.

They were even more “connected” by the French mathematician Renedecart, who created analytical geometry based on a rectangular coordinate system. As a result, any geometric problem can be formulated as an algebraic one. Let us recall the famous Pythagorean theorem - the square of the length of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of its legs. This remarkable fact has a lot of generalizations in geometry, algebra, and number theory (Pythagorean triples, Fermat’s Last Theorem).

The desire to change the lengths of each curve led to a number of discoveries. They learned to change the length of a circle back in ancient times (by applying it with broken lines), although the question of the nature of the number “pi” has been studied by mathematicians for hundreds of years and was resolved only in the last century. The “logarithmic spiral”, given by the equation $\rho = e^{\varphi}$, when changing the angle φ on the interval (-∞; 0), also makes an infinite number of turns, and their total length turns out to be equal to 3.
