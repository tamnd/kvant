Thunderstorm and thunderstorm

Thunderstorm is a very commonly observed atmospheric phenomenon. Every second, about a hundred thunderstorms occur on the globe. There are even places (Central America, Indonesia) where thunderstorms occur so regularly that people agree on a witness with the following words: “meet on Wednesday after the thunderstorm.”

A thunderstorm and its inseparable lightning and thunderclaps are not only an effective, but also a terrifying phenomenon. And unsafe. It is not for nothing that this word has the same root as the words “threaten”, “threat”, “formidable”, etc. A thunderstorm is indeed associated with considerable dangers - for people and for forests, for electrical devices and communications. Hence the need for lightning protection.

But what is a thunderstorm?

A thunderstorm is a huge spark discharge in the atmospheric air that occurs between electrically charged clouds or between a charged cloud and the ground. The length of the channel along which the discharge makes its way can reach many kilometers. The current strength in it is hundreds of thousands of amperes. The temperature of the gas in a thunderstorm channel is many thousands of degrees. The compliance is very high and the gas pressure in it. For such a discharge to occur, an electrical voltage (for example, between the cloud and the ground) is required - up to a billion volts, and an electric field voltage - up to a million volts per meter.

If on the path of a lightning discharge there is an obstacle - a tree, a building, etc., then high pressure can lead to mechanical destruction, and high temperature - to a fire. It is possible that the first fire with which man became acquainted was the fire caused by a thunderstorm that burned a tree. It is no coincidence that in the world Prometheus is spoken of as burning fire from the sky.

How to deal with this danger? How to fix it?

The idea of ​​protection from thunderstorms was first proposed by the American physicist Benjamin Franklin back in the mid-18th century. He also established the electrical nature of lightning. The idea of ​​protection, which is still used today, is based on the properties of conductivity.

Conductor with a sharp edge. It is known that electric charges on a conductor are located on its surface. If the conductor has the shape of a sphere or ball, the charge is distributed evenly over its surface - the same charge comes to each unit of conductivity area.

As stated, the surface charge density is the same. This is due to the fact that the sphere has the same curvature at all points of the surface, i.e., the values ​​characterizing the deviation of a curved surface from a flat one are the same.

On a surface of arbitrary shape, the curvature in different places can be different. It is clear that in this case the electric charge is distributed unevenly over the surface - after all, the charges are always located so that the direction of the electric field inside the conductor is zero. To do this, in those places where there is “less space,” the charges must be located with a larger surface area. That is why the direction of the field around the conductor is especially high near those areas of its surface where the surface curvature is large.

The point on the surface is where the curvature is greatest. The correspondence here is the highest surface charge density, and near the tip there is a particularly strong electrostatic field. This is used in a lightning protection device - a lightning rod.

Lightning rod. It is a metal rod, one end of which is brought into good contact with the ground (reliably grounded), and the other, equipped with a pointed pin, rises above the structure being protected. The rod together with the ground is a giant conductor, and the lightning rod pin is the tip on it.

How does this simple device work?

Let's assume that there is a thundercloud near the lightning rod. Let, for example, the charge on it be positive. Then the electrostatic field of the cloud will cause a separation of charges on the conductor consisting of the ground and the lightning rod, so that a negative charge appears on the lightning rod. Moreover, since there will be a very large surface charge density on the pin, a very strong electrostatic field will arise around it.

In atmospheric exposure there are small numbers (on the order of several

hundreds in one cubic centimeter) positive and negative charged particles - ions and free electrons. Normally they do not interfere with the effects of being an excellent insulator. But in a strong field - near the lightning rod pin - onns and electrons acquire such high speeds that, when they come into contact with neutral molecules, they inactivate them. The ions also accelerate and in turn create more and more pairs of charged particles, their number dissolving like a snowball. The air becomes somewhat conductive.

Currently, the electric field is known to be absent. In the air around the lightning rod, in any case, it does, and a spark discharge becomes less likely. It can also be said that negative particles move towards the positive charge of the cloud and, at least partially, neutralize it. If the discharge always occurs, the lightning rod will serve as a safe “road” for the discharge for surrounding objects.

Lightning and thunder. So, a lightning discharge is a giant electrical discharge in a gas. But electric current, as is known, is neither visible nor heard. How can such impressive thunderstorm phenomena as flashes of lightning and rumbles of thunder be produced?

Let's start in order. It turns out that the electrins formed in the discharge channel (its width reaches several centimeters) are attracted to each other, reunite and form neutral air molecules. This process is called recombination. It is during this recombination that light is released (a flash of lightning occurs).

This is why thunder occurs. During a discharge, the gas pressure in the lightning channel becomes very high. Its rapid occurrence is accompanied by the formation of a powerful shock wave, which we call thunder. Thunderclaps are caused by repeated reflections of sound from clouds and other objects.

Thus, lightning and thunder are like the consequences of a lightning discharge. The descriptions we talked about are associated with the discharge itself, which is neither visible nor audible. Let us note, by the way, that the entire discharge process, which we spent so many words and time describing, occurs almost instantly - in thousandths of a second.

Lightning rod? Lightning rod? Lightning rod? We called the lightning protection device a lightning rod. But for a very long time he was called a lightning rod. Then they began to call it a lightning rod, and it is often called that now. However, from what we have said, it follows that neither thunder nor lightning can be “diverted” - they are only inevitable consequences of a lightning discharge. The task of lightning protection is to divert a thunderstorm, which is why the word “lightning rod,” from our point of view, most accurately reflects its purpose.

In 1932, the composition of atomic nuclei was established: the nucleus of an atom consists of positively charged particles - protons and electrically neutral particles - neutrons, the mass of which is almost equal to the mass of protons. Consequently, the atomic number of an element (formerly the atomic number) is the number of protons in its nucleus and, accordingly, the number of electrons in the electron shell of its atom.

By this time, 92 cells in the periodic table were already filled - from hydrogen to uranium, although 4 cells were still empty. Four elements could not be detected either in the earth's crust, or in water, or in the atmosphere. True, the generosity of nature was reflected in the fact that many cells of the table turned out to be “escaped” not for one atom, but for many - for isotopes of the same chemical element, so that there were many more different atoms than 88 and even 92. But couldn’t chemical elements exist outside the periodic table - to the left of the beginning or to the right of its end?

To the left of the beginning of the table. Back in 1920, E. Rutherford suggested that there might be an element with number zero. And when the neutron was discovered in 1932, it became clear that it was the one suitable for the role of the “inhabitant” of the zero cell of the table. This is indeed a chemical element with zero atomic number, although even in the most modern tables it is not customary to draw a zero cell with a neutron in it.

It seemed that merging the table “to the left” was over - there seemed to be nowhere to go to the left of zero.

To the right of the end of the table. After the discovery of the neutron and the creation of the first neutron sources, they began to be used for irradiation of various

It turned out, for example, that very often reactions like this occur:

$$\text{Na} + \frac{1}{2} \text{n} \rightarrow \text{Na} + \gamma.$$

This is the so-called radiation capture of a neutron - a neutron is “captured” by a sodium nucleus with the emission (radius) of a gamma quantum. But the isotope $$\text{Na}$$ is radioactive and with a period of $T \approx 14$ hours decays with the emission of a $\beta$-particle (electron $-_{0}e$):

$$\text{Na} \rightarrow \text{Na} + \beta.$$ (*)
