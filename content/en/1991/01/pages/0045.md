Element number 102 is the newest (No). The above-mentioned precursor elements were synthesized through the efforts of mainly American scientists led by G. Seabort. For the first time, Soviet physicists from the group of Academician G. N. Flerov also participated in the creation of the 102nd element. They showed that the newest one can be obtained by irradiating $^{238}_{92}U$ with accelerated neon units:

$$
{}^{238}_{92}U + {}^{22}_{10}Ne \rightarrow {}^{256}_{102}No + 4^{1}_{0}n.
$$

All isotopes of this element have short half-lives - from one second to three minutes.

Element number 103 is Lurenian (Lr). To obtain it, the reaction of irradiation of Californian with fast boron compounds was used:

$$
{}^{252}_{98}Cf + {}^{10}_{5}B \rightarrow {}^{257}_{103}Lr + 5^{1}_{0}n.
$$

The half-lives of all known isotopes of lurencium are measured in seconds.

Element number 104 is kurite (Ku). In the synthesis of this element, fast neon compounds were again used, which irradiated plutonium:

$$
{}^{242}_{94}Pu + {}^{22}_{10}Ne \rightarrow {}^{260}_{104}Ku + 4^{1}_{0}n.
$$

Later, other isotopes of curitovium were synthesized; all of them have very short half-lives.

There have been reports of the synthesis of elements with higher atomic numbers - up to 107. The production of such elements is made very difficult by the fact that their half-lives are so short that the elements decay almost immediately after formation. In addition, the radioactive nuclei of zaran elements decay not only by emitting alpha or beta particles, but also by spontaneous fission of nuclei. It is clear that when dividing the nucleus of a zaran element, a zaran element will not result.

All this limits the possibility of further expansion of the periodic table to the right. There are, however, theoretical reports that give hope that some nuclei with high atomic numbers may be long-lived.

Once again to the question of “on the left”. Having found out that the neutron is the zero element, we seemed to have come to the conclusion that the question of leaving the table to the left was over. However, there are reasons to return to it again.

The fact is that, along with the known elementary particles - electrons, protons and neutrons, so-called antiparticles can also exist. So, if we consider an electron to be a particle, then there is its antiparticle - a positron, which differs from the electron only in the sign of the electric charge. Antiprotons were also observed - the same as protons, but only negatively charged particles. Even antinetrons have been observed—particles just as neutral as neutrons, but still “anti.” In this case, antinuclei appeared, consisting of antiprotons and antinetrons, and antiatoms, in which the antinuclei were surrounded not by an electron shell, but by a positron shell. And since an atomic element in the periodic table determines the positive charge of its nucleus, the antielements should be located to the left of the zero element.
