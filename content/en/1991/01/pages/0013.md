This question seems to countries. Can draw an indescribable variety of different curves. Let's first agree on which curves we will consider. Here we need everyday experience. A good elastic rope or wire does not have sharp corners. Therefore, we will study only *smooth* curves (without any kinks) drawn on the earth's surface. Such curves are allowed to have any self-intersection point.

There are also too many smooth curves. It is impossible to count them all. The only way to understand our question is to agree to consider curves that are “not very much” different from each other to be the same.

Types of curves

A curve is a popular mathematical object that has many interesting characteristics: curvature, length, number of self-intersection points, inflection points, etc. All of them are worth studying. (Some of them are described in S. Tabachnikov’s article “On Plane Curves” in “Kvanit” No. 11, 1988.) Which ones are important for us? Maybe the length? But there are still too many curves of the same length. Consider curves that have the same curvature to be identical? Then there will be more different curves than functions - a lot... In order not to guess anymore, we will talk about all the characteristics of the curve at once.

We will understand the expression “the curves will not differ very much from each other” literally and consider the same curves that differ by “small movement”. Now we will have to consider any two curves the same, which can be deformed (strapped) into each other so that they remain smooth all the time (Fig. 1). After all, deformation can also be broken down into a series of “small movements.” We will call such curves curves-

mu of the same titus (mathematicians think the term *zoomotic curves* is more beautiful).

We have discarded all visible differences between the curves. It is natural to assume that with such a naive agreement, all curves are of the same type. For non-closed curves this is true. Let's imagine a rope lying on the ground, starting to straighten at one end. Such a rope will smoothly unfold into a straight line (Fig. 2). So, it is interesting to consider only closed curves.

Now everything is ready to form a rigorous mathematical question:

How many different types of closed curves are there on earth?

This question has many variations and additions, leading us to a very popular area of ​​modern mathematics. This will be decided ahead, but for now let’s consider the Earth flat.
