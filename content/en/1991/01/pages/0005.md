Television is very popular these days. Even non-technical people are keenly interested in the state of affairs in this area and want to know when it will be possible to receive programs from all over the world on their home TV. There is nothing wrong with this anymore, and now it’s a technical matter. However, this is where service problems arise - if the television station does not want to wait until the state decides and prepares everything and it is enough to just turn on the appropriate channel on its TV, it will have to consume a lot of money itself. At the same time, he must more or less clearly understand the essence of the problems that arise. Some of them are actually related to receiving signals from the satellite - these signals are weak and can be drowned in noise, the antenna needs to be pointed at the satellite, and this is not so easy, and the frequency range is very unusual for radio amateurs; at such high frequencies, conventional transistors do not work and you need to use exotic devices (Gunn diodes, for example). Another part of the problems arises after the signal is received. The fact is that many different color television systems are currently used. The main ones are SECAM, PAL and NTSC. The Soviet-French SECAM system is used in several other countries, in Europe they use the PAL system, and in the USA and Japan it works with the NTSC system.
