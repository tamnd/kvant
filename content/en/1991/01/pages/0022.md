M1237. Let the point $O$ inside the triangle $ABC$ be such that $\overrightarrow{OK} + \overrightarrow{OM} + \overrightarrow{ON} = \overrightarrow{0}$, where $K, M, N$ are the redistribution bases dropped from $O$ to the sides $AB, BC, CA$ of the triangle. Prove the inequality

$$\frac{OK + OM + ON}{AB + BC + CA} \leq \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}.$$

Due to the condition on the point $O$, the segments $OK, OM, ON$ can be moved in parallel so that a triangle is formed (see figure). After rotation by $90^\circ$, the sides of this triangle will become parallel to the sides of triangle $ABC$, hence these triangles are similar. We denote the similarity coefficient by $k$: $k = OK/AB = OM/BC = ON/CA$. Then the left-hand side of the inequality is proved to be equal to $k$. On the other hand, representing the area $S$ of triangle $ABC$ as the sum of the areas of triangles $AOB, BOC$ and $COA$, we obtain

$$2S = a \cdot OK + b \cdot OM + c \cdot ON = k(a^2 + b^2 + c^2),$$

where $a, b, c$ is the length of side $\triangle ABC$. Thus, the problem reduces to proving the inequality

$$S \leq \frac{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}{4\sqrt{3}}.$$

Let us present one of the proofs of this fairly well-known inequality, using Heron’s formula and the inequality between the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean of three numbers (the letter $p$, as a general one, denotes the semiperimeter):

$$S = \sqrt{p(p - a)(p - b)(p - c)} \leq \leq \sqrt{p((p - a + p - b + p - c)/3)^3} = p^2/3\sqrt{3} \leq \leq (a^2 + b^2 + c^2)/4\sqrt{3}$$
