Problem 1. A circle $C_1$ is constructed on the segment AB as a diameter, and BD is its chord. Circle $C_2$ touches $C_1$ at point A and line segment BD at point K. Prove that AK is the bisserrisis of angle DAB (Fig. 3).

Solution. The center O of circle $C_2$ lies on the diameter AB of circle $C_1$. The OK radius drawn to the point of contact has been redistributed by BD. Angle ADB is right because it rests on the diameter, so segments AD and OK are parallel, and angles DAK and AKO are equal, lying crosswise. In an isosceles triangle AOK, angles AKO and KAO are equal, therefore AK bisects angle DAB.

However, more often one comes to use a simple corollary of Theorem 1:

Angles inscribed in a circle and resting on the same arc or on equal arcs are equal.

The general principle of the approach to solving problems in which circles are involved is this: look for all kinds of inscribed angles, for which purpose draw various chords. The more equal inscribed angles we find, the clearer the path leading to the solution will be visible.

Problem 2. In a quadrilateral ABCD inscribed in a circle, through vertices A, B and the intersection point P of the diagonal, draw a circle intersecting side BC at point E. Prove that if AB=AD, then CD=CE (Fig. 4).

Solution. Let's draw the chord AE and find equal inscribed angles. Such angles in one circle will be DAC and DBC, and in the other - PAE and PBE. Therefore, $\angle DAC = \angle CAE$. In addition, DCA and ACB based on equal angles are equal. This implies the equality of triangles DCA and ECA and, accordingly, their sides DC and EC.
