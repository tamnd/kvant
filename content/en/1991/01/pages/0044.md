we then the last element of the periodic table - uranium. Perhaps it will also turn into its real neighbor on the right, which does not exist in nature, i.e., into an element with atomic number 93? Fermi and his colleagues carried out such an experiment, but were unable to prove that the “zauranium” element had actually been obtained, although they cautiously pointed out this possibility. As a result, they found out that in their experiments they obtained not even one, but at least two sauranium elements.

Firstly, what Fermi predicted happens - a reaction, like putting two drops of water on the reaction (*):

$$\begin{align*}
238_{92}U + \frac{1}{92}n & \rightarrow 239_{92}U + \gamma,
\\239_{92}U & \rightarrow 239_{93}Np + \beta.
\end{align*}$$

The half-life of $^{239}_{92}U$ is only 2.3 minutes. The resulting new element neptunium is also beta radioactive, and with a half-life of $T \approx 2,3$ days it turns into an element with a new atomic number - 94, called pultonium:

$$\begin{align*}
239_{93}Np & \rightarrow 239_{94}Pu + \beta.
\end{align*}$$

This is how the first two sauranium elements were “discovered” - Np and Pu. The word “opened” is used because they were not discovered, but created. After all, you can discover something that exists but has not yet been discovered; here only the possibility of creating new elements was discovered.

Subsequently, more than ten isotopes of neptunium were “prepared.” The more stable of them is $^{237}_{93}Np$, which has a half-life of about 2.2 million years. If he had survived on Earth, he would have been the “father” of the radioactive family, the final decay product of which would have been $^{209}_{83}Bi$. Many isotopes and pultonium were obtained. One of them is of great practical importance - $^{239}_{94}Pu$, which, along with $^{235}_{92}U$, serves as fuel in nuclear reactors.

The time has come for the creation of the next suburanium elements.

Element number 95 is American (Am). This element is produced “by itself” in atomic reactors: one of the pultonium otops is beta-radioactive and, according to the displacement rule, turns into amertium:

$$\begin{align*}
241_{94}Pu & \rightarrow 241_{95}Am + \beta.
\end{align*}$$

Element number 96 is curium (Cm). This element was first obtained by irradiating $^{239}_{94}Pu$ high-energy alpha particles according to the reaction

$$\begin{align*}
239_{94}Pu + \frac{4}{2}He & \rightarrow 242_{96}Cm + \frac{1}{0}n.
\end{align*}$$

More than ten isotopes of curium are known. The most stable of them is $^{249}_{96}Cm$, which has a half-life of 16 million years.

Element number 97 is berkelium (Bk). To obtain this element, alpha particle irradiation was supported by one of the amertium otopopes:

$$\begin{align*}
241_{95}Am + \frac{4}{2}He & \rightarrow 243_{97}Bk + 2_{0}^{1}n.
\end{align*}$$

The half-life of this berkelium isotope is 4.5 hours, but there are also longer-living isotopes. For example, $^{247}_{97}Bk$, who is $T=7000$ years old.

Element number 98 is californium (Cf). “Discovered” almost simultaneously with berkelium by irradiation with alpha particles of the curium isotope:

$$\begin{align*}
242_{96}Cm' + \frac{4}{2}He & \rightarrow 245_{98}Cf + \frac{1}{0}n.
\end{align*}$$

This element also has an isotope with a significant half-life. This is $^{251}_{98}Cf$ from $T=800$ years.

Element number 99 is einsteinium (Es). To create this element, it was necessary to influence the nuclei of not light alpha particles, but accelerated ones of heavier elements - nitrogen ions with an energy of about 100 MeV:

$$\begin{align*}
238_{92}U + \frac{14}{7}N & \rightarrow 246_{99}Es + 6_{0}^{1}n.
\end{align*}$$

Element number 100 is Fermi (Fm). This element was obtained in almost the same way as the previous one, with the only difference being that oxygen ions accelerated to an energy of 180 MeV were used here:

$$\begin{align*}
238_{92}U + \frac{16}{8}O & \rightarrow 250_{100}Fm + 4_{0}^{1}n.
\end{align*}$$

Element number 101 is Mendleian (Md). It is obtained like this:

$$\begin{align*}
253_{99}Es + \frac{4}{2}He & \rightarrow 256_{101}Md + \frac{1}{0}n.
\end{align*}$$

The most stable isotope of mendleevium $^{258}_{101}Md$ has a half-life of about 5 hours.
