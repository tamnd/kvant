HUNTING
ON MUSTANG

In one of the computing centers, the effectiveness of various algorithms for analyzing conflict situations was comparable. The model used is a chess puzzle with only two pieces that move on unusual boards - one piece catches the other. I especially love the computer's achievements in analyzing games where a rook fights a knight. “Mustang Hunting” - that’s what its authors A. Levin and O. Uskova called this game.

The traditional board doesn't seem interesting. According to his mother, it is impossible to ride a horse on her, except for some particularly unfortunate situations for him. As the size of the board decreases, the knight is supported by increasingly greater dangers. On an 8x5 board, large initial positions are still nothing, but on an 8x4 board, the rook can easily catch the knight at any initial position of the pieces.

Before us is a kind of educational example of “hunting a mustang.” 1. Rs3! Nd1 (1...Kh1 and 1...Kg4 lose immediately due to 2. Rf3) 2. Rc2! Ke3 3. Rd2 Kf1 (3...Kc4 4. Rd3 Kb2 5. Rd4) 4. Re2 Kg3 5. Re1! - the knight is caught and the rook destroys it on the next move. Another option: 1...Ke4 2. Rf3! Kd2 3. Re3!, and after 3...Kf1 or 3...Kc4 the matter comes down to the previous one.

Let's turn to more interesting positions.

1. LD4! Ks3. The faster loses is 1...Ke3 2. Rd2 or 1...Kb6 2. Rd2 Ka4 3. Rs2 and then Rs5-c4-d4.

2. LD2! Ke4 3. Rd3. If now 3...Kc5, then 4. Rd4 and on 4...Kb3 - the familiar route Rc4-b4-e4-e3. On 3...Kf2 decides 4. Re3 Kd1 5. Rf3 Kb2 6. Rs3 Kd1 (6...Ka4 7. Rs4 and 8. Rd4) 7. Rs2, etc. If any

On the first move of the rook there is no longer a win, however, the mustang faces a very dangerous race. Here is one of the options: 1. Rs4? Ke3! 2. Rf4 Kc2! (2...Kd5? 3. Rd4, 2...Kd1? 3. Rf3) 3. Re4 Ka3! 4. Re2 Kb5! (4...Kc4? 5. Rs2 Kd6 6. Rs3) 5. Rd2 Kc3!, and White in zugwang: 6. Rd4 Ke2 7. Rs4 (7. Rd3 Kf4!) 7. Kg1 (7. Kg3? 8. Rs2 Ke4 9. Re2 and 10. Rd2) 8. Re4 Kf3! 9. Rg4 Ke1! (9...Kd2? 10. Rf4 Kb3 11. Rs4) with a draw.

The final task is more difficult than the previous ones. The solution contains 15 moves - a small chess game!

1. Rb3! Kf4. The moves 1...Kf2 and 1...Kc5 speed up the outcome, and with 1...Kc1 and 1...Ke1 the matter turns to the main option. 2. Rf3! Continuing the “love attack” looks pointless, but the mistake was precisely the direction of 2. Re3. 2...Kd5 3. Rf2!! 3...Kb4 4. Rd4! Ka6 5. Rd4! Kc5 6. Rd5! Ke4 7. Rd3! Kc5 (7...Kf2 8. Re3!) 8. Rd4! This position had already broken down after five moves, but this time the knight had to move.

8...Kb3. Now comes the effective manner familiar to us from the second position. 9. Ls4! Kd2 10. Rb4! Kf1 11. Re4! Kd2 12. Re3! Kf1 13. Re2 Kg3 14. Re1, and the next move the rook eats the knight.

This is the kind of meaningful struggle that is possible on a miniatorium board, even with stolid material.

E. Geek
