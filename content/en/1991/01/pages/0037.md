Thunderstorm and thunderstorm

Thunderstorm is a very commonly observed atmospheric phenomenon. Every second, about a hundred thunderstorms occur on the globe. There are even places (Central America, Indonesia) where thunderstorms occur so regularly that people agree on a witness with the following words: “meet on Wednesday after the thunderstorm.”

A thunderstorm and its inseparable lightning and thunderclaps are not only an effective, but also a terrifying phenomenon. And unsafe. It is not for nothing that this word has the same root as the words “threaten”, “threat”, “formidable”, etc. A thunderstorm is indeed associated with considerable dangers - for people and for forests, for electrical devices and communications. Hence the need for lightning protection.

But what is a thunderstorm?

A thunderstorm is a huge spark discharge in the atmospheric air that occurs between electrically charged clouds or between a charged cloud and the ground. The length of the channel along which the discharge makes its way can reach many kilometers. The current strength in it is hundreds of thousands of amperes. The temperature of the gas in a thunderstorm channel is many thousands of degrees. The compliance is very high and the gas pressure in it. For such a discharge to occur, an electrical voltage (for example, between the cloud and the ground) is required - up to a billion volts, and an electric field voltage - up to a million volts per meter.

If on the path of a lightning discharge there is an obstacle - a tree, a building, etc., then high pressure can lead to mechanical destruction, and high temperature - to a fire. It is possible that the first fire with which man became acquainted was the fire caused by a thunderstorm that burned a tree. It is no coincidence that in the world Prometheus is spoken of as burning fire from the sky.

How to deal with this danger? How to fix it?

The idea of ​​protection from thunderstorms was first proposed by the American physicist Benjamin Franklin back in the mid-18th century. He also established the electrical nature of lightning. The idea of ​​protection, which is still used today, is based on the properties of conductivity.

Conductor with a sharp edge. It is known that electric charges on a conductor are located on its surface. If the conductor has the shape of a sphere or ball, the charge is distributed evenly over its surface - the same charge comes to each unit of conductivity area.
