Obviously, if at some point one of the players has all his chips “eaten”, the game immediately ends in his defeat. The shortest duel with such a fial (“baby checkmate” for reversi) consists of nine moves: 1. c5 2. c4 3. e3 4. e6 5. e7 6. d3 7. c3 8. f5 9. g5!, and only black chips remain on the board. Let's look at some elements of the principles of the game. Since enemy pieces located in the center of the board are easier to capture than on the edge, you should always strive to occupy the extreme fields of the board and beat the enemy in this. Capturing corner fields is especially beneficial. Chips that fall on them can never be turned over (there is simply nothing to capture them with) and retain their personal color until the end of the game. The one who breaks into one of the corners of the board receives a serious advantage, and if this happens at the beginning of the game, then a decisive one. Therefore, by the way, it is very dangerous to place your chips next to the corner squares, especially on b2, b7, g2, g7; the opponent can perform a simple combination and take the corner. Here is a striking example that illustrates the power of the corner chips (Fig. 4).

Experienced players in the opening fight in the center of the board - in the square c3 - c6 - f6 - f3, trying to avoid letting the opponent's pieces reach the edge of the board for as long as possible. Then the white and black pieces occupy the edge of the board one after another, and you have to be careful not to let the enemy pieces go into the corner. Towards the end of the game, the options become fewer, and skilled players calculate them almost to the end. Now giving corners is no longer so dangerous.
