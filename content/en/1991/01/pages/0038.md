As stated, the surface charge density is the same. This is due to the fact that the sphere has the same curvature at all points of the surface, i.e., the values ​​characterizing the deviation of a curved surface from a flat one are the same.

On a surface of arbitrary shape, the curvature in different places can be different. It is clear that in this case the electric charge is distributed unevenly over the surface - after all, the charges are always located so that the direction of the electric field inside the conductor is zero. To do this, in those places where there is “less space,” the charges must be located with a larger surface area. That is why the direction of the field around the conductor is especially high near those areas of its surface where the surface curvature is large.

The point on the surface is where the curvature is greatest. The correspondence here is the highest surface charge density, and near the tip there is a particularly strong electrostatic field. This is used in a lightning protection device - a lightning rod.

Lightning rod. It is a metal rod, one end of which is brought into good contact with the ground (reliably grounded), and the other, equipped with a pointed pin, rises above the structure being protected. The rod together with the ground is a giant conductor, and the lightning rod pin is the tip on it.

How does this simple device work?

Let's assume that there is a thundercloud near the lightning rod. Let, for example, the charge on it be positive. Then the electrostatic field of the cloud will cause a separation of charges on the conductor consisting of the ground and the lightning rod, so that a negative charge appears on the lightning rod. Moreover, since there will be a very large surface charge density on the pin, a very strong electrostatic field will arise around it.

In atmospheric exposure there are small numbers (on the order of several

hundreds in one cubic centimeter) positive and negative charged particles - ions and free electrons. Normally they do not interfere with the effects of being an excellent insulator. But in a strong field - near the lightning rod pin - onns and electrons acquire such high speeds that, when they come into contact with neutral molecules, they inactivate them.
