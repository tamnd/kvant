We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than March 15, 1991 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, 《Kvant》。Solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned to different envelopes. On the envelope in the “To” column there is the following: “Problem «Kvant» No. 1—91” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1261” or “F1268”. In the column “...sender’s address” please write your first and last name legibly. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solution).

The condition of each original problem proposed for publication is assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you will study.

Our magazine holds a daily competition for the best solution to the problem from “Problem «Kvant». The results of the competition are announced in December. The winners receive the right to participate immediately in resource-based rounds of the All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics and physics.

M1261. There are 1991 red, black and yellow points on the plane, no three of which lie on the same straight line. Some pairs of points are connected by segments, with the same number of segments coming out of each point. Prove that there is a red dot that is connected to both the black and yellow dots.

S. Genkin

M1262. Let $d_1, d_2, d_3$ be the pairwise differences of the length of the sides of the triangle (according to the abscylotic value), $P$ be its perimeter. Prove the inequality

$$d_1 d_2 + d_2 d_3 + d_3 d_1 \leq P^2/4.$$

L. Kurlyandchik

M1263. Inside the circle there are two more circles touching the outer circle at points $A$ and $B$ respectively and intersecting each other. Prove that if one of the intersection points lies on the segment $AB$, then the sum of the radii between the circles is equal to the radius of the larger one. Is the opposite true?

A. Veselov

M1264*. On an endless white sheet of checkered paper, the square $2 \times 2$ of the cell must be painted black. Is it possible to do this by several operations, each of which is repainting all cells in the square $3 \times 3$ or $4 \times 4$ cells in the opposite color?

I. Kan

M1265*. a) Prove that among 21 pairwise distances between 7 different points on the plane, the same number occurs no more than 12 times.

b) What is the most number of times the same number can occur among 15 pairwise distances between 6 different points on the plane?

N. Sedrakin

F1268. A spherical glass aquarium is filled with water and rotates at a constant angular velocity around a vertical axis. After the shell was instantly braked and released, the angular velocity of rotation became 1.5 times less than it was at the beginning. What proportion of the aquarium's mass is water? Assume that glass has a density three times greater than water.

O. Dutch
