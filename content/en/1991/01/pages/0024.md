Consequently, the angle between rays $AP$ and $BQ$ is equal to $\varphi$ (the angle between any ray and its image when rotated is equal to the angle of rotation, homothety translates any ray in alignment). At the same time, the angle between the rays $BP$ and $AP$ is equal to $\alpha=\angle APB$. Therefore, the angle $PBQ$ between the rays $BP$ and $BQ$, depending on the specific data of the problem, can be equal to $\alpha+\varphi$, $2\pi-(\alpha+\varphi)$ or $|\alpha-\varphi|$ (Fig. 4).

So, to find the point $B$, it is enough to construct arcs on the segment $PQ$, from the point of which this segment is visible at the specified angles: all possible positions of the point $B$ are among the points of intersection of these arcs with the circle $O_2$. Of course, when constructing arcs from two values ​​$\alpha+\varphi$ and $2\pi-(\alpha+\varphi)$, you need to choose the one that is less than $\pi$; if $\alpha+\varphi=\pi$ or $\alpha-\varphi=0$, then the corresponding arcs degenerate into the interval $PQ$ or the set of points of the line $PQ$ that do not belong to the segment $PQ$.
