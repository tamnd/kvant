and circles

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. SHABUNIN

...Or a triangle in a semicircle field,
But not a straightforward one to draw.
Dante. Divine Comedy. Paradise.

The triangle that Dante writes about
cannot be built (Fig. 1). This fact
the author of the immortal pose leads
along with other scientific truths, which, apparently, were considered generally known among educated contemporaries. Here it is appropriate to recall the years of life of the great Dante Aligera - 1265-1321, and also to note that Dante was not interested in mathematics.
He was inspired and went down in history as a poet and philosopher, the creator of the Alliance literary language.

Our article will discuss both triangles written “in the field of a semicircle” and other configurations that are necessary to study.

Theorem 1 (about the inscribed angle).
An inscribed angle is equal to half the central angle subtended by the same arc.

Often uses an equivalent formulation of Theorem 1:

Let α be the central angle subtended by the arc AnB of the given circle (Fig. 2). Then from the points belonging to the arc AnB, the chord AB is visible at an angle α/2.

Note that, by virtue of this theorem, the angle subtended by the diameter is a right angle, therefore a triangle inscribed “in the field of a semicircle” is figuratively rectangular. This is convenient to use, for example, in the following problem.
