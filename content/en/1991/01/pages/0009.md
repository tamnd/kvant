The satellite rotates at the same angular speed as the Earth around its axis, so it appears motionless from the Earth. Thanks to this, antennas can be stationary both on the satellite and on Earth. Signal strength

To estimate the signal power from the satellite, let us imagine that a disk passes through the center of the service area, perpendicular to the direction of wave arrival. The diameter of the disk $D$ will be considered approximately equal to the diameter of the service area. The antenana, as we have already said, radiates only 60% of the transmitter power $P$ in the area of ​​the radiation pattern. Therefore, per unit area of ​​the disk the power drops $p = 0,6P / (\pi D^2/4)$, and the receiving antenna with an aperture diameter $d$ loses power $p(\pi d^2/4)$. But you also need to take into account that the antenna extracts only 60% of the power incident on it. As a result, in the center of the zone the signal power will decrease in accordance with the radiation pattern of the transmitting antenna.

On the satellite, the power supply for the equipment comes from solar panels. Solar panels usually do not allow transmitter power to exceed 200 W. To illustrate the formula, assume $P = 100$ W, $D = 700$ km and $d = 1$ m. The calculation gives the signal power in the center of the zone $P_c \approx 0,7 \cdot 10^{-10}$ W, at the edge - $0,35 \times 10^{-10}$ W. Is it a lot or a little? This question can be answered only when the sensitivity of the receiver is known.

Receiver sensitivity

This characteristic of the receiver determines its ability to provide normal reception with minimal signal power. The sensitivity is higher, the smaller the signal value it is estimated. It can be shown that by increasing the gain, the sensitivity of the receiver can be improved indefinitely and made capable of receiving increasingly weaker signals. However, the sensitivity limit is set by noise. If the signal strength is insufficient, noise in the form of chaotic wandering lines and dots is visible on the TV screen. The image appears as if behind a veil of invoice. Normal reception of the present is only possible when the signal exceeds the noise to a certain extent.

The distortion of noise is twofold: one noise arises in the receiver itself, the other is received by the antenna from the surrounding space. In the receiver, noise arises from the chaotic thermal movement of electrons.
