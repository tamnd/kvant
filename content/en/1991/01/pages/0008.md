Service areas

The presence of line of sight determines the service area of ​​the transmitting TV station, or more precisely, the territory where the station provides reliable reception. Above the earth's surface, the line-of-sight distance, in general, depends on the height of the antennas and the terrain bluff. Above-level spherical earth surface, when the height of the transmitter antenna $H$ is much greater than the height of the approximate antenna, line-of-sight distance (see Fig. 2)

$$D \approx \sqrt{2RH} \approx 3,6 \cdot 10^3 \sqrt{H} \text{ (м)}$$

($R$ is the radius of the Earth).

The station's service area is limited to the visible horizon. At powerful TV stations, the antenna height usually does not exceed 300 m, so the radius of service areas does not exceed 70 km. To cover the entire country with television, a community network of numerous high-power and low-power (with a zone radius of several kilometers) stations was built. However, there are still settlements where, due to their isolation or the complexity of the terrain, there is no terrestrial television.
