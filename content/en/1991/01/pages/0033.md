CAPDIDACI ABOUT LIGHT
AND COLOR

Captain of Pedagogical Sciences
S. TIKHOMIROV

In the new issue of the magazine for last year, we offered our readers several problems on the topic “Light Phenomena,” taken from fiction («Kvant», 1990, No. 11, p. 34). Today, as promised, we are returning to these tasks. Their discussion allows you to take a broader look at physical phenomena familiar to you from textbooks.

Let's start with the smooth spread of light and the formation of shadows. Why do the shadows lengthen in the evening? The reason for this is that the height of the Sun above the horizon changes during the day - it rises before noon, and decreases in the evening. Because of this, the length of the shadows changes, but “in the reverse order.” The writer I. Sokolov-Mikitov observed the sunrise in the tundra, when, when the sun's rays appeared, the shadows from the feet “stretched almost a kilometer.” (We hope that you were able to easily explain why in F. Iskander’s story the boy who determined the time by the shadow was late.)

In an episode from N. Gogol's story, you, of course, recognized the phenomenon of light passing through small branches. At the same time, on the screen (on the screen) it turned out to turn the image of the objects located in front of the answer. Based on this phenomenon, a device called a camera obscura, or “hole” camera, operates. It was invented in the middle of the 16th century and at one time was widely used for accurate natural sketches.

Pozt A. Prasolov wrote about the visible convergence of railway rails in the distance. This is a phenomenon, an illusion of vision. How can its occurrence be explained? The fact is that our visual perception does not always correctly reflect the true sizes of objects and the distances between them. For example, the further an object is from the observer, the smaller it appears. This is due to the fact that each size of an object depends on the angle from which it is viewed. This is clearly shown by figure 1. Observe, the eye whose eye is depicted on figure will evaluate the length of the same vertical stick differently depending on the distance to it, i.e. depending on its angular size. In the extreme left position it seems larger to him.

Now it is not difficult to understand why the rails seem to converge in the distance: as you move away from the observer, the angular size of the distance between the rails (the angular size of the sleeper) decreases. The phenomenon of visible convergence of parallel lines in the distance is called perspective. Perspective perception of space was developed during the long evolution of vision in humans.

The writer V. Korolenko noted that during a solar eclipse, when only a narrow luminous crescent remained of the daylight, the shadows on the ground became “pale and unclear.” Why? The fact is that in low light the contrast color of the eye deteriorates, i.e. the eye is less able to distinguish the boundaries between areas with different illumination.

Let us also discuss what forms the shadows of various objects take during a partial eclipse of the Sun. You've probably noticed that on a sunny day small objects, regardless of their shape (butterfly, bird, ball), have the shape of a circle. This is a shadow image of the Sun.
