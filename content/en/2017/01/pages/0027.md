It's interesting that...

... fleas, which are a few millimeters in size, are capable of jumping over a distance of 200 times greater. Scientists have found that fleas are pushed off the ground primarily by the claws of their hind limbs, which act like springs. ...the speed record among animals, measured in units of body length per second, belongs to the American tick. Moving his legs at a frequency of 135 hertz, he covers 322 body lengths in a second. The speed of a person running at this speed would be 2000 kilometers per hour. ...engineers created a computer model that simulates the ramming impacts of sperm whales, piercing the skin of ships with their heads, and found out that special connective tissues of special sacs that produce fat absorb the load from impacts and protect the sperm whales’ skull from ruptures. ...in one of the museums in England there is a copper-clad piece of oak on display, which is pierced through with a swordfish dagger. To deliver such a blow, she needed to reach a speed of about 100 kilometers per hour. ...an octopus, endowed with good eyesight, a developed brain and the ability to change color to match the color of its environment, also moves deftly. He succeeds in this due to the radial symmetry of the limbs. By resting its tentacle on the bottom or rock, the octopus then stretches it and moves in the opposite direction, allowing movement in any direction. ...the main mechanism of the biological clock is located inside a living cell. This is a periodic process, the fluctuations of which are converted into circadian rhythms. Bees and birds coordinate it with the movement of the Sun or the rotating starry sky. ...scientists were able to advance in the study of bioelectricity by using in their experiments giant axons - processes of cells that conduct nerve impulses - “borrowed” from cephalopods. ...chameleons quickly change color not only due to pigmentation, but also, as biophysicists have discovered, with the help of photonic crystals laid out in a clearly structured lattice in the upper layer of the skin. By changing the distance between the crystals, you can influence the wavelength of the reflected light. ...using natural ultrasonic radar - sonar, a bat can spend less than a second catching one flying insect, such as a mosquito. ...evolution has contributed to the improvement of the organ of vision in animals. Thus, in an earthworm, light-sensitive cells are located throughout the skin, in mollusks the eye resembles a camera obscura, in a snail it already contains a lens, and in warm-blooded vertebrates it has a lens with a variable focal length and aperture. ...Scientists have discovered how monarch butterflies manage to fly due southwest when migrating from Canada to Mexico, covering about two thousand kilometers. In this they are helped by large, complex eyes, which allow them to constantly monitor the position of the sun in the sky. Such a “compass” prevents them from straying from the correct trajectory, and if they deviate from it, it indicates the direction to return to the “given course.”
