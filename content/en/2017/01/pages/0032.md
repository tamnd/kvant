KVANT 2017/№1

The bird flies away!

LET'S CONSIDER SEVERAL PROBLEMS proposed at the Small Mechanics and Mathematics clubs for the 6th grade. In each problem, to justify the answer, you need to give an example that satisfies the condition and prove that this example cannot be improved. Problem 1. 50 birds flew into the photo studio - 18 starlings, 17 wagtails and 15 woodpeckers. Every time the photographer clicks the camera shutter, one of the birds will fly away (for good). What is the maximum number of frames a photographer can take to be sure that he will have birds of all three species in his studio? This task is simple: if the photographer takes 15 frames, it may happen that all the woodpeckers fly away and only starlings and wagtails remain. If there are only 14 frames, then no more than 14 birds of any species can fly away - which means at least one of each will remain. Answer: 14. Here we have solved a dual problem: what is the smallest number of frames enough for the birds to “beat” the photographer (to make sure that his condition is not met). If there are at least 15 frames, then the insidious birds have a “strategy” (the woodpeckers fly away), if there are 14 or less, there is no strategy. Let's look at a more complex problem from this point of view. Problem 2. 50 birds flew into the photo studio - 18 starlings, 17 wagtails and 15 woodpeckers. Every time the photographer clicks the camera shutter, one of the birds will fly away (for good). What is the largest number of frames a photographer can take to be sure:

Here the photographer is “chasing two birds with one stone” (he wants two conditions to be met at once), and in order for the birds to beat him, it is enough to “kill” at least one of them. Translating from hare language into bird language, either make sure that there are no more than 10 birds of each species, or allow as many birds of a certain species as you like, but then there should be no more than 9 of each of the other species.
