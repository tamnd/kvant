PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. M2446. a) Ten children were given 100 pasta on their plates. The guys didn’t want to eat and started playing. In one action, one of the children transfers one pasta from his plate to all the other children. After what is the least number of steps that everyone can have different amounts of pasta on their plates? N. Chernyatyev b) The same question, if there are 100 children and in one action one of the children transfers one pasta from his plate to some (whoever he wants) from the rest. M2448. There are 100 points marked on the line, and one more point is marked outside the line. Consider all triangles with vertices at these points. What is the largest number of them that can be isosceles? E. Bakaev

M2449*. There are a finite number of frogs sitting on a line at different integer points. During a turn, exactly one frog jumps 1 to the right, and the frogs must still be at different points. We calculated how many ways the frogs can make n moves (for some initial position of the frogs). Prove that if we allowed the same frogs to jump to the left, but forbade them to jump to the right, then there would be the same number of ways to make n moves. F.Petrov
