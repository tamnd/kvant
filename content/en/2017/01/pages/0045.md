OUR OBSERVATIONS

A curve turned upside down in the humor “Physicists continue to joke” contains one anecdote about the outstanding theoretical physicist Yakov Ilyich Frenkel: “They say about Ya.I. Frenkel that supposedly at the Physics and Technology Institute in the 30s, a certain experimenter caught him in the corridor and showed him the curve obtained experimentally. After thinking for a minute, Ya.I. gave an explanation of the course of this curve. However, it turned out that the curve was accidentally turned upside down. The curve was put in place, and, after thinking a little, Ya.I. explained this behavior of the curve as well.” No matter how funny it may seem, we actually come across such “anecdotes” at every step. No, not that “theorists can explain anything.” And phenomena that seem to contradict each other, which, however, require their own explanation. You don't have to go far. Imagine that you are skiing and come across a picture captured on figure 1. It seems that there is nothing surprising:

But then you walk a few more kilometers and suddenly you see something opposite. On figure 2 car tracks are clearly visible, but they are not melting - everything around is melting, but they are not! Let us immediately warn you that there is no prank here: these photos were actually taken on the same day, just a few kilometers from each other. Time of year, climate, weather conditions - everything is the same here. And the event also seems to be the same: a car drove through the snow. But the results are just the opposite. Well, let's remember Frenkel with respect and try to explain these phenomena? First of all, we admit that the phenomenon depicted in figure 1 is much more rare (in any case, if it happens in a forest or field, and not near some quarry), but it is easier to explain. Let us clarify that “to explain” means to name the physical phenomenon that underlies the picture we observe, to describe the conditions under which this phenomenon manifests itself, and to understand why exactly such conditions arose here.
