physics+fauna

Since ancient times, scientists have tried to explain the phenomena of not only “inert”, dead nature, but also living matter. Physics has not remained aloof from the desire to penetrate into related sciences, including biology. Many directions have emerged at the intersection of sciences, for example biophysics, which has led to many discoveries and changed our ideas about the structure and functioning of the organisms around us. Today our conversation will focus on what new things we have learned thanks to physics about the animal world - fauna.

Questions and tasks

1. When walking, a duck rolls from side to side, but a chicken does not. Why? 2. Does the position of a fish’s fins change as it moves through the water? 3. Why do deep-sea fish, when removed from the water, have a swim bladder that comes out through their mouth? 4. Falling to the ground from a great height, small insects remain unharmed, but large animals break bones and may even die. How to explain this? 5. Why are there no animals on land larger than an elephant? Why did the largest animals survive in the sea? 6. What will happen to a duck or other waterfowl if it lands on water that has been exposed to a lot of soap or other detergents?

7. A worker bee, flying out of the hive for a bribe, makes an average of 180 wing beats per second. When returning with cargo, this number increases to 280. How can we notice this? 8. Why do bees “hang” droplets of water in the hive on dry and hot days? 9. Why do dogs and cats curl up in cold weather, but in hot weather, on the contrary, tend to spread out? 10. Why aren’t there fly-sized mammals? 11. Why can some fish hunt at night and in completely murky water? 12. How does an electric eel, which produces voltages of up to 600 volts and kills small animals with electrical discharges, manage to avoid shocking itself? 13. Infrared irradiation of grain destroys pest bugs. Why do the bugs die, but the grain does not? 14. The larva of the feather mosquito is not visible in the water, but the eyes are visible in the form of black dots. Why? Will the larvae remain invisible in the air?
