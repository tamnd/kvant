4. The strength of the bones and tissues of an animal is proportional to their cross-sectional area. The force of air resistance when bodies fall is also proportional to this area, while the mass of the animal is proportional to its volume. As the size of a body decreases, its volume decreases much faster than its surface area. Thus, for a smaller animal, both braking force and bone strength increase (per unit mass). 5. The reasons are indicated in the answer to the previous problem - an increase in the size of the animal would lead to a disproportionate increase in the skeleton, until the body would be crushed by its own weight. In the sea, due to the action of buoyant force, animals such as, for example, a whale, with a less massive skeleton, can have significantly larger sizes than an elephant or hippopotamus. 6. The bird remains dry in the water because the water does not wet its feathers, which are covered with a layer of fat. Soap will wash away the fat, water will penetrate into the plumage, the bird will get wet through and through and may even drown. 7. To maintain the increased mass in flight, the insect has to increase the lifting force, throwing the air down more often with its wings. And this leads to an increase in the tone of the sound emitted by the bee. 8. As water evaporates, the temperature in the hive decreases and the humidity increases. 9. By changing the free surface area of ​​the body, animals regulate heat transfer. 10. A decrease in the size of a warm-blooded animal leads to an increase in heat transfer through the surface of its body (see problem 4). Nature compensates for this by improving thermal insulation, which, however, has limits. That is why there are no land mammals smaller than the shrew mouse, whose body length is from 3 to 5 centimeters and its weight is from 2 to 10 grams. 11. This applies to the so-called electric fish, which create an electric field around themselves and are able to perceive negligible changes in it due to the appearance of an obstacle or prey in it. 12. Cells that produce electricity are located in the eel sequentially in the form of chains, in turn connected in parallel to each other. The result is a total current that is sufficient to stun or kill the prey or enemy, but too weak within each cell to damage the fish itself. 13. Bugs, unlike grain, are black
