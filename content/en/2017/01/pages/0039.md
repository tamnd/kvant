PHYSICAL

The answer to this question of some kind of “traction force”, we note, does not explain anything. It is necessary to specifically indicate the interacting bodies and describe the properties of the force. For our purposes, it is better to pose two different questions. One may ask: what force causes a change in the momentum of the car? And you can ask: where does the kinetic energy of a car come from? Oddly enough, below we will see that these are indeed different questions. We will talk about normal driving mode, when the wheels do not slip relative to the road. To answer the first question, it is necessary to indicate a specific external force (internal forces cannot change the movements of the center of mass of the system). And such power is easy to find. Indeed, a torque is transmitted to the drive wheel from the engine, under the influence of which the wheel tends to turn relative to the road, but the friction force arising between the wheel and the road prevents it from doing so. It is clear that the friction force applied to the wheel is directed opposite to possible slippage, i.e. forward, in the direction the car is moving. It is the action of this force that leads to an increase in the momentum of the car, one might say, it makes the car move.

Properties of this force

So, we are talking about the friction force between the drive wheels and the road. Motorists call it traction force. And it has amazing properties. However, we can encounter the properties of this force while solving ordinary educational problems and without thinking about the car. For example, here is a well-known problem that cannot be avoided when studying the dynamics of rotational motion - the problem of rolling a cylinder off an inclined plane without slipping. It is very useful to solve it in several different ways. Note that it is the friction force on the plane that spins the cylinder (see figure). If the reader encounters such a thought for the first time, he will say: “What nonsense? There is strength, there is movement, but there is no work?!” Yes, that's right. The fact is that this force is the static friction force; it is applied to the point of contact of the cylinder with the plane, the instantaneous speed of which relative to the plane is zero. There is movement of the cylinder, but there is no movement of the point of application of the force. Of course, there is nothing new or original in the ideas expressed now. And all this is transferred without any changes to the interaction of the car wheel with the road. So, the adhesion force between the wheel and the road does no work. But both in school problem books and in serious manuals on car mechanics you can see that the authors write down the work of the traction force as the product Fs, where F is the traction force, and s is the distance traveled by the car. They can't all be wrong! And why do their mistakes not immediately appear in practice? This issue was discussed in detail in «Kvant» in the article by A. Chernoutsan “Something about the force of traction” («Kvant» No. 5, 1992). And the answer turned out to be surprising and paradoxical. What part of the energy spent on movement can be considered useful? Of course, the one that was used to increase the kinetic and potential energy of the car and to overcome resistance to movement. The mentioned article shows that this energy expended on the path s, under some reasonable assumptions, can always be written as the product Fs.
