OUR

OBSERVATIONS

obviously it's not just snow. Not exactly ice, but, in any case, well-pressed snow, and not at all loose. And compressed by many wheels that have passed over it. I wonder why the wheels here didn’t dirty the snow, but only compacted it? It can be assumed that this road appeared in deep winter. Winter sunlight is not enough to melt the snow even “with the help” of dark spots, and branches melting into the snow is a typical March phenomenon; you will not see this in January. And then whether the snow gets dirty on the wheels or not, it doesn’t matter: it won’t melt anyway. Yes, and it will be covered with fresh snow: before you have time to look back, it’s white all around again. So, all the necessary components of the explanation are there too. Although, no, not all. We forgot about the most important, most “physical” part: what kind of physical phenomenon is here? Why does compacted snow or ice melt more slowly? It would seem that everything is simple here: both in compacted snow and in ice there is less air, fewer voids, which makes it more difficult to melt it. Wait, but “emptiness”, air, is excellent thermal insulation! “A blanket of snow”, “snow protects the ground from freezing” - we have heard such phrases since childhood. Because of its porous structure, it is easier for loose snow than ice to “not let” the sun’s heat inside – its thermal conductivity is lower. Okay, but because of this porous structure, there is simply less frozen water in the snow that needs to be melted. Therefore, it requires less heat to melt. However... Try an experiment - melt blocks of loose and compressed snow in the sun, and you will see that even with the same masses, loose snow still melts faster. No, the effect is something else. Let's take a closer look at the melting spring snow. It is not difficult to verify that there is water inside, under the snow. The snow is saturated with water formed from it; it literally lies in a puddle of this water. So maybe it’s melting water, flowing down through the porous snow mass, spreading across
