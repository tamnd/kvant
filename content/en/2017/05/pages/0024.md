Tape measure and stiffener

S. DVORYANINOV

IMAGINE THAT YOU WANTED TO glaze your balcony. Call the company. To estimate the monetary costs required to complete this work, you are asked to provide the length, width and height of the balcony. How to measure them? If the measured segments were short, then an ordinary school ruler would do just fine. And the length of the balcony is more than three meters. How many times will you have to apply a ruler 25 centimeters long to the railing and add 25 + 25 + 25 + ...? You can lose count. No, the ruler is clearly not suitable. There is another tool for measuring distances - a tailor's meter. Yes, it can be used. But still, each task requires its own tools. After all, a measuring meter for clean fabrics is not suitable for marking a flower bed on the ground. In carpentry, plumbing and construction, a tape measure is used for measurements. We are now interested in the tape measure as a tool for measuring length. It should be in every home. You never know what you have to measure! And the tape measure takes up very little space. A tape measure is the same tailor's meter, only made of durable material - steel or polymer tape with divisions. On one edge of the tape there are divisions in millimeters, on the other - in inches.

Tape measure as a measuring tape

For home needs, a tape measure 3–5 meters or even 1 meter long is sufficient. The tape is wound on a reel located in the tape measure body. Pull the tape outward by the hook at its end. The tape is wound inside using a handle mounted on the body. If the tape is not long, then there is no handle - the tape is wound inward automatically using a return spring located inside. A tape measure with a spring usually has a stopper, which prevents the tape measure from unwinding spontaneously. Some modern tape measures even have LCD displays that display measurement readings. The tape measure should be used with caution. The new tape measure has a spring that winds the tape inward very quickly, almost instantly. And the edges of the tape are thin and hard and can hurt your hand. The following is interesting. If you look closely at the tape measure, you will notice that its steel tape is not flat, but has the shape of a groove. Why is the measuring tape of a tape measure given this shape?
