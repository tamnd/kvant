LET'S START WITH A VERY SIMPLE question: how to construct a tangent from a point P to a given circle $\omega$? Let us recall that the standard construction here is as follows: we find the center of the circle O (how?), then we construct a circle $\omega_{1}$ with diameter OP, now the intersection points of the circles $\omega, \omega_{1}$ and set the tangents (Fig.1).

Now, finally, let the line QE intersect the circle $\omega$ at the points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$, then it turns out that the lines $PP_{1}$ and $PP_{2}$ are tangent! So you can construct tangents from point P to circle $\omega$ using only one ruler. Note that the secants were chosen absolutely arbitrarily at the first step, but in the end, nevertheless, tangents were obtained, i.e. the position of points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ does not depend on the choice of quadrilateral ABCD. We find that the intersection points of the diagonals of all such quadrilaterals lie on the same straight line (Fig.3).
