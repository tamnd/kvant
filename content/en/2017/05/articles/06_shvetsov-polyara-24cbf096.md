What's next?

The construction of the main theorem is so common that some of its objects have earned their own names. Thus, point P is called the pole of the line $P_{1} P_{2}$, and the line $P_{1} P_{2}$ itself is called the polar of point P. Thus, each point (pole) is associated with its own unique line (polar), such a correspondence is called polar. Polar correspondence finds very wide application in solving the most difficult problems. For a quick and effective introduction to the topic, we recommend a selection of problems [1], in which you can find many interesting examples. Surprisingly, it turns out that the construction of a tangent with one ruler proceeds in exactly the same way if we replace the circle with an ellipse, hyperbola or parabola (Fig.11). At first glance it may seem that our proof is useless in this situation, however, in fact, all three cases mentioned (ellipse, hyperbola and parabola) can be reduced to the case of a circle.
