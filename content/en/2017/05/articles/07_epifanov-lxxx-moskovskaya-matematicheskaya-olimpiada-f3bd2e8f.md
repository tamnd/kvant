LXXX Moscow Mathematical Olympiad

8th grade

1. Replace the letters in the expression $AB^{C} = DE^{F}$ with numbers so that the equality becomes true, using each digit from 1 to 6 exactly once. (Explanation: $AB^{C}$ is a two-digit number consisting of digits A and B, raised to the power C. It is enough to give one replacement method.) E. Bakaev

2. A triangle ABC and 10 lines are given on a plane, none of which are parallel to each other. It turned out that each of the lines is equidistant from some two vertices of triangle ABC. Prove that at least three of these lines intersect at one point. S.Markelov

3. There are 100 non-zero numbers written in a circle. Between each two adjacent numbers their product was written, and the previous numbers were erased. The number of positive numbers has not changed. What is the minimum number of positive numbers that could have been written initially? B.Frenkin

4. See problem 4 of the complex option for grades 8–9 of the spring round of the XXXVIII Tournament of Cities (published in «Kvant» No. 4).

5. The teacher gave grades on a scale from 0 to 100. In the educational part, they can change the upper limit of the scale to any other natural number, recalculating the grades proportionally and rounding to whole numbers. A non-integer number is rounded to the nearest integer; if the fractional part is equal to 0.5, the educational part can choose any rounding direction, separately for each grade. (For example, a score of 37 on a scale of 100, after recalculation to a scale of 40, will go into $37 \cdot ( 40 100 ) = 14,8$ and will be rounded to 15.) Students Petya and Vasya received grades a and b different from 0 and 100. Prove that the training part can make several recalculations so that Petya

First day

1. Find the smallest natural number that is a multiple of 80, in which two of its different digits can be rearranged in such a way that the resulting number will also be a multiple of 80. A. Begunts

2. On the incircle of triangle ABC, tangent to side AC at point S, there is a point Q such that the midpoints of segments AQ and QC also lie on the incircle. Prove that QS is the bisector of angle AQC. M. Vasiliev

4. Three cyclists ride in the same direction on a circular track 300 meters long. Each of them moves at its own constant speed, all speeds are different. The photographer will be able to take a good photo of the cyclists if they all end up on some section of the track d meters long. At what minimum d will the photographer sooner or later be able to take a successful photograph? O. Kosukhin

Second day

1. Given two non-constant progressions $( a_{n} )$ and $( b_{n} )$, one of which is arithmetic and the other is geometric. It is known that $a_{1} = b_{1}, a_{2} : b_{2} = 2$ and $a_{4} : b_{4} = 8$. What can the ratio $a_{3} : b_{3}$ be equal to? D. Goryashin

2. Dunno is familiar only with decimal logarithms and believes that the logarithm of the sum of two numbers is equal to the product of their logarithms, and the logarithm of the difference of two numbers is equal to the quotient of their logarithms. Can Dunno choose at least one pair of numbers for which both of these equalities are actually true at the same time? A. Begunts

3. Detective Nero Wolfe is investigating the crime. 80 people are involved in the case, among whom one is a criminal, another is a witness to the crime (but it is not known who it is). Every day the detective can invite one or more of these 80 people to his place, and if there is a witness among the invitees, but there is no criminal, then the witness will tell who the criminal is. Can a detective knowingly solve a case in 12 days? O. Kosukhin
