The analogy with a squirrel in a wheel can also be seen from the other side: ATP, being a rather large molecule, cannot “leak” through membranes and is therefore locked in the cell. As a result, each cell has its own set of ATP “quanta,” and they are located in the mitochondrial membranes. ATP has an unusually high turnover rate. If we assume that 20 g of ATP is present in the human body at any given time, and take into account that 40 kg of ATP is broken down per day, then it turns out that each ATP molecule is broken down approximately 2000 times and then synthesized again, so that its lifespan is less than 1 minute! This rapid renewal of ATP is extremely important for a living organism. And at rest, the body spends 80% of its metabolic power usefully - on “repairing” cells and transporting substances inside them.
