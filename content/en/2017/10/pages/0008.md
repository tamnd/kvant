Principle 80:20 in biology

A. MINEEV

PRINCIPLE 80:20 IS WIDELY KNOWN and “works” in a variety of areas of human activity, although it has a tinge of mystery, mystery and paradox. Another name for it is the Pareto principle, named after the Italian sociologist and economist. At the very end of the 19th century, while processing statistical data, he discovered that 20% of the population owned 80% of all material resources. Accordingly, 80% of the population owns the remaining 20% ​​of the resources. Further - more. It turned out that 20% of buyers bring 80% of the profits, 80% of users visit 20% of sites, and so on and so forth. In attempts to use this principle, it came to the point that the largest software manufacturers (IBM, Apple, Microsoft, Lotus), having discovered at one time that 80% of the time was spent processing 20% ​​of commands, decided to significantly rework their software. The classic 80:20 rule states: “80% of the work is done by 20% of the people and vice versa.” There is a corresponding addition to this humorous statement: “80% believe that they are among the 20% most valuable.” There is probably something in this, but we won’t dare to develop such a topic; it has little to do with physics and biology... Manifestations of a similar principle (in slightly different proportions) were also discovered “on a global scale” - both breaking out into the universal expanses and hiding deep in the genome. At the same time, both in attempts to understand the structure of the Universe and in deciphering the human genome, something completely mysterious and challenging was discovered, having encountered not even 80:20, but an overwhelming 95:5.
