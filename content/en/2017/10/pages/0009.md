PRINCIPLE

80:20

Thus, to harmonize the picture of the structure and evolution of the Universe, it is currently accepted that ordinary matter (including stars and planets) accounts for only 5% of the mass of the Universe, and the remaining 95% is occupied by unknown things. A name for this “something” has already been invented: dark matter (about 20–25% of the mass) and dark energy (the remaining 75–70%), but we have not made much progress in understanding it. The situation with the deciphered genome is no better. The decoding itself was a huge achievement for scientists, but it turned out that 95% of the DNA length in the genome is also occupied by something unknown. And here they also came up with a suitable name: “junk” or “non-coding” DNA. The hope was expressed that this something - “dark”, “junk”, in other words, “non-coding” - would help explain something very important in both the macro- and living world, namely the evolution, respectively, of the Universe and living organisms. Having puzzled the reader a little, let's move on to deciphering the title and the meaning of our article, focusing on an unusual biological example of the manifestation of the 20:80 rule and the paradox associated with this rule.

"Lost" 80%

This paradox is “hidden” very close by, and everyone encounters it at the everyday level. Imagine a person of average fatness, weighing 70–80 kg, sedentary and consuming 1800 kcal of energy per day from food. Let's put him in a chair and leave him in a state of rest and temperature comfort. In this state, the body consumes it with food and, accordingly, releases about 80 W of power into the environment. What is the specified power used for? A person lives, breathes, his heart pumps blood: under resting conditions, 300 liters of blood passes through the heart per hour. During the same time, the lungs pass 500 liters of air... However, let us quote the multi-volume publication by K. P. Ivanov, “Fundamentals of the Body’s Energy,” which contains unique accumulated material:

“...with complete muscular rest of a person, the heart consumes no more than 8-9%, and the respiratory muscles - no more than 3-4% of the energy budget, i.e. a total of 11–13%. Let's add to this amount the constant energy expenditure of the kidneys, which consume approximately 7% of the budget and work continuously. In total this is 20%. What needs is the remaining 80% spent on?” So, again we are faced with the 80:20 principle. Will it be possible to resolve its nature in this case? Without this, “dark energy” appears in human life, as in the case of the riddle of the structure of the Universe. At first glance, its solution itself carries a hint of paradox: the body spends the bulk of its energy at rest on “rebuilding” itself, in other words, on updating the basic structures of cells. Although the thread of the answer may have already been found: 80% of energy is spent on a seemingly completely useless task - the constant destruction and at the same time recreating the same living structures. As they say in everyday life, breaking is not building. And here the body continuously breaks itself and immediately builds itself, moreover, it spends the bulk of its energy on this. For what? Let's walk through the pages of the mentioned book with a pen and a calculator, making some comments along the way.
