The tale of Pinocchio and his globe

I. BOYARINOV

THE TABLE AT WHICH BURATINO WAS DOING HOMEWORKS stood by the window. It was a homemade table that Papa Carlo had made. It was big - it stretched from wall to wall. And everything fit on it. On one edge there were flasks, test tubes and reagents for chemical experiments, on the other - a microscope for biological observations. There was a globe in the middle of the table; Pinocchio loved to look at it. Sometimes he imagined himself as a traveler: here was his native Italy, and he was sailing on a ship from its shores, first to the Mediterranean Sea, then through Gibraltar to the ocean... Another time he imagined flying in a hot air balloon - he slightly squinted his eyes, slowly rotated the globe, and the map seemed to him like a real Earth floating somewhere far below. And now he was looking at the globe spinning in front of him. - Why is your globe spinning in the wrong direction? - said Papa Carlo. - Why not that one? The globe is on an axis and can rotate back and forth, like a weather vane. When we wind our spring clocks, we only turn the handle in one direction.

If the globe had to be rotated only in one direction, then, probably, a corresponding limiter would have been made at the factory. And so he is not there. The globe can be rotated any way you like,” Buratino objected. - Globe - yes. But the globe is a model of our planet, and the Earth rotates in a certain direction! – Is there a direction for rotation? Right now a fly is crawling around the circumference of the window glass. It seems to us that it is moving clockwise. But for a passerby on the street, the fly moves counterclockwise. - Right. Therefore, a point is selected on the axis of rotation, relative to which the direction of rotation is determined. People have long noticed that if you mentally continue the Earth’s axis from the North Pole upward, then this straight line will reach the North Star. Now let’s imagine that there is an observer on this star and looking at the Earth. Our planet will seem like a circle to him. It will see almost the entire northern hemisphere. Cities will appear as small points, and each such point, due to the rotation of the Earth around its axis, will move in a circle. Now tell me how it moves for this observer - clockwise or counterclockwise? – Papa Carlo asked a question. Buratino did not rush to answer, but cut out a circle from paper, put the letter N in its center - this is the North Pole, and depicted Italy with a small boot. And then Pinocchio thought. The circle lies on the table, we look at it from above, from the side of the North Star. How does it actually spin? Where is Italy heading? Clockwise, i.e. from east to west, or vice versa - from west to east? – these thoughts even made Buratino slightly dizzy. “Let’s improve your model,” suggested Papa Carlo, noticing the difficulties Buratino was experiencing. With these words, he cut out another small one from the paper.
