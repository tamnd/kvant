Our experiments with beads

$Нам^{1}$ managed to select two suitable chains of plastic beads with a diameter of 3 mm and 5 mm - for decorating New Year trees. The lengths of the chains were 8 m and 15 m. The height of their fall from the glass was 2 m, the linear density of both chains was L z 5 g/m. 1) First, we conducted the experiment as described on the Internet. The fountain of beads does not rise from the glass immediately - the force grows as the momentum of the already fallen part increases. We got almost identical fountains for both chains. The height of the observed fountain ranged from 10 to 20 cm. This is about 10% of the height from which the chain falls. The total length of the chain only affects the duration of observation. In the Cambridge experiment, the height of the fountain was about 40 cm. The height of the fountain is significantly affected by the fact that the elements of the chain placed in the vessel cling to each other and additionally inhibit sliding. Our quality of the bundles was not important; the beads had to be carefully placed each time. 2) Then we modified the experiment. In the new version, the beads did not fly up out of the glass, but slipped off the almost flat plate

This means that the point is not in the need for a vertical component of the initial speed, but in a sharp change in the direction of the speed - more precisely, a change in the direction (overturning) of the impulse, in the occurrence of a normal component of acceleration, or in another interpretation - in the occurrence of centrifugal force (similar to the examples considered). The development of the fountain in the horizontal direction is associated with the accelerated forward movement of the beads sideways - before falling, they fly a certain distance. Neglect of this circumstance in school problems about a thread (chain) slipping off the edge of a table is often, and rightly, criticized. To estimate this distance, we approximately solved a two-dimensional equation of curvilinear motion with variable mass—a task unusual for schoolchildren. The lateral displacement turned out to be about 15 cm. This agrees well with the experiment, but this result does not provide anything significant for understanding the essence of the phenomenon. Therefore, here we will not complicate the calculations, limiting ourselves to describing the upward movement only. 3) The next complication of the experiment was necessary in order to eliminate the initially arising considerations about the influence of the backward wave upon impact with the ground. This effect undoubtedly occurs, but not in our case. We carried out appropriate evaluations and measurements of the elasticity of several chains and carried out additional experiments. It consisted of throwing chains from a great height (up to 5 m) onto various underlying surfaces. However, it turned out that the fountain may well arise before the end of the chain hits the surface of the earth. Here, with a soft connection between the links, the recoil momentum is lost in the inelastic impact between the individual balls.
