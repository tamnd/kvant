PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2486–M2489 were offered at the XXXIX Tournament of Cities. Problem F2493–F2496 were offered at the Olympiad “Steps of Knowledge of Physics” (Penza). The author of these problems is A. Seitov.

Problem M2486–M2489, F2493–F2496

M2486. The analyst made a forecast of changes in the dollar exchange rate for each of the next 12 months: by what percentage (a number greater than 0% and less than 100%) will the exchange rate change in October, by how much in November,..., by how much in September. It turned out that for each month he correctly predicted by what percentage the exchange rate would change, but was wrong with the direction of the change (i.e. if he predicted that the exchange rate would increase by x%, then the exchange rate would fall by x%, and vice versa). Moreover, after 12 months the course coincided with the forecast. In what direction did the course change in the end? A. Zaslavsky

M2487. The incircle touches sides AB, BC and AC of triangle ABC at points N, K and M respectively (Fig.1).

Lines MN and MK intersect the bisector of the external angle B at points R and S, respectively. Prove that the lines RK and SN intersect on the incircle of triangle ABC. M. Evdokimov

M2488. The city is a checkered rectangle, each cell contains a five-story building. The renovation law allows you to select two adjacent cells on the side in which houses are located, and demolish the house with fewer floors (or the same number). At the same time, as many floors are built over the second house as there were in the demolished house. What is the smallest number of houses that can be left in the city, using the law on renovation, if the city has dimensions: a) 20 s 20 cells; b) 50 s 90 cells? M. Murashkin
