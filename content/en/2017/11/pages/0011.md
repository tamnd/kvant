On an elementary proof of Feuerbach's theorem

I. KUSHNIR, O. CHERKASSKY

Recall that in any triangle the midpoints of the sides, the bases of the altitudes, as well as the midpoints of the segments connecting the orthocenter with the vertices lie on the same circle. This circle is called the circle of nine points or the Euler circle (see, for example, the article by I. Sharygin and A. Yagubyants “The Circle of Nine Points and the Euler Line” in «Kvant» No. 8, 1981). One of the most beautiful results of classical geometry is considered to be Feuerbach's theorem, which states that a circle of nine points touches a circle inscribed in a triangle and three excircles (Figure 1). Most of the known proofs of Feyebrach's theorem are complex and non-elementary (use inversion, etc.). We will give here a very short elementary proof based on well-known formulas and simple calculations. We will use the following notation for the elements of this triangle ABC: a, b,

As is known, the radius of the Euler circle is equal to R 2, and its center E is the midpoint of the segment OH. The statement of Feuerbach's theorem about the tangency of the inscribed circle and the circle of nine points will be proven if we establish that IE = R 2 - r. This equality corresponds to the internal tangency of the circles under consideration (in fact, it can be shown that R 2 is always not less than r).
