OLYMPICS

XLVIII International Physics Olympiad

From July 16 to July 24, 2017, the XLVIII International Physics Olympiad (IPO) for schoolchildren was held in Yogyakarta (Republic of Indonesia). 390 schoolchildren from 85 countries took part in the Olympiad. The Russian team this year was represented by: Sergey Vlasenko (Voronezh), Stanislav Krymsky (St. Petersburg), Kirill Parshukov (Komi Republic), Dmitry Plotnikov (Moscow), Vasily Yugov (Perm). It is pleasant to note that all our participants received gold medals. Below are the conditions for the tasks of the theoretical round of the Olympiad.

THEORETICAL TOUR

Problem 1. Dark matter

Fritz Zwicky was the first to point out the existence of dark matter. He came to this conclusion by analyzing the dynamics of the Coma galaxy cluster. The cluster (cluster) contains about a thousand galaxies. Using the virial theorem, Zwicky estimated the total mass of the cluster. For systems like the solar system, in which the planets rotate

are in circular orbits around the Sun, the virial theorem states that the kinetic energy of planets is determined by the potential energy of gravitational interaction. In general, for a system of interacting particles, the virial theorem relates the time-averaged total kinetic energy of the system and the time-averaged total potential energy of the system. By studying the velocities of galaxies on the periphery of the Coma cluster, Zwicky came to the conclusion that the total mass of the cluster exceeds the mass of the visually observed galaxies included in it. To explain the velocities of galaxies on the periphery of the cluster, it is not enough to take into account the gravitational interaction only with observable matter (i.e., visible galaxies). This meant that the cluster contained some other invisible matter. If we take into account the gravitational interaction with it, we can explain the higher values ​​of the observed velocities. This invisible matter is “dark matter.” In what follows, we will assume that the mass of each galaxy is equal to the sum of the masses of visible matter and dark matter of this galaxy. Both types of matter move together. Consider that dark matter interacts with visible matter only gravitationally.

A. Cluster of galaxies Let a cluster (cluster) consist of a large number N of galaxies and dark matter distributed uniformly within a sphere of radius R. The total mass of the cluster (i.e., both incoming galaxies and dark matter) is M, and the average mass of the galaxy (consisting of visible and dark matter) m. A1. Let matter be distributed uniformly in the cluster. Find the total gravitational energy of the cluster. Express your answer in terms of M and R. (1 point)
