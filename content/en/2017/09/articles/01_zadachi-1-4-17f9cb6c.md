Thus, the spinning top rotates counterclockwise. In this case, the helicoid slides along the surface of the slot. You can see some analogy with how a bicycle chain runs over a sprocket. When one chain link leaves the sprocket, another link replaces it, and so on. Also, two gears cling to one another: one tooth leaves, the next one immediately comes, followed by another... The force that we apply to the helicoid rod is transferred to the body of the spinning top. The touching (coinciding) surfaces of the helicoid and the slot can be approximately considered the surfaces of the two wedges that we talked about earlier. The surface of the slot is a strip moving along the helicoid rod. We are not constantly spinning the spinning top. Having pushed the helicoid rod to the maximum depth, we release it from our hands - the spinning top is spinning. Noticing a slowdown in rotation, we raise the rod up. At the same time, he does not interact with the spinning top in any way. Then we press down on the rod again. The process is repeated cyclically. In a similar way, energy is recharged when the bicycle moves. We spin the pedals, apply force to them, the bike accelerates. Then we don’t have to pedal – the bike moves by inertia. The coupling device between the rod and the spinning top is similar to the coupling of the driven sprocket with the rear wheel of a bicycle.
