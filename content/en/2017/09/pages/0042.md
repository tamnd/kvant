Circumcenter and orthocenter

Recall that the radius of the circumscribed circle and the height drawn from one vertex of the triangle are isogonal. Then the points O and H are isogonally conjugate.

Fig. 8

Let us denote the point of intersection by Q. The point Q is said to be isogonally conjugate to the point P. We can also say that the points P and Q are isogonally conjugate. Using the corollary of property 2, we can prove the theorem and immediately obtain the geometric meaning of point Q: this is the circumcenter of the double pedal triangle XYZ. Theorem 1 is also used in a slightly different formulation. Theorem 1 a. If AP and AQ, BP and BQ are two pairs of isogonals in triangle ABC, then CP and CQ are also isogonals (Fig.8,b). Note that if point P lies on the circumcircle and is different from the vertices of triangle ABC, then the isogonals to AP, BP and CP are parallel to each other.

Tangent Intercept and Double Median

Let AM be the median of triangle ABC. Let's make a standard construction. Let's double the median AM for point M and get point D. Construct a point P isogonal to D. To do this, it is enough to construct isogonals to BD and CD. Let the angles of the triangle be equal to A A, B B, C G. Let us draw an isogonal from side BA of angle ABC to BD (Fig. 9,a).
