To do this, it is necessary to give a new meaning to the basic geometric concepts - to explain in a new way what a point, a straight line, equality of segments, equality of angles are, and do this in such a way that all Euclidean axioms remain true, except for the axiom of parallels. This can be done in many different ways; we will have one of them - the Poincaré model in the upper half-plane. Only points located strictly above some selected straight line, called the absolute, will be considered points belonging to the Lobachevsky plane. (If the absolute is considered the Ox axis, then we will consider only points with y > 0.) The role of straight lines in this model will be played by semicircles with a center on the absolute, as well as vertical rays. The second type of straight line is called special. It is immediately clear that in this model the Euclidean axiom on parallels is violated: through a given point A one can draw infinitely many lines that do not have common points with a given line a (Fig.12).
