Let us draw altitudes in triangle ABC. A triangle with vertices at the bases of the heights is called an orthotriangle. The orthotriangle is the pedal triangle of the orthocenter H. Then, by property 1, we obtain: The radius of the circumscribed circle drawn from the vertex of the triangle is perpendicular to the corresponding side of the orthotriangle (Fig.5).

Let the points X, Y and Z be symmetrical P with respect to the lines AC, AB and BC (Fig.7). Triangle XYZ will be called the double pedal triangle of point P relative to triangle ABC. The following corollary follows from property 2: Isogonals are perpendicular bisectors to the sides of the double pedal triangle.
