The geometry of a relatively non-singular line is played by a remarkable transformation of the plane called inversion (Fig.15). During inversion with respect to a circle (a straight line in the sense of Lobachevsky) with center O on the absolute and radius R, point A goes to point A a, lying on the ray OA and such that OA $OA a R^{2}$. The inversion is defined for all points of the plane except O - fortunately, point O lies outside the points of the Lobachevsky plane and therefore is not of interest to us. The inversion has all the $свойствами^{6}$ we expect from axial symmetry: i the inversion takes the upper half-plane to the upper half-plane; i inversion with respect to a semicircle (straight line in the sense of Lobachevsky) a keeps the points lying on a itself in place; i inversion transforms straight lines (in Lobachevsky's sense) into straight lines (in Lobachevsky's sense); i inversion preserves angles (the angle between straight lines in the sense of Lobachevsky is considered equal to the Euclidean angle between tangents drawn at the intersection point); i if you apply the same inversion twice, you get an identical transformation. Further, having determined with the help of inversions the equality of segments in the Poincaré model, it is possible to prove in it, as theorems, axioms of absolute geometry and thereby justify that this is really a model of Lobachevsky geometry. But this, as the Icelanders say, is not discussed in this saga.
