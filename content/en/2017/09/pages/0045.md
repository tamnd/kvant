Again the point of intersection of the tangents and twice the median

Consider the intersection point P of the tangents to the circumcircle of triangle ABC drawn at points B and C, and the double median AD (Fig.17). Let us now use the general pedal circle theorem to “invent” the problem. We already know that points P and D are isogonally conjugate and they have a common pedal circle. Now let’s “hide” some structural elements. Namely, let's try to get rid of point D in the problem statement (which would be a hint to the solution). Let's find the height AH. Since ABDC is a parallelogram, the projection X of point D onto BC and point H are symmetrical about the midpoint M of segment BC, i.e. CX = BH. Now we are ready to formulate a problem that is almost equivalent to a complex Olympiad problem (Exercise 13). Problem 3. In an acute non-isosceles triangle ABC, the altitude AH is drawn, and point M is the midpoint of BC. The tangents to the circumcircle of triangle ABC drawn at points B and C intersect at point P. On side BC the segment CX = BH is laid out. Points E and F are projections of point P onto sides AB and AC. Prove that points E, F, M and X lie on the same circle.
