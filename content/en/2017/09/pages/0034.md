Trisectors

Hinge mechanism

Hinge mechanisms are systems of straight rigid rods connected at the ends by hinges that allow the rods to rotate relative to each other. Such mechanisms have been used in technology, apparently, since the 18th century. And now they are almost ubiquitous: from a retractable mirror in the bathroom to some types of car suspension. They were also useful for our task. figure 7 shows the "fan", invented by mathematician J. J. Sylvester in 1875.

You can learn more about hinge mechanisms in the articles by Yu. Solovyov “Inversors” («Kvant» No. 4, 1990) and N. Andreev “Inversors” («Kvant» No. 2, 2017).

Let the angle BAC be given on a piece of paper, which needs to be divided into three equal parts. First you need to build folds PP¢ and QQ¢ parallel to AB, so that QQ¢ is in the middle between PP¢ and AB. Then you need to bend the sheet so that point A (on figure 8 its edge is marked A ¢) A hits QQ¢, and point P simultaneously hits AC. Then angle A ¢ AB will be equal to a third of the original angle BAC.

Exercise 8. Prove it. Explain why this construction cannot be reproduced using a compass and ruler (you cannot use the argument that this would contradict the impossibility of trisection).

Auxiliary curves

It turns out that trisection can be performed with ordinary compasses and a ruler. But for this it is necessary that an auxiliary curve has already been drawn on the plane on which the construction takes place.
