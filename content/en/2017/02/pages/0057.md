Task 2. Bodybuilding One athlete decided to bring his body into line with the proportions of the statue of David by the great Michelangelo and went on a diet. But something went wrong. If all the horizontal dimensions of the athlete turned out to be exactly half as large as David’s, then the height remains only h = 170 cm, while the height of the statue is H = 500 cm. Determine the mass of the athlete if the mass of the marble statue is m = 3200 kg. The density of marble is $\rho = 2700$ kg $м^{3}$, the average density of the human body is $\rho_{0} = 1000$ kg $м^{3}$.

Problem 1. Second half The cart, moving from rest with constant acceleration, covers a distance s and acquires a speed v = 10 m/s. Then, continuing to move uniformly with speed v, it covers the same distance s. Determine the average speed of the cart for the second half of the entire travel time.

Task 2. Flask A metal flask has the shape of a parallelepiped. The mass of a flask filled with water is equal to M. If the flask is placed on the table with its largest face, then it will exert a pressure $p_{1}$. If you put it on the middle face, then the pressure will be equal to $p_{2}$. If the flask is placed on the smallest edge, then the pressure will be equal to $p_{3}$. What are the mass m of the empty flask and the lengths of its edges a, b and c? The thickness of the walls of the flask is negligibly small compared to the lengths of its ribs. The density of water is $\rho$. Note. To be specific, designate the length of the short edge of the flask as a, the length of the middle edge as b, and the length of the long edge as c.

If the depth of immersion is doubled, then to maintain balance you will have to move the fulcrum of the lever to the left so that it divides the lever in a ratio of 1: 3 (Fig.4,b). Determine the densities of cylinders $\rho_{1}$ and $\rho_{2}$.
