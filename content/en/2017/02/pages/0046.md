Fig. 7. Two cones of an autorotating ruler

Paleolithic people were probably also familiar with the Magnus effect. You can hear this music when you spin the ruler and it starts to autorotate, but you can see this music right now. We recorded sound by placing a microphone near the path of the LED, as on figure 7, but you can also use a mobile phone. On figure 8, in time-loudness coordinates, a 32-second sound recording and two of its fragments are depicted - one from 16.6 to 20.2 seconds, the other from 18.12 to 18.42 seconds. The recording clearly identifies eight sound groups, each lasting 4 seconds. They correspond to the rotation of the ruler along one or the other cone. Pay attention to the sound amplitudes in neighboring groups - large ones alternate with small ones. This is because one cone was located closer to the cell phone microphone. Next, figure presents a time interval of 16.6–20.2 seconds, which contains the fifth group. High peaks correspond to the ruler passing close to the microphone.
