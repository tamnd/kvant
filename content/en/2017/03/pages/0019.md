PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2454–M2457 were offered at the regional stage of the XLIII All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics. Problem F2461–F2464 were offered at the Ya.B. Zeldovich Olympics in the city of Sarov.

Problem M2454–M2457, F2461–F2464

M2454. Initially, several (more than one) natural numbers are written on the board. Then, every minute, a number equal to the sum of the squares of all numbers already written on it is added to the board (so, if the numbers 1, 2, 2 were initially written on the board, then in the first minute the number $1^{2} + 2^{2} + 2^{2} ).$ would be added Prove that the hundredth number added has at least 100 different prime factors. I. Bogdanov, P. Kozhevnikov

M2455. An equilateral triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle Ω and circumscribed around

Fig. 1

circle $\omega$. On sides AC and AB, points P and Q are chosen respectively so that the segment PQ touches $\omega$ (Fig.1). Circle $Ω_{b}$ with center P passes through B, and circle $Ω_{c}$ with center Q passes through C. Prove that circles Ω $, Ω_{b}$ and $Ω_{c}$ have a common point. A. Akopyan, P. Kozhevnikov

M2456. A convex polygon is cut by disjoint diagonals into isosceles triangles. Prove that this polygon has two equal sides. A. Gribalko

M2457. Is there a triangle with sides x, y and z such that $x^{3} + y^{3} + z^{3} = = ( x + y ) ( y + z ) ( z + x )$? V. Senderov

F2461. A cylindrical glass with base area $S = 400 см^{2}$ is filled with water, into which an ice cube with side a = 2 cm (Fig.2) is immersed. Such an amount of kerosene is poured into the glass that the top edge of the cube coincides with the surface of the kerosene. The cube is removed from the stack - Fig. 2
