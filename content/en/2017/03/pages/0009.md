Life in a solid

First of all, there may be complications at the boundary: processes at the boundary of an object or material can affect diffusion. For example, remember a cylinder made of lead and a cylinder made of gold - they were pressed to ensure good contact. It was to increase the contact area that soft metals were taken and pressed against each other. True, there is another problem, and not just one. A layer of oxide forms on the surface of lead in the air, which can inhibit the transition of atoms from one metal to another. But that’s not all - for diffusion, an atom must somehow move inside the lattice of its own or someone else’s metal, and to move into someone else’s metal, an atom must break away from its own lattice and squeeze into someone else’s. Any of these processes - separation from one’s own, introduction into foreign ones, introduction into the oxide, diffusion in the oxide, separation from the oxide

– may be diffusion limiting. Moreover, all these processes depend on temperature in different ways, and diffusion itself (we haven’t gotten to it yet!) is not consulted with them and depends on temperature in its own way. Therefore, it may very well turn out that at some temperatures one process dominates, and at others, another. Now let us turn to the actual diffusion inside a solid. There are many mechanisms of diffusion. If it is not a single crystal or an amorphous substance, then the solid consists of individual small crystals, and they have edges and boundaries. So, along these edges-borders, diffusion can proceed into the depths of a seemingly monolithic material - first along the edges, and then into the crystals. Moreover, migration along the boundaries predominates at low temperatures, up to approximately half the melting temperature (naturally, in Kelvin). In this case, we will have to talk about specific values ​​of the diffusion coefficient with a reservation - for a material with crystals of such and such a size. In addition to the edges, there are edges, and some works note accelerated diffusion along the edges.
