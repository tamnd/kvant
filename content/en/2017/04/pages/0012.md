Shadows of sparkling snow

V. PTUSHENKO

TWO MONTHS AGO I HAD TO PUBLISH photographs of one unusual natural phenomenon - snow shadows (Science and Life, 2017, No. 2), which unexpectedly aroused great interest from both people I knew and those I didn’t know. For example, on one of the “scientific forums” on the Internet, a heated discussion broke out about the nature of the phenomenon, which I learned from forum participants who sought me out to clarify details - weather conditions, place and time of observation. I thought it would be useful to consider at least some of the hypotheses put forward by the science-interested community. Useful both for understanding the phenomenon itself, which turned out to be so fascinating, and as some example of a “seed” for future research that readers who are not satisfied with just an exchange of opinions and are ready to spend their own will dare to conduct.
