It's interesting that...

...back in the 17th century, the English physicist and chemist Robert Boyle established that the glow of rotting wood stops without air and resumes again in the air, namely in the presence of oxygen. ...the Dutch scientist Jacob van't Hoff, one of the founders of physical chemistry and the first Nobel laureate in chemistry, explained the nature of osmosis in 1887, extending the laws of ideal gases to dilute solutions, which led to an understanding of the mechanism of plant nutrition with soil water. ...the leaves of Victoria royal (water lily family), growing off the coast of the Amazon, reach two meters in diameter and are penetrated by many thick veins, making them so strong that they can support the weight of an adult. ...In 1729, the scientific secretary of the Paris Academy of Sciences, Jean Jacques de Mairan, reported a remarkable observation. The bean, which lowers its leaves at night and raises them before dawn, continued these cyclic movements in complete darkness. The presence of an “internal clock” in plants was subsequently confirmed by many experiments and turned out to be associated with intracellular periodic processes. ...the mystery of young sunflowers following the sun has been resolved in the work of Californian scientists. Morning light is captured by the plant's photoreceptor, which transmits a signal to the "biological clock", which controls the alternate growth of the shady and heated sides of the trunk. ...the so-called “mad cucumber” - a plant that, when ripe, shoots a stream of sticky juice mixed with seeds at 6-8 meters - accumulates gases inside the fruit, the pressure of which reaches 3 atmospheres before the “shot”! ...stems of bamboo, reaching up to 35 meters, are as light and durable as many building materials. And with the use of modern technologies that make it waterproof, bamboo can compete even with carbon fiber. ...one of the recent important discoveries of domestic scientists took place thanks to blue-green algae. It turned out that the energy of light captured by their photosynthetic molecules is transmitted along the multicellular chain due to electrical communication between cells. ...in a year, land and ocean plants manipulate colossal amounts of matter and energy: they absorb 150 billion tons of carbon dioxide, decompose 120 billion tons of water, release 200 billion tons of free oxygen and store about one hundred and fifty trillion megajoules of solar energy through photosynthesis.
