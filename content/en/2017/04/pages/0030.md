Let's consider supports standing along a straight line. For each such support, the wires suitable for it are pulled in different directions. In this case, the forces acting on the support balance each other. The resultant of all forces applied to the support is zero. When turning, the suspended wires create a force that is applied to the support and directed in the direction of the turn (it is obtained according to the parallelogram rule and is not equal to zero). Additional support prevents this force from acting. If an additional support appears on the left along the path, it means that the power line turns left at this place. There are also supports for bridges connecting the banks of the river. If the river is wide and one span of the bridge is not enough, then two adjacent spans are placed on a support called a bull.
