Exercise 4 (A. Hrasko, 2000). A circle is given on a plane, and inside it is a second circle. The grasshopper jumps from one circle to another and back with a constant jump length. The result is a broken line $A_{1} A_{2} A_{3} A_{4}^{…}$, in which all links are equal, vertices with odd numbers lie on the first circle, and vertices with even numbers on the second. Prove that the segments $A_{1} A_{3}, A_{3} A_{5}, A_{5} A_{7}$, … are tangent to the same ellipse.

A.P. SAVINA

⟦column⟧

Note. Let us draw an arbitrary sphere with a radius greater than the radius of the circle through the second circle. We will also draw a sphere with center $A_{2k}$ and radius equal to the length of the jump. Prove that the common plane of these spheres passes through a fixed point for all k. Then use this supporting fact. Supporting fact (without proof). On a plane A, a circle is given and points A and B outside the plane are given, and the projection of A onto A lies inside the circle. Point M moves along a circle, and a plane perpendicular to AM is drawn through point B. Then it intersects A along a straight line tangent to the fixed ellipse.
