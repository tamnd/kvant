OUR OBSERVATIONS

"Impressionistic" photography

V. PTUSHENKO

MIRROR SMOOTH OF THE LAKE (SEA)",

"Z

“mirror of water”... Similar phrases can often be found in artistic descriptions. But physically they are also quite correct: the surface of water at large angles of incidence of light turns out to be a very good mirror. However, this “mirror” is very mobile, easily changing the shape of its surface with the slightest wind or current. Deviations from the plane, called ripples or waves, create various distortions in the image created by this “crooked mirror”. The classic image is the “moon path”. The photograph shown here at the top right and on page 4 of the magazine cover shows the surface of the water on a slightly windy day. This not entirely calm water reflects the embankment - the fence, trees, cars and walls of houses along the embankment. The austere, realistic surface world looks at its “impressionistic” reflection. If you “turn” this reflection upside down, as shown here in the photograph below on the left, then you will not immediately distinguish it from a real landscape, unless perhaps painted by the hand of an impressionist artist.

⟦column⟧

In the caption on the cover, we assured the reader that the photograph has not been manipulated in any way. However, this effect could well be called “analog processing” of the image - although done with it even before shooting. After a walk along the shore, let's mentally walk through the art gallery (see links). If we believe the well-known statement that the best finds of art are always “seen” in nature, then we can assume that on today’s “walk with physics” we met one of its sources.
