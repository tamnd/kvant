ASTRONOMY

BACK

And finally, it was astronomy that pointed out to physicists the existence of two mysterious entities in nature - dark matter and dark energy. Experimental physicists are now actively engaged in the search for dark matter (or rather, dark matter). Theoretical physicists are trying to understand the anti-gravity essence of dark energy. Without astronomical observations, we would never have known about the existence of these two mysterious natural objects, filling the Universe by 95% with their mass-energy. One can only admire the fact that by observing 2% of the mass of the Universe (stars, interstellar gas, planets), astronomers were able to learn about the existence and some properties of the invisible 98% of the mass of the Universe. This opens up an exciting prospect for physics: the study of our world is, in fact, just beginning!

Technique

Astronomy, like other branches of natural science, uses the most modern technologies and itself stimulates their development. You may recall that the Hubble Space Telescope, launched into orbit in 1990, initially produced poor-quality images because its lens suffered from severe aberration. To correct this shortcoming and restore image quality, powerful mathematical methods for solving the inverse problem were developed, which later found application in computed tomography. To improve the quality of images obtained by ground-based optical telescopes, active and adaptive optics methods are currently being developed. The results are amazing: the image of a space object spoiled by the Earth’s inhomogeneous atmosphere can be restored in real time to an almost ideal state, as if the telescope were operating outside the atmosphere, and not at the bottom of the air ocean.
