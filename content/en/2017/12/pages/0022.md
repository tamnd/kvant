It must be said that this formulation of the task at the Olympiad appeared at the last moment. Before this, the draft version featured a slightly different task. Problem 2 (S. Berlov). The perimeter of triangle ABC is equal to 1. Point I is the center of the inscribed circle, point M is the midpoint of side AC. It turned out that – AIM $= 90 \infty$. Find the length of side AB. Answer. 1/4. First decision. One of the solutions to Problem 2 can be easily obtained from the above solution to Problem 1. Indeed, since I is the midpoint of $CI_{c}$, the radius $r_{c}$ of the excircle is twice the radius r of the inscribed circle. From here and from the formulas for the area of ​​the triangle $S = pr = ( p - c ) r_{c}$ we immediately get the answer: c = 1/4. But problem 2 has several more solutions. Let's give two of them. Second solution. Let N be the midpoint of BC (Fig.3). Since MN P AB and – AIM $= 90 \infty$, then MI is the bisector of the angle AMN. Therefore, line MN is tangent to the incircle of the triangle. So why was the wording of the problem changed right before the Olympics? The fact is that in 2010, at the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad, the following problem was proposed. Problem 3 (A. Zaslavsky). In triangle ABC, point I is the center of the inscribed circle, points M, N are the midpoints of sides AC, BC. It is known that – AIM $= 90 \infty$. Prove that – BIN $= 90 \infty$. It is easy to see that the solution to this problem practically coincides with the second of the given solutions to Problem 2.
