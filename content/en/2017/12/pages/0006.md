Neutron stars with record magnetic fields, so-called magnetars, are now being actively studied by astrophysicists.

⟦column⟧

Another “cosmic bonus” for physics is high-energy particles, which physicists use to probe the internal structure of elementary particles and the birth of new types of them, previously unknown to scientists. The higher the energy of the impactor particle, the more interesting the results. The Large Hadron Collider is capable of accelerating protons to energies of $10^{13}$ eV. The Very Large Hadron Collider (VLHC) project envisions an energy of $10^{14}$ eV. It is unlikely that anything more powerful will be created in the foreseeable future. And from space, as part of galactic cosmic rays, protons with energy up to $10^{20}$ eV arrive to us, millions of times more energetic than those that the collider accelerates. An accelerator with such energy cannot be built on Earth at all, since its size would be larger than that of the planet itself. Not to mention its cost. And from space, fast particles come to us free of charge. Academician Yakov Borisovich Zeldovich said that the Universe is an accelerator for the poor. But, as we see, even the richest are not able to create an accelerator that would compete with the Universe.
