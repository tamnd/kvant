PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Problem M2490–M2493, F2497–F2500

M2490. There were 100 doors, each with its own key (unlocking only that door). The doors are numbered 1, 2, ......, 100, the keys are also numbered, but perhaps with errors: the key number matches the door number or differs by 1. In one attempt, you can select any key, any door and check whether this key fits this door. Is it possible to reliably find out which key opens which door by making no more than: a) 99 attempts; b) 75 attempts; c) 74 attempts? A. Lebedev, A. Shapovalov

M2491. A piece of cheese must be cut into pieces in compliance with the following rules: 1) first we cut the cheese into 2 pieces, then we cut one of them into 2 more pieces, then we cut one of the three pieces into 2 pieces again, etc.; 2) after each cutting, the parts can be different in weight, but the ratio of the weight of any part to the weight of any other must be strictly greater than a given number R. a) Prove that with R = 0.5 it is possible to cut cheese so that the process never stops (after any number of cuts it will be possible to cut another piece). b) Prove that if R > 0.5, then the cutting process will someday stop.

M2492. In the non-isosceles triangle ABC the medians $AA_{0}, BB_{0}, CC_{0}$ and altitudes $AA_{1}, BB_{1}, CC_{1}$ are drawn. Let $A_{2}$ be the intersection point of the circles circumscribed about triangles $BA_{1} B_{0}$ and $CA_{1} C_{0}$, different from the point $A_{1}$. Let us similarly define the points $B_{2}$ and $C_{2}$. Prove that the lines $AA_{2}, BB_{2}, CC_{2}$ intersect at one point. T. Kovalev, A. Lvov (9th grade students)
