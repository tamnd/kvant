Is it possible to transform a plane figure into a torus by an isometric transformation that preserves the lengths of all lines on the surface? As we have seen, sections of the plane can be isometrically transformed into a cylinder and a cone. The isometric transformation of a piece of a plane into a piece of a sphere is the dream of all cartographers! In this case, flat maps could be transferred to a globe while preserving all distances. But alas, this is impossible. The sphere is not even locally isometric to the plane. It's easy to prove. Let's take a sphere of radius 1, a point M on it, and consider the set of all points of the sphere that are distant from M at a small distance a. Distance on a sphere is the length of the arc of a great circle passing through the center of the sphere. From an arbitrary point A of this set we drop the perpendicular AK to the line OM (Fig.3). A rectangular sheet can be easily rolled into a “tube”, i.e. into a cylinder. But it is not possible to further roll this cylinder into a torus, or even simply bend it.
