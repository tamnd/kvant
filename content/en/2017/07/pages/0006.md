Why do we study mathematics?

What do mathematicians themselves think about this?

V. ARNOLD

WHY SHOULD YOU STUDY MATHEMATICS? In 1267, the English philosopher Roger Bacon already answered this question: “He who does not know mathematics cannot learn any other science and cannot even reveal his ignorance.” Actually, this could be the end of the lecture, but people think that maybe something has changed over seven centuries... Let's listen to more modern evidence - one of the creators of quantum mechanics, Paul Dirac, claims that when constructing a physical theory, “one should not trust all physical concepts.” What to trust? “Trust a mathematical scheme, even if at first glance it is not related to physics.” Indeed, all purely physical concepts of the beginning of the century have been discarded by physics, and the mathematical models adopted by physicists are gradually acquiring physical content. And this showed the stability of mathematics. So, mathematical modeling is a productive method of knowledge in natural science. We will approach mathematical models from a different angle, looking at problems in mathematics education.

Academician Vladimir Igorevich Arnold gave a lecture on this topic on April 16, 1992 at the Republican Institute for Advanced Training of Education Workers (Moscow). The recording of the lecture was provided to us by Associate Professor Yu. Fominykh (Perm). This article was first published in «Kvant» No. 1/2, 1993.

In our mathematical education (both secondary and higher) we follow the footsteps of the European system, based on the “Bourbakization” of mathematics. A group of young French mathematicians, speaking under the pseudonym Nicolas Bourbaki, published several books beginning in 1939 that formally (i.e., using the axiomatic method) presented the main branches of modern mathematics based on set theory. The formalization of mathematics also leads to a certain formalization of its teaching. This is where the costs of the “burbakization” of mathematical education are manifested. A typical example. Second grade students in a French school are asked the question: “What is two plus three?” Answer: – Since addition is commutative, it will be three plus two. Wonderful answer! It is absolutely correct, but it doesn’t even occur to the student to add these two numbers, because when teaching, the emphasis is on the properties of operations. Europe has already realized the shortcomings of this approach to education, and a rollback from “bourbakization” has begun. In recent years, the Americanization of mathematics education has been taking place in our country. It is based on the principle: teach what is needed for practice.
