It's interesting that...

...recent studies show that children have innate ideas about physics. Thus, already at the age of two months they understand that objects fall if they are not held and do not disappear if they are hidden, and at five months they realize that water and sand are not solid substances. ...in different parts of the human body one can detect the action of simple mechanisms. For example, all our bones that have some freedom of movement are levers - these are the bones of the limbs, the lower jaw, the phalanges of the fingers and the skull, which rests on the first vertebra. ...when walking, people move their legs with the so-called resonant frequency, determined by the dimensions of their body - this is beneficial from the point of view of saving energy. Therefore, if you need to walk faster, then first of all increase the width of the step, and not the pace of walking. ...with a sharp change in air pressure, a person’s ability to hear deteriorates. This is especially noticeable in an airplane when climbing or descending. ...the human body is affected by sounds that are not perceived by the ear with a frequency below 16 hertz - infrasounds. They can resonate with internal organs, greatly displacing and even deforming them. ...the merit of Julius Mayer in the discovery of the law of conservation of energy was the discovery of the difference in the colors of human venous blood in northern and southern countries. In the south, its bright scarlet color was determined by an excess of oxygen returned to the veins, which indicated less energy consumption by humans. ...during heavy physical work, a person loses up to 10 liters of sweat per day. If the body were not cooled by evaporation, it would heat up to $100 \infty C$! ...the electrical structure of the nerve fiber was guessed in principle by Luigi Galvani in the 17th century. However, for a long time there was no clarity about the carrier of information transmitted from the brain to the muscles, and only in the 20th century it was experimentally shown that these were electrical signals. ...Leonardo da Vinci made a huge contribution to the study of the mechanism of vision by anatomying the eye. He was able to identify its main parts, compare its operation with the action of optical instruments and discover the fact that two eyes see different images of bodies. …human vision is limited to a very narrow range of the electromagnetic spectrum. However, if our eye sensed, for example, infrared radiation, it would experience enormous interference - after all, the human body itself radiates more intensely in this area than most objects around us. ...the book by the outstanding theoretical physicist Erwin Schrödinger, published in 1944, “What is life from the point of view of a physicist?” served as an impetus for the assault on the problem of transmission of hereditary information. A few years later, the brilliant triumph of the interaction between physics and biology was the deciphering of the basic “living” molecule – DNA.
