human physics

Among the vast field of research at the intersection of biology and physics, the most important area stands out - human physics. After the previous issues of Kaleidoscope, which discussed the role of physics in the study of the animal and plant worlds, it’s time to turn to humans. After all, we are the same physical bodies as everyone around us, and mechanical, thermal, electrical and optical phenomena also reveal themselves in us. By getting to know ourselves, we find ourselves in the role of observers, researchers, and sometimes... measuring instruments. So physics is not only outside, but also inside of us! Of course, now you are faced with only a handful of simple, seemingly mundane questions and facts. However, we hope that they will give an idea of ​​​​the vast array of accumulated knowledge and unsolved problems hidden behind them in what is today called life science - and therefore about you and me.

Questions and tasks

1. Why is it that in a flying plane, looking out the window at the cloudless sky, we don’t experience the sensation of flying? 2. When swimming in a river with a muddy bottom, you may notice that your feet get stuck in the mud more in shallow places than in deep ones. How to explain this? 3. Why is the situation described in the epigraph by Baron Munchausen impossible?

4. Why do swimmers throw their outstretched arms forward when throwing themselves into the water? 5. Why is it that high in the mountains the action of a person’s joints is disrupted: the limbs do not “listen” well and are subject to dislocations? 6. Why, when we drink, does the water touching our lips rise up? 7. Normal human temperature is close to $37 \infty C$. Why are we not cold at air temperature $25 \infty C$ and very hot at $37 \infty C$? 8. Why can you warm your hands and cool the tea with almost the same movement of your lips when exhaling? 9. Why do children get colder than adults? 10. Why are we not cold when we come out of the river during the warm summer rain? 11. Why do fingers crack when stretching joints? 12. If you bring your hand to a charged paper sleeve suspended on a silk thread, the sleeve will begin to be attracted to your hand. Why? 13. The Christmas tree garland is soldered from light bulbs connected in series for a flashlight. When the garland is plugged in, each bulb receives a voltage of 3 volts. Why is it dangerous to stick your finger into the socket after unscrewing one of the light bulbs? 14. When they listen to a distant noise, they involuntarily open their mouth. For what?
