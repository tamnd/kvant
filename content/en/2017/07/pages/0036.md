OLYMPICS

Geometric Olympiad named after I.F. Sharygin

Igor Fedorovich Sharygin was an unsurpassed composer of geometric problems. His problems regularly appeared in «Kvant» and in “Mathematics at School”, and were offered at the International, All-Union and All-Russian, Moscow, Soros Olympiads and at a dozen other Olympiads of lower rank. He set very strict standards when selecting problems for any Olympiad. The task must be: a) new, containing fresh ideas; b) non-standard, breaking stereotypes; c) having a geometric rather than a “countable” solution; d) finally, the task should simply be beautiful! To do this, its author must have good geometric taste. Preference has always been given to problems that came from high science, rather than specially invented ones. After Igor Fedorovich left, his friends, colleagues and students had the idea of ​​organizing an Olympiad in his name, which (to the best of his ability) was supposed to meet these high standards. The Olympiad has been held annually since 2005 and contains only geometric problems. However, it is not “one-sided”, as skeptics feared. Topics of problems, in addition to classical geometry, include combinatorial geometry, extremal problems, real-life problems, problems with elements of mathematical analysis. Geometry turned out to be richer and more diverse than we were presented with.

Among the winners there are often children who have never before achieved great success at other general mathematics Olympiads. The form of the Olympiad also corresponds to its geometric orientation. The first round is a correspondence round, where schoolchildren are given several months to leisurely think through complex problems. A variant of a correspondence tour of 20–25 problems (for each problem the classes for which it is intended are indicated) is published at the beginning of the year on the Internet portal www.geometry.ru. Options are published in Russian and English. Solutions can be sent until the beginning of April. The winners of the correspondence round are invited to the final round, taking place in Dubna (Moscow region) at the end of July. The winners of oral geometry Olympiads, which are held in a number of Russian cities, are also invited to the final round. In total, about 100 schoolchildren in grades 8–10 from more than 10 countries take part in the final round. The final round takes place over two days. Every day participants solve 4 problems. The student must defend the solution to each problem himself, explaining it (orally) to the jury members. During the final round, popular lectures on geometry, cultural and sports programs are organized for children and their parents (or teachers). The jury of the Olympiad includes professional mathematicians, school teachers, students - winners and prize-winners of past Olympiads from Russia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Bulgaria, Iran and other countries. Conditions and solutions to the problems of the correspondence and final rounds can be found on the website www.geometry.ru. The materials of the first ten Olympiads were published in issues 113 and 134 of the Kvant Library.
