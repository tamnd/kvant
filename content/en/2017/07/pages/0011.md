Superconductivity:

history, modern ideas, recent successes

A. ABRIKOSOV

WHEN THE EXTERNAL FIELD DECREASES, the vortices diverge and in the field $B_{кр1}$ completely disappear from the superconductor. In fact, the field $B_{кр1}$ is the field at which one vortex can still exist in a superconductor. The region between fields $B_{кр1}$ and $B_{кр2}$ is called the mixed state. In this state, the superconductor is penetrated by vortex filaments - miniature solenoids arranged in the correct order. In cross section they form a triangular lattice. Each vortex has a core whose size is equal to the size of the Cooper pair; this core can be considered a region of normal metal. It turns out to be very significant that by increasing the concentration of defects, it is possible to increase the critical field Bcr2, up to which a superconducting current can flow through the sample. This makes it possible to use type II superconductors to create superconducting magnets. Indeed, in a superconducting ring the current can circulate forever. You can make a solenoid and short circuit it. This will be a superconducting permanent magnet. The difference from a conventional electromagnet is that in a conventional magnet, the current energy is ultimately spent on heating the winding. Because of this, it is necessary to create a bulky and expensive cooling system. It is this circumstance that limits the capabilities of electromagnets with fields up to 5–6 Tesla.
