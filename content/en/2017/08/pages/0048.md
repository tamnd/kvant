Olympics “Conquer the Sparrow Hills!”

PHYSICS

Olympics “Conquer the Sparrow Hills!” in physics in the 2016/17 academic year took place in two stages: qualifying (remotely)) and final (for grades 10–11 - full-time at Moscow State University and in the regions, for other grades - remotely). The qualifying round task consisted of a test part (only answers were checked) and a creative part (solutions were checked and evaluated). The winners and prize-winners of the qualifying stage, as well as the winners and prize-winners of last year’s Olympiad, were invited to the final stage. Below are examples of tasks from both rounds of the Olympiad for grades 10–11.

QUALIFICATION STAGE

Part I (test task) 1. (10 points) A uniform cube lies on a horizontal surface. To make it slide along the surface by pressing horizontally on the center of its side face (perpendicular to this face), you need to apply a force of at least 14 N. A second homogeneous cube is placed at some distance from it. The mass of this cube is 2 times greater than that of the first, and the coefficient of friction on the surface is exactly the same. The faces of the cubes facing each other are parallel and perpendicular to the line $O_{1} O_{2}$ connecting their centers. A weightless spring was inserted between the cubes, the axis of which coincides with $O_{1} O_{2}$. The spring is not initially deformed (its length is exactly equal to the distance between the cubes). With what minimum constant force must one press on the center of the side face of the first cube in the direction of the second (compressing the spring) so that as a result of this impact the second cube moves? The spring does not bend, the cubes do not come off the surface. Write your answer in newtons, rounding to the nearest whole number if necessary. 2. (7 points) Two vessels with volumes $V_{1} =$
