Simpler mathematical considerations—the fact that the laws of nature are described by differential equations—allow us to understand some of the seemingly paradoxical phenomena in our lives. For several decades, the state of our economy has caused alarm among experts: militarization, monopoly and general incompetence of management have led to the fact that the second derivative has become persistently negative (the pace of development has begun to systematically slow down). This did not frighten those who do not understand mathematics too much, since the first derivative was still positive (welfare grew). But mathematicians know that a constantly negative derivative (even of a high order) will eventually lead to a negative first derivative, i.e. to a decline in production and the welfare of society, and this process of deterioration, when it becomes noticeable, will accelerate. Due to the inertia of the system, it is no longer possible to instantly change the position at such a time by any means, since any kind of change affects only the sign of the highest derivative (for our perestroika - the third or even fourth). Thus, the observed economic degradation is caused not so much by incorrect new decisions, but by long-standing mistakes made during the growth of production.

⟦column⟧

Unfortunately, these elementary mathematical facts are very difficult to explain to the robbed people, who are inclined to attribute all the deterioration to reforms. Any reforms, of course, must lead to deterioration, even if they are the most correct. Plans were usually drawn up to optimize production for 20 years in advance (“enough for our century”). It is clear to a mathematician that optimal planning of this kind must lead to the complete destruction of all resources by the end of the period (otherwise the remaining resources could be used and, therefore, the plan would not be optimal). Fortunately, these plans were “corrected” and were not implemented. But nevertheless, the main trends were maintained, and by the beginning of perestroika, roughly speaking, we ate everything we had. Attempts to draw up detailed “programs” for economic reform day by day are similar to attempts to plan the entire economy and are similar to trying to draw up a program for a car driver who must get from Moscow to St. Petersburg (at such and such a minute turn right, at such and such a minute turn left). Success can only be achieved through feedback, i.e. what is needed is not a program (trajectory), but, in mathematical terms, a vector field in the state space of the system - a mechanism for making decisions depending on the actual state achieved, and not on the calendar date. Some of the above should be kept in mind when reforming the education system. Our examples show that “there is nothing more practical than a good theory.” It is necessary that educators do not chase after immediate practical needs, but always see the long-term goals of society.
