PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2476 was offered at the VIII International Olympiad Romanian Master in Mathematics, and problem M2477 at the XXIV International Olympiad “Tuymaada”. The author of problems F2481–F2484 is D. Aleksandrov.

Problem M2474–M2477, F2481–F2484

M2474. The natural numbers x, y, z are such that x y y z z x x y z. Prove that they are the lengths of the sides of some triangle. V. Senderov

M2475. Given a square ABCD. The variable square KLMN is arranged so that K lies on the segment AD, B lies on the segment LM, and C lies on the segment MN (see figure). Find the locus of the points T of intersection of lines AL and DN. V. Rastorguev

M2476. The graphs $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ of the quadratic functions $f_{1} x p_{1} x^{2} q_{1} x r_{1}$ and $f_{2} x p_{2} x^{2} q_{2} x r_{2}$, and $p_{1} 0 p_{2}$, are plotted on the coordinate plane. The graphs $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ intersect at two different points A and B. The four tangents to $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ drawn at points A and B form a convex quadrilateral into which a circle can be inscribed. Prove that graphs $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ have a common axis of symmetry. A. Zaslavsky

M2477. There are 25 masks, each in its own color. k wise men play a game: they are shown all the masks, then they agree among themselves, after which they are put on masks in such a way that each of them sees the masks of everyone else (but does not know who is wearing them) and does not see his own. No forms of interaction are allowed in this case. All sages simultaneously name one color, trying to guess the color of their mask. At what minimum k can they agree in advance so that at least one of them is sure to guess right? S. Berlov

F2481. A stone is thrown with a speed of $v_{0} = 17$ m s at an angle of $A = 60 \infty$ to the horizontal. At what angular velocity does the velocity vector of the stone rotate t = 1 s after the throw? Take g = 10 m $с^{2}$, ignore air resistance. Express the result in radians per second and round to the nearest tenth.
