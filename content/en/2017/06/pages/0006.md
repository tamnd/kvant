When, in the 70s and 80s, Aleksey Alekseevich appeared at a meeting of the Department of Theoretical Physics on the seventh floor of the main building of MISiS, he quickly dealt with current affairs and the most interesting thing began - A.A. started telling stories. He was a brilliant storyteller who you could listen to for hours. It seemed that the world of big physics was opening up behind the dusty window, behind which Leninsky Prospekt had just rustled. Conversations about physics were an integral part of our communication with A.A. and in the hall of the Institute of Physical Problems, where he traditionally made appointments for his students, and with skis in hand in the queue for the ski lift in Bakuriani, where winter symposiums on low-temperature physics were held, and on the beach during Odessa conferences on theoretical physics. From these conversations, from the reports listened to, new tasks were born. Alexey Alekseevich was always extremely attentive to experiments and had an amazing instinct for new phenomena. Thus, after the report of the French experimenter Denis Jerome on the amazing properties of newly synthesized organic superconductors, A.A. advised me to study the nature of the pseudogap observed in them. This served as the basis for a series of works that were later useful in studying the properties of high-temperature superconductors. After the report on Bakurian-

And again, everything around us - gray military-style furniture from the years of the atomic project, walls illuminated by neon lamps - went into the distance: thanks to the power of talent and eloquence of Alexei Alekseevich, we found ourselves in the world of vortices named after him and Cooper pairs, resonant tunneling of electrons and van Hove’s peculiarities, in the world in which he lived and was happy.

We are reprinting an article by A.A. Abrikosov, published in the magazine «Kvant» almost 30 years ago, in 1988. This date is remarkable for two reasons: firstly, the publication was dedicated to the sixtieth birthday of Alexei Alekseevich, and secondly, at about the same time, a new era in superconductivity research began - the era of high-temperature superconductors. The article presents an excellent overview of the history of the study of superconductivity, written by one of the direct creators of this science. At the end of the article, the author shares his vision of the newly discovered phenomenon of high-temperature superconductivity. He perfectly summarizes the events of the first, most important year of these studies. Over the next 29 years, thousands of new superconducting substances were discovered in hundreds of laboratories around the world, and the critical temperature was raised to 165 K. However, a single generally accepted theory of the phenomenon of superconductivity does not exist today.
