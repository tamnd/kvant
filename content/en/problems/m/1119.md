## Condition

Let us define a $k$-star as a figure on the plane, consisting of $k$ rays with a common origin, dividing the plane into $k$ equal angles (along $360^\circ/k$). For every $k>2$ the following statement is true: for any $k$ points of the plane in general position (three of which do not lie on the same line) there exists a $k$-star, each of the $k$ angles of which contains exactly one of these $k$ points?

M. Khovanov, 10th grade student, Moscow

For k=4, consider two cases. If the points lie at the vertices of a convex quadrilateral, then the center of the 4-star can be taken as the point of intersection of its diagonals, and the rays can be directed along the bisectors of the angles between them (Fig. 2). If one of the points D lies inside the triangle ABC with vertices in the other three and ∠ADB>90°, then as O we can choose the point of intersection of the segment DC and a certain semicircle, the diameter of which A₁B₁ lies entirely on the segment AB, and the rays can be directed along straight lines OA₁ and OB₁ (Fig. 3).

To prove that for k=5 (and k>5) the statement is false, it is enough to give an example; 5 points lying on one arc of value α<72° are suitable as such. In fact, if the arc α intersects four points of the rays of a 5-star with center O, then α>72°. (since the half-sum of arcs AB′ and A′B on figure 4 is equal to ∠AOB′=36°.
