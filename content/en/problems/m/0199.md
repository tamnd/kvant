## Condition

a) Prove that the sum
$$C_n^0 - C_{n-1}^1 \frac{1}{4} + C_{n-2}^2 \frac{1}{4^2} - \cdots + (-1)^i C_{n-i}^i \frac{1}{4^i} + \cdots$$
(the sum is taken over all integers i, $0 \leq i \leq \frac{n}{2}$) is equal to $\frac{n+1}{2^n}$.

6)* Prove that if p and q are different numbers and p+q=1, then the sum
$$C_n^0 - C_{n-1}^1 pq + C_{n-2}^2 p^2 q^2 - \cdots + (-1)^i C_{n-i}^i p^i q^i + \cdots,$$

## Solution

a) Prove that the sum**
$$C_n^0 - C_{n-1}^1 \frac{1}{4} + C_{n-2}^2 \frac{1}{4^2} - \dots +$$
$$+ (-1)^i C_{n-i}^1 \frac{1}{4^i} + \dots,$$
(the sum is taken over all goals i, 0 ≤ i ≤ $\frac{n}{2}$) is equal to $\frac{n+1}{2^n}$.

b)* Prove that if p and q are different numbers u p + q = 1, then the sum
$$C_n^0 - C_{n-1}^1 pq + C_{n-2}^2 p^2 q^2 - \dots +$$
$$+ (-1)^i C_{n-i}^1 p^i q^i + \dots,$$

Fig. 9

⟦folio 35⟧

$$\frac{p^{k+1}(1-q) - q^{k+1}(1-p)}{p-q} = \frac{p^{k+2} - q^{k+2}}{p-q}$$

(recall that $p+q=1$).

It remains to check the base of induction. For $n=0$ $S_n=1$ and $\frac{p-q}{p-q}=1$ – the formula is correct.

When $n=1$ $S_n=1$ and $\frac{p^2-q^2}{p-q}=p+q=1$ the formula is correct again.

Thus, both the induction step and the induction base are verified. Therefore, the formula is valid for all $n$.

Comment. In our solution, we used the fact that $C_n^k + C_n^{k+1} = C_{n+1}^k$ Deduce this correspondence yourself from the formula for $C_n^k$ given in the problem statement.

L. G. Limanov
