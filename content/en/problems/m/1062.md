## Condition

a) Points E and D are taken on sides AB and AC of triangle ABC. Lines BD and CE intersect at point M, AM and BC at point P, AM and DE at point N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NA} = 2 \cdot \frac{PM}{MA}.$$

6) Points D, E and F are taken on the edges SA, SB and SC of the terrahedron SABC. Planes ABF, BCD and CAE intersect at point M, line SM intersects planes ABC and DEF at points P and N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NS} = 3 \cdot \frac{PM}{MS}.$$

T. A. Dzhortmenadze, E. Ya. Gleibman

## Solution

a) Points E and D are taken on sides AB and AC of triangle ABC. Lines BD and CE intersect at point M, AM and BC at point P, AM and DE at point N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NA} = 2 \cdot \frac{PM}{MA}.$$
b) Points D, E and F are taken on the edges SA, SB and SC of triangle SABC. Planes ABF, BCD and CAE intersect at point M, line SM intersects planes ABC and DEF at points P and N. Prove that

$$\frac{PN}{NS} = 3 \cdot \frac{PM}{MS}.$$

a) Let $DE \parallel BC$ (Fig. 1) first. Let's put $AN=x$, $NM=y$, $MP=z$, then

$$\frac{PN}{NA} : \frac{PM}{MA} = \frac{z+y}{x} \cdot \frac{x+y}{z} =$$

$$= \frac{xz+y(x+y+z)}{xz} = 1 + \frac{y}{z} \cdot \frac{x+y+z}{x} = 2,$$

(*)

since from the readiness of triangles MBC and MDE, ABC and ADE it follows that

$$\frac{y}{z} = \frac{MN}{MP} = \frac{DE}{BC} = \frac{AN}{AP} = \frac{x}{x+y+z}.$$

General case using central projection. Let us assume that the lines DE and BC intersect at point K. Let us draw an arbitrary plane α through AP, which does not coincide with ABC, and choose a point O so that the straight line OK is parallel to α (Fig. 2). With a central projection from the center O onto the plane α, all points of the line AP project onto themselves (and the relations $PN/NA$ and $PM/MA$ are preserved), and the lines DE and BC, very/no, project into parallel ones - as they say, the point K “goes to infinity”. As a result, from an arbitrary configuration that satisfies the conditions of the problem, we obtain figure 1. This problem relates to projective geometry, in which the properties of figures that are preserved under central projections are studied. An example of a concept from this area of ​​​​geometry is the double ratio of arbitrary four points P, A, N, M of one straight line, equal, by definition, to the value on the left side (*). Try to prove that it really does not change with central projections.

You can also solve the problem using the center of mass (on the application of this concept in geometry, see the article by V. G. Boltyansky and M. B. Balk “The center of gravity will facilitate solutions” in «Kvant» No. 4, 1984). Let us place a certain mass m at vertex A, and such masses m_b and m_c at vertices B and C, so that the center of the three masses falls at point M. To do this, we need to take $m_b=(AE/BE)m$, $m_c=(AD/BD)m$. (Indeed, the center of mass of m and m_b is point E, so the common center lies on the segment CE; similarly it is shown that it lies on BD.) The center of mass of m_b and m_c lies at the intersection of AM and BC, i.e. at point P, and $PM/MA=m(m_b+m_c)$ (Fig. 3, a). Now let's add another mass m to point A. The general center of mass will shift from M to some point on the segment MA. At the same time, grouping one mass m with m_c, and the other with m_b (Fig. 3, b), we find that it must lie on DE, i.e., at point N. Finally, grouping m_c and m_b, we find that $PN/NA=2m(m_b+m_c)=2PM/MA$.

$$\bar{P} = x\bar{A} + y\bar{B} + z\bar{C}.$$

$$x + y + z = 1.$$

The real vector $\bar{AP}$ lies in the $ABC$ plane, so for some $y_1$ and $z_1$ $\bar{AP} = y_1\bar{AB} + z_1\bar{AC} = y_1(\bar{B} - \bar{A}) + z_1(\bar{C} - \bar{A})$, hence $\bar{P} = \bar{A} + \bar{AP} = (1 - y_1 - z_1) \times \bar{A} + y_1\bar{B} + z_1\bar{C}$. And since the coefficients in (1) are determined uniquely, $y_1 = y$, $z_1 = z$, $x = 1 - y - z$. Thus, equality (2) is a necessary (as well as sufficient) condition for the fact that the point $P$ lies in the plane $ABC$.

Let us write this condition for the point $N$ in the plane $DEF$ and for the point $M$, which lies in three planes - $ABF$, $BCD$ and $CAE$. From the expansion of $n\bar{N} = \bar{P} = x\alpha\bar{D} + y\beta\bar{E} + z\gamma\bar{F}$ we obtain that $x\alpha/n + y\beta/n + z\gamma/n = 1$, i.e.

$$x\alpha + y\beta + z\gamma = n;$$

(3)

from the expansion $m\bar{M} = \bar{P} = x\bar{A} + y\bar{B} + z\gamma\bar{F}$ - what

$$x + y + z = m.$$

And, similarly, two more equalities:

$$\alpha x + y + z = m,$$

$$x + \beta y + z = m.$$

Adding the last three equalities and taking into account (2) and (3), we find that

$$n + 2 = 3m.$$

It remains to express the relations of the segments in the specification conditions through $m$ and $n$:

$$\frac{PN}{NS} : \frac{PM}{MS} = \frac{PS - NS}{NS} : \frac{PS - MS}{MS} = \frac{n - 1}{m - 1} = \frac{3m - 3}{m - 1} = 3.$$
