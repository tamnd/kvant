## Condition

Two people play this game: the first one names a natural number from 2 to 9; the second multiplies this number by an arbitrary natural number from 2 to 9; then the first one multiplies the result by any natural number from 2 to 9, etc.; The winner is the one who first produces a product greater than a) thousand, 6) million. Who wins when played correctly - the beginner or his partner?

V. Boltyansky

## Solution

Two people answer this question: the first one names a natural number from 2 to 9; the second multiplies this number by an arbitrary natural number from 2 to 9, etc.; the one who first produces a product of large a) thousand wins; b) million. a) Problem solved by analysis from the end (Fig. 1). If eventually one of the players $I$ offers the other player - call him $B$ - any of the numbers from 112 to 999, then player $B$ wins (by multiplying by 9). Therefore, if $B$ offers his partner $I$ any of the numbers from 56 to 111, then $I$ will get at least 112 (he will then multiply by only 2) and no more than 999 (he will then multiply by 9), and player $B$ will be guaranteed a win. Now it is clear that if $I$ names any of the numbers from 7 to 55, then $B$ can fall into the interval that is favorable to him [56; 111]. Finally, so that $I$ must fall into this fatal interval for him [7; 55], player $B$ just needs to name any of the numbers 4, 5, 6 at the first step. So, the beginner wins with the correct number.

The first inequality holds only for a finite number of numbers $m$, and the second inequality is reduced to the form

$$s \leq \frac{d(a^2-1)+n-1}{2^n}.$$

Since the right part of this inequality tends to 0 for $n\rightarrow\infty$, it can only hold for a finite set of pairs $(n;s)$ for any fixed $a$.
