## Condition

A climber wants to climb a rock 1000 m high. After camping at the foot of the rock, he can climb, using a rope, at a speed of 40 meters per hour, and after a cold rope on the rock - 30 meters per hour. He climbs the finished rope at a speed of 400 meters per hour. In how many days can he reach the top if he works on the rock (including climbing the rope) 6 hours a day? (The time of descent and other operations is pending.)

## Solution

Allupinst wants to climb a cliff 1000 m high. After a new climb in a ladder at the foot of the cliff, he can climb, sewing a rope, at a speed of 40 meters per hour, and after a cold night on the rock - 30 meters per hour. He climbs the finished rope at a speed of 400 meters per hour. In how many days can he reach the top if he works on the rock (including climbing the rope) for six hours a day? Problem solutions

M1021 — M1025; F1033 — F1037

Answer: in five days.

Let $x_n$ denote the highest altitude to which a climber can climb in $n$ days ($n=1, 2, \dots$). If by the end of one day he reaches a height of $h$ meters, the next day he can rise to a height of $h+6\cdot 30=h+180$ meters if he remains insensible on the rock, and to a height of $h+\left(6-\frac{h}{400}\right)40=h+240-h/10$ meters if he goes down insensible. It is clear that the first option is more profitable if $180>240-h/10$, that is, $h>600$, the second - if $h\leq 600$. Thus,

$$x_{n+1}=\begin{cases} x_n+240-x_n/10, & x_n\leq 600, \\ x_n+180, & x_n\geq 600. \end{cases}$$

Starting with $x_0=0$, one can define $x_n$ successively for all $n$. Since $x_4<1000$ and $x_5>1000$, the climber will be able to reach the top of the cliff by the end of the 5th day; To do this, he must spend the first two nights below, and the last two on the rock.
