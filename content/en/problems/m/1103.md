## Condition

a) On an infinite plane divided into square cells, there is a certain - perhaps infinite - number of rectangles of size 1x2 closed in black so that no two black rectangles have common points (hereinafter vertices). Prove that the rest of the plane can be replaced by these rectangles.

6)* Let there be several closed rectangles of size m×n on the checkered plane that have no common points. Prove that if mn is even, then the remaining part of the plane can be replaced by rectangles of size 1x2, but if mn is odd, then this is not always possible.

M. Khovanov, 10th grade student

## Solution

a) On an infinite plane divided into square cells, a certain - perhaps infinite - number of rectangles of size $1\times 2$ are painted black so that no two black rectangles have common points (hereinafter vertices). Prove that the rest of the plane can be tiled with these rectangles. b)* Let several rectangles be shaded on the checkered plane

Let us cover the original (blue) checkered grid with a larger (black) grid, dividing the plane into squares $2\times 2$ cells. Each black domino $1\times 2$ either fits entirely into one square $2\times 2$ of the new grid, or into two adjacent ones forming a rectangle $2\times 4$, and other black dominoes do not enter this section of the black grid. In both cases, the rest of the area is easy to cover with dominoes (at figure 1 these dominoes are circled with pink lines); let cells $2\times 2$ split into two dominoes. This implies the statement of problem a).

Similarly, using a grid of squares $2\times 2$ one can prove statement 6) for rectangles $m\times n$ of an even number of mn cells.
