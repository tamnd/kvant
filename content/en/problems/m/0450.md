## Condition

The rectangular system of $n$ floors (Fig. 2) is constructed as follows. Starting from the lower rectangle forming the first floor, the upper side of each rectangle is divided in the ratio 1: 2: 3; On the three resulting segments, rectangles of the same height as the original ones are built as bases, and so on until the top floor. From the resulting set of rectangles, a certain subset is selected, consisting of pairwise non-congruent rectangles (one such subset on figure is red). Prove that there is a vertical line intersecting at most two of the chosen rectangles.

A. Klentsyn

## Solution

A system of rectangles consisting of n elements (Fig. 1) is constructed as follows. Starting from the lower rectangle forming the first edge, the upper side of each rectangle is divided in the ratio 1: 2: 3; on the three resulting segments, as on the basis of a strict rectangle, the same can be drawn as the original one, and so on - up to the very top edge. From the resulting set of rectangles, a certain subset is selected, consisting of pairwise non-congruent rectangles (one subset on figure is red). Prove that we can find a vertical line intersecting at most two of the selected rectangles.

Let us estimate the total area of ​​pairwise noncontinuous rectangles. The area of ​​each of them is equal to $S/3k^2m$, where $S$ is the area of ​​the lower rectangle, $k$ and $m$ are integers, $k \geq 0, m \geq 0, k + m \leq n$. The sum of different x numbers of the form $1/3k^2m$ does not exceed

$$1 + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3^2} + \dots + \frac{1}{3^n} +$$
