## Condition

Two parabolas are located on a plane so that their axes are mutually perpendicular and the parabolas intersect at four points. Prove that these four points lie on the same circle.
L. P. Kuptsov

## Solution

Two parabolas are located on a plane so that their axes are mutually perpendicular and the parabolas intersect at four points. Prove that these four points lie on the same circle.

Let us choose a rectangular coordinate system $Oxy$ so that the axes of the parabolas are coordinate axes (see figure). Then the equation of one of the parabolas is $x=ay^2+b$ ($a>0, b<0$), the other is $y=cx^2+d$ ($c>0, d<0$). the coordinates $(x;y)$ of the points of intersection of the parabolas satisfy the system of equation $x=ay^2+b, y=cx^2+d$. Dividing the first equation by $a$, the second by $c$ and adding the resulting equations, after further transformations we arrive at the equation
$$\left(x-\frac{1}{2a}\right)^2+\left(y-\frac{1}{2c}\right)^2=\frac{1}{4a^2}+\frac{1}{4b^2}-\frac{b}{a}-\frac{d}{c}.$$
Since its right-hand side is positive, it follows that the points of intersection of the parabolas lie on a circle of radius $R=\sqrt{\frac{1}{4a^2}+\frac{1}{4b^2}-\frac{b}{a}-\frac{d}{c}}$ centered at point $Z(1/2a; 1/2c)$.

L. P. Kuptsov
