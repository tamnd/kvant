## Condition

Prove that there exists an infinite bounded sequence $\{x_n\}$ such that for any distinct $m$ and $k$ the following inequality holds:
$$|x_m - x_k| \geq |m - k|^{-1}.$$ (9, 10 grades)

S. Konyazin

## Solution

Prove that there exists a maximal operational sequence $(x_n)$ such that for any distinct $m$ and $k$ the inequality holds

$$|x_m - x_k| \geq |m - k|^{-1}.$$

We will present two solutions to the problem, leaving the reader to judge the advantages and disadvantages of each of them.

Note that it is sufficient to construct a bounded sequence $(x_n)$ satisfying (1) for $\varepsilon = 1/10$. In this case, it is necessary that the members of the sequence, the numbers of which differ by 1, differ by at least $1/10$, the members by -

1) the cipher of the digit 0 is the digit 0;
2) any digit can be uniquely reconstructed (“deciphered”) by its digit;
3) if the digits of two digits are adjacent, m. e. differ by 1 or 9, then the numbers themselves are not adjacent.

Let us denote the encrypted sequence by $(y_n)$. Let us prove that the sequence $(y_n)$ is such that for any distinct $m$ and $k$ the inequality

$$|y_m - y_k| \cdot |m - k| \geq 1/100.$$

Let $m > k$, $m = \overline{a_l \dots a_1}$, $k = \overline{b_l \dots b_1}$ (we can always assume that $k$ has the same number of digits as $m$, adding, if necessary, the number $k$ on the left with a certain number of zeros; in this case, according to 1) the number $y_k$ will be filled with zeros on the right).

Let $y_m = 0$, $\overline{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_{s-1} \alpha_s \dots \alpha_l}$, $y_k = 0$, $\beta_1 \dots \beta_{s-1} \beta_s \dots \beta_l$,

and $y_m - y_k = 0$, $0 \dots 0$? ...?

(We believe that $y_m > y_k$; the asterisk * indicates the first significant digit, signs? - whatever numbers you like.) Then $|y_m - y_k| \geq 10^{-s}$. Let us now compare the numbers $m$ and $s$. To do this, compare the numbers $\overline{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_{s-1}}$ and $\overline{\beta_1 \dots \beta_{s-1}}$. Since the expansion of the number $y_m - y_k$ has a zero after the decimal point $s-1$, these two numbers are either the same or differ by 1.

With ray 1. If $\overline{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_{s-1}} = \overline{\beta_1 \dots \beta_{s-1}}$, then $\alpha_s \neq \beta_s$; This means $a_s \neq \beta_s$, whence $|m - k| \geq 10^{s-1}$, since when we subtract $k$ from $m$ we get a non-zero number ending in $s-1$ zero.

With ray 2. If $\overline{\alpha_1 \dots \alpha_{s-1}} = \overline{\beta_1 \dots \beta_{s-1}} + 1$, then the numbers $\alpha_{s-1}$ and $\beta_{s-1}$ are adjacent. Then, by property 3), the digits $a_{s-1}$ and $b_{s-1}$ are not adjacent, which means that in the decimal expansion of the number $m-k$ the digit in the digit $10^{s-2}$ is not equal to zero, hence $|m - k| \geq 10^{s-2}$.

In both cases $|y_m - y_k| \cdot |m - k| \geq 10^{-2}$, which is what needed to be proven.

Second solution. Let us put $x_n = n\sqrt{2} - a_n$, where $a_n$ is the largest integer, does not exceed $n\sqrt{2}$. It is clear that $0 < x_n < 1$. Let us show that for any $m > k > 1$

$$|x_m - x_k| \cdot (m - k) > 1/4,$$

that is, that the sequence $(4x_n)$ is excluding.

Let us evaluate the strength of the quantity

$$|x_m - x_k| = |(m - k)\sqrt{2} - a_m - a_k|$$

$$= \frac{|2(m - k)^2 - (a_m - a_k)|}{|(m - k)\sqrt{2} + (a_m - a_k)|}.$$

The numerator is an integer other than zero (the number $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational), so it is not less than 1. The denominator is easy to estimate, using the fact that $a_m < m\sqrt{2} + a_k > k\sqrt{2} - 1$:

$$(m - k)\sqrt{2} + (a_m - a_k) < 2(m - k)\sqrt{2} + 1 < (m - k)(2\sqrt{2} + 1) < 4(m - k).$$

From here we get $|x_m - x_k| > \frac{1}{4(m - k)}$.
