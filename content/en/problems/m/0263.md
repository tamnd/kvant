## Condition

Given two numbers $p$ and $q$, greater than 1. On the sides $BC$ and $DC$ of the rectangular column $ABCD$ the points $P$ and $Q$ are taken so that $|BC| = p \cdot |BP|$ and $|DC| = q \cdot |DQ|$. At what ratio of the length of sides $AB$ and $AD$ will the angle $PAQ$ have the greatest magnitude? What is this largest value in the special case $p = 2$, $q = \frac{3}{2}$ (Fig. 2)?

E. G. Gotman

## Solution

** Given two numbers $p$ and $q$, greater than 1. On the sides $BC$ and $DC$ of the rectangle $ABCD$ the points $P$ and $Q$ are taken so that $|BC| = p\cdot|BP|$ and $|DC| = q\cdot|DQ|$. At what ratio of the lengths of the sides $AB$ and $AD$ will the angle $PAQ$ have the greatest value? What is this largest value in the special case $p = 2$, $q = \dfrac{3}{2}$?

Following the letter from *Boris and Lev Rabinovich*, who proposed this problem, let us immediately consider a board of size $n\times n$ and prove that it is impossible to arrange more than $\dfrac{4n}{3}$ rooks on it as required in the condition.

Let $k$ rooks be located on the board $n\times n$ subject to the condition. On each square where the rook is located, we will write the number 0. In each of the $n$ columns we will perform the following operation: if there are two numbers in the column, then we will add 1 to both, if one is a number, then we will add 2 to it (we will not write anything in the empty column). Then we will do exactly the same operation with each line. It is clear that in place of each of the $k$ rooks the result will be written a number not less than 3 - namely, either 3 or 4 - therefore the sum $S$ of all written numbers is not less than $3k$; on the other hand, since we added at most 2 to each of the $n$ columns and then to each of the $n$ rows, the sum of $S$ is at most $4n$. So, $3k \leqslant S \leqslant 4n$, where $k \leqslant \dfrac{4n}{3}$ comes from.

In particular, for $n = 8$ we obtain $k < \dfrac{32}{3}$, that is, $k \leqslant 10$. An example of a 10 rook arrangement that satisfies the condition is shown in figure 8.

It is easy to check that for any natural number $n$ in the same way it is possible to place $\left[\dfrac{4n}{3}\right]$ rooks on the board $n\times n$ subject to the condition ($[x]$ means the integer part of the number $x$).

Let's move on to the queen placement problem. It is clear that, subject to the conditions of the problem, one can place no more queens than rooks. figure 9 shows that 10 queens can be placed on board $8\times 8$. So, in both problems a) and b) the answer is the same: 10.

As for the generalization of the Rabinovich problem on the placement of queens for the board $n\times n$, none of the readers completely solved it. We can verify that for $n = 3, 4, 5$ the largest number of queens that can be placed on the board $n\times n$ so that each gets hit by at most one blow is one less than $\left[\dfrac{4n}{3}\right]$. But for large $n$ the solution of the problem apparently requires either a very large search of options or the use of additional considerations.

*N. B. Vasiliev*

Let us denote the lengths of the sides of the rectangle $AB$ and $AD$ by $a$ and $b$, respectively. Then

$$\mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle PAQ) = \frac{\mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle PAD) - \mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle QAD)}{1 + \mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle PAD)\cdot \mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle QAD)} =$$

$$= \frac{\dfrac{ap}{b} - \dfrac{a}{qb}}{1 + \dfrac{a^2p}{b^2q}} = \frac{ab\,(pq - 1)}{a^2p + b^2q}.$$

By virtue of the arithmetic mean and geometric mean theorem, we have

$$a^2p + b^2q \geqslant 2\sqrt{a^2pb^2q} = 2ab\sqrt{pq}$$

(the equal sign occurs at $a^2p = b^2q$, that is, when

$$\frac{a}{b} = \sqrt{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg).$$

That's why

$$\mathrm{tg}\,(\sphericalangle PAQ) = \frac{ab\,(pq-1)}{a^2 p + b^2 q} \leqslant \frac{pq-1}{2\sqrt{pq}}\,,$$

and $\sphericalangle PAQ$ reaches a maximum $\mathrm{arctg}\,\dfrac{pq-1}{2\sqrt{pq}}$ when

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{|AB|}{|AD|} = \sqrt{\frac{q}{p}}$$

($\sphericalangle PAQ$ is less than $90^\circ$, and the tangent in the first quarter is an increasing function).

In particular, if $p=2$, $q=\dfrac{3}{2}$, the largest value of the angle $PAQ$ is $\mathrm{arctg}\,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
