## Condition

Find the largest natural number, the tenth of which each digit (except for the outer ones) is strictly less than the two digits adjacent to it.

S. E. Rukshin

## Solution

Find the most natural number in whose decimal notation each digit (except the outer ones) is strictly less than its two adjacent digits.

Answer: 96433469. Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be consecutive digits of the number $N$ that satisfies the conditions of the problem. Let's consider the differences between neighboring numbers $d_i=a_{i+1}-a_i$, $i=1, \dots, n-1$. Condition $a_i<(a_{i-1}+a_{i+1})/2$ can be rewritten as $a_i-a_{i-1}<a_{i+1}-a_i$, or $d_{i-1}≤d_i-1$. Let $a_m$ be the smallest digit of $N$. We can assume that $a_{m-1}>a_m$, $a_m≤a_{m+1}$, i.e. $d_{m-1}≤-1$, $d_m≥0$. Then $d_{m-2}≤d_{m-1}-1≤-2$, $d_{m-3}≤-3,..., a d_{m+1}≥d_m+1≥1$, $d_{m+2}≥2$, $d_{m+3}≥3,...$.

Let us prove that $n≤m+4$. Let's say that this is not the case, then $a_{m+5}=a_{m+4}+d_{m+4}≥a_{m+4}+4≥a_{m+3}+3+4≥...≥a_{m+1}+1+2+3+4≥10$, but $a_{m+5}≤9$. Similarly, it is proved that $m≤4$ (otherwise $a_{m-4}≥a_{m-3}-a_{m-4}≥a_{m-3}+4≥...≥a_m+10$). So, $n≤8$, and if $n=8$, then $m=4$. Substituting $m=4$ into estimates
