## Condition

In a 4x4 square table the numbers 1, 2, 3,..., are arranged.

Fig. 1.
40

16 so that the sum of the four numbers in each row, in each column and on each of the two diagonals is equal to the same number, with 1 and 16 standing in opposite corners of the table. Prove that in this “magic square” the sum of any two numbers located symmetrically about the center of the square is the same.

L. G. Limanov

## Solution

In the square table $4 \times 4$ of distances the numbers are 1, 2, 3, ..., 16 max, that the sum of the numbers in all rows in each row, in each column and on each of the two diagonals is equal to the same number, with 1 and 16 standing in opposite corners of the table. Let us prove that in this quadratic the sum of any two numbers located symmetrically about the center is equal to 17. For numbers 1 and 16 this is true by condition. Let's assume that 1 is in the upper left corner, and 16 is in the lower right corner, then the other two numbers on the same diagonal - let's denote them by $a$ and $a'$ - satisfy the equality $a + a' = 17$. Let us always agree to denote the additional number $x$ up to 17 by $x' : x' = 17 - x$.

Let us now use the notation given on figure 3 ($S_i$ denote the sums of numbers in the corresponding cells).

The following equalities are true:
$$S_1 + c + d + a + a' + S_2 + S_3 + c + d + a + a' + S_4 + 2(b + c + d + e) = 34 \cdot 6$$
