## Condition

In the sequence of triplets of integers (2, 3, 5) (6, 15, 10)... each triple is obtained from the previous one in this way: the first is multiplied by the second, the second by the third, and the third by the first, and the resulting products give a new triple.

Prove that none of the numbers obtained in this way will be powers of an integer: square, cube, etc.

F. A. Bartenev

## Solution

** *In the sequence of triplets of integers (2, 3, 5) (6, 15, 10) . . . each triple is obtained from the previous one in this way: the first number is multiplied by the second, the second by the third, and the third by the first, and the resulting products give a new triple.*

*Prove that none of the numbers obtained in this way will be a power of an integer: square, cube, etc.*

Note that the order of the numbers within the triple is not significant. The numbers included in the next three do not depend on it.

Let us prove that a triple with number $2l + 1$ has the form $\{2^k{}^+{}^13^k5^k,\ 2^k3^k{}^+{}^15^k,\ 2^k3^k5^k{}^+{}^1\}$, and with number $2l$ - $\{2^m{}^-{}^13^m5^m,\ 2^m3^m{}^-{}^15^m,\ 2^m3^m5^m{}^-{}^1\}$, where $k$ and $m$ depend on $l$. Let's use mathematical induction for this. Suppose that the triples numbered $2l$ and $2l + 1$ have this form. It is easy to see that from the triple $\{a,\ b,\ c\}$ after two steps we get the triple $\{a^2bc,\ ab^2c,\ abc^2\}$. Substituting here instead of $a$, $b$ and $c$ the elements of the $2l$th and $2l + 1$th triples, we get

$$\{2^{4m-2}3^{4m-1}5^{4m-1},\ 2^{4m-1}3^{4m-2}5^{4m-1},\ 2^{4m-1}3^{4m-1}5^{4m-2}\}$$

and $\{2^{4k}{}^+{}^23^{4k}{}^+{}^15^{4k}{}^+{}^1,\ 2^{4k}{}^+{}^13^{4k}{}^+{}^25^{4k}{}^+{}^1,\ 2^{4k}{}^+{}^13^{4k}{}^+{}^15^{4k}{}^+{}^2\}$

respectively, that is, triples of the same type. Since the first and second triples satisfy the induction hypothesis, all triples satisfy it.

So, it has been proven that all numbers included in triplets have the form $p_1^{n\pm1} \cdot p_2^n \cdot p_3^n$, where $p_1$, $p_2$ and $p_3$ are the numbers 2, 3 and 5, taken in any sequence. Obviously, none of them can be a power of a natural number.

*AND. N. Klumova*
