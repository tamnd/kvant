## Condition

The circle touches the inner circle of the equilateral triangle $ABC$, as well as the equal sides $AB$ and $AC$ of this triangle at points $P$, $Q$ respectively. Prove that the midpoint of the segment $PQ$ is the center of the circle circumscribed by triangle $ABC$.

## Solution

** The circle internally touches the circumcircle of dimensions ABC, as well as equal sides AB and AC, this is the sea of ​​the triangle at points P, Q respectively. Prove that the midpoint of the segment PQ is the center of a circle circumscribed around the dimensions ABC.

On the symmetry axis of the triangle $BAC$ we mark the points $H \in [BC]$, $K \in [PQ]$, the center $O$ of this circle and the point $T$ of its tangency with the circle circumscribed around the triangle $BAC$ (Fig. 12). Since the segments $TB$ and $OP$ are both perpendicular to line BA (the angle $TBA$ is based on the diameter $[AT]$, and $[OP]$ is the radius going to the point of contact), and the segments $BH$ and $PK$ are perpendicular to the axis of symmetry $(AT)$, we get

$$\frac{|AH|}{|AT|} = \frac{|AK|}{|AO|} = \cos^2 \alpha,$$

where $\alpha = \frac{\widehat{BAC}}{2}$.
