## Condition

There are two groups of $n$ weights, in each of which the weights are arranged in increasing order of their masses. Show that
a) $2n-1$ weights can be arranged and all $2n$ weights in increasing order of their masses;
b) $*$ is less than $2n-1$ using the weighing number, this cannot be done, generally speaking.
(During one weighing, the masses of two weights are compared; the masses $2n$ of weights are different in pairs.) V. Gribere

In the solution we will use the following very important message: if each of two wings divides the area of ​​a given figure in half, then they necessarily intersect. Let's assume that the required circle has been found. Let us draw a straight line $O_1O_2$ connecting the given circles $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ (Fig. 1). It divides the area of ​​the figure $G_1$ in half. But $\gamma$ also divides this area in half. This means that inside $G_1$ $\gamma$ and $(O_1O_2)$ have an intersection point. Likewise, they have intersection points inside $G_2$ and inside $G_3$. We found that a circle and a straight line intersect at more than two points - a contradiction. This means that such a circle $G$ cannot exist.

On each edge of the cube $K$ there must be at least one point belonging to the polyhedron $T$, otherwise the projection $T$ in the direction of this edge of the cube does not coincide with the corresponding boundary of the cube. Let us choose a point of the polyhedron $T$ on each edge of the cube $K$ (some of them may turn out to be vertices of the cube). Let $C$ be their convex hull (Fig. 4). From the convexity of the polyhedron $T$ it follows that $C \subset T$. Setting the length of the edge of the cube equal to 1, we calculate the volume of the additional $K \setminus C$ polyhedron $C$ to the cube $K$.

$K \setminus C$ is the union of all three angular straight lines at the angles of the cube $K$ (Fig. 2). Let's divide these straight lines into pairs - a pair of straight lines for each of the four vertical edges of the cube. The sum of the volume of each pair of straight lines does not exceed $\frac{1}{3} h S_1 + \frac{1}{3} (1-h) S_2$ (see Fig. 2).

Since $S_1 \leqslant \frac{1}{2}, S_2 \leqslant \frac{1}{2}$, we get $\frac{1}{3} h S_1 + \frac{1}{3} (1-h) S_2 \leqslant \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} (h + (1-h)) = \frac{1}{6}$.

So volume $(K \setminus C) \leqslant 4 \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$, therefore volume $T \geqslant$ volume $C \geqslant 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$.

Note that the estimate $1/3$ is exact: the volume of a tetrahedron whose vertices are four non-adjacent vertices of a cube is $1/3$ the volume of the cube.
