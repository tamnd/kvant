## Condition

Two loops $p, q$ are given on the plane with vertices at points $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Two circles—one centered on lunar $p$, passing through point $P$, and the other centered on lunar $q$, passing through $Q$—touch each other externally at point $M$. Find the set of point $M$.

V. V. Shabunin

## Solution

Two loops $p, q$ with vertices at points $P$ and $Q$ of the corresponding one are given on the plane. Two circles—one centered on lunar $p$, passing through point $P$, and the other centered on lunar $q$, passing through $Q$—touch each other externally at point $M$. Find the set of points $M$.

Let us denote by $l$ and $m$ the lines perpendicular to the rays $p$ and $q$, passing through their ends $(P$ and $Q)$; through $\Pi_l$ and $\Pi_m$ are the corresponding half-planes filled by our circles with centers at $p$ and $q$. It is obvious that when one of the circles touching each other increases, the other decreases so that the point of contact $M$ describes a certain line lying at the intersection $\Pi_l \cap \Pi_m$; its ends correspond to the extreme position when one of the circles goes either to the point $(P$ or $Q)$, or to the straight line $(l$ or $m)$; the ends of the line do not belong to the exclusive set. We will prove that this line is an arc of a circle passing through the points $P$ and $Q$; To do this, you just need to check that the angle $PMQ$ is constant.

R is the point of intersection of straight lines l and m, t is the common tangent to the circle at point M, α and β are the angles formed by it with straight lines l and m. Then α + β = 180° – ∠PRQ and ∠PMQ = (90° – α/2) + (90° – β/2) = 180° – (α + β)/2 = 180° + ∠PRQ.

Conversely, through any point M with the head of the arc there pass two circles touching it, satisfying the conditions of the problem (if a straight line is drawn through M, making angles α = 180° – 2∠MPR and β = 180° – α – ∠PRQ, then they form two equilateral triangles with bases MP and MQ).

A similar proof can be found for any other arrangement of rays: the quantity α + β (and, therefore, ∠PMQ) is always expressed in terms of the angle between the lines l and m (i.e., through ∠PRQ), since the angles α and β between the tangents (l and t, t and m) are located on opposite sides of the line t.

Note that the center O of the arc head is the midpoint of the arc PQ of the circle passing through the points P, Q, R: for example, for the arrangement shown in figure 1, ∠POQ = ∠POM + ∠MOQ = 2(180° – ∠PMQ) = 180° – ∠PRQ and, of course, OP = OQ.
