## Condition

The jeweler was ordered a gold ring of width $h$, which has the shape of a body, the bounded surface of a ball with center $O$ and the surface of a cylinder of radius $r$, the axis of which passes through the point $O$ (Fig. 4). The master made such a ring, but chose $r$ too small. How much gold should be added if $r$ needs to be increased $k$ times, but the width $h$ remains the same?

It is not difficult to prove that the volume of a spherical ring - the body obtained by expressing the segment $AB$ around the diameter $MN$, which does not intersect this segment - is equal to $$\frac{\pi}{6} AB^2 \cdot A_1B_1,$$ where $A_1B_1$ is the projection of the chord $AB$ onto the diameter $MN$. This formula is given, for example, in the book of J. Adam “Elementary Geometry”, part II, chapter. IV, 500.

Fig. 4.

⟦folio 38⟧

The volume of the ring is equal to $\frac{\pi}{6} h^3$, that is, it does not depend on the radius $r$, so gold is added to the master.
