## Solution

Let us prove that Merlin, King Arthur’s advisor, can seat a knight at a round table so that the edges of the cube (that is, just like the size of the angle $\varphi$, do not depend on the position of the point $L$).

Problem M250 b) is given here in a precise formulation. This is a very difficult task. The article “Problems about counts and the fairy tale “Ivan Tsarevich and the Gray Wolf” will be devoted to its solution, which can be read in the next issue of our magazine.

Let us present the solution M250 a).

When seating knights at a round table or on the same rock (that is, in a row), we will always mean that they are neighbors as friends. We will need such a fact.

Lemma. Let $k$ knights sit on one rock, and let each of the two outermost knights have more than half of their friends. Then these $k$ knights can be seated at a round table.

*) See Mark Twain, A Yankee with King Arthur's Two.

Proof. Let's denote the knights from left to right in the order they sit by $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_k$.

If $B_1$ and $B_k$ are friends, then the statement of the lemma is obvious. Consider all those knights to the left of whom sits the friend of the knight $B_k$. There are at least $k/2$ of them. Therefore, there will be a friend of the knight $B_1$. Let's denote it $B_s$ (then $B_{s-1}$ is a friend of $B_k$). Let's move the knights in this order:

$B_1, B_2, \dots, B_{s-1}, B_k, B_{k-1}, \dots, B_s$.

Since $B_1$ and $B_s$ are friends, the knights can already be seated at the table.

The statement of the lemma is proven. Telery proves that $n$ knight is always on the same rock. Suppose we have already managed to seat $r$ knights ($r < n$) on the rock; Let us show that then one more knight can be placed on the rock.

Two cases are possible: 1) the extreme knights have all their friends already sitting on the rock; 2) the extreme knights have friends who are not sitting on the rock. In the second case, it is obvious that another knight can be placed on the edge.

If the first case occurs, then $r$ the knights sitting on the rock, according to the lemma, can also be seated at the round table. Let us denote the knights already sitting at the table in order by $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_r$. Let's take knight $A$ from among $n - r$ unseated knights, who is familiar with some knight sitting at the table, for example with $B_m(m < r)$ (there is one, since $r \leq \frac{n}{2}$), or $n - r \leq \frac{n}{2}$). Telery $r + 1$ knights can be seated on the rock like this:

$A, B_m, B_{m+1}, \dots, B_r, B_1, B_2, \dots, B_m-1$.

Reasoning similarly, we find that it is gradually possible to seat all $n$ knights on one rock. Applying the lemma to $n$ knights sitting on the rock, we point them to the round table.
