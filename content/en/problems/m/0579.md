## Condition

Prove that for any numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belonging to the interval [0; 1], the inequality holds
$$\left(x_1+x_2+\dots+x_n+1\right)^2 \geq 4\left(x_1^2+x_2^2+\dots+x_n^2\right).$$
(9)

A. Plotkin, S. Fomin

## Solution

Prove that for any numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belonging to the interval [0; 1], the inequality holds
$$x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n + 1)^2 \geqslant 4(x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2).$$

First decision. With respect to $x_n$ this inequality is quadratic, $P_2(x) = 3x_n^2 + Ax_n + B \leqslant 0$, and it holds on the interval [0; 1], if $P_2(0) \leqslant 0$ and $P_2(1) \leqslant 0$. These two inequalities with respect to $x_{n-1}$ are also square, of the same form, and are valid on the entire interval [0; 1], if valid for $x_{n-1} = 0$ and $x_{n-1} = 1$. Arguing this way further, we find that it is enough to check the original inequality for the sets $x_1, \dots, x_n$ of 0 and 1. If the number of $x_i$ equal to one is equal to $k$, then we obtain the obvious inequality $(k+1)^2 \geqslant 4k$ (that is, $(k-1)^2 \geqslant 0)$.

Second solution. Since the numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belong to the interval [0; 1],
$$4(x_1^2 + x_2^2 + \dots + x_n^2) \leqslant 4(x_1 + \dots + x_n),$$
and our inequality coincides again to the next
$$4S \leqslant (S+1)^2.$$
A. Plotkin.
S. Fomin
