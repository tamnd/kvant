## Condition

Prove that there exists a number $A$ such that at least 1978 pairwise incongruent squares can be written in the graph of the function $y = A \sin x$. (A square is called inscribed if all its vertices belong to the graph.) (10 cells)

V. Fedogov

## Solution

Prove that there is a number $A$ such that the graph of the function $y = A \sin x$ can describe at least 1978 pairwise non-constructive squares. (A square is called inscribed if all its vertices belong to the graph.)

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

In order for the point $(x; y)$ to be the vertex of a square inscribed in the graph $\Gamma$ with center $O = (0; 0)$, the conditions $(x; y) \in \Gamma$ and $(-y; x) \in \Gamma$ are sufficient, that is, the conditions

$$y = A \sin x, -x = A \sin y;$$

in this case, $(-x; -y) \in \Gamma$ and $(y; -x) \in \Gamma$, since $O$ is the center of symmetry of the graph $\Gamma$ (Fig. 1; the function $y = A \sin x$ is odd). Thus, each root of the equation

$$\sin (A \sin x) = -\frac{x}{A}, x > 0, y = A \sin x > 0$$ (1)

corresponds to an inscribed square with center $O$. Let us show that for large $A$ this equation has many roots.

On each of the segments $x \in [2\pi k; 2\pi k + \pi/2]$ the values ​​$y = A \sin x$ increase from 0 to $A$; on each of the segments $x \in [2\pi k + \pi/2; 2\pi k + \pi]$ decreases from $A$ to 0 (Fig. 2; $k = 1, 2, \dots$). Let's take $A = 2\pi n$. Then the values $z = \sin y$ at $y$, change from 0 to $A$, runs through the segment $[-1; 1]$ $2n$ times (this “run” starts and ends at $z = 0$, Let $k < n$. Then on each segment $[2\pi k; 2\pi k + \pi/2]$ and $[2\pi k + \pi/2; 2\pi k + \pi]$ equation (1) will have $2n$ roots - for each transition $z$ from 0 to $(-1)$ and back, the graph $z = \sin (A \sin x)$ intersects the straight line $z = -\frac{x}{A}$ $2n$ times, since $|x/A| < 1$ at $0 \leq x \leq 2\pi k + \pi < A$, and in total equation (1) will have $4n^2$ roots ($k = 0, 1, \dots, n - 1$).

To make sure that the square is pairwise non-constructive, we take only $x \in [0; \pi/2]$. For $A > 1978\pi$, equation (1) will have this segment of at least 1978 roots, and the more $x$, the more $y = A \sin x$ and $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$; This means that all the corresponding 1978 squares are non-constructive.

Another, more geometric solution to problem M513 can be obtained based on the following consideration: let us rotate the graph $\Gamma$ of the function $y = A \sin x$ by $90^\circ$ around the point $O$. Then any point of intersection of graph $\Gamma$ and rotated graph $\Gamma'$ (except $O$) serves as the vertex of a square with center $O$ inscribed in $\Gamma$.

Note that all the squares discussed in our solutions have centers of symmetry at the point $O$. Apparently, any square inscribed in $\Gamma$ has a center of symmetry at one of the points $(2\pi n; 0)$ (i.e., in one of the centers of symmetry of the graph $\Gamma$); try to prove it.
