## Condition

a) Is there a natural number n such that for any natural number k at least one of the numbers $n^{k} - 1, n^{k} + 1$ has the form $a^{b}$ for some natural numbers a and b > 1? b*) Let us call a natural number antiprime if it is divisible by the square of any of its prime divisors. We call two natural numbers twins if they differ by 2. Are there finite or infinitely many pairs of antiprime twin numbers? V. Senderov
