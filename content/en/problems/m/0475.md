## Condition

a) Prove that it is impossible to have a regular triangle on checkered paper so that its vertices fall exactly at the nodes (at the vertices of the cells).
b) $*$ Prove that on checkered paper on the side of the square equal to 1, for any $\varepsilon>0$ one can draw a regular triangle whose vertices would be at distances less than $\varepsilon$ from three different nodes.

⟦folio none⟧

c) * Is it true that for any polygon $M$ and any $\varepsilon > 0$ it is possible to draw on checkered paper a similar $M$ polygon, all of whose vertices would be at distances less than $\varepsilon$ from various nodes?

V. Kalinnikov
