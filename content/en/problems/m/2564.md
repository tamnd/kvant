One of the researchers compared the difficulty of isolating the right signals to trying to hear a friend whispering at the other end of a noisy cafe. Perhaps this comparison is not clear enough - it is even more difficult to “hear” a gravitational signal using one detector. So, two detectors were installed at two points in the United States at a distance of approximately 3000 km from each other. The gravitational wave should have traveled this path in approximately 10 microseconds, assuming its speed was equal to the speed of light. Based on this, from a huge variety of noise it was possible to isolate two similar signals, shifted in time relative to each other by a known delay value (Fig.3). The frequency of recorded oscillations was in the range of 35–250 Hz. At the same time, the reliability of the experiment is considered very high: the probability of a false alarm is estimated at one event every 203 thousand years.
