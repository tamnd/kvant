In this case, the movement of light in the atmosphere changes the properties of the medium not sharply, but smoothly. Along with the change in air density as it approaches the Earth's surface, the vertical direction in which the density changes most rapidly also changes (i.e., the verticals at different places in the path of the light ray are not parallel lines). If, for example, we assume that the thickness of the atmosphere on Earth is approximately 32 km (at this altitude, the air density is approximately 1% of the air density near the Earth's surface at sea level), then with the Earth's radius being approximately 6400 km, the path length of light incident tangentially to the Earth's surface from the point of entry into the atmosphere to the Earth's surface is (without taking into account refraction) approximately 640 km. In this case, the verticals for the place of entry into the atmosphere and at the place where the light beam touches the surface of the Earth make up a very small angle - about 0.1 rad, or about 5.7q. When taking into account the phenomenon of refraction, the path length will be even greater, and the verticals in question will form an even larger angle.
