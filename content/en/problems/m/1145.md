## Condition

From the point $P$ two tangents PB and PC are drawn to the circle, and $\angle BPC > 90^\circ$. Point A is taken on the smaller arc BC. Prove that the area of ​​the triangle cut off from angle BPC by the tangent to the circle at point A does not exceed the area of ​​triangle ABC.

V. Yu. Progasov

## Solution

From the point $P$ two tangents PB and PC are drawn to the circle, and $\angle BPC > 90^\circ$. Point A is taken less than arc BC. Prove that the area of ​​the triangle cut off from angle BPC by the tangent to the circle at point A does not exceed the area of ​​triangle ABC.

Fig. 2.

Let us denote the triangle cut off from angle BPC by KPL (Fig. 1), and the center of its incircle by I. Triangles ABC and ILK are similar. Indeed, each of the angles ABC, CAL and ACL is equal to half the angular value of the arc AC (the first angle is inscribed in the circle and rests on this arc, the other two are formed by the chord subtending this arc and the tangents at its ends). That's why

$$2\angle ILK = \angle PLK = \angle LAC + \angle LCA = 2\angle ABC,$$

i.e. $\angle ILK = \angle ABC$. Likewise, $\angle IKL = \angle ACB$. The similarity coefficient of triangles ILK and ABC is $KL/BC$, therefore, the ratio of their areas is $S_{ILK}/S_{ABC} = KL^2/BC^2$.

Let, further, $p$ be the semiperimeter, $S$ the area, $r$ the radius of the inscribed circle and $h$ the height omitted from the vertex $P$ of the triangle PKL (Fig. 2). Then

$$\frac{S_{PKL}}{S_{ILK}} = \frac{h}{r} = \frac{2S}{KL} \cdot \frac{p}{S} = \frac{2p}{KL} = \frac{2BP}{KL},$$

since $BP = CP$ and $BP + CP = AK + KP + PL + LA = 2p$ (we use here the equality of tangents to the circle drawn from the points $P$, $K$, $L$). So, $S_{PLK}/S_{ABC} = 2BP \cdot KL/BC^2$. It remains to prove that the last ratio is less than 1.

By the triangle inequality $2KL < KL + KP + PL = 2p = 2BP$, i.e. $2BP \cdot KL < 2BP^2$. But in the case when $\angle BPC > 90^\circ$, we have $2BP^2 < BC^2$, i.e. $2BP \cdot KL/BC^2 < 2BP^2/BC^2 < 1$, which is what needed to be proved.

This problem can also be solved using trigonometry. If $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the angles at the vertices $A, B, C$ of triangle ABC, then $BC = KL$ cos $\beta$ cos $\gamma$; the necessary inequality is reduced to the form 2 cos $(\pi - \alpha)$ cos $\beta$ cos $\gamma > 1$ and follows from the fact that $\pi - \alpha = \beta + \gamma < \pi/4$.

V. Yu. Progasov
