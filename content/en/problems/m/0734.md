## Condition

The bisector of angle $A$ of triangle $ABC$ intersects its circumcircle at point $K$. Prove that the length of the projection of the segment $AK$ onto the line $AB$ (or $AC$) is equal to half the lengths of the sides $AB$ and $AC$.

R. Mazov

## Solution

Bissertis point A of triangle ABC intersects the circumcircle at point K. Prove that the length of the projection of segment AK onto line AB (or AC) is equal to half the length of sides AB and AC.**

Let for definition $|AB| \leqslant |AC|$. On the side AC, consider a point P, symmetrical to B relative to the straight line AK (see figure). Obviously $|AP|=|AB|, |PK|=|BK|$. The equality of the angles BAK and CAK implies the equality of the arcs BK and CK on which they rest, and the equality of the arcs implies the equality of the chords BK and CK subtending them. Thus, $|CK|=|BK|=|PK|$, and therefore the projection K' of the point K on the side AC is the midpoint of the segment CP. Hence,

$$|AK'|=|AP|+|PK'|=|AB|+\frac{|AC|-|AB|}{2} = \frac{|AB|+|AC|}{2}.$$

The time $t$ during which the stone flies from point A to point B is determined by the conditions
$$v_y = v \sin \alpha = gt/2 \Rightarrow t = 2v (\sin \alpha)/g.$$

The magnitude of the velocity $v$ is related to the obtuse wall $l$ by correspondence
$$l = v_x t = 2v \cos \alpha \cdot v (\sin \alpha)/g = v^2/g \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{gl}.$$

The connection between $v_0$ and $v$ will be found from the law of conservation of energy:
$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} + mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2} + mgH,$$

$$v_0 = \sqrt{v^2 + 2g(H - h)} = \sqrt{g(l + 2H - 2h)}.$$
