## Condition

Is there a sequence of distinct natural numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$, none of whose terms is equal to the sum of several others, such that (for all $n=1,2,\dots$)
a) $a_n \leq 2(\sqrt{3})^n$;
b) $a_n \leq 10(1,5)^n$;

⟦folio 33⟧

d) $a_n \leq 1000n^{7/2}$; e) $a_n \leq 1000n^{3/2}$

S. Konyatsin

## Solution

Is there a sequence of distinct natural numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3, ...$, none of whose terms is equal to the sum of several others - a progression 1, 2, $2^2$, $2^3$, ...: each term is 1 greater than the sum of the previous ones. But she is growing too fast.

The main idea of ​​​​constructing all the following examples, showing that the answers to questions a) - d) are positive, constitute a sequence “in batches”. In examples a), b), with an exponential increase in the number in the stoves, the distance can be taken approximately in a geometric progression, and the number of numbers in the stove can be taken as constant; in examples with a exponential increase in the length of the stoves, like their first numbers, very

a) Let $a_{2m-1}=3^m-1$, $a_{2m}=3^m$. Obviously,
$(2+3)+(8+9)+...+(3^{m-1}-1+3^{m-1})<2(3+9+...+3^{m-1})=3^m-3$

6) We will also build the sequence $(a_n)$ in batches. The $m$th packet $(m=1, 2, ...)$ will contain 16 numbers:
$$15 \cdot 361^{m-1}, 16 \cdot 361^{m-1}, ..., 30 \cdot 361^{m-1}.$$
Thus, $a_n=361^{m-1}(14+r)$, where $m$ and $r$ are selected from the condition $n=16(m-1)+r$, $m=1, 2, ..., r=1, 2, ..., 16$.
Note that the sum of all numbers in the packs from the first to the $(m-1)$th is equal to
$(15+16+...+30)+(15 \cdot 361+...+30 \cdot 361)+...+
+(15 \cdot 361^{m-2}+...+30 \cdot 361^{m-2})=
=360+360 \cdot 361+...+360 \cdot 361^{m-2}=361^{m-1}-1.$
Let us assume that some number $a$ from the $m$ pack is representable as a sum $b_1+...+b_k$ of several smaller terms $b_1, ..., b_k$ of our sequence. Since $a>361^{m-1}$, among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$ there is at least one from the $m$ pack. Let's consider two cases.
$1^\circ$. Among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$exactly one is from the $m$th pack; we can consider this to be $b_1$. But the equality $a-b_1=b_2+...+b_k$ is impossible, since, on the one hand, $a-b_1$, as the difference of numbers from the $m$-th pack, is not less than $361^{m-1}$, and on the other hand, $b_2+...+b_k$, as the sum of numbers from packs with numbers less than $m$, does not exceed $361^{m-1}-1$.
$2^\circ$. Among the numbers $b_1, ..., b_k$, more than one is in the $m$-th packet. Then the equality $a=b_1+...+b_k$ is impossible, since the sum of any two different numbers of the $m$th pack is already greater than any third number of this pack.
Thus, none of the numbers $a_n$ can be represented as the sum of other terms of the constructed sequence.
Since $361^{m-1} \leq (1,5)^{16(m-1)}$ and $14+r \leq 10 \cdot (1,5)^r(r=1, ..., 16)$, we get
$$a_n=361^{m-1}(14+r) \leq (1,5)^{16(m-1)} \cdot 10 \cdot (1,5)^r=10 \cdot (1,5)^n$$ — conditions 6) are met.
c), d). Let us construct a sequence $(a_n)$, none of whose terms is equal to the sum of several others, such that for all $n$ there are simultaneously $a_n \leq n^{10}$ and $a_n \leq 100n^{7/2}$.
First let's set $b_m=10^{5m-2}$ for all natural $m \geq 2$, that is, $b_2=10$, $b_3=10000$, $b_4$ equals one followed by 25 zeros, etc.
The sequence $(a_n)$ will again be built “in batches”. Let's take the first pack of states from one number - one. As the $m$th pack for $m \geq 2$, we take an arithmetic progression with the first term $2b_m+1$, the difference $2b_m$ and the number of terms equal to $b_m^2/2$:
$$2b_m+1, 4b_m+1, ..., b_m^3+1.$$
Let us estimate the sum of the numbers of the $m$th pack
$$\frac{(2b_m+1)+(b_m^3+1)}{2} \cdot \frac{b_m^2}{2} =
= \frac{b_m^5+2b_m^3+2b_m^2}{4} < \frac{b_m^5}{2} < b_m^5-b_m=b_m+1-b_m.$$
Thus, the sum of numbers in packets from 1st to $m$th inclusive is less
$$1+(b_3-b_2)+...+(b_{m+1}-b_m)=b_{m+1}-b_2+1<b_{m+1}.$$
Let us assume that some number $a$ from the $m$-th pack ($m \geq 2$) is representable as the sum of several other terms of the constructed sequence. Since the sum of all terms of the sequence there are several numbers from the $m$-th pack; let's denote them $c_1, ..., c_k$. We denote the remaining terms of our sum by $d_1, ..., d_l$:

$$a=c_1+...+c_k+d_1+...+d_l.$$

Since the sum of the first $b_m$ numbers of the $m$th packet is equal to
$(2b_m + 1) + (4b_m + 1) + \dots + (2b_m^2 + 1) = b_m^3 + b_m^2 + b_m > b_m^3 + 1,$
that is, greater than the largest number of the $m$th packet, and therefore greater than $a$, we get that $k < b_m$.
That's why
$$r = k + (d_1 + \dots + d_l) < b_m + (d_1 + \dots + d_l) < 2b_m.$$
But $a - r = (c_1 + \dots + c_k) - k = (c_1 - 1) + \dots + (c_k - 1)$. Since each number in the $m$th packet leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by $2b_m$, the numbers $c_1 - 1, c_2 - 1, \dots, c_k - 1$ are divisible by $2b_m$, so $a - r$ is divisible by $2b_m$. Since in this case $r < 2b_m$, $r$ is equal to the remainder of division of $a$ by $2b_m$, that is, $r = 1$; means $k + d_1 + \dots + d_l = 1$, which is impossible. From here we conclude that not a single member of the constructed sequence $(a_n)$ is equal to the sum of several others.

Let us now check that $a_n \leq 100n^{7/2}$ for any $n$.
At $n = 1$ this is obvious. If $a_n$ is in the second packet, then $a_n \leq 1001 < 100 \cdot 2^{7/2} \leq 100n^{7/2}$. Let $a_n$ lie in the $m$th packet and $m \geq 3$. Let's represent $a_m$ as $2kb_m + 1$ ($1 \leq k \leq b_m^2/2$) and the distance is two cases.
$1^\circ$. $k \leq b_m^2 - 1$.
Then
$$a_n \leq 2b_m^2 - 1b_m + 1 < 3b_m^2 - 1b_m = 3b_m - 1 = 3 \cdot 2^{7/2} \cdot (b_m^2 - 1/2)^{7/2}.$$
But $b_m^2 - 1/2 < n$, since $b_m^2 - 1/2$ is the number of terms in the $(m - 1)$-th packet. Therefore $a_n \leq 3 \cdot 2^{7/2} \cdot n^{7/2} < 100n^{7/2}$.
$2^\circ$. $k > b_m^2 - 1$.
It is clear that the number $a_n$ in the $m$-th packet does not exceed its number in the sequence, that is, $k \leq n$. That's why
$$a_n = 2kb_m + 1 < 3kb_m = 3k(b_m^2 - 1)^{5/2} < 3k \cdot k^{5/2} \leq 3n^{7/2}.$$
Thus, the inequality $a_n \leq 100n^{7/2}$ is proven for all $n$. The inequality $a_n \leq n^{10}$ for $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ can be verified directly, and for $n > 2$ it follows from the fact that
$$a_n \leq 100n^{7/2} < 3^{13/2} \cdot n^{7/2} \leq n^{13/2} \cdot n^{7/2}.$$
To save space, we did not give simpler examples of sequences that provide a solution to problem c), but do not solve problem d).
e) The answer to this question is negative: such a sequence does not exist.

Let us prove that at least one term of the sequence $(a_n)$ is such that $a_n < 100n^{3/2}$ for all $n = 1, 2, \dots$ is equal to the sum of several others. (Of course, the number 100 here can be replaced by any constant; the exponent 3/2 in our proof requires that it be less than $(\sqrt{5} + 1)/2$).

Consider a straight head table with $10^{30}$ rows and $10^{18}$ columns. Let's assign numbers from $10^{30} + 1$ to $2 \cdot 10^{30}$ to its rows, and numbers from 1 to $10^{18}$ to its columns. At the intersection of the $n$th row and the $k$th column we write the number $c_{n,k} = (a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_k) + a_n$. It does not exceed $a_1 + \dots + a_{10^18} + a_2 \cdot 10^{30} \leq 100 + 100 \cdot 2^{3/2} + \dots + 100 \cdot (10^{18})^{3/2} + 100 \cdot (2 \cdot 10^{30})^{3/2} < 100 \cdot (10^{18})^{3/2} \cdot 10^{18} + 100 \cdot (10^{30})^{3/2} \cdot 2^{3/2} = 10^{47} + 10^{47} \times 2^{3/2} < 4 \cdot 10^{47}$.

Since the table contains $10^{48}$ numbers, each of which can take less than $4 \cdot 10^{47}$ values, any two of these numbers coincide. Let, for example, $c_{n,k} = c_{m,l}$. Since in the $n$th line all numbers are distinct, $n \neq m$. We can assume that $n > m$ (the case $m > n$ is similar). The equality $c_{n,k} = c_{m,l}$ means that
$$(a_1 + \dots + a_k) + a_n = (a_1 + \dots + a_l) + a_m,$$
whence $k < l$ and $a_n = (a_{k+1} + \dots + a_l) + a_m$. Since in this case $l \leq 10^{18} < m$, we obtain that $a_n$ is represented as the sum of several other terms of the sequence.
