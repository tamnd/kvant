## Condition

Each of the three primroses connecting the midpoints of opposite sides of a convex hexagon divides its area in half. Prove that these three primaries intersect at one point.

V.V. Arbitrary

## Solution

Which of the three straight lines connecting opposite sides of a convex hexagon divides its area in half. Prove that these three lines intersect at one point.

Let $ABCDEF$ be the given hexagon, $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_6$ the midpoints of its side (see figure). The segments $M_1M_4$ and $M_2M_5$ divide the area of the hexagon in half, therefore the quadrilaterals $PM_1BM_2$ and $PM_4EM_5$, where $P$ is the point of intersection of $M_1M_4$ and $M_2M_5$, are equal in size (each of these quadrangles in the sum with the pentagon $PM_2CDM_4$ gives half area of a hexagon). The areas of quadrilaterals $PABC$ and $PDEF$ are twice as large, respectively, as the areas of quadrilaterals $PM_1BM_2$ and $PM_4EM_5$, and therefore they are also equal to each other (we use the fact that the median of a triangle divides its area in half). It follows that the broken line $M_3PM_6$ divides the area of ​​the hexagon in half, just like the segment $M_3M_6$. This means that point $P$ lies on the segment $M_3M_6$, which is what needed to be proved.
