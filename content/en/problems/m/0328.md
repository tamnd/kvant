## Condition

According to the rules, the metrahedron is half a muxa and two nayka. Mucha we crawl only along the ribs, and nayki - along the entire surface. The maximum speed of the muxa is 2 times the maximum speed of the naykov.

a) Prove that for any minimal position of the nayki, the maximum speed of the nayki can appear?

Let us denote the members of the company by points on the plane (no three of which lie on the same straight line). The fact that A is corrected by B will be denoted by a segment with an arrow directed from point A to point B. Then exactly k arrows will come out of each point, and there will be kn arrows in total. We need to find the number k₀ - the smallest value of k at which at least one segment must have arrows in both directions.

There are $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ segments without arrows connecting n points. If there is no more than one arrow on each segment, then there are no more arrows than $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$. So, if $kn > \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, then $k \geq k_0$. From here

$$k_0 \leq \begin{cases} \frac{n}{2}, & \text{ если } n \text{ четное,} \\ \frac{n+1}{2}, & \text{ если } n \text{ нечетное.} \end{cases}$$

Let us prove that inequality (1) can be replaced by equality. Let us put an equal sign in formula (1) and show that n points for any n can be connected by segments with arrows in such a way that each segment has exactly k₀ - 1 arrows, and no segment contains two arrows. Here's one way to do it. It is necessary to place n points at the vertices of the correct n-pleasure and from each point direct arrows to k₀ - 1 vertices following it clockwise. Since k₀ - 1 < $\frac{n}{2}$ for all n, no two arrows end up on the same segment.

Let us denote the maximum speed of pukuk and fly, respectively, by $v_{\pi_{\max}}$ and $v_{\mu_{\max}}$. Let us prove that, regardless of how the pukum is allowed to move (along the edges, faces, or throughout the entire volume of the tetrahedron), the following two statements are true.

1°. If $v_{\pi_{\max}} \leq 2v_{\pi_{\max}}$, then pukuks can spawn a fly at any smallest location.

2°. If $v_{\pi_{\max}} > 2v_{\pi_{\max}}$, then for some smallest positions the pukun will not be able to produce a fly. It is clear that it is enough to prove Statement 1° in the “worst” case for pukuvs - when they are allowed to move only along the edges of the tetrahedron; and the statement 2°, on the contrary, is in the “best” case for pukuvs, when they crawl throughout the entire volume of the tetrahedron.

Let us denote the vertices of the tetrahedron by the letters A, B, C, D, the points where the location of the fly and the pukuka are M, $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$, respectively. We will assume that the length of the edge of the tetrahedron is equal to 1, and that $v_{\mu_{\max}} = 1$.

Let us prove statement 1°, assuming that the pukum is allowed to move only along the edges of the tetrahedron.

Let us denote the projection of point M onto edge AB by M' (if M is on an edge perpedicular to edge AB, then M' coincides with the midpoint of edge AB).

The pukuk strategy that ensures that the fly is captured may be as follows.

Let's move on to statement $2^\circ$ (we believe that the spider is allowed to marvel at the entire volume of the tetrahedron). It follows directly from the following lemma:

If at time $t$ the fly is not understood and is at the vertex of the metrahedron, then in time $1$ it can crawl to other vertices of the metrahedron (i.e., in the time interval from $t$ to $t + 1$ the spiders will not be able to understand the fly).

Let's prove this lemma. Let muxa be at vertex $A$. Let's connect the points $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$, where the spiders sit, with this vertex; we obtain the segments $\Pi_1A$ and $\Pi_2A$. Let us prove that there is an edge of the tetrahedron emanating from the point $A$, which forms with each of the segments $\Pi_1A$ and $\Pi_2A$ angles that do not change $30^\circ$. Indeed, if there were no such edge, then one of the segments, for example, the segment $\Pi_1A$, would form an angle less than $30^\circ$ with at least two edges emanating from $A$, for example, with $AB$ and with $AC$. But then for the plane angles of the three-edge angle $ABC\Pi_1$ it would be $\widehat{BAC} = 60^\circ$, $\widehat{BA\Pi_1} < 30^\circ$, $\widehat{CA\Pi_1} < 30^\circ$, which is impossible (should be $\widehat{BA\Pi_1} + \widehat{CA\Pi_1} \geqslant \widehat{BAC}$).

To make it clear, the muxa must move with maximum speed along the edge that forms smiles with the segments $\Pi_1A$ and $\Pi_2A$ that do not change $30^\circ$. After a unit of time, it will complete the other end of this edge. Let us prove that on the selected edge spiders will not be able to understand the fly. Indeed, let some spider, for example $\Pi_1$, appear a fly at time $t + \Delta t$ at point $P$ (belonging to the selected edge; see figure 4), then

$$|AP| = \Delta t \cdot v_{M_{\max}} = \Delta t$$
$$(v_{M_{\max}} = 1),$$
$$|\Pi_1P| \leqslant \Delta t \cdot v_{\pi_{\max}} < 1/2 \Delta t = 1/2 |AP|$$
$$(v_{\pi_{\max}} < 1/2 v_{M_{\max}}).$$

Let's lower the perpendicular $PQ$ to $\Pi_1A$; we get

$$|\Pi_1P| \geqslant |PQ| = |AP| \cdot \sin(\widehat{AP}) \geqslant \frac{1}{2} |AP|,$$

since $\Pi_1AP \geqslant 30^\circ$ due to the choice of edge.

So, on the one hand $|\Pi_1P| \geqslant \frac{1}{2} |AP|$, and on the other $|\Pi_1P| < \frac{1}{2} |AP|$ is a contradiction; therefore, spiders will not be able to understand the fly in the time interval from $t$ to $t + 1$, and it will fly to another vertex of the tetrahedron. The lemma, and therefore the statement $2^\circ$, is proven.
