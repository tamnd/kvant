## Condition

Consider sequences $\{x_n\}$ of positive numbers satisfying the conditions

$$1 = x_0 \geqslant x_1 \geqslant x_2 \geqslant \dots \geqslant x_n \geqslant \dots$$

a) Prove that for any such sequence $\{x_n\}$ there exists n such that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_{n-1}^2}{x_n} \geqslant 3,999.$$

6) Find a sequence $\{x_n\}$ satisfying the specified conditions such that for any n

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_{n-1}^2}{x_n} < 4.$$

A. Grishkov

## Solution

We consider sequences $\{x_n\}$ of positive numbers satisfying the conditions

$$1 = x_0 \geqslant x_1 \geqslant x_2 \geqslant \dots \geqslant x_n \geqslant \dots$$

a) Prove that for any such sequence $\{x_n\}$ there exists $n$ such that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} \geqslant 3,999.$$

b) Also find a sequence $\{x_n\}$ satisfying the specified conditions, for which for any $n$

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} < 4.$$

a) It is easy to see that the following inequalities are true: $\frac{x_k^2}{x_k} \geqslant 4(x_k-1-x_k)$. Indeed, multiplying both sides by the (positive) number $x_k$ and crossing all terms to the left, we obtain the obvious correspondence: $x_k^2 - 4x_k-1x_k + 4x_k^2 = (x_k-1-2x_k)^2 \geqslant 0$.

Adding the proven inequalities for all $k$ from 1 to $n$, we obtain that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} \geqslant 4(x_0-x_n) = 4(1-x_n)$$

If the sequence $\{x_n\}$ tends to zero as $n \to \infty$, then, as follows from (*), the inequality being proved is satisfied for all sufficiently large $n$.

If the sequence does not tend to zero, then by Weierstrass’s theorem on the existence of a limit for a monotonic killing sequence bounded with space

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = a > 0.$$

Not then the limit of the sequence $x_n^2-1/x_n$ will differ from $a$ by less than $a/2$, and therefore

will be greater than $a/2$. Therefore, on the left side of the inequality being proved, you can add so many terms that the sum becomes greater than 4 (and, in general, any given number).

6) Answer: the required sequence $\{x_n\}$ is a geometric progression with value $1/2$; $x_n = 1/2^n$, $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$. For this sequence, the sum on the left side is

$$2 + 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{2^{n-2}} = 4 - \frac{1}{2^{n-2}} < 4.$$

Comment. The sequence from point 6) is unique. Indeed, as we saw in the solution to part a), it must commit to tending to 0. If it does not coincide with the geometric progression $\{1/2^n\}$, then $x_{k-1} \neq 2x_k$ for at least one $k$, so the value $\delta = \frac{x_k^2 - 1}{x_k} - 4(x_{k-1} - x_k) = \frac{(x_{k-1} - 2x_k)^2}{x_k}$ is positive. But the difference between the left and right sides of the inequality (*) at $n \geqslant k$ is not less than $\delta$. Therefore, if $n$ is large enough, its left-hand side will be greater than 4.

A. P. Savin
