## Condition

The floor of the room, shaped like a regular hexagon with sides 10, is filled with tiles in the shape of a rhombus with sides 1 and an acute angle of 60°. It is allowed to take out three tiles that make up a regular hexagon with sides 1, and replace their arrangement with another (Fig. 2). Prove that
a) from any arrangement of tiles of such operations any other can be obtained;
b) this can be done in no more than 1000 operations;
c) from the tile arrangement figure 3, and it is impossible to obtain the arrangement figure 3.6 in less than 1000 operations.

A. Smirnov

## Solution

The floor of the room, shaped like a regular hexagon with sides of 10, is filled with tiles in the shape of a rhombus with sides of 1 and an acute angle of 60°. You are allowed to take out three tiles that make up a regular hexagon with sides 1, and replace their arrangement with a different one (Fig. 1). Prove that a) from any arrangement of tiles of such operations any other can be obtained; b) from the arrangement of tiles figure 2 it is impossible to obtain the arrangement figure 3 in less than 1000 operations.**

The author of the problem, A. Smirnov, proposed a replacement spatial interpretation, which makes solving the postal problem a priority.

Let's imagine a cube of dimensions $k \times k \times k$, divided into $k^3$ cups $1 \times 1 \times 1$. Let's put it in an “open box” glued together from three squares $k \times k$ with a common vertex $A$, and look at it in the direction of the diagonal $HA$ (Fig. 4). We will see figure 3. If we pour out all the cubes from the box (but assume that there are $k \times k$ left on the inner walls of the box - “traces” from the cubes), then we will see figure 2. In general, if we remove only part of the $k^3$ cups from the top, then we will see exactly the same picture as described in the conditions of the problem (where $k = 10$): a hexagon divided into $3k^2$ identical rhombuses (with an acute angle of 60°)*). In this case, which of the three types of rhombus - the sides of which are parallel to the defined two sides of the hexagon (in Figures 5, 6 and 7 these rhombuses are shown in white, light blue and dark blue) - correspond to the faces of the cubes, parallel to a certain pair of faces of the large cube; It is clear that the correspondence between the partitions of a hexagon into rhombuses and the corresponding “partial”
