## Condition

Prove that from four numbers you can always choose two numbers x and y, also that

$$0 \leq \frac{x-y}{1+xy} \leq 1.$$

I. I. Sergeeev

## Solution

Prove that from four numbers you can always choose two numbers x and y also, that
0≤x−y/1+xy≤1.

Let α, β, γ, δ be the arctangents of these numbers, arranged in ascending order; Then
−π/2<α≤β≤γ≤δ<π/2<α+π.

Points β, γ, δ split the segment [α; α+π] into four segments. The length of at least one of them does not exceed π/4; as x and y we can take the tangents of its right and left ends. Indeed, if, for example, β−α≤π/4, then, taking into account that β−α≥0, we have 0≤tg(β−α)≤1 and at the same time
tg(β−α)=tgβ−tgα/1+tgβtgα=x−y/1+xy.

(In the case of (α+π)−δ≤π/4, we must also use the equality tg(α+π−δ)=tg(α−δ).)

I. N. Sergeev
