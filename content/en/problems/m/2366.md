## Condition

Since the beginning of the school year, Andrei has been recording his grades in mathematics. When he received another rating (2, 3, 4 or 5), he called it unexpected if up to that moment it had occurred less frequently than each of the other possible ratings. (For example, if from the beginning of the year he had received consecutive grades of 3,4,2,5,5,5,2,3,4,3, then the first five and the second four would be unexpected.) For the entire academic year, Andrei received 40 grades - 10 each of fives, fours, threes and twos (it is not known in what order). Is it possible to say exactly how many ratings were unexpected for him? E. Bakaev

## Solution

Since the beginning of the school year, Andrei has been recording his grades in mathematics. When he received another rating (2, 3, 4 or 5), he called it unexpected if up to that moment it had occurred less frequently than each of the other possible ratings. (For example, if from the beginning of the year he had received consecutive grades of 3,4,2,5,5,5,2,3,4,3, then the first five and the second four would have been unexpected.) For the entire academic year, Andrei received 40 grades - 10 each of fives, fours, threes and twos (it is not known in what order). Is it possible to say exactly how many ratings were unexpected for him?

For the solution, see the article by E. Bakaev “Boys, girls, tables, graphs ...” in this issue of the magazine. M2367. Prove that in any polygon circumscribed around a circle there are three sides from which a triangle can be formed.

Let the longest side AB of the polygon touch the incircle at point K, and let BC and AD be adjacent sides (see figure), let them touch the circle at points L and M respectively. Then BC > > BL = BK, AD > > AM = AK, which means BC + AD > BK + + AK = AB. It turns out that the largest of the segments AB, AC and BC is less than the sum of the other two, and, therefore, a triangle can be formed from the segments AB, AC and BC. T. Kazitsyna, B. Frenkin
