## Condition

a) The square is divided into rectangles. Let us call a chain such a set of these rectangles that their projections onto one of the sides of the square completely cover this side without overlapping (Fig. 1). Prove that any two rectangles belong to some chain.
b) Prove a similar statement for a cube divided into rectangular parallelipeds (in the definition of a chain, the side of the square must be replaced by an edge of the cube).
c) Is it true that any two parallelipeds in the partition of a cube belong to the same “slice” -

⟦folio 41⟧

A set of parallelepipeds, the projections of which onto a certain face fill it entirely without overlapping each other?

A. I. Golber, V. A. Gurvich
