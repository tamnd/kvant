## Condition

a) Prove that the number $e$ is contained between the numbers $a_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ and $b_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n+1}$ for any natural number $n$.

6) Prove that the sequence $c_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{4n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ is monotonically increasing and the sequence $d_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{2n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ is monotonically decreasing.

c) Divide the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ into four segments of equal length. Which of them contains the number $e$?

d) Divide the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ into eight equal parts. Which one contains $e$?

e) What if the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ is divided into $2^k$ equal parts (in this problem it is interesting to get the answer for sufficiently large $n$, for example, for $n > 2^k$)?

## Solution

a) Prove that the number e is contained between the number $a_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n u b_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n+1}$ for any natural number $n$.
b) Prove that successively with $c_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{4n}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$ increases monotonically, and successively with $d_n = \left(1 + \frac{1}{2n}\right) \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}$ kills monotonically.
c) Divide the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ into four segments of equal length. Which of them contains the number e?

a) Let us prove that the sequence $(a_n)$ is monotonically increasing. Really,

$$\frac{a_n}{a_{n-1}} = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n; \left(1 + \frac{1}{n-1}\right)^{n-1} = \frac{n}{n-1} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)^n.$$

According to Bernoulli's inequality *),

$$\left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)^n > 1 - \frac{1}{n} = \frac{n-1}{n},$$

where

$$\frac{a_n}{a_{n-1}} > 1.$$

Let us now prove it monotonically. See also “Algebra and Beginning Analysis 9”, paragraph 2.

Let us divide the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ into eight equal parts. Which one contains $e$?

decreases. We have

$$\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_n} = \left(1 + \frac{1}{n-1}\right)^n : \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n+1} = \frac{n-1}{n} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n^2-1}\right)^{n+1}.$$

Applying Bernoulli's inequality again, we get

$$\left(1 + \frac{1}{n^2-1}\right)^{n+1} > 1 + \frac{1}{n-1} = \frac{n}{n-1},$$

that is, $$\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_n} > 1.$$

Since $e = \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} b_n$, it follows from what was proved that

$$a_n < e < b_n.$$

D) Let's solve problem e) - problems 6), c) and d) will follow from this solution.

Divide the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ into $2^k$ equal parts. The length of one such part is equal to

$$\Delta = \frac{b_n - a_n}{2^k} = \frac{1}{2^k \cdot n} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n.$$

The middle of the segment $[a_n; b_n]$ corresponds to the number $a_n + \frac{b_n - a_n}{2} = \left(1 + \frac{1}{2n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n = d_n$. It is easy to see that the $2^{k-1}$th of the resulting segments has the number $d_n$ as its right end, and the number $t_n = d_n - \Delta = \left(1 + \frac{1}{2n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n - \frac{1}{2^k \cdot n}\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n = \left(1 + \frac{2^{k-1}-1}{2^k \cdot n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n (k \geqslant 2).$ as its left end (For $k = 2$, the number $t_n$ coincides with the number $c_n$ from point 6) of the problem.)

Let us prove that for sufficiently large $n$ (namely, for $n > 3 \cdot 2^{k-2}$), the number $e$ lies on a $2^{k-1}-m$ segment of length $\Delta$. To do this, it is enough to show that for such $n$ the sequence of left endpoints $(t_n)$ of this segment monotically returns, and the sequence $(d_n)$ of right endpoints monotically decreases (since $e = \lim_{n \to \infty} t_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} d_n$).

We have

$$\frac{d_{n-1}}{d_n} = \left[ \left(1 + \frac{1}{2(n-1)}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n-1}\right)^{n-1} \right];$$

$$\left[ \left(1 + \frac{1}{2n}\right)\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n} \right] = \frac{2n-1}{2n+1} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n^2-1}\right)^n.$$

Similar to Bernoulli's inequality, it can be proven more precisely. From it

$$\left(1 + \frac{1}{n^2-1}\right)^n \geqslant 1 + \frac{n}{n^2-1} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2(n^2-1)^2} = \frac{2n^3 + 4n^2 + n - 2}{2(n-1)(n+1)^2}.$$

Because
$$\frac{d_{n-1}}{d_n} \geq \frac{2n-1}{2n+1} \cdot \frac{2n^3+4n^2+n-2}{2(n-1)(n+1)^2} = \frac{4n^4+6n^3-2n^2-5n+2}{4n^4+6n^3-2n^2-6n-2} > 1$$

for any $n \in N$. This means that the sequence $(d_n)$ decreases monotonically.

Let us now move on to the sequence $(t_n)$. We have
$$\frac{t_n}{t_{n-1}} = \left[ \left(1 + \frac{2^{k-1}-1}{2^k \cdot n}\right) \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n \right]; \left[ \left(1 + \frac{2^{k-1}-1}{2^k(n-1)}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{1}{n-1}\right)^n \right] = \frac{2^k \cdot n + 2^{k-1}-1}{2^k \cdot (n-1) + 2^{k-1}-1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)^n.$$
But $(1-h)^n \geq 1 - nh + \frac{(n-1)^2}{2} h^2$ at $0 < h < \frac{1}{(n-1)^2}$

(this inequality is proven similarly to Bernoulli’s inequality). Because
$$\frac{t_n}{t_{n-1}} \geq \frac{2^k \cdot n + 2^{k-1}-1}{2^k \cdot (n-1) + 2^{k-1}-1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n} + \frac{(n-1)^2}{2n^4}\right) = \frac{2^k \cdot n + 2^{k-1}-1}{2^k \cdot (n-1) + 2^{k-1}-1} \cdot \frac{2n^4-2n^3+n^2-2n+1}{2n^4}.$$
We are interested in when $\frac{t_n}{t_{n-1}} > 1$, that is, when
$$\left(n + \frac{1}{2} - 2^{-k}\right) \cdot \left(2n^4-2n^3+n^2-2n+1\right) > \left(n - 1 + \frac{1}{2} - 2^{-k}\right) \cdot 2n^4.$$
This inequality is equivalent to the inequality
$$2^{1-k} \cdot n^3 - \left(\frac{3}{2} + 2^{-k}\right) n^2 + 2^{1-k} \cdot n + \frac{1}{2} - 2^{-k} > 0,$$
which follows from the inequality
$$2^{1-k} \cdot n^3 \geq \left(\frac{3}{2} + 2^{-k}\right) n^2.$$
Thus, at $n \geq 3 \cdot 2^{k-2} + \frac{1}{2}$, or $n > 3 \cdot 2^{k-2}$, the sequence $(t_n)$ becomes monotonically age-dependent. From this follows our statement: the number $e$ lies inside the $2^{k-1}$-th segment of length $\Delta$, if $n > 3 \cdot 2^{k-2}$.
