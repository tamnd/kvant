## Condition

On the sides of a convex quadrilateral of area $S$, squares are constructed outside it, the centers of which serve as the vertices of a new quadrilateral of area $S_1$. Prove that
a) $S_1 \geq 2S$;
b) $S_1 = 2S$ if and only if the diagonals of the original quadrilateral are equal in length and mutually perpendicular.

P. Gusyatnikov

Let us denote by φ the angle at the vertex $B$ of the original quadrilateral. Note that

$$S_{EMNF} = S_{EBF} + S_{EMB} + S_{FNB} \pm S_{MBN},$$

Moreover, the “+” sign is taken if $\frac{3\pi}{2} - \varphi < \pi$ (Fig. 2), that is, $\frac{\pi}{2} < \varphi < \pi$, and the “-” sign if $0 < \varphi \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$ (Fig. 3).

By calculating the algebraic sum of the last three terms, we find

$$S_{EMNF} = S_{EBF} + \frac{|AB|^2}{8} + \frac{|BC|^2}{8} + \frac{|AB|\cdot|BC|}{4} \sin \left( \frac{3\pi}{2} - \varphi \right) = S_{EBF} + \frac{1}{8} \left( |AB|^2 - 2|AB|\cdot|BC| \cos \varphi + |BC|^2 \right) = S_{EBF} + \frac{|AC|^2}{8}$$

(we used the cosine theorem for the triangle $ABC$).

Carrying out similar calculations for the remaining pink quadrilaterals, we finally obtain that

$$S_1 = S + \frac{1}{4} \left( |AC|^2 + |BD|^2 \right).$$

But $S = \frac{1}{2} |AC| \cdot |BD| \sin \alpha$ (α is the angle between diagonals $AC$ and $BD$), so $\frac{1}{4} \left( |AC|^2 + |BD|^2 \right) \geq \frac{1}{2} |AC| \cdot |BD| \geq S$ and $S_1 \geq 2S$, we solved problem a).

Since the last inequalities turn into equalities if and only if $|AC| = |BD|$ and $\sin \alpha = 1$, that is, $(AC) \perp (BD)$, we get 6).
