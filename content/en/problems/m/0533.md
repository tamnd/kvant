## Condition

A convex polygon is special if some of its three diagonals intersect at one point. Prove that for every special heptagon there is a vertex A that has the following property: for any $\varepsilon > 0$, the vertex A can be shifted by a distance less than $\varepsilon$ (without changing the remaining vertices), so that the resulting heptagon is non-singular.

B. Voltyansky

## Solution

A convex polygon is special if some of its diagonals intersect at one interior point. Let us prove that for every special heptagon there is a vertex A that has the maximal property: for any ε > 0, the vertex A can be scattered at a distance less than ε (without changing the remaining vertices), so that the resulting heptagon will be non-singular.

◆ The diagonal of a convex hexagon is considered equal if there are two vertices of the hexagon on both sides of the straight line on which this diagonal lies. If MP is a secondary (not main) diagonal of a convex hexagon and N is a vertex adjacent to the vertices M and P (Fig. 5), then at interior points of the MP diagonal only the diagonals emanating from N intersect. In other words, the only projection of the diagonals of a convex hexagon that can define a point of the interior hexagon is the projection of its main diagonals.
