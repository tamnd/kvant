## Condition

Given 20 numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{10}, b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{10}$. Prove that a set of 100 numbers (not necessarily different) $a_1 + b_1, a_1 + b_2, \dots, a_{10} + b_{10}$ can be divided into 10 subsets, each containing 10 numbers, so that the sum of the numbers in each subset is the same.

S. Berkolaiko

## Solution

Given 20 numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{10}, b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{10}$. Prove that a set of 10 numbers (optional different) $a_1 + b_1, a_1 + b_2, \dots, a_{10} + b_{10}$ can be divided into 10 subsets, 10 numbers in each, so that the sum of the numbers in each subset is the same digit). It is easy to see that in each column (in each row) there are representatives of all subsets, so that the indices $i$ and $j$ of the numbers $a_i + b_j$ included in each of the subsets take all values ​​from 1 to 10 (exactly once). Therefore, the sum of the numbers in each of the subsets is the same: $a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_{10} + b_1 + b_2 + \dots + b_{10}$.
