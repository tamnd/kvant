## Condition

On edges $A'D'$ and $C'D'$ of cube $ABCDA'B'C'D'$ two points $K$ and $M$ are chosen so that the plane $KDM$ touches the ball inscribed in the cube (Fig. 3). Prove that the value $\phi$ of the dihedral angle at the edge $B'D$ of the tetrahedron $B'DKM$ does not depend on the choice of points $K$ and $M$. Find this value $\phi$.

I. F. Sharytsin

## Solution

** On edges $A'D'$ and $C'D'$ of cube $ABCDA'B'C'D'$ two points $K$ and $M$ are chosen so that the KDM plane touches the ball inscribed in the cube (Fig. 17). Prove that the quantity $\phi$ is two-edged at the edge $B'D$ of the metrahedron $B'DKM$ and does not depend on the choice of points $K$ and $M$. Find this quantity $\phi$.

Let us present the shortest solution to this problem, using only the symmetry message.

Let us denote the point of contact of the ball with the KDM plane by $L$, and the diagonal of the cube $B'D$ by $d$. Let us draw through $d$ six planes $\alpha, \chi, \lambda, \mu, \gamma$ and $\beta$, passing the corresponding points $A'$, $K, L, M, C'$ and $B$.

⟦folio 26⟧

The dihedral angle $\Rightarrow \alpha d\gamma$ (the angle between planes $\alpha$ and $\gamma$) is equal to $120^\circ$, since when the cube is rotated $120^\circ$ around the diagonal $d$, the angles $\Rightarrow \alpha d\gamma$, $\Rightarrow \gamma d\beta$ and $\Rightarrow \beta d\alpha$ are aligned with each other.

But $\Rightarrow \alpha d\chi = \Rightarrow xd\lambda$: the points at which two planes - KDM and KDA' - passing through the line KD, touch the ball, are located, symmetrical to each other relative to the plane KB'D, passing through the center of the ball, and therefore the planes $\alpha$ and $\lambda$ are also symmetrical to each other relative to the plane $x$. Likewise, $\Rightarrow \lambda d\mu = \Rightarrow \mu d\gamma$.

Since the sum of the angles $\Rightarrow \alpha d\lambda + \Rightarrow \lambda d\gamma = \Rightarrow \alpha d\gamma$ is $120^\circ$, the sum of their halves $\varphi = \Rightarrow xd\lambda + \Rightarrow \lambda d\mu = \Rightarrow xd\mu$ is $60^\circ$.

Most readers, like the author N.F. Shartsein, solved this problem by calculation and obtained the following interesting interval result: the sum of the length $|KD'| + |D'M|$ is always equal to the length of the edge of the cube (that is, just like the value of the angle $\varphi$, it does not depend on the position of the point $L$).

N. B. Vasiliev
