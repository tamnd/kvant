## Condition

Each of the students in the class is involved in no more than two clubs, and for any pair of students there is a club in which they study together. Prove that there is a club where at least 2/3 of the students in this class participate.

A. Sidorenko

## Solution

Each of the students in the class studies in no more than two circles, and for any pair of students there is a circle in which they study together. Prove that there is a circle where at least 2/3 of this class attends.

If the whole class is engaged in one of the circles, then everything is proven. Let the entire class not study in any of the circles. Let's consider one of the circles (let's call it circle No. 1) and one of the students involved in this circle (let's call him student A). Since the entire class is not occupied in circle No. 1, there will be a student B who is not engaged in this circle. By condition, there is circle No. 2, where students A and B study together. Since this circle does not cover the entire class, there is student C, who is not included. But student A studies only in circles No. 1 and No. 2, so student C studies together with student A in circle No. 1. Then there should be a circle No. 3, where students B and C study together. Consider any other student D (if there are other students in the class besides students A, B and C). If student D had not studied in either circle No. 1 or circle No. 2, then there would have been no common circle for him with student A. If D had not studied in circles No. 1 and No. 3, then he would not have studied with student C. Finally, if D had not studied in circles No. 2 and No. 3, then he would not have had a common circle with student B. Thus, each student in the class must be in two of the three circles considered. Denoting by $y_1, y_2$ and $y_3$ the number of students in circles No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3, respectively, we obtain $y_1 + y_2 + y_3 = 2y$, where $y$ is the number of all students in the class. It is clear that at least one of the numbers $y_1, y_2, y_3$ must be at least $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 2y$.

A. Sidorenko
