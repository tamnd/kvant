## Condition

a) Prove that any rectangle of size $m \times 2n$ cells can be tiled with two layers of dominoes (tiles $1 \times 2$ cells) so that each tile of the upper layer rests on two different tiles of the lower layer.
6) A rectangle measuring $2k \times 2n$ cells is already covered with one layer of dominoes. Prove that it can be tiled with a second layer so that the same condition is satisfied (i.e., so that tiles of different words do not coincide).

## Solution

** a) Prove that any rectangle of size $m \times 2n$ cells ($m > 1$) can be tiled with two layers of domino space tiles (tiles $1 \times 2$ cells) so that each tile of the upper layer rests on two different tiles of the lower layer.

6) A rectangle measuring $2k \times 2n$ cells is already paved with one layer of domino space. Prove that it can be paved with a second layer so that the same conditions are satisfied (i.e. the plywood chips of different layers do not coincide).

a) Cut the rectangle of size $m \times 2n$ cells into strips of size $2 \times m$ cells. If $m$ is odd, then in each strip we arrange our two coverings as shown in figure 10; if $m$ is even, then as in figure 11.

6) Let us cut the rectangle of size $2k \times 2n$ cells into squares of size $2 \times 2$ cells. The second cover in each square will be built separately.

In the square $2 \times 2$ there are three possible locations for the domino of the first cover: both dominoes lie entirely in the square, only one domino lies entirely in the square, and none of the dominoes entirely lies in the square. In the third case, two dominoes in a square in the second covering can be placed arbitrarily, and in the first two cases, the dominoes of the second layer must be placed perpendicular to the domino (or dominoes) lying entirely in the square in the first layer.
