## Condition

After several rectangular cuts, the surfaces of the convex polygon were turned onto a plane. The result is a polygon for which it is known which points of its boundary are “glued together,” that is, they correspond to the same point on the surface of the polygon. What was the original polygon like if, when cut, the result is a) a rectangle with sides 1 and √3,
6) an equilateral triangle with an angle of 120°, and points on each side that are symmetrical about its middle are glued together.

N. P. Dolbili, M. I. Shtogrin

## Solution

After several straight cuts, the surfaces of the convex manifold were turned onto a plane. The result is a polygon for which it is known which points of its boundary “glue together,” that is, correspond to the same point on the surface of the manifold. What was the original polygon like if, when cut, the result is a) a rectangle with side 1 and $\sqrt{3}$, b) an equilateral triangle with an angle of 120°, and in both cases the points of each side are glued together, symmetrical about its middle.
