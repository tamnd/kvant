## Condition

A hoop of radius $R$, placed on a stationary circle of radius $r < R$, begins to roll along this circle. Prove that the point of the hoop describes the same trajectory that describes the outside along the same circle of radius $r$ (Fig. 1, a, b). (Rolling occurs without sliding - so that the lengths of the arcs rolling over each other are equal.)
S. G. Gindikin

## Solution

** *A hoop of radius $R$, hanging on a stationary circle of radius $r<R$, begins to roll along this circle. Prove that a point on the hoop describes the same trajectory that would be described by a point on a wheel of radius $R-r$ rolling outside on the same circle of radius $r$ (Fig. 1). (Rolling occurs without sliding - so that the lengths of the arcs rolling over each other are equal.)*

Let us denote the radius of the wheel $R-r$ by $\rho$. The curves described during the indicated rolling by the points of the wheel boundary are called *epicycloids*, their type is determined by the relation $\dfrac{\rho}{r}$; curves described by the points of the hoop - *pericycloids*. Let us prove that with the relationship between the radii ($R=r+\rho$) specified in the condition, the pericycloids coincide with the epicycloids.

Let's fix one point on the wheel and on the hoop. Let at the initial moment the points observed on the wheel and the hoop coincide with the same point $A$ of the boundary of the fixed circle (Fig. 2). For definiteness, let both the wheel and the hoop roll in a circle counterclockwise. If at some moment the wheel touches a stationary circle at point $B$, then the point observed on its boundary (point of the epicycloid) occupies such a position $C$ that the lengths $\smile AB$ and $\smile BC$ are equal ($\smile BC$ is chosen taking into account the direction of rolling (Fig. 3)): this is the condition for rolling without sliding.

Similarly, the position of the point $C'$, observed on the hoop (pericycloid point), at the moment when it touches the fixed circle at the point $B'$, is found from the condition that the lengths $\smile AB'$ and $\smile B'C'$ are equal (taking into account the direction of rolling) (see figure 4).

Let us prove that for any point $B$ on the boundary of a fixed circle, it is possible to select a point $B'$ (also on the boundary of a fixed circle) in such a way that the corresponding points $C$ (epicycloids) and $C'$ (pericycloids) coincide (Fig. 5). (From our proof it will also be clear how to choose $B'$ by $B$.)

Let us take the point $B'$ so that the ratio of the lengths of the arcs $AB$ and $BB'$ is equal to $\dfrac{\rho}{r}$: then the radian measure $\smile BC$ is equal to the radian measure $\smile BB'$ - let’s denote it by $\varphi$. We have

dl. $\smile AB=$ long. $\smile BC=\rho\varphi$, length. $\smile BB'=r\varphi$.

Therefore, dl. $\smile B'C'=$ long. $\smile AB'=\rho\varphi+r\varphi=R\varphi$, and the radian measure of $\smile B'C'$ is also $\varphi$. Let $O$ be the center of the stationary circle, $O_1$ be the position of the center of the wheel at the moment when

Let $0 < \varphi < \pi$. We have (Fig. 6) $OB = OB' = r$, $O_2B' = R$, $OO_2 = R - r = \rho$, $O_1B = O_1C = \rho$, $O_1O = r + \rho = R$, $\sphericalangle\, BOB' = \sphericalangle\, OO_1C = \varphi$.

This means that the quadrilateral $OO_1CO_2$ is a parallelogram, and therefore $O_2C = R$, $\sphericalangle\, CO_2B' = \varphi$. Thus, the point $C$ lies on a circle of radius $R$ with center at $O_2$, and the radian measure of the arc $B'C$ is equal to $\varphi$. This means that $C$ is the same as $C'$. So, we have proven that *if the arcs of a wheel and a hoop of the same radian measure $\varphi < \pi$ roll along a stationary circle, then the resulting points of the epicycloid and pericycloid will coincide.*

It remains to verify the validity of this statement for $\varphi \geqslant \pi$. See for yourself what figure 5 “turns” into with $\varphi = \pi$, as well as with $\pi < \varphi < 2\pi$. Note that since the difference between the lengths of the hoop and the wheel is equal to $2\pi r$ - the length of the boundary of the stationary circle, then at the moment when both the wheel and the hoop make full revolutions, the observed points, again falling on the boundary of the stationary circle, will take the same position $A_1$. Case $2\pi < \varphi < 4\pi$ reduces to case $\varphi < 2\pi$ if we consider point $A_1$ to be the starting point instead of $A$. If we consider $A_1$ the starting point and simultaneously change the direction of rolling, then the case $\pi \leqslant \pi < 2\pi$ will be reduced to the case $\varphi \leqslant \pi$.

This solution can be briefly written using vectors. If you use “generalized angles” - angles of rotation $\varphi$, which can take any values ​​(not only in the range from 0 to $2\pi$), then you will not have to consider separately different cases: you only need to use that for any three vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, $\mathbf{c}$

$$\widehat{\mathbf{ab}} + \widehat{\mathbf{bc}} = \widehat{\mathbf{ac}}$$

(here $\widehat{\mathbf{ab}}$ is the magnitude of the angle between vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$).

Let $\mathbf{e}\,(\varphi)$ be a vector of length 1 resulting from a clockwise rotation through angle $\varphi$ from the initial vector $\mathbf{e}_0 = \mathbf{e}\,(0)$ (say upward). Then $\mathbf{e}\,(\varphi + \pi) = -\mathbf{e}\,(\varphi)$.

For point $M_1$ of the wheel (Fig. 7, a)

$$\overrightarrow{OO_1} = (\rho + r)\,\mathbf{e}\,(\varphi),$$

$$\overrightarrow{O_1M_1} = \rho\mathbf{e}\,(\pi + \varphi + \varphi_1).$$

Since $\rho\varphi_1 = r\varphi$ (rolling condition without sliding), $\rho = R - r$, then

$$\overrightarrow{OM_1} = \overrightarrow{OO_1} + \overrightarrow{O_1M_1} = R\mathbf{e}\,(\varphi) - (R - r)\,\mathbf{e}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\,\varphi\right). \quad (1)$$

For point $M_2$ of the hoop (Fig. 7, b)

$$\overrightarrow{OO_2} = (R - r)\,\mathbf{e}\,(\psi + \pi),$$

$$\overrightarrow{O_2M_2} = R\,\cdot\,\mathbf{e}\,(\psi - \psi_1),$$

and since $R\psi_1 = r\psi$, then

$$\overrightarrow{OM_2} = \overrightarrow{OO_2} + \overrightarrow{Q_2M_2} = R\mathbf{e}\left(\frac{R-r}{R}\,\psi\right) - (R - r)\,\mathbf{e}\,(\psi). \quad (2)$$

Comparing (1) and (2), we see that $\overrightarrow{OM_1} = \overrightarrow{OM_2}$ at $\dfrac{R}{R-r}\varphi = \psi$. This means that the set of points $M_1$ and $M_2$ coincide.
