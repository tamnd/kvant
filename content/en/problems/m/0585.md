## Condition

N scientists were present at the chemical conference - chemists and alchemists, and there were more chemists than alchemists. It is known that chemists answer the truth to any question, and alchemists sometimes tell the truth, sometimes they tell the truth. A mathematician who happens to be at the conference must determine for each scientist whether he is a chemist or an alchemist. To do this, he can ask any scientist the question: “Who is such and such a chemist or an alchemist?” (In particular: “Who are you?”). Prove that a mathematician can figure out everything that is required

a) for 4N questions; 6) for 2N - 2 questions;

b) * try to come up with a way to establish who is a chemist and who is an alchemist in fewer questions (the authors know of a rather cumbersome way that allows you to do this in [3N/2] questions).

S. Konyashin, P. Bleker
