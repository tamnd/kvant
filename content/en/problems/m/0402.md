## Condition

Prove that there is no strictly increasing sequence of non-negative integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ for which the correspondence $a_{nm} = a_n + a_m$ holds for any n and m.

Yu. Ionin

## Solution

Prove that there is no ascending sequence of non-negative integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ for which, for any $n$ and $m$, the correspondence $a_{nm} = a_n + a_m$ holds.**

Let us denote the points of intersection of the continuations of the segments $AP, BP, CP$ with the circle circumscribed around the triangle $ABC$ by $A', B'$ and $C'$, respectively (see Fig. 5). The size of the angle with the vertex inside the circle is equal to the resulting angular values ​​of the other two of which one is between the sides of this angle, and the other is between the extended sides; and the magnitude of the inscribed angle is equal to half the angular magnitude of the others on which it rests. Pozmotu $\widehat{APC} = \frac{1}{2}(AB'C + A'BC') = \widehat{ABC} + A'B'C'$; but according to the condition $\widehat{APC} = \widehat{ABC} + 60^\circ$, we open $\widehat{A'B'C'} = 60^\circ$.

It can be proved similarly that $\widehat{A'C'B'} = C' \widehat{A'B'} = 60^\circ$. A. Yaguyach

**First solution.** Note that $a_{22} = a_2 + a_2 = 2a_2, a_{23} = a_{22} = 2a_2 + a_2 = 3a_2$, and in general $a_{2n} = na_2$ for any $n$. Let's take part of the sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_{2n}$. If the sequence is arranged in ascending order, then, since the numbers are non-negative, $a_{2n} \geq 2^n$. On the other hand, $a_{2n} = na_2$. So, if such a sequence is $a_1, \dots, a_n$,..

exists, then for any $n$ the inequality $2^n \leq n a_2$ must hold. Let us prove that this is impossible; namely, we will show that the ratio $\frac{2^n}{n}$ tends to infinity as $n$ increases.

Let us denote the relation $\frac{2^n}{n}$ by $b_n$ and consider the relation $b_{n+1}: b_n$:

$$\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n} = \frac{2^{n+1}}{n+1}: \frac{2^n}{n} = 2 \cdot \frac{n}{n+1} = 2 \left(1 - \frac{1}{n+1}\right) \geq \frac{4}{3},$$

if $n \geq 2$. It follows from this that $\frac{b_n}{b_2} \geq \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n-2}$, i.e.

$$b_n = \frac{2^n}{n} \geq 2 \cdot \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n-2} > \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n-2}.$$

Obviously, $\lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{n-2} = \infty$, therefore $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2^n}{n} = \infty$.

This means that there cannot be a strictly increasing sequence $a_1, \dots, a_n, \dots$, which is mentioned in the conditions.

Second solution. Let's assume that such a sequence exists. Let $n$ be a natural number greater than $a_2$. Then $a_{2n} = a_n + a_2 < a_n + n$; This means that the natural numbers $a_{n+1}, a_{n+2}, \dots, a_{2n}$ lie in the interval $[a_n+1, a_n+n-1]$ and are distinct, which cannot be the case, since there are only $n-1$ an integer in this interval.
