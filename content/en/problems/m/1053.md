## Condition

Prove that in the sequence of Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ..., where each number is equal to the sum of the previous two, at $m > 3$ there will be at least 4 and at most 5 $m$-digit numbers.

## Solution

Prove that in the sequence of Fibonacci numbers 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ..., where each number is equal to the sum of the two previous ones, at $m \geq 2$ there will be at least 4 and at most 5 m-digit numbers.

$$u_n = \frac{\tau^{n+1} - (-1/\tau)^{n+1}}{\sqrt{5}},$$

where $\tau = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

Let $u_n$ be the Fibonacci sequence: $u_0 = 1, u_1 = 1,$

$$u_{n+1} = u_n + u_{n-1}.$$

(1)

We come across the following simple estimate for the ratio $r_n = u_{n+1}/u_n$ of neighboring Fibonacci numbers: starting from $u_3/u_2 = 3/2$, the ratio $r_n$ is no less than $3/2 = 1,5$ and no more than $5/3 = 1,66... < 1,7$.

It is easy to prove using the method of mathematical induction. By virtue of (1)

$$r_n = \frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n} = 1 + \frac{u_{n-1}}{u_n} = 1 + \frac{1}{r_{n-1}}.$$

Therefore, if

$$\frac{3}{2} \leq r_{n-1} \leq \frac{5}{3},$$

(2)

i.e. $2/3 \geq 1/r_{n-1} \geq 3/5$, then $r_n$ is estimated from above by the number $1+2/3=5/3$, and from below by the number $1+3/5=8/5>3/2$. And since estimate (2) is true for $n=3$, it is also true for all $n \geq 3$.

Let $u_k$ be the smallest $m$-digit Fibonacci number, $m \geq 2$. Then $u_k \geq 10^{m-1}, u_{k+1} \geq 1,5u_k,$

$$u_{k+2} = u_{k+1} + u_k \geq 2,5u_k,$$

$$u_{k+3} \geq (2,5+1,5)u_k = 4u_k,$$

$$u_{k+4} \geq (4+2,5)u_k = 6,5u_k,$$

and then $u_{k+5} \geq 10,5u_k > 10^m$ is no less than a $(m+1)$-digit number. Thus, there are no more than 5 $m$-digit Fibonacci numbers.

On the other hand, $u_{k-1} < 10^{m-1}, u_k < 1,7u_{k-1}$ and similarly to the previous one we get that $u_{k+3} < 7,1u_{k-1} < 10^m$. Therefore, there are at least 4 $m$-digit Fibonacci numbers.

You can solve this problem differently, use the explicit formula for Fibonacci numbers *) given in the margin.
