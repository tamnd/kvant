## Condition

Two circles on a plane intersect at points $A$ and $B$. Prove that it is possible to choose a point $C$ such that any circle with chord $AC$ will intersect the given circle (for the second time) at points equally distant from $C$ (and $C \neq B$).

V. Protasov, 10th grade student (Moscow)

## Solution

Two circles on a plane intersect at points A and B. Prove that you can choose a point C such that any circle with chord AC will intersect these circles (for the second time) at points equally distant from C.

Let us denote by $O_1$ and $O_2$ the centers of these circles. Let us extend the triangle $O_1BO_2$ to a parallelogram (Fig. 1) and prove that its fourth vertex $C(O_1C=BO_2)$ is the required point.

Let $O$ be the center of an arbitrary circle passing through $A$ and $C$, $M$ and $N$ the points of its intersection with the circles $O_1$ and $O_2$. The equality $CM=CN$ is equivalent to the equality of each other CM and CN of the circle O, which, in turn, is equivalent to the fact that the line AC forms equal angles with the lines AM and AN (i.e., either $\angle CAM=\angle CAN$ - figure 1, or $\angle CAM+\angle CAN=180^\circ$ - figure 2). The common chord AM of circles O and $O_1$ is perpendicular to their center lines $OO_1$; similarly, the chord AN is perpendicular

Note that $ACO_1O_2$ is an equilibrium trapezoid (since $O_1C=O_2B=O_2A$, $O_2C=O_1B=O_1A$, i.e. $\triangle O_1O_2C=\triangle O_2O_1A$). Therefore, line $AC$ is parallel to $O_1O_2$. It remains to note that $\angle OO_1O_2=\angle OO_2O_1$ or the point $O$ lies on the perpendicular bisector to the segment $AC$ ($OA=OC$), and therefore to the segment $O_1O_2$.
