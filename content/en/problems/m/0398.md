## Condition

There are n real numbers (n≥3) on a circle, the sum of which is zero. One of these numbers is 1.
a) Prove that there are two adjacent numbers that differ by at least 4/n.
b) Prove that there is a number that differs from the arithmetic mean of its two neighbors by at least 8/n².
b) The assessment proposed in the previous paragraph can be improved. Try replacing the number 8 with some larger number so that the statement of this problem still holds for all natural numbers.
g)* Prove that for n=30 there is a number on the circle that differs from the arithmetic mean of its two neighbors by at least 2/113. Give an example of a set of 30 numbers on a circle,
