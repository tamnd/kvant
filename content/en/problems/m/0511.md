## Condition

Inside the quadrilateral $ABCD$ there is a point $M$ such that $ABMD \leftarrow$ is a parallelogram. Prove that if $CBM = CDM$ then $ACD = CDM$. (8-9 grades)

Yu. Mikheev

## Solution

Inside the quadrilateral ABCD a point M is marked indicating that the quadrilateral ABMD is a parallelogram. Prove that if $\overrightarrow{CBM} = \overrightarrow{CDM}$ then $\overrightarrow{ACD} = \overrightarrow{BCM}$.

Fig. 4.

Let's carry out $(NC) \parallel (BM)$ and $(BN) \parallel MC$ (Fig. 4). Then the quadrilaterals $BNCM$ and $ANCD$ are parallelograms, which means that $\overrightarrow{NAB} = \overrightarrow{NCB}$ and the points $A, B, N, C$ lie on the same circle. Therefore $\overrightarrow{NBC} = \overrightarrow{NAC}$. But $\overrightarrow{NBC} = \overrightarrow{BCM}$, and $\overrightarrow{NAC} = \overrightarrow{ACD}$ (quadrilaterals $BNCM$ and $ANCD$ are parallelograms). Therefore $\overrightarrow{BCM} = \overrightarrow{ACD}$, which is what needed to be proven.

**Yu. Mikheev**

Let us present another solution to this problem, based on the fact that the quadrilaterals $ABCD$ and $MB'CD'$ are similar; here $B' = (BM) \cap (DC), D' = (DM) \cap (BC)$. Indeed, these quadrilaterals have a promising angle $C$, and the remaining angles are pairwise contiguous: $\overrightarrow{A} = \overrightarrow{M}, \overrightarrow{B} = \overrightarrow{B'}, \overrightarrow{D} = \overrightarrow{D'}$. However, concussive angles are not enough to resemble quadrilaterals. Note now that the triangles $BD'M$ and $DMB'$ are similar. From this similarity and the fact that the quadrilateral $ABMD$ is a parallelogram, the equality follows

$$\frac{|MB'|}{|AB|} = \frac{|MD'|}{|AD|},$$

from which the similarity of the quadrilaterals $ABCD$ and $MB'CD'$ already follows. From this similarity it follows in turn that $\overrightarrow{ACD} = \overrightarrow{MCD'}(= \overrightarrow{MCB})$.

L. Limanov
