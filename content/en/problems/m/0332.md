## Condition

Is it possible to construct a cube of size $k \times k \times k$ from white and black cups $1 \times 1 \times 1$ such that for each cube size two of its neighbors have the same color as itself? (Two cubes are considered adjacent if they have a common edge.) (9 cells)

A. G. Gein

## Solution

Is it possible to make a cube of dimensions k×k×k from white and black cups 1×1×1 so that for each cube two of its neighbors have the same color as itself? (Two cubes are considered adjacent if they have a common edge.)

Answer. If k is even, it is possible, but if k is odd, it is impossible.

An example for even k is not difficult to construct: you can, for example, make a cube from alternating black and white blocks of dimensions 2 × 2 × 1 or from alternating layers depicted in figure 3 (this figure shows two sections of the cube k × k × k, k = 8, horizontal planes, located at a distance of one from each other).

Let us assume that even for odd k it is possible to form the required cube. Let's connect the centers of adjacent white cups with segments. From each center there will be two segments. Thus, we will get one or more closed broken lines, the links of which are parallel to the edges of the cube, and the total length is equal to the number of white cups.
