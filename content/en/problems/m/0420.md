## Condition

a) From the fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ it is possible to obtain from three fractions $\frac{a-b}{b}, \frac{a+b}{b}, \frac{b}{a}$. Is it possible to obtain the fraction $\frac{67}{91}$ from the fraction $\frac{1}{2}$ using such transformations?

6)* From a pair of fractions $\left( \frac{a+b}{b}, \frac{c+d}{d} \right), \left( \frac{a-b}{b}, \frac{c-d}{d} \right),$

Is it possible to get the following pairs from a pair of fractions $\left(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{5}{7}\right)$: $\left(\frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{9}\right); \left(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{8}\right); \left(\frac{4}{5}, \frac{7}{8}\right); \left(\frac{5}{19}, \frac{13}{50}\right); \left(\frac{39}{50}, \frac{60}{77}\right)$? (Here we consider the fraction $a/b$ simply as a pair of relatively prime numbers: “fractions” are allowed that have negative numbers or zero in the number or value of the layer.) c*) Try to find out what kind of fractions (respectively, pairs of fractions) can be obtained from the data in problems a) and 6).
