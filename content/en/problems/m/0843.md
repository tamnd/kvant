## Condition

In the upper triangle $ABC$ perpendiculars $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ to its plane on one side of it are restored, equal in length to the corresponding altitudes of the triangle. Prove that the perpendicular dropped from the intersection point of the planes $ABC_1, BCA_1$ and $CAB_1$ onto the plane $ABC$ falls at the center of the inscribed circle $ABC$ in triangle and is equal in length to its radius.

A. A. Yagubyants

## Solution

At the vertices of triangle ABC, the redistributions AA1, BB1 and CC1 to its plane on one side of it, equal in length to the corresponding altitudes of the triangle, have been restored. Prove that the redistribution of the planes ABC1, BB1 and CC1 onto the plane ABC falls at the center of the inscribed circle in triangle ABC and is equal in length to its radius.

◆ Let $Q$ be the intersection point of the planes ABC1, BB1 and CC1, $O$ its projection onto the ABC plane, $P$ the projection $O$ onto one of the sides of the triangle, for definition - onto the line BC. Obviously, triangle QOP is similar to triangle $A_1AH$, where $AH$ is the altitude of triangle ABC (see Fig. 1). Therefore, $|OP|:|OQ| = |AH|:|AA_1| = 1$, that is, $|OP| = |OQ|$. It is similarly proven that the distances from the point $O$ to the lines $AB$ and $AC$ are also equal to $|OQ|$. But this also means that $O$ is the center of the inscribed circle, $a$ $|OQ|$ is its radius.

Let us give a general correspondence between the distance $d$ from point $Q$ to plane ABC and the lengths $a$, $b$ and $c$ of segments AA1, BB1 and CC1:
$$\frac{d}{a} + \frac{d}{b} + \frac{d}{c} = 1$$ or $d = \frac{abc}{ab + bc + ca}$.
