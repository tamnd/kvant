## Condition

There are 3k offset points on a circle, dividing it into 3k arcs, of which k arcs have length 1, another k arcs have length 2, and the remaining k arcs have length 3. Prove that among the offset points there are two diametrically opposite ones. (9)

V. Proizvolov

## Solution

There are 3k points marked on the circle, dividing it into 3k points, of which k lines have length 1, if k lines have length 2, and the remaining k points have length 3. Prove that among the marked points there are two diametrically opposite.

Fig. 1.

Let us divide the arcs of length 2 and 3 formed into circles into equal parts of length 1 and mark these k+2k=3k division points in black, and the data according to the condition of 3k points in red. A total of 6k points will be marked - the vertices of a regular 6k-gon (Fig. 1). Next we will argue by contradiction.

Suppose that the statement of the problem is false, then each point of a given circle L0 is diametrically opposite a marked black point. Therefore, opposite each of the k dues of length 1 with red ends lies an arc of length 1 with black ends, that is, the middle part of one of the k dues of length 3 with red ends (see Fig. 1). Let us cut out two such opposite unit arcs from the circle L0 and make a new circle L1 (Fig. 2) from the remaining two large arcs; it will consist of 6k-2 unit arcs, the ends of which are 3k-1 red and the same number of black points. In this case, diametrically opposite points of circle L0 will go to opposite points of circle L1, so black will continue to be located opposite each point. Red dots will divide L1 into sections of length 1, 2 or 3, and there will be k-1 single sections - 1 less than before the restructuring, k+1 double sections - 1 more, and triple, like single sections, 1 less.
