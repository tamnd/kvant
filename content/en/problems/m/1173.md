## Condition

Three lines are drawn through one point inside a triangle of area $S$ such that two of them intersect each side of the triangle (see figure). Prove that the areas $S_1, S_2, S_3$ of the three resulting triangles satisfy the inequality
$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{9}{S}.$$
G. N. Zaitsev

## Solution

Through one point inside a triangle of area S, three straight lines are drawn so that each side of the triangle is intersected by two of them (Fig. 1). Prove that the areas $S_1, S_2, S_3$ of three educational triangles satisfy the inequality

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{9}{S}$$

We will prove two inequalities that strengthen the inequality of the problem in different ways.

1. Let us show that the inequality of the problem is valid for any three triangles with a common vertex O inside a given triangle ABC, the bases of which lie on its sides (Fig. 2).

It is clear that for a given point O, the smallest value of the left side of the inequality will be achieved for triangles OAB, OBC and OCA. Then $S = S_1 + S_2 + S_3$ and due to the inequality $a + 1/a \geq 2$ at $a > 0$

$$\frac{S}{S_1} + \frac{S}{S_2} + \frac{S}{S_3} = 3 + \left( \frac{S_2}{S_1} + \frac{S_1}{S_2} \right) + \left( \frac{S_3}{S_1} + \frac{S_1}{S_3} \right) + \left( \frac{S_3}{S_2} + \frac{S_2}{S_3} \right) \geq 3 + 3 \cdot 2 = 9$$

Equality is achieved when $S_1 = S_2 = S_3$, i.e. O is the point of intersection of the medians.

strong inequality:

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{18}{S}.$$

Let's connect the ends of these segments so that three more triangles are formed (Fig. 3); let their areas be equal to $T_1, T_2, T_3$. Expressing the areas of all six triangles in terms of their sides extending from the vertex $O$ and the angles between these sides, we easily obtain that $S_1S_2S_3 = T_1T_2T_3$. Now let’s take advantage of the fact that the arithmetic mean of several numbers is not less than their geometric mean:

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{3}{\sqrt[3]{S_1S_2S_3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt[6]{S_1S_2S_3T_1T_2T_3}} \geq \frac{3 \cdot 6}{S_1 + S_2 + S_3 + T_1 + T_2 + T_3} \geq \frac{18}{S}$$

(the total area of ​​all six triangles is, of course, no more than $S$). Equality here is sufficient when $S_1 = S_2 = S_3 = T_1 = T_2 = T_3 = S/6$, that is, when the given segments are medians of the triangle (Fig. 4).
