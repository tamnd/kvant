## Condition

The king walked around the chessboard $8 \times 8$, having visited each square exactly once, and with the last move returned to the original square. (The king moves according to the usual rules: in one move he can move along

⟦folio 61⟧

horizontal, vertical or diagonal to any adjacent field.) When his path was drawn, sequentially connecting the centers of the fields he passed through, a closed broken line without self-intersections was obtained. What is the shortest and greatest length it can have? (The side of the cell is 1.) (10 cells)
A. V. Klimov

## Solution

The king of the general chessboard is $8 \times 8$, raising on each pole once and returning with the last move to the original square. (The king follows the general rules: in one move he can move horizontally, vertically or diagonally to any adjacent square.) When we drew his path, sequentially connecting the valuable fields that he passes through, it turned out to be a closed broken line without self-intersection. What is the shortest and greatest length it can have? (The side of the cell is equal to one).

The king made 64 moves. Since each square of the move is equal to either one or $\sqrt{2}$, then the length of the entire path is set to be at least 64. (A path of length 64 actually exists - see the example at figure 7.) Let us now show that the length of the king’s path cannot be greater than $28 + 36 \sqrt{2}$. To do this, it is enough to show that the king’s path contains at least 28 “direct” moves. The distance is two adjacent king exits to the border of the field. It is easy to say that these field boundaries must be connected, otherwise the king's path turns out to be self-intersecting (see Fig. 8; construct a line to prove this statement). Since the color of these fields differs, and when moving diagonally the color does not change, then there must be a “direct” path between the two connecting exits to the border. Since there are 28 boundary fields, there are also 28 exits to the boundary, and therefore, there are indeed at least 28 straight moves. From the example of the king’s path given in
