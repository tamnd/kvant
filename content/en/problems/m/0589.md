## Condition

On the plane, given a set of $n$ vectors, the length of each of which does not exceed 1. Prove that by replacing some vectors of this set with their opposite ones, you can obtain a set of $n$ vectors, the sum of which has the length
a) does not exceed $\sqrt{n}$;
b) does not exceed $\sqrt{2}$. (9)

P. Gusyatinikov, A. Plotkin

## Solution

On the plane there is a set of n vectors, the length of each of which does not exceed 1. Prove that by replacing the non-actor vectors of this set with their opposite ones, we can obtain such a set

You can prove (try it!) that the orbit of any initial point $(x_0; y_0; z_0)$ - the set of points that are obtained from it by the action of the group of all our transformations - will be dense on a sphere of radius $\sqrt{x_0^2 + y_0^2 + z_0^2}$ with center $O$, that is, it contains points in any angularly small circle of the sphere.

More difficult tasks are to describe the exact orbit of a point, say point (1; 0; 0) or one of the points given in the conditions, or to specify an algorithm that allows one to find out from two points of the sphere whether one of them can be converted to another. We do not know the solution to these problems.

N. Vasiliev

a) Draw through the point $O$ inside the tetrahedron $ABCD$ the segments $|A_1B_1| \parallel |AB|$ and $|C_1D_1| \parallel |CD|$, where $A_1 \in (ACD)$, $B_1 \in (BCD)$, $C_1 \in (ABC)$, $D_1 \in (ABD)$ (Fig. 1). These segments define a plane intersecting faces $ABC$ and $ABD$ along straight lines $PN$ and $MQ$, parallel to $(AB)$, and faces $ACD$ and $BCD$ - along straight lines $MN$ and $PQ$, parallel $(CD)$. This means that the quadrilateral $MNPQ$ is a parallelogram. We have

$$\frac{|A_1B_1|}{|AB|} = \frac{|MQ|}{|AB|} = \frac{|DM|}{|AD|},$$

$$\frac{|C_1D_1|}{|CD|} = \frac{|MN|}{|CD|} = \frac{|AM|}{|AD|}.$$

From here

$$\frac{|A_1B_1|}{|AB|} + \frac{|C_1D_1|}{|CD|} = \frac{|DM|}{|AD|} + \frac{|AM|}{|AD|} = \frac{|DM|+|AM|}{|AD|} = 1.$$

So, the sum of the ratios of the lengths of the drawn segments to the lengths of the crossing edges of the tetrahedron parallel to them is equal to one. Therefore, the sum of all six ratios of the lengths of segments to the lengths of their parallel sides is equal to two.

Let $O$ be a point inside $\Delta ABC$, $A_1A_2$, $B_1B_2$, $C_1C_2$ be segments parallel to the sides of the triangle (Fig. 2). From the similarity of triangles $ABC$, $C_1CC_2$ and $A_1AA_2$ we obtain (for notation see figure 2)

$$\frac{|C_1C_2|}{|AB|} = \frac{|CC_1|}{|AC|} = \frac{x+y}{|AC|},$$

$$\frac{|A_1A_2|}{|BC|} = \frac{|AA_2|}{|AC|} = \frac{z+y}{|AC|}.$$

Also, $$\frac{|B_1B_2|}{|AC|} = \frac{x+z}{|AC|}$$ (see Fig. 2). That's why

$$\frac{|A_1A_2|}{|BC|} + \frac{|B_1B_2|}{|AC|} + \frac{|C_1C_2|}{|AB|} =$$

$$= \frac{z+y}{|AC|} + \frac{x+z}{|AC|} + \frac{x+y}{|AC|} = \frac{2(x+y+z)}{|AC|} = 2,$$

Q.E.D.
