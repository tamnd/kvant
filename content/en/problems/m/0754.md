## Condition

a) Are there polynomials $P = P(x, y, z), Q = Q(x, y, z), R = R(x, y, z)$ in the variables x, y, z such that the identity $(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-2x+1)^3R = 1?$ holds?
b) The same question for the identity $(x-y+1)^3P + (y-z-1)^3Q + (z-x+1)^3R = 1.$ (10)

P. Gusyatnikov, Y. Nesterenko

## Solution

Q=Q(x,y,z),
R=R(x,y,z)
from the variables x, y, z also, that the identity holds
(x-y+1)³P+(y-z-1)³Q+
+(z-2x+1)³R=1?
6) Same question for identity
(x-y+1)³P+(y-z-1)³Q+
+(z-x+1)³R=1.

a) Answer: does not exist. Note that the system of equations
x-y+1=0, y-z-1=0, z-2x+1=0
has a solution (x,y,z)=(1,2,1). When substituting these values ​​of the variables into the identity under consideration, the polynomials at the polynomials P, Q and R, and with them the entire part, vanish, and the identity is violated, no matter what polynomials we choose.

6) Answer: they exist. Let f=x-y+1,
g=y-z-1, h=z-x+1, then f+g+h=1 for all values ​​of x, y and z. Raising the last identity to the seventh power, we find that 1 is represented as a sum of terms of the form f⁵·g⁵·h⁵, where k, l, m≥0 and k+l+m=7, which means that at least one of the numbers k, l, m is not less than 3. Thus, each of these terms is divisible by either f³, or g³, or h³. Grouping all terms divisible by f³ and taking f³ out of brackets, we obtain f³·P. Grouping those of the remaining terms that are divisible by g³, we obtain g³·Q. The sum of the remaining terms is equal to h³·R (P, Q and R are some polynomials in x, y, z). As a result, we get an identity of the desired form:
f³·P+g³·Q+h³·R=1.

P. Gusyatnikov,
Yu. Nesterenko

First decision. Since the point $M$ lies inside the tetrahedron, the ray $DM$ passes inside its trihedral angle at the vertex $D$ and therefore intersects the plane $ABC$ at some point $P$, and the point $P$ is inside the triangle $ABC$ (Fig. 1). Let us assume that the cosine of the angles $AMD, BMD$ and $CMD$ is greater than 1/3 (otherwise there is nothing to prove). Then these angles themselves are less than $\varphi = \arccos(-1/3)$, which means the angles $AMP, BMP$ and $CMP$ are greater than $180^\circ - \varphi$. It follows that the lengths of the segments $AP, BP$ and $CP$ are greater than sin $\varphi$.

Indeed, let us take, for example, the segment $AP$. If the angle $AMP$ is acute, then sin $\overrightarrow{AMP} > \sin(\pi - \varphi) = \sin \varphi$. At the same time, $|AP|$ will not exceed the distance from point $A$ to line $MP$, which is equal to $|AM| \cdot \sin \overrightarrow{AMP} > 1 \cdot \sin \varphi$ (see Fig. 1). If the angle $AMP$ is obtuse or right, then $AP$ is the longest side in the triangle $AMP$ and $|AP| > |AM| = 1 > \sin \varphi$.

Since point $P$ lies inside triangle $ABC$, one of the angles $APB, BPC$ and $CPA$ is at least $120^\circ$. Let $\overrightarrow{APB} \ge 120^\circ$, then

$$|AB|^2 = |AP|^2 + |BP|^2 - 2|AP| \cdot |BP| \cdot \cos \overrightarrow{APB} > \sin^2 \varphi + \sin^2 \varphi - 2 \sin^2 \varphi \cdot \cos 120^\circ = 3(1 - \cos^2 \varphi) = \frac{8}{3}.$$

On the other hand, setting $\alpha = \overrightarrow{AMB}$, we have

$$|AB|^2 = |AM|^2 + |BM|^2 - 2|AM| \cdot |BM| \cos \alpha = 2(1 - \cos \alpha).$$

Thus, $2(1 - \cos \alpha) > 8/3$; hence $\cos \alpha < -1/3$.

Since the point $M$ lies inside the tetrahedron, no three of them are complementary. Let's expand the vector $e_4$ into the other three:

$$e_4 = y_1 e_1 + y_2 e_2 + y_3 e_3.$$

Note that the coefficients $y_1, y_2$ and $y_3$ are different. Indeed, the distance is, for example, $y_3$. The vector $e_4$ is the sum of the vector $y_1 e_1 + y_2 e_2 = \overrightarrow{MK}$, where $K$ is some point of the plane $ABM$, and the vector $\overrightarrow{KD} = y_3 e_3$, the collinear vector $e_3$ (see Fig. 2). The point $M$ lies inside the tetrahedron, therefore the points $C$ and $D$ lie on opposite sides of the plane $ABM$, which means that the vectors $\overrightarrow{MC} = e_3$ and $\overrightarrow{KD} = y_3 e_3$ are oppositely directed, that is, $y_3 < 0$.

Let us set $x_i = -y_i$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$ and $x_4 = 1$, then the resulting decomposition of the vector $e_4$ will be rewritten in the form

$$x_1 e_1 + x_2 e_2 + x_3 e_3 + x_4 e_4 = 0,$$

and all numbers $x_i$ are positive. Let $x_1$ be the largest number (1) scalar to the vector $e_1$:

$$x_1 + x_2 \cos \alpha_2 + x_3 \cos \alpha_3 + x_4 \cos \alpha_4 = 0,$$
