## Condition

The sequence of integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ is given by the conditions $a_1 = 1, a_2 = 12, a_3 = 20,$
$$a_{n+3} = 2a_{n+2} + 2a_{n+1} - a_n \quad (n = 1, 2, \dots).$$
Prove that for any number $n$ the number $1 + 4a_n a_{n+1}$ is the square of an integer.

P. Kozarev and S. Doychev (Volgaria)

## Solution

The sequence of integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ is given by the conditions $a_1=1, a_2=12, a_3=20, a_{n+3}=2a_{n+2}+2a_{n+1}-a_n$ ($n=1, 2, \dots$).

Prove that for any number $n$ the number $1+4a_na_{n+1}$ is the square of an integer.

| $a_n$ | $4a_na_{n+1}+1$ | $q_n$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 49 | 7 |
| 12 | 961 | 31 |
| 20 | 5041 | 71 |
| 63 | 39409 | 197 |
| 154 | . . . | . . |

By writing out the first few terms of the sequence, as done in the margin, we can prove that $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$, where $q_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n$; for example, with $n=1$ and $n=2$ the following equalities are obtained: $1+4 \cdot 12=(20-12-1)^2, 1+4 \cdot 12 \cdot 20=(63-20-12)^2$.

Let us use the induction method: we will prove that the assumption $1+4a_{n-1}a_n=q_n^2$- implies the equality $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$ (for $n=2, 3, \dots$). As follows from the problem conditions, $q_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n=a_{n+1}+a_n-a_{n-1}=q_{n-1}+2a_n$. That's why

$$1+4a_{n-1}a_n=q_{n-1}^2=(q_n-2a_n)^2=q_n^2-4a_n(a_{n+1}+a_n-a_{n-1})+4a_n^2=q_n^2+4a_na_{n-1}-4a_na_{n+1},$$

whence $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$.

From our solution it is clear that for any initial terms for the sequence with the concurrent condition $a_{n+2}+a_n=2(a_{n+1}+a_n)$ the value $(a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n)^2-4a_na_{n+1}$ is constant and does not depend on $n$.

N. V. Vasiliev
