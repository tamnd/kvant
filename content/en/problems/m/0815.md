## Condition

There are $4k$ points placed on the circle, numbered in random order with the numbers 1, 2, ..., $4k$.

a) Prove that these points can be connected $2k$ by pairwise disjoint segments so that the difference in numbers at the ends of each segment does not exceed $3k-1$.

## Solution

** There are 4k points on the distance circle, numbered in random order as 1, 2, ..., 4k.

a) Prove that for any distance of numbers it is possible to connect 2k points with pairwise disjoint segments so that the difference in number at the end of each segment does not exceed 3k-1.

6) Construct an example of number placement showing that the number 3k-1 in part a) cannot be replaced by a smaller one.

6) Let us use the same partition as in the proof of point a). Let's place 2k points on the upper and lower semicircles and name the points of the upper semicircle with numbers of the first group in the following order: 1, 3k+1, 2, 3k+2, ..., k, 4k, and the points of the lower semicircle with numbers of the second group: put the number 3k next to 1, the rest - arbitrarily (see figure). Let us prove that for any connection of a point on disjoint segments, the difference in numbers at the ends of one of them will be 3k - 1 or more.

If some segment connects two points a and b of the upper semicircle, then, obviously, on the arc ab there are two connecting points connected by the segment. The difference in their numbers by construction is equal to 3k - 1 or 3k.

Otherwise, any point on the upper semicircle must be connected to a point on the lower semicircle. Consequently, each of the segments connects the numbers of different groups. The segment coming from point 1 should end on the lower semicircle, and the segment coming from the connecting point 3k should end on the upper one. Since they cannot intersect according to the condition, this must be the same segment. It is the one we are looking for, since the difference between the numbers at its ends is 3k-1.
