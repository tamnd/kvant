## Condition

The function y=f(x) for all x is defined, continuous and satisfies the conditions
$$f(f(x))=f(x)+x.$$
a) Find two such functions f.
6)* Prove that there are no other such functions.

## Solution

The function $y=f(x)$ for all $x$ is defined, continuous and satisfies the condition $f(f(x))=f(x)+x$. (*)
a) Find two such functions $f$ (not identically equal to 0). b)* Prove that there are no other such functions.

a) Answer: $y=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}x$ and $y=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}x$. These functions can be found by assuming that $f(x)=\lambda x$, substituting this function in the condition
$$ \lambda^2x=\lambda x+x $$
and solving the quadratic equation $\lambda^2-\lambda-1=0$ (the roots of this equation are $(1\pm\sqrt{5})/2$).
6) As can be seen from the solution to problem a), it is sufficient to show that any function $f$ that satisfies the conditions of the problem has the form $f(x)=\lambda x$. To do this, let us first establish some properties of the function $f$.
1) $f(0)=0$. (Let $f(0)=c$, then $f(c)=f(f(0))=f(0)+0=c$, therefore $f(c)=f(f(c))=f(c)+c$, that is $c=0$.)
2) At different points the function $f$ takes different values. (If $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$, then $f(f(x_1))=f(f(x_2))$, that is, $f(x_1)+x_1=f(x_2)+x_2$ and $x_1=x_2$.) In particular, $f$ has a single root $x=0$.
From these properties and the continuity of the function $f$ it follows that:
3) on each of the receipts $x>0$ and $x<0$ the function $f$ retains its sign, and when passing through zero its sign changes (otherwise the values ​​$f$ close to zero will be taken at least twice - to the right and to the left of the point $x=0$) and
4) the function $f$ has an inverse function $g$ (defined, of course, on the range $f$; below we see that $f$ takes all real values).
