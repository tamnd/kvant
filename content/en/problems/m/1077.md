## Condition

Let $p_n(k)$ be the number of permutations of a set of $n (n \geq 1)$ elements having exactly $k$ fixed points. Prove that:

a) $\sum_{k=0}^{n} k \cdot p_n(k) = 0 \cdot p_n(0) + 1 \cdot p_n(1) + \dots + n \cdot p_n(n) = n!$

b) $\sum_{k=0}^{n} (k-1)^2 p_n(k) = n!$

At less than A permutation of a finite set $S$ is a one-to-one mapping $f$ of the set $S$ onto itself; the number of permutations of a set of $n$ elements is $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot n$. The element $a$ of the set $S$ is called a fixed point of the permutation $f$ if $f(a) = a$.
