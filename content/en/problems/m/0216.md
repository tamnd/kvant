## Condition

N people do not know each other. It is necessary to introduce some of them to each other in such a way that no three people have the same number of acquaintances. Prove that this can be done for any N. (8 grades)

G. A. Galperin

## Solution

** $N$ people do not know each other. You need to introduce dtype to some of them so that no three people have the same number of acquaintances. Prove that this can be done for any $N$.

Let's solve the problem using the method of mathematical induction. For $N < 3$ the statement of the problem is obvious.

Let us assume that we were able to introduce $k$ people, so that no three of them have an equal number of acquaintances. Let's add $k+1$th. Let us indicate a way to introduce the $(k+1)$th person so that in a group of the $(k+1)$th person, no three people still have an equal number of acquaintances. If among the first $k$ people there is a person who knows everyone else, then $k+1$ will not introduce anyone to anyone. If there is no such thing, then we will introduce the $(k+1)$th to everyone.

In the first case, $(k+1)$th has no acquaintances, and each of the others has at least one acquaintance - the one who was familiar with everyone. In the second case, $(k+1)$ has $k$ acquaintances, the number of acquaintances of each of the first $k$ people increased by one, but none of them had $k$ acquaintances.
