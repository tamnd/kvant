## Condition

An arbitrary triangle is given. Prove that a) it is possible to construct three circles with centers at its vertices, touching each other in pairs (at points K, L, M - see Fig. 1);
b) if through the middle of each arc KL, LM, MK lying inside the triangle, draw a tangent to it, then four triangles are formed, the area of ​​one of which (the central one) is equal to the sum of the areas of the other three.

A. A. Gorbachev

The caital line drawn through the midpoint $C'$ for KL (with center C) cuts off from $\triangle ABC$ an equilateral triangle equal in area to the quadrilateral $CKOL$: the height $CC'$ cuts this equilateral triangle into two right triangles on the left, equal to $\Delta KCO = \Delta LCO$. Here it should also be noted that this tangent (at figure is red) always intersects sides CA and CB (angle COB is obtuse, since the half-sum of two angles B and C of triangle ABC is less than $90^\circ$, therefore the hypotenuse CO of triangle COK is less than CB; similar is $\angle COA > 90^\circ$, and therefore $CO < CA$). A similar rearrangement for the other two red tangents turns the equilateral triangles with altitudes $AA'$ and $BB'$ into quadrilaterals $ALOM$ and $BMOK$. Thus, the sum of the areas of three equilateral triangles with red bases is equal to the area of ​​triangle $ABC$. This implies the last statement of the problem: the area not covered by triangles is equal to the area covered by them twice.
