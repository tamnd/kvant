## Condition

Let $AD$ be the height in the right triangle $ABC$, $\angle A = 90^\circ$. A line passing through the centers of the circles inscribed in triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ intersects sides $AB$ and $AC$, respectively, at points $K$ and $L$. Prove inequality $S_{ABC} \geq 2S_{AKL}$.

## Solution

Let $AD$ be the height in the right triangle $ABC$, $\angle A=90^\circ$. The line passing through the centers of the circles inscribed in triangles $ABD$ and $ACD$ intersects the sides $AB$ and $AC$

Let us prove that $AK=AL=AD$. Let's sequentially rotate around point $D$ by angle $45^\circ$ and prepare with center $D$ and coefficient $DO_1/DB$ (see figure). Then the points $B$ and $A$ will go, respectively, to the centers $O_1$ and $O_2$ of the circles inscribed in the triangles $DBA$ and $DAC$ ($\angle BDO_1=\angle ADO_2=45^\circ$, and $DO_2/DA=DO_1/DB$, since the triangle $DAC$ is obviously similar to the triangle $DBA$). Therefore, the angle between

straight lines BA and $O_1O_2$ is equal to 45°, i.e. $\angle LKA=45^\circ$, which means that triangle KAL is a right triangle. Triangles $AO_1K$ and $AO_1D$ are congruent: they have a common side $AO_1$ and equal angles ($\angle AKO_1=\angle ADO_1=45^\circ$, $AO_1$-angle bisector KAD). Therefore $AL=AK=AD$.

Let $\angle ABC=\beta$, then $AB=AD/\sin \beta$, $AC=AD/\cos \beta$ and $S_{ABC}=AB \cdot AC/2=AD^2/\sin 2\beta \geqslant AD^2=2S_{AKL}$.

Equality is achieved when sin 2β=1, i.e. for equilateral triangle ABC ($\beta=45^\circ$).

This task is an example of an integration exchange. At Optimis it was represented by Greece. In a slightly different formulation: “Prove that AK = AL = AD” it is contained in the book by I. F. Shargin “Problems in Geometry. Planimetry".
