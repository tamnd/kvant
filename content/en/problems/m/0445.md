## Condition

The centers of identical disjoint circles are at the centers of regular hexagons covering the plane as indicated in figure 5. Let $M$ be a polygon with vertices at the centers of the circles. Let's color those circles or their parts (others) that lie inside $M$ red. Show that the sum of the degree magnitudes of the red arcs is $C \cdot 180^\circ$, where $C = C(M)$ is an integer, and give this number a geometric interpretation.

A. Sosinsky

and $\bigcup B_j \subseteq \bigcup A_i$ (*)

(here $\setminus$ is the polygon difference or complement symbol), then

$$C(M) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} C(A_i) - \sum_{j=1}^{l} C(B_j).$$

Below we will show that any polygon $M$ (with vertices at nodes) can be represented in the form (*), where $A_i$ and $B_j$ are triangles. Therefore, it is enough to prove the statement of the problem for triangles $T$ with vertices at the nodes.

So, let's show that $C(T)$ is an integer. Let us inscribe the triangle $T$ into the parallelogram $Π$ with sides parallel to the sides. Since vertex $T$ is in nodes, vertex $Π$ is also in nodes. Let us imagine the parallelogram $Π$ as a union of triangle $T$ and triangles $T_\pi$, whose two sides are parallel to the sides of the lattice (Fig. 9, a), 6). It is obvious that for any parallelogram $Π$ with vertices at the nodes and side lengths $a$ and $b$ (for the unit of length we choose the length of the side of the rhombus generating the lattice, the numbers $a$ and $b$ are integers), the number $C = C(\Pi) = 2ab$ (П is divided into $ab$ generating the lattice of rhombuses $R$ (Fig. 10), and for each point of the rhombus $C(R) = 2$: in a rhombus the sum of the hail magnitudes of the arcs is $360^\circ = 2 \cdot 180^\circ$). So, for each $Π$ with vertices at the nodes, the number $C(\Pi)$ is an integer ($T_\pi$ can always be completed to a parallelogram $Π$ (Fig. 11); then $2C(T_\pi) = C(\Pi) = 2ab$, revealing $C(T_\pi) = ab$ is an integer). For an arbitrary triangle $T$ with vertices at the nodes we have: $C(T) = C(\Pi) - \Sigma C(T_\pi)$, i.e. $C(T)$ is also an integer. So, for a triangle with vertices at the nodes, the statement is proven.

Note, by the way, that from the equality $C(\Pi) = 2ab$ it immediately follows that $C(\Pi)$ is proportional to the area $Π$:

$$S(\Pi) = ab \sin 60^\circ,$$

and $C(\Pi)/S(\Pi) = 1/\sqrt{3}$ ($S(T_\pi))$ is the area of ​​triangle $T_\pi$.

Posny $C(T) = (S(\Pi) - \Sigma S(T_\pi))/\sqrt{3} = S(T)/\sqrt{3}$, and we get that for every triangle $T$ with vertices at nodes $C(T)/S(T) = 1/\sqrt{3}$. Since the function $C(M)$ and the area $S(M)$ are additive, then for an arbitrary polygon $M$

$$C(M) = S(M)/\sqrt{3}.$$

This is the geometric interpretation of the number $C$: for any polygon $M$ the number $C(M)$ is proportional to its area.
