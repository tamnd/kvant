## Condition

A 9 ¥ 9 checkered square is made of matches, the side of each square is one match. Petya and Vasya take turns removing a match, Petya begins. The winner is the one whose move leaves no more 1 ¥ 1 squares. Who can act in such a way as to ensure victory, no matter how his opponent plays? A. Shapovalov

## Solution

A 9 ¥ 9 checkered square is made of matches, the side of each square is one match. Petya and Vasya take turns removing a match, Petya begins. The winner is the one whose move leaves no more 1 ¥ 1 squares. Who can act in such a way as to ensure victory, no matter how his opponent plays?

Answer. Vasya. Note that there will always be an odd number of matches left before Vasya's move. It is clear that the winner will be the one who gets the position when there are one or two adjacent squares (on the side). Therefore, it is enough for Vasya not to leave such a position behind, and then he will win, since a draw is impossible. Let's show how he can do this in different cases. 1) There are more than three squares left. He will take the last match, ruining no more than one square. 2) There are three squares left. He will take a match not from them, and if there are no such matches, then due to the odd number of matches it is clear that two squares are adjacent, and the third is not adjacent to them, then he will ruin one of the adjacent squares. 3) There are two squares left and they are not adjacent. Due to the odd number, there is a match that is not included in them, which Vasya will take. All positions have been considered. A. Shapovalov
