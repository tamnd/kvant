## Condition

Out of 100 triangles, is there always at least one such that it can completely cover the other 99?

G. A. Gurevich

## Solution

Is there always at least one triangle out of 100 that can completely cover the other 99?

This sequence is built according to the following principle. First, we define a sequence $a_n$ of the length of the longest side of the triangles $T_n$ so that for any overlap of triangles $T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_{n-1}$ on $T_n$ there remains an uncovered fixed (not dependent on $n$) for the area of the triangle $T_n$ - this can be ensured by choosing $a_n$, sufficiently large compared to $a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1}$. Then you need to select a sequence of areas $S_n$ of triangles $T_n$ so that the total area of ​​triangles $T_{n+1}, T_{n+2}, \ldots$ is less than the area of ​​the uncovered part of triangle $T_n$ - this can be achieved by quickly decreasing the sequence of areas.

Let us give an example of one such sequence of triangles. Let $T_1$ be a regular triangle with sides $a_1=1$; $T_n$ with $n>1$ is an equilateral triangle with base $a_n=4^{n-1}$ and area $S_n=S_1/4^{n-1}$, where $S_1=\sqrt{3}/4$ is the area of ​​triangle $T_1$ (height $h_n$ of triangle $T_n$ is $2S_n/a_n=2\sqrt{3}/4^{2n-1}$). The distance triangles are $T_1$, $T_2$, ..., $T_n$. Each of the triangles $T_i$, $1\leq i\leq n-1$ is placed in a strip of width no more than $a_i$, perpendicular to the base of the triangle $T_n$ (see Fig.ok). The area covered by this area in $T_n$ is less than $a_i \cdot h_n$, so the total area covered by triangles $T_1$, ..., $T_{n-1}$ in $T_n$ is less than $(a_1+a_2+\dots+a_{n-1})h_n$ (this estimate is very rough, but it will be enough for us). Since

$$a_1+a_2+\dots+a_{n-1}=\frac{1}{3}(4^{n-1}-1)<\frac{1}{3}a_n,$$

The area of ​​the triangle $T_n$ less than $a_nh_n/3=2S_n/3$ is covered, which means that the third area $T_n$ remains uncovered. At the same time

$$S_{n+1}+S_{n+2}+\dots=$$

$$=S_1\left(\frac{1}{4^n}+\frac{1}{4^{n+1}}+\dots\right)=\frac{S_1}{4^{n}(1-1/4)}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{S_1}{4^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{3}S_n.$$

Therefore, the triangles $T_1$, ..., $T_{n-1}$, $T_{n+1}$, $T_{n+2}$, ... cannot be covered by the entire triangle $T_n$.
