## Condition

a) 10 points dividing the circle into 10 equal duels, connected in pairs by 5 chords. Are there necessarily two chords of the same length among them?
b) * 100 points dividing the circle into 100 equal duels,

⟦folio 48⟧

Obviously, $F$ preserves the triple of vertices of any regular triangle with side $r$. Let us prove that $F$ also preserves the quadruple of vertices of any rhombus $ABCD$ with side $r$ and diagonal $|BD|=r$ (hence it follows: $|A'C'|=|AC|=\sqrt{3}$ for $r=1$). It is enough to prove that $C' \neq A'$. To do this, consider together with the rhombus $ABCD$ the equal rhombus $AKLM$, such that $|CL|=r$ (Fig. 3). If $C'=A'$, then $|C'L'|$ is equal to 0 or $\sqrt{3} r$, which is impossible, or $|C'L'|=|CL|=r$.

So, $F$ preserves the quadruple $ABCD$, and therefore the “triangular lattice”, that is, the set of vertices of regular triangles with side $r$ covering the plane, and its subsets - the integral set of points on the line, spaced from each other at a distance $mr$, $m=1, 2, \dots$ (Fig. 4). Therefore $F$ preserves the distance $mr$.

Let us now prove that for any two points $X$ and $Y$ $|XY|-2r \leqslant |X'Y'| \leqslant |XY|+r$ (*)

(the second inequality for $r=1$ is point 6) of the problem). Let $(m-1)r \leqslant |XY|<mr$, where $m$ is a natural number. Then the points $X$ and $Y$ can be connected by a broken line $XX_1X_2\dots X_{m-1}Y$ from $m$ by links of length $r$ (Fig. 5, a) and $|X'Y'| \leqslant |X'X_1'|+|X'_1X_2'|+\dots+|X'_{m-1}Y'|=mr \leqslant |XY|+r$. On the other hand, if $X_m$ is a point in the meadow $XY$ such that $|XX_m|=mr$ (Fig. 5, b), then $|X_mY| \leqslant r$ and $|X'Y'| \geqslant |X'X_m'|-\dots|X'_mY'|$. But $|X'X_m'|=|XX_m|=mr$, and $|X'_mY'| \leqslant |X_mY|+r \leqslant 2r$, therefore $|X'Y'| \geqslant mr-2r \geqslant |XY|-2r$.

Finally, we prove that if $F$ preserves distance 1, then we can specify an arbitrarily small distance $r$ that is preserved under this mapping. From here, using inequalities (*), we will derive statements d) and e).

Consider the ray $l$ with origin $O$ and two sequences of its points $(A_m)$ and $(B_n)$: $|OA_m|=m, |OB_n|=n\sqrt{3}$, $m,n=1,2$, ... The mapping $F$, preserving distance 1, preserves, as we know, each of these sequences. Moreover, it also preserves their union, that is, the rays $O'A_m'$ and $O'B_n'$ coincide. (Otherwise (Fig. 6) one can find columns $n$ such that $|A_m'B_n'|>2$ for all $m$. At the same time $|A_mB_n| \leqslant 1$ for some $m$, and then by (*) $|A_m'B_n'| \leqslant 2$.) It follows that the mapping $F$ preserves the distance $r=|m-n\sqrt{3}|$ for all integers $m$, $n$. It remains to apply the following important lemma; among the numbers $|m-n\sqrt{3}|$ one can find a number less than any positive $\varepsilon$. (A similar statement is true for the set of numbers $|m-n\alpha|$, where $m,n$ are integers, for any irrational $\alpha$.) To prove it, it is enough to note that the number $(2-\sqrt{3})^N$ has the form $a_N-b_N\sqrt{3}$, where $a_N$ and $b_N$ are integers, and for a sufficiently large $N$ is a number less than $\varepsilon$.

So, if $F$ preserves distance 1, then inequalities (*) are satisfied for arbitrarily small positive $r$. Therefore, $|XY| \leqslant |X'Y'| \leqslant |XY|(\text{пункты г})$ and e)), that is, $|X'Y'|=|XY|$ for any two points $X$ and $Y$. In other words, $F$ is simply moving the plane.

We leave the reader as a task to prove a similar theorem in space. (Note that new ideas are needed here, since it is impossible to replace space with regular terrahedra alone! Try to prove that if the mapping $F$ of space into itself preserves the distance $r$, then it preserves not only a regular terraed and a “bipyramid” composed of two terraeds, but also a regular octad with edge $r$.)
