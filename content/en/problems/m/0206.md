## Condition

Given an infinite sequence of numbers. Prove that for any natural number $n$ coprime with the number 10, it is possible to indicate in the sequence a group of consecutive digits such that the number written by these digits is divisible by $n$.

V. Ufnarovsky

## Solution

Given an infinite sequence of numbers. Prove that for any natural number $n$, coprime to the number 10, in a sequence it is possible to indicate such hundred consecutive digits that the number written by these digits is divisible by $n$.

We will assume that we are given an infinite sequence of digits $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_i, \dots$, written from left to right. Let's look at these numbers:

$$a_m, a_{m-1}a_m, a_{m-2}a_{m-1}a_m, \dots, a_1a_2\dots a_m$$

(the bar, as usual, denotes the decimal notation of a number). If $m$ is large enough - greater than $n$ - then it will find two numbers $a_{m-1}a_m, a_{m-2}a_{m-1}a_m, \dots, a_1a_2\dots a_m$ where $i < j$ that give the same remainder when divided by $n$. Indeed, when dividing by $n$, $n$ different remainders are possible: 0, 1, 2, ..., $n-1$. Therefore, if $m > n$, then among the $m$ remainders obtained when dividing the numbers written above by $n$, he will definitely find two identical remainders: after all, if all the remainders were different, then there would be no more of them $n$, but $m < n$.
