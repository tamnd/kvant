## Condition

A total pay B on average √2 rubles. per day. They stipulated that on the nth day A will receive an integer number aₙ rubles (aₙ is equal to 1 or 2) so that the amount received in the first n days (a₁+a₂+...+aₙ) is as close as possible to the number n√2.

For example, a₁=1, a₂=2, a₃=1, a₄=2, a₅=1. Prove that the sequence a₁, a₂, a₃, ... is non-periodic.

A. K. Crowd

## Solution

And we promise to plan $B$ on average $\sqrt{2}$ rubles per day. They stipulated that on the $n$ day $A$ we will receive an integer $a_n$ rubles ($a_n$ is equal to 1 or 2) with the maximum that the amount received in the first $n$ days ($a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$) be as close as possible to the number $n\sqrt{2}$. For example, $a_1 = 1$, $a_2 = 2$, $a_3 = 1$, $a_4 = 2$, $a_5 = 1$.

Prove that the sequence $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ is non-periodic.

The idea of ​​the proof is as follows. Suppose that, starting from some day with number $N + 1$, this sequence is periodic and $T$ is a period, that is, $a_{N+1} + a_{N+2} = a_{N+1}$, $a_{N+2} + a_{N+3} = a_{N+2}$, etc. Then for every $T$ days following the day $N$, $B$ will receive the same integer.

$a_{N+1} + a_{N+2} + \dots + a_{N+T} = c$ (7) rubles. Therefore, on average (over a long period of time) he will receive $\frac{c}{T}$ rubles per day. But from the rule for constructing the sequence $a_n$, on average he should receive $\sqrt{2}$ rubles per day. We come to a contradiction: equality

$$\frac{c}{T} = \sqrt{2}$$

impossible, since $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, and $c$ and $T$ are natural.
