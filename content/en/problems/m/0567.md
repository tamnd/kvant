## Condition

The natural numbers $p$ and $q$ are coprime. The segment $[0; 1]$ is divided into $p+q$ identical segments (Fig. 1). Prove that each of these segments, except the two extreme ones, contains exactly one of $p+q-2$ numbers

$$\frac{1}{p}, \frac{2}{p}, \dots, \frac{p-1}{p},$$

$$\frac{1}{q}, \frac{2}{q}, \dots, \frac{q-1}{q}.$$

Let's give two solutions.

First decision. It follows from the condition that each of the numbers $p$ and $q$ is coprime with the number $n=p+q$, so no two of the point $i/p, j/q, k/n$ (other than 0 and 1) coincide. Since $1/p > 1/n$ and $1/q > 1/n$, any two from the point $i/p$ lie in different segments $\left[\frac{k}{n}; \frac{k+1}{n}\right]$ and any two from the point $j/q$ too. You just need to prove that some two points $i/p$ and $j/q$ cannot fall into the same segment $\left[\frac{k}{n}; \frac{k+1}{n}\right]$ ($k=1, 2, \dots, n-2$). But this immediately follows from the fact that the fraction $\frac{k}{n} = \frac{i+j}{p+q}$ lies between $\frac{i}{p}$ and $\frac{j}{q}$ (see, on Fig. 1).

$p=8, q=5, n=8+5=13$

example, figure 2: the angular coefficient of the diagonal of a parallelogram is interlocked between the angular coefficients of its side*).

Second solution. Let's draw a rectangle with dimensions $p \times q$ cells on checkered paper and draw a diagonal $OE$ (Fig. 3) ​​in it - it will play the role of segment $[0; 1]$ of our problem. The lines of one direction (blue) divide it into $p$ equal parts, the other (red) - into $q$ equal parts. Let's draw another series of parallel straight lines through the tops of the cells - at an angle $45^\circ$ to the grid lines (on figure these are black straight lines $x+y=k$, where $k=1, 2, \dots, p+q-1$). They divide $OE$ into $n=p+q$ equal segments. The statement of the problem becomes almost obvious. In fact, on $OE$ there is necessarily a black dot between any two synchromatic points: after all, crossing some cell, $OE$ must also intersect its black diagonal. (You can say this instead: between any points of intersection of $OE$ with adjacent black lines there is a point of intersection with a blue or red line.)

In this solution, the mutual primality of the numbers $p$ and $q$ ensures that $OE$ does not pass through grid nodes other than $O$ and $E$ (looking at our little figure one might doubt this).

*) The fact that the “mediana” of two fractions $i/p$ and $j/q$ lies between them was used in the article “Close Fractions” («Kvant», 1975, No. 8).

⟦folio 40⟧

Problem M567 allows for a substitutional generalization. Let α and β be any positive numbers related by the relation 1/α + 1/β = 1.

Let us mark on the number line free numbers of the form iα and jβ (i ∈ Z, j ∈ Z). Then each segment [k; k+1] axis (k ∈ Z), neither of whose ends contains the offset number, contains exactly one of the offset numbers iα, jβ.

Our problem is equivalent to this fact for rational α and β: we need to take α = n/p, β = n/q (the role of the segment [0; 1] will now be played by the segment [0; n]). The same fact (for inrational α and β) was mentioned recently in the solution to problem M538 («Kvant», 1979, no. 11), very similar to our second solution to M567.
