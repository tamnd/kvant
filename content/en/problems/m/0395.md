## Condition

At the top of a regular $n$-gon with center at point $O$ the numbers (+1) and (-1) are placed. In one step, the cut changes the sign of all numbers located at the top of any regular $k$-gon with center $O$ (at the same time, we also allow 2-gons, taking under the 2-gon a segment with the midpoint at the point $O$). Prove that in case a), 6), c) there is an initial arrangement of (+1) and (-1) such that in no number of steps can a set of only (+1) be obtained from it:

a) $n=15$,
b) $n=30$,
b) $* n$ - any number greater than 2.
d) $*$ Try to find out for an arbitrary $n$ how many different arrangements (+1) and (-1) there are such that none of them can be obtained from any other in several steps. Prove, for example, that for $n=2100$ there are $2^{480}$ such arrangements. (10 grades)

S. Fomin

## Solution

At the vertices of a regular $n$-line with the center at point O there are numbers (+1) and (-1). In one step, it is cut to change the sign of all numbers standing at the vertices of any regular $k$-line with center O (at the same time, we also allow 2-line, catching under the 2-line a segment with the middle at point O). Prove that in case $a), b), c)$ there is an arrangement called the arrangement (+1) and (-1) such that from it in no number of steps can one obtain a set of only (+1):

a) $n=15$,
b) $n=30$,

Note that the final arrangement depends on the initial arrangement and which polygons were selected, but does not depend on the order in which this was done. Indeed, each vertex contains the same number as in the initial arrangement if this vertex is in an even number of polygons, and a number opposite in sign if it is in an odd number; the order is therefore immaterial. In addition, it is clear that it makes no sense to replace signs more than once in the same polygon.

We call two arrangements equivalent if they can be obtained from each other (if arrangement $N$ can be obtained from arrangement $M$, then vice versa, $M$ can be obtained from $N$). The set of all arrangements equivalent to some arrangement is called an equivalence class; they are equivalent (since one can go from any of them to any one through an arrangement by ordering this class), but arrangements from different classes are not equivalent.
