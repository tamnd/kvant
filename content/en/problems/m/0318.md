## Condition

In triangle $ABC$ bissertrices $[AD]$ and $[CE]$ are drawn. Prove that $|CE| \cdot |AB| = |AD| \cdot |BC|$ if and only if at least one of two conditions is satisfied: 1) $|AB| = |BC|$; 2) $\widehat{ABC} = 60^\circ$.

A. P. Sabin

## Solution

In triangle ABC the bisectors AD and CE are drawn. Prove that $|CE| \cdot |AB| = |AD| \cdot |BC|$ between the molco modulus and the coefficient is at least one of two conditions: 1) $|AB| = |BC|$; 2) $\widehat{ABC} = 60^\circ$.

Let us introduce standard notation: $a$ is the length of side BC, $c$ is the length of side AB, $l_a$ is the length of the bisector of angle A, $l_c$ is the length of the bisector of angle C. We denote the values ​​of the angles at the vertices of the corresponding by $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. In addition, we denote the values ​​of angle AEC by $\varphi_1$, and angle ADC by $\varphi_2$ (Fig. 1). From the considered triangle AEC and ADC it follows that

$$\alpha + \frac{\gamma}{2} + \varphi_1 = \pi,$$

$$\frac{\alpha}{2} + \gamma + \varphi_2 = \pi.$$

Additionally, we can express the area of ​​triangle ABC in two ways: $S = \frac{1}{2} al_a \sin \varphi_2$ and $S = \frac{1}{2} cl_c \sin \varphi_1$, since $h_a = l_a \sin \varphi_2$ and $h_c = l_c \sin \varphi_1$. From here

$$al_a \sin \varphi_2 = cl_c \sin \varphi_1.$$

Let us now show that from the correspondence

$$al_a = cl_c$$

at least one of the conditions given in the problem follows. From (2) and (3) it follows that

$$\sin \varphi_2 = \sin \varphi_1.$$

The sines can be equal in two cases (since $0 < \varphi_i < \pi$):

$$\varphi_1 = \varphi_2,$$

$$\varphi_1 = \pi - \varphi_2.$$

If (5) is fulfilled, then from (1) we obtain:

$$\alpha + \frac{\gamma}{2} + \varphi_1 = \pi,$$

$$\frac{\alpha}{2} + \gamma + \varphi_1 = \pi.$$

Subtracting one correspondence from the other, we find that $\alpha = \gamma$, that is, triangle ABC is equilateral, $a = c$.

Let correspondence (6) be satisfied, then from (1) it follows that

$$\alpha + \frac{\gamma}{2} + \varphi_1 = \pi,$$

$$\frac{\alpha}{2} + \gamma + \pi - \varphi_1 = \pi.$$

Adding these correspondences, we get

$$\frac{3}{2} \alpha + \frac{3}{2} \gamma = \pi,$$
