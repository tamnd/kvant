## Condition

The cross section of a regular tetrahedron is a quadrilateral. Prove that the perimeter of this quadrilateral is greater than 2$a$, but less than 3$a$; where $a$ is the length of the tetrahedron edge.

V. Proizvolov, A. Savin

## Solution

The cross section of a regular metrahedron is a reguolnik. Prove that the perimeter of the meter hedron is not less than 2a, but less than 3a, where a is the length of the edge of the metrahedron.

Fig. 1.

Let us first prove that the perimeter of the quadrilateral is $p \geq 2a$. The development of a tetrahedron (Fig. 2) is shown in figure 2. During the development, the contour of the quadrangular section turns into a red broken line $K_1K_2$. It can be seen that perimeter $p$ will be equal to 2a only when the cutting plane is parallel to two intersecting edges of the tetrahedron, and in all other cases $p > 2a$.

The proof of the inequality $p < 3a$ can be easily obtained by immediately considering the families of its sections of the tetrahedron as parallel planes. Let the section plane move parallel to itself; we consider such an interval of values ​​of $l \in [b_1; b_2]$ (see Fig. 2) that for $b_1 < l < b_2$ the section remains a quadrilateral, and for $l = b_1$ and $l = b_2$ it becomes a triangle (Fig. 3). The length of each side of the section, and therefore the perimeter $p$ of the section, will be a linear function of $l: p(l) = kl + c$ (k, c are some constants). This function takes on the greatest value at the ends of the segment $[b_1; b_2]$, when the section is a triangle. The length of any segment with endpoints on the sides of an equilateral triangle is not greater than the length of its side $a$, and therefore $p < 3a$.
