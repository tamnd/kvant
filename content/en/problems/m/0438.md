Let's move on to problem c). We will need the following fact.

Let $P_m(x)$ be a polynomial in $x$ of degree $m$. Then an arbitrary expression $x^k(x+1)^p P_m(x)$ has the form
$$x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} P_{m+1}(x),$$ where $P_{m+1}(x)$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree $m+1$ ($k$ and $p$ are any real numbers). Really,

$$(x^k(x+1)^p P_m(x))' = kx^{k-1}(x+1)^p P_m(x) +$$
$$+ px^k(x+1)^{p-1} P_m(x) + x^k(x+1)^p P'_m(x) =$$
$$= x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} [k(x+1)P_m(x) + pxP_m(x) +$$
$$+ x(x+1)P'_m(x)] = x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} P_{m+1}(x),$$

where $P_{m+1}(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $m+1$, written in square brackets (recall that the degree of a polynomial $P'_m(x)$ is equal to $m-1$).

Suppose now that the equation
$$ax^k(x+1)^p + bx^l(x+1)^q + cx^m(x+1)^r = 1$$

$$+ x^{m-1}(x+1)^{r-1} C_1(x) = 0$$
(the numbers $k, p, l, q, m, r$ can be any real; $A_1(x), B_1(x), C_1(x)$ are polynomials of the first degree).

Dividing both sides of the equation $(4')$ by $x^{m-1}(x+1)^{r-1}$ (this is a “legal” operation: we are only interested in positive roots!), we get the equation
