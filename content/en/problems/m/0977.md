## Condition

Is it possible to obtain $x$ using the operations of addition, subtraction and multiplication from the polynomials $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ if a) $f(x)=x^2+x$, $g(x)=x^2+2$; b) $f(x)=x^2+x$, $g(x)=x^2-2$; c) $f(x)=2x^2+x$, $g(x)=2x$; d) $f(x)=2x^2+x$, $g(x)=x^2$.

S. I. Kublanovsky

## Solution

Is it possible to obtain $x$ using the operations of addition, subtraction and multiplication from the polynomials $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ if: a) $f(x)=x^2+x$, $g(x)=x^2+2$; b) $f(x)=x^2+x$, $g(x)=x^2-2$; c) $f(x)=2x^2+x$, $g(x)=2x$; d) $f(x)=2x^3+x$, $g(x)=x^2$.

ANSWER: in problem 6) it is possible, in other problems it is impossible.

To solve problem 6) note that $f-g=x+2$, therefore $(f-g)^2-2(f-g)-f=(f-g)(f-g-2)-f=x(x+2)-x^2-x=x$.

In the remaining problems, we will use the fact that if the polynomial $x$ is expressed in terms of $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ using the operations of addition, subtraction and multiplication, then any number $a$ is expressed in the same way through the numbers $f(a)$ and $g(a)$.

a) Take $x=-1$, then $f(x)=0$, $g(x)=3$. Both

These numbers are divisible by 3, therefore any number made up of them by addition, subtraction and multiplication must be divisible by 3 and, therefore, different from -1.

c) The fractional number $x=1/2$ cannot be expressed in the considered way through the integers $f(1/2)=1$ and $g(1/2)=1$.

d) Take $x=\sqrt{2}$, then $f(x)=5\sqrt{2}$, $g(x)=2$. Any combination of numbers $5\sqrt{2}$ and $2$ allowed by the conditions of the problem has the form $m+5n\sqrt{2}$, where $m$ and $n$ are integers. But the equality $\sqrt{2}=m+5n\sqrt{2}$ is impossible, since it would imply that $\sqrt{2}=m/(1-5n)$ is a rational number.
