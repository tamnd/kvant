## Condition

Prove that if $a, b$ and $c$ are the side lengths of the triangle, then

$$2 \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a} \right) \geq \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 3.$$

L. D. Kurlyandchik

## Solution

Prove that if $a, b$ and $c$ are the length of a side of a triangle, then

$$2\left(\frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a}\right) \geq \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 3.$$

Let us rewrite the inequality being proved in the form

$$\frac{2a}{b} - \frac{b}{a} + \frac{2b-a}{c} + \left(\frac{2}{a} - \frac{1}{b}\right)c \geq 3.$$

We fix $a$ and $b$ and find the minimum of the sum of the last two terms on the left side as a function of $c$; in this case, we can assume that $c$ does not exceed $a$ and $b$, since the initial

This inequality does not change under cyclic permutations of the values ​​$a, b, c$. We have:

$$\frac{2b-a}{c} + \left( \frac{2}{a} - \frac{1}{b} \right)c = (2b-a)f(c),$$

where $f(x) = 1/x + x/ab$. Since $2b-a \geq b+c-a > 0$, and the function $f(x)$ kills at $0 < x \leq \sqrt{ab}$ (graph of $f(x)$ for $a > b$ is given at figure) and $f(a) = f(b) = 1/a + 1/b$, at $c \leq \min(a, b) \leq \sqrt{ab}$

$$(2b-a)f(c) \geq (2b-a)\left( \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} \right) = \frac{2b}{a} - \frac{a}{b} + 1.$$

Thus, the left side of the inequality (*) is not less than $\frac{2a}{b} - \frac{b}{a} + \frac{2b}{a} - \frac{a}{b} + 1 = \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 1$, and this number is very large, not less than 3.
