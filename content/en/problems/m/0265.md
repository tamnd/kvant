## Condition

The diagonal $d$ of a rectangular parallelepiped forms angles $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ with its edges $a, b$ and $c$. Prove that $\alpha + \beta + \gamma$ is less than $π$.
M. L. Gerver

## Solution

** *Diagonal d of a rectangular parallelepiped forms angles* $\alpha$, $\beta$ *and* $\gamma$ with its edges a, b and c. *Prove that* $\alpha + \beta + \gamma$ *less than* $\pi$.

⟦figure⟧
Fig. 13.

⟦column⟧

Let us present the solution sent by *Andrey Vyatchin* (Pavlovo, Gorky Region). Let us arrange three equal parallelepipeds as shown in figure 13. Then $\sphericalangle BOC = 2\alpha$, $\sphericalangle AOC = 2\beta$, $\sphericalangle AOB = 2\gamma$. Point $O$ does not lie in the plane passing through points $A$, $B$, $C$ (check). This means $2\alpha + 2\beta + 2\gamma < 2\pi$ (by the theorem on the sum of plane angles of a trihedral angle), that is, $\alpha + \beta + \gamma < \pi$.

The majority of readers who submitted correct solutions reduced the problem to checking the inequality $\sin(\alpha + \beta + \gamma) > 0$.

To conclude, here are two problems - check that they are equivalent to problem M265.

1. For any $\triangle ABC$ we can calculate the following sum:
$$S = \cos^2 A + \cos^2 B + \cos^2 C.$$
Prove that $S = 1$ for right triangles, $S < 1$ for acute triangles and $S > 1$ for obtuse triangles.

2. If $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $0 < y < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\sin^2 x + \sin^2 y < 1$, then $\sin^2 x + \sin^2 y < \sin^2 (x+y)$.
