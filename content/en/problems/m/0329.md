## Condition

A convex $n$-gon is placed in a square with its side 1. Prove that you find three vertices $A, B, C$ of this $n$-gon and also that the area of ​​the triangle $ABC$ is less than $8/n^2$.
G. Shmelev, 10th grade student (Yaroslavl)

## Solution

A convex n-space is placed in a square with side 1. Let us prove that we find three vertices A, B, C of the n-line and that the area of ​​triangle ABC is less than 8/n².

Fig. 5.

Let us denote by $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ the length of the side of our n-opener, and by $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$ the values ​​of its external angles. Let $S_i$ be the area of ​​the i-th triangle (from the sides $a_i$ and $a_{i+1}$ - see Fig. 5, $i = 1, 2, \dots, n-1$), $S_n$ be the area of ​​the triangle from the sides $a_n$, $a_1$. We have: $2S_i = a_i a_{i+1} \sin \alpha_i$, $i = 1, \dots, n-1$, $2S_n = a_n a_1 \sin \alpha_n$. Let $S$ be the smallest of the areas of these triangles. Then

$$2S \leq a_i a_{i+1} \sin \alpha_i,$$

where

$$(2S)^n \leq \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2 \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i < \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i^{2*}).$$

that is

$$2S < \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i \right)^{2/n}.$$

But

$$\left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a_1 \cdots a_n} \leq \frac{a_1 + \cdots + a_n^{**}}{n} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i}{n},$$

pose

$$2S < \left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i}{n} \right)^2.$$

Let $p_i$ and $q_i$ be the lengths of the projections of the i-th side of the n-spacer onto the vertical and horizontal sides of the square. Then $a_i \leq p_i + q_i$, that is $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \leq \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i + \sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i \leq 4$.

Posetom

$$2S < \left( \frac{4}{n} \right)^2,$$

where

$$S < \frac{8}{n^2}.$$

The resulting estimate is quite rough - we discarded $\prod_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i$ from the very beginning, estimating this product as one. Let's clarify this assessment. We have:

$$(2S)^n \leq \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2 \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i,$$

that is

$$2S \leq \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i \right)^{2/n} \cdot \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i \right)^{1/n} \leq \frac{16}{n^2} \cdot \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i}{n}.$$

*) Here $\Pi$ is the sign of the product: $\prod_{i=1}^{n} a_i = a_1 \cdots a_n$.
**) We used the inequality about the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean.

Since $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i = 2\pi$ (as the sum of the external angles of a $n$-gon), we can interpret $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i$ as the satisfactory area of ​​a $n$-gon inscribed in a unit circle with angles $\alpha_i$ at the vertex $Q$ - the center of the circle (Fig. 6). Therefore $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sin \alpha_i < 2\pi$, and therefore

$$S \leq \frac{16\pi}{n^3}.$$
