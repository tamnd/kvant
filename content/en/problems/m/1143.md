## Condition

The mass of each of the 101 weights arranged around a circle is a natural number, and their total mass is 300 g. Prove that from this set one or more weights located in a row can be selected with a total mass of 200 g.

B. B. Prozovolyov

## Solution

The mass of each of the 101 mushrooms located in a circle is a natural number, and their total mass is 300 g. Prove that from this set one can choose one of several mushrooms located in a row with a total mass of 200 g.

Let us mark 300 points on the circle, dividing it into 300 equal parts ($1^\circ 12'$ each), and assign arcs of $m$ such parts to each mushroom of mass $m$; the ends of these arcs (located around the circle in the same order as the mushrooms) are called points, the remaining 300-101=199 points are called black. Let's consider all equilateral triangles inscribed in a circle, one of which has a red vertex. If each of them had two other vertices that were black, then there would be at least $2 \cdot 101 = 202$ black points in total. Therefore, it will find a triangle whose two vertices are red. The larger arc (at $240^\circ$) with ends at these red vertices corresponds to mushrooms with a total mass of 200 g, the smaller one ($210^\circ$) corresponds to mushrooms with a mass of 100 g.

V. V. Proizvolov
