## Condition

The circle is divided by points $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ into $n$ equal parts, each of which is painted a certain color. Two arcs (with ends at the points of division) are called identically colored if, with some rotation of the circle, one of them completely, including the color of each part, coincides with the other. (For example, on figure the 2 arcs $A_2A_6$ and $A_6A_{10}$ are equally colored.)

Prove that if for each point of the partition $A_i$ one can indicate two disjoint, identically colored arcs with a common endpoint $A_i$,

Fig. 1.

then the entire circle can be divided into several equally colored arcs, that is, the coloring is “periodic”. Consider first the case where there are only two colors: say, red and black.

G. A. Gureevi
