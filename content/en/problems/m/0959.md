## Condition

There are air connections between some cities in the country. Prove that it is possible to close no more than $1/(k-1)$ part of the airlines in such a way that among any $k$ cities there are two that are not connected by airlines if $k=3$; 6) $k$ - any natural number.

A. A. Razborov

## Solution

In the country, aviation connections have been established between certain cities. Prove that it is possible to close no more than 1/(k - 1)th part of the avatoria in such a way that among any k cities there are two that are not contiguous to each other if

To do this, note that the number $n$ of domestic airlines (for the chosen “minimum” partition) leaving any city $A$ is not greater than the number $n_i$ of airlines leading from this city to any group of cities $G_i$ ($i=1,...,k-1$): if it turned out that $n > n_i$, we would transfer city $A$ to the group $G_i$ and thereby reduce the number of domestic airlines by $n - n_i$. Therefore, the total number of airlines leaving the city $A$ is at least $(k-1)n$. Summing up such inequalities for all cities and taking into account that each airline connects two cities, we obtain that $2N \geq 2(k-1)N_{\text{вн}}$. Let us now close all domestic airlines of our “minimum” partition. Then, out of any $k$ cities, at least two will fall into one group and will not be connected to each other, and no more than $N/(k-1)$ airlines will be closed.

A. A. Razborov
