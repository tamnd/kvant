## Condition

Let's put
$$x_n = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n}.$$
a) Prove the existence of the limit
$$\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} (x_n - \ln n).$$
b) Prove that for any $n$, $m \in N$
$$\gamma < x_n + x_m - x_{nm} \subseteq 1.$$
b) Find $\gamma$ to the nearest 0.1.

V. Yasinsky

## Solution

Let's put
$$x_n = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n}.$$
a) Prove the existence of the limit
$$\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} (x_n - \ln n).$$
b) Prove that for any $n, m \in N$
$$\gamma < x_n + x_m - x_{nm} \leq 1.$$
c) Find $\gamma$ to the nearest 0.1.

a) From Weierstrass’s theorem (“Algebra and Principles of Analysis 9”, paragraph 32) it is easy to deduce: if $(a_n)$ and $(\beta_n)$ are increasing and killing sequences, $a_n < \beta_n$ for all $n$ and $\lim_{n \to \infty} (\beta_n - a_n) = 0$, then the sequences $(a_n)$ and $(\beta_n)$ have a common limit $c = \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \beta_n$ and $a_n < c < \beta_n$ with all $n$.

For a given sequence $(x_n)$, consider two sequences:
$$\gamma_n = x_n - \ln (n + 1), \quad \Gamma_n = x_n - \ln n.$$
It is easy to see that they satisfy the conditions of the lemma
$$(\gamma_n < \Gamma_n; \lim_{n \to \infty} (\Gamma_n - \gamma_n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \ln \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right) = 0;$$
$$\gamma_n - \gamma_n^{-1} = \int_{n-1}^{n} \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{x+1} \right) dx > 0,$$
since $\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{x+1} > 0$ at $x \in [n-1; n]$; similar to $\Gamma_n - \Gamma_{n-1} < 0$. Therefore the sequences $(\gamma_n)$ and $(\Gamma_n)$ have a common limit $\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} \gamma_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \gamma_n < \gamma < \Gamma_n$ for all $n$.

So the limit $\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} \Gamma_n$ exists. (This was first pointed out by L. Euler. The number $\gamma$ is called Euler's constant.)
b) It is easy to see that $x_n + x_m - x_{nm} = (x_n + x_m - x_n) - (x_{2n} - x_n) - (x_{3n} - x_{2n}) - \dots - (x_{nm} - x_{n(m-1)}) \leq x_m - n \cdot \frac{1}{2n} - n \cdot \frac{1}{3n} - \dots - n \cdot \frac{1}{mn} = 1$.

On the other hand, $\Gamma_{nm} \leq \Gamma_n$, that is
$$x_{nm} - \ln (mn) \leq x_n - \ln n, \quad x_n - x_{nm} - \ln n + \ln (mn) \geq 0.$$

But $\Gamma_m > \gamma$, that is
$$x_m - \ln m > \gamma.$$

Adding inequalities (*) and (**), we obtain
$$x_n - x_{nm} + x_m > \gamma,$$
Q.E.D.

b) To calculate $\gamma$ with an accuracy of 0.1, we use the results of solving the problem.

Since $\gamma_n < \gamma < \Gamma_n$, then, taking $n = 100$ members of the sequence, we get $0,57 < \gamma < 0,58$, therefore $\gamma \approx 0,5$.

The approximate value of the number $\gamma$ is known: $\gamma = 0,577$...
