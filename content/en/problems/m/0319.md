## Condition

A circle $\gamma$ and a point $P$ inside it are given on the plane. Consider the set of tetrahedra $ABCD$, all four of which are congested, and the triangle $ABC$ is attached to the circle $\gamma$ so that its medians intersect at the point $P$.

a) At what position of the point $P$ inside $\gamma$ do tetrahedra also exist?

6) Prove that the vertices $D$ of such tetrahedra are located at one of two fixed points in space (symmetrical to the relative plane).

## Solution

*A circle $\gamma$ and a point $P$ inside it are given on the plane. Consider the set of tetrahedra $ABCD$, in which all four faces are congruent, and the triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in the circle $\gamma$ such that its medians intersect at the point $P$.*

*a) At what position of the point $P$ inside $\gamma$ do such tetrahedra exist?*

*b) Prove that the vertices $D$ of such tetrahedra are located in one of two fixed points in space (symmetrical with respect to a given plane).*

◆ We will call tetrahedra whose faces are all congruent $K$-tetrahedra. Let us establish several properties of such tetrahedra.

$1^\circ$. *The sum of three plane angles at each vertex of a $K$-tetrahedron is $180^\circ$.*

Indeed, these three angles are congruent to the angles of the same triangle $ABC$, opposite its three different sides $AB$, $BC$, $CA$ (this reasoning is also valid for triangles $ABC$, in which some two sides are equal in length), and the sum of the angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$ (Fig. 3).

$2^\circ$. *The faces of the $K$-tetrahedron are acute triangles.*

In fact, in any trihedral angle, each plane angle is less than the sum of the other two. For the angles of a triangle, this condition is satisfied if and only if it is acute.

$3^\circ$. *The development of the $K$-tetrahedron has the form of an acute triangle, in which the middle lines are drawn.*

This follows immediately from $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ (Fig. 4). Of course, the converse is also true: *any acute-angled triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ with midlines $AB$, $BC$, $CA$ drawn in it can be considered as a development of a $K$-tetrahedron.* (Think why it is impossible to form a tetrahedron from an obtuse $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$.)

Note that when we add a tetrahedron from the development, fixing the face $ABC$ and rotating the other three faces relative to the edges $AB$, $BC$, $CA$, then the points $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$ will move along circles (lying in planes perpendicular to $ABC$), and their projections onto the plane $ABC$ will move along the altitudes of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ up to the point of their intersection $H$. So, we also got the following property:

$4^\circ$. *The projection $H$ of the vertex $D$ of the $K$-tetrahedron $ABCD$ onto the plane $ABC$ lies at the point of intersection of the altitudes of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$.*

Note that in any tetrahedron its “medians” - the segments connecting the vertices with the centers of gravity (the intersection points of the medians) of opposite faces - intersect at one point M - the “center of gravity” of the tetrahedron - and are divided by this point in the ratio $3:1$, counting from the vertex (Fig. 5; the segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges also pass through the same point M).

$5^\circ$. *In a $K$-tetrahedron, the center of gravity coincides with the center of the circumscribed sphere.*

There is a displacement of space in which the tetrahedron $ABCD$ is juxtaposed with itself such that the face $DCB$ becomes $ABC$ (i.e. $A—D$, $B—C$, $C—B$, $D—A$). During this movement, only two points of the tetrahedron surface remain motionless: these are the midpoints of the edges $AD$ and $BC$. It is clear that with such a movement, the center of the circumscribed ball $O$ and the intersection point of the medians $M$ are motionless (since the tetrahedron itself is combined with itself). If points $M$ and $O$ are different, then all points of the line $MO$ must also be fixed. This means that this line passes through the midpoints of the edges $AD$ and $BC$. In exactly the same way (considering other self-alignments of the tetrahedron) we will prove that the line $MO$ also passes through the midpoints of other edges. But this is impossible. This means that points $M$ and $O$ coincide.

Let us denote the center of gravity of the triangle $ABC$ by $P$, the center of its circumcircle (projection of the point $O=M$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$) by $Q$, and the projection of the vertex $D$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$ by $H$. figure 6 shows all these points lying in a plane perpendicular to the face $ABC$ and passing through the center of the ball $O$ and the vertex $D$; $E$ and $F$ are the points of intersection of this plane with the circumscribed circle of the triangle $ABC$, the radius of which will be denoted by $\rho$. Since $|DP| = 4|OP|$, $|OF|^2 = |OQ|^2 + \rho^2 = |OD|^2 = 9|OQ|^2 + |QH|^2$, we get

$$|QH|^{\mathbf{2}} + 8|OQ|^{\mathbf{2}} = \rho^{\mathbf{2}}.$$

Let us formulate the property we need:

6°. *In any K-tetrahedron* $|QH| = 3|PQ|$ and $|QH|^2 + |DH|^2/2 = \rho^2$ (since $|DH| = 4|OQ|$).

Now it is easy to solve both points problem M319.

a) The set of centers of gravity of acute triangles inscribed in a given circle $\gamma$ with center $Q$ and radius $\rho$ will be the interior of a concentric circle of radius $\rho/3$. (Prove this using figure 7 and the fact that for a right triangle the center of gravity is exactly at a distance $\rho/3$ from the center of the hypotenuse $Q$.) According to 2° and 3°, this is exactly the set of positions that point $P$ can occupy.

b) According to 6°, for a given circle $\gamma$ and a given position of point $P$ the position of the projection $H$ of the vertex $D$ onto the plane $\triangle ABC$ and its distance $|DH|$ from this plane are uniquely determined.

In conclusion, let us make a few comments.

I. For those who want to understand in detail how the “symmetry group” of the $K$-tetrahedron (found in the proof of 5°) is structured, we give advice to consider a parallelepiped, the non-adjacent vertices of which are the vertices of the tetrahedron (Fig. 8). *For a K-tetrahedron, this parallelepiped is rectangular*. From this we can obtain a number of other characteristic properties of $K$-tetrahedra (see solution problem M9 in «Kvant» No. 9, 1970).

II. The set of all vertices D of K-metrahedrons ABCD, the face ABC of which is connected to a given circle γ, represents the surface of an ellipsoid of rotation *) passing through γ (Fig. 9). This immediately follows from the formula $|QH|^2 + |DH|^2/2 = \rho^2$ and the fact that the point H runs through the weight circle bounded by the circle γ.

III. The main equality $|HP| = 4|QP|$ can be proved without relying on the property $5^\circ$, if we use the following planetary messages: point H is the center of the circumcircle $\triangle A_2B_2C_2$, in which $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$ is a triangle of the middle lines, and point Q is the center of the circumcircle $\triangle ABC$, formed by the middle lines of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$. It is clear that all three triangles have a common center of similarity P - the point of intersection of the medians, and the similarity coefficient $\triangle A_2B_2C_2$ and $\triangle ABC$ is equal to 4.
