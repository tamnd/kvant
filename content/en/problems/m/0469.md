## Condition

a) The equation $x^4 + ax^3 + bx + c = 0$ has four different real roots. Prove that $ab < 0$.

b*) The equation $x^n + a_1x^{n-1} + \dots + a_{k-1}x^{n-k+1} + a_{k+1}x^{n-k-1} + \dots + a_n = 0$ has $n$ different real roots. Prove that $a_{k-1}a_{k+1} < 0$.

V. Vavilov

## Solution

a) The equation $x^4 + ax^3 + bx + c = 0$ has several different real roots. Let us prove that $ab < 0$.

b) Equation $x^n + a_1x^{n-1} + \dots + a_{k-1}x^{n-k+1} + a_{k+1}x^{n-k-1} + \dots + a_n = 0$ has many real roots. Let us prove that $a_{k-1}a_k+1 < 0$.

We will provide various proofs for points a) and b). The idea is based on the following statements: if all the roots of a polynomial are real and different, then all the roots of the derivative of a polynomial are also real and different. Indeed, a polynomial of degree $n$ is a continuous (and differentiable) function on the line. Consequently, at some internal point of the interval, the ends of which are any two adjacent roots of the original polynomial, it takes either the maximum or minimum value (Fig. 4, a). At the extreme point, the derivative of the polynomial vanishes. Thus, in any interval any two adjacent roots of a polynomial have its derivative (Fig. 4, b). Consequently, the derivative of a polynomial of degrees $n$, for which all roots are real and distinct, has exactly $n-1$ real roots, all of which are distinct.
