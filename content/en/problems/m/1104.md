## Condition

In the ABCD tetrahedron, the faces ABC and BCD are perpendicular, ∠BAC=90°. Prove that from segments whose lengths are equal to the product of the lengths of the opposite edges of the tetrahedron, a right triangle can be formed.

V. N. Dubrovsky

Let us now prove that if mn is odd and $k^2$ rectangles $m \times n$ are placed next to gaps one square wide, as shown in figure 4, then with $k \geq 4$ it is impossible to cover the remaining part of the domino plane $1 \times 2$.

Let P be the entire rectangle of $2N-1=(mk+k-1)\times(nk+k-1)$ cells containing $k^2$ rectangles. Let's color $N$ cells P in a checkerboard pattern, including the conditions (inside the rectangles - black, outside - blue). Each of the $k^2$ rectangles has an “extra” black cell, so among the $N-1$ uncovered cells or P clouds there are $k^2-1$ more white cells than blue ones. But each domino covers one white and one blue square.

Let's assume that this entire area is covered with dominoes. Some of them - but not more than $4(k-1)$ - may go (by one cell) beyond P, but since $k^2-1>4(k-1)$ at $k \geq 4$, our assumption leads to a contradiction.

Let's prove that

$$(DA \cdot BC)^2=(DB \cdot CA)^2+(DC \cdot AB)^2.$$

This equality is equivalent to a similar equality for the projection $D_1$ of the point $D$ onto the plane ABC (Fig. 1):

$$D_1A^2 \cdot BC^2=D_1B^2 \cdot CA^2+D_1C^2 \cdot AB^2.$$

In fact, subtracting the second from the first equality, by the Pythagorean theorem for triangles $DD_1A$, $DD_1B$ and $DD_1C$ we obtain

$$D_1D^2 \cdot BC^2=D_1D^2 \cdot CA^2+D_1D^2 \cdot AB^2,$$

and this equality is always true, since in triangle ABC the angle $A$ is right.

The proof of equality (*) can be verified as follows. Let us describe a circle around triangle ABC and denote by $E$ the point of its intersection with the line $AD_1$ (different from the point $A$; see Fig. 2). Triangles $D_1BE$ and $D_1AC$ are similar ($\angle ED_1B=\angle CD_1A$, $\angle BED_1=\angle ACD_1$), so $BE:BD_1=CA:AD_1$, or

$$CA^2 \cdot BD_1^2=BE^2 \cdot AD_1^2;$$

similarly,

$$AB^2 \cdot CD_1^2=CE^2 \cdot AD_1^2.$$

Therefore, since $\triangle BEC$ is straight-headed,

$$CA^2 \cdot BD_1^2+AB^2 \cdot CD_1^2=(BE^2+CE^2) \cdot AD_1^2=BC^2 \cdot AD_1^2,$$

Q.E.D.

Note that equality (*) is, in fact, a special case of the so-called “Stewart formula” for the lengths of the segment $AD_1$ (Fig. 2) in an arbitrary triangle ABC:

$$AD_1^2=p \cdot AB^2+q \cdot AC^2-pq \cdot BC^2,$$

where $p=CD_1/BC$, $q=BD_1/BC$.
