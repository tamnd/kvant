## Condition

A wire stab of radius $R$ has a conductive jumper located along the diameter. Capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ (Fig. 4) are included in the left and right semicircles. A magnetic field with induction $B(t)=B_0 t/T$, perpendicular to its plane, is placed in the buildup linearly with time. At some point, the jumper is removed and then the field is stopped changing. Find the chest mounted on the capacitors.

G. V. Fedogovich

## Solution

A wire stake with radius $R$ has a conductive jumper located along the diameter. The left and right semicircles include capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ (see Fig.ok). The ring is placed in a build-up of a magnetic field linearly with time with induction $B(t) = B_0 t/T$, a variable of its plane. At some point in time, the jumper is removed and then the field is stopped changing. Start the charges installed on the capacitors.

A ring with a conductive jumper can be represented as two circuits - $AC_1B$ and $BC_2A$ - each with an area of ​​$\pi R^2/2$. Magnetic flux, arbitrary in each circuit, changes over time as

$$\Phi(t) = \frac{\pi R^2}{2} B_0 \frac{t}{T}.$$

In this case, the induced emf is maintained in each circuit

$$\mathcal{E} = - \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = - \frac{\pi R^2 B_0}{2T}.$$

This is the potential difference between the plates of each capacitor (see Fig.ok). Consequently, the charges on the capacitor plates when the magnetic field changes will be constant and equal, respectively,

$$Q_1 = \mathcal{E} C_1, \quad Q_2 = -\mathcal{E} C_2.$$

After the jumper is removed and the field stops changing, the charges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ will be redistributed between $C_1$ and $C_2$ until the potential difference between the connecting plates of the capacitors becomes equal to zero, i.e. until the voltages on the capacitors are equal. The charges established on the plates will be determined by the conditions

$$\frac{Q'_1}{C_1} = \frac{Q'_2}{C_2},$$

$$Q'_1 + Q'_2 = Q_1 + Q_2$$

(the last equality follows from the law of conservation of charge). Solving (2) and (3) together, using expression (1) for $\mathcal{E}$, we find $Q'_1$ and $Q'_2$:

$$Q'_1 = \mathcal{E} C_1 \frac{C_1 - C_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \frac{\pi R^2 B_0 C_1}{2T} \frac{C_1 - C_1}{C_1 + C_2},$$

$$Q'_2 = \mathcal{E} C_2 \frac{C_1 - C_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \frac{\pi R^2 B_0 C_2}{2T} \frac{C_2 - C_1}{C_1 + C_2}.$$

G. V. Fedotovich
