## Condition

The electric stove contains three spirals with a resistance of $R=120$ ohm each, connected in parallel to each other. The plate is switched on by a series resistor $r=50$ ohm. How will the time required to heat a kettle of water on this stove to boiling change when one of the spirals is recut?

N. Slovodechkiy

## Solution

The electropolite contains three spirals with a resistance of $R=120$ Ohm each, connected in parallel to each other. The heater is connected in seven series with a resistance of $r=50$ Ohm. How will the time required for cutting on this spiral with water to boil change if one of the spirals burns out?

Fig. 13.

Let $v_0$ be the speed of the river current, $v_1$ be the speed of the boy relative to the current. Then the speed of the boy relative to the shore is

$$v = v_0 + v_1.$$

The boy's velocity vector relative to the current can have different directions, but its end must necessarily lie on a circle of radius $|v_1|$ (Fig. 13). From figure it is clear that the vector $v$ makes a minimum angle with the coastline in the case when it is directed tangent to the circle - the geometric locus of the ends of possible vectors $v_1$. In this case, the boy will obviously be carried away by the current to the minimum possible distance. From figure we find that the boy must swim at an angle to the shoreline equal to

$$\alpha = \arccos \frac{|v_1|}{|v_0|} = \arccos \frac{1}{2} = 60^\circ.$$

O. Savchenko

The resistance of a tile with three parallel-connected spirals is $R_1 = R/3 = 40$ ohms. After one spiral burns out, the resistance of the tile becomes $R_2 = R/2 = 60$ Ohm.

Until the spiral burns out, current $I_1 = \frac{U}{R_1 + r}$ flows through the circuit (U is the network voltage). After burnout - $I_2 = \frac{U}{R_2 + r}$.

The amount of heat $Q$ required to cut a kettle of water to boiling is, in the first case, released in the tile during the time

$$t_1 = \frac{Q}{I_1^2 R_1} = \frac{Q (R_1 + r)^2}{U^2 R_1},$$

in the second case - in time

$$t_2 = \frac{Q}{I_2^2 R_2} = \frac{Q (R_2 + r)^2}{U^2 R_2}.$$

Let's find the ratio of time $t_1$ and $t_2$:

$$\frac{t_1}{t_2} = \frac{(R_1 + r)^2 R_2}{(R_2 + r)^2 R_1} = \frac{243}{242}.$$

Hence $t_2 = \frac{242}{243} t_1$.
