## Condition

A ball of radius $R$ and mass $m$ moves along a horizontal surface under the action of a constant tension force $\vec{F}$ of a light non-tensile thread. The point of attachment of the thread is at a height $h$ above the surface (Fig. 2), the thread is horizontal all the time, the ball moves forward. At what maximum friction coefficient is this possible? What is the uniform acceleration of the ball?

L. G. Markovich

## Solution

A ball of radius $R$ and mass $m$ moves along a horizontal surface under the action of a constant tension force $F$ of a light, inextricable thread. The point of attachment of the thread is at a height $h$ above the surface (see Fig.1), the thread is horizontal all the time, the ball moves forward. Is this possible at every maximum friction coefficient?
What is the acceleration of the ball?

◆ When the ball moves, the thread can be parallel to the horizontal surface only if there is friction between the ball and the surface. Otherwise (in the absence of friction), a non-zero moment of force $F$ (relative to the point $O$) would rotate the ball, that is, the ball moves without translation.

Let us write down the condition for the translational motion of the ball (the sum of the moments of the forces of the relative point $O$ acting on the ball is equal to zero):
$$F_{\text{тр}} R = F(R - h).$$
From here
$$F_{\text{тр}} = F \left(1 - \frac{h}{R}\right).$$
On the other side,
$$F_{\text{тр}} = \mu mg.$$
From the last two equations we find $\mu$:
$$\mu = \frac{F}{mg} \left(1 - \frac{h}{R}\right).$$
We find the acceleration of the ball from Newton's law:
$$ma = F - F_{\text{тр}} = F \frac{h}{R},$$
where
$$a = \frac{F}{m} \frac{h}{R}.$$
L. G. Markovich
