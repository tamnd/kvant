## Condition

Rubber-cord fabric is made of strong elastic threads (cord) filled with soft elastic rubber. The cords form an angle $2\alpha$ with each other (Fig. 2). A circular cylinder is made from such fabric, with metal covers attached to the ends (Fig. 3). Air is pumped inside the cylinder. In experiments, it was noticed that when air is pumped, the cylinder takes one third of the shape shown in figure 4. The shape depends on the size of the angle $2\alpha$ between the threads. What angle value does each of the shapes correspond to?

## Solution

A cool cylinder is made from makoe cloth, and metal caps are attached to the ends of the cylinder (Fig. 7). Air is drawn inside the cylinder. Now consider the equilibrium of a square of the canvas from the side l, which is “visible” at an angle φ from the cylinder axis, with φ ≤ 1. This square is acted upon by a horizontal pressure force F₃ ≈ pl², and from the side of the rest of the canvas is a horizontal force F₄ = 2Q₁l sin φ/2 ≈ Q₂lφ (Fig. 9). Since φ = l/R, then F₄ = Q₂l²/R.

At equilibrium, F₃ = F₄, that is, pl² = Q₂l²/R, whence Q₂ = pR = 2Q₁.

The forces Q₁ and Q₂ represent the equivalent tension forces of the threads. Let's find them, assuming that the tension of the threads is equal to T, and the side of the cell formed by the threads is equal to a. It is easy to see that the horizontal distance between the intersection points of the threads in this case is equal to 2a cos α, and the vertical distance is 2a sin α. This means that on the horizontal side of the square from side l there are n₁ = 2l/2a cos α = l/a cos α threads, and on the vertical side there are n₂ = l/a sin α threads. The vertical equilibrium Q₁l of the tension forces of the threads, estimated, is equal to n₁T cos(π/2 - α) = n₁T sin α = Tl tan α/a, horizontal - Q₂l = n₂T cos α = Tl/a tan α (Fig. 10).

In the case of a quadratic surface, Q₂ = 2Q₁, so T/a tan α = 2T tan α/a, whence tan²α = 1/2, α ≈ 35° 16′. At α > 35° 16′ the surface takes the same shape as at figure 8.6, and at α < 35° 16′ - as at figure 8.8.
