## Condition

The electrostatic generator (Fig. 3) consists of a non-conducting cylinder on which foil strips $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are glued, outer plates $O_1$ and $O_2$, fixed current collectors $T_1$ and $T_2$ on the outside and a fixed jumper inside with current collectors $T_3$ and $T_4$. In the initial position, the foil strips are located along the plates and form capacitors with a capacity of $C_1$ each. A capacitor with a capacity of $C_0$, pre-charged to a voltage of $U_0$, is connected to the outer plates. What is the voltage across this capacitor after $N$ revolutions of the cylinder clockwise? The capacity between the plates and the foil strips in the expanded state is negligible.

## Solution

The electrostatic generator (see Fig.nok) consists of a non-conductive cylinder on which strips of foil $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are glued, outer plates $O_1$ and $O_2$, fixed current collectors $T_1$ and $T_2$ on the outside and a fixed jumper inside with current collectors $T_3$ and $T_4$. In the initial position, the foil strips are located to guide the outer plates and form capacitors with a bone of $C_1$ each. To the outer plates we connect a capacitor with value $C_0$, pre-charged to the point of detection $U_0$. What is the appearance of this capacitor after $N$ cylinder revolutions clockwise? The capacity between the plates and foil strips in the developed state is negligible.

When the cylinder is turned half a turn, the foil strips $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ are placed in places, but again, as in the original position, they, together with the plates $O_1$ and $O_2$, form two capacitors with a value of $C_1$ each. In this case, the voltage on the capacitor em with bone $C_0$ will be in some number $k$ greater than the initial one:

$$U = kU_0.$$

The next half-turn will increase the voltage by another $k$ times, etc. Therefore, after $N$ turns, the voltage on the capacitor will be $C_0$

$$U_N = k^{2N}U_0.$$

All that remains is to find the number $k$.

In the initial position, the tension between the plates $O_1$ and $O_2$ is equal to $U_0$. This voltage is applied to series-connected capacitors with the same emcosts $C_1$. Therefore, the voltage on each capacitor is $U_1 = U_0/2$, and the charge is $q_1 = C_1U_0/2$.

When the rotation of the cylinder brings the strip into contact with current capacitors $T_1$ and $T_2$, the charges from these strips will go to plate capacitors with capacitance $C_0$. Charges will also be removed from plates $O_1$ and $O_2$. Since the capacitance between the plates and the foil strips in the developed state is negligible, the plates will be discharged almost completely. Therefore, to the initial charge $C_0U_0$ of the positive plate capacitor, another charge $C_1U_0/2$ from the foil strip $\Phi_1$ and charge $C_1U_0/2$ from the plate $O_2$ will be added, while negative charges will transfer to the negative plate of the capacitor. Thus, the capacitor em with $C_0$ has a charge

$$q = (C_0 + C_1)U_0.$$

With further rotation of the cylinder, its contact with the current tubes $T_1$ and $T_2$ will stop, exactly after half a turn the strip $\Phi_1$ touches the current tube $T_4$, and the strip $\Phi_2$ touches the current tube $T_3$, the strips approximate

attach to the plates and once again form two capacitors with a capacity of $C_0$ each. The charge $q$ is redistributed between the capacitor plate with capacity $C_0$ and the plate $O_2$:

$$\left(C_0 + C_1\right)U_0 = C_0U + C_1U/2,$$

where

$$U = \frac{C_0 + C_1}{C_0 + C_1/2}U_0.$$

The coefficient of $U_0$ just gives us the number $k$, so the final voltage on a capacitor of capacity $C_0$ will be

$$U_N = \left(\frac{C_0 + C_1}{C_0 + C_1/2}\right)^{2N}U_0.$$

The lowest voltage value corresponds to the following, when $C_0$ is much less than $C_1$:

$$U_{N_{\max}} \approx 2^{2N}U_0.$$

However, in real electrostatic generators, when high voltages are reached, an electrical breakdown will occur.
