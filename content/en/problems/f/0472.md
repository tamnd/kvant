This means that the sum of all projection lengths is at least six. And this sum is equal to exactly the sum of all $a_i$ and $b_i$ ($i=1, \dots, k$), so $a_1+a_2+\dots+a_k+b_1+b_2+\dots+b_k \geq 6$.

Let us prove that the inequality we need follows from (1):
$$\sqrt{a_1^2+b_1^2}+\sqrt{a_2^2+b_2^2}+\dots+\sqrt{a_k^2+b_k^2} \geq 3\sqrt{2}.$$

This can be done algebraically, but we will give a simple geometric proof.

Let us construct a broken line on the coordinate plane from $k$ links, the lengths of the projections of the $i$-th link are equal to $a_i$ and $b_i$ ($i=1, 2, \dots, k$), with the beginning at the point $O(0;0)$ (Fig. 3). From (1) it follows that the end $M(a_1+\dots+a_k; b_1+\dots+b_k)$ of this broken line lies above the line $a+b=6$ (or on this line). The distance from point $O$ to this line is $3\sqrt{2}$. It is clear that the length of any broken line connecting the points $O$ and $M$ is not less than $3\sqrt{2}$; this is inequality (2). Note that the estimate $3\sqrt{2}$ is exact. It is achieved for a section of a cube perpendicular to its diagonal and shaped like a hexagon.
