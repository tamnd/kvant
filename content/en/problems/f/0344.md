## Condition

The charge $Q$ is uniformly distributed. along a thin dielectric ring that lies on a smooth horizontal plane. The induction of the magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the ring varies from 0 to $B_0$. What angular velocity does the ring acquire in this case? The mass of the ring is $m$. (9 grades)

## Solution

Charge Q is uniformly distributed over a thin dielectric ring, which lies on the main horizontal plane. The induction of the magnetic field perpendicular to the plane of the ring varies from 0 to $B_0$. What is the effective speed of treatment with this ring? The mass of the ring is $m$.

When the Earth moves around the Sun, merging gravity $F = \gamma \frac{m_C m_3}{L^2}$ imparts to the Earth a centripetal acceleration $a_ц = \omega^2 L = \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2} L$. Here $\omega$ is the angular velocity, $T = 1 \text{еод}$ is the period of revolution of the Earth around the Sun, $m_C$ is the mass of the Sun, $m_3$ is the mass of the Earth and $L$ is the radius of the Earth’s orbit. According to Newton's second law

$$\gamma \frac{m_C m_3}{L^2} = m_3 \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2} L.$$

(1)

But $\gamma \frac{m_3}{R_3^2} = g$ therefore

$$m_C g \frac{R_3^2}{L^2} = m_3 \frac{4\pi^2}{T^2} L.$$

(2)

Since $m_3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi R_3^3 \rho_3$ and $m_C = \frac{4}{3} \pi R_C^3 \rho_C$, it follows from correspondence (2) that

$$\frac{\rho_3}{\rho_C} = \frac{g R_C^3 T^2}{4\pi^2 L^3 R_3}.$$

Now all we have to do is express $R_C$ in terms of $L$ and $\alpha$. Obviously, $R_C = L \alpha/2$ (Fig. 8). Therefore we finally get

$$\frac{\rho_3}{\rho_C} = \frac{g \alpha^3 T^2}{32\pi^2 R_3} \approx 4,4.$$

◆

When the magnetic field changes, an electric field arises, the direction of which at each point of the ring is directed tangentially to the ring. This directionality can be calculated by dividing the emf. induction excited in a circular contour of radius $R$, equal to the radius of the ring, to the circumference of the contour:

$$E = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_{инд}|}{2\pi R} = \frac{1}{2\pi R} \left| \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \right|.$$

Let us select a small section of the ring with length $\Delta l_i$ with charge $\Delta Q_i = \frac{Q}{2\pi R} \Delta l_i$ and mass $\Delta m_i = \frac{m}{2\pi R} \Delta l_i$.

Thus,

$$N = \frac{Mgv}{2} = \frac{Mg}{2l} \sqrt{\frac{Mg}{\rho\pi}}. \tag{3}$$

Air density can be found from the ideal gas equation of state

$$pV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

From here

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{\rho\mu}{RT}. \tag{4}$$

For estimates, we will assume that the air is at normal atmospheric pressure $p = 10^5 \text{ h/m}^2$ and at temperature $T = 300^\circ\text{K}$. Substituting the corresponding value of the quantity into formulas (3) and (4), we find

$$N \approx 3 \cdot 10^4 \text{ bm} = 30 \text{ квт.}$$
