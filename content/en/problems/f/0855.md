## Condition

A charged plate is completely screwed into a flat capacitor connected to the strips of a battery with emf $\delta$ parallel to its plates at a distance $b$ from one of the capacitor plates (Fig. 4). Find the force acting on the screwed plate if its charge is $q$. Neglect the force of gravity. The area of ​​the capacitor plates is $S$, the distance between them is $d$.

V. P. Borodin, A. P. Erisov

## Solution

A flat capacitor connected to the pole of a battery with an emf of $\delta$ was completely inserted parallel to its plates by a charged plate at a distance of $b$ from one of the plates of the capacitor (see figure). Find the force acting on the input plate if its charge is $q$. Run next. The area of ​​the capacitor plates is $S$, the distance between them is $d$.

The electric field inside the capacitor is the sum of the field created by the plate with charge $q$ and the field created by the charges of the capacitor plates. For example, these fields are designated corresponding to $E$ and $E_1$. (On figure the directions of the fields $E$ and $E_1$ are indicated for the case of positive charges entering the plate.)

The force acting on the insertion plate is equal to

$$F = q E_1.$$

The voltage $E_1$ can be found by writing the potential difference between the capacitor plates in the form

$$\delta = (E_1 + E)(d - b) + (E_1 - E) b.$$

From here

$$E_1 = \frac{\delta + E(2b - d)}{d}.$$

Substituting $E = q/2\varepsilon_0 S$, we get:

$$E_1 = \frac{\delta + q(2b - d)}{2S\varepsilon_0}.$$
