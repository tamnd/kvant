## Solution

In the calorimeter, the dissolved substance under study slowly cools. The specific heat of fusion of this substance (it was determined in previous experiments) is $L = 200$ kJ/kg. Using the graph of the temperature of a substance versus time (Fig. 1), determine the specific heat of the substance in the solid and liquid states. Neglect the thermal capacity of the calorimeter.

Fig. 1.

a) If the product of two ratios (*) is equal to 1, then
$$\frac{PL}{LQ} \cdot \frac{PM}{MQ} = \frac{x(x+a)}{y(y+b)} = 1, \frac{PL}{LQ} = \frac{MQ}{PM},$$
where
$$\frac{PL}{PQ} = \frac{MQ}{PQ}, \quad PL = MQ, \quad KL = MN.$$

6) Four ratios (*) are equal to 1/4, 4, 2/3 and 3/2 respectively if (and only if) when
$$4x = b + y, \quad 4y = a + x, \quad 2y = 3x, \quad 2(x+a) = 3(y+b).$$
If $x = 2d$ and the first three of these equalities are satisfied, then $y = 3d$, $a = 10d$, $b = 5d$. In this case, the fourth equality is also satisfied.

Thus, the need to accept exists if and only if one of the bases of the trapezoid is twice as large as the other.

E. G. Gotman

As can be seen from figure, first the substance, which is in the liquid state, is cooled to the melting point, then within 5 minutes it crystallizes without changing the temperature, after which it cools again, in the solid state.

Obviously, if the temperature does not change over time (during crystallization), then the thermal power released by the substance is constant. It can be expressed using the horizontal section of the graph:

$$P = \frac{Q}{\tau_{\text{кр}}} = \frac{mL}{\tau_{\text{кр}}}.$$

By continuing the section of the graph corresponding to the liquid state (by drawing a tangent to the graph), we can write (Fig. 2):

$$c_{\text{ж}} m \Delta t_{\text{ж}} = P \tau_{\text{ж}},$$
where
$$c_{\text{ж}} = \frac{\tau_{\text{ж}}}{\Delta t_{\text{ж}}} \frac{L}{\tau_{\text{кр}}} \approx 8 \cdot 10^3 \frac{\text{Дж}}{\text{кг} \cdot \text{К}}.$$

Similarly for the solid state we find:

$$c_{\text{TB}} = \frac{\tau_{\text{TB}}}{\Delta t_{\text{TB}}} \frac{L}{\tau_{\text{kp}}} \approx 15 \cdot 10^3 \frac{\text{Дж}}{\text{кг} \cdot \text{К}}.$$

Note that we drew tangents for greater accuracy; it was possible to do without them by using small curves that are adjacent to the horizon of the graph.
