## Condition

An ideal gas is contained in a vessel having the shape of two “joined” cylinders of the same length l and with cross-sectional areas S and αS (α>1). In the middle of each cylinder there are pistons connected by a rigid rod (see figure); in this case, the pressure in compartment I is equal to p, in compartment III the pressure is equal to βp, the pistons are in equilibrium. A body Q was brought to the system so that the temperature increased, remaining the same in all compartments. Determine how the pressure in compartment I has changed. The internal energy of one mole of gas is equal to $cT$ (c is the molar heat of the gas). The heat capacity of the cylinders and pistons is pretentious.

Let $V_i, p_i$ and $v_i$ be the volume of the $i$th compartment, the pressure and the number of moles of gas in the vessel $v=v_1+v_2+v_3$. Since the temperature $T$ is the same in all compartments, the Mendeleev-Cliperon equation gives

$$p_1V_1+p_2V_2+p_3V_3=(v_1+v_2+v_3)RT=vRT.$$ (1)

For the same reason, the pistons will not move, since at constant volumes the pressure ratio in the compartments does not change and at any moment of time the equilibrium condition will be satisfied

$$(p_3-p_2)\alpha S+(p_2-p_1)S=0.$$ (2)

Using the conditions $p_3=\beta p_1$, from (2) we find

$$p_2=p_1\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\alpha-1}.$$ (3)

From (3) it is clear that the problem makes sense at $\alpha\beta\ge 1$, since the pressure must be positive. Substituting $p_2$ and $p_3$ into (1), we find the relationship between the pressure in the first compartment and the temperature:

$$p_1=\frac{vTR(\alpha-1)}{Sl(\alpha^2\beta-1)}.$$ (4)

Since the difference between the final and initial temperatures

After connecting plates 1 and 4 with a conductor, the potential difference between them $U_{14}$ will be zero. In this case, the distribution of charge on the plates and electric fields between them will be as shown in figure 2.

$$U_{14} = E_1d - E_3d + E_2d = \frac{d}{\varepsilon_0S} (q'_1 - q_3 + q'_2) = 0.$$

From the law of conservation of charge on plates 2 and 3 it follows:

$$q'_1 + q_3 = q_1, \quad q'_2 + q_3 = q_2,$$

where

$$q_3 = \frac{q_1 + q_2}{3}.$$

Therefore, the potential difference between plates 2 and 3 is equal to

$$U_{23} = \frac{q_3}{\varepsilon_0S} d = \frac{q_1 + q_2}{3\varepsilon_0S} d.$$

In order to determine the force $N$ acting on the overload $m$ from the side of the load $M$, we use Newton’s second law for the movement of the overload:

$$ma = mg - N.$$

After the weights are released, they perform harmonious oscillations (as one weight of mass $M + m$). This means that acceleration $a$ periodically changes its direction. Strength

$$N = m(g - a)$$

will be maximum at the moment when the acceleration has a maximum absolute value and is directed upward, that is, at the moment the arm is most stretched. To determine acceleration, Newton's second law is used for the movement of a load with an overload:

$$(M + m)a = (M + m)g - kx.$$

The maximum value $x_{\max}$ will be found using the law of conservation of energy (taking into account that at the moment of maximum stretching of the arm, the speed of the load is zero):

$$-(M+m)gx_0 + \frac{k}{2}x_0^2 = -(M+m)gx_{\max} + \frac{k}{2}x_{\max}^2,$$

where $x_0$ is the elongation of the spring without overload $m$. Considering that $kx_0 = Mg$, from (2) we obtain: $kx_{\max} = (M+2m)g$. Substituting this value into (1), we find the maximum acceleration value -

$$|a_{\max}| = \left| -\frac{m}{M+m}g \right| = \frac{m}{M+m}g,$$

and knowing $a_{\max}$, we find $N_{\max}$:

$$N_{\max} = mg \frac{M+2m}{M+m}.$$
