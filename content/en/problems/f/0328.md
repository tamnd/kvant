## Condition

Rigid rods of lengths $l_1$ and $l_2$ are hingedly connected at point $A$. Their free ends move away from each other uniformly with the speeds of the corresponding $v_1$ and $v_2$, directed along one straight line (Fig. 2). Find the acceleration of point $A$ at that moment; when the rods make an angle $90^\circ$ with each other. The movement of the rods occurs in one plane.

B. B. Bukhovtsevo

## Solution

Rigid rods of lengths $l_1$ and $l_2$ are connected by a hinge at point $A$. Their free ends move away from each other evenly at the speed of the corresponding $v_1$ and $v_2$, directed along one straight line (Fig. 13). The most important point is $A$ at the moment when the rods connect to each other at $90^\circ$. The movement of the rods will occur in one plane.

Let us imagine the vector $a$ of the total acceleration of point $A$ at the moment when the rods make an angle $90^\circ$ with each other, as the sum of two accelerations $a_1$ and $a_2$, directed along the corresponding rods (see Fig. 13). To calculate $a_1$ and $a_2$ we take advantage of the fact that the acceleration vector remains constant when moving from one inertial reporting system to another.

Let us move to the reporting system moving at speed $v_2$ to the left. In this reporting system, the end of the left rod (point $B$) ends; the end of the right rod (point $C$) moves to the right with speed $v_1+v_2$, and point $A$ describes a circle of radius $l_2$ (Fig. 14). Therefore, the acceleration $a$ can be decomposed into centripetal (normal) acceleration and tangential (tangential) acceleration. These are the accelerations $a_2$ and $a_1$ of the corresponding one.

Modulus of centripetal acceleration $a_2 = v^2/l_2$, where $v$ is the linear speed of motion of point $A$ around the circle. At the moment when the rods make an angle $90^\circ$ with each other, the velocity vector $v$ is directed along the right rod. The other rod $AC$ has at this moment a speed $v_1+v_2$ directed to the right. Since the rod is rigid, the projections of the velocities of its ends onto the straight line along which it is located must be the same (otherwise the length of the rod would change). Therefore (see Fig. 14),

$$v = \left( v_1 + v_2 \right) \cos \alpha_1 = \frac{\left( v_1 + v_2 \right) l_1}{\sqrt{l_1^2 + l_2^2}}.$$

Then

$$a_2 = \frac{v^2}{l_2} = \frac{\left( v_1 + v_2 \right)^2 l_1^2}{\left( l_1^2 + l_2^2 \right) l_2}.$$

Similarly, the first one in the reporting system, moving to the right with speed $v_1$, we get

$$a_1 = \frac{\left( v_1 + v_2 \right)^2 l_2^2}{\left( l_1^2 + l_2^2 \right) l_1}.$$

Full acceleration module

$$a = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2} = \frac{\left( v_1 + v_2 \right)^2}{\left( l_1^2 + l_2^2 \right) l_1 l_2} \sqrt{l_1^6 + l_2^6}.$$

Vector a makes an angle $\beta$ with the rod $AC$ such that

$$tg \beta = \frac{a_2}{a_1} = \left( \frac{l_1}{l_2} \right)^3.$$
