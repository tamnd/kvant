## Condition

When photographing the Moon from the Earth with a camera whose lens has a focal length F, an image of the Moon in the form of a disk of radius R is placed on the photographic plastic. Then, using the same apparatus, a sharp image of the Moon is obtained. It has radius R1. When photographing the Moon for the second time, at what distance did the camera lens move relative to the photographic lens?

Object diameter D. (Do not take into account diffraction.)

## Solution

When formulating the Moon from the Earth with a photo camera, the lens of which has a focal point of view $F$, a blurry image of the Moon in the form of a disk of radius $R$ was obtained using formulas. Then, using the same apparatus, a sharp image of the Moon is obtained. It has a radius of $R_1$. For what consideration was mixed during the second formulation of the Moon with an objective fomoapparatus relatively formulaically?

Lens diameter $D$. Diffraction is not taken into account.

Since the Moon is very far from the Earth and the dimensions of the lens are small compared to the distance to the Moon, we can assume that from each point on the Moon a parallel beam of rays enters the lens, which is collected at a point in the focal plane of the lens. A sharp image of the Moon is formed in this plane. If the lens is not located in the focal plane, but close to the lens or further from it, then the image of the Moon turns out to be blurred.

To determine this, we will assume that the screen is located behind the focal plane and consider the path of rays coming from the edge of the Moon (Fig. 23). These rays determine the size of the image of the Moon on the formula stick. Triangles $ABM$ and $ACD$ are similar, so we can write the following relation:

$$\frac{R}{R_1} = \frac{y}{x},$$

or

$$\frac{R - R_1}{R_1} = \frac{y - x}{x}.$$

(1)

From the similarity of triangles $OAN$ and $ABM$ we obtain

$$\frac{R_1}{D/2} = \frac{x}{F - x}.$$

(2)

From (1) and (2) we find the distance $\delta = y - x$ at which the lenses are swept relative to the formula:

$$\delta = \frac{R - R_1}{R_1 + D/2} F.$$
