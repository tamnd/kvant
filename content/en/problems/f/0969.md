## Condition

Two cans made of the same material have the same geometric shape, and all the linear dimensions of one can are $k$ times smaller than the other (Fig. 3). A hole was made in two of each can. The jars are simultaneously lowered into the water. Which jar will sink faster? How many times?

I. K. Sobolev

## Solution

Two cans made of the same material have the same geometric shape, and all the linear dimensions of one can are one time smaller than the other (see figure). A hole was made in two of each can. The jars are simultaneously lowered into the water. Which bank will please you most? How many times?

Due to the fact that the jars that are immersed in water are made of the same materials, at first the depth of immersion of each of them is linearly related to the size. That is, if the sizes of the cans differ by $k$ times $l_2=kl_1$, then the distances from the water surface to the surface $h_2=kh_1$ differ by $k$ times.

Since the pressure drop on both sides of the connection depends on the immersion depth $\Delta p=\rho_{\text{воды}}gh$, and the speed of water filling the jar through the connection is related to the pressure difference - $\Delta p=\frac{1}{2}q_{\text{воды}}v^2$, then

$v^2=2gh$.

In turn, the speed determines the time $t$, after which the jar will fill with water and move to the bottom.

Thus, dependencies (*) are the key relation connecting time with linear dimensions through the acceleration of free pressure $g$, which in this problem plays the role of the only dimensional constant that determines the diapasm of the process. Το are quantities that have dimension $m/c^2$, for example $l/t^2$, are expressed only through $g$ and therefore do not depend on $l$:

$$l_2/t_2^2 = l_1/t_1^2.$$

Hence,

$$t_2 = \sqrt{k} t_1.$$
