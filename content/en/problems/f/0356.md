## Condition

How fast does the moon's shadow move across the earth's surface during a total solar eclipse? The eclipse occurs at the equator. For simplicity, assume that the earth's axis is perpendicular to the earth's and lunar orbits.

Fig. 3.

## Solution

At what speed does the moon move across the earth's surface during a total solar eclipse? The eclipse is observed at the equator. For simplicity, assume that the distance of the Moon to the Earth is $R_1 = 384\ 000$ km, and the period of revolution around the Earth is $T_1 = 28$ days. $cym = 2\ 419\ 200$ sec. Similarly, we apply that the radius of the Earth is $R_2 = 6\ 400$ km and the Earth makes a complete revolution around its axis in a time of $T_2 = 1$ days. $cym = 86\ 400$ sec. Then the speed of the Moon in its circular orbit is

$$v_1 = \frac{2\pi R_1}{T_1} \approx 995\ m/sec,$$

and the linear speed of points on the earth's surface

$$v_2 = \frac{2\pi R_2}{T_2} \approx 465\ m/sec,$$

Moreover, for that region of the Earth’s surface where the shadow of the Moon is observed, both velocities are directed in the same direction (Fig. 19).

Since the sun's rays can be considered parallel and the size of the Moon is small, the shadow of the Moon moves relative to the earth's surface at noon at a speed

$$v = v_1 - v_2 = 2\pi \left( \frac{R_1}{T_1} - \frac{R_2}{T_2} \right) \approx 530\ m/sec.$$
