## Condition

What minimum speed must be imparted to a spacecraft on Earth in order for it to reach the Sun? What will be the time of the ship's flight to the Sun?

## Solution

What minimum speed does a spaceship need to reach on Earth for mono to get to the Sun? What will be the time of the ship's flight to the Sun?

Let's imagine that a spaceship is launched from the Earth, the speed of which is slightly less than the speed of the Earth's reverse motion. The trajectory of the ship will be an ellipse, at one of the focuses of which is the Sun. The lower the initial speed of the ship relative to the Sun, the more elongated its orbit. At certain values ​​of this speed, the ship in its movement will touch the surface of the Sun. In other words, the altitude of the perihelion of the ship's orbit will be equal to the Sun. The radius of the Sun is approximately 200 times smaller than the radius of the Earth's orbit, so the small band of the ship's elliptical orbit touching the Sun is much smaller than the larger band. For estimation, we can assume that the small gain is simply equal to zero. With this assumption, the trajectory of the ship turns into a straight line, connecting the launch point of the ship with the Sun, that is, the ship hits the Sun, freely falling onto it. Consequently, at the initial moment the speed of the ship relative to the Sun should be equal to zero. In order for this condition to be fulfilled, the ship must communicate on Earth at a speed equal in abscylotic value to the inverse speed of the Earth, but directed in the opposite direction.

The radius of the Earth's orbit is approximately $1,5 \cdot 10^{11}$ m, the period of revolution of the Earth around the Sun is $\sim 3,15 \cdot 10^{7}$ s. Therefore, the orbital speed of the Earth is

$$v = \frac{2\pi \cdot 1,5 \cdot 10^{11}}{3,15 \cdot 10^7} \approx 3 \cdot 10^4 \text{ м/с}.$$

This speed must be achieved on Earth (Fig. 8).

In order to determine the time of the ship's flight to the Sun, use Kepler's third law. According to this

The squares of the periods of revolution of the ship and the Earth around the Sun are related to the cubes of the semimajor axes of the orbits of the ship and the Earth:

$$\frac{T^2_K}{T^2_3} = \frac{(R/2)^3}{R^3}$$

(R is the radius of the Earth’s orbit). The flight time of a ship to the Sun is half the period of its revolution in an elliptical orbit, that is

$$t = \frac{T_K}{2} = \frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}} T_3 \approx 5.6 \cdot 10^6 \text{ с.}$$
