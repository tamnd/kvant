## Condition

The beetle is photographed at two scales: from a distance $l_{1} = 3F$, where $F$ is the focal length of the object, and from a distance $l_{2} = 5F$. How many times must the diameter of the object's aperture be changed so that the illumination of the image on film in both cases is the same? Consider that the diameter of the object in both cases is much less than $F$.

## Solution

Forming a beetle on two scales: from a distance $d_1 = 3F$, food $F$ - the focal length of the lens, and from a distance $d_2 = 5F$. How many times must the diameter of the lens aperture be changed so that the image quality on the film is the same in both cases? Consider that the lens diameter in both cases is much less than $F$.

Let us denote by $B$ the luminous flux emitted by the unit surface area of ​​the beetle within a single solid angle (surface brightness). Then the luminous flux falls from area $S_0$ (Fig. 3) ​​onto the camera lens

$$\Phi = BS_0\Omega = BS_0 \frac{S_\pi}{d^2}.$$

Here $\Omega = \frac{S_\pi}{d^2}$ is the solid angle (in fact, this equality is not exact, but approximate), $S_\pi$ is the area of ​​the lens aperture, $d$ is the distance from the camera to the beetle.

After the lens, the light flux hits the photographic film, creating an image on it. Illumination of this image

$$E = \frac{\Phi}{S} = \frac{BS_0S_\pi}{d^2S},$$

where $S$ is the image area of ​​the selected area of ​​the beetle’s surface. Let's find her.

Since the linear magnification of the lens is $f/d$ (f is the distance from the lens to the image), the relationship between the areas of the image and the object

$$\frac{S}{S_0} = \left( \frac{f}{d} \right)^2.$$

According to the lens formula, $\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}$, whence

$$f = \frac{Fd}{d - F},$$ and $\frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{d - F}$.

Therefore, the image illumination

$$E = \frac{BS_\pi}{d^2} \frac{S_0}{S} = \frac{BS_\pi(d - F)^2}{d^2F^2}.$$

According to the conditions of the problem, when photographing a beetle at two scales, the illumination of the image should be the same:

$$\frac{BS_\pi(d_1 - F)^2}{d_1^2F^2} = \frac{BS_\pi(d_2 - F)^2}{d_2^2F^2}.$$

From here

$$\frac{S_{\pi 2}}{S_{\pi 1}} = \frac{(d_1 - F)^2 d_2^2}{(d_2 - F)^2 d_1^2} = \frac{25}{36},$$ and

$$\frac{D_{\pi 2}}{D_{\pi 1}} = \frac{5}{6}.$$

In the plane perpendicular to straight line TM, electrons will move in a circle in absolute value with a speed equal to $v_2 = |\vec{v}| \sin \alpha$.

Having made a complete revolution around its axis, the electrons will cross the straight line TM. If the projection $v_1$ of the electron velocity onto the straight line TM was equal to zero, then, having made a full revolution along a circle of radius $R_1$, the electrons would return to point T. In our case, they will intersect the straight line TM at points spaced from each other at a distance of $l = v_1 \tau$, where $\tau$ is the time during which the electrons make one revolution:

$$\tau = \frac{2 \pi R_1}{v_2}.$$

In order for the electron to get to point M, it is necessary that the length $|TM|$ be equal to the integer $l$, that is
