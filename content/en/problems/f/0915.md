## Condition

A liquid with dielectric permeability flows between the plates of an insulated flat capacitor with a speed $v$. A magnetic field with induction $\vec{B}$ is directed perpendicular to the speed $\vec{v}$ and parallel to the plates. Determine the direction on the capacitor plates. Distance between plates $d$.

Lorentz forces directed in opposite directions act on opposite charges of moving diopoles of the dielectric from the side of the magnetic field (see figure). This leads to polarization of the dielectric, and as a result of the upper and lower plates of the capacitor, surface charges of opposite signs will exist in the dielectric, equal in absolute value. These polarized charges create an electric field.

Let the direction of this field be equal to $E$ (at figure $\vec{E}$ directed from top to bottom). Then the force acting on the positive charge of the moving diopole is equal to

$$F = q (vB - E).$$

According to the definition of dielectric permeability, this force should be ε times less than the force $F_0$ that would act on the charge $q$, moving at a speed $\vec{v}$, in the absence of a dielectric. But in the absence of a dielectric $E = 0$, and $F_0 = qvB$. So

$$F = \frac{F_0}{\varepsilon} \Rightarrow q (vB - E) = q \frac{vB}{\varepsilon},$$

where is $E$ from:

$$E = \frac{\varepsilon - 1}{\varepsilon} vB.$$

Thus, the potential difference between the plates of the capacitor is equal to

$$U = Ed = \frac{\varepsilon - 1}{\varepsilon} vBd.$$
