## Condition

Two satellites move in the same orbit at a small (compared to the radius $R_0$ of the Earth) distance from each other. This distance when moving periodically changes from $l_1$ to $l_2$. Determine the minimum and maximum distances of the satellites from the center of the Earth if the satellites' orbital period is $T$.

V. E. Skorovarov

## Solution

Two satellites move in the same orbit at a small (compared to the radius $R_0$ of the earth) distance from each other. This distance when moving periodically changes from $l_1$ to $l_2$. Determine the minimum and maximum distance of the satellites from the center of the earth if the satellites' orbital period is $T$.

According to Kepler's second law, the areas noticeable by the radius vector of the satellite in equal periods of time are equal. During the same time, one satellite moved from point $a$ to point $a'$, and the other satellite moved from point $b$ to point $b'$ (see figure). This means that the areas of sectors $Oaba'$ and $Oba'b'$ are equal. If we subtract the area of ​​sector $Oba'$ from these areas, we obtain the areas of sectors $Oab$ and $Oa'b'$, which are thus the same.

Since, according to the conditions, the distance between the satellites is small, instead of sectors, the corresponding equidistant triangles can be considered. Therefore, the areas of triangles $Oab$ and $Oa'b'$ are the same, that is

$$\frac{1}{2} l_1 R_1 = \frac{1}{2} l_2 R_2. \tag{1}$$

According to Kepler's third law, the ratio of the squares of the satellite's orbital periods in different orbits is equal to the ratio of the cubes of large half-seine orbits. If $T_0$ is the period of revolution of the satellite in a circular orbit near the earth’s surface, then

$$\frac{T^2}{T_0^2} = \frac{\left(\frac{1}{2} (R_1 + R_2)\right)^3}{R_0^3}, \tag{2}$$

where $R_0$ is the radius of the earth. Period $T_0$ will be found from the equation of motion of the satellite in a circular orbit of radius $R_0$:

$$m\omega^2 R_0 = mg,$$

where

$$T_0^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 R_0}{g}.$$

Thus, from (2) we find:

$$R_1 + R_2 = 2R_0 \sqrt{\frac{T^2 g}{4\pi^2 R_0}}. \tag{3}$$
