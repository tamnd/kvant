## Condition

The lower end of a capillary of radius $r=0,2$ mm and length $l=8$ cm is immersed in water, the temperature of which is constant and equal to $0^\circ C$. The temperature of the upper end of the capillary is $t_0=100^\circ C$. The temperature depends on the surface tension coefficient $\sigma(t)$ of the bringing water in the table. To what height does water rise in the capillary? The thermal conductivity of the capillary is much greater than the thermal conductivity of the water in it; body exchange with the surrounding influence can be neglected.

A. I. Buzdin

## Solution

The lower end of a capillary of radius $r=0,2$ mm and length $l=8$ cm is immersed in water, the temperature of which is constant and equal to $0^\circ C$. The temperature of the upper end of the capillary is $t_0=100^\circ C$. The temperature dependence of the surface tension coefficient $\sigma(t)$ of water is given in the table. To what height will the water rise in the capillary? The conductivity of the capillary is much greater than the conductivity of the water in it; body exchange with the surrounding influence can be neglected.

◆ Let $x$ be the height of the water column in the capillary. The temperature of the capillary and, therefore, the water at this height is, obviously,
$$t_x = t_0 \frac{x}{l}.$$
Water in the capillary is held by surface tension. If $\sigma(t_x)$ is the surface tension coefficient at temperature $t_x$, then
$$x = \frac{2\sigma(t_x)}{\varrho g r},$$
where $\varrho$ is the density of water. From here is
$$\sigma(t_x) = \frac{\varrho g r x}{2} = \frac{\varrho g r l t_x}{2 t_0}.$$
