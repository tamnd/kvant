## Condition

figure 5 shows part of the trajectory of a good object object thrown at an angle to the horizontal. At point $A$ the body had a speed equal to 20 m/s in abscylotic value. How long did it take the body to fly from point $A$ to point $B$?

## Solution

figure 11 shows part of the trajectory of good chalk, thrown under the lot towards the horizon. In lobe A the chalk had a speed equal to the abscise value of 20 m/s. How long did it take the lemelo to move from chamber A to chamber B?

A body thrown at an angle to the horizon moves uniformly accelerated by the free fall acceleration $g$; the projection of its velocity onto the $OX$ axis remains unchanged, and the velocity projection onto the vertical $OY$ axis changes with time according to the law

$$v_y = v_0 + gt,$$

where $g = -9,8 \text{ m/c}^2$ is the projection of the vector $g$ onto the axis $OY$. If $t_0$ is the time of movement of the body from point A to point B, then $v_{By} = v_{Ay} + gt_0$, whence

$$t_0 = \frac{v_{By} - v_{Ay}}{g} = \frac{\Delta v_y}{g}.$$

Therefore, to find $t_0$, you need to find the change in the projection of the body’s velocity onto the axis $OY$.

Vectors $v_A$ and $v_B$ of the body’s velocity at points A and B are directed tangent to the body’s trajectory at these points. Let's draw the vector $v_A$ on an arbitrarily chosen scale and draw a tangent to the trajectory at point B. Find the projection $v_x$ of the body's velocity on the axis $OX$ at point A and plot a segment of the same length on a horizontal line drawn through point B. Now draw a vertical line through the end of the postponed segment, find the vector $v_B$ and its projection $v_{By}$ to axis $OY$. Having measured $v_{Ay}$ and $v_{By}$ using a ruler, we find the value $\Delta v_y$ (it does not depend on the chosen scale) and then the time $t_0$ of the body’s movement from point A to point B.

In our case $\Delta v_y = |v_{By}| + |v_{Ay}| = 33 \text{ (м/c)}$, $t_0 \approx 3,3 \text{ c}$.
