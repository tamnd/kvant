## Condition

The Plock capacitor is in a magnetic field that is perpedicular to the plane of the plates (Fig. 6). The distance between the plates $d$, the magnetic induction vector $B$. Inside the capacitor, near the negatively charged plate, there is a source of slow electrons that emits electrons in different directions. At what direction between the plates will the electrons be focused onto the positively charged plate? What determines the size of the “spot”?

A. Trubachev

## Solution

A parallel-plate capacitor is in a magnetic field that is perpendicular to the plane of the plates. Consideration between plates $d$, magnetic induction vector $\vec{B}$. Inside the capacitor, near the negatively charged plates,

Let $Q$ be the charge on the surface of the initial (never folded) triangle of area $S$, $A_0$ be the electrostatic energy that this charge possesses. This energy consists of the electrostatic energies of the pairwise interactions of individual charged sections of the triangle.

Let us consider two small sections $m$ and $n$ of the triangle located at a distance $r$ from each other (see Fig. 1). Let $s$ be the area of ​​each plot; the charge of each site is obviously $q = \frac{Q}{S}$ s. Electrostatic energy of interaction of these areas

$$a_0 \sim q^2 / r = Q^2 \frac{s}{Sr}.$$

Let's see how this energy changes after the first addition of the triangle. From figure 2 it is clear that on the folded triangle the sections similar to $m$ and $n$ are sections $m_1$ and $n_1$. The charge on the folded triangle will still be $Q$, the area of ​​the triangle will become $S_1 = S / 2$, the distance between the sites, as is easy to understand, will become $r_1 = r / \sqrt{2}$, and their areas will be $s_1 = s / 2$. Thus, the interaction energy of the sites will become

$$a_1 \sim q^2 / r_1 = Q^2 \frac{s_1}{Sr_1} = a_0 \sqrt{2}.$$

Since the same considerations apply to any pair of pads, the electrostatic energy of the folded triangle will become

$$A_1 = A_0 \sqrt{2}.$$

The change in energy $\Delta A_1 = A_1 - A_0$ is equal to the work $A$ expended in folding the triangle, that is

$$A_1 - A_0 = A_0 (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A$$

When the triangle is folded again, its electrostatic energy will become

$$A_2 = A_1 \sqrt{2}.$$

The change in energy $\Delta A_2 = A_2 - A_1$ is equal to the work $A_x$, which is three areas when added. Thus,

$$A_x = A_2 - A_1 = A (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A_0 \sqrt{2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) = A \sqrt{2}.$$
