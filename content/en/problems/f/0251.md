## Condition

The photograph of a flying bullet (Fig. 2) shows sound waves that are excited when the bullet moves. (This photograph was obtained due to the fact that areas in which the air density is different refract light rays differently.) Using a ruler, determine the approximate speed of the bullet. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

## Solution

The formation of the bullet's strap (Fig. 9) shows sound waves that are excited when the bullet moves. (It was possible to achieve such a formation due to the fact that areas in which the air density is different refract light rays differently.) Using a ruler, determine the approximate value of the bullet’s speed. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

A spherical sound wave propagates from each point in the bullet's trajectory. These waves, adding up, give a picture that significantly depends on the relationship between the speed of the bullet and the speed of sound. If the speed of sound is greater than the speed of a bullet, then the waves that are emitted at different points do not dare to catch up with each other (Fig. 10). If the speed of the bullet is greater than the speed of sound, then the wave fronts will intersect, as shown in Fig. 11, and, adding up, these waves will form “whiskers” diverging from the bullet, similar to the “whiskers” coming from the nose of the glider.
