## Condition

In an oscillatory RLC circuit, the resistance is low, so the oscillations are weakly damped in it. To obtain undamped oscillations, proceed as follows: twice per period, at moments when the current in the circuit is maximum, the inductor is quickly stretched from length $l$ to length $l+\Delta l$, and at moments when the charge on the capacitor is maximum, the coil is quickly compressed to the previous exchange (parametric resonance). At what relative change in the length of the coil $\Delta l/l$ will the oscillations in the circuit be undamped? Consider the inductance of the coil to be inversely proportional to its length.

A. I. Kirkinsky

## Solution

In an oscillatory RLC circuit, the resistance is low, so the oscillations are weakly damped in it. To obtain an insignificant oscillating system, there must be no less energy lost due to Joule losses in the resistance.

By Farad's law of induction $\mathcal{E}_{\text{инд}} = -\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t}$ and $I_{\text{инд}} = -\frac{1}{R} \frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t}$. When the coil is rapidly stretched, $\Delta t \rightarrow 0$, and since the current cannot reach infinitely large values, then in this case $\Delta \phi \rightarrow 0$, that is, in this process of conservation of magnetic flux $\Phi$.

The work done during stretching only goes to increase the energy of the magnetic field:

$$\Delta A = \Delta W_+ = \frac{L' I'^2}{2} - \frac{L I^2}{2} = \frac{L'^2 I'^2}{2L'} - \frac{L^2 I^2}{2L} =$$

$$= \frac{\phi^2}{2L} \left( \frac{L}{L'} - 1 \right) = \frac{\phi^2}{2L} \left( \frac{l + \Delta l}{l} - 1 \right) = \frac{\phi^2}{2L} \frac{\Delta l}{l} = \frac{L I^2_a}{2} \frac{\Delta l}{l},$$

where the primes are placed at the value related to the state of the system after stretching the coil, $I_a$ is the amplitude value of the current (at the moment of stretching, the current is maximum).

The coil is circuited when the charge on the capacitor is maximum and, therefore, the current in the circuit is zero. Therefore, during shatininikai no work is done.

With low damping, the loss of energy due to the release of heat at the resistance is small compared to the total energy of the system, so we can assume that over the period the amplitude of the oscillations changes little, the current is almost sinusoidal. Then we can obtain the following estimate for energy losses for half a period:

$$\Delta W_- = I_a^2 R \frac{T}{2} = \frac{1}{2} I_a^2 R \pi \sqrt{LC},$$

where $I_a$ is the effective current value.

The oscillations will be undamped if $\Delta W_+ \geqslant \Delta W_-$, that is

$$\frac{LI_a^2}{2} \frac{\Delta l}{l} \geqslant \frac{1}{2} I_a^2 R \pi \sqrt{LC},$$

where

$$\frac{\Delta l}{l} \geqslant \pi R \sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}.$$

A. I. Kirkinsky
