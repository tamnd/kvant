## Condition

figure shows the idealized voltage-voltage characteristic of the diode. The capacitor emkocti $C = 100$ uF, charged to a voltage of $U = 5$ V, is connected through a diode to a resistor with a resistance of $R = 100$ Ohm. What amount of tell will be released on the resistor when the capacitor is discharged?

While the discharge current exceeds $I_0 = 10$ mA, the voltage across the diode remains constant and equal to $U_0 = 1$ V. By the time the current drops to a value of $I_0$, the voltage across the capacitor is equal to

$$U_1 = U_0 + RI_0.$$

The charge that has flowed through the circuit by this time is equal to

$$q = C(U_0 - RI_0).$$

According to the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{CU^2}{2} - \frac{C(U_0 + RI_0)^2}{2} = U_0q + W_1,$$

where $W_1$ is the tello allocated on the resistor up to this moment. Further, the diode is simply equivalent to a resistor with a resistance of $r = U_0/I_0$. With further discharge, a tello is released on resistor $R$.

$$W_2 = \frac{CU^2}{2} \frac{R}{R+r} = \frac{C(U_0 + RI_0)^2}{2} \frac{R}{R + \frac{U_0}{I_0}}.$$

The total amount of tell that will be released on the resistor when the capacitor is discharged is equal to

$$W = W_1 + W_2.$$

Substituting the values ​​$W_2$ and $W_1 = \frac{C}{2} \left[ U^2 - (U_0 + RI_0)^2 \right] - U_0q$, we find

$$W = \frac{C}{2} \left\{ (U - U_0)^2 + RI_0U_0 \right\} = 8,5 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ Дж.}$$
