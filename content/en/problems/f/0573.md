## Condition

Estimate how much water can be carried away in a sieve with square cells measuring $2 \times 2$ mm. The diameter of the sieve is 20 cm. The sieve threads are not wetted with water.

## Solution

Estimate how much water can be taken into account in a sieve with square cells measuring $2 \times 2$ mm. The diameter D of the sieve is 20 cm. The sieve threads are not wetted with water.

The liquid between the sieve threads will form a mesh. The radius of this change cannot be greater than half the distance between the threads, that is, $r_{\max} = 1$ mm. Therefore, the maximum pressure that can be in the liquid above Menascus exceeds atmospheric pressure by values ​​of $\Delta p = \frac{2\sigma}{r}$ (σ is the surface tension of water). This formula is valid for a round mesh, that is, for a sieve with round cells. However, for estimation, we can ignore the complex menisk formula on a square grid and use this formula.

So, $\Delta p = \frac{2\sigma}{r}$. On the other hand, $\Delta p$ is equal to the hydroxotic column of liquid $h$, drunk in a sieve:
$$\frac{2\sigma}{r} = \rho gh.$$
From here we find $h = \frac{2\sigma}{\rho g r}$. The mass of the liquid in the sieve is equal to the product of its volume $V$ and density $\rho$, that is
$$m = V \rho = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} h \rho = \frac{\pi D^2 \sigma}{2gr} \approx 0,458 \text{ кг}.$$
