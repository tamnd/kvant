## Condition

A lens with focal length $F$ and diameter $d$ is inserted in response to the same diameter in a screen made of light-absorbing materials ($d/F<2$). The screen is positioned so that the optical axis of the lens coincides with the bisector plane and is perpendicular to the dual edge

From this relation it is clear that $|AD| = |BC|$ if and only if $\overrightarrow{PQ} \cdot \overrightarrow{MN} = 0$, that is, the segments $PQ$ and $MN$ are perpendicular (or degenerate).

Both solutions pass without changes for any four points $A, B, C, D$ on the plane or in space. In particular, for example, the following statements are true: if the middle lines of a quadrilateral are perpendicular, then its diagonals are equal; if three segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges of a tetrahedra are pairwise perpendicular, then each of its edges is equal to the opposite one (I talk about such “equi-boundary” tetrahedra in the article by V. E. Matizen in «Kvant» No. 7 for 1983). From the above solutions it is also clear that in the conditions of the problem the lines $AD$ and $BC$ form equal angles with $PQ$ (and with $MN$).
