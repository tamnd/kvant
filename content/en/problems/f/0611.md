## Condition

The acrobat, standing on the side surface of a cylinder lying on a very rough floor, moves his legs and moves at a constant speed to the right. Considering the coefficient of friction of the acrobat's boots on the surface of the cylinder equal to $\mu$, determine the limiting angle $\alpha$, which can be equal to the vertical radius of the cylinder drawn to the point at which the acrobat is located. What will be the amount of friction between the acrobat's boots and the cylinder?

## Solution

The acrobat, being on the side surface of a cylinder lying on a very rough floor, moves through positions and moves at a constant speed to the right. Considering the coefficient of friction of the acrobat's boots on the surface of the cylinder equal to $\mu$, determine the limiting angle $\alpha$ that can be made with the vertical radius of the cylinder drawn to the point at which the acrobat is located. What will be the friction between the acrobat's boots and the cylinder?

The forces acting on the acrobat are the gravity force $\vec{mg}$, the friction force $\vec{F}_{\tau p}$ and the normal reaction force $\vec{N}$ (see figure). According to the conditions of the problem, the acrobat moves at a constant speed. Therefore, according to Newton's II law, the vector sum of the forces $\vec{mg}, \vec{F}_{\tau p}$ and $\vec{N}$ is equal to zero. Let us write this condition in projections on the axes $X$ and $Y$, directed in the same way as on figure (the coordinate system $XOY$ moves to the right at the same speed as the acrobat; the origin coincides with the point at which the acrobat is located):

in projections onto the axis $X$ —
$$mg \sin \alpha - F_{\tau p} = 0;$$

in projections onto the axis $Y$ —
$$N - mg \cos \alpha = 0.$$

Considering that $F_{\tau p} \leq \mu N$, from (1) and (2) the angle $\alpha = \alpha_0$ is found:

$$\alpha_0 = \arctg \mu.$$

The magnitude of the friction force at $\alpha = \alpha_0$ is equal to
$$F = mg \sin \alpha_0 = mg \frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}.$$
