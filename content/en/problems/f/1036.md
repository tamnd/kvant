## Condition

Two identical elastic threads of stiffness $k=10^4$ N/m are attached to a body of mass $m=20$ g lying on a smooth horizontal floor. One thread is attached to the wall, the free end of the second thread begins to be pulled in the horizontal direction at a speed of $v=10$ m/s. Which thread will break if each thread breaks at an absolute elongation of $\Delta l_{\text{np}}=5$ cm? Assume that Hooke's law holds true for threads until they break; there is no training.

E. P. Sokolov

## Solution

A body of mass $m = 20$ g lying on the main horizontal floor is attached to two identical elastic threads of stiffness $k = 10^4$ N/m. One thread is attached to the wall, the free end of the second thread begins to be pulled in the horizontal direction at a speed of $v = 10$ m/s. Which thread will break if each thread breaks at an absolute elongation of $\Delta l_{\text{np}} = 5$ cm? Assume that Hooke's law holds true for the threads until they break; there is no training.

It is convenient to monitor the process of stretching the threads; it will move from the stationary system (Fig. 1) to the coordinate system, collapsing to the right with a speed of $v/2$; the body at the first moment of time is at the origin of coordinates ($x = 0$) and moves to the left with a speed of $v/2$.

The elastic force acting on the body does not depend on the movement of the ends of the threads, but depends only on the displacement of the body $x$ from the initial position:

$$F_x = -k(\Delta l + x) + k(\Delta l - x) = -2kx,$$

where $\Delta l = (v/2)t$ is the absolute value of the mixed ends of the thread at the moment when the body mixing is equal to $x$. Consequently, the body will perform harmonic oscillations with a frequency of $\omega = \sqrt{2k/m} = 10^3$ Hz around the first initial position. The mixing of the body changes over time according to the law

$$x = -\frac{v}{2\omega}\sin \omega t,$$

and thread elongation -

$$\Delta l_1 = \frac{v}{2}t - \frac{v}{2\omega}\sin \omega t,$$

$$\Delta l_2 = \frac{v}{2}t + \frac{v}{2\omega}\sin \omega t.$$

From this it is clear that during four half-periods of oscillations -

$$2n \frac{T}{2} < t < (2n + 1) \frac{T}{2} \left( T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}, n = 0, 1, 2, \dots \right)$$

- the thread will stretch more strongly, during odd half-periods - the second one.

Thus, the second thread will break if the condition is met
