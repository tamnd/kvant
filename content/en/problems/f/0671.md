## Condition

There is 1 liter of hot water with a temperature of $T_1$ and 1 liter of cold water with a temperature of $T_2$. Using hot water, cold water is heated. Is it possible to make the final temperature of water that was initially cold become higher than the temperature of water that was initially hotter?

A. Kravchaty, 9th grade student.

## Solution

There is 1 liter of hot water with a temperature of $T_1$ and 1 liter of cold water with a temperature of $T_2$. Using hot water to warm up from cold. Is it possible to make the final temperature of water that was initially cold become higher than the temperature of water that was initially hot?

Answer: you can.

We'll show you how to do it. Let us have at our disposal a vessel $A$ with hot water (hot water temperature $T_r$), a vessel $B$ with cold water (temperature $T_x$), a vessel $C$, the walls of which are thermally conductive, and a vessel $D$, which is thermally conductive.

We heat cold water in two stages:

1) Pour some of the cold water from $B$ into $C$; lower $C$ from cold water into $A$. After some time, the water temperatures in $A$ and $C$ will become equal: $T_A = T_C = T_1$, and

$$T_r > T_1 > T_x.$$

We pour “cold” water from $C$ into a heat-conducting vessel $D$.

2) Pour the remaining cold water into $C$; we lower $C$ into $A$ (in $A$ “hot” water with a temperature of $T_1$). Through
