## Condition

A cylinder with a volume of 10 liters, closed by a piston and placed in a thermostat at a temperature of 40°C, contains 0.05 moles of two substances. Determine the mass of liquid in the cylinder after an isothermal ramp, as a result of which the volume under the piston decreases by 3 times. The saturated vapor pressure of the first liquid at a temperature of 40°C is 0.7·10⁴ Pa, the second - 1.7·10⁴ Pa. Draw the scatium isotherm. The molar mass of the first liquid is 1.8·10⁻² kg/mol, the second - 4.6·10⁻² kg/mol. (10)

M. Vavrukh

## Solution

A cylinder with a volume of 10 liters, closed with a piston and placed in a thermostat at a temperature of 40 ° C, contains 0.05 moles of two substances. Determine the mass of liquid in the cylinder after isothermal rolling, the release of which reduces the volume under the piston by 3 times. The saturated vapor pressure of the first liquid at a temperature of 40° C is 0.7 10⁴ Pa, the second - 1.7 10⁴ Pa. Draw the rolling isotherm. The molar mass of the first liquid is 1.8 10⁻² kk/mol, the second is 4.6 10⁻² kk/mol.

If at first both substances in the vessels are in a gaseous state, then the pressure in the vessels is the sum of the partial pressures of the gases: $p = p_1 + p_2$, where $p_1 = v_1RT/V$, $p_2 = v_2RT/V$ ($v_1 = v_2 = 0,05$ mole, $V = 10$ l, $T = 313$ K). Substituting numerical data, we find

$$p_1 = p_2 \approx 1,3 \cdot 10^4 \text{ Pa}.$$

We see that $p_1$ is greater than the pressure $p_{H.1}$ of the saturated vapor of the first liquid ($p_{H.1} = 0,7 \cdot 10^4 \text{ Pa}$). Therefore, initially part of the first gas is condensed, and the partial pressure of this gas is $p_{H.1}$. Since $p_2 < p_{H.2} = 1,7 \cdot 10^4 \text{ Pa}$, the second substance is actually in a gaseous state and its partial pressure is $p_2$.

Thus, initially the pressure in the vessels is equal to

$$p_0 = p_{H.1} + p_2 \approx 2 \cdot 10^4 \text{ Pa}.$$

When the gases are reduced, the pressure of the first gas will remain unchanged and equal to $p_{H.1}$. The pressure of the second gas during isothermal skatin will increase until it becomes equal to $p_{H.2}$. Let's see at what volume $V'$ the pressure of the second gas becomes equal to $p_{H.2}$:

$$p_{H.2}V' = v_2RT \Rightarrow V' = \frac{v_2RT}{p_{H.2}} \approx 7,6 \text{ л}.$$

Consequently, the pressure in the isothermal vessel increases as the volume decreases to 7.6 liters. A further decrease in volume to $V'' = 3 \frac{1}{3}$ l will occur at constant pressure

$$p' = p_{H.1} + p_{H.2} = 2,4 \cdot 10^4 \text{ Pa}.$$

The graph of $p(V)$ is shown on figure.

Let us find the mass of liquid in volume $V''$. The numbers of moles of the first and second gases in volume $V''$ are equal to the corresponding

$$v_1' = \frac{p_{H.1}V''}{RT} \approx 9 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ моль},$$

$$v_2' = \frac{p_{H.2}V''}{RT} \approx 2,2 \cdot 10^{-2} \text{ моль}.$$

Consequently, there are $v_1'' = v_1 - v_1' \approx 4,1 \cdot 10^{-2}$ moles of the first substance and $v_2'' = v_2 - v_2' \approx 2,8 \cdot 10^{-2}$ moles of the second substance in the liquid state.

The mass of liquid in the vessels is $m = v_1''M_1 + v_2''M_2$. Substituting the numerical data ($M_1 = 1,8 \cdot 10^{-2}$ kg/mol, $M_2 = 4,6 \cdot 10^{-2}$ kg/mol), we obtain

$$m \approx 2,03 \text{ г}.$$
