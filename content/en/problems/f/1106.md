## Condition

A few words about superconductors. Superconductors have the property of pushing out a magnetic field (the so-called Meissner effect), thanks to which they can float above a magnet. This feature of superconductors is supposed to be used to create ultra-high-speed magnetic levitation trains, prototypes of which have already been tested. And now the task itself. A load of exactly the same mass is placed on a superconducting sample of mass $m$, hovering above a permanent magnet. How many times must the magnetic induction of the field created by the magnet be increased in order for the superconductor with the load to float at the same distance from the magnet?

A. I. Buzdin

## Solution

A few words about northern guides. Northern conductors have the property of pushing out a magnetic field (the so-called Meissner effect), thanks to which they can float above a magnet. This feature of northern conductors is supposed to be used to create northern high-speed trains on “magnetic levitation”, prototypes of which have already been tested. And now the task itself. In the northern direction, a sample of mass m, hovering above a permanent magnet, is placed on a load of exactly the same mass. How many times must the magnetic induction of the field created by the magnet be increased in order for the northern conductor with the load to float at the same distance from the magnet?
