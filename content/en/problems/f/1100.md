## Condition

** A capitular of sufficient length, immersed in a vessel with water, is hermetically sealed from above. In this case, the liquid level in the capitular is trampled by $\Delta h=4$ cm. What is the relative humidity of the air at the surface of the water in the vessel if the ambient air temperature is $20^{\circ}\text{C}$?

L. G. Markovich

## Solution

A fairly long capital, immersed in a vessel with water, is hermetically sealed at the top. In this case, the liquid level in the capital falls to $\Delta h=4$ cm. What is the relative humidity of the air at the surface of the water in the vessel if the ambient air temperature is 20 °C?

When the upper end of the capital is closed, the water vapor present in the water-free upper end of the capital becomes saturated and the partial pressure of the steam increases. Because of this, the water level in the capital decreases (since at the water level in the vessel the pressure remains constant).

Let us write down the equilibrium conditions for water in capital, located at the level of the free surface of water in the vessel, before and after closing the capital:

$$p_{\pi} + p_{\beta} - \frac{\sigma}{2r} + \rho gh = p_{\pi} + p_{\beta},$$

after closing -

$$p_{\pi} + p_{\beta} - \frac{\sigma}{2r} + \rho g (h - \Delta h) = p_{\pi} + p_{\beta}.$$

Here $(p_{\pi} + p_{\beta})$ is the pressure at the surface of the water in the vessel, equal to the sum of the partial pressures of steam and dry air; $(p_{\pi} + p_{\beta} - \frac{\sigma}{2r})$ is the pressure above the curved surface of water in the open capital ($r$ is the radius of the capital, $\sigma$ is the surface tension of water); $p_{\pi}$ - saturated water vapor pressure at a temperature of 20 °C; dry air pressure $p_{\beta}$ in the capital can be considered unchanged, since the capital is quite long.

From equations (1) and (2) we obtain:

$$p_{\pi} - p_{\beta} = \rho g \cdot \Delta h,$$

or $$\frac{p_{\pi}}{p_{\beta}} = 1 - \frac{\rho g \cdot \Delta h}{p_{\beta}}.$$

But $\frac{p_{\pi}}{p_{\beta}}$ 100% is the desired air humidity at the surface of the water in the vessel. Thus,

$$\varphi = \left(1 - \frac{\rho g \cdot \Delta h}{p_{\beta}}\right) 100\%.$$

Taking the values ​​$p_{\pi} = 2,33 \cdot 10^3$ Pa from the table and substituting $\rho = 10^3$ kg/m$^3$, $g = 9,8$ m/s$^2$, $\Delta h = 4 \cdot 10^{-2}$ m, we get $\varphi \approx 83\%$.

L. G. Markovich
