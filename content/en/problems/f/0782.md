## Condition

The capacitor was charged to a voltage of $U_0=100$ V and a resistor was connected to it. At the same time, over a certain interval of time, energy $W_1=1$ J was released in the form of heat, and during the same interval, energy $W_2=0,3$ J was released. Determine the capacitance of the capacitor.

A. Zilberman

Problems

M766 — M770; P778 — P782

## Solution

The capacitor was charged to a voltage of $U_0 = 100$ V and a resistor was connected to it. At the same time, over a certain time interval, $W_1 = 1$ J of veal was released, and over the same interval, $W_2 = 0,3$ J of energy was released. Determine the capacitance of the capacitor.

Let the voltage across the capacitor decrease to $U_1$ by the end of the first interval. According to the law of conservation of energy,

$$\frac{CU_0^2}{2} = W_1 + \frac{CU_1^2}{2},$$

where

$$U_1 = U_0 \sqrt{1 - \frac{2W_1}{CU_0^2}}.$$

It is clear that if the initial voltage on the capacitor was n times less than $U_0$, then during the same time interval, veal would be released on the resistor $n^2$ times less than $W_1$. This means that

$$\frac{W_2}{W_1} = \frac{U_1^2}{U_0^2}$$

($U_1$ is the initial voltage for the next time interval). Substituting the previously obtained value $U_1$ into this expression, we find C:

$$C = \frac{2W_1}{U_0^2 \left(1 - \frac{W_2}{W_1}\right)} \approx 29 \text{ мкФ}.$$

A. R. Zilberman
