## Condition

On a clear sunny day, when the Sun is high above the horizon, get on a smooth screen, for example, on a white wall, the shadow of the flat edge of a piece of cardboard. If you now bring your finger to the sheet of cardboard as shown in figure 1, then the shadow of a second finger will stick out on the screen towards the shadow of the finger from the shadow cast by the sheet of cardboard. ## Solution

On a clear sunny day, when the Sun is in the horizon, get on a smooth screen, to the right, on a white wall, straightening the edges of a piece of cardboard. If meterр bring your finger mak to the sheet of cardboard, as shown in figure 5, then on the screen I will direct my finger from me, thrown off by the sheet of cardboard, and dry me with the second finger. Carry out this experiment and explain its result.

It is impossible to use the concept of diffraction to describe the phenomenon, since the dimensions of the diffraction pattern at such distances are small.

The solution to the adult problem is only when we take into account that the Sun is not an exact source of light, but has finite angular dimensions.

To understand how an oncoming shadow appears, refer to figure 6.

Since the Sun as a light source has some angular dimensions (about half a degree), the shadows of all objects on a sunny day are placed. The size of the edge of the shadow depends on the distance from the object to the surface where the shadow is observed. Although in the above experiment it appears that the edge of the shadow of an object is sharply defined, in reality this is not the case. In addition to the full shadow area, there is also a partial shade area. When a finger, approaching an object, blocks part of the rays falling into the penumbra region, an oncoming shadow appears on the screen. The merging of the shadow and the counter shadow with further downward movement of the finger occurs at the border of the penumbra and the illuminated part of the screen.

By measuring the distances between the object and the screen, between the finger and the object, and the size of the oncoming shadow in front of the direct shadow, we can estimate the angular size of the Sun based on simple geometric considerations.
