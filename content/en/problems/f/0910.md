## Condition

A uniformly stretched rubber stab of mass $m$ is placed on a cylinder of radius $R$. The length of the ring in the unexpanded state is $lR$, the rigidity of the elastic band is $k$. The cylinder begins to spin with constant angular acceleration $\beta$. After what time does the ring begin to slip relative to the cylinder, if the coefficient of friction between the ring and the cylinder is $\mu$? What will be the angular velocity of the ring?

A. A. Lapides

## Solution

A uniformly growing rubber ring of mass m is attached to a cylinder of radius R. The length of the ring in the unstretched state is equal to πR, the area of ​​rubber is k. The cylinder begins to spin with a constant angular acceleration β. After what time does the ring begin to slip relative to the cylinder if the coefficient of friction between the ring and the cylinder is μ? What will be the angular velocity of the ring?

Consider a small ring element that is visible from the center of the ring at angle Δα (see figure).

The mass of this element is Δm = $\frac{m}{2\pi} \cdot \Delta\alpha$. It is acted upon by the forces $\vec{N}$ of the normal reaction from the side of the cylinder, the friction force Fτ and the tension force from the neighboring sections of the ring (we neglect the gravitational force). Since the ring grows uniformly, the tension forces are the same in absolute value and equal to T=kπ(2R-R)=kπR. The equal force of all these forces gives the ring element an acceleration $\vec{a}$.
