## Condition

Iron crystals at temperatures up to 910 °C consist of cell elements in the shape of a cube with an edge length of $a_1 = 2,87 \cdot 10^{-10}$ m. Iron atoms are located at the vertices of the cube and in its center (Fig. 3). This type of element - with a body-centered cubic lattice - is called alpha iron ($Fe_a$). At temperatures above 910 °C, Kristalliza cells of iron are a face-centered cube with an edge length of $a_2 = 3,63 \cdot 10^{-10}$ m. Atoms are located at the vertices of the cube and at the centers of its faces (Fig. 4). This type of element is called gamma iron ($Fe_\gamma$). However, what is the density of iron in these states? The relative atomic mass of iron is $\mu = 55,847$.

I. M. Nizamov

## Solution

Iron crystals at temperatures up to 910 °C consist of elematrine cells having the shape of a cube with an edge length of $a_1=2,87 \cdot 10^{-10}$ m. The iron cells are located at the vertices of the cube and in its center (Fig. 1). This type of element - with a volumetric cubic lattice - is called alpha iron ($Fe_a$). At temperatures above 910 °C, the cells of an iron crystal are a bordered cube with an edge length of $a_2=3,63 \cdot 10^{-10}$ m. The cells are located at the vertices of the cube and at the centers of its faces (Fig. 2). This type of element is called gamma iron ($Fe_\gamma$). However, what is the density of iron in these states? The relative atomic mass of iron is $μ=55,847$.

Let's first determine how many atoms of the element there are in each cube cell. If you mentally cut out one elementary cube, then each vertex will contain 1/8 atoms (each vertex is common to 8 cups). There are 8 such vertices. Therefore, per cube cell $Fe_a$ there are $\frac{1}{8} \cdot 8+1=2$ atoms (taking into account the central atoms).

There are $\frac{1}{8} \cdot 8+\frac{1}{2} \cdot 6=4$ atoms per elementary cell $Fe_\gamma$, since there are $\frac{1}{2}$ atoms for each occurrence, and there are 6 faces.

The volume of $V_a=(2,87 \cdot 10^{-10})^3 \text{ m}^3 \approx 23,64 \cdot 10^{-30} \text{ m}^3,$ per alpha-iron cell per gamma-iron cell is $V_\gamma=(3,63 \cdot 10^{-10})^3 \text{ m}^3 \approx 47,83 \cdot 10^{-30} \text{ m}^3.$

$$m = \frac{55,847 \cdot 10^{-3}}{6,02 \cdot 10^{23}} \text{ кг} \approx 9,28 \cdot 10^{-26} \text{ кг}$$

(6.02·10²³ mol⁻¹ is Avogadro’s constant). Thus, the densities of Fe₄ and Fe₇ are equal, respectively,

$$\varrho_a = \frac{2m}{V_a} \approx \frac{2 \cdot 9,28 \cdot 10^{-26}}{23,64 \cdot 10^{-30}} \text{ кг/м}^3 \approx 7850 \text{ кг/м}^3,$$

$$\varrho_\gamma = \frac{4m}{V_\gamma} \approx \frac{4 \cdot 9,28 \cdot 10^{-26}}{47,83 \cdot 10^{-30}} \text{ кг/м}^3 \approx 7760 \text{ кг/м}^3,$$

and $\varrho_a > \varrho_\gamma$.
