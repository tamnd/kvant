## Condition

At what positions of the exact source relative to a thin collecting lens with a focal length $F=1$ m can both the image and the source be seen simultaneously from at least one point?

A. R. Zilberman

Problems

M861—M865; P873—P877

## Solution

At what positions of the exact source relative to a thin proper line with a focal length $F=1$ m can both the image and the source be seen from at least one point?

The image and the source can be seen from one point if region II (see Fig. 1) does not lie entirely inside region I. The extreme case is the position of point $A$ such that the rays $AB$ and $DC$ are parallel. It's easy to see that it will be in that (and only that)

cases when the point $A$ is located at double the focal distance from the line.

To prove this, we draw the ray $EO$ parallel to $AB$ and $CD$ (Fig. 2) and use the fact that the rays $CD$ and $EO$ after refraction in the face of intersection in the focal plane.

So, the image and the source can be seen simultaneously if the source is located from the line plane at a distance greater than $d=2F=2$ m. (Recall, however, that the line formula is applicable only for narrow beams, which limits the validity of the answer.)
