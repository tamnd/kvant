## Condition

Accelerating at the maximum possible speed on a straight section of the highway, the racing car increases speed from 10.0 m/s to 10.5 m/s in 0.1 s. How long would it take him to do the same thing on a stretch of highway with a radius of 30 m? At what radius of the ring would he generally not be able to increase the speed above 10 m/s? The highway plane is horizontal.

## Solution

Accelerating with the maximum possible acceleration on a straight section of the highway, the condenser increases speed from $v_1 = 10,0$ m/s to $v_2 = 10,5$ m/s in a time of $\Delta t_1 = 0,1$ s. How long would it take him to do the same on a ring road section of the highway with a radius of $R = 30$ m? At what radius of the ring would he generally not be able to increase the speed above 10 m/s? The highway plane is horizontal.

Maximum possible acceleration

$$a = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\Delta t_1} = 5 \text{ м/c}^2$$

is determined by the friction force and remains the same for both straight and ring sections of the highway. When moving in a circle, the total acceleration $a$ consists of the tangential acceleration $a_K(a_K = \Delta v/\Delta t)$ and the normal acceleration $a_H(a_H = v^2/R)$:

$$\vec{a} = \vec{a}_K + \vec{a}_H,$$

or

$$a^2 = a_K^2 + a_H^2,$$

where

$$a_K = \sqrt{a^2 - a_H^2}.$$

In our case, the speed changes little, and the tangential acceleration can be considered constant. Then the required time

$$\Delta t_2 = \frac{\Delta v}{a_K} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\sqrt{a^2 - (v_1^2/R)^2}} = 0,15 \text{ с.}$$

Minimum turning radius at speed $v_1$

$$R_{\min} = \frac{v_1^2}{a} = 20 \text{ м.}$$
