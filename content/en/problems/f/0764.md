What speed will the wedge attain immediately after the shock wave front passes through it? ## Solution

A shock wave is a region of high pressure propagating at high speed v in the direction of the X axis (Fig. 1); at the moment the wave arrives, the pressure rises sharply from p0 to 2p0. There is a wedge in the path of wave propagation, the dimensions of the wedge are indicated at figure 2, the mass of the wedge is m. What speed will the wedge acquire immediately after the shock wave front passes through it? Assume that the speed acquired by the wedge is much less than the speed of wave propagation; friction is negligible.

$$v_A = \frac{v}{2\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{d}{2R}\right)^2}}$$

S. Krotov

LET'S CHOOSE THE ORIGIN OF REFERENCE ALONG THE X AXIS AS SHOWN IN FIGURE 2. Then the force acting on the wedge depends only on the x coordinate of the shock wave front. The horizontal projection of this force is equal to

$$F_x = p_0 c x \ t g a = \frac{p_0 c x a}{b} = \frac{p_0 c v t a}{b},$$

where x = vt is the coordinate of the wave front at time t from the beginning of the wave’s passage through the wedge. The acceleration imparted to the wedge at a given time is equal to

$$a_t = \frac{F_X}{m} = \frac{p_0 c a}{b m} \ v t.$$

At that moment of time t0, when the wave front reaches the far edge of the wedge, that is, the coordinate of the front will be equal to b = vt0, the acceleration of the wedge will be

$$a_t0 = \frac{p_0 c a}{m}.$$

Since the wedge depends linearly on time, to calculate the speed u of the wedge at time t0, we use the average acceleration value acp = \frac{p_0 ca}{2m}:

$$u = a_{cp} t_0 = \frac{p_0 abc}{2mv}.$$

When the weight of the wedge is placed in an area of ​​increased pressure, the net force acting on the wedge will be zero.

It can be seen that the condition u << v means that

$$p_0 \ll 2mv^2/abc.$$
