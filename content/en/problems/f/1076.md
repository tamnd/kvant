## Condition

** To study the solar battery, a multi-range voltmeter is used (it consists of a sensitive microammeter and a set of additional resistors). By connecting it to the battery at the 1 V limit, we get a reading of $U_1=0,7$ V. Switching the voltmeter to the 10 V limit, we get a reading of $U_2=2,6$ V. What would happen at the 100 V limit? It is known that under constant illumination, a solar battery behaves like an ordinary source, to which a high resistance resistor is connected in series.

A. R. Zilberman

## Solution

To study a solar battery, a multi-range voltmeter is used (it consists of a four-cell micrometer and a set of additional resistors). By connecting it to the battery at a limit of 1 V, we get a reading of U₁ = 0.7 V. Reading the voltmeter at a limit of 10 V, we get a reading of U₂ = 2.6 V. What would happen at a limit of 100 V? It is known that under constant illumination, a solar battery behaves like an ordinary source, with a high-resistance resistor connected in series to it.
