## Condition

Material points form a closed system. Between any two points of the system there is an attractive force equal to $\vec{F}_{ij} = km_i m_j \vec{r}_{ij}$, where $m_i, m_j$ are the masses of the points, $\|\vec{F}_{ij}\|$ is the force acting on point $i$ from the point $j$, $\|\vec{r}_{ij}\|$ is the distance between the points (Fig. 1), $k$ is a constant coefficient. At the initial moment, all parts were at rest. Prove that after some time all points will collide. Determine this time if the mass of the entire system $M = \sum_j m_j$ and the coefficient $k$ are known.

Let us prove that the particles simultaneously collide at the center of mass of the system.

By definition of the center of mass, the radius vector $\vec{r}$ drawn from the origin of coordinates to the center of mass is

$$\vec{r} = \sum_j m_j \vec{r}_j / M,$$

where $\vec{r}_j$ is the radius vector drawn from the origin to point $c$ with mass $m_j$.

Let us place the origin of coordinates at the center of mass of the system. Thus we put $\vec{r} = \vec{0}$. But according to (1) this means that $\sum_j m_j \vec{r}_j = \vec{0}$. (We will use this equality further.)

The force acting on the $i$th point from the side of the $j$ point can now be expressed as follows:

$$\vec{F}_{ij} = km_i m_j \vec{r}_{ij} = km_i m_j (\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i).$$

So from the sides of all points of the system, a force acts on the $i$th point

$$\vec{F}_i = km_i \sum_j m_j (\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i) = km_i \sum_j m_j \vec{r}_j - km_i \sum_j m_j \vec{r}_i =$$

$$= \vec{0} - km_i \vec{r}_i \sum_j m_j = -km_i M \vec{r}_i$$

(we taught that $\sum_j m_j \vec{r}_j = 0$). Let's rewrite (2) in the form $\vec{F}_i = -\alpha_i \vec{r}_i$, or in scalar form

$$F_i = -\alpha_i r_i,$$ where $\alpha_i = kMm_i$.

We see that each point moves as if it were connected to the center of mass of the system of applied rigidity $\alpha_i = kMm_i$, and in non-growing

In this case, the length of the spring is zero.

Since at the initial moment all the points accumulated (and the springs grew at the same time), in the future each point will move grounded towards the center of mass. The time after which point $i$ must reach the center of mass is a quarter of the period of oscillation of a load of mass $m_i$ on a spring of stiffness $\alpha_i$:

$$t_i = \frac{T_i}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} \sqrt{\frac{m_i}{\alpha_i}} = \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{kM}}.$$

As can be seen, $t_i$ does not depend on the parameters of a specific point; therefore, all particles will simultaneously arrive at the center of mass after time $\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{kM}}$.
