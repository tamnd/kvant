## Condition

The conduction sphere broke into several fragments, scattering great distances from each other. The fragments are connected in random order with thin wires. What is greater: the electrical capacity of the resulting system of fragments or the electrical capacity of the sphere? Neglect the capacitance of the wires.

I. M. Lutsenko, 10th grade student

## Solution

The conducting sphere is divided into several fragments, separated at great distances from each other. Do the fragments randomly connect systems of fragments or the electrical capacity of the sphere? Wire capacity claims.

Let us inform the system of some charge $q$ (let it be $q > 0$ for determination). Let's start collecting fragments connected by wires (whose capacity we neglect) into a sphere. It is clear that the surface charge density on any already assembled section of the sphere will be positive. Therefore, when fragments collide between them, repulsive forces will always act, and in order to collect the fragments into a sphere, work $A > 0$ must be done.

The charge of the collected sphere will be equal to $q$, its power station energy will be $W_{\text{сф}} = q^2/2C_{\text{сф}}$, where $C_{\text{сф}}$ is the efficiency of the sphere. The energy of the system was $W_{\text{ос}} = q^2/2C_{\text{ос}}$, where $C_{\text{ос}}$ is the electrical capacity of the system. It is clear that $A = W_{\text{сф}} - W_{\text{ос}}$, and since $A > 0$, $W_{\text{сф}} > W_{\text{ос}}$, i.e.

$$\frac{q^2}{2C_{\text{сф}}} > \frac{q^2}{2C_{\text{ос}}}.$$

This means that $C_{\text{сф}} < C_{\text{ос}}$ is the electrical capacity of the sphere is less than the electrical capacity of the system of fragments connected by wires.
