## Condition

In the Soviet-French experiment on optical location of the Moon, pulsed radiation from a ruby ​​laser at a wavelength of $\lambda=0,69$ µm was directed using a telexcol with a mirror diameter of $D=2,6$ m onto the lunar surface. A reflector was installed on the Moon, which worked like a perfect mirror with a diameter of $d=20$ cm, reflecting light exactly in the opposite direction. The reflected light is captured by the same telexcope and focused on a photodetector.

1) To what accuracy should the optical axis of the telexcol be set in this experiment?

2) Neglecting light losses in the Earth's atmosphere and in the telescope, estimate what fraction of the laser light energy will be recorded by the photodetector after reflection from the Moon.

3) Is it possible to register a reflected light pulse with the naked eye if the threshold sensitivity of the eye is taken to be equal to $n=100$ light quanta, and the energy emitted by the laser during the pulse is equal to $E=1$ J?

4) Evaluate the gain that comes from using a reflector. Assume that the surface of the Moon scatters $\alpha=10\%$ of incident light uniformly into a solid angle of 2π era.

## Solution

In the Soviet-French experiment on optical ranging of the Moon, pulsed radiation from a ruby ​​laser at a wavelength of $\lambda = 0,69$ µm was directed using a telexan with a mirror diameter of $D = 2,6$ m onto the lunar surface. A reflector was installed on the Moon,

1) The accuracy of setting the optical axis of the telexan is determined by the diffraction divergence of the beam:
$$\delta \varphi \approx \frac{\lambda}{D} = 2,6 \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ рад} = 0,05''.$$
2) The radius of the light spot on the Moon is $\frac{\lambda}{D}$. Therefore, the fraction of laser energy hitting the reflector is
$$K_1 = \frac{d^2}{4(L\lambda/D)^2} = \left(\frac{dD}{2\lambda L}\right)^2.$$
