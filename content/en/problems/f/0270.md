## Condition

In a long test tube, at a distance of $l_1$ from the sealed end there is a short mercury pot (Fig. 4). Mass of mercury $m$. At what angular speed o must the test tube be rotated around the vertical axis so that the mercury reaches the end of the test tube? The length of the test tube is $l$, the air temperature in the test tube does not change. Atmospheric pressure is $p_0$.

## Solution

In a long test tube at a distance $l_1$ from the sealed end there is a short amount of mercury (Fig. 22). Mass of mercury $m$. At what speed $\omega$ should the test tube be moved around the vertical axis so that the mercury reaches the end of the test tube? The length of the test tube is $l$, the air temperature in the test tube does not change. Atmospheric pressure $p_0$.

In a stationary frame of reference, two forces act on a column of mercury in the horizontal direction: the atomospheric pressure force $F_1 = p_0 S$ ($S$ is the cross-sectional area of ​​the test tube) and the air pressure force in the test tube $F_2 = p S$ (see Fig. 22). The difference in these forces imparts to the mercury container a centripetal acceleration equal to $a = \omega^2 l$ (since the mercury is located at the edge of the test tube). Thus,

$$m \omega^2 l = p_0 S - p S.$$

Since the air temperature in the test tube does not change, then according to the Boyle-Mariotte law

$$p_0 Sl_1 = pSl,$$

where

$$p = p_0 \frac{l_1}{l}.$$

Then

$$m \omega^2 l = p_0 S \left(1 - \frac{l_1}{l}\right)$$

And

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{p_0 S (l - l_1)}{ml^2}}.$$
