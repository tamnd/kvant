## Condition

A conveyor belt of length l moves with speed v₀. At what speed must a cube of mass m be pushed against the movement of the conveyor in order for the amount of body released due to the work of friction between the cube and the conveyor belt to be maximum? What is this maximum amount of body if the friction coefficient is μ and the condition v₀² < 2μlg is satisfied?

Fig. 1.

The amount of released body will be maximum provided that the cube travels the maximum distance relative to the transporter. To do this, it is necessary that at roller A the speed of the cube relative to the ground becomes equal to zero. The initial speed (ground ratio) that the cube should have is determined by the conditions

$$-v + at = 0, \quad l = vt - \frac{at^2}{2},$$

where a = μg is the acceleration imparted to the cube by the friction force. From here

$$v = \sqrt{2\mu g l};$$

time of movement of the cube along the conveyor belt to roller A -

$$t = \sqrt{2l/\mu g}.$$

Before stopping, the cube will follow the path of the tape

$$s_1 = l + v_0 t = l + v_0 \sqrt{2l/\mu g}.$$

Next, the cube begins to move uniformly accelerated to the right. The time after which the slippage will stop is τ = v₀/a = v₀/μg. During this time, relative to the ground, the cube will move a distance

$$s = a\tau^2/2 = v₀^2/2\mu g.$$

Since, according to the conditions of the problem, v₀ < √2μgl, then during the time τ the cube will not slide off the conveyor, that is, s < l.

The path that the cube will travel relative to the conveyor belt during this time is equal to

$$s_2 = \left| \frac{v_0^2}{2a} - v_0 \tau \right| = \frac{v_0^2}{2\mu g}.$$

The full path of the cube relative to the tape is

$$S = s_1 + s_2 = l + v_0 \sqrt{\frac{2l}{\mu g}} + \frac{v_0^2}{2\mu g}.$$

The amount of tellote released due to the work of friction is equal to

$$Q = \mu mgS = \frac{m}{2}(v_0 + \sqrt{2l\mu g})^2.$$

Let us give another solution to this problem. It was sent by reader B. Khvoostov.

Let us consider movement in a coordinate system whose speed relative to the ground is equal to v₀ and is directed in the same direction as the speed of the upper part of the conveyor belt

⟦folio 52⟧

(that is, from point $A$ to point $B$ on figure 1). Let the point $B$ coincide with the origin of coordinates at the moment the cube begins to move ($t_0=0$).

On figure 2 there are graphs of the movement of points $A$ and $B$ (fixed relative to the ground) along the axis $X$ directed to the left (from point $B$ to point $A$). The graph of the cube's movement will be parabolic.

$$x(t) = (v + v_0)t - \mu g \frac{t^2}{2},$$

where $v$ is the initial speed of the cube, $\mu g$ is the acceleration imparted to the cube by the friction force (time $t$ varies from $t_0=0$ to some moment $\tau$, corresponding to the stop of the cube relative to the conveyor belt).

The maximum amount of heat will be released provided that during the time $t$ of braking of the cube, the path traveled by it will be the maximum possible and at the same time the cube (does not leave the conveyor belt). Consequently, the graph of the motion of the cube is a parabola touching at some moment $t_k$ the line $x_A(t)$; the vertex of the parabola lies above the straight line $x_B(t)$, that is, $x(\tau) > x_B(\tau)$.

Touch conditions -

$$l + v_0 t_k = (v + v_0)t_k - \mu g \frac{t^2}{2},$$

$$v_0 = v + v_0 - \mu g t_k,$$

whence $v = \sqrt{2\mu g l}$. Let the braking be equal to

$$s_\tau = \frac{(v + v_0)^2}{2\mu g} = \frac{(\sqrt{2\mu g l} + v_0)^2}{2\mu g},$$

braking time -

$$\tau = \frac{v + v_0}{\mu g} = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu g l} + v_0}{\mu g}.$$

During this time, point $B$ will travel

$$s_8 = \frac{v_0 (\sqrt{2\mu g l} + v_0)}{\mu g}.$$

According to the conditions of the problem $v_0 < \sqrt{2\mu g l}$; hence $s_B < s_\tau$, that is $x(\tau) > x_B(\tau)$.

We find the maximum amount of heat from the law of conservation of energy:

$$Q_{\max} = \frac{m (v + v_0)^2}{2} = \frac{m}{2} (\sqrt{2\mu g l} + v_0)^2.$$
