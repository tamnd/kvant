## Condition

* Estimate the maximum temperature that can be heated to due to friction with an airborne assembly that flies at a speed close to the speed of sound. To estimate, assume that the airiness is from two-atomic nitrogen molecules whose energy is equal to $\frac{5}{2} kT$, where $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant and $T$ is the abscilot temperature. The ambient air temperature is considered equal to $-10^\circ\text{C}$.

P. L. Kapitsa

## Solution

Estimate the maximum temperature to which the surface of an aircraft flying at a speed close to the speed of sound can be heated due to air pressure. To estimate the situation, air is composed of two amortine nitrogen molecules, the energy of which is equal to 5/2 kT, where $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant and $T$ is the absolute temperature. The ambient air temperature is considered equal to $-10^\circ C$.

When nitrogen molecules collide with the surface of an airplane, it cannot receive an energy greater than $\frac{5}{2} kT_1 + \frac{mv^2}{2}$, where $v$ is the speed of the airplane (or, in the coordinate system associated with the airplane, the average speed of the ordered movement of nitrogen molecules flying on the nose of the airplane), $m$ is the mass of nitrogen molecules, $T_1$ is the temperature of the surrounding air.

Since nitrogen molecules after a collision with an airplane must have energy $\frac{5}{2} kT_2$ corresponding to the temperature $T_2$ of the surface of the airplane, then

$$\frac{5}{2} kT_2 < \frac{5}{2} kT_1 + \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

From here

$$T_{2\max} = T_1 + \frac{1}{5} \frac{mv^2}{k} \approx 373^\circ\text{K}.$$
