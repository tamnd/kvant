## Condition

In the initial state, the centers of two balls with masses m and radii R are separated by a distance of 10R. On one of them the charge +Q is uniformly distributed, on the second - Q. The first ball is tied to the remote wall with a thread that can withstand breaking tension T. The second ball at some moment

The maximum force of electrical tension between the balls is $F_{\max}=k\frac{Q^2}{4R^2}$. If $T<F_{\max}$, then the thread will break before the balls collide. If $T\geq F_{\max}$, then the associated ball will be at rest before colliding. Let's look at $T<F_{\max}$ first.
Let the distance between the centers of the balls be x at the moment the thread breaks. At this moment, the force of the Coulomb interaction of the balls is equal modulo to the tension force of the thread T:

released. Find the speed of the balls after the collision if the impact is absolutely non-elastic. Charges are not redistributed.

$$k \frac{Q^2}{x^2} = T.$$ (1)

From this moment on, the system of two balls becomes closed, and the total impulse of the system remains unchanged.

Let $v$ be the speed of the left ball at the moment the thread breaks, and $u$ be the speed of the balls after the collision. Then

$$mv = 2mu.$$ (2)

The speed $v$ is found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$-k \frac{Q^2}{10R} = -k \frac{Q^2}{x} + m \frac{v^2}{2}.$$ (3)

Solving equations (1) - (3) together, we find $u$:

$$u = \sqrt{k \frac{Q^2}{2m} \left( \frac{1}{Q} \sqrt{\frac{T}{k}} - \frac{1}{10R} \right)}.$$ (4)

If $T \geqslant F_{\max}$, then in the previous arguments we should immediately accept $x = 2R$. Then

$$u = \sqrt{\frac{0,2kQ^2}{mR}}.$$ (5)
