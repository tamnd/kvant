## Condition

Two rigid rods of length $l$ each are hinged at point $A$ (Fig. 1). Rod $BA$ is rigidly fixed at point $B$, and point $C$ of rod $AC$ can slide in direction $BC$. The rod $BA$ begins to rotate in the plane figure around the point $B$ with a constant angular velocity $\omega$. What will be the maximum speed and acceleration of point $C$, if at the initial moment the rods are extended along the guide $BC$ ($B\hat{A}C = \pi$)?

L. G. Markovich

## Solution

Two rigid rods of length $l$ are hinged at point $A$ (see figure). The rod $BA$ is rigidly fixed at the point $B$, and the point $C$ of the rod $AC$ can slide without friction along the guide $BC$. The rod $BA$ begins to heal in the plane figure around the point $B$ with a constant angular velocity $\omega$. What will be the maximum speed and acceleration of point $C$, if at the initial moment the rods are extended along the guide ($BAC = \pi$)?

Since they are rigid, at any moment of time the projections of the velocities of points $A$ and $C$ onto the direction $AC$ must be equal, that is (see figure)

$$v_A \cos \beta = v_C \cos \alpha.$$

Here $v_A = \omega l$ is the speed of point $A$, $v_C$ is the speed of point $C$ at a given point in time, $\alpha = \omega t$ is the angle that the rod $BA$ ($AC$) makes with the guide $BC$ at a given point in time ($\alpha \leqslant \frac{\pi}{4}$, $\beta = \frac{\pi}{2} - 2\alpha$; at $\alpha \geqslant \frac{\pi}{4}$ $\beta = 2\alpha - \frac{\pi}{4}$). So,

$$v_A \sin 2\alpha = v_C \cos \alpha \Rightarrow v_C = 2v_A \sin \alpha = 2v_A \sin \omega t,$$

that is, the speed of the point $C$ changes with time according to a harmonic law. The value of $v_C$ is maximum at $t = 0$, that is, at the initial moment of time:

$$a_C \max = 2v_A \omega = 2\omega^2 l.$$

L. G. Markovich
