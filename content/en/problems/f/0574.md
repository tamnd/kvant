## Condition

Two balls with masses $m_1 = 0,2$ kg and $m_2 = 0,3$ kg fly towards each other with a speed of $v = 20$ m/s each. The graph of the force of interaction of the balls during a collision as a function of time is shown in figure 2. How much heat was released during the collision of the balls?

I. Slobodechkiy

## Solution

Two balls with masses $m_1=0,2$ kc and $m_2=0,3$ kc fly towards each other at a speed of $v=20$ m/s each. A graph of the force of interaction of the balls during a collision versus time is shown in Fig. 1. How much heat was released when the balls collided?

The change in momentum of each ball is equal to the momentum of the force acting on this ball. The impulse of the force $\vec{F}$ acting on the ball during the time $\Delta t$ is equal to $|\vec{F}| \Delta t$. Consequently, the total impulse of the force acting on the ball during the collision is equal in absolute value to the area of ​​the figure under the graph of the dependence $|\vec{F}|(t)$. From figure 1 we find the impulse of force $|p_c|$:

$$|p_c| \approx 10^5 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 54 \text{ H} \cdot \text{c} = 5,4 \text{ H} \cdot \text{c}$$

(there are approximately 54 squares under the dependence graph $|\vec{F}|(t)$; the area of ​​each square is $10^5 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{ H} \cdot \text{c}$).

For projections of impulses onto the $X$ (Fig. 2) axis, we can write:
$$p_1 = m_1 v - |p_c|,$$
$$p_2 = -m_2 v + |p_c|.$$

Here $\vec{p}_1$, $\vec{p}_2$ balls after collision; $m_1 v$, $m_2 v$ — mods of ball impulses before collision. From (1) and (2) we find the speeds of the balls after the collision:
$$v_1 = \frac{p_1}{m_1} v - \frac{|p_c|}{m_1} = -7 \text{ м/c},$$
$$v_2 = \frac{p_2}{m_2} = -v + \frac{|p_c|}{m_2} = -2 \text{ м/c}.$$

Now he will use the law of conservation of energy. The kinetic energy of the system before the collision is $E = \frac{m_1 v^2}{2} + \frac{m_2 v^2}{2}$, and after the collision it is $E' = \frac{m_1 v_1^2}{2} + \frac{m_2 v_2^2}{2}$. The difference $E - E'$ is equal to the amount of heat $Q$ released during the collision. Hence,

$$Q = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{2} v^2 - \frac{m_1 v_1^2}{2} - \frac{m_2 v_2^2}{2} = 94,5 \text{ Дж}.$$
