## Solution

There are two pistons in a horizontally fixed pipe with a cross-section S open at the ends (see figure). In the initial state, the left piston is connected to the stationary wall by an undeformed spring with stiffness k. The gas pressure p₀ between the pistons is equal to the external pressure, the distance H from the right piston to the edge of the pipe is equal to the distance between the pistons. The right piston is slowly pulled out to the edge of the pipe. What force must be applied to the piston to hold it at the edge?

As the right piston is pulled out, the gas pressure between the pistons will decrease, the left piston will mix to the right, stretching the spring.

Let us write down the conditions for the equilibrium of the pistons in the position when the right piston is extended to the edge of the pipe and a force F is applied to it (see figure):

for the left piston -

$$p_0S - pS - kx = 0;$$

for the right piston -

$$F + pS - p_0S = 0$$

(*) (p - gas pressure between the pistons). From these equations it follows that

$$F = p_0S - pS = kx.$$

Since the temperature is constant, according to the Boyle-Mariotte law for the gas between the pistons we have:

$$p_0HS = p(2H - x)S,$$

$$p = \frac{p_0H}{2H-x} = \frac{p_0H}{2H-F/k} = \frac{p_0kH}{2kH-F}.$$

Substituting this expression for $p$ into (*), we obtain a quadratic equation for $F$:

$$F^2 - (p_0S + 2kH)F + p_0SkH = 0.$$

His decision is

$$F_{1,2} = \frac{p_0S}{2} + kH \pm \sqrt{\frac{p_0^2S^2}{4} + k^2H^2}.$$

Since it is clear that $F=0$ at $k=0$, we finally obtain:

$$F = \frac{p_0S}{2} + kH - \sqrt{\frac{p_0^2S^2}{4} + k^2H^2}.$$
