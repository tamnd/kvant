## Condition

A steel ball of mass $m_2=1$ g is deposited into a heat-filling vessel containing $m_1=20$ g of helium at a speed of $v=100$ m/s. Find the change in temperature into the vessel. The impacts of the ball on the walls of the vessel and the impacts of atoms on the ball are considered to be absolutely elastic.

R. Aleksadrov

## Solution

A steel ball of mass $m_2 = 1$ g is introduced into a heat-insulated vessel containing $m_1 = 20$ g of greens at a speed of $v = 100$ m/s. Start changing the temperature into the vessel. The impacts of the ball on the walls of the vessel and the impacts of atoms on the ball are considered to be absolutely elastic.

◆

After a long time, the ball will lose almost all its speed (its mass is many times greater than the mass of a green atom), and we can assume that the change in the internal energy of the gas into the vessel is equal to the initial kinetic energy of the ball:

$$\frac{m_2 v^2}{2} = \Delta U = \frac{3}{2} N k \cdot \Delta T = \frac{3}{2} k N_A \frac{m_1}{\mu} \cdot \Delta T,$$

(*)

where $N = N_A \frac{m_1}{\mu}$ is the number of green atoms in the vessel, $\Delta T$ is the change in the temperature of the green. Given that $k N_A = R$, from (*) we find:

$$\Delta T = \frac{m_2 v^2 \mu}{3 m_1 R} \approx 0,08 \text{ K}.$$

A more accurate solution should take into account the increase in the temperature of the ball. Then

$$\frac{m_2 v^2}{2} = c m_2 \cdot \Delta T' + \frac{3}{2} \frac{m_1}{\mu} R \cdot \Delta T'$$,

where $c = 0,5 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K) is the specific heat capacity of steel. From here comes $\Delta T'$:

$$\Delta T' = \frac{m_2 v^2}{2 \left[ c m_2 + \frac{3}{2} \frac{m_1}{\mu} R \right]} \approx 0,08 \text{ K}.$$

The answer turns out to be practically the same.
