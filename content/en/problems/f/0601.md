## Condition

On the Moon, a vertical cylinder closed by a heavy piston contains argon at a temperature of $T_1$. The piston can move in the cylinder without friction. A second similar piston is carefully placed on the piston. Determine the temperature $T_2$ of the gas at the new equilibrium position of the piston. The heat capacity of the piston and cylinder, as well as heat transfer, should not be taken into account. The gas is considered ideal. (9)

When the switch is closed, the capacitor $C_1$ and the coil form a condenser circuit; The voltage across the coil and capacitor changes over time according to the harmonic law:

$$u = U_1 \sin \omega_1 t,$$

where $\omega_1 = 1/\sqrt{LC_1}$ is the condensation frequency, $U_1$ is the amplitude. The period $T_1$ capacitor voltage (and current) in this circuit is equal to $2\pi/\omega_1 = 2\pi/\sqrt{LC_1}$. Therefore, voltage $u$ becomes zero at time $t_1 = T_1/2 = \pi/\sqrt{LC_1}$. At this moment, the current in the circuit is maximum and all the energy of the circuit is contained in the magnetic field of the coil.

When the switch is opened in series with the capacitor $C_1$, the capacitor $C_2$ is turned on and a capacitor circuit with inductance $L$ and capacitance $C = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$ is formed. The voltage on the coil in such a circuit changes according to the law

$$u = U_2 \sin \omega_2 (t - t_1),$$

where $\omega_2 = 1/\sqrt{LC} = \sqrt{\frac{C_1 + C_2}{LC_1 C_2}}$. The voltage goes to zero through the shower of the condensation period, that is, at the moment of time

$$t_2 = t_1 + \frac{T_2}{2} = \pi \left( \sqrt{LC_1} + \sqrt{L \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}} \right).$$

Then the key will close, the voltage on the coil will again change according to the law (*).

Let's find a correspondence between $U_1$ and $U_2$. At those moments when the voltage on the capacitor is maximum, the current in the circuit is zero and all the energy is concentrated in the power field of the capacitor. Therefore, according to conservation of energy

$$\frac{C_1 U_1^2}{2} = \frac{CU_2^2}{2},$$

where

$$U_2 = U_1 \sqrt{\frac{C_1}{C}} = U_1 \sqrt{1 + \frac{C_1}{C}}.$$
