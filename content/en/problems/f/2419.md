## Condition

It is known that the mass of the Sun is M = 2 ◊ $10^{30}$ kg. Approximately 73% of the Sun's mass is hydrogen, 25% of the mass is helium, and the rest accounts for only 2% of the Sun's mass. The source of the Sun's energy is the nuclear reaction of converting hydrogen into helium, which occurs in a small (compared to the size of the Sun itself) region near its center. Other reactions, in particular the reaction of converting helium into carbon, etc., according to scientists' calculations, will begin to occur in the Sun only at the final stage of evolution. After what time will the share of hydrogen in the mass of the Sun become equal to 72%? For reference: the solar constant near the Earth is W = 1370 W $м^{2}$, the mass of a proton is $m_{п} = 1,0072765$ amu, the proton is 1836.1527 times more massive than the electron, the mass of the helium-4 nucleus is $m_{г} = 4,00260325415$ amu.
