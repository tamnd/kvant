## Solution

You can simulate the trajectory of a charged particle in a magnetic field by placing a cyclic conductor fixed at the ends through which a current is passed in a uniform magnetic field. What will be the tension of a wire at a maximum of 1 a, if it imitates the trajectory of a proton with an energy of 1 MeV, in a magnetic field perpendicular to the magnetic field lines?

$$F = IB\Delta l,$$

perpendicular to the element $\Delta l$ (Fig. 11). The forces $T_1$ and $T_2$ of the tension of the wires ($|T_1| = |T_2| = T$) act from the applying sections. Since the wiring element in question is in equilibrium, the equilibrium force $T_1$ and $T_2$ should be equal in absolute value to force $F$, that is

$$2T \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} = IB\Delta l;$$

from here

$$T = \frac{1}{2} \frac{IB\Delta l}{\sin \frac{\alpha}{2}}.$$

But $\Delta l = \alpha R$, where $R$ is the radius of the circle that represents the posting. In addition, since we took the wiring element small (compared to $R$), then the angle $\alpha$ is small and

$$\sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \approx \frac{\alpha}{2}.$$
