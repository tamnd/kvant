## Condition

The radnus hoop $R$, rolling at a speed $v$ on a horizontal surface, impinges absolutely inelastically on a height step $h(h < R)$. What speed will the hoop have when it “runs up” the step? At what minimum speed can the hoop “climb” the step?

There is no telling.

M. Gerke, D. Sakharov, A. Cesis (students of Vinnichka p. ii. No. 17)

## Solution

The hoop of the padus $R$, moves with a speed $v$ on a horizontal surface, while we are flying abscilitono inelastic, then when we move away from the step, the impulse of the hoop will change. From the side of the step, a force acts on the hoop directed along the padus $R$ hoop (Fig. 11). Due to this, the momentum of the hoop changes as it steps so that its projection onto the axis $OX$, running along the padus $R$, becomes equal to zero. The projection of the impulse onto the $OY$ axis does not change. Consequently, as a result of the step, the impulse of the hoop becomes equal in abscyl value to $mv \sin \alpha$, and its speed becomes $v \sin \alpha = v \frac{R-h}{R}$ (see Fig. 11). Let us now use the law of conservation of energy. Immediately after removal, the hoop has a kinetic energy of $\frac{mv^2}{2}\left(\frac{R-h}{R}\right)^2$. Raising-

When placed on a step, the hoop will have potential energy $mgh$ and some kinetic energy $\frac{mv_h^2}{2}$; hence,

$$\frac{mv_h^2}{2} \left( \frac{R-h}{R} \right)^2 = mgh + \frac{mv_h^2}{2}.$$

From here we will find the speed that the hoop will have when it “runs up” the step:

$$v_h = \sqrt{v^2 \left( \frac{R-h}{R} \right)^2 - 2gh}.$$

If the speed $v_{\min}$ is the minimum speed at which the hoop will “run up” to the step, then on the step it will have only potential energy, that is

$$v_h = \sqrt{v_{\min}^2 \left( \frac{R-h}{R} \right)^2 - 2gh} = 0.$$

From here

$$v_{\min} = \frac{R}{R-h} \sqrt{2gh}.$$
