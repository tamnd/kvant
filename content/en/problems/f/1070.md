## Solution

A boy blows a soap bubble from a long coarse bar. Inflate the bubble, it releases the grub from the mouth, while the bubble blows back into the grub and time $t$ finally disappears. How long will it take to deflate an input bubble of a larger radius in the same way? Assume that the air moves through the coarse rather slowly.

The air flows through the coarse slowly, so the force of friction can be considered proportional to the speed of air flow. The excess pressure in the bubble is small compared to atmospheric pressure, so we neglect the change in air density in the bubble.

The force of friction at any moment almost balances the force of excess pressure, and $\Delta p$ is inversely proportional to the radius of the bubble. Then we can write:

$$v \sim \Delta p \sim \frac{1}{r}, \quad \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} \sim \frac{1}{r},$$

Lenino, the properties of the soap film are the same for both bubbles.

$$\frac{\Delta V}{V} \sim \frac{\Delta t}{r^4}.$$

If we have two bubbles of radii $r_1$ and $r_2$, then the ratio of time during which the volume of each of them will decrease by the same small part is

$$\frac{\Delta t_1}{\Delta t_2} = \frac{r_2^4}{r_1^4},$$

but at the same time, the ratio of the radii remains the same, which means that the ratio of the time of disappearance of the bubbles will be the same. So,

$$t_2 = 16t_1.$$
