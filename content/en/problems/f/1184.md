## Solution

A cylindrical iron rod 10 cm long was heated in the flame of a gas burner. The temperature of the hot end turned out to be 700 °C, at a distance of 1 cm from it - 500 °C, 2 cm - 300 °C, 3 cm - 200 °C, 5 cm - 150 °C, the temperature of the other end of the rod was 100 °C. After one minute, the temperature leveled off and turned out to be 200 °C. Estimate the amount of heat that the rod lost during this time. The specific heat of iron is 460 J/(kg K), rod weight is 15 g.

If there is no loss of tell, then the temperature will be set to $t_{0\text{бц}} = \frac{1}{10} (t_1 + t_2 + \dots + t_{10})$,

where $t_i$ are the average temperatures of pieces of rod length

one centimeter each (see figure). In our case $t_{0\text{бц}} = \frac{1}{10} (600 + 400 + 250 + 180 + 150 + 150 + 130 +$
$+ 110 + 100 + 100) = 218 °C$.

This means that the tell loss is $Q = cm (t_{0\text{бц}} - t_{200}) \approx 0,12$ kJ.

The number obtained is, of course, approximate, and it may be slightly different - within ±10%.
