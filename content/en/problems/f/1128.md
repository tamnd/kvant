## Condition

A thin homogeneous hoop of mass $M$ and radius $R$ ends on a horizontal surface. The horizontal diameter of the hoop is a light, smooth tube in which a ball of mass $m$ is placed, attached to the hoop by two springs with a stiffness of $k$ each (Fig. 2). Keeping the hoop motionless, the ball was deflected to the left by an amount of $x$, after which the system was imagined. Find the acceleration of the center of the hoop at the initial moment of time. There is no slipping of the hoop.

S. S. Krotov

## Solution

A uniform thin hoop of mass M and radius R rests on a horizontal surface. The horizontal diameter of the hoop is a light smooth tube in which a ball of mass m is placed, attached to the hoop by two springs of stiffness k (Fig. 1). Keeping the hoop motionless, the ball was deflected to the left by amounts x, after which the system was left to its own devices. I will clear the acceleration of the center of the hoop at the initial moment of time. There is no slipping of the hoop.

figure 2 shows the forces acting on the ball and the hoop.

Let the acceleration of the center of the hoop be equal to a. Then the equation expressing Newton’s second law for the construction motion of the hoop (in projections to the horizontal direction) will look like:

$$Ma = 2kx - F_{тр}$$

Here 2kx is the total strength force of both springs, $F_{тр}$ is the friction force from the horizontal surface.

If the acceleration of the center of the hoop is equal to a, then the vertical acceleration of the ball will be equal to $ax/R$, and the equation of motion of the ball (in projections to the vertical direction) is written as:

$$ma \frac{x}{R} = mg - N.$$

Here N is the reaction force on the side of the smooth tube. Finally, let's write down the equation that describes the rotational motion of the hoop around the center:
