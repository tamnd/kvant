## Condition

For clustration of surface tension, one of the multifilms stretched on a paradoxical one is, however, naturally explained by the interaction of gas molecules with the walls of the vessel. The fact is that in the process of establishing equilibrium, the change in momentum of the molecules that interact with the warmer wall will be greater than that of those that interact with the opposite wall. This will lead to the transfer of momentum from the walls of the vessel to the gas, and therefore to a mixing of the center of mass of the gas.

Let us first find out what kind of curve the thread will take under the influence of surface tension. It is easy to prove that in the case of $l < \pi d/2$ it will be an arc of a circle, since, assuming the opposite (that is, that the radius of curvature of the thread is not constant), we come to the conclusion that equilibrium of the thread is impossible.

Taking this into account, we find that in the case of $l > \pi d/2$ the thread will have two straight sections of length $x = \frac{1}{2} \left(l - \frac{\pi d}{2}\right)$ and a semicircle (since points $C$ and $D$ are covered) with radius $d/2$ (Fig. 2).

Let the tension force of the thread $T$ (see Fig. 2). Looking at the symmetrical sections of the thread, we can write:

$$2T = \sum_i \sigma \cdot \Delta l_i \cdot \cos \alpha = \sigma d \Rightarrow T = \frac{\sigma d}{2}.$$
