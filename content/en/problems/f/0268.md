## Condition

To determine the ratio of the heat capacity of a gas at constant pressure and at constant volume, the following method is sometimes used. Determination of the amount of gas whose initial temperature, volume and pressure are equal?

Fig. 3.

## Solution

To determine the relative temperature of the gas at a constant volume and at constant pressure and supply temperature, it is necessary to determine the amount of gas, the initial temperature, volume and pressure of which are equal to the corresponding $T_0$, $V_0$ and $p_0$, is heated with a flame wiring through which an electric valve passes for a certain time, once at a constant volume $V_0$, and the gas reaches pressure $p_1$, another time at constant pressure $p_0$, with the gas object becoming $V_1$. Show that $\frac{c_p}{c_V} = \frac{(p_1 - p_0) V_0}{(V_1 - V_0) p_0}$.

Since the wiring is heated by the same current for the same time, the gas in both cases produces the same amount of heat.

If a gas is heated at constant volume, then the amount of heat received by the gas is equal to

$$Q = c_V m(T - T_0).$$

Let us write the equation of state of the gas:

$$p_0 V_0 = nRT_0$$

And

$$p_1 V_0 = nRT.$$

Here $n$ is the number of moles of gas, $T$ is the gas temperature at pressure $p_1$.

From here

$$T - T_0 = \frac{V_0 (p_1 - p_0)}{n},$$

And

$$Q = c_V m \frac{V_0 (p_1 - p_0)}{n}.$$
