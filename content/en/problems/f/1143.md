## Condition

There are a very large number of cylindrical vessels with water, immersed one in the other so that each next one floats in the limit. The area of ​​the bottom of the smallest vessel is $s_0$ and much less than the area of ​​the bottom of the largest. The smallest vessel should have a volume of water $v_0$. How far will this vessel descend relative to the ground? (After adding water, all the vessels continue to float.)
G. V. Grigoriev

## Solution

There are a very large number of vessels with water, piled one into the other so that each next one floats in the previous one. The day area of ​​the smallest vessel is $s_0$ and much less than the day area of ​​the largest. The volume of water $v_0$ is added to the smallest vessel. How far will this vessel descend relative to the ground? (After adding water, all the vessels continue to float.)

Let us consider the equilibrium condition of any of the floating vessels: the force of gravity of this vessel is balanced by the difference in water pressure forces from the outside and from the inside. Thus, both before and after adding water to any of the vessels, the difference in water levels outside and inside each vessel remains the same. This means that relative to the ground, the position of the water levels in all vessels remains unchanged.

So, relative to the ground, the water level in the smallest vessel does not change after adding water to it. Consequently, this vessel will drop by amounts

$$h = \frac{v_0}{s_0},$$

i.e. to the height of the thin layer of water.

S. S. Krotov
