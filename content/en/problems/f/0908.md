## Condition

Airplanes fly in one straight line towards each other with the same speed $v$. The maximum detection range is $l$. One plane after

detecting another one, makes a turn without changing the speed value, and flies parallel to the second plane, which continues to fly at speed $\vec{v}$. Will planes lose sight of each other after turning if the acceleration during the turn is $a$? How long after detection should the turn begin so that at the end of the turn the distance between the aircraft is the smallest?

## Solution

Airplanes fly in one straight line towards each other with the same speed v. Maximum detection range - l. However, after detecting another plane, it makes a turn without changing the speed value, and flies parallel to the second plane, which continues to fly at speed $\vec{v}$. Will planes lose sight of each other after turning if the acceleration during the turn is a? How long after detection should the turn begin so that at the end of the turn the distance between the aircraft is the smallest?

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 = (l - \pi R - 2v\tau)^2 + 4R^2.$$

It is clear that the value of $L$ (τ) is minimal with the minimum possible value of $|x(\tau)|$ (y does not depend on τ). In this regard, two cases arise: at $l \geqslant \pi R$ the beginning $L = 2R$ is reached at $x=0$, that is, when

$$\tau = \frac{l - \pi R}{2v} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{l}{v} - \frac{\pi v}{a} \right);$$

at $l < \pi R$ the value of $L$ is minimal at $\tau=0$ and is equal to $L = \sqrt{4R^2 + (l - \pi R)^2}$.

In the case of $l \geqslant \pi R$, the starting $L = 2R$ has completed the maximum detection range. Let's find in what direction $l = l_0$ the planes will still remain within visibility after the turn, that is, when

$$L^2 = 4R^2 + (l_0 - \pi R)^2 = l_0^2.$$

Solving this equation for $l_0$, we obtain

$$l_0 = \frac{4 + \pi^2}{2\pi} R = \frac{4 + \pi^2}{2\pi} \frac{v^2}{a}.$$

So, at $l \geqslant l_0$ the planes do not consume each other out of sight. In the opposite case ($l < l_0$), after turning around, they are outside the detection range.
