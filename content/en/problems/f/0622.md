## Condition

When and how many times is the air humidity (water vapor density) greater than absc: in November at a temperature of $t_1 = 0^\circ C$ and a humidity of 95% or in the summer at $t_2 = 35^\circ C$ and a humidity of 40%, if the saturated vapor pressure at $t_1 = 0^\circ C$ is equal to $p_1 = 6 \cdot 10^2$ Pa, and at $t_2 = 35^\circ C$ is equal to $p_2 = 5,5 \cdot 10^3$ Pa?

$a^{b^c}$ means $a^{(b^c)}$.

## Solution

When and how many times greater is the absolute air humidity (air density) in the nubre at a temperature t₁ = 0°C and a humidity of 95% or in the nyula at t₂ = 35°C and a humidity of 40%, if the saturated vapor pressure at t₁ = 0°C is p₁ = 6 10²Pa, and at t₂ = 35°C it is p₂ = 5.5 × 10³Pa?

The water vapor pressure at relative humidity α is equal to

$$p = \alpha p_H,$$

where p_H is the saturated vapor pressure at a given temperature. We find the density of water vapor (absolute humidity) from the equation of gas state:

$$pV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT \Rightarrow \varrho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{\mu p}{RT} = \frac{\mu a p_H}{RT}.$$

The ratio of water vapor densities q₂/q₁ in zero and in nyubre under specified conditions (α₂ = 0.4, T₂ = 308 K, p_H₂ = p₂ = 5.5 10³Pa, α₁ = 0.95, T₁ = 273 K, p_H₁ = p₁ = 6 10²Pa) is equal to

$$\frac{q_2}{q_1} = \frac{\alpha_2 p_2}{\alpha_1 p_1} \cdot \frac{T_1}{T_2} \approx 3,4.$$

Thus, in dry nyubr (40% humidity) there is 3.4 times more water vapor in the air than in raw nyubr (95% humidity).
