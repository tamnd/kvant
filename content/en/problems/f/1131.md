## Condition

Determining the wind speed in a tornado using conventional meteorological instruments is difficult (since the tornado is small in size and moves) and unsafe. It is proposed to change it from a distance using a portable radiator (since there is a lot of dust and small objects inside the tornado, it reflects radio waves). The radar emits radio waves at a frequency of $f_0=10^{10}$ Hz. The spectrum of the signal reflected from the tornado is shown in figure 4 ($\Delta f=f-f_0$). Find the maximum wind speed in Smere.

## Solution

It is difficult to determine the wind speed in the smerya using conventional meteorological instruments (since the smerya is not large in size and moves) and is not zonal. It is proposed to measure it from a distance using a portable radio (since there is a lot of dust and small objects inside the smert, it reflects radiovolts). The radar emits radiovolts at frequency $f_0 = 10^{10} \text{ Гц}$. The spectrum of reflection from the mertz is shown in figure ($\Delta f = f - f_0$). Find the maximum wind speed in Smereta.

The frequency of the reflected signal differs from the frequency of the emitter due to the Doppler effect. The maximum frequency shift will occur if the speed of the object reflecting radiovolts is directed exactly towards the radar (or away from it).

We will assume that the radar sends short radio pulses with a repetition frequency $f_0$, and the frequency of the pulses it receives is $f_1$. The time between arrivals at the radar antenna of the $n$th and $(n+1)$th pulses is equal to
$$\frac{1}{f_1} = \frac{1}{f_0} + T_{n+1} - T_n,$$
where $T_n$ is the propagation time of the $n$ pulse from the radar to the reflecting object and back, $T_{n+1}$ is the propagation time of the $(n+1)$ pulse.

Let us denote by $L_n$ the distance from the radar to the reflecting object at the moment of emission of the $n$ pulse, and by $L_{n+1}$ the same distance at the moment of emission of the $(n+1)$ pulse. It's obvious that
$$T_n = \frac{2L_n}{v + c}, \quad T_{n+1} = \frac{2L_{n+1}}{v + c},$$

where $v$ is the desired wind speed in the tornado, $c$ is the speed of propagation of radio waves (speed of light). The difference $L_n - L_{n+1}$ is equal to the displacement of the reflecting object in a time equal to $1/f_0$:

$$L_n - L_{n+1} = \frac{v}{f_0}.$$

From here we get

$$f_1 = f_0 \frac{(c + v)}{(c - v)} \approx f_0 \left(1 + \frac{2v}{c}\right),$$

And

$$v \approx \frac{c}{2} \frac{\Delta f}{f_0} \approx 100 \text{ m/c.}$$
