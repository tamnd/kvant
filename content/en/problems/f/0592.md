## Solution

There are two heat-insulated vessels. The first of them contains 5 liters of water at a temperature of $t_1 = 60^\circ\text{C}$, the second contains 1 liter of water at a temperature of $t_2 = 20^\circ\text{C}$. First, part of the water flows from the first vessel into the second. Then, when thermal equilibrium was established in the second vessel, so much water was poured from it into the first vessel so that its volumes in the vessels became equal to the original ones. After these operations, the temperature of the water in the first vessel became equal to $t = 59^\circ\text{C}$. How much water flows from the first vessel into the second and back?

As a result of two transfusions, the mass of water in the first vessel remained the same, but its temperature decreased by $\Delta t_1 = 1^\circ\text{C}$. Consequently, the energy of the water in the first vessel has decreased.

$$\Delta Q = c_B m_1 \Delta t_1,$$

where $c_B$ is the heat capacity of water, $m_1$ is the mass of water in the first vessel.

The energy of water in the second vessel increased by values ​​$\Delta Q$. That's why

$$\Delta Q = c_B m_2 \Delta t_2,$$

(m$_2$ is the initial mass of water in the second vessel). Hence,

$$c_B m_1 \Delta t_1 = c_B m_2 \Delta t_2,$$

where

$$\Delta t_2 = \frac{m_1}{m_2} \Delta t_1 = 5^\circ\text{C}.$$

The temperature of the water in the second vessel is $t'_2 = t_2 + \Delta t_2 = 25^\circ\text{C}$. This is how it became by pouring from the first vessel into the second some mass of water $\Delta m$, having a temperature $t_1$. Let's write the heat balance equation:

$$c_B \Delta m (t_1 - t'_2) = c_B m_2 (t'_2 - t_2).$$

From here comes $\Delta m$:

$$\Delta m = m_2 \frac{t'_2 - t_2}{t_1 - t'_2} = \frac{1}{7} \text{ кг.}$$
