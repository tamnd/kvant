## Condition

In the circuit shown in figure 3, at first all the switches are open. Capacitors C1 and C2 are discharged. E.m.f. batteries E. Then the keys K1 and K3 are closed and after a while they are opened. After this, the K2 keys are closed. What potential difference will be established across capacitor C1 after switch K2 is closed?

## Solution

In the circuit shown in figure 24, at first all the switches are open. Capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ are discharged. E.m.f. battery E. Then the keys $K_1$ and $K_3$ are closed and after a while they are opened. After this, keys $K_2$ are closed. What difference will be placed on the capacitor $C_1$ after closing the key $K_2$?

When the keys $K_1$ and $K_3$ are closed, capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ are connected to the source in parallel (Fig. 25), so the voltage on each of them is $E$, and the charges of the corresponding $Q_1 = C_1E$ and $Q_2 = C_2E$. After opening the switches $K_1$ and $K_3$ and closing the key $K_2$, the capacitors are connected to a serial source (Fig. 26).

However, unlike the usual series connection of capacitors, in this case the total charge of plates 1 and 2 is not zero, but $Q = Q_1 + Q_2$. Such a charge was imparted to these plates in the first case (when the keys $K_1$ and $K_3$ were closed). In the second case, the total charge of the plates

1 and 2 is equal to $Q_1' - Q_2'$. Since the section of the circuit under consideration is isolated, we can use the law of conservation of charge:

$$Q_1' - Q_2' = Q,$$

or

$$C_1 U_1' - C_2 U_2' = (C_1 + C_2) E. \tag{1}$$

According to the law of conservation of energy

$$U_1' + U_2' = E. \tag{2}$$

Solving (1) and (2) together, we find

$$U_1' = E \left( 1 + \frac{C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \right).$$
