## Condition

A bullet hits the center of a square, freely suspended dock. If the speed of the bullet is v > v0, then it pierces the dock right through. At what speed will the dock move if the speed of the bullet is 2 v0? nv0? At what bullet speed will the dock speed be maximum?

The mass of the bullet is m, the mass of the dock is M, and the resistance is considered independent of the speed.

Given from the poem by L. Euler “On the impact of bullets when shooting at a dock,” 1770.

Fig. 3.

## Solution

In the center of the square freely suspended point $v > v_0$, we break through the dock to saturation. At what speed will the dock move if the speed of the path is $2v_0$, $nv_0$? At what speed will the dock speed be maximum?

Bullet mass $m$, dock mass $M$, resistance to sieves does not depend on speed.

In the horizontal direction, no external forces act on the bullet-dock system, that is, the system is isolated. Let us write the law of conservation of momentum and energy ($v > v_0$):

$$mv = mv_1 + MV,$$

(1)

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{mv_1^2}{2} + \frac{MV^2}{2} + Q.$$

(2)

Here $v_1$ is the speed of the bullet upon exiting the dock, $V$ is the speed of the dock, $Q$ is the amount of heat released. Moreover, this body is equal to the work of the drag force acting on the bullet as it moves inside the dock:

$$Q = A = F_c d,$$

where $d$ is the thickness of the dock.

Since, according to the conditions of the problem, the resistance force does not depend on the speed, and $d$ is

If the masque is constant, then the amount of heat release $Q$ is the same for all values ​​of the initial bullet velocity $v$.

Let's consider the case when the initial speed of the bullet is $v_0$. Obviously, this is the minimum speed at which a bullet must fly in order to pierce the dock. In this case, the bullet, having pierced the dock, will have the same speed as the dock. Let us denote this speed $u$ and write down the laws of conservation of momentum and energy for this case:

$$mv_0 = (m + M)u,$$
$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = \frac{(m + M)u^2}{2} + Q.$$

From here

$$Q = \frac{mMv_0^2}{2(m + M)}.$$

Now equalities (1) - (3) can be combined into a system and, having solved this system, we can find the values ​​$V$. Eliminating the speed $v_1$ from (1) and (2), we obtain a quadratic equation for $V$:

$$V^2 - 2\frac{mv}{m + M}V + \frac{2mQ}{M(m + M)} = 0,$$

where

$$V = \frac{m}{m + M}v \pm \sqrt{\frac{m^2}{(m + M)^2}v^2 - \frac{2mQ}{M(m + M)}}.$$

Substitute here the value $Q$ from (3) and get

$$V = \frac{m}{m + M}\left(v \pm \sqrt{v^2 - v_0^2}\right).$$

Now let’s analyze whether both roots of the equation correspond to the conditions of this problem. The impulse of the dock is numerically equal to the impulse of the resistance force, that is, the product of the value $F_C$ and the duration of its action $t$. Obviously, the greater the initial speed of the bullet, the faster the bullet passes through the dock, that is, the shorter the time $t$. Therefore, the dock speed is maximum at a bullet speed equal to $v_0$. As the initial speed of the bullet increases, the speed of the dock decreases. This corresponds to the following expression for $V$:

$$V = \frac{m}{m + M}\left(v - \sqrt{v^2 - v_0^2}\right).$$

At $v = 2v_0$
$$V = \frac{m}{m + M}\left(2 - \sqrt{3}\right)v_0,$$

and at $v = nv_0$
$$V = \frac{m}{m + M}\left(n - \sqrt{n^2 - 1}\right)v_0.$$
