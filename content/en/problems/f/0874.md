## Condition

The car moves along straight line $OO'$ (Fig. 1) and at point $A$ begins to turn without reducing speed. At point $B$ the ice column was automatically knocked down. Estimate the speed at which you could drive automatically. Consider the tires' grip on the asphalt to be good. The steering wheel automatically controls its front wheels.

A. R. Zilberman

## Solution

The car moves along straight line $OO'$ (Fig. 1) and at point A begins to turn without compressing its speed. At point B, the automatic column is removed. Estimate the speed at which the automatic machine can travel. Consider the tires' grip on the asphalt to be good. The steering wheel automatically controls its front wheels.

It is clear that we can only estimate the maximum speed of the automaton corresponding to the most circular of possible turns - when the automaton moves along the arc $AB$ of the circle, which touches the straight line $OO'$. The radius of this circle (see Fig. 2) is
$$R = |AC| = \frac{1}{2}|AD|; \quad R \approx 35 \text{ м}.$$

The machine turns (provides it with centripetal acceleration) the friction force acting on the front wheels in the direction perpendicular to the speed of the machine. If we assume that the automatic center of mass is located approximately in the middle

and raise it not very high above the road, and the acceleration is automatically not very large (think for yourself why this is significant), then the normal reaction force acting from the road on the two front wheels is equal to half the force of gravity, and the friction force is equal to $\mu \frac{mg}{2}$. Automatic motion equation when turning -

$$\frac{mv^2}{R} = \mu \frac{mg}{2},$$

where is the maximum speed $v_M$:

$$v_M = \sqrt{\frac{\mu g R}{2}}.$$

Taking $\mu=0,8$, we get $v_M \approx 12$ m/s $\approx 43$ km/h.

So, the speed of automatics is $v \leq v_M \approx 43$ km/h.

Of course, this is a rather rough estimate. A more accurate estimate could be obtained by considering the entire automation trace from point $A$ to point $B$.

A. R. Zilberman
