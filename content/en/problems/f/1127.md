## Condition

A flashlight emits a beam of rays that converge at a distance of $R_0=1$ m from it into a small spot. Two square flat mirrors were placed along the path of the beam so that the line of their contact intersects the beam axis at a distance of $r=70$ cm from the flashlight and is perpendicular to this axis. The planes of the mirrors are perpendicular to each other, and one of the mirrors makes an angle of $\alpha=30^\circ$ with the beam axis. At what distance from the flashlight will the shadow beam converge?

M. M. Tsypin

## Solution

The flashlight emits a beam of rays, converging at a distance of $R_0=1$ m from it into a small spot. On the path of the beam, two square flat mirrors were placed so that the line of their contact intersects the beam axis at a distance of $r=70$ cm from the flashlight and redistributes this axis. The planes of the mirrors overlap each other, and one of the mirrors makes an angle $\alpha=30^\circ$ with the axis of the beam. At what distance from the flashlight will the beam converge?

The course of the rays emitted by the flashlight F is shown at figure. Before placing mirrors in the path of the rays, the beam converged at point A.

Let's consider that part of the rays that is first reflected from mirror 1. After reflection, they go as if they were emitted by a flashlight F', which is a reflection of flashlight F in mirror 1. These rays would intersect at point $A'$ if mirror 2 did not stand in their path. After reflection from it, the rays go as if they were emitted by flashlight F', which is a reflection of F' in the mirror 2. It is easy to see that Ф'' is centrally symmetrical to Ф with respect to point O. As a result, the beam is contained at point $A''$, which is centrally symmetrical to point A and lies on the beam axis at a distance

$$x=R_0-2(R_0-r)=40 \text{ см}$$

from a flashlight.

Similar reasoning shows that the rays first reflected from mirror 2 also intersect at point $A''$. The position of this point obviously does not depend on the angle $\alpha$.
