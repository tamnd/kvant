The most dangerous deformation of a beam bridge is the bending deformation of the beam; To reduce it, spans should be made as short as possible. In the case of large spans, under which it is not possible to install intermediate supports, suspension bridges are used. The main load-bearing element of a suspension bridge is a flexible element called a cable or $цепью^{1}$, fixed to two powerful supports - pylons, located at the beginning and end of the bridge, and the roadway is suspended from a chain on vertical cables. This design eliminates the most dangerous bending stresses. The main stresses of a suspension bridge are tensile stresses in the chain and vertical cables, which are not as dangerous as bending of a reinforced concrete beam, and which modern high-strength steels can easily withstand.

Suspension bridges do not require rigid fastening of the roadway to supports and therefore can dampen wind or seismic loads without compromising the integrity of the structure. However, wind is the main threat to a suspension bridge: in strong winds, the bridge behaves like an airplane wing - stresses arise during the span under the influence of lift, which can destroy the bridge. Such a case happened in bridge building practice - the Tacoma Suspension Bridge in the USA was destroyed during a storm on November 7, 1940. Fortunately, no one was killed during this destruction. The process of destruction of the Tacoma Bridge was filmed on an amateur video camera. Analysis of these materials allowed engineers to understand the design flaws of suspension bridges and develop mechanisms to eliminate them. In subsequent projects, protection from wind loads was introduced, and major accidents with suspension bridges no longer occurred. (An impressive video of the destruction of the Tacoma Bridge can be viewed on the Internet at http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xxgobk5AFrI)
