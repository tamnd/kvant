## Condition

The bead slides without friction along a vertical wire of length $l$, which is bent into an accordion of length $\frac{l}{2}$ (Fig. 4). How many times is the time the bead slides greater than the time it takes to fall from a height of $l$? The amplitude of the wire bends is much less than its length. The dimensions of the bead are negligibly small compared to the length of the “knee” of the accordion.

B. N. Breizman

## Solution

*) The bead slides without friction along a vertical wire of length $l$, which is designated as a harmonic of length $l/2$. How many times is the time the bead slides greater than the time it takes to fall from a height of $l$? The amplitude of the wiring changes is much less than its length. The dimensions of the bead are negligible compared to the length of the harmonic “elbow”.

When a bead moves from one straight section of wiring to another, the absolute peak of its speed does not change. Only the direction of the bead's speed changes: it becomes parallel to the new section of the wiring. This means that the time it takes for the bead to move along the wire is the same as when the bead moves along an inclined plane—the wire unfolds. This time is equal

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2l}{g \sin \alpha}} = \sqrt{\frac{2l}{gh/l}} = 2 \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$$

($l$ is the length of the inclined plane, $\alpha$ is the angle of inclination of the plane to the horizon), i.e. it is $\sqrt{2}$ times longer than the time

$$t' = \sqrt{\frac{2l}{g}}$$The bead falls from the height $l$.

I. Sh. Slobodetsky
