## Condition

The thermally insulated vessel is divided into two parts by a heat-conducting piston, which can move into the vessel without friction. The left side of the vessel contains 1 mole of an ideal monatomic gas, the right side contains vacuum. The piston is contained with the right wall of the vessel by a spring (Fig. 2), the length of which in the free state is equal to the length of the vessel. Neglecting the heat suit of the vessel, piston and spring, determine the heat cost of the system.

B. Klyachin

## Solution

The telloisulated vessel is divided into two parts by a telloisulated piston, which can move into the vessel without friction. The left side of the container contains 1 mol of an ideal monatomic gas, and the right side contains a vacuum. The piston contains a spring with a right stack, the length of which in the free state is equal to the length of the spring. Neglecting the capacity of the fluid, piston and spring, determine the capacity of the system.

Let $T_1$ be the initial temperature of the gas under the piston, $T_2$ be the temperature of the gas after the system is informed by the body ΔQ. Since there is no friction and the vessel is telloisulated, the entire tello ΔQ goes towards changing ΔU within the energy of the system, that is, ΔQ = ΔU. The change within the energy of the system consists of a change within the energy of the gas and a change in the scalar potential energy (we neglect the capacitance of the vessel, piston and spring).

The internal energy of one mole of a monatomic ideal gas when heated from temperature $T_1$ to temperature $T_2$ increases by

$$\Delta U_1 = \frac{3}{2} R \left( T_2 - T_1 \right).$$

Potential energy of scalars changes by quantities

$$\Delta U_2 = \frac{k}{2} \left( x_2^2 - x_1^2 \right),$$

where $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the values ​​of the absolute deformation of the spring at gas temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ corresponding. Let's find the connection between the parameters of the gas under the piston and the deformation of the spring.

From the piston equilibrium condition it follows that

$$p = \frac{F}{s} = \frac{kx}{s} \Rightarrow x = \frac{ps}{k}$$

(p - gas pressure, s - piston area, k - spring elasticity). According to the Mendeleev-Clapeyron law, for 1 mol of a monatomic gas there is a correspondence to the corresponding system of co.

$$pV = RT \Rightarrow p = RT/V.$$
When the spring is deformed $x$, the volume occupied by the gas under the piston is equal to $V = xs$ and $p = RT/xs$. Substituting this expression into (*), we find

$$x^2 = RT/k.$$

Thus, the change in potential energy of the slope spring when the system is heated is equal to

$$\Delta U_2 = \frac{R}{2} (T_2 - T_1).$$

The total change in the internal energy of the system when heated from temperature $T_1$ to temperature $T_2$ is equal to

$$\Delta U = \Delta U_1 + \Delta U_2 = 2R (T_2 - T_1),$$

and the heat of the system is

$$c = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T} = \frac{\Delta U}{T_2 - T_1} = 2R.$$
