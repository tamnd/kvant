## Condition

A small heavy ball of mass $m$, having a charge $q$, is given an initial velocity $v_0$, directed vertically upward. The ball is in a uniform horizontal electric field, the direction of which is $E$. Neglecting air resistance and the dependence of free pressure acceleration $g$ on height, determine the minimum speed of the ball during its movement.

B. Bukhovtsev

## Solution

*) A small heavy ball of mass m, having a charge q, is given an initial speed v₀, directed vertically upward. The ball is in a uniform horizontal electric field, the direction of which is equal to E. Neglecting air resistance and the free pressure acceleration depending on height, determine the minimum speed of the ball during its movement.

*) The solution problem F718 is in the article by S. Krotov on p. 30.

The ball is acted upon by two constant forces: the gravity force mg and the force qE from the power field. Therefore, the ball will move with constant acceleration

$$\vec{a} = \vec{g} + \frac{q}{m} \vec{E}.$$

The vector $\vec{a}$ makes an angle α with the horizon, and

$$tg \alpha = \frac{g}{Eq/m} = \frac{mg}{Eq}.$$

The speed of the ball will change all the time both in magnitude and direction. After time t after the start of movement, the speed module

$$v_t = \sqrt{\left(\frac{qE}{m} t\right)^2 + \left(v_0 - gt\right)^2};$$

the vector $\vec{v}_t$ makes an angle β_t with the vertical such that

$$tg \beta_t = qEt/m (v_0 - gt).$$

The speed of the ball will be minimal at that moment of time τ when the projection of acceleration $\vec{a}$ onto the direction of speed $\vec{v}_τ$ will be equal to zero, that is, when the vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{v}_τ$ will be redistributed. The angle that the vector $\vec{v}_τ$ makes with the vertical at this moment will be equal to α. From the condition tg α = tan β_t we find τ:

$$\tau = \frac{m^2 g v_0}{m^2 g^2 + q^2 E^2}.$$

The minimum speed of the ball during movement is equal in magnitude

$$v_{\min} = v_{\tau} = v_0 \frac{qE}{\sqrt{q^2 E^2 + m^2 g^2}}.$$
