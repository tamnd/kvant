## Condition

The closed end is held vertical by a thread, so that the tube makes an angle α with the vertical, (Fig. 2). Tube length l, mass m, ru density ρ, atmospheric pressure h mm pm. cm. ## Solution

A thin-walled tube of ruttium barament rests with its open end on the bottom of a cup of ruttium. The closed end is held vertically by a thread, so that the tube is aligned with the vertical (Fig. 22). Tube length $l$, mass $m$, ruttium density $\rho$, atmospheric pressure $h$ mm. see Find the tension force of the thread. Neglect the size of the part of the tube embedded in ruth. The cross-sectional area of ​​the tube is $S$.

Since the barometer tube is in equilibrium, the sum of the moments of all forces acting on the tube with mercury relative to any axis must be equal to zero. Let us write down the equilibrium condition for the axis perpendicular to the plane of the drawing and passing through the point $A$, at which the tube rests on the bottom of the vessel:

$$Tl \sin \alpha - mg \frac{l}{2} \sin \alpha - Mg \frac{x}{2} \sin \alpha = 0.$$

(1)

Here $T$ is the tension force of the thread, $mg$ is the gravitational force of the tube, $Mg = \rho x Sg$ is the weight of the mercury in the tube, $x$ is the length of the mercury stand.

The pressure of the mercury in the tube is equal to atmospheric pressure, therefore

$$x = \frac{h}{\cos \alpha}.$$

(2)

Therefore, from (1) and (2)

$$T = \frac{mg \frac{l}{2} + \rho Sx^2 g}{l} = \frac{1}{2} g \left( m + \frac{\rho Sh^2}{l \cos^2 \alpha} \right).$$
