## Condition

A cylinder of radius $R$ and height $H$ contains $N$ electrons. A constant magnetic field of induction $B$ is applied parallel to the cylinder axis. It is assumed that all electrons have the same speed $v$, lying in a plane perpendicular to the magnetic field, and the impacts of electrons on the walls of the cylinder are absolutely elastic. Estimate what is equal and how the pressure on the walls of the cylinder, which creates such an “electron gas”, depends on the magnetic field (the pressure can be found up to a constant coefficient, does not depend on the magnetic field). Electron charge $e$, mass $m$. Consider that $(mv/eB) \ll R$ (“strong” field $B$). Neglect the interaction of electrons with each other.

D. A. Kuptsov

## Solution

A cylinder of radius R and height H contains N electrons. The constructed field is applied parallel to the cylinder axis by induction B. Assuming that all electrons have the same velocity v, lying in a plane perpendicular to the magnetic field, and the impacts of electrons on the walls of the cylinder are absolutely more elastic, estimate what is equal to and how the pressure on the walls of the cylinder, which creates such an “electron gas”, depends on the magnetic field (the pressure can be found accurate to the constructed coefficient, does not depend on the magnetic field). Electron charge e, mass m. Assume that (mv/eB)≪R (“strong” field B). Neglect the interaction of electrons with each other.

In a magnetic field, all electrons will move along arcs of circles with a radius

$$r = \frac{mv}{eB} \ll R.$$

Only those electrons whose orbital center is located from the walls at a distance h<r (see figure) will collide with the walls of the cylinder. With each collision, the electron transfers an impulse to the wall

$$\Delta P = 2mv \sin \alpha,$$

and these collisions will occur with an interval in time

$$\Delta t = \frac{2(\pi - \alpha)r}{v}.$$

To estimate the pressure of the “electron gas” on the cylinder walls, we will assume that each electron acts on the wall with a force

$$f = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv^2 \sin \alpha}{(\pi - \alpha)r}.$$

The number of electrons colliding with a small section of the wall with area S is equal to

$$z = 2rSn = 2rS \frac{N}{\pi R^2H}$$

(here n is the electron density in the cylinder).

Then the desired pressure of the “electron gas” can be represented in the form

$$p = \frac{zf_{cp}}{S} = 2rnf_{cp} = 2rn \frac{mv^2}{r} \left( \frac{\sin \alpha}{\pi - \alpha} \right)_{cp} = 2nmv^2C,$$

where f_{cp} is the force with which the electron acts on the wall, accelerated by the position of the center of its orbit, and C=(sin α/(π-α))_{cp} is some constant number that does not depend on the magnetic field.

The final pressure of the “electron gas” on the cylinder walls (up to the construction coefficient) is equal to

$$p = 2Cnmv^2 = \frac{2CNmv^2}{\pi R^2H}$$

and does not depend on the magnetic field.
