## Condition

An additional small weight and mass $m$, including $M : m = 15$, is placed on the rim of a massive wheel of mass $M$. How fast does a wheel have to roll for it to bounce?

Then work on resistance $R$

$$A_R = Q_1 = A_1 - A_\mathrm{д} - A_\mathrm{c} = \frac{C}{2}\left[(E - U_0)^2 - (I_0 R)^2\right].$$

With a further decrease in current from $I_0$ to 0, the voltage on the diode will not be constant. According to the current-voltage characteristic of the diode, the voltage will decrease with decreasing current according to a linear law. This means that during the period of time $t_2$, when the current in the circuit decreases from $I_0$ to 0, the diode is equivalent to a conventional resistance, the value of which is $r = \dfrac{U_0}{I_0}$ (Fig. 18 *v*).

To find the amount of heat $Q_2$ released at resistance $R$ during time $t_2$, we again use the law of conservation of energy:

$$A_2 = \Delta W_c + Q,$$

where $\Delta W_c$ is the change in the energy of the capacitor, and $Q = Q_2 + Q_r$ is the amount of heat released at resistances $R$ and $r$.

When the current in the circuit stops, the voltages across the diode and across the resistance $R$ will be zero, so the voltage across the capacitor will be $E$ and the charge will be $q' = CE$. Therefore, during the time $t_2$ a charge passed through the circuit

$q_2 = q' - q_1 = CE - C\,(E - U_0 - I_0 R) = C\,(U_0 + I_0 R)$.

Then

$$A_2 = q_2 E = CE\,(U_0 + I_0 R),$$

$$\Delta W_c = \frac{q'^2}{2C} - \frac{q_1^2}{2C} = \frac{CE^2}{2} - \frac{C}{2}(E - U_0 - I_0 R)^2$$

And

$$Q = A_2 - \Delta W_c = \frac{C}{2}(U_0 + I_0 R)^2.$$

Resistances $r$ and $R$ are connected in series, that is, at any time the same current flows through these resistances. Therefore, the amounts of heat $Q_2$ and $Q_r$ released at the resistances $R$ and $r$ at the same time are related as resistance values, that is

$$\frac{Q_2}{Q_r} = \frac{R}{r}.$$

Knowing the sum of $Q_2$ and $Q_r$ and their ratio, we can find $Q_2$:

$$Q_2 = \frac{R}{R+r}\,Q = \frac{R}{R+r}\;\frac{C\,(U_0 + I_0 R)^2}{2}.$$

Then finally

$$Q_R = Q_1 + Q_2 = \frac{C\left[(E-U_0)^2 - (I_0R)^2\right]}{2} +$$
$$+ \frac{R}{R+r}\;\frac{C\,(U_0 + I_0 R)^2}{2}.$$
