## Condition

Two rods of the same shape are made of materials with different elastic properties - the Young's moduli of the materials are equal to the correspondence of E₁ and E₂. The rods are glued together at their ends and painted with the same paint, so that the resulting sample looks like a uniform rod. What Young model of the “substance” of this rod would be modified by an experimenter who does not know what the rod constitutes?

V. A. Davydov

## Solution

Two rods of the same shape are made of materials with different elastic properties—the Young models of the materials are equal to the correspondence of $E_1$ and $E_2$. The rods are glued together at their ends and covered with the same paint, so that the resulting sample looks like a homogeneous rod. What Young model of the “substance” of this rod will be measured by an experimenter who does not know that the rod is composite?

A composite rod consists of two elastic rods connected in series with stiffnesses equal to $k_1$ and $k_2$. The elongation $x$ of a composite rod when stretched is equal to $x_1+x_2$, where $x_1, x_2$ are the elongations of the first and second rods. Since the tensile force is the same for common rods (the rods are at rest), it follows from Hooke’s law that

$$k = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2},$$

where $k$ is the stiffness of the composite rod.

Bond stiffness with Young's models of rod materials:

$$k_1 = E_1 \frac{S}{l}, k_2 = E_2 \frac{S}{l}, k = E \frac{S}{2l},$$

where $l$ is the length of each rod, $S$ is the cross-sectional area, $E$ is Young’s model of the “substance” of the composite rod. Consequently, the value $E$, which the experimenter will receive in measurements,

$$E = k \frac{2l}{S} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2} \frac{2l}{S} = \frac{2E_1 E_2}{E_1 + E_2}.$$
