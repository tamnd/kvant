## Condition

If you put a wiring loop on an ice block, from which a weight is suspended (Fig. 2), the wiring begins to cut the ice. This is explained by the fact that when the pressure increases, the melting point of ice reaches the melting point, the ice under the wiring begins to melt, and above the wiring it begins to freeze again.

However, if the loop is made not from wiring, but from a nylon thread of the same or even smaller diameter, the ice is practically not cut. Why? Try to check the described experience.

G. Kosurov
