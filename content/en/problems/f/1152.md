## Condition

Behind the lens at a distance of $l=4$ cm (large focal length), a flat mirror is located perpendicular to the main optical axis. In front of the lens, also perpendicular to the main optical axis, there is a sheet of checkered paper (Fig.2). On this sheet, images of its cells are obtained at two positions of the sheet relative to the lens. These positions differ by $l=9$ cm. Determine the focal length of the lens.

E. P. Kuznetsov

Problem solutions
M1116—M1120, F1128—F1132

Answer: a) 74; b) 6) $2S+2$.
A tract of size $m \times n$ cells contains $f=(m+1)(n+1)$ nodes. For a given area $mn=S$ the value

$$f=S+m+n+1=S+m+\frac{S}{m}+1$$

takes the most value $2S+2$ for $m=1$ or $m=S$ (for a rectangle of size $1 \times S$). For other $m$ ($1<m<S$), the value of $f$ is less than $2S+2$. This follows from inequality $m+\frac{S}{m}<S+1$, which is equivalent to the next inequality $(m-S)(m-1)<0$. Note that a more general statement is true: given $S$ (S is an integer or the resulting number), of all polygons with vertices at checkered paper nodes, the polygon that has no internal nodes contains the largest number of nodes. This follows from Pick's formula for cell $S$ of such polygons*: $S=i+\frac{r}{2}-1$, where $i$ is the number of nodes lying inside the polygon, and $r$ is the number of nodes on its boundary: the total number of nodes is $i+r \leq 2i+r=2S+2$. (Inequality turns into equality only at $i=0$.)

V. L. Gugenaher

One of the positions of the sheet, when its cells - let's call them an object - are displayed by the optical system on it, is quite obvious. Indeed, if the first image created by the lens turns out to be exactly in the plane of the mirror, then during the secondary passage of the rays through the lens (after reflection from the mirror) they will converge exactly in the plane of the sheet. Thus, in this case, the distance from the line to the image of the object being created is equal to
