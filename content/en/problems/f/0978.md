## Condition

Two identical weights B and C are attached to a rigid weightless rod ABC of length 3l as shown in figure 1: $|AB|=l, |BC|=2l$. The rod is suspended from point A to a very long weightless thread AO. At the initial moment, the rod is held in a horizontal position, and the thread is vertical; then the rod is released. What will be the speed of point A at the moment when the rod passes the lowest pole?

By the time the rod passes the lower pole, the OA thread will deviate very slightly from the vertical (since it is very long). Therefore, the tension force of the thread acting on the rod can be considered vertical all the time (its horizontal component can be claimed). Consequently, the horizontal component of the rod impulse remains unchanged, that is, equal to zero, and at a lower position

$$|mv_B|=|mv_C| \Rightarrow |v_B|=|v_C|=v.$$

The value of $v$ will be found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$2\left(\frac{mv^2}{2}\right)=mgl+3mgl=4mgl,$$

where

$$v=2\sqrt{gl}.$$

Thus, at the moment of passing the lowest pole, the rod rotates around the center K of segment BC (Fig. 2).

In this case $|AK|=2|OB|$, and the speed of point A

$$v_A=2v_B=2v=4\sqrt{gl}.$$
