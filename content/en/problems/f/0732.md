## Condition

The turned object S is located on the axis of a hollow cone with a mirrored inner surface (Fig. 1). Using lens L on the screen E, an image of an object is obtained, created by rays reflected once from the mirror surface of the cone (the received rays from the object do not hit the face). What happens to the image if the face is closed with an aperture such as a) at figure 2, a? b) on figure 2, b?

Rays, once reflected from the surface of the cone, propagate as if they were coming from a set of precise mini sources located around the circle; each such source is symmetrical to the source S relative to the corresponding generatrix of the cone. The image of these sources on the screen will be a colus (Fig. 3). It is important that the beam of rays coming from each mini-source on the face is flat: it does not pass through the entire surface of the lens, but intersects the line along the corresponding diameter (for example, from the source $S'$ the rays propagate in the vertical plane). Therefore, the degree to which such a beam is attenuated by the diaphragm depends on the shape and orientation of the diaphragm.

A symmetrical diaphragm, shown at figure 2, a, very indo, will equally attenuate the beams coming from all mini sources. In this case, the illumination of the wheel on the screen will decrease evenly. The diaphragm, shown in figure 2, b, will completely pass through the beams, the planes of which form angles $a < a_0$ with the vertical plane. Consequently, the illumination of the corresponding (i.e., upper and lower) parts of the wheel on the screen remains the same. Otherwise, the beams will be partially surrounded by the diaphragm, and the stronger the closer the plane of the beam is to the horizontal plane. That's why
