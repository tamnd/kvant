## Condition

A thin metal plate of area $s$ is filled with a layer of liquid dielectric with density $\rho$ and dielectric constant $\varepsilon$, so that the thickness of this layer is much less than the linear dimensions of the plate. What happens to the liquid if the plate is given an electric charge $+Q$?

V. D. Krivenkov

*) In “Kvashta” No. 9, 10 you should read the problem numbers: F173—F177 and F178—F182.

## Solution

A thin metal plate of area S is filled with a layer of liquid dielectric with density ρ and dielectric capacity ε so that the thickness of the layer is much less than the linear dimensions of the plate. What will happen to the liquid if the plate is given an electric field, the direction of which in a vacuum is equal to

$$E_0 = \frac{Q}{2\varepsilon_0 S},$$

and in a dielectric

$$E = \frac{Q}{2\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon S}.$$

The weakening of the field in the dielectric is associated with the polarization of the dielectric. In this case, a charge - Q₁ appears in a thin layer near the plate, and a charge + Q₁ appears on the free surface of the dielectric (Fig. 13). Inside the dielectric, the negative and positive charges of the dipoles compensate each other. Induced charges Q₁ and - Q₁ create a field in the direction emgoy

$$E_1 = 2 \frac{Q_1}{2\varepsilon_0 S} = \frac{Q_1}{\varepsilon_0 S},$$

directed against the plate field.

According to the principle of superposition

$$E = E_0 - E_1$$

or

$$\frac{Q}{2\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon S} = \frac{Q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} - \frac{Q_1}{\varepsilon_0 S}.$$

From this equation you can find the charge Q₁:

$$Q_1 = Q \frac{\varepsilon - 1}{2\varepsilon}.$$

Two electric forces act on the charge Q₁ of the free surface of the liquid: the force of attraction to the charge - Q₁, equal to

$$F_1 = Q_1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} E_1 = Q_1 \cdot \frac{Q_1}{2\varepsilon_0 S} =$$

$$= \frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 S} \left( \frac{\varepsilon - 1}{2\varepsilon} \right)^2,$$

and the repulsion force from the plate charge Q₁.
