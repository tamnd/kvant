## Condition

One U-shaped manual pressure gauge can change pressures up to 1 atm. What is the maximum pressure that can be changed if two such pressure gauges are connected in series with a short tube? (9-10 grades)

## Solution

One U-shaped manual pressure gauge can measure pressure up to 1 atm. What is the maximum pressure that can be measured if two such pressure gauges are connected in series with a short tube?

Fig. 8.

Fig. 9.

The U-shaped manual pressure gauge (Fig. 8) changes the excess pressure $\Delta p$, i.e. it shows how much the pressure $p$ in the left leg of the pressure gauge is greater than the atmospheric pressure $p_0$. A limitation on the variable pressure is imposed by the length of the pressure gauge tubes: it is impossible to change the excess pressure greater than that at which the mercury reaches the edge of the right knee. Otherwise the pressure gauge will fail. From here it is clear that

$$\Delta p = p - p_0 = \rho |\vec{g}| H = 1 \text{ атm}.$$

Let the atmospheric pressure also be 1 atm. Then

$$\rho |\vec{g}| H = p_0.$$

When two pressure gauges are connected in series (Fig. 9), the pressure $p_1$ in the left elbow of pressure gauge 1 will be greater than the pressure $p$ in the case of one pressure gauge. In fact, the pressure $p_2$ in the left leg of pressure gauge 2 is not equal to atmospheric pressure, but is greater than it by the pressure of a mercury column with a height $h$. Thus,

$$p_1 = \rho |\vec{g}| H + p_2 = p_0 + p_2,$$

And

$$p_2 = p_0 + \rho |\vec{g}| h.$$

On the other hand, $p_2$ is the pressure of the rolled air occupying the volume $S (H/2 + h/2)$ (S is the cross-sectional area of ​​the tubes). Initially, this air occupies a volume $SH/2$ in the right elbow of pressure gauge 1 and the same volume in the left elbow of pressure gauge 2. The air pressure was equal to $p_0$. Considering the air rolling by production, we write the following correspondence:

$$\frac{p_2}{p_0} = \frac{2S H/2}{S (H/2 + h/2)},$$

where

$$p_2 = 2p_0 \frac{H}{H + h}.$$

Multiply the numerator and denominator on the right side of this equation by $\rho |\vec{g}|$:

$$p_2 = 2p_0 \frac{\rho |\vec{g}| H}{\rho |\vec{g}| H + \rho |\vec{g}| h} = 2 \frac{p_0^2}{p_0 + \rho |\vec{g}| h}.$$

From equalities (2) and (3) we obtain

$$p_2^2 = 2p_0^2,$$

or $p_2 = \sqrt{2}p_0$.

Then from equality (1)

$$p_1 = (1 + \sqrt{2})p_0 \approx 2.4 \text{ amm},$$

i.e. pressure $p_1$ can exceed atmospheric pressure by amounts

$$\Delta p_1 = p_1 - p_0 \approx 1.4 \text{ amm}.$$
