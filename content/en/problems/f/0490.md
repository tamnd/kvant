## Condition

When a neutron flow passes through a 1 mm thick cadmium plate, the amount of part in the beam decreases by 15%, and their speed does not change. What fraction of the neutron flux passes through a 10 mm thick cadmium plate?

Let us write the equation of the electron in the plane perpendicular to the straight line TM:

$$\frac{mv_2^2}{R_1} = e |\vec{B}| v_2.$$

From here

$$R_1 = \frac{mv_2}{e |\vec{B}|} = \frac{1}{|\vec{B}|} \sqrt{2U \frac{m}{e}} \sin \alpha.$$

Therefore, electrons will reach point M if

$$d = \frac{2\pi k}{|\vec{B}|} \sqrt{2U \frac{m}{e}} \cos \alpha,$$

that is, if

$$|\vec{B}| = \frac{2\pi k \cos \alpha}{d} \sqrt{2U \frac{m}{e}} = k \cdot 6,6 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{T}.$$

Possible values ​​for k and |$\vec{B}$|:

for k = 1 |$\vec{B}$| = 6.6.10^{-3} T;

for k = 2 |$\vec{B}$| = 13.2.10^{-3} T;

for k = 3 |$\vec{B}$| = 19.8.10^{-3} T;

and finally, for k = 4 |$\vec{B}$| = 26.4.10^{-3} T.

Let us mentally divide the plate into an elephant 1 mm thick. If a total of N neutrons fall on the plate, then the first layer passes through the number of neutrons

$$N_1 = (1 - \alpha) N,$$

where $\alpha = 0,15$. Likewise, the second layer passes through the number of neutrons

$$N_2 (1 - \alpha) N_1 = (1 - \alpha)^2 N.$$

Obviously, after the nth layer there will remain in the flow

$$N_n = (1 - \alpha)^n N$$ neutrons.

The number of neutrons passing through a 10 mm thick cadmium plate is equal to

$$N_{10} = (1 - 0,15)^{10} N = 0,85^{10} N.$$

Thus,

$$\frac{N_{10}}{N} = 0,85^{10} \approx 0,2.$$

At the moment when the larger cylinder is deposited from the plane, the system does not slide to the right if the force $\vec{F}$ is equal in absolute value to the force $\vec{F}_2$ of friction of the system on the plane at the place where the small cylinder touches it. With the minimum possible friction coefficient, the force $\vec{F}_2$ at this moment is equal in absolute value to $\mu |\vec{N}_2|$ (Fig. 9). Hence,

$$\mu |\vec{N}_2| \geq |\vec{F}|.$$

For the system to be in equilibrium at this moment, it is also necessary that the moments of forces $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ be equal to the center of the smaller cylinder, that is

$$|\vec{F}_1| = |\vec{F}_2| = |\vec{F}|.$$

Since $\|\vec{F}_1\| = \mu \|\vec{N}_1\|$, the condition $\mu \|\vec{N}_1\| \geq \|\vec{F}\|$ must be satisfied.

It is easy to see that when condition (2) is satisfied, the large cylinder is not checked against the small one. In order for a large cylinder not to be checked, the sum of the moments of all forces about its axis must be equal to zero. This just means that the model of the friction force between the cylinders should be equal to $\|\vec{F}\|$.

Let us now find what the forces $\vec{N}_1$ and $\vec{N}_2$ are. Since the large cylinder is in equilibrium, the sum of the horizontal projections of the forces $\vec{F}_1$, $\vec{N}_1$ and $\vec{F}$ must be equal to zero:

$$\|\vec{N}_1\| \sin \alpha - \|\vec{F}\| - \|\vec{F}\| \cos \alpha = 0.$$

From here

$$\|\vec{N}_1\| = \frac{\|\vec{F}\| (1 + \cos \alpha)}{\sin \alpha}.$$

Substituting this value $\|\vec{N}_1\|$ into inequality (3), we find that the condition must be satisfied

$$\mu \geq \frac{\sin \alpha}{1 + \cos \alpha}.$$

In order to find $\vec{N}_2$, it is enough to equate to zero the sum of the vertical projections of all forces acting on the system as a whole. This gives

$$\|\vec{N}_2\| = (m_1 + m_2) \|\vec{g}\|.$$

Since, when a large cylinder is in equilibrium, the sum of the moments of all forces relative to the horizontal axis passing through the point $K$ must also be equal to zero, then

$$m_1 \|\vec{g}\| |KL| = \|\vec{F}\| |KM|,$$

or

$$m_1 \|\vec{g}\| R \sin \alpha = \|\vec{F}\| (R + R \cos \alpha).$$

From here

$$\|\vec{F}\| = m_1 \|\vec{g}\| \frac{\sin \alpha}{1 + \cos \alpha}.$$

Substituting this expression for $\|\vec{F}\|$ and the expression found above for $\|\vec{N}_2\|$ into inequality (1), we obtain

$$\mu \geq \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \frac{\sin \alpha}{1 + \cos \alpha}.$$

This means $\mu$ must be greater than the largest of the expressions on the right in inequalities (4) and (6). Obviously, for this to happen, condition (4) must be satisfied, that is,

$$\mu \geq \frac{\sin \alpha}{1 + \cos \alpha}.$$

Since $\cos \alpha = \frac{OP}{OO'} = \frac{R - r}{R + r} = \frac{D - d}{D + d}$ and $\sin \alpha = \frac{2 \sqrt{Dd}}{D + d}$, then

$$\mu \geq \frac{2 \sqrt{Dd}}{(1 + \frac{D - d}{D + d}) (D + d)} = \sqrt{\frac{d}{D}},$$

$$|\vec{F}| = \sqrt{\frac{d}{D}} m_1 \|\vec{g}\|.$$
