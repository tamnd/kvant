From the side of the load, a force $\mathbf{Q}$ (Fig. 19, *b*) acts on the wheel, equal in absolute value to the force $\mathbf{N}$, but directed in the opposite direction. It is clear that the wheel will jump if the vertical component of the force $\mathbf{Q}$ is greater than the gravity force of the wheel $M\mathrm{g}$. The vertical component of the force $\mathbf{Q}$ is maximum and equal to the force itself $\mathbf{Q}$ at the moment when the load is at the top point of the wheel. Consequently, the speed at which the wheel jumps will be determined from the condition

$$Q \geqslant Mg. \qquad (1)$$

According to Newton's second law, for the load at the highest point we can write (Fig. 19, *c*)

$$mg + N = m\,\frac{v^2}{R}\,,$$

where

$$N = m\left(\frac{v^2}{R} - g\right).$$

Since $Q = N$, expression (1) can be represented as

$$m\left(\frac{v^2}{R} - g\right) \geqslant Mg,$$

where

$$v \geqslant \sqrt{Rg\left(\frac{M}{m} + 1\right)} = 4\;\sqrt{Rg}.$$
