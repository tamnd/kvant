## Condition

Plate A of a parallel-plate capacitor is stationary, plate B is attached to the wall by a spring and can move while remaining parallel to plate A (Fig. 4). After closing key K, plate B began to move and remained in the new equilibrium position. At the same time, the distance between the plates decreased by $\alpha_1=10\%$. How much would the equilibrium distance between the plates change if switch K were closed for a short time? It is assumed that during this time plate B does not have time to noticeably shift. (9)

A. I. Buzdin

## Solution

Plate A of the flat capacitor is motionless, plate B is attached to the wall by a spring and can move, remaining parallel to plate A (see figure). After closing key K, plate B began to move and stopped in a new equilibrium position. At the same time, the distance between the plates decreased by $a_1=10\%$. How much would the equilibrium distance between the plates change if switch K were closed for a short time? It is assumed that during this time plate B does not have time to noticeably shift.

When the switch $K$ is closed, the direction to the capacitor is maintained constant and equal to the emf $\mathcal{E}$ of the source. Let the initial distance between the plates of the capacitor (when the spring is not stretched) be equal to $d$, and the displacement of plate B when reaching a new equilibrium position is equal to $x_1=a_1d=0,1d$. The charge on the capacitor will be

$$Q_1 = C_1 \mathcal{E} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}}{(d-x_1)},$$

where $S$ is the area of ​​the capacitor plate. For example, fields in capacitor $E_1=\mathcal{E}/(d-x_1)$. This field is created by two plates, so that the field created by one plate has a direction of $E_1/2$. In accordance with this, for the forces acting on the plate $B$, we can write

$$\frac{E_1 Q_1}{2} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}^2}{2(d-x_1)^2} = kx_1$$

(k is the spring stiffness).

Let us now consider the situation when the key $K$ is closed for a short time. In this case, the capacitor becomes contaminated

$$Q_2 = \varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}/d$$

(the plates did not have time to move), which subsequently remains constant. Let the displacement of the plate $B$ be equal to $x_2$ in the new equilibrium position. then the direction of the field in the capacitor will be $E_2 = Q_2/C_2(d-x_2)$, where $C_2=\varepsilon_0 S/(d-x_2)$. The equilibrium condition of the plate $B$ will be written in the form

$$\frac{E_2 Q_2}{2} = \frac{Q_2^2}{2\varepsilon_0 S} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}^2}{2d^2} = kx_2.$$

From equation (1) and (2) we find $x_2$:

$$x_2 = x_1 \left(\frac{d-x_1}{d}\right)^2 = 0,081d,$$

that is, the distance between the capacitor plates decreases by $a_2=8,1\%$.

A. I. Buzdin
