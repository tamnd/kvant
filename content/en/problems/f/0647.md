## Condition

A large dzhedeva drop left the clouds in calm weather at high altitude. At the moment when the acceleration of the drop was equal to 7.5 m/s$^2$, its speed was 20 m/s. Near the Earth, the drop fell at a constant speed and, hitting the side glass automatically, left a mark on it at an angle of 30° to the vertical. Will a traffic police inspector fine a driver for speeding if the permitted speed is 60 km/h? Consider the air resistance force to be proportional to the square of the drop velocity. (9)

V. Belonukkin

## Solution

A large raindrop left the clouds on a windless ride at a higher altitude. At the moment when the acceleration of the drop was equal to 7.5 m/s², its speed was 20 m/s. Nearby, a drop fell at a constant speed $u$, hitting the side glass automatically and leaving a mark on it at an angle $30^\circ$ to the vertical. Will the GAN inspector fault a driver for speeding if the permitted speed is automatically 60 km/h? Consider the air resistance force to be proportional to the speed of the drop.

♦

Let's write down Newton's second law for the moment when the acceleration of the drop is $a=7,5$ m/s², and the speed is $v_1=20$ m/s:

$$ma=mg-kv_1^2,$$

where $kv_1^2$ is the value of the resistance force at this moment.

Near the ground, the drop moves with some constant speed $v_2$, therefore:

$$mg=kv_2^2.$$

Since the trace left by a drop on the glass makes an angle $\alpha=30^\circ$ with the vertical, the speed of the drop and the speed of the automatic $v$ are related by the relation

$$v=v_2tg\alpha.$$

From equation (1) - (3) we find the automatic speed:

$$v=\frac{v_1tg\alpha}{\sqrt{1-a/g}} \approx 23,8\text{ м/c} \approx 85,7\text{ км/ч}.$$

The fine adds to the flame.
