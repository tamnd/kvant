## Condition

A heater was placed in a glass of water and the dependence of the water temperature on time was measured (see table).

1) How many degrees will the water cool in 1 minute if the heater is disconnected from the network at a temperature of $t_1=50^\circ\text{C}$?
2) Will water boil if the heater is left on for a long time?
The heater power is considered constant. (8)

## Solution

A cutter was lowered into a glass of water and the dependence of water temperature on time (Table 1). 1) How many degrees will the water cool in 1 minute if the cutter is disconnected from the mains at a temperature of $t_1 = 50^\circ\text{C}$? 2) Will the water boil if the cutter is not turned off long enough? The power of the cutter is considered unchanged.

We will assume that the power that the cutter supplies to the water does not change, and the temperature of the water increases unevenly due to an increase in heat output with increasing temperature. To take into account the influence of heat transfer, we will trace how the temperature increment $\Delta t$ changes during 1 minute of operation of the cutter, depending on the water temperature (we will take the average temperature between its values ​​at the beginning and end of each minute). The corresponding data are given in Table 2. Let us construct a graph of the dependence $\Delta t(t)$ using these data. As can be seen from the graph (see figure), all points are good Logasian on a straight line intersecting the temperature axis at point $t_{\text{кр}} = 85^\circ\text{C}$. It is clear that even if the law of heat transfer does not change (and with increasing temperature, evaporation should increase, which will increase heat transfer), water

will only heat up to 85°C and therefore will not boil under normal conditions.

From the same graph it is clear that at a temperature of $t_1=50^\circ\text{C}$ water heated up by 3.5 degrees in one minute, and without heat transfer it would have heated up by 6.5 degrees (ambient temperature 20°C). This means that if you turn off the heater at a temperature of 50°C, then in 1 minute the water will cool down by 3 degrees.
