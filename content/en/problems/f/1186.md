## Condition

At what value of the capacitor $C_x$ in the circuit shown in figure 3, the phase shift between the applied voltage and the current in the external circuit will be zero at any source frequency? The inductance of the coil is $L$, the resistance of each resistor is $R$. All elements of the circuit are considered ideal.

V. E. Skorovarov

## Solution

At what value of the capacitor $C_x$ in the circuit shown in figure 1, the phase change between the supplied direction and the current in the external circuit will be zero at any source frequency? The inductance of the coil is $L$, the resistance of each resistor is $R$. All elements of the circuit are considered ideal.

This problem can be solved in various ways, but the simplest and most understandable is the method of vector diagrams$^*$).

The main idea of ​​this method is that direction variables and points are represented as vectors of corresponding lengths and added using right-hand vector algebra. In the resistor, the current is in phase with the direction, and $U_R = I_R R$, where $U_R$ is the model of the direction vector on the resistor, $I_R$ is the model of the current vector in the resistor, $R$ is the resistance of the resistor. For example, the current on the inductor is ahead by $90^\circ$, and $U_L = I_L \omega L$, where $U_L$ is the model of the direction vector on the inductor, $I_L$ is the model of the current vector, $L$ is the inductance of the coil, $\omega$ is the transmission frequency

low current. The voltage on the capacitor is 90° away from the current, and $U_C = I_C \frac{1}{\omega C}$, where $U_C$ is the model of the voltage vector across the capacitor, $I_C$ is the model of the current vector through the capacitor, $C$ is the capacitance of the capacitor.

Now let's get back to our task. Let current $I_0$ flow in the external circuit. Then for a circuit containing inductors and a resistor, the vector diagram will have the form shown in figure 2, while

$$\vec{I}_0 = \vec{I}_{LR} + \vec{I}_L, \quad U_{LR} = I_{LR}R \quad \text{и} \quad I_{LR}R = I_L\omega L.$$

For a circuit containing a capacitor and resistor, the vector diagram will be similar (Fig. 3):

$$\vec{I}_0 = \vec{I}_C + \vec{I}_{CR}, \quad U_C = I_{CR}R \quad \text{и} \quad I_{CR}R = I_C \frac{1}{\omega C}.$$

In order for vector $\vec{U}_0$ to be in phase with vector $I_0$, it is necessary that the angles between vectors $\vec{I}_0$ and $\vec{I}_{LR}$, as well as between $\vec{I}_0$ and $\vec{I}_C$, be equal (Fig. 4). Then

$$\frac{I_L}{I_{LR}} = \frac{I_{CR}}{I_C},$$

whence it follows that

$$\frac{\omega L}{R} = \omega CR, \quad \text{или} \quad C = \frac{L}{R^2}.$$

Obviously, if you choose $C = L/R^2$, then the current will be in phase with the voltage at any frequency, and it can be easily shown that $U_0 = I_0R$.
