## Condition

The world's largest Soviet telephone has a mirror with a diameter of $D=6$ m as a lens. How long will it take for, by comparing photographs taken on this telephone, to notice the mutual rotation of our Galaxy and the Andromeda nebula around a common center of mass?

The distance to Andromeda is $R=1,42 \cdot 10^{11}$ $R_0$, where $R_0$ is the radius of the Earth’s orbit. The masses of the Galaxy and Andromeda are equal, respectively, $M_\Gamma=2,5 \times 10^{11}$ $M_0$, $M_A=3,6 \cdot 10^{11}$ $M_0$, where $M_0$ is the mass of the Sun. Photography is carried out in visible light at a wavelength of $\lambda=5 \cdot 10^{-7}$ m.

V. E. Belonuchkin

## Solution

The world's largest network telephone has a mirror with a diameter of D = 6 m as an object. How long will it take for, comparing the photos taken on this telephone, to notice the mutual rotation of our Galaxy and the Andromeda nebula around a common center of mass? Distance to Andromeda R=1.42·10¹¹ R₀, where R₀ is the radius of the Earth’s orbit. The masses of the Galaxy and Andromeda are equal, respectively, M₇=2.5·10¹¹ M₀, M₄=3.6·10¹¹ M₀, where M₀ is the mass of the Sun. Photoefficiency is carried out in visible light at a wavelength λ=5·10⁻⁷ m.
