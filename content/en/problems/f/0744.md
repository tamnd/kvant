## Condition

The spacecraft is a rigid thin-walled sphere of radius $R=2$ m, directed by gas. Inside the apparatus there is a ball of radius $r=R/2$, directed by the same gas as the weight of the apparatus, but at higher pressure. The ball touches the inner surface of the apparatus. As a result of the damage, the ball burst. Find how many times the pressure inside the apparatus changed if it turned out that the weight of the apparatus shifted by a distance of $a=0,5$ m. Neglect the mass of the shells; assume the temperature remains constant.

A. Buzdin

## Solution

The spacecraft is a rigid thin-walled sphere of radius $R=2$ m, filled with gas. Inside the apparatus there is a ball of radius $r=R/2$, filled with the same gas as the eighth apparatus, but at higher pressure. The ball touches the inner surface of the apparatus. As a result of the damage, the ball burst. Calculate how many times the pressure inside the apparatus changed if it turned out that the eighth apparatus was swept away at a distance of $a=0,5$ m. Consider the mass of the shells; temperatures are considered unchanged.

When changes occur inside the apparatus, the position of the center of mass (centre of mass) of the apparatus does not change.

At the initial moment m. of the apparatus is determined by how. m. system “ball with density $\varrho_1$ + ball with density $\varrho_2$ - $\varrho_1$”, where $\varrho_1$ is the density of the gas filling the sphere of radius $R$, $\varrho_2$ is the density of the gas filling the sphere of radius $R/2$.

After the balloon burst, c. m. of the apparatus is determined by how. m. sphere of radius $R$, filled with gas with density $\varrho$, where $\varrho$ is the gas density in the sphere after damage to the ball.

The fact that after the damage to the ball the entire apparatus was swept away to a distance $a$ means that... m. apparatus with a solid ball is located at a distance $a$ from the center of the sphere (see figure). From this condition we find a correspondence between $\varrho_1$ and $\varrho_2$:

$$\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \varrho_1 a g = \frac{4}{3} \pi \frac{R^3}{8} \left( \frac{R}{2} - a \right) g \Rightarrow \frac{\varrho_2}{\varrho_1} = \frac{R+14a}{R-2a}.$$

The gas density in the apparatus after the balloon burst is determined by the condition

$$\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \varrho_1 + \frac{4}{3} \pi \frac{R^3}{8} \left( \varrho_2 - \varrho_1 \right) = \frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \varrho \Rightarrow \varrho = \frac{7}{8} \varrho_1 + \frac{1}{8} \varrho_2.$$

The gas pressure $p$ in the apparatus after damage to the ball can be found from the condition $\frac{p}{p_1} = \frac{\varrho}{\varrho_1}$, where $p_1$ is the initial gas pressure inside the thin-walled sphere.

Thus,

$$\frac{p}{p_1} = \frac{\varrho}{\varrho_1} = \frac{7}{8} + \frac{1}{8} \frac{\varrho_2}{\varrho_1} = \frac{R}{R-2a} = 2,$$

that is, the gas pressure inside the apparatus increased by 2 times.
