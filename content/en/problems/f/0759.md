## Condition

A weightless rod of length $l$ with a small load of mass $m$ at the end of the hinge is fixed at point $A$ (Fig. 5) and is in a strictly vertical position, touching the body of mass $M$. With a little push the system starts to move. At what ratio between $m$ and $M$ will the rod at the moment of “separation” from the body $M$ make an angle $\alpha=\pi/6$ with the horizon? What will be the speed of the body $M$ at this moment? Let's make a claim.

## Solution

A weightless rod of length $l$ with a small load of mass $m$ at the end is hinged at point $A$ (Fig. 1) and is in a vertical position, touching a body of mass $M$. With a little push the system starts to move. At what ratio between $m$ and $M$ will the rod at the moment of “separation” from the body $M$ make an angle $α = \pi/6$ with the horizon? What will be the speed of the body $M$ at this moment? Neglect the trence.

As long as the load touches the body, the speed of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the speed of the load, and the acceleration of the body is equal to the horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load.

Let $\vec{a}$ be the total acceleration of the load. Then we can write: $\vec{a} = \vec{a}_\tau + \vec{a}_u$, where $\vec{a}_u$ is the centripetal acceleration of the load as it moves along a circle of radius $l$, that is, $|\vec{a}_u| = v^2/l$, where $v$ is the modulus of the speed of the load. The horizontal projection of the acceleration of the load is equal to (see Fig. 2)

$$a_r = a_\tau \sin \alpha - \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

The body moves with such acceleration. Equation of body motion -

$$N = Ma_r = Ma_\tau \sin \alpha - M \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha,$$

where $N$ is the normal pressure force from the load. At the moment of lifting off the load $N = 0$, and

$$a_\tau \sin \alpha = \frac{v^2}{l} \cos \alpha.$$

Acceleration $a_\tau$ at the moment of separation is imparted to the load only by the force of gravity and (see Fig. 2)

$$a_\tau = g \cos \alpha.$$

Thus, the speed at the moment of separation is equal to

$$v = \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha},$$

and the speed of the body is

$$u = v \sin \alpha = \sin \alpha \sqrt{g l \sin \alpha}.$$

According to the law of conservation of energy, at the moment of time when the rod makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizon,

$$m g l = m g l \sin \alpha + m \frac{v^2}{2} + M \frac{v^2 \sin^2 \alpha}{2}.$$

Substituting into this equality the found expression for $v$ at the moment of separation and the value $\sin \alpha = \sin \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, we find the ratio $M/m$:

$$\frac{M}{m} = \frac{2 - 3 \sin \alpha}{\sin^3 \alpha} = 4.$$

The speed of the body when the load is lifted is equal to

$$u = v \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{g l}{2}}.$$
