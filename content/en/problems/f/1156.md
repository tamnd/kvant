## Condition

Two identical electromagnets $L_1$ and $L_2$ are connected in series to a direct current circuit (Fig. 3). Using the key $K_1$, diode $D$ can be connected in parallel to one of them in a non-conductive direction. When the key $K_2$ is closed, there is a nice iron plate to the electromagnets. If the key $K_1$ is open, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate breaks away from the common magnets at the same time and falls, maintaining a horizontal position. If the key $K_1$ is closed, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate first breaks away from the magnet $L_1$, and then from $L_2$, which leads to its rotation. Explain the difference in the behavior of the plate in the first and second cases.

V. V. Golubev

## Solution

Two identical electromagnets $L_1$ and $L_2$ are connected in series to a direct current circuit (Fig. 1). Using the key $K_1$, a diode $D$ can be connected in parallel to one of them in a non-conducting direction. When the key $K_2$ is closed, the iron plate will be attracted to the electromagnets. If the key $K_1$ is open, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate breaks away from the designated simultaneous one and moves forward, maintaining a horizontal position. If the key $K_1$ is open, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate first breaks away from the magnet $L_1$, and then from $L_2$, which leads to its rotation. Explain once - In the first case, when the key $K_1$ is open, when the key $K_2$ is opened, the current in both electromagnets stops simultaneously, and both ends of the iron plate are “released” at the same time.

Let us now consider the second case, when the switch $K_1$ is open. When the switch $K_2$ (Fig. 2) is closed, identical points $I_0$ flow through the series-connected windings of the electromagnets, since no current flows through the diode $D$ connected in the non-conducting direction. Let's open the key $K_2$ (Fig. 3). The current through the electromagnet $L_1$ stops almost instantly, since the target for it is open. The current through the electromagnet $L_2$ does not change at all at the first moment. This occurs due to the fact that the self-induced emf, which arose in the winding $L_2$, is applied to the diode $D$ in the conducting direction, and current $I_0$ begins to flow through it. Subsequently, the current through $L_2$ gradually decays, in accordance with Faraday and Ohm’s laws:

$$-L_2 \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t} = IR, \quad I = I_0 e^{-Rt/L_2},$$
