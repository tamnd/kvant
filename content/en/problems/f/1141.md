## Condition

Currently, in connection with the discovery of the phenomenon of high-temperature superconductivity, the question of creating direct current transmission lines without energy loss on a Joule body is being studied. It is proposed to use a coaxial cable for direct current transmission, consisting of an inner cylindrical liquid and an outer cylindrical shell made of a superconductor. The electric and magnetic fields in such a system are drawn at figure 2. It is known that the magnetic field induction at the surface of a superconductor cannot exceed a certain value $B_{\max}$, otherwise superconductivity is destroyed, and the direction of the electric field should not exceed $E_{\max}$, otherwise an electrical breakdown of the insulating layer of the cable occurs. Determine how many times the maximum direct current power transmitted through such a cable will change if the diameters of the inner and outer sheath are doubled. What is the maximum power that can be transmitted through a cable with sheath diameters $D=8$ cm, $d=3$ cm, if $E_{\max}=20$ kV/cm and $B_{\max}=5 \cdot 10^{-2}$ T? Note: the magnetic field induction in the space between the cylindrical conductors coincides with the field of a straight conductor with current $I$:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \frac{I}{r}$$

($\mu_0=4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ H/A}^2$ is the magnetic constant).

S. M. Kozel

As can be seen from the problem conditions, the induction $B$ of the magnetic field takes the maximum value of the value of the internal core of the cable. It should not exceed a given value $B_{\max}$, otherwise the superconductivity of the internal core will be destroyed. This condition determines the maximum current $I_{\max}$ that can be passed through the cable:

$$B_{\max} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \frac{I_{\max}}{d/2} = \frac{\mu_0 I_{\max}}{\pi d},$$

$$I_{\max} = \frac{\pi d B_{\max}}{\mu_0}.$$

Thus, for a given value $B_{\max}$ the maximum current is proportional to the diameter of the internal cable core:

$$I_{\max} \sim d.$$

The voltage $E$ of the electric field in the space between the cylindrical conductors varies inversely proportional to the distance $r$ from the axis of the cylinders:

$$E(r) \sim \frac{1}{r}.$$
