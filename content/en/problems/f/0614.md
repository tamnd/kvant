## Condition

In a closed vessel there was nitrogen gas at room temperature $t_0 = 20^\circ\text{C}$ and pressure $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa. Some liquid nitrogen at a temperature of $t_1 = -196^\circ\text{C}$ (the boiling point of nitrogen at normal atmospheric pressure) was injected into the vessel. The liquid nitrogen quickly evaporated, after which the temperature in the vessel turned out to be $t_2 = -140^\circ\text{C}$. After the vessel warmed up to room temperature, a pressure of $p = 1,5 \cdot 10^5$ Pa was established in it. Determine the molar heat of evaporation of liquid nitrogen. The heat capacity of nitrogen gas at constant volume is $C_V = 20,8$ J/(mol K).

V. Belonchkin

## Solution

In a closed vessel there was nitrogen gas at room temperature t₀ = 20°C and pressure p₀ = 10⁵ Pa. Some vibrations of the load have been injected into the vessel; the box begins to jump up on the table with a temperature t₁ = -196°C (the temperature of nitrogen at normal atmospheric pressure). Liquid nitrogen quickly evaporated, after which the temperature in the vessel turned out to be t₂ = -140°C. After the vessel reached room temperature, a pressure p = 1.5 10⁵ Pa was established in it. Determine the molar heat of vaporization of each nitrogen. The heat capacity of nitrogen gas at constant volume is Cₙ = 20.8 J/ (mol K).

Since the evaporation of each nitrogen, according to the conditions of the problem, occurred quickly, temperature exchange with the environment can be avoided. Evaporation of a mass of nitrogen Δm and its heating from temperature t₁ to temperature t₂ occurs due to heat taken away from the initial mass of nitrogen m. Let's write the heat balance equation:

C_V m/M (t₀ - t₂) = Δm/M [λ + C_V (t₂ - t₁)].

From here we find the molar heat of evaporation of each nitrogen λ:

λ = [ (t₀ - t₂) / Δm/m] (t₂ - t₁)] C_V.

According to the conditions of the problem, the final pressure in the vessel turned out to be 1.5 times greater than the initial pressure P₀ at the same room temperature t₀. This means that the mass of nitrogen in the vessel has increased by 1.5 times, that is, (m + Δm)/m = 1.5, and Δm/m = 0.5. Substituting this value from the condition for the values ​​of t₀, t₁, t₂ and C_V into expression (*), we find

λ = 5500 J/mol.
