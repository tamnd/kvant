## Condition

A body of mass $m$ rises on a weightless thread with rigidity $k$. The maximum tension that the thread can withstand is $T$. The body is raised to a height $x$ from the equilibrium position and released. At what minimum $x$ will the thread break?

V. G. Kharitonov

## Solution

A body of mass $m$ stands on a weightless thread with stiffness $k$. The maximum tension that the thread can withstand is $T$. The body is raised to a height of $x$ from the equilibrium position and released. At what minimum $x$ will the thread break?

Note that $T > mg$, otherwise equilibrium would be impossible. In the equilibrium position, the thread is stretched by $\Delta l_0 = mg/k$.

After the load has been lifted to a height $x$ from the equilibrium position and released, it will move downwards. Having passed the position corresponding to the length of the free thread, the load begins to stretch the thread. Let $\Delta l$ be the maximum stretch of the thread. Then the conditions for breaking the thread are $k \cdot \Delta l = T \Rightarrow \Delta l = T/k$.

Depending on the ratio of parameters $T$ and $m$, two cases are possible: 1) $x < \Delta l_0$, 2) $x > \Delta l_0$. Let's look at them separately.

1) $x < \Delta l_0$. Let us write down the law of conservation of energy, taking as the zero level of potential energy of a body in a gravity field the position when the thread is not stretched:

$$-mg(\Delta l_0 - x) + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l_0 - x)^2 = -mg \cdot \Delta l + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l)^2.$$

Substituting the values ​​$\Delta l_0$ and $\Delta l$ from (1) and (2), we find $x$:

$$x = \frac{T - mg}{k}.$$

It is easy to see that $x \leq l_0$ is equivalent to the condition $T \leq 2mg$. 2) $x > \Delta l_0$. From the law of conservation of energy -

$$mg(x - \Delta l_0) = -mg \cdot \Delta l + \frac{k}{2}(\Delta l)^2$$

— taking into account (1) and (2) we find:

$$x = \frac{mg}{2k}\left(\left(\frac{m}{mg} - 1\right)^2 + 1\right) \text{ (при } T \geq 2mg).$$
