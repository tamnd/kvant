## Condition

One of the hypotheses about the origin of astronomers goes back to the ancient Greek legend about the son of the boron of the sun Helus - Phaethon, struck by Zeus (Jupiter). According to this hypothesis, the rock blocks from which the planet Phaethon was supposed to form came too close to Jupiter. Under the influence of Jupter's gravitational field, the swarm broke up into separate blocks - astronds. The swarm radius was estimated to be approximately $10^4$ km; the mass of the swarm (the total mass of the astronds) is $10^6$ times less than the mass of Jupter.

How far from the center of Jupter would the swarm have to travel before it began to fall apart?

## Solution

One of the legends about the origin of the asteroid belt goes back to the ancient Rechensk legend about the son of the sun Gelyus - Phaethon, struck by Zeus (Jupiter). According to this theory, a swarm of stone swarms, from which the planet Fazton was supposed to form, came very close to Jupiter. Under the influence of Jupiter's gateway swarm, the swarm split into separate swarms - asteroids. The swarm radius was estimated to be approximately 10⁴ km; the mass of the swarm (total mass of asteroids) is 10⁶ times less than the mass of Jupiter.

How far from the center of Jupiter would the swarm have to travel before it began to fall apart?

Let's look at the block closest to Jupiter. The collapse of the swarm means that the distance between this clump and the center of the swarm will increase.

The swarm as a whole falls on Jupiter with acceleration, which is imparted to it by the force of Jupiter’s attraction:

$$ma_1 = G \frac{mM}{R^2} \Rightarrow a_1 = G \frac{M}{R^2},$$

where $m$ is the mass of the swarm, $M$ is the mass of Jupiter, $R$ is the distance between the centers of the swarm and Jupiter. The block moves under the influence of the attractive force of Jupiter and the attractive force acting on it from the swarm as a whole. The acceleration with which the block moves towards Jupiter is

$$a_2 = G \frac{M}{(R-r)^2} - G \frac{m}{r^2};$$

where $r$ is the radius of the swarm. If $a_2 > a_1$, then the distance between the block and the center of the swarm will increase with time
