## Condition

Geysers can be considered as large underground reservoirs filled with groundwater and heated by the earth's body (Fig. 4). The exit from them to the surface of the Earth is through a narrow channel, which during the “quiet” period is almost completely filled with water. Assuming that the “active” period is when the water in the underground reservoir boils, and that during an eruption the channel is filled only with steam that is thrown out, estimate how much water the geyser reservoir loses during one eruption. Channel depth $h=90$ m; heat of evaporation of water $\lambda=2,26 \cdot 10^6$ J/kg; heat capacity of water $c=4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K). The dependence of saturated water vapor pressure on temperature is shown in figure 5. (9 cells)

## Solution

Geysers can be thought of as large underground reservoirs filled with groundwater and heated by the earth's surface (Fig. 12). The exit from them to the surface of the earth is through a narrow channel, which during the “quiet” period is almost completely filled with water. Considering that the “active” period begins when we fill the underground reservoir with water, and that during an eruption the channel is filled with milky steam that is thrown out, we can estimate what part of the water the reservoir heats up during a single-zone eruption. Channel depth $h = 90$ m; water temperature $\lambda = 2,26 \cdot 10^6$ J/kg; water temperature $c = 4,2 \cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K). The dependence of saturated water pressure on temperature is shown in figure 13.

(M is the mass of the appeared steam) we find

$$\frac{\Delta M}{M} \approx \frac{c(t-t_0)}{\lambda} \approx 0,15.$$

The above solution is an estimate, since it does not take into account a number of factors that in practice can slightly change the result obtained. For example, the solution does not take into account the supply of the body to water from heated layers during the “active” period, as well as the entry of new portions of water into the reservoir through underground channels during the eruption.
