## Condition

A hoop of radius $r$ rolls down from a height $H$ ($r \ll H$) without slipping along an inclined plane with an angle $\alpha$ at the base and absolutely elastically hits a smooth wall perpendicular to the inclined plane (Fig. 1). To what height does the hoop rise after impact if the sliding friction coefficient is $\mu$?

M. Borodovsky

## Solution

The radius hoop $r$ rolls down from a height $H$ ($r \ll H$) without a calcined plane with an angle a at the base and absolutely elastically hits the ella wall perpendicular to the inclined plane (Fig. 1). To what height does the noble impact hoop rise if the sliding friction coefficient is $\mu$?

The hoop rolls down an inclined plane without piercing. Let us determine the speed $v$ of its translational motion at the moment of impact with the wall. At this moment, the linear velocity model of the points of the hoop is also equal to $v$, and the total kinetic energy of the hoop is the sum of the energy of rotational and translational motion, that is

$$K = \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{mv^2}{2} = mv^2$$

(m is the mass of the hoop). From the law of conservation of energy, taking into account that $r \ll H$, we have

⟦folio 42⟧

$$mgH = mv^2 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{gH}.$$

Since the impact of the hoop on the wall is absolutely elastic, the direction of the translational movement of the hoop will change to the opposite. Since the wall is smooth, after the impact the hoop will still rotate counterclockwise (see Fig. 1).
Consequently, after the impact, the hoop will slide up the inclined plane, rotating counterclockwise.

When the hoop reaches its maximum height, its direction will not change. Indeed, the time $\tau_1$ during which the counterclockwise rotation of the hoop stops, according to Newton’s second law, is determined by the condition

$$f_{\tau_1} = \tau_1 \mu mg \cos \alpha = mv,$$

where $f_{\tau_1}$ is the friction force (Fig. 2). From here

$$\tau_1 = \frac{v}{\mu g \cos \alpha}.$$

The time $\tau_2$ during which the hoop rises to its maximum height, with a constant direction of rotation, is determined by the condition

$$(mg \sin \alpha + \mu mg \cos \alpha) \tau_2 = mv,$$

where

$$\tau_2 = \frac{v}{g(\sin \alpha + \mu \cos \alpha)}.$$

Thus, $\tau_2 < \tau_1$, and at the moment of maximum failure the hoop will continue to rotate counterclockwise.

The movement of the hoop up the inclined plane is uniformly slow. The distance covered by it until it comes to a complete stop is equal to

$$l = v_{cp} \tau_2 = \frac{v}{2} \tau_2 = \frac{H}{2(\sin \alpha + \mu \cos \alpha)}.$$

The maximum height of the hoop after impact is

$$h = l \sin \alpha = \frac{H \sin \alpha}{2(\sin \alpha + \mu \cos \alpha)}.$$
