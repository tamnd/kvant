## Condition

To maintain a constant water temperature in a flow-through aquarium, use two identical heaters (Fig. 2). In normal mode, one of them is used, and if a second heater is connected in parallel, then the cold water consumption has to be increased 3 times. How should the cold water flow be changed if the heaters are connected in series? What should the cold water flow rate be if one spiral with a power of P=100 W is turned on? Cold water temperature t₁=10 °C, water temperature in the aquarium t₂=27 °C. The water is quickly mixed.

A. R. Zilberman

## Solution

To maintain a constant water temperature in a flow-through aquarium, use two identical heaters (see figure). In normal mode, the arrival is tripled. How should the cold water flow be changed if the heaters are connected to a series network? What should the cold water flow rate be if one spiral power is turned on?

The heat received by the water from the heaters compensates for thermal losses - the heat given off by the aquarium to the environment. The power loss depends on the difference in temperature between the water in the aquarium and the environment. If these temperatures do not change, then the power losses remain unchanged.

Let us denote the second water consumption in the first case (one spiral is turned on) $\Delta m$. Then

$$P = c \cdot \Delta m \cdot (t_2 - t_1) + q,$$

(1) where $q$ is the power loss. In the second case (two spirals connected in parallel are included), the power returns twice, and

$$2P = c \cdot 3m \cdot (t_2 - t_1) + q.$$ (2)

From (1) and (2) we can immediately obtain

$$q = \frac{1}{2} P.$$

But in the case when the spirals are connected in series, the power released by the immi will be exactly $P/2$. This means that in this case -body should be disabled.

Now from equations (1) and (2) we find the second water flow rate, provided that one spiral is turned on:

$$\Delta m = \frac{P}{2c(t_2 - t_1)} = \frac{100}{2 \cdot 4,2 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 17} \text{ кг/c} \approx 7 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ кг/c}.$$
