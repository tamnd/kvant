## Condition

The tire of the automatic machine involved in the tonka bursts. Estimate at what speed the car should go so that the tire does not wrinkle.

P. L. Kapitsa

## Solution

At the end of automation, the tire bursts. Estimate at what speed the examb automation should be used so that the tire does not wrinkle.

We will assume that the vehicle moves uniformly and without slipping, and consider what forces act on the section of the tire of one wheel that is in contact with the road: In the inertial coordinate system associated with the center of the wheel, this section of the tire moves along a circle of radius $R$ (wheel radius) with a linear speed equal in magnitude to the speed of the vehicle v. The centripetal acceleration of this section is $a_u = \frac{v^2}{R}$.

What forces act on such a section of the tire (let’s call it “leading”)? If we ignore the deformation of the tire and the forces associated with it, then there is only one force in the leading area of ​​action - the road reaction force $N$.

(The force mg acting on this area is much less than $N$, and it can be avoided).

Since conventional automatics have 4 wheels,

then this force (on average) is equal to $N = \frac{1}{4} Mg$,

where $M$ is the automatic mass. The acceleration imparted to the leading section of the tire of mass $m$ by the force $N$ is the centripetal acceleration, that is

$$\frac{1}{4} Mg = m \frac{v^2}{R}.$$

(1)

Since the left side of equality (1) was constructed (for a given machine gunner), then the conditions $R =$ const (the condition of the tire’s invariability) and, as a consequence, $m =$ const uniquely determine the required speed:

$$v = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{M}{m} g R}.$$

(2)

To obtain a quantitative assessment, we substitute the following values ​​into this formula: $g = 10 \, m/c^2$, $R = 0,5 \, m$, $M = 2 \cdot 10^3 \, \text{кг}$. The value of $m$ needs to be estimated. The tire weighs about 30 kg, and a small section of it collides with the road. We will assume that its length is $l = 10 \, \text{см}$. Then

$$m = m_{\text{ш}} \cdot \frac{l}{2\pi R} \approx 30 \cdot \frac{10}{2 \cdot 3,14 \cdot 0,5} \approx 1 \ (\text{кг})$$

(we assume that the radius of the tire is small compared to the radius of the wheel).

Now substituting all these quantities into formula (2), we obtain

$$v = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{2000}{1} \cdot 10 \cdot 0,5} = 50 \, m/c = 180 \, \text{км/ч}.$$

For geological automation, this speed is quite low.
