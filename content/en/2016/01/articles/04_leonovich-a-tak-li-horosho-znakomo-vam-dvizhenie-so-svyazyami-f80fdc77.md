Are you so familiar with the connected movement?

Excerpts from the works of a whole line of prominent scientists testify to their long-term existence - several centuries! – attention to the topic of our today's issue. What is striking in this “summary” is the painstaking search for the most general laws underlying mechanics, the so-called first principles, or, in the words of Lagrange, a certain “general formula”. From these universal principles, scientists hoped to derive, as special cases, all the variety of discovered laws. And they succeeded! The creation of analytical mechanics - an alternative to the Newtonian approach - turned out to be extremely productive not only for solving its own problems, but its methods grew from classical physics to modern physics, closely connecting different historical scientific eras. You may have to face this in the future, and you will appreciate both the intellectual power of your predecessors and the amazing beauty of the results they obtained. But this is ahead - far and high. What should we, who have not yet grown out of the “pants” of school physics, do now? You couldn't help but notice that the epigraphs constantly mention obstacles, restrictions imposed on the position or movement of mechanical systems - what are called connections. And this is nothing more than inclined planes, point hinges, rigid and light rods, inextensible and weightless threads, ideal blocks... Do you know the characters? So let’s now, without fear, “get in touch” with the heroes of our topic, because one of the meanings of the word “connection” is contact. Let it be close and fruitful.

It is curious that... ...the first, albeit hidden, formulation of the principle of virtual movements was contained in Aristotle's Physics, which helped him derive the law of leverage. The same principle in a different form was used by Stevin in deriving the laws of block equilibrium. ...Galileo, having corrected Aristotle, applied the principle of virtual displacements to the calculation of the conditions of equilibrium of a body on an inclined plane, and also with its help derived the laws of hydrostatic pressure established by Archimedes on a different basis. ...the “golden rule of mechanics”, well known to schoolchildren - a gain in strength is compensated by a loss in movement and vice versa - is nothing more than the principle of virtual movements in the simplest case. ...in 1717, Johann Bernoulli developed a new approach to finding the equilibrium conditions of a mechanical system, based on the property of bond reaction forces: with a small deviation of the system from the equilibrium position, the total work of the bond reaction forces is zero. ... Lagrange wrote the classic work “Analytical Mechanics”, which was based, among other things, on the principle of virtual displacements. ...the concept of “absolutely rigid body” is absolutely necessary when solving problems on kinematic constraints. It is a model of a system of material points, the distances between which remain unchanged. And this allows, for example, to use the so-called projection rule, when the projections of the velocities of body points are equated to the axis connecting them. ...in the presence of rigid connections, the equilibrium conditions are simplified: it is enough to consider only the equilibrium of forces in those directions in which the connections do not interfere with movement (for example, for a piston - along the cylinder). The balance of forces in other directions is ensured by itself, since even with a small deformation of the connection, reaction forces will appear that balance the applied force. ...the principle of virtual displacements is the “first swallow” in a whole “flock” of so-called variational principles that were then born, not only of mechanics, but also of optics, electrodynamics, thermodynamics, and in the 20th century - the theory of relativity and quantum theory.
