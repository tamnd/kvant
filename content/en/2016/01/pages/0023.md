PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 1–2016” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M2406” or “F2413”. In the “From” column, please write your first and last name legibly. In the letter, enclose an envelope with your address written on it and the required set of stamps (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking the decisions). Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics can also be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively. Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you study. Problem M2406–M2411, M2413 were offered at the XXXVII Tournament of Cities.

Problem M2406–M2413, F2413–F2419

M2406. K numbers were removed from integers from 1 to 100. Is it necessary to choose k different numbers with a sum of 100 among the remaining numbers if: a) k = 9; b) k = 8? A. Shapovalov

M2407. All coefficients of a certain polynomial are integers and their modulus does not exceed 2015. Prove that any positive root of this polynomial is greater than 1/2016. A. Khrabrov

M2408. In triangle ABC, the medians $AA_{0}, BB_{0}, CC_{0}$ intersect at point M. Prove that the circumcenters of triangles $MA_{0} B_{0}, MCB_{0}, MA_{0} C_{0}, MBC_{0}$ and the point M lie on the same circle. P. Kozhevnikov

M2409. A 9 ¥ 9 checkered square is made of matches, the side of each square is one match. Petya and Vasya take turns removing a match, Petya begins. The winner is the one whose move leaves no more 1 ¥ 1 squares. Who can act in such a way as to ensure victory, no matter how his opponent plays? A. Shapovalov

M2410. Given a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD. The extensions of its opposite sides intersect at points P and Q. Let K and N be the midpoints of the diagonals. Prove that the sum of angles PKQ and PNQ is equal to $180\infty$. M. Didin

M2411. Petya saw several different numbers on the board and decided to create an expression whose values ​​include all these numbers, but not others. When composing an expression, Petya can use any numbers, the special sign “$\pm$”, as well as the usual signs “+”, “–”, “¥” and brackets. The values ​​of the compiled expression

⟦column⟧

It calculates it by choosing for each sign “$\pm$” either “+” or “–” in all possible combinations. For example, if there were numbers 4 and 6 on the board, the expression $5 \pm 1$ would be suitable, and if there were numbers 1, 2 and 3 on the board, then the expression $( 2 \pm 0,5 ) \pm 0,5$ would be suitable. Is it possible to compose the necessary expression if the following were written on the board: a) the numbers 1, 2, 4; b) any 100 different real numbers? Ko Bong Gyun (South Korea)

M2412. It is easy to check that the volume of a regular octahedron described around a sphere of radius 1 is equal to 4 3. Find out whether the following statement is true: “the volume of any octahedron described around a sphere of radius 1 is not less than 4 3.” A. Merkulova

$M2413^{*}$. The line consists of N guys of different heights in pairs. It was divided into the smallest possible number of groups of guys standing in a row, in each of which the guys stand in ascending height from left to right (groups of one person are possible). Then in each group the children were rearranged in descending order of height from left to right. Prove that after N – 1 such operation the children will stand in descending order of height from left to right. N. Gladkov

F2413. On New Year's Eve in calm weather, an airplane takes off from the airfield of St. Petersburg $( 60\infty$ northern latitude and flies at a constant altitude h = 5 km with a constant speed v = 1000 km/h, always heading northeast (according to the stars). With what acceleration relative to the Earth (Ptolemaic system) does the plane move exactly after T = 4 hours of flight? The Earth can be considered a ball with radius R = 6400 km. V. Chkalov
