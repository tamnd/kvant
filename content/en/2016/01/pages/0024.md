F2414. A small block of mass m is located on a smooth horizontal surface at a distance $L_{0}$ from a vertical pillar on which a small weightless block with a fixed horizontal axis is fixed at a height h. A weightless, inextensible long thread is attached at one end to a block, thrown over a block and tensioned with a constant force F > mg. There is no friction in the block axis. At the initial moment, the block slides along the surface and has a speed $v_{0}$, directed away from the pillar. What will be the distance from the pillar to the block at the moment when the block stops for a moment? What will be the speed of the block at the moment when the block stops pressing on the surface? A. Starov

F2415. A long, L = 1 m, rectangular block of square cross-section a ¥ a = 10 ¥ 10 cm has a density of $\rho = 500$ kg $м^{3}$. The block was lowered into the water of the lake and held in such an unstable equilibrium position that one of the long edges of the block was dry and horizontal, while half the volume of the block was immersed in water. The block was released, and it took a stable position, rotating around the axis of symmetry at an angle of $\alpha = 45 \infty$. How much has the potential energy of the “water-block” system decreased? S. Dmitriev

F2416. Vasya blows a soap bubble through a long tube. Having inflated the bubble, he releases the tube from his mouth, while the air from the bubble escapes through the tube to the outside. The bubble finally disappears without bursting after time $\tau$. How long will it take for a soap bubble of twice the radius inflated in the same way to deflate? Assume that the air moves through the tube quite slowly, and the properties of the soap film are the same for both bubbles. V. Puzyrev
