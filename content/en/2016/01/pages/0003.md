The current award is already the fourth (the previous ones were in 1988, 1995 and 2002). So what is a neutrino and its oscillations? In the Standard Model, the modern theory of the microworld, along with quarks and bosons, there are 6 leptons - fundamental (structureless) particles that obey special, “weak” $взаимодействиям.^{1}$ The most famous lepton is the electron. Surrounding quark proton-neutron nuclei, electrons unite atoms into molecules and crystals, creating all the material structures of the world, from stones to humans. Other leptons fly up from space to the surface of the Earth; they are called mu-mesons, or muons. They are born in the upper atmosphere, where cosmic (and solar) protons break up the atomic nuclei of nitrogen and oxygen. They were also discovered in experiments at particle accelerators. The third meson is called the tau meson; it was found only in accelerator experiments. The three remaining leptons are neutrinos: electron, muon and tauneutrino. Unlike other leptons, all three neutrinos have a very small mass, at least a million times less than the mass of an electron. For a long time it was believed that neutrinos have no mass at all (like, for example, a photon). Takaaki Kajita, in a post-Nobel interview, noted that the main theoretical idea that needed to be tested was expressed 60 years ago by the Italian and Soviet physicist Bruno Pontecorvo (1913–1993). Kajita called him the "godfather" of neutrino oscillations. Since 1950, Potekorvo lived and worked in Russia, near Moscow (in Dubna), at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research. He was elected a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences and became a laureate of the State (Stalin) Prize for his work in the field of neutrino physics. And it is no coincidence that our famous bard Vladimir Vysotsky, who gave no less than five concerts in Dubna in the 70s, dedicated a verse in the “March of Physicists” to the neutrino and its researcher. Monuments were erected to both the scientist and the poet in Dubna. Of the 132 Nobel Prizes in physics, more than 30 have been awarded for the experimental discovery of new elementary particles or for theoretical work that led to these discoveries. And for the discoveries and research of neutrinos, neutrinos began to be called “flavors” of neutrinos (the English word flavor is not quite accurately translated into Russian). There may be other “flavors” of neutrinos – the Standard Model is not yet a sufficiently complete theory. In addition, it should be noted that all leptons are represented in the Standard Model by particles and antiparticles. So, for example, an electron (particle) can be born in a pair with an antineutrino, and a positron (antiparticle) - in a pair with a neutrino. For simplicity, in all cases we will continue to call neutrinos and these particles themselves and their antiparticles. Neutrinos arise in nuclear reactions, in transformations and in nuclear interactions. When at the dawn of the birth of the world the first atoms appeared in the Universe - hydrogen and helium, then free neutrinos also appeared. These “relict” neutrinos, like relict photons, wander throughout the Universe, gradually weakening to lower energies.
