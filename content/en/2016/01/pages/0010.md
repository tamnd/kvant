Such a helicopter was, for example, bought by the father of the Wright brothers for half a dollar. The brothers, who had not yet thought about flying, were fascinated by the toy helicopter. Lanchester was familiar with Penaud's work, and his book contains two images of the planophora. Lanchester himself had tested similar models and knew that with a certain setting they could perform uniform horizontal flight. In this case, the resistance force was completely compensated by the traction force, and the movement was phugoid. Moreover, phugoid movement can be realized in controlled flight, when the pilot or automatic machine can regulate the thrust force, exactly compensating the drag force at each moment of time. Zhukovsky was motivated by other considerations. He proposed dividing the entire flight path of the glider into separate sections and assuming that the movement in each section is phugoid, i.e. There is no resistance in each section. Pentasection of a regular triangle

(See the beginning on the 2nd cover page)

The reader will probably be surprised that in this example one part is a complex, disconnected figure (Fig.1). But, firstly, no restrictions were imposed on the properties of the parts. Secondly, to illustrate that nothing terrible is happening here, we can give an example from life: the Kaliningrad region is not connected to the rest of Russia by land, but this does not prevent anyone from considering it part of our country.

Fig. 1

Nevertheless, the question of whether a regular triangle is partitioned into five identical connected parts is quite logical. But he is still waiting for someone who can answer him. Another open question that arises here is: for what N does a regular triangle cut into N equal parts exist? For example, cutting a regular triangle into 3, 4 or 6 equal parts is easy (do it!). Now, as we see, cutting into 5 equal parts is known. But whether it is possible to somehow list all such N is still unknown. The equality of such complexly arranged parts should be understood as follows. Take a sheet of transparent film and trace the outline of any of the parts. The result will be what is shown in figure 2. Now, moving and turning the film over, we will try to cover any of the remaining parts with this contour. In our example, fortunately, this will work, i.e. the parts are truly equal. E.Epifanov
