The museum also displays a flat wind tunnel, built under the leadership of Zhukovsky at the Technical School. Zhukovsky's great interest was aroused by the flights of the German engineer, one of the pioneers of aviation, Otto Lilienthal, on gliders he personally designed and manufactured. In 1895, Zhukovsky took advantage of Lilienthal's kind invitation and attended his flights. The Lilienthal glider purchased during this trip is kept in the Zhukovsky Museum. It seems that this is the only copy that has survived to this day. Frederick William Lanchester (1868–1946) was a prominent English inventor, automobile manufacturer and aerodynamicist. In 1897, Lanchester presented his paper “The Soaring of Birds and the Possibility of Mechanical Flight” to the Physical Society of London. The work did not receive support and was rejected. Disappointed, Lanchester abandoned aerodynamics and concentrated on automotive engineering. After the American Wright brothers made their first glider flight in 1900, Lanchester returned to his research; in 1907, the first volume of his works on the aerodynamics of flight was published, and in 1908, the second volume, which deals with issues of flight stability and contains a description of phugoid motion. And again, his work was not accepted in England, but was read and developed by German scientists. Zhukovsky was also familiar with Lanchester's work.
