SCHOOL AT KVANT

What will change if...?

E.SOKOLOV

ONE day I was asked to come up with a problem for eighth graders on the topic “Photometry”. After some time the task was ready.

Problem about Pinocchio

Buratino noticed that if lamp $Л_{2}$ is turned on together with lamp $Л_{1}$ (the lamps are the same), then the illumination of point O (Fig. 1) will increase by 80%.

Fig. 1

“I wonder,” thought Pinocchio, “by how much would the illumination at point O increase after this if the black school board $D_{1} D_{2}$ turned into a mirror?” On the way home from school, Pinocchio met his old friends, the fox Alice and the cat Basilio.

- Hello, our smart and sensible Pinocchio! What new things did you come up with in class today? “I came up with a problem about light bulbs and turning a chalkboard into a mirror,” Pinocchio said proudly and quickly drew figure in the sand (see Fig. 1). - Smart and prudent Pinocchio, do you know the correct answer? “I don’t know yet, but I think I’ll find out soon.” - Of course, you will soon find out the correct answer, because your best friends are with you! And it will cost you very little - only the two soldi that you have in your pocket. “Look,” the fox continued to chatter without stopping. – Let the first light bulb create illumination $E_{1} = E_{0}$ at point O. Then, according to the conditions of the problem, the second light bulb gives illumination $E_{2} = 0,8 E_{0}$ at this point. When the board turns into a mirror, it reflects all the rays falling on it: both from the first lamp and from the second lamp. So, if the mirror reflects all the rays falling on it, then the light at point O will become exactly twice as much, i.e. $E_{кон} = 2 ( E_{1} + E_{2} ) = 3,6 E_{0}$. This means that the illumination has doubled, i.e. 100%, and your money became mine! Well, give me my two soldi here! - But no! But no! - suddenly shouted the cat Basilio, who by this time had managed to draw figure 2 on the sand. - Of course, all the rays falling on it are reflected from the mirror, but after that only the rays coming from the lamp $Л_{1}$ get to point O. Therefore, the illumination at point O will become $E_{кон} = ( E_{1} + E_{2} ) + E_{1} = 2,8 E_{0}$ instead of the initial value $E_{нач} = E_{1} + E_{2} = 1,8 E_{0}$. This means that the illumination will increase $E_{кон} E_{нач} = 2,8 E_{0} 1,8 E_{0} = 1,56$ times, i.e. by 56%. So two soldi become mine! Mine! Mine! - Oh, yes! Take it, smart guy,” and Alice the fox grabbed onto Basilio the cat. In search of truth, friends rolled across the earth... The reader undoubtedly knows that the debates of these respected experts are characterized by extreme temperament and duration. Therefore, our Pinocchio left the fox and the cat and hurried home to finally find out the correct answer. You, of course, know that due to their established reputation, Alice the fox and Basilio the cat are always simply forced to give incorrect answers. What exactly are the mistakes these characters have made now? Let us recall the basic principles that underlie geometric optics (Fig.3). Light is a stream of rays. They are emitted by luminous bodies (light sources) in infinite quantities and in all directions. A non-luminous surface becomes illuminated when light rays fall on it. We see objects when light rays coming from them enter our eyes. So says theory number 1. But there is also theory No. 2 in geometric optics, it says everything
