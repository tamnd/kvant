OLYMPICS

Regional stage of the XLII All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics

The regional stage of the XLII All-Russian Olympiad in Mathematics was held on February 5 and 6, 2016. Based on its results, the composition of participants in the final stage of the All-Russian Olympiad is formed, therefore, quite complex tasks were included in the options for the regional stage. Problems 8 for 10th grade and 11th grade turned out to be the most difficult.

Tasks

9th grade

First day

1. Given are quadratic trinomials $f_{1} ( x ), f_{2} ( x )$, … $, f_{100} ( x )$ with identical coefficients for $x^{2}$, identical coefficients for x, but different free terms; each of them has two roots. For each trinomial $f_{i} ( x )$ we chose one root and designated it $x_{i}$. What values ​​can the sum $f_{2} ( x_{1} ) + f_{3} ( x_{2} ) + …^{…} + f 100 ( x 99 ) + f 1 ( x 100 )$ take? N. Agakhanov

2. Given an isosceles triangle ABC, AB = BC. In the circle Ω circumscribed about the triangle ABC, the diameter $CC '$ is drawn. A line passing through point $C '$ parallel to BC intersects segments AB and AC at points M and P, respectively. Prove that M is the midpoint of the segment $C ' P$. B.Obukhov

3. Petya chose several consecutive natural numbers and wrote each one down in either a red or blue pencil (both colors are present). Can the sum of the least common multiple of all red numbers and the least common multiple of all blue numbers be a power of two? O. Dmitriev, R. Zhenodarov

4. King Hiero has 11 metal ingots, indistinguishable by sight; the king knows that their masses (in some order) are 1, 2, ..., 11 kg. He also has a bag that will tear if you put more than 11 kg in it. Archimedes found out the masses of all the ingots and wants to prove to Hiero that the first ingot has a mass of 1 kg. In one step, he can load several ingots into a bag and demonstrate to Hieron that the bag has not torn (you cannot tear a bag!). In what is the smallest number of loadings of the bag that Archimedes can achieve the required result? I. Bogdanov, K. Knop

Second day

5. There are 23 students in the class. During the year, each student in this class celebrated his birthday once, which was attended by some (at least one, but not all) of his classmates. Could it be that every two students in this class met at such celebrations alone?

how many times? (It is considered that any two guests met at each holiday, and also that the birthday person met all the guests.) I. Bogdanov

6. Let us call a non-empty (finite or infinite) set A consisting of natural numbers complete if for any natural numbers a and b (not necessarily distinct and not necessarily lying in A) such that a + b lies in A, the number ab also lies in A. Find all complete sets of natural numbers. N. Agakhanov

7. In the white table $2016 \times 2016$ some cells are colored black. Let us call a natural number k lucky if $k \le 2016$ and in each of the checkered squares with side k located in the table there are exactly k cells colored. (For example, if all the cells are black, then only the number 1 is lucky.) What is the largest number of numbers that can be lucky? E. Bakaev

8. Given a convex quadrilateral ABCD, in which $\angle$ DAB $= 90 ^\circ$. Let M be the midpoint of side BC. It turned out that $\angle$ ADC $= \angle$ BAM. Prove that $\angle$ ADB $= \angle$ CAM. E. Bakaev

10th grade

First day

1. See problem 1 for 9th grade.

2. Petya chose 10 consecutive natural numbers and wrote each one down in either a red or blue pencil (both colors are present). Can the sum of the least common multiple of all red numbers and the least common multiple of all blue numbers end in 2016? O. Dmitriev, R. Zhenodarov

3. On side AB of a convex quadrilateral ABCD, points K and L are taken (point K lies between A and L), and on side CD, points M and N are taken (point M is between C and N). It is known that AK = KN = DN and BL = BC = CM. Prove that if BCNK is a cyclic quadrilateral, then ADML is also a cyclic quadrilateral. T. Zimanov, P. Kozhevnikov

4. Given a checkered table $100 \times 100$, the cells of which are painted black and white. Moreover, all columns have an equal number of black cells, while all rows have different numbers of black cells. What is the maximum possible number of pairs of adjacent colored cells? I. Bogdanov
