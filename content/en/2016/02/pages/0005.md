To estimate more precisely, we move from the geometric problem of distances between points to a purely number-theoretic question: which natural numbers not exceeding 2n can be represented as the sum of two squared integers and how many are there? The answer to the first half of the question is quite well known and very beautiful: these are exactly those numbers in the decomposition of which into prime factors any prime number of the form 4k + 3 appears to an even power (this remarkable fact is called the Fermat–Euler theorem, for more details about it see, for example, the article by V. Senderov and A. Spivak “Sums of squares and Gaussian integers” in «Kvant» No. 3 for 1999 year). But to estimate the number of such numbers, a very advanced analytical technique is required, which precisely gives us the estimate indicated by Erdős: cn log n. In all the time that has passed since the appearance of this work, no one has learned to construct sets with smaller D ( P ), so Erdős’s hypothesis that this example is the most extreme seems very justified (especially with the appearance of the latest results).
