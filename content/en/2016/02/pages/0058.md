Before discussing this topic, I solve the following problem with mathematically literate schoolchildren. There are two point charges, say q and –3q. How are the points with zero potential located on the plane? Apart from the infinitely distant ones? This is an interesting task. It makes its way into the forehead; one can write complex, rather disgusting relationships. Why did I talk about mathematically educated schoolchildren? They know the answer without solving this problem. Usually some quick-thinking child immediately shouts: “Circle of Apollonius!” In order for a point to have zero potential in our case, it must be three times farther from a large charge than from a small one. Then the sum of these potentials gives zero. And they study such a geometric figure in geometry: how points are located on a plane that are three times farther from point A than from point B. This is a circle, the circle of Apollonius. This means that in space it will be a sphere. And that's what I need.
