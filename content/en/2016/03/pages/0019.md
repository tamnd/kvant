PROBLEM WORKER

a vertical cylindrical pipe with an internal diameter d = 10 cm was placed on the surface. The height of the pipe is H. The light source and light sensor are located on the axis of symmetry of the pipe. The inner walls of the pipe are smooth, mirror-like and reflect 100% of light. What illumination $E_{2}$ is now recorded by the sensor? What will the sensor show if the walls, at any angle of incidence of light, reflect 99% of the incident light? (Numerical calculations can be carried out using a computer.) M. Svetlov

Solutions to problems M2405–M2413, F2413–F2419

M2405. The straight line connecting the centers of the circumscribed and inscribed circles of a triangle intersects one of its sides at the base of the altitude, and the other at the point of tangency with the corresponding excircle. Find the angle between these sides of the triangle.

For the solution, see the article by A. Zaslavsky “On two classes of triangles, or Where problems come from” in this issue of the magazine.

M2406. K numbers were removed from integers from 1 to 100. Is it necessary to choose k different numbers with a sum of 100 among the remaining numbers if: a) k = 9; b) k = 8?

a) Answer. Optional. Let's remove the numbers 1, 2,..., 9. Then the sum of even the smallest nine of the remaining numbers (10 + 11 +... + 18 = 126) is greater than 100. b) Answer. Necessarily. Consider 12 pairs of numbers that add up to 25: (1, 24), (2, 23),..., (12, 13). After removing 8 numbers, at least four intact pairs will remain. They will give a total of 100. A. Shapovalov

M2407. All coefficients of a certain polynomial are integers and their modulus does not exceed 2015. Prove that any positive root of this polynomial is greater than 1/2016.

For the solution, see the article by S. Dorichenko, P. Kozhevnikov “From root substitution to a transcendental number” in this issue of the journal.

M2408. In triangle ABC, the medians $AA_{0}, BB_{0}, CC_{0}$ intersect at point M. Prove that the circumcenters of triangles $MA_{0} B_{0}, MCB_{0}, MA_{0} C_{0}, MBC_{0}$ and the point M lie on the same circle.

Let $O_{1}, O_{2}, O_{3}, O_{4}$ be the circumcenters of triangles $MA_{0} B_{0}, MCB_{0}, MA_{0} C_{0}, MBC_{0}$, respectively. Let us carry out the reasoning for the location of the points shown in figure (for other cases the location of the centers the reasoning is similar; to avoid analyzing the cases, we can consider all angles to be oriented).

Let us prove that $O_{4}$ lies on the circle $( MO_{1} O_{3} ).$ Line $O_{1} O_{3}$ is the perpendicular bisector to $MA_{0}$. Angle $MO_{1} O_{3}$ is equal to half the central angle $MO_{1} A_{0}$ of circle $( MA_{0} B_{0} ),$ i.e. angle $MB_{0} A_{0}$ inscribed in it. Line $O_{4} O_{3}$ is the perpendicular bisector to segment $MC_{0}$, therefore the angle between lines $MO_{4}$ and $MO_{3}$ is equal to half the central angle $MO_{4} C_{0}$ of circle $( MBC_{0} ),$ i.e. angle $MBC_{0}$ inscribed in it. And the angles $MB_{0} A_{0}$ and $MBC_{0}$ are equal due to the parallelism $B_{0} A_{0}$ and $BC_{0}$. Therefore – $MO_{1} O_{3} +$ – $MO_{4} O_{3} = 180 \infty$, i.e. $O_{4}$ lies on the circle $( MO_{1} O_{3} ).$ Similarly, $O_{2}$ lies on the circle $( MO_{1} O_{3} ).$ P. Kozhevnikov

M2409. A 9 ¥ 9 checkered square is made of matches, the side of each square is one match. Petya and Vasya take turns removing a match, Petya begins. The winner is the one whose move leaves no more 1 ¥ 1 squares. Who can act in such a way as to ensure victory, no matter how his opponent plays?

Answer. Vasya. Note that there will always be an odd number of matches left before Vasya's move. It is clear that the winner will be the one who gets the position when there are one or two adjacent squares (on the side). Therefore, it is enough for Vasya not to leave such a position behind, and then he will win, since a draw is impossible. Let's show how he can do this in different cases. 1) There are more than three squares left. He will take the last match, ruining no more than one square. 2) There are three squares left. He will take a match not from them, and if there are no such matches, then due to the odd number of matches it is clear that two squares are adjacent, and the third is not adjacent to them, then he will spoil one of the adjacent squares. 3) There are two squares left and they are not adjacent. Due to the odd number, there is a match that is not included in them, which Vasya will take. All positions have been considered. A. Shapovalov
