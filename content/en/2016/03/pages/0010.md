We will say that a mapping from a set of vertices to a set of r colors is an r-coloring if neighboring vertices are always colored in different colors. This problem arose in the production of geographical maps: if two countries border, they must be painted in different colors to distinguish them. But the Petersen graph is not planar: no matter how you draw it, some edges will intersect. The four-color conjecture, proposed in 1852, states that four colors are always sufficient to color the vertices of a planar graph. It was proven at the end of the 19th century, but 12 years later an error was found in its proof, and this proof was refuted. It was only more recently, in 1976, that Appel and Haken proved the four-color conjecture; it became the four-color theorem. Not everyone knows how this proof works.
