XXXVII Tournament of Cities

OBJECTIVES OF THE SPRING TOUR

BASIC OPTION

8–9 grades

$1 (3)^{1}$. Boys and girls stand in a circle (there are both), 20 children in total. It is known that every boy has a clockwise neighbor - a child in a blue T-shirt, and every girl has a counterclockwise neighbor - a child in a red T-shirt. Is it possible to unambiguously determine how many boys are in the circle? E. Bakaev

2 (4). In acute triangle ABC, angle C is equal to $60^\circ$. Let H be the point of intersection of the heights of this triangle. A circle with center H and radius HC intersects lines CA and CB for the second time at points M and N, respectively. Prove that AN and BM are parallel (or coincident). E. Bakaev

3 (5). Are there 2016 integers whose sum and product are equal to 2016? Folklore, suggested by M. Evdokimov

4 (5). In square $10 \times 10$, all cells of the upper left square $5 \times 5$ are colored black, and the remaining cells are colored white. What is the greatest number of polygons that this square can be cut into (along the boundaries of the cells) so that in each polygon there are three times fewer black cells than white ones? (Polygons do not have to be equal or even equal in size.) E. Bakaev

5 (5). Draw a triangle on a piece of paper with a blue pencil, and then draw in it with a red pencil the median, bisector and altitude (perhaps not all from different vertices) lying inside the triangle. We have obtained a division of the triangle into parts. Could these pieces include an equilateral triangle with red sides? M. Evdokimov

3 (6). Given a square with side 10. Cut it into 100 equal quadrangles, each of which is inscribed in a circle of diameter 3. I. Bogdanov

4 (8). Abstract artist took a wooden cube

$5 \times 5 \times 5$, divided each face into unit squares and painted each of them in one of three colors - black, white or red - so that there are no squares of the same color adjacent to the side. What is the smallest number of black squares that could result? (Squares that have a common side are considered adjacent both if they lie on the same face of the cube and if they lie on different faces of the cube.) M. Evdokimov

5 (8). Let p be a prime number greater than $10^{k}$. We took a number divisible by p and inserted a k-digit number A between some of its two adjacent digits. We got a number divisible by p. A k-digit number B was inserted into it - between two adjacent digits of the number A - and the result again turned out to be divisible by p. Prove that number B is obtained from number A by rearranging the digits. I. Bogdanov

6 (9). A robot vacuum cleaner, shaped like a circle, drove across a flat floor. Is it necessary for the center of the robot to remain on a certain straight line during the entire movement? I. Weinstein
