Problem M2421–M2428, F2428–F2434

M2421. The company recorded its expenses in rubles for 100 budget items, receiving a list of 100 numbers (each number has no more than two decimal places). Each accountant takes a copy of the list and finds an approximate amount of expenses, proceeding as follows. First, he selects any two numbers from the list, adds them, discards the decimal places from the sum (if any) and writes the result instead of the selected two numbers. With the resulting list of 99 numbers, he does the same, and so on, until there is only one integer left in the list. It turned out that in the end all accountants got different results. What is the largest number of accountants that could work in the company? M. Evdokimov

M2422. The rectangle p ¥ q, where p, q are coprime natural numbers, p < q, is divided into unit squares. A diagonal is drawn from the lower left corner of the rectangle to its upper right corner. It cuts off triangles from some squares. Find: a) total perimeter; b) the total area of ​​all these triangles. A.Tolpygo

M2423. Circles $\omega_{1}$ and $\omega_{2}$ of the same radius intersect at points $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$ (Fig. 1). Circle $\omega$ touches circle $\omega_{1}$ externally at point $T_{1}$ and circle $\omega_{2}$ internally at point $T_{2}$. Prove that the lines $X_{1} T_{1}$ and $X_{2} T_{2}$ intersect on the circle $\omega$. Ch. Latham (Luxembourg)

M2427. There are N numbers written on the board, they are all different, and one of them is equal to 0. You can take any polynomial, each coefficient of which is equal to one of the written numbers (some of the coefficients can be equal), and add all the roots of this polynomial to the board. After several such operations, all the integers from –2016 to 2016 (and possibly some other numbers) ended up on the board. Find the smallest possible value of N. G. Zhukov

M2428. On a spherical planet with equator length 1, they plan to lay N ring roads, each of which will go along a circle of length 1. Then several trains will be launched along each road. All trains will travel along the roads at the same positive constant speed, never stopping or colliding. (Consider the trains as arcs of zero thickness, from which the end points are ejected.) Under such conditions, what is the maximum possible total length of all trains: a) for N = 3; b) at N = 4? c*) Can the total length of trains be greater than 1000 for some N? A. Berdnikov

F2428. A group of three tourists must move from point A to point B along a road of length s = 45 km. Everyone starts at the same time. There are only two bicycles for the entire group of tourists, and if two people are riding a bicycle, then their speed is 3v, and if one person is riding a bicycle, then their speed is 4v. If a tourist walks, then his speed is v = 5 km/h. In what minimum time can all tourists reach their destination? V. Sergeev
