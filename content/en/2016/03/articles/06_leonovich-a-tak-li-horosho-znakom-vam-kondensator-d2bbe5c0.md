capacitor

Isn't there too much honor for one device in a section traditionally devoted to fundamental laws, phenomena and concepts? Firstly, for more than two and a half centuries he has been an indispensable participant in many important studies. The discovery of the Leyden jar simply revolutionized experiments with electricity, and subsequently capacitors were used in all the decisive experiments in which modern science was born, right up to the confirmation of the theory of relativity. Secondly, capacitors are still extremely important for practice. Discharging a capacitor in a flash lamp, exciting lasers and fluorescent lamps, and the action of defibrillators that bring a patient back to life by passing current pulses through the heart are just a few areas of their application. And what can we say about televisions and radios, pocket calculators and large computers, microphones and telephones and many other means of communication! Capacitors can be found wherever integrated circuits are used. True, they no longer resemble the first devices for accumulating charges - miniaturization of a liter “Leyden jar” would make it possible to fit it into an element barely visible on the head of a pin. Well, and thirdly, you will come across capacitors more than once in your school physics course. What field is formed in them, what are the possible options for their connection, how do they accumulate and release energy, how do they behave in direct or alternating current circuits, how charged particles fly through them, how they receive and transmit information with their help - these are the questions that you need to be able to answer. We hope this episode of Kaleidoscope will help you deal with them.
