Isaac Newton's cows

I. AKULICH

AS Y.I. PERELMAN WRITES IN THE BOOK “Fun Algebra” (Chapter II, article “Cows in the Meadow”), according to some information, this agricultural problem was proposed by Isaac Newton himself, or at least one of his contemporaries: The grass in the entire meadow grows equally thick and fast. It is known that 70 cows would eat it in 24 days, and 30 cows in 60 days. How many cows would eat all the grass in a meadow in 96 days? A straightforward and seemingly natural approach encounters insurmountable difficulties. In fact, 96 days are exactly 4 times longer than 24 days. Therefore, if 70 cows eat grass in 24 days, then in order for the meal to last four times longer, the number of cows must be reduced by the same amount, and it becomes equal to 70: 4 = 17.5. With such an answer, one immediately remembers Victor’s famous one and a half diggers $Перестукина^{1}$, but that’s not so bad. Another thing is worse: if we rely on other given conditions, namely, not on 70 cows, but on 30 that devoured grass in 60 days, then 96: 60 = 1.6, and therefore the required number of cows becomes equal to 30: 1.6 = 18.75. The result is a gloomy feeling of hopelessness, slightly mitigated by the hope that the condition simply contains an error, and therefore there is no point in taking on such a task. Meanwhile, there are no errors in the problem, and more attentive people will immediately pay attention to the first sentence - about growing grass. This is where $собака^{2}$ is buried - it turns out that while the cows are eating, the grass has time to grow, and the supply of feed in the meadow increases (although it cannot provide the animals with infinitely long nutrition). How to solve such a problem? A possible approach is proposed by Ya.I. Perelman himself in the same book. Let us quote his reasoning. Let us introduce an auxiliary unknown, which will denote the daily growth of grass in shares of its supply in the meadow. In one day y increases, in 24 days – 24y; if the total supply is taken to be 1, then within 24 days the cows eat 1 + 24y. per day
