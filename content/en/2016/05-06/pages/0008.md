Active materials – adaptive and protective

L.ASHKINAZI

Statement of the problem The reason for writing this article was a mysterious phrase in the book by Hermann Huber “Mountaineering Today”. Talking about the equipment, he writes: “The down “breathes”, and it must be allowed to breathe. Down jackets are very hygroscopic, absorb moisture (body evaporation) and release it when ventilated. Under the influence of the heat generated by the body, down swells and retains up to 50% of this heat - better than any other warm clothing made from artificial fiber." From the point of view of physics, the phrase is not accurate, but what is the idea - a material that itself reacts to the situation and changes so as to protect a person from impact! Note that this book is quite old (1980), but goose down still stands between a person and minus $40 \infty C$ at 10 m/s. The last thing is the wind speed, which is carefully called “unfavorable”, because in the mountains it can be many times higher. A person has to be protected from the influence of many environmental factors - from heat and cold, from high and low humidity, from electromagnetic radiation (including light), from radiation and mechanical influences. The methods of protection against all these troubles themselves are known and studied in detail. The first desire is to create a material that, according to the main parameter, i.e. In terms of protection, it is better than others. For example, we want to protect a person from electromagnetic radiation, in particular from light. Let's say, an astronaut - so that he doesn't overheat while creeping up to the Sun (I'd like to check 6000 K). Polished metals reflect most radiation, but not all. You can try to optimize the interference coverage so that the absorption integrated over the real spectrum is less, and the heat loss by its own radiation, occurring in a different range, is greater. White across the entire spectrum will not reduce heating - such a coating will emit less radiation. The coating should also be “black” in that part

spectrum in which to emit, and in the far infrared - in order to emit more. The exact boundary between “preferably white” and “preferably black” can be chosen if we know what temperature of the outer surface of the spacesuit we are going to maintain. For example, if we want to get closer to our Sun and we like our room temperature, the boundary will lie in the infrared part of the spectrum, in the region of a few microns. For other stars and other biologies the estimate may be different. Even more efficient would be to have a large flat black radiator positioned so that the solar radiation hits it at small angles, then the intercepted power would be small and the radiated power would be large. True, as we approach the Sun, the angles of incidence will still increase, and as a result, a white-black solution may become more effective: white towards the Sun, black away from it. Radiation protection, thermal protection and any protection in general can also be improved and improved - for example, simply by using it repeatedly, sequentially, making the wall thicker, thicker and thicker. You can improve the main parameter ad infinitum, but this is associated with a deterioration in other parameters - weight, cost, reliability, service life, maintainability, beauty. Accordingly, by solving an optimization problem, we can create a material that is better not in terms of the main parameter, but in terms of weight, cost, or something else, or a combination of parameters. And, therefore, in some cases it will be preferable. An interesting opportunity arises: a material can realize its wonderful properties, for which so much has been paid, not always and not everywhere, but only there and at the moment when a harmful factor stands on its threshold. To do this, the material must know from somewhere that it is time to get to work. This can happen in two ways. So, some sensitive element, sensor or sensor can sense a threat and transmit this signal to something that affects the material.
