A question similar to Exercise 3 for four points in space is a problem from “Problem Book «Kvant»: Problem (M2420). Given a tetrahedron. For each of its edges, take a plane perpendicular to this edge and passing through its middle. How many areas will six such planes divide space into? It is not so easy to imagine the geometric picture of what is happening, especially if you consider that there are tetrahedrons for which the center of the described sphere (through which all the indicated planes pass) lies outside the tetrahedron itself. Let us apply the considerations from Rubanov’s proof. Each of the regions into which the space is divided by six given planes specifies its own ordering of the distances x, y, z, t to the vertices of the tetrahedron (we will denote the vertices of the tetrahedron as X, Y, Z, T, respectively). For points lying in different areas, the orderings are different, since these points are located on different sides of one of the bisector perpendiculars. This already means that the number of areas does not exceed 24 -
