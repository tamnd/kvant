It is best to measure the output voltage using a two-channel electronic oscilloscope O. This is no longer a problem: for example, the MasterKit VM8020 electronic oscilloscope is quite affordable not only for a university educational laboratory, but also for the amateur radio budget. Modern scientific research usually combines several areas. And here the amateur ceases to be self-taught: while doing something like alternative aviation, he becomes acquainted not only with the design of a simple transformer with a variable gain, but also with the basics of processing weak electrical signals. An electronic oscilloscope is very easy to connect to a computer, and the procedure for detecting weak periodic signals is not a task to be afraid of. The second channel of the oscilloscope is needed if not only the lifting force is of interest, but also the energy loss spent on its creation. The first thing that must be done is to perform calibration, i.e. establish a connection between the average weight of an oscillating and resting body (not the flywheel, of course) and the voltage drop across the spring. There is more than enough new here, which means there is something to think about. Yes, the lifting force is small, but the frequency is also small, and as the frequency increases, the force still increases. This experimental dependence is somewhat reminiscent of a quadratic one (solid line in figure 4), characteristic, for example, of the same helicopter, if by frequency we mean the rotor rotation frequency. But why at three and a half hertz the lifting force is almost zero is a reason for further activity. I immediately want to not only simply repeat the measurements, but at the same time change some parameter of such a far from primitive aircraft. The lift of almost any heavier-than-air aircraft is characterized by the so-called angle of attack. This design of the flywheel also allows you to change it. It is enough to pay attention to the fact that a spar is installed above the fastening of the middle of the deformable wing (see Fig.2), which allows you to change the position of the center of gravity in relation to the spring. At five degrees of angle of attack the situation is almost repeated (Fig.5), but the meaning of such repetition is quite clear. Now it is in no way possible to attribute the dip in lift that occurs at three and a half hertz of frequency to measurement errors. Most likely, the reason for such a failure is resonance.
