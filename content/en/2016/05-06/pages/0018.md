M2424. There are several lines on the plane that have a common point O. Prove that each of these lines can be colored red or blue so that there is a point on the plane, different from O, the sum of the distances from which to all red lines is equal to the sum of the distances from it to all blue lines.

For each straight line, one of the two half-planes into which it divides the plane will be called positive, and the other negative. Let us define the oriented distance from a point to a straight line as the distance taken with a plus or minus sign, depending on whether the point is in a positive or negative half-plane. For each point X, we define f(X) as the sum of oriented distances from point X to all straight lines. If X and Y are two points symmetric with respect to O, then obviously $f ( X ) = - f ( Y )$. Let's go from point X to Y along a semicircle with center O. Due to the continuity of the function f, there is a point Z on the semicircle for which f ( Z ) = 0. The point Z will be the desired one if we color red all the lines with respect to which Z lies in the positive half-plane, and blue - all the remaining lines. In fact, f ( X ) is a linear function (this is also true for given straight lines of arbitrary location). Therefore, if there is at least one point at which f becomes 0, then there is a whole straight line all points of which have this property. Let us present another solution in which the required point is indicated explicitly (see figure). Let's draw a circle with a center

The moment of the mass system relative to straight line l is equal to zero, whence (taking into account that the masses are equal), the sum of the distances from points $A_{1}$, … $, A_{k}$ to straight line l is equal to the sum of distances from points $A_{k} +_{1}$, … $, A_{n}$ to the same straight line. Let N be the point of intersection of line l with the circle. Obviously, the distance from $A_{i}$ to straight line l is equal to the distance from N to straight line $OA_{i}$. Then it is sufficient to color straight lines $OA_{1}$, … $, OA_{k}$ red, and straight lines $OA_{k} +_{1}$, … $, OA_{n}$ – blue: the sum of the distances from point N to the red lines will be equal to the sum of the distances from N to the blue lines. In conclusion, we will give another mechanical interpretation of the plot from the second solution. Let's imagine that our circle is a weightless wheel with a fixed axis (O), which can rotate freely in a vertical plane. Segments $OA_{i}$ are weightless spokes of a wheel, at the ends of which there are unit masses. The masses are acted upon by the force of gravity directed vertically downwards. It is easy to see that the distance from point $A_{i}$ to the vertical line passing through O is proportional to the moment of force relative to the axis of rotation. Thus, the statement of the problem is related to the existence of a static equilibrium position. I. Vainshtein, P. Kozhevnikov
