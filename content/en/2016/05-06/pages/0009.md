Managed Materials

The expression "controllable material", so natural and simple, is used surprisingly rarely. The unfortunate expression “smart materials” is repeated in the media; the expression “active materials” is similar in meaning, but it is used in three different senses. Firstly, instead of the words “materials that determine the main parameters.” For example, in the formulation “the active materials of a transformer are the ferromagnetic material from which the magnetic core is made and the conductor from which the windings are made.” Secondly, regarding materials and substances that strongly influence specific processes. For example, in the expressions “biologically active substances”, “surfactants”, “sorption-active materials”. These two meanings are close and not clearly defined. Thus, under certain conditions, the parameters of a transformer strongly depend not only on iron and copper, but also on insulation - both in the magnetic core, and in the winding, and even between the terminals. The third sense of the expression "active materials" can be defined more strictly and is somewhat opposed to the first two, although a particular material may be active in all three senses. These are materials that change their properties under the influence of environmental factors. This is exactly what should be called “controlled materials”. When they talk about active materials, they do not mean at least some dependence on just some parameters, but rather strong enough, and such parameters that it can be effectively used. Here we will return to definitions for a moment, since the most frequently used expression “optically active” means two types of substances, media and materials. Firstly, those that do something with radiation, and secondly, those that change their properties under the influence of external factors. Although keep in mind that in many cases the interesting “controlled property” is precisely the property “to do something with radiation.” Thus, turpentine or a solution of sugar in water rotates the plane of polarization of light, and this rotation depends on the solution itself, or more precisely, on its concentration. And there are substances that also rotate the plane of polarization, and this rotation depends on external factors, for example, on the magnetic field (Faraday effect). What makes the situation a little more confusing is that not every “doing something” with radiation earns a material the “active” label. For example, any optical material, through its refractive index, affects the speed of propagation and absorption of radiation and therefore can affect its propagation (lens, prism). The refractive index depends on the electric field (Kerr effect), but substances in which it is significant and used are usually not called optically active. Although optical shutters and modulators are made on its basis, exotic devices have also been proposed - for example, lenses with electrically controlled focal length.
