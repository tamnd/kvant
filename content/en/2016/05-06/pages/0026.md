What will happen if we consider other linear equations in our interpretation (x, y are the distances to two points on the plane X and Y)? Consider, for example, the equation y = 2x. There are two points on the line XY for which y = 2x; let’s call these points U and V so that U lies on the segment XY, and V lies on its extension. Let A be an arbitrary point for which y = 2x, i.e. AY = 2AX (Fig.6).

Since AY AX = UY UX = 2, we obtain that AU is the bisector of the angle XAY. Similarly, based on the properties of the external bisector, AV is the external bisector of the angle XAY. Since $AU \perp AV$, A lies on a circle with diameter UV. By reasoning in the opposite direction, we can show that this circle is the set of solutions to the equation y = 2x. Similarly, the solution to the equation y = kx, where k > 0 and $k \ne 1$, is also a circle. It is called the circle of Apollonius.
