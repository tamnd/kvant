Statement of the problem The reason for writing this article was a mysterious phrase in the book by Hermann Huber “Mountaineering Today”. Talking about the equipment, he writes: “The down “breathes”, and it must be allowed to breathe. Down jackets are very hygroscopic, absorb moisture (body evaporation) and release it when ventilated. Under the influence of the heat generated by the body, down swells and retains up to 50% of this heat - better than any other warm clothing made from artificial fiber." From the point of view of physics, the phrase is not accurate, but what is the idea - a material that itself reacts to the situation and changes so as to protect a person from impact! Note that this book is quite old (1980), but goose down still stands between a person and minus $40 \infty C$ at 10 m/s. The last thing is the wind speed, which is carefully called “unfavorable”, because in the mountains it can be many times higher. A person has to be protected from the influence of many environmental factors - from heat and cold, from high and low humidity, from electromagnetic radiation (including light), from radiation and mechanical influences. The methods of protection against all these troubles themselves are known and studied in detail. The first desire is to create a material that, according to the main parameter, i.e. In terms of protection, it is better than others. For example, we want to protect a person from electromagnetic radiation, in particular from light. Let's say, an astronaut - so that he doesn't overheat while creeping up to the Sun (I'd like to check 6000 K). Polished metals reflect most radiation, but not all. You can try to optimize the interference coverage so that the absorption integrated over the real spectrum is less, and the heat loss by its own radiation, occurring in a different range, is greater. Managed Materials

The expression "controllable material", so natural and simple, is used surprisingly rarely. The unfortunate expression “smart materials” is repeated in the media; the expression “active materials” is similar in meaning, but it is used in three different senses. Firstly, instead of the words “materials that determine the main parameters.” For example, in the formulation “the active materials of a transformer are the ferromagnetic material from which the magnetic core is made and the conductor from which the windings are made.” Secondly, regarding materials and substances that strongly influence specific processes. For example, in the expressions “biologically active substances”, “surfactants”, “sorption-active materials”. These two meanings are close and not clearly defined. Thus, under certain conditions, the parameters of a transformer strongly depend not only on iron and copper, but also on insulation - both in the magnetic core, and in the winding, and even between the terminals. The third sense of the expression "active materials" can be defined more strictly and is somewhat opposed to the first two, although a particular material may be active in all three senses. These are materials that change their properties under the influence of environmental factors. This is exactly what should be called “controlled materials”. A question similar to Exercise 3 for four points in space is a problem from “Problem Book «Kvant»: Problem (M2420). Given a tetrahedron. For each of its edges, take a plane perpendicular to this edge and passing through its middle. How many areas will six such planes divide space into? It is not so easy to imagine the geometric picture of what is happening, especially if you consider that there are tetrahedrons for which the center of the described sphere (through which all the indicated planes pass) lies outside the tetrahedron itself. Let us apply the considerations from Rubanov’s proof. Each of the regions into which the space is divided by six given planes specifies its own ordering of the distances x, y, z, t to the vertices of the tetrahedron (we will denote the vertices of the tetrahedron as X, Y, Z, T, respectively). For points lying in different areas, the orderings are different, since these points are located on different sides of one of the bisector perpendiculars.
