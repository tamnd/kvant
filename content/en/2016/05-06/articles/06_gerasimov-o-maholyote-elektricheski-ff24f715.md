About the flywheel electrically

S. GERASIMOV

A LONG TIME AGO, AT THE VERY BEGINNING OF THE XX CENTURY, A DOCTOR

Nikolai Fedorovich Arend blew frozen birds in a wind tunnel. His conclusion was unexpected: devices with bird wings should not fly. After this, models, stuffed animals, and even live birds were blown through, and it turned out that they should not fly either. And they, contrary to all such statements, flew and fly. In 1960, the DOSAAF publishing house published the book “Models with Flapping Wings” by the famous flapping flight enthusiast G. Vasiliev, and in 1986, a mechanical model of a pterosaur worth 700 thousand dollars, built by the famous aircraft designer P. McCready, was launched using a winch, but crashed on the concrete of the runway. Meanwhile, the flywheels of another enthusiast, V. Toropov, were flying, and quite successfully. However, they could take off only in two cases: with a vertical position of the body and horizontal flapping of the wings, or with an almost horizontal position of the body with almost vertical flapping of the wings, i.e. after a long acceleration. Something similar is done by a tiltrotor, in which the propellers are turned to take off vertically. Built in 1998 at the Canadian University of Toronto, the C-GPTR ornithopter with a pilot on board after acceleration stayed in the air for several seconds and buried its nose in the concrete surface... Despite countless failures, flapping flight still attracts a large number of enthusiasts today. Its apparent ease encourages enthusiasts to build models and submit projects, believing that they know the secret that will finally allow a person to achieve flapping flight. Then disappointment sets in: no one has yet managed to step further than flying models and achieve vertical takeoff. Meanwhile, insects can not only take off from a place without acceleration, but also hang in the air for a long time. Unfortunately, almost everyone treats the idea of ​​a flywheel with irony. You can be sure that at present not a single reputable aviation institute, not a single aerospace organization, not a single aviation enterprise is interested in the idea of ​​flapping flight; Not a single scientific foundation is ready to support a grant for the creation of a working model of an ornithopter capable of not only horizontal flight, but also vertical take-off. This is probably correct and quite understandable. This means that this is a matter for amateurs, enthusiasts and amateurs. Note that there is nothing offensive in the last word. Although recently the concept of “amateur” has acquired different meanings, most often disparaging, such

But the conical pendulum makes circular movements due to horizontal force, but we are concerned with vertical take-off, i.e. vertical component of force. There is no doubt that it has a right to exist. When an asymmetrical wing of a bird or a deformable wing of a flywheel moves upward, the aerodynamic drag force is less than when the wing is lowered. This means that the so-called vibrational movement must take place, which, formally speaking, occurs under the influence of the resistance force of the medium. And if we take into account that the wing is not weightless, then such a movement can become quite effective, since, other things being equal, lowering the body is easier than raising it. In some ways, this problem is reminiscent of the desire to weigh a constantly jumping frog, but not to kill it! Ordinary scales will not help - the frog will jump away, and the cups of the scales will interfere with the flywheel. And then what such scales will show is unclear.
