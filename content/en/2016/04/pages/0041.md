Physics Days in Dubna

From March 27 to 29, 2016, the Days of Physics, organized by the Interschool Physics and Mathematics Faculty and the Educational and Scientific Center of the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research, were held at the Mir House of Culture in Dubna, Moscow Region. The first impression of what is happening in the halls of the House of Culture is chaotic, Brownian movement. There are a lot of visitors, mostly children, moving around, curious, pouring liquids, launching metal balls, observing optical effects. Among them you can notice (by the identical T-shirts) the organizers and volunteers. Flocks of spectators are attracted to them like magnets - they come up, listen, try to repeat, and move on to the next ones. And only after a more detailed acquaintance with the participants of the holiday, an analogy with a neural network arises - everyone is somehow connected with physical science, but everyone came to it in their own way. Here is one of the algorithms for involving in the Dubna experimentarium. Physics teacher of Moscow school 2086 V.V. Shchavelev, who visited the School for Teachers at CERN last fall, came with his students to Dubna to demonstrate experiments. One of his students, Dima Grigoriev, said: “My class and I decided to make a non-Newtonian fluid. This is starch that is mixed with water in a certain proportion. Starch molecules are held together, and if you act on the surface of our liquid, you get the feeling that you are in contact with a solid body. And if you put your hand in the liquid, it sinks. It turns out to be an interesting effect.” And on the stage of the small hall there were guests from the Czech Republic, one of the Ostrava gymnasiums. Eight ninth grade students and two Russian language teachers with them demonstrated experiments with a plasma ball, explained the processes,

talked about the gymnasium and at the same time practiced Russian speech. By all accounts, M.S.Nitishinsky, an Elective teacher, is recognized as the initiator of the Physics Days. This year he managed to involve the Dubna school, the club of future scientists, the JINR Museum, a mathematical circle, the international computer school, the Dubna University, and the universal library into the cycle of mathematical problems and physical experiments. The festival program includes a demonstration of experiments by senior schoolchildren and a “Trail of Researchers”, where children will have to do experiments on their own, a scientific show with liquid nitrogen and mathematical battles, a lecture on the history of accelerators given by JINR Vice-Director Corresponding Member of the RAS G.V. Trubnikov, and a robotics tournament. M.S. Nitishinsky’s closest associates are Dubna physics teachers I.A. Lomachenkov and A.A. Leonovich. In their opinion, every year more and more new experiments appear at the festival, and more and more volunteers are involved in the work. This platform not only helps schoolchildren acquire knowledge on the subject, here teachers can share sore points, get advice, and “spot” new techniques and methods. This year's Physics Days were held under the auspices of the 60th anniversary of JINR. A large number of visitors, Dubna and visiting participants, and various experiments indicate that the right vector, the right form of communication with schoolchildren has been found. In a sense, the Physics Days are a projection of JINR’s activities onto the world of children and teenagers, an introduction to the model of an international scientific center, when scientific research takes place in a community of scientists from different organizations.
