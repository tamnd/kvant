Young received this award three times.) Light beams and wave fronts. In geometric optics, one of the main objects of study is a point source, from which light rays diverge in different directions. In modern terms, this is something like a small LED, which we look at from a distance of several meters, without distinguishing its size. Another situation is also important when a source of limited size is located at a gigantic distance from us. Then the rays coming from it are practically parallel. Such a source is called infinitely distant, and the beam of rays emanating from it is parallel. It is precisely such beams that come to us from distant stars, and almost such a beam comes from the sun. In geometric optics, a wave front is a surface (in a flat case, a curve) that is perpendicular to all rays from a given light beam. For a beam of parallel rays, the wavefront is flat. In the case of a point source, the wave front is a sphere (circle), the center of which coincides with the location of the source.
