At the entrance to a drop of unit radius, we have a beam of parallel rays and, accordingly, a family of fronts parallel to each other, spaced apart by a wavelength. Entering the drop, the rays are refracted, and the fronts are curved. Moreover, inside the drop the distance between the fronts decreases by n times, where n = 4/3 is the refractive index of water. But the light wavelength decreases exactly the same amount. So in water the wave fronts are also equidistant and the distance between them is also equal to the light wavelength. Now let's look at the wave fronts as they exit the drop. Each such front consists of two arcs (see Fig.23). The large arc is perpendicular to those outgoing rays that have a droplet entry height from 0 to 0.86 of the droplet radius (see Fig.7). And the smaller arc is perpendicular to the outgoing rays with an entrance height of 0.86 to 1 (see Fig.8). The system of large arcs is similar to circular arcs with a common center located to the right of the drop. The system of smaller arcs is also similar to circular arcs, but with a common center located at the bottom of the drop.
