11th grade

1. See problem 1 for grade 10.

2. In space, three segments $A_{1} A_{2}, B_{1} B_{2}$ and $C_{1} C_{2}$ are given that do not lie in the same plane and intersect at one point P. Let $O_{ijk}$ denote the center of the sphere passing through the points $A_{i}, B_{j}, C_{k}$ and P. Prove that the lines $O_{111} O_{222}, O_{112} O_{221}, O_{121} O_{212}$ and $O_{211} O_{122}$ intersect at one point.

P. Kozhevnikov

3. Several pairs of non-overlapping cardboard isosceles rectangular triangles with leg 1 were placed on a checkered sheet of paper of size $100 \times 100$; Each triangle occupies exactly half of one of the cells. It turned out that each unit segment of the mesh (including the boundary ones) is covered by exactly one leg of the triangle. Find the greatest possible number of cells that do not contain a single triangle.

D. Khramtsov

4. All planes with equations $x \pm y \pm z = n$ (for all integers n) were drawn in coordinate space. They divided space into tetrahedrons and octahedrons. Let the point $( x_{0}, y_{0}, z_{0} )$ with rational coordinates not lie in any drawn plane. Prove that

there is a positive integer k for which the point $( kx_{0}, ky_{0}, kz_{0} )$ lies strictly inside some octahedron of the tiling.

A. Glazyrin

⟦column⟧

5. Let n be a natural number. 2n + 1 cards contain a non-zero integer; the sum of all numbers is also non-zero. It is required to replace the asterisks in the expression ∗ $x^{2} n +$ ∗ $x^{n} -^{1} +$ … + ∗ x + ∗ with these cards so that the resulting polynomial does not have integer roots. Is it necessary to do this?

I. Bogdanov

6. There are n > 1 cities in the country, some pairs of cities are connected by two-way non-stop flights. Moreover, between any two cities there is a single air route (possibly with transfers). The mayor of each city X has counted the number of such numberings of all cities with numbers from 1 to n such that on any air route starting in X, the city numbers are in ascending order. All mayors, except one, noticed that their calculation results are divisible by 2016. Prove that the remaining mayor’s result is also divisible by 2016.

F.Petrov

7. See problem 8 for 9th grade.

8. In triangle ABC, medians $AM_{A}, BM_{B}$ and $CM_{C}$ intersect at point M. Let us construct a circle $Ω_{A}$ passing through the midpoint of segment AM and tangent to segment BC at point $M_{A}$. Circles $Ω_{B}$ and $Ω_{C}$ are constructed similarly. Prove that circles $Ω_{A}, Ω_{B}$ and $Ω_{C}$ have a common point.

A. Yakubov
