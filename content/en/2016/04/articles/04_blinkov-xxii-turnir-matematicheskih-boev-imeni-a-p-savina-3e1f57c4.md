XXII Mathematical Fights Tournament named after A.P. Savin

The XXII A.P. Savin Tournament was held from June 26 to July 2, 2016 at the Berendeyev Polyany recreation center (Kostroma region) for schoolchildren who completed grades 6–9. We wrote more about these tournaments, for example, in «Kvant» No. 1 for 2015. For questions about participation, please contact the chairman of the organizing committee, A.D. Blinkov, by email blinkov@mccme.ru.

Selected tasks

1 (7). A stack of nineteen sheets of paper was folded in half and stapled together with two small paper clips to form a notebook. On the “cover” Sasha drew vertical and horizontal lines (from edge to edge, without touching the paper clips). When he cut the notebook along these lines, it broke into 2016 separate pieces. What is the smallest number of lines that Sasha could draw? A. Khachaturian

2 (7–8). Starting with a certain natural number, Petya, with each step, increases or decreases it by some of its digits (perhaps by the number 0), strictly alternating addition and subtraction. The first operation is addition. From what is the smallest natural number that Petya can get 2016 like this? A. Shapovalov

3 (8). A circle is inscribed in square ABCD and touches sides AB and AD at points K and M, respectively. Line CK intersects the circle a second time at point N. Find angle BMN. M. Volchkevich

4 (8). In an acute triangle ABC, the bisector $BB '$ is drawn. Point N is the intersection of the circumcircle of triangle ABB $'$ and the outer bisector of angle A, point M is the intersection of the circumcircle of triangle $BB ' C$ and the outer bisector of angle C. Line NM intersects segment $BB '$ at point K. Prove that the circumcircles of triangles ANK and CMK intersect at $BB '$. L.Popov

The classes in which it was proposed are indicated in parentheses after the problem number.

⟦column⟧

5 (8). In an equilateral convex hexagon, the six diagonals are equal to each other. Are all its angles necessarily equal? M. Evdokimov

6 (7–8). Is it possible to cut a cube into cubes of 2016 different sizes so that there are equal numbers of cubes of each size? A. Shapovalov, I. Raskina

7 (7). What is the smallest number of squares of a chessboard that must be stained with tar so that five rooks that do not attack each other cannot be placed on clean squares? E. Bakaev

8 (7–8). The board squares of size $10 \times 10$ are filled with numbers. We call a four-cell corner ordered if in this corner each of two numbers that have two neighbors is greater than one of its neighbors and less than the other. What is the largest number of ordered corners (possibly overlapping) that can appear on the board? E. Bakaev

9 (6–7). There is a pile of 55 nuts on the table. Petya and Vasya take turns walking, Petya begins. During a turn, you need to divide one of the existing piles into two smaller ones and add two more piles of nuts equal to one of the received ones (the supply of additional nuts is unlimited). Those who cannot make a move pay a fine. Will any of the boys be able to play in such a way that they are sure not to pay a fine? A. Shapovalov

10 (8). An equilateral triangle with side N is divided by lines parallel to the sides into equilateral triangles with side 1. At the vertex of the original triangle there is a counter. Petya and Vasya are playing a game (Petya starts). With each move, you are allowed to move a piece to an adjacent (located at a distance of 1) node of the triangular grid, in which the piece was not previously located. The one who cannot make a move loses. Which boy can guarantee victory? R. Krutovsky
