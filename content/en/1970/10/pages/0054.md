⟦folio 52⟧

The work required to transfer a single positive charge from one plate of our capacitor to the other is $A = E_1 d_1 + E_2 d_2$. But the capacitor is short-circuited, and the potential difference between the plates is zero. Therefore, $E_1 d_1 + E_2 d_2 = 0$.

Or $\frac{2Q + q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} d_1 + \frac{2Q - q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} (d - d_1) = 0$. Simplifying this equation, we get

$$Q (d - d_1) + q d_1 = 0. \tag{1}$$

When the middle dam moves over a distance $x$, a charge $\Delta Q$ will pass through the wire connecting the plates, and the charges of the plates will be equal to $Q + \Delta Q$ and $-Q - \Delta Q$. Since in this case the potential difference between the plates is zero, then, reasoning in the same way as in the first case, we obtain the equation

$$\frac{q + 2(Q - \Delta Q)}{2\varepsilon_0 S} (d_1 + x) + \frac{2(Q - \Delta Q) - q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} (d - d_1 - x) = 0$$

or

$$(Q - \Delta Q) (d - d_1 - x) + q (d_1 + x) = 0. \tag{2}$$

Subtracting equation (1) from equation (2), we find that $\Delta Q = q \frac{x}{d}$.
