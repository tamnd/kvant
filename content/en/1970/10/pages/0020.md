For our specific functions

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = |\sin \frac{\pi}{4} - \left( \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi^3}{384} \right)| \approx 0.0025.$$

(It can be proven that the maximum is reached at the point $x = \frac{\pi}{4}$.)

In the same way, in the general case, we take as the distance between the functions $f_1, f_2$ defined on the segment $[a, b]$ the quantities

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = \max_{a \leqslant x \leqslant b} |f_1(x) - f_2(x)|.$$

(7)

Check that the axioms $1^\circ - 3^\circ$ are satisfied here! Moreover, the $r$-neighborhood of this function (Fig. 8) consists of all such functions whose graphs lie in a strip of width $2r$ around the graph of the function $f$.

The following distances are often used:

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = s(f_1, f_2)$$

where $s$ is the value of the area enclosed between graphs $f_1$ and $f_2$ (8) (Fig. 9) and especially this:

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = \sqrt{S(f_1, f_2)},$$

(9)

where $S(f_1, f_2)$ is the area enclosed between the graph of the function $y = (f_1(x) - f_2(x))^2$ and the axis $Ox$.

Distance (7) is small when the values ​​of the function $f_1$ and $f_2$ are close for all values ​​of the argument, and distances (8) and (9) show how close the functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ are “on average” (on small segments they can differ significantly from each other). Let, for example, $f_1(t)$ and $f_2(t)$ be the potentials of two specific points of the electric circuit at time $t$; then the power released in the section of the circuit between these points during the period of time $a \leqslant t \leqslant b$ is proportional to $S(f_1, f_2)$ (resistance is constant; power is proportional to $(f_1(t) - f_2(t))^2$); thus, the greater the distance (9), the greater the power. And if it is important for us that the voltage $f_1(t) - f_2(t)$ does not always exceed a certain value $V$, then we must estimate the distance using formula (7): it is necessary that the value max $|f_1(t) - f_2(t)|$ does not exceed the length $V$.
