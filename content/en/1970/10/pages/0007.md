If we take the point of view of our ordinary mechanical concepts, which completely comprehensively describe the behavior of ordinary substances under ordinary conditions, then it turns out that supercurrent gels, as experience shows, cannot transfer heat as intensely as a change in convection currents requires. We are coping with the difficulty of finding a mechanism that could cause the necessary rapid flow of the gel by convection. In the general mechanism of body transfer by convection, we owe the movement of the medium to the fact that a more heated liquid or gas becomes somewhat less dense, so it tends upward, as if floating up in a denser medium, while colder, more dense ones tend downwards, “taunt”. Mixing occurs, and it is very important that the reason causing the movement is the force of gravity. But calculations show that this force in gels II is not enough to cause such a large thermal conductivity, which was observed in the experiment. This made the phenomenon again incomprehensible. It was necessary to look for some other, new mechanisms to explain it. A number of wholesalers finally managed to come across a completely new mechanism for the movement of liquid gel II.

It turned out that, under the influence of the temperature, very strong currents arose in liquid gel II, somewhat filling the convection currents. Under the influence of the disposition, the liquid begins to move, but this movement is of a special kind, specific to liquid gel II, unknown in any other liquid and under no other conditions.
