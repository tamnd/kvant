AXIOMS ​​AND FIRST EXAMPLES

Let us dado some set $X$. We say that a consideration is defined on it if each two elements $a$ and $b$ can be assigned some non-negative number $\rho(a, b)$ - “the distance from $a$ to $b$” - and the following three conditions are satisfied (see Fig. 1, $a, \delta, \theta$):

1°. $\rho(a, b) = 0$ if and only if $a = b$.
2°. $\rho(a, b) = \rho(b, a)$ for any two elements $a$ and $b$ of $X$.
3°. $\rho(a, c) \leqslant \rho(a, b) + \rho(b, c)$ for any three elements $a, b$ and $c$ of $X$.

A set defined on it by a distance (“metric”) is called a metric space $^*$. The elements $x$ themselves are generally called washers of the metric space.

Let us read again the formulations of the axioms that must be satisfied by the function $\rho$ of pairs of points given the distance.

1°. The distance from $a$ to $b$ is 0 if and only if $a$ is the same as $b$.
2°. The distance from $a$ to $b$ is equal to the distance from $b$ to $a$ ("symmetry axiom").

In these pictures, for every two points, the “distance” between them is indicated. Does the axiom $3^\circ$ of metric space hold for this “distance”?

*) The root “meter” is found in many Russian words and comes from the word meter - measure, size.
