Crystals can also be grown by evaporation. After all, if a saturated solution evaporates, its volume decreases, but the amount of dissolved substance remains the same. In other words, an excess of substance is again created, which precipitates.

Let us now consider how the release of excess substance occurs.

Take a saturated solution and heat it. Cover the vessel with the resulting undersaturated solution with glass and allow the solution to cool quietly to a temperature lower than the saturation temperature. In this case, the precipitate may not fall out, and we will get a supersaturated solution. The fact is that for education

To form a crystal, a “seed” is necessary. It can be a small crystal of the same substance or a simple speck of dust. Sometimes it is enough to quickly remove a vessel with a supersaturated solution or remove the glass covering it, as instant crystallization begins. In this case, many small crystals are usually formed. In order to grow a large crystal, it is necessary to limit the number of “seeds”. It is best to introduce an artificial “seed”, the role of which will be played by one of the crystals obtained earlier.

The “seed” is prepared as follows. Take two glass jars and wash them thoroughly. Pour warm water into one of them and saturate it with alum. When placing the solution, watch for dissolution. When the substance stops dissolving, carefully pour the solution into the second jar so that no undissolved substance gets into it. Then cover the jar with glass. When the solution has cooled, chemistry the glass. After a while, you will see many crystals forming in the jar. Let them grow and select the largest ones for “seeding”.

Now you can start growing the crystal. First of all, you need to prepare the dishes. To destroy unwanted items -

So, you have a warm saturated alum solution. Heat it a little more, cover the jar with glass and leave to cool. When the temperature of the solution approaches the saturation temperature, put the previously prepared “dressing” into the jar. Since the solution is still undersaturated, the “priming” crystal begins to dissolve. But as soon as the solution cools to the saturation temperature, the dissolution of the crystal will stop, and soon its growth will begin. (If the crystal dissolves entirely, you can add a new “filling” to the solution.))

When the solution stops cooling, crystal growth can be continued. To do this, lift the glass so that the water evaporates, but dust particles do not fall into the solution. Crystal growth continues for two to three days.

When growing a crystal, be careful not to touch or move the jar. When the crystal is ready, just remove it from the solution and blot it thoroughly with a paper towel, otherwise it will quickly fade.

The crystals come out differently in shape depending on whether you drop the dressing at the bottom of the sauce or hang it on a string (Fig. 2). In this way you can, for example, grow “beads”. To do this, you need to “thread” the thread, that is, pass it several times along the crystal, and then lower the thread into the solution.
