⟦folio 12⟧

Fig. 2. Comparison of the length of the reference meter with the length of the light wave is carried out using a device called interference comparators. One of the diagrams of such a device is shown in figure. A parallel beam of light rays from a source S falls at an angle of 45° onto a silvered translucent glass plate P, which divides the light flux into two parts. Reflected ray 1 goes to the mirror 3₁, is reflected from it and, having cleared the plate P₁, falls into the telescope T. Beam 2, having cleared the plate P, is reflected from the mirror 3₂, then reflected from the translucent side of the plate P and also falls into the tube T. (For clarity, rays 1 and 2 are depicted in different colors.) Ray 2 passes through the plate P three times, and the ray 1 - only once. To compensate for the difference in the path of the rays that arises because of this, a different plate P₁ is placed on the path of the first ray, made of the same materials as the plate P. The light beams 1′ and 2′ are correct and give an interference pattern in the field of view of the tube T. Depending on the difference in the path of the rays, the field of view of the pipe is light or dark. When the mirror 3₁ is moved by a distance equal to a quarter of the wavelength, the difference in the path of both rays increases by half the wavelength and the illumination of the visual field changes. Instead of a pipe, you can install a photomultiplier, which will produce electrical impulses at each level of illumination. This way you can count the number of waves that have decreased at a given length.
