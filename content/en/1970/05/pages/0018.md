Let us now see how small extensions are determined.

It is not difficult to divide a meter into a thousand parts. It is a little more difficult to divide a millimeter into a thousand parts. For this you only need a good microcopper. This is how we get a micron - a millionth of a meter. But further division is difficult, since it is impossible to see objects shorter than the wavelength of visible light (about 5·10⁻⁷ m).

With the help of an electron microcoppe, we can see and change objects with dimensions up to 10⁻⁸ m. The shorter the wavelength of electron radiation, the smaller objects we can “see”. For example, gamma rays allow you to “examine” objects whose dimensions do not exceed 10⁻¹¹ m.

To determine nuclear dimensions, completely different methods are used: the so-called effective transverse section is measured.

reading of nuclei. It can be determined by passing a beam of high-energy particles through a thin plate of matter and measuring the number of particles that do not pass through. The ratio of the number of particles that did not pass through to all those emitted is proportional to the ratio of the area occupied by atomic nuclei to the area of ​​the plate. Similar experiments have shown that the radius of the nuclei ranges from 1·10⁻¹⁵ to 6·10⁻¹⁵ m*).

Each two adjacent divisions indicate sizes, perfectly exhausted from each other by 10 times. 10° m - one meter, 10¹ m - ten meters, 10² m - one hundred meters, etc. Similarly, 10⁻¹ m is a tenth of a meter (or ten centimeters), 10⁻² m is a hundredth of a meter (one centimeter), etc. Using logarithmic scales, you can show on one graph all sizes found in farming and technology: from the smallest to the largest.
