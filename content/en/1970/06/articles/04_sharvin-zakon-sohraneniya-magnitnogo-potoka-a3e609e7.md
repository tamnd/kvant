If experience shows that during processes of a certain type some physical quantity does not change, it is possible to formulate a “conservation law” of this quantity during processes of this type. The most famous example of such a law is the law of conservation of energy, which is satisfied in any processes occurring in systems that are well isolated from the influence of other bodies. Using conservation laws, one can often imagine the final result of an experiment only based on the initial conditions, without knowing what happens at the intermediate stages. For example, if the kinetic energy of a body is transferred to a body, its quantity does not depend on the way in which this happens.

This article will discuss the law of conservation of magnetic flux. This law, however, is fulfilled only under special conditions and does not pretend to have such a universal role as the law of conservation of energy, but in its field it is also a very useful law, I would like to add, beautiful.

Let us first define what is called magnetic flux. Let's imagine that in space there is a constant underexposed magnetic field of any most complex shape. It can be visually depicted using magnetic lines of force, the direction of which will indicate the direction of the field (for this you need to put arrows on the lines), and the density, that is, the number of lines passing through an area of ​​1 cm² perpendicular to them, will indicate the strength of the magnetic field. With the help of continuous non-intersecting lines of force, which are either closed or go into the distance, to infinity, it is possible to depict any magnetic field, no matter how complex its shape. Moreover, in the described model there will be no place where it would be necessary to add or remove a line segment in order to correctly depict the field voltage. That’s how the magnetic field works, as they say. In any case, all attempts to detect deviations from this rule in experiment have so far failed. As we now see, it is only thanks to this property of the magnetic field that the concept of magnetic flux can be introduced.

If there is some kind of closed curve in space - a “circuit” (let’s imagine it made of thin wire), then the number of magnetic lines of force covered by this circuit will be called the magnetic flux through this circuit. To correctly calculate this number, you must proceed as follows. First, tighten this outline with an imaginary film (any shape, just not holey) and mark it on

The flow is denoted by the letter F (perhaps because it is similar to a line of force producing a circuit). If the magnetic flux covered by the circuit changes over time by values ​​$\Delta \Phi$ in time $\Delta t$, and the circuit is made of wire with electrical resistance $R$, then according to the law of electromagnetic induction, the current begins to cycle along the circuit

$$I = -10^{-8} \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \cdot \frac{1}{R} \text{ amper} \quad (1)$$

($\Phi$ is expressed in $ec \cdot cm^2$, $t$ is in seconds, and $R$ is in ohms). This current in the circuit flows under the influence of the electromotive force of induction

$$\varepsilon = -10^{-8} \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \text{ волт.} \quad (2)$$

The minus sign in these formulas is connected with this: we consider positive the current in the circuit, which itself creates a magnetic field in it with a positive flux (such a current flows around the circuit clockwise, if viewed from the inside out). The negative sign, therefore, shows that if the flux decreases with time $\left( \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \text{ отрицательно} \right)$, then the excited current will be positive and will itself create a positive flux. On the contrary, if the flow returns, the current in the circuit creates a flow in the opposite direction (Lenz's rule). Since the flux Ф consists of the flux of the external field and the flux created by the circuit itself, the induction of current in the circuit leads to a slowdown in the change in the total flux through the circuit.

Let the current be zero at the initial moment, and the flow is created by some external field source, for example a magnet. Now let's quickly remove the magnet. Then, at the same time, a current will arise in the circuit, which itself creates a magnetic flux of the same magnitude, that is, it will not allow the magnetic flux higher out of the circuit. In order for a current to exist in a circuit with electrical resistance $R$, it is necessary, of course, for the flow to change. Therefore, in subsequent moments, Ф will still gradually decrease, but the slower, the smaller $R$. Likewise, if we want to increase the flow of the internal circuit, this can only be done gradually.

Let us now assume that our circuit is made of a superconductor, the resistance of which is known to be zero. The reader, of course, knows that superconductivity occurs in many metals and alloys at low temperatures, on the order of several degrees of the absolute scale. It should also be recalled that superconductivity is destroyed under the influence of a magnetic field with a strength exceeding a certain limit - the critical field $H_c$, which depends on temperature approximately as shown in figure 2. For a fistula, say, at the lowest temperatures $H_c=800$ gauss. At fields greater than $H_c$, the metal again acquires resistance.

If in a circuit with low resistance the magnetic flux changes slowly, then it is natural that in a superconducting circuit it will generally be constant. This can be seen directly from formula (1), but in order not to get a zero in the denominator, it must be written in the form $IR=-10^{-8}\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$. At $R=0$ $\Delta\Phi=0$. This is the law of conservation of magnetic flux in a circuit with zero resistance.

It was the experimental verification of this law that showed that the resistance of a superconductor is practically zero. The most accurate methods cannot detect a decrease in the magnetic field in a closed superconducting coil, even if the experiment lasts several years.

Let us now consider several examples of the application of the law of conservation of magnetic flux. If you make a pipe with an opening area $S$ from a superconductor, place it at a high temperature in a longitudinal magnetic field $H$, then cool it below the transition temperature to superconducting states and turn off the magnetic field, then a “frozen” flux $\Phi=HS$ remains in the pipe. Let us now compress the pipe or insert a superconducting rod into it so that the free flow is reduced to $S_1=\frac{1}{10}S$. Then we will get a stronger field $H_1=10H$ in the pipe, since it follows from the law of conservation of flux that $H_1S_1=HS$. It is only necessary that the new field $H_1$ not be greater than the critical field $H_c$ of the superconductor at the temperature of the experiment, otherwise superconductivity is destroyed and the flux in the pipe will decrease until the field reaches the value $H_c$.

By doing this experiment quickly enough, you can obtain a large field in a pipe made of any normal metal. If you surround a pipe in which there is a magnetic field,

The examples discussed above refer to various methods of creating a magnetic field. In conclusion, let us mention a device designed to perform the direct opposite task - the most complete removal of the field from any volume. Using the phenomenon of superconductivity, this problem can be solved in a simple and perfect way. Let's take the flattened superconducting shell and straighten it. Then in the formed cavity the magnetic field will become equal to zero (practical, of course, some field will remain, but, in any case, it acts in this way, you can get as close to $H=0$ as you like). The resulting zero field will remain indefinitely in a closed cavity, despite changes in the field outside.

In the case of superconductors, the law of conservation of magnetic flux is satisfied with great accuracy, but it must be borne in mind that it can be approximately applied to circuits made of any other materials - solid, liquid and even gaseous (plasma), if the process proceeds fast enough. True, in order to accurately define what “quickly enough” means, we would have to go beyond the scope of this article.
