Fig. 3. Connection diagram of a superconducting coil with a superconducting switch. Part of the circuit is at low temperature. The power circuit heating $B$ to figure is not shown.

After the superconducting switch is closed, wires $B$ are no longer needed and are even harmful, since heat penetrates into the dewar through them.

There is another ingenious way of “pumping” magnetic flux into superconducting coils, which allows you to bypass it without inserting wires into the dewar at all. The circuit of a device based on this method is shown at figure 4. The coil, which for simplicity we have replaced at figure with a flat circuit $A$, is connected in parallel by two superconducting wires $B$ and $B$. Magnet $M$ can move around the contour. The path of its lower end is shown in figure 4 point. The magnet must be so strong that its field can destroy the superconductivity of wires $B$ and $B$.

The successive positions of the magnet when pumping flux into the circuit are shown at Fig. 5,$a$—5,$\delta$. The magnetic flux coming out of the end of the magnet is indicated by dots - traces of the lines of force. In position $a$ the magnet destroyed superconductivity
