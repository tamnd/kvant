12. 1) Analysis. Since $KB = KH$, then $\angle KHB = \angle B$. Let's do $KC$. Since $KH = HC$, then $\angle HKC = \angle HCK$. But their sum is equal to the external angle $\angle KHB$: $\angle HKC + \angle HCK = \angle KHB$ (see Fig. 8). This means that each of them is equal to half of it:

$$\angle CKH = \angle KCH = \frac{1}{2} \angle KHB = \frac{1}{2} \angle B.$$

2) Construction. Let $\triangle ABC$ be given. Divide $\angle B$ in half and construct an angle $\angle BCM = \frac{1}{2} \angle B$ so that the ray $CM$ lies on the same side of the line $BC$ as the point $A$. Let ray $CM$ intersect side $AB$ (not its extension) at point $K$. Let's construct the angle $\angle CKN = \frac{1}{2} \angle B$ so that the ray $KN$ lies on the same side of the straight line $CK$ as the point $B$. Let ray $KN$ intersect side $BC$ (not its extension) at point $H$. The constructed points $K, H$ are exceptional.

3) Proof. By construction, $\angle HKC = \angle HCK$, therefore $KB = KH$.

Next,

$$\angle KHB = \angle HKC + \angle HCK = \frac{1}{2} \angle B + \frac{1}{2} \angle B = \angle B,$$

pose $KB = KH$.
