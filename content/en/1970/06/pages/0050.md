1) the point $P_1$, symmetrical to $P$ with respect to $AB$, lies on the circumcircle;
2) the point $P_2$, symmetrical to $P$ with respect to $BC$, lies on the circumcircle;

Let us first find all points $P$ that satisfy the third option 1). All such points $P$ form a circle symmetrically circumscribed by $AB$ (drawn on figure 2 in white).

Thus, requirement 1) imposed on the point $P$ can be reformulated as follows:

1') point $P$ lies on the circle $O_1$, symmetrical to the circumcircle with respect to $AB$.

In a completely similar way, we are convinced that requirements 2), 3) can be reformulated in the requirement that the point $P$ lie on the circle $O_2, O_3$, symmetrical to the circumcircle with respect to $BC$ and $CA$.

We need to find out whether for every triangle there is a point $P$ lying on all three circles $O_1, O_2, O_3$ at once, in other words, for every triangle these three circles $O_1, O_2, O_3$ pass through one point. Let us prove that this is true for any triangle $ABC$.

It is quite difficult to come up with such a miracle that would be suitable for all triangles $ABC$ at once (the fact is that we do not know in advance where the point $P$ of the intersection of circles $O_2$ and $O_3$ lies - inside the triangle or outside it, on which side of each of the straight lines $AB, BC$ and $CA$, etc., and if the miracle depends on location of the point $P$, then it is necessary to analyze several special cases). But to save space, we will still present just such a solution, which was suitable for all cases at once, albeit somewhat artificial.
