If the magnetic flux covered by the circuit changes over time by values ​​$\Delta \Phi$ in time $\Delta t$, and the circuit is made of wire with electrical resistance $R$, then according to the law of electromagnetic induction, the current begins to cycle along the circuit

$$I = -10^{-8} \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \cdot \frac{1}{R} \text{ amper} \quad (1)$$

($\Phi$ is expressed in $ec \cdot cm^2$, $t$ is in seconds, and $R$ is in ohms). This current in the circuit flows under the influence of the electromotive force of induction

$$\varepsilon = -10^{-8} \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \text{ волт.} \quad (2)$$

The minus sign in these formulas is connected with this: we consider positive the current in the circuit, which itself creates a magnetic field in it with a positive flux (such a current flows around the circuit clockwise, if viewed from the inside out). The negative sign, therefore, shows that if the flux decreases with time $\left( \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \text{ отрицательно} \right)$, then the excited current will be positive and will itself create a positive flux. On the contrary, if the flow returns, the current in the circuit creates a flow in the opposite direction (Lenz's rule). Since the flux Ф consists of the flux of the external field and the flux created by the circuit itself, the induction of current in the circuit leads to a slowdown in the change in the total flux through the circuit.

Let the current be zero at the initial moment, and the flow is created by some external field source, for example a magnet. Now let's quickly remove the magnet. Then, at the same time, a current will arise in the circuit, which itself creates a magnetic flux of the same magnitude, that is, it will not allow the magnetic flux higher out of the circuit. In order for a current to exist in a circuit with electrical resistance $R$, it is necessary, of course, for the flow to change. Therefore, in subsequent moments, Ф will still gradually decrease, but the slower, the smaller $R$. Likewise, if we want to increase the flow of the internal circuit, this can only be done gradually.
