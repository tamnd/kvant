3. Let us draw a trapezoid with the required segment $EF$ and extend its sides until they intersect each other at point $M$ (Fig. 1). The continuation of line $EF$ will also pass through point $M$, since $MF$ will be the median of the resulting $\angle AMD$. By condition $\angle AMD = 180^\circ - \angle A - \angle D = 90^\circ$. From the property of the median of a right triangle it follows that
$$MF = \frac{1}{2}AD \text{ и } ME = \frac{1}{2}BC.$$
Many did not use this property of the median and had difficulty solving the problem. Meanwhile, it is not difficult to report that the right angle rests on the diameter of the circle circumscribed about the triangle. The center of such a circle lies at the middle of the hypotenuse, i.e. at the base of the median. Therefore, $MF$ and $AF$ are equal as radii. This implies the above property. Then it is easy to find the required segment:

$$EF = MF - ME = \frac{1}{2}(AD - BC) = \frac{1}{2}(7 - 3) = 2.$$

4. In the notation figure 2 the lateral surface of the cone
$$S_{бок} = \pi \cdot AO \cdot AS.$$
Let's denote it by $AS = BS = x$. Then, since $\angle ASB$ is straight, $AB = x\sqrt{2}$ is, and $AC = \frac{x\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is.
By condition $\angle AOB = 120^\circ$, therefore $\angle AOC = 60^\circ$ and
$$AO = \frac{AC}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{x\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{3} = x\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}.$$
Cone height
$$SO = \sqrt{AS^2 - AO^2} =$$

$$\sqrt{x^2 - \frac{2}{3}x^2} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} = h.$$
From here we find $x = h\sqrt{3}$, and $AO = h\sqrt{2}$.
The lateral surface of the cone will be
$$S_{бок} = \pi h\sqrt{2} \cdot h\sqrt{3} = \pi h^2\sqrt{6}.$$
5. We have $4\sin^2 x - \frac{1}{\cos x} = 1$, where $\cos x \neq 0$,
i.e. $x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + \pi k$. Reducing to the general value, we get
$$4\sin^2 x \cdot \cos x - 1 = \cos x,$$
$$4(1 - \cos^2 x) \cdot \cos x = 1 + \cos x$$
or
$$(1 + \cos x)(4\cos^2 x - 4\cos x + 1) = 0$$
and then we equate each of the factors to zero. Here, for many things, we did not put the common factor $(1 + \cos x)$ out of brackets, but opened the brackets in the penultimate expression and we come to the cubic equation
$$4\cos^3 x - 3\cos x + 1 = 0,$$
which they failed to resolve.
However, simple cubic equations, such as those that can be solved by factoring, may appear in entrance exams and need to be able to solve them.
In this case, we express 1 as the difference between the numbers 4 and 3 and present the equation in the form
$$4\cos^3 x - 3\cos x + 4 - 3 = 0$$
Grouping, let's write
$$4(\cos^3 x + 1) - 3(\cos x + 1) = 0,$$
and then
$$(\cos x + 1)[4(\cos^2 x - \cos x + 1) - 3] = 0$$
