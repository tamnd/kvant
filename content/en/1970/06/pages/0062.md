To the article “Law of Conservation of Magnetic Flux”

1. The current does not change.
2. The rate of change of magnetic flux through the coils is proportional to the rate of change of current, that is, constant. This means that constant e. d. With. induction in the second coil. The current is also constant.
3. The total flow through the loop must be zero. The flux through the first coil is equal in absolute value to $0,4\pi \frac{I_1 n_1^2}{l_1 S_1}$.
and through the second $0,4\pi \frac{I_2 n_2^2}{l_2 S_2}$ ($I_1$ and $I_2$ are the magnitudes of the currents in the coils). Equating these expressions, we get $\frac{I_1}{I_2} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)^2 \frac{S_2 l_1}{S_1 l_2}$.
Considering that $I_1 + I_2 = I$, we find

$$I_1 = I \frac{\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)^2 \frac{S_2 l_1}{S_1 l_2}}{1 + \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)^2 \frac{S_2 l_1}{S_1 l_2}}$$

$$I_2 = I \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)^2 \frac{S_2 l_1}{S_1 l_2}}$$

4. The charge flowing through the coil is proportional to the change in its arbitrary magnetic flux:

$$I \sim \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}.$$

The change in magnetic flux is proportional to the change in the area of ​​the circuit.

If the ring was not folded, but scalped in such a way that it turned out to be a figure eight, then the new value of the magnetic flux $\Phi_1$ will be equal to $\frac{S_1}{S} \Phi = \frac{5}{8} \Phi$ ($\Phi$ is the initial flux through the circuit), $\Phi_1$ – $\Phi = -\frac{3}{8} \Phi$, therefore $Q_1 = -\frac{3}{8} Q$.

If you squeeze the turn and turn over an even smaller circle, then

$$\Phi_1 - \Phi = -\frac{3}{8} \Phi - \frac{1}{16} \Phi +$$

$$+ \left(-\frac{1}{16} \Phi\right) = -\frac{1}{2} \Phi и Q_2 = -\frac{1}{2} Q$$

If you turned over a larger circle, then

$$\Phi_1 - \Phi = -\frac{3}{8} \Phi - \frac{9}{16} \Phi +$$

$$+ \left(-\frac{9}{16} \Phi\right) = -\frac{3}{2} \Phi и Q_3 = -\frac{3}{2} Q$$

The minus sign indicates that the direction of movement of the charge is opposite to the one in which it flowed when the field was turned on.

5. If the ring is superconducting, then as it moves, the current in the ring will increase so that the total flux of magnetic induction through the ring does not change. the direction of the current in the ring will be such that the ring begins to repel the magnetic field. At some point, the force acting on the ring from the magnet will balance all the rings, but since the ring will have some speed at this moment, it will slip through the equilibrium position; the ring will have a speed directed to the side. The ring will again pass through the equilibrium position, etc. Since no heat is released when current passes through the superconductor, oscillations will occur relative to the equilibrium positions.
