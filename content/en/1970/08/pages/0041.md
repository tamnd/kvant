The lower roller, and therefore the tape, can be turned by hand. But we use an asynchronous single-digit electric motor from an electric player 12 and connected it to the lower roller with a clutch 13.

The electric motor is turned on, the tape runs along the rollers. Along the way, from friction with brush 8, it picked up and deposited electric charges on the ball conductor. The more the tape has to rotate, the higher the potential of the ball will be. But there is a limit here. The fact is that at high electric field strength near the ball, the impact becomes electrically conductive, and charges begin to drain from the ball. The ball's potential stops growing.

We can calculate to what potential we can charge the ball. The field voltage on the surface of the ball is calculated by the formula $E = \frac{V}{R}$, where $V$ is the potential of the ball, and $R$ is its radius. If $R = 5$ cm, and $E$ for exposure at atmospheric pressure is equal to 30 kV/cm, then the ball can be charged up to 150 thousand volts.

This calculation is correct only for an ideal smooth ball, located very far from all other conducting objects. Therefore, the voltage created by our generator will be lower than the calculated one.

If you manage to make a larger ball, then you can charge it to a higher potential. Above only means that the ball should be very smooth and even.

Now do some experiments with the model.

First of all, determine the sign of the ball's charge. How to do this is clear. You just have to remember that an ebonite rod rubbed against fur is electrified negatively, and a glass rod rubbed against silk is positively electrified.

Determine how many full revolutions the tape must make in order for the high-voltage electrode to be fully charged. To do this, place a clearly visible mark on the tape and count how many full revolutions the tape makes until the electrode potential increases to its maximum value. Please note that the “generator performance” depends significantly on the position of the upper brush.

If the brush is positioned so that it is close to the place where the tape enters the ball, then the charges from the tape will not be completely removed, and you will have to wait a long time until the high-voltage electrode is charged. By changing the position of the upper brush, find one in which the high-voltage electrode will be fully charged at a minimum number of revolutions of the belt.

Our model uses nylon tape. And Van de Graaff used silk. In the electrostatic generator of the Kharkov Institute, the charging tape was made of rubberized fabric. Try making a charging tape from a different material.

Our charging brush is made from a polystyrene plate; it can be made from limited stack or copper. If you use a wire brush, ground it. The generator begins to work better. Why?

Don't despair if you fail at first. Remember that even the best existing electrostatic generators produce voltages that are only a third of what they are designed for. All the reasons for this are not yet clear. So there is something to think about and something to invent.

For those who want to learn more about electrostatic generators, we recommend reading the book “Electrostatic accelerators of charged particles”, Gosatomizdat, 1963.
