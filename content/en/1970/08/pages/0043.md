M39. Prove that non-negative integers $x, y$ satisfy the equation
$$x^2 - mxy + y^2 = 1$$
(where $m$ is a given integer greater than one) if and only if $x$ and $y$ are adjacent numbers of the sequence

$$\varphi_0 = 0, \quad \varphi_1 = 1, \quad \varphi_2 = m,$$
$$\varphi_3 = m^2 - 1, \quad \varphi_4 = m^3 - 2m,$$
$$\varphi_5 = m^4 - 3m^2 + 1, \quad \dots,$$
in which $\varphi_{k+1} = m\varphi_k - \varphi_{k-1}$ for all $k \geqslant 1$.

For example, all solutions to the equation $x^2 - 3xy + y^2 = 1$ for the number $0 \leqslant x < y$ are pairs $(0, 1)$; $(1, 3)$; $(3, 8)$; $(8, 21)$ adjacent members of the sequence $0, 1, 3, 8, 21, 55, \dots$ defined by the conditions $\varphi_0 = 0, \quad \varphi_1 = 1, \quad \varphi_{k+1} = 3\varphi_k - \varphi_{k-1}$ for all $k \geqslant 1$. (This red fact was used in the work of Yu. Matiyasevich related to Hilbert's 10th problem, which was discussed in the previous issue «Kvant».)

M40. Find the amount
$$1 \cdot n + 2 \cdot (n - 1) + 3 \cdot (n - 2) + \dots$$
$$\dots + (n - 1) \cdot 2 + n \cdot 1.$$

Try solving the following, more general problem: find the sum
$$S_n, k = [1 \cdot 2 \dots k] \times$$
$$\times [n(n - 1) \dots (n - k + 1)] +$$
$$+ [2 \cdot 3 \dots (k + 1)] \times$$
$$\times [(n - 1)(n - 2) \dots (n - k)] +$$
$$+ [3 \cdot 4 \dots (k + 2)] \times$$
$$\times [(n - 2)(n - 1) \dots (n - k - 1)] + \dots$$
$$\dots + [(n - k)(n - k + 1) \dots (n - 1)] \times$$
$$\times [(k + 1)k \dots 2] +$$
$$+ [(n - k + 1)(n - k + 2) \dots n] \times$$
$$\times [k(k - 1) \dots 1].$$
