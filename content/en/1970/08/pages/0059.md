⟦folio 57⟧

Everyone knows that every real number $a$ can be assigned an opposite number, that is, the number $-a$. We condition the number opposite $a$ to be denoted by the symbol $a$. For example: $\overline{2,5} = -2,5; \overline{-V^2} = \sqrt{2}; \overline{0} = 0$.

It is curious that the operations of “addition” and “multiplication” turn out to be connected with the operation of taking the opposite number:

$$\overline{a \oplus b} = \overline{a} \otimes \overline{b}; \overline{a \otimes b} = \overline{a} \oplus \overline{b}$$

(6) (in general arithmetic the formulas are also incorrect!).

Prove these formulas yourself.

In conclusion, try to solve the following problems yourself:

1. Verify that for $n \geq 2$ arbitrary real numbers $a_1, \ldots, a_n$
   $a_1 \oplus \ldots \oplus a_n = \max(a_1, \ldots, a_n), a_1 \otimes \ldots \otimes a_n = \min(a_1, \ldots, a_n)$.

2. Are the matches correct?
   $c \otimes (a \oplus b) = (c \otimes a) \oplus (c \otimes b), c \oplus (a \otimes b) = (c \oplus a) \otimes (c \oplus b)?$

3. Prove that for any real numbers $a, b, c$ the equality
   $\min [\min(a, c), \min(\max(a, b), c)] = \min(a, c)$ and write it using the operations $\oplus$ and $\otimes$. How can you immediately be convinced of its validity after this?

4. Let $a, b, c$ be arbitrary real numbers. Find a simple expression for the quantity
   $\min[a, -\max(\min(a, b), \max(a, c))]$.

5. For any real numbers $a, b, c$ is the equality true
   $(a \oplus b) \otimes (a \oplus b \oplus c) = a \otimes [c \otimes (c \oplus b)]?$

6. Is it possible to find a real number $x$ such that
   $\max [\min(a, \max(a, b), x)] = b$?

Below are several problems taken from the competition section of the Matematika magazine.

1. In some kingdoms, in some states there are several cities, and the distances between them are all different in pairs. On one morning, one plane takes off from each city and is used in the nearest neighboring city.

Can there be more than one heel of planes in any city?

2. prove; that if $a, b, c$ are the lengths of the sides of the triangle, and $l_a, l_b, l_c$ are the lengths of the bisectors of the internal angles drawn to them, then
   $$\frac{l_a}{a} + \frac{l_b}{b} + \frac{l_c}{c} \leq \frac{p^2}{2S}$$

where $p$ is the half-perimeter, and $S$ is the area of ​​the triangle; the equal sign is achieved only for an equilateral triangle.

A. Mameev

3. Prove that if the real number $a$ is the root of the equation
   $2(x^2 + x)\sin 2x + (2x + 2 - x^3)\cos 2x = x^3 + 2x + 2$,
then this number satisfies the inequality $|a| < 1$.

B. Petkov

4. Let there be a point in the plane of a convex polygon that has the property that any line drawn through this point divides the polygon into two equal parts. Prove that then this polygon is centrally symmetric.

P. Grozdanov

5. The fly, without stopping, will climb in one direction at a constant speed along the circumference of the upper base of a cylindrical tin can, the height of which is 4 times the diameter $2r$ of the base. At some point, from the point of the jar's surface farthest from the husband, the flour begins to follow the path, and it climbs along the surface of the jar at a constant speed equal to the speed of the flour (and also does not stop). Find the length of the shortest path along which the path can overtake the flour.

I. Dimovski
