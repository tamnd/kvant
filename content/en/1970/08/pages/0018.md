Recall that the numerators and denominators of suitable fractions are formed according to formulas (3.1). Let us assume that the fraction $\frac{p_n}{q_n}$ is reducible, i.e. its numerator and denominator have a common factor $\lambda$, different from one:

$$p_n = \lambda p_n', \quad q_n = \lambda q_n'.$$

Then formula (4.1) gives

$$\lambda (p_{n+1} q_n' - p_n' q_{n+1}) = (-1)^n.$$

We are stuck with an absurd equality: the left side is divisible by $\lambda$, but the right is not. This means that the fraction $\frac{p_n}{q_n}$ is irreducible.

III. Introduction of infinite fractions

7. The meaning of an infinite continued fraction. The principle of nested segments will serve us as a key that will open the meaning of an infinite continued fraction. Let us recall a similar situation: how to extrude an infinite decimal fraction? An infinite decimal fraction can be considered as a shorthand notation of a sequence of nested segments: successive roundings with a deficit give the left ends of these segments, and with an excess - the right ones. What is the meaning of the statement that $\sqrt{2}$ is expressed as an infinite decimal fraction

$$\sqrt{2} = 1,4142...?$$

This means that

$$1 < \sqrt{2} < 2,$$

$$1,4 < \sqrt{2} < 1,5,$$

$$1,41 < \sqrt{2} < 1,42,$$

There is a single number that satisfies all these inequalities at once. This is $\sqrt{2}$.

Having symbolic infinite continued fractions

$$[a_0; a_1, a_2, \dots],$$

it is possible to form an infinite sequence of finite continued fractions

$$a_0, [a_0; a_1], [a_0; a_1, a_2], \dots,$$

$$[a_0; a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n], \dots,$$

(**)

which can be written as suitable fractions

$$\frac{p_0}{q_0}, \frac{p_1}{q_1}, \frac{p_2}{q_2}, \dots, \frac{p_n}{q_n}, \dots$$

(***)

These suitable fractions determine the sequence of nested segments

$$\left[ \frac{p_0}{q_0}, \frac{p_1}{q_1} \right], \left[ \frac{p_2}{q_2}, \frac{p_1}{q_1} \right], \left[ \frac{p_2}{q_2}, \frac{p_3}{q_3} \right],$$

$$\left[ \frac{p_4}{q_4}, \frac{p_3}{q_3} \right], \dots$$

Each next segment is in the previous one (Fig. 4) and their lengths tend to zero (see formulas (4.2)). Therefore, there is a number $\alpha$ that belongs to all segments. It belongs to the value of an infinite continued fraction**).

This definition can be expressed in another way. Here are two options.

1) The value of an infinite continued fraction lies between any two adjacent suitable fractions.

2) The value of a .befinite continued fraction is greater than the doi of a suitable fraction

(*) Marks 0, 1, 2, ... on figure 4 read the numbers of suitable fractions.

**) This defines the term "fitting fraction", it "fits" closer and closer to the point $\alpha$.
