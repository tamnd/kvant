About two actions
with real numbers
Kiril Chimev

The operations of addition and multiplication of real numbers are subject to a number of laws. Let's recall some of them:
1. Commutativity of addition: $a+b=b+a$.
2. Addition associativity: $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$
3. Commutativity of multiplication: $ab=ba$.
4. Multiplication associativity: $(ab)c=a(bc)$.
5. Distributivity of multiplication relative to addition:
$$(a+b)c=ac+bc$$

It is curious, however, that these laws remain valid in some other cases, when “addition” and “multiplication” are understood as other operations that differ from the addition and multiplication of real numbers that are familiar to us.

By “sum”*) of two real numbers $a$ and $b$ we agree to understand max $(a, b)$, that is, the largest of the numbers $a$ and $b$, if $a \neq b$, and any of them, if $a=b$. The operation of “addition,” which assigns any two real numbers $a$ and $b$ their “sum,” will be denoted by the sign $\oplus$, so
$$a \oplus b=\max(a, b).$$

For example:
$$(-3) \oplus 2=2,$$
$$0 \oplus (-\sqrt{2})=0,$$
$$\pi \oplus \pi=\pi.$$

Commutativity of "addition"
$$a \oplus b=b \oplus a$$
obvious because
$$\max(a, b)=\max(b, a).$$

By the “product” of two real numbers $a$ and $b$ we agree to understand the number min $(a, b)$, that is, the largest of the numbers $a$ and $b$, if $a \neq b$, and any of them, if $a=b$. The operation of “multiplication”, which assigns any two real numbers $a$ and $b$ their “product”, will be denoted by the sign $\otimes$, so that
$$a \otimes b=\min(a, b).$$

For example:
$$(-3) \otimes 2=-3,$$
$$0 \otimes (-\sqrt{2})=-\sqrt{2},$$
$$\pi \otimes \pi=\pi.$$

Commutativity of "multiplication"
$$a \otimes b=b \otimes a$$
obvious because
$$\min(a, b)=\min(b, a).$$
