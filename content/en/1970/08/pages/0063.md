make sure that the three-dimensional picture that we see in the mirror does not disappear even after removing the mirror?
In other words, is it possible to capture a three-dimensional image of opaque objects in the same way that regular photographic film does with a flat image? This problem was solved by Yu. N. Denisok (before him, a similar problem of obtaining a volumetric image of solid bodies was solved by the English physicist Gabor). It turns out that for this it is necessary to record in a special transparent fine-grained emulsion the distribution of light waves near the object under consideration (as they say, to obtain a hologram of the object). If you develop the resulting “photograph,” it will be completely different from the object depicted. However, having illuminated the developed plate with white light, we will see close to it a magnificent three-dimensional image of the object photographed in this way.

If you insert a mirror and take a photo in the usual way, you will get a circle with light reflections. If you get a hologram of such a mirror, then the image created with its help will be so real that it can focus the light falling on it like a real mirror.

A new method for obtaining three-dimensional images, created by Yu. N. Denisok, opens up the possibility of creating color three-dimensional cinema and television, improving radar and sonar.

In the field of mathematics, the Lenin Prize was awarded to a team of Leningrad scientists - academician Yu. V. Linnik, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Yu. V. Prozhorov, professor I. A. Ibragimov and Yu. A. Rozanov for a series of works on limit methods of probability techniques. In these works, the most important patterns of a number of complex random processes that play a major role in many areas of science and technology were found. The patterns found greatly facilitate the study of random processes that are widespread in nature and society.

The Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR also decided to award the 1970 Lenin Prizes in the field of science and technology to a large group of scientists, designers and industrial workers for the following works:

— a set of scientific and technical work carried out with the aim of creating an experimental orbital station and implementing it during launches of the Soyuz rocket and space system;

— creation of automatic mechanical stations “Venera-4”, “Venera-5”, “Venera-6” and a complex of scientific research to determine the physical parameters and chemical composition of the atmosphere of the planet Venus;

- the creation of a meteorological system that, with the help of artificial Earth satellites "Meteor", provides the receipt and prompt processing of global meteorological information for those in need of addressing these complex scientific and technical problems, a great contribution was made by Soviet mathematicians and physicists.
