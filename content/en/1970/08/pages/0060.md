⟦folio 58⟧

1970 is the year of the Great Jubilee, the 100th anniversary of the birth of V.I. Lenin, whose name is given to awards for the most outstanding research and achievements in the field of science and technology. It is gratifying to note that the majority of the anniversary year prizes went to physiomathematical research. This is convincing evidence of the successful development of modern physics and mathematics, which occupy the forefront of world science. Next, a short story about the works awarded the Lenin Prize will, of necessity, take up several pages in our magazine.

A team of employees from four leading scientific institutes - the Institute of Experimental and Theoretical Physics, the Radio Engineering Institute, the Institute of High Energy Physics and the Research Institute of Electrophysical Equipment - includes Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences V.V. Vladimirsky, D.G. Koshkarev, A.A. Kuzmin, Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A.A. Logunov, R.M. Sulyaev, I.F. Malysheva was awarded the Lenin Prize for the development and commissioning of the promon synchropron of the Institute of High Energy Physics for an energy of 70 GeV.

The main experimental tool for studying the wonderful world of elementary particles is the loaded particle accelerator. By accelerating particles to gigantic energies, it makes it possible to study the interaction of accelerated particles with matter, from the various features of which one can judge the nature of the forces acting between particles. With such interactions, new elementary particles are born that do not exist under normal conditions.
