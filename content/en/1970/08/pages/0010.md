Problem 6. Prove that the vertices of a polyorganic can be divided into black and white poppies so that no two vertices of a monozoche tsempa are adjacent moeda and molko moeda, when each face of the polyorganic has an even number of sides.

e is also indicated. To prove it, it is enough to take any vertex and declare it white, its neighbors black, etc. It is necessary to prove that no contradictions arise in this case, i.e., that in every closed broken line composed of the edges of a polyorganic there is an even number of links.

Let's return to the polyorganic $M$ with colored vertices. Since $M$ three faces converge at each vertex, the number of white vertices is equal to the number of black vertices. Let us select some of its black vertices (one, two or all) and cut them off using planes. Each vertex is a segment of one plane, intersecting only those edges that emanate from this vertex, and not containing any vertices $M$. We denote the resulting polyorganic by $M'$. It differs from $M$ in that it has, together with some black vertices, triangular faces. Let us prove that the polyorganic $M'$ is absolutely indescribable.

Let us assume that the polyorganic $M'$ or another polyorganic having the same structure is inscribed in a sphere. Let us select three faces that correspond to those faces of the polyorganic $M$ that converge at one trimmed vertex. These three faces are pairwise adjacent, the vertex of the truncated trihedral angle obviously lies outside the sphere, since the polyorganic is inscribed, therefore the sum of the relative angles for them is less than l. Let us assign to each edge of the polyorganic $M$ a relative angle with the corresponding edge of the polyorganic $M'$. Then the sum of these assigned angles for all white and all unselected black vertices is equal to l, and for selected black vertices it is strictly less than l. As in the proof of Steinitz’s theorem, we write out these equalities and inequalities, multiply the sums corresponding to the white vertices by -1 and add them. The result should be strict inequality. However, on the left side we get zero, or each of the assigned angles is included in the sum once for a white vertex, and once for a black one. On the right side we will also get zero, since the number of white vertices is equal to the number of black ones. The resulting contradiction ($0<0$) proves our statement.

Exactly the same reasoning can be verified if the marked vertices are defined not by one, but by several planes, as long as the faces converging in them remain pairwise adjacent and do not converge at one vertex.

This method can also obtain other sufficient conditions. Now some necessary conditions have become known for a polyorganic to be absolutely indescribable, but in general the problem has not yet been solved.

The relative angle method allows us to solve other interesting problems.
