Professor of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow University A. A. Vlasov was awarded the Lenin Prize for a series of works on the theory of plasma, containing a fundamental method for studying its properties.

Plasma is an inoculated gas consisting of a huge number of loaded particles. We will encounter it in various electronic devices. It forms the upper layers of the earth's atmosphere and fills outer space. The behavior of plasma is very different from the behavior of an ordinary gas consisting of neutral particles. In an ordinary gas, the main role is played by direct collisions of its particles. The behavior of such a gas is described by the Boltzmann equation. A. A. Vlasov was the first to show that in plasma such collisions play practically no role, since the electrostatic response does not allow the particles to come close to each other. Therefore, in his equation for plasma, Vlasov excluded the classical term that takes into account the direct abundances of particles, and introduced a term that takes into account the collective influence of loaded neighbors on the behavior of individual particles. This is how the famous “Vlasov equation” was obtained, which formed the basis of plasma theory.

The method of plasma research developed by A. A. Vlasov is used in work on the creation of controlled thermonuclear reactions, in the study of the propagation of radio waves in the atmosphere and outer space, as well as in a vast new field of physics, the so-called magnetic hydrodynamics, associated in part with methods of direct conversion of thermal energy into electrical energy.

Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences R.V. Khokhlov and Professor S.A. Akhmanov, working at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow University, were awarded a prize for the study of nonlinear co-ferential interactions in optics.

Object optics deals with processes in which the intensity of light is insufficient to change the physical properties of the medium through which the light passes. The emergence of powerful sources of light beams - lasers - makes it possible to create new optics, so-called nonlinear. She studies the numerous changes in the physical characteristics of the environment under the influence of the passage of light. The foundations of this area of ​​physics were laid by R.V. Khokhlov and S.A. Akhmanov. However, one of the most important achievements of these scientists was the creation of lasers with varying wavelengths of radiation. Typically, each laser has its own strictly fixed wavelength of emitted radiation, its own color. It is determined by natural radiatingweise. The range of possible wavelengths for different lasers is quite poor; it does not contain many tricks needed for physics, chemistry and biological sciences. R.V. Khokhlov and S.A. Akhmanov developed new principles that allowed less colorization of the laser in a very wide range of wavelengths. For example, using their methods, a laser operating on invisible infrared waves can be made to emit ultraviolet rays. Such lasers have many important practical applications. They greatly expanded the possibilities of studying the nature of substances.
