1970 is the year of the Great Jubilee, the 100th anniversary of the birth of V.I. Lenin, whose name is given to awards for the most outstanding research and achievements in the field of science and technology. It is gratifying to note that the majority of the anniversary year prizes went to physiomathematical research. This is convincing evidence of the successful development of modern physics and mathematics, which occupy the forefront of world science. Next, a short story about the works awarded the Lenin Prize will, of necessity, take up several pages in our magazine.

The main experimental tool for studying the wonderful world of elementary particles is the loaded particle accelerator. By accelerating particles to gigantic energies, it makes it possible to study the interaction of accelerated particles with matter, from the various features of which one can judge the nature of the forces acting between particles. With such interactions, new elementary particles are born that do not exist under normal conditions.

The largest accelerator in the world is the Soviet proton synchrotron, recently built in the young science city of Protvino near Serpukhov. It accelerates the atomic nuclei of hydrogen - protons - to an energy of 70 billion electron volts (70 GeV; 1 electron volt equals energy

The main part of the accelerator is the pressing chamber - a giant underground ring 1480 m long. The chamber is located in a magnetic field formed by 120 magnetic sections weighing 200 tons, between which 54 accelerating devices (resonance) are placed. The creator of the accelerator had to offer enormous difficulties associated with unusually strict requirements for the characteristics of the magnetic field and the arrangement of the blocks. The accelerated particles make about 400,000 revolutions in 2.6 seconds and reach speeds very close to the speed of light in vacuum. When they hit targets, they generate streams of secondary particles (mesons, antiprotons, neutrinos, etc.), which are sorted by special separators into different channels for further research.

During the short period of operation of the accelerator in Protvino, a number of unique experiments have already been performed on it. One of them was devoted to the Japanese so-called “quarks” (hypothetical elemental particles with fractional values ​​of electron charges), the other ended with the creation of atomic nuclei of the anti-helement - antihelium with a mass of 3. Research on elementary particles requires not only accelerators capable of creating such particles, but also special methods for recording traces of these particles. There are few such methods, and each of them brings fundamental discoveries to science. A track spark chamber is one of the best modern methods for recording elementary particles. It consists of several electrodes (plates or wires) connected to a high voltage source and placed in a vessel filled with a neutral gas. Passing through the gas gaps between the electrodes, a rapidly charged particle destroys gas atoms. A track appears - a visible trace of a particle formed by a chain of bright sparks. The design of the cameras is such that they are capable of recording particles for very short periods of time, amounting to millionths of a second. This allows experimenters to target the camera to record specific types of particle elements and their characteristic interactions. Track spark chambers have helped make important discoveries, for example, the discovery of the existence of two types of special particle elements - neutrinos, one of which is associated with the birth of electrons,

Professor of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow University A. A. Vlasov was awarded the Lenin Prize for a series of works on the theory of plasma, containing a fundamental method for studying its properties.

Plasma is an inoculated gas consisting of a huge number of loaded particles. We will encounter it in various electronic devices. It forms the upper layers of the earth's atmosphere and fills outer space. The behavior of plasma is very different from the behavior of an ordinary gas consisting of neutral particles. In an ordinary gas, the main role is played by direct collisions of its particles. The behavior of such a gas is described by the Boltzmann equation. A. A. Vlasov was the first to show that in plasma such collisions play practically no role, since the electrostatic response does not allow the particles to come close to each other. Therefore, in his equation for plasma, Vlasov excluded the classical term that takes into account the direct abundances of particles, and introduced a term that takes into account the collective influence of loaded neighbors on the behavior of individual particles. This is how the famous “Vlasov equation” was obtained, which formed the basis of plasma theory.

The method of plasma research developed by A. A. Vlasov is used in work on the creation of controlled thermonuclear reactions, in the study of the propagation of radio waves in the atmosphere and outer space, as well as in a vast new field of physics, the so-called magnetic hydrodynamics, associated in part with methods of direct conversion of thermal energy into electrical energy.

Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences R.V. Khokhlov and Professor S.A. Akhmanov, working at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow University, were awarded a prize for the study of nonlinear co-ferential interactions in optics.

Object optics deals with processes in which the intensity of light is insufficient to change the physical properties of the medium through which the light passes. The emergence of powerful sources of light beams - lasers - makes it possible to create new optics, so-called nonlinear. She studies the numerous changes in the physical characteristics of the environment under the influence of the passage of light. The foundations of this area of ​​physics were laid by R.V. Khokhlov and S.A. Akhmanov. However, one of the most important achievements of these scientists was the creation of lasers with varying wavelengths of radiation. Typically, each laser has its own strictly fixed wavelength of emitted radiation, its own color. It is determined by natural radiatingweise. The range of possible wavelengths for different lasers is quite poor; it does not contain many tricks needed for physics, chemistry and biological sciences. R.V. Khokhlov and S.A. Akhmanov developed new principles that allowed less colorization of the laser in a very wide range of wavelengths. For example, using their methods, a laser operating on invisible infrared waves can be made to emit ultraviolet rays. Such lasers have many important practical applications. They greatly expanded the possibilities of studying the nature of substances.

The fifth Lenin Prize in Physics was awarded to Yu. N. Denisyuk, an employee of the State Optical Institute, for a series of works “Holeraphy with recording in a three-dimensional environment.”

Objects give us a flat image of the objects imprinted on them. And in the mirror we see a three-dimensional image - the object appears to be located in the depths of the mirror. Of course, what we see behind the mirror is not really there. The image is created by light waves incident on the mirror and reflected from it. Is it not possible

make sure that the three-dimensional picture that we see in the mirror does not disappear even after removing the mirror?
In other words, is it possible to capture a three-dimensional image of opaque objects in the same way that regular photographic film does with a flat image? This problem was solved by Yu. N. Denisok (before him, a similar problem of obtaining a volumetric image of solid bodies was solved by the English physicist Gabor). It turns out that for this it is necessary to record in a special transparent fine-grained emulsion the distribution of light waves near the object under consideration (as they say, to obtain a hologram of the object). If you develop the resulting “photograph,” it will be completely different from the object depicted. However, having illuminated the developed plate with white light, we will see close to it a magnificent three-dimensional image of the object photographed in this way.

If you insert a mirror and take a photo in the usual way, you will get a circle with light reflections. If you get a hologram of such a mirror, then the image created with its help will be so real that it can focus the light falling on it like a real mirror.

A new method for obtaining three-dimensional images, created by Yu. N. Denisok, opens up the possibility of creating color three-dimensional cinema and television, improving radar and sonar.

In the field of mathematics, the Lenin Prize was awarded to a team of Leningrad scientists - academician Yu. V. Linnik, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Yu. V. Prozhorov, professor I. A. Ibragimov and Yu. A. Rozanov for a series of works on limit methods of probability techniques. In these works, the most important patterns of a number of complex random processes that play a major role in many areas of science and technology were found. The patterns found greatly facilitate the study of random processes that are widespread in nature and society.
