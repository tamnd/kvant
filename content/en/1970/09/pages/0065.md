2. To solve the second problem, it is necessary, by changing the scale along both axes, to combine the curve of Figure 1 with the generalized curve (Fig. 8). This can be followed in different ways and it is better not to look for a general solution, but to take advantage of the specific features of the curves. In this case, it is convenient that far from the zero point the curves become practically primordial. Continuing (extrapolating) these straight sections to the x-axis, we arrive at point $T=60^\circ K$ in Figure 1 and $\frac{T}{\theta}=0,15$ in Figure 8.

If we consider the curves to be similar, then this means that $\frac{60}{\theta}=0,20$, hence $\theta=300^\circ K$.

Using the same method as in the previous problem, we find $N=8,5 \cdot 10^{22}$ atoms in cm$^3$ and to determine the speed of sound we obtain the equation: $300 = 2,07 \cdot 10^{-11} c \sqrt[3]{8,5 \cdot 10^{22}}$, from which $c = \frac{300}{2,07 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \sqrt[3]{8,5 \cdot 10^{22}}} = \frac{300}{2,07 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot 4,3 \cdot 10^7} = 3,37 \cdot 10^5 c m/sec = 3,37 km/sec$. Experimental value according to the reference book: 3.56 km/sec. The agreement between calculation and experiment should be considered very good, if we take into account that, in addition to thermal, there is also structural resistance and that for an accurate calculation it would be necessary to know the detailed shape of the background spectrum of copper.
