Nevertheless, we found out that such “considerations” lie at the very basis of the edifice of mathematics that has been built over centuries, and its future fate depends on them. Although a number of mathematicians, with more or less consistency, are trying to present mathematics on the basis of an understandable framework that allows for a finite description, and along this path many very meaningful facts have been obtained that have made it possible to look at many things from a different perspective, however, the claims that the development of mathematics should take place within these frameworks have not received widespread support. “No one can drive us out of the series that Cantor created for us,” said one of the greatest mathematicians, David Hilbert.

We place one of the problems proposed at the 5th traditional Olympiad in linguistics and mathematics (at the first type in 1969).

Problem.
Letter 1.

Dear friend!
Some time ago I bought two very beautiful dogs: a shepherd and a dachshund, but quickly discovered that they were very poorly trained. The fact is that they really love to lie down. As a result, living with them in the same house is quite difficult. However, I do not despair, or I have established through practical testing that their behavior is subject to a certain law, incomprehensible but indisputable (although this may be explained by the fact that dogs used to perform in the circus), and that I can influence them by playing the piano or closing a real lamp.

During every minute, each dog either barks or remains silent: they do not detect any transitions. Their behavior in the next minute depends only on the events of the moment, and this dependence is as follows.

The shepherd dog in the next minute behaves in the same way as in the previous one: barks or is silent, unless in that previous minute the piano was played and the dachshund did not bark. In the latter case, the shepherd changes its behavior to the opposite: barking to silence and vice versa.

As for the dachshund, if the lamp was on in the previous minute, the dachshund will lie down or be silent depending on whether the shepherd dog barked or was silent, so the dachshund copies the shepherd dog with a minute delay. If, however, the lamp was not on, the dachshund would do the opposite of what the shepherd did.
