Electrical resistance is a quantum phenomenon

D. A. Frank-Kamenetsky

The resistance of a conductor to the flow of electric current is not at all as simple a thing as it seems at first glance. As we will see, we cannot do without quantum here either. Let's first see what can be done without its help.

Classical theory

We are used to the fact that “classic” is a similar word. They say: Streltsov scored a “classic goal.” Instead of “classic,” you could say “cool” or even “iron” in more modern jargon. A physicist has a completely different attitude towards this word. In modern physics, classical are called theories created by the classes of science of the past, before the emergence of quantum physics and the theory of relativity. Most often, the word “classical” is used by physicists as the opposite of the word “quantum”. The classical theory is different in that it does not involve quanti. Such theories are very simple; to modern physicists they seem somewhat naive. They often bring a big role, but, as a rule, they do not fully resolve the issue. In classical theory, there is always something missing for a modern physicist.

In many books, including textbooks (and even very well), it is written that the electrical resistance of a metal originates from modern electricians,

carrying current, with atoms*) of the crystal lattice. Is this concept of resistance correct?

It is best not to say either “yes” or “no”, but to say that it expresses the classical theory of electrical resistance. Everything we just said about classical theories in general applies to it. But in this case, the classical theory turns out to be very weak: it cannot explain many of the main features of electrical resistance. In the classical theory, one thing is certainly true: resistance comes from the fact that electrons transfer part of their energy and momentum (momentum) to the crystal lattice. But exactly how this transmission originates is not a simple and very interesting question.

Residual resistance

When the resistance of pure metals is changed at very low temperatures, a remarkable thing is discovered. Continuing the curve figure 1 to absolute zero, we get the resistance that should remain if it were possible to reach absolute zero in the experiment. This is called residual resistance. It turns out that it varies greatly from sample to sample. The remaining resistance is extremely sensitive to insignificant impurities and to mechanical and thermal treatment. By carefully cleaning the metal, shaping it so as to achieve a perfect crystalline structure, the residual resistance can be reduced, and there is no limit to this decrease. So, experience leads to an amazing conclusion: an ideal crystal at a temperature of absolute zero should have no electrical resistance. In other words: the electrical resistance of pure metals occurs only from disturbances in the crystal structure, which are caused by thermal movements, impurities and defects (irregularities) of the crystal solution.

Miracles without miracles

Even more amazing facts have long been known. Mechanical and thermal treatment significantly affect the electrical resistance of metals. For ease of use in technology, we will use units of resistivity $om \cdot mm^2/m = 10^{-4} \cdot om \cdot cm = 10^{-6} \cdot om \cdot m$. In these units, the resistivity of total commercial copper at $20^\circ C$ is expressed as 0.0172. After cold drawing, the resistance of copper wiring increases to 0.0177. Further winding the wire around the coil is enough to increase its resistance. If you subject the wiring to annealing, that is, prolonged heating, and then cool it again to $20^\circ C$, the resistance value returns to normal. Obviously, the resistance is sensitive to small disturbances in the crystal structure (such properties are called structure sensitive). Sound quantum - phonon

We became acquainted with some quantum phenomena, but the quantum itself remained in the shadows for now. Now he will come to the fore.

Thermal motion in a solid can be thought of as vibrations of atoms around their equilibrium positions. But particles in a solid cannot vibrate independently. They are all firmly connected with each other and always perform collective oscillations. Not like on a dance floor, where each couple moves independently of the others, but like in the mass system of a large ballet, where everyone performs their parts in harmony.

Collective vibrations of a huge number of parts of matter are the same as sound waves. If you remove any particle from its equilibrium position, it will possibly spread throughout the body at the speed of sound. It turns out that thermal motion in a solid can be considered as the propagation of sound waves. By heating the body, we make it sound. Fortunately for us, the frequency of these vibrations is billions of times higher than what our ear can hear. Obviously, living beings in the process of evolution have adapted not to hear the “tell sound”, otherwise we would not have been able to withstand the continuous noise of “screaming” objects.

Thus, a necessary connection is discovered between electrical resistance and sound. Electrons are scattered by sound waves generated by thermal movement of the crystal lattice. But every wave consists of quanta. For a light or electromagnetic wave in general, these are photons, for a sound wave, they are phonons (from the Greek words that introduced light and sound). Photons are real particles, quite equal to other elementary particles. Phonons are not entirely equal in the sense that they can only exist inside matter (phonons cannot exist in emptiness). They - and others like them - are called quasiparticles (that is, “almost particles”). Phonon is the simplest representative of the vast family of quasiparticles. Its other members do not interest us now.

We said that electron waves are scattered by roughness created by thermal movements, that is, sound waves. But if a sound wave is described as a flow of phonons, then electrons can also be considered particles, forgetting about their wave properties. Now we seem to return to the simple explanation of resistance: electrons are prevented from moving freely by the fact that they collide with other particles. But not with particles of matter, but with sound quanta - phonons.
