AND LAZINESS CAN BE USEFUL

However, mathematicians who lived after Eudoxus were not too eager to discover new formulas. The center of scientific thought at this time moved from Greece to Egypt, where, under the tutelage of the Ptolemies in the Alexandrian Museum, scientists studied the sky, systematized previously accumulated mathematical knowledge, wrote books in honor of the high patrons of science, and copied ancient manuscripts. It must be said that the Alexandrian scientists did not so little, only their work was more of an epigone character. They developed what was previously available rather than selling new paths.

One of the few exceptions in the quiet family of Alexandrian scientists was a relative of the Syracusan king, Archimedes, who came to study from distant Syracuse. Unlike most scientists of that time, he was interested not only in high theory, but also in the practical applications of science, invented a screw pump, which was then used for many centuries, made mechanical toys, and later, when his hometown was besieged by the Romans, built military machines. It is clear that Archimedes became interested in mechanics, the balance of levers, the floating of bodies, etc. And mechanics has been separated from pure science since the time of Plato and was considered something akin to craft. Therefore, mechanics did not carefully monitor the rigor of reasoning, and mechanics widely used atomism in their research, splitting the wedges into atoms-wedges having a common vertex with the whole wedge, water outlets - into a number of “atomic” circles of small thickness, etc. *).

And in many of Archimedes’ works he encountered a curious combination of the method of exudation with reasoning drawn from mechanics. He learned to apply the concepts of the center of gravity and leverage to the solution of geometric problems and in this way obtained new results that were inaccessible to the “pure mathematics” of that time**). And since the results of mechanics were ultimately based on atomistic reasoning, then in fact Archimedes in these works relied on atomism. But obviously he didn’t write anything about it. The good mathematical tone of the time seemed to require that scientists present their results without referring to the anathematized and den of Democritus. Or maybe Archimedes had not yet read Democritus - this is indicated by some details of his works.

They only had to follow the bends of Archimedes' thoughts, only at the very end becoming convinced of his correctness.
