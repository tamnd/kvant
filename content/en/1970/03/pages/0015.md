In 1906, a private assistant professor at St. Petersburg University, Popadopulogo-Keramevs, found some Bogoslav treatise in the library of one of the non-Rusalem monasteries. Since parchment was very expensive in the Middle Ages, they usually took ancient books, washed or erased pagan texts from them, and wrote the life of some holy martyr. The hand that interested Popadopul was also of the same origin. The assistant professor was very weak in mathematics and was not too interested in the remnants of the erased text (fortunately, the monk who wrote the treatise was lazy and only erased the text, and did not erase it). He gave only a small excerpt from the ancient manuscript. But for the famous Danish source of mathematics, Heiberg, this excerpt was enough to establish that the monk washed away the text of Archimedes. Among the many works read by Heiberg was the last one by Eratovfenu.

In this message, Archimedes pays tribute to the merits of Democritus, saying that he was the first to establish the theorems on the volume of a cone and a pramid, without, however, giving them proper proof. And the method with which Archimedes conducted a preliminary study of the problem turned out to be close to the methods of Democritus. He decomposes cylinders, cones and balls into extremely thin circles, proves the position he needs for one of them, notes that the conclusion must be true for the rest, and finally says: “Since the whole body is made up of such circles and is completely filled with them, the statement is true for the whole body.” But this coincides almost word for word with the reasoning of the atomists!

Archimedes well understood the value of his method and hoped that with the help of this method “one of the current or future researchers... will find other theorems that have not yet occurred to me.”

Unfortunately, the posture of Archimedes turned out to be unknown to precisely those mathematicians who needed it most - the creators of the calculus of infinite malch, Kepler, Galil, Cavalieri. But they, using individual hints, reconstructed Archimedes’ train of thought.

The story of how atomistic ideas were revived and how modern mathematical analysis was created will appear on pages «Kvant».
