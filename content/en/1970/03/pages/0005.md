A MOMENT OF ETERNITY

Interest in very large numbers dates back to ancient times. The Egyptians, in order to say about something that it was very large, resorted to figurative comparisons. The text of one tomb of the priest of the god Ai (XIV century BC) says:

“May he (God) reward you with anniversaries like the number of the sand of the seashore, measured by iron, and like the measure of the sea, determined by jayet, or the weight of a mountain weighed on scales, or the feathers of birds, or the leaves of trees.”

It was difficult for the Egyptians to express their thoughts differently, since they did not sufficiently develop a system of numerical notations. But about 5000 years ago, the sexagesimal number system * appeared in Ancient Babylon, and Babylonian mathematicians were able to easily handle large numbers. One of the ancient Babylonian tables lists, for example, all the divisors of the number $60^8+10\cdot 60^7=195\ 955\ 200\ 000\ 000$.

Very large numbers are also spoken of in Indian legends about Buddha. According to one of them, he was tested in numbers even in childhood, and, moving from one size to another, he must reach numbers that are expressed by the number of grains of sand of ten lakh rivers (lakha = 100,000) such as the Ganges.
