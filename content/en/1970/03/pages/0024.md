But since there is no force acting on the planets, Galileo reasoned, then such movement is a natural property of the planets. Galileo called it inertia and very convincingly proved the erroneous statement: the reason for the rotation of the planets is inertia, motion by inertia can only be motion in a circle, all points of which are equal (!).

Galileo considered motion in a straight line to be excluded: the transition over time from one point to another was familiar because of their non-equivalence; in addition, in this case the body would never reach the final goal (in modern language - an equilibrium stable state), and nature never sets itself inaccessible goals (that is, an equilibrium state occurs sooner or later).

This reasoning led Galileo to a contradiction not only with Kepler, but also with himself: after all, he had previously proven that motion in a circle is associated with a force acting towards the center!
