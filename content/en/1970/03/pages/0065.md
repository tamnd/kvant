Option 1

1. 2.5 $v$. Use Newton's second law in the form $\Delta(mv) = F\Delta t$.

2. $P_1 = \frac{1}{50} m_a \delta \frac{RT_1}{\mu(V - V_{a\Delta})} + P \frac{T_1}{T} \approx 25,35 \text{ am}$.
$T$ and $T_1$ are absolute gas temperatures. It is convenient to solve the problem using the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation: $PV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT$.

3. $\rho = \frac{2m}{Ne^2\tau}$. The current through the sample is $i = NeSv$,
where $S$ is the cross section of the sample, and $v$ is the average electron velocity. During the time from 0 to $\tau$ in the field $E$ the electron speed changes from 0 to value $\frac{eE}{m}\tau$. The average speed is $\frac{1}{2}\frac{eE}{m}\tau$.

4. $x_{\text{max}} = \frac{F_2(L_1 - F_1)}{F_1 + F_2 - L_1} = 45 \text{ cm}$,
$x_{\text{min}} = \frac{F_2(L_2 - F_1)}{F_1 + F_2 - L_2} = 7,5 \text{ cm}$.

The image of the object, obtained in the form of the first lens, is viewed through the second lens, and the minimum image given by the second lens should appear in the field of sharp visibility with the naked eye.

Option 3
1. $\frac{3}{2} r$. The corners and center of the grid are equipotential. Therefore, they can be connected by a conductor without resistance, without changing the distribution of current in the circuit and, therefore, without changing the resistance of the circuit.

2. $P = \frac{2P_0(P_0 \pm \Delta P)}{2P_0 \pm \Delta P}$. You can use Dalton's law (the pressure in a vessel is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of gases filling parts of the vessel) and the Boyle-Marnott law. The ± sign corresponds to the moving in or out of the piston.

3. $\frac{m_1 m_2 v^2}{(m_1 + m_2) l}$. The system rotates in a circular center of mass, which is located at a distance $x = l \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}$ from the ball with mass $m_1$. Therefore, it is most convenient to solve the problem in a coordinate system associated with the center of mass. Centripetal acceleration is imparted to the balls by the tension force of the thread.

4. $E = \frac{q}{r^2} \left(1 - \frac{a}{b}\right)$. The charge of the internal sphere can be found from the condition that the potential of this sphere is equal to zero (that is, the work spent on transferring a unit positive charge from infinity to the sphere): $\frac{q_1}{a} + \frac{q}{b} = 0$.
