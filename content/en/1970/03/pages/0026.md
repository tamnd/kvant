Parquets made of regular polygons

A. N. KOLMOGOROV

WHAT IS PARQUET

The simplest, but also the most boring parquet is obtained if the plane is divided into equal squares as shown in figure 1, a. Here two squares either have a common side, a common vertex, or no common points at all.

We will call parquet any covering of a plane with regular polygons, in which two polygons have either a common side, a common vertex, or no common points at all.

You should probably see a parquet made of regular octagons and squares (Fig. 2, a). A beautiful parquet can be made from regular hexagons, squares and equilateral triangles (Fig. 2, b).

Parquet makes a pleasant impression if it is sufficiently symmetrical. A figure is called symmetrical if it can be superimposed on itself in a “non-attributive” way (i.e., without a maxim, when all the points remain in their place).
