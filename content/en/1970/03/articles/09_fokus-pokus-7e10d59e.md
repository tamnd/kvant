Hocus Pocus

In Vyacheslav Shishkov’s novel “Strandiki” there are the following lines:

- Do you want me to show you arithmetic hocus pocus? You'll gasp.
- Oh! Well, show me, milliny.
Ivan Petrovich tore a page out of the notebook, handed it to the boy, and asked:
- Do you have a pencil? Write any number.
The boy wrote. Ivan Petrovich looked at this number in chalk, wrote his own number on a separate key of paper, put the piece of paper in the straw and covered it with his hat.
- Write something else under it. Pissed? Now I'll write the third one myself. Now all three numbers are added. Just be more careful, don’t lie.
Two minutes later the verified answer was ready. Engineer Voshkin submitted his calculations:

46 853
21 398
78 601
146 852

- One hundred and forty-six thousand eight hundred and fifty-two, Ivan Petrovich.
- You've been counting for a long time. And here is my answer. I already knew it when you wrote the first number. Here. Pull from under the hat.
The boy grabbed the piece of paper. It read: 146,852. Engineer Voshkin’s surprised face stretched out, and the hair on the back of his neck stood up. With wealth, with surprise, he stared at Ivan Petrovich
- Well... so... how? ... A?..
Ivan Petrovich, smiling and moving his eyebrows, explained twice and gave another example...”
The question is, what was Ivan Petrovich explaining, “smiling and moving his eyebrows”?
