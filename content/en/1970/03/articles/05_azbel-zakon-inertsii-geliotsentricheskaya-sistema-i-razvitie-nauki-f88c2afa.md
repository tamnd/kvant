The nature of the development of science is dramatic. But over a great historical distance, even large masks on the canvas of science merge, contradictions become invisible, and the development of science seems consistent, strictly logical and always directed in one direction. At a short distance, science is truly understandable only to professionals, but today’s decrees seem unconditionally true. Therefore, in order to see the nature of the development of science, the flourishing and this also happened - the extinction of its individual fields *), to see the drama of it, its characters, its convictions, one must sweat into that science that has already become history and is understandable to the end - it has turned from the arena of beats into a school truism. Then, perhaps, you will be able to imagine the present day of science in a new and more correct way.

It is very convenient to trace in this regard the path that led to the discovery of the law of inertia. In a school presentation, this law looks very simple and primitive, and it is not clear why the genius of Galileo, Descartes and Newton was needed to discover it.

Meanwhile, the law of inertia is one of the greatest and, in accordance with this, one of the deepest laws of physics.

One of the greatest, or it is universal, being valid for both electrons and galaxies. He was not shaken by any of the natural revolutions of the 20th century: neither the theory of relativity, nor quantum mechanics.

The reason for such stability of the law of inertia is its depth. It is therefore valid for all objects without exception, because it is associated not with a specific property of objects, but with a fundamental property of space and time: with

But let's return to the law of inertia. It's not just not obvious. He is the first crazy law in the history of science. The afterlifer has never seen uniform motion on Earth without the action of an external force. Absolutely all experiments, without a single exception, convince us: without forces there is no movement. It is no coincidence that before Galileo it was believed that speed is proportional to the acting force. And this is the correct law for the usual speeds of that time. But it’s just very unrestrained! What does the coefficient of proportionality in it depend on? Generations of scientists can work (and are working!) to study this coefficient, especially since with increasing speed the law itself is conditioned, the very dependence between the speed of movement and the applied force. And it is not surprising: after all, the force lies, strictly speaking, outside the boundaries of mechanics (when friction occurs, the body is released).

But how can one manage to remove this always approaching force? How to abandon absolute value and the natural path of ever new, ever more subtle and complex experiments?

The answer of history is unexpected and received. The law of inertia, like all the basic laws of mechanics - Newton's laws, was born not on Earth, but in heaven. And who knows when it would be open if our sky, like the sky of Venus, was always covered with clouds? Moreover, the law of inertia was discovered as a result of an error! Galileo, who discovered the law, believed that by inertia a body can only move in a circle!

To understand how this happened, we will have to follow the history, which lasted 25 centuries, of the formation of the heliocentric system, which was opposed by such geneticists as Aristotle and Archimedes. Let's try to present in modern language - and therefore, inevitably changing somewhat - the arguments of the disputants.

The Earth was first deprived of immobility back in the fifth century BC. This was done by Philolaus, one of Pythagoras’ students. He believed that the Earth, together with the Sun and planets, revolves around a certain central fire.

A century later, Philolaus had a brilliant opponent - Aristotle *). He responded to Philolaus's hypothesis as contradicting the existence of fixed stars. After all, if the Earth is moving, all the stars should appear to be moving to an observer on Earth! It took 23 centuries to refute this objection - 3 of them after the death of Copernicus!

In the 3rd century BC. Aristarchus of Samosko proposed a system identical to the future Copernican system: the Earth and all the planets rotate in circles around the motionless Sun. Archimedes and Apollonius of Perga became opponents of Aristarchus, or Aristarchus’ system contradicts astronomical observations - in the 2nd century BC. this was strictly proven by Hipparchus. Both scientists, Aristarchus and Hipparchus, were both right and wrong: the Earth and the planets revolve around the Sun, but not in circles, but in ellipses! It would seem to be true
