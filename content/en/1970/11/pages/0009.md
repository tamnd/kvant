Let us take an arbitrary point $A$ (Fig. 7) inside the sphere. Let's imagine a narrow cone that comes from point $A$, cutting out two small spherical areas $\Delta S_1$ and $\Delta S_2$ on the surface of the sphere. If $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the distances from point $A$ to these sections, then $\frac{\Delta S_1}{\Delta S_2} = \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2}$.

Since the sphere is charged uniformly, the charges of the areas are proportional to their areas. Therefore, $\frac{q_1}{q_2} = \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2}$, and the fields created at point $A$ by these sections cancel each other out:

$$\frac{E_1}{E_2} = \frac{\frac{q_1}{r_1^2}}{\frac{q_2}{r_2^2}} = 1.$$

In this way, the entire sphere can be divided into pairs of sections. Therefore, the field at point $A$ is zero.

The idea of ​​plum lines made it possible to obtain the same result easier and faster.
