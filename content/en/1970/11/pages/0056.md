DEVELOP
SPATIAL
IMAGINATION

N. G. Fedin

Exams often offer stereometric problems, for which the most important thing is to imagine how the figures are located in space. Foreign messages suggest ideas for solving planimetric problems. In this article we will talk about some questions and tasks that should, to some extent, interest high school students and help them expand the horizons of their spatial imagination.

Crossbreeding primo

1. Let's draw two crossing primaries $a$ and $b$. On figure 1 their image is given.
Are you surprised that the primes are shown parallel? In vain. This is a correct drawing, although not visual. It is known that crossing primes $a$ and $b$ can be placed in parallel planes $a$ and $b$ so that the prime $a$ lies in the plane $\alpha$, and the straight line $b$ lies in $\beta$, and then project them onto the plane $\gamma$, intersecting the planes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (Fig. 2). In this plane we will obtain images $a_1$ and $b_1$ of straight lines $a$ and $b$.
