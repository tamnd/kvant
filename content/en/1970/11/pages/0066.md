By the way, “Maximum, minimum and the theorem of averages”

1. Let us denote the radius of the desired sector by $R$, the length of the arc by $L$ and the area of ​​the sector by $S$. Then $P = 2R + L$, $S = \frac{RL}{2}$. By theorem

$$4S = 2RL \leq \left(\frac{2R + L}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{P}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{P^2}{4},$$

whence $S \leq \frac{P^2}{16}$. Equality is achieved at $2R = L = \frac{P}{2}$.

2. Let us denote by $R$ the radius of the circle and by $a$ the distance from the point $A$ to the center of the circle. Then the chord $MN$ passing through the point $A$ satisfies the correspondence $xy = (R + a)(R - a)$, where $x = MA, y = AN$. The length of chord $MN$ is equal to $x + y$. We get by theorem

$$x + y \geq 2\sqrt{xy} = 2\sqrt{(R + a)(R - a)},$$

equality is achieved at $x = y$.

Consequently, the length of the chord is minimal when at point $A$ it is divided in half, i.e., perpendicular to the diameter passing through point $A$.
