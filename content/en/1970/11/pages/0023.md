Fig. 9.

speed of light propagation in glass. Let's find at what distance $b$ from the surface of the sphere a narrow beam of rays searching from the source $S$ will be collected. According to Fermat's principle, this distance is determined by the condition that the time of light propagation along a rectilinear path $SDS_1$ is equal to (the application of) the time of light propagation along an arbitrarily chosen path $SAS_1$, if only the point $A$ is close to the straight line $SDS_1$, perpendicular to the surface of the sphere.

From the point $A$ we drop the perpendicular $AB$ onto the line $SS_1$. Let's denote its length by $h$. Since we are considering a paraxial ray of rays, then $h \ll a$, $h \ll b$ and $h \ll R$. We denote the distance $DB$ as $x$. On ray $SA$ we plot the segment $SD_1 = a + x$, and on ray $AS_1$ the segment $S_1B_1 = b - x$. Then, along the trajectory $SAS_1$, the light will travel only a path $\Delta_1 = D_1A$ in the air and $\Delta_2 = AB_1$ in the glass compared to the trajectory along the straight line $SS_1$. But then, on a straight path, the light will travel a path $x - \Delta_2$ longer in the glass than in the case when it follows a path $SAS_1$. The light delay in the glass on path $x - \Delta_2$ must be the same as the delay in air on path $\Delta_1$ plus the delay in the glass on path $\Delta_2$. (At $x \leq \Delta_2$ such compensation is very impossible.)

Let us now find the paths $\Delta_1$, $\Delta_2$ and $x$.

The square of $h$ of the right triangle $SAB$, according to the Pythagorean theorem, is equal to

$$h^2 = (a + x + \Delta_1)^2 - (a + x)^2,$$

or

$$h^2 = (a + x + \Delta_1 + a + x)(a + x + \Delta_1 - a - x),$$

but $a + x + \Delta_1 - a - x = \Delta_1$, and $a + x + \Delta_1 + a + x = 2a + \Delta_1 + 2x \approx 2a$ (since $\Delta_1 \ll a$ and $x \ll a$). That's why

$$h^2 = 2a\Delta_1$$

And

$$\Delta_1 \approx \frac{h^2}{2a}.$$

Similarly, from the right triangle $ABS_1$ we find that

$$\Delta_2 \approx \frac{h^2}{2b}.$$

Finally, from the right triangle $ABC$ one can obtain the equation to determine $x$:

$$h^2 = R^2 - (R - x)^2.$$

From here

$$x \approx \frac{h^2}{2R}.$$

If the speed of light in air is $v_1$, and the speed of light in glass is $v_2$, then the light travels $\Delta_1$ in time $\tau_1 = \frac{\Delta_1}{v_1} = \frac{h^2}{2av_1}$, and the light travels $\Delta_2$ in glass in $\tau_2 = \frac{\Delta_2}{v_2} = \frac{h^2}{2bv_2}$.

On the segment $x$, the delay of light, caused by the fact that it travels in glass and not in air, is equal to the location of the time it takes light to travel through segment $x$ in glass and in air:

$$\tau = \frac{x}{v_2} - \frac{x}{v_1} = \frac{x}{v_1}\left(\frac{v_1}{v_2} - 1\right) = \frac{x}{v_1}(n - 1) = \frac{h^2(n - 1)}{2Rv_1}.$$

To focus rays, the travel time of light from point $S$ to point $S_1$ along all trajectories must be the same. Hence,

$$\tau_1 + \tau_2 = \tau.$$

Substituting here the expressions for $\tau_1$, $\tau_2$ and $\tau$ and then canceling by the common factor $\frac{h^2}{2}$, we obtain the equation

$$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{n}{b} = \frac{(n - 1)}{R}.$$

This equation allows, using the known assumption exponent $n$ and the radius of the spherical surface $R$, to find the image position (distance $b$) for a given distance from the source to the spherical surface. The fact that the distance $b$ does not depend on $h$ shows that all the rays in a narrow beam, distinguishing the values ​​$h$ from each other, converge at one point.

In the partial case when $a \to \infty$ (the beam consists of parallel rays), the position of the point at which the rays converge (focus) is determined by the formula

$$f = \frac{Rn}{n - 1}.$$
