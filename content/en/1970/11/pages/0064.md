⟦folio 62⟧

New America, which existed many centuries ago, or rather our modern calendar? These questions are answered by “The History of the Calendar and Chropology.”

The first chapter of the book describes primitive forms of telling time, starting with notches on a stick or knots that were tied on special laces. Here you can also find interesting information about the counting of the beginning of the year among different peoples of our planet.

The astronomical foundations of the calendar form the content of the second chapter. It examines in detail the three main units of time: the solar day, the lunar month and the solar year, which form the foundation of any calendar system.

The third chapter, devoted to solar calendars, begins with their mathematical theory. It contains a detailed description of various calendars, starting with the ancient Egyptian calendar and ending with the calendar of the French Revolution of the late 18th century, developed by a special commission led by Gilbert Romm (1750-1785).

The book (chapters IV and V) gives a detailed introduction to the lunar, lunisolar calendars and the calendars of the peoples of Asia.

Until recently, it was believed that the birthplace of astronomy was the countries of the Middle East, Ancient China and India. However, in recent decades, archeology has discovered a new cultural center located in the New World. They found that in the late Yucatan, which was once inhabited by Mayan Indians, there was a distinctive culture; Astronomy, associated with practical needs, received special development among this people. Please, for the first time in literature, the author devotes such a significant place to the Mayan calendar and chronology (Chapter VI), introducing readers in detail to the calendar systems used.

A description of the history of the calendar in Russia and the USSR, starting with the ancient Slavs, is brought by the author to the present day. The origin of the seven-day week is no longer clogged here.

The book ends with a detailed story about the World Calendar. Analyzing the shortcomings of the current calendar, the author cites various projects for its reform, including the draft World Calendar, considered by the UN Social and Economic Council.

Chropology and some calendar eras are described in the last chapter. The appendix contains various calendars and a lot of interesting materials, including an “eternal” timesheet calendar for determining the day of the week of any calendar date.

Despite the small volume of the book, the author managed to highlight and systematize a large range of issues related to the “calendar gap”.

The task set by the author - to create “... a short encyclopedic reference book on letonics and calendars” - has been completed, and this book is of undoubted interest to a wide range of readers.

L. S. Khrenov
