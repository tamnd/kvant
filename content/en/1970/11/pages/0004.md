Electrostatics on the tongue
power lines

Generally, electrostatics is studied this way: they write Coulomb’s law for the force of interaction between two exact charges $q_1$ and $q_2$: $F = \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$ (r is the distance between the charges) - and then, with the help of sometimes simple and sometimes complex mathematical calculations, they obtain all sorts of consequences of this law, considering that the charge of any body can be represented as the perfection of properly distributed exact charges.

But often an accurate mathematical analysis of the electric field and charge distribution turns out to be difficult. Then simple messages come to the rescue - symmetry and patterns of field lines. Of course, they cannot give as complete a picture as mathematical calculations, but they are visual and help to “see” and predict what features the electric field will have and how charges will interact.

What are field lines

Let's take the exact charge $q$. This charge creates an electric field in the surrounding space, which is completely determined if at each point in space the direction of the field is given - the force that would act on an exact unit positive charge if it were placed at that point. In order to visually represent the electric field throughout space, you can try to represent it by a variety of vectors, for example, at different points in space so that each of them shows the direction of the field (its magnitude and direction) at the point from which it emerges. However, it immediately becomes obvious that this method of depicting the field is unsuccessful: individual arrows, overlapping each other, create a confusing picture, which will need to be understood.

Faraday proposed that the field be represented by lines of force. These are lines, the direction of which at each point coincides with the direction of the field at this point. To accurately charge, drawing field lines is quite simple. These are straight lines (rays) passing through the point at which the charge is located.
