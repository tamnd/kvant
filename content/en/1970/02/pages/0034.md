The barometer, invented by the Italian Torricelli, applied to the study of the atmosphere, discovered the law according to which the density of atmospheric air decreases with increasing altitude. This law, called the barometric formula, states the same thing: the density of each of the gases that make up the atmosphere kills along with the increase in altitude in geometric projection.

[This law can also be expressed mathematically, in the form of a formula.

Suppose that at some height $h_0$ above the Earth, each cubic centimeter contains $n_0$ molecules of some gas. At some other, higher altitude $h$ there will, of course, be fewer of the same molecules in one cubic centimeter, for example $n$. Then the barometric formula can be written as follows:

$$\lg \frac{n_0}{n} = A mg (h - h_0)^*.$$ (1)

*) If $k$ is the number of “steps” for which we rise to the height $h$, and $\Delta h$ is the height of the “step”, then $h = h_0 + (k - 1) \Delta h$, and $n = n_0 \left( \frac{1}{q} \right)^{k - 1}$, where $\frac{1}{q}$ is the denominator of the progression, which is the density of the gas ($q > 1$). From these two formulas, eliminating $k$, we find that

$$\lg \frac{n_0}{n} = \frac{\lg q}{\Delta h} (h - h_0).$$ (*)

A similar formula can be written for another gas; we provide the quantities relative to it with a prime:

$$\lg \frac{n'}{n'} = \frac{\lg q'}{\Delta h'} (h' - h_0').$$ (**)

It turns out to be very important that if the densities of the first and second gases decreased by the same number of times, that is, $\frac{n_0}{n} = \frac{n'}{n'}$, then the ratio of the height differences at which this will happen, $\frac{h - h_0}{h' - h_0'}$, is exactly equal to the inverse ratio of the mass of the molecules of this gas in question, $g$ - the acceleration of gravity.

It is immediately clear that the left side of the equality is the ratio of the numbers of the part per unit volume (though under the logarithm sign), and the right side is the difference in heights. This means that if the heights form an argumentative progression, then the numbers of the part form a geometric progression.

Let us choose a height difference $h$ - $h_0$ such that the number of part $n$ at height $h$ is half as large as the number of part $n_0$ at height $h_0$. Then formula (1) will take the form

$$\lg 2 = A mg (h - h_0).$$ (2)

In the table of logarithms it is easy to find that $\lg 2 = 0,30103$, so

$$0,30103 = A mg (h - h_0).$$ (3)
