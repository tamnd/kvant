⟦folio 35⟧

The coincidence of two numbers that were obtained by completely different methods is the best proof of the correctness of all the assumptions made. This means that molecules and atoms really exist, and are not just a fiction convenient for chemists. Even those who for a long time and stubbornly did not want to admit the existence of atoms were forced to make this conclusion.

... The age-old dispute between the parties and opponents of atoms thus ended with the victory of the supporters of the atomic theory. And now we can confidently say that all things in the world - water, stones, plants, animals, air, iron, etc., etc. - all of this consists of tiny atoms invisible to the eye.

OBJECTIVES FOR THE ARTICLE
"HOW ATOM WAS WEIGHED"

1. Using the data given in the article, find at what altitude the pressure decreases by half. Air is lighter than oxygen in the ratio 28.8:32.

2. $P_0$ - air pressure at sea level, $P_1$ - at altitude $h$. What is the air pressure at altitudes: $2h$, $3h$, $nh$? Assume that the air temperature and the acceleration of gravity do not change with height, and therefore the constant $A$ does not depend on height.

3. Find the height above the Earth's surface where the air pressure is 0.25 and 0.125 atmospheric. Draw a curve of air pressure versus altitude.

LOGICAL PROBLEM

Let us conventionally assume that if a person does not eat for seven days or sleep for seven days, he will die. Let a person not eat or sleep for a week. What should he do first by the end of the seventh day: go to bed or sleep in order to stay alive. (Despite its humorous nature, the problem has a strict and unique solution.)

QUESTIONS
IN PHYSICS

1. Why is it that when you go swimming on a hot day, you enter the water and the water feels colder than the air, but when you get out, it feels the opposite?

2. Why does the refrigerator come and thaw from time to time?

3. The box containing the mouse is thrown vertically upward. When is the mouse in a state of weightlessness if there is no air resistance?

4. Two identical pans with equal amounts of water were placed on the stove at the same temperature. After some time, boiling water from the kettle should be poured into one pan. In which pan will the water boil first?

4a. From the pan with more water, add so much to the second pan that they again contain equal amounts of water. In which pan will the water boil first?
