The word "coherence" literally means coherence, connectedness. The concept of coherence plays an important role and is widely used in physics.

The overall light wave is not monochromatic. The fact is that the light that we see - sunlight, light from fluorescent lamps or incandescent lamps - is emitted by a large number of individual independent emitters (atoms), each of which emits light without any connection with whether its neighbors emit or do not emit light. As a result of such chaotic emission, the light waves that make up the radiation we observe are not coherent with each other, and the resulting light wave is not monochromatic. It is the non-monochromaticity and incoherence of light that translates into the concentrations of light energy in the beam cord (hence the impossibility of constructing the “hyperbolus of engineer Garin”).

We know that light is emitted by atoms. It is atoms that are those individual independent emitters that we just talked about. At the atomic level, representing light radiation as a continuous wave of light is unfair. Atom can absorb or emit light only in the form of certain portions of light energy - light quanta. The quantum energy value is $\varepsilon = hv$, where $h = 6,6 \cdot 10^{-34} \text{ дж}/\text{сцц}$ is Planck’s constant, and $v$ is the frequency of light.

The energy of visible light quanta will range from $2,5 \cdot 10^{-19} \text{ дж}$ to $5 \cdot 10^{-19} \text{ дж}$. Although this energy is very small, the quantum nature of light is significantly manifested in the interaction of light with matter. At the same time, light has not lost its luminous wave nature, which manifests itself in all those phenomena where physical relationships are important (the addition of waves, the formation of light beams, etc.). Thus, light has its stable wave and corpuscular, that is, properties inherent in particles.

So, micro-receivers of light are atoms, which under normal conditions produce a flow of incoherent radiation quanta. It is obvious that the radiation is emitted by atoms that have some excess energy, the so-called excited atoms. But an excited atom is unstable; it can spontaneously emit radiation quanta without any external reason. The spontaneous emission active occurs by chance. Such spontaneous emission is irregular and chaotic in nature. It is incoherent. But the possibility of emitting light energy is not limited to spontaneous processes. It must be remembered that the internal energy of atoms can only take on certain specific values. As they say, an atom can only be on one energy or another. Transitions of an atom from one level to another are accompanied by a change in its internal energy. When transitioning from a lower energy level to an upper one, an atom absorbs energy; when transitioning from an upper to a lower energy level, an atom releases energy. These transitions are therefore accompanied by either absorption or emission during the transition from energy level $E_1$ to level $E_2$, given by

$$v = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{h}.$$

In addition to spontaneous transitions of atoms from an upper energy level to a lower one, there is an amazing phenomenon of stimulated radiation, which is described by A. Einstein.
