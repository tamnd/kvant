But with all this, atoms remained only a certain idea in people’s heads. Not only did no one see them, because they were very small, but no one knew how small they were, what the masses of the atoms were, how many atoms there were in this or that body. It cannot be said that attempts are not made to somehow find out all this.

But these attempts did not lead to convincing results. Some scientists have even become confident that nothing can be learned about atoms and molecules, because they don’t really exist. These scientists believed that atoms are something like meridians and parallels on a geographical map: they are convenient to use, but in reality they do not exist in nature.

Physicists, however, did not lose hope of proving the reality of atoms and molecules, weighing them, counting their number, determining their sizes.

In this excerpt from the book “Atoms, Electrons, Nuclei” by the outstanding Soviet theoretical physicist Matvei Petrovich Bronstein, he talks about how for the first time it was possible to measure the mass of atoms and even count them. This book, published in 1935 in a small edition (only 10,000 copies), has long become a bibliographic rarity.
