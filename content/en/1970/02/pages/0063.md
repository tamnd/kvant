Not all instructions can be considered successful; Some of them are not advice on how to look for solutions, but a condensed plan of the entire solution, and if the plan is clear, then all that remains is to carry out very extensive calculations. In many cases, it would be more appropriate to ask the reader a leading question rather than immediately recommending “consider this” or “do this.”
In the preface, the authors write that the theoretical introductions that are presented to the chapters of the problem book “must either be read from beginning to end, or not read at all.” Unfortunately, in many cases it is better to recommend the second second to another reader. The introduction to some chapters (for example, 7, 9, 23) is completely unsuccessful, or it contains fragmentary information, often debatable and not corresponding to the material of stable textbooks.
A number of other defects can be noted. The solutions to some problems are not the best. For example, read problem 6.10 “head-on”, following the rules for multiplying a multi-digit number by a single-digit number; the solution to Problem 13.13 is greatly reduced if we analyze its conditions and note that for all admissible x the inequality cos x>0 must hold. There are inaccuracies and typos in the book. So, the answer to problem 18.13 should be: 120+90√2 km; in the driving solution of problem 2.9, it is essential that the points A and C around which the rotation is made are the vertices of the acute angles of the triangle (we strongly recommend that the reader figure out where in the solution this is implicitly used!), the condition of problem 12.7 is not precisely formulated - it is necessary to additionally suggest that aB+bA≠0.

However, it hardly makes sense to give here a complete list of individual shortcomings. The young reader, we hope, understands that creating a book is a very complex matter, and it is not always possible, despite the successful authors, to do everything perfectly in the first edition.

The overwhelming majority of the problems included in the book were taken by the authors from versions of written works offered in different years of the advanced exams in mathematics at Moscow University, as well as in a number of other universities in Moscow. It is very good that these problems, equipped with instructions and solutions, become accessible to the broad masses of mathematics lovers and are included in school practice. But at the same time, it would be appropriate to accurately indicate the source of knowledge, since these tasks are the fruit of the collective creativity of the examination commissions. Anyone who has ever been involved in composing a problem knows how much work and how long it takes to create a new original problem. By the way, these links would also be useful to the reader - he could get an idea from them about the level of requirements for applicants to various universities).

RELEASED BY THE MSU PUBLISHING HOUSE, the book “Introduction to Electronics” by candidate of physical and mathematical sciences A. M. Khazen is addressed to schoolchildren who are interested in physics, mathematics and especially electronics and radio engineering.

The book is intended for the general reader and at the same time written on a good physical level. The clarity of the presentation and the original examples given are combined with sufficient rigor. It can be said with certainty that for a schoolchild who reads this book carefully, many of the understandings of electronics will become clear.

Young readers will be interested to know that a fundamental vacuum diode can be used to create an electric current generator operating from an external source of the body, how a linear electron accelerator and a traveling wave tube are similar and different, what an explosive magnetic generator is, etc.

The book highlights key issues for various classes of electronic devices. Despite the large number of specific devices described in the book, it is not overloaded with examples.

The books can be recommended as a guide for schoolchildren who have chosen technical disciplines or physics as their future specialization.
