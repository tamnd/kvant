⟦folio 47⟧

In this section, continuing from issue to issue, we place several tasks, usually quite difficult. The best solutions to these problems, submitted by readers, will be published in the journal in a few months.

In mathematics

M6. There is a clock in front of you. How many hand positions are there that cannot be used to determine the time unless you know which hand is the hour hand and which hand is the minute hand?

(It is believed that the position of each of the arrows can be determined accurately, but following how the arrows move cannot.)

M7. a, b, c are the sides of the triangle. Prove that

$$\frac{a}{b+c-a} + \frac{b}{c+a-b} + \frac{c}{a+b-c} \geq 3.$$

S. Berkolaiko

M8. Two people play this game. From a pile of 25 matches, everyone takes one, two or three matches in turn. The winner is the one who, at the end of the game - after all the matches have been sorted out - turns out to have only matches.

Who wins when played correctly - the starter or his partner? How should he play to win? How will the answer change if we assume that the one who takes an odd number of matches wins?

Explore this game in the general case when there are 2n+1 matches and you are allowed to take any number of matches from 1 to m.

M9. Consider the following properties of a tetrahedron (we call an arbitrary triangular paramid a tetrahedron):

a) all faces are equal in size;
b) each edge is equal to the opposite one;
b) all faces are equal;
d) the centers of the circumscribed and inscribed sphere coincide;
d) the sums of plane angles at each vertex of the tetrahedron are equal.

Prove that all these properties are equivalent.

Try to find other equivalent properties of the tetrahedron.

M10. Four circles, the centers of which are the vertices of a convex quadrilateral, completely cover this quadrilateral. Prove that from them it is possible to choose three circles that cover a triangle with vertices at the centers of these circles.

G. Galperin
