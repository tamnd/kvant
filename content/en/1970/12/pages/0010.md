Let us arbitrarily mark the points $A_3$, $B_3$, $C_3$ (Fig. 12). We construct points $P_1$ and $P_2$ and draw a straight line $P_1P_2$ (it turns out to be parallel to the straight line $A_1A_2$). We carry out a straight line $A_3C_3$. We find the point $P_3$ of intersection $A_3C_3$ and $P_1P_2$. We are holding $B_3P_3$. The intersection point $B_3P_3$ with $D_1D_2$ is the desired one.

Depicting flat sections of polyhedra requires the use of only two rules:

1) If planes $\beta'$ and $\gamma'$ intersect along line $l'$, and plane $\alpha'$ intersects their corresponding lines $b'$ and $c'$, then $b'$ and $c'$ either intersect at a point lying on $l'$ or are parallel to $l'$.

2) If planes $\beta'$ and $\gamma'$ are parallel, and plane $\alpha'$ intersects their corresponding lines $b'$ and $c'$, then $b'$ and $c'$ are parallel.

We draw images of linear sections $RQ$ and $QP$. The point of their intersection must lie on the line $A_2B_2$, otherwise the lines $PQ$ and $QR$ are arbitrary. Next we carry out $RS \parallel QP$ and $PS \parallel QR$.

On figure 13 the lines $PQ$ and $QR$ are drawn so that the point $Q$ falls outside the segment $A_2B_2$. Mark the points $T \equiv PQ \times A_2D_2$ and $U \equiv QR \times A_1A_2$ and connect $U$ and $T^*$). We carry out $RS \parallel QP$ and $PS \parallel QR$. It is clear that the point $S$ must lie on the line $C_1D_1$. Example 6. Image of a plane section of a quadrangular paramid (Fig. 14).

We carry out links $PQ$ and $QR$. From point $R$ it is necessary to draw a section line along the rear edge. The planes of the front and rear faces intersect along a straight line $S'X'$. The image of this line can be easily obtained by constructing the point $X \equiv AB \times CD$. The traces of the cutting plane $\alpha'$ on the front and back faces must intersect at $S'X'$. We find point $T \equiv PQ \times SX$ and draw $TR$. We mark the segment of this straight line $RU$, which is the interior of the triangle $SCD$. And $AB$ represents the line of intersection of the front and bottom edges. We build point $Y \equiv AB \times PQ$ and find point $V \equiv AD \times UY$.

*) The notation $M \equiv AB \times CD$ means: “M is the point of intersection of the lines $AB$ and $CD$”, and $m \equiv A \times B$ - “m is the line defining the points $A$ and $B$”.
