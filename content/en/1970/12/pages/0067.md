COLLECTOR'S CORNER

Alexander Stepanovich Popov (1859-1905) - an outstanding Russian physicist, inventor. A number of postage stamps are dedicated to him. The first two stamps with his portraits (by figure artist N. Kachur), shown in the photograph, were issued in October 1925 on the 20th anniversary of the scientist’s death. In December 1927, one of these stamps was overprinted “8 kopecks.”

In 1945, the 50th anniversary of the invention of the radio was celebrated by A. S. Popov. In honor of this event, a series of three stamps (figure by I. Dubasov) was issued (July 1945). On two of them, Popov is depicted with the world’s first radio receiver, which he invented in 1895, and on the third, his portrait is reproduced.

In 1949, three stamps were issued for Radio Day (based on the drawings of the artist I. Dubasov). On two of them, A. S. Popov is depicted against the background of a radio receiver he invented in 1895, and on the third, he demonstrates a radio installation to Admiral S. O. Makarov in the world (figure after a painting by the artist I. S. Sorokin).

In 1955, the 60th anniversary of the invention of the radiotelegraph by A. S. Popov was celebrated. To commemorate this event, two stamps were issued with portraits of A. S. Popov (based on drawings by I. Dubasov).

Two stamps are issued for the 100th anniversary of the birth of A. S. Popov (one by figure N. Kruglova, the other by figure S. Novsky). The first of them, in addition to the portrait of A. S. Popov, depicts an episode of the rescue of fishermen in the Baltic Sea.

The second stamp also depicts a portrait of A. S. Popov and the Moscow radio mast, which emits radio waves carrying the word “PEACE” in different languages.

Stamps dedicated to A.S. Popov were issued not only in the Soviet Union, but also abroad. The photo shows stamps with portraits of A. S. Popov, issued in Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia and Romania.
