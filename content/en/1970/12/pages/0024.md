What is the friction force?

So, let's return to the calculation of siliprene acting on a body moving at a relatively low speed $u$ through a rarefied gas, the temperature of which is $T$. It is convenient to consider the body to be at rest and the gas to be moving at speed $-u$. Let us select an infinitesimal area $^*$ $\Delta S$ (Fig. 3) on the surface of the body. The momentum of a gas molecule, if it collides elastically with a body at a point on the visible area, will change to $2P_n$, where $P_n$ is the projection of the molecule’s momentum onto the normal to the surface of the body. This means that a gas molecule, when colliding with a body, gives it an impulse equal to $2P_n$. Now our task is to calculate the total impulse transmitted by the gas to the body per unit time. The change in momentum per unit time is the force (in this case, acting from the gas on the body). To calculate the total transmitted momentum, it is necessary, firstly, to take into account all the molecules falling per unit time onto a given area, and secondly, to sum over all areas.

*) See regarding the “infinitesimal platform” the article by N. Ya. Vilenkin “Secrets of Infinity” («Kvant» No. 3, 1970).

A similar operation was carried out in the article by Ya. A. Smorodinsky “Ideal Gas” («Kvant» No. 10). Here we will use ready-made results. The gas pressure on a wall at rest is equal to $1/3$ nmv², where $n$ is the number of gas molecules per unit volume, $m$ is the mass of the molecule and $v^2$ is the arithmetic mean of the squares of the velocities of the molecules. If the body ends ($u = 0$), the number of blows to the right and left is on average the same, and therefore no impulse is transferred to the body. At $u \neq 0$ this is not the case. Let the body move. In this case, the speed of molecules hitting the front is $u$ greater, and the speed of molecules hitting the rear is $u$ less. Therefore, the force acts on section $\Delta S$ of the moving plane

$$F = (P_{\text{спереди}} - P_{\text{задди}}) \Delta S = \frac{1}{3} nm \Delta S \left[ (v + u)^2 - (v - u)^2 \right] = \frac{2}{3} nmvu \Delta S.$$

Recalling that $v = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$ (see the article “Ideal Gas”), we obtain

$$F = \frac{2}{3} n \sqrt{3kTm} u \Delta S.$$

For a body of arbitrary shape we must obtain the formula

$$F = \beta Sn \sqrt{mkT} u.$$

Here $S$ is the surface area of ​​the body, and $\beta$ is a numerical coefficient, depending on the shape of the body. It takes into account that molecules enter one area perpendicular to the surface, and others at an angle.

If we use Clapeyron’s law $PV = NkT$ ($N = nV$ is the total number of the gas part in the volume $V$), then we can express the friction force through the pressure $P$ and temperature $T$ of the gas:

$$F_{\text{Tp}} = \beta SP \sqrt{\frac{m}{kT}} u.$$
