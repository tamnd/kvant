4. Sections of polyhedra

Now we will consider images encountered when studying stereometry.

Example 4. At figure 11, a the flat shape of a triangular feature is depicted.

It is “constructed” very simply: arbitrary points $A_3$, $B_3$, $C_3$ are taken on the edges of the feature and connected by segments of the feature. At figure 11, $\delta$ the same “method” is applied to the construction of a section of a quadrangular feature.

Figure 11 is correct, but on figure 11, $\delta$ there was a mistake (a very common one!). In fact, the plane is determined by the third point. Therefore, when depicting the plane shape of a triangular feature, we can take points on its edges arbitrarily. You cannot do this with a quadrangular sign. Noting the pro-

arbitrarily the points $A_3$, $B_3$, $C_3$, we have already defined the cutting plane, and the point of its intersection with the four edges of the recognition cannot be taken arbitrarily.

We will show you how to correctly find the point of intersection of a cutting plane with four edges.

The plane passing through the edges $^*$) $A'_1A'_2$ and $C'_1C'_2$ intersects the plane section $A'_3B'_3C'_3D'_3$ along the diagonal $A'_3C'_3$, and the plane passing through the edges $B'_1B'_2$ and $D'_1D'_2$, along the diagonal $B'_3D'_3$. The intersection of these two planes is parallel to the edge of the plane. Consequently, in all plane sections of the line the points of intersection of the diagonal line lie on a straight line parallel to the edge of the line.
