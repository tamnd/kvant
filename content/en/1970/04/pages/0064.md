1. The second cart does not move vertically. This means that the force $T$ tension (Fig. 1) is balanced by the attraction of the cart to the Earth: $T=m_2g$. The same force $T$ imparts to the first cart an acceleration $a$ equal to

$$a = \frac{T}{m_1} = \frac{m_2}{m_1} g.$$

Fig. 1

With acceleration $a$ all systems as a whole move under the influence of force $F$, therefore from the equation of motion of the system (Newton’s second law) $F=(m_1+m_2+m_3)a$ is found

$$F = \frac{m_2(m_1+m_2+m_3)}{m_1} g.$$

2. Let us write down the equations of motion of the feather and the second cart: $T=m_1a_1$ and $m_2g-T=m_2a_1$, where $a_1$ is the magnitude of the acceleration of the carts. Solving these equations together, we find

$$a_1 = \frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2} g \quad \text{и} \quad T = \frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2} g.$$

Voldya Merkulov received a special prize for solving this problem. He solved the problem in general form, writing down the equations of motion of all bodies and then setting either the vertical acceleration $a_1$ of the second cart or the acceleration $a$ of the third cart equal to zero.

Problem 2

Let us denote: $c_2$ is the heat of water, $t$ is the temperature of the calorimeter and its contents in a state of equilibrium, $\Delta m$ is the change in the amount of ice ($\Delta m$ is positive if the amount of ice has increased, and negative if the amount of ice has increased $Q_2$, equal to $-m_3c_3t_3$).

Heat exchange in a calorimeter can be considered as follows: water and calorimeter cooled to the melting temperature of ice, while an amount of body $Q_1$ equal to $(m_2c_2+m_1c_1)t_2$ was released, and the ice heated up to the melting temperature, absorbing an amount of body $Q_2$ equal to $-m_3c_3t_3$.

If $Q_1=Q_2$, then thermal equilibrium will be established. Otherwise, the body quantity remains $Q_0=Q_1-Q_2$. At $Q_0>0$ this body is exposed to melting ice, and at $Q_0<0$ part of the water will freeze.

If $Q_0>m_3l$, then all the ice will melt and the mixture will heat up to temperature $t$, which is found from the heat balance equation, $t = \frac{Q_0-m_3l}{m_1c_1+(m_2+m_3)c_2}$. Similarly, we find that at $Q_0<-m_2l$, all the water will freeze and the mixture will cool to a temperature of $t = \frac{Q_0+m_2l}{m_1c_1+(m_2+m_3)c_3}$.

Substituting numerical values, we find that $Q_0=87\text{ ккал}, m_2l=70,7\text{ ккал}, a m_3l=141\text{ ккал}$. Thus, $-m_2l<Q_0<m_3l$. This means that the calorimeter will contain a mixture of ice and water at a temperature of $0^\circ\text{C}$. Since $\Delta m=0,11\text{ ккал}$, the ice will be 2.11 kcal, and the water will be 0.89 kcal.
