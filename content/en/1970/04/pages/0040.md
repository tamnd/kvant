6) Putting $y = \frac{2x - 1}{2x - x^2 - 4}$, we obtain a quadratic (with respect to $x$) equation $yx^2 + 2(1-y)x + 4y - 1 = 0$, the discriminant of which must be non-negative: $(1-y)^2 + y(4y - 1) \geq 0$, since $x$ is a real number, whence
$$-\frac{1 + \sqrt{13}}{6} \leq y \leq \frac{-1 + \sqrt{13}}{6}.$$
Therefore, the smallest value of $y$ is $-\frac{1 + \sqrt{13}}{6}$; the corresponding value of $x$ is, as can be easily calculated, $\frac{1 + \sqrt{13}}{2}$.

Problem solutions
1. Range of acceptable values ​​(OD3):
$$-\frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi,$$
$k = 0, \pm 1, \dots$

Let's put
$$y = \sqrt{\cos x} - 4 \sqrt[4]{2} \sqrt[4]{\cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)} + 5 \sqrt[4]{2},$$
Then
$$y^2 - 2 \sqrt{\cos x} y - \sin x = 0,$$
where
$$y = \sqrt{\cos x} \pm \sqrt{\sqrt{2} \cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)},$$
i.e. we have
$$\sqrt{\cos x} - 4 \sqrt[4]{2} \sqrt[4]{\cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)} + 5 \sqrt[4]{2} = \sqrt{\cos x} \pm \sqrt[4]{2} \sqrt{\cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)}.$$

Putting $z = \sqrt[4]{\cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)}$, we get $-4z + 5 = \pm z^2$. After the equation has roots $z_1 = 1, z_2 = -5, z_3 = 2 \pm i$. Roots $z_2, z_3, z_4$, very/no, construction.
From $z_1 = 1$ follows $\cos \left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 1$. Thus, $x = \frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi, k = 0, \pm 1$.
