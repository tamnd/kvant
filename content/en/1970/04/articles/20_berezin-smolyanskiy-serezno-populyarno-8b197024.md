SERIOUSLY
POPULAR

In this article we publish a solution to books that are of interest to the reader of our magazine.
In the future, our field of vision will include educational literature, books generally classified as “popular,” and other publications. Of course, this “classification” is more than arbitrary, but we are interested in one thing—that all these books be accessible and interesting to a wide range of our readers.

This review is dedicated to a group of books published under the general title “Popular Lectures”*). There are currently 48 issues in this series.

The authors of issues in this series, as a rule, are well-known mathematicians, and the topics covered in the issue generally coincide with the area of ​​scientific interests.

The books in this series are called lectures. This is not accidental—most of the issues are written on the basis of lectures that the author once gave to schoolchildren.

The series is called popular. A popular book is not necessarily an entertaining book or a book for easy reading. Science does not need ornamentation; it is attractive in itself. Therefore, any of the issues of “Popular Lectures” is traditionally intended for server reading,

Everything indicates that the authors of books try to assimilate them as much as possible during reprints. As a rule, this means that the author tries to further saturate each book with scientific information, leaving it no less accessible.

With this we can consider that our first acquaintance with the serial book “Popular Lectures on Mathematics” consists. Let us now get acquainted, so to speak, personally with five books that were published in 1969-70, the covers of which are depicted here. Each of them has its own individual characteristics.

Let's start with the book:

N. N. Vorobyov. "Fibonacci numbers". 112 pages. Price 17 kopecks.

Elementary mathematics is rich in developmental tasks and puzzles that turn out to be closely related to each other. Forming new connections after small and large open mathematicians, this complex formation imperceptibly turns into a serious mathematical theory. This is exactly what was done with the problem of the Intayan mathematician Leonardo of Pisa on the origin of Fibonacci, published by him for the first time in 1202. Growing out of the famous “rabbit problem”, which is more than seven hundred and fifty years old, the theory of Fibonacci numbers remains one of the most fascinating chapters of elementary mathematics. Moreover, this ancient problem turned out to be “closely related” not only to such a science as number theory, but even to the very young theory of the concept of optimal solution.

And at first the task read like this:

How many pairs of rabbits does one pair give away in one year?

《Someone placed a pair of rabbits in a certain place, surrounded on all sides by a wall, to find out how many pairs of rabbits would be born during the year, if the nature of rabbits is such that after a month a pair of rabbits gives birth to another pair, and rabbits are born from the second place after their birth...》

How this problem is solved and what mathematical consequences it leads to, you will learn by reading this book.

The book is fully accessible to schoolchildren in grades 8-10.

By applying several conditions, you will have the opportunity to get acquainted with the reflection of mathematics, the application of which in practice promises a great future for it. Each of the reincarnations of Fibonacci problems is bright in itself.

Now about the next book:

S. V. Fomin. "Number systems". 48 pages. Price 7 kopecks.

It is extremely easy to read. However, the ease of presentation did not allow the author to tell in a completely sincere and very interesting way everything necessary to create in the reader an idea of ​​the number system.

First, it talks about the applications of the decimal system, to which the reader has become accustomed during his time at school, and therefore is inclined to accept these applications as they should. Then it talks about

Let's move on to the book:
Y. S. Dubnov. "Errors in geometric proofs".
64 pages. Price 9 kopecks.

Ya. S. Dubnov’s book contains a number of erroneous proofs and data analysis. In one problem, an error in the proof emerges, in others, both the inconsistency of the proof and the fallacy of statements. It is very possible that you will not be able to open yourself to it after you become acquainted with the proof of the theorem “All triangles are isosceles”, given on pages 14-16.

The book is written in clear, engaging language. It contains subtle and elegant geometric sophistry. It is quite accessible even to 7th grade students.
