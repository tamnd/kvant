During the First World War, among French officers who came to the army as students, during the sometimes long intervals between battles, compositions on solving problems in elementary mathematics were common. The winner must find the correct and most interesting solution according to the general opinion.

Here is one such task:
Prove that a triangle is equilateral if its bissertrices are equal.
Many people tried to solve this problem, but not all found the right ways. Would you like to try your hand?

In 1939, in the second type of the 5th Moscow Mathematical Olympiad, participants were given the task: “Decompose the expression $a^{10} + a^5 + 1$ into rational integer factors.”

This problem can be easily solved if we use the theorem: The polynomial $1 + a^{3k+1} + a^{3p+2}$ is divisible without remainder by the polynomial $1 + a + a^2$ for any natural numbers $k$ and $p$.

Try to determine whether the theorem is true:
The polynomial $1 + a^{4m+1} + a^{4l+2} + a^{4k+3}$ is divisible without remainder by the polynomial $1 + 1 + a + a^2 + a^3$ for any natural numbers $k$, $l$ and $m$.
