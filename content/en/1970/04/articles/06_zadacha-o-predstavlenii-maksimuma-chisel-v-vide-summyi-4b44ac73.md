TASKS

1. Let every boy be friends with no less than $m$ girls, and every girl be friends with no more than $m$ boys ($m$ is an arbitrary natural number). Prove that in this case the conditions of the matchmaking theorem are satisfied.

2. Schoolchildren solved problems in a circle. It turned out that everyone solved 4 problems and each problem was solved by four schoolchildren. Prove that it is possible to organize the analysis of a problem so that each student solves exactly one problem and so that all problems are analyzed (one time).

3. A transport network is given. The set of vertices that contain an output but do not contain an input is called a network cut. The capacity of the cut $B$ is the number $c(B)$, equal to the number of blows included in $B$. If $\phi$ denotes flow, then $|\phi|$ will denote the magnitude of the flow. Prove that for any flow $\phi$ and any cut $B$ the following transformation is true: $|\phi| \leq c(B)$.

4. A cut $B$ is called minimal if its capacity $c(B)$ is the smallest among the capacities of all cuts. Using the Ford-Folkerson algorithm, deduce that the throughput of the minimum cut is equal to the maximum flow - max $|\phi| = \min c(B)$ (Ford-Folkerson theorem).

5. Check that if $\phi$ is a maximal flow, then the cut obtained by unifying all unlabeled vertices (labels are made based on the flow $\phi$ using the Ford-Falkerson method) is minimal.

6. Prove that the union and intersection of any two minimal cuts is again a minimal cut.

7. Prove that a cut of unlabeled vertices (for a maximum flow) is the union of all minimal cuts. Deduce from this that this cut does not depend on the maximum cut used to make the flows.
