Because the electron gas behaves like a glue that binds nonions, the valence of metals does not affect their crystal structure, as is the case with nonmetals. Crystals of metals can be so strongly interconnected by a gas of free electrons that the boundaries between them are almost invisible. It is very difficult to break cold metal along grain boundaries unless the boundaries are weakened by impurities. This “promiscuity” of the metal bond leads to the fact that two pieces of metal can be connected to each other by simply pressing their clean surfaces. metallic bonding makes it possible to obtain alloys of various metals in very diverse combinations and proportions.

How does the presence of free electrons affect the strength and ductility of metals? We have already considered one effect: the formation in metals (and in some alloys, in which the sizes of the atoms do not differ too much from each other) of simple crystalline structures with densely filled planes and lines along which sliding occurs. The plasticity of metals is also explained by the fact that atoms are not directly connected to each other, but are attracted to one another due to the gas of free electrons. Therefore, sliding of one layer relative to another will occur easily.

If the sliding of the layers occurred in the complete absence of resistance, then the material would not have any rigidity at all. The solid body, however, has a well-defined stiffness, the measure of which is the shear pattern, which determines the reinforcement required for very small deformation.

Consider two fusion planes (Fig. 4). In one raft (a) the atoms in each direction are more densely packed, and, therefore, the distances between planes in the vertical direction are smaller than in raft b. It is clear that the same deformation in raft a will require less effort than in raft b. For clarity, we can say that it will take less xmlns to “climb” (in reality, this means that less force will be required to deform the electron shells). In other words, along densely packed planes the shear pattern is smaller.
