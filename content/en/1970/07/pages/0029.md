⟦folio 27⟧

Metals are opaque, shiny and comparatively hard. Metals are strong, but their shape can be changed through forging and rolling. They can also be floated and welded. Metals have good electron and thermal conductivity. They owe all this to the metal connection. The nature of this connection is that in a metal each atom is surrounded by a large number of points of the same atoms, each of which has only a few electrons in its outer electron shell, and the electron shells overlap so much that the weakly held outer electrons can no longer be attributed to any one of the atoms. While the atoms, or rather ions, remain in place, the “electron gas” moves freely among the ions, bonding them to each other like glue.

Due to the fact that electrons are free and can mix in an electric field, metals are conductors. Because free electrons absorb and then re-emit most of the light that hits them, metals are opaque and shiny. Due to the fact that free electrons can transfer thermal energy, metals have high thermal conductivity.

This article does not discuss the thermal, electrical, or optical properties of metals. I will mainly talk about their mechanical properties.

Although it has been known for several centuries that some substances have a crystalline structure, the fact that ordinary metals are crystals remained in doubt until recently. Properties such as the solidification of the melt at a very specific temperature and the presence of shiny grains on the surface of fractures spoke of a crystalline structure, but other facts seemed to indicate amorphism: molten metals after solidification took the shape of a vessel, solid metals were plate-shaped and their shape could be changed by forging, the polished surface of the metal looked completely uniform.

The key to elucidating the structure of metals was found in 1864, when the Englishman Henry Sorby developed a method for studying metals under a microscope using reflected rather than transmitted light. In addition, he owed his success to the fact that, in addition to the polished surface, he examined the metal surface treated with weak chemicals.
