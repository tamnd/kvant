A few words about the author of the solution to Hilbert's ten problems

Yuri Vladimirovich Matiyasevich was born on March 2, 1947 in Leningrad. The first person who paid attention to the boy’s outstanding mathematical abilities and encouraged him to develop an early interest in mathematics was Sofya Grigorievna Generson, a mathematics teacher at School 255 in Leningrad. Starting from class, Yuri took part in city mathematical optimids, invariably taking first place in them, and in 1961 - 1964 - in the All-Russian and Moscow optimids, where he was also among the winners. (Yuri Matiyasevich graduated from 10th grade at boarding school No. 18 at Moscow State University). From 7th to 9th grade, Yuri studied in the mathematical circle of the Leningrad Palace of Pioneers under the guidance of LOMI employee (Leningrad Branch of the Steklov Mathematical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences) N.M. Mitrofanov.

Participating as part of the USSR team in the VI International Mathematical Optimide (Moscow, 1964), Matiyasevich was awarded a 1st degree Diploma.

In 1964-1969. Yuri Matiyasevich is a student of the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics of Leningrad University.

While still in his second year, Matiyasevich completed two works on mathematical logic, which were then published in the “Reports of the USSR Academy of Sciences.” The main results of these works were supplemented by Yu. Matiyasevich at the International Mathematical Congress in Moscow in 1966.

Currently, Matiyasevich is a graduate student at the Leningrad Department of the Mathematical Institute. The mathematical creativity of Yu. Matiyasvich is a striking example of the continuity of scientific tradition. Matiyasevich's supervisor at the university and graduate school is a young but already well-known specialist in mathematical logic, Sergei Yuryevich Maslov, on whose advice Matiyasevich turned to Hilbert's tenth problem in December 1965. Teacher S. Yu. Maslova prof. Nikolai Aleksandrovich Shanin is the closest student of Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Andrei Andreevich Markov.

Thus, Yuri Matiyasevich is a representative of the “fourth generation”, headed by the outstanding Soviet mathematician Markov, of the so-called constructive direction in logic and mathematics.
