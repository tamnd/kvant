About gravity

Anyone who has read I. A. Efremov’s novel “The Andromeda Nebula” remembers the iron star - the “horror of astronomers”, which with its enormous attraction captures bodies flying past.

Even more amazing celestial lenses have to be the stars predicted by German astronomer Karl Schwarzschild. The gravitational forces near such stars are so strong that the light emitted by them cannot move beyond the immediate vicinity of the star. And a ray of light sent from afar to such a star will never be able to reach its surface. Some kind of strange gravitational mousetrap! But, despite all the necessity of such celestial bodies, the theory predicts their existence with a sufficient degree of rigor.

It would seem that the gravitational forces of the present are accustomed and harnessed, so that a person should have known everything about them that interests him a long time ago. It’s so simple: let’s put a stone on a scale and measure the force with which it is attracted to the Earth! But, having thought about the nature of such a phenomenon, we immediately find ourselves captive to unresolved issues.

In fact, why do any two bodies always attract each other and not repel under the influence of gravitational forces? Why can’t we isolate ourselves from the forces of gravity, just as a curtain separates us from the sun’s rays? What is the nature of the invisible
dim “springs” that attract bodies even very far from each other? There are many questions related to gravity that science has not yet been able to answer.

But we already know a lot. In 1687, Isaac Newton discovered the law of universal gravitation, which made it possible to understand how celestial bodies move. The next important step in understanding gravity belongs to Albert Einstein. In 1916, he created the so-called general theory of relativity, which is commonly called the theories of space, time and gravity. This theory forced scientists to shift their views to such important physical concepts as space and time. Newton believed that the properties of space and the passage of time do not depend on the surrounding bodies. Einstein proved that the heavier the body, the more the space near it is curved and the slower the time in its vicinity. Space, time and gravity turned out to be not independent of each other, but it is not easy to designate test. But its significance in modern physics is exceptionally great. Therefore, what new popular science book devoted to this topic (and there are very few such books) promises to arouse more interest.

We will talk here about some books that cover various problems of the modern theory of gravity.

A much simpler and more accessible presentation of the fundamentals of the theory of gravitation

is contained in the popular book by M. Gardner, “The Theory of the Relative Length of Millions,” published in 1966 by Atomizdat. The fifth chapter talks about the main initial postulate of the theory - the equality of the gravitational and inertial masses of any body - confirmed with a great degree of accuracy by specially performed experiments. Chapter six describes the views of modern physics on gravitational forces as a result of changes in the geometric properties of space and time under the influence of gravitational masses. Chapters (7, 9 and 10) are related to various cosmic problems related to the theory of gravity. They consider different versions of the model of the Universe and discuss in detail questions about the so-called “red shift” - a change in the color of celestial objects under the influence of their movement, which occurs at enormous speeds.

(This brochure is based on three articles by the author in the magazine “Nuka and Life” Nos. 1, 2, 3 for 1968.)

According to Einstein's model, the Universe forms a closed three-dimensional sphere that does not change over time. In the model of the Soviet scientist A. A. Friedman, the Universe is either expanding or contracting. In Lemaître’s model, the Universe at some stage replaces its development, as if frozen in immobility, and then begins to rapidly expand again.
