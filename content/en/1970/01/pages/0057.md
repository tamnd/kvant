⟦folio 55⟧

CORRESPONDENCE MATH SCHOOL

There is probably no larger school in the Soviet Union than this: after all, about ten thousand people study in it, living in different cities and villages of the country. We are talking about a correspondence mathematical school (CMS) at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University.

It was organized on the basis of the Moscow Secondary Mathematical School No. 2 in 1964 on the initiative of the famous Soviet mathematician, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. M. Gelfand, who heads the scientific council of the school. Its main goal is to help schoolchildren better master the school mathematics curriculum, develop their abilities, and teach them how to work with a book.

Once a month, schoolchildren receive assignments - brochures that outline some sections of mathematics adjacent to the school curriculum and contain many interesting problems *). Having independently studied the brochure, the student completes the test task and sends it to the ZMSH. About 500 teachers, students and graduate students of Moscow State University check and solve these works.

*) Some tasks compiled at the ZMSH were printed in the form of separate brochures from the series “Library of Physics and Mathematics Schools” (Nauka Publishing House): these are “Coordinate Method” by I. M. Gelfand, E. G. Glagoleva, A. A. Kirillova, “Functions and Graphs” by I. M. Gelfand, E. G. Glagoleva, E. E. Shnolya, “Limits” by A. A. Kirillov, “Straights and Curves” by N. B. Vasilyev and V. L. Gutenacher and others. Many of «Kvant»'s readers have seen these brochures with colored covers and drawings in the margins.

ZMSH does not have a special goal of preparing university students, but along with general assignments, ZMSH students are also sent materials to prepare for entrance exams. Almost all graduates of ZMSh after graduating from school successfully take entrance exams in mathematics in various.by. Many of them choose mathematics as their profession. For example, a quarter of second-year students at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University are recent graduates of the ZMSH.

There are 29 films in ZMSH. They are organized at pedagogical institutes and universities and work according to a general program. In addition, there is another form of ZMS work, which is called “collective student”. This is a school math circle, working under the guidance of a teacher according to the ZMS program. The tasks are divided into busy circles, then each participant completes a test, which is checked by the teacher. After this, the circle draws up one common “collective work” and sends it to the ZMS. Currently, there are about 350 such circles at the ZMS.

In 1965, the Northwestern Correspondence School of Mathematics at Leningrad University began its work. Both ZMSH - Moscow and Leningrad - maintain mutual contact and “divided” among themselves those entering the ZMSH on a territorial basis (see admission rules). Now there is a correspondence school of physics and mathematics at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, a ZPS at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University, etc.

ZMSH methodologist J.M. Rabbom
