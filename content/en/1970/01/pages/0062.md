⟦folio 60⟧

“That night, despondency reigned in the laboratory. In an attempt to obtain and identify elements 101 - the next link in the chain of artificial human preparation of chemical elements beyond uranium - we carried out many careful experiments, but they all ended in failure. Now we were finishing our last experiment, the origins of which seemed, however, unconvincing. A tiny sample of our materials preparation, even at best, could contain only one or two atoms of the elusive element 101...

We closely monitored the recording of impulses associated with the use of the camera. An hour has passed. The time was approaching dawn. The wait seemed endless. Finally this case! The pen of the beeping mechanism jumped to the middle of the scale and fell down, leaving a clear red line: it was a large initialization pulse... About an hour later, a second pulse, similar to the first, was recorded. We were now confident that we were witnessing the disintegration of two atoms of element 101 and thus had added a new element to the chemical family.**

Thus begins the story of American physicists Glenn Seaborg and Albert Ghiorzo about the creation of new chemical elements. In Russian translation, it is included in the first issue of the collection “What Physicists Are Thinking About,” which was published in 1962. Since then, this series of popular science collections has been replenished with five more issues. The first two of them were published by the State Publishing House of Physics and Mathematics Literature, the rest by the Nauka Publishing House. Here are the titles of these releases:

Issue 1. Physics of the atomic nucleus (1962, price 23 kopecks).
Issue 2. Elementary particles (1963, price 25 kopecks).
Issue 3. Elementary particles (1965, price 43 kopecks).
Issue 4. Physics of the atomic nucleus (1965, price 29 kopecks).
Issue 5. Quantum macrophysics (1967, price 62 kopecks).
Issue 6. Astrophysics (1967, price 1 rub. 06 kopecks).
All these books are from the very cutting edge of science. That’s why they are called “What Physicists Think About.” Indeed, they talk about what scientists are doing these days, and sometimes about what they are still planning to do. It’s not for nothing that the article will end in much the same way as Robert Marshak’s “Nuclear Forces”, published in four issues:

“What can be said about the cherished dream of physicists - a true theoretical understanding of nuclear forces? All we can do at the moment is to express the hope that we are on the right track... However, we are confident that someday we will be able to write the right issue."
