The lateral faces of the second paramid are equilateral right triangles, and the paramid itself is a part of the cube, cut off by a plane passing through the diagonals of the faces having a common vertex (Fig. 9). The laying of balls on the side faces of the paramid will occur on a square grid with rows parallel to the diagonals of the cube face.

It is easy to discover that we did not receive two different packages, but one, only with a different orientation. It is enough to find and remove the balls located on the edges of the triangle, and the faces of the cube will be exposed, just as by removing the balls lying along the edges of the cube, we gradually turn the cube into a triangle. This packing is called the densest cubic packing. So the crystallization of neon, argon, copper, gold, platinum, lead. The densest cubic packing has all the symmetrical elements of a cube. In particular, the third-order symmetry axes of the tetrahedron coincide with the spatial diagonals of the cube, which are also third-order symmetry axes for it. The construction of this package is based on an elementary cube of fourteen balls. Eight of them are located at the tops of the cube and six at the centers of its faces. If you carefully examine the second paramid (Fig. 8), this cube can be found at the top of the paramid. The densest cubic packing can also be considered as a set of four simple cubic lattices, shifted one into the other. With this consideration, the equality of all the balls in the pack becomes especially clear. By the very method of obtaining hexagonal and cubic dense packings by superimposing hexagonal layers, it is very clear that both packs, despite different symmetries, have the same density, or, as they say, the same filling factor.

If we make a square tray and place the balls on a square grid, we will also get a tight package. Although the balls in each layer are not tightly packed, the wells between the balls will be deeper, and therefore the layers will be more closely packed than in a hexagonal structure.
