To the article “Dry friction”
(pp. 37-43)

1. Let us decompose the friction forces acting on the front wheels automatically into two components: F₁, lying in the plane of the wheels, and F₂, perpendicular to the wheels (see figure). F₁ forces make the wheels turn, and F₂ forces turn automatically.

2. If the center of gravity of the stick is not in the middle between the fingers, then the pressure of the stick on the fingers is different. The friction forces acting on the stick from the fingers are also different. The stick moves to the side where there is less friction.

3. Until the block slides along the plane, the friction is equal in magnitude to the projection of the weight of the block onto the inclined plane F_TP = P sin α. The block begins to slip when the friction reaches its maximum rest value F_TP = kN = kP cos α. In this case, the condition kP cos α = P sin α is satisfied. Therefore, the sliding of the block begins at the angle of inclination of the plane to the horizon α = arctan k. After this, the friction will be equal to F_TP = kN = kP cos α.

The angle α = arctan k at which the block begins to slip is called the friction angle. It also has another geometric meaning: if a force is applied to a block lying on a horizontal plane that is less than the friction angle with the vertical, then the block cannot be moved from its place, no matter how much the applied force is applied. You can prove it like this. Let us place the observer on an inclined plane on which a block of wood lies.
