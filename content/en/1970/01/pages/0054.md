MATEMATIKE SOFTWARE

M1. In the country of Anchuria, where President Miraflores* rules, the time for new presidential elections has approached. There are approximately 20 million voters in the country, of which only one percent (the regular army of Anchuria) supports Miraflores. Miraflores naturally wants to be elected, but on the other hand he wants the elections to appear democratic. What Miraflores calls “democratic voting” is this: all voters are divided into several equal groups, then each of these groups is again divided into a number of equal groups, then these last groups are again divided into equal groups, etc.; in the smallest groups, a representative of the group is chosen - an elector, then an elector is chosen by a representative to vote in an even larger group, etc.; finally, representatives of the largest groups choose a representative. Miraflores divides voters into groups as he wishes and instructs his parties on how to vote. Will he be able to organize “democratic elections” in such a way that he will be elected president? (If the votes are equal, the opposition wins.)

M2. Given a sphere of radius 1. Equal circles $\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n$ of radius $r (n \geqslant 3)$ are located on it. Circle $\gamma_0$ touches all circles $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_n$; in addition, circles $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ touch each other; $\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_3$; ...; $\gamma_n$ and $\gamma_1$. At what $n$ is this possible? Calculate the corresponding radius $r$.
