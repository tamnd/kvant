In one of these systems of holes the balls of the second layer will fall, forming a hexagonal network of collapsing balls, similar to the first. However, the attractive forces due to the elasticity of the film may not be enough to hold the balls of the second, much less the third layer. Therefore, knowing how the balls are placed in the bottom layer, we will make a tray from plywood in the shape of a regular triangle (Fig. 5), such that an integer number of balls are tightly placed along each side (in our model there are seven), and fill it with the balls of the first layer.

It makes no difference which system of holes the balls of the second layer are placed into, but for the third layer the systems of holes turn out to be nonequivalent: the centers of one system are located above the centers of the balls of the first layer, and the second system is located above the empty holes of the first layer. Let's start by placing the balls in the holes lying above the balls of the first layer. In this case, the third layer will exactly repeat the arrangement of the balls of the first, the fourth - the second, etc. The elephant will repeat after one. We will get a not very stable paramid (Fig. 6), which, however, is only due to the fact that in our model the force of “attraction” acts only downward and the balls lying in the outer holes are easily squeezed out by the balls in the upper row.

This arrangement of balls is called the closest hexagonal packing. This is how beryllium, magnesium, cadmium, and helium are crystallized at low temperatures and pressures of more than five atmospheres. It has only one system of parallel densely packed words. Perpendicular to this system, the axis of a symmetrical triangle passes through the center of any ball. The decrease in symmetry is due to the fact that if it passes through the centers of the balls, for example, of four words, being an axis of the sixth order for them, then for odd words it will pass through the centers of the holes, and the order of their symmetrical relative to this axis will be only the third. However, the packing is called hexagonal because it can be considered as two hexagonal lattices of separate four and odd words, pushed into one another with drainage. Please also note that the empty holes in all layers are located one above the other and that channels pass through the entire hexagonal structure into which to insert rods with a diameter of 0.155 times the diameter of the ball. The centers of these channels are also the symmetry axes of the triangle. In figure 6 the hexagonal structure model is shown with inserted rods.

Now we will place the balls of the triangular layer into the holes lying above the free holes of the first layer. Depending on which system of holes we put the balls of the second layer in, we can get two paramids (Fig. 7 and 8). The first paramid is limited by regular triangles with hexagonal arrangement of balls in the planes of the faces, which are no different from the first layer of our arrangement, which lies at the base of the paramid. In other words, our paramid is one of the fifth regular polyhedra - a tetrahedron. The resulting packing contains four tightly packed words, the normals to which coincide with the symmetry axes of the tetrahedron triangle passing through its vertices. In this installation, the rows are repeated every two to three.
