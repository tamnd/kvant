By placing about thirty balls on the film and shaking the box slightly, we will see that the balls will be arranged in regular rows. The centers of the balls will lie at the vertices of equilateral triangles on a side equal to the diameter of the ball, and the balls themselves will fill the entire plane and form a network, which is called hexagonal *). Each ball is surrounded by six Sharimi touching it and each other. Their centers form regular hexagons. (Fig. 2).

If you turn your entire body around an axis passing through the center of any ball by one sixth of a revolution, then one ball will take the place of the others, and the general location of the system in space will remain unchanged. The network of balls will turn into itself. After six such turns, each ball will return to its original place. The crystallographer will say in this case that the symmetry axis of the sixth order, perpendicular to the plane of the balls, passes through the center of each ball. It is because of these axes that the network is called hexagonal. In addition to sixth-order symmetries, there are also third-order axes passing through the centers of the holes between the Sharimi. (The axis of symmetry of the third order is such a straight line, when turning around - each time at an angle of 120° - we return to the original picture.
