XIV International Tournament "Computer Physics"

The International Intelligence Club (IIC) “Gluon” once again gathered gifted schoolchildren and talented students for its “Computer Physics” tournament. The purpose of the tournament is to support talented youth who have shown interest in fundamental science and new information technologies. The task of the tournament organizers is to create temporary creative teams to solve modern scientific problems. Such groups include schoolchildren, teachers, students, graduate students, and scientists. Traditionally, the tournament is held in two rounds – correspondence and full-time. The correspondence round of the XIV Tournament began in October 2009 with the distribution of the assignment for the correspondence tour “Planetary System” upon applications to lyceums, schools and gymnasiums. (The task was published in the magazine «Kvant» No. 4 for 2009, which significantly increased the number of teams participating in the tournament.) The six best teams were invited to the final - the head-to-head round of the competition. The full-time round of the XIV Tournament was held from January 31 to February 7, 2010 in the city of Protvino at the Institute of High Energy Physics with the participation and support of Moscow State University. M.V. Lomonosov, the Dynasty Foundation, the companies “Cyril and Methodius”, “Physikon”, “1C”, magazines «Kvant» and “Physics at School” and the Publishing House “First of September”. The tournament organizing committee expresses gratitude to all named organizations for their assistance in holding the tournament and supporting gifted schoolchildren and talented students. Participants in the full-time round competed in solving the “Puck on a Hemisphere” problem. Based on the results of two rounds, the absolute winner of the XIV Tournament was the team of Lyceum 1511 at the Moscow Engineering Physics Institute (MEPhI), which received the challenge prize “Crystal Globe”, a 1st degree diploma and memorable signs. Diplomas of the second degree this year were received by FML 1580 at the Moscow State Technical University (MSTU) named after. N.E. Bauman and the Samara International Aerospace Lyceum, and a third degree diploma - a team from Kazakhstan. The competition participants were awarded many prizes from sponsors and tournament organizers. Assignment for the correspondence round of the XV Tournament

"Raman scattering"

Nonlinear optical phenomena arise in a medium as a result of the excitation of oscillations at frequencies that do not coincide with the frequency of incident electromagnetic radiation. The phenomenon of Raman scattering was first discovered in 1928 by Soviet physicists G.S. Landsberg and L.I. Mandelstam when studying the scattering of light in crystals and by the Indian physicist Ch. Raman when studying the scattering of light in liquids. This phenomenon lies in the fact that when a medium is irradiated with light with a frequency $\omega$, radiation appears in the scattered light at a frequency Ω $= \omega \pm \omega_{0}$, where $\omega_{0}$ is the natural frequency of oscillations in the medium. In the case of a medium such as a rarefied gas, the natural frequency of vibrations is the frequency of vibrations of electrons in atoms or molecules. For the difference in frequencies we speak of Stokes Raman scattering, and for the sum of frequencies we speak of anti-Stokes Raman scattering. Spontaneous Raman scattering (SRS) is observed when radiation interacts with atoms and molecules undergoing thermal vibrations. The creation of powerful laser radiation sources led to the discovery in 1962 of the effect of stimulated Raman scattering (SRS), which arises as a result of the buildup of atomic vibrations by strong electromagnetic radiation affecting the medium. Accordingly, the stimulated scattering signal turns out to be significantly stronger than the spontaneous scattering signal. Raman scattering is a modern method for studying the structure and structure of a medium. The most effective method is coherent anti-Stokes light scattering (CARS). The idea of ​​this method is the forced excitation of natural oscillations of the medium under the action of two beams of light waves (pump waves) with frequencies $\omega_{1}$ and $\omega_{2}$ such that their difference is equal to the natural frequency of oscillations in the medium: $\omega_{1} - \omega_{2} = \omega_{0}$. In this case, resonant vibrations of atoms in molecules occur. We study the Raman signal that appears when a probe wave with a frequency of $\omega_{3}$ passes through the medium.
