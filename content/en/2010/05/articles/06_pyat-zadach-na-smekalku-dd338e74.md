Send solutions to problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» or by email: math@kvant.info (marked “Competition “Mathematics 6–8”). 6. Insert numbers from 1 to 12 into the unit triangles of the figure so that the sum of the four numbers in each triangle with side 2 is equal to 20. N. Avilov

7. The apartment is a square $3 \times 3$, divided by walls into squares $1 \times 1$ – rooms. There is a door between every two rooms adjacent to the wall, but now all the doors are locked. What is the minimum number of doors that needs to be opened so that a cat sitting in one of the rooms can walk throughout the entire apartment? N.Strelkova

8. Two identical point balls move at different speeds along the same circle in one direction. Launched at these speeds, each individually, they would make 7 and 12 revolutions per minute, respectively. When moving together, they collide, exchanging their speeds after impact. Prove that the balls will meet at a finite number of points located at the vertices of some regular polygon, and find the number of these points. G. Galperin

Folklore

10. The company produces sets of 7 tennis balls in two types of packaging: a long cylinder one ball thick and a flat cylinder one ball high, as shown in figure. The balls touch each other and the walls so that they do not dangle. The empty space is filled with plastic chips. a) Which package – long or flat – requires more crumbs to fill? b*) How many grams of crumbs does it take to fill a flat package, if it takes 90 grams to fill a long one?

Note. To solve point b) you will need an interesting fact that Archimedes knew: the ball occupies exactly 2/3 of the volume of the cylinder in which it is inscribed (the ball touches the walls, bottom and lid of the cylinder).

G. Galperin, A. Shapovalov
