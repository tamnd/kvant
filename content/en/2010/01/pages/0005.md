DELAUNAIS

It goes to Lake Issyk-Kul, from where it immediately flies to Frunze, now Bishkek, where the heat is 40 degrees. After spending several hours in line at the airport ticket office, Boris Nikolayevich, with the help of an academic ID, gets a plane ticket and late in the evening of the same day he finds himself at the airport near Moscow, from where he has to get to his dacha located in the vicinity of Abramtsevo near Moscow. Arriving at the nearest station on the last train late at night, Boris Nikolaevich with a heavy backpack goes to the dacha through the forest and loses his way. Having lost his way in the forest at night, he throws off his backpack in a secluded place and, lightly, finds his home by dawn. These biographical details show that, on the one hand, B. N. Delaunay’s versatile talent manifested itself at a very early age. On the other hand, until his old age he retained his youthful temperament, and his outstanding physical health allowed him to engage with full dedication not only in scientific work, but also in serious tourism. Geometry of numbers, mathematical crystallography, discrete geometry - Boris Nikolaevich wrote works in these areas when he was already over eighty. Altai and the Caucasus, the Carpathians and Tien Shan - this is the geography of the travels he made in old age.

Miracle child

Boris Nikolaevich Delaunay was born on March 15, 1890 in St. Petersburg in the family of the famous professor of mathematics and mechanics, author of very good university textbooks, Nikolai Borisovich Delaunay. The Delaunay family came from France. Great-grandfather B.N. Delaunay, Pierre Delaunay, was a doctor in the Napoleonic army, captured during the War of 1812. After his release he returned to France, but, in love with a beautiful girl from a noble Russian family, he returned to Russia, got married and stayed here $навсегда...^{1}$ Boris was a multi-talented child and received, as they said then, an excellent upbringing. His studies in music were very thorough: he played many pieces by Bach and Mozart, all of Beethoven's sonatas, and composed a lot himself. The music teacher saw musical talent in him and recommended that he definitely enroll in a composition class at the conservatory. But the art teacher had no less grounds when he insisted that Boris enter the art academy. His studies in drawing and painting were imbued with talent and a serious attitude; portraits of loved ones, made in pencil, are striking in their resemblance.
