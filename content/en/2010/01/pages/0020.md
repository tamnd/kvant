Nobel Prize - this is solid state physics (semiconductors) and the phenomenon of the internal photoelectric effect. The wording of the award includes the term “CCD matrix”. This is a charge-coupled device designed to move a small excess negative charge along a chain of identical devices that form a line or a sum of lines - a matrix. Boyle and Smith initially developed the CCD as the original computer memory device. It works as follows. A small plate of semiconductor

A p-type ka (silicon single crystal doped with an acceptor boron impurity) is covered with a thinner layer of dielectric and a metal plate (Fig.1). A potential difference is applied between this plate and the semiconductor substrate. This creates a kind of double-layer capacitor. The thickness of the semiconductor is about 10 microns, the dielectric is 1 microns, and the metal layer is even smaller. In a p-type impurity semiconductor, the concentration of the majority carriers - holes - is of the order of $10^{22} м^{-} 3$, while the concentration of intrinsic charge carriers - electrons and holes - is much smaller and at room temperature is approximately $10^{16} м^{-} 3$. In an electric field, holes (the majority of current carriers) move in the direction of the field, so the concentration of holes near the semiconductor-dielectric interface becomes smaller. But in this region the concentration of own (minority) carriers increases - an electron cloud appears. If the same device is nearby, then the electron cloud will feel the “alien” electric field and move to the neighboring element, in which an asymmetrically located cloud of electrons will also appear (Fig.2,a). If we now turn off the field in element 1, then the cloud in element 2 will become symmetrical (Fig.2,b). If next to element 2 there is element 3, to which an electric field is applied, then the electron clouds in these elements will move (Fig.2,c). And if you now turn off the field in element 2, then the cloud that appears in element 3 will again become symmetrical (Fig.2,d). From such elements it is possible to create linear chains and, by complicating the design, create a two-dimensional matrix in which information about the presence of a charge and its magnitude will move from the place where the charge appears to the output element, where it can be expressed as a number in the binary system. A pair of elements in which charge seems to transfer from one element to another is called a pixel. However, it turned out that the low concentration of intrinsic electrons in a semiconductor does not ensure reliable information transfer. This concentration should have been increased. And an idea appeared - to increase it due to the photoelectric effect. When a photon is absorbed, the valence bond of an electron in the crystal lattice can be broken, and the electron becomes free (inside the crystal). This photoelectric effect is called

The resulting holes will move in the direction of the field (at figure 2 - down), and electrons will move towards the boundary with the dielectric, increasing the concentration of the electron cloud. Moreover, the concentration will be proportional to the number of absorbed photons, i.e. illumination The quantum yield (efficiency) of such a photoelectric effect turns out to be very high - up to 80%. Information from each pixel will run along the lines of the matrix to a device that converts it into a “digit”. In this case, turning on and off the voltage on individual elements must be done with such a frequency that the resulting photoelectrons do not have time to “find” holes with which they could recombine. On a plate - chip - measuring $8_{¥} 15$ mm, you can place a matrix consisting of five million pixels, i.e. obtain information about illumination at several million image points. Then changes were made to the design of the elements. A quartz glass plate is now used as a dielectric, and the metal lining of the capacitor is a very thin (no more than 0.1 micron) layer of aluminum, applied by sputtering - such a layer is quite transparent. To have information about color, you need to alternately apply light to the pixels, passing through various light filters. To summarize, we can say that such a chip is an integrated circuit consisting of light-sensitive photodiodes and using charge-coupled device technology. This chip is the “heart” of digital video and photo cameras, which have practically replaced conventional (analog) devices. L. Belopukhov
