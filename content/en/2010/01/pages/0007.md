Diophantine equations of the third degree

At the same time, B.N. Delone began research on the theory of Diophantine equations of the third degree with two unknowns. The series of works on the theory of cubic Diophantine equations turned out to be, by Boris Nikolaevich’s own admission, the best achievement in his entire mathematical career. The Diophantine equation is an equation of the form

$p ( x_{1}$, … $, x_{n} ) = 0$,

(1) where $p ( x_{1}$, … $, x_{n} )$ is a polynomial with integer coefficients, for which it is necessary to find integer, sometimes rational, solutions. Thus, the well-known equation $x^{2} + y^{2} = z^{2}$, in which we need to find all integer solutions, is an example of a 2nd degree equation with three unknowns. It has infinitely many integer solutions, well known as Pythagorean triples because they correspond to right triangles with integer sides: (3,4,5), (5,12,13) ​​and $т.д.^{3}$ In one of the 23 famous problems, namely the 10th problem, Hilbert posed the question: is there an algorithm that allows one to determine from the coefficients of equation (1) whether a solution to this equation exists or not? Now, after the work of Yu.V. Matiyasevich, who solved Hilbert’s 10th problem (1970), it is known for sure that such an algorithm does not exist. It must be said that specialists in Diophantine equations, who knew very well how difficult every step in this field was, always doubted the existence of such an algorithm. They were fully studied by the great mathematicians Leonhard Euler (complete theory of Pell's quadratic equations $ax^{2} + y^{2} = 1$, where a is not equal to the square of an integer), as well as J. Lagrange, K. Gauss. Regarding Diophantine equations of degree 3 and higher, a profound and unexpected result was obtained by the Norwegian mathematician Axel Thue in 1908. Thue proved that a Diophantine equation of the form f ( x, y ) = c, where f ( x, y ) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 3 or higher, can have only a finite number of integer solutions. Here it is assumed that the polynomial f(x, y) does not expand into a product of polynomials of degree 2 and lower. In this case, solving a high-degree equation can be reduced to solving a quadratic Diophantine equation. And quadratic equations, such as Pell's quadratic equation, have an infinite number of solutions. Recall that a polynomial is called homogeneous (or form) if all monomials included in it have the same degree. However, it should be noted that Thue's theorem did not provide any means for finding solutions. From it it was impossible to obtain any upper bound for the solution values, which would make it possible to find entire solutions at least by brute force.
