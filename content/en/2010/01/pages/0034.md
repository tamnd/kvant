Nano (from the Greek na ѣ nos - dwarf) is a prefix to form the name of submultiple units equal to one billionth of the original units. Large encyclopedic dictionary

nano...

Generally speaking, one should answer “of course, yes, and for quite a long time” - after all, even our distant ancestor, smeared in soot, already dealt with the carbon nanoparticles it contained. Scientists who dealt with fine powders and colloidal solutions also encountered similar “crumbs,” and probably more consciously. But few people imagined what amazing changes in the physical properties of objects can occur when they are reduced to nanosize. It was possible to detect these changes quite recently with the advent of nanodevices - special, completely different from conventional devices, capable of measuring and exploring new properties. The transition to such scales entailed the discovery of extremely unconventional behavior of particles, exhibiting the laws of quantum physics. And this, in turn, gave rise to new branches of knowledge and technology, built on a fundamentally different ideology and on different approaches than before - nanoelectronics, nanomedicine, nanophotonics, nanoenergy... then everywhere. A prefix with a “dwarf” color hides a whole storehouse of huge reserves, the mastery of which, as scientists predict, in the very near future will transform the entire world around us and help humanity cope with the many problems accumulated by civilization. You can also familiarize yourself with these perspectives in the publications of our magazine - an impressive list of them is given, as usual, at the end of the issue. In the meantime, responding to the call of R. Feynman, who wrote exactly half a century ago the article “There is plenty of space below. An invitation to the new world of physics”, we will go in search of nano-objects that live in abundance around us, and, of course, in the school physics course. It seems that their direct or indirect discovery will help you better prepare to comprehend such tempting nanoscience, fraught with mega-opportunities.

Question and tasks

1. Is it possible to see one ten-millionth of a gram (i.e. 100 nanograms) of a substance with the naked eye? 2. Very accurate weighing can be achieved with an error of 10 nanograms. How many atoms are there in a grain of iron weighing 10 nanograms? 3. How to estimate the sizes of water and alcohol molecules? 4. If you combine 50 milliliters of water and 50 milliliters of alcohol, the volume of the solution will be less than 100 milliliters. Why? 5. Estimate how many times the average distance between hydrogen molecules under normal conditions is greater than the diameter of the molecule itself. 6. Why does electric discharge occur at lower voltages when air density decreases? 7. How can we explain that the water in a clay vessel has a temperature lower than the ambient air temperature? Under what conditions will the temperature of the water in this vessel be the same as the surrounding air? 8. Water is boiling in the kettle, and we see water vapor coming out of the kettle’s spout. Is this true? 9. The vessel is divided into two sections by a porous partition. In one section there is a gas consisting of light molecules, in the other - from heavy ones. The gas pressures at the initial moment are the same. After some time, the pressure in the section of the vessel where the heavy molecules were located increased. Then, after a longer period of time, the pressures in both sections of the vessel equalized. How to explain this? 10. Why should Brownian particles be relatively small? 11. It takes considerable force to break a piece of wire. However, if you heat the wire in the flame of a burner, it is much easier to break it. Why?
