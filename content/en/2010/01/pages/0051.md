Using these considerations and the properties of conics, one can prove the existence of an incircle in Theorems 1 and $1 '$. Take, for example, statement (ii) Theorem 1. Points A and C lie on the same ellipse with foci B and D. By condition, the 4-broken line (AC|BD) is not central, therefore points A and C are not diametrically opposite points of the ellipse. The tangents to the ellipse drawn at points A and C intersect at some point I. It turns out that I is equidistant from the lines AB, BC, CD, DA, i.e. The 4-broken line (AC|BD) is described around a circle with center I. Indeed, due to the optical property of the ellipse (see, for example, [3]), I lies on the external bisectors of the angles BAD and BCD and, in addition (see, for example, problem 1.4 from the book [1]), the straight line BI is the bisector of the angle ABC. Similar reasoning can be carried out using a hyperbola and a parabola - each time the center of the desired circle is found. at the intersection of the corresponding tangents to the conic (see Fig.9) The situation occurring in transitivity theorems 2 and $2 '$ can now be interpreted as follows: in statement (i) of Theorem 2, the points X, Y, Z lie on the same branch of the hyperbola with the foci A, B or on the perpendicular bisector to the segment AB;

Exercises

12. Given $4_{\infty}$ - broken line $( AC BD_{\infty} ),$ in which $AB \ne BC$. Prove that it is described if and only if $AD'$ is parallel to one of the asymptotes of the hyperbola with foci A and C passing through B. 13. Prove that the center of a circle inscribed in a degenerate 4-broken line or a degenerate $4_{\infty}$ -broken line lies on the directrix (for the definition of a directrix, see, for example, [2]) of the corresponding conic.
