It is curious that... ...the oldest information about the use of iron as a compass is contained in Chinese chronicles compiled more than three thousand years ago. The name “magnet,” as evidenced by the ancient Greek philosopher Plato, was introduced almost five hundred years before the new era by the author of the famous tragedies, Euripides. ...for centuries, fantastic tales about the properties of a magnet have been passed down from generation to generation. Thus, linking the strength of a magnet, unusual for inanimate nature, with the machinations of the devil, they believed that it helps thieves by opening constipations and locks, that the magnet “sleeps” at night and therefore is inactive, that the influence of the magnet will stop if you rub it with garlic, and if it loses its strength, it should be moistened with goat’s blood. ...in his fundamental tour “On the Magnet...” Gilbert first stated that the Earth is a large magnet. ...Daniel Bernoulli gave the magnets a horseshoe shape; the connection between lightning strikes and the magnetization reversal of ship compasses, as well as the influence of magnetic storms on auroras, was established by Dominic Arago; The scientific program for the study of magnetic phenomena, which was actually followed by scientists of the 19th century, was developed, but, alas, not published by Henry Cavendish.

...trying to find the relationship between various areas of physics, Faraday discovered the rotation of the plane of oscillation of linearly polarized light propagating in matter along a constant magnetic field. The magneto-optical Faraday effect, implemented today in thin films, has proven to be indispensable in studying the properties of magnetic domains, in verifying the authenticity of video and audio recordings, and in deciphering “black boxes.” ...the hypothesis about the existence of regions of spontaneous magnetization - domains - in ferromagnets was put forward by the French physicist Pierre Weiss in 1907 and was confirmed 12 years later in a spectacular experiment. Magnetization reversal of micron-sized domains with the help of an electronic signal amplifier invented by that time was converted into clicks heard throughout the laboratory. And already in 1932, magnetic domains were observed directly through a microscope. ...although the poles of magnets are inseparable, the hypothesis about the existence of magnetic monopoles does not contradict the theory, many of their properties have been studied “on paper,” and the search for monopoles does not stop both in space and in terrestrial experiments. ...The 2007 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for the development of technology that significantly increased the storage density of information on hard drives. It is based on the discovery of giant magnetoresistance in so-called “sandwiches”, consisting of two layers of ferromagnetic material separated by a thin layer of non-magnetic material. This effect was the first practical application of nanotechnology in the modern electronics industry. ...in recent years, physicists have been able to create “superlenses” that collect light rays into a much narrower beam than allowed by the laws of optical diffraction, which made it possible to distinguish points located just a few tens of nanometers from each other. Metamaterials used in the manufacture of “superlenses” have a negative refractive index, i.e. are “left-handed” optical media in which the dielectric constant and magnetic permeability are negative.
