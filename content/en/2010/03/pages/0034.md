Are you really familiar with this?

permanent magnets

But of course! Let's look around - here they are on the refrigerator door in toys stuck to it, on tables in “traps” of paper clips, pins and buttons, in magnetic strips of plastic cards, or at least in the same compasses that are already inserted into school bags and watch straps. Having thought a little, let us remember that tape recorders, microphones, and telephones cannot do without magnets - textbooks that have not kept up with the times tell us this. And what do they fill various types of equipment with today? It may no longer contain magnets in the classical sense, but this does not mean that magnetic materials have ceased to be used in the devices that we use every day. They just changed beyond recognition, sometimes becoming almost invisible, but most importantly, acquiring completely new, special qualities. However, in the roll call of times that arose in the epigraphs, perhaps something common is noticeable - the constant increased attention to this amazing natural phenomenon. Reflections on magnets can be found among ancient philosophers, medieval naturalists, and our contemporary researchers. Once upon a time, they managed to breathe a “soul” into a magnet and likened it to living organisms; Today they are trying to solve the mystery of the magnetic monopole and explain the unusual properties of magnetic materials created in laboratories. To some extent, this “Kaleidoscope” is a continuation of the previous issue “Nano...” And just like last time, we will try to build a bridge from seemingly simple situations and tasks where we meet our magnet characters to those often stunning technological innovations, where they continue to play a noticeable, if not decisive role, changing the face of the world around us.

Questions and tasks

1. Can a steel rod have the same magnetic poles at both ends? Can a permanent magnet have an even number of magnetic poles? What about an odd number?

2. Two identical rectilinear magnets were connected once as shown in figure a), another time as shown in figure b). Draw the magnetic field lines in each case. 3. Will a magnet act on a magnetic needle if you place your hand between them? What if it's an aluminum sheet? 4. Metal filings are evenly poured onto a sheet of paper. This sheet is placed in a magnetic field. If you lightly tap the sheet, the sawdust will be arranged in chains in the direction of the magnetic lines. Why is it necessary to tap the sheet? Why do sawdust line up in chains? 5. There are two identical steel rods, one of which is more magnetized than the other. How to find this rod? 6. A strip magnet was cut into several pieces of equal length. Which of the resulting pieces will be more magnetized: which was closer to the ends or closer to the middle of the magnet? 7. The strip magnet was divided into two equal parts and two magnets were obtained. Will these magnets have the same effect as a whole magnet? 8. Why, when storing an arc-shaped magnet, are its ends connected with an iron bar (anchor)? 9. Strong arc magnet is able to hold the steel ball. Why is the ball not held in the same place if the magnet is closed with an armature? 10. A strong magnet can hold a garland of several iron cylinders suspended. What will happen if you bring such a garland closer to the garland from below?
