MATHEMATICAL WORLD

105th anniversary of Academician S. M. Nikolsky

On April 30, 2010, the outstanding Russian mathematician and teacher, Academician of the Russian Academy of Sciences Sergei Mikhailovich Nikolsky turned 105 years old. S.M. Nikolsky is the recognized head of the scientific school of the theory of functions and its applications, the author of over two hundred scientific publications, including three monographs, two textbooks for universities and seven for schools. Many of his books are included in the golden fund of domestic and world literature on mathematics due to their high scientific level and accessibility of presentation. His textbooks on mathematical analysis have been translated into many languages ​​of the world. As a scientist, S.M. Nikolsky has many followers, more than forty of his students defended their candidate dissertations, eleven became doctors of physical and mathematical sciences. S.M. Nikolsky made an invaluable contribution to improving the education system in our country. S.M. Nikolsky is a three-time laureate of the State Prize, a laureate of the Government of the Russian Federation, and the Lomonosov Moscow State University Prize “For outstanding contribution to the development of education.” In 2005 he was awarded the title “Legend of the Century”. He was awarded the Order of the Red Banner of Labor, Lenin, the October Revolution and medals of a number of academies. Congratulations to Sergei Mikhailovich on his anniversary, we wish you health, joy from communicating with family and friends, success in all your endeavors!

(According to the memoirs of S.M. Nikolsky from the book “How I became a mathematician”)

I was born on April 30, 1905 in the Perm province, 100 km from Tyumen, in the village of Talitsa Plant. In this village there was a Forest School, where my father, an assistant forester, taught. My father graduated from the St. Petersburg Forestry Institute named after Alexander III in 1896. A year after my birth, he received the position of forester and an assignment to work in the far west, on the border with Germany. Now this territory belongs to Poland. There I spent my childhood in a forest village, playing with local Polish village boys. I mastered literacy and numeracy casually, easily. I received some training from my mother - she was a village teacher before her marriage. Science was quite easy for me. For half an hour before dinner, my mother taught arithmetic, reading, and prayer. But my father instilled the main interest in exact sciences. He studied higher mathematics at the institute and knew how to tell very interesting stories. At the age of eight I was accepted into a preparatory class. Sergei Mikhailovich Nikolsky already knew. A year later there was an exam for 1st grade. A math teacher with a university badge asked me what 10 times 11 was. Although this was not part of the 1st grade entry program, I quickly answered. Then he asked what 11 times 12 would be. I also answered. Then he perked up and began asking how much 13 times 14, 15 times 17, 16 times 17, and so on up to almost a thousand. After some thought, I gave the correct answers. He gave me a high five. This is how I passed my first arithmetic exam. In the summer of 1914, the world war began, and we lived 20 km from the German border. The father sent the family to the east, to the rear, and he himself remained in the forestry. This is how we ended up in Chernigov. Here I entered the classical Chernigov gymnasium named after Alexander I the Blessed. Due to the war, transfer exams from class to class were cancelled. The first two years of training proceeded normally. The front was far away. I managed to master arithmetic well - according to Kiselev. Algebra was studied in the 3rd and 4th grades of the gymnasium. In mathematics they usually gave me "A's". I loved solving problems.
