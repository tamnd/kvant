Competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

We continue our next competition on solving mathematical problems for students in grades 6–8. Send solutions to problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» or by email: math@kvant.info (marked “Competition “Mathematics 6–8”). Be sure to include your name, class, and home address. As before, we welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by mathematical clubs. We ask circle leaders to provide an email address or contact phone number. According to tradition, the winning clubs of the correspondence competition are invited to the final in-person tournament.

11. The table clock with hour and minute hands is shaped like a cube with a round dial in the center of one of the faces. There are no numbers on the clock or any markings to indicate which way it is up. Therefore, you can accidentally place them on their side or even upside down. a) What time does the clock show at figure? b) Is there at least one moment in the day when it will be impossible to determine what time this clock shows? N.Strelkova

12. There are 10 barrels of water in a circle. First, half of the water from the first barrel was poured into the second, then a third of the water from the second barrel was poured into the third, then a quarter of the water from the third barrel into the fourth..., after which 1/10 from the ninth barrel was poured into the tenth and, finally, water from the tenth barrel was added to the first to its original level. Could each barrel contain the same amount of water as was in it at the beginning? G. Galperin

13. Let N be a natural number. Which triangles with integral lengths of sides are greater?

14. 60 children lined up in pairs and went to the museum. They walked along the pedestrian crossing in a crowd, and after it they lined up again in pairs (but some pairs could become different). Prove that in a museum children can be divided into three equal groups so that children in one group are never in the same pair. I. Bogdanov

15. Angle C at the vertex of isosceles triangle ABC is equal to $120^{o}$. From vertex C, two rays were released into the triangle, which, reflected from the base AB at points M and N (according to the law “the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection”), respectively hit the side AC at point P and the side BC at point Q. It is known that AP = CQ. Let the segment PQ intersect CM at point X, and CN at point Y. Prove that triangles PMX, XCY and QNY are congruent. V.Proizvolov

Summer tournament named after A.P. Savin

With the onset of summer, schoolchildren leave for vacation. But for some kids, solving mathematical problems is an exciting way to relax. And also communication with those who have the same ardent love for mathematics. Every year at the end of June there is a tournament of mathematical battles in memory of A.P. Savina. In 2010 it took place for the 16th time. As before, it took place on the shore of a picturesque lake, at the Berendeyev Polyany recreation center near Sudislavl (Kostroma region). 38 teams from Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kostroma, Tambov, Chernogolovka, Kharkov and Kyiv took part in the competition. The initiators of the tournament are Grigory Vyacheslavovich Kondakov and the educational program “Big Change”. On the first day of the tournament, the game “Mathematical Abacus” was played.

This is an exciting competition where children have to choose tasks both by difficulty and by topic. For example, seventh-graders were offered assignments on logic and number theory, arithmetic and algebra, as well as assignments combined into a group with the vague name “Numbers.” The winners will be the teams that took first place. Among 6 classes, this is the team of the circle «Kvant» (Moscow). Among the seventh graders is the first team from gymnasium 1514 (Moscow). The team from school 82 from Chernogolovka (Moscow region) became the best in 8 classes. Team “A” TsO 218 (Moscow) took 1st place among 9 classes. The next day, teams from grades 6–8 participated in a team competition, based on the results of which they were divided into leagues. And the ninth graders have already begun to fight: 6 teams formed a separate league and played a full round robin in 5 days
