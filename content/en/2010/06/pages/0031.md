A mathematical battle tournament is not only about fighting with problems. Every evening, groups of children and leaders gather near the sheets of paper with the results of the next round, discuss the results for a long time and try to make predictions for the next round. But we must admit that in most of the league groups the intrigue did not work out: the teams ended up lined up in such an order that the team that ended up higher beat the team that ended up lower. It was difficult to make predictions in 9th grade: here three teams persistently beat the other three. Everything was heading towards the fact that the first three teams would beat each other in a circle, and the same would happen among the remaining three teams. Will the teams not be distributed in places? Everyone was waiting for the last round. As a result, an unexpected “draw” determined 3rd and 4th places, and 1st and 2nd places were determined by a personal meeting between the teams. In the 6-grade league, the best team was the Moscow gymnasium 1514, winning in the final battle against the sixth-graders of school 179. But the seventh-graders of school 179 took first place, defeating last year’s winner - the team of the “Fractal” club from St. Petersburg. The first place in the 8th grade league was taken by the national team of A.V. Spivak’s circle, in which only one 8th grade student played, and the rest of the players were seventh graders. As a result of persistent struggle in the 9th grade league, the team of T.P. Zorina’s circle with the name “Moscow-South” became the best. Note that this team won not only two summer tournaments - in the fall of 2009 they took away the cup of the Kostroma Open 8–9 tournament. The list of all tournament winners is presented on page 30. For one day of the tournament, the guys take a break from mathematical battles. At this time, there is a personal Olympiad, or rather, as many as four (for each of the 6th, 7th, 8th and 9th grade parallels). Among the sixth-graders, the best were Stepan Petrov from St. Petersburg (school 30) and fifth-grader Daria Nikolaeva (school 464, Moscow). The result of Yulia Zaitseva (school 179, Moscow) in the 7th grade Olympiad was awarded the “grand prix”, and the first diplomas were received by two of her classmates Illarion Dmitriev and Sasha Vatuzov, as well as Andrei Zertsalov from Central Education Center 218 in Moscow. But the diplomas earned by the seventh graders did not end there - Andrei Volgin (7th grade, gymnasium 1543, Moscow) became one of the best in the 8th grade Olympiad. First degree diplomas were also received by two Kiev residents Nikita Shcheglov and Danil Khilko (both from Lyceum 208) and Muscovite Vanya Zverev (school 853, Moscow). The teams of the Kyiv Lyceum 208, who came to the tournament for the first time, took away another first-degree diploma - it was received by Maxim Chaudhary in the 9th grade Olympiad.
