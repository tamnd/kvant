Tasks

1.

There is a rule in a photo studio: if there are less than a hundred photographs, then printing one photo costs 3 rubles. 50 kopecks, and if there are 100 photos or more, then printing one photo costs 3 rubles. It turns out that it is more profitable to print 100 photographs for 3 rubles than 99 for 3.5 rubles. What is the smallest number of photos that can add a few more photos so that the printing fee will then decrease? A. Kovaldzhi

4.

Sasha, Dima and Fedya collect pieces of the Little Raccoon puzzle. A puzzle is a picture cut into intricately shaped pieces, none of which are the same. If Fedya had a yellow piece with a Raccoon's nose, Sasha had a green piece with a tail, and Dima had a blue piece with a paw, then it would turn out that any two boys, having gathered together, could make up a whole picture from their pieces. Prove that now the three boys can complete the entire puzzle. F. Bakharev

2.

Illustration by D. Grishukova

Matches depict a figure representing the combination of four identical cubes. In the image, the fourth cube is not visible, it is hidden behind the three visible cubes. Rearrange the nine matches so that all four cubes of this figure can be seen. N. Avilov

5.

Cut a square of size $10 \times 10$ squares into 5 rectangular parts according to the pattern shown in figure, so that the area of ​​rectangle A is greater than the area of ​​each of the other parts and the perimeter of rectangle B is greater than the perimeter of each of the other parts. K.Kokhas

3.

Come up with a four-digit number that is not divisible by 2010, but if its notation is repeated 15 times, then the resulting multi-digit number will be divisible by 2010. N. Kushpel

These problems are primarily intended for students in grades 6–8. Problems 3, 4, 5 were proposed at the regional round of the 77th St. Petersburg Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics.
