Send solutions to problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» or by email: math@kvant.info (marked “Competition “Mathematics 6–8”). 11. The table clock with hour and minute hands is shaped like a cube with a round dial in the center of one of the faces. There are no numbers on the clock or any markings to indicate which way it is up. Therefore, you can accidentally place them on their side or even upside down. a) What time does the clock show at figure? b) Is there at least one moment in the day when it will be impossible to determine what time this clock shows? N.Strelkova

12. There are 10 barrels of water in a circle. First, half of the water from the first barrel was poured into the second, then a third of the water from the second barrel was poured into the third, then a quarter of the water from the third barrel into the fourth..., after which 1/10 from the ninth barrel was poured into the tenth and, finally, water from the tenth barrel was added to the first to its original level. Could each barrel contain the same amount of water as was in it at the beginning? G. Galperin

Summer tournament named after A.P. Savin

With the onset of summer, schoolchildren leave for vacation. But for some kids, solving mathematical problems is an exciting way to relax. And also communication with those who have the same ardent love for mathematics. Every year at the end of June there is a tournament of mathematical battles in memory of A.P. Savina. In 2010 it took place for the 16th time. As before, it took place on the shore of a picturesque lake, at the Berendeyev Polyany recreation center near Sudislavl (Kostroma region). 38 teams from Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kostroma, Tambov, Chernogolovka, Kharkov and Kyiv took part in the competition. The initiators of the tournament are Grigory Vyacheslavovich Kondakov and the educational program “Big Change”. On the first day of the tournament, the game “Mathematical Abacus” was played.

This is an exciting competition where children have to choose tasks both by difficulty and by topic. For example, seventh-graders were offered assignments on logic and number theory, arithmetic and algebra, as well as assignments combined into a group with the vague name “Numbers.” The winners will be the teams that took first place. Among 6 classes, this is the team of the circle «Kvant» (Moscow). Among the seventh graders is the first team from gymnasium 1514 (Moscow). The team from school 82 from Chernogolovka (Moscow region) became the best in 8 classes. Team “A” TsO 218 (Moscow) took 1st place among 9 classes. The next day, teams from grades 6–8 participated in a team competition, based on the results of which they were divided into leagues. The lack of current inside a disconnected fruit battery is explained by the peculiarities of redox chemical reactions, due to which voltage appears on the electrodes. As a result of a chemical reaction under the influence of fruit juice, zinc ions leave the surface of the zinc electrode, and the zinc electrode acquires a negative potential relative to the solution. The chemical reaction does not depend on the presence of another electrode in the juice. A similar reaction occurs with a copper electrode, but, according to the electrochemical series, with less intensity. Thus, a potential difference is established between the zinc and copper electrodes. When the source terminals are shorted, electrons flow through the external circuit from the zinc to the copper. The removal of ions of the corresponding chemical element from the metal during a chemical reaction inevitably leads to the destruction of the electrodes, which in this case cannot be reversible. This circumstance makes it impossible to recharge fruit batteries and, thus, the impossibility of manufacturing batteries based on them. The maximum power of a fruit battery is small and amounts to tenths of a milliwatt. Therefore, the fruit battery can be used to power only low-power devices, such as an electronic watch, calculator, low-power LED, etc. Take, for example, a calculator and your battery. If the calculator has a built-in solar battery, it must be covered with opaque paper. Connect two sources from a lemon or an apple in series into a battery and connect a calculator to it (observing the polarity). You will see that the calculator is working. figure 3 shows a photograph of an electronic thermometer powered by a battery of current sources made from two apples. To power more powerful devices, it will be necessary to connect fruit current sources to a battery. Think about how many fruit batteries are needed and how they need to be connected to power an Aspire One netbook (with specifications of 19 V and 1.58 A) or a cell phone and whether you can save on the number of fruits by connecting them into a battery.
