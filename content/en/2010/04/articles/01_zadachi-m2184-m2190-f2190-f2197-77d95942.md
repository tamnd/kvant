PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent no later than November 1, 2010 to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 4–2010” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M2184” or “F2190”. In the “From” column, please write your first and last name legibly. In the letter, enclose an envelope with your address written on it and the required set of stamps (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking the decisions). Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics can also be sent to the email addresses math@kvant.info and phys@kvant.info, respectively. Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you study. Problem M2184–M2186, M2188–M2190 were offered at the XXXI Tournament of Cities. Problem F2195 was offered at the final stage of the XLIV All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics.

Problem M2184–M2190, F2190–F2197

M2184. The following is known about the function f (x): any straight line on the coordinate plane has as many common points with the graph y = f (x) as with the parabola $y = x^{2}$. Prove that the function f(x) is identically equal to $x^{2}$. A. Shapovalov

M2185. Given a natural number $n \ge 5$. It is known about the pyramid $SA_{1} A_{2}$ … $A_{n}$ that its base $A_{1} A_{2}$ … $A_{n}$ is a regular n-gon, and all the side faces are isosceles triangles (not necessarily with vertex S). Does this pyramid have to be correct? G. Galperin

M2186. Baron Munchausen asked to conceive a non-constant polynomial P(x) with integer non-negative coefficients and to tell him only the values ​​of P(2) and P(P(2)). Baron claims that he can always reconstruct the intended polynomial only from these data. Is the Baron mistaken? S.Markelov

M2187. In an acute triangle ABC, the altitudes $AA_{1}, BB_{1}$ and $CC_{1}$ are drawn, intersecting at point H. Lines $A_{1} C_{1}$ and AC intersect at point D. Prove that line DH is perpendicular to the median drawn from the vertex B. S. Ilyasov (8th grade student, Almaty)

M2189. Thirty-three heroes ride on horseback along the ring road counterclockwise. Can they drive indefinitely at various constant speeds, if there is only one point on the road at which the heroes have the opportunity to overtake each other? A. Klyachko, E. Frenkel

M2190. A natural number is given. You are allowed to place pluses between the digits of the number in an arbitrary manner and calculate the sum (for example, from the number 123456789 you can get 12345 + 6 + 789 = 13140). With the resulting number, a similar operation is again allowed, and so on. Prove that from any number you can get a single digit by performing no more than 10 such operations. A.Tolpygo
