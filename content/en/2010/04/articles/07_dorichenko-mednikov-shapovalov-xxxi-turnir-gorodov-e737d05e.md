$1 (3)^{1}$. Six baskets contain pears, plums and apples. The number of plums in each basket is equal to the number of apples in the remaining baskets combined, and the number of apples in each basket is equal to the number of pears in the remaining baskets combined. Prove that the total number of fruits is divisible by 31. M. Murashkin, A. Shapovalov

5 (6). See problem M2188 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

2 (3). The Kid and Carlson are cutting a square cake. Carlson selects a point on it (not on the border). After this, the Kid makes a straight cut from the selected point to the edge (in any direction). Then Carlson makes a second straight cut from the selected point to the edge, perpendicular to the first, and gives the smaller of the resulting two pieces to the Kid. The kid wants to get at least a quarter of the cake. Can Carlson stop him? M. Murashkin

8–9 grades

1 (3). There is a piece of cheese. You are allowed to choose any positive (possibly non-integer) number $a \ne 1$ and cut this piece in the ratio 1: a by weight, then cut any of the available pieces in the same ratio, etc. Is it possible to proceed in such a way that after a finite number of cuts, all the cheese can be divided into two piles of equal weight? A. Shapovalov

2 (4). In triangle ABC, point M is the midpoint of side AC, point P lies on side BC. The segment AP intersects BM at point O. It turns out that BO = BP. Find the ratio OM:PC. M. Volchkevich

3. There are 999 numbers arranged on a circle, each equal to 1 or –1, and not all numbers are the same. Let's take all the products of 10 consecutive numbers and add them up. a) (3) What is the smallest amount that can be obtained? b) (3) Which is the largest? A.Tolpygo

4 (6). The sum of the digits of the natural number n is 100. Can the sum of the digits of the number $n^{3}$ be equal to $100^{3}$? A. Kanel-Belov

5. a) (3) Three heroes ride on horseback along the ring road counterclockwise. Can they drive indefinitely at various constant speeds, if there is only one point on the road at which the heroes have the opportunity to overtake each other? b) (5) What if there are ten heroes? A. Klyachko, E. Frenkel

6 (8). On the plane there is given an open, non-self-intersecting broken line, in which there are 31 links (neighboring links do not lie on the same straight line). Through each link a straight line is drawn containing this link. We received 31 direct ones, some of them may have coincided. What is the smallest number of different lines that could be obtained? A.Tolpygo

7 (11). On some squares of the board $10 \times 10$ there is a flea. Once a minute, the fleas jump simultaneously, each one jumping into the adjacent cage (on the side). The flea jumps strictly in one of four directions parallel to the sides of the board, maintaining the direction as long as possible, otherwise changing it to the opposite. The dog Barbos watched the fleas for an hour and never saw two of them sitting on the same cage. What is the largest number of fleas that could jump on the board? M. Murashkin
