How do you solve physics problems?
Perhaps this question will seem to many of you countries - the answer is very obvious: first read the conditions, then think about the solution, then write it down, get an answer, if the answers are given in the task, then I check my answer against the task.

Well, what if a solution doesn’t immediately emerge? Are you looking at the answers? We think that many of you are doing just that. It's a pity. Puzzle over the problem, approach it this way, and then figure out the answer yourself - and we are sure that when you get the right result, you will experience a feeling of great satisfaction. The benefits that such work on a task brings are undeniable.

Of course, there are very difficult problems, and it is impossible to give any universal solution. However, there are conditions that are absolutely necessary to fulfill if you want to learn how to solve problems. Everyone is clear that before starting the tasks, you need to understand the theory. But now you have learned the theoretical material, understood it and take on the task. We read what is “given” and what “needs to be determined.” Start solving? Wait! Before you think about a solution, think about the conditions. Imagine the physical phenomenon, the process that is discussed in the problem. Analyze all the data given in the conditions - after all, as a rule, nothing that is “given” in the conditions happens in vain. And very often, having fully comprehended this “given”, you will be able to prove (not guess, but prove) that “you need to determine”. If this connection has been established for you, you can consider it done.

To better understand what we are talking about, we suggest you do several tasks. In the conditions of these tasks, only what is “given” is given. You yourself come up with what “needs to be determined” and determine *).
