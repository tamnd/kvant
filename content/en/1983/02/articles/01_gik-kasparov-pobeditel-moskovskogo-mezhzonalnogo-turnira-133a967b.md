KASPAROV IS THE WINNER OF THE MOSCOW INTERZONAL TOURNAMENT

Two years ago on our page you already met Garry Kasparov («Kvant», 1981, No. 5). The youngest grandmaster on the plan - now he is only 19 years old - then became the world chess champion among youths. And now he is already one of the contenders for the “adult” crown. In the fall of 1982, showing a brilliant result - 10 points in thirteen games (without a single defeat), he became the winner of the Moscow international tournament. Look at his two effective minators played in this competition.

I. Van der Wiel - G. Kasparov. Queen's pawn opening 1.d4 Nf6 2.Cg5 Ke4 3.Cf4. The Dutch chess player is learning from the usual 2.c4, fearing the Old Indian Defense - Kasparov's favorite debit. 3...c5 4.d5 Qb6 5.Cc1. Was it worth making two moves in a row with the bishop in order to immediately return to its place? 5...e6 6.f3 Qa5 + 7.c3 Nf6 8.e4 d6 9.Ka3 ed 10.ed Ce7 11.Kc4 Qd8 12.Ke3 0—0 13.Ke2 Re8 14.g4.

As you know, the record chess match lasts only two moves - 1.f3 e5 2.g4 Qh4 × (such a checkmate is called “duracim”). Here White adheres to approximately the same strategy - he has already played f3, and now plays g4, exposing the el - h4 diagonal. The correct move was 13.Cd3, 14.Ke2, and it is not so easy to match the white king. 14...Kfd7! Release the mentioned diagonal. True, there is no way to checkmate in this partner game. 15.Kg3 Cg5 16.Kpf2 Ke5 17.Cb5 Cd7 18.Cd7 Kb:d7 19.Kef5 c4! 20.Kh5. He loses a piece, but even after the best 20.Kpg2 C:c1 21.Jl:c1 Nc5 White’s position is unenviable. 20...Kd3+ 21.Kpg2 C:c1 22.Jl:c1 g6. White resigned, and 22.Kh6+ Kpf8 23. Kg3 is solved by 23...Qg5.

If in the previous game it was easy to make claims against White, then Kasparov owes his victory over the Rumyansk grandmaster to himself. G. Kasparov - F. Georgnou New Indian Defense 1.d4 Kf6 2.c4 e6 3.Kf3 b6 4.a3 Cb7 5.Kc3 d5 6.cd K:d5 7.Fc2 c5 8.e4 K:c3 9.bc Ce7 10.Cb5+ Cc6 11.Cd3 Kd7 12.0—0 h6. It was probably better to retreat the bishop back to b7. 13.Jld1 Qc7 14.d5! Kaspov's favorite technique, with the help of which he won many red victories - White sacrifices the central pawns, but at the same time opens up lines for attack. 14...ed 15.ed C:d5 16.Cb5 a6 17.Cf4! Then everything goes like clockwork. 17...Q:f4 18.C:d7+ Kp:d7 19.Jl:d5+ Kpc7 20.Jle1! Cd6 21.Jlf5 Qc4 22.Jle4 Qb5 23.Jl:f7+ Kpb8 24.Jle4 Jld8 25.c4 Qc6 26.Ke5 Qc8 27.Fb1. The blacks surrendered.

The second place in the candidates' matches was won by the Soviet grandmaster A. Belyavsky. The next game with the Cuban grandmaster was important in many respects. Garcia scored 5.5 points in the first six rounds! In subsequent meetings with the Soviet participants, he made three draws. A. Belyavsky managed to stop the confident march of the Cuban.

G. Garcia - A. Belyavsky Queen's Gambit 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Kc3 Ce7 4.Kf3 Kf6 5.Cg5 h6 6.Ch4 0—0 7.e3 b6 8.Jlc1 Cb7 9.C:f6 C:f6 10.cd ed 11.Ce2 Qe7 12.0—0 Jld8 13.Fb3 c5 14.dc bc 15.Jld1 d4! This move by Black caught the enemy by surprise. Garcia thought for a long time, but didn’t come up with anything good. 16.Ka4 Ka6 17.C:a6 a6 18.K:c5. Of course, Belyavsky had foreseen the loss of the pawn in advance. The initiative he receives in return forces White to return the material back. 18...Ce2 19.Jl:d4. We have to give up the exchange, on 19.Jld2 there is a strong 19...Lab8, then C:f3 and de, 19...Jldc8! Rook no way

without money, before taking it, black ties the knight tightly. 20.Fd5. The quality can be preserved, but after 20.Jld5 Lab8 none of the white pieces feel comfortable. 20...C:f3 21.F:f3 C:d4 22.ed. In principle, White has a sufficient equivalent for the exchange - two pawns. However, skillfully maneuvering, Belyavsky opened one of them. 22...Jab8 23.b3 Jld8 24.Fe3 Qd6 25.Jlc4 Jle8 26.Fd2 Qf4! 27.Fd1 Jbd8 28.g3 Qf5 29.Kpg2 Jld6. Perhaps White expected to stage the position with 30.Ff3, but now this is followed by 30...Q:f3+ 31.Kp:f3 Rlf6+ 32. Kpg2 Rle2. 30.a4 Rf6 31.Jlc2 Qd5+ 32.Kpg1 Rlb6 33.Jle2 Jld8 34. Fe1 Qd4 35.Jle8+ Kph7 36.Jld8 Qd8 37.Fe4+ Kpg8 38.b4 Jld6 39.b5 Jld1+ 40. Kpg2 Qd5. The Whites surrendered.

Let us recall that the tournament in Moscow was the last international one; in the other two successes it was accompanied by V. Smyslov, Z. Riblii, L. Portisos and E. Torre. If we add the finalists of the previous cycle, V. Korchnoi and R. Hübner, then we now fully know the rise of the eighth contenders for the chess crown. This fall we will find out the name of A. Karpov’s new opponent.
