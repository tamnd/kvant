Programming is the second literacy

Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences
A. P. ERSHOV

This article is based on the author's speech at the opening of the International Federation for Information Processing and UNESCO conference "Computer Applications in Education" in Lausanne (Switzerland) in July 1981. The text of the speech has been revised specifically for «Kvant».

I recognize that comparing programming to literacy is risky in many ways. On one side of our comparison there is an unusual profession that requires ability and long training, and on the other there is a common property, the most important property of a modern person.

Nevertheless, I will immediately try to explain why this comparison is respectful.

First of all, we must not forget that universal literacy did not exist from time immemorial. Like programming, it had its own origin, emergence and development. We are accustomed to the fact that the USSR is a country of practical complete literacy; But 100 years ago in Russia only about 20% were literate. There are still about 800 million illiterate people on Earth.

Further, both the basis for universal literacy and the basis for the widespread dissemination of the program is a technical invention: the printing press and the computer, respectively. The development and spread of printing led to universal literacy. The development and spread of computers will lead to universal programming.

Just as the word “literacy” now has a much broader meaning than just the ability to write and read, the word “programming” takes on a broader meaning. This is not just the ability to write computer programs in some specialized language, but the ability to draw up clear, targeted rules for action in a wide variety of situations. In this broad sense, programs are as much an organizational ability of a person as literacy. This ability is prepared by the organization of the human nervous system; it is inherent in people at work, when communicating with each other, in contemplating nature and in interacting with it.

Finally, although literacy and programming can, of course, be compared in many ways, finding many analogies between them, the most important thing, perhaps, is that they complement the others and together form a new idea of ​​the harmony of the human mind.

It’s worth dwelling on the last point a little.

We all remember the simple and succinct words of Maxim Gorky: “Love books, the source of knowledge.” We understand literacy as the ability to express knowledge in the form of text. However, the problem remains: how to move from knowledge to action. "Inform

actions with words, and words with action,” says one of Shakespeare’s heroes. It is here - in the transition from knowledge to action - that programming arises.

In the pre-book period, the unity of word and deed was realized naturally and clearly. With words and example, the master (teacher) taught the younger (student) the job. Understanding was not a purely mental insight, but the achievement of a set goal, mastery of the techniques of the matter being studied.

The appearance of the book dealt a severe blow to the unity of word and deed. The process of accumulating knowledge was separated from its application, new forms of thirst for knowledge and new desires for realizing the truth appeared. All languages ​​have developed special expressions for people who prefer books in their quest for knowledge. In these expressions there was a tinge of disdain, generally unfair, but sometimes deserved.

Gradually it became clear that the bookish accumulation of knowledge required new mechanisms for transforming this knowledge into action. Thus, the task of programming appeared long before the computer. The advent of computers only made the problem of programming relevant, just as printing made the problem of literacy relevant.

But literacy and the ability to act by themselves do not know anything. In this contrast, it is difficult to say from which we suffer more: from ignorance or inactivity. Goethe warned: “There is nothing more terrible than activity.” However, experience suggests that it grows when the active life position of educated and cultured people is insufficient. Hence our desire for a second literacy - not only for learning to write commands, but for the perception of a person who is decisive and prudent at the same time.

Everyday life of a person, especially in the city, consists of activities according to programs. Every person who adheres to the regime will proudly feel like a programmer if he imagines his usual morning sequence of actions, starting from the alarm ringing and ending with the start of work.

We complain about the social ills of the century, in particular - detachment from common affairs, passivity, and advocate for the formation of an active position in life. What is it? Simply put, it is the ability to develop a program of action and follow it.

Thus, the world of software is far from just the stuffing of computer memory. This is, first of all, a huge store of operational knowledge. This “action knowledge” is stored in libraries of computer programs, in general libraries, in the gene pool of all living things. And it is used both by computers, robots, automatic devices, and by living organizations, in particular by humans.

Until now, it seems to many that mastering programming should begin with becoming familiar with the structure of some computer and painstakingly memorizing the details of writing a program for this computer in some specific algorithmic language. Without skills, of course, you can’t get around anywhere, but the basic laws of programming, methods of transition from knowledge to action, organization of action in space and time are, in fact, much more fundamental and general in nature, bringing them closer to the basic laws of mathematics or speech and writing.

You will feel that this is both an easy and difficult task, since, on the one hand, it is clear what is being done, and, on the other hand, it is difficult to explain these actions in a general form.

Thus, we are faced with the most important task - to develop general principles of programming, highlight the basic concepts in it and express it in a general form. Such tasks accumulate, and this means that sooner or later the world of computers and programs will clash with the world of schools.

Computer at school

Now, after the advent of microprocessors, there is no point in raising the question of whether or not to have a computer in school. Computers are already in many schools and will be coming there in increasing quantities.

It is often actively expressed to me that a computer with a program is the same as a problem with a known solution; the advent of electronic help will breed laziness of the mind and nothing more. However, one of the best pictures in the American magazine The New Yorker depicts poor Johnny, sadly looking at a pile of pocket calculators in front of him, and no less exhausted Mom, patiently repeating his

These warnings describe the “push-button” formation in sections earlier. However, numerous experiences with children of all ages using computers confirm the opposite: activity, drinking ability, and with them abilities increase very strongly.
In our local school, half of one of the classes calculated historical data scientifically, after one of the young programmers inserted into the computer a dialogue program reporting historical data, and the other subjected the teacher to an exam using the computer, almost several times on inaccuracies.
Some company has released a computer game for fish that tests the correct English words. With the help of a speech synthesizer, words are pronounced, the child spells this word, the machine checks and reacts. It's difficult to put a good speech synthesizer into a pocket game, but the authors turned its disadvantage into an accessible one, making it very similar to Pinocchio's voice. You can imagine the delight of the children when the toy says to them in Pinocchio’s voice: “Try again, try again, you don’t know this word!”

Before the competitive entrance exams, a consultative information system implemented on a computer was installed in one of the universities. There was always a queue around the terminals. In the interests of the children, two teachers were seated nearby to unload the car. The teachers were left idle, and there was still a queue at the terminals. Why? “We are not ashamed to reveal our ignorance in front of the machine, but we are embarrassed in front of the teacher,” the guys said. The computer creates a gaming environment, which is more valuable here than real life - after all, you can leave the game without losing your dignity.

Conclusion

I started my article with a comparison. Now we can reveal its specific content. We stand on the threshold of practically unlimited development and spread of electronic computing technology in society. The machine becomes an intellectual ordiem and practical partner in all spheres of human life and activity. The development of the world of the program and the constant complication of the environment require and at the same time make possible a significant increase in the intellectual power of a person.

In order not to get lost in this rapidly developing world, a world of amazing technical and wearable programs, there is no longer enough literacy in its traditional sense. It is also necessary to overcome the milestone that I called the milestone of second literacy. And, first of all, this applies to students.
