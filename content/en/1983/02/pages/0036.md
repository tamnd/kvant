Exercises

1. (MSU, Faculty of Economics, 1972). In triangle $ABC$ $|AC|=12$ cm, $|AB|=|BC|=3\sqrt{10}$ cm. Two balls touch the plane of triangle $ABC$ at points $A$ and $C$ and are located on opposite sides of this plane. The distance between the centers of these balls is 15 cm. The center of the triangle is at point $B$, and this ball touches two given balls. Determine the radius of the triangle.

2. (MSU, Faculty of Computational Mathematics and Cybernetics, 1972). The lateral edges of a regular triangular paramid $SABC$ are inclined to the plane of the base at an angle $45^\circ$. The ball touches the base plane $ABC$ at point $A$, and also touches the extension of the edge $BS$ beyond the vertex $S$. A plane is drawn through the center of the ball and the height $BD$ of the base. Find the angle of inclination of this plane to the plane of the base.

3. (MSU, Geological Faculty, 1980). On a sphere of radius 11 cm there are points $A$, $A_1$, $B$, $B_1$, $C$ and $C_1$. The lines $AA_1$, $BB_1$ and $CC_1$ are mutually redistributed and intersect at the point $M$, located at a distance of $\sqrt{59}$ cm from the center of the sphere. Find the length of the segment $AA_1$, if it is known that $|BB_1|=18$ cm, and the point $M$ divides segment $CC_1$ in relation to $(8+\sqrt{2}):(8-\sqrt{2})$.

4. (MSU, mechanism, 1981). The segment $DE$ lying in the dihedral angle $AD$ from the points $B$ and $C$ on its faces is parallel to the plane of the triangle $ABC$ having area $S$. A ball is inscribed in the tetrahedron $BCDE$, $k$ is the ratio of the distance from the center of the ball to the straight line $DE$ to the distance from $(DE)$ to the plane $ABC$. Let $B'$ be the projection of the point $B$ onto the plane $CDE$; it is known that $tg B'DE:tg BDE=l$. Through the middle $[AD]$ a plane $P$ is drawn parallel to the plane $ABC$. Find the area of ​​the polyhedron $ABCDE$, composed of tetrahedra $ABCD$ and $BCDE$, by the plane $P$, if it is known that the sum of the areas of all faces of the tetrahedron $BCDE$ is equal to $\sigma$.

On the front cover we publish a photograph of the microprocessor's "large integrated circuit" (enlarged 50 times).

The emergence of microprocessors is associated with the enormous progress of microelectronics, which included the successes of many areas of science and technology - physics, chemistry, computer technology, precision metallurgy, instrument making and mechanical engineering. Nowadays, a “large integrated circuit” like the one on the cover (its dimensions are up to 50 square millimeters, its thickness is less than a millimeter) contains decimal elements 2-3 microns in size and consumes only a few Watts of energy.

One of the first domestic computers, BESM-1, occupied an area of ​​tens of square meters, divided meters and consumed tens of kilowatts of energy. Modern microcomputers the size of a small book, running on large integrated circuits, have a significantly large production in all areas of the national economy.
