⟦folio 26⟧

In particular, is the pressure in the vessel higher or lower than atmospheric?

In this case, the volume of the gas does not change, and the pressure is not proportional to the temperature. Is this possible?

Let us turn to the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation. In addition to the parameters p, V and T, it also includes m and μ. The molar mass of the gel should not change - the gel gas is monatomic and cannot dissociate. There shouldn't be any condensation either, as the temperature rises. Apparently, the only possibility is to change the mass m of the gaseous gel.

Since the pressure increases more slowly than it would in an isochoric process for a constant mass, the mass of the gas must decrease; that is, the gas must flow out of the vessel. This means that the pressure in the vessel is greater than atmospheric pressure, otherwise the gas would not flow out.

The Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation allows you to compare not only different states of one gas, but also the states of different gases. Here's an example.

Problem 3. Ball lightning is a faintly luminous ball of gas floating freely in the air. Created by the Stakhanov model (one of the models built to explain the nature and behavior of ball lightning), the gas inside the ball is a complex compound, part of which consists of a nitrogen ion associated with several water molecules. The electrons lost by nitrogen are captured by water, so that each complex molecule as a whole turns out to be neutral. Determine how many molecules of water bind each nitrogen, if the temperature inside the ball is t=600°C, and the temperature of the surrounding air is t0=20°C.

Since ball lightning floats in the air, the density of its constituent gas is equal to the density of the surrounding air. The pressure in lightning is apparently also equal to atmospheric pressure. These two conditions together give

$$\frac{p}{\varphi} = \frac{p_0}{\varphi_0},$$ or $$\frac{pV}{m} = \frac{p_0V_0}{m_0}$$

(here all quantities without an index refer to the compound, and with an index - to air).

Using the equation of gas state, we obtain

$$\frac{T}{\mu} = \frac{T_0}{\mu_0},$$

from where, knowing the molar mass of air ($\mu_0=29 \cdot 10^{-3}$ kg/mol), we find the molar mass of the complex compound:

$$\mu = \mu_0 \frac{T}{T_0} \approx 86 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ кг/моль.}$$

The molar mass of atomic nitrogen is $14 \cdot 10^{-3}$ kg/mol, and that of water is $18 \cdot 10^{-3}$ kg/mol; therefore, each nitrogen binds four water molecules.

The external conditions in which the gas is located can be changed in various ways, but each time the state of the gas can be described by the equation of gas state.

Problem 4. A cylindrical joint S=10 cm² is closed by a massive piston. There is gas inside. The vessel begins to be lifted with an acceleration of 2g. When the gas temperature became equal to the initial one, the volume of gas under the piston decreased by 1.5 times. Find the mass of the piston. External pressure $p_0=10^5$ Pa.

In a stationary state, the force of the piston balances the difference in gas pressure forces inside the vessel and outside:

$$mg = (p - p_0)S.$$

When the connection moves, the force acting on the piston gives it an acceleration of 2g, directed upward:

$$2mg = (1,5p - p_0)S - mg,$$ or

$$3mg = (1,5p - p_0)S$$

(at a constant temperature, the volume will decrease by 1.5 times; this means that the pressure will also increase by 1.5 times). Solving the two equations obtained jointly, we obtain

$$m = \frac{p_0S}{3g} \approx 3,4 \text{ кг.}$$
