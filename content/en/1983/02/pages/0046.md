**M768. The sum of $n$ numbers, each of which does not exceed modulo 1, is equal to $s$. Prove that you can choose several numbers from them so that the sum of the selected numbers differs from $s/3$ by no more than 1/3.**

Let us prove that these numbers can be divided into three groups so that the sums of numbers in these groups $s_1 \leq s_2 \leq s_3$ differ by no more than 1, that is, $s_3 - s_1 \leq 1$. (We also allow “empty” groups consisting of 0 numbers - the sum of the numbers in such a group is considered equal to 0.) To do this, it is enough to select numbers in random order one at a time and write them in three columns, and each successive number is added to the column where the sum of the numbers is by and greater than , if this number is negative, and in the column where the sum is by and less than , if this number is non-negative (first, we will take three numbers of the same sign from the data $n$ numbers and write them first in different columns). It is easy to see that after each step the sums of the numbers in the columns differ by no more than 1, so as a result we will obtain the required resolution *).

Now let’s check that the average sum $s_2$ satisfies the requirements - $1/3 \leq s_2 - s/3 \leq 1/3$, where $s = s_1 + s_2 + s_3$ is the sum of all numbers:

$$3s_2 \leq (s_1 + 1) + s_2 + s_3 = s + 1,$$

$$3s_2 \leq s_1 + s_2 + (s_3 - 1) = s - 1.$$

It is interesting to find out (for each $\theta$ between 0 and 1), for each smallest $d = d(\theta)$ a similar inequality $|s' - \theta s| < d$ is true, where $s$ is the sum of arbitrary $n$ numbers,

*) A similar algorithm was used to solve problem M575, one of the most difficult problems at the 1979 World Olympiad in Tbilisi. Note that for $n = 3$ and $n = 4$ numbers whose averages are no more than two numbers of the same sign, our middle group may turn out to be empty; You can verify the validity of the problem statement (print a non-empty group of one or more numbers with the required sum) using brute force.
