Guess and assumptions

Now let's get back to the conversation about solar heating of water in the ocean and try to answer some questions. Why do the boundaries between different layers in the ocean not move over time, although there is a gradual equalization of temperature and “salinity”? Why does the leveling of “salinity” occur much slower than the equalization of temperature?

This can again be explained by convection, which exists in all layers of the ocean (and not just in the hot layer, as in our experiments).

Let's consider two adjacent layers. As a result of diffusion and collision between molecules, both “cold” and “hot” molecules appear on both sides of the interface. They are picked up by convective currents and carried away from the boundaries. Moreover, if convection is equally intense on both sides of the boundary, the boundary itself does not move, although energy transfer occurs and temperatures gradually equalize.

As for equalizing the “salinity” of the water, this process is much slower. This is due to the surface tension that the interface has. It is known that the coefficient of surface tension of the solution layer is greater than that of pure water. The boundary, like any physical system, tends to have minimal surface energy. Therefore, the molecules into which the molecules break up in water are mostly located far from the boundaries, and their diffusion begins to noticeably manifest itself only when the surface tension of the boundary decreases significantly.

The appearance of the boundary between warm and cold water can probably be attributed not only to the layering of the ocean. It is possible that this phenomenon necessitates the existence of four and stable boundaries of ocean currents and the recently discovered giant outlets in the ocean, as well as much, much more.

Application

Let us denote by $W$ the power of the heater (let it be greater than the minimum). Some part of this power is wasted on the evaporation of the liquid, but if the temperature is constant, then these losses are also constant. Therefore, for convenience, we introduce the value $W_1$, equal to the power of the heater and the power looking for the evaporation of the liquid and we will consider it constant.

It is natural to assume that the power $W_2$, finishing in the surrounding space through the side surface of the vessel, is proportional to the thickness $h$ of the hot layer: $W_2 = kh$. The proportionality coefficient $k$ depends only on the experimental conditions and does not change during the experiment.

Let the boundary move a distance $dh$ during time $dt$. This means that a layer of liquid with a thickness of $dh$ was heated from the temperature of the cold layer to the temperature of the hot layer, that is, by $\Delta T$ degrees. This requires an amount of heat $dQ = cQ\Delta Tdh = k_1dh$ (here $c$ is the specific temperature of the liquid, $Q$ is its density, $S$ is the horizontal sectional area of ​​the vessel).

According to the law of conservation of energy,
$$W_1dt = dQ + W_2dt,$$
or
$$W_1dt = k_1dh + khdt.$$
Let's rewrite this equality differently:
$$\frac{dh}{W_1 - kh} = \frac{dt}{k_1},$$
Let’s integrate it and obtain the dependence of the thickness of the hot layer $h$ on time $t$:
$$h = a - be^{-at},$$
where $a = W_1/k$, $b = W_1/k - h_0$ and $\alpha = k/k_1$ are constant coefficients, $h_0$ is the initial depth at which the boundary is formed.

Now let’s find the speed of movement of the boundaries $v$:
$$v = \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{W_1 - kh}{k_1}.$$
From this it can be seen that the speed of the boundary linearly depends on the thickness of the layer, and at the next thickness ($\frac{равной}{W_1/k}$) the speed should become equal to zero. This happens because as the thickness increases, energy losses increase and there must be a moment when all the energy from the heater goes into the surrounding space.

At the moment the boundary stops, the convective flows of the gliding do not weaken so much that the movement of liquid practically stops, and due to diffusion and thermal conductivity, the boundary will gradually “disintegrate.”
