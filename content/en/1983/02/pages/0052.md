Relation (2) allows us to establish connections between $\Delta V$ and $\Delta T$ at constant pressure. Let us rewrite this relation in the form
$$NkT = V \left( p - \frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \right).$$

Then
$$Nk \cdot \Delta T = \Delta V \cdot \left( p - \frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \right) + \frac{2\alpha N}{V^2} \cdot \Delta V = \left( \frac{NkT}{V} + \frac{2\alpha N}{V^2} \right) \cdot \Delta V,$$

where
$$\Delta V = \frac{NkV^2}{NkTV + 2\alpha N} \cdot \Delta T.$$

Substituting into equality (1) the found expressions (2) and (3) for $p$ and $\Delta V$, we obtain:
$$\Delta Q = C_V \cdot \Delta T + \frac{Nk}{1 + 2\alpha/VkT} \cdot \Delta T.$$
Consequently, the heat capacity of a given mass of gas at constant pressure is
$$C_p = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T} = C_V + \frac{Nk}{1 + 2\alpha/VkT}.$$
The difference in molar heat capacity of a gas at constant pressure and at constant volume is equal to
$$c_V - c_p = \frac{N_A k}{1 + 2\alpha/VkT} = \frac{R}{1 + 2\alpha/VkT}.$$
(Note that the heat capacity $C_p$ depends on the volume occupied by the gas, which is a direct consequence of the interaction of molecules. The heat capacity $C_V$ is the same as that of an ideal gas, since at a constant volume the potential energy between molecular interactions in the gas does not change.)

Let the direction of the source be $U = at$, where $\alpha = \cos \theta$. Then at the moment of time $t_1$ and $t_2$ after the key is closed, the following conditions are satisfied:
$$\alpha t_1 = RI + \frac{Q_1}{C},$$
$$\alpha t_2 = RI + \frac{Q_2}{C},$$
where $I$ is the direct current flowing through the resistor, $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are the charges on the capacitor at moments $t_1$ and $t_2$. Subtracting the first from the second equality, we get
$$\alpha \left( t_2 - t_1 \right) = \frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{C} = \frac{I \left( t_2 - t_1 \right)}{C},$$

whence $I = \alpha C$.

At the moment of time $t_x$, at which the key $K$ is closed (the absence is from the moment $t = 0$ at $U = 0$), the charge on the capacitor was equal to zero, and the conditions were met
$$\alpha t_x = RI = R\alpha C.$$

From here comes $t_x$:
$$t_x = RC.$$
