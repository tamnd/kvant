Let us denote by C=C(B) the convex hull of B, that is, the smallest convex polygon covering B (its vertices are some points from B, which we will call extreme).

For any two points P, Q from B, we call the axis of the pair (P, Q) the straight line l.(P, Q) drawn through the middle of the segment PQ perpendicular to this segment. Note that the l(PQ) axis serves as the polygonal center of all circles passing through the points P and Q. We will also come across the following very-id statement, which we will refer to as a “clear lemma”: the l(P, Q) axis divides the plane into two parts, in one of which there are points that are closer to P than to Q, and in the other - closer to Q than to P.

For a given point P from B, we call the country with column P the region D_p, obtained as follows: draw the lines l(P, Q), where Q are all points of B other than P, and the averages of the regions into which these lines divide the plane, select the region D_p containing the point P (Fig. 2). This will be either a convex polygon, or (if P is the extreme point of B) an unlimited, “going to infinity” convex area, the boundary of which consists of two rays and, possibly, one or more segments.

Using the main lemma, we see that each country D_p consists of all points of the plane that are located closer to point P than to other points from B (on the border of two countries D_p and D_q there are points equally distant from the columns and Q). It follows that the countries D_p, where P runs through the entire polygonal B, cover the plane and intersect only along the boundaries (Fig. 3)*).

Let's call points P, Q from B close if their countries D_p and D_q have a common section of borders lying on the straight line l(P, Q), and far if their countries do not have a single common border point. (In the exceptional case when the borders of more than three countries meet at one point O, pairs of points P, R arise, for which the intersection of the borders D_p∩D_r consists of one point - while the points P, Q are neither close nor far.)

Now let's construct a triangulation of the polygon C(B) with vertices at all points of B that have the properties required in the conditions. To do this, we connect the segments of all pairs of close points (red segments in our drawings); these segments will dissect the polygon C(B) into triangles (and polygons). Note that if PQ and PR are adjacent segments leading from a certain column P to the columns Q and R close to it, that is, in the corner QPR there is no point close to P, then the countries D_p, D_r and D_p have a common border point O (Fig. 4). Let us consider one of the triangles (or polygons) on which C(B) is divided by drawn segments. Countries with capitals at its vertices have a common border point O; point O is removed from these columns at the same distance, and from all other points B - at a large distance, that is, inside the circumscribed circle of our triangle (polygon) with center O there is no point B. Polygons with more than three vertices (they arise only in exceptional cases when more than three countries have a common border point) we can arbitrarily divide diagonals into triangles (Fig. 5). The resulting triangulation satisfies the conditions of the problem.
