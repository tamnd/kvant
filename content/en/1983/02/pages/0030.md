Assignment from the oral exam at MIPT

Below is a task assigned in different years for the entrance exams to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. Here are collected tasks regarding only one topic - the simplest property of integers.

1. Is 123456789 a prime number?
2. Prove that the equation $x^2 - y^2 = 1982$ has no integer solutions.
3. Let $p_1$ and $p_2$ be two consecutive odd primes and $p_1 + p_2 = 2n$, $n \in N$. Prove that the number $n$ is composite.
4. Prove that if the number $1 \cdot 2 \dots n + 1$, $n \in N$, is divisible by $n + 1$, then the number $n + 1$ is prime.
5. Prove that for every natural number $n$ greater than two, one of the numbers $2^n - 1$ and $2^n + 1$ is composite.
6. Can the number $3n + 2$, where $n$ is a natural number, be the square of some integer?
7. Prove that the sum $n^3 + 6n^2 - 4n + 3$ is divisible by 3 for any natural number $n$.
8. Prove that the averages of three prime numbers greater than 3, always at least two, have the property that either their sum or their difference is divisible by 12.
9. Prove that the number $333^{777} + 777^{333}$ is divisible by 37.

Exercises

1. figure 3 shows a graph of changes in the state of a certain mass of an ideal gas. Indicate in which parts of the graph the pressure increases and in which it decreases.
2. An electrical discharge is produced in the vessel containing oxygen. As a result, all the oxygen was converted into ozone, and the air temperature doubled. How did the pressure change? The volume of the vessel is considered unchanged.
3. A vessel with a cross section of $S = 10$ cm² is closed by a piston of mass $m = 5$ kg. There is gas in the vessel. When the vessel moves downward with an acceleration of 4g, the volume of gas under the piston doubles. The gas temperature has not changed. Find the external pressure.
4. A satellite with a cross section of $S = 1$ m² moves at the first escape velocity of $v = 7,8$ km/s in a near-Earth orbit. Atmospheric pressure at the orbit altitude (200 km) is $p = 1,37 \cdot 10^{-4}$ Pa, temperature $T = 1226$ K. Determine the number of collisions of the satellite with air molecules in 1 second.
