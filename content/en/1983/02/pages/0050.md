S. S. Krotov

From the conditions of the problem it follows that the force $\vec{F}$ is directed at a certain angle α ≠ 0 to the initial velocity vector of the body v (otherwise, that is, at α = 0, the changed speed for the same time intervals would be the same).

Let $\vec{OA}$ be the vector of the initial velocity of the body, $\vec{AB}$ be the vector of the change in velocity over time t from the beginning of the force F (see figure). Then $\vec{OB}$ is a vector equal to the Modilian velocITY v₁ = v/2 of the body after time t. Let us set aside the vector $\vec{BC}$ from point B, which makes an angle α with the direction of the vector OA and is equal in modulus |$\vec{AB}$|. Then the vector $\vec{OC}$ will be equal in modulus to the velocity v₂ = v/4 of the body after a time of 2t from the beginning of the action of force F. Repeating the same operation again, we will construct a vector OD equal in modulus to the velocity v₃ of the body after a time of 3t from the beginning of the action of force F.

Let the projections of the vector $\vec{AB}$ on the X and Y axes (see figure) be equal to the correspondence between Δvₓ and Δv_y. Then
$$(v + \Delta v_x)^2 + \Delta v_y^2 = v^2/4,$$
$$(v + 2\Delta v_x)^2 + (2\Delta v_y)^2 = v^2/16.$$
Considering that the modulation v₃ must satisfy the correspondence
$$v_3^2 = (v + 3\Delta v_x)^2 + (3\Delta v_y)^2,$$
taking into account the previous equations we find v₃:
$$v_3 = v\sqrt{7}/4.$$

S. K. Serdeev

Let us mentally replace the oil with water so that the position of the ice relative to the glass does not change. Since water is denser than oil, the water level in the glass in this case will be lower than the upper oil level (Fig. 2). In this new situation, when the ice melts, the overall liquid level will not change (Fig. 3).

Now we will “return” the oil in place by removing the added water (Fig. 4). Some of the oil will fill the “hole” formed when the ice melts.

Thus, the total liquid level (water + oil) will decrease after the ice melts; the level at which the oil-water interface is located will increase (Fig. 5).
