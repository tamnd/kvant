c) Let no four points lie on the same circle (then each pair of these points is close or far). Let us prove that any triangulation $T$ that satisfies the conditions of the problem necessarily coincides with the one we constructed above (by connecting close points).

Suppose that some point $P$ is connected to a point $R$, far from it. The center $O$ of any circle passing through $P$ and $R$ lies on the axis $l(P,R)$ and (since $P$ and $R$ are distant points) is separated from $P$ by a certain line $l(P,Q)$, where $Q$ is close to $P$ dot; therefore, $Q$ lies inside the circle. This means that $PR$ cannot be a side of the triangulation triangle $T$.

So, we have proven that in the triangulation $T$ only pairs of $6$ or $3$ and $x$ points can be connected. But in order to obtain a triangulation, that is, a partition of $C(B)$ into triangles with vertices $B$, as we have seen, it is necessary to connect all pairs of close points.

Note: You may have noticed that the requirement that no 4 points lie on the circle is redundant to prove uniqueness. It is enough to require that any circle passing through 4 points from $B$ contains at least one point from $B$ inside.

Think about how you can generalize this problem and its solution to finite sets of points lying in three-dimensional space.

M767. a) The line $l$ divides the area of ​​a convex polygon in half. Prove that the ratio in which this line divides the projection of the polygon into the line perpendicular to it does not exceed $1+\sqrt{2}$.

b) Each of the three straight lines divides the area of ​​this figure in half. Prove that the area of ​​the parts of the figure contained in the triangle between the lines does not exceed $1/4$ of the entire area of ​​the figure.

Let $A$ and $B$ be the points of intersection of line $l$ with the contour of the polygon, $h$ and $k$ be segments of its projection onto the line perpendicular to it, $h>k$ (Fig. 1; segment $h$ is the projection of the head, $k$ is the red half polygon). Let us prove that $h/k \leq 1+\sqrt{2}$. Let us connect the sum far from $l$ point $C$ of the polygon in the head of the half-plane (it is projected at the end of the segment $h$) with points $A$ and $B$ and continue the segments $CA$ and $CB$ until they intersect at the points $E$ and $F$ with a line parallel to $l$ and containing the point $G$ farthest from $l$ in the red half-plane (this line passes through the end of the segment $k$). Since the polygon is convex, the segments $AC$ and $BC$ and all of $\triangle ABC$ are contained in the head half of the polygon, and the segments $EA$ and $BF$ lie outside the polygon, so the trapezoid $EABC$ contains its red half. Since the points and the other half of the polygon are equal, then the area of ​​the trapezoid $EABC$ is not less than the area $\triangle ABC$, therefore $S_{ECF} \geq 2S_{ABC}$, and since the triangles $ECF$ and $ACB$ are similar, and the similarity coefficient (height ratio) $(k+h)/h$, then

$$\left(\frac{k+h}{h}\right)^2 \geq 2, \frac{k}{h} + 1 \geq \sqrt{2}, \frac{h}{k} \leq \sqrt{2} + 1.$$

From the solution it is clear that equality is achieved only for triangles, one side of which is parallel to $l$.
