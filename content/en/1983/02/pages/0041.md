$$|f(x) - f(y)| = |x - y|?$$

Let it be known about the mapping $F$ of the plane into itself that it maps any two points $X, Y$ located at a distance of 1 to two points $F(X), F(Y)$ also located at a distance of 1:

$$\varrho(X, Y) = 1 \Rightarrow \varrho(F(X), F(Y)) = 1;$$

Then for any two points $X, Y$ of the plane

$$\varrho(X, Y) = \varrho(F(X), F(Y)),$$

that is, the mapping $F$ preserves distance. Prove the following statements from which this theorem follows: for any $X, Y$

b) $\varrho(F(X), F(Y)) \leq \varrho(X, Y) + 1$;

c) $* \varrho(X, Y) = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow \varrho(F(X), F(Y)) = \sqrt{3}$;

f) $* \varrho(F(X), F(Y)) \leq \varrho(X, Y)$;

e) $* \varrho(F(X), F(Y)) \geq \varrho(X, Y)$.

(You can, of course, propose another plan for proving the theorem.)

A. Tyshchka

Find the speed of the balls after the collision if the impact is completely inelastic. Charges will not be redistributed.
