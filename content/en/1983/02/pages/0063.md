3. The mass of water coming from the pump per unit time at a speed $v$ through the section $S$ is equal to $qvS$. The power of a pump without a tube is
$$N_0 = qvS \cdot \frac{v^2}{2}.$$
In the presence of a tube of height $h$, water, in addition to kinetic energy, also has potential energy associated with the rise of the liquid to a height $h$, therefore the power
$$N = quS \cdot \frac{u^2}{2} + quS \cdot gh$$
where $u$ is the speed of water. From kinematics we have
$$v^2 = 2gH, u^2 = 2g(H-h).$$
Then we finally get
$$\frac{N}{N_0} = \frac{u}{v} \left(\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^2 + \frac{2gh}{v^2}\right) = \frac{u}{v} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{h}{H}}.$$
The problem can be solved more simply, since the force applied in both cases is the same (since the heights of the fonts are the same). Then, using the formulas $N = Fu$ and $N_0 = Fv$, we obtain $N/N_0 = u/v = \sqrt{1-h/H}$.
