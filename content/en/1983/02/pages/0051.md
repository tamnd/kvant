⟦folio 49⟧

F786. In a non-ideal monatomic gas, attractive forces act between the molecules. Assuming that the potential energy between molecular interactions is proportional to the density of the gas, that is, $\Pi = \alpha \frac{N}{V}$, where $N$ is the number of gas molecules, $V$ is the volume occupied by the gas, $\alpha$ is the proportionality coefficient ($\alpha < 0$). Determine the difference between the molar heat capacity of the gas at constant pressure ($c_p$) and at constant volume ($c_v$).

The validity of this qualitative solution can be verified by quantitative calculation. Do it yourself.

S. V. Kazakov

Let us impart a certain amount of heat $\Delta Q$ to the gas. According to the first law of thermodynamics,

$$\Delta Q = \Delta U + A$$

where $\Delta U$ is the change in the internal energy of the gas, $A$ is the work done by the gas.

The internal energy of a non-ideal gas consists of the kinetic energy of the thermal motion of molecules, which is the same as in an ideal gas, and the potential energy between molecular interactions: $U = U_{нд} + \Pi$. From the conditions of the problem it follows that attractive forces act between gas molecules. Indeed, the fact that the energy between a molecular bond is negative ($\alpha < 0$) means that to remove any molecule from the region of the bond, positive work must be done, that is, attractive forces are assumed.

Let us assume that when a small amount of heat $\Delta Q$ is imparted to the gas, the volume occupied by the gas changes by a small amount $\Delta V$, and the gas temperature changes by $\Delta T$. Change in internal energy of gas -

$$\Delta U = \Delta U_{нд} + \Delta \Pi,$$

work done by gas -

$$A = p \cdot \Delta V.$$

According to the conditions of the problem,

$$\Delta \Pi = \frac{\alpha N}{V + \Delta V} - \frac{\alpha N}{V} = \frac{\alpha N}{V} \left( \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\Delta V}{V}} - 1 \right) \approx \frac{\alpha N}{V} \left( 1 - \frac{\Delta V}{V} - 1 \right) = -\frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \cdot \Delta V.$$

The change in internal energy of a nonideal gas when its temperature changes by $\Delta T$ is equal to

$$\Delta U_{нд} = C_V \cdot \Delta T,$$

where $C_V$ is the heat capacity of a given mass of gas at constant volume (at $\Delta V = 0$).

Thus, $\Delta U = C_V \cdot \Delta T - \frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \cdot \Delta V$, and

$$\Delta Q = C_V \cdot \Delta T - \frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \cdot \Delta V + p \cdot \Delta V = C_V \cdot \Delta T + \left( p - \frac{\alpha N}{V^2} \right) \cdot \Delta V.$$

In the absence of molecular interaction (when solving a problem with the “ideal gas” model)

$$\Delta Q = C_V \cdot \Delta T + p' \cdot \Delta V,$$

where $p' = \frac{N}{V} kT$ is the ideal gas pressure. Thus, as a result of the action of molecular forces of attraction, the gas pressure is less than the pressure of an ideal gas by amounts

$$p' - p = -\frac{\alpha N}{V^2}$$
