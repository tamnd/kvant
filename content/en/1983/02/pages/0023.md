Let's heat the water from above

V. I. PENTEGOV

People heat water every day and almost always place the heater below, under a vessel with water. This is understandable - the convection of the liquid occurring throughout the entire volume of the vessel ensures uniform heating of the entire liquid to the required temperature.

What happens if the heater is placed on top? This question is not different. Let us remember that 70% of the earth's surface is covered with water, for which the main source of energy is the Sun. So in nature, on a huge scale, water is heated from above. Many of the processes associated with this are so complex that there is still no consensus on their causes. For example, relatively recently it was found that the temperature of water in the ocean changes with depth not by swimming, but by jumps. These jumps occur in a very narrow gap between layers in which the temperature is constant. Within each layer, the density and “salinity” (percentage of soli) of water are also constant.
