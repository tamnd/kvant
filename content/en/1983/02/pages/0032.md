Fig. 2. The first bunch of figures.

1) pyramid and inscribed ball;
2) the base of the pyramid and two balls (underlining: only the base, not the whole pyramid);
3) base of the pyramid, second ball and cutting plane;
4) cutting plane and pyramid (and here it is impossible to bypass one straight line SA, since it is essential that this is the edge of the pyramid).

Let's make a separate drawing for each link, unfold it so that it is convenient to see its constituent figures. Next to the segments whose lengths we know, we write these lengths (in the form of formulas). This is a very important point: the lengths in the drawing will help you see the equations. We will call the process of arranging lengths in a drawing marking the drawing. Now for each connective we write down the resulting correspondence.

1st bunch of figures. The plane passing through the height of the pyramid and the apothem SE of the face SBC intersects the pyramid along an equilateral triangle, and the ball through a circle of radius $r$ inscribed in it (Fig. 2, a). (This and similar statements in the recording of the decision must be proven, no matter how consistent they may seem. But for us now the search for a solution and obtaining an answer is more important, so we omit this kind of assertion. And what and how to prove, how to “impose rigor” is given quite a lot of attention at school and in various aids for applicants*).

*) See, for example, G. V. Dorofeev, M. K. Potapov, N. Kh. Rozov “A manual on mathematics for university applicants” (M., “Nauka”, 1976), section III, § 1.

Let's draw the section separately (Fig. 2, b). The center O of the inscribed ball lies on the bisector of the angle SEH, which is equal in magnitude to the angle between the side face and the base of the pyramid. This leads to the first correspondence:

$$r = \frac{a}{2} \mathrm{tg} \frac{a}{2}.$$

Note that the same figure 2, b could be obtained using not a section, but a projection of the pyramid onto a plane perpendicular to the edge BC.

2nd bunch of figures. Let's put both balls and the base of the pyramid (Fig. 3, a) on a separate drawing. Let's choose such an angle so that the segments and angles we need are visible in natural size (Fig. 3, b). Carrying out the obvious auxiliary constructions (do not forget to do the necessary proofs) and marking the drawing, we obtain the equation

$$(R + r)^2 - (R - r)^2 = \left( \frac{a \sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2.$$

(The same drawing is obtained in a flow passing through the drawing of the centers of the balls and the points of their contact with the base.)

3rd bunch of figures. The procedure is similar: taking the connection to a separate drawing (Fig. 4, a), carrying out auxiliary constructions and markings (prove that $PBA = \phi$!) and drawing a section (with plane PAB - Fig. 4, b), from the right triangle PAB we obtain the equation

$$R = a \mathrm{tg} \phi.$$
