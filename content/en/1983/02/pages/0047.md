M769*. The bissertrices of triangle ABC intersect at point L, their continuations intersect the circumference of the triangle at points $A_1, B_1, C_1$ (Fig. 2). Let R be the circumradius and r the circumradius of triangle ABC. Prove the equalities

$$\begin{align*}
a) & \frac{|LA_1| \cdot |LC_1|}{|LB|} = R; \\
\sigma) & \frac{|LA| \cdot |LC|}{LB_1} = 2r; \\
\theta) & = \frac{S_{ABC}}{S_{A_1B_1C_1}} = \frac{2r}{R}.
\end{align*}$$

By the law of sines, $|A_1B| = 2R \sin \alpha$, $|C_1B| = 2R \sin \gamma$. From $\triangle A_1LB$ we get $|LB| = 2|A_1L| \sin \gamma = 4R \sin \alpha \cdot \sin \gamma$.

So, $\frac{|A_1L| \cdot |C_1L|}{|BL|} = \frac{2R \sin \alpha \cdot 2R \sin \gamma}{4R \sin \alpha \cdot \sin \gamma} = R$.

b) Since $|A_1L| = \frac{r}{\sin \alpha}$, $|CL| = \frac{r}{\sin \gamma}$, and $|B_1L| = \frac{|CL|}{2 \sin \alpha} = \frac{r}{2 \sin \alpha \cdot \sin \gamma}$ ($\triangle B_1CL$ is equiled, and $|B_1C| = |B_1L|$, then $\frac{|AL| \cdot |CL|}{|B_1L|} = 2r$.

c) According to the well-known formula, $S_{ABC} = \frac{abc}{4R}$, $S_{A_1B_1C_1} = \frac{a_1b_1c_1}{4R}$, and therefore $\frac{S_{abc}}{S_{A_1B_1C_1}} = \frac{abc}{a_1b_1c_1}$. From the similarity of $\triangle CLB \sim \triangle B_1LC_1$ it follows that $\frac{a}{a_1} = \frac{|LB|}{|LC_1|}$. Likewise, $\frac{b}{b_1} = \frac{|CL|}{|LA_1|}$; $\frac{c}{c_1} = \frac{|LA|}{|LB_1|}$. So, $\frac{S_{ABC}}{S_{A_1B_1C_1}} = \frac{|LA| \cdot |LB| \cdot |LC|}{|LA_1| \cdot |LB_1| \cdot |LC_1|} = \frac{2r}{R}$ according to what was proven in points a) and b).

From the solution of the problem it is easy to obtain the inequality $2r \leq R$, as well as the correspondence $|AL| \cdot |LA_1| = 2Rr$, which is convenient to use when proving Euler’s formula $d^2 = R^2 - 2Rr$, where d is the distance between the centers of the inscribed and circumscribed circles of the triangle. A number of interesting consequences of the results of the problem can be obtained by replacing that the lines $A_1A, B_1B, C_1C$ contain the heights $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$; one of them: the area of ​​a triangle is equal to the product of the circumscribed circle and the perimeter of the triangle, determined by the bases of the heights.

R. A. Mazov

M771. Triangle ABC contains bissertrix AK. It is known that the centers of the circles inscribed in triangle ABK and circumscribed about triangle ABC coincide. Find the corners of triangle ABC.

Answer: $\widehat{A} = \widehat{C} = 72^\circ, \widehat{B} = 36^\circ$. Let O be the common center indicated in the condition of the circles. Then AO and BO are bissertrices of the angles KAB and ABC, respectively (see Fig. 1). In addition, point O is equilateral to the vertices of triangle ABC, so triangles AOB, BOC and COA are equilateral. Therefore, if $\widehat{OAB} = \alpha$, then $\widehat{OAK} = \widehat{OAB} = \widehat{OBA} = \widehat{OBC} = \widehat{OCA} = \alpha$. Hence $\widehat{ABC} = 2\alpha, \widehat{CAB} = 2\widehat{KAB} = 4\alpha, \widehat{BCA} = \widehat{BCO} + \widehat{OCA} = \alpha + (\widehat{OAK} + \widehat{KAC}) = 4\alpha$. And since the sum of the angles of a triangle is $180^\circ, \widehat{ABC} + \widehat{CAB} + \widehat{BCA} = 2\alpha + 4\alpha + 4\alpha = 10\alpha = 180^\circ$, and therefore $\alpha = 18^\circ, \widehat{ABC} = 36^\circ, \widehat{CAB} = \widehat{BCA} = 72^\circ$.
