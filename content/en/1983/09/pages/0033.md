Let some matrix point $M$ move along a circle of radius $A$ under the influence of others (Fig. 1). The spring is mounted on a rod that can rotate around point $O$. One end is attached to the connection point $M$, and the other is fixed to the edge of the rod. If the rod is rotated with an angular frequency $\omega$, then the pressure increases by a length $A$ and an elastic force arises, which will act on the point $M$. This force will give the point a centripetal acceleration equal to $\omega^2 A$, where $A$ is the radius of the circle along which the point will move. The modulus of the elastic force is, as is known, $k A$, where $k$ is the stiffness of others. So, according to Newton's second law,

$$m\omega^2 A = k A,$$

where

$$\omega^2 = \frac{k}{m}, \text{ и } \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}. \quad (*)$$

The time during which the point makes one revolution is called the period of revolution of the point and is denoted by the letter $T$. We can easily get the value of $T$ if we divide the circumference of $2\pi A$ by the speed $v$, which can be expressed in terms of $\omega$: $v = \omega A$, so that

$$T = \frac{2\pi A}{\omega A} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}.$$

**Bim and Bom**

In the circus arena there was a phenomenal calculator named Bim, who could perform in a reduced number. He had just raised two to the twenty-ninth power in front of an astonished audience. The result was a nine-digit number in which all the digits were different. Bim had ten cards with all ten numbers written on them. From nine cards, Bim laid out the number he needed on the arena, and put ten in his pocket. Arriving home after the performance, Bim told his brother, the mathematician Bohm, everything that was written above, and was about to take the card out of his pocket, but Bohm stopped him:

“Even though I can’t count as well as you can, I can say that you have a four in your pocket.”

How did Bim know this without calculating the nine-digit number?
