Check for yourself that conditions (1) - (3) taken together are not only necessary, but also sufficient.

Algebraic solution. Two real numbers $x_1 - d$ and $x_2 - d$ are positive if and only if their sum and product are positive. Therefore, the conditions of the problem are equivalent to the combination of the following three conditions:
$$D = b^2 - 4ac > 0,$$
$$(x_1 - d) + (x_2 - d) > 0,$$
$$(x_1 - d)(x_2 - d) > 0.$$
Using Vieta’s formulas, the second condition can be rewritten as
$$x_1 + x_2 - 2d > 0, \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} > d, -\frac{b}{2a} > d.$$
The third one is rewritten like this:
$$x_1x_2 - (x_1 + x_2)d + d^2 > 0,$$
$$a(ad^2 + bd + c) > 0, af(d) > 0.$$
Thus, we have again proved that the set of conditions (1) - (3) is equivalent to the conditions of the considered general problem.

Returning to Problem 5, we write down conditions (1) - (3) for it. We obtain a system of inequalities
$$\begin{cases} (r - 3)^2 - r(r - 4) = 9 - 2r \ge 0, \\ \frac{r - 3}{r - 4} > -1, \\ (r - 4)(4r - 10) > 0. \end{cases}$$
Solving it, we get $r < 5/2$, $4 < r \leq 9/2$. To these solutions we must add solution $r = 4$. Answer:
$$] -\infty, \frac{5}{2} \cup [4, \frac{9}{2}].$$

Exercise

6. Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be the roots of the equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$. Find $p$ and $q$ if $x_1 + 1$ and $x_2 + 1$ are known to be roots of the equation $x^2 - p^2x + pq = 0$.

7. The graph of the quadratic function $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ cuts out the segments $[AB]$ and $[CD]$ on two parallel lines. Prove that the line passing through the midpoints of these segments is parallel to the ordinat axis.

8. It is known that the square trinomial $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ has no roots and its coefficients are related to the relations $a - b + c < 0$. Identify the sign $c$.

9. Let the roots $x_1$ and $x_2$ of the square trinomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ be different. Prove that the number $x_0$ is between $x_1$ and $x_2$ if and only if $a(ax_0^2 + bx_0 + c) < 0$.

10. Let the equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ have no non-negative roots and $a < 0$. Identify the sign $c$.

11. Let the coefficients of the equation $x^2 + p_1x + q_1 = 0$ and $x^2 + p_2x + q_2 = 0$ be related to the relations $p_1p_2 = 2(q_1 + q_2)$. Prove that at least one of these equations has roots.

12. Can the equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$, where $p$ and $q$ are the difference in number, have the following roots:
a) $x_1 = \sqrt{3}, x_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$?
b) $x_1 = \sqrt{3} + 2, x_2 = 2 - \sqrt{3}$?
13. Prove that any fractional root of the equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$ with integer coefficients $p$ and $q$ is an integer.
14. Equations $x^2 + p_1x + q_1 = 0$ and $x^2 + p_2x + q_2 = 0$ with integer coefficients $p_i, q_i$ ($i = 1, 2$) have a common non-integer root. Prove that $p_1 = p_2, q_1 = q_2$.
15. Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be the roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ and $S_m = x_1^m + x_2^m$ ($m$ is a positive integer). Prove the formula $aS_m + bS_{m-1} + cS_{m-2} = 0$.
