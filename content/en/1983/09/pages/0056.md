⟦folio 54⟧

Firstly, not all examinees firmly understood the fact that force arises only when bodies interact. A flying body interacts only with the Earth, and it is affected only by the force of gravity towards the Earth. Of course, when the body was thrown, some force was applied to it (it could be called the throwing force). However, we will consider the body not during the throwing, but after it has been thrown, that is, during the flight. It is important to remember that force cannot be “accumulated”: there is interaction between bodies - there is a corresponding force, when the interaction ends - the force immediately disappears.

Secondly, there is a fairly widespread misconception that the nature and trajectory of a body’s movement are determined only by the forces acting on it. In reality, force determines the acceleration of a body, that is, a change in speed. The speed itself at each moment of time is determined by two factors - the change in speed and the initial speed. In other words, the nature of the body’s motion depends not only on the forces acting on the body, but also on the initial conditions. In all problems of mechanics, the initial conditions are the initial coordinate and initial velocity of the body. In this case, the initial conditions should be understood as the angle at which the body was thrown and the model of the speed with which it was thrown.

Question 4. A body (a stone on a rope) moves in a circle in a vertical plane. What forces act on the body? How are they directed at the moment when the body finds itself at point A of the trajectory (Fig. 1, a)?

Typical wrong answer. Three forces act on a body: force

$$\vec{F}_T, \vec{N}, \vec{F}_T'$$

the elastic force of the rope and the centripetal force $\vec{F}_T$, which holds the body on the circle (Fig. 1, b).

Error analysis. The body in question interacts with the Earth (the result is the gravity force $\vec{F}_T$) and with the rope (the result is the elastic force of the rope $\vec{N}$). Thus, only two forces act on the body. The force that holds the body on the circle and gives it centripetal acceleration $a_u$ is the sum of the projections of these forces onto the direction of the radius (Fig. 1, c).

$$m a_u = N - F_T \cos \alpha.$$

These same forces, more precisely, the sum of the projections of these forces onto the direction of the tangent to the circle, impart tangential acceleration $a_k$ to the body:

$$m a_k = F_T \sin \alpha.$$

Question 5. A box of sand with mass $M$ located on a horizontal plane begins to move translationally after it is hit by a bullet with mass $m$, flying horizontally at a speed $v_0$. Find the speed of the box. The friction of the box on the plane can be neglected.

Typical wrong answer. Since it is not taken into account, the kinetic energy of the box together with the bullet stuck in it is equal to the initial kinetic energy of the bullet:

$$(M + m) v^2 / 2 = m v_0^2 / 2.$$

Hence,

$$v = v_0 \sqrt{m / (M + m)}.$$

Error analysis. When a bullet hits a box, part of its initial kinetic energy is converted into tello; therefore $(M + m) v^2 / 2 < m v_0^2 / 2$. To find the speed of the box, you need to use the law of conservation of momentum
