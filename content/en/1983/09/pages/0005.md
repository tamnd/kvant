Metals have memory!

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. A. ZAIMOVSKY

The phenomena of living and inanimate nature of the present are diverse and so closely intertwined with each other that, when faced with something new in the inorganic world, we involuntarily look for an analogue with the manifestations of human life and activity. This desire is reflected in scientific terminology. Terms such as “lifetime” or “settled life time” (in relation to parts), “survivability”, “fatigue” (in relation to structural materials) are considered strict and have become generally accepted along with hundreds of others similar to them in this sense. It is no coincidence that the relatively recently discovered property of metal alloys is called “shape memory.” Can metals remember, remember, score? Let's try to attribute these abilities of living beings to metals, and then we will see that metals are even capable of being themselves!

Elastic and plastic deformation

First, let us remember what we know from the school physics course about the deformation of metals, and agreements on some notations.

If a load is applied to a metal sample—an external force that imparts acceleration to one part of the body relative to another—then the sample will change its size and shape—it will deform. Deformation is usually characterized by the ratio of the change Δl in the sample size to the initial size l: ε = Δl/l. The specific measure of load is stress - the ratio of the magnitude of the force acting on the sample to the cross-sectional area of ​​the sample.
