How is metal memory used?

The possibilities for practical applications of alloys with a unique shape-filling property are extremely diverse and tempting. The building before the designers is a wide field of activity, dotted with fundamentally new engineering solutions. For example, in space technology, these alloys effectively solve the traditional problem of saving space in a building. Antennas, energy emitters, etc., folded or twisted into a compact shape and placed in the small bottoms of the space corpus, are straightened after the vehicle is launched into orbit from the action of solar heat. Memory alloys can be used to create a space radiotelexan - a compact motor of thin wiring unfolds when heated into a circle with a diameter of about 2 km!

Of course, memory alloys have many uses on Earth as well. Their ability to lift loads when heated opens up the possibility of creating engines for the spicy transformation of the body into mechanical work. Models of such engines have already been built. Their efficiency is low, but for their operation it is possible to use low-temperature heat sources - solar energy, thermal waste from industrial enterprises, etc.

This same property of memory alloys has found application in creating joints in a way that replaces welding, soldering and other traditional methods. Let's say we need to connect two tubes to create, say, an airplane engine fuel line. We take a bushing made of a low-temperature memory alloy, the inside diameter of which is 4% less than the outer diameter of the three-piece connection (figure 5). In liquid nitrogen (-196 °C) we deform the bushing so that its inner diameter becomes 4% larger than the outer diameter of the three-hole bushing. Now we can insert bushings into the ends of the three-pieces, which, upon warming up to room temperature, recalls its previous shape, bends and compresses the ends of the three-pieces, providing a firmly sealed connection. Here, perfect from an engineering point of view, especially the manifestations of metal memory are used. After all, all normal, that is, “forgetful” metals, when heated, expand in all directions.
