⟦folio 21⟧

Chemical geometry

Chemical compounds, especially organic ones, have a complex structure. A clear example of this is the deoxyribonucleic acid molecule, which is two polymer chains twisted into a double helix (see, for example, the stats of M.D. Frank-Kamenetsky “The most important molecule” in «Kvant» No. 8 for 1982 and “The left-handed DNA helix” in «Kvant» No. 6 for 1983).

Finding out the structure of complex molecules created by nature and understanding their geometry is not so easy. However, now chemists have learned not only to dissolve known compounds, but also to create new ones, the geometry of which is unknown. We will talk about two implemented geometric “projects” later. Reports about them appeared in scientific journals at the end of 1982.

The first such compound was called “dodecahedrane”. Its molecules have the shape of a dodecahedron - a regular dodecahedron (see figure at the beginning of the article). Each molecule consists of 40 atoms: 20 carbon atoms located at the vertices of the dodecahedron, and 20 hydrogen atoms attached to the carbon atoms.

The story of how to “assemble” such a polyhedron, how to monitor the correct “assembly” at each stage, deserves a separate story. Let us only note that molecules with such symmetry have not yet been known, and in books such symmetry was discussed only for the canvas of a picture. Now this is not the case, dodecahedrane is a completely real substance, although so far obtained in very small quantities. It is a white powder that begins to melt at a temperature of 450 °C.

The molecules of the second non-volatile compound obtained by chemists have the form of a Möbius strip. Let's try to explain how they work.

Among organizan compounds there are many whose molecules are rings. For example, some ethers. Ether rings are often combined in several pieces to form a monomer chain connected by double bond bridges. At their ends there are active groups that can be combined into ribbon-shaped polymer chains. However, if there are few monomers in the solution, they, by bending, can connect with themselves, forming a ring. In this case, in approximately half of the cases, not a simple ring is formed, but a twisted one. This is how the first chemical Möbius strip works.

To our geometric collection we should also add catenanes - recently obtained ring-shaped molecules in which the rings are not chemically linked, but connected, like in a jewelry chain.

One can hope that over time the collection will be replenished with more and more new exhibits.
