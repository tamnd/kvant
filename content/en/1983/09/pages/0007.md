To better understand the essence of effective shape memory in metals, it is necessary to understand the mechanism of their plastic deformation. More precisely, we need to consider only the geometric aspect of this mechanism. Being crystalline bodies, plastic metals are deformed by shear

We cannot increase the size of the deck by pulling all the cards at once in a continuous direction, but the length of the deck will increase if we slide the cards along it one by one. In the same way, when the metal rod is stretched, the adjacent layers of the metal shift from each other. To ensure that the continuity of the metal is not disturbed, the layers separated by shear planes are gradually rotated in the direction of the load. These shifts, as we already know, are irreversible; they cause residual deformation.

Are there shear processes in metals that can occur without the occurrence of an external force? It turns out that they exist and have been using humans for 3000 years. Even in ancient Greece and Rome, the phenomenon of hardening steel was used to give it special hardness and strength. However, only in the 20th century it became clear that this change in the properties of steel was associated with the transformation of one crystalline modification of iron into another.
