For most metals, $E$ is of the order of magnitude $10^{10}-10^{11}$ N/m$^2$, and the order of $\sigma_{\text{упр}}$ is usually $10^7-10^8$ N/m$^2$; therefore, the maximum elastic deformation is most often of the order of magnitude $10^{-3}$, that is, a fraction of a percent. The formal feature of elastic deformation is its reversibility: the deformation disappears upon unloading, and the sample returns to its original size and shape. By the way, this means that, being under any stress not exceeding $\sigma_{\text{упр}}$, the metal remembers its original shape and restores it immediately after the stress is removed. For example, what if we increase the stress and the stress goes beyond $\sigma_{\text{упр}}$ and the strain goes beyond $\varepsilon_{\text{упр}}^{\max}$? Elastic deformation continues to increase in direct proportion to the growing stress value (in accordance with Hooke's law), but a second component of deformation appears - plastic. The total amount of deformation corresponding to a certain point $B$ on figure 1 now consists of $\varepsilon_{\text{упр}}$, which will disappear during unloading (the unloading line $BC$ appears parallel to the elastic section $AO$), and $\varepsilon_{\text{ост}}$ - the residual deformation that remains when the stress is removed. From figure 1 it is clear that $\varepsilon_{B\text{упр}}^{\max}$ is greater than $\varepsilon_{\text{упр}}^{\max}$, so the symbol “max” here does not mean that it is impossible to obtain an elastic deformation greater than the value. It only means that if the total deformation exceeds $\varepsilon_{\text{упр}}^{\max}$, then it will no longer be purely elastic and the shape of the body after unloading will differ from the original one.
