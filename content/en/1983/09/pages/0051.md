F822. Your owner, with whom you are sitting opposite each other, wears glasses. Can you determine what kind of vision defect - myopia or farsightedness - he has?
(Naturally, you will not ask the owner to let you try on his glasses and you will not start a conversation about them).

In a nearsighted person, the optical power of the eyes is greater than normal, while in a farsighted person it is less. Therefore, if you are nearsighted, you wear glasses with diverging lenses, and if you are farsighted, you wear glasses with converging lenses.

In principle, the optical power of glasses could be judged by the change in the apparent size of the owner’s eyes behind the glasses: behind a diverging lens, the eyes appear smaller than their true size, and behind a converging lens, the eyes appear larger. However, if the optical power of the glasses is low or if you have not seen your owner without glasses, then this method is not suitable.

The easiest way to answer the question of the problem is by observing the mixing of the edge of the face visible behind the glasses in relation to the parts of the face not covered by glasses: for a myopic person - inside (Fig. 1, a), and for a far-sighted person - outwards (Fig. 2, b). The edge of the glass of glasses can be thought of as a prism that deflects rays. Figures 1, b and 2, a show the path of rays incident on the edge of the diverging and converging lenses, respectively.
