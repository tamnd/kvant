Question 6. Does the melting of ice floes floating along the river during ice drift increase the water level in the river?

Typical wrong answer. Yes, it does.

Error analysis. When an ice floe floats in water, its gravitational force is balanced by the Archimedean force, equal to the gravitational force of water in the volume displaced by the ice floe. After the ice floe melts, it turns into water, the volume of which is exactly equal to the volume of the underwater part of the ice floe. It follows that when the ice floes melt, the water level in the river does not change.

Question 7. The gas in the sauce with a volume of $V_0$ at a pressure of $p_0$ is opened with a piston pump having a working chamber with a volume of $V$ (Fig. 2). How many piston strokes must be made for the gas pressure in the sauce to rise to $p$?

Typical wrong answer. Let the required number of moves be $n$. Considering that the volume of gas increased by $nV$, and the temperature of the gas remained unchanged, in accordance with the Boyle-Mariotte law we obtain
$$ p_0V_0 = p(V_0 + nV). $$
This is where we find $n$.

Error analysis. The Boyle-Mariotte law is valid only when not only the temperature, but also the mass of the gas remains unchanged. In this case, after each stroke of the piston, part of the gas leaves the system; when the piston moves from right to left, it closes cap A and opens cap B, through which the gas leaves the system (see Fig. 2). Therefore, it is necessary to consider each of the piston strokes separately. Let's start with the first move. For the mass of gas that was initially in the sauce, we write the Boyle-Mariotte law:
$$ p_0V_0 = p_1(V_0 + V), $$
where $p_1$ is the gas pressure when the piston occupies the extreme right position. When the piston returns to its original left position, a mass of gas remains in the vessel, changing compared to the initial one, but its pressure is $p_1$. For this mass of gas
$$ p_1V_0 = p_2(V_0 + V), $$
where $p_2$ is the gas pressure at the end of its piston stroke. Considering successively $n$ strokes of the piston, we obtain a system of $n$ equations:
$$ \begin{align*}
p_0V_0 &= p_1(V_0 + V), \\
p_1V_0 &= p_2(V_0 + V), \\
p_2V_0 &= p_3(V_0 + V), \\
p_{n-1}V_0 &= p(V_0 + V);
\end{align*} $$
Solving this system, we find
$$ p = p_0(V_0 / (V_0 + V))^n, $$
where
$$ n = \log_{V_0 / (V_0 + V)} (p / p_0). $$

Question 8. What will the voltmeter show in the circuit at figure 3 (see p. 56)? The resistance of the voltmeter is considered infinitely large, and the resistance of the connecting wires can be exceeded.

Typical wrong answer. The voltmeter will show the EMF of the element: $U = \mathcal{E}$.

Error analysis. In circuit ABCD (see Fig. 3) current flow
$$ I = 2\mathcal{E} / (2r) = \mathcal{E} / r, $$
where $r$ is the internal resistance of each element. A voltmeter connected to the element terminals shows the voltage
$$ U = \mathcal{E} - Ir = \mathcal{E} - (\mathcal{E} / r)r = \mathcal{E} - \mathcal{E} = 0. $$
