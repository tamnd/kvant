Let us imagine that a wave has formed on the lower surface of the liquid in an inverted state, the length of which is equal to the linear size of the hole in the vessel (figure 3). Let's calculate the change in fluid energy, that is, the difference between the energies before and after the wave occurs.

The surface area of ​​the liquid (see figure 3) increases by

$$\Delta S = S_2 - S_1 = 4\pi Rl - 4Rl.$$

The increase in energy surface due to an increase in surface area is equal to

$$\Delta W_1 = \sigma (2\pi Rl - 4Rl) = 2\sigma (\pi - 2)Rl.$$

On the other hand, when a wave occurs, part of the liquid sank, and accordingly the change in potential energy is equal to

$$\Delta W_2 = -mgh,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the descending liquid, $h$ is the distance at which its center of gravity dropped, $g$ is the acceleration of free fall (the minus in this formula means that the change in energy is negative, that is, the energy has decreased).

The mass of the descending liquid $m$ is easy to find - it is the product of the density of the liquid $q$ and the volume of half a circular cylinder of radius $R$ and height $l$. The distance at which the center of gravity of the liquid dropped is $h = 2d$, where $d$ is the distance from the center of gravity of half $2^*$
