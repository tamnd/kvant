There are many ways to define them. Physics 9 talks about two of them. One uses the property of some (very little) liquid to grow in the form of a film one molecule thick. In another, the particle size is determined using a complex device - an inn projector.

There is, however, a very simple, although not the most accurate, way to calculate the radii of molecules (or atoms). It is based on the fact that the molecules of a substance, when it is in a solid or liquid state, can be considered to be closely adjacent to each other. In this case, for a rough estimate, we can assume that the volume $V$ of the neotoric mass $m$ of the substance is simply equal to the sum of the volumes of the molecules contained in it. Then we get the volume of one molecule by dividing the volume $V$ by the number of molecules $N$.

The number of molecules in a body with mass $m$ is equal, as is known, to $N_A m/M$, where $M$ is the molar mass of the substance and $N_A$ is Avogadro’s number. Hence the volume $V_0$ of one molecule is determined from the equality

$$V_0 = \frac{V}{N} = \frac{VM}{mN_A}.$$

This expression includes the ratio of the volume of a substance to its mass. The inverse relation $m/V = \rho$ is the density of the substance, so

$$V_0 = \frac{M}{\rho N_A}.$$

The density of almost any substance can be found in tables accessible to everyone. Molar mass is easy to determine if the chemical formula of a substance is known.

The volume of one molecule, if we consider it a ball, is equal to $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$, where $r$ is the radius of the ball. That's why

$$\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 = \frac{M}{\rho N_A},$$

from which we obtain the expression for the radius of molecules:

$$r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3M}{4\pi\rho N_A}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3}{4\pi N_A}} \sqrt[3]{\frac{M}{\rho}}.$$

The first of these two roots is a constant magnitude equal to $\approx 7,4 \cdot 10^{-9} \text{ м}^2/\text{м}$, so the formula for $r$ becomes

$$r \approx 7,4 \cdot 10^{-9} \sqrt[3]{\frac{M}{\rho}} (\text{м}).$$

For example, the radius of a water molecule calculated using this formula is $r_b \approx 1,9 \cdot 10^{-10} \text{ м}$.

The described method for determining the radii of molecules cannot be accurate only because the balls cannot be placed so that there are no gaps between them, even if they are in contact with each other. In addition, with such a “packing” of the molecules of the balls, molecular movements were impossible. Nevertheless, calculations of molecular sizes using the formula given above give results that almost coincide with the results of other methods, which are unequally more accurate.
