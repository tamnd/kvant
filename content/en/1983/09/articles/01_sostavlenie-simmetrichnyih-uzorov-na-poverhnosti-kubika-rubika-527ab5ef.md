If you have already learned how to solve a Rubik's Cube (one of the assembly algorithms is given in the article “Cube in Cards” in this issue), you can try your hand at creating symmetrical patterns on its surface, for example those shown on this cover page. In Prince, this is not so difficult, but if in all cases (except for one) you manage to achieve (starting from the “correct” state) 40 “moves” - repeating faces, you can consider yourself a “grandmaster of the cube.” In an exceptional case, even 1000 moves will not be enough, because - don’t be offended by us - one of the patterns we depicted is completely impossible to obtain. (The article “Matematika of the Magic Cube” - «Kvant», 1982, No. 8 will help you discover it.) The solutions will be published in the next issue of the magazine. If you managed shorter solutions, send them to the editor.

Necessary follow-up: Each pattern is shown on both sides, so that all six edge cubes are visible. The initial calculation of the cube is as follows: the top side is blue, the bottom is green, the front is yellow, the back is white, the right is orange, the left is red.
