It is difficult to count how many times you have had to empty water from buckets, glasses, jugs and other vessels. Have you ever wondered why it pours out of there?

We foresee non-permission: “What is there to think about? This is completely clear! The force of gravity acts on water, and if the vessel is turned over, then under the influence of this force the water will lose its visibility. What could be incomprehensible here?

Let's do a few experiments that will show you that not everything is so simple.

Everyday routine 1. Pour water into a glass and turn it upside down. As you would expect, the water pours out.

Routine 2. Pour water into the same glass, cover it with a piece of paper, press the sheet tightly to the edge of the glass, turn the glass over and release the sheet. Water does not pour out.

This experiment is well known, and it can be explained simply: water does not pour out because atmospheric pressure prevents it. Under the influence of the weight of water, the leaf bends, the water level in the inverted glass is trampled, the volume occupied in the glass of air increases, so the air pressure decreases and becomes lower than atmospheric (figure 1).

The difference in pressure forces between atmospheric air and the air inside the glass is directed upward and is balanced by the force of gravity acting on the water. So the water does not pour out, although with the exception of a piece of paper, the conditions of this experiment - the dimensions of the vessel, the amount of water poured, atmospheric pressure, temperature, etc. - completely repeat the conditions of experiment 1.

Routine 3. Pour water into a bottle from two bottles and turn it over. Water does not pour out.

Recall that molecules on the surface have more energy than molecules inside the liquid. The attractive forces acting on a molecule inside a liquid from its neighbors are directed in all directions, and their resultant force is zero. And the resultant of the attractive forces acting on a molecule on the surface of a liquid is not zero. To move a molecule from the depths to the surface, work must be done against this force. Consequently, molecules lick up surface energy compared to molecules located inside the liquid. This

Let us imagine that a wave has formed on the lower surface of the liquid in an inverted state, the length of which is equal to the linear size of the hole in the vessel (figure 3). The surface area of ​​the liquid (see figure 3) increases by

$$\Delta S = S_2 - S_1 = 4\pi Rl - 4Rl.$$

The increase in energy surface due to an increase in surface area is equal to

$$\Delta W_1 = \sigma (2\pi Rl - 4Rl) = 2\sigma (\pi - 2)Rl.$$

How can one answer the question: “why does water pour out of water?” The correct answer can be formulated approximately like this: the radius of the city must be a critical radius, starting from which unstable waves can form on the lower surface of the water. Therefore, waves that accidentally arise on the lower surface of the water tend to increase their amplitude without limit, which leads to water pouring out of the inverted water.
