First of all, let’s try to clearly formulate the task. If you look closely at the samovar, it is easy to notice that the lid from under which steam came out and the tap from which cold water flowed are at different levels. This means that in the upper part of the samovar the water is hot, and in the lower part it is cold. A natural question arises: is it possible to heat water in such a way that it is hot at the top and cold at the bottom? So we formulated the problem. In a school textbook for grades 6-7, on page 142, an experiment is described in which water in a test tube is heated at the top, remaining cold at the bottom, if the test tube is heated only at the top. This occurs due to the flat thermal conductivity of water and the impossibility of convection with this heating method. Obviously, in the samovar, for some reason, the water was heated only from above. Since the samovar is a charcoal samovar, it can be assumed that for some reason the lower corners did not heat up, and only the upper ones burned.
