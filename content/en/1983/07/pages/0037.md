The reader, who has grasped the analogy between the tetrahedron and the triangle, is probably now expecting the following phrase: “All the points (perpendiculars dropped from the vertex to the opposite face) of the tetrahedron intersect at one point, called its orthocenter.” But, alas, this statement is not true for an arbitrary tetrahedron, but is true only for a class of tetrahedra called orthocentric. We will not touch on these classes in this article, but we hope to return to this topic in «Kvant».

The described paralleled (Fig. 1) helps to imagine a tetrahedron well in space, which is determined by the fact that the crossing edges of the tetrahedron are diagonals of its opposite faces. In this figure it is easy to “highlight” another important element of the tetrahedron: the common perpendiculars of the crossing edges - they form the heights of the parallelepiped (for example, the segment KH at figure 1).

Isohedral tetrahedra

As already mentioned, a tetrahedron is called isohedral if all its faces are constructive. To imagine an isohedral tetrahedron, different from a regular one, perhaps an arbitrary acute triangle made of paper and we will bend it along the middle lines. Then the three vertices flow into one point, and the halves of the sides come together, forming the side edges of the tetrahedron (Fig. 2).

Let us now list the properties of the tetrahedron, each of which is a necessary and sufficient condition for equihedral, starting with the definition.

(0) The faces are constructible.
(1) Crossing edges are constructed in pairs.
(2) Trihedral angles are constructive.
(3) Opposing dihedral angles are constructed.
(4) Two plane angles resting on one edge are constructive.
