Solution to Problem M793, 6 and the Hedgehog Theorem

We will call the vector perpendicular to the face $\Gamma$ of a given triangle, equal in length to its area and directed outward*), the vector of the face $\Gamma$. It is necessary to prove that the sum $\vec{v} = \vec{PA}' + \vec{PB}' + \vec{PC}'$ of the vectors of the three faces of the triangle $PABC$ is perpendicular to the four faces $ABC$, that is, that $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{AB} = \vec{v} \cdot \vec{AC} = 0$. Using the fact that the vector $\vec{PA}'$ is perpendicular to $\vec{PB}$ and $\vec{PC}$, $\vec{PB}' - \vec{PA}$ and $\vec{PC}$, and $\vec{PC}' - \vec{PA}$ and $\vec{PB}$, we obtain

$$\vec{v} \cdot \vec{AB} = (\vec{PA}' + \vec{PB}' + \vec{PC'}) (\vec{PB} - \vec{PA}) = \vec{PB}' \cdot \vec{PB} - \vec{PA}' \cdot \vec{PA}.$$

But $\vec{PA}' \cdot \vec{PA} = S_{PBC} \cdot |PA| \cdot \cos APA'$, and $APA' = \varphi + \frac{\pi}{2}$, where $\varphi$ is the angle between the edge $PA$ and the plane $PBC$ (Fig. 4); in addition, $|PA| \sin \varphi = H_A$ is the length of the altitude of the triangle dropped onto the face $PBC$.

Therefore, $\vec{PA}' \cdot \vec{PA} = -S_{PBC} |PA| \sin \varphi = -S_{PBC} \cdot H_A = -3V$. The same goes for $\vec{PB}' \cdot \vec{PB} = -3V$. Thus, $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{AB} = -3V + 3V = 0$. The equality $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{AC} = 0$ is proved in a similar way.

It is clear that the proven statement holds for any three vectors of faces, that is, the sum of the vectors of any three faces of a triangle is perpendicular to the four faces. But the vector of the four faces is also perpendicular to it! This means that the sum of all four vectors of the vector coincides with the direction of the perpendicular, lowered to $\Gamma$ from any interior point of the tetrahedron.

*) In other words, the direction of this vector coincides with the direction of the perpendicular, lowered to $\Gamma$ from any internal point of the tetrahedron.
