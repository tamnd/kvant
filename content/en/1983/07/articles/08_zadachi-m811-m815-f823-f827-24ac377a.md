⟦folio 43⟧

Tasks

M811—M815; F823—F827

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these tasks are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than September 30, 1983 to the address: 103006, Moscow, K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». In the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 7—83” and the numbers of the problem whose solutions you are sending, for example, “M811, M812” or “F823”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision verification in this envelope). The conditions for each original problem proposed for publication are assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.

M811. Let $h_a, h_b, h_c$ be the altitudes, $m_a, m_b, m_c$ the medians of the acute triangle (drawn to the sides $a, b, c$), $r$ and $R$ the radii of the inscribed and circumscribed circle. Prove that

$$\frac{m_a}{h_a} + \frac{m_b}{h_b} + \frac{m_c}{h_c} \leq 1 + \frac{R}{r}.$$

D. M. Milosevic

M812. Prove that for any natural number $n$

$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}} + \frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}} + \dots + \frac{1}{(n+1)\sqrt{n}} < 2.$$

S. I. Mayzus

M813. Given segments $OA, OB$ and $OC$ of the same length (point $B$ lies inside angle $AOC$). Circles are built on them as on diameters. Prove that the area of ​​a curved triangle bounded by two of these circles and not containing the point $O$ (see Fig. 1, p. 44) is equal to half the area (of the oblique triangle $ABC$).

V. V. Prasolov

M814. Let us note in the natural series numbers that can be represented as the sum of two squares of natural numbers. Averages of numbers occur in triplets of consecutive numbers, for example

$$72 = 6^2 + 6^2, 73 = 8^2 + 3^2, 74 = 7^2 + 5^2.$$

a) Explain why consecutive numbers cannot occur.

Prove that the averages of numbers occur in infinitely many 6) pairs, c*) triplets of consecutive numbers.

L. D. Kurlyandchik

M815*. There are $4k$ points placed on the circle, numbered in random order with the numbers 1, 2, ..., $4k$.

a) Prove that these points can be connected $2k$ by pairwise disjoint segments so that the difference in numbers at the ends of each segment does not exceed $3k-1$.
