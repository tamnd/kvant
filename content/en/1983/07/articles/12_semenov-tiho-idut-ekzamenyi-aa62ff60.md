Note that $\vec{O D}$—the vector of face $A' B' C'$ for terzer $A' B' C' D'$—is also the vector of face $A' B' C'$ for terzer $O A' B' C'$. Indeed, points $O$ and $D'$ lie on the same side of the plane $A' B' C'$ (otherwise the sum of $\vec{O A}' + \vec{O B}' + \vec{O C}' + \vec{O D}'$ would not be equal to $\vec{O}$), therefore the vector $\vec{O D}$ is directed to the outer side of the terzer $O A' B' C'$. By the “jezhe” theorem, the vector - $\vec{O D}$ is equal to the sum of the vectors of the faces $O A' B'$, $O B' C'$ and $O C' A'$ of the terzer $O A' B' C'$. But this terzar is constructed in exactly the same way (up to a parallel transfer) as the terzar $P A' B' C'$ in task $a$, therefore $\vec{O D}' = -k(\vec{D A} + \vec{D B} + \vec{D C})$, where $k = 3V/4$. Likewise $O A'' = -k(\vec{A B} + \vec{A C} + \vec{A D})$, so $D'' A'' = \vec{O A}' - \vec{O D}' = k(\vec{D A} + \vec{D B} + \vec{D C} - \vec{A B} - \vec{A C} - \vec{A D}) = k \cdot 4 \vec{D A} = 3V \cdot \vec{D A}$. In the same way it is proved that $D'' B'' = 3V \cdot \vec{D B}$, $D'' C'' = 3V \cdot \vec{D A}$. This means that terzers $A'' B'' C'' D''$ and $ABCD$ are prepared with a coefficient of 3V.

It immediately follows that for any four non-complan vectors that add up to $\vec{O}$, there is a unique terzar, up to parallel translation, for which these vectors serve as face vectors. (If $\vec{O A}$, $\vec{O B}$, $\vec{O C}$ and $\vec{O D}$ are given vectors and $\vec{O A'}$, $\vec{O B'}$, $\vec{O C'}$, $\vec{O D'}$ are vectors of the faces of terzer $ABCD$, then the desired terzar is prepared $A' B' C' D'$.) This statement is a very special and simple case of the so-called Minkowski problem: is it possible, given a set of non-complanar zero-sum vectors, to construct a convex manifold for which they would serve as face vectors? Will there be only one such variety? This problem was posed and solved (positively) by the eminent German mathematician Hermann Minkowski at the end of the last century.

Exercise
9. In terzarra $ABCD$ the faces $ABC$ and $ABD$, as well as $CDA$ and $CDB$ are equal in size. Prove that the segment connecting edges $AB$ and $CD$ is their common perpendicular.

10. Prove that the following properties of terzarra are equivalent*): 1) all faces are congruent; 2) all faces are equal in size; 3) the center of the circumscribed sphere coincides with the center of gravity (the center of gravity is determined by the fact that the sum of the vectors drawn from it to the vertices of the terzar is $\vec{O}$).

*) The termerdras discussed in this task are called isohedral. They are described in detail in the article by V. E. Matizen in this issue of the magazine; see also problem 8 above, d.
