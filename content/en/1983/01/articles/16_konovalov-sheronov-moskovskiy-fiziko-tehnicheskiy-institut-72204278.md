River meanders

Let us find the shape of the surface of a liquid rotating in a glass with an angular velocity $\omega$. Let's turn to figure 1 (see p. 18). If the linear size of the selected cube is $\Delta r$, then its mass is equal to

$$\Delta m=\varrho \cdot \Delta s \cdot \Delta r.$$

Using this expression for $\Delta m$, from equation (1) and (2) (see p. 18) we obtain:

$$\Delta h=(\omega^2/g)r \cdot \Delta r.$$

Thus, the tangent of the angle $\alpha$ tangent to the curve depicting the cross section of the free surface of the liquid at figure 1 increases in proportion to the distance from the center:

$$tg \alpha=\Delta h/\Delta r=(\omega^2/g)r.$$

If the height of the liquid level on the rotation axis is $h_0$, then in order to find it at a distance $r$, you need to add to $h_0$ the average value $tg\alpha$ multiplied by $r$. The average value of a quantity that changes according to a linear law can be easily found by taking the result obtained.

$$h(r)=h_0+\frac{\omega^2 r}{2g} r.$$

As you can see, the dependence $h(r)$ is square, and its graph is represented by a parabola. (Those familiar with integral calculus will, of course, understand that we calculated the integral in this way.)

It is curious that the parabolicity of the shape of a rotating fluid was used in practice to create a telecopole. The famous American physicist Robert Wood built a telecopole with a parabolic mirror by placing a vessel with mercury at the bottom of a rotating well.

Hence, the last digit can be either 0, or 4, or 8. In order for a number to be divisible by 9, the sum of its digits must be divisible by 9. From here we get for the digit replaced by the second asterisk, in the first case 9, in the second 5 and in the third 1. Thus, the numbers 52920, 52524 and 52128 satisfy the condition.

2. The yellow napkin was placed first, and the brown one was placed equal to the white one.
3. The problem is based on the same names for units of time and angles. In terms of “seconds” it introduces a unit of time, “minute” – a unit of angle.
4. For numbers 43 and 63. Let's create an equation expressing the condition of the problem: $10x + y = x^2 + y^3$. Let's transform it: $x(10-x) = (y-1)(y+1)$. The expression on the right is the product of three consecutive numbers and therefore $x(10-x)$ is divisible by 2 and 3. This implies that $x$ is even. Additionally, either $x$ or $10-x$ must be divisible by 3. This can be in two cases: $x=6$ and $x=4$. In both cases $y=3$. We get two solutions: $43=16+27$ and $63=36+27$.

Task 19. 1. Nf3 Nf6 2. Ke5 d6 3. Nc6 Kfd7 4. N:b8 N:b8.

Task 20. In three moves the position is very simple: 1. e4 e6 2. Cb5 c6 3. C:c6 dc (or 2. Cc4 c6 3. C:e6 de). It's much more difficult to get it in four moves. The goal is achieved by a very cunning and, moreover, single maneuver: 1. e4 e6 2. Bb5 Kre7!! 3. C:d7 c6! 4. Ce8! Kp:e8.

It is interesting that this problem was proposed to be solved in many large scales, but only Mikhail Tal could quickly solve it with a sum.
