OUTSTANDING
THEORETICAL PHYSICIST
XX century

(on the occasion of the 75th anniversary of the birth of L. D. Landdu)

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. I. KAGANOV

The 20th century—the century of revolutionary transformations in theoretical physics—will go down in the history of the creation of relativistic mechanics (relative theory) and quantum (wave) mechanics. It is rich in scientific names - the creators of new physics. One of these creators was Lev Davidovich Landdu. He influenced the development of theoretical physics both with his personal scientific results and the creation of an actively working school

theoretical physicists - “Skoda Landdu” (it was a concept given by the teacher), and “Course of Theoretical Physics”, started in 1937 and completed (by E. M. Lifshtz and L. P. Pitaevsky) in 1979. Whatever L.D. Landdu does, in any of his activities he carries out a special style inherent to him. In his own works there is a clear statement of the problem, mathematical study, brevity of the solution; in the “Course” - the optimal path from the fundamentals of theory to solving specific problems; in his presentation, complex questions are transformed into simple ones, illusory difficulties are dispelled.

It is impossible in a short article to fully characterize the activities of L. D. Landdu. We will have to limit ourselves to short notes, largely based on the author’s personal memories.

* * *
On the bookshelf of almost every physicist, both teacher and researcher, there is a volume of “A Course in Theoretical Physics” by Landdu and Lifshtz. The Chastlivtsy have all ten. And it's not for decoration. When preparing a lecture or in the process of direct work, one comes to them endlessly. Landdu's name has been included in many terms: Landdu damping and Landdu levels, Landdu diamagnetism and the Landdu-Lifshtz equations, Landdu's superfluidity criterion, Landdu's theory of second-order physical transitions, Landdu's singularities, Landdu's Fermi-liquid theory and the Ginzburg-Landu equation... But Landdu did not make arbitrary changes, did not work with instruments in the laboratory, but only as he himself said, “he wrote formulas on paper.” He was one of the great theoretical physicists of the world.

Landdu's abilities showed up very early. He said that he did not remember himself and did not know how to differentiate and interpret. At the age of fourteen (in 1922) he entered the Baku University, where he studied simultaneously at two faculties -

cultets - physico-mathematical and chemical; At the age of seventeen he graduated from the physics department of Leningrad University, where he transferred in 1924 (he did not continue his chemical education); At the age of eighteen, he published his first work - the theory of intensity in the spectra of diatomic molecules (one, number 1, is included in the “Collected Works” of L. D. Landdu). Then - graduate school at the Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute; in 1929-30 - half-yearly trains abroad: in Denmark, England and Switzerland. “The most important thing for him was his stay in Copenhagen, where theoretical physicists from all over Europe gathered at the Institute of Theoretical Physics under the great Niels Bohr and all the main issues of theoretical physics of that time were discussed at the famous seminars led by Bohr. This scientific atmosphere, fueled by the charm of Bohr’s personality, had a decisive influence on Lev Davidovich’s physical worldview, and in the future he always considered himself a student of Niels Bohr. He visited Copenhagen two more times: in 1933 and 1934.”*)

Bohr visited the Soviet Union three times. Meetings and long conversations with Landdu were part of Bohr’s mandatory program of stay in our country. The last time Bohr and Landdu met was in 1961 in Moscow. They took part in the traditional celebration of physicists at Moscow University, “Archimedes Day”.

In 1932, Landdu moved to Kharkov. Here he - twenty - headed the theoretical department of the new Ukrainian Physico-Technical Institute and at the same time completed the department of theoretical physics at the physical-mechanical faculty of the Kharkov Mechanical Engineering Institute, and from 1935 he completed the department of general physics at Kharkov University. Since the spring of 1937

Landdu in Moscow - heads the theoretical department of the Institute of Physical Problems, recently created by P. L. Kapitsa. Here he remained until the end of his life. The Institute of Physical Problems is mentioned and, in fact, was his home for many years. On one of this institute there is an emornal doxa: “Here, from 1937 to 1968, the greatest physicist, academician Lev Davidovich Landdu, lived and worked.” The windows of the current theoretical department of the institute, between which the emornal doxa is placed, are adjacent to the windows of his apartment. The main books of the “Course of Theoretical Physics” were written here (together with E.M. Lifshtz). At the Institute of Physical Problems in 1941, the theory of gel superfluidity was constructed - an extremely interesting phenomenon discovered in 1938 by P. L. Kapitsa.

Once (at the end of the fifties) I asked Lev Davidovich: “What work do you consider your best?” Answer: “The theory of gel superfluidity. There’s still a lot of misunderstanding about her.”

Scientific creativity of L. D. Landdu bespresdento on his shpot. It covers all theoretical physics from hydrodynamics to quantum field theory. Our century is the century of specialization. There are no scientists who have an equally good understanding of all physics. Landdu, apparently, was the last encyclopedist physicist. Both theoretical physicists, who deal with the physics of the created body, and astrophysicists, and those specializing in elementary particles, consider themselves to be his students. Their scientific paths diverged during Landdu’s lifetime, but he, their Teacher, led everyone around. All of them gathered every Thursday at 11 a.m. at the Institute of Physical Problems for the famous Landdus seminar. Not only Muscovites, but also Kharkovites, Leningraders, theoretical physicists from Kyiv, Tbilisi...

The seminar, which Landdu led, will go down in the history of theoretical physics. At its meetings, what was called the “Landdu school” was created and built. Here one could perform a work from any

Combat the field of theoretical physics. And not just to speak, but also to receive qualified advice either during the report or before - during a preliminary discussion with Landdu. The seminar was completely democratic. Lev Davidovich turned his back to the audience, in the first row, and although the speaker, as a rule, addressed him directly, he was not the Chairman, the Curator - no solemnity, no importance (on the day of Landdu's pentatel, A.I. Shalnikov appointed him “the most unimportant person” he knew). Everyone could be the first speaker, demanding clarification or expressing their disapproval. Landdu also took advantage of this opportunity. Naturally, it was difficult to report in such an environment. If it turned out that the work was unsuccessful or the speaker could not explain the essence of the matter, he was mercilessly deprived of the word. But we must remember: the reason for such “rigidity” was Landdu’s uncompromising attitude towards science. The correctness or incorrectness of a result does not depend on whether it was obtained by a close friend or completely constructed. Landdu defended the speaker from the attacks of the listeners. One could often hear: “Let’s listen further, the author is usually right.”

* * *

Theoretical physics studies nature using the methods of mathematics. Landd attached greater importance to theoretical physicists and mastery of mathematical techniques. The degree of proficiency should be such that mathematical difficulties do not distract from the physical difficulties of the task, and overcoming mathematical difficulties does not become an end in itself. “We need to start with mathematics, which, as you know, is the basis of our science... Keep in mind that by knowledge of mathematics we do not mean all the theorems, but the ability to integrate in practice, solve ordinary differential equations in quadratures, etc.” Or: “...a theorist first of all needs knowledge of mathematics. At the same time we need

As a method of study, they can only emphasize that it is necessary to carry out all the calculations yourself, and not provide them with the author of the books you read.”
These are quotes from L. D. Landd’s letters to young people who were passionate about theoretical physics and turned to Landd asking for help to figure out how to master the fundamentals of science. Lev Davidovich always responded to such letters if he felt the sincere desire of the author of the letter to be seriously engaged in science. L.D.’s letters contain not only precise advice and instructions to young correspondents, but also simple words of support, important at the beginning of the path, humorous, but necessary remarks: “Instructional languages, alas, are necessary. Don’t forget that you certainly don’t need any special abilities to be able to do them, since very poor English speakers speak English well...” “You shouldn’t be afraid of me - I don’t bite at all,”*) and then there’s an invitation to call, “It’s best from 9.30 to 10.30 in the morning, when I’m almost always at home, but you can do it at any other time... I’ll replace you and give you a program for further training.”

Lev Davidovich often spoke about the incentives of scientific creativity. A young scientist should not set himself the goal of discovering new laws. “Just don’t bother solving any problems. It’s easy to figure it out, and the solution to problems comes on its own.” Understanding physics is, it comes in the process of daily, seemingly routine work, brings joy and satisfaction, which pays off endless science.

Everyone who communicated with L.D. was amazed at his unabated interest in the various problems posed and solved by physics over the years. He talked about science with hundreds of physicists. They tell him about a wide variety of works, varying in difficulty, in depth, in significance, works that relate to a variety of objects - solids and elementary particles, stars and gases. Any work was of interest to him if it satisfied a simple principle: the work should unravel something incomprehensible. Sleeplessly explaining himself and endlessly posing new mysteries - this is how Landdu perceived the world. Essentially, from childhood, he had the mistake of the beauty of science about nature. “He tells how at that time (he was 16 years old at the time) he was shocked by the incredible beauty of the general theory of relativity... He carried this thought (a person is able to understand what he cannot imagine!) throughout his entire life.

The preoccupation with scientific tasks made Landda completely insensitive to the fashionable hobbies of the readership: Bigfoot, telepathy, flying saucers, etc. He considered such hobbies to be an existence and sharply ridiculed them. He was irritated by the chatter and superficiality that, as a rule, accompanied attempts to solve “mysterious” problems. However, after reading a popular article about the navigational device of birds, I tried to talk about this topic with Landdu. He was rather indifferent to my words and said that before we judge the correctness or incorrectness of certain hypotheses, we need to get to know the problem in essence, and not second-hand. I soon learned from the Nature magazine that the results of the experiments discussed by the author of the popular book could be refuted.

There is no need to think that Landdu was a “scientific cracker”, completely immersed in theoretical physics. Lev Davidovich's range of interests outside of physics was very wide. He loved history very much and knew it well, often acquainting himself with the details of historical events; he was interested in all types of art (though with the exception of music), he read a lot, knew it by heart and recited a lot of poetry well. Friends teased Landda, saying that he had childish taste. He loved Dreiser more than Heminghus. He liked everyday dramas in the theater. But...
