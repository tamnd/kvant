⟦folio 37⟧

Pyramids and cube

Y. A. SMORODINSKY

I wanted to play with a simple but beautiful puzzle that I came across in one of Martin Gardner's articles.

I went to a mechanical workshop and asked them to make three parts for me according to two drawings (Fig. 1). The mechanic I contacted was almost offended, thinking that I was playing him stupidly. But I didn’t even think of joking - the drawings were made according to all the rules of technical drawing (see Fig. 2).

(Without looking any further, try to draw these details or imagine what they look like.)

Trying to make the task easier, I decided to make only three parts together from six according to a different drawing (Fig. 3). Since this drawing turned out to be difficult to decipher right away, I did not test the mechanic’s patience and glued together models from paper myself, from which it would be easy to make them from metal. But they never got around to it: the paper parts were quite suitable for the puzzle. (You can make these parts using the reamer shown in figure 4).

The parts themselves are shown on figure 5. The reader will figure out which drawing is used to glue which part.
