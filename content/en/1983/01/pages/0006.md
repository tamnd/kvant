On the basis of mathematical analysis in the 18th century, the foundations of theoretical (or, as they said then, analytical) mechanics were created. The motion of a system of point materials and a rigid body was described by a system of differential equations. These equations made it possible, knowing the forces acting on individual machine parts, to determine how they would move. Theoretical mechanics has become the mathematical apparatus that allows the operation of machines even before they were made in metal.

In modern production, mathematical methods are extremely widely used. First of all, before its creation, any technical system - machine, device, apparatus, etc. - must be designed; it is necessary to calculate the state of its components under mechanical, thermal, electrical and other influences. This is a big and large job, often far from tra

faret character. Further, these units must be designed for operational reliability, since equipment that is not reliable enough will stand up for itself during operation. Nowadays the development of reliability theory is quite advanced, and mathematical methods occupy a gray place in it.

The design has been created and tested, but it usually needs to be produced in large numbers. To do this, it is necessary to organize arbitrariness so that all components and parts are produced in the required quantity and proper quality. In addition, discretion is required to organize the technological process in such a way that the units do not require unproductive transportation or carrying. The location of workshops and machine shops within workshops should also be calculated to avoid unnecessary transportation.

We started the random process. We should organize it in such a way that during the process of making our repairs, we replace products that are not of sufficiently high quality (not to mention defective). In other words, we must learn to manage quality in the production process. This is a very large and important task, especially now that we are attempting to manufacture our products in tens of thousands and even millions of copies.

The production of insufficiently high-quality (and therefore insufficiently reliable) equipment products for society due to the enormous needs of equipment, the need to manufacture a large number of spare parts and the attraction of labor not for the manufacture of new products, but for the repair of spoiled manufactured ones. Calculating losses from quality degradation also requires mathematical calculations, which often cannot be reduced to purely arithmetic ones. To do this, you have to create mathematical models, collect and process data in copies, and evaluate the feasibility of improving the quality of certain components.
