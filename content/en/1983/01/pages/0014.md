⟦folio 12⟧

OUTSTANDING
THEORETICAL PHYSICIST
XX century

(on the occasion of the 75th anniversary of the birth of L. D. Landdu)

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. I. KAGANOV

The 20th century—the century of revolutionary transformations in theoretical physics—will go down in the history of the creation of relativistic mechanics (relative theory) and quantum (wave) mechanics. It is rich in scientific names - the creators of new physics. One of these creators was Lev Davidovich Landdu. He influenced the development of theoretical physics both with his personal scientific results and the creation of an actively working school

theoretical physicists - “Skoda Landdu” (it was a concept given by the teacher), and “Course of Theoretical Physics”, started in 1937 and completed (by E. M. Lifshtz and L. P. Pitaevsky) in 1979. Whatever L.D. Landdu does, in any of his activities he carries out a special style inherent to him. In his own works there is a clear statement of the problem, mathematical study, brevity of the solution; in the “Course” - the optimal path from the fundamentals of theory to solving specific problems; in his presentation, complex questions are transformed into simple ones, illusory difficulties are dispelled.

It is impossible in a short article to fully characterize the activities of L. D. Landdu. We will have to limit ourselves to short notes, largely based on the author’s personal memories.

* * *
On the bookshelf of almost every physicist, both teacher and researcher, there is a volume of “A Course in Theoretical Physics” by Landdu and Lifshtz. The Chastlivtsy have all ten. And it's not for decoration. When preparing a lecture or in the process of direct work, one comes to them endlessly. Landdu's name has been included in many terms: Landdu damping and Landdu levels, Landdu diamagnetism and the Landdu-Lifshtz equations, Landdu's superfluidity criterion, Landdu's theory of second-order physical transitions, Landdu's singularities, Landdu's Fermi-liquid theory and the Ginzburg-Landu equation... But Landdu did not make arbitrary changes, did not work with instruments in the laboratory, but only as he himself said, “he wrote formulas on paper.” He was one of the great theoretical physicists of the world.
