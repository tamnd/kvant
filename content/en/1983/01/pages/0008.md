Based on the problem of equipment disassembly, A. N. Kolmogorov and his student A. N. Shirev began developing the theory of control of random processes, which has now become one of the central directions in the development of probability theory. The task is formulated as follows: as a result of the impacts on the equipment, the voluntary process gradually departs from the normal level. This evasion can reach such a level that further arbitrariness becomes impractical, since the breakdown of the equipment has become too great. Methods are being developed that would make it possible to promptly replace the moment when the discord has not yet occurred, but there is a danger that it may soon occur. This problem can be given many other applied formulations. In particular, the issues of quality management are directly related to this task and Kolmogorov’s theory and it was tested. Teler on STANCE-automation produces hundreds of products in a short time. Initial verification takes much more time than production. It is easy to immediately increase the number of inspectors who do not produce anything and are only busy checking manufactured products. For some types of products, checking the quality of each manufactured product would lead to the following picture: for each random worker you need to have 10-15 inspectors, and yet there is no complete confidence in the accuracy of the quality assessment of the produced batch. But this is still not enough. There are types of arbitrariness (say, photographic film, matches) in which it is impossible to check the quality of each manufactured product, since the check renders the product unsuitable for further use.

Finally, continuous testing of manufactured products requires more money and testing stands are available.

Another accessible and reliable method of quality assessment is needed that does not have these disadvantages. This method has been proposed and is called statistical acceptance control. It is based on the laws of probability theory and is currently widely used in industry.
