How river meanders form

The shape of the river bed is largely determined by the terrain. A river flowing through a nervous area also meanders like a river, in order to avoid high places and fill lowlands, it chooses a path with a maximum slope.

What about the text of the river on the plains? How does the instability of the straight flow of the river described above in relation to bends affect the shape of the channel? This instability causes the length of the river to increase and the river begins to meander. It is natural to think that in the ideal case (perfectly homogeneous terrain) a periodic curve should arise. What is its shape?

Geologists have suggested that the beds of rivers flowing along the plains, at bends, should use the form of ... a curved ruler.
