Ziganshin's solution is based on four auxiliary statements that are of interest in themselves. They are formulated in the form of tasks, think about them yourself.

Task 1. If the face KLM has a perimeter that does not change than the perimeter of each of the other three faces of the triad KLMN, then

$$P_{KLMN} \leq 2P_{KLM}.$$

Task 2. If triangle KLM lies inside a convex broken line Г, then

$$P_{KLM} \leq P_\Gamma.$$

Task 3. If the broken line Γ limits the projection onto the plane of the triad ABCD and, at the same time, $A_1, B_1, C_1, D_1$ are the projections of the vertices of the triad, then

$$P_\Gamma < \frac{2}{3}P_{A_1B_1C_1D_1}.$$

Task 4. If $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ are the projections of the vertices of a triad ABCD onto some plane, then

$$P_{A_1B_1C_1D_1} \leq P_{ABCD}.$$

From correspondence (1) - (4) the solution to the main problem follows immediately

$$P_{KLMN} \leq 2P_{KLM} \leq 2P_\Gamma < 2 \cdot \frac{2}{3}P_{A_1B_1C_1D_1} \leq \frac{4}{3}P_{ABCD},$$

Q.E.D. If you carefully apply and prove all the statements of problem 1-4, then you will certainly receive satisfaction from the naturalness and beauty of this solution.
