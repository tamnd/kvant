⟦folio 5⟧

Changing one type of product to another also requires mathematical means, since it is necessary to compare the reliability of new products with the reliability of previously produced ones, the cost-effectiveness of manufacturing and operation, as well as savings in materials. Often new designs, especially at first, turn out to be more expensive. However, if their quality is higher and their reliability exceeds the reliability of older products, then the release of such products is justified. Moreover, when the technical process becomes established and improved, the cost, as a rule, decreases.

In recent years, due to an increase in product output, the complication of technical processes and the conditions of the product itself, the need for rational production management has emerged. Managing many industries “manually” turned out to be impossible. So, when pulling steel wire, it will mix at the speed of an express train. It is simply impossible to notice emerging broaching defects with the eye. The physiological capabilities of the operator or controller are insufficient for this. What to do? The solution was found in the compilation of mathematical models of the production process, which included submodels that take into account one or another change in production conditions (temperature changes, the presence of emissions, high humidity, etc.). Without a mathematical model of the process, it is impossible to transfer control to an automatic device, since the automatic device does not understand the specified qualitative nature - do it better, process more accurately, adjust the heating temperature according to need. In order for the machine to control well, it must, at the appropriate moments, receive information from special sensors about the state of the production process parameters and, in accordance with the model, select the necessary control.

Since all control problems have many common EROs, there is no need to develop a special theory for each problem. Of course, it may be that a simpler solution can be found for particular control problems, but this does not remove the issue of constructing a general theory. Such a theory was proposed in our country by academician L. S. Pontryagin, and in the USA by R. Bellman. Ponttryagin's theory was called optimal control, and Bellman's theory was called dynamic programming.
