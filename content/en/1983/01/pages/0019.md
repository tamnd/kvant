⟦folio 17⟧

River meanders

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. G. ASLAMAZOV

Has anyone ever seen a river flowing straight without bends? Some section of the river, of course, can be straight, but there are no rivers that flow without bends at all. Further, if a river flows across a plain, it usually “winds”, and often the bends of rivers are repeated with a certain period. And at bends, one bank of the river is usually circular, and the other is flat. How to explain these features of river behavior?

Hydrodynamics, a branch of physics that studies the movement of fluid, is a very well-developed science in our time. But when applied to such complex natural objects as rivers, even it is not able to explain all the features of their flow. Nevertheless, many questions can be answered.

Einstein deals with the question of the reasons for the formation of meanders in the rivers. In his report “The reason for the formation of meanders in river channels and the so-called Beer’s law,” made in 1926 at a meeting of the Prussian Academy of Sciences, Einstein compared the movement of swirling water in a glass and in river channels. This analogy helps explain the tendency of river beds to take on a meandering shape.

Let's try to understand this phenomenon at least qualitatively. And we’ll teach you with a glass of tea.

How the jars move in a glass

Take a glass of tea, stir the tea well with a spoon, and then remove it. The water will gradually remain, and the jars will gather in the center of the glass. What makes them “run” to the center? To answer this question, let’s first find out what shape the surface of water takes when swirling in a glass.

From experience it is clear that the surface of the liquid is distorted. It's easy to see why this happens. In order for particles of water to perform rotational motion, an equal amount of all the forces acting on each particle must lead to centripetal acceleration.
