Our guest is a mathematical radio circle

S. L. TABACHNIKOV

Today Professor Scema and his permanent landowners: the boy Alpha and the girl Beta are visiting “Quait”. They are participants in the radio program “Scema’s Mathematical Radio Circle”. Classes of this non-educational radio club will be held once a month on one Wednesday at 16-00 according to the first weight-bearing radio program. And now Professor Scema, Alpha and Beta will introduce the readers of “Quait” to the topic of one of the classes of their mathematical radio circle.

Sigma. We will start with a problem from “Quite for Junior Schoolchildren” (No. 1, 1981): In the Soviet Union, the population is 260 million people. It would seem that on a map of the USSR with a scale of 1:1,000,000 (10 kilometers in one centimeter) a million times fewer people can fit than on the entire territory of the country, that is, 260 people can fit. However, from experience we know that even ten people will not be able to do this easily. Why?

Alpha. Yes, I solved this problem right away! After all, people live in multi-storey buildings, which means that per unit of the earth’s surface there is not one, but several people.

Beta. But in my opinion, Alpha, you are wrong. Take, for example, a densely populated building such as a school building. On the first of September, everyone in this building goes out into the courtyard to attend a ceremony dedicated to the beginning of the school year. Also, residents of other houses can be accommodated in courtyards and on the streets - they will not even have shade. And there will still be steppes, deserts, and tundra!

Sigma. Beta is right. You, Alpha, will have to find another explanation. In the meantime, I’ll try to compare the areas of the squares on sides 2 and 1.

Alpha. Well, of course - the area of ​​the first will be four times larger than the area of ​​the second. In general, when the size of any figure decreases by N times, its area decreases by N² times. It’s not for nothing that area changes in square units!

Beta. Now everything is clear! Man occupies a certain part of the area on Earth. When depicted on a map with a scale of 1:1,000,000, the areas of all figures are reduced by $(1\ 000\ 000)^2$ times, that is, by $10^{12}$ times. It is by this number, and not by a million, that populated countries should be divided. In this case, you get about one four-thousandth of a person and no paradox arises.

Sigma. Very good, guys, you figured it all out. I can only add that when solving the problem you used so-called similarity messages. The topic of our lesson is precisely similarity messages.

Now let's solve one more problem. Shot is poured into two identical buckets filled with water: coarse shot into the first bucket, and fine shot into the second. As much shot as can fit is poured into each bucket. Which bucket produces more water?

Alpha. Well, of course, from the second one, they pour small shot into it!

Beta. So what?

Alpha. Since the shot is small, the gaps between the individual shotguns will be small. Therefore, small fractions will settle more densely.

Beta. Although the gaps between large shotguns are large, the gaps between them will be smaller. So it is not yet known which fraction will settle more densely: small or large.

Sigma. Indeed, you need to find out which fraction will settle more densely: small or large. Let's assume for simplicity that the ratio of pellet diameters is two. Now let's look at the bucket of small shot through binoculars with 2x magnification. What will we see?

Alpha. We will see a bucket filled with large shot. When doubled, both the volume of the bucket and the volume of each fraction will increase by a factor of 8 $(8=2^3)$, but their ratio remains unchanged. This means that the density of small and large fractions will be the same.

Beta. It turns out that the same amount of water will be subtracted from the buckets. I wouldn't have thought that!

Sigma. However, this is the correct answer. And similar messages helped us get it. I just want to make a small clarification: our answer is correct if the size of the pellets is much smaller than the size of the bucket. If the pellets are large (or the bucket is very small), then our reasoning loses its validity.

The reason is that we neglected to disturb the correct location of the pellets near the walls of the bucket. And if there are more pellets, then the influence of the walls of the bucket can no longer be neglected.

Beta. Professor Sigma, what other problems can be solved using similarity messages?

Sigma. Similarity messages have proven to be very useful in biology. And they were first used to study the structure of animals by the great Italian scientist of the 16th century, Galileo Galilei. Galileo was interested in the question of what a very large land animal, such as a giant dog, might look like. In one of Galileo's books you can even find figure bones of such an imaginary dog.

Alpha. Is there much to think about here? Enlarge the general skeleton of the dog by 10 times and that’s it!

This means that the bones of a giant dog can withstand a hundredfold load. But the fact of the matter is that the load returns not 100, but 1000 times.
