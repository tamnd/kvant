First decision. We can assume that the length of the base side is 1. Let $x \geq y \geq z$ be the lengths of the edges PA, PB and PC. It is clear that $x+y \geq x+Z \geq y+z \geq 1$.

By the cosine theorem (see Fig. 1)

$$x^2+1=y^2+ax,$$
$$y^2+1=z^2+ay,$$
$$z^2+1=x^2+az,$$

where $a=2\cos \alpha (0<a<2)$. It follows that

$$\begin{cases}
x^2-y^2=y^2-z^2+a(x-y), \\
y^2-z^2=z^2-x^2+a(y-z), \\
z^2-x^2=x^2-y^2+a(z-x),
\end{cases}$$

$(x+y-a)(x-y)=(y-z)(y+z),$
$(y+z-a)(y-z)=(z-x)(z+x),$
$(z+x-a)(z-x)=(x-y)(x+y).

Легко видеть, что если какие-либо два из чисел $x, y, z$ равны, то равны и все три числа.

Предположим, что $x \neq y, y \neq z, x \neq z$. Переменная уравнения последней системы, получим после сокращений

$$(x+y-a)(y+z-a)(z+x-a)=(y+z)(z+x)(x+y).$$

Поскольку права часть (*) положительная, должна быть положительная и левая часть. При этом, если все три сомножителя положительных, то равенство (*) невозможно.

Следовательно, $y+z-a<0; y+z-a<0, a+x-y-a>0$. Кроме того, $(x+z)+(y+z)>2>2a$. Поэтому $(y+z-a) \times (x+z-a)=(y+z)(x+z)-(x+z+y+z)a+a^2=(x+z) \times (y+z)+a(a-(h+z+y+z))<(x+z)(y+z)+a(a-2)<(x+z)(y+z).

Therefore, the left side of the product (*) is less than the right side. Contradiction.

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} < 4.$$

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} \geqslant 4(x_0-x_n) = 4(1-x_n)$$

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = a > 0.$$

F788. A long smooth pipe of radius $R$ inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizon (Fig. 1). From point A along the inner surface of the pipe, releasing a non-sick body upward. The initial velocity vector of the body makes an angle $\varphi$ with straight line AB (see Fig. 1). At what minimum initial velocity will the body move without leaving the surface of the pipe?

The complex movement of a body inside a pipe at the left moment of time can be represented as a superposition of two independent movements - movement along these pipes and movement along a circle in a plane perpendicular to the axis of the pipe (see Fig. 1). The separation of the body from the surface of the pipe will affect only the second movement - the body will not move in a circle. Therefore, we will consider only this movement.

A body moving in a circle is affected by the normal reaction force $N$ from the walls of the pipe (vector $\vec{N}$ lies in the plane perpendicular to the axis of the pipe) and the “gravitational force” $mg' = mg \cos \alpha$. Let us write down the condition of a body in a circle:

$$mg' \cos \beta + N = \frac{mv^2}{R},$$

where $\beta$ is the angle that makes the radius vector of the point of the circle in which the body is currently located with the vertical》 $Y'$ (Fig. 2). In order for the body not to come off the pipe, the following condition must be met:

$$N = m \frac{v^2}{R} - mg' \cos \beta \geqslant 0 \Rightarrow v^2 \geqslant mg'R \cos \beta.$$

We will find the connection between the speed $v$, with which the body is currently moving in a circle, and the initial speed $v_0$, using the law of conservation of energy: for any value of the angle $\beta$ the corresponding relation must satisfy:

$$m \frac{v^2}{2} + mg'R \cos \beta = m \frac{(v_0 \sin \varphi)^2}{2} + mg'R.$$

From here

$$v^2 = v_0^2 \sin^2 \varphi + 2g'R - 2g'R \cos \beta.$$

Substituting this expression into (*), we find at what values ​​of $v_0$ the body does not come off the pipe:

$$v_0^2 \geqslant 3 \frac{g'R}{\sin^2 \varphi} \cos \beta - 2 \frac{g'R}{\sin \varphi}.$$

Since this condition must be satisfied for any value of $\beta \in [0, 2\pi]$, the final result is:

$$v_0^2 \geqslant \frac{Rg'}{\sin^2 \varphi} = \frac{Rg \cos \alpha}{\sin^2 \varphi}.$$

At any moment of time, the piston is acted upon by gravity forces $Mg$, force $p_0S$ of atmospheric pressure and force $pS$ of gas pressure. According to Newton's law, with uniform motion of the piston

$$pS = Mg + p_0S.$$

Therefore, the gas pressure in the cylinder is constant.

Let the amount of heat $q$ be supplied to the gas per unit time. Then, according to the law of conservation of energy,

$$q \cdot \Delta t = \frac{3}{2} R \frac{m}{\mu} \Delta T + p \cdot \Delta V.$$

Here $\frac{3}{2}R \frac{m}{\mu} \Delta T$ is the change in the internal energy of the gas, $p \cdot \Delta V$ is the work done by the gas ($\Delta V = Sv \cdot \Delta t$ is the change in the volume $\Delta V$ of the gas during time $\Delta t$). From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation it follows that

$$p \cdot \Delta V = \frac{m}{\mu} R \cdot \Delta T.$$

Thus

$$q \cdot \Delta t = \frac{3}{2} p \cdot \Delta V + p \cdot \Delta V = \frac{5}{2} p \cdot \Delta V = \frac{5}{2} pSv \cdot \Delta t.$$

From here

$$q = \frac{5}{2} pSv = \frac{5}{2} (Mg + p_0S)v.$$
