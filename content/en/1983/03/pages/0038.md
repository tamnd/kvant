Masonry. Since the potential of the outer sphere is zero, the potential difference

$$\Delta \varphi = \frac{q}{4\pi \varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right).$$

Hence the capacitance of the capacitor

$$C = \frac{q}{\Delta \varphi} = 4\pi \varepsilon_0 \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_2 - R_1}.$$

Problem 4. An uncharged metal ball of radius r surrounding a conducting shell of radius R with potential φ. What will the shell potential be equal to if the ball is grounded (Fig. 9)?

Before grounding, the shell potential φ is created by some charge q: φ = $\frac{q}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 R}$. Since the ball is not charged, it has the same potential. After grounding, the potential of the ball became zero. This means that some kind of charge has appeared on it. We find its values ​​$q'$ from the condition that the total potential created by charges q and $q'$ on the surface of the ball is equal to zero:

$$\frac{q}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 R} + \frac{q'}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 r} = 0, \text{ и } q' = -\frac{r}{R} q.$$

Now it is easy to find a new shell potential, which is created by the same charges q and $q'$:

$$\varphi' = \frac{q}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 R} + \frac{q'}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 R} = \frac{q(R-r)}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 R^2} = \frac{R-r}{R} \varphi.$$
