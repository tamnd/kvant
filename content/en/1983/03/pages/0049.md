Problem solving

M770, M775—M779; F788—F792

First decision. We can assume that the length of the base side is 1. Let $x \geq y \geq z$ be the lengths of the edges PA, PB and PC. It is clear that $x+y \geq x+Z \geq y+z \geq 1$.

By the cosine theorem (see Fig. 1)

$$x^2+1=y^2+ax,$$
$$y^2+1=z^2+ay,$$
$$z^2+1=x^2+az,$$

where $a=2\cos \alpha (0<a<2)$. It follows that

$$\begin{cases}
x^2-y^2=y^2-z^2+a(x-y), \\
y^2-z^2=z^2-x^2+a(y-z), \\
z^2-x^2=x^2-y^2+a(z-x),
\end{cases}$$

$(x+y-a)(x-y)=(y-z)(y+z),$
$(y+z-a)(y-z)=(z-x)(z+x),$
$(z+x-a)(z-x)=(x-y)(x+y).

Легко видеть, что если какие-либо два из чисел $x, y, z$ равны, то равны и все три числа.

Предположим, что $x \neq y, y \neq z, x \neq z$. Переменная уравнения последней системы, получим после сокращений

$$(x+y-a)(y+z-a)(z+x-a)=(y+z)(z+x)(x+y).$$

Поскольку права часть (*) положительная, должна быть положительная и левая часть. При этом, если все три сомножителя положительных, то равенство (*) невозможно.

Следовательно, $y+z-a<0; y+z-a<0, a+x-y-a>0$. Кроме того, $(x+z)+(y+z)>2>2a$. Поэтому $(y+z-a) \times (x+z-a)=(y+z)(x+z)-(x+z+y+z)a+a^2=(x+z) \times (y+z)+a(a-(h+z+y+z))<(x+z)(y+z)+a(a-2)<(x+z)(y+z).

Therefore, the left side of the product (*) is less than the right side. Contradiction.

Second solution. Let's construct the angle $a$ on the plane. On one of its sides is the segment $|OK|=1$, and on the other side we put aside the segments $OP=x, |OQ|=y, |OR|=z$. The resulting triangles are loaded with edges of the paramid, and $|PK|=y, |QK|=z, |RK|=x$.

From the triangle RQK we get $x=|RK|<|QR|+|QK|=y-z+z=y$, that is, $x<y$ - a contradiction!

M775. For which natural numbers $n \geq 3$ are there different natural numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ or $a_n-a_1$?

Answer: at $n=4k$ or $n=4k-1, k \in N$. Let's consider the sum $S=|a_1-a_2|+|a_2-a_3|+\dots+|a_n-a_1|$. By condition, all $n$ of its constituent terms are different and are contained between the numbers 1 and $n$, therefore $S=1+2+\dots+n=n(n+1)/2$. On the other hand, if we replace the modils of the differences of the numbers $a_i$ in this sum with the differences themselves with the corresponding signs and given similar ones, each of the numbers $a_1, \dots, a_n$ will be included in the sum with a coefficient of 2, -2 or 0 (since any of them is contained in exactly two modils). Therefore, $S$ is an even number. But since $n+1$ are numbers of different parities, the number $n(n+1)/2$ will be even if and only if $n$ or $n+1$ is divisible by 4, that is, $n=4k$ or $n=4k-1, k \in N$.

In general cases the numbers need to be specified: see Fig. 1 for $n=4k$ and Fig. 2 for $n=4k-1$.
