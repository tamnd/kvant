Problem 2. A sharp charge +q is placed at the center of an uncharged conducting sphere, the inner radius of which is a, and the outer radius is b. Where and what kind of charges will arise? Image of the pattern of direction lines and the electric field potential from the distance r to the center of the sphere.

The image is also a picture of the direction lines inside and outside the sphere when the charge +q is mixed at an arbitrary point inside the sphere.

When a +q charge is introduced, charges are induced on the inside and outside surfaces of the sphere. Let them be equal to q₁ and q₂ respectively. It is known that electric field lines (direction lines) always start on positive charges and end up on negative ones. Since there is no field in a sphere that conducts only the walls, there are no lines of force there. Inside the sphere, the field lines start at the exact charge q and end up at the induced charges q₁

inner surface. It follows that the total charge inside the sphere is zero:

$$q + q_1 = 0, \quad q_1 = -q.$$

In turn, from the electron density of the conducting sphere for the charge on its outer surface we have

$$q_2 = -q_1 = q.$$

Due to symmetry, the induced charges are distributed evenly over both surfaces of the sphere. The picture of the force lines has the form shown in figure 4.

To find the strength and potential of the field created by the entire system of charges, we apply the principle of superposition. The field outside the sphere coincides with the total field of point charges q, q₁ and q₂ placed at the center of the sphere. Since the induced charges are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign: q₁ = -q₂, the total field is created only by the exact charge q:

$$\text{при } r > b \quad E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}, \quad \varphi = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}.$$

There is no field inside the sphere, the intensity is zero, and the potential is the same at all points and is equal to the potential on the outer surface of the sphere:

$$\text{при } a < r < b \quad E = 0, \quad \varphi = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 b}.$$
