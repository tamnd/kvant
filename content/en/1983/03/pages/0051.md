M778*. Given an unequal triangle $A_1A_2A_3$. Let $a_i$ be its side lying opposite the vertex $A_i$ ($i=1, 2, 3$), $M_i$ the midpoint of side $a_i$, $T_i$ the point of tangency of the side with the circle inscribed in the given triangle, and $S_i$ the point symmetrical to $T_i$ with respect to the bisector of the angle $A_i$ triangle.

Prove that the lines $M_1S_1$, $M_2S_2$ and $M_3S_3$ have a common point.

The sides of triangle $M_1M_2M_3$ are respectively parallel to the sides of triangle $A_1A_2A_3$. We will prove that the sides of the triangle $S_1S_2S_3$ are parallel to the sides $A_1A_2A_3$. It follows that $\triangle S_1S_2S_3$ is prepared into it by parallel transfer. The second case disappears, or the circumcircle of triangle $M_1M_2M_3$ is larger than the circumcircle of triangle $S_1S_2S_3$. Consequently, the lines connecting the corresponding vertices of triangles $S_1S_2S_3$ and $M_1M_2M_3$ must intersect at one point - the center is being prepared.

Let us show, for example, that the lines $S_1S_2$ and $A_1A_2$ are parallel (see figure). If the relative bisector of the angle $A_1$ is symmetrical, the point $S_1$ will go to $T_1$, and $T_3$ to $T_2$,

the positional arcs $S_1T_3$ and $T_1T_2$ of the incircle of triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ are equal. Similarly, if the relative bisector of the angle $A_2$ is symmetrical, the arc $T_1T_2$ will transform into the arc $T_3S_2$. Consequently, the arcs $S_1T_3$ and $T_3S_2$ are equal, and the position of the points $S_1$ and $S_2$ is at the same distance from the straight line $A_1A_2$, that is, $S_1S_2 \parallel A_1A_2$. M779*. We consider sequences $\{x_n\}$ of positive numbers satisfying the conditions

$$1 = x_0 \geqslant x_1 \geqslant x_2 \geqslant \dots \geqslant x_n \geqslant \dots$$

a) Prove that for any such sequence $\{x_n\}$ there exists $n$ such that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} \geqslant 3,999.$$

b) Also find a sequence $\{x_n\}$ satisfying the specified conditions, for which for any $n$

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} < 4.$$

a) It is easy to see that the following inequalities are true: $\frac{x_k^2}{x_k} \geqslant 4(x_k-1-x_k)$. Indeed, multiplying both sides by the (positive) number $x_k$ and crossing all terms to the left, we obtain the obvious correspondence: $x_k^2 - 4x_k-1x_k + 4x_k^2 = (x_k-1-2x_k)^2 \geqslant 0$.

Adding the proven inequalities for all $k$ from 1 to $n$, we obtain that

$$\frac{x_0^2}{x_1} + \frac{x_1^2}{x_2} + \dots + \frac{x_n^2}{x_n} \geqslant 4(x_0-x_n) = 4(1-x_n)$$

If the sequence $\{x_n\}$ tends to zero as $n \to \infty$, then, as follows from (*), the inequality being proved is satisfied for all sufficiently large $n$.

If the sequence does not tend to zero, then by Weierstrass’s theorem on the existence of a limit for a monotonic killing sequence bounded with space

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = a > 0.$$
