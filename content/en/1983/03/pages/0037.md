Let us now consider the last question of the problem. When the charge $+q$ is mixed from the center of the sphere to some other point, the magnitude of the induced charges will obviously remain the same, but the charge distribution density on the inner surface will change. Negative charges will be redistributed so that the field at the top of the walls remains equal to zero. The density of these charges will be greater in that place of the inner surface, which is located close to the charge $+q$. In this same area, the inner sphere will be larger and the strength of the force lines will be greater. Since the sphere is a surface of equal potential, the lines of force are curved to approach the equipotential surface at right angles. The charges on the outer surface have nothing to do with the field of the inner sphere; there their total contribution is zero. The “external” charges are still distributed evenly. Thus, the field of the outer sphere remains unchanged; it does not depend on the charge distribution $+q$ of the inner sphere. The picture of the force lines in this case is shown in figure 6.

Problem 3. Two concentric metal spheres of radii $R_1$ and

$R_2$ have charges $q_1$ and $q_2$. Draw graphs of tension and potential versus distance $r$ to the center of the system.

Consider the case of a spherical capacitor ($q_1 = -q_2$) and find its em-capacity.

Let $q_1 > q_2$. Then

$$\begin{align*}
\text{при } 0 < r < R_1 & \left\{ \begin{array}{l}
E = 0, \\
\varphi = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{q_1}{R_1} + \frac{q_2}{R_2} \right);
\end{array} \right. \\
\text{при } R_1 < r < R_2 & \left\{ \begin{array}{l}
E = \frac{q_1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} r^2, \\
\varphi = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{q_1}{r} + \frac{q_2}{R_2} \right);
\end{array} \right. \\
\text{при } r > R_2 & \left\{ \begin{array}{l}
E = \frac{q_1 + q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} r^2, \\
\varphi = \frac{q_1 + q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} r.
\end{array} \right.
\end{align*}$$

The corresponding graphs are shown in Figures 7, a and 6.

A spherical capacitor has plate charges equal in magnitude and opposite in sign: $q_1 = -q_2$. Therefore, the voltage and potential in the external region vanish, and between the plates ($\text{при } R_1 < r < R_2$) they have the form

$$E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} r^2, \varphi = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right).$$
