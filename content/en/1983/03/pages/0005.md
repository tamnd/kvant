⟦folio 3⟧

Turbine room of the Krasnoyarsk hydroelectric power station.

One of the first electric motors was created by St. Petersburg academician B. S. Jacobi.

The phenomenon of electromagnetism served as the basis for the creation of the electromagnetic telegraph, which later became the main type of communication between cities and countries. The first electromagnetic telegraph was created by corresponding member of the Russian Academy of Sciences P. L. Schilling.

In 1831, the English physicist Faraday made a discovery that led to a revolution in the main fields of physics and technology. We are talking about the discovery of electromagnetic induction. This phenomenon consists in the fact that around a magnetic field that changes over time, a closed electric field and a corresponding electromotive force will arise. This electromotive force, like the electric field itself, is directly proportional to the rapid change of the magnetic field. Before Faraday, physicists talked about electric and magnetic fluids. The concept of a field, both electric and magnetic, was first introduced by Faraday. The discovery of electromagnetic induction made it possible to generate electric current. Essentially, any modern power plant is a question on the grand scale of Faraday’s laborator experiments.

The discovery of electromagnetic induction was perceived by the entire scientific world as a major scientific sensation and brought Faraday worldwide fame. There is a legend associated with this. The then Prime Minister Agglia learned about Faraday's discovery from the newspapers. To become more familiar with the existing discovery of the worldwide predicate word, the Prime Minister visited Faraday's laboratory. Faraday demonstrated to him his famous experiments using homemade primitive instruments (they are now demonstrated in physics classes in any high school). After watching the experiment, the Prime Minister exclaimed, turning to Faraday: “And these children's toys brought you such slavery?!” Legend claims that this exclamation was followed by the following response from Faraday: “Sir, I think that you will extract taxes from these toys!”
