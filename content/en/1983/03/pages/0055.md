$$\frac{R \cdot R_{06\mu}}{R + R_{06\mu}} + 2r = R_{06\mu}.$$

From here we find $R_{06\mu}$:

$$R_{06\mu} = r + \sqrt{r^2 + 2rR} = 0,05 + \sqrt{(0,05)^2 + 10^6} \approx 10^3 \text{ Ом}.$$

If voltage $U$ is connected to the input terminals of the circuit at figure 2, then after this additional link the voltage will decrease by amounts

$$\Delta U = 2rI = 2r \frac{U}{R_{06\mu}}.$$

This means that after one cell the voltage becomes $k = \frac{U}{U - \Delta U} = \frac{1}{1 - 2r/R_{06\mu}} \approx 1,0001$ times less, and each subsequent cell decreases by the same amount. This means that after $n$ cells the voltage on the remaining part of the circuit will be equal

$$U_n = \frac{U}{k^n}.$$

With our partition, the number of cells is $N = \frac{6 \cdot 10^5 \text{ М}}{1 \text{ М}} = 6 \cdot 10^5$; This means that the voltage at the end of the line is

$$U_N = \frac{U}{k^N} = \frac{100}{(1 + 0,0001)^{6 \cdot 10^5}} \text{ В}.$$

Let's transform the expression in the denominator:

$$(1 + 0,0001)^{6 \cdot 10^5} = [(1 + 0,0001)^{10000}]^{60} \approx e^{60} \approx 1,14 \cdot 10^{26}$$

(the expression in square brackets is an approximation of the value of the number $e \approx 2,718$ - the base of natural logarithms).

So, the voltage at the end of the line is

$$U_N \approx \frac{100}{1,14 \cdot 10^{26}} \text{ В} \approx 10^{-24} \text{ В}.$$

It is simply impossible to change such a value; the voltmeter will show 0.

Tension last line -

$$U_{N/2} = \frac{100}{e^{30}} \text{ В} \approx 10^{-11} \text{ В}.$$

What will change if we take a cell “long” not 1 m, but 1 cm or 1 km (or 100 km)? In our case, “shrinking” the cells is, of course, acceptable, but clearly does not make sense; but their sizes must be increased carefully - over a Linney length of 10 km (10,000 cells of 1 m each), the voltage drops by a factor of $e$, and it is clear that the “length” of a cell should, in any case, be many times less than 10 km.

Considering the chain to be infinite, we seem to have added many more cells to the end of it. It is clear that the calculation $R_{06\mu}$ was carried out correctly (the voltage at the end of the circuit is many times less than the input voltage, and when additional resistors $R_{06\mu}$ are connected, it is practically no less). But we didn’t really estimate the magnitude of the finding. Think for yourself what the resistance of the voltmeter should be so that our solution method gives an accurate result.
