F790. A block of mass m is attached to the wall using a spring of stiffness k. At a distance d from the block lies a second block of the same mass. What minimum speed must be imparted to the right block (see figure) so that after the collision of the bars the left block returns to its original position? The friction coefficient of the bars on the top does not depend on the speed at the height H of the water level in the vessel, the volume of the part of the ftsurra immersed in water is equal to V. The force acting on the ftsurra from the water is equal to

$$f = \rho_B g H - \rho_B g H_S,$$

where $S_0$ is the area of ​​the base of the ftsurra. Let us write down the ftsurra equilibrium condition:

$$F'(H) - Mg + \rho_B g V - \rho_B g H_S = 0,$$

where M is the mass of the ftsurra, $F'(H)$ is the absolute value of the normal reaction force from the bottom of the vessel at a given water level in the vessel. According to Newton's III law, $F'(H) = F(H)$; hence,

$$F(H) = Mg - \rho_B g V + \rho_B g H_S.$$

It is clear that after the ftsurra is completely covered with water, the schedule remains unchanged. Based on this part of the graph, we will determine $S_0$ (we select points $H_1 = 4$ cm and $H_2 = 5$ cm):

$$S_0 = \frac{1}{\rho_B g} \cdot \frac{\Delta F}{\Delta H} = \frac{1}{10^3 \cdot 9,8} \cdot \frac{0,1}{0,01} \mathrm{m}^2 \approx 10^{-3} \mathrm{m}^2.$$

Now you can determine the volume of the futsurra:

$$V_0 = \frac{Mg - F(H) + \rho_B g H_S}{\rho_B g} = \frac{F(0) - F(H) + \rho_B g H_S}{\rho_B g}$$

(we taught that at $H = 0$ $F(0) = Mg$). Taking a point lying on a linear portion of the graph—for example, a point corresponding to $H_2 = 5$ cm—we find:

$$V_0 \approx \frac{0,25 - 0,4 + 10^3 \cdot 9,8 \cdot 0,05 \cdot 10^{-3}}{10^3 \cdot 9,8} \mathrm{m}^3 \approx 3,5 \cdot 10^{-5} \mathrm{m}^3.$$

The density of the materials from which the ftsurra is made is equal to

$$\rho = \frac{M}{V_0} = \frac{F(0)}{g V_0} \approx 7 \cdot 10^2 \mathrm{kg/m}^3.$$

To study the shape of the ftsurra, we will find a small increment of force $\Delta F$ due to a small increment of height $\Delta H$ of the water level:

$$\Delta F = -\rho_B g \cdot \Delta V + \rho_B g S_0 \cdot \Delta H = \rho_B g (S_0 - S(H)) \Delta H$$

(we taught that small volume increment is equal to $\Delta V = S(H) \cdot \Delta H$). This shows that where F increases with $H (\Delta F > 0)$, $S(H) < S_0$; and vice versa, for $\Delta F < 0$ $S(H) > S_0$. At the points of the curve that correspond to section areas equal to $S_0$, $\Delta F = 0$, that is, at these points the tangent to the curve is horizontal. This condition is satisfied at points $H_3 \approx 1,7$ cm and $H_4 \approx 2,2$ cm and near the base of the ftsurra. (Readers familiar with differentiated can perform a more detailed analysis of the ftsurra form by taking the first and second arbitrary functions $F(H)$.)
