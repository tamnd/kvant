Birth of a quantum

Academician of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR
V. A. FABRIKANT

Presiding over a specific goal, the further light of which does not fade from the first failures, is a necessary prerequisite, although it is far from a guarantee of success.

M. PLANK.
Nobel speech.

This year, April 23, marks the 125th anniversary of the birth of Max Planck, who made one of the greatest discoveries in the history of physics. He discovered the existence of quanta. There is a not very worthy story that, while walking with his seven-year-old son, Planck said to him: “Either what I am doing now is complete nonsense, or we are talking, perhaps, about the greatest discovery in physics since Newton.” Indeed, the discovery made by Planck put his name on a par with the names of outstanding physicists in the world. But the path to discovery was long and difficult. It was about the beginning of all quantum physics, and it was not for nothing that Karl Marx wrote: “Every beginning is difficult - this truth is true for every science.”

The interesting question is whether the absence of these difficulties would be good for a person. Dostoevsky’s “Diary of a Writer” gives the answer: “Well, what would happen, for example, if ... suddenly a series of discoveries began to appear, such as that the sun stands, and the earth revolves around it (because, probably, there are many more discoveries of the same exact size, which have not yet been discovered, and our sages cannot remove them); suddenly all knowledge fell on a person, and, most importantly, completely for nothing, in the form of a gift? I wonder: what would happen to people then? Oh, of course, at first everyone would be delighted... But it’s unlikely that even one generation of people would be enough of these delights! People would suddenly see that they no longer have life, there is no freedom of spirit, no will and personality, that someone has stolen everything at once... People would understand that there is no happiness in inaction, that the thought that does not work will fade away... Boredom and melancholy will set in: everything is done and there is nothing more to do, everything is known and there is nothing more to know.”

Planck begins to solve the problems of black radiation

Planck began studying the problems of black radiation in 1894, already having extensive experience as a theoretical physicist. He spent a lot of time clarifying the formulation of the basic laws of thermodynamics, adding great rigor and thereby reducing the rigor of his thinking. At the same time, as we will see below, while performing his main scientific feat, he was forced to give up this rigor, which creates additional difficulties.

Planck began with an attempt to build a theory of black radiation based on Maxwell's electrons, considering the radiation of a body as a set of oscillators emitting and absorbing electromagnetic waves $^*$). He showed that the intensity of radiation $I_v$ is proportional to the energy $E_v$ of the oscillator emitting radiation from a given part. This result made it possible to move from considering black radiation to analyzing the properties of oscillators, which are more familiar objects for physical time.

In 1896, Wien, using, in his words, the “happy idea” of the Russian physicist V. A. Michelson, obtained a formula connecting the intensity of study with the frequency and temperature of the emitter:

$$I_v = a v^3 e^{-b v/T}$$

(a, b are non-tortic constants). This formula (called “Wien’s formula”) appears to be in good agreement with experimental data in the region of large values ​​of the v/T ratio. However, experimental data were soon obtained in the region of small values ​​of v/T (infrared part of the spectrum, high temperatures), which clearly contradicted Wien's formula.

It is clear that Wien's formula needs significant improvement, and Planck took up this task. He managed to solve it using the Western method. Planck presented the result obtained in his report “On an Improvement of Wien’s Law of Radiation,” delivered at a meeting of the German Physical Society on October 19, 1900. This is where Planck's famous formula first appeared:

$$I_v = \frac{a v^3}{e^{b v/T} - 1}$$

(1)

In “Scientific Auto-Evaluation,” published in 1955, Planck recalled: “The next day (after the report) in the morning, my colleague Rubens found me and told me that after closing the middle that same night, my formula was carefully compared with the data of his measurements and a satisfactory agreement was found everywhere. A coincidence was also found with the data of Lummer and Pringheim... Later measurements again and again confirmed the formula for radiation and, moreover, more accurately, the more subtle methods of measurement were switched to.”

Planck's scientific approach

Planck was by no means satisfied with the victory he had achieved. In the same autograph he writes: “However, even if the formula for radiation is assumed to be valid with absolute accuracy, it still has only the formulaic meaning of a successfully guessed law.”

For a mind trained in thermodynamic research, this situation was intolerable. In a speech given upon receiving the Nobel Prize in 1920, Planck said: “Therefore, from the day it (the formula) was found, I was occupied with the task of establishing its true physical meaning, and this question was brought ... to Bolshoi Man’s way of thinking. After several weeks of intense dark work in my life, the work of the dark dissipated and new, previously unsuspected distances emerged.”

Planck's results were due on December 14, 1900. This day should be considered the quantum's birthday, although Planck's report does not contain the corresponding term. In Planck's report, he referred to a larger paper of 1877, in which Bolshman used the methods of probability theory to construct the kinetic theory of gases. Planck decided to apply probability theory to the question of the energy distribution of oscillators, and then move on to the intensity distribution in the spectrum of absolute black radiation. All of Planck's attempts to make this choice within the framework of classical physics were unsuccessful. In search of a way out of this situation, Planck came to use the technique used by Bolshman as an intermediate stage of probabilistic calculations.

Bolshman in the mentioned topic introduced, as he wrote, a “useful fiction”, which consisted in the assumption that the kinetic energy of molecules can only have a discrete series of values ​​that are multiples of the same value ε. He wrote out an arithmetic progression of molecular energy values:

$$0, \varepsilon, 2\varepsilon, 3\varepsilon, ..., n\varepsilon.$$

Having carried out the appropriate calculations, Bolshman then moved on to the prelude, directing ε to zero and n to infinity.

inaccuracy, since in fact the kinetic energy of molecules forms a continuous series of values. Thus, the discreteness of the values ​​of the kinetic energies of molecules was simply an intermediate computational technique, devoid of physical meaning. Despite the formal nature of the technique used by Bolshoi, it obviously played a significant role in the formation of the views of Planck, who suggested that the energy of oscillators also forms a discrete series of values ​​that are multiples of the same value. Attempts to direct this value to zero did not give the desired result, that is, it did not lead to formula (1). Then Planck abandoned these attempts. In a letter to the famous American physicist Wood, Planck wrote: “In short, I can call this whole thing an act of despair. Because I think I am peace-loving and not inclined to risky adventures. But I had already been working on the problem of balance between radiation and matter since 1894, without achieving any success; I knew that this problem was of fundamental importance for physics, and I knew the formula that reproduces the distribution of energy in the normal spectrum; a theoretical explanation had to be found at any cost, and no price was too high.” And Planck paid this price by introducing into physics a perfect idea of ​​the discreteness of possible values ​​of the energy of oscillators, no longer as an intermediate stage due to the convenience of calculation, but as an essential element of the entire consideration of problems. This violated the basic principle of classical physics, according to which physical quantities always change in a continuous manner. Everyone believed in the truth of Aristotle's saying: “Nature makes no leaps.”

Taking into account the restrictions indicated by Wien for the type of dependence $I_v(T)$, Planck was forced to assume that portions of the energy of the oscillators should be proportional to the frequency:

$$\varepsilon = h\nu .$$

This is how a new function, the constant $h$, came into physics - Planck’s constant. The quantity $\varepsilon$ allows was called a quantum of energy. Using condition (2), Planck obtained an expression for the energy of the oscillator $E_v$, and then moved on to the integrability of radiation:

$$I_v = \frac{2\pi h\nu^3}{c^2(e^{h\nu/kT}-1)},$$

where $c$ is the speed of light, $k$ is Bolshiman’s constant.

Planck spoke repeatedly about the “tortuosity” of the path he had to take. Indeed, we were talking about deriving formula (1'), which represents an improvement of Wien’s formula. This was done through energy quantization. But Wien's formula diverged from experiment in the region of low frequencies, where $\varepsilon$ is small and quantization is of little importance. In the region of high frequencies, where $\varepsilon$ is large and the discreteness of the oscillary energy values ​​is significant, Wien’s formula gave good agreement with experiment. The appearance of unity in the denominator of Planck's formula ensured that the results of theoretical calculations coincided with experimental data in the low-frequency region. At the same time, in this unit the germ of a new technical branch was laid - quantum electronics, that is, the possibility of creating masers and lasers. But that, as I say, is another story.

No matter how important Planck's formula is, the idea of ​​quantization used in the process of its derivation and the introduction of a new fundamental constant turned out to be even more important. This was a real revolution in physics, which also had consequences such as the creation of the quantum theory of matter and radiation. The presence of the quantity $h$ in the physical formula means that this formula can only be obtained from quantum concepts.

The path that led Planck to complete victory was essentially illogical.
