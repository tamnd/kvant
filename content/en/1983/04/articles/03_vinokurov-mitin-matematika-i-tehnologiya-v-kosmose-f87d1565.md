Coordinates in space flight

Mathematics played an even more important role than the birth of aviation for the emergence of astronautics. Theoretically, cosmonautics K. E. Tsiolkovsky constantly used mathematics in his proofs of the occurrence of flight to other planets and in the projects of space trains (see «Kvant», 1982, No. 9, p. 12).

In addition to the theoretical justification and calculation of the rocket design, mathematics is necessary every second of space flight. We are already accustomed to hearing the standard phrase when announcing the launch of another spacecraft: “The coordination center is constantly processing information.” The processing of data on a ship's trajectory is based on an important mathematical concept - a coordinate system, invented in the 17th century by the Great French mathematician R. Descartes (Geometry 9-10, § 59).

The first practical application of the coordinate system, carried out by Descartes himself, was not entirely of a worldly nature: at one of Descartes’ lectures, an unknown random person knocked his feet; Descartes, not the first lecture, asked his assistant to go to the basement room under the auditorium and measure the coordinates of the noise source; the returning assistant set aside a certain distance from one wall of the audience and a certain distance from the other wall - randomly, sitting at the intersection of imaginary perpendiculars, he asked to leave. The demonstration of the practical significance of the rectangular coordinate system was very convincing.

In many cases, when a rocket moves, in addition to the three coordinates of its center of mass, it is necessary to know three more angles, given its orientation relative to the Earth. The ability to unambiguously determine the position of a body in space using a finite set of (six) numbers allows all operations to control the flight and predict the position of the rocket in space to be freed up to mathematical operations. Thus, mathematics becomes the main tool for controlling the movement of rockets.

Computer revolution and space

It is known that currently fighter pilots flying the fastest aircraft undergo careful selection and medical control. They must have impeccable health and ideal mental reactions. A modern fighter can reach speeds four times the speed of sound, that is, more than 1,200 meters per second. Since the human reaction time is about 0.1 seconds, during the pilot’s reaction time the plane manages to fly a distance of 120 meters. Before the pilot applies, the situation in the air can change significantly. The speeds of space objects and rockets exceed the speed of sound by more than 20 times, so the human reaction time creates a “dead zone” of about one kilometer when controlling the flight of rockets. Thus, a person in a number of situations is not able to control directly, as they say

This means is microprocessor computing technology, powerful small-sized computers on large intergal circuits that “think” fast enough to control the movement of a rocket “in real time.” Modern airplanes, rockets and spaceships, and many other new technical devices, are equipped with such computers. In this regard, they began to talk about the “second industrial revolution”, or the “computer revolution” (see «Kvant», 1983, No. 2, p. 2).

Game of agreement for rockets

With the help of computers, the children's game of “talk” has risen to a qualitatively new level and lost its harmless character. Nowadays, missiles have become the main means of combat against the impact of targets. Moreover, in the case when a missile is used against another missile or aircraft, equipped with a computer and making the defenses of the ship, we have a game of “negotiation” between two flying computers.

It turned out that the mathematical problems that arose when controlling the movement of one or several flying objects have a fundamentally new mathematical character. A new mathematical theory for solving such problems was founded in the works of the Soviet mathematician Academician L. S. Pontryagin in the 50s and was called “optimal control theory.”

How to make a rocket?

“Aerospace technology” - at the sound of these words, the hand jumps and the pulse quickens even in the most seasoned spinner.

Gigantic flows of information of a political nature are engulfing the globe, overflowing cable and radio relay communication lines and spilling out into space. Worldwide systems of communication satellites, satellite television and radio broadcasting, a space service for the study and conservation of nature - all this is necessary, all this is important for the economy, for science, for technology, but how, how to do all this?

“How to make an avenger?”, “How to make a satellite?” - from what materials, by what operations and on what devices - this is called technology. An airplane and a spaceship will remain a fantasy until you know what and how they can be built from. Already in ancient China, small rockets were launched on fireworks displays, but in order for Yuri Gagarin to rise into space, a fundamentally higher level of technology was needed. Therefore, the rise of Gagarin is the rise and triumph of Soviet technology, proof of its highest world level.

And it needs to be repeated again that just as the construction and design of airliners and spaceships is based on mathematical calculations and the laws of physics, so too is technologically based on the achievements of mathematics and physics. The production of aircraft elements requires such precision and such a degree of production organization that are not available without computer control systems and monitoring of machines and production lines.

Powder nozzle

Let's say we have already designed a rocket and already know what shape the nozzle movement should be (see «Kvant», 1982, No. 9). But what material is it made of? The requirements for this material are strength and high temperature resistance. After all, the stream of gases coming out of the rocket nozzle has a temperature of 4000°C. At this temperature, iron, aluminum and most tools fly.

In addition, the plasma structure can have a noticeable physical effect on the walls of the alloy, because today’s plasma torch is used for cutting the most durable metals and alloys. So, the alloy material must withstand temperatures of 3000–4000°C and at these temperatures be resistant to physical influences.

In practical nature there are no materials that in their pure form could satisfy these conditions. Therefore comes the use of principles to cool the alloy material during fuel combustion. The most suitable material is tungsten, whose melting point is 3400°C. However, it is impossible to obtain a solid profile from it and with sufficiently thin (from the minimum weight message) walls using traditional metalworking methods. It is very difficult to obtain products by casting, and the quality of the metal will be low. In addition, even if this were possible, tungsten would not be able to work, since the temperature of the exhaust gases during the combustion of some types of modern fuel is higher than its melting point.

This is where powder metallurgy, combined with known physical principles, comes to the rescue. The method of powder metallurgy, which consists of placing metal powder into the cavity of the tool, and then subjecting it to pressure and sintering, produces a porous structure of the “pumice” type product. Then this “pumice” is impregnated with copper (its melting point is only 1083°C) and the resulting material can work for a short time, even if the temperature of the exhaust gases is about 4000°C. So here, copper evaporates and cools the tungsten frame, and since it is all penetrated by channels filled with copper, it always protrudes on the surface of the alloy and cools it.
