It is known that several color samples located in one plane are usually perceived as light in several planes located close to or further from the real one. This is effectively chromatic stereoscopy. When examining a color wheel placed on a black base, it becomes very obvious that its yellow area protrudes significantly, the blue area recedes into the depths, food comes off the black background, red and green colors occupy an intermediate position. Similarly, the overwhelming majority of people with normal color vision evaluate color in space.

A single cube is perceived as a single geometric image. If you break it into colored cubes, it will seem extensively dissected. This picture can be highlighted in the form of a tonal rebluff, taking into account the significant difference in the arrangement of the component cups. We will reproduce here two examples illustrating the connection between color images and their tonal reflashes, suggested by the artist A. V. Efimov.
