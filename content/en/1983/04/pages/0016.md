— When do you usually show aptitude for mathematics? Were you tortured, like you, at an early age?

— I taught quite a lot in high school. I have the impression that interest in mathematics in middle school, at the age of twelve or thirteen, often turns out to be temporary and disappears completely by high school. This happens especially often in girls. In my opinion, those schoolchildren who are passionate about mathematics at the age of 13-14-15 are worth working with. With skillful cultivation, their abilities gradually develop and, as a rule, are no longer lost. There are, of course, many more skillful things. Of course, a serious interest in mathematics may appear later.

— Which mathematicians of the older generation had the most influence on you?

— During my student years, I was a student of Nikolai Nikolaevich Luzin. Besides him, Vyacheslav Vasilievich Stepanov, Alexander Yakovlevich Khinchin, Pavel Sergeevich Alexandrov and other mathematicians of their generation had a great influence on me.

— What would you like to say about your students and which of them would you like to mention?

— I was lucky to have talented students. Many of them, having started working with me in some area, then moved on to a new topic and, completely independently of me, received wonderful results. It would be difficult to single out the most worthy of mention.

I will say only as a joke that at present one of my students controls the dark atmosphere, and the other controls the oceans *).

— Andrey Nikolaevich, what is your daily routine?

— Naturally, during my rather long life, the daily routine at different periods was different. I will, perhaps, only describe the daily routine that Pavel Sergeev Aleksaandrov and I established for ourselves for those 3-4 days a week that we spent outside the city, near Moscow, in the village of Komarovka.

The day starts at 7 am. The first hour was devoted to gymnastics and jogging. At 8 o'clock we had breakfast and got to work at the table - with or without a typewriter. At one or two o'clock there was an afternoon snack consisting of milk or kefir with bread. After afternoon tea we worked a little more, but usually went for a long walk on foot or - in winter - on ski slopes, until 4 o'clock in the afternoon. Then we snuck off to sleep for half an hour. At 5 o'clock there was lunch. After lunch, we sometimes did some more work, some minor community work: copying or the like. The evening was dedicated to reading, music, and receiving guests. Before going to bed, we also liked to take a short walk. We went to bed around 10 o'clock.

But, of course, when you work and begin to achieve a solution to some important problem, everything disappears into the background, there was no longer any daily routine.
