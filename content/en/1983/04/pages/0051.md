⟦folio 49⟧

F797. A flat capacitor is filled with a dielectric, the dielectric permeability of which depends on the voltage $U$ on the capacitor according to the law $\varepsilon = \alpha U$, where $\alpha = 0,1$ $B^{-1}$. In parallel with this dielectric, dielectric, and capacitance, a “nonlinear” capacitor (uncharged) is connected to an ordinary capacitor charged to a potential difference of $U_0 = 60$ V. What will be the voltage across the capacitor?

Since the “nonlinear” capacitor differs from the general one only in the presence of a dielectric between its plates, the capacitance of the “nonlinear” capacitor at a voltage $U$ across it is equal to

$$C = \varepsilon C_0 = \alpha UC_0,$$

where $C_0$ is the capacity of the common capacitor. In this case, the charge on the “nonlinear” capacitor is

$$q_H = CU = \alpha C_0 U^2,$$

and the charge on the common capacitor is

$$q = C_0 U$$

(voltage $U$ on the capacitors with a parallel connection is the same).

The initial charge of the common capacitor was equal to

$$q_0 = C_0 U_0,$$

and by virtue of the law of conservation of charge at any voltage $U$ on the capacitors

$$q_H + q = q_0 \Rightarrow \alpha C_0 U^2 + C_0 U = C_0 U_0.$$

From here the voltage $U$ is found:

$$U = \frac{\sqrt{4\alpha U_0 + 1} - 1}{2\alpha} = 20 \text{ В.}$$

A. I. Buzdin

Inverse trigonometric functions

(For the beginning, see p. 30)

On the segment $[0; \frac{\pi}{2}]$, both sine, cosine, and tangent are monotonous. Let us calculate, for example, the cosine of the numbers being compared:

$$\cos \left( \arcsin \frac{2}{5} \right) = \sqrt{1 - \left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{21}}{5},$$

$$\cos \left( \arccos \frac{2}{5} \right) = \frac{2}{5}.$$

Since at $[0; \frac{\pi}{2}]$ the cosine kills, from the inequality

$$\cos \left( \arcsin \frac{2}{5} \right) > \cos \left( \arccos \frac{2}{5} \right)$$

it follows that

$$\arcsin \frac{2}{5} < \arccos \frac{2}{5}.$$

Example 6. Let's calculate $\arcsin(\sin 11)$. To do this, first compare $11$ with $\frac{\pi}{2}$ $k$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$). From the inequality $3 < \pi < 3,142$ ($\pi = 3,14159...$) it follows that $\frac{7}{2} \pi < 11 < 4\pi$. So, $-\frac{\pi}{2} < 11 - 4\pi < 0 < \frac{\pi}{2}$. Therefore $\arcsin(\sin 11) = \arcsin(\sin(11 - 4\pi)) = 11 - 4\pi$.
