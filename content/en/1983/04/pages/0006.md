“in real time”, by the flight of the devices he creates. Fortunately, man encountered this difficult problem when a means to overcome it had already been created.

This means is microprocessor computing technology, powerful small-sized computers on large intergal circuits that “think” fast enough to control the movement of a rocket “in real time.” Modern airplanes, rockets and spaceships, and many other new technical devices, are equipped with such computers. In this regard, they began to talk about the “second industrial revolution”, or the “computer revolution” (see «Kvant», 1983, No. 2, p. 2).

Game of agreement for rockets

With the help of computers, the children's game of “talk” has risen to a qualitatively new level and lost its harmless character. Nowadays, missiles have become the main means of combat against the impact of targets. Moreover, in the case when a missile is used against another missile or aircraft, equipped with a computer and making the defenses of the ship, we have a game of “negotiation” between two flying computers.

It turned out that the mathematical problems that arose when controlling the movement of one or several flying objects have a fundamentally new mathematical character. A new mathematical theory for solving such problems was founded in the works of the Soviet mathematician Academician L. S. Pontryagin in the 50s and was called “optimal control theory.”

How to make a rocket?

“Aerospace technology” - at the sound of these words, the hand jumps and the pulse quickens even in the most seasoned spinner.

Gigantic flows of information of a political nature are engulfing the globe, overflowing cable and radio relay communication lines and spilling out into space. Worldwide systems of communication satellites, satellite television and radio broadcasting, a space service for the study and conservation of nature - all this is necessary, all this is important for the economy, for science, for technology, but how, how to do all this?

“How to make an avenger?”, “How to make a satellite?” - from what materials, by what operations and on what devices - this is called technology. An airplane and a spaceship will remain a fantasy until you know what and how they can be built from. Already in ancient China, small rockets were launched on fireworks displays, but in order for Yuri Gagarin to rise into space, a fundamentally higher level of technology was needed. Therefore, the rise of Gagarin is the rise and triumph of Soviet technology, proof of its highest world level.

And it needs to be repeated again that just as the construction and design of airliners and spaceships is based on mathematical calculations and the laws of physics, so too is technologically based on the achievements of mathematics and physics. The production of aircraft elements requires such precision and such a degree of production organization that are not available without computer control systems and monitoring of machines and production lines.

Powder nozzle

Let's say we have already designed a rocket and already know what shape the nozzle movement should be (see «Kvant», 1982, No. 9). But what material is it made of? The requirements for this material are strength and high temperature resistance. After all, the stream of gases coming out of the rocket nozzle has a temperature of 4000°C. At this temperature, iron, aluminum and most tools fly.
