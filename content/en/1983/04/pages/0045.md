Of the many solutions to this problem, we chose two that seemed to us the most rewarding.

The first solution is based on the comotety method. Note that for the volume Hᵉk with center B and coefficient k=|AB|:|DB| segment DE will go into AC (Fig. 1), since (DE)||(AC); in this case, △ABC will turn into △A₁BC₁. It is clear that |A₁A|=|BC| and that lines A₁C and AE are parallel. Therefore, to construct the segment DE, it is enough to plot the segment |AA₁| on the ray BA, equal to |BC|, (see Fig. 1) and draw a straight line l through point A, parallel to A₁C. The point of its intersection on the side BC is the desired point E. (We actually didn’t need the vertex C₁). This construction can be slightly simplified: it is obvious that the line l=(AE) intersects with the line parallel to AB and passing through C, at point K, distant from the vertex C at a distance |CK|=|AA₁|=|CB| (see Fig. 1). And constructing point K is quite easy.

The second solution uses displacements, or more precisely, a certain type of displacement - sliding symmetry. The sliding symmetry of Sᵗᵗ with the l axis and the vector a is called the composition of the axial symmetry of Sᵗᵗ and parallel translation to the vector a, while the vector a must be parallel to the axis (Fig. 2); in particular, at a = 0 sliding symmetry turns into ordinary symmetry. It is obvious that the midpoint of any segment PP₁, where P₁ is the image of the point P under the sliding of the symmetry, lies on its axis (see Fig. 2). In addition, it is easy to see that for any two rays PQ and P₁Q₁ one can specify a (uniform) sliding symmetry that transforms the ray into the second one: the l axis of this symmetry passes through the middle of the segment PP₁ and forms equal angles with the given rays *) (see Fig. 2), and the vector is equal to P'P₁, where P'=Sᵗᵗ(P).
