Coordinates in space flight

Mathematics played an even more important role than the birth of aviation for the emergence of astronautics. Theoretically, cosmonautics K. E. Tsiolkovsky constantly used mathematics in his proofs of the occurrence of flight to other planets and in the projects of space trains (see «Kvant», 1982, No. 9, p. 12).

In addition to the theoretical justification and calculation of the rocket design, mathematics is necessary every second of space flight. We are already accustomed to hearing the standard phrase when announcing the launch of another spacecraft: “The coordination center is constantly processing information.” The processing of data on a ship's trajectory is based on an important mathematical concept - a coordinate system, invented in the 17th century by the Great French mathematician R. Descartes (Geometry 9-10, § 59).

The first practical application of the coordinate system, carried out by Descartes himself, was not entirely of a worldly nature: at one of Descartes’ lectures, an unknown random person knocked his feet; Descartes, not the first lecture, asked his assistant to go to the basement room under the auditorium and measure the coordinates of the noise source; the returning assistant set aside a certain distance from one wall of the audience and a certain distance from the other wall - randomly, sitting at the intersection of imaginary perpendiculars, he asked to leave. The demonstration of the practical significance of the rectangular coordinate system was very convincing.

In many cases, when a rocket moves, in addition to the three coordinates of its center of mass, it is necessary to know three more angles, given its orientation relative to the Earth. The ability to unambiguously determine the position of a body in space using a finite set of (six) numbers allows all operations to control the flight and predict the position of the rocket in space to be freed up to mathematical operations. Thus, mathematics becomes the main tool for controlling the movement of rockets.

It is known that currently fighter pilots flying the fastest aircraft undergo careful selection and medical control. They must have impeccable health and ideal mental reactions. A modern fighter can reach speeds four times the speed of sound, that is, more than 1,200 meters per second. Since the human reaction time is about 0.1 seconds, during the pilot’s reaction time the plane manages to fly a distance of 120 meters. Before the pilot applies, the situation in the air can change significantly. The speeds of space objects and rockets exceed the speed of sound by more than 20 times, so the human reaction time creates a “dead zone” of about one kilometer when controlling the flight of rockets.
