Let us write down these conditions (Newton’s II law) for the volume ABCD allocated to figure. The forces acting on it are the gravitational force $F_\tau = \omega gabh$ (b - the width of the flow), the pressure force of atmospheric air $F_a = p_0ab$ (p$_0$ - atmospheric pressure), the pressure force of the underlying layers of water $F_H = p_hab$ (p$_h$ - pressure at the "depth" h), the pressure forces $F_\pi$ and $F_\pi$ from the side water on the left (upstream) and on the right (downstream) and the friction force on the adjacent layer of water at depth h. Let's write down Newton's II law in projections onto the Y axis (see figure):

$$p_0ab + \omega gabh \cos \alpha - pab = 0.$$

From this equation it is clear that the pressure p in the flow is determined only by the “depth” h.

Now let us write down the conditions for the constancy of the average speed for the volume MBCN allocated to figure. In projections onto the X axis we have:

$$\omega gabH \sin \alpha - k \frac{v}{H} = 0.$$

Here $\omega gabH$ is the gravitational force, v is the average flow velocity, $kv/H$ is the friction force (k=const). (The pressure forces from the water on the left and right cancel each other, and therefore we did not include them in the equation). We express the depth H in terms of the amount Q of water flowing through the channel section per unit time: $Q = Hbv \Rightarrow H = \frac{Q}{bv}$.

Substitute this expression into (*) and find v:

$$v = \left( \frac{\omega g \sin \alpha}{kb} \right)^{1/3} Q^{2/3}.$$

From this it can be seen that when the equation Q is 2 times, the average flow velocity increases by $\sqrt[3]{4} \approx 1,6$ times. In this case, the flow depth increases by $\sqrt[3]{2} \approx 1,3$ times.

F796. On the main optical axis AB of the collecting line with focal length F there is a plane mirror, which is directed with angular velocity w towards the axis perpendicular to the axis AB (Fig. 1). A parallel beam of rays is projected onto the mirror, which, after reflection, is focused on a screen located in the focal plane of the line. Find the speed of the light spot on the screen at the moment when it passes the focus of the line.

Let at the initial moment of time the angle between the main optical axis of the line and the perpendicular to the mirror be equal to α, and the light spot be at the point of intersection of the axis with the screen (point B at figure 1), that is, after reflection from the mirror, the rays go parallel to the main optical axis. After a short period of time $\Delta t$ the mirror will rotate through a small angle $\Delta a = \omega \cdot \Delta t$. The rays reflected from the mirror are “rotated” by an angle $2\Delta a$ relative to the axis (Fig. 2) and the light spot will be located at point C on the screen. Let's find the displacement $|BC|$ of the spot in time $\Delta t$:

$$|BC| = |OB|tg 2\Delta a = Ftg 2\Delta a \approx 2F \cdot \Delta a$$

(we took advantage of the fact that $\Delta a$ is small and tg $\Delta a \approx \Delta a$). Knowing the displacement, we find the instantaneous speed of the light spot at point B:

$$v = \frac{|BC|}{\Delta t} = \frac{2F \cdot \Delta a}{\Delta t} = \frac{2F\omega \cdot \Delta t}{\Delta t} = 2F\omega.$$
