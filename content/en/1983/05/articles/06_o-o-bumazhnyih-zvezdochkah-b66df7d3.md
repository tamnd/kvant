Example 2. A periodic continued fraction with a distance
[2; 1, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 1, 4, ...].

Briefly it can be written like this:
[2; $\overline{1, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, 1, 4, ...}$].

Let's find the number it matches. If this number is denoted by $\alpha$, then the equality will be fulfilled (why?)

$$\alpha = 2 + \frac{1}{|1|} + \frac{1}{|1|} + \frac{1}{|1|} + \frac{1}{|2+a|}$$

or, after conversion,

$$\alpha = \frac{21+8\alpha}{8+3\alpha}$$

It follows that $\alpha^2 = 7$, and since $\alpha > 2$ we get $\alpha = \sqrt{7}$.

It is easy to see that this method can be used to find the value of any periodic continued fraction.

Best approximations and continued fractions

The best approximation to the number $\alpha$ is the fraction $\frac{p}{q}$ ($q > 0$) such that

$$|q' \alpha - p'| > |q \alpha - p|$$

for all $1 \leq q' < q$ and all $p'$. Fair

Theorem. Every best approximation to $\alpha$ is a suitable fraction $\alpha$; for $k > 1$ the converse is also true, that is, any suitable fraction $\frac{p_k}{q_k}$ for $k > 1$ provides the best approximation to the number $\alpha$.

The proof of this theorem is not difficult, and we leave it to the reader. (If it is difficult to prove insurmountable, then you can turn to S. Lang’s book “Introduction to the Theory of Diophantine Approximations” (M., Mir, 1970).

Number equivalence

Two numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are said to be equivalent if

$$\alpha = \frac{a\beta + b}{c\beta + d},$$

where $a, b, c, d$ are integers and $ad - bc = 1$ or - 1.

Let us denote by $\sigma$ and $\sigma_k$ (where $k$ is an integer) the operations defined as follows:

$$\sigma \alpha = \frac{1}{\alpha}, \quad \sigma_k \alpha = k + \alpha.$$

It is clear that by applying $\sigma$ and $\sigma_k$ to the number $\alpha$ we always obtain an equivalent number. It is also clear that the use of operations of the form

$$\sigma \sigma_k \alpha = \frac{1}{\alpha + k}, \quad \sigma_k \sigma_\alpha = k + \frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{k\alpha + 1}{\alpha}$$

also gives an example of numbers equivalent to $\alpha$.

Our cover

The paper stars shown on the first page of the cover are not just a decorative plot for the First Day holiday. They are connected with mathematics, and with the development of its branches. On the one hand, this is a model of star polyhedra, which were already written about in «Kvant» (1973, No. 5, p. 26), and therefore they relate to the theory of symmetry in space (geometry, group theory). On the other hand, these stars are in contact with the theory of knots (see «Kvant», 1975, No. 7, p. 6 or 1981, No. 3, p. 8) and with the so-called theory

braid In the theory of braids, algebraic methods are used to study techniques for weaving braids with twists or strips, and our stars are precisely woven from paper strips. We will discuss how this is done in one of the upcoming issues of the magazine.
