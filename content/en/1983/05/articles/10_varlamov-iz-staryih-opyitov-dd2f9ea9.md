From old experiences

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. VARLAMOV

Half a year ago we published the first article about interesting experiments carried out by inquisitive young men in the last century.*) Today we continue this topic and offer the reader's attention a story about several amusing experiments in electricity.

At the beginning of the 19th century, the Administrative Council of the Royal Institution of Great Britain, in order to educate young people in the field of natural sciences, established a special course of scientific readings. (Note that natural subjects were not taught at all in the English school at that time.) In its organization, it was something like a Sunday school, which, as you remember, Tom Sawyer diligently attended. However, together with the “shabby director with a prayer book and boring sermons” they ascended to the pulpit of the Royal Institution and spoke to young people.

The outstanding teaching of that time was Michael Faraday, John Tyndall and others.

Some of the courses taught were recorded and then published. Thus, a series of lectures entitled “The Chemical History of the Candle,” given by Faraday in 1860 and first published in 1861, gained worldwide fame. Since then, this book has been republished many times in different languages, including Russian. The next edition was published very recently in the Library «Kvant»*).

No less remarkable is Tindalay's book Lessons in Electricity. Today you will learn about some experiments from this book.

The study of electricity began to develop later than mechanics. Thus, for almost two thousand years, human knowledge in this area was limited only to the fact that amber can attract light objects. It was only in the 17th century that the first laws describing electrical phenomena were discovered.

Tyndall saw the reason for such stagnation in the neglect of experiments. It is the art of conducting experiments,

Set up a simple fountain using a water tank placed on a raised platform (say, on a cabinet) and an ordinary siphon made of a rubber tube. Strengthen the end of the tube so that the stream of water is not vertically upward, but slightly at an angle, reaching a height of about fifty centimeters. After reaching its maximum height, the stream usually disintegrates into a sonopod. If you bring an electrified wand to it in advance, the spray will immediately obediently contract into a calm stream. Try to explain why.

This experiment is shown effectively in the dark, illuminating only a stream of water from below.

Now let’s describe a beautiful experiment demonstrating the opposite action of electric charges.

Fix the funnel motionless and stick the wire into it. Wrap the other end of the wire around the heating glass tube. Pour metal filings into the funnel - they will pour out of an uneven continuous stream. But as soon as you rub the tube with a piece of leather or silk, the picture changes dramatically. The charges generated on the tube as a result of the vibration are transferred through the wire to metal filings; they will acquire one-dimensional charges and scatter, forming a kind of fountain.

How to make an electric machine

If you want to go beyond electrified wand wholesalers, you need to learn how to communicate much larger charges to your topics. For example, using the proposed machine.

The first electric machine was designed by the burgomaster of Magdeburg, military engineer Otto von Guericke in 1660. Guericke is known primarily for his experiments on the detection of atmospheric pressure, which went down in the history of physics under the name “experiments with the Magdeburg hemispheres” (they are mentioned in the physics textbook for grades 6-7).

Guericke's electric machine is a ball of sulfur the size of a child's head, mounted on an iron axis with a crank and mounted on a wooden stand. Various experiments could be carried out with this ball. For example, by rotating the ball with a hand and rubbing it directly with a dry hand, Guericke observed the appearance of small sparks from the ball - the ball glowed in the dark. By removing the ball from the stand and moving around the room with it, he managed to catch light bodies, such as a feather, floating in the air. Other bodies are first attracted to the electrified ball, but after touching it they are immediately repelled (think why?) and keep a certain distance from it until they touch other objects, after which they can again be attracted to the ball. Subsequently, the sulfur ball was replaced by a glass cylinder, and then by a more convenient glass disk. These machines produced sparks so strong that they were capable of igniting the ether.

In all subsequent images of electrophoric machines, which make it possible to obtain even more electrical charges, there is an additional element characteristic of modern machines - a conductor, that is, to collect charges.

Let us describe the structure of one of the simplest machines, which is easy to make yourself. It consists of two main elements - an extended cylinder, electrifying by friction, and a conductor.

Cylinder $A$ is made from a simple bottle. Together with the plug, a cranked handle $P$ enters the neck, with the help of which the cylinder is rotated. The stands $B_1$ and $B_2$, glued to the base $O$ of the machine, are cut out of plywood, their height is 10-12 cm. In the upper part of the drain, holes for the rotation axis are made at the same height. A piece of wood is glued to the bottom of the bottle, in which a screw $M$ passed through the stand rests. It serves as the left end of the rotation axis and at the same time attaches the bottle to the opposite post. To prevent the screw from cutting into the wood, it is best to place a metal plate with a recess in the center under it.

The rubbing brush is a board covered with leather, 4-5 cm wide, which is pressed against the bottle using two tight springs. The brush is attached to the base in the following way: two short, wide-open thin-walled tubes are included in the base, and tubes of smaller diameter that fit freely into the lower ones are included in the brush.

The conductor $K$ consists of a metal prutta 4-6 mm thick and a metal ball attached to the left end of the prutta. A copper attachment is glued to the rod, with the help of which the conductor is secured in a tube included in the top of the left post. The connection points between the prutta and the ball and copper nozzle must be well cleaned.

Electric wind and electric network wheel

With the help of the electrophoric machine you have made, you can conduct many interesting experiments. One of the simplest is the creation of the so-called electric wind. Using a plate, attach a pin to the conductor ball so that its head always touches the conductor, and the pin itself is positioned horizontally. If you now turn the handle of the machine with one hand and place the fire on the fire with the other, you will feel the wind blowing. This wind can be so strong that its gusts blow out the flame of a candle.
