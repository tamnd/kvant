The first electric machine was designed by the burgomaster of Magdeburg, military engineer Otto von Guericke in 1660. Guericke is known primarily for his experiments on the detection of atmospheric pressure, which went down in the history of physics under the name “experiments with the Magdeburg hemispheres” (they are mentioned in the physics textbook for grades 6-7).

Guericke's electric machine is a ball of sulfur the size of a child's head, mounted on an iron axis with a crank and mounted on a wooden stand. Various experiments could be carried out with this ball. For example, by rotating the ball with a hand and rubbing it directly with a dry hand, Guericke observed the appearance of small sparks from the ball - the ball glowed in the dark. By removing the ball from the stand and moving around the room with it, he managed to catch light bodies, such as a feather, floating in the air.
