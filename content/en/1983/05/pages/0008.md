⟦folio 6⟧

If it were possible to isolate practical $^{235}\text{U}$ in its pure form, then, by ensuring its critical mass, it would be possible to obtain a huge amount of energy. As calculations have shown, the process of distribution of practical pure $^{235}\text{U}$ under the influence of neutrons occurs very quickly and has the character of an explosion (such calculations were carried out by Ya. B. Zeldovich, Yu. B. Khariton in the USSR and a number of foreign physicists). Moreover, for this purpose a special source of neutrons is not even required, because in the Earth’s atmosphere there is always a small amount of neutrons of cosmic origin, which is sufficient to cause an explosive reaction if uranium reaches a critical mass. It became clear that in this way it was possible to create a formidable weapon - an atomic bomb.

Many physicists around the world have begun a detailed study of the fission process of uranium nuclei. Almost every issue of physics journals around the world published scientific articles or notes elucidating details regarding the distribution of uranium nuclei. But obtaining pure $^{235}\text{U}$ in the required quantities, on the order of tens of kilograms, seemed fantastic at that time.

However, it soon became clear that a natural mixture of uranium isotopes could be subject to a nuclear chain reaction. To do this, you just need to somehow reduce the energy of the neutrons emitted during the fission of the $^{235}\text{U}$ nucleus. Fortunately, it turned out that the speed of neutrons can be adjusted to decrease it in a very simple way. To do this, it is enough to pass neutrons through a substance consisting of atoms, the mass of which differs little from the mass of the neutrons themselves. Then, when neutrons collide with such atoms, they quite quickly transfer part of their energy to these atoms, thereby reducing their speed to the level of the speed of thermal motion of the atoms.

Substances whose atomic mass is not too large are suitable for replacing neutrons. It is only necessary that these substances do not absorb neutrons, only the ability to replace them and distribute the corresponding uranium nuclei. Heavy water, which contains two atoms together, has the ability to absorb neutrons little.
