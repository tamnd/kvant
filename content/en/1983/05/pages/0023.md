⟦folio 21⟧

Tasks
P. L. Kapitsa

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. V. MITROFANOV

Some of the readers «Kvant» have probably heard about “Physical Problems” by Lenin and Nobel Prize laureate Academician P. L. Kapitsa. They were published in full (153 problems) in 1972*). These tasks were intended mainly for undergraduate and graduate students of the Faculty of Physics and Technology - the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. Now Kapitsa's problems are popular among young physicists. What should they use?

P. L. Kapitsa studies the formulation of problems, but is reluctant to use similar material from other problem books. There is a lot of humor and jokes in the tasks. Curious experiments, examples from the history of physics, funny physical experiments, principles of operation of some toys and advanced technical devices - these are often also the topics of Kapitsin’s problems. Some of them are more convenient to solve, experimenting with the simplest homemade devices - in this case you can easily pay attention. In many problems, numerical values ​​of physical quantities are not given; The problem solver must choose these values ​​himself. This is done so that students can concretely imagine the scale of those physical quantities that have to be operated (for example, speed, voltage, temperature, etc.).

A sufficiently complete solution to all problems requires good professional training. But grasping the idea of ​​solving a particular problem and evaluating the result obtained, at least in terms of the order of magnitude, is possible even for a schoolchild.

With the kind permission of Academician P. L. Kapitsa, we publish several of his new problems in this issue of the journal. We will try to solve one of them here. Considerations on choosing an approach to solving other problems, answers and solutions to some of them will be published in one of the following issues «Kvant». Try to solve the proposed problems yourself*). Don't be upset if you don't succeed right away. The path of searching for truth has never been easy. But these searches will help you discover a new world of physics. We wish you success in this!
