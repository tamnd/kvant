SYMMETRY AND ASYMMETRY

Today we will talk about one geometric idea on which a number of problems and studies are based.

R. Bianchetti; 1925 Linen: Kra1, Ls3, Ss1; black: Kh8, Rlf6. Win

After 1.Bb2! all the pieces are lined up on a large diagonal, and depending on where the black rook moves, two elegant symmetrical options are obtained: 1...Rf8 (1...Rf7 2.Rh3++ Kpg83.Rh8×) 2.Rc7+ Kpg83.Rg7+ Kh8 4.Ka2! (but not 4.Kpb1 Rf1+ 5.Kpa2 Ra1+ 6.Kpb3 Ra3+ 7.Kpc2 Rc3+ 8.C:c3 stalemate), and White will win the rook. The answer 1...Rh6 leads to the same ending.

W. Pauli, 1920. Checkmate in 4 moves.

At first glance, the position is completely symmetrine, and then the solutions must be symmetrine. But the chess geometry is such that the symmetry axis of the position (vertical d) does not coincide with the symmetry axis of the board (the border of the vertical d and e). In this problem the solution is 1. Bg7! Ca5(c7) 2. Cf8 Cd8 3. Cd6!, and checkmate with the next move. If on the first move the black knight b1 moves, then this should be followed by 2.Kc3+ Kc5 3.Cf8+ and 4.C:e7×. It is interesting that the move 1.Ba7, symmetrical to the raster one, is not suitable - the black bishop uses the vertical h:1...Ch4! 2.Bb8 Cf2! 3.Cd6 Cd4! and there is no swearing.

T. Dawson, 1924 Linen: Ke8, Ke6, p.e7; Black: Ke2, p.p.d3, f3. Win

White has two logical continuations at his disposal - 1.Kd4+ or 1.Kf4+. The vertical a, which breaks the symmetry, does not seem to play any role. But it turns out that only 1.Kd4+ leads to victory! Ke3 2.K:f3 Kp:f3 3.Kpf8! d2 4.e8Q d1Q 5.Fh5+ (if Black does not place the queen, then the “queen against the central pawn” ending is easily won), and 1.Kf4+ leads to a draw - 1...Ke3 2.K:d3 K:d3 3.Kpd8 f2 4.e8Q Kpd2! The difference is that the second game “queen against bishop pawn” is nothing: 5.Fd5 Ke1 6. Qb1+ Ke2 7.Fe4+ Kpf1 8.Kpe7 Kpg1 9.Fg4+ Kph1 10.Ff3+ Kpg1 11. Qg3+ Kph1! 12. F:f2 stalemate.

Obviously, mirroring the position relative to the axis of symmetry of the board allows you to get another, symmetrical position. There is a move that does not save when mirrored - castling.

W. Pauli, 1911. Checkmate in 3 moves.

Only 1.0—0—0 solves! Nd3+ 2.R:d3 cd 3.Rc8× (other options: 1...Ka2+ 2.R:a2, 1...Kd5 2.R:d5, 1...Kc6 2.bc and everywhere 3.Rd8×). Does not lead to the goal 1.R:a6 (with 2.Rd2 and 3.Rd8× found on 1...K:a6) due to the answer 1...0—0!

Place a mirror position on the board with the same task - checkmate in 3 moves, and you will see that the mirror solution is not suitable - long castling is now impossible. Here 1.R:h6 decides! The trick is that not only whites, but also blacks lost castling. 1...K:f2+ 2.Ke2, 1...Ke3+ 2.Kpc1 and 3.Rh8×). 2.Re2! and 3.Re8×.
