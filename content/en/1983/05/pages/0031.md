⟦folio 29⟧

From old experiences

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. VARLAMOV

Half a year ago we published the first article about interesting experiments carried out by inquisitive young men in the last century.*) Today we continue this topic and offer the reader's attention a story about several amusing experiments in electricity.

At the beginning of the 19th century, the Administrative Council of the Royal Institution of Great Britain, in order to educate young people in the field of natural sciences, established a special course of scientific readings. (Note that natural subjects were not taught at all in the English school at that time.) In its organization, it was something like a Sunday school, which, as you remember, Tom Sawyer diligently attended. However, together with the “shabby director with a prayer book and boring sermons” they ascended to the pulpit of the Royal Institution and spoke to young people.

The outstanding teaching of that time was Michael Faraday, John Tyndall and others.

Some of the courses taught were recorded and then published. Thus, a series of lectures entitled “The Chemical History of the Candle,” given by Faraday in 1860 and first published in 1861, gained worldwide fame. Since then, this book has been republished many times in different languages, including Russian. The next edition was published very recently in the Library «Kvant»*).

No less remarkable is Tindalay's book Lessons in Electricity. Today you will learn about some experiments from this book.

The study of electricity began to develop later than mechanics. Thus, for almost two thousand years, human knowledge in this area was limited only to the fact that amber can attract light objects. It was only in the 17th century that the first laws describing electrical phenomena were discovered.

Tyndall saw the reason for such stagnation in the neglect of experiments. It is the art of conducting experiments,

*) A. Varlamov. A hundred years ago. «Kvant», 1982, No. 10.
