The following must be said right away. When dividing radio waves into ranges, they proceed from the qualitative features of propagation, methods of generating, studying and receiving radio waves. However, when moving from one range to another, significant changes are not always observed; therefore, the boundaries of the ranges are conditional. For simplicity, they are defined by “round”, easy-to-remember numbers.

Long waves (LW band) are radio waves with a length of 10 km to 1 km, which corresponds to frequencies from 30 kHz to 300 kHz.

Medium waves (CB band) - from 1000 m to 100 m, corresponding to frequencies from 300 kHz to 3 MHz.

Short waves (KB band) - from 100 m to 10 m, frequencies from 3 MHz to 30 MHz.

Ultrashort waves (VHF range) - from 10 m to 1 cm, frequencies from 30 MHz to 30,000 MHz; The VHF range is divided into three sub-bands: meter waves (1÷10 m), desimeter waves (10÷100 cm) and centimeter waves (1÷10 cm).

Long waves

A little over 50 years ago, at the dawn of the development of modern radio technology, predominantly long waves were used. Under the influence of the theory of diffraction, it was believed that the longer the wave, the better it goes around the globe (after all, the longer the wavelength, the larger the obstacle the wave can go around).

The technology of that time did not make it possible to obtain vibrations of higher frequencies. The powers generated in the Far East range were “decent”. Thus, the power of machine generators created by V.P. Vologdan reached 600 kW at frequencies of 18÷50 kHz. Vologdan's machines made it possible to establish radiotelegraph communication between Moscow and New York (1925).
