Let us recall that the ionosphere is a layer of ionized gas in the upper part of the atmosphere - at altitudes from 60 km to 450 km. Gas ionization occurs under the influence of ultraviolet rays and a stream of charged particles emitted by the Sun.

Medium waves are reflected from a layer located at an altitude of 100–120 km (this layer is designated by the letter “E”). But reflection is only possible at night. During the day, in the atmosphere at an altitude of 60–80 km, under the influence of solar radiation, a layer appears in which medium waves are strongly absorbed (this layer is designated by the letter “D”). So in the daytime, only the ground wave arrives at the antenna, for example. At night, in those reception areas where interference of the earth and sky waves occurs, the signal fades (at figure 1 this occurs at the reception point closest to the source; the earth wave does not reach the far point - it is attenuated, and at this point only the reflected wave is received). If the phases of the waves coincide, then the resulting directivity of the field returns the loudness of the sound. If the waves compensate each other, the station’s audibility deteriorates. The trajectory of the reflected wave and the degree of its reflection changes all the time, so the nature of the fading is random.

The study of the ionosphere has led to a change in view of the propagation of long waves. According to modern concepts, long radio waves propagate in a kind of spherical waveguide. The lower wall of the waveguide is the earth's surface, the upper wall is the ionosphere.

Short waves

The short-wave range is nine times wider than the range of long and medium waves. To operate on these waves, directional antennas can be created; at the same time the level of interference is less. The dimensions of transmitters and antennas operating on KB are relatively small. However, short waves did not immediately become a reality. According to the theory of diffraction, large transformations in the path of propagation of short waves (large houses, forested areas, hills) shield the transmitting antenna. Therefore, it was believed that reception on HF was only possible in the line of sight zone, and these waves were given away by the radio amateur for close communications and experimentation.
