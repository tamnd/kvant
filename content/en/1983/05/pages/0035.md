⟦folio 33⟧

This, at first glance, mysterious phenomenon is explained by the electrical properties of the tip. Since charges of the same sign repel, they, flowing off the conductor, tend to move away from it as far as possible and accumulate in large numbers at the end of the tip. These charges attract charges of the opposite sign from the air, which “sit” on various parts suspended in the air. Having touched the tip, the particles are immediately recharged and, having acquired a charge of the same sign as on the conductor, fly away from the tip. The flow of these parts creates the thunderous wind.

The described property of the tip can be used to construct an electric analogue of the Segner wheel, which has been known since ancient times (rotating with the help of reactive force, water flowing out of its nozzles). Take the cork, hollow out its middle so that a metal thimble fits in there. Outside, insert several metal rods, bent in the form of hooks, placing them symmetrically. Now install this “wheel” on a metal vertical axis connected to the conductor of the electrophore machine. When the handle of the machine is rotated, the charges are deposited on the tips and, as in the previous experiment, a stream of recharging particles will be received from them. These flows cause the “wheel” to rotate.

The action of a lightning rod is based on this same property. Its inventor, Benjamin Franklin, extremely obscurely demonstrated the principle of operation of a lightning rod as follows. It is known that thunderclouds are always very uneven, some of them are located at different distances from the ground, some of them sometimes pass very low above the ground. Franklin believed that such descending “links” of a thundercloud are steps that clothe the lightning’s choice of path. To demonstrate this, he hung several cranberry papers from the conductor of the electrophore machine, attaching them to each other. When a charge was applied to them, the sticks were pulled towards the ground. Traying to the bottom of the cranberry needle, Franklin forced it to rise to the previous one and did this until all the paper returned to the conductor, as if he had not been charged.
