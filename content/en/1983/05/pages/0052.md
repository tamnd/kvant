released. Find the speed of the balls after the collision if the impact is absolutely non-elastic. Charges are not redistributed.

$$k \frac{Q^2}{x^2} = T.$$ (1)

From this moment on, the system of two balls becomes closed, and the total impulse of the system remains unchanged.

Let $v$ be the speed of the left ball at the moment the thread breaks, and $u$ be the speed of the balls after the collision. Then

$$mv = 2mu.$$ (2)

The speed $v$ is found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$-k \frac{Q^2}{10R} = -k \frac{Q^2}{x} + m \frac{v^2}{2}.$$ (3)

Solving equations (1) - (3) together, we find $u$:

$$u = \sqrt{k \frac{Q^2}{2m} \left( \frac{1}{Q} \sqrt{\frac{T}{k}} - \frac{1}{10R} \right)}.$$ (4)

If $T \geqslant F_{\max}$, then in the previous arguments we should immediately accept $x = 2R$. Then

$$u = \sqrt{\frac{0,2kQ^2}{mR}}.$$ (5)

G. V. Fedotovich

F802. The heat-conducting mass cylinder $m$, closed at the ends, is partitioned by a stationary mass piston $M$. On both sides of the piston there is one mole of an ideal gas, the internal energy of which is equal to $U = cT$. With a short blow, the cylinder imparts speed $\vec{v}$, directed along its axis. How much will the gas temperature change after the oscillating piston oscillates? There is no friction between the piston and cylinder.

Let $u$ be the speed at which the cylinder and piston move after the oscillating piston has died down. According to the law of conservation of momentum

$$mv = (M + m) u \Rightarrow u = \frac{m}{M + m} v.$$ (6)

Reducing the kinetic energy of the system -

$$\Delta K = m \frac{v^2}{2} - (M + m) \frac{u^2}{2} = \frac{Mm}{M + m} \frac{v^2}{2}$$

- is due to the transition of part $\Delta K$ of this energy into heat and is equal to the increase in the internal energy of the gas in the cylinder (we consider the heat capacity of the cylinder and piston to be negligible compared to the heat capacity of the gas). Since the piston, as in the beginning, is in equilibrium, both moles of gas (to the right and to the left of the piston) again have the same volume, pressure and temperature. Thus, the change in internal energy of the gas is equal to

$$\Delta U = 2c \cdot \Delta T = \frac{Mm}{M + m} \frac{v^2}{2}.$$ (7)

From here the gas temperature is found:

$$\Delta T = \frac{Mm}{M + m} \frac{v^2}{4c}.$$ (8)

G. V. Meledin
