⟦folio 32⟧

Other bodies are first attracted to the electrified ball, but after touching it they are immediately repelled (think why?) and keep a certain distance from it until they touch other objects, after which they can again be attracted to the ball. Subsequently, the sulfur ball was replaced by a glass cylinder, and then by a more convenient glass disk. These machines produced sparks so strong that they were capable of igniting the ether.

In all subsequent images of electrophoric machines, which make it possible to obtain even more electrical charges, there is an additional element characteristic of modern machines - a conductor, that is, to collect charges.

Let us describe the structure of one of the simplest machines, which is easy to make yourself. It consists of two main elements - an extended cylinder, electrifying by friction, and a conductor.

Cylinder $A$ is made from a simple bottle. Together with the plug, a cranked handle $P$ enters the neck, with the help of which the cylinder is rotated. The stands $B_1$ and $B_2$, glued to the base $O$ of the machine, are cut out of plywood, their height is 10-12 cm. In the upper part of the drain, holes for the rotation axis are made at the same height. A piece of wood is glued to the bottom of the bottle, in which a screw $M$ passed through the stand rests. It serves as the left end of the rotation axis and at the same time attaches the bottle to the opposite post. To prevent the screw from cutting into the wood, it is best to place a metal plate with a recess in the center under it.

The rubbing brush is a board covered with leather, 4-5 cm wide, which is pressed against the bottle using two tight springs. The brush is attached to the base in the following way: two short, wide-open thin-walled tubes are included in the base, and tubes of smaller diameter that fit freely into the lower ones are included in the brush.

The conductor $K$ consists of a metal prutta 4-6 mm thick and a metal ball attached to the left end of the prutta. A copper attachment is glued to the rod, with the help of which the conductor is secured in a tube included in the top of the left post. The connection points between the prutta and the ball and copper nozzle must be well cleaned.

Electric wind and electric network wheel

With the help of the electrophoric machine you have made, you can conduct many interesting experiments. One of the simplest is the creation of the so-called electric wind. Using a plate, attach a pin to the conductor ball so that its head always touches the conductor, and the pin itself is positioned horizontally. If you now turn the handle of the machine with one hand and place the fire on the fire with the other, you will feel the wind blowing. This wind can be so strong that its gusts blow out the flame of a candle.
