F827. A cube with an edge of length a moves at a speed v = 0.8 s (c is the speed of light) in a direction perpendicular to one of the faces of the cube. What happens in a photograph of a cube taken by a remote camera with a very short exposure time if the optical axis of the camera is perpendicular to the face of the cube and intersects the trajectory of its center (Fig. 1)?

Let us mentally reverse the process of photographing in time: let the cube move at a speed of $-\vec{v}$, at some moment the camera emits a light flash that reaches the cube at the time when it crosses the optical axis. The points of intersection of the wave front with a point on the cube will be those points whose images are obtained in the photograph (this is due to the fact that light travels different distances from different parts of the cube to the camera, and therefore in different times).

Near the cube, the wave can be considered flat (since the camera is removed from the cube). Light travels the distance a from the nearest to the far edge of the cube in time $\tau \approx a/c$.

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

In the photograph, the images of the close and distant edges of the cube will be mixed in the direction of movement of the cube at a distance s=vτ=0.8a relative to each other. The cube in the photograph will appear to be surfaced at an angle of $\varphi = \arcsin \frac{v}{a}$ (Fig. 2).
