**Absence system is crashing**

Suppose that a truck with a person in the back, already familiar to us, is turning. Then a person can hit the side and even get a bump. Why is this happening? Regarding the Earth, everything is clear: a person continues to move in a straight line, and the side of the truck, turning, approaches him. In the absence system associated with the truck, on the contrary, the person moves closer to the side, although no new real forces act on the person in this system. In order to explain this movement in the non-inertial system of the truck, it is necessary to introduce a fictitious force - the centering of the inertial force, directed along the radius from the center of rotation. That's it - the forces are fictitious, but the bumps are real!

Another example is the man on the carousel. Why does it move in a circle? In an inertial system of absence, the answer is given by Newton's second law: the reaction force from the side wall of the chair, directed towards the center, causes centrostrement. Is it possible to understand this movement and use Newton's laws in a rotating system? Again, no: in this system a person will die, and at the same time the force of reaction from the cross acts on him. But everything falls into place if you introduce a centering of inertial force directed from the center of rotation. Then you can explain why there is no movement in this system.

To find the values ​​of the centration of the inertial force, we first consider the movement of a person in a stationary (inertial) system of absence. Centripetal acceleration $a_u = \omega^2 r$ (ω is the frequency of rotation of the carousel, r is its radius) is caused by the reaction force $T$ acting on a person from the side of the carousel chair (Fig. 3):

$$m\omega^2 r = T.$$
