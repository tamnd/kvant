metric arrangement to another.

So far we have been talking about what the surface of a solid body looks like from the outside. It is equally important to know how it appears to the conduction electrons adjacent to it, so to speak, from the inside. For this purpose, a recently presented original method is used, which illustrates figure 3. At points a and b, two current contacts are connected to the surface, and a constant magnetic field with induction B is applied perpendicular to line ab in the plane of the surface. Electrons emitted from contact a with speed v will move in the plane figure along a circle of radius r, which is determined by the Lorentz force and is equal to mv/eB, where m and e are the mass and charge of the electron*). If the distance ab is less than the diameter of the circle 2r, then at contact b there will be registrations of electrons escaping from a not normal to the surface. When ab > 2r, electrons will stop coming to contact b. Let us now double the magnetic field. The radius r will decrease accordingly by half. Electrons can now arrive at contact b, reflecting the specular from the surface

*) Not in any real metal the trajectory of an electron in a magnetic field is a circle. This only happens in alkali metals. In general, due to the interaction of an electron with a crystal lattice, its trajectory may be distorted.
