a) Explain why four consecutive canceled numbers cannot occur.

Prove that among the excellent numbers there are infinitely many b) pairs, $\theta^*$) tracks of consecutive numbers.

First decision. Continuing the reasoning of point 6), consider three consecutive numbers $n^2k^2-1, n^2k^2$ and $n^2k^2+1$, where $n^2=a^2+b^2$. The second and third of these numbers are sums of two squares for all $k=1, 2, \dots$, so the problem will be solved if we can choose a $n$ such that for an infinite set of positive integers $k$ the number $n^2k^2-1$ can be represented as a sum of two squares.

Let's take $n=17$ $(17^2=8^2+15^2)$. Let us represent the number $n^2k^2-1$ in the following form: $n^2k^2-1=(17k)^2-1=(17k-1)^2+(34k-2)$, and show that the number $34k-2$ is an exact square for the infinite set $k$.

Let $34k-2=(2m)^2$. Then $k=(2m^2+1)/17$. It is an integer if $m^2$ when divided by 17 leaves a remainder of 8, that is, if $m=17l\pm 5$ and therefore $k=34l^2\pm 20l+3$. So, we get the following triples of consecutive excellent numbers: $(578l^2\pm 340l+50)^2+(34l\pm 10)^2,(510l^2\pm 300l+45)^2+(272l^2\pm 160l+24)^2,(578l^2\pm 340l+51)^2+1^2$.

The second solution is based on the beautiful Christmas trees written in the margins. From this lie it follows that if we represent two numbers as the sum of the squares of two natural numbers, then their product is also the sum of two squares or is itself a square (if $ac-bd=0$). In particular, the square of an odd canceled number is a canceled number (if $n=a^2+b^2$, then $n^2=(a^2-b^2)^2+(2a^2b^2)^2$, and $a^2-b^2\neq 0$, since $n$ is odd).

Let $n-1, n$ and $n+1$ be canceled numbers and $n$ odd. Then $n^2-1, n^2$ and $n^2+1$ are also three consecutive canceled numbers. Indeed, $n^2-1$ is the product of the cancelable numbers $n-1$ and $n+1$, and this number itself is obviously not a square; $n^2$ - square of an odd perfect number; $n^2+1=n^2+1^2$. From the numbers $n^2-1, n^2$ and $n^2+1$, the cancelable numbers $n^4-1, n^4$ and $n^4+1$ are similarly obtained, etc. Thus, the triple of cancelable numbers $72, 73, 74$, given in the conditions, gives an infinite set of the form $73^{2k}-1, 73^{2k}, 73^{2k}+1$ ($k=0, 1, 2, \dots$).

Third, decision. Consider triples of consecutive numbers of the form $8n^2=(2n)^2+(2n)^2, 8n^2+1$ and $8n^2+2=(2n-1)^2+(2n+1)^2$. Let us show that for $n=(m^2+m)/2$, where $m=1, 2, \dots$, all such numbers are cancelled. It is enough to consider the second numbers:

$$8n^2+1=2(m^2+m)^2+1=2m^4+4m^3+2m^2+1=\left(m^2-1\right)^2+(m^2+2m)^2.$$

This solution is the shortest, but also the most artificial of the three.
