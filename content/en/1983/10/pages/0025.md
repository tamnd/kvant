⟦folio 23⟧

Nini Friedrich was doomed to remain a second-class citizen.

After the end of the Seven Years' War (1763), Euler was still thinking decisively about returning to Russia. In 1746 and 1750 he had already received an invitation through the representative of the Academy, Count K. G. Razumovsky, but then he politely postponed the decision for an indefinite period.

With the rise of Catherine II in Russia (1762), negotiations on the move resumed with renewed vigor. Catherine II really wanted to get Eilar. Euler almost left for Russia in 1763, but still refused to move. However, two years later, Euler aroused the king’s anger by interceding for the academic treasurer during an audit.

Euler informs Russia of his conditions: salary of 3,000 rubles. (a representative received such a salary; an academician’s salary usually did not exceed 1,200 rubles), a place as an academician in physics for his son Johann Albrest, a suitable place for other sons (an artilleryman and a doctor), an apartment free of soldiers’ quarters, and, finally, the establishment for him of the post of vice president with the appropriate rank. Once upon a time, Friedrich refused to give Euler the vacant post of chairman of the Berlin Academy. Now Euler wanted to at least partially implement his pure plans in St. Petersburg (he did not apply for the position of chairman, believing that in Russia it should be filled by a nobleman). On January 6, 1766, Catherine writes to Chancellor Count Vorontsov: “Mr. Eilar’s ​​letter to you brought me more convenience, because I learned from it about his desire to re-enter my service. Of course, I find it perfect for the desired title of Vice-President of the Academy of Sciences, but for this some measures must be taken before I establish this title - I say I will establish it, since its bottom did not exist. In the current state of affairs, there is no money for a desire of 3,000 rubles, but for a person with such merits as Mr. Euler, I will add to the academic desire from state income, which together will amount to the required 3,000 rubles. He will have a government card and not the slightest shadow of sweets. ...I am confident that my Academy will be reborn from the ashes of such an important acquisition, and I congratulate myself in advance for returning a great man to Russia.” Having learned of Eilar’s ​​desire to take part in the restructuring of the Academy, the Empress promises “not to make any changes at the Academy before his arrival, in order to better negotiate with him about improvement...”. With great diplomatic skill, Euler is denied a rank: “I would give him a rank when he wants, if I were not afraid that this rank would compare him with many people who are not worthy of Mr. Eilar. Since his fame is the best way to show him due respect.” Euler probably quickly realized that the generous empress had to clearly explain the boundaries of what was permitted, agreed to all the conditions and decided to “end his days in the service of this unjust sovereign.”
