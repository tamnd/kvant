The first years of the Academy

Peter I thought about organizing an Academy in Russia in the last years of the 17th century. Beginning in 1711, he discussed his plans with Leibniz three times and even enlisted the latter in Russian service.

Leibnitz did not consider the lack of science in Russia to be an obstacle to the creation of the Academy and even to find some advantages in this. However, few people in Russia shared this optimism. There were many difficulties along the way of the project, but in 1724 the Senate decided to create the Academy of Sciences. At this time, even the word “science” did not yet exist in the Russian language and the Academy was called “Di Sciences Academy”.

In 1725, Peter died without waiting for the opening of the Academy. Catherine I, not without hesitation, carried out her husband’s plan, although she did not share his interest in science (as a contemporary writes, “the laudable recipes of scientists were incomprehensible to Her Majesty”). The fate of the Academy was always hanging in the balance. It was perceived as an exclusively German phenomenon, and the Russian party, in particular Menshikov, was opposed to it. The academicians tried to establish contacts with the Russian public, who well understood the functions of the Academy. Twice a week its doors opened to visitors. Sometimes you could see something amazing there.

On February 24, 1729, “Professor Leitman managed to change the image of the state coat of arms (using prizes) into a portrait of the reigning emperor.” The academies have somewhat established themselves with their success in organizing “amusing lights” and illuminations, in combination with solemn od.
