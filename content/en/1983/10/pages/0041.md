⟦folio 39⟧

And if...,
what then?

Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
I. L. NIKOLSKAYA,
N. G. SABLINA

At the end of the last lesson, Tatyana Ivanovna, their class teacher, entered the class where Petya is studying.

“Guys,” she said, “I have good news for you: twenty students from the class will go to Moscow in the summer.”

“Hurray!” everyone shouted.
— Who will go to Moscow? - Petya asked.

“In order to go to Moscow, it is enough to finish the school year without C grades,” answered Tatyana Ivanovna.

Petya came home excited. Kata immediately noticed this and asked what was the matter. Petya told her about the trip to Moscow.

“Well,” Kata drawled, “you’ll get a C in Russian this year, and maybe also in mathematics.”

“Katka is right,” thought Petya. “I can still get a B in math this year, but in Russian... I’ll go see Mitya; he is a student, he has to come up with something.

Mitya listened attentively to Petya and advised him not to give up too soon.

“Yes,” Petya said sadly, “Tatyana Ivanovna, you know what she’s like: if she’s already said it, she’ll certainly do it.”

“Wait, let’s figure it out,” said Mitya. “What does the phrase “In order to go to Moscow, all you have to do is finish the school year without C grades?” The same thing can be said like this:

“If a student finishes the school year without C grades, he will go to Moscow.” This complex sentence is formed using a logical connective “if... then...” from two simple sentences: “The student will finish the school year without C grades” and “He will go to Moscow.” Let's denote the first of these sentences by the letter A, and the second by the letter B.
