Petya, having quickly completed his homework, went to the cinema, and for you, the reader, we offer you to solve several problems.

Exercise
1. Which of the following statements are true and which are false?
a) If Paris is located on the Seine, then Australia is in the northern hemisphere.
b) If Australia is in the northern hemisphere, then Paris is located on the Seine.
c) If Australia is in the northern hemisphere, then Paris is located on the Thames.
d) If 1 = 0, then 2 = 1.
e) If 1 = 0, then 2 = 2.

2. For any x and y the following statements are true:
a) “If x is the brother of y, then y is the brother of x”?
b) “If x is the brother of y, then x and y are relatives”?
3. Does the statement “If a student studies a lot, then he passes exams successfully” follow from the statement “A student who fails an exam did not study much”?

Changing the figure of the Earth and its rotation
(For the beginning, see p. 25)

Secondly, this is not enough, but the constant increase in the length of the day is superimposed on seasonal changes in the Earth’s rotation, caused by various movements of the masses of the atmosphere and ocean. Thirdly, the speed of the Earth's rotation changes (usually in jumps) during earthquakes that move the masses of the Earth's interior. For example, these movements, as a rule, are such that they tend to make the figure of the Earth more spherical (as shown by the arrows in figure), while the speed of rotation of the Earth increases.

In particular, such an increase in rotation speed was observed in the period from 1973 to 1978, when the length of the day decreased by 0.006 s. During these observations, information was obtained about a regular change in the acceleration of free fall.

As the Earth's shape approaches spherical, the acceleration force also tends to an average value of 9.7977 m/s², increasing at low latitudes and decreasing north and south of latitude 35°. Now it is clear why the first changes showed a decrease in acceleration: Moscow, Novosibirsk, and Potsam are located at a latitude from 52 to 56°.
