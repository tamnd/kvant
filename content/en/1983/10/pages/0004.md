Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. A. FALKOVSKY

What does the surface of a solid look like? The answer to this question, please, is very clear: the type of surface depends on the method of its preparation. If you take a crystal and walk along its edge with sandpaper, the picture under a microscope will resemble the view of mountain ranges from an airplane window. And before processing with sandpaper, the surface of a good crystal seems to us to be ideally robust, mirror-like. In reality, there are nerves on such a surface, and its mirror image is only that the size of the nerves does not exceed the wavelength of visible light - our instrument. Light does not “see” defects whose size is relative to its wavelength. Just as we do not notice the nervousness on the road that is in comparison with our step. How to detect these defects? What will we see on the surface with higher magnification? In order to answer this question, various and powerful research methods have now been developed. But before we talk about them, a few words about why detailed information about the structure of the surface is needed.

At first glance, it may seem surprising that in our space research and elementary particle physics, the “nearby” field of science - physics, chemistry and surface mechanics - is experiencing rapid development. This is dictated, of course, by the demands of new technology. The behavior of small-sized elements used in microelectronics is largely determined by the properties of their surface. The problem of plasma confinement requires clarification of the mechanism of particle interaction and study with the boundaries of the volume where the plasma is located. The design of vacuum pumps, as well as the separation and purification of gases, faces the problem of adsorption, that is, the absorption of gas by a branched surface of a solid.
