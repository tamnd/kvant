Until such time were we distracted from the fact that the conductor has boundaries. Now let's teach it.

We see that the resistivity of a massive conductor is determined by the reciprocal of the time between two successive collisions of an electron with atoms or with defects in the conductor. In the presence of boundaries, the role of m is played by the time between two successive Stolnov electrons with boundaries. This time is equal in order of magnitude to $d/v$, where $v$ is the characteristics of the movement of electrons in the metal, $d$ is the thickness of the sample. The contribution made by insignificant (stationary) currents. They arise when a magnet approaches the surface of a superconductor due to the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction. For example, the induction current in a superconductor is determined by Lenz's law, and the magnetic field created repels the ring magnet, balancing the effect of gravity. The slightest movement of the magnet causes a change in the magnetic field of superconducting currents - the field “follows” the magnet; In this case, the concave surface of the superconductor leads to the fact that the equilibrium position of the magnet turns out to be stable.

The installation with a floating magnet is jokingly called the “coffin of Mohammed” (according to legend, the coffin of the vice of Mohammed hung in space without any support). The device, which is shown on the first page of the cover, is needed for a convenient collision resistance with boundaries, we can estimate it by substituting 1/τ values ​​$v/d$ into (a) together:

$$\varrho_s = q \frac{mv}{ne^2d},$$

(b)

where the factor $q$ is introduced in order to take into account the efficiency of surface interactions: for an ideal mirror surface $q = 0$, in the case of completely diffuse reflection of electrons from the surface $q = 1$.

Each electron can, in principle, experience so much energy both on the surface and inside the conductor. Therefore, the total resistance of the sample is equal to the sum of terms (a) and (b):

$$\varrho = \varrho_0 + \varrho_s = \varrho_0 \left( 1 + q \frac{v\tau}{d} \right) = \varrho_0 \left( 1 + q \frac{l}{d} \right),$$

where $l = v\tau$ is the average length of the electron's free run, that is, the average distance traveled by the electron between two successive collisions.

The superconductor is a thin lead disk with a diameter of 16 cm, the surface of which is made slightly concave. (The outer wall of the vessel follows the shape of the superconductor; the concavity of the surface ensures the stability of the magnet's equilibrium.)
