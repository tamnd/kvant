Non-Inertial Reporting Systems
Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. G. ASLAMAZOV

When Kolernik replaced the geocentric system of the world of Ptolemy with a heliocentric system and explained the movement of all planets in it, he produced a solution in human thinking. For many centuries, people considered the motion of all bodies only relative to the Earth. In modern language, they used a single, dedicated reporting system associated with the Earth, although the movement of the planets and the Sun was described in a very complex way.

Later, from the works of Galileo and Newton, it became clear that not only the reporting system associated with the Sun and the fixed stars is suitable for describing motion, but also any other systems that move uniformly and rectilinearly in relation to it. This is how inertial reporting systems were discovered, in which the motion of bodies is controlled by simple Newtonian laws.

Well, what happened next with the earthly reporting system? After all, for us the Earth is, after all, a separate system; When studying the movement of bodies on Earth, most often you can completely forget about the Sun and stars. Strictly speaking, this reporting system is not inertial, since the Earth rotates around its axis and the Sun. However, the accelerations associated with these movements, we, and usually we make only a small mistake, use Newton's laws to describe the movement. Usually, but not always.

Many have probably been to Leningrad and seen the famous mint in St. Isaac's Cathedral, or at least heard about it (Fig. 1). This pendulum not only oscillates, but the plane of its oscillations also slowly turns. This observation was first made by the French scientist Foucault in 1851. The experiment was carried out in the huge hall of the Paris Pantheon, the pendulum ball had a mass of 28 kg, and the length of the thread was 67 m. Since then, such a pendulum has been called a Foucault pendulum. How to explain its movement?

If Newton’s laws were strictly observed on Earth, then, as is known from the school physics course,

This means that in the reporting system associated with the Earth, Newton’s laws must be “corrected”. This is done by introducing special forces - inertial forces.

Inertial forces come into play in any reporting system moving with respect to inertial acceleration. Such systems are called non-inertial reporting systems.

To find the inertial forces, it is necessary to consider the motion of the same body in two reporting systems - non-inertial and inertial. The result depends, first of all, on how the non-inertial system moves. She can move

slowly, it can rotate, and combinations of these movements also occur.

No new forces act on the person standing in the back (for simplicity, we will assume that the friction forces are small), and the person begins to move relative to the truck with acceleration. It is clear that it is impossible to simply accept Newton’s laws in this system: there is acceleration, but no forces. That is why it is necessary to introduce fictitious forces - inertial forces.

Another example of a non-inertial reporting system, which we will examine in more detail, is a moving accelerated elevator. When the elevator moves upward, you feel “heaviness” during acceleration and “lightening” during sudden braking. If you use Newton's laws in the elevator system, then this cannot be understood. Indeed, the body is acted upon by gravity forces $m\vec{g}$ and reaction forces $\vec{N}$. Since the person will die, the reaction force must be equal in magnitude to the force of gravity. But from experience it is clear that this is not so. Therefore (if you count in an elevator), you need to add some fictitious force to the force of gravity when accelerating the elevator and subtract it when separating. This is the force of inertia.

What is the force of inertia? In order to find out this, we first find the reaction force acting on a person when accelerating and decelerating the elevator in a stationary reporting system. Newton's second law is valid for such a system

$$\pm ma = N - mg,$$

where the “+” sign corresponds to the acceleration of the elevator (acceleration is directed upward), and the “-” sign refers to the case of its deceleration (acceleration is directed downward). Hence the reaction force

$$N = mg \pm ma.$$

In a system connected to an elevator, the reaction force must be the same, so it is necessary to add the inertial force to the force of gravity when accelerating the elevator and subtract it when decelerating. From the last equality we obtain that the inertial force acting on a person in the elevator is equal to

$$\vec{F}_n = -m\vec{a}.$$

Now everything is in order: when the elevator accelerates, inertia seems to make the body heavier, and when it slows down, on the contrary, it lightens it (Fig. 2). Please note that physical inertia is similar in appearance to gravity: both are proportional to the mass of the body.

Sometimes you hear, for example, that light inertia throws passengers back when the train accelerates and only them forward when it slows down, that is, they talk about inertial forces as ordinary forces. In this case, it is necessary to add that the movement is considered relative to the accelerated moving reporting system (in this case, relative to the train). But this system should not forget about the origin of inertial forces. These forces are fundamentally different from ordinary forces: they are not related to the interaction of bodies. This is why they are often called fictitious forces or pseudo forces. The inertial force is introduced artificially in order to describe the change in the acceleration of a body in a non-inertial reporting system compared to an inertial one for the same real forces driving the body.

Suppose that a truck with a person in the back, already familiar to us, is turning. Then a person can hit the side and even get a bump. Why is this happening? Regarding the Earth, everything is clear: a person continues to move in a straight line, and the side of the truck, turning, approaches him. In the absence system associated with the truck, on the contrary, the person moves closer to the side, although no new real forces act on the person in this system. In order to explain this movement in the non-inertial system of the truck, it is necessary to introduce a fictitious force - the centering of the inertial force, directed along the radius from the center of rotation. That's it - the forces are fictitious, but the bumps are real!

Another example is the man on the carousel. Why does it move in a circle? In an inertial system of absence, the answer is given by Newton's second law: the reaction force from the side wall of the chair, directed towards the center, causes centrostrement. Is it possible to understand this movement and use Newton's laws in a rotating system? Again, no: in this system a person will die, and at the same time the force of reaction from the cross acts on him. But everything falls into place if you introduce a centering of inertial force directed from the center of rotation. Then you can explain why there is no movement in this system.
