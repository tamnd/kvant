Renju

V. A. SAPRONOV

So, you have established the basics of the attack. But it is equally important to be able to defend yourself. Only skillful defense allows White to survive in the initial period of the game and create the foundation for the transition to countertillion.

In the game on figure 1, White builds a defensive network (the triangles show the possibility of developing this network), so that the four black checkers rest against its “nodes”. Effective in some cases, the network still cannot become a universal means of defense: firstly, Black will try to occupy one of White’s strongholds and gain operational space; secondly, with this method of defense you can only achieve a draw - it is difficult to switch to counterplay.

Pozny, if possible, should build his defense on counters. A particularly effective remedy is contrichach. In the game Kotsev - Sapronov from the first World Correspondence Championship (Fig. 2), Black, after moving in point 1, believed that he had gains at points A and B. However, White moved in point 2, and Black found that his attack was faltering, and there was no satisfactory defense.

As a result, the Swede Janson and I scored the same number of points; Based on the best ratio of moves in won and lost Japanese games, the judge declared me the winner in October 1982. (5 more people took part in the championship - one person each from Bulgaria, Denmark and Canada and two Japanese.)

You've probably already noticed how expensive a mistake in the opening is, especially for White. However, it is inaccurate and the party cannot be saved.

In the third, openings are classified according to the first three moves.

On the second move, White has, up to symmetry, the fifth varrant (Fig. 3). Of course, they have the right to play further away from the center, but in this case their checker may be too far from the “theater of war.”

On the second move to points A or B, it is advisable for Black to move to one of points 1-3, 5-8.

Less studied are the consequences of the second move to points C, D, E. In any case, Black can move to points 1 or 2 and transfer the game to the same openings as after A or B. However, many players respond with an attack at points distant from the white checker. At figure 4 (game Sapronov - Sapronov) after the ninth move White has no defense.
