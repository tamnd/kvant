V Moscow horizontal bar for young physicists

I will not reveal these secrets here, -
The question, not the answer, is my confession.
G. I b s e n

The V Moscow horizontal bar was held by the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University from December 20, 1982 to April 3, 1983. The organizing committee of the Turinik was headed by the Vice-President of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Academician E. P. Velikov, the jury was a professor of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University V. L. Bonch-Bruevich. 40 schools in Moscow and the Moscow region took part in this modern high school class.

As usual, the horizontal bar was carried out in three stages.

I round - correspondence collective competition. The school was offered 20 problems for a period of two months for collective solution. Based on the results of this competition, teams from 15 schools were admitted to the second round.

2nd round - qualifying rounds, which determined the financial tournament. Qualifying physiognomies were held in Moscow schools according to the instructions of the correspondence competition.

The third round - the financial tour was held at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. Its program included: a homework competition, a captain's competition, a physical competition for three teams - a financial tournament, a large-scale competition, and loading of the winners.

The 1st degree diploma and the challenge prize of the horizontal bar were awarded to the teams from school No. 57 and No. 7 in Moscow, the 2nd degree diploma to the team from school No. 710 in Moscow, the 3rd degree diploma to the teams from school No. 444 in Moscow, No. 82 village. Chernogolovka and Physics School No. 18 at Moscow State University. For winning individual horizontal bar competitions, 17 schoolchildren were awarded certificates and gifts. The Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University awarded the OLS schools whose teams showed high results in this condition with valuable physical instruments. Magazine «Kvant» awarded a number of schoolchildren with books in the “Library «Kvant» series with authors’ names.

The VI horizontal bar of young physicists will study on October 15, 1983. TYUF Organizing Committee.

The ass of the correspondence collective competition

The conditions of the tasks are formulated as briefly as possible. Necessary additional data and qualifications should be inserted using common sense. You can use these tasks when organizing quizzes at school, evenings of entertaining science, tours for young physicists, and use them in physics clubs.

1. "Goll." “...a powerful blow! Go-o-ol!!!" What is the maximum pressure in a soccer ball when it is kicked?

2. "Rain". Estimate how many times the amount of poplar fluff in the air will decrease after a thunderstorm (Moscow, June, thunderstorm of medium strength and duration).

3. "Earth's gravitational field." How does the voltage of the Earth's gravitational field depend on the distance to its center?

4. "Earth's Electric Field". Suggest a method and change the voltage of the Earth's electric field.

5. "Explosion". On a small island in the Kuril ridge, the physicist was hunting for butterflies. Suddenly, at the moment when the butterfly folded its wings, it was sharply carried 10 cm to the sides. It turned out that 300 km from the observed physicist, a volcanic eruption began - it produced the first powerful explosion with high gases and ash. Estimate the energy of this explosion.

6. "Swamp". Explain why a person can drown in more, even if the average density of the swamp environment is significantly greater than the density of water.

7. "Well". They say that on a bright sunny day you can see the stars from a deep well. Explain what stars can be observed this way and what the well should be like for this.

8. "Marina Trench". What is the water pressure at the bottom of the deepest ocean fin?

9. "Balloon". A balloon with a volume of 1000 m³ is in equilibrium at a height of H = 300 meters above the Earth's surface. Suddenly a golden eagle (m=10 kg) sat on top of him. How will the balloon descend?

10. "Balloon" Explore the dependence of excess pressure in a balloon on its diameter.

11. "Paraffin". Measure the coefficient of volumetric expansion of paraffin in the temperature range 0-80°C.

12. "Bouncing Ball" A horizontal elastic plate oscillates harmoniously up and down with amplitude A and frequency v. Determine the maximum height that a steel ball placed on this face can reach. Rate via

14. "Scrap". Estimate how much work is required to tie a knot in a piece of steel.

15. "Lens". A point source of light is placed at the focus of a converging lens. What is the intensity distribution of the light beam produced in this way along its diameter? Suggest a method for creating a parallel light beam with a uniform intensity distribution across the cross section of the beam.

16. "Billiard". When hitting billiard balls, there is no sound from LIB. What is the pitch of the fundamental tone? What are the overtones?

17. "Magnetic moment". In a cylindrical vessel there is an electron gas ($n=10^{13}$ cm$^{-3}$, $T=300$ K). The walls of the vessel elastically reflect electrons. Electrons do not interact with each other. The cylinder is placed in a magnetic field, the induction line of which is parallel to the cylinder axis. Determine the total magnetic moment of the electron gas.

18. "Plumbing." How, without destroying a water pipe, can you determine in which direction the water flows in it if you only have access to a limited area ($l=2$ m) of this pipe?

19. "Spray". A pebble falls into the water from a height $H$. What is the maximum height of splashing water?

20. "Capstan". The rope, one end attached to the wall, is wound on the electric motor shaft ($n$ turns). What is the tension in the fixed section of the rope if a force $F$ is applied to the free end of the rope? Rope-shaft friction coefficient m, shaft rotation frequency $v$.

Homework competition

1. "Performance". Perform a performance on a physical topic with the participation of team members and big boys. The duration of the performance is 5 minutes. The genre is arbitrary.

2. "Weightlessness". Explain the principle of operation and demonstrate a device made in the school laboratory that demonstrates the phenomenon of weightlessness (for example, a flashlight that lights up when it is in zero gravity).

Main Problems Competition

The team was given two hours to solve these problems and submit written reports to the Turir jury.

1. "Helium". The experimenter makes a deep vidho, and then with a deep breath fills the lungs and airways with gel (through a gasold tube). After this, the experimenter pronounces dry several sounds of his speech. Firstly, the height becomes several times higher. Secondly, the timbre of the voice is greatly distorted, making it unrecognizable. As the helium is exhausted, practically after the first breath of air, the normal sound of the voice is restored. Explain why the pitch of the human voice is impaired in this experiment. Make numerical estimates.

2. "Magnet". It is known that using a magnet you can lift weights. In the experiment demonstrating the lifting force of a magnet, the following were used: a torid ceramic magnet, to which a handle made of non-magnetic materials was glued for convenience, a lifting platform in the form of two steel disks connected along the axis by a rod, and a set of removable weights. It turned out that the maximum weight of the load that can be lifted with this magnet is $P_1$.

If now a thin strip of tin is tightly placed on the side surface of the magnet, the lifting force of the magnet will increase significantly: $P_2>P_1$. In the experiment: the outer diameter of the magnet is 60 mm, the inner diameter is 30 mm, the width is 15 mm, the thickness of the tin strip is 0.15 mm, $P_1=25$ N, $P_2=35$ N. How can the column explain the significant increase in the lifting force of the magnet?

3. "Refractive index". Determine the refractive index of the glass from which the cylindrical rod is made. The experimenters have at their disposal: a cylindrical glass at the end of the rope or through the end of the rope attached with a ladder, a light source - a gelyan-neon ladder, a disk with a cabbed hole ($\varnothing=1$ mm and 2 mm) on a shakit, a white screen, a caliper and a tape measure (2 meters).
