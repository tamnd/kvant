M826. Three numbers were written on the board. Then one of them was erased and the sum of two other numbers was written together, reduced by one. This operation was repeated several times and as a result the numbers 17, 1967, 1983 were obtained. Could the numbers a) 2, 2, 2 be originally written? 6) 3, 3, 3? (8)

A. A. Berzins

M827. It is known that the four synch triangles on figure 1 (p. 44) are equal in size.
a) Prove that the three red quadrilaterals on this figure are also of equal size.
b) Find the area of ​​one quadrilateral if the area of ​​one singo triangle is 1.(8)

B. I. Chinik

M828*. Is it possible to arrange integers in the cells of an infinite sheet of checkered paper so that the sum of the numbers in each rectangle of size 4 × 6 cells, the sides of which go along the lines of the grid, is equal to a) 10; 6) 1? (8)

N. Yu. Nechevgaev

M829. Prove that among any 2m + 1 distinct integers whose modulo does not exceed 2m - 1, there are three numbers whose sum is equal to 0. (9)

N. V. Kartatsov

M830*. A student practices solving quadratic equations. Having written out some equation $x^2+p_1x+q_1=0$, he solves it and, making sure that it has two roots, composes the second equation $x^2+p_2x+q_2=0$, in which $p_2$ is the smaller root, and $q_2$ is the larger root of the first equation. From the second equation he makes up the third, if possible, etc.
a) Prove that this equation cannot continue indefinitely.
b) Find the most simple square trilens satisfying the specified condition.(10)

M. V. Sapir

F839. If a hexagonal pencil is placed on an inclined plane making an angle $\alpha$ from the horizontal surface, perpendicular to its generatrix (the line of intersection of the plane with the horizontal surface), the pencil will dig up. If you put it parallel to the generatrix, it will roll down. At what angles $\phi$ between the axis of the pencil and the generatrix of the inclined plane (Fig. 3) will the pencil be in equilibrium? (8)

S. S. Krotov

F840. In a horizontal flat box measuring $10\times10$ cm, 1000 small steel balls of mass $m=0,5$ mg each lie randomly. The box begins to move at a speed of $v_0=10$ m/s perpendicular to one of the side walls. Considering the impacts of the balls on the walls of the box and on each other to be absolutely elastic, determine: a) what impulses the balls will transmit to each of the side walls in the first 10 seconds; b) what impulses do the walls receive over the next 10 seconds after the box is sharply braked. (9)

A. R. Zilberman

F841. The descent vehicle of a spacecraft approaches the surface of a certain planet at a constant speed, transmitting data on external pressure to the spacecraft. A graph of pressure $p$ (in terms of units) versus time $t$ is shown in figure 4. Having descended on the surface of the planet, the device changed and transmitted on board data on temperature and free fall acceleration: $T=700$ K, $g=10$ m/$c^2$. Determine the descent speed of the spacecraft if it is known that the planet’s atmosphere consists of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$). Also determine the temperature at an altitude of $h=15$ km above the surface of the planet. (9)
