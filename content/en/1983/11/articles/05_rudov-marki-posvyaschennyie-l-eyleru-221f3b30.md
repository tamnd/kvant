⟦folio 23⟧

In mechanics, Euler was the first to move from Newton's geometric language to an analytical language, which made it possible to use purely mathematical techniques from the field of differential equations. Following Mauperthune, Euler proposed the first correct formulation of the principle of least action, which makes it possible to derive the laws of mechanics of central forces from the assumption that some quantities in motion must be minimal (see «Kvant», 1975, No. 12, p. 34). He also moved from the mechanics of a matter point to the mechanics of a solid body of finite dimensions.

Euler's studies in astronomy were a continuation of his studies in mechanics. His area of ​​interest is celestial mechanics. He was able to realize his powerful computing abilities here (as the French astronomer Arago wrote, he “calculated how a person breathes”). His theory of the Moon allows Mayer to compile lunar tables of unprecedented accuracy. Euler spent a lot of time calculating the elliptical orbits of comets, in particular the famous comet Lexaeus (1769).

***

In our review, we did not have the opportunity to touch on many aspects of Euler’s work, for example, optics, potatoes, ballistics and ship theory. We also left aside his pedagogical legacy, in particular, his famous “Letters to the Princess” - a kind of attempt at an encyclopedia of all science contemporary to Euler. Great mathematicians of the 19th century studied from Euler's work. “Read Euler - this is our common teacher,” Lapaz said. According to Gauss, "the study of Euler's work remains the best school in the various branches of mathematics, and nothing else can replace it." Euler was appreciated primarily by those who studied his works, and did not evaluate his legacy by “peaks,” who learned from him and used his guiding ideas.

Collector's Corner

There are only a few postage stamps associated with Leonhard Euler. The figure shown here reproduces all stamps issued before 1983. The first of them were printed in the GDR in 1950 for the 200th anniversary of the Berlin Academy of Sciences. The rest were released in 1957 in the USSR,

GDR and Switzerland for the 250th anniversary of Euler's birth.
