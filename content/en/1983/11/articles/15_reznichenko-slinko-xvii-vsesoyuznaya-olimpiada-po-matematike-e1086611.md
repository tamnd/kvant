XVII All-Union Olympiad in Mathematics

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. V. REZNICHENKO,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. M. SLINKO

From April 13 to April 20, the final stage of the XVII All-Union Schoolchildren Olympid in Mathematics was held in Chisinau. It was attended by 146 schoolchildren (42 eighth-graders, 51 ninth-graders) - winners of Resursikan Olympids, winners of the Moscow and Leningrad City Olympids, and schoolchildren awarded I and II degree diplomas at the previous All-Union Olympiad. It was worth Soviet Moldova being awarded this part for the second time. Time flies quickly. Each, just recently, Chisinau hosted the final stage of the XVII All-Union Mathematical Olympiad, and today two of its winners - S.V. Konyagin and E.I. Khukhro - came to Chisinau as members of the jury.

On April 13, the day of the race, the participants in the Olympiad were shown documents and films telling about the history, culture and economy of Moldova. Upon registration, each participant was given a commemorative folder with the program of the final stage of the Olympiad, an invitation card to the grand opening, a special concert program, memorable signs and Moldovan souvenirs.

On April 14, Olympiad participants laid flowers at the monument to V.I. Lenin and the Memorial of Glory. And then at the Palace of Culture of Trade Unions of the state there was a trade opening. The Minister of Education of the Moldavian USSR D. G. Zidu addresses the participants of the Olympids with these words of welcome. The secretary of the Central Committee of the Komsomol of Moldova E.M. Beiko, the chairman of the jury, academician, wished the children a successful performance, interesting, creative work, and pure struggle. Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B.V. Gnedenko and deputy. Chairman of the Jury Corresponding Member APN USSR V. D. Belousov. The application was unanimously decided to be sent to the Honorary Chairman of the Academic Olympida jury. A. N. Kolmogorov, who turned 80 these days, is privileged to telephone.

The participants wrote the assignments for the final stage of the Olympiad on April 15 and 16. The task for each of the two days consists of four tasks, the solution of which was allocated five hours.

Below are the conditions for all tasks. Some of these problems are included in the Problem Book «Kvant» («Kvant» Nos. 9, 10; the number under which the problem is placed there is indicated after the problem statement), readers will be able to find their solutions in subsequent issues of the journal.

Quests

First day
8th grade

1. In the grid shown in figure 1, each cell is 1x1 in size. Can this grid be represented as a union of the following sets: a) eight broken lines, each of which has a length of 5; b) broken heels, each of which has a length of 8? (M824).

2. Three integers were written on the board. Then one of them was erased and the sum of the other two remaining numbers, reduced by one, was written together. These operations were repeated several times as a result of obtaining

Chile numbers 17, 1967, 1983. Could the numbers originally be written on the board: a) 2, 2, 2; b) 3, 3, 3? (M826).

3. Three circles touched each other in pairs externally at points X, Y, Z. Then the radii of these circles were increased by more than $\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ times, maintaining the centers. Prove that every point of triangle XYZ is covered by at least one of the enlarged circles.

4. Given several different natural numbers, enclosed between the squares of two consecutive natural numbers. Prove that all their pairwise products are also distinct.

9th grade

1. Find all solutions to the system of equations
$$\begin{cases} y^2 = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x \\ x^2 = y^3 - 3y^2 + 2y \end{cases}$$

2. The natural number $K$ has $n$ digits in decimal notation. This number was rounded to the nearest ten, then the last digit was rounded by zero and the number of tens was increased by one if that last digit was greater than four. The resulting number was similarly rounded to the nearest hundred, and so on. As a result of the last, $(n-1)$ rounding, the result was $\tilde{K}$. Prove that $\tilde{K} < \frac{18}{13} K$.

3. In triangle $ABC$, point $D$ is the midpoint of side $AB$, points $E$ and $F$ lie on segments $AC$ and $BC$, respectively. Prove that the area of ​​triangle $DEF$ does not exceed the sum of the areas of triangles $ADE$ and $BDF$.

4. Will there be periodic sequences $(a_n)$ and $(\beta_n)$, consisting, respectively, of the last digits of the integers $[(\sqrt{10})^n]$ and $[(\sqrt{2})^n]$? (Here $[x]$ is the integer part of the number $x$).

10th grade

1. The values ​​$\alpha$ and $\beta$ of two acute angles satisfy the equality $\sin^2 \alpha + \sin^2 \beta = \sin (\alpha + \beta)$. Prove that $\alpha + \beta = \frac{\pi}{2}$.

2. Task No. 2 for 9th grade.

3. The vertices of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ are orthogonally projected onto two planes. Points $A_1, B_1, C_1, D_1$ and $A_2, B_2, C_2, D_2$ are projections of the corresponding vertices. Prove that one of the planes can be moved in space so that the lines $A_1A_2, B_1B_2, C_1C_2, D_1D_2$ become parallel.

4. A student practices solving quadratic equations. Having solved the next quadratic equation and made sure that it has two roots, he composes the following equations according to the rules: the free term is equal to the larger root, the coefficient of the variable $x$ is equal to the smaller root, the coefficient of $x^2$ is equal to one. Prove that this exercise cannot continue indefinitely. What is the most number of equations he can possibly solve? (M830)

The guys coped quite successfully with the tasks proposed on the first day. The greatest difficulties, as expected, were caused by the tasks

8.3, 8.4, 9.4, 10.3 and 10.4. Problem 8.4 was especially difficult; Only one participant solved its sum completely, and three more participants solved it with minor shortcomings. Problem 9.1 turned out to be unexpectedly difficult. However, in none of the classes was there a problem that no one could solve.

On April 15, after completing work on the competition tasks, the guys made a fascinating examination of the Pushchino places in Moldova.

Second day

8th grade

5. The natural numbers $m, n, k$ are such that the number $m^n$ is divisible by $n^m$, and the number $n^k$ is divisible by $k^n$. Prove that the number $m^k$ is divisible by $k^m$.

6. There are two letters in the Abba flame tongue. It is known that no word of this language is the beginning of another word. can the vocabulary of the language of this flame contain 3 four-letter, 10 fifth-letter, 30 six-letter and 5 seven-letter words?

7. Is it possible to arrange integers in the cells of an infinite checkered sheet of paper so that in each straight line the markings divided on it are also equal in size? What is the area of ​​one quadrilateral if the area of ​​one triangle is 1 cm²? (M827)

9th grade

5. The group of children's cadas lined up in pairs behind each other. It turned out that in each column there are equal numbers of boys and girls, and the number of pairs containing girls and boys is equal to the number of other pairs. Prove that the number of children in the group is divisible by 8.

6. The lengths of two parallel sides of a direct equation are 1 cm. In addition, it is known that by two perpendicular lines it can be divided into four straight lines, three of which have a plane of at least 1 cm², and the fourth - at least 2 cm². For what minimum length of the other two sides of the direct equation is this possible?
