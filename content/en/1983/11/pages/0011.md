However, the small size of “supercomputers,” while being an undoubted advantage, also gives rise to certain difficulties: to manufacture elements of such small, “micron” sizes, a whole set of complex technological operations and expensive equipment are required.*

The study of solitons in semiconductors and superconductors is of interest not only to researchers specializing in the physics and technology of semiconductors and low temperatures. The fact is that the mathematical equations describing the motion of electrical domains and magnetic clots have the same form as the equations of motion of solitary waves in some biological media, the equations of interaction of particle elements, and the propagation of laser pulses. Over the course of a century and a half, the science of solitons has come a long way: from the observation of solitary waves on water to a complex and strictly mathematical description using solitons of the interaction of particle elements and, finally, the development of “supercomputers” based on superconductors. Here another property of fundamental physical phenomena manifests itself: open and detailed research, sooner or later, is necessarily followed by their active use for practical purposes.
