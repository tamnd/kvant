Experimental tour of the Olympiad in Physics

Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
V. A. ORLOV

The implementation of experimental tasks of the final round of the XVII All-round Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren took place in three universities in Vilnius: eight classics worked at the Pedagogical Institute, nine classics worked at Vilnius University. V. Kapsuk, tenth graders - at the Institute of Civil Engineering. Teachers from the physics departments of these institutes took part in preparing the problems. Of particular note are the authors of the proposed tasks: A. Kiveris, A. Medeišis, A. Brilingas and J. A. Semenov.

We present the conditions of all experimental tasks and options for their solution proposed by Olympiad students.

8th grade
Task 1
1) Investigate the dependence of the speed of movement of a body in a tube with water on the angle of inclination of the tube and plot this dependence.
2) Estimate the coefficient of friction between the body and the wall of the tube with water.
3) Assuming that the resistance force when moving a body in a liquid is proportional

speed: $F_c = \alpha v$, determine the proportionality coefficient $\alpha$.

Equipment: glass tube with water and a body (volume and density of the body are given), ruler, tripod, stopwatch, graph paper, mathematical tables.

Experiments show that the body begins to move only after the tube reaches a certain minimum inclination $\beta_0$. Let us apply to this limiting case Newton’s second law, written in projections onto the coordinate axes (Fig. 1):

$$F_A \sin \beta_0 - F_{\tau p} - mg \sin \beta_0 = 0,$$
$$F_A \cos \beta_0 - mg \cos \beta_0 - N = 0.$$

Taking into account that $F_{\tau p} = \mu N$, we get

$$(F_A - mg) \sin \beta_0 = \mu (F_A - mg) \cos \beta_0,$$

from where we find the coefficient of friction between the body and the wall of the tube with water:

$$\mu = tg \beta_0.$$

When the angle of inclination of the tube $\beta$ is greater than $\beta_0$, the body begins to move at uniform speed. But since a resistance force appears, proportional to the speed, the body quickly, as follows from experience, reaches a constant speed. It is easy to determine by the formula $v = l/t$, where $l$ is the length of the path traveled by the body at a steady speed, $t$ is the time of movement. By measuring the speed of the body in this way at different angles of inclination $\beta$, it is possible to construct an experimental graph of the dependence $v = v(\beta)$. In figure 2 this graph is shown in blue.

Students who compared the experimental curve with the theoretical one received extra points. Let us write down Newton’s second law for the case of body motion at an arbitrary angle $\beta$ and at an angle $\beta = \pi/2$:

$$(F_A - mg) \sin \beta - \mu (F_A - mg) \cos \beta - \alpha v = 0,$$
$$F_A - mg - \alpha v_{\pi/2} = 0.$$

From here

$$v = v_{\pi/2} (\sin \beta - \mu \cos \beta) = v_{\pi/2} \frac{\sin(\beta - \beta_0)}{\cos \beta_0}.$$

Graphically, this dependence is shown on figure 2 black line.

The coefficient $\alpha$ is easy to determine for the case of a body in the vertical direction:

$$\alpha = \frac{V(\rho_0 - \rho) g}{v_{\pi/2}}.$$

where $V$ is the volume of the body, $\rho$ and $\alpha$ are the densities of water and the corresponding body.

Due to the asymmetry of the body, and therefore different conditions of flow around it, when the tube is turned over, the coefficient $\alpha$ may become different. Those who studied this and found two meanings of $\alpha$ received extra points. True, as experience has shown, the difference in values ​​was within the measurement error.
