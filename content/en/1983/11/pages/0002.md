⟦folio 8⟧

These figures depict the propagation of an individual wave: the wave “runs” along the x-axis, and each wavy line is, as it were, its exchange photograph; These photographs were taken at regular intervals. Note that the wave travels at a constant speed and its shape remains unchanged. Such solitary waves - they are called solitons - can arise under certain conditions in a wide variety of media - in water, in plasma, in semiconductors and superconductors, in a chain of series-connected atoms, etc. Although the physical nature of the wave arising in a columnar system may be different, the equations describing their propagation turn out to be similar or even identical in appearance. In this issue of the magazine we publish the article “Solitons”, which talks about the history of the discovery of solitons, the research of their properties, and the practical use of these properties.
