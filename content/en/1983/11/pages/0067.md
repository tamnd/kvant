There are many known combination techniques and motifs that chess players use during the game. Some of them are geometric in nature, as evidenced by even their names: linking, distraction, enticement, overlap, clearing space, blocking, through action of pieces (reptgen), etc. On our chess pages we gradually become familiar with each of the listed techniques. Today we are talking about binding.

Donner - Huber (Büsum, 1968)
The white rook is pinned - it is on the same line (in this case, the diagonal) with the king and cannot move. But the bundle itself may not be dangerous, because it is easy to get rid of. Combinations can occur if the tying of figures is managed to be attacked along one more line, and the side can no longer withstand this action in two directions of tying. 1...R:c5 2.F:c5.
As if the danger had passed, but... 2...Rs8! So, on the fatal square it is not the rook that perishes, but the queen (3.Q:b6 R:c1+4.Npf2 ab). White people, enjoy.

Brundtrap - Budrich (Berlin, 1954)
1.Сс5! Bb6! The queen is pinned, but the bishop pinning it is also pinned. After placing b6 the matter ends in a draw, but White still uses the pin. 2.Qf4+! The blacks settled down.

Bongasser - Weiner (Turinr by correspondence, 1976)
It seems that White cannot realize the material advantage, because his rooks are tightly tied. The player who played black for several days was completely calm about his position and, probably, expected a peace proposal from his opponent. But when he opened the envelope, he was shocked...
