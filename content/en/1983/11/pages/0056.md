For this purpose, it is necessary to charge the capacitor from the external capacitance $C$ to the external voltage $U$ and discharge it into a resistor (Fig. 4), lowered into a thermostat with liquid, and this process should be repeated $n$ times. Then the energy of the electric current

$$W = n \frac{CU^2}{2}.$$

c) Lowering the resistor into a thermostat with water, and then with a liquid with a specific heat, discharge the capacitor a certain number of times and write down the law of conservation of energy for each case:

$$n_1 CU^2/2 = C_T \Delta T_1 + c_B Q_B V_B \Delta T_1,$$
$$n_2 CU^2/2 = C_T \Delta T_2 + c_x Q_x V_x \Delta T_2,$$

where $c_B$, $Q_B$, $V_B$ are the specific heat, density and volume of water in the thermostat, $c_x$, $Q_x$, $V_x$ are the corresponding parameters of the liquid liquid, $C_T$ is the thermal heat of the thermostat.

To simplify, we can select values ​​of $n_1$ and $n_2$ such that temperature changes $\Delta T_1$ and $\Delta T_2$ are the same in general experiments ($\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 = \Delta T$). You can also pour equal volumes of liquid into the thermostat ($V_B = V_x = V_\text{ж}$). Then, excluding the heat of the thermostat, for the specific heat of the liquid we obtain

$$c_x = c_B \frac{Q_B}{Q_x} - \frac{CU^2/2(n_1 - n_2)}{V_\text{ж} \Delta T_\text{ж}}.$$

However (due to the fact that it is not so easy to achieve the same temperature increase), it is more expedient to set the same energy of the electric current, that is, the same number of switchings $n$. In this case

$$c_x = c_B \frac{Q_B}{Q_x} + \frac{nCU^2/2}{Q_x V_\text{ж} \Delta T_2} - \frac{nCU^2/2}{Q_B V_\text{ж} \Delta T_1}.$$

Both methods were considered correct if they gave a correct estimate of the specific heat of the liquid (alcohol).

d) To estimate the optimal number of switchings ($n=1$ - clearly few, $n>100$ - obviously many), it was useful to estimate the time of noticeable cooling of the liquid in the thermostat, that is, the heat sensor in the surrounding space. In addition, it is important to estimate the discharge time of the capacitor (meaning its capacitance and the resistance of the resistor) in order to determine the required switching frequency of the capacitor.

When performing this task, G. Baranauskaya (Shlya), A. Korol (Novokuznetsk) and S. Orlov (Moscow) showed better results.

Characteristics of the shortcomings: neglect of the thermal capacity of the thermostat, single discharge of the capacitor, as well as insufficient movement of liquid into the vessel.
