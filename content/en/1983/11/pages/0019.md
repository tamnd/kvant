Great Legacy

Euler's scientific legacy is striking in its completely unchallenged dimensions. During his lifetime, his 530 books and articles were published. The last years of his life, academic publications could not cope with the flow of scientific production of the blind scientist, and he playfully promised Count V.N. Orlov that his works would fill the “Commentaries” of the Academy for 20 years after his death. This assessment turned out to be “optimistic”: the Academy had been publishing Euler’s works for 47 years. The number of works reached 771, but the bibliography compiled by Enster in 1910 contained 886 titles, divided into headings: philosophy, mathematics, mechanics, astronomy, physics, geography, agriculture. Since 1910, the Swiss Society of Natural Sciences has been publishing a collection of Euler's works, distributed by international subscription: according to preliminary estimates, it will amount to 75 large volumes. This volume reflects not only the amazing speed with which Euler worked, but also his habit of systematically printing scientific texts, including those prepared in a relatively special manner. The wide range of topics reflects not only the wide range of interests and the ability to quickly enter further science, but also numerous academic responsibilities both in St. Petersburg and in Berlin. Some publications are in the nature of short replicas. Euler easily entered into scientific contacts, gave various consultations, and willingly thought about random, isolated problems reported by his correspondents. It may seem that the scientist was scattered, always showed his attention, but this is only at first glance.
