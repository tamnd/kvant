In a rotating reference frame (Fig. 9), each element of a chain of length $\Delta r = r\Delta \alpha$ ($r = l/2\pi$ is the radius of the chain, $\Delta \alpha$ is the angular size of the length element) is acted upon by a centrifugal inertial force $\Delta F_u6 = \Delta m\omega^2 r$, directed radially from the center ($\Delta m = m\Delta r/l$). Let us find the total force of inertia acting on half the chain. To do this, you need to add the projections of forces in a direction perpendicular to the diameter connecting the ends of half the chain. As a result we get

$$F_u6 = \int_0^\pi \frac{m\omega^2 r}{l} r \sin \alpha \cdot d\alpha = 2mln^2.$$

This force is balanced by the sum of two tension forces acting on half the chain. Therefore, the chain tension

$$T = mln^2.$$

7. A water particle with mass $\Delta m$ is acted upon by the Corilis force directed horizontally to the west

$$F_k = 2\Delta m\omega v \sin \varphi,$$

where $\omega$ is the angular velocity of the Earth's rotation. For a particle located at the surface of the water (Fig. 10), the equilibrium force of gravity and Corilis force should be directed by the redistribution of the water surface. Therefore, the surface of the water tilts to the horizon at an angle $\alpha$ such that

$$tg \alpha = F_k / (\Delta m g) = \frac{2\omega v}{g} \sin \varphi.$$

Accordingly, the difference in levels

$$h = d tg \alpha = 2\omega v d \sin \varphi \approx 10 \text{ см.}$$

When a river flows along a parallel, the Corilis force is directed at an angle to the horizon. However, the parallel does not coincide with the equator, then the Corilis force always has a horizontal component and a conservation coefficient. For a river flowing along the screen, the Corilis force is directed vertically, and the banks are equally washed away. But if the river crosses the ecrator, then the solution of the channel changes - after crossing the ecrator, the opposite birch tree becomes round. The Georgfs actually observed this phenomenon.
