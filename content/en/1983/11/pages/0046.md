$$F_M = 2a |kx| i = a^2 ki,$$

Thus, the equation of motion of the frame has the form

$$mz'' = -mg + a^2 ki = -mg - \frac{a^4 k^2}{L} z.$$

This equation is similar to the equation of oscillations of a body of mass $m$ suspended on a spring of stiffness $K = a^4 k^2/L$:

$$mz'' = -mg - Kz.$$

From this analogy it is clear that the frame will perform harmonic oscillations along the $Z$ axis around the equilibrium position, determined from the condition

$$\frac{a^4 k^2}{L} z_0 = -mg \quad z_0 = -\frac{mg L}{a^4 k^2}.$$

The oscillation frequency will be equal to

$$\omega = \frac{a^2 k}{\sqrt{L m}}.$$

The coordinate $z$ of the frame after time $t$ after the start of movement is equal to

$$z = \frac{mg L}{a^4 k^2} \left( -1 + \cos \frac{a^2 k}{\sqrt{L m}} t \right).$$

An old eastern parable

A long time ago in the east there lived an old man who, when he died, left 19 camels to his three sons. He gave the old son half, the middle son a quarter, and the youngest a fifth. Unable to find a solution on their own (after all, the problem in “whole camels” has no solution), the brothers turned to the sage.

O sage! - said the elder brother, - my father left us 19 camels and ordered us to divide them among ourselves: the eldest - half, the middle - four, the youngest - a fifth. But 19 is not divisible by 2, 4, or 5. Can you, venerable one, help our grief, or do we want to fulfill the will of our father?

“There is nothing simpler,” the sage answered them.
