Additive number theory

The problem of number representation of natural numbers in the form of sums of terms of a certain nature (as Euler said, the question of the “partition of numbers”) should be at the center of his attention. Perhaps the initial inspiration for the task is contained in a letter to Node, whose surname means nothing to our contemporary. Euler applied the apparatus of infinite products to these problems. Here are some examples. Euler shows that

$$\begin{align*}
(1+x)(1+x^2)(1+x^3) &\dots = \frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^3)(1-x^5)}.
\end{align*}$$

The reasoning is that if you multiply the left side of the sequence by 1−x, 1−x^3, 1−x^5, ..., then all non-zero powers will gradually disappear, and this means identity (this reasoning can be made rigorous using the theory of limits). After opening the brackets on the left side, we get the series 1+a₁x+a₂x²+a₃x³+..., where aₖ is the number representing k as a sum of different natural terms. The right side, using the sum of an infinite geometric progression, is written in the form

$$\begin{align*}
(1+x+x^2+x^3+...) (1+x^3+x^6+x^9+...) \times (1+x^5+x^{10}+x^{15}+...) \cdot ...
\end{align*}$$

from which we again obtain the series 1+b₁x+b₂x²+b₃x³+..., where bₖ is the number representing k as a sum of odd terms, among which there may be identical ones (why?). Euler concludes that the representation number a_k=b_k coincides. Try to prove this coincidence in a non-representational way and you will see that it is not clear how it fits into this problem.

The following reasoning comes from the point

$$\begin{align*}
(1+x)(1+x^2)(1+x^4)(1+x^8) &\dots = \frac{1}{1+x+x^2+x^3+...} \\
= 1+x+x^2+x^3+...
\end{align*}$$

(to verify its plausibility, you can multiply both sides by 1 − x and observe how the non-Lu powers of x consistently disappear in both sides). It immediately follows from it that each number is represented in one and only one way as a sum of different powers of two (the numbers of such representations are coefficients in the power series obtained after transforming the left product).

The task of dividing them was carried out by the arithmetic of Dufant and Fermat, not only in terms of methods, but also in terms of settings. They are familiarized with the science of the new field of ARIFmetics - additive number theory, which is still developing today.

Briefly about other works

In mathematics, Euler was primarily an analyzer. A significant part of his life was devoted to the creation of a systematic (semitic) course in mathematical analysis. Euler was the first to work with the general concept of a function defined by an arbitrary curve (graph), and he connects the general concept of a function with the problem of the number of strings. Euler did a lot in considering special classes of functions important for applications. Here is the famous Γ-function, which takes the values ​​n! at point n of the natural series, its twin the B-function (Eulerian integrals), cylindrical and elliptic functions. Euler systematically carried out analytical considerations in a complex of one area, discovering a substitutional connection between the complex exponential function and trigonometric functions. His studies in geometry were more sporadic. They mainly relate to problems related to cartography, or to the application of analysis in geometry (beginning with differential geometry). But we can also recall the meaning of Euler’s formula for polyhedra: B+Γ=P+2, where B is the number of vertices, Γ is the number of faces, P is the number of edges, as well as the problem of the seven Könggerg places, in which Euler correctly sensed the problems of the new geometry (topologists).
