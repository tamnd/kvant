Four proofs of the bisectrice theorem

The theorem about the property of bisectrices of the interior angle of a triangle is not proven in the textbook “Geometry 6-8”, but is proposed in the form of a problem (No. 914, p. 232). This theorem is often used, for example, when establishing the fact that three bisectors of a triangle intersect at one point. Here we give several proofs of it.

Theorem. The bisector BD of the interior angle of triangle ABC divides the opposite side into segments proportional to the sides |BC| and |BA| triangle.

$$\frac{|AC|}{|AD|}; \text{ отсюда последовательно получаем}$$
$$\frac{|AB|+|BC|}{|AB|} = \frac{|AD|+|DC|}{|AD|},$$
$$1 + \frac{|BC|}{|AB|} = 1 + \frac{|DC|}{|AD|},$$
$$\frac{|CB|}{|AB|} = \frac{|CD|}{|AD|},$$
Q.E.D.

Second proof (for 8th grade).
We have (see Fig. 1):

$$\frac{S_{CBD}}{S_{ABD}} =$$
$$= \frac{0,5 \cdot |CB| \cdot |DB| \cdot \sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}}{0,5 \cdot |AB| \cdot |DB| \cdot \sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}} =$$
$$= \frac{0,5 \cdot |CD| \cdot h}{0,5 \cdot |AD| \cdot h},$$
where $h$ is the length of the altitude of triangle ABC drawn from vertex B. Hence
$$\frac{|CB|}{|AB|} = \frac{|CD|}{|AD|}.$$

Third proof (8th grade). By the theorem of sines (see Fig. 1)

$$\frac{\sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}}{|CD|} = \frac{\sin \hat{C}}{|BD|}$$

And
$$\frac{\sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}}{|AD|} = \frac{\sin \hat{A}}{|BD|},$$

or
$$\frac{|CD|}{|AD|} = \frac{|CB|}{|AB|}.$$

Fourth proof (grades 7-8). Perform axial symmetry S of triangle ABC with respect to (BD) (Fig. 2), we obtain
$$S_{(BD)}(A) = A_1, S_{(BD)}(C) = C_1 \text{ и } S_{(BD)} = B.$$

Third proof (8th grade). By the theorem of sines (see Fig. 1)

$$\frac{\sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}}{|CD|} = \frac{\sin \hat{C}}{|BD|}$$

And
$$\frac{\sin \frac{\hat{B}}{2}}{|AD|} = \frac{\sin \hat{A}}{|BD|},$$

$$\frac{|CD|}{|AD|} = \frac{|CB|}{|AB|}.$$

Then $\triangle CDC_1 \sim \triangle ADA_1$ and $\triangle CC_1B \sim \triangle AA_1B$ (make sure of this!). Hence (given that $|AB| = |A_1B|$) $$\frac{|CD|}{|AD|} = \frac{|CC_1|}{|AA_1|},$$
$$\frac{|CC_1|}{|AA_1|} = \frac{|CB|}{|AB|}.$$

Hence,
$$\frac{|CD|}{|AD|} = \frac{|CB|}{|AB|}.$$
