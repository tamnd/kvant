When
$$|a + \theta| = |a| + |\theta|$$?

L. B. PECHERSKY

As is known (“Algebra and principles of analysis 9-10”, paragraph 3),
$$|a| - |b| \leqslant |a+b| \leqslant |a| + |b|$$ (1)

From the geometric meaning of the model it easily follows that $|a+b| = |a| + |b|$ if and only if $a$ and $b$ are numbers of the same sign, more precisely (since it is not prohibited for any of them to be zero) - when $a \geqslant 0$ and $b \geqslant 0$ or when $a \leqslant 0$ and $b \leqslant 0$.

Obviously, the formulated equality criterion can be written as follows:
$$|a+b| = |a| + |b| \Leftrightarrow ab \geqslant 0.$$ (2)

Hence,
$$|a+b| < |a| + |b| \Leftrightarrow ab < 0.$$

The criterion for equality “in complex form” is valid for any number of terms: $|a_1+a_2+\dots+a_n| = |a_1| + |a_2| + \dots + |a_n|$ if and only if $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are numbers of the same sign (more precisely, when among the numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ there are no numbers of different signs).

By the way, statement $|a+b+c| = |a| + |b| + |c| \Leftrightarrow abc \geqslant 0$ is incorrect (why?).

Example 1. Solve the equation
$$|\lg(2x-3) + \lg(4-x^2)| = (3) = |\lg(2x-3)| + |\lg(4-x^2)|.$$

According to criterion (2), this equation is equivalent to the equation
$$\lg(2x-3) \cdot \lg(4-x^2) \geqslant 0$$,
which, in turn, is equivalent to the system
$$\begin{cases} \lg(2x-3) \cdot \lg(4-x^2) \geqslant 0 \\ 2x-3 > 0 \\ 4-x^2 > 0 \end{cases}$$

Since $\frac{3}{2} < x < 2 \Leftrightarrow 0 < 2x-3 < 1$, the latter system is equivalent to the system
$$\begin{cases} \lg(2x-3) < 0 \\ \lg(4-x^2) \leqslant 0 \\ \frac{3}{2} < x < 2 \end{cases}$$

$$\begin{cases} \frac{3}{2} < x < 2 \\ 0 < 4-x^2 \leqslant 1 \end{cases}$$

$$\begin{cases} \frac{3}{2} < x < 2 \\ x^2 \geqslant 3 \end{cases}$$

$$\begin{cases} \frac{3}{2} < x < 2 \\ x \geqslant \sqrt{3} \end{cases}$$

Answer. $[\sqrt{3}; 2]$.

Solution to inequality
$$|\lg(2x-3) + \lg(4-x^2)| < |\lg(2x-3)| + |\lg(4-x^2)|$$

can be obtained by removing the $\frac{3}{2}; 2$ [solution $[\sqrt{3}; 2]$ of the corresponding equation (3) from its domain of definition. Answer. $\frac{3}{2}; \sqrt{3}$[.

Example 2. Solve the “equation with a parameter”
$$|a-2\sin x| + 2|\sin x-\cos x| + |2\cos x-1| = a-1.$$

For $a < 1$ this equation obviously has no roots. Let now $a \geqslant 1$. Because
$$(a-2\sin x) + 2(\sin x-\cos x) + (2\cos x-1) = a-1,$$

You can use the equality criterion for three terms. Since, in addition, $a-1 \geqslant 0$, the solution of the equation is equivalent to the system
$$\begin{cases} a-2\sin x \geqslant 0 \\ \sin x-\cos x \geqslant 0 \\ 2\cos x-1 \geqslant 0 \end{cases}$$

or "triple inequality"
$$\frac{1}{2} \leqslant \cos x \leqslant \sin x \leqslant \frac{a}{2}.$$

The characteristic size of a fluctuation—its width—cannot be less than the length of the gap of free holes, that is, the distance that they project between two captures on the traps. Otherwise, the holes will “LET” through the fluctuation without being captured in it. On the other hand, the electrons must remain free as long as the fluctuation occurs. Thus, for the process under consideration, both in space and in time, there are characteristic intervals that determine the possibility of the wavelength and its period.

Propagating in the crystal, the wave creates regions enriched and depleted of carriers. Having reached the end of the sample, these areas gradually disappear. As a result, the resistance of the sample periodically changes, and current fluctuations are observed in the circuit. This is how vibrations are generated in the crystal. Since these oscillations are associated with the process of recombination, they, and therefore the waves generated by them, are called recombination.

Studying the peculiarities of recombination waves, scientists noticed that their frequency and amplitude depend not only on the characteristics of the generating crystal, but also on external conditions - pressure, temperature, lighting, and the presence of a magnetic field. This means that such a crystal can be used as a miniator converter of mechanical, thermal, light and other influences into electrical signals.
