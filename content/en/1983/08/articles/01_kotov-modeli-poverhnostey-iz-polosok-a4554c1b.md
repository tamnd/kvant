⟦folio none⟧

Shown here are models of the two surfaces, as well as the strips from which they are woven. Unlike, for example, a cylinder, most surfaces cannot be made accurately by bending one or more sheets of paper (or other non-stretchable material). However, in practice, a piece of any surface can be modeled with any accuracy using narrow strips of paper or (as done here) colored film.

The secret is that the stripes are of variable width, which valley has its own curve.

The calculation of the shape of the stripes was carried out on a computer, and their “cutting” was carried out on a plotter.

The surface is represented by a multitude of curved quadrangular cells, the lengths and diagonals of which are calculated; then each cell is replaced by two triangles, and the triangles adjacent to each other are drawn to form a strip. After gluing the strips with their edges or interweaving longitudinal and transverse strips in a checkerboard pattern, the surface takes on “its” shape.

Yu. V. Kotov
