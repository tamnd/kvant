Around the bisector

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. F. SHARYGIN

This article collects some geometric facts directly or indirectly related to the bisector of a triangle. Among them, the reader will find simple but often used “lemmas”, more solid and difficult “theorems” and simply beautiful “tasks”. But we will not classify them in any way, but will simply number them in the order of appearance in the statistics. Statements that are given in the article without proof are automatically included in the exercises. However, we tried to present the evidence available in the article as concisely as possible in order to leave the reader room for thought.

Let us first recall some generally accepted notations: $ABC$ is a given triangle, $S_{ABC}$ is its area, $|BC|=a$, $|CA|=b$, $|AB|=c$, $2p=a+b+c$, $O$ and $R$ are, respectively, the center of the circumscribed circle and its radius, $I$ and $r$ is the center and radius of the inscribed circle. In addition, the triangle has three outer circles, each of which touches one side of the triangle and is extended by the other two sides. Their centers and radii will be denoted, respectively, by $I_a$, $I_b$, $I_c$, $r_a$, $r_b$, $r_c$ ($I_a$ is the center of the out-of-circle tangent to the side $BC$ and the extended side $AB$ and $AC$, $r_a$ - its radius).

All other symbols will be explained in accordance with the location.

(1) Let the bisector of the angle $A$ intersect the side $BC$ at the point $A_1$. Then
$$\frac{|BA_1|}{|A_1C|} = \frac{|BA|}{|AC|} = \frac{c}{b}.$$

(2) Let the bisector of the external angle at the vertex $A$ intersect the line $BC$ at the point $A_2$. Then
$$\frac{|BA_2|}{|A_2C|} = \frac{|BA|}{|AC|} = \frac{c}{b}.$$
