Stability and death of "Captain"

Stability is the ability of a vessel, removed from an equilibrium position by the influence of external forces, to return to it again after the cessation of this influence.

Loss of stability is a transient type of crash, most often leading to the death of not only the ship, but also the crew.

The seller will distinguish between the floating stability of the vessel. The greatest danger when swimming is the loss of lateral stability. The inclination of the vessel in a transverse plane perpendicular to the flattener is called roll, while inclination in the full plane is called trim.

Let us explain the concept of stability in the case of a roll.

Obviously, for the floating vessel to be balanced, it is necessary that the forces $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{\gamma}$ (see Fig. 1, ​​a) be equal in magnitude, and the points of their application lie on the same vertical.

When the vessel tilts on any side under the influence of a heeling moment $M_k$ (for example, a sudden increase in wind, as was the case with the battleship “Captain”) at an angle $\theta$, the center of the vessel’s size, due to a change in the shape of the underwater volume of the vessel (Fig. 1, ​​b), will move towards the roll from position $C_0$ to position $C_0$. In courses on the theory of ship structure, it is proven that at small angles of roll the trajectory of the center of the magnitude can be taken as a circle with a center at a certain point $M$ (see Fig. 1, b) and a radius $r$. Point $M$ is called the transverse metacenter, $r$ is the transverse metacentric radius.

Then the pair of forces $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{\gamma}$, applied, respectively, at points $G$ and $C_0$, forms the so-called restoring moment $M_B$. In theoretical mechanics courses it is proven that the restoring moment can be determined by the formula

$$M_B = |\vec{P}| \cdot |GK| = |\vec{P}|h \cdot \sin \theta,$$

where $h = r - z_G + z_C$, and $z_G$ and $z_C$ are, respectively, the coordinates of the center of gravity and the center of magnitude in the selected coordinate system. According to this expression, there can be three cases of roll.

1. $^\circ M_B > 0$, that is, the center of gravity lies below the metacenter (see Fig. 1, b). Then the righting moment acts in the direction of returning the vessel to an upright position; the ship is stable.
