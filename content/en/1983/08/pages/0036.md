Two extremal properties of the center of the incircle

Take an arbitrary point $M$ inside the triangle $ABC$. There are quite a few inequalities regarding the distances from this point to the vertices of the triangle. We will consider two such inequalities that are relevant to the topic of the article.

(13) Let us denote by $A_1$ the point of intersection of the straight line $AM$ with the circumscribed circle. Then

$$\frac{|BM| \cdot |CM|}{|A_1M|} \geq 2r.$$

(14) $|AM| \sin B\widehat{MC} + |BM| \times \sin C\widehat{MA} + |CM| \sin A\widehat{MB} \leq p$.

In denote by the straight line the inequalities are achieved if $M$ coincides with $I$ - the center of the inscribed circle.

Let us assume that the smallest value of the expression $f(M) = \frac{|BM| \cdot |CM|}{|A_1M|}$ on the left side of inequality (13) is achieved at some point $M$ inside the triangle $ABC$. We will show that $M = I$. And since $f(I) = 2r$ (this follows, for example, from the similarity of the right angle triangles $BID$ and $I_aIC$ on figure 4*)), it will follow that if $f(M)$ belongs to its smallest value inside the triangle $ABC$, then $f(M) \geq 2r$. The highlighted proposal is non-trivial and very significant. We'll discuss it a little later.

Let us describe a circle (Fig. 5) around the triangle $AMC$. All triangles $CMA_1$, which when moving the point $M$ along its arc $AC$, are similar to each other (why?), therefore, the relation $|CM| / |A_1M|$ will be the same for them. Therefore, if $M$ is the minimum point of $f(M)$, the line $BM$ must pass through the center of the circumcircle of triangle $AMC$, otherwise we could reduce $|BM|$ while keeping the ratio $|CM| / |A_1M|$ constant. Let now $B_1$ and $C_1$ be the points of intersection of the lines $BM$ and $CM$ with the circle,

$$\frac{|BM| \cdot |CM|}{|A_1M|} = \frac{|CM| \cdot |AM|}{|B_1M|} = \frac{|AM| \cdot |BM|}{|C_1M|}.$$
