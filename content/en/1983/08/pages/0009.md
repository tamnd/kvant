Three maritime tragedies

This is an accident in the middle of the last century. Soon after the bark “Star”, en route from Ceylon to Europe, passed the island of St. Helena, the “Gloomy Joke”, the black brigatine of the pirate Benito de Soto, who gained fame, quickly approached it. On the Zvezda, where, in addition to the small crew, there were 25 disabled soldiers and almost as many women and children, they did not think about resistance, but hoped to pay off the cargo in the triome. However, things did not come to negotiations - the pirates who landed on the barque brutally killed several people, including the captain, and drove the rest into the trio, hammering the hatch cover with nails. Then the sea robbers plundered the ship, knocked down the masts, chopped up the rangut and takelaj and started a drinking binge, after which they returned to their brigatine. But before the “Gloomy Joke” sailed away with all sails, Benito do Soto was convinced that in the hull of the “Star”, below the line, there were holes drilled through which seawater flowed into several compartments of the hold. But the pirate’s sinister plan was not destined to come true. Why? We will talk about this later, when we understand the theory a little.

In London, a bronze plate has been attached to the wall of St. Paul's Cathedral since the end of the century. The text engraved on it tells about the death of the battleship "Captain" and, perhaps, involuntarily expresses a reproach to those through whose fault it occurred.

The battleship "Captain" was built in 1870.
