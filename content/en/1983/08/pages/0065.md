(21) Prove that the area of ​​a triangle with vertices at the points of tangency of the outer circle with center $I_a$ can be calculated using the formula

$$Q_a = S_{ABC} \cdot \frac{r_a}{2R} = \frac{S^2_{ABC}}{2R} \cdot \frac{1}{p-a}.$$

Similar formulas can be obtained for the areas of other triangles.

(22) Let $K_1$ and $L_1$ be points on $BC$ and $BA$ such that $K_1K \parallel L_1L \parallel BB'$. It is enough to prove that the triangles $BK_1K$ and $BL_1L$ are similar, that is, that $|BK_1| : |K_1K| = |BL_1| : |L_1L|$. We have $$\frac{|BK_1|}{|BA'|} = \frac{|B'K|}{|B'A'|}, \quad \frac{|K_1K|}{|BB'|} = \frac{|A'K|}{|B'A'|},$$ and by Theorem (1)

$$\frac{|BK_1|}{|K_1K|} = \frac{|B'K|}{|A'K|} \cdot \frac{|BA'|}{|BB'|} = \frac{|CB'|}{|CA'|} \cdot \frac{|BA'|}{|BB'|} = \frac{c}{b} \cdot \frac{|CB'|}{|BB'|} = \frac{ca}{(a+c) |BB'|}.$$

The last expression is symmetrical with respect to $a$ and $c$, and therefore is also equal to $|BL_1| : |L_1L|$.

(23) Let us prove that both statements are equivalent to the equality $|AB| \cdot |CD| = |AD| \cdot |BC|$. Let us take a point $A_1$ on arc $BAD$ such that $|DA_1| = |AB|$. The condition of the problem is equivalent to the fact that the line $A_1C$ passes through $N$ - the middle of $BD$, that is, the equality of the areas of triangles $DA_1C$ and $A_1BC$, whence $|DA_1| \times |DC| = |BA_1| \cdot |BC|$ or $|AB| \cdot |CD| = |AD| \cdot |BC|$.

(24) Let $B_1$ be the midpoint of $AC$. Let us continue the bisector until it intersects at point $B_2$ with the perpendicular restored by $KAC$ at point $B_1$. Point $B_2$ lies on the circumcircle $ABC$. Let us draw a perpendicular to $AC$ through $M$, let $L$ be the point of its intersection with $AC$, $K$ with $BB_1$, then $|KM| = |ML|$. Let us draw through $K$ a line parallel to $AC$, intersecting $AB$ and $BC$ at points $D$ and $E$. If $G$ and $F$ are projections of $D$ and $E$ onto $AC$, then $M$ is the center of the straight line $GDEF$, and $\triangle DME \sim \triangle AB_2C$ (△DME is obtained from △AB_2C when preparing with the center $B$). We have

$$\text{ctg} \hat{MCL} = \frac{|LC|}{|ML|} = \frac{|LF|}{|ML|} + \frac{|FC|}{|ML|} = \frac{|AB_1|}{|B_1B_2|} + 2 \frac{|FC|}{|EF|} = \text{ctg} \frac{\hat{B}}{2} + 2 \text{ctg} \hat{C},$$

that is, $\hat{MCA} = \hat{NCB}$.

(25) The product of the lengths of the segments from the vertex $A$ of the triangle to the points of intersection of the sides $AB$ with a given circle will be equal to the same product for the sides $AC$. These lengths can be easily expressed in terms of the lengths of the chords under consideration and the lengths of the side of the triangle. Similar equalities can be written for the vertices $B$ and $C$. Thus, we will obtain a system of equations that allows us to express the lengths of the chords in terms of the side lengths and verify the statement of the problem by direct calculation. To avoid enumerating options, it is convenient to choose some direction for traversing the triangle and consider the segments to be directed, and their lengths to be arbitrary real numbers.

(26) Let $\hat{KAL} = \hat{KLA} = \varphi$, $\hat{KCL} = \hat{LKC} = \psi$. Then $\hat{BKL} = 2\varphi$, $\hat{BKL} = 2\psi = 180^\circ - B$. If $Q$ is the intersection point of $AL$ and $KC$, then $\hat{AQC} = 180^\circ - (\varphi + \psi) = 90^\circ + \frac{\hat{B}}{2}$. Let us draw through $M$ a line parallel to $BC$ until it intersects $KC$ at point $N$, then $MQ$ is the bisector of the angle $AMN$ and $\hat{AQN} = 90^\circ + \frac{\hat{B}}{2}$. It follows that $Q$ is the point of intersection of the bisectors of the triangle $AMN$, which means $\triangle AMN \sim \triangle KBL$. Let $|AK| = |KL| = |LC| = x$, $|AM| = y$, $|MN| = z$. From the fact that $\triangle AMN \sim \triangle KBL$ and $\triangle KMN \sim \triangle KBC$ we get

$$\frac{z}{a-x} = \frac{y}{c-x}, \frac{y-x}{c-x} = \frac{z}{a},$$

whence $y = a$.

(27) The perpendicularity of the bisectors is proved by labor. Let's prove the second statement. Let $M$ be the midpoint of $AC$, $N$ the midpoint of $BD$. From the similarity of triangles $AMK$ and $BKD$ it follows that $\hat{MKA} = \hat{NKD}$ and $\frac{|MK|}{|KN|} = \frac{|AC|}{|BD|}$, that is, the bisector of angle $BKC$ is also the bisector of angle $MKN$ and divides the segment $MN$ in relation to $\frac{|MK|}{|KN|} = \frac{|AC|}{|BD|}$. Obviously, $MN$ also divides the angle bisector $ALB$ in the same relation.
