Okein... For the Earth, this is two-thirds of its surface. Okein is the roundabout of all life on Earth, okein is the kitchen of the weather (unfortunately, man is not yet a cook in this kitchen), okein is a road that has served mankind for thousands of years and, presumably, will last for a long time without repair; finally, okaine is a natural storehouse of proteins and, as they like to say, an “inexhaustible storehouse” of mineral raw materials. I think we should speak more carefully, since the “inexhaustible reserves” of many species of fish, alas, have already been exhausted.

In the 20th century, the United States became too small for man, its borders became too small for him, and he began to rush beyond its borders - into the ocean, into the air, into space.

What can a person find in the ocean? The products have already been mentioned. There is no less oil in the sea, or rather under the sea, than on land. Now, from today, the sea will add about 20% of the world's added oil, and by the year 2000, presumably, ocean oil will account for 50% of the total. A large amount of coal will be added today from offshore mines. More than 4% of the world's added sulfur comes from marine industries. A significant portion of the world's supply of rare metal ores comes from Russia's maritime regions. Just add olive oil, gold, silver, diamonds, manganese, colt, nickel to the sea... So what's the matter? Okein is nearby, he has untold riches within himself! It would seem, take it and master it!

Why is ocean development progressing so slowly? Why in the first half of the 20th century was the rebeth of the visible side of the Moon better known to us than the rebeth of the day of the ocean? Why, finally, at an altitude of more than 100 km above the Earth were already more than a hundred people rising, but at the depths of the ocean, ten times less, only two? And why did man call the depths of the ocean hydrocosmos?

There are two main obstacles to ocean development. Odanitsa is obvious - this is hydrocosmic pressure. At a depth of 10 km, every square meter of the surface is pressed by many tons of water. In addition, we must not forget that sea water is a very aggressive liquid that quickly dissolves most metals.

The second reason hindering the development of the ocean is the enormous attenuation of electromagnetic waves in highly conductive salt sea water. Because of this, a person in the ocean is practically deprived of the basic means of obtaining information that he is accustomed to.
