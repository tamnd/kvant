The picture of parallel horizontal layers so beautifully drawn by us is actually distorted in space by the existence of deep, so-called “internal” waves caused by the oscillations of layers of water of different densities. Vertical oscillations can have periods of the order of a day and reach a range of 100 m. The thickness of ocean waters can be affected by waves caused by underwater earthquakes—tsunamis.

The complex picture of acoustic fields in the ocean is also associated with the presence of uneven boundaries - damage and the bottom, which reflect and scatter the sound energy falling on them. In this case, the upper boundary (water - air) is practically opaque to sound, and its shape changes over time; the lower bound - to - is constant over time, but is partly practical for acoustic waves that penetrate to the ocean.

Reception of weak signals is complicated by the presence of ambient noise. This background consists of noise caused by dynamic processes, mainly wind waves, biological noise (it is known that marine animals widely use acoustic waves), seismic background, noise of ships passing, etc.

Further, from this brief listing of factors influencing the propagation of sound in the ocean, the complexity of the process of transmitting and receiving acoustic signals in the ocean is visible. One can imagine the difficulties encountered when studying the laws of sound propagation in the ocean. But experiments are carried out in a very wide frequency range and at distances from hundreds of meters to thousands of kilometers.

The study of acoustic fields in the ocean is only part of the problems that ocean acoustics studies. There are tasks that are commonly called processing tasks. The nature of propagation, reflection, and scattering of acoustic waves provides information about the properties of the medium, about objects that reflect and scatter sound. Determining the characteristics of the ocean from such information is the ultimate goal of the study. For example, the propagation time of an echo sounder pulse to the bottom and back allows you to change the depth of the ocean. Acoustic methods of ocean research replace traditional “contact” methods with express “non-contact” ones.

Now I would like to dwell in more detail on some issues. I have already said that ambient noise interferes with the reception of useful acoustic signals. I might add that backscatter also generally interferes with the reception of signals, such as communications signals. But, on the other hand, noise also carries information about its sources, for example, about dynamic processes in the ocean, and scattering signals gives us information about dispersion, in our case - about the news of the ocean.
