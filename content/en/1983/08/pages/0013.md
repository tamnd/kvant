Yes, after the damage, the floating island stayed on the water for only two hours. And this is an accident because during the design and construction of the Titanic, the basic requirements of unsinkability were violated: the transverse waterproof bulkheads separating one section from another were not extended to the upper deck, and the gun faces were not waterproof. In this regard, and bearing in mind the decision of the London court that took place after the sinking of the Titanic, academician Alexei Nikolaevich Krylov wrote: “Thus, all the blame is placed entirely on the captain, who also died at his post; the design flaws themselves, which caused his death with relatively minor damage, are not mentioned in a single word in the court ruling.”

Ship safety today

But perhaps the achievements of science and technology in the field of navigation and shipbuilding have significantly reduced concerns about the seaworthiness of the ship and, thus, reduced the importance of mathematics? And is mathematics necessary in other important areas of a navigator’s work?

Nowadays the sea element remains as formidable and insidious as it has always been. The achievements of modern science and technology, of course, have increased the degree of safety of navigation, but, on the other hand, the volume, variety and complexity of the tasks that today's marines solve have sharply increased.

So, at the beginning of 1982, the large-scale vessel “Serebryanka” led an industrial beast through the Arctic ice environment. When the external movement of the pack ice occurred, the skill of the captain and the sailors led by him made it possible to avoid disastrous consequences, but the ship still received two problems: in the auxiliary engine room and in the first three holds. The sailors managed to seal the holes. But in the process of fighting for the survivability of the ship, the captain and his assistant had to quickly make numerous complex and accurate mathematical calculations, showing what exactly needs to be done to reduce the dangerous trim in the event of flooding of damaged compartments (of course, taking into account the distribution of the cargo on the ship).
