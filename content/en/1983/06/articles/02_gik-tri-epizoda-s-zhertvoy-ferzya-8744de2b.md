THREE EPISODES
WITH THE SACRIFICE OF THE QUEEN
The most beautiful and striking combinations on the chessboard are those in which one of the sides sacrifices a queen (see «Kvant», 1982, No. 10). Today we return to this topic again.

M. Tal - A. Hecht
(Chess Olympida, 1962)

Having discovered that 30. N:h6+B:h6 31. R:f8+Kxf8 32. Q:h6+Ke8 is not dangerous for Black, the Swedish chess player made a “window” - 30. g3. However, 30...h5 followed! and after 31. Ng5 Q:b2 32. Ke6 R:f1+33. Q:f1 Bf6! 34. Rd8+Npf7 35. Ng5+Ke7 36. N:e4 K:d8 white deals. Meanwhile, they could create an “immeasurable work” - 30. Qxh6!! Rf6 (30...C:h6 31. N:h6+Nph8 32. R:f8×, 30...R:f7 31. Rd8+) 31. Q:h8+!! Black would be forced to accept the sacrifice and after 31... B:h8 receive an effective “epaulet checkmate” - 32. Kh6× (epaulets - black rook and bishop, because of which the king cannot move).

And one more case - from the party of the page leader.

E. Gik - M. Mukhin (Moscow, 1969)

19. ef! ba. So, the Spherzem Whites parted, but this is not the end. 20. fg Rg8 21. Bf5!! In the case of a capture on c4 (or f5), White, without even having a pawn for the queen, quickly reached the goal, for example: 21...Q:c4 22. Rfe1+Qe6 23. R:e6+fe 24. B:g6+Kpd7 25. Rd1+Kpc7 26. Bg3+Kpb6 27. Rb1+Kpa6 28. Cd3+Kpa5 29. Bc7×. It's a pity that this cute brew didn't get better black. The chosen continuation still didn’t save them, and aesthetically the game clearly lost. 21...K:h4 22.С:e6 Ca6 23. Kd6+Kpe7 24. Bc4! White has a decisive advantage, which they carried to victory.

In the next episode, participant Mihanl Tal, the queen's sacrifice remained behind the scenes. It happened again at the Olympics, but exactly 20 years later.

In this situation, the game was postponed, and when the judge opened the envelope, I barely managed to make joy. The recorded move 41...Qc2 allowed me to make a beautiful home preparation: 42. Rxf4! gf43. Qe6+Rf7 44. Rg6+Kpf8 45. Qg4 Qf5 46. Rg8+Ke7 47. Qe2+Kpd7 48. Qe6+! etc. But my calculation was too far. As soon as I took the envelope 42. Rxf4, an erroneous assessment of 42 immediately followed...

Q:g2+!! When consciousness returned to me, I was convinced that I was left without a clean rook - 43. Kr:g2 gf+ and 44...fe. Well, who could expect such an insidious argument from a partner! I had to give up immediately. By the way, when each of us showed a distinguished position to our players, they all offered for White the “decisive” taken with the rook on f4. Useful material for psychologists! My partner Mihanl Mukhin was an extremely talented chess player. Unfortunately, he did not have time to become a grandmaster, as he died very wounded. In 1972, in the national championship, he shared third - fifth place with grandseurs V. Savon and G. Kuzmin (the title of USSR champion was won by M. Tal). Almost all the participants in the tour who turned out to be his were either already grandseurs or soon became one. In the additional two-round match tournament, Mukhin did not lose to Kuzmin and Savon, all three scored 2 points, and only the judges’ coefficient placed Mukhin in the international tournament.
