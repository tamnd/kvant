Tasks

M806—M810; F818—F822

M806. a) Prove that if
$$a_1 + \frac{a_2}{2} + \frac{a_3}{3} + \ldots + \frac{a_n}{n} = 0,$$
then the polynomial $a_n x^{n-1} + a_{n-1} x^{n-2} + \ldots + a_2 x + a_1$ has a root between 0 and 1.

6) Prove that if for some $p > 0$
$$\frac{a_1}{p+1} + \frac{a_2}{p+2} + \frac{a_3}{p+3} + \ldots + \frac{a_n}{p+n} = 0,$$
then this polynomial also has a root between 0 and 1.

A. Gokhbere, M. Ovetsky, 10th grade students, Donetsk

M807. a) From an arbitrary point $M$ inside an equilateral triangle, perpendiculars $MK_1, MK_2, MK_3$ are dropped onto its sides. Prove that the sum of the vectors $\vec{MK}_1 + \vec{MK}_2 + \vec{MK}_3$ is equal to $\frac{3}{2} \cdot \vec{MO}$, where $O$ is the center of the triangle.

6) From an arbitrary point $M$ perpendiculars $MK_1, \ldots, MK_n$ are dropped to all sides of a regular $n$-gon (or their extensions). Prove that
$$\vec{MK}_1 + \ldots + \vec{MK}_n = \frac{n}{2} \vec{MO},$$
where $O$ is the center of the $n$-gon.

c) From an arbitrary point $M$ inside an equilateral triangle, perpendiculars $MK_1, MK_2, MK_3, MK_4$ on its face are dropped. Prove that
$$\vec{MK}_1 + \vec{MK}_2 + \vec{MK}_3 + \vec{MK}_4 = \frac{4}{3} \vec{MO},$$
where $O$ is the center of the triangle.

V. V. Prasolov

M808. On an infinite sheet of checkered paper, two players play the following game: the first one paints some cell red, the second one paints $k$ (uncolored) cells blue, then again the first one (uncolored) - red, the second one - $k$ cells blue, etc. The first one strives to ensure that some four red cells are located at the vertices of the square (with sides parallel to the grid lines). Will the second one be able to place a) at $k=1$; 6) * at $k=2$; c) ** for any $k>1$?

D. G. Azov

M809. Find the amount
$$\frac{1}{2!} + \frac{2}{3!} + \dots + \frac{n-1}{n!}$$
(the product $1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot k$ is denoted by $k!$).

V. V. Proizvolov

M810*. Prove that any convex polygon $M$ can contain a right triangle whose area is at least 1/4 of the area of ​​the polygon $M$.

F. W. Vaintain

F818. Two rigid rods of length $l$ each are hinged at point $A$ (Fig. 1). Rod $BA$ is rigidly fixed at point $B$, and point $C$ of rod $AC$ can slide in direction $BC$. The rod $BA$ begins to rotate in the plane figure around the point $B$ with a constant angular velocity $\omega$. What will be the maximum speed and acceleration of point $C$, if at the initial moment the rods are extended along the guide $BC$ ($B\hat{A}C = \pi$)?

L. G. Markovich

F819. When small bodies, whose masses are in the ratio 3:4:5 (the mass of the lightest body is $m$), are held at three different points on the inner surface of a smooth hemispherical part of radius $R$, which is attached to the horizontal surface at the lowest point. At some points they let go and imagine themselves. What is the maximum amount of heat that can be released in this system? At what initial position of the bodies will this happen? All collisions between bodies are completely inelastic.

S. K. Strokov

F820. Process $A—B—C—A$ produces over one mole of an ideal monatomic gas (Fig. 2). Determine the efficiency of the cycle.

A. G. Izertsin, Yu. P. Malyshev, V. R. Saulit

Problems

M806—M810; P818—P822

M806. a) Prove that if

$$a_1 + \frac{a_2}{2} + \frac{a_3}{3} + \ldots + \frac{a_n}{n} = 0,$$

then the polynomial $a_nx^{n-1} + a_{n-1}x^{n-2} + \ldots + a_2x + a_1$ has a root between 0 and 1;

b) Prove that if, for some $b > 0$,

$$\frac{a_1}{b+1} + \frac{a_2}{b+2} + \frac{a_3}{b+3} + \ldots + \frac{a_n}{b+n} = 0,$$

then this polynomial also has a root between 0 and 1.

A. Gohberg, M. Ovetski, 10th grade students, Donetsk.

M807. a) Perpendiculars $MK_1, MK_2, MK_3$ to the sides of an equilateral triangle are drawn from an arbitrary point $M$ inside it. Prove that the vector sum $\overrightarrow{MK_1} + \overrightarrow{MK_2} + \overrightarrow{MK_3}$ equals $(3/2)$. $\overrightarrow{MO}$, where $O$ is the triangle’s centre.

b) Perpendiculars $MK_1, \ldots, MK_n$ to all the $n$ sides of a regular polygon are drawn from an arbitrary point inside it. Prove that

$$\overrightarrow{MK_1} + \overrightarrow{MK_2} + \overrightarrow{MK_3} + \overrightarrow{MK_4} = \frac{4}{3} \overrightarrow{MO},$$

where $O$ is the centre of the polygon.

c) Perpendiculars $MK_1, \ldots, MK_4$ to the faces of a regular tetrahedron are drawn from an arbitrary point inside it. Prove that

$$\overrightarrow{MK_1} + \overrightarrow{MK_2} + \overrightarrow{MK_3} + \overrightarrow{MK_4} = \frac{4}{3} \overrightarrow{MO},$$

where $O$ is the centre of the tetrahedron.

M808. Two persons play the following game on an infinite plane square lattice: the first player colours one node in red, the second colours $k$ (uncoloured) nodes in blue, then the first colours one more node in red, the second $k$ more in blue, and so on. The first player’s goal is to colour four nodes forming the vertices of a square with sides parallel to the lines of the lattice. Can the second player prevent him from doing this a) when $k=1$; b) when $k=2$; c) **for some $k>1$?**

D. G. Azov
