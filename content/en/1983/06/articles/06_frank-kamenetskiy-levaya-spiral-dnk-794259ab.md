Left-handed DNA helix

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. D. FRANK-KAMENETSKY

Thirty years have passed since, in 1953, J. Watson and F. Crick proposed their model of the structure of the DNA molecule. And all this time, the double helix of DNA has attracted great attention from physicists and mathematicians, chemists and biologists, doctors and pharmacists. Such an interest is completely understandable - after all, a half-dimensional DNA molecule in the form of a certain sequence of monomeric units (nucleotides) of only four varieties contains all the genetic information and contains the structure plan of an entire living organism.

Let us recall that, according to the Watson-Crick model, the DNA molecule consists of two polymer chains twisted into a double helix, or rather, into a helix. It is characteristic that the twisting of the chain is the rule, that is, the same as with ordinary screws or screws.

For many years, scientists have been convinced that this is the only structure DNA can have both in a cell and in a test tube under normal conditions. This was confirmed by numerous experiments carried out using a variety of physical methods. However, in 1979, a group of American scientists from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology unexpectedly discovered a new form of the molecule

DNA. This appears to be the most important discovery in the field of DNA structure since the work of Watson and Crick.

Scientists worked with very short molecules consisting of only six nucleotides (therefore they are called hexanucleotides) of two types - cytosine (C) and guanine (G):

Tsgtsgtsg
GCGCGC,

and they managed for the first time to obtain real crystals from DNA molecules.

It is known that now the most accurate method for studying the internal structure of crystals is RENT STRUCTURE analysis. The method is based on the phenomenon of wave diffraction. By analyzing the diffraction pattern obtained from a crystal, you can find out everything about it - what is the arrangement of molecules in the crystal and how atoms are “laid” inside the molecules.

What a surprise it was for American scientists when, after receiving all the necessary data and carrying out cumbersome calculations on a computer, they finally found the structure of the hexanucleotide. It turned out that the very first structure determined by the RENT STRUCTURE analysis method is very different from the Watson-Crick model. And first of all, we direct the twisting of the chain into a spiral. In a hexanucleotide it is not right, but left! There are other differences between this new shape, which is called the Z-shape, and the classic one, now called the B-shape.

So, is the Watson-Crick model wrong?! No, such a category statement did not follow from the discovery of American scientists. Studies of other short DNA molecules carried out in recent years have shown that the Z-form can only contain molecules in which each of the complementary chains strictly contains nucleotides of the C and G types.
