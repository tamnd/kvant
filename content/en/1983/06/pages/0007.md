Thermal equilibrium of planets

We have seen that it depends on temperature in space. But first of all, the temperature of the atmosphere interests us, the inhabitants of the Earth, from a practical point of view - living conditions greatly depend on it.

The main source of thermal energy on Earth is the Sun. Humanity has always been aware of this. Here is how, for example, the Russian poet Aleksandr Sumarokov wrote in 1760:

O Sun, you are the belly and beauty of nature, the Source of eternity and the image of the deity!
Through you the Earth is alive, the air is alive, the waters are alive, the Soul of time and matter!

Let's try to quantify these eternal truths. The sun is a ball of radius $R_C = 6,96 \times 10^8$ m, heated by thermonuclear reactions; solar surface temperature $T_C = 5800$ K.

Everyone knows that hot bodies glow and emit energy. At the end of the last century, the Austrian physicists Stefan and Boltzmann discovered the law of radiation: the flow of energy, that is, the energy emitted per unit time per unit of surface area of ​​a body, which is in a state of thermal equilibrium (having a constant temperature), is proportional to the color degree of temperature: $\varepsilon = \sigma T^4$, where $\sigma = 5,67 \cdot 10^{-8}$ W/($\mathrm{m}^2 \cdot \mathrm{K}^4$) is the proportionality coefficient, which is called the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

The sun is not in complete thermal equilibrium, but for it the Stefan-Boltzmann law is approximately satisfied. According to this law, the power emitted by the Sun is equal to

$$P_C = 4\pi R_C^2 \sigma T_C^4 \approx 3,8 \cdot 10^{26} \text{ Вт}.$$

This power is radiated evenly in all directions. It is not difficult to calculate how much of it ends up on Earth. At a distance $R = 1,5 \times 10^{11}$ m, equal to the radius of the earth's orbit, a power of $P_C / 4\pi R^2$ falls on each square meter of surface perpendicular to the rays.

The earth “shades” the area $\pi R_C^2$ on such a surface. Therefore, the power received from the Sun to the Earth is equal to

$$P = P_C \frac{\pi R_C^2}{4\pi R^2} = 1,75 \cdot 10^{17} \text{ Вт}.$$

Where is this power spent? Some of it is reflected by the Earth into space. Everyone knows that the planets and the Moon are visible in the starry sky precisely thanks to reflected sunlight. In the same way, when moving into outer space, you can see the light reflected by the Earth. The fraction of reflected light power is called albedo (from the Latin albus - white; albedo is like the degree of whiteness). The accuracy with which we now know the albedo of our planet is low. The Earth's albedo $A$ is believed to range from 28 to 36 percent. Where does the rest of the energy $P(1-A)$ go? It is clear that it is precisely this that is the reason that there is a body on our planet. However, solar radiation reaches the Earth constantly, and if there were no energy removal, the temperature of the planet would constantly increase. Therefore, the heat conductor exists. It is carried out by exactly the same physical process as the radiation of the Sun itself. Yes, imagine, both the Earth and the other planets are also sources of radiation. But the spectrum of this radiation lies in the clouds invisible to the eye, in the infrared range.

Let's calculate the temperature of the Earth's equilibrium thermal radiation, assuming its albedo to be 0.28 (this is the latest changes from satellites). We need to equate the power of solar radiation absorbed by the Earth $P(1-A)$ to the power of thermal radiation from the Earth. If the Earth's temperature is $T_3$, then the power emitted from its surface is $4\pi R_C^2 \sigma T_3^4$. So,

$$P(1-A) = 4\pi R_C^2 \sigma T_3^4,$$

where

$$T_3 = \left( \frac{P(1-A)}{4\pi R_C^2 \sigma} \right)^{1/4} = 258 \text{ K} = -15 \text{ °C}.$$
