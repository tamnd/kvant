The simplest contours

Let us first consider the case when the contour is not too strongly curved - namely, when it can be one-to-one*) projected onto a convex contour lying in a certain plane. Then it follows from mathematical theory that there is one and only one minimal surface that spans the given contour. When this contour is flat, the “existence and uniqueness theorem” seems obvious: the contour can be enclosed by a piece of the plane that it bounds, and any other surface subtending it will have a larger area (Fig. 2).

Different films on one circuit
