F804. Two identical stars A and B rotate under the influence of mutual attraction at a constant distance R from each other. At some unknown distance x from the stars, a light planet C is moving in the plane of their orbits, and |AC| = |BC| = x, and triangle ABC retains its dimensions. At this distance x.

$$\vec{v} = \vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_0,$$

$$|\vec{v}_1| \equiv v_1 = v_0 \text{tg} \alpha.$$ (1)

Let's write down the equations of motion of the sleigh. In a fixed reference system in projections to the vertical direction (see Fig. 2)

$$T \sin \alpha + N - mg = 0$$ (2)

(T is the force of the rope, $\vec{N}$ is the force of normal reaction from the road). In a reference frame moving with speed $\vec{v}_0$, in projections onto the direction of the rope (see Fig. 3)

$$T + N \sin \alpha - mg \sin \alpha = m v_0^2 / l.$$ (3)

Solving equations (2) and (3) together, taking into account equality (1), we find T:

$$T = m \frac{v_0^2 \text{tg}^2 \alpha}{l \cos^2 \alpha}.$$

The problem can be solved more briefly by noting that the acceleration of the sled $\vec{a}$ is the same in all inertial frames of reference. Therefore, the centripetal acceleration of the sled in a system moving with speed $\vec{v}_0$ is equal in magnitude to the projection of acceleration $\vec{a}$ onto the direction of the rope:

$$\frac{v_1^2}{l} = \frac{v_0^2 \text{tg}^2 \alpha}{l} = a \cos \alpha.$$

From here we find α:

$$\alpha = \frac{v_0^2 \text{tg}^2 \alpha}{l \cos \alpha}.$$

From the equation of motion of the sleigh in a fixed frame of reference in projections to the horizontal direction (to the direction $\vec{a}$) -

$$T \cos \alpha = ma = m \frac{v_0^2 \text{tg}^2 \alpha}{l \cos \alpha}.$$

— find T:

$$T = m \frac{v_0^2 \text{tg}^2 \alpha}{l \cos^2 \alpha}.$$

$$M \frac{R}{2} \omega^2 = G \frac{M^2}{R^2}.$$

Hence the angular velocity of rotation of the star is equal to

$$\omega = \frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}.$$
