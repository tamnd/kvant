If you carry out this experiment in a dimly lit room, and then illuminate the light bulb with bright light, for example, using a powerful lamp and a lens that focuses the light beam onto the light bulb, then the ignition voltage will noticeably decrease. (You will have to look carefully when the light bulb flashes - in bright light its glow is barely noticeable.) The decrease in voltage occurs because the light, falling on the electrodes coated with an adhesive composition, knocks electrons out of them and thereby contributes to the start of the discharge. Thus, the light bulb can work as a kind of light-sensitive element.

Readers have the opportunity to independently come up with circuits that use this property of the light bulb.

Migraous light bulb

The fact that the discharge ignition voltage in a neon light bulb is higher than the operating combustion voltage makes it possible to make a simple oscillation generator using a light bulb. One of the possible circuits is shown at figure 4. The circuit uses a capacitor with a value of C = 1 μF, designed for voltages up to 300 V; resistor resistances are equal to R₁=1 kOhm and R₂=47 kOhm; diode D can be of any type, as long as its reverse voltage is greater than 350 V (in principle, you can work with the rectifier mentioned in the previous experiment).

The generator works as follows. The moment it is turned on, the capacitor begins to charge through a resistor with resistance R₁. In this case, the light bulb does not light (its resistance can be considered infinitely large). Over time, the voltage on the capacitor plates increases. When it becomes equal to U₃agig, the light bulb will flash, its resistance will sharply decrease and the capacitor begins to charge through the light bulb and the second resistor. In our circuit, this process occurs at a faster rate than charging and continues until the voltage on the light bulb drops to the discharge voltage U₃aschen (≈60 V). The lamp will go out and the cycle will repeat again. If we use an oscilloscope, then on its screen we can see a picture similar to the one shown in figure 4.

By changing the values ​​of capacitance C and resistance R₁ and R₂, you can get different flash durations and the development of the oscillation period T value.

The described generator (improved in a variety of ways) can be used, say, to construct a sound generator. To do this, you need to connect a telephone in parallel with the light bulb through another capacitor (for example, TOH-1). With such a device you will be able, in particular, to observe and feel the neon light bulb’s sensitivity to light.

In conclusion, we would like to advise the reader who is interested in experiments with a neon lamp to get acquainted with the book by V. F. Shilov “Designs on a neon lamp.” It contains ten schemes available for amateur radio schoolchildren to complete.
