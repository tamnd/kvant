Solving physics problems

T. S. PETROVA

In the federal issue «Kvant» for this year the article “Given... It is required to determine...” was published. This article provides the conditions for the eighth tasks. The conditions said what was “Given” and what “required to be determined” - the reader was asked to come up with it himself and determine it.

The editors received many letters in which readers provide their completed formulations of problems and their solutions. We got better at how seventh-grader Ismail Erkenov from the village of Korf in the Kamchatka region, sixth-grader Natasha Petrina from the city of Kokand of the Uzbek SSR, ninth-grader Ikhteyr Umzakov from Novosibirsk, sixth-grader Vologda Lyubomirov from Vilnius, fifth-grader Svetlana Gorokhova from Angarsk, seventh-grader Pavel Lushnikov from Moscow, and sixth-graders from school no. 68 from Ugleuralskaya station in the Perm region, Mikhail Gavevsky from the city of Botkinsk in the Udmurt Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, eighth-grader Kakha Babukashvili from Kutansi...

Let's look at the possible solutions to the problem and, along the way, discuss the mistakes that were encountered in many letters.

Problem 1. Two roads intersect at right angles. The sections of roads forming the intersection were covered with asphalt. The length of each section is 25 m, width 4 m. 5520 kg of asphalt were used for the coating. How much asphalt is used to cover 1 m² of dugol?

Answer: 30 kg.

Many solutions made the same mistake - they did not take into account that at the intersection of roads (figure 1) only one of them is covered with asphalt. Some readers brought up another question to this problem: what is the thickness of the coating? Well, formally this question is legitimate. But life experience is

Problem 2. A steel ball whose mass is 1.2 kg has a volume of 200 cm³. What is the volume of the cavity inside the ball?

Solution. The density of steel is 7.8 g/cm³ (we take this value from the tables). A steel body of mass m=1.2 kg=1200 g must have a volume

$$V_{ct} = \frac{m_{ct}}{q_{ct}} = \frac{1200 \text{ г}}{7,8 \text{ г/cm}^3} \approx 150 \text{ cm}^3.$$

Consequently, in a steel ball with a volume of 200 cm³ there is a cavity whose volume is equal to

$$V_{n} = V - V_{ct} \approx 200 \text{ cm}^3 - 150 \text{ cm}^3 = 50 \text{ cm}^3.$$

Answer: 50 cm³.

In many letters to this problem the question was posed: what is the density of steel? The solution to this problem is very convenient: $\rho = m/V = 6 \text{ г/cm}^3$. And they left it at that. But this answer can be verified using tabular data. The discrepancy between the received answer and the table value $\rho_{ct} = 7,8 \text{ г/cm}^3$ suggests that something is wrong in the condition. What? It seems to us that with this line of reasoning you will come to the conclusion that there is a cavity in the ball. And then the question about the volume of this density is directed by itself.

Problem 3. The length of the railway station platform is 60 m. A freight train moving at a speed of 45 km/h takes 16 s to complete the platform. Determine the length of the train.

The solution is clear from figure 2:

$$l + x = vt \Rightarrow x = vt - l.$$

Substituting v = 45 km/h = $\frac{45,000}{3600}$ m/s = 12 m/s, t = 16 s and l = 60 m, we get x = 140 m.

Answer: 140 m.

The same mistake was repeated in several letters: when calculating x, the speed v was substituted numerically equal to 45, that is, in units of km/h, and the time was equal to 16, that is, changed in seconds. This is a very bad mistake!

Problem 4. Atmospheric pressure at the surface of Venus is 10.3 MPa, the force of gravity is 1.2 times less than on Earth. What will be the altitude of a distance in a barometric tube on Venus?

Solution. The height h of this path is determined from the condition p_{B} = q_{pt} g_{B} h, where g_{B} is the acceleration of free fall on Venus. The gravitational force acting on a body of mass m is equal to F_{B} = g_{B} m on Venus, and F_{3} = g_{3} m on Earth. The body mass on both Earth and Venus is the same, and F_{3}/F_{B} = 1.2. Therefore, g_{B} = g_{3}/1.2 = 8.2 m/s². Thus, h = \frac{p_{B}}{q_{pt} g_{B}} = \frac{10.3 \cdot 10^6 \Pi a}{13.6 \cdot 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3 \cdot 8.2 \text{ m/s}^2} \approx 91 m.

Problem 5. A metal ball weighing 900 g, heated to 155°C, was lowered into a calorimeter containing 3 liters of water at a temperature of 10°C. As a result, the calorimeter reached a temperature of 15°C. The heat capacity of the calorimeter is infinitely small compared to the temperature of the ball and water. Determine what metal the ball is made of.

Answer: The ball is made of steel. (From the law of conservation of energy, we determine the specific temperament of the metal: c = 500 J/(kg °C). From the tables we find that steel has this temperament.)

Problem 6. A cylindrical sauce with a bottom area of ​​125 cm² contains water. When an ice cube was placed in the vessel, the water level increased by 9 mm. What is the length of the edge of the ice cube?

Solution. Let's imagine that an ice cube placed in a vessel

The mass of water in the vessel will be equal to the mass of the original water (in a vessel without ice) the plane mass of water formed from ice, that is, the plane mass of the cube. The volume occupied by this "extra" water (it is highlighted at figure 3) is equal to $V_B = Sh$; the mass of this water - $m = \rho_B V = \rho_B Sh$ - is equal to the mass of an ice cube; but the mass of the cube is $m = \rho_B V_B = \rho_B a^3$ (a is the length of the edge of the cube). $$a = \sqrt[3]{\frac{\rho_B Sh}{\rho_B}} = \sqrt{\frac{1 \, \text{г/cm}^3 \cdot 125 \, \text{cm}^2 \cdot 0,9 \, \text{cm}}{0,9 \, \text{г/cm}^3}} = 5 \, \text{см}.$$

Most readers formulated the question for this problem as follows: find the volume of the cube. Essentially, the solution to such a problem is similar to the one we just presented. However, this formulation of the question does not use the fact that the piece of ice has the shape of a cube.

Problem 7. The resistance of iron wiring, the mass of which is 390 g, is 5 Ohms. Determine the length and cross-sectional area of ​​the wiring.

Solution. The wiring resistance is equal to $R = rl/S$, where $r = \frac{9,8}{10^8} \, \text{Ом} \cdot \text{м}$ is the resistivity of the iron (we denoted it as $r$ so as not to be confused with the density $\rho$). The mass of the wiring is $m = \rho l S$, where $\rho = 7880 \, \text{кг/m}^3$ is the density of iron. From the last equality we express $l$ through $S$: $l = m / (\rho S)$; substitute this value into the expression for $R$: $R = rm / (\rho S^2)$. From here we find $S$:

$$S = \sqrt{\frac{r m}{R \rho}}$$

$$\frac{(9,8/10^8) (\text{Ом} \cdot \text{м}) \cdot 0,39 \, \text{кг}}{5 \, \text{Ом} \cdot 7880 \, \text{кг/m}^3} \approx \frac{1}{10^6} \, \text{м}^2 = 1 \, \text{мм}^2.$$

Now we find $l$:

$$l = \frac{m}{\rho S} = \frac{0,39 \, \text{кг}}{7880 \, \text{кг/m}^3 \cdot (1/10^6) \, \text{м}^2} \approx 50 \, \text{м}.$$

Answer: 50 m, 1 mm².

For different readers, the wiring length ranged from 5 cm to 500 m. In this regard, let’s talk about one mistake. More precisely, this is not an error itself, but a consequence of an error made in the decision. You received an answer, but you can’t verify it - the answer is not given in the task. See if your result is awkward. Remember the poem by S. Ya. Marshak about a careless student who solved a problem and received the answer “two diggers and two thirds”? This absurdity speaks for itself. And in other cases, observation, communication, and intuition will help you assess the reasonableness of the obtained values. Let's return to our task. Everyday reports suggest that iron wiring 5 cm long cannot have a mass of 390 g, and a coil of iron wiring polyklometre long is not so easy to lift.

Problem 8. Two slider restats, designed for a resistance of 20 ohms each, one is wound with wires below the line, and the other with nichrome wires. The lengths of the wires used for the windings are the same. Which wire has the largest cross-sectional area? How many times?

Let us write down the solution to this problem briefly:

$$R = \rho_{ник} \frac{l}{S_{ник}} = \rho_{ник} \frac{l}{S_{ник}};$$

$$\frac{S_{ник}}{S_{ник}} = \frac{\rho_{ник}}{Q_{ник}} = \frac{1,1 \, \text{Ом} \cdot \text{м}^2 / \text{м}}{0,4 \, \text{Ом} \cdot \text{м}^2 / \text{м}} = 2,75.$$

Answer: The cross-sectional area of ​​the lower wiring is 2.75 times larger.
