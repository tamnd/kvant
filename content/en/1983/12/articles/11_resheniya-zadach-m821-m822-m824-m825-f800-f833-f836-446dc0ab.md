M821. Solve the equation
$$x^3 + x^2 + x = -\frac{1}{3}.$$

Let's rewrite the equation in the form $3x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1 = 0$; we get the sum of cubes: $(x+1)^3 + 2x^3 = 0$. Let's write it in the form $(x+1)^3 = -2x^3$ and take the cube root from both sides: $x+1 = -x\sqrt[3]{2}$; hence $x = -\frac{1}{1 + \sqrt[3]{2}}$.

Yu. I. Ionin

◆ We will assume that the set M is located on the number line and is composed of the segments [a1, b1], [a2, b2], ..., [ak, bk], a1 < b1 < a2 < b2 < ... < bk; The lengths of these segments are denoted by the corresponding ones, δ1, δ2, ..., δk. For each pair of numbers i and j (1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ k), we denote by Mij the set of lengths of segments with endpoints lying on the segments [ai, bi], [aj, bj]. These sets obviously form segments on the number line whose lengths are equal to δi if i = j, and δi + δj if i < j. According to the conditions of the problem, the sum of the lengths of the segments is not less than 1. Therefore, δ1 + (δ1 + δ2) + ... + (δ1 + δk) + δ2 + (δ2 + δ3) + (δ2 + δ4) + ... + δk ≥ 1.

In the sum written on the left side of the inequality, each δj (j = 1, ..., k) occurs k times. Thus, k (δ1 + δ2 + ... + δk) ≥ 1, whence δ1 + δ2 + ... + δk ≥ 1/k, as required.

F834. A ball of mass $m_1 = 0,1$ kg is suspended from the ceiling on a very long weightless thread, and a ball of mass $m_2 = 0,05$ kg is attached to it on a thread of length $l = 0,2$ m. The lower ball is effectively given a speed $v_0$ in the horizontal direction. At what value $v_0$ can the balls be at the same height?

With a very long upper thread, we can assume that the upper ball moves along a horizontal straight line. At the moment when the balls are at the same height, that is, the lower thread takes a horizontal position (see figure), the horizontal projection of the speed of the second ball will be equal to the speed of the first ball. This follows from the inextensibility of the thread connecting them. In order for the minimum value of speed $v_0$ at which the balls will be at the same height, it is necessary to assume that at this moment the vertical projection of the speed of the second ball is zero (that is, the speeds $v$ of the common balls are equal and directed horizontally).

All external forces acting on the system (tension of the upper thread and gravity) are directed vertically. Therefore, the horizontal projection of the total momentum of the system is preserved:

$$m_2 v_0 \min = (m_1 + m_2) v.$$

From here

$$v = v_0 \min \frac{m_2}{(m_1 + m_2)}.$$

According to the law of conservation of mechanical energy,

$$\frac{1}{2} m_2 v_0^2 \min = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v^2 + m_2 gl.$$

Substituting the found expression for $v$ into this equation, we find

$$v_0 \min = \sqrt{2 gl \left(1 + \frac{m_2}{m_1}\right)}.$$

Substituting the numerical data, we get

$$v_0 \min = \sqrt{3 gl} \approx 2,4 \text{ m/c.}$$

In the limiting case $m_2 \ll m_1$, the upper ball remains motionless, and in order for the lower ball to rise to the height $l$, it needs to be given a speed $v_0 = \sqrt{2 gl}$.

A cutter was lowered into a glass of water and the dependence of water temperature on time (Table 1). 1) How many degrees will the water cool in 1 minute if the cutter is disconnected from the mains at a temperature of $t_1 = 50^\circ\text{C}$? 2) Will the water boil if the cutter is not turned off long enough? We will assume that the power that the cutter supplies to the water does not change, and the temperature of the water increases unevenly due to an increase in heat output with increasing temperature. To take into account the influence of heat transfer, we will trace how the temperature increment $\Delta t$ changes during 1 minute of operation of the cutter, depending on the water temperature (we will take the average temperature between its values ​​at the beginning and end of each minute). F836. Plate A of the flat capacitor is motionless, plate B is attached to the wall by a spring and can move, remaining parallel to plate A (see figure). After closing key K, plate B began to move and stopped in a new equilibrium position. At the same time, the distance between the plates decreased by $a_1=10\%$. How much would the equilibrium distance between the plates change if switch K were closed for a short time? It is assumed that during this time plate B does not have time to noticeably shift.

When the switch $K$ is closed, the direction to the capacitor is maintained constant and equal to the emf $\mathcal{E}$ of the source. Let the initial distance between the plates of the capacitor (when the spring is not stretched) be equal to $d$, and the displacement of plate B when reaching a new equilibrium position is equal to $x_1=a_1d=0,1d$. The charge on the capacitor will be

$$Q_1 = C_1 \mathcal{E} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}}{(d-x_1)},$$

where $S$ is the area of ​​the capacitor plate. For example, fields in capacitor $E_1=\mathcal{E}/(d-x_1)$. This field is created by two plates, so that the field created by one plate has a direction of $E_1/2$. In accordance with this, for the forces acting on the plate $B$, we can write

$$\frac{E_1 Q_1}{2} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}^2}{2(d-x_1)^2} = kx_1$$

(k is the spring stiffness).

Let us now consider the situation when the key $K$ is closed for a short time. In this case, the capacitor becomes contaminated

$$Q_2 = \varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}/d$$

(the plates did not have time to move), which subsequently remains constant. Let the displacement of the plate $B$ be equal to $x_2$ in the new equilibrium position. then the direction of the field in the capacitor will be $E_2 = Q_2/C_2(d-x_2)$, where $C_2=\varepsilon_0 S/(d-x_2)$. The equilibrium condition of the plate $B$ will be written in the form

$$\frac{E_2 Q_2}{2} = \frac{Q_2^2}{2\varepsilon_0 S} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S \mathcal{E}^2}{2d^2} = kx_2.$$

From equation (1) and (2) we find $x_2$:

$$x_2 = x_1 \left(\frac{d-x_1}{d}\right)^2 = 0,081d,$$

that is, the distance between the capacitor plates decreases by $a_2=8,1\%$.
