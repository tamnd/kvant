⟦folio 14⟧

If there are free facts, then the scientist’s task is to check.

Worse, perhaps, something else. In the endless stories with spiritualists, some of the methodological spirit of the men of science, the conviction, was revealed: I just need to look around, look, and everything will be explained. And it is useful to remember that any average magician can easily demonstrate a series of “supernatural” effects of the collected Nabel laureates, and whether any of them can almost hold his hand.

As far as I know, the first person who immediately rose to the occasion was Michael Faraday. He prepared a stack of circles held together with soft, sticky putty. There was a new one - a weak, slightly inhibiting rotation of the circles, but not eliminating it completely. After the “appearance of the spirit,” they found out that the top circle turned as much as possible, the next circle turned less, and the bottom one didn’t move at all. (It followed from this that the source of the turning force was located above, where the hands of the spiritualists lay. Consciously or unconsciously, they themselves rotated the “magic saucer”.) It goes without saying that a lot of mental and non-thoughtful explanations for this effect immediately appeared. But Faraday was no longer interested in spiritualism. And others checked and wasted time. In 1871, our great correspondent D.I. Mendeleev headed the St. Petersburg University commission for the study of spiritualism. The commission's conclusion was clear: spiritualism is sovereignty. So it is not appropriate to reproach scientists for unsubstantiated denial of scientific knowledge.

The result: tens and hundreds of clear cases of deception or unconscious desire to get the desired result. And not a single reliable fact.

Nevertheless, even the deepest saucers are turning, calling up Julius Caesar and Napoleon. To Cornwall, to Texas, to Ufa. Moreover, in England and Holland there are sections of spiritualism in certain magazines.

Then mediums appeared. These turned out to be more stable. Until then, they have enjoyed a certain popularity, only now they are sometimes called “psychics.” It was more difficult with them; the less talented ones turned out to be not bad, perhaps even brilliant magicians.

The history of “mediumism” is interesting and funny; respectful, but the result is still the same: not a single reliable fact.

Although “occult phenomena” can still feed a “smart” person very well, the fashion for them has faded somewhat. A kind of vacuum has formed again.

“Our” myths have already poured into it - the myths of the 20th century: the Bermuda Triangle, the Loch Nes monster, Bigfoot, telepaths, tenth units of space aliens...

What is so attractive about the idea of ​​living in some lakes (primarily the Scottish Loch Ness) that have survived to the present day?
