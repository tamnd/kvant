F834. A ball of mass $m_1 = 0,1$ kg is suspended from the ceiling on a very long weightless thread, and a ball of mass $m_2 = 0,05$ kg is attached to it on a thread of length $l = 0,2$ m. The lower ball is effectively given a speed $v_0$ in the horizontal direction. At what value $v_0$ can the balls be at the same height?

With a very long upper thread, we can assume that the upper ball moves along a horizontal straight line. At the moment when the balls are at the same height, that is, the lower thread takes a horizontal position (see figure), the horizontal projection of the speed of the second ball will be equal to the speed of the first ball. This follows from the inextensibility of the thread connecting them. In order for the minimum value of speed $v_0$ at which the balls will be at the same height, it is necessary to assume that at this moment the vertical projection of the speed of the second ball is zero (that is, the speeds $v$ of the common balls are equal and directed horizontally).

All external forces acting on the system (tension of the upper thread and gravity) are directed vertically. Therefore, the horizontal projection of the total momentum of the system is preserved:

$$m_2 v_0 \min = (m_1 + m_2) v.$$

From here

$$v = v_0 \min \frac{m_2}{(m_1 + m_2)}.$$

According to the law of conservation of mechanical energy,

$$\frac{1}{2} m_2 v_0^2 \min = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v^2 + m_2 gl.$$

Substituting the found expression for $v$ into this equation, we find

$$v_0 \min = \sqrt{2 gl \left(1 + \frac{m_2}{m_1}\right)}.$$

Substituting the numerical data, we get

$$v_0 \min = \sqrt{3 gl} \approx 2,4 \text{ m/c.}$$

In the limiting case $m_2 \ll m_1$, the upper ball remains motionless, and in order for the lower ball to rise to the height $l$, it needs to be given a speed $v_0 = \sqrt{2 gl}$.

E. I. Butikov
