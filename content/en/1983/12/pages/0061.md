XXIV International Mathematical Olympiad
We present the solution to the problem formed on p. 46.
Let first $f$ be the required function. Putting $y=x$ in 1) we obtain that for any $x \in \mathbb{R}^+$ the number $b=x \cdot f(x)$ is a fixed point of the function $f$ (that is, $f(b)=b$). Let $a$ be an arbitrary fixed point of the function $f$. Let us prove that $a=1$. If at $n \ge 2$ we have $f(a^{n-1})=a^{n-1}$, then $f(a^n)=f(a \cdot a^{n-1})=f(a \cdot f(a^{n-1}))=a^{n-1} \cdot f(a)=a^{n-1} \cdot a=a^n$.
Thus, all numbers $a^n(n \in \mathbb{N})$ are also fixed points. Next, $a=f(a)=f(1 \cdot a)=f(1 \cdot f(a))=a \cdot f(1)$; from $a \neq 0$ follows $f(1)=1$. Then $a \cdot f\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)=f\left(\frac{1}{a} \cdot f(a)\right)=f\left(\frac{1}{a} \cdot a\right)=f(1)=1$, from where $f\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)=\frac{1}{a}$. Finally, it is verified in a similar way that $f\left(\frac{1}{a^n}\right)=\frac{1}{a^n}$. Thus, all numbers $a^n(n \in \mathbb{Z})$ are fixed points. From condition 2) we conclude that $a=1$. So, for any $x \in \mathbb{R}^+$ we have $x \cdot f(x)=1$, hence $f(x)=\frac{1}{x}$.

Conversely, it is easy to check that the function $\frac{1}{x}$ satisfies conditions 1), 2).

$$a_{1x}=\frac{1}{m}(F_x+F_{\tau p}) \text{ и } a_{2x}=\frac{1}{m}(F_x-F_{\tau p})$$

Task 2
1) Let us denote by $U$ and $I$ the effective values ​​of the voltage and current of the source. Then
$$U=IZ=I\left(\frac{1}{R^2}+\left(\omega C-\frac{1}{\omega L}\right)^2\right)^{-1/2}$$
Here
$$C=C_1+C_2, \quad L=\frac{L_1L_2}{L_1+L_2}$$
The power released in the resistor is
$$P=\frac{U^2}{R}=\frac{I^2}{R}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{R^2}+\left(\omega C-\frac{1}{\omega L}\right)^2}$$
According to the conditions of the problem, $I$ is a constant value and does not depend on the frequency of the generator $\omega$. Therefore $P=P_M$ provided $\omega_M C-\frac{1}{\omega_M L}=0$, or $\omega_M=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}=10^5 \text{ c}^{-1}$.
The power will decrease by half compared to $P_M$ at frequencies $\omega_\pm$ satisfying $\omega_\pm C-\frac{1}{\omega_\pm L}=0$, from where
$$\omega_\pm=\frac{\pm L \pm \sqrt{L^2+4R^2LC}}{2RLC}$$
