The very name “mathematical physics” suggests that this discipline occupies an intermediate area between mathematics and physics, being under the direct and strong influence of both. What exactly does this “intermediate field” of science do? Speaking briefly and perhaps not entirely clearly, this question can be answered like this: “Mathematical physics is the theory of mathematical models of physical phenomena.” To understand this brief definition, let us turn to some (well-known to schoolchildren) examples of such mathematical models.

Describing the laws of motion of solid bodies, physicists formulate Newton’s second law: the acceleration of a material body is directly proportional to the sum of all forces acting on a given body.
