In 1960, a special expedition led by Hillary, the first conqueror of Everest, arrived in Nepal, where the yeti was recognized, one might say, at the state level. Thorough, comprehensive studies were carried out for several months. The results were extremely disappointing for the Yeti. As biological analysis showed, the Yeti warehouses stored in the monasteries were a trivial forgery. Tannstevnye tracks in the snow received the simplest explanation: under the mountain sun, the snow quickly melts, and in a matter of minutes the track of a mountain goat can turn into the track of an unknown monster. All the stories of the “eyewitnesses” are either declared to be a meeting with a rare species of Himalayan bear, or were clearly made up. But of course, local residents promised to obtain indisputable evidence next year. 23 years have passed. Almost every year Hillary comes to Nepal for a long period of time, but... the Yeti have not been announced.

Do you think this story made any impression on most Bigfoot people? Of course not. Any arguments are incomprehensible when the place of reason is taken by unreasoning faith.

In the United States, a private institute has been operating for ten years, organized by art UFO enthusiasts. It is arranged in the most plentiful place. Result: not a single fact, not a single sensational publication. But it didn't make the slightest impression on UFO fans. They believe in aliens. They believe and that’s it. However, fashion still subsided somewhat.

Now psychics have again taken the lead.

I wonder what's next?

Thoreau's Thorough Paths
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. V. BYALKO

Asterode Toro was discovered in 1948 by American astronomer K. Wirtanen. Thoreau is a minor astronomer; its diameter is about 3 km, its mass is about 10$^{17}$ kg. In its motion around the Sun in an elliptical orbit, Thoreau finds himself near the orbit of Venus at perihelion, and moves beyond the orbit of Mars at aphelion.

figure 1 shows the orbits of Thoreau, Venus, Earth and Mars. You see: Thoreau's orbit intersects the orbits of Earth and Mars. On February 1, 1984, Toro will pass near our planet at a distance of ~0.15 AU. e.*), that is, it will approach the Earth at 2.25 · 10$^{7}$ km. Such collisions occur on average once every 4 years. Could it be that Thoreau will collide with the Earth? This would be a catastrophe that cannot even be compared with natural phenomena on our planet. The energy that would be released in such a collision is close to the energy of 10$^{10}$ atomic bombs from the Hiroshima tragedy.

It turns out that such descriptions are correct. The likelihood of Toro colliding with the Earth is extremely low. The reason for this smallness was discovered by American scientists L. Danielson and W. Yip. They showed that the ratio of the Earth's and Thoreau's orbital periods is 5:8. In other words, in 8 Earth years, Toro makes 5 revolutions around the Sun, and, therefore, every 8 years, the relative position of the Sun, Earth and Toro is repeated.

*) 1 a. e., or 1 astronomical unit of length, is a unit of distance in astronomy, equal to the average distance of the Earth from the Sun: 1 a. e. = 149.6 · 10$^{6}$ km.
