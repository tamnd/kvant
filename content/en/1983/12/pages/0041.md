◆ We will assume that the set M is located on the number line and is composed of the segments [a1, b1], [a2, b2], ..., [ak, bk], a1 < b1 < a2 < b2 < ... < bk; The lengths of these segments are denoted by the corresponding ones, δ1, δ2, ..., δk. For each pair of numbers i and j (1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ k), we denote by Mij the set of lengths of segments with endpoints lying on the segments [ai, bi], [aj, bj]. These sets obviously form segments on the number line whose lengths are equal to δi if i = j, and δi + δj if i < j. According to the conditions of the problem, the sum of the lengths of the segments is not less than 1. Therefore, δ1 + (δ1 + δ2) + ... + (δ1 + δk) + δ2 + (δ2 + δ3) + (δ2 + δ4) + ... + δk ≥ 1.

In the sum written on the left side of the inequality, each δj (j = 1, ..., k) occurs k times. Thus, k (δ1 + δ2 + ... + δk) ≥ 1, whence δ1 + δ2 + ... + δk ≥ 1/k, as required.

◆ This problem is quite complicated, and analyzing it with mechanical vibrations will help us understand it.

The mechanical system we need is depicted in figure 2. These are two bars with masses m1 and m2, lying on a smooth horizontal plane and connected by a spring with stiffness k. Each of the bars is equipped with a side, allowing it to move freely to the right, but not allowing it to move backwards. The coordinates of the bars at the initial moment are denoted by x1 and x2', where x2' = l + x2, l is the length of the uncrucified spring.

Let us establish the correspondence between electrical and mechanical quantities in our vibration systems.

Let the positive charge Q on the plate A of the capacitor correspond to the stretching of the spring by a length x. Changing x is possible by mixing each of the bars: x = x2 - x1; the rate of elongation of the spring is determined by the difference in the speeds of the bars: Δx/Δt = v2 - v1. Similarly, the rate of change of charge Q is determined by the difference in currents flowing through the coils: ΔQ/Δt = I2 - I1. Sides S1 and S2 allow velocities of only one sign: v1 > 0, v2 > 0; similarly, diodes D1 and D2 pass currents in only one direction.
