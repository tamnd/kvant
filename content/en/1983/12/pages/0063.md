Note that the first process of photon scattering on a moving electron, considered in the conditions of the problem, is a reversal of a temporary process - scattering on a stationary electron (Compton effect). It immediately follows that $\lambda_i = \lambda_f = 1,25 \cdot 10^{-10}$ m and $P_{e1} = P_{e2} = P_f$. The de Broglie wavelength of an electron can be found through its momentum $P_e$:

$$\lambda_e = \frac{h}{P_e}.$$
To determine $P_e$, it is sufficient to apply the laws of conservation of energy and momentum to any of the two scattering processes (for example, to the second):

$$h v_0 - h v_f = E_\kappa,$$
$$\frac{h v_0}{c} = \frac{h v_f}{c} \cos \theta + P_e \cos \varphi,$$
$$0 = \frac{h v_f}{c} \sin \theta - P_e \sin \varphi.$$
Here $E_\kappa$ and $P_e$ are the kinetic energy and momentum of the electron, $\varphi$ is the angle that determines the direction of its movement after scattering. The task can be completed in various ways. You can, for example, simply use the well-known formula for the Compton effect, which follows from the relationships written above:

$$\lambda_f - \lambda_0 = \frac{h}{mc} (1 - \cos \theta),$$
where $h/(mc) = 0,24 \cdot 10^{-11}$ m is the so-called Compton wavelength. In this case, the solution is compatible. Really,

$$E_\kappa = h v_0 - h v_f = h \frac{c}{\lambda_0} - h \frac{c}{\lambda_f} =$$
$$= h c \frac{\lambda_f - \lambda_0}{\lambda_0 \lambda_f} \approx \frac{h c}{\lambda_f^2} \frac{h}{mc} (1 - \cos \theta) \approx$$
$$\approx 1,56 \cdot 10^{-17} \text{ Дж} \approx 97,4 \text{ эВ}.$$
