Force and deformation

The word "strength" is one of the most used words. In offensive language it can have the most significant meanings. But in mechanics the meaning of this word is completely clear. Everything that can be said about mechanical force in general is contained in Newton's laws.

Law number three states that force, or rather dreams, arise from the interaction of bodies. From Newton's second law it follows that force is the cause of the acceleration of bodies, that is, the change in their speed. Finally, Newton's first law states that in the absence of forces or when the geometric sum of forces applied to a body is equal to zero, the body moves without acceleration. All three laws are formulated for the case when the motion is considered relative to the inertial frame of reference.

It would seem that the role of force in mechanics is clear: force imparts acceleration to the body to which it is applied. According to the laws of mechanics, this is her only “specialty”.

Meanwhile, sometimes there are statements (later in physics textbooks) that force not only imparts acceleration to a body, but also deforms it. In the first case, they talk about dynamic, and in the second, about static manifestation of forces. Thus, in one of the textbooks recommended for high school students, we read: “The result of the interaction of bodies is either deformation (a change in the size or shape of a body) or acceleration (a change in the magnitude or direction of speed). Of course, it is not excluded that both deformations and accelerations can occur simultaneously.” Another example. In a textbook for higher schools, widely known throughout the world, strength is defined in the following words: “The concept of “strength” comes from our muscular sensation. Qualitative force is determined by two characteristics: it can deform a stationary solid body and accelerate a moving body.” In other books we read: “Force is something that causes or stops movement, changes the direction of movement or changes the shape of a body” or “force is a vector quantity that characterizes the interaction of bodies, as a result of which the bodies are deformed or acquire accelerations... The static action of force is manifested in the presence of deformations, the dynamic action - in the presence of accelerations.” The number of such examples can be increased, although in most modern books on physics force is not directly related to deformation.

How, in fact, are forces, accelerations and deformations related to each other?

Force is the cause of acceleration. Therefore, once again, in Newton’s three laws, with the help of which any mechanical problem can be solved, there is no hint of such a phenomenon as deformation of a body. And it can’t be, because Newton’s laws relate to the movement of materials of points (this is constant exposure to Physics 8 acceleration). And the points, of course, cannot be deformed! It follows from this that forces, in accordance with Newton’s laws, should be considered as the cause of acceleration and only acceleration.
