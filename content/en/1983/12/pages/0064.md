$$\lambda_e = \frac{h}{P_e} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mE_K}} \approx 1.24 \cdot 10^{-10} \text{ m}.$$

If the “ready-made” Compton formula is unknown to the solver, then one will have to look for the electron’s momentum from the equation, which expresses the laws of conservation of energy and momentum. But then the question will arise, what formulas describe the electron - relativistic or classical. In this case, both solutions give the same result, but when using classical formulas from the very beginning, it is necessary to give a reasonable conclusion that the electron is non-relativistic. We present challengers with the opportunity to complete the task on their own. For reference, we point out that the kinetic energy $E_K$ and momentum $P$ of a particle with rest mass $m$ are related in the relativistic case by the correspondence

$$P^2 c^2 = E_K (E_K + 2mc^2),$$

from which, for $E_K \ll mc^2$, the classical correspondence $E_K = P^2/(2m)$ is obtained.

Experimental task

1) figure 9 shows two diagrams. In the first of them, both voltmeters are connected to the source in series, in the second, only one voltmeter is used. Let us denote by $U_1$, $U_2$ and $U'_1$ the readings of voltmeters and by $I_1$ and $I_2$ the currents in the circuits. Then

$$U_1 + U_2 = \mathcal{E} - I_1 r, \quad U'_1 = \mathcal{E} - I_2 r.$$

Note that

$$\frac{U_1}{U'_1} = \frac{I_1}{I_2}.$$

From these correspondences we obtain

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{U_2 U'_1}{U'_1 - U_1}.$$

Thus, to determine the source emf, it is sufficient to use two circuits.

2) The voltmeter and resistance magazine can be connected to the source in two ways (Fig. 10). It is assumed that during the experiment the resistance $R$ of the magazine changes, and at each value $R$ the voltmeter $U$ shown is missing. In the case of $a$) connects by the formula

$$\frac{1}{U} = \frac{1}{\mathcal{E}} \left( 1 + r \left( \frac{1}{R_V} + \frac{1}{R} \right) \right),$$

where $r$ and $R_V$ are the internal resistances of the source and voltmeter, respectively. This shows that the dependence of the quantity $1/U$ on $1/R$ is linear. Based on the experimental data, it is possible to construct the corresponding graph (Fig. 11, a). Using the slope of the graph and the point of intersection with the ordinary axis, determine the values

$$k_1 = \frac{r}{\mathcal{E}} \text{ и } b_1 = \frac{R_V + r}{\mathcal{E} R_V}.$$

In the case of $6$) we have the correspondence

$$\frac{1}{U} = \frac{R_V + r}{\mathcal{E} R_V} + \frac{R}{\mathcal{E} R_V}.$$

Here the relationship between the quantities $1/U$ and $R$ is linear. Based on the experimental data, a corresponding graph is constructed (Fig. 11, b), and from it it is determined

$$k_2 = \frac{1}{\mathcal{E} R_V} \text{ и } b_2 = \frac{R_V + r}{\mathcal{E} R_V}.$$

Therefore, the experiment allows us to find the following parameters:

$$k_1 = \frac{r}{\mathcal{E}}, \quad k_2 = \frac{1}{\mathcal{E} R_V} \text{ и } b = b_1 = b_2 = \frac{R_V + r}{\mathcal{E} R_V}.$$

From here, for $\mathcal{E}$, $r$ and $R_V$ we obtain quadratic equations with two positive solutions each. Thus, the proposed method without additional data does not allow us to unambiguously determine the excluded values.
