⟦folio 22⟧

Where it's thin, that's where it breaks

V. V. MAISKY

This article can be considered as an example of setting up any physical experiment. First of all, the problem is clearly formulated and an idea for its solution is developed. Then the experiment itself is carried out: a setup is selected and appropriate measurements are made. After which the results obtained are processed and theoretically applied.

When Vsevolod Maisky wrote his article, he was in tenth grade at Moscow School No. 179. Now he is a student at the Moscow Institute of Chemical Engineering.

“Durable”, “fragile” - we often use these terms in everyday life and in technology, characterizing various objects and materials. What is “strength” and how can it be measured?

Fishermen, for example, are well aware of a simple method for determining the strength of a fishing line: a piece of fishing line tied at one end is loaded with various weights; The maximum load a line can withstand is its tensile strength. This method can be used to test the strength of other materials.

What determines strength, such as tensile strength? Firstly, from the cross-sectional area. Thus, to break a fishing line with a diameter of 0.1 mm, a force of about 50 N is required, and a fishing line with a diameter of 0.5 mm can withstand the weight of an adult. In order to eliminate dependence on the thickness of the sample, a special concept is introduced - tensile strength. This is the maximum stress that the sample can withstand without failure.

Secondly, the strength of materials depends on its temperature. For metals, say, as the temperature increases, the tensile strength decreases (correspondence to the graphs of the dependence of the tensile strength on temperature for various metals is given, for example, in the “Handbook on Elementary Physics” by N. I. Koshkin and M. G. Shirkevich).

Could strength depend on... length? At first glance, it seems that no, and for large plums this is indeed true. But there is a material whose strength depends on the length of the sample. This material is wool.

One of the correspondence problems of the IV Tour of Young Physicists is based on the small properties of six-blade. Here are its terms:

Wool thread is woven from many individual fibers. Investigate the tensile strength of such a thread depending on its length. Explain the results of the experiment.
