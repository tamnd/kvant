1) Forward movement.
The equation of translational motion has the form:

$$\vec{N} + Mg\vec{T} + \vec{T} + F_{\tau p} = Ma,$$

or (in projections on the coordinate axes)

$$N - Mg - T \sin \alpha = Ma,$$
$$T \cos \alpha - F_{\tau p} = Ma_x.$$

Since the cube must be at rest, the acceleration is $\vec{a} = 0$, that is, $a_x = a_y = 0$, from which we get

$$N = Mg + T \sin \alpha,$$
$$F_{\tau p} = T \cos \alpha = F/2.$$

Since $0 \leq F_{\tau p} < \mu N$, the final result is:

$$F < \frac{2\mu Mg}{1 - \mu tg \alpha}.$$

Since $F > 0$, this condition is a constraint only for $1 - \mu tg \alpha > 0$. If $\mu tg \alpha \geq 1$, then “jamming” occurs, that is, for any value $F$ the cube cannot move forward.

2) Rotation.

The cube can tip over by rotating about the axis passing through the point $O$, at which the support reaction force $\vec{N}$ will be applied when tipping over. The maximum value of the tension force of the thread, at which the cube will not yet tip over, can be found from the condition that the sum of the moments of forces is equal to zero:

$$Mg \frac{a}{2} - Ta \cos \alpha = 0.$$

Considering that $T \cos \alpha = F/2$, we get

$$F = Mg.$$

Obviously, it does not cause capsizing if

$$F < Mg.$$
