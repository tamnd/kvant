The main suppliers of the equation are triangles and circles (with chords, tangents, secants) and their stereometric analogues: tetrahedrons (triangular pyramids) and spheres. The auxiliary elements are partially regular quadrangles (parallelograms, rhombuses, rectangles, squares) and their stereometric analogues (parallelopedes, prisms). Planimetric figures are richer in equations and more visible, so you should always strive to obtain planimetric fragments of the drawing. Let's consider the basic relationships supplied to us by triangles, tetrahedra, circles and balls.

1. A triangle is defined, generally speaking, by its 3 elements (not just any). If 3 given elements completely define a triangle, then all other elements can be expressed through them, for which it is useful to know the basic relationships in triangles. Let us denote: $a, b, c$ - side lengths, $a, \beta, \gamma$ - angles, $S$ - area, $r, R$ - radii of the inscribed and circumscribed circle, $h_a, m_a, l_a$ - height, median and bisector drawn from the angle $a$ (Fig. 1, a). Then we can write down the following relations:

a) $a/\sin a = b/\sin \beta = c/\sin \gamma = 2R$ (sine theorem);
b) $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos \gamma$ (cosine theorem);
c) $S = \frac{ah_a}{2} = \frac{(a+b+c)}{2} \cdot r =$
$$\sqrt{\frac{a+b+c}{2} \cdot \frac{a+b-c}{2} \cdot \frac{a-b+c}{2} \cdot \frac{-a+b+c}{2}}$$
(Heron's formula);
d) $h_a = \frac{2S}{a} = b \sin \gamma$;
e) $m_a^2 = a^2/4 + b^2 - ab \cos \gamma$
(by the cosine theorem from $\triangle ACM$, Fig. 1, $\sigma$);
f) $l_a = \frac{b \sin \gamma}{\sin \left(\frac{a}{2} + \gamma\right)}$
(by the theorem of sines from $\triangle ACL$, Fig. 1, $\theta$).
