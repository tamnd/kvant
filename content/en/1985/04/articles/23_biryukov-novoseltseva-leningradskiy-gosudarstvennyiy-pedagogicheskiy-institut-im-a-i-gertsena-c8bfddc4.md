1) Forward movement.
The equation of translational motion has the form:

$$\vec{N} + Mg\vec{T} + \vec{T} + F_{\tau p} = Ma,$$

or (in projections on the coordinate axes)

$$N - Mg - T \sin \alpha = Ma,$$
$$T \cos \alpha - F_{\tau p} = Ma_x.$$

Since the cube must be at rest, the acceleration is $\vec{a} = 0$, that is, $a_x = a_y = 0$, from which we get

$$N = Mg + T \sin \alpha,$$
$$F_{\tau p} = T \cos \alpha = F/2.$$

Since $0 \leq F_{\tau p} < \mu N$, the final result is:

$$F < \frac{2\mu Mg}{1 - \mu tg \alpha}.$$

Since $F > 0$, this condition is a constraint only for $1 - \mu tg \alpha > 0$. If $\mu tg \alpha \geq 1$, then “jamming” occurs, that is, for any value $F$ the cube cannot move forward.

The task with four pages on the cover of the third issue is a good illustration of the fact that a very detailed drawing can even make it somewhat difficult to find a solution. From the drawing on the cover of the third issue, you should see that the desired angle is equal in magnitude to the plane angle ADO, where O is the center of the ball (straight line DA is perpendicular to BD (linear

plane intersection niches) according to the condition, and DO - according to the theorem of three perpendiculars (the projection of the straight line DO onto the plane ABC is the straight line DA)); After this, you can get rid of unnecessary details of the drawing (Fig. 8) and move on to the solution.

First decision. Let φ be the value of the desired angle; then tan φ=OA:DA=2R:a, where R is the radius of the ball, a is the length of the edge of the base of the straight line. Let us express R through a by composing and equating two expressions for the distance OS from the center of the ball to the vertex of the straight line. From the right triangle OPS (P is the point of tangency of the straight line BS with the ball) we have

$$OS^2=OP^2+PS^2=R^2+(a-b)^2$$

(1) (PS=PB=BS=BA-BS=a-b, where b is the length of the side edge of the straight line; segments BP and BA are equal as tangents drawn to the ball from one point).

From the triangle OAS, in which the angle OAS is equal to 45° (AO is perpendicular to the plane ABC, and the straight line AS is inclined to it at an angle of 45°), using the law of cosines we obtain the second equality:

$$OS^2=OA^2+AS^2-2OA \cdot AS \cdot \cos 45^\circ = R^2+b^2-Rb\sqrt{2}.$$

(2) Now we express b through a from right triangles AHS and AHD (SH is the height of the straight line):

$$b=AS=AH\sqrt{2}=AD \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \sqrt{2}=a\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}},$$

Let's substitute this expression into (1) and (2) and equate the right-hand sides of these relations:

$$R^2+\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\right)^2a^2=R^2+\frac{2}{3}a^2-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}Ra,$$

We have:

$$\overrightarrow{BA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BO}=\overrightarrow{BA} \cdot (\overrightarrow{BA}+\overrightarrow{AO})=\overrightarrow{BA}^2(\angle BAO=90^\circ).$$

Likewise,

$$\overrightarrow{BS} \cdot \overrightarrow{BO}=(\overrightarrow{BH}+\overrightarrow{HS}) \cdot (\overrightarrow{BA}+\overrightarrow{AO})=\overrightarrow{BH} \cdot \overrightarrow{BA}+\overrightarrow{HS} \cdot \overrightarrow{AO}=\overrightarrow{BH} \cdot \overrightarrow{BA} \cdot \cos 30^\circ+HS \cdot AO.$$

Substituting these expressions into (3) and obtaining that $\angle HBS=45^\circ$, we obtain the answer.
