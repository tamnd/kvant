Usually in packages it is required that the material “last” for a few minutes, and pseudo-alloy soppa can fully cope with this resource. Various parts options are available to increase their service life. For example, you can imagine a reservoir of molten copper connected to a pseudo-alloy - a replenishment of copper will come from it, compensating for its evaporation from the material. These are, as a rule, technological details that are very important, but have nothing to do with the fundamental solution of the problem.

In fact, more complex processes occur in pseudo-alloys than those that I have outlined. The material is heated unevenly throughout its thickness, and large thermal stresses may arise in it, which can sometimes cause cracks to appear. The intensity of evaporation will depend on the size and structure of the pores, the chemical purity of the components and a number of other factors. But this is also particular. It is important that we found a fundamental way to solve the problem. We forced the material to work in an environment whose temperature is higher than its melting point.

It turns out that there is also an impossible impact on the new impact of our fountains.

As you know, a body thrown at an angle to the horizontal flies in a parabola. Therefore, the jets of ordinary fountains, city and palace, also form parabolas. However, in our case this is clearly not the case - the trajectories along which water drops move are more strongly curved than parabolas in the direction of the vertical axis of the system. Moreover, it is possible to select the experimental conditions in such a way that in some places the trajectories become vertical, and then seem to be pressed against the axis of the system.

This can be explained by taking into account the phenomenon of surface tension in a liquid. If we make a mental horizontal section of our fountain (in close proximity to the spoon), then the flow of water will look like a ring. This ring appears to be sandwiched between two surface films, which act on individual sections of the ring with forces directed towards the center. It is these forces that lead to a more significant curvature of the trajectories of part of the water than during their free movement.

The question naturally arises: does surface tension have an effect in the case of ordinary fountains? It turns out, yes, only much less noticeable. But try to explain it yourself why this is so.
