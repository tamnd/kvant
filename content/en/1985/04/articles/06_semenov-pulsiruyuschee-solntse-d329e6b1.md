Pulsating Sun

“A solar circle, a small circle,” everyone knows what the Sun is and can depict it. How does the Sun work?

“I made a ball of cotton wool, soaked it in alcohol and clamped it. It didn’t burn well, huh?” - this is how the “great and terrible” wizard Goodwin modeled the Sun from A. Volkov’s book “The Wizard of the Emerald City”.
The idea is not bad, but such a sun, of course, would go out in a few minutes.

Today physicists know that the real Sun “burns” with thermonuclear fuel. Hydrogen nuclei (proTONs) combine with neutrons to form gel nuclei, and colossal energy is released. But this is a general principle, and many details of the process still cannot be established. Moreover, there are many contradictions in the science of our lightILE.

It is known, for example, that thermonuclear reactions produce neutrinos - the lightest and most elusive particles from the wide family of elementary particles. Despite the fantastic elusiveness of neutrinos, physicists have learned to register them, and having learned, they decided to find out how many of them fly from the Sun. It turned out that the recorded neutrino flux from the Sun is three times less than it should be if calculated according to the hypothesis of thermonuclear reactions. What's the matter?

There are other questions. However, one of them is associated with a change in the light flux sent by the Sun to the Earth; physicists believe that during the existence of our planet, the radiation power of the Sun should have increased by at least 20-30 percent, but geologist and paleoclimatology (climate specialists of past eras) believe that this did not happen.

Everyone agreed on one thing: close attention to the Sun and its careful study were necessary.

They began to look up and, as happens in science, along with the answers to the questions posed, new unexpected contradictions were discovered. It all started with the fact that the American physicists R. Dicke and G. Goldenberg decided to check whether the Sun is really a ball. From the results of their measurements it followed that the Sun is flattened. This “find” did not fit into any theories at all, and they began to recheck it in different countries.

Thorough research in this direction began about ten years ago at the Crimean Astrophysical Observatory of the USSR Academy of Sciences. Soviet astrophysicists under the leadership of Academician A. B. Severny also discovered a previously unknown phenomenon - fluctuations in the diameter of the Sun. It turns out that the Sun periodically expands and contracts, that is, it pulsates. The frequency of the “solar pulse” is once every 160 minutes, and the amplitude of the pulsation is towards the center of the outer layers. Such a “subtok” can lead to the emergence of a spherical wave from the center to the surface of the Sun. True, the number of calculations based on such a model gives a pulsation period of 120 minutes, but as a first consideration this wave is satisfactory.

This is how close attention to the Sun did not answer the questions facing scientists, but led to the discovery of a whole new region of “solar gestation” - helioseistolgin. Now, by studying the pulsations of the surface of the Sun, we can hope for new information about the structure of its internal layers.

A. V. Semenov
