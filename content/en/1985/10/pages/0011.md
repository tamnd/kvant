⟦folio 9⟧

The mental idea of ​​a computing machine was the Englishman Charles Babage (1791-1871).

A prominent mathematician, a talented engineer, a versatile inventor, a famous natural scientist, an inquisitive researcher, C. Babage, with a wide variety of interests and accomplished activities, spent his whole life tirelessly and persistently working on the development and design of computers.

In 1822, he built a model of a machine, which he called a “difference machine,” and began to create a large difference machine designed for calculating scientific and astronomical tables. However, this work was never completed for various reasons.

In 1834, C. Babidge began work on his main product - a computer, which he called “analytical” and which was supposed to automatically solve all the computational problems faced by engineers and mathematicians. The grandiose project of such a machine, developed by the author with amazing detail, was so ahead of its time that its practical implementation could not but turn out to be unrealistic.

What exactly is the step forward taken by Ch. Babidge in the development of computer technology? First of all, Babage's machines, both analytical and difference, are designed not to perform each arithmetic operation according to individual human commands, received each time after the completion of the previous operation, but to carry out the entire computational exchange as a whole without human intervention at intermediate stages. Moreover, if the difference engine was focused on one fully defined, unchanging program of actions with given numbers, then the analytical engine allowed the implementation of different calculation programs depending on the problem under consideration. In modern language, a difference engine is a specialized computer with a fixed program, and an analytical engine is a universal automatic calculation

Charles Babidge is the author of the project for the first universal software computer.

It should be noted that Babage's analytical engine is a single set of specialized units: a device for entering initial information (initial data), a storage device (memo), an arithmetic device (performing operations on numbers), a control device (ensuring the execution of operations in the required sequence), a device for entering processed information (result). In other words, this machine, a precursor to the architecture of modern computers, had virtually all of their current basic structural components.

However, he is a child of his era! - the Analytical Engine, like all the calculating devices that preceded it, was (and could not help but be) a purely mechanical calculator. After all, centuries of research in the field of electricity have passed in the first half; work on its use has only just begun.
