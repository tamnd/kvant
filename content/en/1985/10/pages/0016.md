Another of the well-known types of radioactive transformations of heavy nuclei found in nature (alpha and beta decays, spontaneous fission of nuclei, etc.), a new type has been added - the decay with radiation of a carbon isotope nucleus heavier than the gel nucleus (alpha particle).

More recently, this year, employees of the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (Dubna), as well as a group of American physicists, discovered another type of decay, with radiation from an even heavier nucleus - the neon nucleus.

For example, the uranium isotope with mass number 233 decays:

$$\begin{align*}
233U &\rightarrow 209Pb + 24Ne \\
233U &\rightarrow 208Pb + 25Ne
\end{align*}$$

— the decay occurs with the emission of a neon isotope with a mass number of 24 or 25. It is not yet clear what kind of decay process it is.

The probability of such a decay is very low: approximately every 10^{12} emitted alpha particles arrive at a neon nucleus. If there were no alpha decay of uranium, the uranium nucleus would live on average about 10^{17} years, waiting for its neon decay (the half-life of the uranium-233 isotope with alpha particle radiation is 1.62×10^5 years).

Another isotope of uranium also decays - with a mass number of 232:

$$\begin{align*}
232U &\rightarrow 208Pb + 24Ne \\
232U &\rightarrow 208Pb + 25Ne
\end{align*}$$

The probability of a neon nucleus escaping in this case is also approximately 10^{12} times less than the probability of an alpha particle escaping.

The same decay of another nucleus was discovered - the protactinium nucleus:

$$\begin{align*}
231Pa &\rightarrow 207Tl + 24Ne \\
231Pa &\rightarrow 207Tl + 24Ne
\end{align*}$$

The resulting thalium-207 is converted to lead-207 (the average lifetime of thalium-207 is 6.8 minutes) with the emission of an electron (beta decay). The probability of neon decay of protactinium is approximately 10^{11} times less than the probability of its alpha decay.

All new decays discovered, as we see, end in lead isotopes, which also end all natural radioactive series - the families of uranium, thorium and actinium. Nature has found another - a workaround - way to convert various elements into stable lead.
