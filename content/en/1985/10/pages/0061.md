15. $x = c$, where $c$ is the lesser of $a$ and $b$. Solution. Let us denote $a - x$ by $y^4$, $b - x$ by $z^4$, then $a + b - 2x = y^4 + z^4$. After this, the equation is written as $y + z = \sqrt[4]{y^4 + z^4}$. Let's raise both sides of this equation to the fourth power; after transformation we obtain the equation $yz(2y^2 + 3yz + 2z^2) = 0$, from which either $z = 0$, or $y = 0$, or $2y^2 + 3yz + 2z^2 = 0$. With $z \neq 0$, the equation $2y^2 + 3yz + 2z^2 = 0$ can be written as $2\left(\frac{y}{z}\right)^2 + 3\left(\frac{y}{z}\right) + 2 = 0$. The resulting quadratic equation (with respect to the variable $\frac{y}{z}$) has no solutions. So, either $y = 0$, from which $x = a$, or $z = 0$, from which $x = b$: Checking shows that only the lesser of the two values ​​obtained is suitable.
