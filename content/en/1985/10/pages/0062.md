5. Let us show that $x_n < 100m$ for all $n$. (Since the numbers $x_n$ are natural, it follows that among them there are identical ones.) We carry out the proof by induction on $n$. At $n=1$ $x_1=1 < 100m$. Let $x_n < 100m$, and let $10^p \leq mx_n < 10^{p+1}$, that is, the number $mx_n$ is written with the $(p+1)$th digit. Then the sum $x_{n+1}$ of its digits does not exceed

$$9(p+1) < 10(p+1) \leq 10 \cdot 10^{p/2} \leq 10 \sqrt{mx_n} < 10 \sqrt{m \cdot 100m} = 100m,$$

since, obviously, $p+1 \leq 10^{p/2}$ at $p=0, 1, 2, \dots$
