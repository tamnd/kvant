Inequalities for the area and perimeter of a convex polygon

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. V. GASHKOV

Perhaps the reader knows that among all the figures of a given perimeter, the circle has the largest area.

If not, we advise you to read the article by V.V. Trofimov “Princess Dido, isopermeters and soap films” in the fifth issue of the magazine for this year.

Here we will introduce the reader to similar extremal properties of regular $n$-gons, distinguishing them from all other $n$-gons, and as consequences we will obtain several interesting inequalities relating the area and perimeter of the $n$-gon, the radius of the “inscribed” and “circumscribed” circles and its other characteristics. To accurately formulate and prove these statements, we will have to define several auxiliary concepts, and for triangles the mentioned statements can be formulated already now.

Administration
1. Among the triangles inscribed in a given circle, the regular triangle has the most area (and perimeter).
2. Among the triangles circumscribed about a given circle, the one with the most area (and perimeter) is the regular one.
3. Among the triangles of a given perimeter, the one with the most area is the regular one.
4. Among triangles with a given minimum height, the most area (and perimeter) is regular.
5. Among the triangles with a given maximum side, the most area (and perimeter) is regular.

Further analysis will prove statements 1-5 for arbitrary convex $n$-gons.

What is a convex polygon?

Definition 1. A polygon $P$ is called convex if it lies on one side of each straight line passing through any two of its adjacent vertices. Such lines are called tangents to the polygon $P$, so a tangent is a line containing some of its sides. Further, only convex polygons are considered, and the appearance of new polygons is specially discussed.

7. Prove that a line that has common points with a convex polygon either cuts it into two polygons or passes through its vertex so that it lies entirely in one of the half-planes defined by the line in question.

Definition 2. A line passing through the vertex of a convex polygon so that it lies entirely on one side of this line is called a support line to this polygon. Every tangent is a support, but not every support is a tangent (see Fig. 1).

Blending and symmetrizing polygons

The concepts presented below were introduced into geometry by G. Minkowski, who used them in his famous studies on convex sets.

Definition 6. A mixture (half-sum) of two exact sets is the set of midpoints of all segments whose ends belong to this set (one end to the first set, and the other to the second). (At figure 8 the mixture of segments AB and CD is shown.)

Exercise 19. a) A mixture of parallel lines is a straight line that bisects the stripes between them.

6) The mixture of segments lying on parallel lines is the middle line of the trapezoid (parallelograph) formed by these segments.
