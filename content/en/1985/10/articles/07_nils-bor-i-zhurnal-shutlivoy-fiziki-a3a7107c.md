Niels Bohr and the Journal of Playful Physics

In 1935, on the occasion of Niels Bohr’s 50th birthday, the staff of the head of the Institute of Theoretical Physics published the first volume of the guide collection “The Journal of Jocular Physics”. The preface to the collection stated:

“The great event that we were preparing to celebrate was an excellent occasion to finally take that step that had already been prepared by the rapid development of our science and in which the need to organize and legalize a rather old field of physics - The Jocular Physics - had long been overlooked. For reasons that have not yet been clarified, it has still eked out a simply miserable existence against the general background of the rapid development of science. As a first measure, we have undertaken the publication of this collection, which marks the birth of a new periodical, and it will be dedicated exclusively to the humorous side of the multifaceted life of the world of physics.”

The collection really became periodic and was published... once every 10 years (the second volume was published on the 60th anniversary of N. Bohr, the third on the 70th anniversary). A similar collection entitled “The Journal of Unclear Physics” was released for the 50th anniversary of the English physicist P. Peierlas. Here we place two materials from these collections.

N. Bohr and Westerns

Niels Bohr loved going to the cinema, and of all the genres he recognized only one - cowboy westerns. It is difficult to imagine what attracted Bohr to these paintings. “I can easily admit,” he said, “that the pretty heroine, fleeing for her life, may end up in danger.”
