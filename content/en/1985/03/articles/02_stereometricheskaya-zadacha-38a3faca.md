⟦folio none⟧

We continue in this issue to begin a series of drawings for stereometric problems, the purpose of which is to help the reader develop spatial imagination. When solving problems independently, you should also try to imagine pictures in your mind. (As a rule, accurate drawings can be constructed only by solving the problem at least partially; moreover, they are most often not needed directly for the solution.)
The conditions of the problem for this issue are given below, and you will find its solution inside the magazine under the heading “Our Cover”.

The lateral ribs of the regular triangular rectangle SABC are inclined to the plane of the base at an angle of 45°.
The ball touches the base plane ABC
at point A and, in addition, touches the extension of the edge BS beyond the vertex S. A plane is drawn through the center of the ball and the height BD of the base. Find the angle of inclination of this plane to the plane of the base.
