Latvian Open Olympids and Summer School "Alpha"

In the Latvian SSR, various forms of work with schoolchildren are practiced: a system of circles, both school and those working at the universities of the republic and stations of technical technicians, small universities, seminars conducted by the Latvian State University. P. Knocks for graduating students, free practice in laboratories NNN and VZO, daily week of science and technology for youth, Republican Olympids, Republican Open Olympids, summer school-seminars for the winners of Republican Olympids “Alpha” and others.

This article discusses open Olympids and the Alpha summer school.

The word “open” emphasizes the main difference between these Olympids and ordinary Republican ones. The latter take place in several stages (school, district or city, finance), the winners of the previous one advance to the next stage, and in finance there can be 100 schoolchildren. This is very little. As a result of the action, a number of school physics and mathematics directions or with in-depth study of radio electronics and programming fundamentals, in which training is conducted with increased requirements in physics and mathematics. In these schools there are many well-prepared children who are qualified to study at the Physics Division of the Republican Olympiad. However, many of them remained in an observing role, since the total number of students and LIMITS of representation from districts and schools imposed their own limitations. The student could remain “beyond the line” and look like a servant’s “creative cheese” or even during one of the preliminary stages.

How are the Olympids different from ordinary ones?

Firstly, there are no restrictions on representation - everyone can participate by listening in advance by letter or by general opening an application (it is only necessary so that it is possible to better plan the placement of different groups of participants in the audience).

The second feature of open olympids is that they are single-stage, that is, they do not have representative selection stages and are held on one day.

This opens the way for many guys who did not get into the financial round of traditional Olympids, mainly due to quantitative restrictions, to achieve their achievements at the “highest level”.

Mathematicians have been holding open Olympids since 1972, new ones are attended by more than 1000 schoolchildren in grades 4-10 (11). The first opening of the Olympiad in Physics was in 1976, this year the tenth will be used. Pupils from 8-10th grades from Russian-language schools and 9-11th grades from Latvian-language schools, as well as vocational and technical school students, about 400 people in total, come to the Olympiad.

Open Olympics are held every day on Sunday in April. They start at 10:30 a.m. and 4.5 hours of “clean” time are allotted to complete the work. A lot of work was done and time was given to the chosen ones in such a way that the guys could arrive at the Olympics with practically all the resources and have time to return home in the evening.

At open Olympiads in physics, 6 problems are usually offered, both analytical and qualitative.

Experimental tasks have some features compared to traditional Olympids. Due to the large number of participants, it is impossible to prepare individually to perform experimental tasks. Therefore, the Olympids demonstrate some experience or phenomenon to participants and require a description and explanation of it. As a rule, Olympiad participants are given the opportunity to repeat the experiment.

Olympic problems are selected so that, to the extent possible, they reflect the traditional areas of physics for the republic.

At the end of the work, a task analysis is carried out, during which Olympiad students are introduced to the correct solutions. Students from schools separate from Riga who do not have the opportunity to stay for analysis, as well as teachers and everyone else can get acquainted with the problems of open olympids and their solutions in the collection of articles of the Astrophysical Observatory of the Academy of Sciences of the Latvian SSR “Starry Sky”.

Repeatedly representing the organizing committee and journal of discoveries of the Olympiad in Physics is Corresponding Member of the Academy of Sciences of the Latvian SSR, Professor K. K. Schwartz, the journal includes employees of the Latvian State University, the Institute of Physics and other research institutes and resource specialists. Jorni is determined by the winner, who receives personal invitations to the Loading Day. The day ends with experimental inspections and valuable mementos.
