Return of the FIGURES
In general chess problems it is assumed that the first move is the only one. However, there is a so-called return theme in the composition, where this rule can be broken as many times as possible, and the more opening moves, the better! Today we will get acquainted with some recommendations of this kind.

A. Ellerman, D. Oudot.
Checkmate in 2 moves (9 solutions).

This task is the founder of the topic. Any overlap of the a1 - h8 diagonal with rooks, knights and the king leads to the goals. In response, the black king takes the b2 pawn, and the white piece returns to its place, declaring checkmate. It is curious that there is a complete set of white pieces and pawns on the board, and removing any of them will violate the idea - the need to return some pieces to their place will no longer be necessary.

E. Enkin. Checkmate in 2 moves (41 solutions).
Before us is “the whole royal army”! Forty queen moves to squares b4, b5, b6, b7, e4, f5, g6, as well as the Cg8 maneuver! solve the matter. The black king takes the b2 or c2 pawn, and the return of the white piece to its place leads to checkmate.

For orthodox problems that do not require a superset of chess pieces, the best production is this.

B. Sidorov. Checkmate in 2 moves (21 solutions).
Fifteen retreats by the queen or six by the bishop and then returning them to their place leads to checkmate of the black king. If in the first problem White was represented by a full set of pieces, then here is another remarkable touch - the idea was implemented in the mitator genre - there are only seven pieces on the board!

The record, in fact, should be sought in “fairytale chess.”

A. Shuryakov, M. Gorislavsky. Checkmate in 2 moves (70 solutions).
If in Enkin's problem all the king's army are present, then now there is also a whole herd of knights on the board... The last necessary diagram is also distinguished by the external symmetry of the relative diagonal a8 - h1 (several small violations do not count). After any of the moves of white queens, rooks, bishops and knights and their return to their place, checkmate is given either along this diagonal or along one of the extreme lines.

In today's “chess page” there were too many white kings, and therefore, at the end of one problem, we present a joke in which the white king is not on the board at all, as well as the rest of the white pieces. But unlike the very first problem, this time the army of black pieces is fully represented.

F. Bondarenko. White starts and does nothing.

Amazing case! Despite the fact that Black has all the pieces, and White does not even have a leader yet, they manage to escape. It turns out that at this moment the king is completing his move, which is why he is not visible. What kind of saving move is this?

1. Kb2:a1! Here's the thing. The king stood on b2, and the pawn hastily checked him. Now Black, although he has the largest material advantage possible in chess, is unable to prevent a stalemate.
