However, the actual motion of the ball is not simply moving from wall to wall through the center. It moves, pressing against the wall of the drain hole, while the point of contact A moves along the circumference of the cross-section of the drain hole, and the center of the ball O describes a small circle shown on figure 5 of the dash line. It is easy to understand that the angular speed of rotation of the ball is $\Omega = \omega \frac{R_{\text{отв}} - R}{R_{\text{отв}}}$, that is, it is approximately ten times less than $\omega$. It is easy to change using a stopwatch, counting the number of revolutions per second of the marks on the ball (about 2 - 3 revolutions per second).

Finally, the following question arises: does the ball rotate clockwise or counterclockwise? Before answering this, note that the central position of the ball in the drain hole is stable. To make sure of this, close the tap and press the ball on top with your finger. Then carefully release your finger - the ball remains motionless. This means that the specific situation depends on the background, and in principle three cases are possible: if the ball was stationary, it remains stationary; if the initial current moved the touch point A clockwise, then the ball will rotate counterclockwise, and vice versa.
