F907. Two metal balls of mass m each with radii r and 2r are placed in an electric field, for example, the magnitude of which is directed from the large ball to the smaller one and is equal to E. The distance between the centers of the balls is $R_0=4r$. The large ball carries charge q ($k \frac{q}{r^2} \ll E$), the small ball is not charged. The balls are released. The time between the first and second collisions of the balls is equal to m. Find the time between the nth and (n-1)th collisions and the distances the balls will travel between these collisions. What is the average acceleration of the balls over a sufficiently long period of time? The collisions are considered to be absolutely elastic.

When the balls are released, the small ball remains motionless (we do not take into account the effect of gravity). The large ball begins to move with acceleration $a=qE/m$; after time $t=\sqrt{2(R_0-3r)/a}=\sqrt{2r/a}$ he will approach the smaller ball, having speed $u=at=\sqrt{2ra}$. As a result of the collision, the balls will exchange velocities: the large ball immediately after the collision will have a speed of 0, and the small one will have a speed of $u=\sqrt{2ra}$. Since the balls are metal, upon collision the charge $q$ will be distributed over both balls. Let $aq$ be the sight of the large ball after the collision ($\alpha<1$); then the target of the smaller ball will be $(1-\alpha)q$. It is clear that during subsequent collisions the charges of the balls will not change. The accelerations at which the balls will move between collisions are $aqE/m=\alpha a$ (larger ball) and $(1-\alpha)qE/m=(1-\alpha)a$ (smaller ball). With each collision the balls will exchange velocities.

The second collision will occur a time t after the first. From the condition of equality, the path traveled by the balls between the first and second collisions is

$$\frac{a a}{2} \tau^2=\sqrt{2ra} \tau+\frac{(1-\alpha)a}{2} \tau^2,$$

— find the value $a a$:

$$a a=\frac{1}{2} a+\frac{\sqrt{2ra}}{\tau}.$$

Before the second collision, the larger ball has a speed of $a a\tau=\frac{1}{2} a\tau+\sqrt{2ra}$, and the smaller ball has a speed of $\sqrt{2ra}+(1-\alpha)a\tau=\frac{a\tau}{2}$.

To determine the time between the second and third collisions, we will move to a coordinate system moving at speed $a\tau/2$ (the speed of the large ball immediately after the second collision). In this system, at the first instant of time, the larger ball has a speed of 0, and the smaller one has a speed of $\sqrt{2ra}$. But this means that the time between the second and third collisions is also equal to m.

Reasoning in a similar way, we come to the conclusion that the time between all successively collided ones is equal to i.e.

From the calculations performed, it is clear that the speed of the large ball after the $n$-th collision is equal to $\frac{a \tau}{2}(n-1)$. Taking this into account, we find the path traversed by the balls between the $n$th and $(n+1)$th collisions:

$$S=\frac{a \tau}{2}(n-1)\tau+\frac{a a}{2} \tau^2=\frac{a \tau^2}{2}\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{2ra} \tau}{2}.$$

The average acceleration with which the system will move (the center of mass of the balls) is equal to

$$a_{\mu,\nu}=\frac{qF}{2m}.$$
