$$F_2 = F_x,$$

where $F_2$ is the force of pressure on the right hemisphere from the side of the liquid to the left hemisphere, $F_x$ is the force that must be applied to the right hemisphere so that the liquid does not spill out.

Let's find the value of $F_2$. The fluid pressure inside the sphere increases linearly as the depth increases from 0 to $2\varrho g R$. Hence,

$$F_2 = \sum_i p_i s_i = p_{\text{cp}} S = \varrho g R \pi R^2 = \varrho g \pi R^3.$$

(here $p_i$ is the pressure on the section of area $s_i$ lying in the central upper section).

Thus, the minimum force that must be applied to the right hemisphere so that the liquid does not spill out is equal to

$$F_x = \varrho g \pi R^3.$$

F905. A thin-walled gas-filled cylinder of mass $M$ with height $H$ and base area $S$ floats in water (Fig. 1). As a result of the loss of thermal properties in the lower part of the cylinder, its penetration depth increases by $\Delta H$. What was the initial gas pressure in the cylinder? Atomic pressure $p$, temperature does not change.

Fig. 1.

Let the cylinder initially be immersed in water to a depth of $h$ (see Fig. 1). Then

$$Mg = \varrho_b g h S \Rightarrow h = \frac{M}{\varrho_b S}.$$

It is not difficult to show that if, as a result of loss of thermal properties, the cylinder dropped to a further depth $\Delta H$, then this means that the height $\Delta x$ of the water level that flowed into the cylinder is exactly equal to $\Delta H$. Indeed, in the new equilibrium position (Fig. 2)

$$Mg + \varrho_b g S \cdot \Delta x = \varrho_b g S(h + \Delta H) = \varrho_b S h.$$

Let us find the pressure $p_1$ of the gas in a cylinder partially filled with water. The sum of pressure $p_1$ and the pressure of a column of water of height $\Delta H$ at up to the cylinder must be equal to the pressure in water at depth $h + \Delta H$, that is

$$p_1 + \varrho_b g \cdot \Delta H = p + \varrho_b g (h + \Delta H).$$

From here

$$p_1 = p + \varrho_b g h = p + \frac{Mg}{S}.$$

So, the gas occupies a volume $V_1 = S(H - \Delta H)$ and the gas pressure is $p_1$. According to the Boyle-Mariotte law

$$p_1 V_1 = p_0 V_0,$$

where $p_0$ is the initial gas pressure when it occupies volume $V_0 = SH$. Thus,

$$\left(p + \frac{Mg}{S}\right) S (H - \Delta H) = p_0 SH,$$

where

$$p_0 = \left(p + \frac{Mg}{S}\right) \left(1 - \frac{\Delta H}{H}\right).$$

O. Yu. Nikshina
