Let us draw a section of the cylinder perpendicular to its axis and passing through the point $B$. Let $C$ be the top point of this section (see Fig. 1). When the top four cylinder is “turned”, points $A$, $B$ and $C$ turn out to lie at the vertices of a right triangle with right angle $BCA$ (Fig. 2). The angle $ABC$ is equal to $\pi-\pi/2-\beta=\alpha$, and $|BA|=|BC|/\cos \alpha$. But $|BC|=\pi R/2$, so

$$|BA|=\frac{\pi R}{2\cos \alpha}.$$

Therefore, for the freely hanging part of the rope is equal to

$$|BD|=l-\frac{\pi R}{2\cos \alpha}.$$

After the load is given a small speed in a vertical plane parallel to the cylinder's axes, it will remain in this vertical plane throughout its entire movement. Since the rope is always tense, in any intermediate position the hanging part $(B'D)$ will form a straight line, and the entire rope will lie in the plane passing through the point $A$. The role of curve $AB'$ will be the same as if point $A$ were on the vertical above point $B$ at a distance $\pi R/2$ cos $\alpha$ above it.

From here, for the period of small oscillations of the load, we obtain

$$T=2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}.$$

1) Let us examine the equilibrium condition of the lower hemisphere filled with liquid. This system is acted upon by gravity

$$F=mg+\varrho g \cdot \frac{2}{3}\pi R^3,$$

force $F_1$ pressure from the liquid from the upper hemisphere; the vertical force $F_y$, which must be applied to the hemisphere so that the liquid does not spill out, is determined by the equilibrium condition:

$$F_y=F+F_1.$$

According to Pascal's law, the pressure in the fluid at the center of the sphere is equal to

$$p_1=\varrho g R,$$

and therefore

$$F_1=p_1\pi R^2=\varrho g \pi R^3.$$

Thus,

$$F_y=mg+\frac{2}{3}\varrho g \pi R^3+\varrho g \pi R^3=g\left(m+\frac{5}{3}\varrho \pi R^3\right).$$
