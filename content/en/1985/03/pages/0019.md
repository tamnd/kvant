Let us now consider the central flow around the ball when the jet falls exactly on its top (Fig. 3). In this case, the water flows around the ball symmetrically, and therefore $F=0$. For a central flow it is not difficult to find the layer thickness $d$. From the condition for constructing the flow it follows that

$$d \sim \frac{r_0^2}{R}$$

(show it yourself).

Finally, for the intermediate case, when the displacement $x$ of the ball from the symmetry axis of the jet is insignificant compared to its radius $R$ (Fig. 4), it turns out that the force $F$ linearly depends on $x$ and is directed so as to return the ball to the equilibrium position:

$$F \sim -\varrho v_0^2 d \cdot x \sim \frac{\varrho v_0^2 r_0^2}{R} x.$$

(Unfortunately, the exact value of the force $F$ for this case is difficult to obtain.)

Under the influence of this force the ball will oscillate. The expression for the frequency of oscillations can be written by analogy with the oscillations of a weight outward:

$$F_{\pi p} = -kx \Rightarrow \omega_{\pi p} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}},$$

$$F_{\pi} \sim -\frac{\varrho v_0^2}{R} x \Rightarrow \omega_{\pi} \sim v_0 r_0 \sqrt{\frac{\varrho}{RM}}.$$

With a ball mass $M \approx 3$ g we obtain $\omega_{\pi} \sim 40 \text{ c}^{-1}$, and $v_{\pi} \sim 6 \text{ Гц}$.
