temperature according to the law $R=R_0(1-\mu t)$, where $R_0$ is the resistance at $t=0^\circ C$, $\mu>0$. Determine the steady-state temperature of the conductor if the ambient temperature is $0^\circ C$, and the thermal power released by the conductor into the environment is $W=b \cdot \Delta t$, where $\Delta t$ is the temperature difference between the conductor and the environment. Changes in the size of the conductor due to thermal expansion are prevented.

The conductor will be in thermal equilibrium if this power is equal to the thermal power $W_-=bt$ released by the conductor into the environment. From condition $W_+=W_-$ we find at what temperatures thermal equilibrium of the conductor is possible:

$$\frac{U^2}{R_0(1-\mu t)}=bt,$$

where

$$t=\frac{1}{2\mu}\pm \sqrt{\frac{1}{4\mu^2}-\frac{U^2}{R_0b\mu}}=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(1\pm \sqrt{1-U^2\frac{4\mu}{R_0b}}\right).$$

Let us rewrite the radical expression in the form

$$1-(U/U_0)^2,$$ where $U_0=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{R_0b}{\mu}}$:

$$t=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(1\pm \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{U}{U_0}\right)^2}\right)$$

(the physical meaning of the parameter $U_0$, which has the dimension of stress, becomes after analyzing the solution to equation (*)).

Let's consider three possible cases:

a) $U<U_0$; equation (*) has two solutions:

$$t_1=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{U}{U_0}\right)^2}\right),$$

$$t_2=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{U}{U_0}\right)^2}\right).$$

b) $U=U_0$; solution to equation (*) -

$$t_0=\frac{1}{2\mu}.$$

c) $U>U_0$; equation (*) has no real roots. This means that under condition $U>U_0$ thermal equilibrium is impossible.

To better understand the physical meaning of the obtained mathematical expressions, let us plot the functions $W_+(t)$ and $W_-(t)$ for the case $a)$, $b)$ and $c$).

In the case of $a)$, the graphs of these functions have two common points corresponding to the state of thermal equilibrium - at $t=t_1$ and at $t=t_2$. However, at $t=t_2$ the equilibrium is unstable: if $t_1<t<t_2$, then $W_->W_+$—the wire cools; if $t>t_2$, then $W_+>W_-$ is the heating wire (in principle, up to the melting point). Stable thermal equilibrium is established at a temperature of $t=t_1$.

In the case of $b)$, the graphs $W_+(t)$ and $W_-(t)$ have one common point - at $t=t_0$. But this equilibrium state is also unstable: with increasing temperature, $(t>t_0)$ $W_+(t)$ grows faster than $W_-(t)$, the heating wire.

In the case of $c)$ the graphs do not intersect; in case of $U>U_0$ thermal equilibrium is impossible.

So, thermal equilibrium of the wire is possible at $U<\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{R_0b}{\mu}}$; set temperature $t=\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(1-\sqrt{1-U^2\frac{4\mu}{R_0b}}\right)$.
