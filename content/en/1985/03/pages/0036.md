Let us show that $S_4=S_0/2$ ($S_0$ is the area of ​​the cube face). Obviously, $S_{ABK}=S_{ABB_1}/2$, where $B_1$ is a point symmetrical to $B$ relative to $K$ (see Fig. 6), since triangles $ABK$ and $ABB_1$ have a common base $AB$, and the height of the first is less than the side of the second. Likewise, $S_{BVL}=S_{BVA}/2$, $S_{AUN}=S_{AUV}/2$. Therefore, $S_{ABK}+S_{BVL}+S_{AUN}=\frac{1}{2}(S_{ABB_1}+S_{BVA}+S_{AUV})=\frac{1}{2}S_{BVUB_1}=\frac{1}{4}S_0$ and, in the same way,

$$S_{CDM}+S_{CVL}+S_{DUN}=\frac{1}{4}S_0.$$

Adding these two equalities, we get that $S_4=S_0/2$.

From the solution it is clear that the equality $S_4=S_0/2$ is true for any points $A, B, C, D$ on the same edges, provided that the centers of the faces $K$ and $M$ are on opposite sides of the edge UV relative to the lines $AB$ and $CD$, respectively. If the point $A$ or $D$ coincides with $U$, and $B$ or $C$ with $V$, then $S_3=S_4$; in particular, the case when $A=U$, $B=V$, and $C$ and $D$ are the midpoints of the corresponding edges, can be obtained as the limit position of the section tangent to the inscribed sphere at $A \rightarrow U$, $B \rightarrow V$, so in this problem the estimate is also exact.

Try to prove on your own that the lateral surface of a truncated pyramid (or prism) $AUDBVC$ does not exceed $S_0$, and equality is achieved precisely in the case of recognition, that is, when the tangent plane $ABCD$ is parallel to the edge $UV$.

1) the length of the hanging part of the rope in the load equilibrium position;
2) the period of small oscillations of the load in a vertical plane parallel to the cylinder axis.

It is obvious that when the load is in the equilibrium position, the hanging part of the rope is vertical (at figure 1 this is section $BD$, where $B$ is the last point of contact between the rope and the cylinder) and, therefore, forms an angle $\beta=\pi/2-\alpha$ with the cylinder axis.

Let's look at the part of the rope that lies on the cylinder. Let us divide it into many small sections, each of which can be considered rectangular. Each of these involves the action of tension forces from two adjacent sections and reaction forces from the cylinder. Since there is no friction, the reaction force at each point of contact between the rope and the cylinder is perpendicular to the surface of the cylinder (that is, directed perpendicular to the corresponding generatrix, which passes through the axes of the cylinder). Since the rope is weightless, the tension forces acting on a separate section have equal projections on the direction of the generatrix in terms of abscission value. This means that all sections of the rope intersect the corresponding generators at the same angle equal to $\beta=\pi/2-\alpha$.
