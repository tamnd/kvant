4. If all lines are parallel to each other, then they divide the plane into $n+1$ regions. Therefore, the number of colored areas does not exceed $n \leqslant n(n+1)/3$.

Let now not all lines be parallel to each other. The boundary of each region consists of several segments and rays belonging to different straight lines. These segments and rays are called sides of the region. Each area has at least two sides. By $m_2$ we denote the number of colored areas that have two sides, by $m_3$ we denote the number of colored areas with three sides, etc. Finally, by $m_k$ we denote the number of colored areas with the maximum number of sides.

Let us show that $m_2 \leqslant n$. The border of any region with two sides consists of two rays, and each ray can lie on the border of only one of the colored regions. The number of all such rays does not exceed $2n$ (no more than two on each straight line). Therefore, the total number of side-painted areas with two sides does not exceed $2n$ or $m_2 \leqslant n$. Each of the $n$ straight lines is divided into no more than $n$ parts (segments and rays). Therefore, the total number of all parts does not exceed $n^2$. Each of the parts is the sides of no more than one of the painted areas. Consequently, the total number of sides of such areas does not exceed $n^2$, that is

$$2m_2+3m_3+\dots+km_k \leqslant n^2.$$

The number of colored areas is $m_2+m_3+\dots+m_k$. Using the inequalities proven above, we find that

$$m_2+m_3+\dots+m_k \leqslant \frac{m_2}{3} + \frac{2m_2+3m_3+\dots+km_k}{3} \leqslant
\frac{n}{3} + \frac{n^2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}(n^2+n).$$
