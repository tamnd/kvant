Having repeated the experience of the French physicist, osmosis was discovered for the first time, let’s try to understand this phenomenon.

Ostmosis and one-way diffusion

Let us remember what happens when a substance is dissolved in a solvent. The molecules of the substance penetrate into the solvent, and the molecules of the solvent penetrate into the area occupied by the solvent. This mutual diffusion (penetration) leads to equalization of the concentrations of the dissolved substance and solvent throughout the entire volume.

Now let’s imagine a situation in which a solution and a pure solvent are separated by a semi-permeable partition - it allows molecules of the solvent to pass through, but does not allow molecules of the dissolved substance to pass through. Obviously, in this case, equalization of concentrations can occur only due to one-way diffusion of the solvent.

This is exactly what will happen in the experience we have delivered. Cellufane is “opaque” for sugar molecules, but “transparent” for water molecules, therefore, at the same height of liquid (the same pressure on the partition) in the jar and tubes, at the initial moment, more water molecules pass through the cellulophane upward than from top to bottom. As a result, the liquid level in the jar begins to fall into the water and penetrates into the solution under the influence of the forces of the so-called osmotic pressure. As soon as the hydrolysis pressure of the solution column balances the osmotic pressure, the process stops. The height of this column serves as a quantitative measure of osmotic pressure.

Semi-permeable partitions and “artificial cage”

After Nolle’s work, many scientists - botanists, chemists, physicists - began to conduct experiments and study the phenomenon of osmosis. For semi-permeable partitions, they used a variety of natural materials: egg skin, bull and pork bladders, membranes, etc.
