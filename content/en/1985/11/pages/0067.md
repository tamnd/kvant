TASKS
ON A HEXAGONAL BOARD

Last time we told you how hexagonal chess is played and noted its geometric features. Let us pay attention to some more nuances that distinguish the use of chess from ordinary ones. One king on a hexagonal board is capable of trapping the enemy king if it is in the corner. For example, the white king f3 completely hides the black king f1. It follows from this that the king and any piece can checkmate without the help of other pieces. On the other hand, it is not easy to get the king into a corner, and therefore it is not noticeable even with two minor pieces; a third is needed. But the queen, without any support, can checkmate, say, from the same square f3 to the king f1.

Some of the proposed problems are taken from the collection of S. Sokolov and S. Tsygankov, big fans of hexagonal chess.

The hexagonal board offers unlimited possibilities for lovers of mathematical problems and puzzles. Well-known chess and mathematical problems about the placement of pieces, their strength, routes, etc. can also be posed for a hexagonal board. Try, for example, to solve the following problem: what is the most pure queens that can be placed on a hexagonal chessboard so that they are not opposite each other? Let us remember that on a common board, 8 queens (and no more) can be placed in the indicated manner, and there are 92 arrangements.

The minimum number of queens that can cover all free squares on the board is 5, and it has been proven that the number of setups is 4860, here is one of them: c6, d3, e5, f7, g4. The hexagonal board contains almost 30 more squares, but it can only be guarded by five queens! It is enough to place them on the squares c5, d3, g5, h8, k7.
