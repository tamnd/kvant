The final stage of the 19th All-Union Physical Olympiad for schoolchildren was held this year from April 17 to 24 in Kuibyshev.

On April 18, the grand opening of the Olympiad took place. The participants were brought by members of the Organizing Committee of the Olympiad, members of the jury, and the winners passed the Olympiad. On the same day, participants brought brooms to the monument to V.I. Lenin and to the Monument of Glory.

On the morning of April 19, a theoretical round took place. As usual, the Methodological Commission has prepared various options for students in grades 8, 9 and 10. The 8th grader of the state subject solved 4 problems (4 hours were allotted for their solution), and the 9th grader and 10th grader each had 5 problems (within 5 hours). These are the tasks (some of them were sent to Task «Kvant» in the 9th and 10th issues of the magazine for this year):

Theoretical tour assignment
8th grade

1. To fine-tune the rotational speed of the electrical conductor disk (33¹/₃ revolutions per minute), marks are placed on its side surface, which are illuminated with a neon lamp at a frequency of 100 flashes per second. How many marks should there be so that they appear stationary at rated speed?

After turning off the engine, the dry friction forces begin to slow down the disk, and the marks “run”. For the first 4 with mismo points observing the “mileage” of 10 marks. After what time should I leave the disc? After what period of time will the marks “run” in the other direction?

2. A decorative square curtain measuring 1.5 x 1.5 m hangs on a cornice along a vertical wall. The lower edge of the curtain is lifted up, so that the curtain is folded in half, and released. Find the time dependence of the forces acting on the cornice. The curtain, thin and smooth, weighs 3 kg.

3. To maintain a constant water temperature in a flow-through aquarium, it uses two identical spiral heaters (Fig. 1). In general mode, one of them is turned on, and if a second heater is connected in parallel, then the cold water consumption increases threefold. How should we change the flow of cold water if we turn on the heaters in series? What should the cold water flow rate be if one spiral of power P=100 W is turned on? Theoretical cold water t₁=10 °C, water temperature in the aquarium t₂=27 °C. The water is quickly mixed.

4. For LAB work, a powerful battery with a tocography resistor connected in series to it is used as a current source. First, a voltmeter and a milliammeter connected in series were connected to this source; their readings turned out to be equal to U₁=4.1 V, I₁=8 mA. Then these devices were connected in parallel and connected to the source (it’s good that we agreed on a resistor in the circuit!); their readings in this case were equal to U₂=1.1 V, I₂=22 mA. Using these data, calculate the resistance of the tocography resistor.

On April 19, all the guys took part in the All-Union Communist Cleanup Day.

An experimental tour took place on April 20. Each student was given 2 tasks, which were given 4 hours to complete.

The first problem for 8th grade was very similar to the famous problem about the crown of King Hiero (determining the presence of impurities in gold), only with more accessible materials - plasticine and iron (a piece of iron designated plasticine).

The second problem for eighth graders required them to determine the polarity of a battery. To do this, it was necessary to build similar galvanometers from angles, threads and a coil wound from wire (a battery with the indicated plasticine was also given).

In 9th grade, mechanical and electrical problems were also proposed. In the first task, it was necessary to determine the mass of a block of plasticine, use only threads, a weight of a given mass, a sewing machine with a foot, and graph paper. A possible (before the start of work, the jury believed that the only possible) solution is to place a block and a weight on the threads, carry out an inelastic impact between them and, based on the correspondence of the angles of deflection of the threads, estimate the mass ratio. Many did just that. Note that the jury usually tries to specify a set of equipment in such a way as to exclude “any” solution - the set of equipment for this task did not include scales or even a ruler (you can use it to make a lever). However, one of the participants developed shitiv and used his shitang as a lever, and the accuracy of the results was no worse than with the “official” method. If one cunning ninth-grader used a block of plasticine itself as a lever. Although the accuracy was somewhat worse, the jury still found this method to be completely reasonable.

In the second task for ninth graders, it was necessary to determine the polarity and magnitude of the emf of a battery using a known battery, two identical restats, a coil of wire, thread, stick, connecting wires and a sheet of paper. This task is a conditional version of the second task for 8th grade; it also required the use of a homemade galvanometer. In addition to choosing the optimal method, this task required a lot of skill and accuracy in measurements.

The first problem for grade 10 was related to electrostatics. Using a school electric meter, it was necessary to determine the capacitance of a capacitor made from a piece of foil dialectric - fiberglass. The electrometer could be charged using a zonite stick. If the set of instruments included a ruler. The idea of ​​the experiment was to first determine the neutron capacity of the electrometer itself, and then the unknown capacitance of the capacitor - by reducing the readings of the charged electrometer when a capacitor is connected to it. The capacitance of the electrometer could be found by charging it without the sphere's fields and then placing the sphere in place. The capacity of a sphere can be easily calculated by changing its radius. (By the way, measuring the radius of a sphere using a ruler is not such a simple experimental task.)

In the second task for tenth graders, it was necessary to determine the type of conductivity of an unknown transistor, find the choice of base, and then assemble a circuit of an amplifier stage and measure its success rate.

If in the theoretical type the errors of the participants were varied and “individual”, then in the experimental tasks several errors can be noted, typical of many. Let's list them.

1) The chosen measurement method is not justified (although usually only a few words are needed). For example, in an electronics problem for grade 10, it was enough to write that the capacitance of a capacitor can only be found by comparison with a known value (since there is nothing to measure charges and potentials with), and the internal body, the capacitance of which can be calculated in this experiment, is the sphere of the electromet.

2) From the description given, the measurement method is not always clear (this is very bad!). Of course, when there is a lack of time, it is not easy to perfectly format the work, but still part of the description of the perfect is necessary. Usually there are enough figure with short errors. It should be remembered that the easier it is to understand how the work was carried out, the better for the experimenter himself (if, of course, the experiment was done correctly).

3) Many studies do not even attempt to estimate the measurement error. The result of measurements, the error of which is unknown, has absolutely no value. Generally, it is enough to use any simple method for estimating instrument errors, and for indirect measurements, the boundary method. It is also important to be able to oblige whether it is necessary to conduct an experiment several times and obtain successful results. In this article it is not possible to write about measurement errors in any detail. They can read this, for example, in the excellent book by J. Taylor “Introduction to the Theory of Errors” (M.: Mir, 1985).

4) The data of direct measurements and measurement conditions are not always given in the work (often they are randomly scattered in the draft). This data must be entered into a table (you can use another convenient method if you come up with it).

The Olympiad participants not only solved problems, but also had an active rest. The cultural program of the Olympics was very rich - performances, a large concentration at the opening of the Olympics, a circus performance, excursions, a meeting with young artists from Kuibyshev, sports games, evenings at schools. The hosts of the Olympics did a lot to make the living rooms interesting. This program was also greatly diversified by students of the Moscow Institute of Physics - former participants (and winners) of the Olympiad who came to Kuibyshev. They hung out posters—some of them serveral, some of them humorous—answered the guys’ questions, and talked about student life at MIPT. (Having heard these stories, one of the authors of the article wanted to give up everything and go to Physics and Technology again. However, then he changed his mind.)

Before the closed Olympiad, a meeting of the participants with members of the Journal and members of the Editorial Collection of the magazine «Kvant» was arranged. The meeting was lively and interesting, the guys expressed their opinions and wishes regarding the magazine (one of them especially improved for us - to double the volume of the magazine!).

On April 23, the loading of the winners and the ceremonial closing of the Olympiad took place. In addition to the prize, the winner of the Olympiad was awarded many special recognitions - for the best work in the theoretical as well as in the experimental type, for the youngest and most remote (geographically) student and many others, among which there was even a prize for the best result on the appeal (this caused a kinship revival in the audience).

We wish great success to all participants of the XIX All-Union Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren.
