F963. Planes $P$ and $Q$ intersect at right angles and plane $P$ makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizon (Fig. 1). From some point $A$ of space onto the plane $P$ a small ball falls freely. A impacted ball with absolutely elastic planes. Under what conditions does the ball return to point $A$? Draw one of the possible return trajectories.

G. S. Lapidus

F964. Material points form a closed system. Between any two points of the system there is a gravitational force equal to $\vec{F}_{ij} = km_i m_j \vec{r}_{ij}$, where $m_i, m_j$ is the mass of the point, $|\vec{F}_{ij}|$ is the force acting on point $i$ from the point $j, |\vec{r}_{ij}|$ is the distance between points (Fig. 2), $k$ is a constant coefficient. At the initial moment, all particles are at rest. Prove that after some time all points will collide. Determine this time if the mass of the entire system $M = \sum_i m_i$ and the coefficient $k$ are known.

A. G. Samosvat

F965. A long cylindrical vessel is filled with an ideal gas to a pressure of $p_0$. First, the temperature of the cylinder is kept constant and equal to $T_0$. Then the temperature of one of the end walls of the vessel increases by $\Delta T$, and the temperature of the opposite wall remains the same. Find the steady pressure in the vessel and the position of the center of mass of the gas. Consider that $\Delta T \ll T_0$.

V. I. Komov

F966. To illustrate surface tension, one of the soap films, stretched on parallel “guides” and separated by a thread $AB$ (see Fig. 3), is pierced; At the same time, the thread $AB$ is stretched. Determine the tension of the thread. The distance between the “guides” is $d$, the length of the thread is $l$ ($l > \pi d/2$), the surface tension coefficient of the soap solution is $\sigma$.

L. G. Markovich

F967. A stack of very thin metal plates located at equal distances from each other is charged from a battery as follows. The negative terminal of the battery is connected to the rightmost plate, and the positive terminal is connected in turn to the leftmost plate, then to the second from the left, to the third, etc. until the second to last (second from the right; Fig. 4). Find the ratio of the charge on the rightmost plate to the charge on the third plate from the right. Assume that the thickness of the stack is much smaller than the linear dimensions of the plates.

E. P. Sokolov
