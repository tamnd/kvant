⟦folio 8⟧

When the American company IBM began producing its IBM 360 series of computers, its standard software package included the RANDU program, which generates a sequence of random numbers uniformly distributed on the interval [0; 1]. The company's engineers checked the program and, not finding a correlation for pairs of numbers, considered further testing unnecessary. This program was used for several years to perform Monte Carlo calculations before it was discovered that the sequence of numbers being seen had a strong correlation between triplets of numbers. It turned out that if the numbers are divided into triplets that define $x, y, z$ - the coordinates of a sequence of points, then instead of being distributed evenly over the unit cube, the points will be located in it on some surface. (Of course, if, when simulating the Ising problem, atoms are scattered over a three-dimensional lattice using such a sensor, then the distribution of atoms will be very different from random.) Therefore, those Monte-Carlo calculations, which were performed for several years on IBM 360 series machines, needed to be redone.

Curious coincidence

In the second edition of the book by the Italian scientist, Professor Orazio Zvelto, one of the pioneers of quantum electronics, “Principles of Lasers,” interesting material is placed before the author’s foreword. It begins with a quote from the “Natural History” of Pliny Elder, a Roman scientist and writer who lived in the first century AD (this is the only work of Pliny Elder who has survived to this day and is an encyclopedia of the natural scientific knowledge of antiquity; it also contains information on the history of art, history and life of Rome):

“The laser is one of the most studied gifts of nature, with many applications.”

The following is an explanation of the quote (we present it in a slightly abbreviated form):

“During the period of the Treco-Roman civilization (from the 6th century BC to the 2nd century AD), the laser was widely known and greatly glorified. This was actually the name of a plant that grew wild in a large area around Cyrene. [This is an ancient city in North Africa, founded around 630 BC. e. Greeks; one of the cultural and commercial centers of antiquity; currently belongs to Libya. Note ped.] They are used for a variety of illnesses - from colds to various epidemic diseases. It was used as a countermeasure in baptizing and baptizing them into computers. It turned out that only one of them contains an error.

Analytical transformations on a computer are successfully used in many physical problems.
