Rays and waves

At the beginning of the 19th century, thanks to work on the interference and diffraction of light, the idea that the propagation of light in space is a wave process was finally established. However, long before this, the so-called geometric optics arose, was formed and continues to exist today. This section of physics studies the laws of light propagation based on the concept of light rays. It talks about incident, reflected and refracted rays, diverging, converging and parallel rays of rays, etc.

The "ray" picture of light allows us to describe much of what happens when light hits mirrors, lenses, or lenses (Physics 10, Chapter 6). This refers to the path of rays, for example, in a lens, a lens, or some more complex optical system.

Does this mean that it is not light waves, but rays that pass through prisms and lenses, that it is not waves, but rays that are reflected from mirrors? What are light rays? What is their physical essence?

It turns out that there is no physical reality behind the rays. Light rays are lines along which a light wave propagates. All laws of geometric optics can be obtained from the wave of the new theory as the limiting case when the size of obstacles in the path of light is much greater than the length of the light wave. Thus, using the concept of a light ray is not at all necessary to understand what happens when light propagates. It just makes it easier to look at.

Let's see this with a few specific examples.
