Novosibirsk State University named after. Lenin Komsomol

Physics
Written exam
Faculty of Physics

Each varnat consisted of five problems of three types.

The first three tasks are computational, of varying difficulty: from almost standard to relatively complex, requiring ingenuity, deep knowledge, and the ability to understand a non-privileged or conventional physical situation.

The fourth task is an assessment task. To solve it, you need to understand the physical phenomenon under consideration, formulate a simple (since only an estimate is needed) physical model of this phenomenon, select the results - numerical values ​​of physical quantities and, finally, obtain a numerical result that more or less corresponds to reality. In the text of the problem I assume that the applicant can choose the physical quantities and their numerical values ​​necessary to solve the problem.

The fifth task is a demonstration task, in which you need to explain a physical phenomenon demonstrated in the classroom. Here it is important to understand the existing phenomena and highlight the main one among various factors.

Five hours were given to solve the problem, starting with the completion of the demonstration.

Option 1

1. The ACB wire is bent so that points A, C and B form a regular triangle (Fig. 1). A jumper EF made of wire with half the cross-sectional area is connected to the middle of sides AC and BC. A voltage U is applied to points A and B. Find the voltage drop across the jumper.

2. Two fixed cylinders, the cross-sectional areas of which are equal to S1 and S2, are connected by a tube (Fig. 2). The cylinders are covered with pistons and are rigidly connected to each other. The volume of gas limited by the pistons was initially equal to V, and the pressure inside the system was equal to the atmospheric pressure outside p0. The atmospheric pressure then changes to p1. How far will the piston move? The temperature is constant. Let's make a claim.

3. The wheel is equipped with a brake BC, which is hinged at point B on the same rigid frame as the axle of wheel A (Fig. 3). The stretched spring AC presses the brake pad C against the wheel rim so that the angle ACB is equal to a. When the wheel rotates clockwise, the friction force created by the brake pad is F1. The coefficient of friction between the pad and the rim is μ. Find the friction force when the wheel rotates counterclockwise.

4. Continue mentally a straight line passing along the plumb line (a hanging vertical thread with a weight). For medium-sized shipots, estimate at what distance from the center of the Earth this straight line will pass.

5. If a piston is inserted into a sealed cylinder, capable of sliding along the cylinder stacks without friction, and a load is attached to it, then the piston slides out of the cylinder relatively easily. If you drink water into the cylinder, the piston holds on despite the significant mass of the load. Explain the phenomenon.
