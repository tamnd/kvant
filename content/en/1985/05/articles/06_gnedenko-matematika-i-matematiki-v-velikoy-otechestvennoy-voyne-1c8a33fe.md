Mathematics and mathematicians in the Great Patriotic War

Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR V. V. GNEDENKO

Forty years have passed since the victory of the Soviet people in the Great Patriotic War. The country suffered countless victims in the name of independence, freedom and social ideals: millions of dead and wounded, suffering from hunger, thousands of destroyed cities and villages, hundreds of thousands driven away to fascist hard labor. Despite everything, the Soviet people survived and won.

To defend the Motherland

From the first days, warrior mathematicians took part in the defense of the country: they joined the army, enlisted in the people's militia, and went to the front as volunteers. In the most difficult days for the country, they showed themselves to be faithful sons of the Motherland, capable of self-sacrifice and ready to give their lives in the name of the freedom of the Fatherland. And indeed, many of those who went to the front did not return and did not start their favorite work. Among the dead there were many talented mathematicians who showed more hope and were able to contribute more to the progress of our knowledge.

At that time I worked at Moscow University, was an associate professor in the department of probability theory and scientific secretary of the Institute of Mathematics of Moscow State University. As soon as it became known about the attack of Nazi Germany on our Fatherland, rallies were held in factories and institutions, and a nationwide movement arose to enroll in the people's militia. At our Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, almost all undergraduate and graduate students and the overwhelming majority of hospital assistants, associate professors and professors, including those who were exempt from military service due to age and health, signed up to serve. Later, some militias were removed from the lists because they had professorships or doctoral degrees. I also fell into this category, since by that time I had defended my doctoral dissertation. As a result, I had to not defend the country with weapons in my hands, but to participate in the creation of these weapons, as well as in the development of methods for their rational use.

A large number of mathematicians, as we have already noted, were mobilized or went to the front as volunteers. They fought bravely and exercised their civic duty. There is no doubt that at the same time the country lost a huge number of talented young people who could become the pride of domestic science. We can judge this, firstly, by the fact that among those who returned after participating in the civil Great Patriotic War, a significant number were major scientists - professors, corresponding members and academicians of the All-Union and Republican Academies of Sciences. It is enough to name such names as academicians Yu. V. Linnik, Yu. A. Mitropolsky, G. G. Cherny, N. P. Erugiin, O. S. Parasok to be convinced of this. And secondly, each of the universities lost many scientists who had already managed to prove themselves and promised to prove a lot in the future, but did not return from the war. Thus, Moscow University lost talented young mathematicians G. M. Bavli, M. V. Bebutov, N. V. Vedenisov, V. N. Zasukhin and many, many others. They could have been the pride of our science, but the war of the pass crossed out the development of the scientific path that was so weak and frequented by them.

In the people's militia:
battle near Yelnya

In addition to teachers, graduate students and students who received mobilization extracts already in the first days of the war and ended up in regular military units, the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow University for 213 people in the 8th Krasnopresensk militia division. All of them were enrolled in the 975th artillery regiment of this division and, after a short training, already in August they occupied a defensive line in the Rzhev-Vyazma direction. Together with all our comrades, they created a defensive line: they dug trenches, anti-tank ditches, minefields, and installed guns. But at the front, not everything will happen as the defender assumes. At the beginning of October, the situation in the Yelninsky direction sharply worsened, as the Germans concentrated large fresh forces here and began an offensive operation. In this regard, the front commanders transferred the 8th Krasnopresensk division from the 32nd to the 24th artillery and promptly transferred it from prepared positions to the area of ​​​​the village of Uvarovo, located somewhat south of Yelnya. Unfortunately, there were no prepared positions there and we had to detain the enemy literally “on the march.” The division completed its combat mission: it detained the enemy for several days and thereby provided significant assistance to Moscow. According to the surviving information, this battle cost the occupiers dearly: they lost about 45 thousand soldiers and officers near Yelnya as a result of the staunch defense of Soviet soldiers. But we also paid a high price for this: about a third of the militia of the 8th division died in these battles. The middle ones were graduate students or those who had already completed their dissertations A. I. Gerchikov, M. E. Glerman, V. N. Zasukhin, I. R. Lepekhin, Kh. M. Milshtein, S. S. Kudashev, S. Ya. Karnov, A. T. Pavlov, M. I. Pesin and a number of others. It is difficult to overestimate the severity of this loss for the country and for Soviet science, and for us, faculty members who knew them personally and appreciated their abilities and passion for science, this loss is especially bitter.

Academician and director of the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University G. G. Cherny said that the 975th Artillery Regiment on October 5 repelled the fierce attacks of the enemy pressing from all sides. However, after another, the guns failed. When the order to disengage was given. The crews of the remaining two guns prepared for battle, and the infantry lay down nearby, on the edge, in hastily dug trenches. At this time, intelligence arrived that a large column of the enemy was moving near the forest - vehicles with soldiers, self-propelled guns, mortars, ammunition. An immediate decision was made - to attack the careless enemy with cash. The gunner of one of the guns was G.G. Cherny, the other was also of Mekhanites I. Stepanov. Careful aiming, a salvo and both shells exploded in the column. Further furious fire on the enemy, smashed cars, motorbikes, destroyed soldiers. However, the enemy’s confusion passed, and shells and mines began to fire in response. The duel lasted as long as ours had shells left.

Nurses, pilots and artillery

Many students, after completing two local nursing courses, were sent to hospitals, medical battalions and directly to the front line. In addition, female university students responded to the recognition of the pilots of Hero of the Soviet Union Marina Raskova and became pilots and pilots, in particular, of the 46th Guards Night Bomber Regiment. These pilots flew on a quiet U-2 aircraft that was not protected from fire, but inflicted significant damage on the enemy. The heels of the female pilots who graduated from Mekhanite were awarded the status of Hero of the Soviet Union. Here are their names: E. Rudneva, E. Pasko, R. Gasheva, A. Zubkova, E. Ryabova. Three more graduates of the faculty were Heroes of the Soviet Union - G. Varykov, G. Volokhov, L. Ratushnaya.

He not only fought bravely, but also contributed a lot of value to the rules of the arrow. But here he used his experience as a mathematician, who tends to look for the best solutions. The mathematicians of our country, during the period of the most difficult trials, proved themselves to be true patriots, showed the greatest courage, and were brave and prudent warriors.

Mathematical problems - for the front

We must bow to the endurance, dedication and loyalty to the Fatherland that the warrior mathematicians showed. However, we must not forget about another thing: we include mathematicians in the victory of the Soviet people over a strong and insidious enemy. This contribution consists of using the specific knowledge and skills that mathematicians possess. Knowledge of this factor is especially important in our days, when war has become, first of all, the perfection of reason, ingenuity and accurate calculation. The fact is that military operations will involve all the achievements of the natural world, and with them mathematics in all its manifestations. The creation of atomic and package weapons required not only the use of physical laws, but also extensive mathematical calculations, the creation of new mathematical models and even new branches of mathematics. Without such preliminary mathematical research, not a single technical system is created; the more complex it is, the more diverse and broad its mathematical apparatus is. For example, a cruiser is such a complex technical system. Before starting its construction, it is necessary to identify the geometric objects of the ship’s hull so that during movement no additional resistance is created and at the same time it is obedient to the control action of the rudder. It is first necessary to ensure the survivability of the ship, the reliability of its control, to calculate the influence on the stability of the location of various types of masses - machines, guns, torpedo tubes, etc. But this is not enough - it is necessary to ensure communication with all combat units of the ship, that is, to create an effective control system for the ship and its weapons.

We have listed only a tiny fraction of the problems that a mathematician must solve before a ship can be built. But server tasks need to be solved during its operation - navigational calculations, shooting calculations, etc.

Improving military equipment

During the Great Patriotic War, technology was varied and complex. It also required extensive use of mathematical

calculations for its manufacture and operation. Increasing the flight speed of aircraft required not only increasing engine power, but also choosing the optimal profile of the fuselage and wings, as well as solving many other issues. In Russia, a number of scientists have been working on these issues since the last century, and first of all N. E. Zhukovsky (1847-1921), called by V. I. Lenin the father of Russian aviation. He is naturally considered the founder of a new mathematical science - aerodynamics, in which he managed to create a number of strong research methods and solve numerous pressing problems, found a large scientific school, consisting of the closest students at the university and the senior higher technical institution of Moscow - the Moscow Higher Technical School. Zhukovsky laid the foundations of the Air Force Academy, which later received his name, as well as the Central Aerodynamic Institute. This scientific institution worked for many years under the leadership of one of N. E. Zhukovsky’s closest scientists and collaborators - S. A. Chaplyagin (1869-1942) and carried out many outstanding studies - M. V. Keldysh (1911-1978), V. V. Golubev (1884-1954), M. A. Lavrentiev (1900-1980), etc. The theoretical department has developed many important problems, including those for military aviation. Many of these developments were useful and were widely used to create new systems of tools, attack aircraft and bombers with increased maneuverability, speed, and reliability.

The theories of two phenomena received free attention - the corkscrew and the shimmy (or flutter), which at that time represented the main danger for aviators. As a rule, an airplane that got into a spin or shimmy (a type of aircraft vibration that led to its destruction) could no longer get out of it. The theory of these phenomena was created by M. V. Keldysh (sung by a resident of the USSR Academy of Sciences, chief theorist of astronautics). However, he went further and, based on the theory, made conclusions on how to eliminate these phenomena. As a result, flight practice received a reliable means of combating shimmy and spin, and during the entire war there were practically no deaths of aircraft or pilots in our aviation based on these principles. It is impossible to overestimate the results of the study, since they helped not only save the lives of pilots and aircraft, but also allowed them to fly at high speeds.

This was done by M.V. Ostrogadsky (1801-1862) and P.L. Chebyshev (1821-1894), and subsequent generations of scientists. The problems of zeroing, developed back in the 19th century in connection with the emergence of new types of artillery, required additional research and the compilation of tables during the Great Patriotic War. Shooting from an airplane at an airplane and at ground targets also led to mathematical problems that had to be solved on time. They were pursued persistently by both artillery and mathematics specialists. Combination problems led to the need to compile tables that would allow one to find the optimal time to drop bombs on a target, an area that would be covered by a bomb attack. Such tables were compiled before the start of the war, but for aircraft with high speeds. During the war, a useful opportunity arose to use standard training aircraft for new bombing missions. Specialized bomber regiments were created, but there were no timely bombing tables created for them. The urgent task of making the appropriate calculations arose. The tables were created and they provided invaluable assistance to our pilots.

An interesting problem arose among the sailors in connection with the desire to increase the probability of hitting the target in a torpedo hall. The idea arose to increase this probability through artificial dispersion. This task was taken up by one of our greatest mathematical academicians, A. N. Kolmogorov. He managed to find a complete solution to the problem and bring it to practical use. There is no doubt that some share of the successes of our sailors should be attributed to this problem solved by Kolmogorov. Later, his conclusions were transferred to problems associated with anti-aircraft artillery firing at aircraft. In general, it must be said that a topical mathematical problem, solved in one practical situation, very quickly finds other applications, sometimes very far from the first initial direction of research.

Statistical control in military production

There is one more aspect of the work of Soviet mathematicians to help the front, which cannot be minimized - this is the work on organizing the production process, focusing on increasing labor productivity and improving product quality. Here we are faced with a huge number of problems that, by their very essence, need to be addressed in mathematical methods and in successful mathematicians. We will touch upon here only one issue, which is called quality control of mass industrial products and quality management in the production process. This problem arose with all its severity before the industry already in the first days of the war, as mass modeling and skilled work as sweeteners took place. They were replaced by women and teenagers without qualifications or work experience.

I remember this incident: I had to be at one of the nearby factories in Sverdlovsk. He produced extremely necessary instruments for aviation and artillery. I saw practically only teenagers 13-15 years old at the machines. I also saw huge piles of defective parts. The master who accompanied me revealed that these parts were outside the tolerance limits and therefore unsuitable for assembly. But if we could assemble suitable devices from these “screwed up” parts, we would be able to immediately satisfy the needs on the spot in advance.

The master’s words haunted me. As a result of a promise with the plant engineers, soap was born, divided into 6 groups by size, which could already be matched with each other. Six groups included parts that were completely unsuitable for assembly. Research has shown that the devices assembled in this way turned out to be completely suitable for use. They have a disadvantage: if any part fails, it could only be replaced by a part from the same group from which the device was assembled. But at that time and for the purposes for which they were intended

We have devices, it was possible to get by by replacing devices, not parts. We were able to successfully use the “debris” of parts damaged by teenagers.

The task of quality control of manufactured products is as follows. Let $N$ products be manufactured, they must satisfy certain requirements. Let's fold, the outside must be of a certain diameter, not extending beyond the segment $[D_1, D_2]$, otherwise they will be unsuitable for the arrow. They must have a certain accuracy when shooting, otherwise there will be difficulties when shooting at the target. And if the first task is easy to cope with - you need to replace the diameters of manufactured projectiles and display those that do not meet the requirements, then with other requirements the situation is much more complicated. Indeed, to check the accuracy of the boom, it is necessary to check the booms. What will remain after the test? Tests must be carried out in such a way that the vast majority of products remain suitable for further use. We were faced with the main requirement: by testing a small part of the product, we could judge the quality of the entire batch. The methods that were proposed for this purpose were called static. Their theory originates from one work in 1848 by academician M.V. Ostrogadsky. Later, Professor V.I. Romanovsky (1879-1954) in Tashkent and his students dealt with this problem. During the war, A. N. Kolmogorov and his students began improving them.

The problem that has just been described has one defect in its very formulation: a batch of products has already been manufactured and it must be said whether it can be accepted or should it be rejected? But, okay, then make a batch and then reject it? Is it possible to organize the production process in such a way as to put a barrier to the production of low-quality products already during production? Such methods have been proposed and are called static monitoring methods. From time to time, several (say five) newly manufactured products are taken from the machine and their quality parameters are measured. If all these parameters are within acceptable limits, then the completed process continues, but if at least one product is outside the tolerance limits, then a signal is given about the need to readjust the machine or change the selected tool. What deviation of a parameter from the nominal value is acceptable for the entire batch to be manufactured with high quality? This requires special calculations.

After the end of the war, they found out that similar studies were carried out by US mathematicians. They calculated that the results of their work brought billions in savings to the country during the war years. The same can be said about the work of Soviet mathematicians and engineers.
