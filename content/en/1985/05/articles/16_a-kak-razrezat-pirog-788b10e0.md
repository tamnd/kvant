Mathematics is a science that not only shows in each case the correspondences, but also determines the principle on which they depend in the nature of things themselves.

L. Euler

Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) is one of the greatest mathematicians who laid the foundations of mathematics in the 17th–18th centuries. The son of a Swiss pastor, he was called to Russia in 1726 to work at the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences and remained here until the end of his life, with the exception of the period (1741-1765) of work at the Berlin Academy of Sciences.

Both his productivity (about 800 works, collected in 72 volumes) and his ability to see the main thing in any field of mathematics he studied are absolutely amazing. In most mathematical sciences, there are necessarily fundamental theorems, formulas, and equations bearing the name of Euler.

You can read more about Euler and his work in the 10th issue of our magazine for 1983.

How to cut a pie

Matematika is an exact science. She teaches counting, measuring and much more. Therefore, it is not surprising that when at your birthday party you had to cut the cake into 11 pieces, everyone shouted: “Let Misha do it!” - after all, Misha is the best mathematician in the class. Misha looked at the pie. The pie was round, but not very round, and was thicker on one side and thinner on the other. Yes, the task turned out to be difficult.

“Let me really cut the pie into equal parts, but someone else will think that less is enough for him than his neighbor. How can I prove that the parts of the real are the same? - thought Misha.
