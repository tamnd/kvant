We continue in the second issue a series of drawings for stereometric problems, the purpose of which is to help the reader develop spatial imagination. When solving a problem independently, you also need to imagine the pictures mentally.
(As a rule, accurate drawings can be constructed only by solving the problem at least partially, and besides, they are most often not very necessary for the solution.)

You will find the solution to the problem below in the next issue of the magazine.
Task. The cone and cylinder have a common base and a common height. Inside the cylinder, but all the cones, there are six equal spheres, each of which touches the upper base and side surface of the cylinder, two adjacent spheres and the side surface of the cone. The radius of each sphere is r. Find the radius of a sphere extended into a cone.
