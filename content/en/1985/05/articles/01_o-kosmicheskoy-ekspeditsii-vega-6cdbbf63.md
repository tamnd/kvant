In December last year, two Soviet automatic stations “Veza-1” and “Veza-2” were launched. This launch is the beginning of the International Expedition "Venus - Halley's Comet" (abbreviated as "Vesa"). The Veza project provides for a joint study of Venus and Halley's comet. Ground-based research of the comet is carried out under the International Program for Observing Halley's Comet - IHW (International Halley Watch). The decision to organize the IHW program was made at the 18th General Assembly of the International Astronomical Union. At figure there is a diagram of the “scenario” of the flight of the Veza station and the position of the “actors” during the main stages of scientific observations. In June of this year, “Vezi” will fly near Venus. Aerosat zones, separated from the descent vehicles, will transmit to Earth the results of research into the atomosphere of Venus. In March 1986, a meeting of spacecraft with Halley's comet was expected. We offer readers a story about the Veza expedition in this issue of the magazine (see the article “A Date with a Comet”).
