Date with a comet
Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. S. MAROCHNIK

Introduction from the editors

In the July 1982 issue of our magazine, an article by L. S. Marochnik “Comets” was published. It talked about what comets are, what is clear in the physics of comets and what is not clear today, why comets are interesting to scientists, what questions they, according to researchers, can answer.

That article dealt with the “biography” of one of the most significant comets—Halley’s Comet; The biography is quite dramatic; the names of many remarkable scientists are associated with it. It also talked about experimental projects for Halley's comet, and what scientific goals these experiments would pursue.

Then, in 1982, everything was still ahead - both launches and meetings of space missionaries with a significant comet. Today's meeting is still far away - they happened in March 1986, but the first start has already taken place. Two Soviet space stations, Vega 1 and Vega 2, successfully launched on December 15 and 21, 1984. The name of the stations (as well as the name of the entire project) is “Vega” - an abbreviation for the full name of the International Expedition “Venus - Halley’s Comet”. The Vega project involves Australia, the People's Republic of Bulgaria, the German Democratic Republic, the Hungarian People's Republic, the Polish People's Republic, France, the USSR, the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic, and the Max Planck Society (FRG). Project Vega involves joint exploration of Venus and Halley's Comet.

In the “Library «Kvant»” series, the book “A Date with a Comet” is being prepared for publication. Below we publish small postcards from this book, prepared by its author, L. S. Marochnik, for readers «Kvant».
