F916. In the circuit shown in figure 1 (D is an ideal diode), switch K is closed for a time τ and then opened. At the moment of opening the layer, the point in the inductor is equal to $I_0$. How long after the switch is opened does the current $I_L$ in the coil reach its maximum value if it is equal to $2I_0$? Construct a graph of $I_L$ versus time $t$ ($0 < t < \infty$).

After closing the key K in a very short period of time, the direction to the capacitor and to the inductor becomes the emf of the source $\mathcal{E}$ (see Fig. 1: $\mathcal{E} = \varphi_A - \varphi_B$, the current through the diode is not flow). During time τ, a constant charge $q = C\mathcal{E}$ will remain on the capacitor, and the current through the coil will increase according to a linear law:

$$L \frac{di}{dt} = \mathcal{E} \Rightarrow i = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{L} t \quad (0 < t \leq \tau).$$

At moment $t = \tau$, according to the conditions, $i = I_0$, that is

$$I_0 = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{L} \tau.$$

If at this moment the key K is opened, then the coil will continue to work over time due to the discharge of the capacitor, as happens in a conventional oscillatory circuit, that is, according to the law with a period of $T = 2\pi \sqrt{LC}$.

When the current reaches its maximum value, the direction to the inductor, diode, capacitor becomes zero, that is, the capacitor is completely discharged. Based on the law of conservation of energy, we have:

$$\frac{LI_0^2}{2} + \frac{C\mathcal{E}^2}{2} = \frac{LI_{\max}^2}{2}.$$

Given that $I_{\max} = 2I_0 = \frac{2\mathcal{E}}{L} \tau$, we find $LC = 3\tau^2$.

The value of the function $y = \sin x$ changes from 0.5 to 1 when the argument changes from $\pi/6$ to $\pi/2$, that is, for 1/6 of the period. Therefore, the time $\Delta t$, after which the weak point in the coil changes from $I_0$ to $2I_0$, is equal to $T/6$, that is

$$\Delta t = \frac{\pi}{3} \tau \sqrt{3} \approx 1,8\tau.$$

At time $t = \tau + \Delta t \approx 2,8\tau$ the potential of points A and B are compared, the resistance of the section containing the diode in the direction from B to A becomes zero. Thus, at subsequent moments of time there will be exactly a constant current $I = 2I_0$ through the inductor and diode, the charge of the capacitor will be zero.

There is one more important overall decision to be made. At time $t = \tau$ there will be a smooth (that is, without an arbitrary jump) transition from the linear section to the part of the sinus sinus. This is due to the fact that arbitrary $di/dt$ is proportional to the direction on the inductor $U_L = LI'$, which at each instant is equal to the voltage on the capacitor $U_C = q/C$. And since the charge on the capacitor cannot, say, change, there cannot be kinks in the graph $I(t)$.
