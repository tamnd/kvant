A careful study of beta decay has shown that this phenomenon contains two mysteries.

Riddle one: “loss” of energy. If the $X$ nucleus spontaneously transforms into the $Y$ nucleus, then this means that the $W_X$ energy of the $X$ nucleus is greater than the $W_Y$ energy of the $Y$ nucleus. And the energy emitted by the beta particles should be equal to the energy difference $W_X - W_Y$ (if we neglect the recoil energies).

Since all the original $X$ nuclei are the same, as well as all the $Y$ nuclei receiving from them are the same, all emitted beta particles must have the same energy. Experiments show that the energy of almost all beta particles is less than the energy difference $W_X - W_Y$. More precisely: β-particles have different energies, and they all range from zero to a maximum value of $W_X - W_Y$. For example, for beta particles emitted by $^{210}_{83}$Bi nuclei (half-life 5 days), the maximum energy value is about 1 MeV, and the average energy per particle is less than 0.4 MeV.
