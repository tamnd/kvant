Problem 2. A homogeneous chain of length 2l and a system of two rods connected by a hinge, each of length l (Fig. 3, a), are suspended from points B and C, located on the same horizontal line. The mass of the chain is equal to the mass of the common rods. Which center of gravity - a chain or a system of rods - is located below?

By acting on the chain so that its position coincides with the position of the system of rods (Fig. 3, b). It is obvious that in this case the centers of gravity of the chain and rods also coincide. Since in order to move the chain to a new position it was necessary to do some work on it, it can be argued that its new position of the center of gravity is higher than the previous one.

Consequently, the initial center of gravity of the chain is the center of gravity of the rod system.

Problem 3. In the first experiment, a spring with stiffness k and length l0 in the unbalanced state is stretched to length l. In the second experiment, this spring is first cut into two equal parts, then one of them is taken and it is also stretched to length l. Determine the work that needs to be done in the first and second opitech.

According to Hooke's law, when a spring is stretched by x

an elastic force F_ypr=-kx arises (the minus sign indicates that the elastic force tends to return the spring to its original state). In order to grow a spring, an external force \vec{F} must be applied to it, equal in the model to elasticity, but opposite to it in direction:

F=-F_ypr=kx.

Let's plot the dependence F=F(x) (Fig. 4, a) and use it to find the work that must match the external force to stretch the undeformed spring by an amount x:

A=\frac{1}{2}(kx)x=\frac{kx^2}{2}.

In the case when the spring has already been stretched by values ​​x1, and now it needs to be increased to x2, the work of the external force will be equal to (Fig. 4, b)

A=\frac{kx^2}{2}-\frac{kx^2}{2}.

Now let's get back to our task. In the first experiment, to stretch a spring chain with stiffness k from an undeformed state to length l, it is necessary to match the work:

A_1=\frac{kx^2}{2}=\frac{k(l-l_0)^2}{2}.

In the second experiment, to the same length l, he only stretched the spring so that the extension x2=l-l0/2. In addition, the stiffness of the spring base is not the same as the stiffness of the spring chain: it is twice as strong (show this yourself). Therefore, in the second experiment it is necessary to match the work.
