Mouse films

For those readers who have begun to doubt the existence of a solution to isopermetric problems, as well as for all lovers of red physics experiments, we offer several very simple experiments with soap film that confirm the correctness of our solutions. Of course, physical experiment cannot replace a strict mathematical proof; it only makes the result more plausible.

To test the first - the simplest - experiments, you will need a saturated soap solution (it is better to use a good extinguishing soap, you can add a little glycerin), thin wire, ordinary thread; for the next one you will also need small rings (sliding freely along the wire) and several straight thin straws.

First experience Immerse the wire frame in the soap solution and carefully remove it from it. The frame will be covered with a thin film of soapy water. It floats on the film, having an irregular shape (photo 1). Then pierce the film inside the loop with a hot corner - the loop instantly and accurately takes the shape of a circle (photo 2).

The described phenomenon is explained as follows: from general physical principles it follows that a stable equilibrium position of a soap film corresponds to the minimum energy of its tension.
