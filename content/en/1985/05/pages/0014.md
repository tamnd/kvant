Increasing the flight speed of aircraft required not only increasing engine power, but also choosing the optimal profile of the fuselage and wings, as well as solving many other issues. In Russia, a number of scientists have been working on these issues since the last century, and first of all N. E. Zhukovsky (1847-1921), called by V. I. Lenin the father of Russian aviation. He is naturally considered the founder of a new mathematical science - aerodynamics, in which he managed to create a number of strong research methods and solve numerous pressing problems, found a large scientific school, consisting of the closest students at the university and the senior higher technical institution of Moscow - the Moscow Higher Technical School. Zhukovsky laid the foundations of the Air Force Academy, which later received his name, as well as the Central Aerodynamic Institute. This scientific institution worked for many years under the leadership of one of N. E. Zhukovsky’s closest scientists and collaborators - S. A. Chaplyagin (1869-1942) and carried out many outstanding studies - M. V. Keldysh (1911-1978), V. V. Golubev (1884-1954), M. A. Lavrentiev (1900-1980), etc. The theoretical department has developed many important problems, including those for military aviation. Many of these developments were useful and were widely used to create new systems of tools, attack aircraft and bombers with increased maneuverability, speed, and reliability.

The theories of two phenomena received free attention - the corkscrew and the shimmy (or flutter), which at that time represented the main danger for aviators. As a rule, an airplane that got into a spin or shimmy (a type of aircraft vibration that led to its destruction) could no longer get out of it. The theory of these phenomena was created by M. V. Keldysh (sung by a resident of the USSR Academy of Sciences, chief theorist of astronautics). However, he went further and, based on the theory, made conclusions on how to eliminate these phenomena. As a result, flight practice received a reliable means of combating shimmy and spin, and during the entire war there were practically no deaths of aircraft or pilots in our aviation based on these principles. It is impossible to overestimate the results of the study, since they helped not only save the lives of pilots and aircraft, but also allowed them to fly at high speeds.
