The task of quality control of manufactured products is as follows. Let $N$ products be manufactured, they must satisfy certain requirements. Let's fold, the outside must be of a certain diameter, not extending beyond the segment $[D_1, D_2]$, otherwise they will be unsuitable for the arrow. They must have a certain accuracy when shooting, otherwise there will be difficulties when shooting at the target. And if the first task is easy to cope with - you need to replace the diameters of manufactured projectiles and display those that do not meet the requirements, then with other requirements the situation is much more complicated. Indeed, to check the accuracy of the boom, it is necessary to check the booms. What will remain after the test? Tests must be carried out in such a way that the vast majority of products remain suitable for further use. We were faced with the main requirement: by testing a small part of the product, we could judge the quality of the entire batch. The methods that were proposed for this purpose were called static. Their theory originates from one work in 1848 by academician M.V. Ostrogadsky. Later, Professor V.I. Romanovsky (1879-1954) in Tashkent and his students dealt with this problem. During the war, A. N. Kolmogorov and his students began improving them.

The problem that has just been described has one defect in its very formulation: a batch of products has already been manufactured and it must be said whether it can be accepted or should it be rejected? But, okay, then make a batch and then reject it? Is it possible to organize the production process in such a way as to put a barrier to the production of low-quality products already during production? Such methods have been proposed and are called static monitoring methods. From time to time, several (say five) newly manufactured products are taken from the machine and their quality parameters are measured. If all these parameters are within acceptable limits, then the completed process continues, but if at least one product is outside the tolerance limits, then a signal is given about the need to readjust the machine or change the selected tool. What deviation of a parameter from the nominal value is acceptable for the entire batch to be manufactured with high quality? This requires special calculations.

After the end of the war, they found out that similar studies were carried out by US mathematicians. They calculated that the results of their work brought billions in savings to the country during the war years. The same can be said about the work of Soviet mathematicians and engineers.
