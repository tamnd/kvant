6) Note. Each of the conditions "heights $AA_1$ and $BB_1$ intersect" and "heights $CC_1$ and $DD_1$ intersect" is equivalent to the condition "edges $AB$ and $CD$ are perpedicular". It is enough to show the equivalence of the conditions “heights $AA_1$ and $BB_1$ intersect” and “edges $AB$ and $CD$ are perpedicular”. Let us prove this equivalence. Let the indicated heights intersect, then the plane passing through them contains the edge $AB$ and is perpedicular to the edge $CD$, therefore $(CD)$ is perpedicular to any straight line of this plane, in particular, the straight line $AB$. On the other hand, if lines $AB$ and $CD$ are perpedicular, then there is a plane passing through $(AB)$ and perpedicular $(CD)$. Both heights lie in this plane and therefore intersect.

1. $\pi/4 + \arctg (3/4)$, $\pi/4 + \arctg (3/4)$,
$\pi/2 - 2 \arctg (3/4)$.
Solution. Note that $|AC| = 2 \cdot 4a \cos(a + \pi/4)$ (Fig. 16). By the sine theorem for $\triangle AEC$:
$$\frac{a}{\sin \pi/4} = \frac{2 \cdot 4a \cos(\alpha + \pi/4)}{\sin(\pi/2 - \alpha)},$$
whence $\alpha = \arctg (3/4)$.
Note that the problem can be solved in other ways - without using the theorem of sines. The answer can also be written in the following form:
arctg 7, arctg 7, $\pi - 2 \arctg 7$
(since $\pi/4 + \arctg (3/4) = \arctg 7$).

2. $\frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \frac{3}{4} (\pi - \alpha)}$. Solution. By the theorem of sines for $\triangle ABD : |AD| / |AB| = \sin \alpha / \sin \widehat{ADB}$, but $\widehat{ADB} = \pi - \hat{B} - \hat{A} / 2 = 3(\pi - \alpha) / 4$ (Fig. 17).

3. $|AD| = \frac{c \sin \alpha}{\sin (3\alpha/2)}$, $R = \frac{c}{2 \cos (\alpha/2)}$. Solution. By the theorem of sines for $\triangle ABD : |AB| / \sin \widehat{ADB} = |AD| / \sin \alpha$, but $\widehat{ADB} = \pi - (3\alpha) / 2$ (Fig. 18).

4. $\sqrt{c(b + c)}$. Solution. Let's denote $|BC| = a$ and draw bissertirus $AD$ (Fig.19). From the similarity of triangles $ABD$ and $ABC$ we obtain $c / |BD| = a/c$. By the bissertira property $|BD| / (a - |BD|) = c/b$, from where, excluding $|BD|$, we find $a = \sqrt{c(b + c)}$.
Note that other solutions are possible using the sine theorem for $\triangle ABC$.
