In the people's militia:
battle near Yelnya

In addition to teachers, graduate students and students who received mobilization extracts already in the first days of the war and ended up in regular military units, the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow University for 213 people in the 8th Krasnopresensk militia division. All of them were enrolled in the 975th artillery regiment of this division and, after a short training, already in August they occupied a defensive line in the Rzhev-Vyazma direction. Together with all our comrades, they created a defensive line: they dug trenches, anti-tank ditches, minefields, and installed guns. But at the front, not everything will happen as the defender assumes. At the beginning of October, the situation in the Yelninsky direction sharply worsened, as the Germans concentrated large fresh forces here and began an offensive operation. In this regard, the front commanders transferred the 8th Krasnopresensk division from the 32nd to the 24th artillery and promptly transferred it from prepared positions to the area of ​​​​the village of Uvarovo, located somewhat south of Yelnya. Unfortunately, there were no prepared positions there and we had to detain the enemy literally “on the march.” The division completed its combat mission: it detained the enemy for several days and thereby provided significant assistance to Moscow. According to the surviving information, this battle cost the occupiers dearly: they lost about 45 thousand soldiers and officers near Yelnya as a result of the staunch defense of Soviet soldiers. But we also paid a high price for this: about a third of the militia of the 8th division died in these battles. The middle ones were graduate students or those who had already completed their dissertations A. I. Gerchikov, M. E. Glerman, V. N. Zasukhin, I. R. Lepekhin, Kh. M. Milshtein, S. S. Kudashev, S. Ya. Karnov, A. T. Pavlov, M. I. Pesin and a number of others. It is difficult to overestimate the severity of this loss for the country and for Soviet science, and for us, faculty members who knew them personally and appreciated their abilities and passion for science, this loss is especially bitter.

Academician and director of the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University G. G. Cherny said that the 975th Artillery Regiment on October 5 repelled the fierce attacks of the enemy pressing from all sides. However, after another, the guns failed. When the order to disengage was given. The crews of the remaining two guns prepared for battle, and the infantry lay down nearby, on the edge, in hastily dug trenches. At this time, intelligence arrived that a large column of the enemy was moving near the forest - vehicles with soldiers, self-propelled guns, mortars, ammunition. An immediate decision was made - to attack the careless enemy with cash. The gunner of one of the guns was G.G. Cherny, the other was also of Mekhanites I. Stepanov. Careful aiming, a salvo and both shells exploded in the column. Further furious fire on the enemy, smashed cars, motorbikes, destroyed soldiers. However, the enemy’s confusion passed, and shells and mines began to fire in response. The duel lasted as long as ours had shells left.

Nurses, pilots and artillery

Many students, after completing two local nursing courses, were sent to hospitals, medical battalions and directly to the front line. In addition, female university students responded to the recognition of the pilots of Hero of the Soviet Union Marina Raskova and became pilots and pilots, in particular, of the 46th Guards Night Bomber Regiment. These pilots flew on a quiet U-2 aircraft that was not protected from fire, but inflicted significant damage on the enemy. The heels of the female pilots who graduated from Mekhanite were awarded the status of Hero of the Soviet Union. Here are their names: E. Rudneva, E. Pasko, R. Gasheva, A. Zubkova, E. Ryabova. Three more graduates of the faculty were Heroes of the Soviet Union - G. Varykov, G. Volokhov, L. Ratushnaya.
