§ 3. Energy belongs to those few physical quantities for which conservation laws are satisfied.

In particular, if the system of bodies is closed and the bodies interact with each other only through gravity and elasticity, then the total mechanical energy of the system (that is, the sum of kinetic and potential energy) remains constant. The action of the friction force leads to the fact that part of the mechanical energy is converted into the internal energy of the system. But even in this case, the sum of all types of energy in the system remains unchanged.

Problem 7. A boy on skates will accelerate to speed v and roll onto a hill covered with ice. To what height, counting from the base of the slide, can it lift if the friction coefficient is μ and the slope of the slide to the horizon is α?

At the initial moment, the boy has kinetic energy $$mv^2/2$$, and at the final moment - potential energy $$mgh$$ (here m is the boy’s mass, h is the desired lift height). Due to the decrease in total mechanical energy, work is done against the friction force $$F_{tp} = \mu mg\cos \alpha$$:

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - mgh = (\mu mg \cos \alpha) \frac{h}{\sin \alpha}.$$

From here we find the desired height h:

$$h = \frac{v^2}{2g(1 + \mu ctg \alpha)}.$$

Problem 8. A stone of mass m slides off the main slide of height H. This process is measured by distance in two different inertial reporting systems. From the point of view of an observer in a stationary system, the potential energy of the stone mgH transforms into kinetic energy $$mv^2/2$$, so that after sliding down the hill the stone has a speed v (Fig. 7). Observes, being in a reporting system moving with speed v to the right, will say that at the beginning the stone has potential energy mgH and kinetic energy $$mv^2/2$$, and at the end it has neither one nor the other. Where did the energy “disappear”?*)

Let's say right away that the law of conservation of energy is also true in this case. The reason was formed in a paradox that the reasoning was carried out for a non-freezing system - one stone, and the Earth, with which the stone interacts, was not taken into account. Let's fix this error.

a) In a stationary reporting system (associated with the center of mass of the stone - Earth system), at the initial moment the stone and the Earth are at rest, and all energy is equal to the potential energy of the system. At the final moment, the stone acquires speed $\vec{v}$, and the Earth acquires speed $\vec{u}$, which can be found from the law of conservation of momentum:

$$mv - Mu = 0,$$

where

$$u = \frac{m}{M} v,$$

where M is the mass of the Earth.

According to the law of conservation of energy,

$$mgH = \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{Mu^2}{2} = \frac{mv^2}{2} \left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right).$$

Since the mass of the stone is nothing small compared to the mass of the Earth, the values ​​of m/M can be exceeded compared to unity. Then we get

$$mgH = \frac{mv^2}{2},$$

*) Similar questions are discussed in detail in the article by V. A. Orlov “The Paradox of the “Bigger” Body”, published in the third issue of the magazine in 1978. (Note ped.)
