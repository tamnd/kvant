F914. A closed sauce with a volume of $V = 33,6$ dm³ contains nitrogen $u = 1$ moles of water. At a temperature of $t = 100^\circ\text{C}$ the pressure in the sauce is $p = 2 \cdot 10^5$ Pa. Determine the amount of nitrogen in the sauce.

The pressure $p$ in the sauce consists of the partial pressure of nitrogen $p_a$ and water vapor $p_n$. The maximum possible value $p_n$ is the saturated water vapor pressure $p_H$ at a given temperature. At $T = 373$ K $p_H = 10^5$ Pa. Let's see what the amount of $v_1$ steam in the sauce should be to create pressure $p_H$:

$$p_H V = v_1 RT \Rightarrow v_1 = \frac{p_H V}{RT} = \frac{10^5 \cdot 33,6 \cdot 10^{-3}}{8,3 \cdot 373} \text{ моль} \approx 1,1 \text{ моль.}$$

This means that when $v = 1$ mole of water evaporates, the resulting vapor will not be saturated. The pressure of this steam is

$$p_n = v \frac{RT}{V} \approx 0,9 \cdot 10^5 \text{ Pa.}$$

Therefore, the partial pressure of nitrogen in the sauce is

$$p_a = p - p_n \approx 1,1 \cdot 10^5 \text{ Pa},$$

and the amount of nitrogen in the sauce -

$$v_a = \frac{p_a V}{RT} \approx 1,2 \text{ моль.}$$

F915. A liquid with dielectric permeability flows between the plates of an insulated flat capacitor with a speed of $v$. A magnetic field with induction $\vec{B}$ is directed perpendicular to the speed $\vec{v}$ and parallel to the plates. Determine the direction on the capacitor plates. Distance between plates $d$.

Lorentz forces directed in opposite directions act on opposite charges of moving diopoles of the dielectric from the side of the magnetic field (see figure). This leads to polarization of the dielectric, and as a result of the upper and lower plates of the capacitor, surface charges of opposite signs will exist in the dielectric, equal in absolute value. These polarized charges create an electric field.

Let the direction of this field be equal to $E$ (at figure $\vec{E}$ directed from top to bottom). Then the force acting on the positive charge of the moving diopole is equal to

$$F = q (vB - E).$$

According to the definition of dielectric permeability, this force should be ε times less than the force $F_0$ that would act on the charge $q$, moving at a speed $\vec{v}$, in the absence of a dielectric. But in the absence of a dielectric $E = 0$, and $F_0 = qvB$. So

$$F = \frac{F_0}{\varepsilon} \Rightarrow q (vB - E) = q \frac{vB}{\varepsilon},$$

where is $E$ from:

$$E = \frac{\varepsilon - 1}{\varepsilon} vB.$$
