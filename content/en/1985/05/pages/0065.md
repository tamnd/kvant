1. When a satellite is launched along the screen towards the rotation of the Earth, the linear speed of the daily rotation adds up to the speed imparted to the satellite by the engine of the packets. Naturally, when launching along the meridian (and even more so along the screen in the direction opposite to the rotation of the Earth), more energy will be required to impart to the satellite the first escape velocity.
2. When sliding down a slide, the potential energy of the skier is completely spent on working against the friction force. By lifting a skier standing on his skis, the lift replenishes his reserve of potential energy and, in addition, does work against the friction force. Energy consumption in this case is greater than when returning to the starting point in a lift chair. (Of course, we do not smile at the force of air resistance, considering the speed of the flyer to be small.)
3. When the guitar cools, part of its internal energy goes to increase the potential energy of controlling the deformation of the body of the “stretched” bow and string. It is clear that problems arose when the bow and string were deformed and the forces were equal. However, the string in a good bow has a short distance, that is, it has a large coefficient of elasticity k. We can assume that elastic energy depends on the value of k in approximately the same way as urushina: U=kx²/2=F²/2k. It is clear that the bow body makes a greater contribution to energy.
