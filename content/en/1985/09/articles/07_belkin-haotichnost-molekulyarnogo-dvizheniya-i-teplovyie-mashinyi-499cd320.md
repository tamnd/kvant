where $v_A$ is the velocity model at point A, $a$ is the model of the desired acceleration. During the next period of time τ the body moves from B to C.
Its displacement $s_2$ is determined by a similar formula, in which the initial speed $v_B = v_A + a \tau$:

$$s_2 = v_B \tau + \frac{a \tau^2}{2} = (v_A + a \tau) \tau + \frac{a \tau^2}{2} = v_A \tau + \frac{3a \tau^2}{2}.$$

Let us now find the displacements $s_2 - s_1$ for two consecutive equal intervals:

$$s_2 - s_1 = \left(v_A \tau + \frac{3a \tau^2}{2}\right) - \left(v_A \tau + \frac{a \tau^2}{2}\right) = a \tau^2.$$

Hence, for the acceleration $a$ we obtain

$$a = \frac{s_2 - s_1}{\tau^2}.$$

Thus, having a linear picture of the movement of a body, one can find its acceleration by changing the lengths of any two adjacent segments corresponding to the movement of the body during equal periods of time between flashes. Chaoticity of molecular motion and heat engines

As is known, heat engines are distinguished by the fact that their coefficient of performance (efficiency) is low compared to other machines. For example, the efficiency of steam locomotives and thermal locomotives, which are practically no longer used, does not exceed 10%, and the diesel locomotives that replaced them operate with an efficiency of only about 25%. The efficiency of the best modern heat engines does not exceed 50%.

This means that the heater, 200 years after the appearance of the first heat engines, half of the energy of the fuel burned in them is wasted uselessly? What's the matter? Perhaps it is that scientists and engineers were unable to invent and design “good” heat engines in which fuel energy would be used more efficiently?

It turns out that the creators of the car are not to blame for this. We will find the true “culprit” of low efficiency values ​​if we take a closer look at what is happening in heat engines.

Disorderly and orderly movements. Tell machines are built to perform mechanical work in them. This is usually done like this: some gas receives a certain amount of heat from heating and expands; During this expansion, the gas pressure force does work, for example, to move a piston or rotate a turbine. In other words, heat engines convert thermal energy, that is, the energy of disordered molecular motion, into mechanical energy, the energy of ordered motion.
