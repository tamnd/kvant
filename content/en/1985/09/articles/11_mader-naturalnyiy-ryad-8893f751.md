Natural series

Legend says that the natural series - a sequence of positive integers: 1, 2, 3, ... - was given to people by Prometheus. But in fact, the invention of the natural series was one of the first and greatest achievements of Muselli man, born from practice. The history of the emergence of the natural series began long before the invention of writing. It took many centuries to understand that there is no most number in this series. For a long time it was thought that counting arose only within certain limits, beyond which the “theme” began. The Babylonians believed, for example, that there was a certain fantastic number “Levinathan”, beyond which would extend that which could not be counted.

Only in the 3rd century BC, the ancient Greek mathematician Archimedes, in his book “Psammit” (“Calculus of the Sands”) showed that any number of sands can still be counted, since the natural series can be continued indefinitely, and each subsequent number can be written and named in a certain way. Thus, we came to the understanding that there are infinitely many natural numbers. This fact is what mathematicians mean when they say that the set of natural numbers is infinite.

Well, what can we say about prime numbers - is their set finite or infinite? In Euclid's Elements (3rd century BC) it is shown that there is no most formula

$$1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n = \frac{1}{2} n (n + 1)$$

$$1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + \dots + n^2 = \frac{1}{6} n (n + 1) (2n + 1)$$

$$1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + \dots + n^3 = \frac{1}{4} n^2 (n + 1)^2$$
