This year the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad was held for the 48th time; More than 700 schoolchildren took part in it. Although its number is not “round”, this Olympics can rightfully be considered an anniversary one - the first Olympics took place exactly 50 years ago. The final round of the 1985 Olympiad took place on March 24 at Moscow University. We present the tasks proposed there.

7th grade
1. Find all values ​​of $x$ and $y$ satisfying the equality
$$xy + 1 = x + y.$$
2. Given five different positive numbers, which can be divided into two groups so that the sums of the numbers in these groups are the same. How many ways can this be done?
3. The lengths $a, b, c, d$ of the four segments satisfy the inequality $0 < a \leq b \leq c < d, d < a + b + c$. Is it possible to make a trapezoid from these segments?
4. A zats sits in the center of the square, and in each of the four corners there is one wolf. Can a zac run out of a square if wolves can only run along the sides of the square at a maximum speed of 1.4 times the zac's maximum speed?
5. A milk tank was brought to Matazin. The seller has everything without weights (you can put flasks on the sides of the scales), as well as three identical flasks, two of which are empty, and the third contains 1 liter of milk. How can 85 liters of milk be poured into one flask without more than weighing?
8th grade
1. Find all values ​​of $x, y$ and $z$ satisfying the equality
$$(x - y + z)^2 = x^2 - y^2 + z^2.$$
2. The numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{1985}$ represent the numbers 1, 2, ..., 1985 rearranged in some order. Each number $a_k$ is multiplied by its number $k$, and then the largest one is chosen among all 1985 products obtained. Prove that it is not less than $993^2$.
3. A paper square is placed on a sheet of paper “in a cage”, the area of ​​which is equal to four times the area of ​​the cage. What is the smallest number of nodes that can cover this square? (A node is the point of intersection of the linear paper; if a node lies on the border of a square, then it is considered covered.)
4. An infinite number of rich people lined up behind Uncle Chernomor. Prove that he can order parts of them to fail so that there are infinitely many rich ones left in the row and they all stand in order of height (not necessarily in descending order of height).

5. Prove that if the length of each of the three bisectors of a triangle is greater than 1, then its area is greater than 1/√3.

9th grade
1. Find all values ​​of $x, y$ and $z$ satisfying the equality
$$\sqrt{x - y + z} = \sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y} + \sqrt{z}.$$
2. Some countries have 1,985 airfields. An airplane took off from each of them and landed at an airplane airfield from the launch site. Could it be that as a result, all 1985 aircraft ended up at 50 airfields? (The earth can be considered flat and the routes straight.)
3. See problem 3 for 8th grade.
4. Prove that in any group of 12 people you can choose two, and among 10 people there are still five remaining, so that each of these fifth satisfies the following condition: either he is friends with all those chosen at the beginning, or he is not friends with any of them.
5. Prove that any number $2^n$, where $n=3, 4, 5, \dots$ can be represented as $2^n = 7x^2 + y^2$, where $x$ and $y$ are odd numbers.

10th grade
1. Solve the equation
$$\frac{x - 49}{50} + \frac{x - 50}{49} = \frac{49}{x - 50} + \frac{50}{x - 49}.$$
2. See problem 3 for 7th grade.
3. Let’s define the “complexity” of a given number by the shortest length of a numerical sequence (if there is one), which starts from zero and ends with this number, and each subsequent sequence is either equal to zero $m/2^{50}$, where $m=1, 3, 5, \dots, 2^{50}-1$, find the number with the most “complexity”.
4. Data 1985 sets, each of which consists of 45 elements, and the union of any two sets contains exactly 89 elements. How many elements does the union of all these 1985 sets contain?
5. Prove that if the distances between the crossing edges of a tetrahedron are equal to $h_1, h_2, h_3$, then the volume of the tetrahedron is not less than $h_1h_2h_3/3$.

Novosibirsk Region Physics Olympiad

8th grade
1. A Konchek-shaped barrel, partially filled with water, is closed with two identical plugs, applying equal forces to them (Fig. 1). Work to move the bucca from one place to another, holding it with its wide diameter down, and then slowly turn the bucca over with its narrow diameter down. After this, the plug flew out and water began to flow out of the barrel. Why did the plug come out?

2. A flexible chain with small links connects two small balls, much more massive than any of the links in the chain. However, one of the balls is held in the center of a smooth horizontal table shaped like a circle, while another, together with a small piece of chain, shines from its edge. Assuming that the height of the table is greater than the length of the chain, determine where the points of first contact of the balls with the floor will be if you imagine the system to itself: 1) inside a circle with a table as its “roof”; 2) outside the mentioned circle; 3) on its border. Trennem pretender.

3. The body begins to slide down the rough inclined plane and at the lowest point hits an elastic wall perpendicular to the direction of its movement. After the impact, the body rose to half its original height. Assuming that the velocity modulus did not change during the impact, determine what took longer: the ascent or descent. How many times?

4. A small body of mass $m$, suspended on a weightless thread, is deflected from the equilibrium position by an angle $a$ and released. Determine the tension of the thread at the first moment of time.

5. The dynamometer consists of two cylinders connected by a light spring (Fig. 2). Find the ratio of the masses of these cylinders if, with forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ applied to them, the diameter reading is $F$.

9th grade

1. The monorail car has a motor capable of developing a power of $N=100$ kW. The trailer starts moving on one side and stops on the other. Find the minimum time required for this. Wheel friction coefficient on rail $\mu=0,2$. The distance between stations is $l=800$ m, the weight of the carriage is 100 kg. Air resistance when moving is reduced.

2. How many times will the lifting force of a balloon communicating with the atmosphere change if hydrogen is used as the gas the first time, and helium the second time? The mass of the shell of the ball is pretenbrets.

3. A thin closed tube is filled with liquid of density $Q$ and rotates in a horizontal plane around an axis passing through one of the ends of the tube (Fig. 3). The liquid pressure in the tube is measured by two pressure gauges located at distances $l_1$ and $l_2$ from the axis of rotation. What will be the difference $\Delta p=p_2-p_1$ shown by the pressure gauges when the tube rotates uniformly with an angular velocity $\omega$?

4. See problem 5 for 8th grade.

5. On some sections of the highway, automatic vehicles in a convoy reduce their speed from $v_1$ to $v_2$. What should be the initial distance between the autopilots so that they do not become stunned? The length of each machine is $l$.

10th grade

1. The system consists of two vessels $A$ and $B$, connected to each other by a long thin tube and filled with a homogeneous liquid (Fig. 4). Two experiments are carried out with the system. In the first experiment, the vessel $A$ is heated, in the second - $B$. Will this cause liquid to flow through the tube? If yes, which way? The expansion of vessels and tubes when heated, as well as the thermal conductivity of the system is pretenbrets.

2. A load of mass $M$ was aimed at the lower end of a vertical undeformed beam and released without an initial speed, after which it began to perform a vertical harmonic swing. How many times will the period and amplitude of the oscillations change if a beetle whose mass $m$ is 96% of the mass of the load falls onto the load without a push? Landing without a push means that at the moment of contact the speed of the beetle and the load are the same. Consider the landing case: 1) at the highest and 2) at the lowest points of the trajectory.

3. The device, the diagram of which is shown in figure 5, is connected to an alternating current network with a voltage of $U=220$ V. What voltage will the voltmeter show over a long period of time?

4. See problem 5 for 9th grade.

5. Two small identical bodies of mass $m$ are each charged with identical charges $q$ and are located at a distance $r$ from each other (Fig. 6). First, body 1 was released. When it moved away to a distance $2r$, body 2 was also released. Find the velocities of the bodies at a large distance from each other. There is no training.
