⟦folio 4⟧

This story seems to us an interesting, meaningful and instructive concrete example of the dialectical process of the formation of human knowledge. Each of the fundamental ideas that, in their totality, led to the creation of a computer, on the one hand, was the product of a sharp, original mind, and on the other, being a natural and inevitable product of the historical evolution of human conditions, his needs, and the development of science and technology.

"Machine" that is always with you

The need to count was, probably, one of the first intellectual needs experienced by nomo sapiens already at the beginning of his historical journey. It was predetermined and stimulated by the scientific problems of the very existence of primitive man and, above all, by the constant need to know and learn the changing quantities of food, livestock, grain and other animal objects. However, man mastered man very slowly, because the formation of the abstract concepts of “one”, “two”, etc. required the accumulation of thousands of years of practical, everyday experience.

In the long and difficult process of understanding the very idea of ​​counting, a person was especially helpful... his fingers. This is how nature works, that a person is given a natural “counting machine” - ten fingers on his hands - and constantly, throughout his life, carries it with him.

This simple, convenient and reliable “calculating machine” has all the capabilities of a computer in a miniature: you can enter numerical information into it, it helps you perform arithmetic operations, reports the resulting result and, if necessary, allows you to save it for some time. Of course, the duration of information storage and the memory capacity of such a “machine”, its computing capabilities and weight are very modest, although not as small as it seems.

Some books on mathematics describe very skillful techniques that allow you to “on your fingers” count to fairly large numbers and perform not only addition and subtraction, but also multiplication. Now this is just fun, tricks, but even in the 13th century, one of the creators of medieval mathematics in Europe, Leonardo of Pisa, better known as Fibonacci, recommended using fingers as an aid in counting.

Finger counting played a special role in the development of mathematics. It seems reliable that it was here that the servers’ fingers in practice gave birth to, and then psychologically reinforced, the habit of people counting in tens, which led to the emergence of the decimal (ten) number system. It gradually evolved from other number systems (say, duodecimal or sexagesimal) used by some peoples, and has long become generally accepted throughout the world.

Since it is naturally impossible to leave numerical information on one’s fingers for a long time, man was faced with the practical task of inventing ways to designate and store numbers. At the dawn of mankind, notches and notches on various objects, special counting sticks, pebbles, tags, etc. were used for this. But the most reliable and convenient way to designate and store numbers appeared with the advent of writing. By the way, some stories claim that people learned to write numbers even earlier than letters.

An outstanding historical event that determined all subsequent developments of mathematics was the discovery of positional numbering. Apparently she was born around 4000 years old
