⟦folio 8⟧

Convection and self-organizing structures

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
E. E. GORODETSKY.
V. S. ESIPOV

Winds and ocean currents, movement of materials and flows of solar matter... Each of these phenomena is very complex and diverse. And yet they (and much more) have a characteristic common feature: convection plays a significant role in them - one of the most beautiful phenomena in the physics of the macrocosm.

In recent years, interest in this “old” and seemingly familiar phenomenon has increased sharply. It turns out that convection is one of those processes as a result of which systems, initially disordered, come to an ordered state. The birth of order from chaos (or, in other words, self-organization) is a fundamental mechanism of evolution. And the concept of convection helps us advance our understanding of this fascinating and, to a large extent, still obscure problem.

Conditions for the existence of convection

In the narrow sense of the word, convection is the occurrence of ordered movement in unevenly heated liquids or gases.

Under what conditions will such a movement occur? Let's turn to a simple example: imagine that we heat up a flat horizontal layer of liquid. How can the process of heat transfer from the lower boundary of the liquid to the upper occur?

When the temperature difference ΔT of the lower and upper layers of liquid is small, the process of heat transfer occurs due to the heat-conducting mechanism: “hotter” molecules, when exposed to “colder” ones, transfer their excess energy to them, but there is no macroscopic movement of the liquid. However, when the temperature difference reaches a certain critical value ΔTcr, the heat-conducting mechanism is the transfer of thermal energy associated with the occurrence of macroscopic motion in the liquid.
