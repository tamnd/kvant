Physics and mathematics conference for schoolchildren

On March 16, 1985, a scientific conference of schoolchildren was held at the physics and mathematics boarding school at Moscow State University.

The participants of the conference—students of the 9th and 10th grades of the Physics School at Moscow State University, the 57th, 179th, 444th school in Moscow, as well as the Faculty of Physics School at Moscow State University—talked about the independent research that they conducted under the guidance of teachers from their schools, as well as employees of Moscow University, the Institute of Applied Mathematics of the USSR Academy of Sciences and other scientific institutes.

The conference was opened by the presentation of the jury by the director of the Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University, candidate of physical and mathematical sciences V.L. Natyaganov, who wished to participate in the conference of successes and noted that the main thing in their work is not even the final results (in our time it is difficult to expect scientific discoveries from schoolchildren), but rather the acquisition of science, its spirit and independent creativity, which will be very useful to them in the future.

Then a plenary session took place, at which a report was read by M. Erikov (Physical School at Moscow State University, 10th grade) “On the causes of Fermat in optics.” The author of the report explored the possibility of using Fermat's principle to analyze optical systems studied in a school physics course. He not only collected and summarized material on this topic, but also obtained new interesting results.

Further work of the conference was carried out in 3 sections. At the designated sections, 22 reports were read: 8 on mathematics and 14 on physics.

All 8 reports in the mathematics section were very interesting.

The reports by V. Sadov (school No. 57) “On the modeling of a physical experiment”, M. Kudryashov and I. Balaban (school No. 179) “Flight to the Moon” and V. Volokitin (school No. 179) “On a method for numerically solving the Riccati equation” stood out for their application focus and were devoted to solving problems with the help of modern computers, often associated with server-side practical problems. These works were carried out under the guidance of employees and on computers of the Institute of Problems of Mathematics of the USSR Academy of Sciences. It is pleasant to note that the authors of the reports discovered that schoolchildren have insignificant knowledge in the field of application of mathematics, as well as the ability to program on modern computers.

The remaining five reports, completed by students of the Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University, were devoted to various sections of “numerical” mathematics.

V. Roganov in his report “On some generalizations of Euler’s theory” found out the conditions that must be satisfied by the radii r and R of the circumscribed and circumscribed circles of a convex triangle (of course, we are talking about polygons that are simultaneously written and circumscribed about a circle) and the distance d between the centers of these circles. According to Euler's theorem for a triangle d²=R²-2Rr. The speaker obtained generalizations of this theorem for n>3.

O. Vasov’s report “Games and Groups” discussed problems that make it possible to understand the structure of evaluating the trial seconds of heads such as a Rubik’s cube. S. Partley in the report “Functional Roots” found out under what conditions a given continuous function f(x) can be represented in the form h(h(x))=f(x), where h is some continuous function. It turned out that a sufficient condition here is the possibility of the function h.

Examples are given when the function h does not exist. In general, the problem of the existence of “functional square roots” is still far from being solved. For example, it is not clear whether there exists a differential function h(x) for which h(h(x))=sinx.

S. Chepanov and N. Shamarov studied dynamic equations of the form $\frac{1}{x_1} + \frac{1}{x_2} + \frac{1}{x_3} + \dots + \frac{1}{x_n} = \frac{1}{N}$, where x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ are pairwise distinct natural numbers, and N is a natural number. In some cases they obtained interesting results.

In A. Slepukhin's report “On transformations of planar graphs,” apparently a new theorem from graph theory was proven!

The reports at two physics sections were very interesting and informative.

In the report by A. Soldatov “Estimation of the binding energy of liquid molecules by saturated vapor pressure”, Y. Prokorov “Estimation of the binding energy of water molecules in association by the temperature distribution of water” and V. Lushikov “On one dimensionless CONSTANTE characterizing a liquid,” the authors obtained new interesting results. It should be noted that the authors of two reports used a computer to analyze the experimental curves, and in the second report the computer program was quite complex. The students of the section also expressed great interest in the report of V. Costunn “Testing the laws of motion of bodies using the example of the planet Venus.” In the report, the author used his own observations for processing, and to facilitate observation, he designed an original device.

The report by I. Tuzov “Experimental study of Wenar cells in liquid media” was very interesting. This work is characterized by functional and thorough research. S. Kozyrev’s report “On the speed limit of projectiles ejected from a fire weapon” shows that when using conventional explosives, a certain maximum projectile speed (v ~ 10 km/s) does not significantly affect. This speed is high, but it is not enough to send a projectile, for example, to the Moon. In the report by D. Ishchenko “On the mechanical stability of towers in a field of strength,” the author relatively simply obtained an estimate of the upper limit of the height of towers, without using a complex apparatus, with the help of which both this phenomenon is analyzed. The report by M. Kuznetsov and O. Fominov “Some issues of increasing the dielectric field” was also interesting.
