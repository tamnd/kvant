⟦folio 38⟧

A satellite flying at an altitude of $h=200$ km has a radio transmitter that emits radio waves from the particle $v$ evenly in the entire direction. Neglecting the absorption of radio waves in the ionosphere, estimate the fraction of the power emitted by the transmitter that is carried by radio waves beyond the Earth's atmosphere. When making estimates, consider the Earth's surface to be flat, and the reflection of radio waves from it to be specular and lossless. What does it have to do with it? The solid angle of a cone with vertex angle $\beta$ is equal to $\Omega=2\pi\left(1-\cos \frac{\beta}{2}\right)$.

V. I. Chuvilev

M941. The regular $(4k+2)$ vertex polygon $A_0A_1 \dots A_{4k+2}$ of center $O$ in given. Prove that the sum of the segments of the lines $A_1A_{2k}, A_2A_{2k-1}, \dots, A_kA_{k+1}$ inside the angle $A_kOA_{k+1}$ (see Fig. 1 on p. 37 for $k=2$) equals the radius of the circumcircle of the polygon, if a) $k=2$, b) $k$ is any natural number.

I. F. Sharygin

M942. The numbers 1, 2, ..., $2n-1$, $2n$ are split into two groups of $n$ numbers each. Suppose $a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_n$ are the numbers of the first group in increasing order, and $b_1 > b_2 > \dots > b_n$ the numbers of the second one in decreasing order. Prove that $|a_1-b_1|+a_2-b_2|+\dots+|a_n-b_n|=n^2$.

V. V. Proizvolov

M943. The sequence $a_1, a_2, \dots$ is given by the rules: $a_{2n}=a_n$ if $n \geqslant 1$ and $a_{4n+1}=1$, $a_{4n+3}=0$ if $n \geqslant 0$. Prove that this sequence is not periodic.

Yu. V. Nesterenko

M944*. A regular hexagon is split into 24 equal triangles as shown on Fig. 2, p. 37. Different numbers are written at each of the 19 vertices of the figure thus obtained. Prove that among the 24 triangles at least 7 have vertices with counterclockwise increasing numbers.

A. A. Berzinsh

P953. In order to regulate the turntable of a $33^{1/3}$ RPM record player with great precision, little evenly spaced marks are made on the disk's outside surface; they are lighted by a neon lamp of frequency 100 flashes per second. How many marks are needed to make them appear motionless at nominal velocity? After the turntable motor is turned off, dry friction forces begin slowing the disk down — the marks begin to "run". In the first 4 seconds 10 marks "run past" the observation point. How soon will the disk stop? How soon will the marks start "running the other way"?

A. R. Zilberman
