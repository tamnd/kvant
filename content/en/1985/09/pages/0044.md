M924. Each two of the $n$ points (no three of which lie on the same line) are connected by a segment, and arrows are placed on all segments. A triangle ABC with vertices at these points is called oriented if the arrows are placed in the directions AB, BC, CA or AC, CB, BA.
a) Explain how to arrange the arrows so that no oriented triangles appear.
b) What is the maximum possible number of oriented triangles (for each n)? (Draw appropriate examples for $n=4$, 5 and 6.)

◆ Let us denote the data of $n$ points by $A_1, \dots, A_n$.

a) Answer: you need to arrange the arrows in ascending order of point numbers: from $A_i$ to $A_j$ with $i < j$ (see Fig. 1 for $n=5$). Then in any triangle two arrows come out from the vertex with the lowest number, so it is not oriented.

b) Answer: the most number of oriented triangles is $n(n^2-1)/24$ for odd $n$ and $n(n^2-4)/24$ for even $n$ (see Fig. 2-4 for $n=4$, 5, 6).

It will be more convenient for us to find the smallest number of oriented triangles, and then subtract it from the total number of triangles, equal to $n(n-1)(n-2)/6$ (by choosing successive vertices of a triangle from these points, we will have $n$ choices for the first vertex, $n-1$ for the second, $n-2$ for the third; in this case, each trio of vertices in a different order will be selected 6 times).

We will call a pair of arrows (directed segments) consistent if they have a common beginning or a common end. Obviously, of the three pairs of sides of any unoriented triangle from arrow-blooded, two are consistent (on figure 5 - AB and AC, AC and BC), and any consistent pair of arrows belongs to exactly one unoriented triangle.
