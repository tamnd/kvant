⟦folio 57⟧

The domestic computer EC-1060 is one of the most powerful machines of the 3-20 generation. The element base is an IC based on bipolar transistors. Speed ​​- 3 million operations per second. Contains more than 10 million transistors.

"Electronka BZ-38" is a microcalculator based on a single crystal containing 35 thousand field-effect transistors. Crystal size ~5×5 mm². Contains microprograms for 34 mathematical operations. Consumes less than 600 µW, weighs less than 50 g.

The human brain has together approximately 10¹³ pieces of information. To store this information in computer memory, in 1960 a room with a volume of 500 ml would have been required. m³.

If the current rate of reduction in the size of semiconductor elements in ICs continues, then in 2050 this information could be contained in a volume smaller than the volume of a human head.

The plastic was cut into many small crystals, each of which represented one future transistor. Then began a long sequence of laborious manual operations. The crystal was attached to a special holder, wire leads were welded to the crystal under the microsom on both sides, the entire structure was placed in a special housing and sealed. And as a result, the transistor was ready to serve as an element of any radio-electronic circuit.

It turned out that at first the transistors were separated from one another (when the plate was cut), and then, during the manufacture of the circuit, they were again connected to each other.

The idea of ​​an IC is to produce both transistors and the necessary connections between them on a wafer. As a result, each plate contains ready-made electronic circuits. The housing now houses not just one transistor, but a circuit with many (up to hundreds of thousands) transistors and a relatively small number of external terminals. The dimensions of electronic circuits are greatly reduced, and their reliability increases.

If the familiar microcalculator were assembled not on ICs, but on ordinary discrete transistors, it would be the size of a desk and break down every hour. Electronic watch violations could not exist at all. Yes, and modern computers, numbering ten million transistors, would be practically impossible: they would break down every few seconds.

The computer uses ICs containing two main types of transistors: bipolar and field-effect.

In our subsequent notes we will discuss the principles of operation of bipolar and field-effect transistors and the simplest logical circuits that underlie the operation of a computer.
