⟦folio 37⟧

Should there be a number of marks so that at rated speed they appear stationary?
After turning off the engine, dry friction forces begin to slow down the disk, and the marks “run”.
During the first 4 s mismo points, 10 marks are observed to “run through”. How long will it take for the disk to stop?
After what period of time will the marks “run” in the other direction?

A. R. Zilberman

F954. A decorative square curtain measuring 1.5x1.5 m hangs on a cornice along a vertical wall. The lower edge of the curtain is raised flush with the top, so that the curtain is folded in half, and released. Find the dependence of the force acting on the cornice on time. The curtain is thin, smooth and weighs 3 kg.

S. S. Krotov

F955. A gas mixture consisting of $m_1=100$ g of nitrogen and an unknown amount of oxygen is subjected to isothermal rolling at a temperature of $T=74,4$ K. A graph of the pressure of the gas mixture versus its volume is shown in figure 3 (in units of conditions). Determine the mass of oxygen. Calculate the saturated oxygen vapor pressure at this temperature.

Note: $T=74,4$ K is the boiling point of liquid nitrogen at normal pressure; oxygen boils at a higher temperature.

A. I. Buzdin

F956. In one of the variants of the classical experiment, carried out by Academician I.K. Kikon, a single-point short-circuited inductor $L=3 \cdot 10^{-5}$ Gn made of thin superconducting wire is suspended on pruguroidinites in a magnetic field directed vertically upward along the axis of the coil (figure 4). The suspension thread in the initial state is not twisted, the current strength in the coil is zero. The magnetic field induction $B$ slowly increases from zero to a value $B_0=0,1$ T, at which superconductivity abruptly disappeared and the wire returned to the normal state. At the same time, the coil is twisted. Determine the maximum angle of twist of the coil, assuming that the elastic moment of the thread is proportional to the angle of twist (proportionality coefficient $G=10^{-7}$ N m/rad). The number of turns of the coil is $n=100$, the radius of the turns is $R=1$ cm, the mass of the coil is $M=10$ g. The ratio of the electron charge to its mass is $e/m=1,76 \cdot 10^{11}$ C/kg.

S. M. Kozel
