F935. A current $I_0$ flows through a long straight conductor of constant cross-section. In section AB of the conductor (see figure), its resistivity decreases from $\rho_1$ in section A-A to $\rho_2$ in section B-B. What size and sign of the object breast is formed in area AB?

Let us select an infinitesimal element of length $\Delta l$ on the section AB of our conductor in an arbitrary place (see figure) and write Ohm’s law for this element:

$$\Delta U = I_0 \rho \Delta l / S.$$

Here $\rho$ is the resistivity of the conductor in the selected volume, and $S$ is the cross-section of the conductor. Considering that the limit of the ratio $\Delta U / \Delta l$ at $\Delta l \rightarrow 0$ determines the magnitude of the electric field strength ($\lim_{\Delta l \rightarrow 0} (\Delta U / \Delta l) = E$), we can write the first equality in the form

$$E = \rho I_0 / S.$$

The resulting correspondence shows, for any section of the conductor, the relationship between the electric field strength $E$ and the resistivity of the conductor in the same section.

Let an object background $\Delta Q$ be formed in the selected elementary object. Let's find the electric field strength to the left ($E_1$) and to the right ($E_2$) of the selected element. Using the principle of superposition of electrostatic fields, we can write

$$E_1 = |\vec{E}_1| = |\vec{E}_l| - \frac{\Delta Q}{2\varepsilon_0 S},$$

$$E_2 = |\vec{E}_2| = |\vec{E}_l| + \frac{\Delta Q}{2\varepsilon_0 S}.$$

Here $|\vec{E}_l|$ is the electric field strength in the volume of our element, created by all charges except charge $\Delta Q$, and the second terms unify the field of charge $\Delta Q$, which will be equivalent to the field near a uniformly charged surface with a surface charge density $\Delta Q / S$. Changing margin at element borders

$$\Delta E = |\vec{E}_2| - |\vec{E}_1| = \Delta Q / \varepsilon_0 S.$$

On the other side,

$$\Delta E = \Delta \rho \cdot I_0 / S,$$

where $\Delta \rho$ is the difference in resistivity of the conductor to the right and left of the selected element at its boundaries. From the last two equalities we obtain a connection between the volume of the charge $\Delta Q$ and the difference in resistivity at its boundaries:

$$\Delta Q = \varepsilon_0 I_0 \cdot \Delta \rho.$$

The volume charge in the entire area $AB$ is equal to the sum of similar elementary charges, that is

$$Q_{AB} = \Sigma \Delta Q = \varepsilon_0 I_0 \Sigma \Delta \rho = \varepsilon_0 I_0 (\rho_2 - \rho_1).$$
