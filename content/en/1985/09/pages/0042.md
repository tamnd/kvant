We will solve the problem immediately in the general case c). Let us consider the auxiliary quadrilateral $ABCD'$, where $D'$ is a point symmetrical to $D$ with respect to the perpendicular bisector to the diagonal $AC$ (Fig. 1), and prove that its smiles at vertices $A$ and $C$ are straight.

Representing the quadrilateral $ABCD'$ as the union of triangles $ABD'$ and $CBD'$, we find that its double area is

$$AB \cdot AD' \sin \angle BAD' + BC \cdot CD' \sin \angle BCD'.$$

The double area $ABCD$ is equal to

$$AB \cdot CD + BC \cdot AD.$$

But these quadrilaterals are obviously equal in size, and $AD'=CD$, $CD'=AD$, therefore $\sin \angle BAD' = \sin \angle BCD' = 1$, that is, $\angle BAD' = \angle BCD' = \pi/2$.

From what has been proved it follows that around the quadrilateral $ABCD'$ one can describe a circle (with Diameter $BD'$). The point $D$ ($\angle ADC = \angle AD'C$) also lies on it. Let us denote by $\varphi_1$, $\varphi_2$, $\varphi_3$, $\varphi_4$ the angular values for $AB$, $BC$, $CD$, $DA$ (Fig. 2), then $AB=BD' \sin \frac{\varphi_1}{2}$, $BC=BD' \sin \frac{\varphi_2}{2}$, etc. d. It remains to express these quantities through $\alpha$ and $\beta$. According to the theorem about the size of the angle inscribed in a circle

$$2\alpha = \varphi_2 + \varphi_3, \ 2\beta = \varphi_3 + \varphi_4, \ 2\angle BCD' = \pi = \varphi_1 + \varphi_3 = 2\pi - \varphi_2 - \varphi_4$$

(since the arcs $CD'$ and $AD$ are equal). From this we obtain formulas (1). For a quadrilateral to exist, it is necessary and sufficient that all numbers $\varphi_i$ ($i=1, ..., 4$) be positive, which is equivalent to inequality (2). The answers to problems a) and b) are found after substituting the given values ​​for $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

It is easy to show that the conditions of the problem are satisfied by any quadrilaterals $ABCD$ with perpendicular diagonals around which a circle can be described, and only they. This can also be deduced from one of the vertices of the so-called Ptolemy’s theorem: for any quadrangle $A, B, C, D$ plane the inequality is true

$$AC \cdot BD \leq AB \cdot CD + AD \cdot BC,$$

which turns into a liche equality for points lying on the same circle or on the same straight line (see, for example, «Kvant» No. 3 for 1973 (p. 27)). At the same time, the last statement of this theorem can be easily proven by passing to the quadrilateral $ABCD'$.

V. L. Gutenmacher
