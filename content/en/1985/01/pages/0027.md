Which of Newton's three laws is more important?

Many people answer this question without thinking: “Of course, the second one!” The first one doesn’t even have a low formula, but the third one is simply very ideal.” However, in reality this is not entirely true.

The discovery of the laws of motion makes it possible, in a relatively short period of time, to achieve great success in explaining numerous phenomena in the surrounding world - from describing the motion of a thrown stone to calculating deviations in the motion of planets from Kepler's laws under the influence of other planets.

The first step in understanding the laws of motion was taken by Galileo Galilei, who formulated the principle of inertia. He states that a body presented to itself, that is, when no other bodies act on it or the actions of other bodies are compensated, retains its applied motion with a constant speed if it was moving before, or remains at rest if it was accumulated before. Now this statement seems very significant to us. However, since the time of Aristotle, it has been generally accepted that in the absence of external influences, a body can only accumulate, and for its movement, even at a constant speed, action from other bodies is necessary. Indeed, in an elevated life, practically always, when moving, certain forces of tremism arise, which over time transform this movement (if it is not supported by anything).

Isaac Newton went further. He answered the question of how exactly the speed of a body changes under the influence of other bodies. To do this, he formulated three laws of dynamics, called Newton's laws.

The first of them actually repeats Galileo’s principle of inertia, but in the modern formulation it includes a slightly different meaning: “There are such reporting systems, called inertial, relative to which moving bodies maintain their speed constant if they are not acted upon by other bodies or the actions of other bodies are compensated.” In other words, Newton's first law allows you to choose the most convenient reporting systems in which the second and third laws will be valid. The result is simple: combine external influences on any body and consider its movement in the selected reporting system. If it rests or moves uniformly and rectilinearly, the reporting system is inertial, but if the bodies move accelerated, the system is non-inertial and is not suitable for describing direct motion using Newton’s second law.

Newton's second law connects the magnitude of the force applied to the body with the resulting acceleration: $\vec{F} = ma$. It allows you to solve the main problem*) of mechanics - to determine the position and speed of a body at any moment in time using known forces and initial conditions (“Physics 8”, Chapter 6). In this case, force is constructed as a physical quantity that characterizes the interaction of a body. With the help of the second law, in principle, any mechanical problem can be solved. However, nothing is said about explicit expressions for forces in the laws of dynamics, but simply indicates the need to study the forces that serve the principle of changing the motion of a body. Only in its law of universal gravitation, which is not one of the fundamental laws of dynamics.

*) The inverse problem - finding velocities, accelerations and forces from the known dependence of the body’s coordinates on time - is solved much more simply. However, it should be noted that in this way it is possible to determine only the equilibrium weight applied to the body, and not each force separately.
