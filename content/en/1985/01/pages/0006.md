Peaceful professions of laser beam

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. V. TARASOV

The laser beam is used in welding and cutting of metals, in surgical operations (from large cavity to the finest operations on the retina), fine technical processes in electronics, micro-calls in thermonuclear fusion installations. Every little boy now knows the word “laser”.

President of the USSR Academy of Sciences
Academician A. P. Aleksaidrov
(from a speech at the XXVI Congress of the CPSU)

Having created a laser, man was able to unusually strongly concentrate light energy and send it in one direction or another in space. This flow of light energy can be continuous or in the form of powerful impulses following one another. You can control both the repetition rate of laser pulses and their parameters - energy, duration. The laser beam can be formed on the irradiated surface into a light spot with a diameter of up to 1-10 microns. Modern laser technology easily makes it possible to concentrate light intensity $10^5$—$10^6$ W/cm² on the surface of a matrix. In other words, scientific irradiation with a size of ~1 micron on the surface of the matrix receives energy of the order of 0.1 J every second. At such high intensities, the metals begin to float. Today it is not difficult to provide even higher light intensity: $10^6$—$10^8$ W/cm². In this case, intense evaporation of the irradiated matrix occurs.

A powerful laser beam can be a formidable weapon, but it can be successfully used for peaceful purposes. Soviet scientists and engineers have convincingly shown how many round laser beam professions there are. They have proven the effectiveness and promise of using lasers for processing materials, in medicine, in modern computer technology, in precision control and measuring devices, and for scientific research. However, among the greatest inventions of the 20th century, the laser can and should be a labor of love, not a soldier.

Within one stat it is impossible to even briefly talk about all the world's professions of a laser beam. We will limit ourselves here to only two topics: processing of materials with a laser beam and the use of lasers in medicine.
