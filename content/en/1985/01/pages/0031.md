When studying functions and constructing their graphs, it is useful to take into account the symmetry of the resulting curves. From the school course you are familiar with the properties of graphs of even and odd functions. For example, if a function $f$ is symmetric about zero, it is called even if $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in D(f)$, and odd if $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \in D(f)$. It is easy to show that the graph of the function $f$ is symmetric about the axis $Oy$ if and only if the function $f$ is even, and symmetric about the origin if and only if $f$ is an odd function. Thus, the noted properties of the graphs reflect the simple features of the corresponding formulas.

A more general way to define curves in a plane is to define them using an equation in two variables. For example, a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin is given by the equation $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$. By going through the exercises offered in this article, you will learn which properties of the equation defining curves correspond to certain types of symmetric curves. You will “discover” all the new material yourself by solving the exercises below. We will only recall the necessary information and consider one simple example.

1. The graph of an equation with two variables is the set of all points on the coordinate plane whose coordinates serve as solutions to this equation. In other words, $\Gamma$ is the graph of the equation $f(x, y) = 0$ if $M(x; y) \in \Gamma \Rightarrow f(x, y) = 0$. Note that the graph of the function $y = f(x)$ can also be considered as the graph of the equation $y - f(x) = 0$.
