To our readers

Many journals are published in our country: They are needed by mathematicians and physicists, biologists and doctors, geographers and geologists, engineers of various specialties, teachers and, of course,... schoolchildren. But the schoolchildren did not yet have their own scientific journal. The time has come to fill this gap. For schoolchildren interested in mathematics and physics, we would like «Kvant» to be their first science magazine.

Of course, we are counting on schoolchildren who are simply “getting better” at mathematics and physics or can get better. But now the country’s need is very great for young people to engage in science with passion and beyond the mandatory school requirements. In the USSR at the end of 1968 there were more than 800,000 scientific workers, and among them more than 80,000 specialists in physical and mathematical sciences. The total number of scientists, engineers, economists, etc., who must master mathematical methods and the art of physical experiment with incision and their own invention, will soon number in the millions. Our country is committed to ensuring that schoolchildren who learn

lack the ability to cope with mathematical and physical problems, they thought in a timely manner about choosing a further studied path on which their abilities and interests will not be in vain. Therefore, all opportunities will be created for successful studies in mathematics and physics. Special physics and mathematics schools are organized, additional elective classes are conducted for older schoolchildren, and physical and mathematical Olympiads are established. This is a whole case and «Kvant». The magazine will contain a lot of materials suitable for work in school circles. But you can work with the magazine yourself.

We must immediately support this word - work. In the magazine you will find both spring pages and materials for comparative_LEGACIOUS_MATHEMATICS_WORKS. But the main materials of the magazine have been expanded to include schoolchildren who will be working on it. You need to solve problems, read articles with paper and pencil in your hands, and try to reproduce the described experiments yourself.

Most of the magazine's materials are aimed at high school students, but we hope that students in grades 6-7 will also find something interesting in the magazine.

Don't be alarmed if you find yourself inaccessible and difficult. Pick up another magazine material, say goodbye, but after a while try to return to those that you initially left aside.

In conclusion, why the magazine is called «Kvant». At one time, the emergence of the idea of ​​quantum caused profound changes in physical science. We want that the appearance of the new journal will also cause greater progress and even a noticeable leap forward in the acquisition of schoolchildren to modern science. Let us repeat once again: for our hopes to become a reality, we need our readers to immediately join us in active work.

We wish you success!

Editorial collection
(«Kvant», No. 1, 1970)
