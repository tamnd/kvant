Cut and glue

If you cut a flat geometric figure into pieces and put them together into a new figure, then no one will doubt that the areas of the old and new figures are equal. This "law of conservation of area" is used on figure 1 to prove the Pythagorean theorem. Isn't it elegant?

Conversely, if given plane figures (say, polygons) are of equal area, is it always possible to cut one of them into a finite number of parts and add a second finite number of parts from them and add a second finite polygon from them. This theorem was proven in 1832 and is called the Bolyai-Gerwin theorem. Its proof can be read in the popular book by V. G. Boltyansky “Equal-sized and equal-stellar figures” (M.: Nauka, 1973). This proof is illustrated by puzzle number 4 on this spread.

Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky (1792-1856) is the pride of Russian mathematical science, the creator of non-Euclidean geometry, named after him Lobachevsky geometry. He was the first to express the idea of ​​the possibility of the existence of geometries different from the traditional geometry of Euclid. However, his works did not receive recognition from his contemporaries. You can read about the amazing history of the formation of Lobachevsky’s geometry in «Kvant» for 1982 (No. 11, p. 12 and No. 12, p. 7), and about Lobachevsky himself - in «Kvant» for 1976 (No. 2, p. 5).
