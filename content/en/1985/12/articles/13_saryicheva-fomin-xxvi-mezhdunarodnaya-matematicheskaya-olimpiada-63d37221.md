XXVI
International mathematical optimization

T. A. SARYCHEVA,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. FOMIN

XXVI International Mathematical Optimization (IMO) took place in Finland in the cities of Helsinki, Heinola and Juutsa from June 29 to July 10, 1985. The popularity of this modern event is increasing, the number of participating countries is growing constantly and quite quickly, which creates certain difficulties for the organizing countries. This year, for the first time, Iran, Iceland, the People's Republic of China, Türkiye took part in the IMO, and India was also present.

Full teams of 6 schoolchildren each took part in the IMO from 31 countries, from Italy and Kuwait - 5 each, from Spain and Tunisia - 4 each, from Iceland and China - 2 each, from Iran - 1 participant. Thus, the ability to solve problems of modern 209 schoolchildren from 38 countries in all parts of the world.

The optimization jury, consisting of team leaders, has been working since June 29 in the small picturesque town of Heinola, 140 km north of Helsinki, to draw up problems. After the modern, two-type competition on July 4 and 5, the jury members moved to the even smaller town of Yuutsu, where the schoolchildren had been staying for several days in a well-equipped hotel in a pine forest on the shore of a large lake. In Yuutse, execution and coordination of work. The optimization participants spent the last two days in Helsinki, where the closing stage of optimization, several excursions, as well as the final scales in black schoolchildren were able to demonstrate their talents in songs, dances, and jokes.

The optimization organizers coped well with all the difficulties. Particularly noteworthy is the clear and dedicated work of the chairman of the jury Illju Laine, the main coordinator Juha Oikonen, the secretary of optimization and the head of the task selection committee Matti Lehtin, and the chairman of the organizing committee Heimo Latva.

Traditionally, on each of the two days, 3 tasks were proposed for 4.5 hours of work. Each task was worth 7 points.

First day
1 (Great organization). Given a convex quadrilateral ABCD around which a circle can be drawn. Some circles centered on side AB touch the other three sides. Prove that $|AD| + |BC| = |AB|$.

2 (Australia). Given relatively prime integers n and k (0<k<n). Each of the numbers in the set M={1,2,...,n-1} is either in the head or white, so that the following conditions are met:

a) for each i from the set M, numbers i and n-i must be colored the same color;
b) for each i of M different from k, the number i and |k-i| must be painted the same color.

Prove that all numbers in the set M will be colored the same color.

3 (Inderland). For any polynomial $P(x)=a_0+a_1x+\dots+a_kx^k$ with integer coefficients, let $w(P)$ denote the number of coefficients that are odd numbers. Let $Q_i(x)=(1+x)^i$ for $i=0, 1, 2, \dots$. Prove that if the numbers of the number $i_1, i_2, \dots, i_n$ satisfy the condition $0 \leq i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_n$, then

$$w(Q_{i1}+Q_{i2}+\dots+Q_{i_n}) \geq w(Q_{i1}).$$

Second day
4 (Mongolia). Given a set M consisting of 1985 different natural numbers. The prime divisors of each number from the set M are not convertible. 26. Prove that from the set M you can choose four pairwise distinct numbers whose product is the fourth power of some integer.

5 (SSR). Given a triangle ABC and a circle with center O passing through vertices A and C, which repeatedly intersects segments AB and BC at different points K and N, respectively. The circles circumscribed around triangles ABC and KBN have exactly two common points B and M. Prove that angle OMB is right.

6 (Sweden). For each real number $x_1$ we construct the sequence $x_1, x_2, \dots$, assuming

$$x_{n+1}=x_n \cdot (x_n+\frac{1}{n})$$

The USSR team, which this year consisted of schoolchildren from various cities and different resources of our country, won in a difficult struggle one gold, two Serbian and two bronze medals.

Olga Leontyeva from Leningrad, with 36 points, entered the top six, which were loaded with personal computers in addition to gold medals. Olga was the only girl at the Olympiad who was among the first fourteen winners.

Oleg Bondarenko from Gorlovka, Donetsk region and Anatoly Malevaets from Krasny Luts, Voroshilgrad region, both students of the Physics School at KSU, received Serbian medals with 29 points each. Lev Ivanov from Saratov, a student of the Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University, topped the list of third prize-winners with 21 points, including Dmitry Bibin from Vitebsk (16 points). Mati Pentus from Tallinn (9 points) was limited to a participation diploma. Mati, who caught a cold on the eve of the Olympiad, performed below his capabilities, however, as was said at the closing ceremony, at the International Mathematical Olympiad there are winners, but there are no winners.

A subcommittee of the jury, consisting of six leaders of the country teams that proposed the problems, considered the issue of special prizes for original solutions to the problems. Although the schoolchildren found excellent solutions, including previously unknown to the jury, especially for geometric problems, the subcommittee decided not to award special solutions to the prizewinner, since none of these solutions were unique, but were repeated in a number of works.

MMOs are perfect for schoolchildren, not countries. However, it can be noted that schoolchildren from socialist countries traditionally perform very well. Among the six teams that collectively scored the most points were five teams from socialist countries: Romania (201), Hungary (168), Bulgaria (165), Vietnam (144), USSR (140). Team USA (180) also performed well. In addition, the top ten teams are Germany (139), East Germany (136), France (125), Great Britain (121).

It was clear to everyone who attended the closing ceremony at the University of Helsinki, who saw the happy faces of the winners and heard warm applause, what a big role IMOs play in the development of mathematics education in the world, in building confidence and other international relations.

The next, XXVII International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren will take place in Warsaw in July 1986.
