The XVI International Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics will take place in Yugoslavia from June 23 to 30 in the resort town of Portorz on the Adriatic Sea.

Teams from 20 countries are approaching to participate in the Olympiad: Australia, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Iceland, Canada, Cuba, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Romania, the Soviet Union, Turkey, Finland, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia. For the first time in the Olympics, the teams of Canada and Turkey accepted the teaching. In addition, observers from Italy, China and the USA were represented at the Olympiad.

According to the international statue of the Physics Olympiad, each country can send a team of five schoolchildren. The USSR team included*):
Victor Barzykin - graduate of the village. sh No. 82, Chernogolovka village, Moscow region,
Georgy Grigoriev is a graduate of Physics School No. 2 in Kyiv,
Yuri Zhestkov is a graduate of the Russian Federal Medical School in Almaty,
Taras Ivanovko - graduate of the village. sh No. 145, Kyiv,
Olets Chern - graduate of the village. sh No. 50 Minsk.
The team leaders were the authors of this article - research associates of the Research Institute of Content and Teaching Methods of the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences.

The theoretical round of the Olympiad was held in Portorz, and the experimental round in the city of Kohler.

Scientists from the University of Ljubljana were also involved in preparing the tasks and conducting the theoretical type.

Clarification of the conditions of the tasks, their solutions and evaluation criteria, as well as approval of the results of the verification of the work, was carried out at meetings of the International Commission, which, along with the presentation of the Olympiad and the Secretary General, included the leaders of the team of all countries.

The Olympiad participants were asked to solve 3 theoretical problems (in 5 hours) and complete 2 experimental problems (in 4 hours). Here are their conditions*):

Theoretical tour

Experimental tour

Task 1
Determine the position of the centers and the orientation of the magnets hidden in a box painted black. The magnet is shown at figure 1; its x, y and z coordinates should be varied from the angle placed in red on figure 2. Determine the component of the magnetic induction vector $\vec{B}$ in the XY plane using a previously calibrated measuring system. Determine the maximum value of magnetic induction created by your magnet.

Equipment: permanent magnet, the same as those hidden in the box; voltage source (0-24 V); measuring coil (1400 turns, R=230 Ohm); black box with hidden magnets; two coils to create a magnetic field (8800 turns each, R=990 Ohm); voltmeter (with measurement ranges of 1 V, 3 V, 10 V, recommended for use); electronic circuit; ammeter; variable resistance ($R_{\max}=3,3$ kOhm); connecting wires; plate with holes for attaching the coil; rubber rings for various uses, for example for attaching a reel; wooden sticks for the same purposes; ruler; threads

Instructions
Any non-destructive magnetic method can be used for research. The final answer should contain the measurement results, formula, graphs and figure (the latter should be used together with the annotator where possible).

The correct use of the measuring system is shown in figure 3. When coil 1 is connected via electronic circuit 2 to voltmeter 3, the maximum deflection of the voltmeter needle is proportional to the change in magnetic flux through the coils.

Task 2
Explore the process of acceleration and deceleration of a copper disk rotated by an AC motor. By measuring the time of half a revolution of the disk, find the dependence of the angle of rotation, angular velocity and angular acceleration of the disk on time. Determine the torque and power of motor as a function of angular velocity.

Equipment: AC motor with switch and copper disk; inductive sensor; multi-drop stopwatch with computer.

Instructions
The indicator sensor registers the movement of the iron rods on the disc when they are close to 0.5 mm from it, and sends a signal to the stopwatch. The stopwatch is connected to the computer in such a way that it records the moment in time when the iron rod passes under the sensor and remembers it. To turn on the stopwatch, you need to press the “5” and “6” buttons on the computer control panel in sequence.

The computer displays the results in graphical form. The vertical axis represents the time intervals between two successive moments of recording the iron rod, and the horizontal axis represents the interval numbers.

When you press the “7” button, the computer displays the measurement results in the form of a table. The first column of the table shows the measurement number, the second - the time elapsed since the start of the measurement, the third - the presented time intervals between subsequent moments of passage of the iron rods near the induction sensor.

Each theoretical and experimental problem was worth 10 points.

For the first problem of theoretical research, 12 sections received 10 points (3 from the USSR), for the second only one section (from the USSR) received 9.5 points (the highest score), for the third 2 sections received 10 points (1 from the USSR). The absolute best result after the theoretical test was from Czechoslovakian schoolboy P. Spaniel - 29 points. G. Grigoriev had the second result - 27.5 points. Next came T. Ivanenko and R. Badami (Great Britain) - 27 points each. The best team result after completing the theoretical tasks was the USSR team - 121.0, the teams of Germany and Great Britain, who took 2nd and 3rd places, had 99.5 and 94.5 points, respectively.

The maximum number of points for the first experimental task was received by Schoolboy from Sweden H. Svenson (8.5), for the second - Schoolboy from Great Britain D. Mackay (9.5) and from Romania C. Malureanu
