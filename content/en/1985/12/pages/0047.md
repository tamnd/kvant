Lattice intersection

Let us mark points with integer coordinates on the coordinate plane $Oxy$—lattice nodes. Let's place a sheet of thick paper on this plane and intersect a lattice of nodes onto it (in a different color). Let us now rotate this second lattice at some angles $\alpha$ around the point $O=(0;0)$ so that the condition is satisfied: the non-octoral node of the lattice surface (different from $O$) coincides with the node of the original one.

For example, at figure 1 node (1; 3) of the black lattice coincides with node (-1; 3) of the blue one. At the same time, many other nodes coincide - they are marked in red, and the intersection of two lattices itself turns out to be a larger lattice (in the article by A. L. Petelin and A. I. Fedoseev it is called a “lattice of coinciding nodes”, or RSU for short), that is, node $M$ belongs to both lattices if $\overrightarrow{OM}=k\overrightarrow{p}+l\overrightarrow{q}$, where $k$ and $l$ - any integers, and $\overrightarrow{p}=(2;1)$ and $\overrightarrow{q}=(-1;2)$ are the basis vectors》（sides of a square cell of the red lattice).
