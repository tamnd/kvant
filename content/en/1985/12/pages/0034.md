F948. It is known that in a small “gap” between cars standing at the sidewalk, it is recommended to drive in reverse, rather than forward. Why?

Let the car drive into a small gap between two cars standing at the sidewalk (see Fig.nok). Let's pose the question this way: how is it easier for the car to leave now - forward or reverse?

When you turn the steering wheel, the front wheels immediately turn, while the rear wheels initially maintain the same orientation; so that during any maneuvering of the car, the center of the circle along which it is currently moving (forward or backward) always lies on a straight line passing through the center of the rear wheels.

figure shows the trajectory along which the car can exit the gap. It can be seen that when driving in reverse, it is more likely to back up the neighboring car than when driving forward.

It is easy to understand that the departure of a car is an entry reversed in time. Therefore, vehicles should drive into a small gap in reverse.
