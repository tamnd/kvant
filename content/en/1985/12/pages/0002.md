⟦folio 10⟧

On the top left splice there is a cross section of a copper splice. A similar structure appears during the solidification of the melt. The photo on the right (magnification 200) shows a different structure. It arose during the rolling of the match - the grains-crystals are elongated along the rolling direction. The bottom photo, taken using an electron microscope (magnification 1000), shows a picture of destruction caused by the presence of “dangerous” impurities in the metal, accumulating at the boundaries between crystalline grains.

What role do boundaries between grains play in the life of a metal? This question is the subject of an article by A.L. Petelin and A.I. Fedoseev in this issue of the journal.
