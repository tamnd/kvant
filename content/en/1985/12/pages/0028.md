Tasks

M956—M960; F968—F972

M956. Four circles of the same radius are drawn on the plane so that three of them pass through point A and three through point B (Fig. 1). Prove that the four points of their pairwise intersection, other than A and B, are the vertices of a parallelogram.

V. and I. Kapovits, 10th grade students

M957. Prove that from 1985 different natural numbers, all of whose prime factors are contained among the first 9 prime numbers 2,3,...,23, you can choose four numbers whose product is the fourth power of an integer.

M958*. Let $0 \leq i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_n$ be integers. Prove that the number of odd coefficients of a polynomial

$$(1+x)^{i_1}+(1+x)^{i_2}+\dots+(1+x)^{i_n}$$

no less than that of the polynomial $(1+x)^{i_1}$.

M959*. There are air connections between some cities in the country. Prove that it is possible to close no more than $1/(k-1)$ part of the airlines in such a way that among any $k$ cities there are two that are not connected by airlines if $k=3$; 6) $k$ - any natural number.

A. A. Razborov

M960*. If the difference between the cubes of two consecutive natural numbers is the square of some natural number $n$, then the number $n$ is represented as the sum of the squares of two consecutive natural numbers.

a) Prove this statement.

6) Bot is one example of such numbers: $8^3-7^3=(2^2+3^2)^2$; Give at least one more example.

b) Prove that there are infinitely many such examples.

P. Lyness (UK)
