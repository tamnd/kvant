M906. a) Prove that for any natural number a the equation
$$\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} = \frac{1}{a}$$

M907. For triangle ABC with side lengths a=BC, b=AC, c=AB it is known that $3\hat{A} + 2\hat{B} = 180^\circ$. Prove that $a^2 + bc - c^2 = 0$.

T. A. Dzhortmenadze

M908. On side AB of triangle ABC, point P is selected, and lines parallel to BC and AC are drawn through it until they intersect with sides AC and BC, respectively, at points M and N. For which choice of point P does segment MN have the shortest length?
Solve this problem a) for a triangle with right angle C; 6)* for an arbitrary triangle ABC.

E. G. Gotman

M909. a) Prove that there is an arithmetic progression of 4 different terms containing only powers of natural numbers $n^k (k \geqslant 2)$. Does such a progression exist from 6) any finite number, c) an infinite number of terms?
Is there an infinite (non-constant) arithmetic progression that does not contain d) any power of a natural number, e) not a single number composed of identical digits?

P. H. Azizyan, V. Tolstykh, A. V. Alleyev

M910. The points $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_6$ (Fig. 1) are taken on the sides of a regular hexagon. It is known that three pairwise non-funny sides of the hexagon $A_1 \dots A_6$ ($A_1A_2, A_3A_4, A_5A_6$) define the triangle KLM, the vertices of which lie on the extensions of the diagonals of a regular hexagon. Prove that this is also true for the other three sides of the hexagon $A_1 \dots A_6$.

S. Yu. Orevkov

F919. figure 3 shows the fractional cross-section of the device using length as a standard. F920. Air is blown through a variable cross-section tube. The inlet hole of the tube has an area of ​​$S_1$, and the outlet has an area of ​​$S_2$. G. L. Kotkin

F922. Photographing the ants at close range, the experimenter used an extension ring that increased the distance from the film to the subject by $\Delta l=7,5$ mm.
