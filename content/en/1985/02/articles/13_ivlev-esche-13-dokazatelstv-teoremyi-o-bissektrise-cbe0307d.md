13 more proofs of the bicsectrix theorem

In the magazine «Kvant» No. 8 for 1983, a note by S. R. Sefi-bekov “Four proofs of the bicsectrix theorem” was published. The editors have received a number of letters from readers providing new proofs of this theorem. The basis of this note was the materials sent by S.G. Guba. In addition, the evidence indicated by A.V. Alyaev, A.G. Goldberg, V.N. Dubov, A.I. Mostov was used.

Let us recall the conditions of the theorem.
In any triangle, the bicsectrix of the interior angle divides the opposite side into segments proportional to the adjacent sides. In other words, if triangle ABC contains the bicsectrix CD, then AD:BD=AC:BC.

Let us present seven more proofs of this theorem, six of which are quite accessible to a seventh-grader after completing the topic “Transformations of Figures.”

Obviously, if AC=BC, then the theorem is true. Therefore, we will assume that AC≠BC. Let, for example, AC>BC. then the bicsectrix CD cannot be redistributed to the side AB, since otherwise the right triangles CAD and CBD would be equal in leg and adjacent acute angle, from which it would follow that AC = BC.

First proof. Let us construct a point E on the ray CD such that AE=AD (Fig. 1). Then ∠AEC=∠ADE=∠CDB. Therefore, triangles ACE and BCD are similar (at two angles). This means that AE:BD=AC:BC. Taking into account that AE=AD, we get AD:BD=AC:BC.

Second proof. Let us construct a point E on the ray CD such that AE=AC (Fig. 2). Then ∠AED=∠ACD, that is, triangles AED and BCD are similar. From their similarity we have AD:BD=AE:BC. Since AE=AC, the theorem is proven. Construction option - checking through point A a straight line parallel to CB until it intersects with straight line CD at point E.

Third proof. From vertices A and B we lower the redistributions AE and BF onto the straight line CD (Fig. 3). From the similarity of right triangles ADE and BDF we obtain AD:BD=AE:BF. At the same time, from the similarity of right triangles ACE and BCF we will have AC:BC=AE:BF. In the resulting proportions, the right ratios are equal, therefore the left ones are equal, that is, AD:BD=AC:BC.
