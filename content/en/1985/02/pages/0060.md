No matter how sophisticated the geometers were - and among them there were also outstanding scientists such as Mercator, Euler, Gauss, Chebyshev, Lambert - they were not able to come up with a theoretically perfect way to construct a flat map of a spherical surface. This is not surprising.

Take an ordinary sheet of paper and try to wrap it around the surface of the globe - either folds will immediately appear, or the paper will tear. Why? The imposition of a plane on a sphere (as opposed to, say, a cylinder or cone) is followed by... a mathematical theorem stating that the total curvature of a surface does not change with change; for a sphere it is positive, for a plane (as well as for a cylinder and a cone) it is zero. That is why it is impossible to turn the sphere onto a plane and make a map of the Earth without distortion.
