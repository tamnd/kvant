A speaking tube the length of the equator?

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. VARLAMOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. I. MALYAROVSKY

Sitting in the kitchen at breakfast, you can often hear a not very pleasant rattling sound, which some joker made by “singing water pipes.” You can sometimes stop this “communal music playing” by opening the water tap in your apartment. Most people then return with relief to their over-stuffed breakfast, without particularly thinking about the physical nature of the incident. And it's worth asking. Why does the sound generated by the flow of water in a faulty tap in one of the apartments haunt the inhabitants of the entire entrance, connected by one water riser?

After all, if you blow a whistle in the same apartment, then only the neighbors in the nearest apartments will hear it, and even then if the house is new. The “singing” of the water pipe can be heard everywhere, from the first to the last floor.

This difference is due to two reasons. The first is the sound-absorbing effect of walls and ceilings, which reflect and absorb the sounds of a whistle. And the sound wave propagating through the water pipe does not interact with it and passes from floor to floor free of charge. Now - about the second reason. When the whistle sounds, the resulting accutic wave propagates in space in all directions, and its wave surface (see “Physics 10”, § 31), or wave front, has the shape of a sphere. The area of ​​this sphere increases as it moves away from the sound source, and the intensity of the sound wave - that is, the energy passing through a unit area of ​​the wave front per unit time - will decrease as it moves away from the source (find out for yourself by what law).

And the sound wave that arises in a water pipe is “one-dimensional”: when reflected from the walls of the pipe, it does not diverge in all directions in space, but propagates in one direction - along the pipe itself - without distribution of the wave front, so that the intensity of the sound does not change as it moves away from the source of practice. In this sense, the water pipe is an accutic waveguide - a channel in which sound waves propagate in a certain direction of practice without attenuation.

Another example of an Akkutichi waveguide is the speaking pipes, with the help of which, from ancient times to the present day, commands have been transmitted on ships from the captain's bridge to the engine room. Note that the attenuation of sound in the air when propagating along a waveguide turns out to be so small that if it were possible to make such a tube 700 km long, it could serve as a kind of “telephone” for transmitting a conversation from Moscow to Leningrad and back.
