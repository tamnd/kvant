About Lagrange's theorem

There may be more than one point $x=c$ for which equality (1) holds. You can verify this by doing the following exercise.

1. Find all values ​​of $x=c$ for which the function $f(x)=(x-1)^3$, $a=0$, $b=2$ satisfies equality (1). (Answer: $c=1-1/\sqrt{3}$ and $c=1+1/\sqrt{3}$.)

We would like to introduce you to the mechanical interpretation of Lagrange's theorem. Let a material point move in a straight line and at the moment $t$ have coordinate $s(t)$. then its average speed over the time interval from $t_1$ to $t_2$ will be equal to $(s(t_2)-s(t_1))/(t_2-t_1)$. It is well known that the instantaneous speed at time $t$ is equal to $s'(t)$. It is quite clear that at some points in time the instantaneous speed will be greater than the average speed, at others - less. However, it is necessary to find such a moment in time (and there may be more than one!) such that the average speed will be equal to the instantaneous speed. Therefore, for some $t_0 \in ]t_1; t_2[$

$(s(t_2)-s(t_1))/(t_2-t_1)=s'(t_0)$, that is $s(t_2)-s(t_1)=s'(t_0)(t_2-t_1)$. (Compare this formula with formula (1).)

Note also that in the case where $f$ is not arbitrary at any point lying between $a$ and $b$, Lagrange’s theorem cannot be applied: consider the functions $f(x)=|x^2-1|$, $a=1/2$, $b=\sqrt{7}/2$ as an example.
