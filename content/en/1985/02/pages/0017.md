Let us consider a stack of plane-parallel plates with different refractive indices $n_0, n_1, n_2, \dots, n_k, \dots$, and $n_0 < n_1 < n_2 < \dots < n_k < \dots$ (figure 2). A ray incident from the topmost plate onto plate 1 at an angle of $a_0$, after refraction at the boundary 0-1, will make an angle of $a_1$ with normal to this boundary; at this angle it will fall on the boundary 1-2; after refraction at this boundary, the ray, having passed plate 2, falls on the boundary 2-3 at an angle $a_2$, refracts again, etc. According to the law of refraction,

$$\frac{\sin a_0}{\sin a_1} = \frac{n_1}{n_0}, \frac{\sin a_1}{\sin a_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1},$$
$$\frac{\sin a_2}{\sin a_3} = \frac{n_3}{n_2}, \dots, \frac{\sin a_{k-1}}{\sin a_k} = \frac{n_k}{n_{k-1}}, \dots$$
($c_0 > c_1 > c_2 > \dots > c_k > \dots$). Variable sequentially these equalities to each other, we get the correspondence

$$\frac{\sin a_0}{\sin a_k} = \frac{c_0}{c_k}.$$

By directing the thickness of each plate to zero, and the number of plates to infinity, we arrive at a generalization of the law of refraction, which describes the path of a light ray in a medium with a smoothly varying refractive index (this law is called the generalized Snell’s law):

$$c(z) \cdot \sin \alpha(0) = c(0) \cdot \sin \alpha(z),$$

where $c(0)$ is the speed of light at the point where the beam enters the medium, and $c(z)$ is the speed of light at a distance $z$ from the boundaries of the medium. With such a limiting transition, the broken line showing the path of the ray turns into a smooth curve. The phenomenon of bending of light rays as light propagates in a medium with a continuously changing refractive index is called refraction.

Knowing how the speed of light changes in the medium, we can use the correspondence (*), which indicates what the path of a particular ray entering the medium will be. In the same way, according to the same law (*), curvature of the bite rays occurs when the bite spreads in an inexpensive environment, where the bite speed smoothly changes. A special case of such a medium is the ocean.

Now let's return to the question of how sound propagates in the PZK. Let's imagine that the sound source is at a depth of $z_m$ (figure 3). What will be the sound rays emerging from the source? The ray going along the horizontal $z = z_m$ will be rectilinear. And those rays that come out at odd angles to this horizontal will be bent. By analogy, this phenomenon is called refraction of sound. Since both above and below the level $z_m$ the bite speed returns, the back beams will bend in the direction of the horizontal $z = z_m$; at some point the ray becomes “parallel” to this horizontal line and, having been “reflected”, turns towards it (see figure 3).
