How to answer the questions asked?

Essentially, the entire explanation of the property is an answer to the first question - why sparkling water resembles a superheated liquid.

Probably many of you know how to make sparkling water. No, not in an industrial way, but in an ordinary home way - using a siphon and carbon dioxide (CO₂) canisters. Gas from the canister under pressure enters the space above the liquid. In order for it to quickly dissolve in water, a siphon was met. Dissolution of the gas occurs before equilibrium is reached.

If it does not react chemically with the liquid, then the equilibrium content (density, concentration) of the gas in the liquid is proportional to its pressure above the surface.*)

(Other home-made methods for preparing “spongie” are also known - by adding lemon juice, drinking juice, etc. to water.)

When we quickly open a bottle of sparkling water, the gas under pressure above the surface expands rapidly and cools at the same time. The water vapor released along with it condenses, forming a foggy cloud (smoke). After the pressure above the surface decreases significantly, carbon dioxide gas dissolved in water begins to leave it, both through the gas-liquid surface and through the formation of bubbles in the volume of the liquid. At first, after opening the bottle, when the balance is particularly disturbed and there is a greater excess of carbon dioxide in the water, bubbles form easily and there are many of them. Therefore, the liquid begins to foam. Gradually the foam subsides, although there is still “extra” gas in the water. But now the formation of bubbles is difficult - the concentration of gas molecules has become smaller and randomly works together, and it is difficult to immediately form a bubble that would not be crushed by the force of the surface of the tension surface. Bubbles begin to grow only on imperfections in the water, and this is the actual position on the surface of the surface.

*) In the case of carbon dioxide and water, the situation is somewhat more complicated, since they react with each other. But the resulting acid is an unstable compound and breaks down easily.
