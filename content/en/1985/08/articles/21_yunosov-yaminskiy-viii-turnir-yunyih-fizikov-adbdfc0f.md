Captains and Fans Competition

Captains performed these tasks with two mates. Fans worked inductively or in groups and sent answers to one of the finalist teams. The time to complete each task is 5 minutes.

Most of the problems in this competition were proposed by school teams that competed in the second type of tournament.

1. "Voltmeters". In the electrical diagram shown at figure, all voltmeters are the same.

The testimony of two is known: $U_1=10$ B, $U_2=-6$ B. What are the testimony of the other two?

2. “Bus” (problem proposed by school No. 179). Why are the rear stacks of a bus usually much dirtier than the side stacks?

3. "Paradoxical collision." After a loving collision of a brass cylinder of mass $m$ with a stationary billiard ball of mass $2m$, the cylinder stops and the ball is deflected at an angle $\beta<\frac{\alpha}{2}$.

![Diagram of a pulley system with weights and a spring](image)

Propose a model of the internal structure of the cylinder.

4. “Ignition wave” (proposed by school No. 679 and recognized as the best problem).

The sleepy city lies entirely in darkness, the bright moon does not look from the heavens. On the bolshar, with that dark kidney, a dangerous bandit lurks.

The long street is illuminated by electric incandescent lamps, connected in parallel to a power source. Why do light bulbs further away from the source light up later?

5. "Smerch" (school No. 43). Why does a whirlpool form when draining the bathtub? Which direction does the water spin?

6. "Laser" (school No. 542). The laser beam is directed horizontally onto a flat transparent tray (aquarium) with water perpendicular to the walls of the tray. If the beam passes above or below the surface of the water in the vessel, then a spot from the laser beam can be observed on the screen behind the vessel. If the laser beam passes along the surface of the water, then a vertical stripe is observed on the screen. Explain the origin of the strip and calculate its parameters.

7. “TV” (school No. 57). In some feature films in which the characters are watching TV, you can notice that a wide dark horizontal stripe sometimes runs across the TV screen. Why and for which televisions is it observed, in which direction (up or down) does it strike and with what frequency? What is its width relative to the width of the screen and the degree of darkness?

8. "Jar." Any plastic jar with a sufficiently large and tight-fitting lid (but without threads) is suitable for the experiment. At the tournament, a jar with a volume of 0.4 liters and a lid diameter of 8 cm was demonstrated. When the jar is rolled sharply from the sides, the lid comes off with a characteristic pop and flies quite far. Estimate the magnitude of the maximum force acting on the lid in this experiment.

I round - correspondence collective competition. Starting this year, the correspondence competition becomes all-Union. Solutions to the problems of TYUV-VIII, published below, can be sent no later than November 30, 1985 to the address: 119899, ​​Moscow, GSP, Moscow State University, Faculty of Physics, Council for Work with Schoolchildren, Organizing Committee of TUV-VIII. Assignments for the correspondence collective competition TUF-VIII

Most of the tasks are formed on the basis of specific physical phenomena and are designed to conduct server-side theoretical and experimental research, going beyond the “school” approach. The conditions of the problem are formed as briefly as possible and allow for various interpretations and degrees of simplification.

1. “Come up with it yourself.” Create a task-problem yourself and solve it.

2. "Anchor". How to declare that an anchor weighing 5 tons reliably holds a ship weighing 10 thousand tons?

3. "Poisson's ratio." It is known that any longitudinal deformation of a solid body also causes its transverse deformation. Explain this phenomenon. Propose a theoretical calculation of Poisson's ratio for metals. Conduct experimental and theoretical studies on the rubber rod.

4. "Double glazing". In residential premises in the central zone of the USSR, it is customary to install double frames on windows. Do you think we should switch to using triple frames?

5. "Oil film" “In 1757 I was on board one of the 96 ships of the flotilla sailing from Leesburg. I noticed that the surface of the water around the ships was surprisingly smooth, while around the others there was a strong disturbance caused by the sharp gusts of wind. Puzzled by the table at the strange distinction, I finally turned to the ship and asked what it could mean. “The cooks,” he said, “just like me, just poured greasy water through the scupper, and it got a little greasy on the sides of these ships.” In the captain’s intonation there was a slight shade of contempt expressed by a person who does not know some generally known truths.” (Venjamin Franklin)

Explain the phenomenon noticed by scientists. Conduct experiments on pouring oil onto a rough surface of water.

6. "Self-oscillations." Many people know a children's toy: a long duckling bends down to a trough of water and, showing itself, “tortures” the water, then throws it away from the trough, but after a while it bends down again and “tortures,” etc. From a physics point of view, this is an example of self-oscillations that occur in the presence of an external source of energy.

Design and make a visual device or toy that demonstrates self-oscillations.

7. "Surface Charge". It is known that two diopoles located at a distance r from each other, as shown in figure, come together with a force F~p₀²/r⁴, where p₀=ql is the diopole moment.

How will the diopoles interact if their moments are variable in time: p₁(t)=p₂(t)=p₀sinωt?

9. "The power of a magnet." Experimentally study the distance dependence of the force of attraction of a steel ball to the pole of a position magnet. Explain the results. Specifically study the behavior of magnetized and non-magnetized balls.
