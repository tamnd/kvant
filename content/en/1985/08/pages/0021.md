⟦folio 19⟧

The temperature of the air, and therefore of the benches in a well-steamed room, is from 80 to 120 °C. Upon contact, the process of heat transfer from a hot body (skama) to a cold body (person) begins. How fast is the process? This is determined, in particular, by the thermal conductivity of the hot body. The greater the thermal conductivity of a material, the faster heat is transferred from its more heated areas to less heated ones. Iron, like other metals, has a significantly higher thermal conductivity than wood - about 300 times. When we come into contact with a hot wooden bench, the part that is in close contact cools down, and the heat “taken away” from the small volume of the bench is transferred to us. It’s a different matter if there is a nail in the contact zone: heat will quickly be “pulled” to the points of contact from the entire length of the nail. In addition, the volumetric heat capacity (that is, the heat capacity calculated per unit volume) of iron is approximately 40 times greater than that of wood; so, under the same conditions, we will receive tens of times more heat from a piece of iron than from a piece of wood of the same volume.
