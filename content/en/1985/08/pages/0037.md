It would seem that as a result of this, water should flow to the “lunar” side of the Earth, where a water hump is formed. Since the Earth rotates around its axis, in each place the water will rise (high tide) and fall (low tide) once a day. However, in reality, both high and low tides occur twice a day. Why is this so?

The reason is that the Earth and Moon are not stationary relative to each other. Imagine two balls - a heavy one and a light one - connected by a thread and lying on a smooth horizontal surface. A light ball can be brought into rotation around it; it will not remain in place - its center will also move around the circle, albeit of a small radius (see figure 2). Only a certain point O remains motionless on a line along the stretched thread, located near the heavy ball (it is called the center of mass of the system). Around this point both the heavy and light balls will rotate.

In the same way, the Earth and the Moon rotate around a certain point, which, due to the fact that the Earth is much more massive than the Moon, even turns out to lie inside the Earth (but not at its center). The center of the Earth rotates around a point, and the water on the surface of the Earth is thrown away from the center of rotation. This leads to the formation of a hump of water on the side of the Earth opposite the Moon. The surface of the ocean takes on a distant shape, elongated in the direction of the Moon and (slightly less) in the exact opposite direction. Since the Earth rotates around its own axis, a “double-humped” tidal wave “runs” along the surface of the Earth. In those areas that appear after the redinema between the humps, differences are observed.

Studying tides presents many challenges. The simplified model we considered is far from the real picture. The Earth, as you know, is not a sphere, it is flattened at the poles; water is not distributed evenly over its surface. The Moon, as it moves through its orbit, finds itself at different distances from the Earth. The Sun also contributes to the tidal force. And the relative positions of the Sun, Earth and Moon change periodically. These and many other factors significantly affect the real picture of the circulation and movement of tidal waves and can mix up the positions of humps in the waters, change their values, etc.

But the main thing is now clear to us: the cause of the ebb and flow of the tides is universal gravity.

It can be said with exaggeration that even in ancient times, priests knew how to calculate the time of tides. Otherwise, how could they create “miracles”, which are described, for example, in the tales of Ancient Egypt: “...When they reached the vessel of the “Two Fishes” channel, they saw that the shallows were exposed and it was impossible to advance further. Then His Majesty Pharaoh called Djedi and commanded... And Djedi began to cast magical spells over the water. At his word, the water in the canal rose and covered the shallows with a layer of four cubits. And Pharaoh’s ships moved on...”
