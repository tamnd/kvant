This means that the forces that hold the planets in their orbits in one case may be imperceptibly small in another case.

Let us write the law of universal gravitation in the form of a formula, introducing a coefficient of proportionality (G) connecting the gravitational force (F) of two bodies with their masses ($m_1$ and $m_2$) and the distance between them (r):

$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}.$$

(*)

To calculate the force of mutual attraction between two bodies, you need to know what the coefficient G is, that is, know with what force two bodies with masses equal to one are attracted to each other at a distance equal to one.

This coefficient can only be determined experimentally. It is called the gravitational constant (“gravity” is a Latin word, translated into Russian as “gravity”). This is the first constant in the history of physics (“constant” in Latin), the first in the family of Constants, which are called universal, that is, the same for all bodies of nature. Newton introduced this constant into science, but he did not determine its meaning.

The difficulties in determining the constant of gravity are due to the fact that the gravitational attraction of bodies used in laborator experiments is “hopelessly” small, and the most subtle techniques are needed to measure it.
