When flying through the capacitor again, the particle again acquires additional energy $\Delta W$, in the next cycle - another $\Delta W$, etc. We “invented” a cyclic accelerator that does not require an energy source. A little more imagination - and we will design a so-called engine that operates without consuming energy!

We arrived at this erroneous result because we did not take into account one of the fundamental properties of the electrostatic field - its potential nature. The potentiality of the electrostatic field means that if we transfer a charged body from one point in space to another, then the work of electric forces does not depend on the shape of the path, but is determined only by the positions of the starting and ending points. It follows that if a charged body is transferred along a closed trajectory, then when returning to the starting point, the total work done by the electric forces goes to zero. (Recall that the gravitational field also has the same property.) It is this property that allows us to introduce the concepts of potential energy and potential.

In our case, when a charged particle moves inside a capacitor, the electric field accelerates it, and outside the capacitor it slows it down, and when the particle approaches the response in the first plate, its energy takes on its previous value. This means that in the example under consideration we fundamentally cannot handle the field outside the flat-plate capacitor. Of course, there is a significant weak field between the plates, but in it the particle flies through the city to a greater distance; As a result, in the decelerating field outside the capacitor, it loses exactly the energy that was imparted to it by the accelerating field inside the capacitor.
