The school textbook says that bodies, including conductive ones, located outside the capacitor have practically no effect on its capacitance. Most often this is the case. For example, let another conducting uncharged plate “a” (Fig. 2) be located behind the plate of a flat capacitor. This plate will not change the field inside the capacitor, and its capacitance remains the same. Let us now bring another plate to the capacitor - “6” and place it in front of the second plate of the capacitor. And this plate does not affect the capacitance of the capacitor.

Now let's connect the outer uncharged plates with a conductor. Our capacitor appears to be placed inside a conducting box. What will happen?

Many applicants argue that in this case, the field between the plates of the capacitor will not change (since the outer plates were not charged) and, therefore, the capacitance of the capacitor will not change either. Meanwhile, the circuit shown in figure 2, with the outer plates closed, is equivalent to a circuit of three capacitors (Fig. 3). If all the spaces between the plates are the same, then the em-capacity of such a complex capacitor is equal to $C = \frac{3}{2} C_0$, where $C_0$ is the initial em-capacity.
