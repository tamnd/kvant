CHESS
ON PARALLEL BOARDS

Chess is known to develop logical thinking, combinatory abilities, intuition, and geometric imagination. We have been convinced of this more than once, considering various geometric additions on the chessboard, non-educational chess games and problems. Here is another example - chess on parallel boards. This idea of ​​“parallel worlds,” often using science fiction, has not escaped chess composers.

The game is played on two boards located one above the other. On each of them, the moves are normal, but the pieces can also be mixed in space - from one board to another. The queen enters the space in the same way as the king, and the pawn is cut to change plane only when captured. The starting position is placed on the bottom board as in regular chess, diagrams are drawn on two boards, and in the position entry the board number (1 or 2) is indicated in parentheses. When unloading the batch, the figures sending into space can be placed on inverted glasses standing on the bottom board, however, we hope that in the problems under consideration, geometric imagination allows you to get around without special devices.

White: Kra7(1), Qb7(2), Rb3(2); black: Kra3(1). Checkmate in 2 moves.

1. Rb1(2)! The rook remained on the top board, but cleared the way for the queen. 1...Npb4 (b3, b2)(1) 2. Qb4 (b3, b2)(2)×, on the file a(1, 2) the king is checkmated by the queen from the same squares.

Checkmate in 2 moves.

1. Qf7(1)! The queen returns to the first plane, and the black king cannot follow his example (all squares are at risk). 1...Kp:f3(2) 2. Qf3(1)×, 1...Kpf4(2) 2. Qf4 (1)×, 1...Kpf5(2). 2. Qf5(1)×, 1...Kpd5(2) 2. Qd5(1)×.

To figure out the next problem, you will have to scan the space very carefully.

1. Cooperative checkmate in 2 moves.

In the problem of cooperative checkmate, Black starts and helps White checkmate his king. 1. Rc8(2)! Only in parallel chess can the c8 rook, surrounded on all sides, disappear without interfering with the king's castling. 1...d8(2)K! 2.0—0—0(1)! L:c8(2)×.
