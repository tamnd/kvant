About universal gravitation, ebbs and flows

Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences
N. A. RODINA

One of the books on travel describes the following gradient phenomenon: during high tides, powerful streams of water rush into the lagoon of one of the Aldabra islands (these islands lie in the Gulf of the Indian Ocean off the eastern coast of Africa); in the pier connecting the lagoon with the ocean, the current speed reaches 25 km/h, and the mass of inflowing water is several billion tons. At low tides, the water rushes back into the ocean, almost two-thirds of the lagoon is exposed, and thousands of birds roam around the lagoon, looking for prey.

What colossal forces “splash out” water from the lagoon at low tide and raise it during high tide? These are the forces of universal gravity.

It is often said that Newton came to the idea of ​​the existence of attractive forces between all bodies of nature, observing the fall of an apple to the Earth.

But the discovery and study of gravity has a complex history.

Even the scientists of the first Greek schools (V-IV centuries BC) argued that “like tends to unite with like.” Throughout the Middle Ages, this idea was supported by the analgoria with the attraction of magnets.

We met many wonderful names while studying the history of the formation of the doctrine of universal gravitation. This is Tycho Brahe, who observed the planets of the solar system for twenty years and left numerous accurate data on their movement, and Johannes Kepler, who established the laws of planetary motion on the basis of these data and expressed the idea that the attraction of the Moon around the Earth is attraction, and Galileo Galilei, who discovered the satellites of Jupiter, and much more. But we will turn to the work of Newton, who discovered the law of universal gravitation.

For Newton, space served as a “laboratory”; the bodies from which he conducted “experiments” were the Sun and the planets of the solar system. Studying data on the movement of the planets, he came to the conclusion that in all cases the body located in the center (the Sun in relation to the planets and the planets in relation to its satellites) acts on the body revolving around it with a certain force, which keeps the body in orbit. A very important and decisive conclusion for establishing the law of universal gravitation was the conclusion he made: the force holding a celestial body in orbit is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the central body.

And the next position established by Newoton says: “Gravity is essential in all bodies in general and is proportional to the mass of each of the bodies.”

So, all bodies are attracted to each other. But if gravity is a property of all bodies, then why in our offended life do we replace only one of its manifestations - the attraction of bodies by the Earth (and we replace it mistakenly when we carry a heavy load!)? Why, sitting at a party next to a friend, do we not feel mutual affection?
