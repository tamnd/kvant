The most likely candidates for non-freezing liquids are hydrogen and helium. In these lightest substances, the energy of zero-point vibrations is maximum. But helium is also an inert gas. Its atoms interact with each other very weakly, and it is relatively easy to melt the crystal lattice of the gel. It turns out that the energy of zero-point vibrations in helium is sufficient for this, and it does not freeze even at zero absolute. But hydrogen, although its atoms have higher zero-point vibration energies than helium, still freezes, since the interaction between hydrogen atoms is much stronger.

All other substances also freeze at absolute zero temperatures. So helium is the only substance that always remains liquid at normal pressure. One might even say that it is precisely the uncertainty relation that prevents it from freezing. Physicists call the liquid a quantum liquid. It has such an amazing property as superfluidity. Academician L.D. Landdu said that liquid helium is a window to the quantum world.

At a pressure of about 30 atmosferium helium still solidifies. Solid helium, however, is also a very unusual crystal. In it, zero oscillations determine, for example, the kinetic energy of atoms at the boundary, and as a result, the surface of the crystal can perform gigantic oscillations, like a boundary between two immutable liquids (Fig. 4). Physicists call solid helium a quantum crystal, and its properties are now being studied integratively.

In 1984, the publishing house "Children's Literature" published the book "The World of Physics" by Professor M. M. Koltun.

The book shows a complex, diverse and contradictory, but at the same time unified world of inextricably linked physical phenomena and processes, popularizes and occupies the location that governs this world, and the history of their discovery. The author managed to trace the internal logic of the development of physics and reveal the connection of this science with various spheres of human activity - with other areas of science, technology and even art. The book is beautifully designed, it contains many interesting illustrations - rare historical photographs, colorful diagrams, reproductions of paintings, images of modern equipment and experimental setups. In short, this is an excellent book to read on physics, capable of captivating any reader.
