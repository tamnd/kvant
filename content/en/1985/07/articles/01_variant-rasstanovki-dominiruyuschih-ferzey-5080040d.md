The seventeenth black queens standing on this “30x31 chessboard” dominate it, that is, they break through all its 930 squares without exception. You can check! This ingenious arrangement of queens was invented by V.G. Chvanov; it is only one of an endless series of solutions to the “DOMINATION problem on an n×n board” - namely, for the case n=(7m-1)/2, m=9, n=31. This tricky setup is derived from the simpler setup of the seventeenth queens (13 black, 4 white) along lines 1-1, 2-2, 3-3. These queens do not break through all the squares of the board (which ones exactly?), but after db of four white queens (along the red arrows), the resulting system of 17 black queens already solves the problem of DOMINATION on a 30x31 board. However, you can add another 31 cells to this board (in what place?) and get a solution to the problem for a 31x31 board. You can read more about these constructions in the article by V. G. Chvanov inside this issue.
