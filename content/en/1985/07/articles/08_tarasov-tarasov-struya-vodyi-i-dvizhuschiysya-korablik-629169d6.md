A stream of water and... a moving boat

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. V. TARASOV,
M. L. TARASOV

Several times already on the pages of “Laboratory «Kvant»” a very interesting and important physical phenomenon has been talked about - the presence of a reaction of an outflowing scab and the absence of a reaction of an inflowing scab of liquid (see «Kvant», 1978, No. 9; 1980, No. 9).
This issue publishes another article on this topic.

Let's start with a story about one forgotten children's toy that was extremely popular in the 30s. It was called “Self-propelled boat”. Now this toy is no longer sold in stores, but it is not difficult to make it yourself at home or in the “Skillful Hands” circle.

The device of the “Self-propelled boat” is shown in figure 1. On the boat 1 there is a sealed rigid flat box 2, which can be heated by a candle 3 (in modern experiments, it is better to use a dry alcohol tablet together). A curved metal tube 4 comes out of the box under the bottom of the boat (the tightness of the connections is ensured by soldering 5). For great effect, one side of the box can be made in the form of a clicking membrane (see "Young Technical", 1983, No. 8, p. 65).

This toy works as follows. The tin box was filled with water (through a hole with stopper 6), and the toy was lowered on the surface of the water. Then the candle was lit. After some time, the boat began to move - at first slowly and smoothly, then like fish,

producing characteristic sounds that imitate the operation of a steam engine. She moved all the time while the candle was burning.

Let’s now try to figure out why the “self-propelled boat” is moving.

When we light a candle, the water in the box heats up and boils (Fig. 2, a). At the same time, the pressure in the box increases, the water begins to come out through the tube, and the boat begins to move smoothly and slowly. This first phase of the toy’s operation ends when the water in the box boils away.

Since the thermal conductivity of steam is low, the steam practically does not take away heat from the box and does not heat up, which means it does not expand. Moreover, it condenses in the tube (at the point of contact with water), as a result of which the pressure in the box drops, and the water gradually rises up the tube (Fig. 2, b). This is the second phase of the toy's operation.

When the water rises through the tube to the very top and a certain amount of water, overflowing the edge, falls onto the hot box, instantaneous evaporation of water occurs. In this case, the pressure in the box will jump, and a certain amount of water from the tube will be pushed out (Fig. 2, s). This is the third phase of the toy's operation.

Subsequently, while the candle is burning, the second and third phases will pass through. When the candle goes out, the box cools down, all the steam condenses, and the box is filled with water again.

It may seem incomprehensible how by mixing the same amount of water - pushed out of the tube in the third phase and sucked in by the tube in the second phase - one can achieve forward movement of the boat. It turns out that the whole point is in the existence of a reaction of the ejected jet and the absence of a reaction of the ejected jet of liquid.
