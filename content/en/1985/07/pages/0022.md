Among other biological observations, the first thing to note is the description of the cellular structure of plants. The term “cell” itself, firmly established in the language of science, was introduced by Hooke.

Although “Mikrofragiya” is mainly devoted to the description of microsoptic observations, it contains quite a few pages where issues from various areas of natural history are discussed. Thus, Hooke deals with issues related to preeumatic and barometric experiments; discusses the phenomenon of capillarity; sets out the original theory of the body and combustion; describes his astronomical observations. Among the astronomical problems that affect the lunar craters. To answer this question, model experiments have been carried out; it is observed in crater-like formations on the surface of boiling albestra and on the surface of softened clay in which small nucleoli fall. These model experiments can be considered as an indirect substantiation of the two main modern hypotheses about the origin of craters on the Moon: volcanic and meteorite.

Among the observations and hypotheses concerning a variety of problems, the part of the book in which Hooke discussed the question of the nature of light was of particular importance for the development of physics. Having become interested in the phenomenon of colors in thin films and having completed a series of original observations, he formulated a hypothesis about the nature of light, which is considered the source of the wave theory of light.*) Hooke acquired such authority in this matter that it was he who was commissioned by the leadership of the Royal Society of London in 1672 to express an opinion on the work on the theory of colors presented to the Society by I. Newton. In Newton's work, some messages were made regarding the nature of light, with which Hooke did not agree. Being a decisive man, he sharply criticized Newton's point of view. From this time on, for a number of years, Hooke and Newton argued over issues of optics.

In the history of physics, Hooke's name is most firmly associated with the formulation of the law of urgosity, known to high school students as "Hooke's law." Research on the urgological properties of Hooke's substances
