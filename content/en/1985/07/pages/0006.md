2. Waves of confidence

In the simplest atom model, the Rutherfor model, electrons are scattered in orbits around the nucleus, similar to how planets move around the Sun. But electrons are loaded particles, and when they rotate, alternating electric and magnetic fields are necessarily created - radiation occurs, leading to energy loss. That is why, in the planetary model, electrons have an independent fate - they will definitely fall onto the nucleus, and the atom will collapse. But the stability of atom is a firmly established experimental fact.

It was necessary to “correct” Rutherfort’s model, and Niels Bohr did this in 1913. Electrons in the Bohr model are allowed to rotate only in certain orbits, where they have strictly specified energies. Electrons can change this energy only abruptly, emitting and absorbing quanta when moving from one orbit to another. This “quantum” behavior of electrons in an atom allows us to explain a lot - in particular, the stability of the atom and atomic spectra, but it contradicts the correspondence to uncertainty. After all, when moving even along a quantum orbit, momentum and coordinate can be determined simultaneously, but in the microworld, as we now know, this cannot happen.

I had to abandon this atom model as well. The picture describing the behavior of an electron in an atom turned out to be even more complex.
