Is the stew in which the fatty broth is boiling greater than the density of oil vapor? The saturated vapor pressure of oil at temperature $t=100^\circ C$ is $p_{\text{hm}}=120$ Pa. The molar mass of the oil is $M_{\text{m}}=80$ g/mol.

The pressure of saturated oil vapor at a temperature of $t=100^\circ C$ is applied a thousand times less than atmospheric pressure ($p_{\text{atm}}=10^5$ Pa), which means that the fatty broth will practically boil at this temperature (the effect of boundary boiling discussed above in this case leads to a shift in the boiling temperature by only $0,2^\circ C$. From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron law it follows that the ratio of the density of water vapor is $\rho_{\text{bp}}$ and oil vapor $\rho_{\text{mpp}}$ will be equal

$$\frac{\rho_{\text{bpi}}}{\rho_{\text{mpp}}} = \frac{p_{\text{hb}} M_{\text{b}}}{p_{\text{hm}} M_{\text{m}}} = \frac{p_{\text{atm}} M_{\text{b}}}{p_{\text{hm}} M_{\text{m}}} \approx 190.$$

Problem 8. In the upper cylinder under a weightless piston with an area of ​​$S=100$ cm$^2$ there is $m_1=18$ g of saturated steam. $m_2=18$ g of water is injected into the cylinder at a temperature of $t_0=0^\circ C$. How far will the piston go down? Atmospheric pressure $p_0=10^5$ Pa, specific temperature of water $c=4,2$ kJ/kg, specific vaporization temperature $r=2,3$ MJ/kg. The temperature of the cylinder and heat transfer to the environment can be neglected.

According to the conditions of the problem, the steam in the vessel is saturated. Since the piston is weightless, the steam pressure is equal to atmospheric pressure $p_0=10^5$ Pa, and the temperature of the steam before water injection was $t=100^\circ C$. This moment is the most important in this task. It is not difficult to further verify that the final temperature of the system after establishing equilibrium will also be equal to $t$ (show this yourself). Consequently, the water in the cylinder will be heated to a temperature of $t$, which can only occur due to the condensation of a certain mass of steam $m_{\text{n}}$:

$$cm_2 (t-t_0) = m_{\text{n}} r,$$

where

$$m_{\text{n}} = cm_2 (t-t_0)/r \approx 3,3 \text{ г.}$$

The volume $V$ occupied by this vapor before condensation can be found from the equation of the gas state:

$$V = \frac{m_{\text{n}} RT}{Mp_0} \approx 5,7 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ m}^3.$$

Considering the volume occupied by water condensed from steam to be negligible (check this yourself), we obtain that the displacement of the piston is equal to

$$h = \frac{V}{S} \approx 0,57 \text{ m} = 57 \text{ cm.}$$

***

In conclusion, I will dwell on one curious fact: the pressure of saturated vapors can also depend on the shape of the surface of the liquid (and not just on its temperature). So, for example, at the same temperature, the density (and therefore the pressure) of saturated vapors above a convex surface of water is greater than above a flat one. (Try to explain this phenomenon.)
