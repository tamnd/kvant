M914. On the island of Seroburomalin there are 13 gray, 15 brown and 17 crimson chameleons of different colors, then they simultaneously change their color to a third (gray and brown become both crimson, etc.). Could it be that after some time all chameleons will be single-zoned?

ANSWER: it cannot. We must prove that from the triple (13, 15, 17) using the operations described in the conditions, that is, increasing one of the numbers by 2 and simultaneously decreasing the other two by 1 (if they are positive), it is impossible to obtain one of the triples (45, 0, 0), (0, 45, 0) and (0, 0, 45) (45 = 13 + 15 + 17). As in most similar problems with a negative answer, for the proof it is necessary to construct an invariant - a quantity that is defined on the tracks of integers ($c, \delta, m$) and does not change under the indicated transformations of the tracks, which would take the value for the first train and the last three.

Such an invariant is, for example, the remainder $r$ of division by 3 differences $c - \delta$ of the first two numbers of the triple ($c, \delta, m$).

Effective, when “changing the color of chameleons” the numbers $c$ and $\delta$ can be replaced by $c - 1, \delta - 1$, $c + 2, \delta - 1$ or $c - 1, \delta + 2$; in the first case $c - \delta$ does not change, in the other two it changes by 3, which means the remainder $r$ remains the same. It remains to replace that for the original triple (13, 15, 17) it is equal to 1, and for the last three it is $r = 0$.
