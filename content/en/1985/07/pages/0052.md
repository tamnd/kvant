⟦folio 50⟧

F926. In space 1985 there are non-touching metal balls, the charges of which are equal, respectively, q, -2q, 3q, -4q, ..., 1984q, 1985q (q>0). Prove that among them there is a ball whose surface charge density is completely non-negative. The distances between the balls are finite.

Let's imagine the electrostatic field of charged balls in the form of some pattern of lines of force. Since there is no field inside each ball, the field lines begin and end on the surface of the balls.

Let's take any ball, for example ball A, and find on its surface the place where the end of some field line is located (the red line at figure). If we do not find such a place, then this will mean that ball A is the iskoy - it is charged non-negatively (after all, the lines of force end at negative charges).

We will transfer the positive test charge from the place we found on the ball along the field line opposite to its direction. Following this path, we will arrive at the surface of some other ball B. We will walk along its surface, find the end of another line of force on it and again appear along it (“towards”). We will continue this process, following through balls C, D, etc. (blue dotted line at figure).

The following statement is true:

1. Such a movement will not increase us to infinity, since the total charge of the system is

$$Q = q - 2q + 3q - 4q + \dots - 1984q + 1985q = 993q$$

is positive (by condition q>0), and therefore the lines of force do not come from infinity. Indeed, at all points sufficiently distant from our system, which has limited linear dimensions (the distances between the balls are finite), the field created by the system is equivalent to the field of an exact positive charge, and the lines of force at such points are directed away from the system and go to infinity.

2. On our journey we cannot visit again any of the balls that have already been passed. If such an accident, it means that some part of our path is a closed line, which cannot be in an electrostatic field.

Consequently, since the number of balls is finite, our movement will sooner or later end, that is, we will find a ball in which the lines of force do not enter. The surface of such a ball is non-negatively charged everywhere.
