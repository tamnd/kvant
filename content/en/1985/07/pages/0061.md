⟦folio 59⟧

Uniform Arrangements

(For the beginning, see p. 51)

Several tasks

1. Let us divide the circle into $n$ equal arcs and insert into it a regular $k$-gon (the vertices of which do not fall into the ends of the arcs, Fig. 7). Let us paint black those $k$ arcs in which the vertices fall. Prove that their location among all $n$ arcs is uniform. (This problem provides a new way to construct $P(n; k)$).

2. a) We will place $n$ (black and white) points at the vertices of a regular $n$-gon. Prove that uniformly distributed black points $P(n; k)$ form a set symmetrical with respect to some line. $6)$* How many such axes of symmetry $y$ $P(n; k)$?

In the following tasks we study a slightly more general concept: uniform placement on a straight line. Let there be an infinite (in both directions) sequence of points on a straight line - in part, for example, integer points of the number axis - “colored” in black and white. It is called uniform if for any natural number $m$ the number of blacks among $m$ its successive points varies by at most 1. Prove the following theorem.

3. For the given numbers $a, b$ ($a > 1$), the integers $[a+b]$, where $l$ runs through all possible integers, are called black, the remaining integers are called white. The resulting arrangement is uniform. It is periodic if and only if $a$ is a rational number ($\frac{n}{k}$ it has period $n$ and the arrangement of black and white points on the period coincides with the arrangement of $P(n; k)$ on the circle).

4*. For any uniform arrangement there is a number $a$ such that the number of black dots among any $m$ consecutive points of arrangement differs from $a m$ by less than 1.

5*. Let's draw a straight line on an infinite sheet of checkered paper that does not pass through the nodes, mark in black the points of its intersection with the lines of one direction, and in white - in the other (Fig. 8). The resulting arrangement is uniform.

6. Two infinite (in both directions) arithmetic progressions $a_1 l + b_1, a_2 l + b_2$ ($l \in \mathbb{Z}$) do not have common terms. On the number line, the numbers of one progression are placed in black, the other in white. The resulting arrangement of black and white dots is uniform.
