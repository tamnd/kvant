As already mentioned, Hooke began his scientific work by participating in Boyle’s experiments. However, Boyle's discoveries served as the topic of Hooke's first independent study. Boyle then stated that liquid in thin tubes (capillaries) does not obey the law of communicating fluids: water in a glass capillary rises above the water level in a wide vessel. Huctls thoroughly studied phenomena of this kind, which were called capillary. He was unable to find out their cause, but he made several important observations and generalizations. In particular, he established a connection between capillarity and the movement of sap in tree trunks.
