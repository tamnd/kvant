Let us now remember that boiling of a liquid begins at a temperature when the saturated vapor pressure becomes equal to the external pressure. As can be seen from the liquid-vapor coexistence curve, the pressure of saturated vapor increases as its temperature increases, and, conversely, as the temperature decreases, the vapor pressure decreases.

Task 6. Cooking food in a pressure cooker occurs at high pressure. In this case, the temperature turns $t_k = 100^\circ\text{C}$. It was found that immediately after the pressure cooker was sized, 3% of the water contained in it evaporated. Determine the temperature the water had before sizing. Specific heat of water $c = 4,2 \text{ кДж/(кг} \cdot \text{К)}$, specific heat of vaporization $r = 2,3 \text{ МДж/кг}$. Temperatures during the establishment of a new equilibrium state and the change in the specific heat of vaporization with the transformation temperature.

After the pressure cooker is sized, the pressure will become equal to atmospheric pressure. Since the temperature of water $t$ in a pressure cooker turns the temperature $t_k = 100^\circ\text{C}$ of boiling water at normal atmospheric pressure, intensive evaporation (boiling) of water begins. It will erupt until its temperature decreases to $t_k$. In this case, to evaporate 3% of water, that is, a mass of 0.03 mr, where $m$ is the initial mass of water in the pressure cooker, an amount of heat of 0.03 mr will be required. This heat is released when water cools from a temperature of $t$ to $t_k$. As a result, we can write

$$0,03mr = cm(t - t_k),$$

from which it follows that the temperature of the water in the pressure cooker was equal to

$$t = t_k + 0,03r/c \approx 116^\circ\text{C}.$$

Let us pay attention to the fact that when calculating the amount of heat released during cooling of water, we avoid reducing its mass. This is justified since only 3% of the water evaporates.

Question 1. Will water boil in a test tube placed in a pot of boiling water?

The boiling process, as is known, requires a continuous supply of heat. When the water in the test tube heats up to a temperature of 100°C, the supply of heat from the butter will stop. Therefore, naturally, the water in the test tube will not boil.

Question 2. What will happen if napittol (this is a lighter liquid not mixed with water), whose boiling point is 111°C, is placed on top of water in a test tube?

Here we are faced with an interesting phenomenon - the so-called boundary boiling. Its essence is that boiling begins at the boundary of two liquids, when the sum of the partial pressures of their saturated vapors is equal to the external atmospheric pressure. It is clear that in this case the pressure of saturated water vapor is less than atmospheric, and therefore the temperature is below 100°C. Thus, when poplar is added to a test tube with water, boiling at the heat-water boundary begins earlier than the boiling of water in kasrol.
