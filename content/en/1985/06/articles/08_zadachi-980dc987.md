⟦folio 27⟧

Quests

1. There are 20 mushrooms in the basket: porcini, aspen and boletus. How many porcini mushrooms are there in it if it contains 9 times more boletus than boletus?

2. Restore the division:
$$3^{***} \times 3$$
$$\frac{3}{3}$$
$$\frac{3}{2}$$
$$\frac{3}{4}$$

3. The surface of the cube $1 \times 1 \times 1$ cannot be covered entirely with a piece of paper $1 \times 6$, without causing tears. Is it possible to cover such a cube with two layers of paper $1 \times 12$?

4. All possible triangles with sides $a \leqslant 2, b \leqslant 3, c \leqslant 4$ are considered. Find the length of the tallest triangle.

5. The photographer followed his SIM card while standing in the boat (see figure). Estimate the length of the boat shown, assuming that you were the photographer.

These tasks were suggested to us by S. N. Olehnik, N. K. Antonovich, V. V. Proizvol, G. A. Galperin, Ya. L. Kalda.

⟦folio none⟧

WHY, IF YOU THROW A LARGE PIECE OF GLASS, IT BREAKS INTO A LARGE NUMBER OF SMALL FRAGMENTS? AND IF YOU THROW A SMALL PIECE, IT WILL ALWAYS BEAT ONCE?

5. Timid Bill could not immediately climb onto the stairs. He rose up and then fell again, but finally, overcoming his fear, he found himself on the lid. What is the minimum number of steps he could take if for each step he moved 1 rung, ended up on the ground once, on the top rung also once, on the other rungs the same number of times, and if the ladder has 9 rungs in total.

6. Punch + punch = fight.

7*. You have lost yourself in a convex forest with an area of ​​100 km². Prove that you can get out of the forest by walking a path no more than 30 km.

⟦folio 32⟧

1. A small object is thrown into a round vessel with water (bucket, basin, plate), trying to get into the center. How to check the accuracy of a throw?

2. Connect with a thread the middle of the hangers of two matniks of equal length hanging next to each other so that the thread easily pulls them together. While holding one mat, push the other one slightly perpendicular to the tightening thread. What happens if you now let go of the first matnikok? Why?

3. The period of one of two matniks of different lengths is known. Without using a meter and a ruler, how can you determine the period of another matnik?

4. How to explain the calculation of a dragonfly's wings, and why does it change if observations are made from different angles?
