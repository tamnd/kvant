F920. Air is blown through a variable cross-section tube. The inlet hole of the tube has an area of ​​$S_1$, and the outlet has an area of ​​$S_2$. At the inlet air speed is $v_1$, temperature - $T_1$, pressure - $p_1$; at the outlet the air temperature is $T_2$, the pressure is $p_2$. What is the exit air speed?

The volume of air entering the tube during time $t$ is equal to $V = S_1 v_1 t$. The volume of air escaping during the same time is equal to $V_2 = S_2 v_2 t$, where $v_2$ is the desired air velocity at the outlet. Since the speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$ do not change with time, the masses of the incoming and outgoing air are the same. Therefore, according to the unified gas law,

$$\frac{p_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2 V_2}{T_2} \Rightarrow V_2 = V_1 \frac{p_1 T_2}{p_2 T_1},$$

that is

$$S_2 v_2 t = S_1 v_1 t \frac{p_1 T_2}{p_2 T_1}.$$

From here comes $v_2$:

$$v_2 = v_1 \frac{S_1 p_1 T_2}{S_2 p_2 T_1}.$$

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{f} \Rightarrow f = \frac{Fb}{b - F} = 5,25 \text{ см.}$$

When photographing with a colute, the distance between the object and the lens was $f' = f + \Delta l = 6$ cm. Using the lens formula again, we find at what distance $b'$ from the object (with the colute) the ant is located:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{b'} + \frac{1}{f'} = \frac{1}{b'} + \frac{1}{f + \Delta l},$$

where

$$b' = \frac{F(f + \Delta l)}{f + \Delta l - F} = 30 \text{ см.}$$
