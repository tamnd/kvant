Light "dumbbell"

Before describing the results of photographing the light “dumbbell”, a few words should be said about why such an experiment is needed.

According to the relative theory, the length of a body, measured in some reporting systems, depends on the speed at which the body moves relative to this reporting system. If, in particular, the body is at rest and its length is equal to l₀, then when this body moves with speed v, its length will be l = l₀√(1-v²/c²), where c is the speed of light. The factor √(1-v²/c²) is always less than one, so when a body moves, its dimensions in the direction of movement (this is the size we had in mind when we said “length”) are reduced by 1/√(1-v²/c²) times.* (For speeds achievable in terrestrial conditions at the present time, the set √(1-v²/c²) differs very little from unity. If the body has speed is 10 km/s (this is the order of magnitude of the speed of spacecraft - the package and satellites), then this set is equal to 0.9999999944, that is, it differs from one in the tenth decimal place. Therefore, when reaching low speeds, it is extremely difficult to detect a reduction in size during movement. But this statement of the theory of relative length during movement leads to a change in their shape. The moving ball, if the speed of its movement is close to the speed of light, should solidify and turn into. “pancake”, the plane of which is perpedicular to the direction of velocity; the cube should turn into a parallelepiped, etc.

*) Such a contraction is often called “Lorentzian” - after the Inderland physicist and theorist H. A. Lorentz, who was one of the first (in 1892) to predict this effect.
