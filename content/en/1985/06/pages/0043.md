M910. The points $A_1, A_2, ..., A_6$ (Fig. 1) are taken on the sides of a regular hexagon. It is known that three pairwise non-funny sides of the hexagon $A_1...A_6$ ($A_1A_2, A_3A_4, A_5A_6$) define the triangle KLM, the vertices of which lie on the extensions of the diagonals of a regular hexagon. Prove that this is also true for the other three sides of the hexagon $A_1...A_6$.

Consider the given regular hexagon as an orthogonal projection of the cube along its diagonal $PQ$. Let us take the extensions of the edges of the cube emerging from the vertex $P$ along a point so that these points are projected into points $K, L, M$ (on figure 2 they are denoted by the same letters $K, L, M$). Then, obviously, the section of the cube by the KLM plane will be a hexagon, which, upon projection, turns into a hexagon $A_1...A_6$. Let $K_1, L_1, M_1$ be the points of intersection of this plane with the extensions of the edges emerging from the vertex $Q$, then both in space (Fig. 2) and on the projection (Fig. 1) the segments $A_2A_3, A_4A_5$ and $A_6A_1$ will lie on the sides of the triangle $K_1L_1M_1$, from which the statement of the problem follows.

From the above reasoning it is clear that the opposite sides of the hexagon $A_1...A_6$ are parallel (the section plane intersects the opposite faces of the cube along parallel straight lines). This can also be deduced directly from the condition: for example, triangles $BA_1A_2$ and $PML$ on figure 1 are prepared with the center of preparation $K$ ($KA_1:KM=KB:KP=KA_2:KL$) and, therefore, $A_1A_2 \parallel A_4A_5$. From here it follows that triangles $CA_4A_5$ and $PM_1L_1$ (where $M_1$ and $L_1$ are the points of intersection of lines $A_4A_3$ and $A_5A_6$ with $A_1A_2$) are also prepared as triangles with respectively parallel sides, and therefore straight lines $A_3A_4, A_5A_6$ and $BC$ intersect at one point - the center of cooking. Considering in a similar way two other pairs of opposite sides of the hexagon $A_1...A_6$, we obtain another solution to the problem that does not use “exit into space”.
