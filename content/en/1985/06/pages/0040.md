**M906. a) Prove that for any natural number $a>1$ the equation**
$$\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}=\frac{1}{a}$$

has at least three solutions in natural numbers $x$ and $y$.

b) Find the number of natural solutions to this equation for $a=1985$.

**a) The solutions to this equation for any natural number $a$ are pairs of natural numbers $(2a, 2a), (a+1, a(a+1))$ and $(a(a+1), a+1)$.**

6) Answer: for $a=1985$ this equation has 9 solutions. Let us show that the equation
$$\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}=\frac{1}{a}$$

has as many solutions as the divisor of the number $a^2$. (With $a=1985=5 \cdot 397$, the divisor of $a^2$ is 9, since any of them can be represented as $5^n \cdot 397^k$, where $n$ and $k$ insignificantly take any of the three values ​​0, 1, 2.)

To prove it, it is enough to rewrite our equation in the form $ay+ax=xy$ or
$$a^2=(x-a)(y-a),$$

and replace that the number of decompositions of the number $a^2$ into two components is equal to the number of its divisors.

M. V. Slavinsky, A. Yu. Vaitrov

**M907. For triangle ABC with side lengths $a=BC$, $b=AC$, $c=AB$ it is known that $3\angle A+2\angle B=180^\circ$. Prove that $a^2+bc-c^2=0$.**

![Diagram of triangle ABC with sides $a, b, c$](image)

It is convenient to rewrite the correspondence to be proved in the form of equality of ratios of segments
$$\frac{a}{c}=\frac{c-b}{a}.$$

Now it can be proven using similar triangles. On the side $AB$ we take a point $D$ such that $AD=b$ (a $DB=c-b$; see figure). Equality (*) is a condition of proportionality to the sides of triangles $ABC$ and $CBD$, enclosing the common angle $B$ of these triangles, so it is enough to prove that these triangles are similar, that is, that, for example, angles $ACB$ and $CDB$ are equal. We have
$$\angle CDB=180^\circ-\angle ADC=180^\circ-\frac{180^\circ-\angle A}{2}$$
$$=180^\circ-(\angle A+\angle B)=\angle ACB.$$

(We took advantage of the fact that the triangle $ACD$ is equilateral and the given correspondence $3\angle A+2\angle B=180^\circ$.)
