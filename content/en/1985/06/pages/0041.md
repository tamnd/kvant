**M908.** On side AB of triangle ABC, point P is selected, and lines parallel to BC and AC are drawn through it until they intersect with sides AC and BC, respectively, at points M and N. For which choice of point P does segment MN have the shortest length?

Solve this problem a) for a triangle with right angle C; b)* for an arbitrary triangle ABC.

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

a) Answer: Point P must be the base of the altitude of triangle ABC. Indeed, in the case when the angle C is a right angle (Fig. 1), the parallelograms CMPN are rectangles. Therefore, MN = CP, and the segment CP has the shortest length when it is perpendicular to AB.

6) We will begin to solve the problem in the standard way: we introduce the parameter $x \equiv AP/AB$ $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$, and express the vector $\vec{MN}$ in terms of $\vec{CA}$ and $\vec{CB}$ to then write its length as a function of x and find the smallest value of this function. However, as we see, the resulting correspondence vectors themselves suggest a simpler and essentially “purely geometric” completion of the solution.

From the readiness of triangles BPN and BAC, APM and ABC (Fig. 2) it follows that CN/CB=AP/AB=x, that is $\vec{CN}=x\vec{CB}$, CM/CA=BP/BA=1-x, that is $\vec{CM}=(1-x)\vec{CA}$ and (see Fig. 2)

$$\vec{MN}=\vec{CN}-\vec{CM}=x\vec{CB}-(1-x)\vec{CA}=\vec{AC}+x(\vec{CA}+\vec{CB})=\vec{AC}+x\vec{CD}=\vec{AQ}$$

where $\vec{CD}=\vec{CA}+\vec{CB}$, and the point Q is determined by the condition $\vec{CQ}=x\vec{CD}$. When point P runs through segment AB, the parameter x changes from 0 to 1, and point Q runs through segment CD. Consequently, the line MN=AQ will be minimal when Q is the point of the segment CD close to A. To find the position of the point P corresponding to this point, we note that $\vec{QN}=\vec{AM}$ (since $\vec{MN}=\vec{AQ}$), which means Q lies on the line passing through N parallel to the line AM, that is, on the line PN.

So, the answer to our problem can be formulated as follows: let’s build this triangle into a parallelogram ACBD; Let us find a point Q on its diagonal CD, close to A, and draw it through a straight line parallel to side AC; the point of intersection of this line with side AB is the exclusion point P. In particular, if angles ACD and ADC are acute, then Q is the projection of point A onto CD; if ACD is not an acute angle, then Q=C, and P=A; if ADC is not an acute angle, then Q=D, and P=B.
