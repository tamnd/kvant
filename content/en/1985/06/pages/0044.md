mulu lenses:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f},$$

where $F$ is the focal length of the lens, $d$ is the distance between the lens and the object, $f$ is the distance between the lens and the image. Since the magnification is determined by the formula $\Gamma = f/d$, then, excluding the lens formula $f$, we obtain

$$\Gamma = \frac{F}{d - F}.$$

(1)

For a minimal image, the lens formula takes the form

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{d} - \frac{1}{f},$$

and the increase is given by the expression

$$\Gamma = \frac{F}{F - d}.$$

(2)

If we denote by $d_1$ the distance from the object to the lens, by $\Gamma_1$ the magnification with which the lens depicts this object, by $d_2$ the distance from the lens to the image of the object in the mirror and by $\Gamma_2$ the magnification with which the lens depicts this reflection, then using formulas (1) and (2) it is easy to see that if the images created by the lens are both active or both mini, then condition $\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2$ cannot be satisfied.

It remains to assume that one image is real and the other is minimal. Obviously, the image of the object will be minimal, since it is close to the lens, and the image of its reflection will be real. Then, in accordance with formulas (1) and (2), we can write:

$$\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 \Rightarrow F - d_1 = d_2 - F.$$

(3)

No, the distance from the lens to the image of the object in the mirror is $d_2 = 2a - d_1$, where $a$ is the distance from the lens to the mirror. Then from (3) we obtain $a = F$, that is, the mirror is located in the focal plane of the lens. The lens creates an image of its own reflection in the mirror. But this reflection has the same dimensions as the lens and is located at a distance of $d = 2F$ from it. Consequently, according to formula (1), the lens creates a real image of its reflection in the mirror with magnification $\Gamma = 1$.
