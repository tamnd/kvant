At time $t$ the energy of the system is

$$W = C_0 \frac{U^2}{2} + m \frac{v^2}{2} = W_0 + m \frac{v^2}{2}.$$

Where did the extra energy $mv^2/2$ come from?

During the movement of the plate from plate 1 to the impact on plate 2, a charge $q_0$ passed between the plates of the capacitor. The same charge passed through the source. This means that the source has done work $A = q_0 U$. This work sent a message to the energy plate $mv^2/2$.

So,

$$m \frac{v^2}{2} = q_0 U = C_0 U^2 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d_1 - d_2} U^2.$$

From here comes $v$:

$$v = U \sqrt{\frac{2 \varepsilon_0 S}{m(d_1 - d_2)}}.$$

A. P. Ershov

F922. When photographing the ant at close range, the experimenter used an extension ring, which increased the distance from the film to the object by $\Delta l = 7,5$ mm. A sharp image of the ant was obtained when the distance of $b = 1,05$ m was set on the object scale (the scale indicates the distance from the object to the object without depicting extension tracks). The focal length of the object is $F = 50$ mm. How far is the ant from the object?

From the conditions of the problem it follows that without using an extension ring on the film, a sharp image of an object located at a distance of $b = 1,05$ m from the object will be obtained. This allows us to determine the distance between the object (without a ring) and the film using the lens formula:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{f} \Rightarrow f = \frac{Fb}{b - F} = 5,25 \text{ см.}$$

When photographing with a colute, the distance between the object and the lens was $f' = f + \Delta l = 6$ cm. Using the lens formula again, we find at what distance $b'$ from the object (with the colute) the ant is located:

$$\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{b'} + \frac{1}{f'} = \frac{1}{b'} + \frac{1}{f + \Delta l},$$

where

$$b' = \frac{F(f + \Delta l)}{f + \Delta l - F} = 30 \text{ см.}$$

A. P. Ershov
