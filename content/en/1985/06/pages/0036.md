⟦folio 34⟧

Tasks

M926—M930; F938—F942

M926. Prove that if
$$x^2 + y^2 = u^2 + v^2 = 1, \quad xu + yv = 0,$$
That
$$x^2 + u^2 = y^2 + v^2 = 1, \quad xy + uv = 0.$$
S. V. Duzhin

M927*. On the given plane there is a finite set of points, no three of which lie on the same line. Several segments were drawn with ends at these points. These segments are allowed to be changed: if any two of them, AC and BD, intersect, they can be erased and drawn:
a) segments AB and CD,
b) segments AB and BC.
(If the “new” segment has already been drawn, there is no need to draw it a second time.)
Is it possible after several such replacements (only according to rule a) or only according to rule b), but not according to both) to return to the original set of segments?

V. E. Kolosov

M928. There is N+1 place in the book theater. First, N is an integer, having tickets indicating the place (including Igor), if for arbitrary N places, without looking at their tickets. The last (N+1)th spectator to arrive wants to take his place; if it is occupied, the person sitting there groans, he does the same, and so on, until it is necessary, according to the place, to be free. What is the probability that Igor will have to change seats? (In other words, which of all the possible audience placements are unimportant for Igor?)

I. V. Alekseev-Astafev

M929. Natural numbers a, b, c, d, e satisfy the conditions $a^4 + b^4 + c^4 + d^4 = e^4$. Prove that at least a) three of them are even, 6) three are divisible by 5, c) two are divisible by 10.

V. D. Yakovlev

M930*. Numbers from 1 to 1985 are divided into 6 sets. Prove that one of them contains three numbers, one of which is equal to the sum of the other two (or two numbers, one of the two is greater than the other).

A. D. Valtsev (9th grade student).
