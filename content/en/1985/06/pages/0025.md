III. Now it is necessary, at least at a qualitative level, to understand what the rigidity of a structure with respect to bending depends on.

Do one more experiment. For it you will need a rectangular block of rubber (or other elastic material) with a cross-section of approximately 1-2 cm² and a length of about 10 cm.

Apply a scale grid of longitudinal and transverse straight lines (Fig. 3,a) to the block. Then, according to the block, the mesh will be distorted (Fig. 3,b). In this case, the transverse lines will remain straight, but the surfaces relative to each other will no longer be parallel, and the longitudinal lines will be curved. It is easy to notice that the material of the block experiences tension on one side and rolling on the other. But among the longitudinal lines there is one, looking in the middle, which does not change its length. Obviously, all horizontal layers of materials lying behind this line do not experience deformation. Therefore, it is called a neutral layer (or neutral surface).

From this experiment it follows that the further a section of the block is located from the neutral surface, the greater the stretching (or rolling) it experiences. Then, according to Hooke's law, the force of elastic resistance to bending increases with distance from the neutral layer. In other words, the main contribution to the stiffness of the block comes from the layer farthest from the neutral one. And from here the conclusion is: in order to increase the rigidity of the structure, it is necessary to place its main material as far as possible from this layer. This is why the piece of bar in Umov’s Experiment offers almost no resistance to the load, while, for example, an I-beam made of the same material behaves much more rigidly.

However, removing the base material from the neutral surface does not increase too much. So, if an I-beam is made very thin in the middle, it becomes unstable and will twist. At the same time, the I-beam design, with the same rigidity as a solid square beam, turns out to be four and a half times lighter.
