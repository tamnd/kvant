Galileo clearly understood that the problem of the oscillation of a mathematical matrix is ​​equivalent to the problem of the movement of a heavy material point along a circular slot (which coincides with the trajectories of the end of the matrix). Identical bonds are applied to the point moving along the slot and the end of the matrix. The statement about isochronism is equivalent to the fact that, from whatever place on the crevice a point begins to move, it ends up at the bottom point after the same moment of time. One should not be surprised by the possibility of the existence of such an effect: if the movement begins at a higher altitude, then the point travels a longer distance, but gains greater speed along the way. However, as Geugens found out, a circular gap, contrary to the opinion of Galileo, does not have the property of isochronism.
