Length of the inner pipe at $t_1=20^\circ C$ so that the total length of the standard (CD) does not change when the temperature changes?

$$\Delta l_1 = l_1 - l_{10} = l_{10} \alpha_1 t_1,$$
$$\Delta l_2 = l_2 - l_{20} = l_{20} \alpha_2 t_1.$$

The design of the standard is such that for any change in temperature, the change in pipe length $B$ should compensate for the total elongation of parts $A$ and $C$. Therefore, condition $\Delta l_2 = 2\Delta l_1$ must be satisfied, that is

$$l_{20} \alpha_2 t_1 = 2 l_{10} \alpha_1 t_1.$$

Substituting the expressions written above for $l_{10}$ and $l_{20}$ into this equality, we find $l_2$:

$$l_2 = 2 l_1 \frac{\alpha_1 (1 + \alpha_2 t_1)}{\alpha_2 (1 + \alpha_1 t_1)} \approx 2 l_1 \frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2}.$$

A. P. Ershov

F920. Air is blown through a variable cross-section tube. The inlet hole of the tube has an area of ​​$S_1$, and the outlet has an area of ​​$S_2$. At the inlet air speed is $v_1$, temperature - $T_1$, pressure - $p_1$; at the outlet the air temperature is $T_2$, the pressure is $p_2$. What is the exit air speed?

The volume of air entering the tube during time $t$ is equal to $V = S_1 v_1 t$. The volume of air escaping during the same time is equal to $V_2 = S_2 v_2 t$, where $v_2$ is the desired air velocity at the outlet. Since the speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$ do not change with time, the masses of the incoming and outgoing air are the same. Therefore, according to the unified gas law,

$$\frac{p_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{p_2 V_2}{T_2} \Rightarrow V_2 = V_1 \frac{p_1 T_2}{p_2 T_1},$$

that is

$$S_2 v_2 t = S_1 v_1 t \frac{p_1 T_2}{p_2 T_1}.$$

From here comes $v_2$:

$$v_2 = v_1 \frac{S_1 p_1 T_2}{S_2 p_2 T_1}.$$

G. L. Kotkin

F921. A parallel-plate capacitor is connected to a voltage source $U$. A flat capacitor has an area of ​​$S$, and the distance between the plates is $d_1$. A metal plate of area $S$ and thickness $d_2$ (Fig. 1), having a mass $m$, is pressed against one plate. Flat otnuskator. At what speed will it hit the other plates of the capacitor? Neglect the influence of gravity.

figure 1 shows the distribution of charges in the system at the initial time $t_0$. Under the influence of the attractive force from the charge - $q_0$ plate 2. The plate, when released, begins to move with acceleration towards plate 2. figure 2 shows the position of the system at time $t$, when the plate hits plate 2.

Let us denote the speed of the plate at the moment of impact as $v$. To find $v$, we use the law of conservation of energy.

At moment $t_0$ the energy $W_0$ of the capacitor + plate system is equal to the energy of the capacitor charged to the potential difference $U$; the distance between the plates of this capacitor is $d_1 - d_2$, and its capacitance is $C_0 = \varepsilon_0 S / (d_1 - d_2)$. So

$$W_0 = C_0 \frac{U^2}{2}.$$
