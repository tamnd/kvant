Now it is clear why nature uses tubular structures so widely. And they were born in the process of evolution. While life was concentrated in water, there was no particular need for a small mass of the skeleton - the force of the liquid that was pushed out helped. Therefore, sharks, for example, have preserved a massive cartilaginous skeleton from their ancestors. But then living creatures came out on land. Now the strength of the skeleton had to be combined with its possible lower mass. The process of transforming cartilaginous formations into tubular bones lasted for millions of years and ended with the creation of a strong, lightweight and economical structure in terms of materials consumption.
