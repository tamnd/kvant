If you were interested in the challenging "Super Evil" problems from the article "Transformations of Admiral Makarov's Puzzle" (p. 70), try your hand at the following problems.
1. Come up with a puzzle that has the following algorithm:
$(6z)^2, 3x, 1z, 4x, 2x, 2y, 5x, 5z, 3z, 3y^\dagger$.
Please note that this will be an improvement on puzzle figure 5 (p. 74), increasing its difficulty to 10.
2. Find algorithms for solving the puzzles shown on figure 1 (difficulty 10) and figure 2 (difficulty 12).

3. Create a puzzle with a solution algorithm
$4z, 1z, (2, 3)x, 2z, 3x, (6z)^2, 3x, 2z, (2, 3, 6)x^\dagger$.

4*. Is it possible to add a move 6y at the end of the algorithm of the previous problem and create a puzzle with this or another algorithm of complexity 13? The solution to this problem is unknown. Send your developments along with models to the editor.
Maybe you will be able to discover new transformations of the “Admiral Makarov puzzle” and break existing records.

D. Vakarelov
