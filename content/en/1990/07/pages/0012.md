According to modern concepts, the Universe is limited in time and space, i.e. its age is finite (about 15 billion years), and so is its volume. The number of particles in the Universe is estimated by scientists to be colossal, but still a finite number $N \sim 10^{80}$. The Universe “started” from a terribly sloped and hot state, the initial radius of curvature was $\sim 10^{-34}$ cm. Since then, the Universe has been continuously expanding, like a balloon inflated with air, and the galaxies, stars, nebulae (matter) that “fill” it are moving away from each other, like patterns on a balloon. Matter, space and time are interdependent; they started together, so questions like “what happened before the universe came into being?” or “what will you see if you reach its edge?” are formulated incorrectly. “Before that there was no time, and it is impossible to reach the limits of the Universe, just as you cannot reach the limits of the Earth.

In the first moments after the “start,” the temperature is so high that no stable structures can form. Further, elementary particles continuously transform into each other. As it expands, the temperature drops and stable particles are formed - electrons, protons, neutrons. This is the first structure, and the first surprising circumstance is associated with its appearance. The circumstance is this: in the initial boiling cauldron there were equal numbers of particles and antiparticles, but when they remained, the symmetry was broken, and the number of particles $N_i$ turned out to be greater than the number of antiparticles $N_a$. Therefore, the Englishness of matter and anti-procession was not complete, not all matter was transformed into light. The wonderful thing is that $(N_i - N_a)/N_i \sim 10^{-9}$, i.e., only one billion particles did not turn into light (we also encountered this number on another occasion).

With further remaining, atoms of the simplest elements are formed - hydrogen and gel. They need a stable proton to exist. As is known, the masses of the proton $m_p$ and neutron $m_n$ differ little: $m_n - m_p \approx 2,5$ $m_e$ ($m_e$ is the mass of the electron). The heavier neutron lives only 16 minutes, then decays into a proton, electron and electron antineutrino:

$$n \rightarrow p + e^{-} + \tilde{v}_e.$$

Inside the nuclei, where the density of nuclear matter and kinetic energy are partial, a figurative reaction occurs. Therefore, inside nuclei, neutrons exist in a state of dynamic equilibrium. If a neutron were lighter than a proton, the proton would decay -

$$p \rightarrow e^{+} + n + v_e$$

(е$^+$ is a positron, $v_e$ is a neutrino), and hydrogen, which serves as the main fuel of stars and the main water necessary for life, would not exist.

But the path of formation of atoms of elements is guaranteed. Note that for now there are only the simplest ones - hydrogen and gel. They were synthesized everywhere at once, and for the synthesis of nuclei of heavier elements they require special conditions: for the synthesis of such nuclei, the simplest stable nuclei are needed as the starting material and high temperatures at which these nuclei can only fuse, and these temperatures must last for a long time (billions of years) - thermonuclear reactions do not proceed quickly. But only over billions, and even hundreds of thousands of years, the Universe already finds itself in a state that fills the present weight - the average density and temperature in it are very low. Such conditions are favorable for the existence of atoms (after all, at high temperatures, electrons will not stay near the nucleus), but are not suitable for the synthesis of their nuclei.

So, the conditions for the sources of atoms and the synthesis of nuclei are incompatible. In order for the conditioning process to go further, it is necessary that the Universe lose its spatial homogeneity. This is due to the action of gravitational forces.
