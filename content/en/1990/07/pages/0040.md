F1220. A small canister containing the remaining neon for replenishment is connected for a short time to a large reservoir, where the pressure p is twice as high as in the canister. The container will be disconnected immediately after it is filled. What will be the final pressure of the gas in the can? The exchange of gas with the walls of the can when filling it is to claim.

First, the container contains gas under pressure p/2 at temperature T:

$$\frac{p}{2} V = vRT,$$

where V is the volume of the can, v is the amount of gas. After connecting, a portion of gas $v_1$ will flow from the balloon reservoir into the balloon, which will bring the pressure inside the balloon to the value p at the initial steady temperature $T_x$:

$$pV = (v + v_1)RT_x.$$

For the calculation, we assume that the “external” gas has done some work on the water portion of the gas at a constant pressure p, and due to this work the internal energy of the gas in the can has increased. This allows us to write down a correspondence that includes the total amount of gas in the can and the established temperature.

After some time, the temperature inside the can will be equal to the temperature in the tank, and the pressure $p_x$ will be finally established - it will drop in relation to p by the same number of times as the gas temperature drops:

$$\frac{p_x}{p} = \frac{T}{T_x}.$$

Now let's do the calculations.

Let us select (see figure) that volume $V_H$ of the “external” gas, which then ends up inside the can, and write down the expression for the work:

$$A = pV_H.$$

The volume $V_H$ can be expressed from the equation of state of the gas:

$$V_H = \frac{v_1RT}{p}.$$

Since neon is a monatomic gas, the change in the internal energy of the gas that ends up in the can can be written as

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2} (v + v_1)RT_x - \frac{3}{2} (v + v_1)RT.$$

From the law of conservation of energy

$$A = \Delta U,$$

Taking into account the equation of state of the gas, we obtain

$$T_x = 5/4 T.$$

Then finally

$$p_x = pT/T_x = 0,8 \ p.$$
