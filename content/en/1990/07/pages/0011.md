ANTHROPIC PRINCIPLE -
WHAT IS THIS?

A. KUZIN

Small introduction

My article is devoted to science’s attempt to answer one of the eternal questions. This question: does a small person mean anything in this huge world? Who is he - the goal of the development of the Universe, the leader of Creation, or something random, nothing in principle different from other processes of nature? Is the appearance of man the result of the entire development of the Universe or is it some kind of random turn? Has life adapted to external conditions that “didn’t even know anything about it,” or are these conditions themselves adapted for the emergence of life?

For a long time, science did not have the facts to answer these questions in a reasoned manner (although there was no shortage of non-reasoned answers). The information about the world that science had was fragmentary; the overall picture of the development of the Universe was not visible. But almost clear centuries of organized service data are bearing fruit. Now we can talk about everything about the scientific picture of the world, about a holistic view of the Universe. Oh, there's still a lot we don't know! Therefore, one should not think that this answer is final; this is not a judicial verdict by which a person is “condemned” to be the crown of Creation. Skrepik is always right about the limitations of scientific knowledge. Questions are eternal because they cannot be answered once and for all.

Scientific formulation of the question about the world and man

The anthropic principle is a child of a thought experiment. The experiment is like this: let’s mentally change something in the laws of nature and think about whether a person can exist at the same time? When answering this question, we must, of course, focus on something important in a person - after all, it is clear that we cannot count on his complete immutability when the rest of the world changes. Please, we will not be mistaken if we say that the two main qualities of a person, without which he cannot be thought of as a person, are reason and freedom. Freedom is the ability to remain oneself, that is, not to be determined entirely by what is around you at a given moment, but to act from within, from oneself. Reason is the condition of freedom, since without it an active response to the environment is impossible (in this sense, animals also have reason). But the natural sciences are not ready to deal with questions about reason and freedom in their general formulation. We need to find something in this global problem that they could cling to. And here is this bake.

Only a complex existing thing can actively resist the environment, understand it and change the communicated demands of its existence. The degree of complexity can be characterized quantitatively, and this is what the natural sciences do.

The anthropic principle is the answer to the question: does every mental world order presuppose the emergence of progressively more complex structures?

I emphasize that the emergence of such structures is only a necessary, and not a sufficient, condition for the emergence of man. But already the analysis of this necessary condition gives us a lot for understanding the process of evolution.

Let us consider the evolution of the Universe from this angle.
