Here the parameter is the gravitational constant $G$. Another example: the background energy is proportional to its frequency $\omega$: $E = \hbar \omega$. The parameter is Planck's constant $\hbar$.

Quantities like $e, \hbar, G, c$ (speed of light), etc. are called world constants in physics. By themselves, the numerical values ​​of these constants do not know anything, since they change when moving from one system of units to another. But their dimensionless combinations have universal significance. For example, from $e, \hbar$ and $c$ you can make the only dimensionless combination - the number $\alpha = e^2 / \hbar c \approx 1 / 137$. This number is called the fine structure constant; it characterizes the “strength” of electromagnetic interaction. The characteristic energies of interaction between electrons in atoms and free electrons among themselves are only a small (of the order of $\alpha$ to some extent) correction to the rest energy $m_e c^2$. For example, the energy of electrons in atoms with atomic number $Z$ is

$$E \sim m_e c^2 (Z \alpha)^2.$$

Due to the smallness of $\alpha$, processes in which an electron turns into other particles are unlikely. The smallness of $\alpha$ ensures the stability of electron-opposite structures - atoms, solids. Heavy atoms with $Z \gtrsim \alpha^{-1} = 137$ are unstable: the electromagnetic field near the surface of the nucleus is so compressed that it begins to produce electron-positron pairs, which screen the “excess” charge of the nucleus, bringing its value to the value of lesser $e \alpha^{-1}$.

Similar dimensionless constants can be written for all interaction processes - strong (S), weak (W) and gravitational (G). For the last one

$$\alpha_G = G m_p^2 / \hbar c \sim 10^{-39} (m_p - \text{масса протона}).$$

It is known that $\alpha_S \sim 15$, $\alpha_W \sim 10^{-5}$. Both of these interactions occur only at distances on the order of the radius of the nucleus.

So, four interactions are four free parameters $\alpha_k$ ($k \rightarrow e, G, S, W$). To these we can add the dimension of our space $d = 3$, the number of parts in the Universe $N \sim 10^{80}$, the ratio of the number of photons to the number of parts $S \sim 10^9$ and the ratio of the masses of the electron and proton $m_e / m_p \approx 1 / 1837$. The last number, in principle, should be expressed through $\alpha$, $\alpha_S$ and $\alpha_W$, but we don’t know how to do this yet. In general, the cherished dream of physicists is to derive all the named numbers from a single number. But for now this is still just a dream.

In order for life to be possible, a number of correspondences must be satisfied between the free parameters. I will give a few examples, just to illustrate.

The optimal expansion rate of the Universe at which stars are successfully processed ensures compliance with the relations

$$N \alpha_G^2 \sim 1.$$

The possibility of the existence of stars like the Sun - not too hot and not too cold - rests on correspondence.

$$\alpha_G \sim \alpha^{12} (m_e / m_p)^4.$$

In order for a flow of neutrinos (they participate only in weak interactions) to be able to throw off the shell of a supernova, a necessary condition is

$$\alpha_W^4 \sim \alpha_G (m_p / m_e)^6.$$

In order for atoms to be formed during the evolution of the Universe before gravitational instability has time to develop, the Universe is made inexpensive, necessary for the correspondence to be fulfilled

$$S \sim \alpha^{-2} (m_p / m_e),$$

$$S \leq \alpha^{10} (m_p / m_e) \alpha_G^{-1}.$$
