the planetary system could have formed as a result of some catastrophic events - explosions, mergers of stars, etc. For example, the hypothesis is being considered that at a certain stage of the evolution of the flooded nebula, a star passed near it, which at that very moment exploded as a supernova. The shock waves that arose in the flooded nebula created the necessary conditions for the further formation of planets. This hypothesis received strong support, in particular, as a result of an analysis of the chemical state of meteorita Allende. It turned out to have an unusually high amount of calcium, barium and neodymium - exactly the kind of compositional anomaly that should have arisen under the influence of a nearby supernova explosion. But supernova explosions are extremely rare phenomena. Therefore, the stated hypothesis assumes that the planets of the system form a negligible number of stars, i.e., as a rare exception.

Is this hypothesis correct? Perhaps the meteorite Allende is an international wanderer who actually once witnessed a supernova explosion, but that’s all? At the same time, in recent years, with the help of space and then ground-based means, it has been possible to discover that some stars are surrounded by dust disks of great extent. Many scientists believe that this is the future of the planet system. Along with dust disks, it is also possible to indirectly establish the presence of satellites in some stars with a mass intermediate between the star and the planet.

An interesting possibility of discovering a planet exists if the planet is inhabited and its civilization is close to our level. Let's look at this in a little more detail.

If the formation of planets and the emergence of life on them is a natural process and if the emerging life in many cases reaches a reasonable and technical level, the existence of a planet in the system could be judged by the signs of the technical activity of some civilization. Thus, if we assume that a developed society necessarily uses radio communications, radio emissions from artificial production could be detected using high-sensitivity receivers. But so far the search for constant signals has ended in disappointment.

In fact, it would be very naive to look for inhabited worlds with a civilization at approximately the same level of technological development as ours. Further, there would be many of them, they would certainly be separated from each other not only by tens or hundreds of parsecs*), but also by huge time intervals. In other words, our present may be “their” very distant past, and radio communications for “them” may be something like rock paintings. However, the problem is not hopeless. A technologically advanced and energy-rich society must emit very intense infrared or microwave radiation (sometimes called a “cosmic miracle”).

All these assumptions are based on the premise of the debts of the life of civilization. If the duration of their existence is short, it is useless to search for them by radio or microwave radiation.

Ponski radioirradiated with specific signs is a fascinating task, but what is important for us here is their invariably negative result. It is possible that the most likely reason for this is the low prevalence of diverse life in the Galaxy. In turn, the low prevalence can be associated with both the low probability of the emergence of life and its evolution into intelligent forms on the existing planet.

*) Parsec (pc) - a unit of length used in astronomy; 1 pc≈3·10¹⁶ m. Light travels this distance in just over three years.
