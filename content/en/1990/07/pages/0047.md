Tasks

1. A certain merchant increases his fortune every year by one-third, a reduction of one hundred pounds sterling, which he daily spends on his family. Three years later, the merchant discovered that his fortune had doubled. How much money did the merchant have at the beginning?

2. Solve the arithmetic puzzle shown at figure. One oblique letter corresponds to the same numbers, different letters correspond to different numbers.

3. Cut the figure at figure along the cell lines into four equal figures.

4. What four-digit number has this property: it is itself the square of an integer and the numbers formed by the first two digits and the last two digits are also perfect squares?

5. During his stay in Laputa, Guliver was interested in the monetary system of this country. He was told that the country uses coins of 1, 2 and 4 lapti, made of pure gold. The coins are round, and if you place a 1-lapti coin on a 2-lapti coin so that its edge passes through the center of the two-lapti coin, then the intersection points of the edges of the coins lie on the diameter of the one-lapti coin. The same size correspondence is observed for coins of 2 and 4 lapti. The weights of the coins are proportional to their denominations. The thickness of the single-lap coin is 1 mm. What is the thickness of the remaining coins?

Problem 1 belongs to the great English mathematician and physicist I. Newton; the remaining tasks were suggested to us by I. Antonovich, V. Vyun, A. Baboyan, A. Savin.
