Prospects for searching for habitable planets

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. XANFOMALITI

The problems of habitability and life in the Universe are among the deepest questions of philosophy. The idea of ​​existing on distant worlds by reason leaves almost no one indifferent. Thinkers from various historical eras have always addressed this topic. Man has always dreamed of meeting his own kind and with his mind populated them first with unknown lands, then with unknown celestial bodies.

When science and technology reached modern levels, the problem of searching for life in the Universe - in one form or another - was included in the scientific research plans of many countries.

Are there planets in other star systems?

Without talking about all sorts of fantasies, you can always look for life - in the only form known to us - only on planets. Now scientists know only one planetary system - ours, and there are no further results of searching for planets around other stars. Therefore, we have to use some indirect information about how far a system like the Solar one extends.

The generally accepted hypothesis of the origin of a planetary system involves the formation of planets through the condensation of a protoplanetary gas and dust cloud captured by the protocol or remaining after its formation. This hypothesis was proposed in our country by academician O. Yu. Shmidt and was developed in detail first by himself, and subsequently by his students. Regardless of how many assumptions were made by foreign scientists, in particular K. Weizsäcker in Germany.

Let us note that the transition from qualitative reasoning to a more or less rigorous theory revealed the extreme complexity of the processes involved in the formation of planets. Many authors believe that after the formation of the Sun it is more likely that the nebula's remains will condense into a second, dwarf star than into a planetary system. In other words, the probability of the formation of a double star is much higher than the formation of a star with planets. And indeed, it is double and multiple stars that make up the overwhelming majority of the “population” of the Galaxy. Therefore, the very fact of the existence of our planet requires explanation.

In order to somehow “save the situation,” several “catastrophic” hypotheses have been proposed, according to which

the planetary system could have formed as a result of some catastrophic events - explosions, mergers of stars, etc. For example, the hypothesis is being considered that at a certain stage of the evolution of the flooded nebula, a star passed near it, which at that very moment exploded as a supernova. The shock waves that arose in the flooded nebula created the necessary conditions for the further formation of planets. This hypothesis received strong support, in particular, as a result of an analysis of the chemical state of meteorita Allende. It turned out to have an unusually high amount of calcium, barium and neodymium - exactly the kind of compositional anomaly that should have arisen under the influence of a nearby supernova explosion. But supernova explosions are extremely rare phenomena. Therefore, the stated hypothesis assumes that the planets of the system form a negligible number of stars, i.e., as a rare exception.

Is this hypothesis correct? Perhaps the meteorite Allende is an international wanderer who actually once witnessed a supernova explosion, but that’s all? At the same time, in recent years, with the help of space and then ground-based means, it has been possible to discover that some stars are surrounded by dust disks of great extent. Many scientists believe that this is the future of the planet system. Along with dust disks, it is also possible to indirectly establish the presence of satellites in some stars with a mass intermediate between the star and the planet.

An interesting possibility of discovering a planet exists if the planet is inhabited and its civilization is close to our level. Let's look at this in a little more detail.

If the formation of planets and the emergence of life on them is a natural process and if the emerging life in many cases reaches a reasonable and technical level, the existence of a planet in the system could be judged by the signs of the technical activity of some civilization. Thus, if we assume that a developed society necessarily uses radio communications, radio emissions from artificial production could be detected using high-sensitivity receivers. But so far the search for constant signals has ended in disappointment.

In fact, it would be very naive to look for inhabited worlds with a civilization at approximately the same level of technological development as ours. Further, there would be many of them, they would certainly be separated from each other not only by tens or hundreds of parsecs*), but also by huge time intervals. In other words, our present may be “their” very distant past, and radio communications for “them” may be something like rock paintings. However, the problem is not hopeless. A technologically advanced and energy-rich society must emit very intense infrared or microwave radiation (sometimes called a “cosmic miracle”).

All these assumptions are based on the premise of the debts of the life of civilization. If the duration of their existence is short, it is useless to search for them by radio or microwave radiation.

Ponski radioirradiated with specific signs is a fascinating task, but what is important for us here is their invariably negative result. It is possible that the most likely reason for this is the low prevalence of diverse life in the Galaxy. In turn, the low prevalence can be associated with both the low probability of the emergence of life and its evolution into intelligent forms on the existing planet.

*) Parsec (pc) - a unit of length used in astronomy; 1 pc≈3·10¹⁶ m. Light travels this distance in just over three years.

In assessing the likelihood of both the emergence of intelligent life and the formation of a planetary system, we will consider the only evidence - this is ourselves and the Solar system. Therefore, the probability can be anything - practically from one to zero, and that is why the study of the planets of the Solar System and the search for planetary systems around other stars are so important.

Thanks to space research, planetary astronomy over the past 15-20 years has become one of the most rapidly developing areas of astronomical science. It will distract us from what may await us near distant stars and get acquainted with the rather bleak results of the search for life on the planets of the solar system.

Moon and Mercury

The chronological Moon was the first celestial body whose sterility*) was established experimentally. The absence of an atmosphere, the associated irradiation of the surface with all types of radiation existing in the near-Earth space, and sudden temperature changes are such physical conditions on the Earth’s natural satellite.

The Moon is a very large celestial body, one of the largest natural satellites in the Solar System. Its diameter is 3476 km, its mass is 1/81 that of Earth. The Moon does not have a magnetic field. (As is known, the magnetic field forms a kind of “roof” over the Earth, which protects it from contaminants and collects them in radiation fields. At the stage of the emergence of terrestrial life, this was an important factor, since irradiation of contaminants with high-energy particles also creates a sterilizing effect.) The rotation of the Moon is slowed down by the tidal influence of the Earth. As a result, during a long lunar day, the surface temperature rises to 340-370 K, and at night it drops to 130 K and below.
