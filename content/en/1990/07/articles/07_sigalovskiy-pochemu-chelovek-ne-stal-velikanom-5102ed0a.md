But in space, the load experienced by structural materials will no longer be determined by the action of the Earth’s gravitational field, but by the action of gravitational forces between parts of the structure. If these parts are very large and massive, then in the calculations it is necessary to take into account the gravitational forces between them.

So, we came to the conclusion that the existence of giants is impossible and the reason for this is gravity. But among the Liputians, from the point of view of skeletal strength, everything is fine; Moreover, they even have a twelvefold safety margin. It turns out that the less living I exist, the stronger it is. Why are there no warm-blooded animals smaller than the shrew?

Precisely because they are warm-blooded. A thermal animal, including humans, cannot be considered as a purely mechanical system. With fairly significant fluctuations in the external temperature, practically warm-blooded animals will maintain a constant body temperature (with the exception of the state of anabiosis, into which some animals, such as bears, fall in winter). Constancy of body temperature is the most important condition for the existence of highly organized life. We emit body all the time, we lose it when we exhale air heated in our lungs, due to the evaporation of moisture from the surface of the body, and we spend it on doing work. We will use the lost energy foodly. It has been experimentally established that the first law of thermodynamics, in other words, the law of conservation of energy, is completely true in relation to living organisms. There is the same amount of energy in the animal's body when describing liberation foods as when simply burning these foods to the same final substances outside the organization. Only about a third of the chemical energy of the food we digest is converted into muscle energy, while more of the energy is used for other needs - maintaining a constant body temperature, nutrition and tissue renewal, the formation of animal deposits (the body's "saving bank" in case of hunger or illness). Any transformation of energy in the body - be it digestion or muscle degeneration - ends with transformations into the tello. This body continuously moves into a colder environment. The heat balance between the international and the environment is established.

Now let's discuss one more question. Why are the smallest small-feeding waters still larger than the smallest tell-blooded inhabitants of the soup?

The fact is that shallow-feeding waters, even those with an insulating fatty layer, give up a lot of body water, and their existence becomes possible only with a sufficiently large body volume.

The main mechanism that regulates body temperature is its “central heating” - the circulatory system. The blood delivers the body from the internal organs to the capillaries under the skin, and it then separates the body from the surrounding air. For example, for a person at rest at a body temperature of 37 °C and an ambient temperature of 18 °C, body consumption is distributed in this way: approximately 75% is lost due to radiation in the infrared range with a wavelength of 9-10 microns, the rest is due to sweating and the release of air heated in the lungs. As the ambient temperature increases, the radiation consumption of the body decreases: at 30 °C they are about 30%.

Overheating of the body is eliminated by profuse sweating - it takes about 2,500 joyles to evaporate every gram of moisture from the surface of the skin. It is interesting to note that the specific power of a person (that is, the power per unit of his mass) is about 200 W/kg, which is almost 10,000 times the specific power of the Sun.

The thermoregulation system is very flexible and in tropical conditions provides a person with the opportunity to do without clothing. In general, the human body and many other animals are better adapted to cooling than to overheating. A decrease in body temperature by 10-12° is not yet fatal, whereas with an increase in temperature by 5-6° the proteins begin to coagulate. (This is why a medical thermometer does not have divisions above 42 °C.) For humans, death occurs when the body temperature is above 43 °C or below 25 °C. Over millions of years of evolution, humans have “acquired” a system of thermostats that help them maintain their temperature at a constant level.
