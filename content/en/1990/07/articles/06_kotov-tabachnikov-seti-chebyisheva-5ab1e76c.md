You've probably used carnival costumes made from paper. And you pointed out that the suits also don’t fit well and are uncomfortable to wear. The reason is that a sheet of paper is inextensible and is not able to fit tightly to an artificial surface (see the article “Geometry of a sheet of paper” in «Kvant» No. 9, 1988). But the fabric perfectly fits the convexities and concavities of the human body. How is the fabric constructed?

It consists of two practical inextensible threads forming a rectangular lattice (Fig. 1). If you press a piece of fabric against an artificial surface, the component rectangles are distorted and turn into parallelograms (Fig. 2 and 3). The fabric can be deformed without leaving the plane - to do this, it is enough to stretch a piece of fabric diagonally.

Mathematicians love precise definitions; we give them too.

A Chebyshev network *) is two families of pairwise non-touching lines on a surface; Moreover, through each point of the surface there passes exactly one line of each family and the lengths of the side of each curved quadrilateral formed by the curves are equal to (Fig. 4). Then it will be noted that the lines that make up the network are inextensible.

The simplest example is a network on a plane formed by two families of parallel lines (the cells of such a network are a parallelogram.

*) This concept was introduced by the famous Russian mathematician P. L. Chebyshev. «Kvant» already talked about his work this year: see the articles “On Bertrand’s Arrival” - in No. 1, “Inversors” - in No. 4, “Polynomials that least deviate from zero” and “The Chebyshev Problem and Trigonometric Polynomials” - in No. 6.
