In the device shown in figure 5, two golf balls - smooth and with a ribbed surface - are mounted on an axis and can be quickly rotated by an electric motor. The air flow supplied from the fan through tube D can be directed towards one of the balls; the axis carrying the balls can be raised or lowered, respectively placing either a smooth or a corrugated ball in the air flow. The pressure in this case is changed using a pressure gauge PQ, to which special tubes L and M are attached, adjacent to opposite sides of the golf ball; if the air pressure on the M side is greater than on the L side, the liquid level in the Q tube of the pressure gauge will drop; if, on the contrary, the air pressure on the L side is greater than on the M side, the liquid level in the pressure gauge tube P will decrease.

First I will show that when the air flow is directed towards the ball, but they are not spinning, the pressures on each side are the same; you see that the liquid in the device does not move. Then I turn off the fan and spin the balls. The result is the same - there is no fluid movement in the device at all. But when the ball is rotated in the direction shown in figure 5, and I apply a stream of air to them, the level of the liquid in tube Q falls and the level in tube P rises. The pressure in this case is higher on the M side, where the speed of the points on the surface of the spinning ball is opposite to the speed of the air, than on the L side, where the rotation tends to carry the ball out of the air stream. (If you reverse the direction of the ball's rotation, you will see that the pressure difference also changes.)

The case when the ball is stationary and the air moves from right to left is, from the point of view of dynamics, no different from the case when the ball flies in stationary air from left to right. So, we see that the pressure is greater on the side where the rotation makes the speed of the surface relative to the air greater than it would be without the rotation...

To show the difference between smooth and corrugated balls, place the smooth ball in the air stream and note the difference in liquid levels in the left and right knees of the device. If you now replace the smooth ball with a corrugated one, the difference in levels will more than double; this suggests that at the same rotation speed and air flow rate, the pressure difference on different sides of a ribbed ball is more than twice as high as that of a smooth one.
