There is no longer any need to assign zero values ​​to the temperature of such ice. It can be simply calculated. In fact, if the temperature of a gas is directly proportional to pressure, then the ratio of gas pressure at the temperature of boiling water and such ice is equal to the ratio of these temperatures themselves, i.e.

$$\frac{p}{p_0} = \frac{T}{T_0}.$$ (4)

But the ratio on the left side of this equation is 1.3661. Therefore, the right part is equal to this number:

$$\frac{T}{T_0} = 1,3661.$$

From here we get

$$T = 1,3661 T_0.$$

Substituting this value for $T$ into equality (3), we find

$$1,3661 T_0 - T_0 = 100,$$

and we immediately get

$$T_0 = \frac{100}{0,3661} \approx 273,15.$$

This is what distinguishes the new scale from the Celsius scale: the temperature of such ice on this scale is not zero, but 273.15 degrees. And the zero temperature is 273.15 (273 for short) degrees lower than the temperature of such ice. This is, as they say, absolute zero. This is the temperature at which the pressure of an ideal gas would become zero if that temperature were reached and if the gas were still a gas at that temperature. Since the gas pressure cannot be less than zero, the temperature on such a scale cannot be negative (less than zero).

The temperature scale just described (we omit some subtleties in its definition, practically non-existent) is called the absolute temperature scale or Kelvin scale. And the temperature itself, which is absent on this scale, is called absolute temperature. It is denoted by the letter $T$ and is expressed in kelvins (abbreviated as $K$), so the temperature of such ice is 273.15 $K$, the boiling point of water is 373.15 $K$, etc.

But the Celsius scale is also used in practice. Temperature not present on this scale is indicated by the letter $t$ and is expressed in degrees Celsius (abbreviated $^\circ C$). On this scale, the temperature of such ice is 0 $^\circ C$, the boiling point of water is 100 $^\circ C$, etc. It is clear that

$$t^\circ C = (T - 273,15) K.$$

In physics, the Kelvin scale is almost always used.
