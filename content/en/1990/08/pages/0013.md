TEMPERATURE,
HEAT, THERMOMETER

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. KIKONN

Temperature is one of those not very many physical quantities that a person learned about not only before he learned to study physics, but also before he learned to read and write. Already in early childhood, we learned that the words hot, warm, cold, reflecting our sensations, correspond to different temperature values. We hear that the temperature is high in summer and low in winter, that a healthy person has a body temperature of 36.6 degrees, and if it is higher, then you need to call a doctor...

Due to the attractiveness of the concept of temperature, we are not really aware of how unique this quantity is, how different it is from other attractive quantities, such as length, mass, volume. And the difference here is very significant. This is what it consists of.

If you connect ten rods 1 m long each, placing them next to each other, you will get a rod 10 m long. Ten masses of 1 kg each will give a total mass of 10 kg, etc. But if you connect ten bodies, each of which has a temperature of 20 degrees, then we will get a body whose temperature is 20 degrees, not 200. The temperatures of the bodies when they are connected do not add up, as they add up lengths, volumes, masses, etc. A length of 100 m - this sum has become a length of 1 m, but a temperature of 100 degrees is not the sum of these temperatures of 1 degree each, just as a person aged 15 is not the same as 15 one-year-old children! Temperature, as they say, is not an additive quantity. This is one of the most important features of this quantity.

This feature is related to the method of temperature change. To change the length of a body, you need to compare it with other bodies whose length is taken as one. Determining the mass of a body means comparing it with another mass, taken as one. After all, both the length and mass of a body are equal, respectively, to the sum of the lengths and masses of its parts.

But the temperature cannot be changed like that. But this means that the temperature value itself cannot be changed at all, since it cannot be compared with a temperature standard. How do they change the temperature?

A little history

An instrument for measuring temperature, the thermometer, was first depicted by Galileo around 1592 (the word “thermometer” itself first appeared in literature in 1624). The method of measuring temperature proposed by Galileo was assumed to be no different from the one used in our time.

The diagram of the device he invented is shown in figure 1.

A small glass balloon (a) is soldered to a thin long tube (b) with an open end. The balloon is heated by hand and the lower end of the tube is immersed in a vessel with water (c). As the container cools to ambient temperature, the water level in the tube rises above the water level in the container.

It is easy to understand that Galileo's device takes advantage of the fact that the volume of gas in a cylinder with a tube depends on temperature. Therefore, by changing the volume of gas one can judge the change in temperature.
