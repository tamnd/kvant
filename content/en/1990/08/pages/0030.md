Let us return, however, to our envelope.

The envelope has no chero. There are no two externalities: double tangents and inflection points. A double tangent is a line that touches a curve at two points, as in figure 8, $a$. And the inflection point is the point at which the curve intersects the tangent, as in figure 9, $a$. At arbitrary proximity to both the double tangent and the tangent at the inflection point, parallel tangents (Fig. 8, 6 and 9, 6) can be checked for the curve.

These parallel lines cannot cut off segments of equal area; This means that the envelope cannot have double tangents or inflection points.

But let's ask ourselves a question:

Where do cusp points appear on the envelope?

Theorem. If the midpoint of the chord $AB$ is the cusp point of the envelope, then the tangents to our curve at its ends are parallel.

Proof. Let these tangents not be parallel. Then the curve licked by points $A$ and $B$ can be approximated by tangents forming an angle. In the article “A family of straight lines dividing areas in half” it is proven that the envelope of a family of chords cutting off a triangle of fixed area from an angle is a hyperbola. That is, a smooth curve that has no keys. Therefore, our envelope will also not have keys in the middle of the chord $AB$ (the behavior of the envelope around this point is clear from figure 11).

Now suppose that the tangents at points $A$ and $B$ are parallel. Then we need additional data: at which of these two points the curvature of the curve is greater. I also wrote about the curvature of «Kvant» more than once. But we don’t believe that you don’t already know what curvature is. For example, on a larger turn (more curvature!) at the same speed, the automation skids more.

So, suppose that the curvature at point $A$ is less than at point $B$ (as at figure 12). From this figure it is clear that the midpoints $E'$ and $E''$ of the chords $A'B'$ and $A''B''$ are located on opposite sides of the chord $AB$ and are shifted relative to its midpoint $E$ towards the point $A$. This means that at point $E$ there is a tip similar to point $B$.
