In mechanics, the concept of mass is essentially about the property of inertia. And the most common way to measure the mass of bodies is by their inertia. However, in physical experiments and in practice, mass is more often measured using scales.

Inertia and weightiness in classical physics are different concepts, however, since the times of Galileo and Newton it has been established that the same characteristic is responsible for them - mass. This remarkable fact - the equality of inertial and gravitational masses - later formed the basis of the general relative theory - the modern theory of the structure of “space-time”.
In this issue of Kaleidoscope we will try, albeit using simple examples, to find out how the “two-faced” mass manifests itself.
