The planet Mars should remain at the top of the list of celestial bodies where the existence of life in its simplest forms was considered possible.

In any case, the physical conditions on Mars, quite well known from the results of space research in recent years, do not contradict this. Moreover, it is assumed that a number of microorganisms could exist in Martian conditions. In reality, the problems are, of course, much more complex. Hypothetical Martian life would originate and evolve under Martian conditions.

A necessary condition for the existence of life forms similar to those on earth is the presence of water. Information about water on Mars has expanded significantly in recent years. It was proven, for example, that huge masses of water ice on the Martian polar caps, having melted, could form a layer of water 5-10 m thick, evenly distributed throughout the planet. A large amount of water is apparently concentrated in the subsoil permafrost.

On the surface of Mars there are a large number of water erosion formations, which are associated with glaciers, mudflows, local subsoil secretions of large masses of permafrost and heavy rainfall in the distant past (2-3 billion years ago).

The latter case implies that the climate of Mars in the past was much milder than it is now. An analysis of the finished composition of some small components of the Martian atmosphere seems to confirm this possibility. Now the carbon dioxide atmosphere of Mars is very rarefied - the pressure at the surface is about 150 times lower than at the surface of the Earth.
