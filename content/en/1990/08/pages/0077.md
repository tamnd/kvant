Let us neglect capillary phenomena and assume that the core flows out of the tube very slowly. Then it is very likely that the core will flow out until the difference in air pressure forces outside and inside the tube balances the force of the remaining column of core. From here we find the pressure inside the tube: $p = p_0 - \rho gx$, where $x$ is the desired length of the remaining core core. On the other hand, the same pressure can be found from the Boyle-Mariotte law (we assume the temperature is constant)

$$p_0 \frac{l}{2} S = p(l - x)S.$$

Eliminating $p$ from the general equations, we obtain

$$p_0 \frac{l}{2} = (p_0 - \rho gx)(l - x).$$

This equation can be simplified, taking into account that the atmospheric pressure is specified in millimeters of ground (in problems of this kind this is actually more convenient). Substituting $p_0 = \rho gH$, where $H = 750$ mm, we obtain the quadratic equation

$$x^2 - (H + l)x + \frac{Hl}{2} = 0.$$

The larger root of this equation transforms the length of the tube and is not suitable for us. The second root remains:

$$x = \frac{H + l - \sqrt{H^2 + l^2}}{2} = 0,25$$ mm = 25 cm.

Problem 4. In a communicating cylindrical joint of identical dimensions, one of which is sealed and the second is open, a rtul is placed. The ground levels in the vessels are the same, the length of the part of the sealed vessel filled with air is $l_0$, the atmospheric pressure $p_0$, changed in millimeters of the ground column, is $H$. What will be the difference in air levels in the vessels if the absolute temperature of the air in the sealed vessel is doubled?

The solution to this problem is similar to the solution to the previous one, with the only difference being that the Boyle-Mariotte law must be replaced by the Obedian gas law, since the temperature of the gas changes:

$$\frac{p_0 l_0}{T} = \frac{p(l_0 + x)}{2T}.$$

Here $x$ is the distance at which the ground level in the sealed vessel drops (Fig. 4). The level of water in an open vessel rises by the same amount (the diameters of the vessels are the same), so the hydrostatic equation takes the form

$$p = p_0 + 2\rho gx.$$

Hence, substituting $p_0 = \rho gH$, we obtain the quadratic equation

$$2x^2 + (H + 2l_0)x - Hl_0 = 0.$$

One root of this equation is negative and cannot be the answer to the problem. Therefore we get

$$x = (\sqrt{(H + 2l_0)^2 + 8Hl_0} - (H + 2l_0))/4.$$

Thus, the required difference in the levels of gru in the vessels is equal to

$$h = 2x.$$
