Gas laws and mechanical equilibrium

D. ALEXANDROV

It is customary to develop a school physics course on individual topics, for example, “Fundamentals of Dynamics,” “Gas Laws,” “Electrostatics,” etc. Matching and physics problems are usually subject to the same classification. Is this good or not?

With a certain amount of experience, such a “distribution” can help the applicant, even after a quick acquaintance with the conditions of the problem, to estimate which physical laws will be useful for solving it. However, often in entrance exams there are problems that are difficult to attribute to any specific topics. This can cause certain difficulties, as a result of which a generally simple task will not be solved.

In this article we will consider a range of tasks that at first glance could be called “gas”. In fact, when dealing with their solutions, in addition to the gas laws, you also come across other laws, primarily the laws of mechanical equilibrium.

Task 1. A balloon taken from a warm room ($t_1 = +27^\circ C$) into a cold room ($t_2 = -23^\circ C$) floats freely in the air for some time. Determine the mass of the rubber shell of the ball. This is the diameter $d = 40$ cm, molar mass of air $M = 29 \text{ г/mоль}$, atmospheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5 \text{ Pa}$. The elasticity of the shell can be claimed.

In appearance, this is a general problem based on gas laws. This impression is realized under conditions under conditions - except in the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation (the equation of state of an ideal gas), there is nowhere else to substitute them. On the other hand, it is very surprising that the mass of the shell cannot be found from this equation. How is life?

Let's read the conditions of the problem again, more carefully; Sometimes the most valuable information is found not in numbers, but in words. Let's pay attention to two phrases: “The balloon... floats freely in the air” and “The elasticity of the shell can be claimed.” In the first sentence, the condition of mechanical equilibrium of the ball is stated: the force of the gravity of the rubber shell and the warm air inside the ball is balanced by the buoyant force (Archimedes' force). According to Archimedes' law, this force is equal to the weight of the liquid or gas in the volume of the body immersed in it. The second phrase means equal air pressure inside the ball and outside.
