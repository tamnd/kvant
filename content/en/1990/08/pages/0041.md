If you hit the ball so as to give it spin around its axis, you will see that it moves with tremendously increased spin to the right if the "nose" of the ball moves to the right, and with an equally increased left spin if the "nose" of the ball moves to the right.

A well-known property of shots with side spin is that the bending of the trajectory caused by such spin becomes much more pronounced as the ball speed decreases compared to the initial one. This can be easily understood if we consider how halving the speed affects movement to the side. Suppose a curved ball is thrown out of

Fig. 13.

point $A$ in the direction of point $B$. The spin will cause the ball to be disturbed, and it will hit not the point $B$, but the point $C$ (Fig. 13). As we already know, the force acting in the direction $BC$ is proportional to the product of the speed of the ball and the angular speed of rotation. Let the angular velocity of rotation be the same at different initial speeds. Then, by halving the speed, we halve the force acting in the direction; therefore, in the same amount of time as before, the ball has flown half the distance, and the time required for the ball to travel from $A$ to $B$ is doubled. Displacement occurring under the influence of a constant force is proportional to the square of time; therefore, if the force remained unchanged, the deviation to the side ($BC$) would increase fourfold, but since reducing the speed in the direction will reduce the force by half, the displacement to the side only doubles.

If the angular velocity decreased as quickly as the displacement speed, the bending of the trajectory would not increase as the speed decreased. But since air resistance has a greater effect on the speed of the ball than on its rotation, it is the linear speed that decreases faster.

The effect of wind on the movement of a spinning ball can be easily understood from the general principles that we discussed in the previous part of the lecture. Let us first consider the case of a headwind, in which the speed of the ball relative to the air decreases. Since the force caused by a spin is proportional to the speed, the headwind reduces this force, and the effective spins are more pronounced in a headwind than on a calm day.
