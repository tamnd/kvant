The tasks and questions offered to Banim’s attention are taken from the book of the American scientist J. Walker “Physical Fireworks”.
(Last year, the Mir publishing house released the second edition of this book, and the first edition was published ten years earlier.) Almost all tasks are in one way or another connected with the world around us. Therefore, you can answer many questions only by checking the relevant observations or experiments. The answers given at the end of the magazine, as a rule, only clarify the problem or suggest a path to its solution.
So, observe and experiment.

Bioelectricity

1. “Attribution” to an electrical wire. If you accidentally grab an electrical wire, so that the current through your hand is about 25 mA, then it is possible that you will not be able to remove your hand from the wire. Why? Just don’t try to test this on yourself by hand - it’s dangerous.

2. Electric eel. How can an electric eel give you an electric shock? A large eel produces voltages of up to 600 V at currents of up to 1 A. Where does it get such incredible power? Will a continuous eel discharge occur through seawater? Why doesn't the eel shock itself?

The ability of various marine animals to navigate underwater has long remained a mystery. However, research in recent years has shown that some of these animals respond to electric fields that arise from the movement of ocean currents in the Earth's magnetic field. It is believed that these fields help sea creatures navigate underwater. First, let's explain how the movement of water creates an electric field. Secondly, how can an animal detect a weak field post?

Electrostatics

Sometimes you can get an "electric shock" if you walk on the carpet or fart automatically in your seat. Obviously, in this case, the riddle somehow accumulates. Can you explain in more detail what exactly is happening? Why, for example, do you get shocked when you walk on a carpet, but nothing if you stand on it? Why are these effective depending on the time of year?

Physics students demonstrate another experiment: in order to electrify glass rods, they are furiously rubbed with cat fur or something similar. Why are they rubbed like that?
