“After installing the pipe at the bottom of the barrel, I tried to extract water from it. However, before the water followed the piston, the iron hoops and bolts with which the pipe was attached to the barrel burst.
However, the effort was not at all hopeless... After the shortcomings of the barrel installation were eliminated by using stronger bolts, three strong men pulling the rod were finally able to extract the water following the piston...
At the same time, however, a noise was heard in all parts of the barrel, as if the water was boiling violently, and this continued until the barrel, together with the extracted water, was filled with air.”
The experiment, as you can see, ended in failure. However, Guericke did not give up and came up with a technique with which he still managed to create a void in the barrel.

Later, Guericke learned to create emptiness using a new device he invented - an air pump. He, in particular, used it in his famous experiment with the Magdeburg hemispheres. You most likely know about it: two teams of lassen tore two closed hemispheres from each other, from which the air had previously been removed. The experiment was extremely effective and even the Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire, Ferdinand III, took part in its demonstration.
