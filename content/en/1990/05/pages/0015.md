Let us consider the conditions of resonant absorption during a “head-on” collision of an atom moving with a speed $v$ and a photon with some energy $\varepsilon$ (Fig. 3). According to the law of conservation of momentum

$$mv_1 = mv - \varepsilon/c,$$

where $v_1$ is the speed of the atom after photon absorption. According to the law of conservation of energy,

$$\frac{mv_1^2}{2} + E = \varepsilon + \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

Solving these equations gives the photon energy $\varepsilon$, which provides resonant absorption, and the speed of the atom $v_1$ after absorption.

At a temperature of the order of $10^3$ K, necessary for the evaporation of sodium, the speed of atoms is much less than light speed. It is reasonable to use the smallness of $v$ and $v_1$ compared to $c$ and find an approximate solution with accuracy that reflects us. To do this, we first write down the value of the condition for the balance of momentum and energy:

$$\varepsilon = mc(v - v_1),$$

(2)

$$E - \varepsilon = \frac{m(v^2 - v_1^2)}{2},$$

(3)

and divide equation (3) by equation (2):

$$\frac{E - \varepsilon}{\varepsilon} = \frac{v + v_1}{2c}.$$

The relative difference between the photon energy and the excitation energy turns out to be small. Approximated by the difference, as a first approximation we have:

$$\varepsilon \approx E,$$

(5)

$$v - v_1 \approx E/mc \approx 3 \cdot 10^{-2} \text{ m/c}.$$

The speed difference was obtained by replacing $\varepsilon$ in (2) with a slightly larger value $E$, so that the exact value of the difference is still slightly smaller.

In the next approximation, we take advantage of the smallness of the difference in speed compared to the speeds themselves and replace $v_1$ in (4) with $v$; Then

$$\frac{E - \varepsilon}{\varepsilon} = \frac{v}{c}.$$

Taking into account the difference in speeds will lead to a shift in the energy $\varepsilon$ by values ​​much smaller than the width of the level $\Gamma$, and therefore does not exist for us - it will not affect the resonance conditions. For the same reason, in expression (6), we allow the replacement of $\varepsilon$ in the denominator by $E$, so that

$$E - \varepsilon = E \frac{v}{c}$$

So, the conditions for intense absorption of counter photons have been found: their energy must be less than the energy of the level by a fraction equal to the ratio of the speed of atom to the speed of light. We must also keep in mind that the velocities of the atoms in the beam are different, so only a portion of the atoms resonate. In fact, according to (7) for the upper and lower boundaries of the level we have
