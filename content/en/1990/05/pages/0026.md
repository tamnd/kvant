Consider the points $P(x, y, z)$ and $P'(x', y', z')$ lying on the ellipses $M$ and $M_t$. One ellipsid is obtained from another by stretching in the directions of three coordinate axes. Points $P$ and $P'$ are called corresponding if, with such a stretch, $P$ turns into $P'$. In other words,

$$\frac{x}{a} = \frac{x'}{a'}, \frac{y}{b} = \frac{y'}{b'}, \frac{z}{c} = \frac{z'}{c'},$$

Where

$$(a')^2 = a^2 + t, (b')^2 = b^2 + t, (c')^2 = c^2 + t.$$

Ivory's Lemma. Let $P, P'$ and $Q, Q'$ be two pairs of corresponding points lying on the confocal ellipses: $P, Q \in M, P', Q' \in M_t$. Then $PQ' = P'Q$.

I haven't been able to come up with a geometric proof for this statement, so we'll have to do a little math. Let the coordinates

$$\left[ \left( \frac{a}{a'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] x^2 + \left[ \left( \frac{b}{b'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] y^2 +$$

$$+ \left[ \left( \frac{c}{c'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] z^2 = \left[ \left( \frac{a}{a'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] u^2 +$$

$$+ \left[ \left( \frac{b}{b'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] v^2 + \left[ \left( \frac{c}{c'} \right)^2 - 1 \right] w^2.$$

According to formulas (6), this is the equality of the equivalent equation

$$t \left( \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} \right) = t \left( \frac{u^2}{a^2} + \frac{v^2}{b^2} + \frac{w^2}{c^2} \right),$$

which is satisfied, since the coordinates of the point $P$ and $Q$ satisfy equation (3). Ivory's lemma is proven.
