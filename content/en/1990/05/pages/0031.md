mitsey, we will increase the number of units at the beginning of the word and in several such steps we will obtain words (*).)

Let us show that in our problem each word is reduced to the form

$$1...1 \underbrace{0...0}_{a} \underbrace{1}_{b} \underbrace{0...0}_{b}$$

(or, by the way, all zero). If this type has not yet been achieved, then we will select into words the segment 0...010...01 (or 0...011), starting with the first word and ending with the second unit after it. Flip this segment over and we will increase the number of ones at the beginning of the word. This can be done as long as there are at least two ones left to the right of the first zero.

The number of words of the form (**) is equal to the number of pairs of integers $(a, b)$, where $a \geq 0, b \geq 0, a + b \leq 9$, i.e. $1 + 2 + ... + 9 = 45$ (Fig. 1).

It remains to prove that among the words (**) there are no synonyms. To do this, note that with each reversal, the number of zeros, to the right of which there is an even number of ones, and the number of zeros preceding an odd number of ones (these numbers are invariants*) of our operations are preserved. Moreover, if you mark any of the zeros of the original word and count the number of ones behind it before and after reversing, it turns out that this number preserves parity: “even” zeros remain “even”, and “odd” ones remain “odd”. This is obvious for zero, remained stationary. And for a zero on the segment being turned over, this follows from the fact that the number of units of this segment of the word from the chosen zero has the same parity as to the right of it (Fig. 2). Since for words (**) the indicated invariants are equal to $a$ (the number of “odd” zeros) and $b$ (the number of “even” zeros), such words for different $a$ and $b$ cannot be synonymous.

M1199. Prove that if the equation $ax^2 + (c-b)x + (e-d) = 0$ has a root greater than 1, then the equation $ax^4 + bx^3 + cx^2 + dx + e = 0$ has at least one root.

Let the root of the equation $ax^2 + (c-b)x + (e-d) = 0$ be equal to $x = t^2$, where $t > 0$; Then

$$at^4 + ct^2 + e = bt^2 + d.$$

Let's put $f(x) = ax^4 + bx^3 + cx^2 + dx + e$. From the previous equality it follows that the numbers

$$f(t) = (at^4 + ct^2 + e) + t(bt^2 + d),$$

$$f(-t) = (at^4 + ct^2 + e) - t(bt^2 + d)$$

have different signs (since $t > 1$, the sign determines the second term - large in modulus). Therefore, on the interval $[-t, t]$ the equation $f(x) = 0$ must have a root. (We use here the fact that a continuous function that takes values ​​of different signs at the ends of a segment must vanish at some point.)

D. Fomin
