I wondered: couldn’t sound be transmitted through other media? He came up with an original experiment that showed that sound also travels in water.

What experiment would you do to confirm this fact?

If you were able to answer all the questions, this is a very good result. But you shouldn’t get too carried away. The fact is that correctly posing a problem is no less difficult than solving it. When you know what to think about, it is much easier to think.

And now - about how Otto von Guericke solved the problems he himself posed.

Situation 1. Guericke placed a barrel from which water was drained inside a larger barrel, which was also filled with water. In this way, he was able to more reliably isolate the inner barrel from the outside air. However, it was not possible to completely prevent the procession into the inner barrel. When it was opened three days later, it was partially filled with water and air. However, before opening, part of it was still empty (air entered the barrel during opening).

Situation 2: The Guericke air pump is shown at figure. The pumped-out vessel $R$ is connected by tap 1 to the cylinder $C$, in which a wooden piston $P$ with an oil-lubricated leather tire moves tightly to the walls of the cylinder. There is a valve 2 on the left side of the cylinder. To pump out air from $R$, first close valve 1, open valve 2 and move the piston to the leftmost position to remove all the air from the cylinder $C$. After this, valve 2 is closed, valve 1 is opened and the piston is moved to the extreme right position. Part of the air from the vessel $R$ passes into the cylinder $C$. After this, tap 1 is closed again, tap 2 is opened and the first step is repeated. Guericke's piston was driven by a rack and pinion. Workers will sweat a lot when they are absent.

Situation 3. Guericke placed the bell in a vessel from which the air could be pumped out using a pump. In addition, he adapted the clock mechanism so that the bell would ring automatically at certain intervals. And then he observed a weakening of the sound as the pressure in the vessel decreased. After this, it became very effectively transmitted through the air. What about through water?

Situation 4. Gericke was able to find an answer to this question with the help of an experiment that can be called physical with a very big stretch. Soon he is closer to the physiological experiments of Ivan Pavlov on dogs. Nevertheless, the idea is very insular. The “laboratory” was a pond with a bell on the shore. The bell rang whenever food was thrown to the fish in a certain place. When the fish were trained, it began to come to the feeding place simply at the signal of the bell, although the experimenters did not sweat anything on it. This was quite convincing evidence that sound is also transmitted by water. Guericke's result was later confirmed by divers who hear the sound underwater.
