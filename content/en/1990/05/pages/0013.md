I. VOROBYEV

Didn’t it seem to you that in the combination of these words - “cooling with light” - there is some kind of unsettledness? In fact, on a hot day we look for coolness in the shade, trying to hide from the sun. How about cooling a substance by irradiating it with light? After all, to understand temperature, you need to take away energy, but light brings it. However, a light refrigeration unit has been created, and with its help the scales reach a low temperature of $2,4 \cdot 10^{-4}$ K.

Let's try to find a way to “produce cold” with light.

Team of atoms and team of photons

First of all, let us remember that temperature is associated with the average kinetic energy of thermal motion of atoms:

$$\frac{3}{2} kT = \frac{m v^2}{2}.$$ (1)

To make it lower, it is enough to notice the atoms. Why not use photons for braking? A photon, as is known, has a momentum $p_\phi = \varepsilon/c$, where $\varepsilon$ is the energy of the photon, $c$ is its speed, i.e. the speed of light. If a flying atom absorbs an oncoming photon, then it, the atom, will slow down. But how can a photon “get” into a flying atom?

It is clear that there is no point in “targeting” a single atom; you need to “target” a bunch of atoms with a beam of photons. Naturally, a laser must be used as a source of decelerating light. It emits a highly directed stream of photons of the same energy. To get a directed flow of atoms, let's do the following. Let's evaporate a grain of substance in a vacuum chamber (with an electric discharge or a laser light pulse). Two partitions with small responders will separate a thin beam of vapor from the flying object. This way we get a team of atoms lying towards the laser beam (Fig. 1).

Great, the contours of the refrigeration unit are beginning to emerge. But will the light be absorbed by a relatively rarefied beam? (After all, gases, as a rule, are not transparent. And this means that photons will sing through the thickness of the gas, practically without colliding with its atoms.) And if so, will the atom be forced to slow down in this process and lose some of its energy?

Moreover, both of these issues are closely related.
