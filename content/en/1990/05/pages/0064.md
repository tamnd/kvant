On trisection of an angle and doubling of a cube

As you know, with the help of a compass and a ruler it is impossible to trisection an arbitrary angle (construct an angle smaller than a given one) and doubling a cube (construct an edge of a cube whose volume is twice the volume of a cube with a given edge). However, if additional mechanical devices are involved, both tasks are supported.

A device for trisection of an angle was invented by the Yugoslav scientist V. Sabrich (Fig. 1). It consists of rods $a$ and $b$ of length $2R$, hinged in the center of a circle $O$ with radius $R$. The masses of rods $a$ and $b$ are equal to $m$ and $2m$. A weightless rod $OM$ of length $R$ connects the center of the circle with the end $M$ of rod $b$. The pin $T$, connecting the rods $a$ and $b$, is capable of moving around the circle.

Let's hang the described vertical device at the point $V$ and fix the rod $OM$ at an angle $a$ to the vertical. Point $T$ will occupy a position where the potential energy of the system is minimal (i.e., the center of gravity of rods $a$ and $b$ occupies the lowest position). Let the rod $a$ make an angle $\beta$ with the vertical. The center of gravity of the rod $a$ is located at a distance $R$ from $\beta$ from the horizontal $OO'$ passing through the point $O$, and the center of gravity $b$ is at a distance $R \left( \cos \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} - \cos \alpha \right)$ from this horizontal. Therefore, the center of gravity

$f(\beta) = \frac{1}{3m} \left( mR \cos \beta + 2mR \left( \cos \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} - \cos \alpha \right) \right) = \frac{1}{3} R \left( \cos \beta + 2 \cos \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} - 2 \cos \alpha \right)$

from horizontal $OO'$.

The minimum of functions $f(\beta)$ is achieved at $f'(\beta) = 0$, i.e. at $-\sin \beta + \sin \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} = 0$.

Hence $\beta = \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2}$, i.e. $\beta = \alpha/3$. So, the angle $\beta$ is equal to a third of the given angle $a$.

The device for doubling the cube (Fig. 2) has been known for a very long time. It consists of a rigid right angle $AKC$, two rods $AD$ and $DC$, capable of sliding along the sides $AK$ and $KC$, leaving them perpendicular, and a diagonal straight cross. On the cross there are pins $A$ and $B$, capable of sliding angles $AKC$, including $OA = 2OB$. From the similarity of triangles $BOC$, $COD$ and $DOA$ it follows that

$$\frac{BO}{OC} = \frac{OC}{OD} = \frac{OD}{OA}.$$

From these proportions, using the equality $OA = 2OB$, we find $OA^3 = 2OD^3$.

So, if given a cube with an edge of length $OD$, then a cube with edge $OA$ has twice the volume.
