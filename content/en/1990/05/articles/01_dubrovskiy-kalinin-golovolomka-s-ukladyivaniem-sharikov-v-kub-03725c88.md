To make this puzzle, you will need 14 identical marbles and a cube-shaped box with edge $(1+\sqrt{2})d \approx 2,41d$, where $d$ is the diameter of the marble. From 13 balls, 4 figures are glued together, shown in figure 1 (obtuse angles - no $120^\circ$). It is required to place these figures in a box so that each side touches 4 balls (Fig. 2). The second task is to add the remaining ball and rearrange the figures so that each side of the box touches 5 balls, as in figure 3. (Solutions are in the next issue.)
If you mentally mark the centers of the balls of both configurations inside the box, then all 27 points will fall into the nodes of the cubic lattice. More precisely, the centers of the 14 balls in Figure 3 coincide with the top of the cube with edge $d\sqrt{2}$ and the centers of its face, and the centers of the 13 balls in Figure 2 coincide with the midpoints of the edges of this cube and its center. In this case, each “corner” ball (Fig. 3) touches three “central” ones, and each “centrized” ball touches four corner ones; each "rib" (Fig. 2) touches four other "ribs" and the inner central one, and the last one touches all 12 "ribs". You can check all these properties of most confidurations yourself or you can read the note by V. Proizvoloba “Thirteen balls in one cell” («Kvant», 1986, No. 10, p. 22), from which you will learn about what relation... Isaac Newton had to 13 balls.
