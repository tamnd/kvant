AS THE BURGOMIST OF MAGDEBURG
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. STEINBERG

This article is not entirely comprehensive. We will present to your attention several situations from the life of one of the remarkable physicists who passed away - Otto von Guericke. We will tell you how Guericke solved the problems that confronted him. But before you get acquainted with his solutions, we invite you to test your strength: think about how you would cope with such a task? Perhaps you will be able to repeat Guericke's train of thought. Or maybe you'll come up with something original. This does not require any special knowledge beyond the scope of your textbook. Only fantasy, imagination and driving sense.

Otto von Guericke (1602-1686) was born in Magdeburg. After completing his education (he studied law, mathematics and engineering at the universities of Leipzig, Hellstedt, Jena, traveled around France and England, where he met many scientists) he returned to his hometown. Here he was elected a member of the city council and devoted a lot of effort to the construction and fortification of Magdeburg. In 1646, having served Magdeburg, he was elected its burgomaster and became famous in this post not only for his reasonable management, but also for his remarkable physical experiments.

The goal of Guericke's first experiments was to obtain empty space - space not filled with number, not even with air. A widely upset view on this issue was that there should be no empty space, since “nature abhors emptiness.” This misconception could only be refuted by experience.

Later, Guericke learned to create emptiness using a new device he invented - an air pump. He, in particular, used it in his famous experiment with the Magdeburg hemispheres. You most likely know about it: two teams of lassen tore two closed hemispheres from each other, from which the air had previously been removed. The experiment was extremely effective and even the Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire, Ferdinand III, took part in its demonstration.
