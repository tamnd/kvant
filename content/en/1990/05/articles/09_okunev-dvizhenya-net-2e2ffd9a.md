No movement?

There is no movement, said the bearded sage.
The other fell silent and began to walk around with him.
He could not have objected more strongly;
Everyone praised the intricate answer.
But, gentlemen, this is a funny story
Another example comes to mind:
After all, every day the sun walks around us,
However, stubborn Galileo was right.

A. S. Pushkin. Movement

I was reading Pushkin here once. He writes excellently. About bread, about love. About pride...
And suddenly I read: “There is no movement...” How so? Well, everyone knows that there is no justice, but that there was no movement? Doubtful.
I'll find out. It turns out that 2500 years ago there was a Zelon, a philosopher. And one day he asked: “10,000 grains is a heap, 2 grains are not a heap, but where is the border?” Well, naturally, everyone around is embarrassed, they don’t know the answer. He's smarter than everyone else, you see! Then they got over it and called his prank an aporia, or a paradox. They used to say that a fool can come up with so many aporias that the wise men cannot answer. Feel better.
But Zeno does not let up, in accordance with the latter - aporia after aporia. In particular, about movement: the aporia of the arrow, the wall, Achilles.
Let the arrow fly. At some point in time it is at some point. If it is at some point, then it does not fly. Contradiction. There is no movement.
Let Hercules (they had such a weightlifter) stand at a great distance from the wall.

Could he be with her right away? No. And immediately halfway? No. And immediately halfway? No. So, no matter how much you cut it in half, you won’t get a clean zero. Contradiction. There is no movement.
Naturally, smart people object to him, saying, what do you want from a weight lifter? Of course, he doesn't pick it up very quickly. Think of something about our sprinter Achilles, he’s already outrunning everyone.
Please speak. Let him overtake the turtle, but the turtle has a head start, is he going? We agreed. Guys! - speak. When Achilles runs through this head start, the scoop will crawl though it wants to. Again a head start. It turns out to be a fairy tale about a white bull. Contradiction. There is no movement.
He expressed himself so harshly because in those days philosophers had harsh rights. If something is contrary to logic and truth, then it is a no. The truth was even dear to them.
But in our time such philosophers have appeared - just hold on! In general, they say, nothing happened without contradiction. This, in my opinion, is overkill. I think you need to at least know approximately where each theory of application is. No theory at all is also bad.
I’ll find out more about Zeno later. About 2200-2300 years later, Leibniz and Newoton began to slowly eliminate Zeno’s mathematical aporias. They came up with differential and integral calculus and learned to accurately determine when Achilles finishes the scoop. Infinite series have learned to sum and so on.
However. In all their mathematical things they rely on the theory of limits. And there is the main principle: if you have something infinitesimal, consider it zero in the limit. Guys, this means only one thing: in all Zeno’s asses, neglect the physical subjury! And from the aporia of the arrow and in general it is not clear what to do - there is no mathematics at all, just logic. And a little bit of physics. Well, of course, you can brush it off, saying that these are just words. But guys! After all, physics is not only mathematics, words are needed in it. Further, there is such a term: the interpretation mechanism of a mathematical model.
I go to my brother: what will he say? Said. First of all, quit smoking. Secondly, it is intuitively clear and no one needs it. And thirdly, every person has a biofield, that is, an aura. Or maybe there is not even one of these auras, but several. And with the help of beta and these auras you can improve your health and evaluate the quality of foods and the quantity of nitrates. This is really personal and what is needed. Like this.
Lenka's nephew also supported the conversation. Vidik says. And hard rock.
Well, about auras and hard rock. May be. What about Zeno?
And here is the most interesting thing. I read Bohr's argument with Einstein about Heisenberg's uncertainty principle and felt something like Zeno. Moms, this is the same thing! Heisenberg said so: we can only determine the coordinates and speed of a simultaneous thing approximately approximately. And the more precise the coordinate, the worse the speed, and vice versa. I sat down and counted. The calculation is elementary, only in addition to the uncertainties of the coordinates and speed, you also need to specify the mass of the arrow, Hercules, Achilles and the turtle.
Here's the formula:
$$\Delta x \cdot \Delta v \geq \frac{\hbar}{2m},$$
where $\hbar$ is Planck’s constant, a world constant.
And then any schoolchild can prove to Zeno where his goal of the stories is declared, since he enters the field of quantum mechanics.
The aporia of the arrow is a little more complicated, but it is also clear how to bring it to a form convenient for solution - to agree on what “Moves” ($v \pm \Delta v$) means and what “is at a given point” ($x \pm \Delta x$).
