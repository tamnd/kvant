We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent no later than July 15, 1990 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Solutions to problems from different issues of magazines or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. On the envelope in the “To” column there is our “Problem «Kvant» No. 5 - 90” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1221” or “F1228”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. In the letter, send an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solutions).

The condition of each original problem proposed for publication is assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you are a teacher.

M1221. Construct a triangle along two sides so that the median drawn to the triangle divides the angle of the triangle in a ratio of 1:2.

B. Chuchin

M1222. Let $m > 1$ be a natural number, $s$ the most integer for which $2^s \leq m$. Prove that a) for any $s + 1$ integers, you can choose several numbers and place the signs of the plane and the minute between them so that the resulting sum is divisible by $m$. 6) the estimate in point a) is unimprovable: there are s integers such that no sum of several of them, with any arrangement of signs, is divisible by $m$.

B. Leo

M1223. Several blots are placed on a square sheet of paper from side $a$, the area of ​​each of which is no more than 1. Prove that if each straight line parallel to the sides of the sheet intersects no more than one blot, then the total area of ​​the blots is no more than $a$.

A. Razborov

M1224. From the vertex of the triangle a segment is drawn to a point on the opposite side, divided by the inscribed circle into three equal parts. can this segment be a) height; 6) median; c) the bisector of a triangle?

B. Senderov

M1225*. Prove that a) if for the natural numbers $a$ and $b$ the number $(a^2 + b^2)/(ab - 1)$ is a natural number, then it is equal to 5. 6) the equation $x^2 - 5xy + y^2 + 5 = 0$ has an infinite number of solutions in natural numbers.

S. Mamikonyan, G. Odanesyan

F1228. Two hoops of different radii are made from a standard elastic thin tape. When sliding along a horizontal table, the hoops experienced braking due to friction, and the forces were proportional to the speeds of the hoops and their transverse size. If you push the smaller hoop with speed $v_0$, it will travel $L_0$ to a stop. Let's push the small hoop so that it collides with the larger one, having a speed of $v$ before the impact. How far apart will the hoop stop?

A. Korshkov

F1229. The boiler was lowered into a glass of water, and the water began to gradually heat up. A graph of water temperature versus time is shown in figure 1. After three minutes, the boiler is disconnected from the network. How long will it take for the water to cool to 50 degrees? up to 30?

Z. Rafailov

F1231. Two cylindrical coils with ferrite cores are placed close to each other and connected in series. The inductance of one of them is 1 mH, the other is 2 mH, and the modified inductance of the coils connected in series is 3.6 mH. Without changing the relative position of the coils, they are switched to parallel (Fig. 2). What inductance will we change now?
A. Zilberman

F1232. At a great distance from the Earth there is the planet DVK-1, around which the BK-0010SH satellite is orbiting in a circular orbit. A radio station is installed on the satellite, emitting a constant frequency signal. This signal is received on Earth, however, interruptions occur - for 45 minutes there is a signal, then for 45 minutes it is absent, then again for 45 minutes there is, etc. The matter is not limited to this - the frequency of the received signal changes relative to the average value $f_0 = 1,5 \cdot 10^9$ Hz according to the law shown at figure 3 ($\Delta f = 3 \cdot 10^4$ Hz). Assuming that the Earth-planet segment lies in the orbital plane, find the mass of DVK-1.
M. Gavrilov
