M1194. a) From point $M$ inside the straight left ABCD of area $S$, bic sectors ME, MF, MG, MH of triangles AMB, BMC, CMD, DMA are drawn. Prove that the area $S_0$ of the quadrilateral EFGH satisfies the inequalities
$$\frac{3}{8} S < S_0 \leq \frac{1}{2} S$$

b) For which points $M$ does the equality $S_0 = S/2$ hold?

If the point $M$ lies on one of the midlines of the direct left, then, obviously, $S_0 = S/2$ (Fig. 1). Let us show that for all other points $M$
$$\frac{3}{8} S < S_0 < \frac{1}{2} S$$

Let $A$ be the vertex of the direct left closest to the point $M$ (it is uniquely determined; Fig. 2). Let us show that $AE < BG < BC/2$. Obviously, these inequalities are equivalent to the following: $AE^2/ED^2 < BG^2/GC^2 < 1$. Replacing here the relations in which the bic sectors divide the segments $AD$ and $BC$ by the relations of the corresponding sides of the triangles $AMD$ and $BMC$, we arrive at the inequality
$$\frac{AM^2}{MD^2} < \frac{BM^2}{MC^2} < 1.$$ (1)

Let $x, x_1, y$ and $y_1$ be the distances from point $M$ to side $AD, BC, AB$ and $CD$ (Fig. 3), then
$$\frac{AM^2}{MD^2} = \frac{x^2+y^2}{x^2+y_1^2} = 1 - \frac{y_1^2-y^2}{x^2+y_1^2}, \frac{BM^2}{MC^2} = 1 - \frac{y_1^2-y^2}{x^2+y_1^2}.$$
Hence inequalities (1) follow, since $x_1 < x_1$, $y < y_1$. Similarly, it is proved that $AF < DH < DC/2$. Let us draw lines parallel to $AB$ through points $E$ and $G$, and through points $F$ and $H$ lines parallel to $AD$. These lines limit the straight line $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ area $s$ (see Fig. 2). From the proven inequalities it follows that triangles $EFA_1, FGD_1, GHC_1$ and $HEB_1$ cover the quadrilateral EFGH in one layer, with the exception of the straight line $A_1B_1C_1D_1$, which is covered twice. Therefore, the sum of the areas of these triangles is $S_0 + s$. Adding the area $S_0$ to the equal sum of the areas of triangles $AEF, BFG, CGH$ and $DHE$, we obtain the area $S$ of the entire given straight line. Therefore, $2S_0 + s = S$, i.e. $S_0 = (S - s)/2$. It remains to note that $0 < s < S/4$ (the right inequality follows from the fact that $FD_1 = BG < BC/2, EA_1 = DH < DC/2)$.
