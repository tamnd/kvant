F1206. Two long and wide bands of uniform charging with a charge density of +σ (upper) and –σ (lower). Find the magnitude and direction of the electric field at point M, which is located at a height h above the edge of the stripes on an axis lying in their symmetry plane (Fig. 1). The distance between the stripes d is small compared to h.

Fig. 1.

Fig. 2.

Let us depict a cross section of the system by the plane of its symmetry passing through a given point M (Fig. 2); charged stripes continue indefinitely on both sides of the drawing plane). Let's draw two close rays MA₁A₂ and MB₁B₂. Note that a very narrow strip of width A₁B₁ is equivalent to a band with a linear charge density ρ₁ = σ·A₁B₁ located from point M at a distance r₁ ≈ A₁M. The voltage ΔĒ₁ of the electric field created by this thread at point M is directed along the line A₁M and is equal to

$$\Delta E_1 = \frac{\rho_1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r_1} = \frac{\sigma \cdot A_1B_1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 \cdot A_1M},$$

where ε₀ is the electrical constant.*)

The contribution of the corresponding lower band A₂B₂ is similar:

$$\Delta E_2 = \frac{\rho_2}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r_2} = \frac{-\sigma \cdot A_2B_2}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 \cdot A_2M}.$$

From the similarity of the triangles MA₁B₁ and MA₂B₂ it follows that ΔE₂ = -ΔE₁, i.e. the stripes A₁B₁ and A₂B₂ (or rather, the electric fields created by them) exactly compensate each other. Ultimately, only the P₁Q band remains uncompensated (see Fig. 2). Since the width of the stripes is large (P₁N ≫ h), the direction Ē of the electric field created by the P₁Q strip is directed practically horizontal (to the right). Since the triangles MQN and P₁QP₂ are similar, P₁Q/QN = d/h ≲ 1, i.e., the width of the strip P₁Q is small compared to the distance from it to point M. This means that this strip can again be replaced by a thread with a linear charge density ρ = σ P₁Q, located at a distance r ≈ QM ≈ QN from point M.

Thus, the desired direction of the electric field is directed almost horizontally to the right and is approximately equal to

$$E = \frac{\rho}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} \approx \frac{\sigma \cdot P_1Q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 \cdot QN} = \frac{\sigma d}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 h}.$$

A. Semenov
