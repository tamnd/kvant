You will find that as the centers of vapor condensation in the flask decrease, the white circle observed in the experiment with its center at the light source develops a reddish, or rather brownish, border. Gradually the white circle turns yellow, and the border forms a brownish-red ring. Then this ring turns red, followed by a blue one. Each time the picture becomes more and more vibrant and new colors appear in it: crimson, greenish-blue, lilac - you can’t list everything!

Let us recall that all these phenomena are caused by a gradual increase in the diameter of fog droplets. At some point, looking at the flask from an arm's length distance, you will suddenly notice that all of it is almost uniformly painted in a certain bright and saturated color (Fig. 3). This non-privibuous and extremely red phenomenon can be explained by the interference of light focused by the droplets and diffracted by them.
