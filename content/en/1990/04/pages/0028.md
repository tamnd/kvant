Since the height of the steam cylinder is greater than the height of the pump cylinder and, as a result, the stroke of the piston rod of the steam cylinder is greater than the stroke of the piston rod of the pump cylinder, Watt strengthened the head of the steam rod at point $n$, which has a larger amplitude, while the head of the pump rod was strengthened at point $m$.

The general view of the steam engine with Watt's parallelogram is depicted on figure 6. In this form it appeared in 1784.

Watt himself considered his highest scientific achievement to be the invention of straightening mechanisms, and the relative regulator, which now bears his name and is the cornerstone of the theory of automatic control.

Chebyshev straightening mechanism

The famous Russian mathematician and mechanic, Academician P. Chebyshev, invented several replacement approximate straightening mechanisms that

Fig. 8.

Fig. 10.

he found them with the help of his theory of functions that least deviate from zero (1858). Without being able to present this theory in any detail, we will only describe the structure of one of the most practical Chebyshev mechanisms.

This mechanism (Fig. 8) consists of a link $AB$, in the middle of which there is a hinge $C$. This hinge needs a ringing $OC$ equal to $\frac{AB}{2}$, so $OC=AC=BC$. The other end $O$ of link $OC$ is fixed in a fixed hinge $O$. Point $A$ moves along a circular arc with the help of a third link $DA$, fixed in a fixed hinge $D$. When the following correspondences between link sizes are met:

$$OD = \frac{OC + CA + AD}{3}, OC = AC = BC$$

the point $B$ of the Chebyshev mechanism is described by the curve $mPn$, the part of which $mn$ differs very little from the straight line. Chebyshev showed that the most deviation $\delta$ of the curve segment $mn$ from the rectilinear direction parallel to $OD$ is calculated by the formula

$$\delta = \frac{1}{2} \left( \sqrt{\frac{4}{9} (r-a)(2r+a)} + \frac{(4a-r)^3 r}{12(2r+a)^2} \right) - \sqrt{\frac{4}{9} (r-a)(2r+a)},$$

where $r=AB$, $a=2AD$. This is a very small value. Let's say at $AC=OC=BC=32$ inches (81.3 cm), $OD=25$ inches (63.5 cm), $DA=11$ inches (27.9 cm), $\delta=0,032$ inches (0.081 cm).
