4. Connect the tube with a rubber hose 5 to the pressure pump 6. The position of the eye is also shown on figure, observed 7, the distance from which to the bottle can be arbitrary ranging from 1 to 6 m.

Turn on the light bulbs, create a temperature or twilight in the office and pump air into the bottle. Ask the audience to look carefully at the bulbs and after a short pause, quickly remove the cap from the neck of the bottle. Immediately around the light bulb, within the size of the bottle, you can see bright and rather bright multi-colored beads.

What happens in the bottle? You pump air and the pressure in the bottle increases. When you pause, the air in the bottle becomes room temperature. There is a little water remaining after rinsing for two bottles, and there is a thin film of water on the walls. Therefore, in addition to air, the bottle contains saturated water vapor.

You release the cork, and the bottled air quickly expands. At the same time, since the process is close to adnabatic (that is, it occurs without heat exchange with the environment), the air temperature in the bottle drops, the steam becomes supersaturated and easily condenses into droplets of water on dust particles, which are so abundant in the air.

This creates fog in the bottle. You can easily detect it if you look at the bottle from the side: at the moment fog appears, the light beam coming from the light bulb becomes visible due to scattering. It is immediately noticeable that the mist in the bottle is quite rare. This indicates that, despite the significant, according to our subjective estimates, dust content in the air, there are relatively few dust particles as centers of condensation of water vapor into bottles.

Why do rings appear? The spherical droplets of water that make up the fog float in the air filling the bottle. They are transparent, which means light can be refracted into them, and they are quite small, which means light can diffract onto them. Both assumption and light diffraction can, in principle, produce multi-colored rings. What phenomenon is responsible for the results of our experiments?

The refraction and reflection of light in spherical drops of water explains the rainbow. But you can observe the radius only at certain angles of the wave. In addition, a rainbow, to a first approximation, does not depend on the size of the drops. In our continuation of the experiments, the diameters of the rings changed intermittently, and this can only be explained by a change in the size of the fog droplets. finally, a well-bright rainbow is obtained on large drops of water with a diameter of 1-2 mm, and there are obviously no such drops in bottles. So, we conclude that the multi-colored rings observed in the described experiments are most likely due to the diffraction of light on small drops of water.
