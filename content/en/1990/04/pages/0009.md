AND THE WIND COME BACK..."
OR PERIODICITY
IN MATHEMATICS

A. VELOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. SAPIR

The wind goes south and goes north,
it spins and spins as it goes, and the wind returns to its spin.

Ecclesiastes, ch. 1, art. 6

Perhaps the greatest attraction of mathematics is that the same ideas arise in solving a wide variety of problems that seem to have nothing in common.

In this article we will try to follow one of the general ideas - the idea of ​​repetition, cyclicity, recurrence, which was discussed at the Kirov Summer Mathematical School in 1986-1988, as well as at the Winter School of Young Mathematicians in 1988 at the Sverdlovsk Pedagogy.

Many ideas formulated in “pure form” look trivial, for example Dirichlet’s principles. But - an amazing fact - often the mere mention of such a “trivial” makes a previously difficult task seem arbitrary. What's the matter here? You've probably come across figure for children, where you need to find, say, a hare in the forest thickets. We know well what a hare looks like, but where it sits is not immediately visible. And if you don’t say that it’s a hare sitting, then it’s very difficult to see it. Likewise in mathematics, it is useful to know in advance what simple but important ideas are hidden in the wilds of possible reasoning. In the future, your experience will tell you what “animals” can be found in a particular forest. Now let's get back to the curve.

Ecclesiastes is found everywhere in nature: carcels and the rotation of planets, seasons and numbers in the decimal notation of dobes - practically everything in existence occurs in some kind of cyclic movement, a “round dance”. It turns out that there is a common reason causing periodicity.

What is the secret of periodicity? Just as when studying living tissue one of its cells is examined under microsap, let us first consider a simple “single-cell” problem.

Problem 1. Find the last digit of 21000.

Let's ask a more general question: what does the sequence of ending powers of two look like? First, let's write down a few terms:

1, 2, 4, 8, 6, 2, 4, 8, 6, 2, 4, ...
The number two was repeated first, then they began to repeat the numbers 4, 8, 6 and almost very much thought that this would continue indefinitely. But why? Yes, because only the last digit of each degree determines the last digit of the next degree, and the remaining digits do not affect, so the repetition of one digit causes the repetition of the next one, and therefore all subsequent ones.

This will create a period - a segment of a series between two repetitions. Now it is easy to determine that the last digit of the number 21000 is 6 (check).

The next problem is already “two-cell”: its solution is similar to the solution to problem 1, if you can first prove that repetition will occur.
