We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After creating the task, we are promised to indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than June 15, 1990 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 4—90” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1216” or “F1223”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope).

Please attach the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.

Tasks
M1216—M1220, F1223—F1227

M1216. Find the angles of an acute triangle ABC if it is known that its bissertis AD is equal to the side AC and perpendicular to the segment OH, where O is the center of the circumscribed circle, H is the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle ABC.

K. Nistorecky (Romania)

M1217. For any natural number n, prove the equality
$$\frac{1}{n^2} + \left( \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{n-1} \right)^2 + \dots + \left( \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{n-1} + \dots + 1 \right)^2 = 2n - \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n} \right).$$
Yu. Burman

M1218. On the segment AC, point B is taken and arcs are constructed: $\overline{AC} = \alpha$ and $\overline{BC} = \beta$, the sum of their degree magnitudes is equal to $\alpha + \beta = 360^\circ$, lying in the same half-plane from the straight line AC. An arbitrary arc AB intersects them at points K and L (Fig. 1). Prove that it is possible to wash AC at the same point.

V. Mikhailov (Bulgaria)

M1219*. Prove that for any n positive numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ ($n > 1$) the following inequality holds:
$$(x_2 + \dots + x_n)^{x_1} + \dots + (x_1 + \dots + x_{i-1} + x_{i+1} + \dots + x_n)^{x_i} + \dots + (x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_{n-1})^{x_n} > n - 1.$$
For example, for any positive numbers $x, y, z$ the inequality
$$(x + y)^2 + (y + z)^x + (z + x)^y > 2.$$
A. Mikhailov

M1220*. Let us define the sequence $b_n$ by the conditions: $b_1 = 0, b_2 = 2, b_3 = 3, b_{n+1} = b_{n-1} + b_{n-2}$ for $n \geqslant 3$. Prove that when p is prime, the number $b_p$ is divisible by p.
D. Fomin

F1223. The horizontal surface of the ejection conveyor is driven by eccentrics that synchronously rotate around the fixed horizontal axes O and O' (Fig. 2). At what angular speed of rotation of the eccentrics will the parts lying on the surface learn to move? In what direction will they move when the eccentrics rotate clockwise? Coefficient of friction of parts on the surface k, radius of eccentrics r.

E. Butikov

F1224. A round hole with a diameter of $d = 10$ cm is made in the horizontal bottom of a cylindrical vessel to drain water. If the hole is opened, not all the water pours out, some of it remains at the bottom. Estimate the mass of this remaining water if the vessel is poorly liquefied
