Proof. Conditions about the following notations:
$AB=DC=m, AD=BC=n$.

Let's draw a segment $BL$ parallel to $AD$ and draw an arc from point $B$ with radius $BL$. This arc will pass through the point $C$, since
$BL=DA=BC$.

Let us draw a tangent $AM$ from point $A$ to this arc. The square of the tangent $AM$ is equal to the product of the secant and its outer segment. Hence,
$AM^2=AL \cdot AC=DB \cdot AC$. (4)

From triangle $ABM$ we have
$AM^2=AB^2-BM^2=AB^2-BC^2$
$=m^2-n^2$.

Comparing this equality with (4), we obtain
$DB \cdot AC=m^2-n^2=\text{const}$,
Q.E.D.

Letters on Physics

(For the beginning, see p. 2)

At the Moscow Polytechnic Institute, at the Faculty of Engineering and Physics, there are special groups that train theoretical physicists. The choice of specialty will occur, as a rule, in the 3rd year, and for the first two to two and a half years, future experimental physicists and theoretical physicists study together. Those who early (from the first months of study) made a choice, deciding to become a theoretical physicist, are in danger: considering that the main thing for them is mathematics, they “do not notice” general physics - the course that introduced the student to the world of physics... We must remember: you can become a good theoretical physicist only by knowing well and understanding not only mathematics (of course, you cannot do without it!), but also physics, which many schoolchildren and first-year students have difficulty studying. I wanted to write “for some reason difficulties arise,” but I felt it was “for some reason” in physics. General physics is the most difficult subject both to teach and to master.

M. Kaganov
