Problem 2. Prove that the sequence of remainders from division by 1990 walls of the number 7 is periodic, and the length of the period is not greater than 1990.

You are well aware that a rational number can be decomposed into a periodic decimal fraction. Let's distance the sequence of digits of this periodic fraction. How do we set periodicity? When dividing by the “corner”, we work with a sequence of remainders (multiply, subtract, take the remainder; number method, find the incomplete quotient, multiply, subtract, take the remainder; etc.). So, each remainder uniquely determines the partial quotient (decimal digit) and the next remainder.

The remainders will begin to repeat (their finite number), which means that the decimal places will begin to repeat.

So, the periodicity of the decimal places of a rational number is obtained as a consequence of the periodicity of the corresponding remainders. Now we can begin the “multicellular” task.

Task 3. Residents of the Lawai Islands are proud that they have had a resident form of government since time immemorial. Every 4 years, either a resurvlik or a democrat is elected as a representative. Local sociologists have discovered a constructive law by which the party affiliation of the next resident is determined. Although this law is secret, information has leaked to the press that the party membership of the next resident is completely determined by the party membership of the previous ten residents. Resurvlike has ruled Lawaikh for the last three terms. Prove that there will always be periods of government of three resources in a row.

It is enough for us to show that the party affiliations of residents are periodically repeated. We call it "pre-election state", or simply "state", the sequence of parties to which the last 10 residents belonged. Each state determines the party affiliation of the next resident, and hence the next pre-election state. There are a finite number of states (no more than $2^{10}$), therefore, according to the Dirichlet principle, some of them will repeat. The repetition of one state leads to the repetition of the following states, and from this moment the state begins to repeat periodically, which means that the sequence of right-handed games will be periodic. But since the resident form of government on Lawaikh has existed since time immemorial, the repetition of the two states is already happening, and the state has been repeating itself for a long time?

As you can see, in all the analyzed tasks there is the same reasoning that establishes periodicity. Let us formulate it in general terms.

Reasoning 1. If something can only be in a finite number of states, and the state at a given moment in time is uniquely determined by the state at the next moment in time, then, from a certain moment, the state begins to periodize.

However, in the task of the pro-residents, it was necessary to check that at this moment we are in a period - only then can we say that there will always be moments of government of three resource offices in a row. For this it took “time immemorial.” This is an important point: reasoning 1 asserts a periodicity that has existed since a certain moment, but it is not at all necessary that the periodic state repeat itself. (This can already be seen in the example of Problem 1. Another example is periodic fractions.) If the period does not begin immediately, then the sequence of states preceding the period is called pre-period. To prove that the initial state will ever occur, one must also show the absence of a preperiod.
