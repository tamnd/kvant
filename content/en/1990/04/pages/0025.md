Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
Y. SOLOVIEV

With the invention of the steam engine and steam pumps, the theory of hinged mechanisms began to rapidly develop - systems composed of solid links interconnected by hinges and designed to convert the movement of one or more links into the required movements of other links. For about a year, the development of the theory of hinge mechanisms was determined by the desire to solve in the most satisfactory way the problem that was so necessary for the English mechanic James Watt in developing a steam engine.

Watt decided to arrange a steam engine as follows (Fig. 1). He placed a piston in the steam cylinder AB, which moved back and forth inside the cylinder. From the piston came the piston rod CD, passing

sew through the cylinder cover. Since the piston rod is integral with the cylinder, the head D of the piston rod made a linear movement back and forth. The yoke EF was mounted on the column HK using the hinge joint K, to which the connecting rod FM was connected via the hinge F,
