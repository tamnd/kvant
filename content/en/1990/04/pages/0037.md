M1195. The sequence $x_1, x_2, \dots$ is such that for any natural numbers $m$ and $n$

$$|x_{m+n} - x_m - x_n| < \frac{1}{(m+n)}.$$

Prove that this sequence is an arithmetic progression.

By condition, for any natural $n$ and $k$

$$|(x_{n+1} - x_n) - (x_{k+1} - x_k)| = |(x_{n+k+1} - x_n - x_{k+1}) - (x_{n+k+1} - x_{n+1} - x_k)| \leq |x_{n+k+1} - x_n - x_{k+1}| + |x_{n+k+1} - x_{n+1} - x_k| < \frac{2}{n+k+1} < \frac{2}{n}.$$

Fixing $k$ and $n$ tending to infinity, we obtain that the sequence $x_{n+1} - x_n$ has a limit equal to $x_{k+1} - x_k$.

No $k$ can be chosen here by anyone, therefore, the difference $x_{k+1} - x_k$ does not depend on $k$, i.e. $x_k$ is an arithmetic progression.

O. Izhboldin
