Here is a typical example (see article by A.I. Orlov “Dirichlet’s Principle”, «Kvant», 1971, No. 7):

Problem 4. Prove that there is a term in the Fibonacci sequence that is divisible by 1990. (Recall that the Fibonacci sequence starts with two ones, and each subsequent term is equal to the sum of the previous two.)

Let's write down the formula to determine the estimated Fibonacci number:

$$u_n = u_{n-1} + u_{n-2}.$$

First of all, let's study the remainders of the Fibonacci numbers when divided by 1990. We would like to find a remainder equal to zero. Well, how to look for it? That's the task. However, let's turn to the past (the problem about previous ones showed the usefulness of studying history). Note that the sequence can be “continued into the past” using the formula:

$$u_{n-2} = u_n - u_{n-1}.$$

Then we get that $u_0 = 0$. Wonderful! Zero is divided by 1990.

It remains to establish the periodicity of the remainders, starting from the zero term. Note: we do not need to find a member of the Fibonacci sequence with zero remainder - it is enough to establish its existence. It is not difficult to understand that the remainder of one Fibonacci number cannot be said about the remainder of the next one. Here the problem about previous ones comes to the rescue: after all, in order to predict the outcome of the elections, it is not enough to know the party affiliation of the previous resident - the results of the last 10 elections are needed. That’s why we called the party affiliation of the last 10 residents “pre-election state.” Now it is clear that as the state of the term $u_n$ we need to take a pair of remainders from division by 1990 terms $u_{n-1}$ and $u_{n-2}$. The two previous remainders already determine the next one. Here is the key to the solution: firstly, the "state" determines the remainder of the number, and secondly, each "state" determines the next state. Now everything is clear: the state is a finite number (estimate how many), they will teach it to repeat, which means repeating the remainders of Fibonacci numbers. However, it’s too early to rejoice. Why does the period start with a zero term? Here the idea of ​​“reverse motion” will help us: the state of each remainder determines the state of not only the next, but also the previous one (the formula $u_{n-2} = u_n - u_{n-1}$ convinces us of this), and hence the absence of a preperiod.
