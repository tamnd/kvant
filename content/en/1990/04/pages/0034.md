M1192. It is known that all edges of the polyhedron $M$ are equal to each other and touch a certain ball. a) Let one of the boundaries $M$ have an odd number of sides. Prove that there is a circumscribed ball around $M$.
b) Is it necessary under the conditions of point a) that the ball described in $M$ exists?
c) Let all faces $M$ have the same number of sides. Prove that the ball described in $M$ exists.
d) Under the conditions of point b), is there necessarily a circumscribed ball?

Let $A_1A_2...A_n$ be one of the faces of our polyhedron. It is described around the circle along which a given ball intersects the plane of this face. From the equality of its side and the equality of tangents to the circle drawn from one vertex, it follows that tangents drawn from two vertices taken through one (for example, $A_1$ and $A_3$, $A_2$ and $A_4$) are equal (Fig. 1). The poses of the triangle $OA_1A_2$, $OA_2A_3$, ... (where $O$ is the center of the circle) are also equal to each other.

a) Going around the vertices of the face $A_1...A_n$ through one, if $n$ is odd, at some moment we will end up at a vertex adjacent to the original one. Consequently, the tangents to the circle drawn from these two vertices, and therefore from any vertex, are equal. In other words, this ball touches all sides $A_1...A_n$ at their midpoints. But then this is also true for any adjacent face. And since any two faces can be connected by an integer of adjacent faces, each edge of the polyhedron touches the ball in its middle. It follows that all vertices are removed from the center of a given ball at the same distance (equal to $\sqrt{R^2 + a^2}/4$, where $R$ is the radius of the ball, $a$ is the length of the edge), i.e. all the vertices lie on the same sphere.

b) Answer: not necessarily. For example, a ball cannot be inscribed into a regular triangular prism with equal edges, but a ball touching all its edges exists.
