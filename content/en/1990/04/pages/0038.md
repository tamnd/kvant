where

$$N_1 = \frac{1}{2} mg - \frac{H}{2L} F_{\text{тр}}.$$

When accelerating the platform $F_{\text{тр}} = ma_0$. On the other hand, at the moment the container begins to slide, $F_{\text{тр}} = \mu N_1$, where $\mu$ is the friction coefficient. From here we get

$$ma_0 = \frac{1}{2} \mu mg - \frac{H}{2L} \mu ma_0 \Rightarrow \mu = \frac{2a_0}{g - a_0 H/L}.$$

When the platform is braking with acceleration $a$, the friction force is directed to the right and is equal to $F_{\text{тр}} = ma$, and at the moment the container begins to slide, it is $F_{\text{тр}} = \mu N_1$. Similar to the previous one, we get

$$ma = \frac{1}{2} \mu mg + \frac{H}{2L} \mu ma \Rightarrow \mu = \frac{2a}{g + a H/L}.$$

From two expressions for $\mu$ the acceleration is found:

$$a = \frac{a_0}{1 - 2a_0 H/(gL)}.$$

Note that with strong friction, the container can turn over without starting to slide. Let's find the “critical” values ​​of the friction and acceleration coefficients. When flipping $N_2 = 0$ and $F_{\text{тр}} = ma_{\text{кр}}$; hence,

$$N_1 = mg,$$
$$mg \frac{L}{2} = ma_{\text{кр}} \frac{H}{2}.$$

From here

$$a_{\text{кр}} = g \frac{L}{H}, \quad \mu_{\text{кр}} = \frac{L}{H}.$$

This value $\mu$ corresponds to acceleration during acceleration

$$a_0 = g \frac{L}{3H}.$$

So,

$$a = \frac{a_0}{1 - 2a_0 H/(gL)}$$ at $a_0 < g \frac{L}{3H}$.

If $a_0 > g L/(3H)$, then with sufficiently intense braking the container will turn over without sliding.
