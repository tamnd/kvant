Wire, tape recorder, burning machine and mathematics

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. SAVIN

There is a simple way to fairly accurately measure the diameter of a thin wire using a ruler. To do this, wrap the wire around a pencil, pressing the coil tightly against the coil; counting the number of turns, measure the length of the resulting cylinder (Fig. 1), and then divide

this length by the number of turns. If, say, you made 20 turns, and the length turned out to be 5 mm, then the diameter of the wire is 0.25 mm.

It's even easier to find the length of a tape. The tape wound on a reel has the shape of a ring, the area of ​​which is equal to $\frac{\pi D^2}{4} - \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4} (D^2−d^2)$. Let's find this area differently. If the film thickness is l and the length is L, then the area of ​​the ring is lL. Hence $lL = \frac{\pi}{4} (D^2−d^2)$ and $L = \frac{\pi}{4l} (D^2−d^2)$.
