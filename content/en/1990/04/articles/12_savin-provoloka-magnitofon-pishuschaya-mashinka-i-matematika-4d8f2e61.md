Let the distance between the axes of the coil be $R$. At the beginning, the distance between the coils is $(R - \frac{D + d}{2})$ (see Fig. 4). Let the length of the rewound film be equal to $x$. Let's calculate the distance between the coils now. The area of ​​the large coil decreased by $xl$ ($l$ is the thickness of the film) and became equal to $\frac{\pi D^2}{4} - xl$, hence the radius of this coil becomes $r_1 = \sqrt{\frac{D^2}{4} - \frac{xl}{\pi}}$. Matching the radius of the second coil will increase, its area becomes $\frac{\pi d^2}{4} + xl$ and its radius $r_2 = \sqrt{\frac{d^2}{4} + \frac{xl}{\pi}}$. Now the distance between the coils will be equal

$$R - \left( \sqrt{\frac{D^2}{4} - \frac{xl}{\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{d^2}{4} + \frac{xl}{\pi}} \right).$$

Sitting down at the fast machine to retype this story, I again saw the subject of my thoughts - the tape, rewinding from one reel to another (Fig. 8). This is a red ribbon that transfers the shape of the letter striking the card onto the paper. Unlike a tape reel, the reel on which the typewritten tape is wound rotates evenly - at the same angle $\alpha$ (in my machine at $12^\circ$). In this case, the tape moves along the shotgun by a certain amount, and the next blow falls on a different place on the tape.

Let's try to determine this displacement value. If we denote the radius of the reel with wound tape as $r$ (Fig. 9), then this movement is equal to $\frac{2 \pi r \alpha}{360^\circ}$ if $\alpha$ is expressed in degrees, and $\alpha r$ if $\alpha$ is expressed in radians. As you can see, counting angles in this case is more convenient in radians.

But the value $r$ is not constant! At the beginning of winding the tape $r$ is small - about 5 mm, and at the end - about 25 mm, while the amount of movement varies from 1 to 5 mm. This means that for some sections of the tape there are more literals than for other sections of the same length.

Let's hold the long-running tape up to the light - it is worn more uniformly. Why? But because the reels rotate first in one direction and then in the other, and that part of the tape that was at its beginning then ends up at its end.
