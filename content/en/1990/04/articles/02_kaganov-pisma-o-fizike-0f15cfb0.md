The editors receive a lot of letters with a variety of questions. We try to be dear to each of our readers. But there are some, one might say, global questions that are difficult to answer in an individual letter: in what directions is modern physics developing? What do scientists do at physics institutes? how to become a physicist?...
In response to such letters, we will begin a new publication cycle, which can be called “Letters on Physics”. The future will tell how many of these letters there will be. Physics is incredibly diverse, and there are almost no scientists who are developing equally well in all its areas. Or perhaps they don’t exist at all. A physicist who attends a conference that is not his own, as a rule, does not get much. Therefore, letters to our readers will be written by different authors. And we will try to attract real professionals so that you receive answers, as they say, first-hand.
The author of the first letter is a famous theoretical physicist, a major specialist in the field of solid state physics, Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Professor Moisei Isakovich Kazanov. We give him the floor.

Dear colleague!
I think I can address the reader this way. The magazine «Kvant» is usually read by those who have decided to become a physicist or mathematician. I am addressing those who see themselves as physicists in the future.
I don’t know if anyone is analyzing the choice of specialization. Of course, every mature person remembers how his life turned out and why he became a physicist, or an engineer, or a worker... I remember too. There were no representatives of exact sciences in our family. But there were a lot of books. And I started reading popular science literature on physics early. Now it’s clear to me: I understood little (especially when I first became acquainted with the then-new quantum physics). But there was a mistake in some amazing adventure, in which Rutherford, Bohr, Einstein, Heisenberg, Schrödinger took part... - the experience gave a special place to the “superstars”; the second-row actors no longer left a trace. And if in an adventure film one admires the ability to jump on a horse from a standing position or, instantly pulling out a pistol, accurately hit an enemy, then in scientific adventures I was amazed by the amazing power of the mind, the ability to go beyond old theories, to build a new system presented, adequate to new experimental data. To think, the books I read were well written, because I understood (and perhaps today it seems to me that I understood then): replacing a Fundamental theory, which perfectly describes a huge body of facts, with a new theory - only because the theory cannot cope with something - is a painfully difficult matter. After all, ostatrics inherited all previously known facts, and they still require explanation...

These are the problems that worried me when I made the decision to become a physicist.

What physics is, what physicists actually do - nothing was said about this in the books I read. Modern physics began (in the 17th century) with Newton's formulation of the fundamental equations of mechanics. In the 19th century, Maxwell formulated the equations of electromagnetism. Tracing the path of the basic equations, it is necessary to name A. Einstein, N. Bohr, E. Schrödinger, W. Heisenberg and P. A. M. Dirac. Einstein formed the equations of mechanics that made it possible to study movements at speeds close to the speed of light, and he built a theory of gravity. Bohr, Schrödinger, Heisenberg and Dirac created quantum (or wave) mechanics.*)

The discovery and formation of new equations does not exhaust the activity of a theoretical physicist. Moreover, the overwhelming majority of theoretical physicists do not pretend to change the existing basic equations (read: basic concepts), but are content with solving the problem based on these equations. The verb “to be content” does not carry any destructive meaning.

Problems in theoretical physics arise in two ways.

First way. An experiment discovers something that is called a new phenomenon or property. The choice of word (phenomenon, property) depends on the assessment of the importance of the discovered phenomenon, which independently gives a complete interpretation of what he discovered. But more often, even knowing in general terms what caused his discovery of the phenomenon, he cannot, using known equations (representations), calculate the necessary explanations

It is possible that even with a detailed and detailed study of the discovered phenomenon, its nature does not become clearer. Finding out the nature of a phenomenon, that is, finding out which of the basic equations physicists should use to explain it, is one of the most important problems solved by theoretical physicists. It happens that many years pass from the emergence of a problem to its solution, and all this time the experiment adds new information about an open phenomenon, and the theory, as it develops, secretly prepares itself for solving the problem. Superconductivity was discovered in 1911 and explained in 1956. It took forty-five years to clarify the nature of this amazing phenomenon. Moreover, the explanation did not affect the basic equations of physics. It was found within the limits of existing ideas.

the problem belongs to the class of problems studied by mathematicians. But before the theoretical physicist, having formulated, as it seems to him, a strictly and completely physical problem, he discovers that mathematicians have not solved such problems at all, or they (the mathematician) know only the fundamental solvability of such problems, and not the method of obtaining a solution. Then it is up to the theoretical physicist to create the method... With such an abstract presentation (as in this letter), everything looks too “sorted out.” In reality, most often the situation is intermediate: in mathematics, it seems, there is a necessary method, but it is slightly inappropriate, it needs to be improved a little. And small improvements in equipment are complex work that requires a lot of mental effort.

More often than not, future theoretical physicists do their work under guidance. An experienced theoretical physicist always has in mind either specific problems that, as it seems to him, should be solved, or a field of physics, having become familiar with which, a young theoretical physicist can find a feasible task for himself. In this phrase, it is important to note the words “a feasible task.” A good teacher always imagines how difficult a task can be given to a student... I remember well that state of mind (at the end of my studies at the University), when all the problems seemed to be divided into two classes: solved and unsolvable. And I am still grateful to my teachers, with the help of whom I realized that from unsolved problems a subclass of solvable problems can be distinguished.

Of course, the work of a theoretical physicist is to solve a problem, that is, to obtain an answer, a result. But joy comes not only from the result—the result of the work. The very process of solving, the assumption arose from solving difficulties, bypassing them, getting to know new methods, mastering them - all this brings joy...

I would like to provide a future theoretical physicist. One of my friends, a very experienced and talented theoretical physicist, said that the main quality that a theoretical physicist should have is optimism, the belief that he will be able to get the answer. In the process of work, this faith is extremely concretized. One must hope: everything that is needed will be mutually destroyed when similar terms are added, the paratic singularity will be reduced, etc., etc. But (and this is a warning!) one cannot take wishful thinking: one cannot discard the terms in advance, because they must cancel out each other, reduce the paratic singularity, and generally allow oneself to act differently than on a general test or when solving a problem from an assignment. It is necessary to cultivate extreme criticism in yourself from the first independent work. The author is the most severe critic of his work. We must always remember: when receiving a new result, you need to gain confidence in its correctness. The absence of a ready-made answer (as in the assignment) forces the creation of special methods for checking the result obtained.
