Technical means

It is advisable to build the Mars Expeditionary Complex (MEC) according to a modular principle, according to which the entire complex is divided into separate functional blocks, independently and sequentially delivered from Earth to low-Earth orbit. These include a habitable module, a scientific module, an energy module, a lander, etc.

When creating the IEC and implementing the expedition, four fundamental problems must be solved, namely:

— launching cargo of large mass and dimensions from the Earth into low-Earth orbit and assembling them in orbit;

— interorbital transportation of an interplanetary spacecraft with astronauts on board from Earth to Mars and back;

— ensuring the safety of the crew at all stages of the flight of the expeditionary complex;

— creation of artificial gravity on board an interplanetary spacecraft or the development of special measures to prevent the undesirable consequences of a long stay in weightlessness.

Flight from the orbit of an artificial Earth satellite (AES) to the orbit of an artificial satellite of Mars (ISM) can be performed using special acceleration and deceleration rocket units, including propulsion systems (PS) of various types. Chemical and nuclear rocket engines produce large thrusts, while the thrust of electric engines is negligible. But the speed of the jet source for chemical engines is 3-4 km/s, for nuclear engines - 10 km/s, and for electric engines - 40-50 km/s, which makes them much more efficient.

It should be noted that in order to save fuel mass, the transfer of the expeditionary complex from the international trajectory to the orbit of an artificial satellite of Mars and the Earth can be carried out by braking in the atmosphere of the planets. In this case, the structure must be protected by special heat treatment in order to ensure the tightness of the structure.

Artificial heaviness in a liquid MEC module can be created by rotating all individual compartments. An increment with an angular velocity of three revolutions per minute with a radius of 60 m is provided by artificial gravitation of 0.6g₀ (g₀ is the acceleration of free fall on the Earth’s surface).

The expedition complex should include a landing vehicle, including scientific equipment for research on the surface of Mars, a return package and a capsule for the crew. The descent of the vehicle from the ISM orbit is carried out by braking in the atmosphere and a rocket-dynamic maneuver at the final section of the descent trajectory in order to select a landing site and ensure a low speed of contact with the group. The lander serves as housing for the crew members during their entire stay on the planet's surface. After completing the scientific program, the crew enters the capsule and, using a return rocket, is delivered to an international spacecraft rotating in orbit around Mars.

In order to design a Mars expeditionary complex, certain assumptions can be made that allow us to classify the variety of possible methods of organizing an expedition and flight schemes.
We will assume that the formation of the IEC is carried out in low Earth orbit by assembling payload quanta previously launched into space by cargo launch vehicles. Assembly is carried out from on board the station, which is an orbital assembly and installation center. After the completion of the formation of the Mars expeditionary complex in low-Earth orbit and a comprehensive check of the operability of its system, the expedition crew is delivered on board in a Soyuz-type transport ship or in a Buran orbital aircraft. The crew is delivered to the same assembly and installation orbital center after returning from Mars. In this case, the interplanetary spacecraft decelerates in the Earth's atmosphere and is transferred from the trajectory to a circular near-Earth orbit. The option of returning the crew to Earth in a descent vehicle directly from the landing trajectory, as was implemented in the Apollo program, is also being considered.
Let us briefly describe some of the characteristic schemes for carrying out the expedition.

“Classical” flight scheme (minimal energy costs, but a long stay at Mars; block packages with a “large” thrust propulsion system are used).

Interplanetary flight trajectories are equipment tangent to the orbits of Earth and Mars. The flight time between the orbits of Earth and Mars is about 240-280 days. The waiting time for Mars to have a favorable planetary position for a return flight is 330-450 days. The duration of the entire expedition is 810–1000 days.

Total energy costs - 12 km/s*), including: acceleration near the Earth from the satellite orbit - 3.8 km/s, deceleration at Mars with entry into the circular orbit of the satellite - 2.0 km/s, acceleration from the ISM orbit to the Earth - 2.2 km/s, deceleration at Mars with entry into the low circular orbit of the satellite - 4.0 km/s.

Flight scheme with the accompanying object of Venus (acceptable energy costs and short stay time near Mars; block packages with a “large” thrust propulsion system are used).

One of the sections of the interplanetary flight is a transition equipment, the other is a combination of two fragments of transition equipment connecting in the vicinity of the planet Venus.

The flight time from Earth to Mars is 240 days, the return flight time from Mars to Venus is 185 days, from Venus to Earth is 145 days. The duration of the entire expedition is 600 days.

The total energy costs are 16.2 km/s, including: acceleration near the Earth - 4.3 km/s, braking near Mars - 2.3 km/s, acceleration from the ISM orbit to the flight path Mars - Venus - Earth - 4.0 km/s, braking near the Earth - 5.6 km/s.
