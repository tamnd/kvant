XXIV National Olympiad in Physics

A. ZILBERMAN

The final round of the All-Union Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren this year took place in Vologda. Actually, the Olympics were supposed to be held in Dushanbe, but the organizers encountered difficulties, and the proposal of the Vologda residents turned out to be very timely. Holding the All-Olympiad is always associated with numerous efforts for local organization, and the Organizing Committee is very grateful to the representative of Vologda - if not for them, the Olympiad could have been in jeopardy.

Teams from all resource groups came to Vologda, except for Azerbaijan and Georgia, where for various reasons the resource group rounds of the Olympiad were not held (schoolchildren who came from Tbilisi and some other participants performed at the Olympiad in Vologda outside the competition).

The grand opening of the Olympiad took place on April 19, and on April 20 the participants had already solved the problems of the theoretical round. As usual, in the 9th grade (formerly the eighth) four problems were given and the round lasted four hours, and in the 10th and 11th grades there were five problems for five hours. Here are the conditions of these problems (often of them, according to tradition, have already been published in the section “Problem Book «Kvant»”).

An experimental round took place on April 22. Each participant was given two tasks. Let's talk about them briefly.

In the first problem for the 9th grade, a high straight block of wood was given and it was necessary to change two values ​​of the friction coefficient of the block on the table - along the grain (side surface) and across (the end of the block). The table was not allowed to tilt; The equipment included thread, a pushpin, a sharpened pencil and graph paper. The task is quite interesting, in fact there are even two tasks. When the block is lying on the table, you can attach a thread to its end with a button and pull it at an angle to the horizon so that the load moves and its edge does not rise. If the block is standing vertically (measuring friction across the grain), you can only use it with the sharp end of a pencil - so that it moves but does not fall. It is clear that by finding the highest possible stop point, the friction coefficient can be calculated. The dimensions of the bar and angles can be obtained using graph paper. For the selected bar, the values ​​of the friction coefficient along and across the fibers differed by approximately 10-15%; the accuracy of the changes turned out to be quite sufficient to reliably determine this difference. This kind of work can easily be done in a school physics classroom.

In the second task it was necessary to change small resistances. The equipment included a 5 mA milliampere, a 4.5 V battery, a potentiometer with a resistance of 1 kOhm, resistors of 2 Ohms and 3 Ohms - their resistances were considered known, a resistor with an unknown resistance and a piece of wiring. It was necessary to find the resistance of an unknown resistor (it was 1 Ohm) and the resistivity of the wiring materials (the organizers did not spare the participants the optimization of several registers and unwound them into pieces). The dynamics of the wiring could be changed in a general way - by tightly winding several turns around a pencil. The simplest solution to this problem is to use small resistances as shunts to milliampere. By turning on known resistors alternately, as well as in parallel and in series with each other, you can program the scale in ohms and quite accurately change the unknown resistances that fall within the resulting range (this was the case in this work).

In the first problem for grade 10, you need to determine the density of an unknown liquid (a slightly tinted concentrated solution of table salt in water) using a small flask of known volume with a thin long top and graph paper. The lower liquid was poured into one large vessel, normal water into another. However, one way to change it is to make a hydrometer out of a flask by pouring a certain amount of liquid into it and letting it float in a vessel with water. The second task was more difficult. It was necessary to determine the temperature dependence of the reverse current of a low-power germanium diode (D9V diode) in the range of 20–70 °C. Equipment: 4.5 V battery, 100 µA microammeter, capacitor (its capacity is from 3.3 to 4.7 µF), a vessel with hot water, a thermometer, a thread with a weight for making a magnet, graph paper. In the conditions of the problem it was said that the reverse current is small and varies in the range of reverse voltages from 1 to 4.5 V. Also in the conditions there was a warning that water, although not a very good one, is still a conductor. The difficulty was that at low temperatures the reverse current of the diode is only 2-3 μA and it was impossible to change it without a microammeter (the accuracy of which is 4% - a standard school device). A possible option is to contaminate the capacitor with reverse current for a given period of time (this is where the magnet is prepared), and then observe the drop of the microammeter needle when a charged capacitor is connected to it. At other temperatures, it was possible to change the charging time to obtain the same needle throw, without the need to check the proportionality of the throw and the flowing charge. However, it is possible to establish the dependence of the needle drop on the capacitor charge when the charging time changes. To simplify the calculations, it is important to indicate that the reverse current is constant at a given temperature. At a temperature of 50-70 °C, the current can easily be changed in an unrepresented way. The parts of the graph obtained in two different ways can be “counted” taking into account the fact that they smoothly transition into one another. It must be said that few students have completed this task completely. Most placed what they could, simply with a microammeter and kept the capacitor, thread and weight intact (the jury did not award points for saving materials and devices!).

In the first task for 11th grade, participants were asked to change the temperature of their palms using a large vessel with water at room temperature (this temperature was known), a small flask with a long thin pot (the volume of the flask up to the mark on the pot was specified) and graph paper. One of the possible ways: lower the flask, heated in your palms, upside down into water, and then let it cool and change the amount of water that goes into the pot. These changes required a great deal of accuracy and literacy. Note that with such simple equipment it is possible to change small temperature differences quite accurately.

The second task was more difficult - it was necessary to change the capacitance of the capacitor using the following equipment: an alternating voltage source, the frequency of which was not known to the sections, a millimeter of alternating current at 5 mA (detector system), a capacitor of known capacitance, a rod made of materials with high magnetic permeability (ferrite antenna for a radio receiver), a thin wire in insulation for winding the coil. the alternating voltage source had a fairly large internal resistance (which the sections were aware of); The millimeter resistance was also quite high (and for a detector system device, this resistance strongly depends on the current flowing through it - many students did not know about this). Because of this, it was impossible to take advantage of direct changes in currents in the circuit with capacitors. The work was checked in a general way: theoretical work was “encrypted”, while the inspectors did not know whose work they were evaluating; Each work was fully checked at least twice, by different jury members - for greater objectivity. In addition, the participant is told in detail about the criteria for evaluating the tasks (specifically, after the round), and they can, if necessary, appeal to the jury if they considered the assessments they received to be unfair. It was also possible to appeal based on the results of the test of the experimental round.

The Olympians were awarded diplomas and various specialists. A special prize from Academician I.K. Kikon - a microcomputer MK-85 - was awarded for the best experimental work to a ninth-grader from

School 145 in Kyiv Nikolay Ivchenko. I would like to specially note an extremely pleasant event: for the first time in many years, a first-degree diploma was awarded to a representative of the fair sex - Anya Lempert from the village of Chernogolovka near Moscow, who not only solved theoretical problems well, but also performed excellently in the experimental type.

The hosts of the Olympics tried to properly entertain the participants. The children attended department evenings at city schools, took part in an author's tour of historical places in the region, visited the theatre, watched a performance concert at the opening, and also had as much fun as they could. She met with representatives of the editorial board of the journal «Kvant», at which numerous strict requirements were presented to the journal (fortunately, they turned out to be mutually exclusive, so there is no need to fear for «Kvant»). The students who came to the Olympiad, themselves former participants and winners of various Olympiads, actively interacted with the guys, giving them a lot of useful advice and recommendations. At the same time, each participant could see with his own eyes what wonderful results can be achieved (of course, if one behaves well).
