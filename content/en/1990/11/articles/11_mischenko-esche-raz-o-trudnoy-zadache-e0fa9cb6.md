Again
about a difficult task

In the eighth issue of «Kvant» for 1989, V. Bronshten’s article “The Difficult Problem” was published, telling about a problem that has a three-century history: where will a projectile fired from a vertical upward ordium fall? In the article, the author gives his approximation of the solution for the case when the initial velocity of the projectile is equal to the first escape velocity. However, it is of interest to find a solution for an arbitrary initial velocity of the projectile. Let's try to do this.

Unfortunately, a systematic solution to the problem requires knowledge of laws that go beyond the scope of the school curriculum (they can be found, for example, in the book “Mechanics” by L. D. Landdu and E. M. Lifshitz). Therefore, here we present only the main ideas of the solution and the final result.

1) Relative to the center of the Earth, the projectile, after being fired, moves along an ellipse, all parameters of which are determined by the following conditions: the initial velocity of the projectile v, the linear velocity of rotation of the Earth u, its radius R and the free fall strengthening g. The flight path is shown at figure.

From the equation of the ellipse, you can find the point A at which the projectile touches the earth's surface and the corresponding displacement OA=Δs.

2) The earth does not remain motionless, and during the flight of the projectile ordine will move from point O to point B. Having determined the “swept” (shaded at figure) area, using Kepler’s second law we will find this time, point B and the distance OB “traversed” by ordinem.

3) The required displacement of the projectile relative to the Earth is

Δ=OB-OA.

As a result of all calculations, after significant improvements (the author expresses recognition)

thanks to V. Bronshten for the proposed variant of improving the final formulas) we obtain

Δ≈ πguR²/(2Rg-(v²+u²))³/2.

As an example, let's find Δ for the case v=8000 m/s (i.e. for the first escape velocity):

Δ≈1226 km (in Bronshten’s article ≈1210 km).

Note that the solution we obtained is valid not only for vertical shots, but also for shots at any angle α to the horizon. In this case, you only need to replace v with v sin α, and u with v cos α+u.

E. Mishchenko
