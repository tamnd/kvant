Let us follow the question: what numerical estimates of the size of the Universe could the ancient Greek posture have obtained, based on our modern physical concepts and on their statements about time scales?
Like him, the scope of research is divided into two stages: the first stage - from heaven to Earth, lasting $t_1=9$ days, and the second stage - to Tartarus, also lasting $t_2=9$ days. (Of course, by the word “days” we will mean “day” - it’s unlikely that an anvil can fall during the day and not move along the noses.) At the same time, we will have to remember the most important thing - the forces acting on the anvil. In space, the force of universal gravity will prevail, and when immersed in the Earth’s atmosphere, the force of air resistance must also be taken into account. Further, since no one will be asked where Tartarus is located, we will think that it is in the very center of the Earth (there is nowhere further) and, obviously, access to the anvil must be somehow ensured - for example, through a special mine dug, in order to save public funds, strictly radially. So, the first stage is

From heaven to earth

As is known, the gravitational force acting on a body of mass $m_H$ outside the Earth at a distance $r$ from its center is equal to

$$F = -G \frac{M_\oplus m_H}{r^2} = -m_H g_\oplus \frac{R^2_\oplus}{r^2},$$

where $R_\oplus$ is the radius of the Earth, $g_\oplus = \frac{GM_\oplus}{R^2_\oplus}$ is the acceleration of gravity on its surface. Then the work that we must do to move the body over a small distance $dr > 0$ is equal to

$$dA = \frac{GM_\oplus m_H}{r^2} dr.$$

This means that when moving from the surface of the Earth ($r = R_\oplus$) to the boundaries of “Hesiod’s Universe” ($r = R_\Gamma$) we would have to do work

$$A = \int_{r=R_\oplus}^{R_\Gamma} \frac{GM_\oplus m_H}{r^2} dr = GM_\oplus m_H \left( \frac{1}{R_\oplus} - \frac{1}{R_\Gamma} \right).$$

If now this body is given the opportunity to freely fall from a distance $r = R_\Gamma$ to the surface of the Earth, then all the energy we expended will turn into the kinetic energy of the body. Let's write it like this:

$$\frac{m_H v^2_\oplus}{2} - 0 = GM_\oplus m_H \left( \frac{1}{R_\oplus} - \frac{1}{R_\Gamma} \right).$$

($v_\oplus$ is the speed of the body at the surface of the Earth, and the zero on the left indicates that the body has no initial speed.)

From similar reasoning we obtain that at any distance $r < R_\Gamma$ the law of conservation of energy can be written in the form

$$\frac{v^2(r)}{2} = GM_\oplus \left( \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R_\Gamma} \right) = g_\oplus R^2_\oplus \left( \frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R_\Gamma} \right)$$

(we reduced by mass $m_H$). All this can (although not necessarily) be written in the form

$$\frac{v^2(r)}{2} + \varphi(r) = 0 + \varphi(R_\Gamma),$$

where the function $\varphi(r) = -g_\oplus R^2_\oplus \frac{1}{r}$, called potential, is introduced, and it is taken into account that the initial speed $v_\Gamma = 0$. In such

The law of conservation of the sum of kinetic and potential energy (per unit mass) is already completely clear.

You can also depict on figure (Fig. 1) the dependences of the gravitational acceleration $g(r) = g_{\oplus} R^2_{\oplus}/r^2$ and the potential $\varphi(r)$ at $r > R_{\oplus}$; the same figure also shows their changes inside the Earth at $r < R_{\oplus}$ - all these are quite familiar pictures, which were discussed many times in «Kvant» (see, for example, No. 3-90, p. 49).

So, at an arbitrary point outside the Earth, an anvil freely falling from a “height” of radius $G_{\text{есиода}} R_{\Gamma}$ will have a speed

$$v(r) = v_{\text{II}} \sqrt{\frac{1}{r/R_{\oplus}} - \frac{1}{R_{\Gamma}/R_{\oplus}}} = -\frac{dr}{dt},$$

where $v_{\text{II}} = \sqrt{2g_{\oplus} R_{\oplus}} = \sqrt{-2\varphi_{\oplus}}$ is the second escape velocity*. To determine the required distance $R_{\Gamma}$ from here, you need to be able to take the integral

$$\frac{1}{v_{\text{II}}} \int_{r=R_{\Gamma}}^{R_{\oplus}} \frac{dr}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{r/R_{\oplus}} - \frac{1}{R_{\Gamma}/R_{\oplus}}}} = -t_1.$$ (1)

But you can, without taking this integral (if it’s boring), make some rough estimates. Well, for example, it is known that the Moon revolves around the Earth in approximately 28 days, while simultaneously falling on the Earth. (Remember: Newot saw here an analogy with an apple falling.) Consequently, from point $P$ to point $Q$ (Fig. 2) it will arrive in 28/4 = 7 days. Perhaps it is not without reason that this value is close to Hesiod’s estimate of “nine days”? If there is something to suspect in this, then the size of “Hesiod’s Universe” should be of the order of the radius of the Moon’s orbit ($\approx 380$ thousand km $\approx 60$ $R_{\oplus}$).

Further, our integral (1) can be written in dimensionless form, expressing the linear dimensions in units of the desired radius $R_{\Gamma}$:

$$\left(\frac{R_{\Gamma}}{R_{\oplus}}\right)^{3/2} \int_{r=R_{\oplus}}^{R_{\Gamma}} \frac{\sqrt{r/R_{\Gamma}}}{\sqrt{1-r/R_{\Gamma}}} d\left(\frac{r}{R_{\Gamma}}\right) = \frac{v_{\text{II}} t_1}{R_{\oplus}}.$$ (2)

Then, denoting the integral in terms of some (dimensionless!) quantity $C$, we obtain

$$\left(\frac{R_{\Gamma}}{R_{\oplus}}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{v_{\text{II}} t_1}{CR_{\oplus}}.$$ (3)

And since $v_{\text{II}}/CR_{\oplus}$ is also a constant, this correspondence, written in the form $R_{\Gamma}^3 \sim t_1^2$, is already very reminiscent of Kepler’s third law on the proportionality of the cubes of large stripes of ellipses of planets to the squares of their times of revolution around the Sun. It feels like we, in any case, are circling around the same ideas. Substituting into (3) $v_{\text{II}} = 11,2 \cdot 10^3$ m/s, $t_1 = 9 \cdot 24 \cdot 3600$ s $7,8 \cdot 10^5$ s, $R_{\oplus} = 6,4 \cdot 10^6$ m, we obtain

$$\frac{R_{\Gamma}}{R_{\oplus}} = \frac{123}{C^{2/3}}.$$

If we assume that our dimensionless integral in (2), which we denoted by $C$, is of order one (this gentleman’s agreement is always accepted in the theory of dimensions and similarity), then we can say that the desired radius is two orders of magnitude larger than the radius of the Earth, $R_{\Gamma} = 10^2 R_{\oplus}$, and this is the same order as in the story with the Moon.

Those who want to further clarify the dimensions of Hesiod’s Universe will have to go through the process of calculating the integral in equality (2). We follow the change of variable $r/R_{\Gamma} = \sin^2 \theta$, then

$$\sqrt{1 - \frac{r}{R_{\Gamma}}} = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 \theta} = \cos \theta,$$

$$\frac{dr}{R_{\Gamma}} = 2 \sin \theta \cos \theta d\theta,$$

From the surface of the Earth to Tartarus

Everything is more complicated here. How, according to Hesiod, did the anvil fall further? Did he know about the existence of arose as an inhibitory environment? Let's try to answer this question with the help of Hesiod himself.

Let's consider two cases:

1) There is no air in the shaft that serves as the supposed entrance to Tartarus, and this Tartarus, according to our assumption, is located no further from the surface of the Earth than its center.

So, let the body then fly at the moment of time $t = 0$ past the surface of the Earth into a shaft that extends to its very center. Let's write the equation of motion of a body under the influence of gravity only. Inside a homogeneous planet this force is proportional to the distance from the center, $F = -m_{\text{н}} g_{\oplus} r / R_{\oplus}$, so that

$$m_{\text{н}} r'' = -m_{\text{н}} g_{\oplus} r / R_{\oplus},$$

or

$$r'' + \frac{g_{\oplus}}{R_{\oplus}} r = 0 \quad (0 < r < R_{\oplus}).$$

This is the equation of harmonic oscillations with frequency $\omega = \sqrt{g_{\oplus}/R_{\oplus}}$, the solution of which is

$$r(t) = A \cos \omega t + B \sin \omega t$$

we specify, we learn the initial conditions: for $t = 0$ we have

$$r = R_{\oplus}, v(R_{\oplus}) = \frac{dr}{dt} \Bigg|_{t=C} = -v_{\Pi}.$$

We get

$$r(t) = R_{\oplus} \cos \omega t - \frac{v_{\Pi}}{\omega} \sin \omega t.$$

In the center of the Earth, where $r = 0$, the body will appear after time $t = t_2$ (the second period of time of falling forged).

From here we get

$$0 = R_{\oplus} \cos \omega t_2 - \frac{v_{\Pi}}{\omega} \sin \omega t_2,$$

$$tg \omega t_2 = \frac{R_{\oplus}}{v_{\Pi}} \omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},$$

$$t_2 = \frac{1}{\omega} \arctg \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{R_{\oplus}}{g_{\oplus}}} \arctg \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

Let's remember that

$$g_{\oplus} = G \frac{M_{\oplus}}{R_{\oplus}^2} = G \frac{4}{3} \pi R_{\oplus}^3 \langle \rho \rangle = \frac{4}{3} \pi GR_{\oplus} \langle \rho \rangle,$$

where $\langle \rho \rangle$ is the average density of the Earth’s matter, for $t_2$ we obtain

$$t_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle \rho \rangle}} \sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi G}} \arctg \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

Consequently, after this assessment, Gesid would have to reject the assumption of the absence of an inhibitory environment. Therefore, consider the second assumption:

2) There is still air.

Then in the equation of motion (4), instead of zero on the right, you need to write down the force of air resistance (divided by the mass of the anvil) - this force is proportional to the air density, the square of the body's speed and the square of its size (or cross-sectional area $S_\perp$), and also the Archimedes force, which increases, presumably, due to the inevitable increase in air density $Q$ with depth. We get

$$\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{g_\oplus}{R_\oplus} r = \frac{\rho(r)v^2(r)S_\perp}{m_H} C_c + \frac{\rho(r)}{\rho^0} \frac{g_\oplus}{R_\oplus} r. \tag{5}$$

Here $\rho^0$ is the air density of the anvil ($m_H = \rho^0 V_H$, $V_H$ is its volume) and a dimensionless resistance coefficient $C_c$ is also introduced, which, although it depends on many parameters, however has a value of the order of unity. But even if it is considered constant, an important question remains: how does the air density $\rho$ depend on the depth of the shaft $R_\oplus - r$ (or on the distance from the center $r$)?

It is known that the air density depends on the height $h = r - R_\oplus$ above the Earth and can be described by Boltzmann’s barometric formula

$$\rho(h) = \rho_\oplus e^{-mgh/(kT)},$$

where $T$ is the temperature of the atmosphere (assumed constant), $m$ is the mass of molecules, $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant, $\rho_\oplus \sim 1 \text{ кг/м}^3$ is the value of $\rho$ at the Earth’s surface (“at sea level”; see Fig. 3). But what does the exponent sign mean? - this is the ratio of the values ​​of two types of energy: potential (with a zero mark at sea level) $mgh$ and the energy of thermal motion of molecules $kT$. Let's assume that we make a shaft with walls with the same temperature $T$ ($T \sim 300$ K) and that at some depth the gravitational acceleration can be considered approximately equal to $g_\oplus$ (actually it kills with depth (see Fig. 1), but we are not going to flatter too deeply just yet). Under these assumptions, formula (5) is correct. We obtain the depth value $h_* < 0$, at which the air will roll down to such a density $\rho_*$ that its molecules collide. Whether it can be called liquid is a question of the thermodynamics of the phase state; To be cautious (and not in vain), we will use the words “liquid” air in the signs. Let us assume that this density $\rho_*$ is of the order of the density of water ($\sim 10^3 \text{ кг/м}^3$). Then from (5) we obtain $\rho_* = \rho(h_*) = \rho_\oplus e^{-mg\phi/h/(kT)} \sim 10^3 \text{ кг/м}^3$, $h_* = \frac{kT}{mg_\oplus} \ln \frac{\rho_\oplus}{\rho_*} = \frac{RT}{M_g_\oplus} \ln \frac{\rho_\oplus}{\rho_*} = \frac{8,31 \cdot 300}{29 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 10} \ln 10^{-3} \text{ м} \approx -60 \cdot 10^3 \text{ м}$.

(It is obvious that at this depth, which is about a percent of the Earth’s radius, the gravitational acceleration will change little.)

It is clear that the anvil will pass through an atmosphere with a characteristic thickness of about 10 km in a time of about a second. For a few more tens (or hundreds?) of seconds it will move in the condensing air of the mine, until, finally, almost all of its kinetic energy will be absorbed by the work of resistance forces. This demonstrative disregard for the length of time for the anvil to “get used to” new conditions of motion (or, as they say in the scientific space, to the time of relaxation) simply indicates our hope that this period of time is small compared to $t_1$ and $t_2$ and our reluctance to waste our energy and government paper on proving this (which, however, is not very difficult).

Further movement in “liquid” air with density $\rho_*$ will already take place under conditions of equilibrium of gravity, the Archimedes force and the air resistance force, so from equation (5) we obtain

$$\frac{\rho_* v^2(r) S_\perp C_c}{m_H} = \frac{g_\oplus}{R_\oplus} r \left(1 - \frac{\rho_*}{\rho^0}\right),$$

where

$$v(r) = \sqrt{r} \frac{v_{\text{II}}}{R_\oplus \sqrt{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\rho^0}{\rho_*}} - 1 \sqrt{\frac{m_H}{\rho^0 S_\perp C_c}} = - \frac{dr}{dt},$$

and therefore

$$\int_0^t \frac{v_{\text{II}}}{R_\oplus \sqrt{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\rho^0}{\rho_*}} - 1 \sqrt{\frac{m_H}{\rho^0 S_\perp C_c}} dt = - \int_0^t \frac{dr}{\sqrt{r}}.$$

And from here we get the time for the “drowning” of the anvil to the center of the Earth

$$t_2 = \frac{R_\oplus^{3/2} 2\sqrt{2}}{v_{\text{II}} \sqrt{\rho^0 / \rho_*} - 1 \sqrt{V_H / (S_\perp C_c)}}.$$

One can estimate the order of this quantity by taking $ \rho^0 / \rho_* - 1 \sim 10, V_H / S_\perp \sim 1$ m, $C_c \sim 1$; we get $ t_2 \sim 10^6$ s, which is very reminiscent of Hesiod’s “9 days”.

Of course, those who have a computer can solve equation (5) if they wish. What is important for us is that the words of the ancient poet Hesiod allowed us to discuss several physical ideas and show that position and physics are not so far from each other.
