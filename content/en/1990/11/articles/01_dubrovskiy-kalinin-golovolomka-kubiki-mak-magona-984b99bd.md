The “McMason cubes” puzzle, known for several decades, is a set of cups, each of which is painted in six colors (each side has its own color).
The set includes all coloring pages that cannot be combined with each other by repeating the cube; mirror-equal colorings are considered different.
There are many CRs associated with this set of cups, as well as interesting math puzzles.
Before you think about the most popular of them, try to calculate how many cups are in the McMason set?
And now the main task.
Let's lay out one cube from the set. Prove that among the remaining ones there are also cubes

from which you can make a cube $2 \times 2 \times 2$, colored on the outside exactly the same as the first small cube. At the same time, inside a larger cube, the cups should be adjacent to each other with the same color edges. On figure you see a given small cube and (from two angles) a larger cube, composed in its image and similar.
The famous American mathematician J. Conway came up with a system by which you can instantly find 8 cups that meet a given question. Try and you will find something similar.
Our last question: in how many ways can the cube $2 \times 2 \times 2$ be composed using a given coloring?
