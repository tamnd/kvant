To prove Theorem 2, it is enough for us to establish that if the polyhedron $S$ is obtained by placing the polyhedra $S'$ and $S''$ to each other, then the Dehn invariant of the polyhedron $S$ is equal to the Dehn invariant of the polyhedron composed of $S'$ and $S''$ as separate parts. In this case, the polyhedra $S'$ and $S''$ are placed next to each other along one or more faces or parts thereof. (Along with the composition of polyhedra, we would have to consider their cutting, but these operations are mutually inverse, so if one does not change the Dehn invariant, then the other does not change it either.)

Let $L'_1, \dots, L'_N$ be the edges of the polyhedron $S'$, $L'_1', \dots, L'_M$ the edges of the polyhedron $S''$. Each edge $L'_i$ is divided into parts when gluing polyhedra; The parts are the intersections of the edge $L'_i$ with the faces and edges of the polyhedron $S''$ (we do not take single-point intersections into account) and pieces of the edge $L'_i$, the non-collapsing one is figure 7, on which the edge $L'_i$ is cut in this way into 5 parts: $\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2, \Lambda_3, \Lambda_4, \Lambda_5$. In this case, part $\Lambda_2$ coincides with a segment of the face of the polyhedron $S''$ (blue polyhedron), part $\Lambda_4$ with an edge, and part $\Lambda_5$ with an edge segment of the polyhedron $S''$. Thus, we fake every edge of each of the polyhedra $S'$ and $S''$. We transform the Dehn set accordingly: together with $(l'_i, \varphi)$ we write $(\lambda_1, \varphi), (\lambda_2, \varphi), (\lambda_3, \varphi), (\lambda_4, \varphi), (\lambda_5, \varphi)$ (here $l'_i$ is the length of the edge $L'_i$, and $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_5$ is the length of the edges $\Lambda_1, \dots, \Lambda_5$). This set conversion is valid.

Let $\Lambda'_1, \dots, \Lambda'_P$ and $\Lambda'_1', \dots, \Lambda'_Q$ be pieces of the edges of the polyhedra $S'$ and $S''$, obtained by partitioning. Let, further, $\lambda'_1, \dots, \lambda'_P$ and $\lambda'_1', \dots, \lambda'_Q$ be the lengths of these pieces and $\varphi'_1, \dots, \varphi'_P$ and $\varphi'_1', \dots, \varphi'_Q$ the corresponding dihedral angles. The Dehn set of the polyhedron $S'$ is similar to the set $(\lambda'_1, \varphi'_1), \dots, (\lambda'_P, \varphi'_P)$, and the Dehn set of the polyhedron $S''$ is similar to the set $(\lambda'_1', \varphi'_1'), \dots, (\lambda'_Q, \varphi'_Q)$. When constructing polyhedra, the following can happen to each of our edge pieces:

(1) The piece $\Lambda'_i$ or $\Lambda'_j$ does not collide with $S''$ or $S'$, respectively (like $\Lambda_1$ or $\Lambda_3$ on figure 7). Then the corresponding pair enters the set $S$ with the same angle with which it entered the set $S'$ or set $S''$.

(2) Piece $\Lambda'_i$ is combined with piece $\Lambda'_j$ and the sum of angles $\varphi'_i$ and $\varphi'_j$ is not equal to $\pi$ or $2\pi$ ($\Lambda_5$ by figure 7). Then $\lambda'_i = \lambda'_j$ and $S$ have an edge of the same length with dihedral angle $\varphi = \varphi'_i + \varphi'_j$. Thus, the Dehn set of the union of polyhedra $S'$ and $S''$ includes the pairs $(\lambda'_i, \varphi'_i), (\lambda'_j, \varphi'_j)$, and the Dehn set of the polytope $S$ together includes the pair $(\lambda'_i, \varphi)$, which is the same from the point of view of the Dehn invariant.

(3) The same, but $\varphi'_i + \varphi'_j = \pi$ or $2\pi$ (case $\pi$ is represented on figure 7 by segment $\Lambda_4$, case $2\pi$ is shown on figure 8).
