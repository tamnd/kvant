It is understood that in our case, the merging of liquid drops of a layer of viscous friction can also depend only on the thickness of the substance of the drops, their linear dimensions and the speed of the merging process. Therefore, to estimate, in order of magnitude, the merger of frictional friction, it is completely acceptable to use the Stoks formula. Let's just make a small change in it. Namely: instead of $R$ we will substitute the radius of our drops $r_0$, instead of the speed of the ball $v$ - the speed of the merging process, denoting it also by $v$, and by $\eta$ we will understand the viscousness of the substance of the drops. Then for the merger of frictional friction in our case we obtain

$$F = 6\pi \eta r_0 v.$$

Note that the scale of movement of the liquid mass when drops merge is of the same order as the radius of the drops: $\Delta x = r_0$. Therefore, the work of combined friction will be equal to

$$A = F \Delta x = 6\pi \eta r_0^2 v.$$

From the resulting expression it is clear that the faster the drops merge, the more energy is required for this (due to the increase in fusion friction). But our energy supply is limited by $\Delta E_\pi$. This determines
the desired droplet merging time $\tau$ is divided (the so-called Frenkel merging time). Estimating the speed of the process as $v = r_0/\tau$, from the condition $A = \Delta E_\pi$ we obtain

$$6\pi \eta r_0^2 \frac{r_0}{\tau} = 4\pi \sigma (2 - 2^{2/3}) r_0^2,$$

or

$$\tau \sim \frac{r_0 \eta}{\sigma}.$$

Here are some examples. For water drops with $r_0 \sim 1$ cm, $\sigma \sim 0,1$ N/m and $\eta \sim 10^{-3}$ kg/(m·s) the corresponding time is only $\sim 10^{-4}$ s. And for significantly more viscous glycerol with $\sigma \sim 0,01$ N/m and $\eta \sim 1$ kg/(m·s) the fusion time is $\sim 1$ s. Thus, for different liquids, depending on their thickness and surface tension, the time of the process of merging droplets of the same radius can vary within very wide limits. Moreover, due to the strong dependence of temperature on temperature (which cannot be said about the coefficient of surface tension), this time can vary significantly for the same liquid.

A. Varlamov
