Theorem 4. If $\varphi$ coincides with $\pi$ and its cosine is rational, then $\cos \varphi = 0, \pm 1$ or $\pm \frac{1}{2}$ (and no longer $\frac{1}{3}$).

Proof. If the angle $\varphi$ coincides with $\pi$, then in the sequence

$$\varphi, 2\varphi, 4\varphi, 8\varphi, 16\varphi, \dots$$

there are certainly repeating angles (indeed, if $\varphi = \frac{p}{q}\pi$, then all members of this sequence have the form $\frac{r}{q}\pi$ with $r = 0, 1, \dots, 2q - 1$, i.e., for the terms of this infinite sequence there are generally a finite number of possibilities). Consider the sequence

$$\cos \varphi, \cos 2\varphi, \cos 4\varphi, \dots$$

If the number cos $\varphi$ is rational, then all terms of this sequence are rational: $\cos 2\varphi = 2\cos^2 \varphi - 1$, $\cos 4\varphi = 2\cos^2 2\varphi - 1$, ...
