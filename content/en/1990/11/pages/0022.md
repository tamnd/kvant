Fig. 2. A mechanical system that performs forced oscillations with a frequency $f$; $\varepsilon$ - reached the body in a weakened state. We can consider a simple mechanical system that simulates this process: two absolutely rigid bodies with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$, connected by an incredible spring with a stiffness $k$ (Fig. 2). If the first body is acted upon by a force that changes time according to the harmonic law with a frequency $f$, then the system performs forced oscillations. Here the first body plays the role of a tram chassis, the second - the body, and the spring - a shock absorber. Select the values ​​$k$ so that the amplitude of the oscillation speed of the second body $v$ is as small as possible, this is the purpose of depreciation. You can write down the equation of motion of both bodies and, having solved them, obtain the dependence of the value $v$ on the parameters of the system and the frequency of sound. However, if it does not return, I will present the finished results, mostly in quality form.

The proper part of the oscillations of this system is

$$f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{k \left( \frac{1}{m_1} + \frac{1}{m_2} \right)}.$$

If the frequency $f$ of the driving force is equal to $f_0$, resonance occurs - the amplitude of oscillations of the second (and the first) body increases sharply, and the greater it is, the less mechanical energy is lost in the system due to its conversion into heat (for example, due to friction). In the frequency range $f \lesssim \frac{1}{2} f_0$ the system oscillates as one rigid body with mass $m = m_1 + m_2$ and the amplitude of the oscillation speed $v$ practically does not depend on the elasticity of the spring. In the case of $f > f_0$, the value of $v$ also depends on the mass of the system, but decreases very quickly with increasing frequency. Therefore, shock absorbers are selected so that the resonant frequency of the system is at least half the frequency of the driving force...

— The layer affected by the plates (Fig. 3) plays the role of a shock absorber. And we can appreciate its toughness. If the thickness of the layer is $d$, then the volume of the impact gap is $V = Sd$ (S is the area of ​​each plate). When mixing the plates in the case of their oscillatory motion, the thickness of the gap can change by a small amount $\Delta x$. Then, according to the Boyle-Mariotte law, we have:

$$(p + \Delta p)(V + \Delta V) = pV,$$

where $\Delta V = S \cdot \Delta x$ is the change in the volume of impact, $\Delta p$ is the change in its pressure. Considering that $\Delta V \ll V$, $\Delta p \ll p$, and neglecting the product of small quantities, we obtain:

$$\Delta p = -p \cdot \Delta V / V = -p \cdot \Delta x / d.$$

Thus, the maximum force with which the oscillating influence acts is

$$F = -\Delta p \cdot S = -\frac{p S}{d} \Delta x,$$

and, therefore, the rigidity of the impact between the plates $k = pS / d.$*
