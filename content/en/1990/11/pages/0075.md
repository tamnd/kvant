consists of two parts, one of which can be rotated or shifted relative to the other, and the “chips” form several non-intersecting wheels, as if cut in half - one half fits on one part of the “frame,” the other half fits on the other. When parts of the “frame” are shifted, a “rearrangement” of the halves of the wheels will occur, which makes it possible to transfer “chips” from one ring to another.

If you start solving puzzles, as they say, “at random,” then, sequentially putting one element into place, you will soon be faced with the need to destroy what has already been built. This means that we need operations that would move incorrectly placed elements and not disturb the order of correctly placed ones. Try to come up with such operations yourself. And for those who are impatient, we give the rules for solving OLIDUUS.

It is convenient to represent the movable plates as points on two circles (Fig. 5).

The assembly sequence could be, for example, like this. First, “directly” collect 9 chips in the lower ring (this is easy to do). The remaining 9 chips of this ring are put into place using the operation $A$ (see Fig. 5). In this case, it is necessary that the elements $a$ and $b$ be the same color, and in place $c$ put an element of the desired color from the top ring from the place $d$. In this way, the entire lower ring is assembled. After this, the puzzle is turned over and they begin to restore order on the second ring. The elements $a$ and $b$ make this happen. The ring, which has already been assembled, is installed each time operation $A$ is used so that in place $d$ there is the same element as in place $c$.

10. To what temperature must air be heated at atmospheric pressure so that its density becomes equal to the density of the gel under normal conditions? Molar mass of air $M = 29 \cdot 10^{-3}$ kg/mol.

11. Some quantities of a monatomic ideal gas with an initial temperature of $T_0 = 300$ K are heated first isochorically to $T_1 = 500$ K, and then isobarically to $T_2 = 700$ K. Then its isochoric is cooled to the initial volume. Determine the efficiency of thermal motion operating in such a cycle.

12. It is known that with small changes in atmospheric pressure, a change in the boiling point of water is associated with a change in pressure in the following relation: $p = p_0 - p_0 L M (T - T_0) / (RT_0^2)$, where $T_0$ is the boiling point at a pressure $p_0$, $T$ - at a pressure $p$, $L = 2,3 \cdot 10^6$ J/kg is the specific temperature. M is the molar mass of water, $R$ is the universal gas constant. Assuming that the change in air density with altitude is small and taking $T_0$ at sea level equal to 100 °C, determine at what altitude the boiling point of water will decrease by 1 °C.
