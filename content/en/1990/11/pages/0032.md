Let's draw the vector $\vec{U}$ - it will depict the voltage at $C_x$. Now you can draw the current vectors through $L$ and $C_x$ (they are oppositely directed) and the total current $I_0$ - it flows from the source through the capacitor $C$. Then we depict the voltage vector at $C$ and, finally, the vector of the total voltage at the input of the link - the sum of the voltage at $C$ and $C_x$ (of course, taking into account the signs).

Now a little arithmetic:
$$I_L = U / (\omega L), I_C_x = U \omega C_x, I_0 = U / (\omega L) - U \omega C_x, U_0 = I_0 / (\omega C) - U = I_0 / (\omega C_x).$$

The relationship between the voltage and current amplitudes for the entire circuit is the same as for the capacitor $C_x$. From here you can find $C_x$, only it is more convenient to distance the resistance $X = 1 / (\omega C_x)$ associated with it as an unknown quantity. With respect to it we obtain the equation
$$X^2 - X / (\omega C) + L / C = 0,$$

where
$$X = 1 / (2 \omega C) \pm \sqrt{1 / (4 \omega^2 C^2) - L / C} = 1 / (\omega C) (0,5 \pm \sqrt{0,25 - \omega^2 LC}),$$
$$X_1 = 1 / (1,25 \omega C), X_2 = 1 / (9 \omega C),$$
$$C_{x1} = 1,125 C, C_{x2} = 9 C.$$

It can be seen that two answers are possible—two capacitance values ​​that satisfy the conditions of the problem. Of course, this result is not obvious and needs to be verified. You can, for example, construct vector diagrams to obtain specific values ​​and make sure that everything is in order. I did it and was convinced, try it too.

And one more thing: an infinite target made up of resistors has a very specific resistance - there cannot be several answers.

Teorema. The crocodile is longer than wide.

Lemma 1. The crocodile is longer than wide.

Proof of the Lemma
1. Let's look at the crocodile from above. It's long

and green. Now let's look at the gray crocodile. It is long, but not cocked green (its belly is white). This means that the crocodile is longer than the green one. The lemma is proven.

Lemma 2. The crocodile is greener than wide.

Proof of the Lemma
2. Let's look at the crocodile from above. It's green

both in length and width. And it is only wide in width.

The proof with other maturity points is similar. Lemma 2 is proven.

Proof of the Theorem. According to Lemma 1, the crocodile is longer than green, and according to Lemma 2, the crocodile is greener than wide. Therefore, the crocodile is longer than wide. CTD.
