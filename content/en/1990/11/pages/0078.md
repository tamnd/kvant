T 1.1. Let us denote the vertices of the broken line by the letters $M_i$. Each vertex of the broken line, except the extreme ones, determines the angle of separation of this vertex, in which the vector of parallel translation of one of the parts of the circle should be located (this is one of the vertical angles formed by the continuation of the adjacent links of the broken line intersecting at a given vertex). The existence of a spreading vector for a part of a circle is equivalent to the presence of a common spreading angle for all vertices of the broken line.
Let us denote by $\langle \vec{a}, \vec{b} \rangle$ the angle between the vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ (the determined rotation of the vector $\vec{b}$ to the vector $\vec{a}$ counterclockwise). The angle $\langle \vec{r}_i, \vec{s}_i \rangle$ of the vertex $M_i$ can be found by choosing from two angles $\langle \vec{M}_i M_{i-1} \rangle$ and $\langle -\vec{M}_{i-1} \vec{M}_i, -\vec{M}_{i+1} \vec{M}_i \rangle$ the one that does not exceed $\pi$. Then it is necessary to select from the vectors $\vec{r}_i$ the vector $\vec{r}$ that forms the smallest angle with the axis $Ox$; we denote this angle by $\alpha_r$. Similarly, let us denote by $\vec{s}$ that of the vectors $\vec{s}_i$ that forms the smallest angle $\alpha_s$ with the axis $Ox$. If $\alpha_r \geq \alpha_s$, then any vector whose angle $\alpha_r$ with the axis $Ox$ satisfies the conditions $\alpha_s \leq \alpha_r$ is a vector of separation of a part of the circle.
If $\alpha_r < \alpha_s$, then it is impossible to dilute part of the circle. T 1.2. For point c) a complete analytical solution is possible. In this case, three cases need to be considered.
1) $P$ and $Q$ are coprime parity numbers. It can be shown that there is then a series of moves leading to the result (0, 1) (moving the knight to an adjacent square). In this case, one horse is enough.
2) $P$ and $Q$ are relatively prime numbers. Then each knight will move on cells of only one color, but it can be shown that there is a series of moves with the result (1, 1). This means that two knights are enough.
3) $P$ and $Q$ are not coprime. Let $k = \text{НОД}$ ($P, Q$). To control the required $k^2$ or $2k^2$ knights, depending on whether the numbers $P/k$ and $Q/k$ are the same or different parity.
For a bounded (in one or two directions) doxie with $P$ and $Q$ not coprime, the problem can be simplified. For example, for doxy $10 \times 14$ with gcd ($P, Q$) = 3 we get $f(10, 14) = 2f(4, 5) + f(4, 4) + 4f(3, 5) + 2f(3, 4)$.
For coprime $P$ and $Q$ on a bounded doxie of size greater than $(2M + 1) \times (2M + 1)$, where $M = \max(P, Q)$, the answer is the same as the result for an infinite doxie (a more accurate estimate of the doxie size: $(2M + 1) \times (P + Q + 1)$ for $Q = 1$ and odd $P$ and $(2M + 1) \times (P + Q)$ in other cases). On a smaller doxy, the result can be obtained by brute force. For a strip of width $L$ and coprime $P$ and $Q$ the result is the same as for infinite doxie at $L \geq 2M + 1$ (more accurate estimate: $P + Q + 1$ at $Q = 1$ and odd $P$ and $P + Q$ in other cases). For smaller values ​​of $L$ the answer is the same as the answer for the final doxie $L \times (2M + 1)$.
T 2. The denominators of the sum $S(N, a, b)$ grow slowly, this allows them to be calculated without errors in the form of rational fractions (pairs of integers). Using standard functions to calculate the logarithm and the integer part of a number is not advisable here. The best accumulate the denominator by multiplying its current value by the values ​​$a$ at the moment when the parameter $p = [\log_b k]$ changes its value. In turn, the value $p$ is expediently accumulative, increasing it at the moment when the condition $k < b^{p+1}$ is violated. The accumulation of the sum $S(N, a, b)$ is performed sequentially by bringing the fractions to a common denominator value, adding them and then reducing them. When converting a fraction to a decimal, you need to find the period of the decimal. Here it is advisable to use the following well-known fact: in order for a regular non-multiple generalized fraction to be represented as a pure periodic fraction, it is necessary and sufficient that its values ​​are not divisible by the integer either 2 or 5.
