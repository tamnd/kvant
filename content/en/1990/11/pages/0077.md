5. Each drop is acted upon by two forces: the force of gravity, which accelerates the movement of the drop, and the force of air resistance, which slows down its movement. As the speed of the drop increases, the resistance force increases, so at each moment it will balance the force of gravity, and the further movement of the drop will be uniform. As the size of the drop increases, the gravitational force increases in proportion to the volume, i.e., the third step of the radius of the drop, and the drag force increases in proportion to the section, i.e., the second step of the radius. Thus, the force of gravity increases faster than the force of resistance. This means that the constant speed at which a drop falls to the ground increases as the size of the drop increases. In other words, large drops fall quickly.

$$\varphi = \frac{p_\pi}{p_H} \times 100\%.$$

$$p_\pi + p_B = p.$$
From the increase in the state of an ideal gas, we express $p_\pi$ and $p_B$:
$$p_\pi = \frac{m_\pi RT}{M_\pi V}, p_B = \frac{m_BRT}{M_BV}.$$
Considering that
$$m_\pi + m_B = m,$$

$$\varphi = \frac{pVM_B - mRT}{p_HV(M_B - M_\pi)} \times 100\%.$$
