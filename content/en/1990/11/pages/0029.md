F1238. There is a small air bubble in the middle of the long cylindrical tube with a face. If you place the tube vertically, the bubble will move at a constant speed of $v_0=1$ cm/s.
The tube was placed horizontally and accelerated along the long side to a speed of $v=20$ m/s. Where will the bubble stop? Where will it shift if the floating speed is increased to 30 m/s? Where does he end up after the tube is slowed down?

Glycerin is a very important liquid, so we can assume that at any moment the speed of the bubble relative to the tube is proportional to the acceleration of the tube (and bubble!) relative to the ground - the high friction force is proportional to the relative speed. Considering the relationship between acceleration, speed and displacement of a point, we can say that the movement of a bubble relative to the tube is related to its speed relative to the tube in the same way as the speed of the tube relative to the ground is related to the acceleration of the tube relative to the ground.

The movement of the bubble in a vertical position of the tube is the same as if in a horizontal position the tube was moved with a constant acceleration $a=g=10$ m/s² (the velocity of the bubble is directed in the same direction as the acceleration - the bubble is lighter than water). Let's take into account the numbers from the condition: if the tube accelerates 10 m/s² in 1 second, it will acquire a speed of 10 m/s, and the bubble will shift by 1 cm.

So, a tube speed of 10 m/s corresponds to a displacement of 1 cm. This means that at a speed of 20 m/s the bubble will stop at a distance of 2 cm from the initial position. When the speed increases to 30 m/s, it moves another 1 cm, and when the tube stops, the bubble returns to its original position.

F1239. In a vertical telloisulated cylindrical vessel under a massive piston there is 1 mol of an ideal monatomic gas at a temperature of $T_0$. Let's begin to compress the gas, lowering the piston. After work $A$ was done, the piston was released and it stopped in a new equilibrium position. Find the temperature $T_x$ in this state.

The work $A$ performed on the system goes to change the internal energy of the gas $\Delta U$ and the potential energy of the piston $\Delta E_p$:

$$A = \Delta U + \Delta E_p.$$

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2} R(T_x - T_0).$$
