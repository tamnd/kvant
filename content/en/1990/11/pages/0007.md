CANNOT BE GET FROM ANOTHER BY CUTTING, GLIPPING PARTS AND, POSSIBLY, ADDING SOME OTHER PARTS WITH THE SUBSEQUENT CUT OFF OF THE SAME? AT THE FIRST LOOK AT THE PROBLEM, YOU WILL BE AWARE OF THE INCREDIBLE DIVERSITY OF OPPORTUNITIES. HOW TO OVERVIEW EVERYTHING THAT CAN BE TRANSFORMED INTO A CUBE OR TERAZDR BY SUCH OPERATIONS? AN EXPERIENCED MATHEMATICIAN, HOWEVER, KNOWS THAT TO SOLUTION THE PROBLEM, IT IS NECESSARY TO INVENT A WAY TO PUT SOMETHING IN CORRESPONDENCE WITH EVERY POLYHEDON SO THAT THIS SOMETHING DOES NOT CHANGE DURING ALL THE LISTED OPERATIONS ON POLYHEDONS (LIKE VOLUME) AND SO THAT THE CUBE HAS ONE IT, AND THE TERAZDRA HAS ANOTHER ONE (not as a VOLUME). HOW TO DO THIS IS A DIFFERENT QUESTION, BUT, BY DOING THIS, WE WILL BE ABLE TO NOT ENTER THE GEOMETRICAL VARIETY OF POLYHEDARS EQUAL TO THE TERASDRUS AND CUBE. “UNCHANGEABLE” IN PORTLAND IS “INVARIANT”, THEREFORE OUR SOMETHING IS CALLED INVARIANT.

The solution to our problem using invariants is divided into the following stages. Need to:

(a) agree on what our invariant will be - a number, a figure, or something else (in our case, “what else”);
(b) describe a method for comparing an invariant with a polyhedron (in our case this will be quite simple);
(c) make sure that this is really an invariant, i.e., does not change under our transformations (in our case, this is not particularly difficult);
(d) calculate the invariant for the cube and terazdar (this will take us one second);

(e) prove that the receiving invariants are different (this will seem very complicated to us, but a rigorous proof will be required).

So let's get started.

Dehn invariant

Let $S$ be a polyhedron with $N$ edges. Let $l_1, \dots, l_N$ be the lengths of these edges, $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_N$ the values ​​of the corresponding dihedral angles, measured in radians. Let us compare the set of pairs $(l_1, \varphi_1), \dots, (l_N, \varphi_N)$ with our polyhedron. In each pair, the first place (positive, but this is not particularly important for us) is a real number, the second place is a real number between 0 and $2\pi$ (since the polyhedron is not assumed to be convex, angles, in principle, can be larger than l. It is more convenient for us to assume that the second place is occupied by a real number, determined up to a term of the form $2k\pi$, $k \in Z$. This set will be our invariant, only we first apply agreement, which sometimes allows us to consider sets made up of different pairs to be identical.

So, the word `набор` will denote a finite set of pairs in which the first place is a real number (any), the second is a real number, which determines, up to a term of the form $2k\pi$, $k \in Z$. We emphasize that when we say “set,” we do not necessarily mean the set that receives from the POLYHEDON; for example, the set may be empty ($N=0$).
