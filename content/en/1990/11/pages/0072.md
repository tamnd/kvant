Leningrad city region in mathematics

Third type assignment for grades 6-8

6th grade
1. Pet bought a general terrace with a volume of 96 sheets and went through all its pages in order with numbers from 1 to 192. Vasya took out some 25 sheets from this terrace and added up all 50 numbers that were written on them. Prove that the amount of 1990 could not be obtained from him.
2. There are 101 coins. The average of them is 100 identical real coins and one counterfeit one, different in weight. It is necessary to find out whether the counterfeit coin is lighter or heavier than the real one. How to do this using two weighing cups without mushrooms?
3. Can a 55x39 rectangle be cut into 5x11 rectangles?
4. The Wolf and the Hare are playing the following game. A number is written on the board, and the move is to subtract any non-zero digit from the number and write a better number in the place of the old one. They take turns. The one who gets zero wins. Let the number 1234 be written on the board. The Wolf goes first. Who wins in a ruling game?
5. Pet, Kolya and Vasya solved 100 problems, and each solved 60 problems. We call a problem “difficult” if only one of the boys solved it. Let’s call a problem “easy” if all three solved it. Prove that there are exactly 20 more “difficult” problems than “easy” ones.
6. In the village of Martyshkin, every boy knows all the girls he knows. Among each girl's acquaintances there are more boys than girls. Prove that there are no fewer boys living in Martyshkino than girls. (6th and 7th grade)
7th grade
7. John and Marie live in a skyscraper with 10 apartments on each floor. John's floor number is equal to Marie's apartment number, and the sum of their apartment numbers is 239. Which apartment does John live in?
8. 30 chairs stand in a row. From time to time a person comes and sits on one of the empty chairs. At the same time, one of his neighbors (if there are any) gets up and leaves. What is the maximum number of chairs that can be occupied if they are all empty at first?
9. On the computer screen there is the number 123. Every minute the computer adds 102 to the number on the screen. Programmer Fedya can change the number on the screen at any time by rearranging its digits in any way. Can Fedya act in such a way that a three-digit number always remains on the screen?
10. In quadrilateral ABCD: BC=AD, M—midpoint AD, N—midpoint BC. The midpoints of the perpediculars to AB and CD intersect at point P. Prove that P also lies on the midpoint of the perpedicular to the segment MN. (7th and 8th grade)
11. A 2x2 square is cut into rectangles. Prove that some of them can be shaded so that the projection of the shaded figure on one side of the square has a length of at least 1, and on the other, no more than 1.
12. In some cells of a 50x50 square table the numbers +1 and -1 are arranged in such a way that the sum of all the numbers in the table in terms of the abscid value does not exceed 100. Prove that in some squares of 25x25 the sum of the numbers in terms of the abscid value does not exceed 25.
8th grade
13. Dima bought a notebook with 96 sheets in the store and ran through all the numbers from 1 to 192 in order. Seredka took 24 sheets out of this notebook and added up all the 48 numbers written on these sheets. Could he have gotten the number 1990? (Compare with problem 1.)
14. Find all triples of natural numbers (A, B, C) also that
$$A^2 + B - C = 100, A + B^2 - C = 124.$$
15. There are 101 cities in the Far Country; cities are connected by one-way roads such that any two cities are connected by at most one road. It is also known that there are 40 roads leading out of any city and 40 roads leading into any city. Prove that you can get from each city to any other by driving no more than those roads.
16. Among the 103 coins, two coins are counterfeit, a different number from the real ones. It is known that all real coins weigh the same, as well as both counterfeit ones. After three weighings on a double-bar scale without mushrooms, determine which is heavier: a real coin or a counterfeit one?
17. On the island of Logic, every cell is either a “liar”, always telling a lie, or a “knight”, always telling the truth. Each ostrovitin uttered the following two phrases:
a) all my friends know each other;
b) among my acquaintances there are no fewer liars than a knight.
Prove that on the island a knight is no less than a liar.
18. How many pairs of natural numbers (m, n) are there such that m, n ≤ 1000 and
$$\frac{m}{n+1} < \sqrt{2} < \frac{m+1}{n}$$
