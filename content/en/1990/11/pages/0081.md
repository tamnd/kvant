Lewis Carroll's problems
(see «Kvant» No. 10, p. 38)

1. 24 mi. Note. Travelers travel one mile uphill in 1/4 hour, uphill in 1/3 hour, downhill in 1/6 hour. So it takes 1/2 an hour to walk one mile there and back.
2. 19 positions.
3. The first one will meet 12 poseds, the second one - 8.
4. $5\frac{1}{2}, 6\frac{1}{2}, 7, 4\frac{1}{2}$ and $3\frac{1}{2}$ feet. Note. The sum of the results of all 5 weighings is 61 feet, and in this sum everything from the third bag is taken into account three times, and the rest - twice. To get triple the weight of the third bag, subtract twice the sum of the results of the first and fourth weighings from 61 feet.
5. This is a joke task. On the day the letter was sent, the brothers took a walk near the board of the Bank of England.
6. 8 pence, 1 shilling 7 pence. Note. Let $x, y$ and $z$ be the cost of the corresponding glass of lemonade, sandwich and biscuit. Then (1 shilling=12 pence) $x+3y+7z=14$, $x+4y+10z=17$. All that remains is to find $x+y+z$ and $2x+3y+5z$.
7. 10%. Note. This problem can be interpreted as follows: it is required to measure four segments on a segment of length 100, the lengths of which are 70, 75, 80 and 85, so that the segment covered by all four segments has the shortest length.
8. 15 and 18. Note. The sum of the ages of the two youngest sons is always equal to the double age of the eldest son.
9. Let $x$ and $y$ be rational numbers such that $x^2+y^2=2$. Then there exists a rational number $k=\frac{p}{q}$ such that $x=\frac{k^2-2k-1}{k^2+1}$, $y=\frac{1-k^2-2k}{k^2+1}$, and all rational points on the circle $x^2+y^2=2$ are corrected by this parameter. Note. Circle $x^2+y^2=2$ passes through point $A$ (1; 1). Priyama $y=k(x-1)+1$, where $k$ is a rational number, passes through the point $A$ and intersects our circle at the point $\left(\frac{k^2-2k-1}{k^2+1}; \frac{1-k^2-2k}{k^2+1}\right)$.
Conversely, if $M(x_0; y_0)(x_0 \neq 1)$ is an arbitrary rational point on our circle, then the equation of the line $AM$ has the form $y=k(x-1)+1$, where $k$ is some rational number ($k=\frac{y_0-1}{x_0-1}$).

10. This is a triangle formed by the main altitudes of a given triangle.
11. 7 people, 2 shillings. Note. If $m$ is the number of people, and $k$ is the number of shillings of the last (the poorest of them), then either $mk+m-1=4(m-2)$ or $4(mk+m-1)=m-2$. The second equation has no solutions. From the first equation we get the answer.
12. $3(a^2+b^2+c^2)=(a+b+c)^2+(a-b)^2+(a-c)^2+(b-c)^2$.
13. $\alpha+\varepsilon+\gamma-2$. See instructions for problem 7.
14. $(a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=(ac+bd)^2+(ad-bc)^2=(ac-bd)^2+(ad-bc)^2$.
15. It is noon on July 31 when the steppe clock shows 12 hours 2 minutes $29\frac{277}{288}$ s.
Note. By comparing the readings of the pocket watch with the wall, as well as the readings of the pocket watch with the exact time, make sure that the wall clock moves ahead compared to the exact time by 5 minutes 19 seconds over the course of 29 days.
16. Meetings of pawns occur after 2 days 6 and and after 4 days at distances of 23 and 34 miles from the point from which pawn $A$ left.
