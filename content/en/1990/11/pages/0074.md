Four
PUZZLES
with one idea

The puzzles that are shown in our drawings are not similar to each other, however, as we will see at the end of the note, the method of moving the elements that make them up is basically the same. That's why they are solved using similar techniques. The oldest of them is at figure 1.

The author of this forgotten toy, the Englishman Prothero, decided to make a model of a turntable - a mechanism that is used on railways to transfer locomotives and carriages from one track to another. At the same time, he discovered that if there are a lot of cars and not enough space, then rearranging them in the right order is not at all easy. So in 1885 this puzzle was born. It requires you to rearrange the chips in a given order.

The second puzzle (Fig.2) is called OLIDUUS. This unusual word is made up of the initials of an inventive puzzle, workers of the Penza Radio Plant O. Dmitriev, L. Yurin, I. Stulnikov. Their toy contains two movable end rings of multi-colored stripes, with 18 stripes of six colors in each ring. There is also a central one, the strips on which are tightly fixed and serve only as guides when arranging the rest. A hinge is hidden inside the puzzle, allowing its halves to be rotated in a vertical plane.

In the "cut stakes" (Fig. 4), depicted in Hungary, the balls move from one ring to another by shifting the top and bottom of the toy. This toy is easy to make yourself. Chips can be taken from a checkers game, and the frame can be glued together from cardboard. It is no less interesting to solve these puzzles “in your head”, more precisely, with the help of a pencil and paper.
