Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
Y. GUREVICH

A roaring flame in the jet engine of a spaceship, the calm fire of a fire at a rest stop, bluish tongues of gas on a kitchen stove - they are all generated by one process, which is usually called combustion. In everyday life this process is associated with flame. The connection in the consciousness of combustion and flame goes back centuries: after all, it was fire, that is, the flame that gives the body, that Prometheus brought to people.

And only relatively recently it became clear that combustion can be cold. Moreover, from the standpoint of “pure benefit” (disregarding the natural beauty of the flame), cold combustion is the most effective. Devices in which the process of cold or, as it is also called, electrical combustion is carried out are called fuel cells.

In the language of strict science, combustion is a chemical reaction consisting of the transfer of an electron (or several electrons) from one compound to another and accompanied by the release of energy. In this case, the substance that gave up electrons serves as a fuel and during the combustion process it is described. A substance that accepts electrons is called an oxidizing agent.

Thus, the combustion of coal in air is a chemical reaction of carbon oxidation with oxygen; it can be conditionally written in the form

$$\text{C} + \text{O}_2 = \text{CO}_2 + \text{энергия.}$$

It is this reaction that occurs in the furnaces of thermal power plants running on coal and other types of fuel, consisting mainly of carbon. The same reaction of carbon oxidation underlies the combustion of wood in a stove or fire.

The example given is the process of “hot” combustion that has become common to everyone. However, combustion does not always proceed smoothly. Illustrates the features of cold (electrical) combustion in the practically important case of the hydrogen combustion reaction. Hydrogen gas can, as is known, be directly burned in atmospheric oxygen. As a result of this reaction, water is produced and heat is released:

$$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 = 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{энергия.}$$

However, you can do it differently: divide the reaction of combustion of hydrogen in oxygen into two processes, spatially separated from each other. Let us first consider a process involving only hydrogen. Let us assume that a hydrogen molecule is brought together with the metal-electrolyte solution boundary. The hydrogen molecule consists of two atoms, and on the surface of some metals it disintegrates into them. (Decomposition is due to the fact that hydrogen atoms chemically interact with the surface of the metal, as if bonding to it.) The hydrogen atoms located at the metal-solid boundary (they are called adsorbed) are further broken into two more - now unequal - parts: an electron and the positively charged nucleus of a hydrogen atom - a proton. The electron goes deep into the metal, and the proton (or hydrogen) goes into the solution.

Similarly, a gaseous oxygen molecule at the three-phase metal-solute-gas interface also enters into an electrical reaction. At the same time, due to strong chemical interactions with oxygen, electrons are torn away from the metal. As a result, negatively charged OH⁻ particles - hydroxyl ions - are formed from oxygen, water molecules and electrons. They acted

Thus, the energy of the chemical reaction described, i.e., combustion, in the fuel cell is removed and converted into electrical energy. In accordance with the name of “combustible” materials, the particular element that we considered is called oxygen-hydrogen.

Interestingly, the idea of ​​​​creating a fuel cell in itself is not new - last year it turned 150 years old. In 1839, the English scientist W. Grove discovered the flow of current between two platinum electrodes, partially immersed in saline acid, under conditions where one electrode was in contact with oxygen and the other with hydrogen. By the day, Grove's design was the prototype of the oxygen-hydrogen element. However, at that distant time, neither he nor his contemporaries attached due importance to the results of Grove's experiments. Only a few decades later, many scientists and artists tried to purposefully create a fuel cell suitable for practical use. In the seventies, the remarkable Russian electrical engineer and artist P. N. Yablochkov was an enthusiast of the technical solution to this problem. But then the decision was not given. And now, in our time, fully armed with the scientific knowledge and technical fundamentals of the late twentieth century, scientists and engineers are once again exploring the same terrain. Why did it replace the walls of the fortress and report that these walls are impregnable?

First, about the importance of the task.

The basis of today's energy industry is thermal steam turbine power plants running on coal, oil, gas, and gas. The system for converting fuel into electrical energy here is multi-stage: the heat released during scraping heats the water, the water turns into high-pressure steam, the steam drives the turbine, which, in turn, turns the rotor of the electric generator.

With such a complex, cumbersome process, one cannot do without large energy losses. But if in relatively recent times it was believed that fuel reserves on Earth were practically inexhaustible, today eyes have been opened to a lot, and the efficiency of energy conversion is becoming one of the main problems.

Another problem is environmental protection. It’s relatively easy to get rid of waste such as soot, dust, and harmful substances; in any case, there are no fundamental difficulties here. The situation with energy blockage is much more complicated. Already now, human energy “turnover” in certain regions of the globe is comparable to the thermal turnover of natural processes, and energy consumption is inexorably calculated. The question arises: where to “hide” the energy needed without creeping from thermal power plants. After all, at the current production rates, not today, but tomorrow our planet will “become hot.” Let's not paint a picture of what this could lead to. It is clear, however, that energy must not only be economical, but also economical.

And even if conventional thermal power plants give way to nuclear power plants, there is no need to delude yourself - the existing problems will remain. The reason is that external nuclear and even future fusion power plants remain essentially warm power plants. It’s just that the furnace of the archang steam boiler is replaced by a nuclear reactor, but, as in the ancient times, here it is necessary to first receive heat by discharging the energy carrier and only then, through intermediate steps, convert the warm energy into electrical energy.

The source of the difficulties that arise is very serious - here we come across a fundamental prohibition imposed by the inexorable laws of thermodynamics, which limits the maximum efficiency (efficiency) of any heat engine. Mathematically, this limitation is described by the meaning of the formulation obtained by the twenty-thought French physicist and engineer Nicolas Sadi Carnot and published by him in 1824 in the only scientific work of his entire life, “Reflections on the driving force of fire and on machines capable of developing this force.” Using Carnot's formulation, the results are not very positive: the maximum efficiency of the most modern thermal installations using steam heated under high pressure is only slightly more than 60%. Moreover, this is an ideal value, and taking into account the inevitable loss of efficiency does not exceed forty (at best, forty and a half) percent. Some benefits can be expected from the use of magnetohydridydynamic installations, where the heat bed is plasma (a mixture of infused atoms and electrons). But even here the actual efficiency will exceed 55%.

The laws of thermodynamics cannot be overstepped or circumvented, therefore the only way to resolve problems is to abandon the ineffective “vulgar” waste of energy carriers. It turns out that the implementation of cold combustion is the most important step along this principle of a different path. In a fuel cell (electrochemical generator), the released chemical energy is converted directly into electrical energy. It follows that a fuel cell is not a heat engine and the strict restrictions arising from Carnot’s thermodynamic formula do not apply to it. In other words, the efficiency based on the functioning of fuel cells can, in principle, be close to the coveted Sta percent. Let us note in passing that what has been said applies to other chemical current sources, for example, to galvanic elements on the basis of which batteries in radios, microcalculators, electronic watches, etc. work.

The reason for the sharp difference in efficiency for hot and cold combustion processes is given in a completely clear explanation. Combustion, being a chemical reaction of the joining of atoms or molecules, is accompanied by the transfer of electrons from one particle to another. The container act of the combustion reaction is accompanied by the flow of electric current, which is a kind of short circuit current between the reacting particles. When released during each act, the electrical energy dissipates, turning into the energy of the otic combustion of particles, i.e., into heat. The observed total chemical reaction consists of an astronomical huge number of elementary processes.

In other words, conditional - "hot" - combustion can be imagined as random individual short-circuit processes occurring between an innumerable number of molecules or atoms with different electrical potentials. As a result, the chemical energy of the initial reagents (which is actually electrical in nature) is converted into thermal energy. However, thermal energy, being inherently the energy of chaos, is with great difficulty converted into the energy of ordered motion. Individually, randomly moving particles have significant kinetic energy, but they move in different directions and, like the swan, crayfish and shuka included in the last, do not in any way carry out their services in one direction. Every day, this is precisely the reason for the strict limitation of the efficiency of heat engines: when transforming the energy of chaos into useful work, losses of the fundamental are inevitable.

In a fuel cell, all the circumstances: chaotic currents associated with the occurrence of a chemical reaction do not arise at all. Let us recall the scheme of its operation: electrons from the fuel (hydrogen in the example under consideration) directly enter the electrical target. There, these electrons perform useful work, and are connected to the oxyplet (in our example, oxygen) at another electrode. Thus, an electrical target was inserted between the reactants, causing the erratic electrical currents characteristic of “normal” combustion to be directly converted into a regular electrical current. In other words, chaos in the fuel cell can be avoided at the very initial stage of the combustion process (no wonder it is cold!) and due to this, the value of the ideal efficiency factor can be sharply increased. So, if you burn coal in oxygen not in the usual way, but electrochemically, this coefficient, according to calculations, can reach 99.8%! Moreover, since the fuel cell does not have numerous intermediate stages of energy conversion (each with its own “leaks”, collapsing parts, etc.), there is reason to expect that the actual efficiency will not be too far from the ideal, i.e., close to the desired Sta percent.

The opportunities opening up here were clearly recognized already at the end of the last century, when energy, economic and other crises were not yet looming before man. The greatest physical chemist of the time, Nobel laureate Wilhelm Ostwald wrote in 1894: “The way that can solve the most important of all technical issues - the question of obtaining liquid energy - must be found electroxim... If we have an element that produces electricity directly from coal and atmospheric oxygen, this will be a technical revolution, far surpassing in its significance the invention of the steam engine... We are dealing here with cases when science predicts in advance complete success."

Everything in these words, written almost a hundred years ago, is absolutely true. And the stop, it would seem, is small - to cope with issues not of a fundamental, but of a purely technical nature. But this “little” turned out to be only a tough nut to crack, and even now they have not been able to crack it completely.

In the Soviet Union, research into fuel cells began shortly after the end of World War II. Much credit for this goes to Academician A.N. Frumkin, who was the head of the Soviet electrochemical school for many decades. The problem of creating fuel cells was significantly advanced, which was associated with the successful solution of a number of scientific problems (giving electrode materials catalytic properties, developing the theory of porous electrodes (one cubic centimeter of a porous electrode can have a developed surface area of ​​tens of square meters)).

But the real turning point in the fate of fuel cells came with the beginning of the space age, ushered in by the launch of the Soviet satellite. Almost forty percent of its total mass consisted of chemical current sources. Attitudes towards fuel cells have now changed completely. True, the “bad ones” turned out to be mainly oxygen-hydrogen elements, functioning on the principle discovered by Grove. The obstacles that prevented widespread use on Earth—primarily high cost—were now discarded. The undeniable advantages of fuel cells in space turned out to be more important: low weight with high power, compactness, the ability to create a convenient configuration, insensitivity to overloads, etc. Once again, the “old” advantages came to the fore - the absence of harmful emissions, cost-free operation, and “new” ones were also discovered - the absence of interfering radar interference and radiation. So, the first serious application of fuel cells was found not on the Earth itself, but beyond its borders - on spaceships and stations. Now they have even visited the Moon.

After the brilliant space launch, interest in terrestrial applications of fuel cells increased again. At the same time, there are no disadvantages for oxygen-hydrogen elements. For example, electric vehicles powered by electrochemical current generators have a number of important advantages in urban conditions compared to conventional automatic machines with domestic-type divans. First of all, it is smokeless and noiseless, which provides electric vehicles with competitiveness, even despite the higher (today) cost of operation and less autonomy. According to experts, the future here most likely lies with electric vehicles, both fuel cell and battery powered.

Power plants of various capacities based on the electrochemical generation of hydrogen are, naturally, used not only for transport. Thus, in the USA, fuel cells with a capacity of about 40 kW are already operating for small enterprises.

Let us return, however, to the super-task of research in the field of fuel cells, as it was originally formed. The goal is to implement cold combustion of coal or other natural fuels, and in the future, to solve the problems of large-scale energy on this basis. Therefore, pioneering researchers turned to the problem of electrochemical combustion of coal (carbon). And here a serious difficulty immediately awaited them. The fact is that the carbon oxidation reaction occurs at room temperatures with an insignificant, practically zero rate. This is its significant difference from the rapid reaction of hydrogen oxidation, which, ultimately, allows classon-hydrogen elements to pull ahead.

It’s hard to resist being surprised once again by the harmony of the world around us. After all, almost all living things consist of carbon molecules. And if carbon were easily described by oxygen in the air, life would be impossible. On the other hand, the energy problem in living organisms is solved by description (combustion), and at ordinary temperatures. It is remarkable that the obligatory link in the chain of descriptive processes here turns out to be the cold combustion of hydrogen, the source of which is food. In other words, the classon-hydrogen fuel cell in the biological version is the foundation of the energy of living things, and the classon-carbon fuel cell, if it could function under normal conditions, would lead to the death (or rather, to the “non-emergence”) of this living thing.

It is now easy to understand that the implementation of cold combustion of coal encounters relatively unintentional difficulties. We have to overcome the barBER created by nature itself, which protects living matter. Thus, relying on natural carbon fuels requires a transition to high temperatures. In this case, melts or so-called solid electrolytes are used together with regular aqueous solutions of electrolytes.

High-temperature fuel cells are designed for large-scale, high-power stationary installations that can operate continuously for a long time and provide high efficiency in the use of natural fuel energy. Creating installations of this type requires solving problems at the intersection of many fields of knowledge - from mathematics and electrical engineering to materials and electronics. In terms of its scale and complexity, this problem, taken as a whole, is comparable to the problem of creating nuclear energy. A fairly complete solution can only be expected in the next generations of electrochemical power plants. At the same time, the foundation for a future solution is being laid now. The creation of electric power plants using natural gas is already feasible today. Especially in large cities, where such advantages of fuel cells as powerlessness, smokelessness and “waterlessness” are most realized. In one of the most balanced countries, Japan, by the end of this century it is planned to obtain approximately 13% of the required energy using a fuel cell installation.

If we look even further, 20-30 years ahead, the end of the era of petroleum products and natural gas will become noticeable. Apparently, the coal era or a unique coal generator will come. By this time, we should expect not only the appearance, but also the development of electrochemical generators that “burn” coal. There are other approaches and other difficulties. But they are all foreseeable. However, there is no doubt that the process of cold combustion still represents a serious consequence for humans.
