Go to Mars

Doctor of Technical Sciences
L. GORSHKOV

Putting man on Mars. What is this - a dream or reality? In any case, engineers are currently developing a design for the first Martian ship.

Let's look at some features of human flight to a neighboring planet.

The Earth and Mars revolve around the Sun in almost the same plane, and the average distance between their orbits is approximately 80 million km.

What is needed to fly from Earth orbit to Mars orbit?

Let’s imagine that the interplanetary spacecraft is in a near-Earth orbit, for example, in the one in which the Mir station is currently flying, and the speed

The ship's growth rate is about 8 km/s. If, after turning on the engine, the speed of the ship increases by 3.5-5 km/s, it begins to fly towards the orbit of Mars. If the timing of the engine activation is chosen accordingly, the ship will enter Mars orbit at the moment when, moving along its orbit, Mars finds itself in the same area. All that remains is to turn on the engine again and move to the near-Matrix orbit. From this moment on, the ship will rotate around Mars, just as it rotated around the Earth before.

figure 1 shows the possibilities of a flight trajectory from near-Earth orbit to near-Matrix orbit.

The most economical (in terms of fuel consumption) trajectory is the poliptic one (the so-called Hohmann orbit, named after the scientist who proposed such a flight). But it will be useful for quite a long time - about 9 months.

If Mars and Earth maintained their relative positions, transfers along Hohmann orbits to Mars and back would occur. But... Earth and Mars are changing their relative positions. Therefore, if we flew to Mars along the Hohmann orbit, then in order to fly back along such a trajectory, we must wait several years.

figure 2 shows an example of a flight trajectory to Mars and back. Shown here is the trajectory when, during the flight to Mars and back, the Earth will make almost one and a half revolutions around the Sun, i.e. almost a year and a half will pass.

What propulsion systems are capable of transferring the ship to such interplanetary trajectories? Today in rocket technology, liquid-jet engines are widely used. They are installed on all spacecraft. However, one of the main characteristics of the engine is the speed of gas flow from the jet nozzle. The higher the exhaust velocity, the less fuel is required to accelerate the ship. For liquid-reactive installations, the speed does not exceed 4500–4800 m/s.

Calculations show that if the mass of a ship without a rocket launcher is 120-150 tons, then the initial mass of the entire ship with liquid rocket fuel should be 2000 tons.

Today there are no carriers that can launch such cargo into orbit. Therefore, the ship will have to be assembled in space, placing its individual parts into low-Earth orbit. This will require about 20 launches of the Energia rocket launcher. This will be a very expensive version of a Mars expedition.

Currently, electric jet engines have been developed with gas flow speeds of 8-9 km/s. Such speeds are achieved due to the acceleration of the working fluid (as the substance of the jet stream is called) in an electrostatic field. These are the most economical engines; their operation requires almost 20 times less fuel than liquid jet engines. But in this case, a powerful power plant is required to create an electrostatic field.

Such a power plant can be built on various principles. However, one of the options is a mini-nuclear power plant on a ship. Another option is a power plant using solar energy.

A simple method is to directly convert solar rays into electricity using photoconverters, similar to those installed on spacecraft and orbital stations.

Today, designers are leaning towards a version of a Martian ship with electric propulsion engines and a power plant in the form of large solar panels.
The interplanetary spacecraft consists of three parts: a living block, in which the crew works throughout the flight and where the main control equipment is concentrated; a landing vehicle in which the crew or part of it descends and returns from the surface of the planet (this is something like a lifeboat on an ocean ship); propulsion system for interplanetary flights (see Fig. 3).
Let's take a closer look at each of these parts.
The living block consists of several sealed compartments. There are cabins for a crew of four. Why exactly 4 people? The limitation is dictated by free savings, by the fact that an increase in the crew sharply complicates the work on creating the ship.
And it is impossible to have less than four people from the safety message: when landing on Mars, the crew will have to split up - part should go down to the surface of the planet, the other will remain on the ship, and at the same time there should not be less than two people either on the ship or on the surface of Mars (mutual assistance is necessary). So it turns out that the crew of the first interplanetary expedition should consist of four people.
The living block contains systems that provide the crew with water and oxygen; Here are food supplies and waste disposal facilities. figure 4 shows a diagram of providing the crew with water and oxygen. The basic principle is the maximum use of carbon dioxide and water emitted by humans.
The water entering the drainage device and the water vapor released by the crew into the atmosphere of the accommodation unit pass through what is called a water regeneration system. There the water is purified from harmful impurities, enriched with the necessary components -

ly and is ready to drink again. Such systems are already operating at the Mir orbital station.
Oxygen for breathing is obtained by electropyretic decomposition of water - 2H₂O=2H₂+O₂. At the Mir station there is such a system, it is called “Electron”. Hydrogen in this system is a by-product; it will also be used, but in the carbon dioxide processing system - CO₂+4H₂=CH₄+2H₂O. The resulting methane is removed overboard, and the water is sent to the electrozergia system to produce oxygen.
Thus, the water cycle organized at the station provides the crew with oxygen for breathing and water for drinking. But losses are inevitable, which are compensated by special reserves of water on board.
For normal operation of the crew and equipment, the temperature inside the living compartment must be constant, about 20 °C. But the compartment is exposed to various temperature effects (sun rays, heat generated by the crew, operating equipment). These impacts are variable. to the Sun (in the Earth’s orbit the intensity of solar radiation is more than two times greater than in the orbit of Mars); The heat generation inside the compartment depends on which device is turned on at that moment. To ensure normal temperature conditions, a special thermoregulation system and thermal insulation are provided. The shell of the residential block is wrapped in a “fur coat” - the so-called screen-vacuum thermal insulation. This is essentially a multi-layer thermos made of metalized film. And so that the living block does not overheat from the heat generated by the apparatus and the crew, a regulatory heat removal overboard is introduced.

A simplified regulation scheme is shown in figure 5. A radiator is located outside the residential block, and inside there is a heat exchanger through which the air of the compartment is blown. Liquid circulates between the radiator and the heat exchanger. The flow of circulating liquid can be controlled: if you need to increase heat removal, increase the flow of liquid through the heat exchanger; if you need to reduce cooling and raise the temperature in the compartment, reduce the circulation speed. The reflective surface of the radiator is designed in such a way that even when the radiator is illuminated by direct sunlight, it acts as an emitter.

One of the dangers supporting the ship is meteorology. Small meteors - the so-called micrometeories - quite often encounter spaceships, but with the thickness of the shell that these ships have, the encounter is also not scary.

However, events with larger meteors that can damage the thermal shell cannot be ruled out. To prevent the dangerous consequences of such meetings, a special screen is installed on some shells, serving as a kind of umbrella. The meteor hits the screen, the heat released in the process evaporates the meteora substance, and it is no longer the meteor that hits the thermal shell, but a gas jet. Of course, the screen does not fall off the shell when meeting larger meteors, but such a meeting is extremely unlikely. If, however, a breakdown of the shell does occur, the crew will have at their disposal the means of repairing the response. For greater safety, the living block is divided into several compartments connected by hatches. Frames with the apparatus are located along the cylindrical shell. They can be answered to provide access to the thermal shell if repairs are required.

In near-Earth orbits, the crew is protected by the radiation poisons of the Earth, but when traveling to Mars, this protection will not be available. Particularly powerful radiation from the hearts will be maintained during solar flares. On short expeditions, for example to the Moon, there is a risk of being hit by a powerful solar flare. During a long, almost two-year journey, there will definitely be outbreaks. In this case, the residential block is provided with a special room with radiation protection.

In general, it must be said that the residential block is very similar to the orbital station, only larger in size. And this is understandable: after all, the tasks of the residential block are almost the same as those of the orbital station - to provide conditions for long-term operation of the crew and equipment.

The apparatus for landing on the surface of Mars consists of a crew cabin, landing and take-off propulsion systems. There is a special room in which the crew lives in comfortable conditions for several days, which are allocated for work on the surface of the planet. The lander has an aerodynamic shape that provides braking in the atmosphere of Mars, the density of which is 100 times less than the density of the Earth's atmosphere. It also uses a liquid-jet propulsion system. This is what makes it possible to perform a soft “primation”. The life-support systems of the device are built on the same principles as the systems of the residential unit.

The propulsion system of the ship - a solar tug - consists of electrojet engines, tanks with a “working body” and an electron station with two large solar panels measuring $200 \times 200$ m each. A thin film of photoconverter is stretched onto a truss frame, similar to the structure that cosmonauts deploy at the Salyut-7 station (it was called Mayak). The batteries can be rotated relative to the ship's hull so that their plane is always directed toward the Sun, and the engines create thrust in the desired direction.

That's the entire interplanetary spacecraft. How will the flight proceed?

First, parts of the Martian spacecraft are launched into low-Earth orbit. This will require 4-5 launches of the Energia launch vehicle. These parts are assembled in orbit using an automatic assembly system (the system has already ensured the safe docking of more than 50 Progress spacecraft with the Salyut and Mir stations).

everything is in order, at the specified time the electronic digitizers are turned on, and the ship begins its flight. Since the thrust of the electronic digitizers is quite small (its total value is about 300 N), before entering the flight path to Mars, the ship will move in an unfolding spiral for about three months, slowly moving away from the Earth. But then the ship leaves the Earth's sphere of gravity and begins to move towards Mars. The flight to Mars orbit will last eight to nine months. Having reached it, the ship begins to descend in a collapsing spiral until it enters a working orbit around Mars at an altitude of 500-700 km.

Here, two crew members enter the Martian lander, undock from the ship and descend.

Which area will be chosen for planting? The bed of an ancient river or an interesting pile of rocks? It's hard to say now. It is also unknown what discoveries await the first earthlings on the neighboring planet.

After the crews finish their work, the flight takes their places in the Martian apparatus and returns to the ship.

Now the interplanetary expedition is heading back. Again, in an untwisted spiral, the ship leaves the sphere of gravity of Mars and then moves along an interplanetary trajectory towards Earth.

The total flight time (including sections of “spiral” acceleration and deceleration) will be about two years.

After the ship enters low-Earth orbit, the return of the crew will be the same general transport operation as changing the crew at an orbital station. But before returning he will need to undergo a thorough quarantine. The fact is that it is possible that there are some forms of life on Mars that could be dangerous to Earth. Therefore, caution is required here: long-term studies must confirm the absence of such a danger before astronauts are returned to Earth.

Today, designers are developing a project for the first interplanetary spacecraft, and its crew may still be in school. But the time of the first interplanetary expedition is getting closer and closer...
