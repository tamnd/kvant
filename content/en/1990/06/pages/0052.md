about seventy words. Dr. Strauss constantly reminds me of the need to write and speak more simply so that others can understand me.

Last Tuesday, Algern and I were shown at the American Psychological Association convention. We made a big sensation. Dr. Nemur and Dr. Strauss were very proud of us.

I suspect that Dr. Nemur, who is sixty (he is ten years older than Dr. Strauss), considers it necessary to reap the fruits of his labors now. This is undoubtedly the result of pressure from Mrs. Nemur.

Contrary to the impression I had of him earlier, I now realized that Dr. Nemur was by no means a genius. He has more ability, but is hampered by his lack of self-confidence. He wants people to think he is a genius. Therefore, it is important for him to know that his work is being recognized. In my opinion, Dr. Nemur richly further added precisely because someone else could make a similar discovery and deprive him of this part.

But Dr. Strauss can be called a genius, although I feel that his knowledge is too limited. He was trained in a tradition of overly narrow specialization.

I was shocked to learn that of all the ancient languages ​​he could read only Latin, Greek and Hebrew, and that he knew almost no higher mathematics beyond the elementary expressions of calculus. When he admitted this to me, I felt some anger. I took it as if, in order to deceive me, he had been hiding this side of his personality for some time, trying to appear (as I discovered to be the case with many people) as something different from what he really was.

Doctor Nemur clearly feels some kind of impossibility towards me. Inoda, when I try to talk to him, he just looks at me strangely and answers. At first I was even angry when Dr. Strauss explained to me that I was making Dr. Nemur feel inferior. I thought that he was making fun of me, and I react very sharply when people make fun of me.

How could I have known that such a highly respected experimental psychologist as Nemur was unfamiliar with both Hindi and Chinese? After all, this is not lazy, if we take into account the research that is currently being conducted in India and China in this area.

I asked Dr. Strauss how Nemur would be able to refute Rahajamati, who criticized his method and research results, if he could not read his works at all. The strange expression that appeared on Dr. Strauss's face at this time could only be recognized by one of two things. Either he doesn’t want to tell Nemur what they write in India, or - and this gives me great peace of mind - Dr. Strauss doesn’t know this himself.
