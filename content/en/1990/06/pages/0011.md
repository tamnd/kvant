Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
Y. GUREVICH

A roaring flame in the jet engine of a spaceship, the calm fire of a fire at a rest stop, bluish tongues of gas on a kitchen stove - they are all generated by one process, which is usually called combustion. In everyday life this process is associated with flame. The connection in the consciousness of combustion and flame goes back centuries: after all, it was fire, that is, the flame that gives the body, that Prometheus brought to people.

And only relatively recently it became clear that combustion can be cold. Moreover, from the standpoint of “pure benefit” (disregarding the natural beauty of the flame), cold combustion is the most effective. Devices in which the process of cold or, as it is also called, electrical combustion is carried out are called fuel cells.

In the language of strict science, combustion is a chemical reaction consisting of the transfer of an electron (or several electrons) from one compound to another and accompanied by the release of energy. In this case, the substance that gave up electrons serves as a fuel and during the combustion process it is described. A substance that accepts electrons is called an oxidizing agent.

Thus, the combustion of coal in air is a chemical reaction of carbon oxidation with oxygen; it can be conditionally written in the form

$$\text{C} + \text{O}_2 = \text{CO}_2 + \text{энергия.}$$

It is this reaction that occurs in the furnaces of thermal power plants running on coal and other types of fuel, consisting mainly of carbon. The same reaction of carbon oxidation underlies the combustion of wood in a stove or fire.

The example given is the process of “hot” combustion that has become common to everyone. However, combustion does not always proceed smoothly. Illustrates the features of cold (electrical) combustion in the practically important case of the hydrogen combustion reaction. Hydrogen gas can, as is known, be directly burned in atmospheric oxygen. As a result of this reaction, water is produced and heat is released:

$$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 = 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{энергия.}$$

However, you can do it differently: divide the reaction of combustion of hydrogen in oxygen into two processes, spatially separated from each other. Let us first consider a process involving only hydrogen. Let us assume that a hydrogen molecule is brought together with the metal-electrolyte solution boundary. The hydrogen molecule consists of two atoms, and on the surface of some metals it disintegrates into them. (Decomposition is due to the fact that hydrogen atoms chemically interact with the surface of the metal, as if bonding to it.) The hydrogen atoms located at the metal-solid boundary (they are called adsorbed) are further broken into two more - now unequal - parts: an electron and the positively charged nucleus of a hydrogen atom - a proton. The electron goes deep into the metal, and the proton (or hydrogen) goes into the solution.

Similarly, a gaseous oxygen molecule at the three-phase metal-solute-gas interface also enters into an electrical reaction. At the same time, due to strong chemical interactions with oxygen, electrons are torn away from the metal.
