This is a valid sphere

At first, this still raises doubts. Alexander's sphere looks like a hypnastic weight with a sawn handle and some kind of complex figure made of wire of various thicknesses placed in a sawn slot.
Do the two pieces of the handle agree with each other? After all, at each stage of construction we refer some parts of the sphere to each other - won’t they merge in the limit?

No! The previous construction can be carried out so that when the sphere is deformed, the distance between any two of its points does not increase or decrease by more than, say, 100 times. (I say “let’s say” because in Figures 4 and 5 the dimensions are not indicated - finger lengths, slit width, etc.; all this can be made a little larger or a little smaller; you can choose the sizes so that the number indicated above is exactly 100.)

Let's look at our construction step by step. The first stretch of the fingers affects two circles on the round sphere; the remaining parts of the sphere remain motionless at this stage of deformation and at all subsequent stages. The second step affects four small circles (Fig. 8). The parts of the sphere lying outside these four circles are not mobile at all stages of deformation, except for the first. The third stage affects eight smaller circles (see Fig. 8), the fourth - 16 eight smaller circles (at Fig. 8 they are no longer visible), etc.
There are $2^n$ circles in the $n$-th queue; in each circle of the $n$th order there are two circles of the $(n+1)$th order; points of the sphere that do not lie in any circle of the $n$th order are not mobile at all stages of deformation, starting from the $n$th.
