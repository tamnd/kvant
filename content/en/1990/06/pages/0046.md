I am for YOU"
or prisoner's digemma
(according to D. HOFSTADTER*)

With your heads bowed low, you sit in the director's office and wait for him to tell you. “I heard the glass break and thought you broke it. If she opens up to admit her guilt, you will receive a note in your diary for running around the school. If one of you confesses and the other denies that he broke the glass, I ask the one who confessed and the other to part with our school. Finally, if both confess, you will have to give you a “D” in the quarter for behavior. It's clear?"

“I see,” you answer in unison: and the director takes you to different rooms so that you think about his proposal. You cannot advise each other, and everyone will have to make their own decisions. So: confess or deny your guilt? Let's try to distract ourselves from moral messages - betraying a comrade, of course, is not good - and we will assume that you are only interested in getting off as lightly as possible. You reason like this: “If my friend decides to confess, then I need to confess too - otherwise I will be kicked out of school. If he decides not to confess, then I still need to confess - then I won’t be punished at all. Be that as it may, you need to confess!” And you confidently go to the director to tell him how you broke the glass. But your friend reasoned in exactly the same way and also came to the conclusion that you need to admit your guilt. As a result, obonkhs have a “two” in behavior. But if she hadn’t confessed, she would have gotten away with just a remark in her diary. This results in a paradoxical situation—the perfect rule of reasoning leads to an undesirable outcome.

This is the famous paradox known as the “prisoner’s dilemma” (the story is usually told about two prisoners sitting in different cells, and the role of the director is played by a judge). This paradox is associated with numerous articles by philosophers and mathematicians - specialists in logic and game theory. But make sure-

After school, you and your friend started playing in the school corridor. And suddenly - “bam!” - the glass that accidentally got in your way broke with a ringing sound. There was no witness, and without saying a word, you rushed down the stairs to the side of the butcher. And, as luck would have it, we ran straight into the school principal.
