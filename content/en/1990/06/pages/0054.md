On November 27, 1895, the prominent Swedish engineer and industrialist Alfred Nobel signed a will, according to which: “... my executors must transfer the capital into securities, creating a fund, the interest from which will be given in the form of premii to those who during the previous year have brought the greatest benefit to humanity. The indicated percentages should be divided into five equal parts, which are intended: the first part to the one who made the most important discovery or invention in the field of physics, the second - to the one who made a major discovery or improvement in the field of chemistry, the third - to the one who was successful in the field of physiology or medicine, the fourth - to the one who created the most significant literary work reflecting human ideals, the fifth - to the one who will make a significant contribution to the unity of peoples, the destruction of work, the reduction of the number of existing armies and the contents of the peace agreement."

As disappointing as this fact is, mathematics was not mentioned in this conclusion. Now, apparently, it is no longer possible to establish the true reasons why mathematics was thrown out. Nobel from the first version of completion, but he undoubtedly had a certain inclination towards mathematics.
