And the theorem, Schoenflies? Its spatial generalization would have to assert that the parts into which space is subdivided by a deformed sphere are standard: one is “structured in exactly the same way” as the area delimiting the general sphere, i.e., a ball (without boundaries), and the other is like the exterior of the ball in space. (The word “appearance” hereinafter is used not in the everyday meaning of “appearance”, but in the mathematical sense: “a part located outside.”) And this wave-correct hypothesis was unexpectedly refuted by everyone in 1924, then a very young American topologist John Alexander. The refutation was convincing: Alexander built a curved sphere in space, dividing space into non-standard parts. You will now see what the design consists of.

And simple. The main element of the design is shown in figure 4: on a flat disk we take two small circles and stretch out the “fingers” from them, so that their ends are close, but not in contact. We also consider these ends themselves to be flat disks.

This stretching of the fingers will be performed not from one disk, but from two parallel disks. The extended fingers will be hooked into the “lock” (Fig. 5).

Now let's go over the entire formation. Take a round sphere. From it we extend two fingers, almost closing, as in figure 4 (Fig. 6). The resulting surface consists of two parallel disks located close to each other (top figure 6). From these two disks we extend the fingers - exactly as shown in figure 5. On the surface shown in figure 5 - stick to this figure - there are two pairs of parallel disks (the ends of the extended fingers). These disks will appear (in a reduced form) on our surface. We transform each of these pairs of disks - by stretching our fingers - into a figure figure 5 (even more reduced). On the new surface there will already be four pairs of parallel disks, and we again pull out (very small) fingers from these disks, turning each pair into a kind of figure 5. And so on.

What happens after “and so on” is Alexander’s Horned Sphere.
