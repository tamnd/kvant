One of the types of elements used is represented by figure 2. The element consists of shaped ceramic parts (a kind of “thimbles” without a day) assembled into a tubular battery. Fuel gas passes inside the tube, separating the electrons to the negative electrode. The outer wall of the tube is in contact with hot air. The oxidizing agent (oxygen) is discharged at the positive electrode; They contain oxygen, passing through a layer of solid electrical power, reaching the negative electrode, where they enter into a chemical reaction with positive ions of the fuel gas. The electrical circuit is closed through current leads connected to the electrodes.

Noticeable conditions are achieved, in particular, when carbon monoxide and hydrocarbons in the form of natural gas are used as fuel. Of course, the combustible gases used are not just coal, as we would ideally like, but natural fuel. The operating temperature here reaches several hundred degrees, and such combustion can hardly be called “cold”, but this is precisely an electromic process, and not “ordinary” combustion.

The creation of electric power plants using natural gas is already feasible today. Especially in large cities, where such advantages of fuel cells as powerlessness, smokelessness and “waterlessness” are most realized. In one of the most balanced countries, Japan, by the end of this century it is planned to obtain approximately 13% of the required energy using a fuel cell installation.

If we look even further, 20-30 years ahead, the end of the era of petroleum products and natural gas will become noticeable. Apparently, the coal era or a unique coal generator will come. By this time, we should expect not only the appearance, but also the development of electrochemical generators that “burn” coal. Perhaps such installations will operate in conjunction with gasifiers, where volatile coal is treated with water steam; As a result of gasification, the so-called water gas (a mixture of carbon monoxide and hydrogen) is processed, which is, in principle, a suitable fuel. However, this path has its own problems: for example, since coal usually contains sulfur impurities, during gasification sulfur compounds are also processed, which have the unpleasant property of “poisoning” the electrodes. Cleaning is required, which is an additional cost.

There are other approaches and other difficulties. But they are all foreseeable. However, there is no doubt that the process of cold combustion still represents a serious consequence for humans.
