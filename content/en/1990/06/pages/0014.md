The container act of the combustion reaction is accompanied by the flow of electric current, which is a kind of short circuit current between the reacting particles. When released during each act, the electrical energy dissipates, turning into the energy of the otic combustion of particles, i.e., into heat. The observed total chemical reaction consists of an astronomical huge number of elementary processes.

In other words, conditional - "hot" - combustion can be imagined as random individual short-circuit processes occurring between an innumerable number of molecules or atoms with different electrical potentials. As a result, the chemical energy of the initial reagents (which is actually electrical in nature) is converted into thermal energy. However, thermal energy, being inherently the energy of chaos, is with great difficulty converted into the energy of ordered motion. Individually, randomly moving particles have significant kinetic energy, but they move in different directions and, like the swan, crayfish and shuka included in the last, do not in any way carry out their services in one direction. Every day, this is precisely the reason for the strict limitation of the efficiency of heat engines: when transforming the energy of chaos into useful work, losses of the fundamental are inevitable.

In a fuel cell, all the circumstances: chaotic currents associated with the occurrence of a chemical reaction do not arise at all. Let us recall the scheme of its operation: electrons from the fuel (hydrogen in the example under consideration) directly enter the electrical target. There, these electrons perform useful work, and are connected to the oxyplet (in our example, oxygen) at another electrode. Thus, an electrical target was inserted between the reactants, causing the erratic electrical currents characteristic of “normal” combustion to be directly converted into a regular electrical current. In other words, chaos in the fuel cell can be avoided at the very initial stage of the combustion process (no wonder it is cold!) and due to this, the value of the ideal efficiency factor can be sharply increased. So, if you burn coal in oxygen not in the usual way, but electrochemically, this coefficient, according to calculations, can reach 99.8%! Moreover, since the fuel cell does not have numerous intermediate stages of energy conversion (each with its own “leaks”, collapsing parts, etc.), there is reason to expect that the actual efficiency will not be too far from the ideal, i.e., close to the desired Sta percent.

The opportunities opening up here were clearly recognized already at the end of the last century, when energy, economic and other crises were not yet looming before man. The greatest physical chemist of the time, Nobel laureate Wilhelm Ostwald wrote in 1894: “The way that can solve the most important of all technical issues - the question of obtaining liquid energy - must be found electroxim... If we have an element that produces electricity directly from coal and atmospheric oxygen, this will be a technical revolution, far surpassing in its significance the invention of the steam engine... We are dealing here with cases when science predicts in advance complete success."

Everything in these words, written almost a hundred years ago, is absolutely true. And the stop, it would seem, is small - to cope with issues not of a fundamental, but of a purely technical nature. But this “little” turned out to be only a tough nut to crack, and even now they have not been able to crack it completely.
