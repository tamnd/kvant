M1226. If a square is surfaced at 45° relative to its center, then the resulting square will divide the sides of the first initial one in some ratio. Let's take an arbitrary convex quadrilateral, divide its sides in the same ratio and draw straight lines through the division points to form a new quadrilateral (Fig. 1). Prove that the areas of these quadrilaterals are equal.

A. Savin

M1227. For example, a chess tournament in which $n$ players played one game against each other is logical if the following conditions are met for any two players: the one who scored no more points did not win in the personal match. (Recall that 1 point is given for a victory, 1/2 for a draw, and 0 for a loss.) Prove that whatever the tournament, the same result (distribution of points between participants) can be obtained in some logical tournament.

A. Zelevinsky, S. Orevkov

M1228*. Prove for any positive numbers $a, b, c$, not greater than 1, that they are not equal
$$\frac{a}{bc+1} + \frac{b}{ca+1} + \frac{c}{ab+1} \leq 2.$$
D. Fomin

M1229. Prove that for every natural number $m$ the number
a) $4^m + 5$;
b) $8^m + 9$;
b) $a^m + a + 1$ (where $a$ is an integer and is not divisible by 8)
is not the square of an integer.

P. Khairushaev

M1230. In some cells of the square table $50 \times 50$ the numbers +1 and -1 are arranged in such a way that the sum of all numbers in the table in absciscal value does not exceed 100. Prove that in some square $25 \times 25$ the sum of the numbers in absciscal value does not exceed 25.

S. Genkin
