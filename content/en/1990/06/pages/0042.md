The hero of this issue of Kaleidoscope is undoubtedly Archimedes. The ancient Greek scientist, whose law you become acquainted with almost at the very beginning of studying physics, laid the scientific foundations of hydrostatics. They were developed only in the 17th-17th centuries by Stevin, Galileo, and Pascal, and received their final formulation already in the 19th century. Costati, it was the works of Archimedes that had a decisive influence on the development of Galileo as a scientist. There is no need to talk about the practical application of the results of Archimedes’ research; they are well known to you - from car-building to the impact on aeronautical navigation.

However, the clarity and simplicity of many problems based on Archimedes’ law are amazing; sometimes they require serious thought. Try your hand, not forgetting that experiment will be your reliable assistant - and this summer provides so many opportunities for testing experiments!

Questions and tasks
1. A block floats in a vertical position in a vessel with water. How will the water level in the vessel change if the block moves to a horizontal position?

3. At the scream of the dynamometer, the water tank rises. Will the dynamometer reading change if a bucket is filled with water and immersed in water?
4. The man carrying the car camera decided to lighten the load. To do this, he pumped up the chamber, increasing its volume and hoping to use the buoyancy force of the air. Did he achieve his goal?
5. What is heavier - a ton of wood or a ton of iron?
