In near-Earth orbits, the crew is protected by the radiation poisons of the Earth, but when traveling to Mars, this protection will not be available. Particularly powerful radiation will be present in the hearts during solar flares. On short expeditions, for example to the Moon, there is a risk of being hit by a powerful solar flare. During a long, almost two-year journey, there will definitely be outbreaks. In this case, the residential block is provided with a special room with radiation protection.

In general, it must be said that the residential block is very similar to the orbital station, only larger in size. And this is understandable: after all, the tasks of the residential block are almost the same as those of the orbital station - to provide conditions for long-term operation of the crew and equipment.

The apparatus for landing on the surface of Mars consists of a crew cabin, landing and take-off propulsion systems. There is a special room in which the crew lives in comfortable conditions for several days, which are allocated for work on the surface of the planet. The lander has an aerodynamic shape that provides braking in the atmosphere of Mars, the density of which is 100 times less than the density of the Earth's atmosphere. It also uses a liquid-jet propulsion system. This is what makes it possible to perform a soft “primation”. The life-support systems of the device are built on the same principles as the systems of the residential unit.

The propulsion system of the ship - a solar tug - consists of electrojet engines, tanks with a “working body” and an electron station with two large solar panels measuring $200 \times 200$ m each. A thin film of photoconverter is stretched onto a truss frame, similar to the structure that cosmonauts deploy at the Salyut-7 station (it was called Mayak). The batteries can be rotated relative to the ship's hull so that their plane is always directed toward the Sun, and the engines create thrust in the desired direction.

That's the entire interplanetary spacecraft. How will the flight proceed?

First, parts of the Martian spacecraft are launched into low-Earth orbit. This will require 4-5 launches of the Energia launch vehicle. These parts are assembled in orbit using an automatic assembly system (the system has already ensured the safe docking of more than 50 Progress spacecraft with the Salyut and Mir stations).
