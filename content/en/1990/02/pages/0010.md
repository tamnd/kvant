Now let's return to our formulas. Let's see at what angle φ a ball thrown at an angle $\theta = \theta_{\text{опт}} = 45^\circ + \alpha/2$ enters the stab. From expression (3) using simple trionometric transformations (those who are already tired of transformations can immediately look at “Appendix”, paragraph 2) we find:

$$\varphi(\theta_{\text{опт}}) = 45^\circ - \frac{\alpha}{2}.$$

This means that at $\alpha > 30^\circ$ a throw at an angle $\theta_{\text{опт}}$ will not be accurate at all, since at $\varphi < 30^\circ$ the ball cannot pass freely through the stab. Generally speaking, even at $\alpha \approx 20^\circ$ (i.e. $\varphi \approx 35^\circ$), the probability of a hit is still small due to the fact that in this case the value $l$ is relatively small (see conditions (1)). The situation when the angle $\alpha$ is relatively large occurs in the case of a throw from a close distance (up to 1-2 m), when the height of the ring above the center of the ball at the initial moment of its free flight is practically the same, and sometimes even greater than the throw range. However, the angle $\alpha$ can be reduced by throwing the ball in a jump, that is, by bringing the center of the ball at the initial moment to the level of the hoop. Obviously it is believed that a jump throw is needed in order to “outplay” the defender, however, when throwing from close distances, as follows from the results obtained, the jump also helps to increase the accuracy of the throw.

If we approximately consider the impact of the ball on the backboard to be absolutely elastic, then the initial parameters of the throw should be such that, in the absence of a backboard, ensure that the ball hits the mirror image of the hoop relative to the plane of the backboard (along the trajectory of BKA'). A decrease in angle $\alpha$ will occur in this case due to an increase in the throw distance (BK+KA>BA). It is clear that such a throw will occur to a player located slightly to the side of the hoop.
