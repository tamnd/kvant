However, it doesn’t hurt to believe in “harmony with algebra.” The point here is not simply to satisfy curiosity, although for people who are interested not only in basketball, but also in science, this is always interesting. At present, it is unlikely that anyone will argue about the usefulness of scientific research for the growth of sports achievements, which has become a fact. In any case, this may be useful for beginning basketball players.

figure 1 shows the basic physics of a one-handed standing throw. Let's try to find out at what angle $\theta$ to the horizon it is advisable to release the ball from your hands in order to ensure the most accurate throw.

Let us limit ourselves to an approximate study using elementary physical and mathematical concepts. The height of the ring above the floor is $H=3,05$ m (interestingly, this is the very height at which the upper rims of the peach baskets Naismith nailed to the balcony of the college gymnasium in Springfield are located); the internal diameter of the ring is $D=0,45$ m, the diameter of a basketball is approximately half that. Let the ball enter the ring at an angle $\varphi$ to the horizon (Fig. 2), and we will assume that the trajectory of the center of the ball lies in a vertical plane passing through the center of the ring (we will not take into account the possible deviation of the ball from this plane, considering that the main reason for a miss can only be the overshoot or undershoot of the ball).

Then the conditions under which the ball will pass through the ring without setting it can be written as follows:

$$|\Delta L| \leq l = \frac{D}{2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{2 \sin \varphi}\right),$$

where $\Delta L$ is the displacement of the center of the ball ($O$) from the center of the ring ($A$). This condition makes sense at $\varphi > 30^\circ$. If $\varphi < 30^\circ$, then the ball must hit the ring and, as usually happens (especially if the speed of the ball at the moment of hitting the ring is quite high), it is reflected without hitting the basket.
