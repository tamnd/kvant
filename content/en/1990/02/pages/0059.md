$$x + \frac{1}{x} = t.$$ (3)

Let's show how to apply this substitution using the example of a returned fourth-degree equation:

$$ax^4 + bx^3 + cx^2 + bx + a = 0,$$ where $a \neq 0$.

Let's divide it by $x^2$ and rewrite it like this:

$$a \left(x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}\right) + b \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) + c = 0.$$

Let us square equality (3):

$$t^2 = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} + 2,$$

i.e.

$$x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2} = t^2 - 2.$$

Introducing a new variable $t$, we obtain a quadratic equation

$$a(t^2 - 2) + bt + c = 0.$$

As for recurrent polynomials of odd degree, they are all divisible by $x + 1$ and after such division they give recurrent polynomials of even degree. Let's show this using the example of the returned equation of the fifth degree

$$ax^5 + bx^4 + cx^3 + bx + a = 0.$$

It is easy to see that $x = -1$ is the root of this equation, therefore, according to Bezout’s theorem, the polynomial on the left side of the equation is divisible by $x + 1$. However, the result of division can also be obtained by grouping the terms:

$$a(x^5 + 1) + bx(x^3 + 1) + cx^2(x + 1) = 0.$$

As is known, both $x^3 + 1$ and $x^5 + 1$ are divisible by $x + 1$:

$$x^3 + 1 = (x + 1)(x^2 - x + 1),$$

$$x^5 + 1 = (x + 1)(x^4 - x^3 + x^2 - x + 1).$$

And in general, $x^{2k+1} + 1$ is divisible by $x + 1$ for any $k$:

$$x^{2k+1} + 1 = (x + 1)(x^{2k} - x^{2k-1} + \dots - x + 1)$$

Correspondence school style assignment

When the Correspondence Mathematical School, which later became the All-Union School, was created, the tradition of testing optimization and large-scale studies had already spanned decades. This tradition, of course, greatly helped in the creation of VZMSH, but at the same time, it could not be completely transferred to the distance learning environment. The Olympiads have always been aimed at identifying talented children who do not yet have a mathematical culture. A typical optimization problem is a nut to crack problem that requires communication, but does not present technical difficulties, the solution of which, if one has already figured it out, can be written briefly and simply. Here is a successful example of problem optimization: “Prove that any triangular paramid has an edge such that all four plane angles adjacent to it are acute.” A positive rating (“flat-s-minus”) can be given for just one phrase: “Let’s take the longest rib.”

A correspondence school is precisely a school whose goal is to develop a mathematical culture. It requires tasks that are “ugly” from the point of view of optimization, presented difficulties in writing down solutions that require calculations, enumeration of cases, and a little independent research. A VZMSH student who has to complete the next task must work systematically this month. The Correspondence School also requires tasks that are inappropriate for optimization or an exam.
