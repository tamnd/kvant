However, the final velocity vector of the first ball $\vec{u}'_1$ will rotate relative to the vector of its initial velocity by some angle $\alpha$. Depending on the relative position of the balls at the moment of impact, this angle can vary from zero (light touch of the balls) to $180^\circ$ (frontal impact). Possible positions of the end of the vector $\vec{u}'_1$ lie on a circle of radius $u_1$ (Fig. 6). The final speed of the first ball relative to the ground is $\vec{v}'_1 = \vec{u}'_1 + \vec{v}_\pi$. The maximum angle between vectors $\vec{v}'_1$ and $\vec{v}_\pi$ is obtained when the vector $\vec{v}'_1$ touches the circle.

From here we find the desired angle $\theta_{\text{max}}$:
$$\sin \theta_{\text{max}} = \frac{u_1}{v_\pi} = \frac{m_2 v_1}{m_1 + m_2}: \frac{m_1 v_1}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{m_2}{m_1},$$
