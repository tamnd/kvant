As for the error $\Delta \theta$ in the throwing angle, we will evaluate its influence on the values ​​$\Delta L$, assuming for simplicity that $\Delta V = 0$ (i.e., the initial throwing speed is a constant value). To estimate using error (2), neglecting the possible change in angle $\alpha$. Assuming that the error in the throwing angle is relatively small, after simple calculations we find:

$$\Delta L = L \cdot 2 \frac{\Delta \theta \cdot \cos(2\theta - \alpha) - (\Delta \theta)^2 \sin(2\theta - \alpha)}{\sin(2\theta - \alpha) - \sin \alpha}.$$

(If the reader has not bothered to get this, frankly speaking, not very elegant expression on his own, we advise you to look at the “Appendix” at the end of the article.)

From expression (5) it follows that the change in the throwing range, due to an error in the throwing angle, is directly proportional to the throwing range (it’s not for nothing that throws from behind the six-meter line are more expensive than throws from a closer distance). However, others are important in this case. If we neglect the term that includes the estimate of the value $(\Delta \theta)^2$, then at $\theta = \theta_{\text{опт}}$ we have a paradoxical result: $\Delta L \equiv 0$, i.e. the throw distance does not depend on the error in the throwing angle. If nothing is neglected in (5), then $\Delta L$ depends on the error $\Delta \theta$ exists, although it is quite weak:

$$\Delta L = - \frac{2L(\Delta \theta)^2}{1 - \sin \alpha}.$$

Thus, throwing at an angle of $\theta = \theta_{\text{опт}}$ is optimal not only because the minimum initial speed is required, but also because the error in the throwing angle at $\theta = \theta_{\text{опт}}$ has very little effect on the subsequent throw.

So, the effectiveness of the throw largely depends on the player’s ability to “withstand” the required value $\theta$. What is the acceptable value of $\Delta \theta$? Let us estimate it for the special case $\alpha = 0^\circ$, $L = 6$ m. In this case $\theta_{\text{опт}} = \varphi = 45^\circ$ (see formulas (4) and (3)), and as follows from (1), $l \approx 0,066$ m. Using expression (6), for $\Delta \theta$ we obtain (translating the radian measure into a degree): $|\Delta \theta| \lesssim 4,2^\circ$. Is it a lot or a little? Is it possible for an average person according to psychophysical data not to go beyond this limit?
