ABOUT MASS

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
Y. SMORODINSKY

You've read a physics textbook and, of course, you know how you can compare the masses of two bodies. Let you continue your research operations and make sure that two bodies A and B have the same masses. Denoting the masses of the bodies by $m(A)$ and $m(B)$, we can write this result in the form of equality

$$m(A) = m(B).$$

Now imagine that we compared the mass of one of these bodies - for example, body B - with the mass of a third body - body C - and found that body C has the same mass as body B. In other words, we are convinced from experience that

$$m(B) = m(C).$$

Let us now ask: is it possible to conclude from equalities (*) and (**) that body C has the same mass as body A, i.e. that

$$m(C) = m(A)?$$

It would seem obvious. It is enough to refer to the basic law of logic or even just the law of arithmetic, according to which the statement (?) follows from statements (*) and (**). But let’s try to prove that if $m(A) = m(B)$ and $m(B) = m(C)$, then $m(C) = m(A)$.
