The following question may arise: we found the minimum distance between stones in the S.O. associated with the underlying stone; Maybe in S.O., associated with the earth, the answer will be different? No, that's not true. The distance between two moving points refers to the so-called invariant quantities, i.e., quantities whose value does not change when moving from one system to another. Here are examples of other quantities invariant in classical mechanics: inverted time between proper ones, sizes of objects, their orientation in space, etc.

Let us now move on to the problems of the diamka. Here the circle of “allowed” S.O. is sharply reduced. Since the rules of action in non-inertial S.O. are beyond the scope of the school course, we are forced to limit ourselves to only inertial S.O. In any of them we can use Newton’s laws that are familiar to us and the laws of conservation of energy and momentum

Problem 3. A cart of mass M and length l stands on the main horizontal plane. There are two people on the cart, whose masses are $m_1$ and $m_2$. How far will the cart move if these two people switch places?

On the one hand, we are interested in the movement of the cart relative to the ground; on the other hand, we know the final movements of people not relative to the ground, but relative to the cart. How can this be?

We will assume that the movements of all bodies - both people and carts - are uniform, and we will move to S.O., the speed of which is equal to the speed of the cart $v_T$ at some instant of time. Relative to this SO, the initial speed of all bodies is equal to - $v_T$. Let us write down the law of conservation of momentum for the closed system “cart - people”:

$$-v_T(m_1 + m_2 + M) = v_1_{\text{отн}} m_1 + v_2_{\text{отн}} m_2.$$

Multiplying this equation by $\Delta t$, we find the connection between the corresponding movements:

$$s_T(m_1 + m_2 + M) = -s_1_{\text{отн}} m_1 - s_2_{\text{отн}} m_2.$$

Obviously, the same connection will exist between the total displacements for all movements. Considering that $s_1_{\text{отн}} = l$ and $s_2_{\text{отн}} = -l$, we get

$$s_T = \frac{m_2 l - m_1 l}{m_1 + m_2 + M} = l \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2 + M}.$$

This problem can also be easily solved in a S.O. associated with the center of mass of the system (see this for yourself)*. For example, that the coordinates and speed of the center of mass are found by the formula

$$x_\text{ц} = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + ... + m_n x_n}{m_1 + m_2 + ... + m_n},$$

$$\vec{v}_\text{ц} = \frac{m_1 \vec{v}_1 + m_2 \vec{v}_2 + ... + m_n \vec{v}_n}{m_1 + m_2 + ... + m_n}.$$

From the second equality it is clear that if the system of bodies is closed, the speed of the masses remains constant. Therefore, the SO associated with the center of mass of the closed system is inertial.

*) You can learn more about the properties of the center of mass, for example, from the note “What is the center of mass” («Kvant», 1988, No. 3).
