Let us immediately note that we will only discuss the forward motion of the S.O. relative to each other.

Now let's look at a few specific problems. First - from kinematics. Let us note right away that within the framework of kinematics, all SOs (at rest, moving uniformly or accelerated, rotating, etc.) are equal; the choice of S.O. is determined only by convenience and common sense.

Problem 1. The speed of the river is $\vec{u}$, the speed of the boat in still water is $\vec{v}_{ОТH}$. What course should a person in a boat keep so that the current carries it away as little as possible?

It is clear that here it is reasonable to discuss two S.O. The requirement to provide the smallest method relates to the S.O. associated with the ground - the angle between the speed of the boat $\vec{v}$ and the perpendicular to the shore line should be minimal. In the S.O. associated with water, the speed of the boat $\vec{v}_{ОТH}$ is given and it is required to find the direction of this speed, for example, the angle $\alpha$ between the speed $\vec{v}_{ОТH}$ and the perpendicular to the shore line. After all, “keeping the course” means setting the direction of the boat’s hull, and it coincides with the direction of the boat’s speed in that S.O. where the water is motionless.

*) All these issues were discussed in more detail in the note “Relative Motion” («Kvant», 1989, No. 9).

The conditions of the problem say nothing about the correspondence between the values ​​$u$ and $v_{ОТH}$. Let's consider two options.

1) $v_{ОТH} > u$. In this case, it is possible to ensure that the boat moves perpendicular to the shore (again, not at all). Let's write down the law of addition of velocities

$$\vec{v} = \vec{v}_{ОТH} + \vec{u}$$

and draw it on figure 1. From the right triangle we obtain

$$\sin \alpha = u/v_{ОТH}.$$

2) $v_{ОТH} < u$. The equality expressing the law of addition of velocities for this case is depicted in figure 2. When the course changes, the end of the vector $\vec{v}_{ОТH}$ is described by a semicircle. The minimum angle between the vector $\vec{v}$ and the perpendicular to the shore line corresponds to the condition that this vector touches the semicircle. From here we get

$$\sin \alpha = v_{ОТH}/u.$$

So, for $v_{ОТH} > u$ sin $\alpha = u/v_{ОТH}$; for $v_{ОТH} < u$ sin $\alpha = v_{ОТH}/u$ (examine the case $v_{ОТH} = u$ yourself).

When considering the free fall of several bodies, it is convenient to use the SO associated with one of these bodies. In it, all other bodies will move rectilinearly and uniformly (we do not take into account air resistance). This example is often called the “Baron Monchhausen method” (do you understand why?). We will use it in the next problem.
