F1196. The operating direction of the light bulb, the current-voltage characteristic of which is shown in figure, is equal to 3.5 V (the volume curve with a direction of 4 V - the light bulb burns out). Two such light bulbs are connected in series and connected to a source with a direction of 5 V. What current will flow through the circuit? What resistor should be connected in parallel with one of the bulbs so that the direction on the other is 3.5 V? Will any of the light bulbs burn out if this resistor is replaced with another identical light bulb?

When two identical light bulbs are connected in series, the directions on them will be the same and equal to 2.5 V each (the voltage of a sufficiently powerful source does not change when different loads are connected to it). Thus, current will flow through the circuit (see current-voltage characteristic)

$$I = 0,26 \text{ A.}$$

In the second case, when a resistor is connected in parallel with one of the bulbs, the direction on the second bulb is 3.5 V, and on the first it is 5 V - 3.5 V = 1.5 V. From the volt-ampere characteristic we find that points of 0.22 A and 0.28 A flow through the bulbs, respectively. This means that the current through the resistor is 0.28 A - 0.22 A = 0.06 A, and its resistance

$$R = \frac{1,5 \text{ B}}{0,06 \text{ A}} = 25 \text{ Om.}$$

Now let's discuss whether any of the light bulbs will burn out in the third case. It is clear that we need to talk about a “single” light bulb. Let the direction on it be equal to the limit value of 4 V, which corresponds to the 0.28 A point. At currents of 0.14 A (half of 0.28 A), the direction on the bulbs connected in parallel with each other is 0.7 V. This means that the direction on a “single” bulb can reach its limit value of 4 V when the source direction is 4 V + 0.7 V = 4.7 V. Therefore, when the source direction is 5 V “single” the light bulb should burn out.

A. Zilberman

F1197. When studying the electrical properties of a thin plate of the new compound, it was discovered that its conductivity is significantly anisotropic: along one direction (X axis) it is maximum, and along the perpendicular direction (Y axis) it is minimum. A sample was cut from the plate in the form of a strip of length L and width d (L ≫ d) at an angle α = 45° to the X and Y axes and connected to the ends of the strip

Conductivity σ is, by definition, a coefficient of proportionality between the current density $j$ and the direction of the electric field $\vec{E}$. In the manufactured conductor, the electric field and current density coincide and $j = \sigma \vec{E}$. In an analysis medium, for example in a crystal, the conductivity along different axes can be different.

In our case, the conductivity $\sigma_x$ along the X axis exceeds the conductivity $\sigma_y$ along the Y axis, so the current density does not coincide in direction with the direction of the electric field:

$$j_x = \sigma_x E_x, j_y = \sigma_y E_y, \text{ но } \frac{j_x}{j_y} \neq \frac{E_x}{E_y}!$$
