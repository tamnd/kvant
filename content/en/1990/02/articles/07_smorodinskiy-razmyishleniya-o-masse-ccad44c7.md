REFLECTIONS
ABOUT MASS

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
Y. SMORODINSKY

You've read a physics textbook and, of course, you know how you can compare the masses of two bodies. Let you continue your research operations and make sure that two bodies A and B have the same masses. Denoting the masses of the bodies by $m(A)$ and $m(B)$, we can write this result in the form of equality

$$m(A) = m(B).$$

Now imagine that we compared the mass of one of these bodies - for example, body B - with the mass of a third body - body C - and found that body C has the same mass as body B. In other words, we are convinced from experience that

$$m(B) = m(C).$$

Let us now ask: is it possible to conclude from equalities (*) and (**) that body C has the same mass as body A, i.e. that

$$m(C) = m(A)?$$

It would seem obvious. It is enough to refer to the basic law of logic or even just the law of arithmetic, according to which the statement (?) follows from statements (*) and (**). But let’s try to prove that if $m(A) = m(B)$ and $m(B) = m(C)$, then $m(C) = m(A)$.

What does “prove” mean in physics? This is to verify by experience that this statement (assumption)

does not contradict any known (proven, tested by experience) law. In this case, certain “rules of the game” must be observed. And the first rule is that you cannot use the word “obviously.”

So, let's get to the proof. Let us mentally carry out such an experiment (it was discussed by E. Mach*)). Let's bend a ring of wire and place three balls A, B and C on it. The masses of these balls satisfy the equalities (*) and (**). Let us now push ball A, giving it speed $\vec{u}$. If there is no friction, then the ball will move along the ring without changing the speed (modulo) until it collides with ball B. After the collision, ball A will stop, and B begins to move with the same speed $u$ (again modulo) with which ball A moves before the collision. (If we did not know in advance that the masses of A and B are the same, then we could find out about this by comparing the velocities of A and B before and after the collision - this is quite good method, or by checking that ball A has stopped.**))

While moving, ball B will collide with ball C after some time. We can also imagine the result of the collision: B will stop, and C begins to move with a speed of $u$ (after all, $m(B) = m(C)$).

The next event will be the collision of ball C with ball A. We have not verified experimentally that $m(C) = m(A)$, and then we cannot state in advance that A begins to move with the same speed $u$ with which it moved at first. To reach this conclusion we must bring to our aid the law of conservation of energy.*)

*) Ernst Max (1838-1916) - a major Austrian physicist and physical scientist. He studied mechanics, acoustics, and optics.

**) These statements are not obvious. They can be justified by citing the fact that balls A and B are the same. But let's not give in!

***) It must be admitted that we could use the law of conservation of energy (or momentum) equally when we argued that balls A and B were placed in rolls after a collision: A stopped, and B “took over” the speed of A. Although this is “obvious,” it cannot be proven without conservation laws. (Beware of the word "obviously"!)

If the speed of ball A after the collision turned out to be greater than $u$, then A would impart this greater speed to ball B when contained, and in this way energy could be obtained from nothing: after each collision with ball C, ball A would move faster and faster!

If the speed A turned out to be less than $u$, then the balls would eventually stop and their kinetic energy would disappear without a trace (recall that the balls move without friction). Both conclusions are false, and therefore the statement that after colliding with C, ball A will move with speed $u$ is true.

Now everything has fallen into place. To satisfy the law of conservation of energy, the speed of ball A after the collision $A \rightarrow B$, $B \rightarrow C$, $C \rightarrow A$ returned to its original value.

We see that for a rigorous proof, logic is not enough; it is necessary to bring to the aid of a physical law - the law of conservation of energy. Without this it is impossible to say that $m(C) = m(A)$!

But for practical purposes, a strict definition is not needed everywhere. In an offended life, we have enough of a less strict, but more understandable definition.

Ныютон на первых страницах своей великой книги «Математические начала натуральной физофии» объяснял, что такое масса, так: «Количество материалов есть мера такой, пропорциональная плотности и объему ее»*. Если, не задумываясь, считать, что количество материалов обозначает ньютона массу, то такое определение представляет лишьенным содержания: для того чтобы узнать плотность $\varrho$, надо разделить массу $m$ на объем $V$, и потому формула $m = \varrho V$ есть следствие формулы $\varrho = m/V$. I must say that many people perceived it this way.

They didn’t understand what Newton said. And that was a mistake. Надо понять смысл того, что писал Ньютоон, а не обнаружить его в тривиальной нелогичности. Определя массу до введения понятия импульса, не зная о существовании закона сохранения энергии (само понятие «энергия» появилось спустя сто с лишним лет), Ньютоон оказался в трудном положении. And he found the only way out, coming up with his Definitio.

Newton himself did not use the word mass and included a different meaning in his definition. In the 17th and 18th centuries, it was believed that all bodies consisted of identical very small particles - they were called monads. The monads were all the same, and different bodies differed from each other only in that the monads in them were packed with different densities. What monads look like and what properties they have cannot be learned from experiments; but scientists at that time were not very worried about experiments; they believed that the laws of nature could be discovered by reasoning alone. The theories of monads were studied by Leibnitz, Euler and other scientists.

Крупным специализатом по монадам считался немецкий философ Христан Волф, популяризатор идей Лейбницца. Almost all of Europe learns from Wolf's textbooks. Ломоносов перевал один из учебников Волфа, и это был первый учебник физики в России. Тогда о монадах все знали и, говорят, даже приворные дамы при Прусском дворе любили о них рассуждать. Therefore, for the 18th century reader, Newton's definition was used to make sense.

The theory of monads, of course, came from science a long time ago. In the 19th century they already talked about atoms with different chemical and physical properties, but, surprisingly, in our 20th century it is useful to return to Newton’s idea and say that the mass of a body is proportional to the number of nucleons - neutrons and protons it contains - or even proportional to the volume of the body and “nucleon density” - the average number of nucleons in 1 cm³.

What are the possibilities for causing such a determination? The mass of a neutron is greater than the mass of a proton by about 0.2%. One can, of course, take this difference into account by counting neutrons and protons separately; but, on the other hand, an error of tenths of a percent in many cases does not bother us (at least when solving school problems).

A more fundamental error is due to the fact that nucleons in nuclei are “lighter” than nucleons in a free state. The fact is that the energy of a bound system of particles (atomic nucleus) is less than the total energy of these particles in a free state. To split a nucleus into its constituent nucleons, it is necessary to expend energy equal to the difference between these energies (it is called binding energy). And since there is a connection between energy and mass, expressed by Einstein’s formula - $E = mc^2$, - the difference in energy represents the difference in mass. This means that the mass of an atomic nucleus is less than the sum of the masses of its constituent nucleons. This decrease in mass is quite large and amounts to almost 1% (more precisely, 0.6-0.8%).

We know that the atomic mass of hydrogen*) is equal to 1.0078 atomic mass units, and the atomic mass of the main uranium isotone is equal to 238 amu, i.e. less than the sum of the masses of 92 protons and 146 neutrons that make up the nucleus of this isotone. You can make an adjustment for “binding energies,” but you shouldn’t complicate the task.

So, Newton's definition is to distinguish what is not "empty." The concept is correct, it determines the mass with an error of less than a percent.

But we haven't finished our story yet.

The mass we are talking about does not change in kilograms or grams - the unit of measurement is the atomic mass unit (let me remind you that an amu is defined as 1/12 of the mass of a carbon atom¹²C). Convert atomic mass units in the book

*) This is the mass of a neutral hydrogen atom (proton + electron). To use the atomic mass, there is no need to correct for the mass of the electron.

The logogram turned out to be not so easy, although, of course, there are no fundamental difficulties in this part of the problem. The idea of ​​measurement is the simplest - you need to divide the mass (in kilograms) of a sample of an element by the number of atoms in this sample. The difficulty lies in the fact that it is necessary to count the atoms “piece by piece”. Such precision is needed today by both physicists and chemists.

We will not talk in detail about ingenious experiments in which arbitrary calculations are made. Let's just add that for this they made a very good crystal, measured its dimensions very accurately, then, using RENT's analysis, determined the distance between the atoms... Each operation required great ingenuity - after all, we were talking about very high accuracy.

In the problem of mass this was the most difficult part.

One more note in conclusion.
In popular books, and even in textbooks, there is a concept that is long overdue to be abandoned. There is no benefit from it, and it lives only according to the old habit. This concept is the mass of a moving body, which grows with speed. In reality, the mass of a moving body never changes, it is its energy that changes (for example, the energy of a proton in an accelerator). The mass is calculated using the formula $m = E/c^2$. Physicists have not used such mass for a long time and carry out all calculations only with energy. Mass is now called only “rest mass” - the mass of a particle at rest. And in the tables of elemental particles the values ​​of the rest masses are collected (although the word “rest” is omitted).

Try to remember that “mass grows with speed” - this phrase now makes little sense. Energy increases with speed.
