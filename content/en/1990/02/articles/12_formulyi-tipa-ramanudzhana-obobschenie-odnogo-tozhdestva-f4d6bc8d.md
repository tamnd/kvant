Thus, in the sample, the flow is along the strip, and the direction of the electric field is directed at an angle to the current and consists of a longitudinal component equal to $E_1 = U_0/L$ and a transverse component equal to $E_2 = U/d$. Directivity projections on the $X$ and $Y$ axes are equal, respectively

$$E_x = (E_1 - E_2) \cos 45^\circ = \frac{E_1 - E_2}{\sqrt{2}},$$

$$E_y = (E_1 + E_2) \cos 45^\circ = \frac{E_1 + E_2}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

Then for the current density projections we can write

$$j_x = \sigma_x E_x = \sigma_x \frac{E_1 - E_2}{\sqrt{2}},$$

$$j_y = \sigma_y E_y = \sigma_y \frac{E_1 + E_2}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

Since the current flows along the strip, $j_x = j_y$, i.e.

$$\sigma_x \frac{E_1 - E_2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sigma_y \frac{E_1 + E_2}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

This means that the required conductivity ratio is equal to

$$\frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = \frac{E_1 + E_2}{E_1 - E_2} = \frac{U_0/L + U/d}{U_0/L - U/d}.$$

A. Vuzdin

The article “Three Formulas of Ramanujan” («Kvant» No. 6, 1988) contains a number of interesting formulas with trigonometric functions. The author of these formulas, V.S. Shevelev, sent us two more formulas of this type:

$$\sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{4\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos \pi}{2\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{8\pi}} +$$

$$+ \sqrt{\frac{\cos \pi}{2\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 4\pi}{8\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{4\pi}} = -\sqrt{3}7,$$

$$\sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{4\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 4\pi}{8\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{8\pi}} +$$

$$+ \sqrt{\frac{\cos 8\pi}{2\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 4\pi}{8\pi}} + \sqrt{\frac{\cos 2\pi}{4\pi}} = -\sqrt{3}9.$$

Try to prove these beautiful formulas using the methods of the article “Ramanujan's Three Formulas”. In «Kvant» No. 10 for 1988, in the section “Selected school problems”, under number 2, a problem was published in which it was stated that for $xyz = 1$

$$\frac{1}{1+x+xy} + \frac{1}{1+y+yz} + \frac{1}{1+z+zx} = 1.$$

A. M. Kolesnikov from the Rostov region discovered the following generalizations. Let $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ be numbers such that $x_1 x_2 \dots x_n = 1$. Then

$$\frac{1}{1+x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4+\dots+x_n} + \frac{1}{1+x_2+x_3+x_4+\dots+x_n} + \dots + \frac{1}{1+x_n+x_1+x_2+\dots+x_n} = 1.$$

The proof is obtained by adding $n$ very strong equalities:

$$\frac{x_1 x_2 \dots x_i}{1+x_1+x_2+\dots+x_n-1} = \frac{1}{1+x_{i+1}+x_{i+1}x_{i+2}+\dots+x_{i+1}x_{i+2}}.$$

where $i=1, 2, \dots, n$, and by definition $x_{n+j} = x_j$.
