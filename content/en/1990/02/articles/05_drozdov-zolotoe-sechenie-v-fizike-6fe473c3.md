"Golden Ratio"
in physics

The golden ratio is such a division of a segment into two parts, in which the larger part is related to the entire segment, as the smaller is related to the larger one. It is easy to calculate that this ratio is equal to $\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} \approx 0,618$. The golden ratio has an amazing property that appears in a variety of fields of knowledge.

It is known that the acceleration of a force also when moving away from the earth’s surface is described by the following formula (proof):

$$g_h = \frac{g_0 R^2}{(R+h)^2},$$

where $h$ is the height above the earth’s surface, $R$ is its radius. When the body is lowered deep into the earth, the character depends $g$ on $h$ changes:

$$g_{-h} = g_0 \left(1 - \frac{h}{R}\right).$$

Let's ask ourselves when $g_h = g_{-h}$? It is clear that one of the solutions will be $h = 0$. The second solution (which you can easily find) is:

$$h = \left(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\right) R.$$

This is one example of the appearance of the golden ratio in a real physical problem.

V. Drozdov
