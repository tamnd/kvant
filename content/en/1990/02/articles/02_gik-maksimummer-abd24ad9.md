MAXIMUMER
The section of fairytale chess that we will get acquainted with today is closely related to geometry. In the maximizer problem, one of the parties is obliged to make the longest moves (geometric - along a straight line connecting the centers of the fields). Most often, this requirement is presented to Black in figurative mat problems, when White forces Black to mat the White king.
The first diagram belongs to the classic of fairy-tale chess, and it presents two problems at once.

K. Fabel, 1938
Reverse mat in 9 moves (maximummer).
1. Cg6! Rh1 2. Kpd4 Ra1 3. Cb1! Forces the black rook to change another corner. 3...Ja8 4. Ca2! Rh8 5. Cg8! The rook is locked. 5...h5 6. Ce6. The rook is released from captivity and sent to the “h” line. 6...Ja8 7. Cc8 Ra1 8. Ca6 Rh1 9. Cf1 Rh4×. An elegant duel between rook and bishop.

T. Dawson, 1920
a) Reverse mat in 5 moves (maximummer).
6) The same task, but with the white pawn e3 together with the bishop e5.
All the figures are lined up on the same vertical and each one suggests that there must be two symmetrical solutions. However, there is an “extra” vertical on the left side of the board, and there is only one solution.
a) 1. Cd6! Qh4+. Not 1...Qa7, because the length of the move Qe7-a7 is 4, and the move Qe7-a7 is length: 3²+3²>4².
2. Cf4 Qd8 3. Ce5 Qd1. Transfer along a straight line. 4. Kpf4 Qd8 5. Cd7! Qh4×.
In the case of the symmetrical 1. Cf6 Qa3 2. Cd4 Qf8 3. Ce5 the black queen goes to a3, and the combination falls apart.
6) 1. Kpf4 Qa3 2. Cb5+ Qd3 3. e4 Q:b5 4. e5 Qe8 5. Kre4 Qa4×. It is easy to see that 1. Kpd4 does not lead to the symmetrical goal.
Here are two more classic examples on the theme of maxiumer, illustrating precise geometric maneuvers, unexpected rearrangements of figures and the ingenious construction of a matte network.

M. Klasic, 1980
Reverse mat in 2 moves (maximummer).
1. Qh1! Capturing the pawn is longer than its move one square, so Black is forced to capture the d1 knight. In this case, four transformations are possible and each time the white rook d2 forces the promoted piece to give mat. 1...cdQ (R, S, K) 2. Rd7 (Rd3, Rc2, Rf2+) and correspondingly 2...Q:d7 (R:h1, Cg4, K:f2)×.
There are also some interesting false leads in this problem. Any other opening move by the white queen is refuted by promoting the pawn to the corresponding piece. 1. Qd8 (d3, d7, :c5)? cdФ (L, S, K)!

F. Palati, 1925
Reverse mat in 5 moves (maximummer).
After the move Ka1-b3 the white king is covered. But how can we force Black to move this knight? 1. Cg8! Rg1 (or 1...Ja6 - a move of the same length) 2. Ca2 Rg8 (Rh6) 3. Qh1 Rg1 (Rb6) 4. Qh7+ Rg6 5. Cb1!
All Black pieces, except the knight a1, are “patched”, leaving 5...Kb3×.
As you can see, maximizer problems are real puzzles!
In conclusion, we will consider several maximizing problems of modern authors.

K. Alheim, 1980
Reverse mat in 3 moves (maximummer).
It is important to make the right opening move. Black has four “true” answers - 1...Qb1 (Q:b7, Qh7, Qh1). Followed by the corresponding 2. Qe3+ (Kra2, Qe1+, Rf3) Kc2 (Qh1, Kc2, Qh8) 3. Qb3+ (Kra1, b8A, Rb3) Q:b3 (Qa8, Qa7, Qa1)×.
1. Cf3! Now in the case of 1...Qb1 and Qh7 familiar warpants arise, on 1...Qb7 follows 2. Kra2 Qh7 3. Kra1 Qb1×. The move 1...Qh1 disappeared, but 1...C:c4 lit up, and then 2. Qe1+ Qe1 3. b5 Qa5×.
