EXPERIMENTS
with high frequency
generator

M. TSYPIN

In the previous article*) of this section of the magazine, we talked about the design of a tube generator, which makes it possible to obtain a voltage of 3-4 thousand volts at a frequency of about 5 mHz. It also describes a simple experiment with the glow of a neon light bulb brought to a step-up coil of a generator at a distance of approximately 10 centimeters. This glow indicates that the direction of the electric field in the rarefied neon filling the light bulb near the generator exceeds the critical value at which electrical breakdown of the gas occurs.

In a similar way, you can cause an electrical discharge not only in neon. Use, for example, the spectral tubes from the school kit. It includes tubes filled with hydrogen, helium, neon, krypton, etc. Also holding the tube by one end, bring the other to the boost coil of the generator (you can even insert it inside the coil). The glow appears first in the part of the tube closest to the generator, and then spreads to the entire tube. The discharge in the wide part of the tube often has an intricate shape and contains bright and dim areas, which can also be mixed. These phenomena are too complex to be discussed here; plasma physics deals with them.

their emission spectra. To do this, you can use a spectros, or you can do without it - just have a triangular glass prism.

Move away from the glowing tube at a distance of 2-3 meters and look at it through a prism. Hold the prism directly next to the eye, and move it away from it (taking into account the refraction of light rays by the prism). Once you find the correct position, you will see a spectrum consisting of individual bright spectral lines. More maturely, it is extremely beautiful: no figure or even colored fogtrafini can be compared with the observed picture.

A gas discharge can be caused not only in spectral tubes, but also in any other devices containing gas at low pressure. These include luminescent lamps, starters for them, tyrarons, and even ordinary krypton incandescent lamps. Experiment and after a while you will learn to recognize the composition of the gas in the device by the type of spectrum.

An electrical discharge in a gas is often a source of not only visible, but also invisible ultraviolet radiation. In this regard, the discharge at the tip, which ends in the boost coil of the generator, is especially interesting. With such a discharge, along with radiation in the blue region of the visible spectrum, the same, if not stronger, radiation also appears in the near ultraviolet region. How to detect it? It turns out that this can be done by taking advantage of the curious properties of certain types of paper.

Of course, you have noticed that the paper is sometimes yellow, sometimes more or less white, and sometimes completely white. This whiteness is often added using an optical brightener, a limited substance that emits blue light when exposed to ultraviolet light. Since natural sunlight always contains a certain amount of ultraviolet radiation, this blueness makes the paper whiter. If you bring such paper to a burning gas discharge, it will emit a fairly bright blue glow, indicating the presence of ultraviolet radiation. The effect becomes especially clear when comparing paper grades: some glow better, others worse, and others don’t glow at all. Cut out some figure from luminous paper, stick it on a piece of non-adhesive paper, and you will get a very convenient indicator of ultraviolet radiation.

Another area of ​​phenomena that can be investigated using our generator is related to alternating current. Since the high direction is not needed for this, it is better to remove the boost coil from the generator. We are accustomed to the fact that a transformer always has an iron core, and its windings contain hundreds of turns. At a frequency of 5 megahertz, the transformer can have a first winding of six turns, a secondary winding of one, and no core at all. At the same time, the transformer, oddly enough, works!

Take a piece of insulated wire about 40 centimeters long and make a loop out of it, that is, a coil of one turn, with a diameter of approximately 4 centimeters. Twist the leads about 15 centimeters long together, connect this turn to a light bulb from a flashlight and place the turn close to the generator loop coil. When the coil is too close, the light bulb will light up (and may even overheat). By somehow securing your turn, which serves as the secondary winding of an improvised transformer, you will get a convenient source of small high-frequency voltage. With its help you can do, for example, the following experiment.

But this is not the case at high frequencies, when the inductance of the wire begins to affect itself - now it exhibits noticeable resistance, which, moreover, strongly depends on its shape.

The more you experiment with a high-frequency generator, the more questions you will have. For example, these:

— How to change high high-frequency voltage? Is it possible to use an electric sopper for this?

— What is the spectrum of the discharge at the tip?

— Will ultraviolet radiation penetrate glass?

— Does the spectrum of a hydrogen gas discharge tube coincide with the “theoretical” spectrum of atomic hydrogen?

— How does the inductance of a wire depend on its shape? Which shape has the minimum inductance and which has the maximum?

— Is it possible to observe resonance by connecting a coil and a capacitor in series with a light bulb?

Try to find answers to these questions. And if you yourself come up with something that will be interesting not only to you, but also to others, write about it to the editor «Kvant».

A little commotion at the pharmacy

(For the beginning, see p. 50)

Each pill with an increased dose of medicine is 10 mg heavier than the normal one, then dividing 270 by 10, we get 27 - the number of heavier pills.

Let's write the number 27 in the double system: 11011. The binary digits in which there are ones tell us what powers of the number 2 add up to the double number 11011 (or the decimal number 27): 1, 2, 8 and 16. The ones are in the first, second, fourth and fifth double digits. Consequently, unusable pills with increased drug content are in the first, second, fourth and fifth bottles. The dual system also takes pride of place in entertaining mathematics.

Here's a simple card trick that allows you to surprise and amuse your friends. Although it looks nothing like the problem of finding bottles of unusable pills, both the problem and the trick are essentially “binary” - they are based on a binary number system.

Have one of the triels carefully recalculate the card deck. Having placed it in your pocket, ask your landowner to call any number from 1 to 15, after which, putting your hand in your pocket, deliver cards whose total values ​​are equal to the named number (Ace is considered equal to 1).

The secret of the trick is simple. You put an ace, a two, a four and an eight in your pocket in advance. It is impossible to determine at the head the lack of four cards in the deck, and your viewers will be confident that you asked for a recalculation of the full deck. You place the shuffled deck under the four cards already in your pocket. After the number is named, you mentally imagine it as the sum of a power of 2 (for example, if the number 10 is named, then you mentally decompose it into the sum 8 + 2 = 10) and, putting your hand in your pocket, bring out a two and an eight.

From the book “There is an Idea” by M. Gardner, M.: Mir, 1982.
