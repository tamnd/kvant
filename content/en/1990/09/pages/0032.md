$$m\vec{v} = m\vec{v}_1 + M\vec{v}_2,$$
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{mv_1^2}{2} + \frac{Mv_2^2}{2}.$$

Let's make some transformations:
$$m(\vec{v} - \vec{v}_1) = M\vec{v}_2,$$
$$m(v^2 - v_1^2) = Mv_2^2$$

Note that
$$(\vec{v} - \vec{v}_1)(\vec{v} + \vec{v}_1) = v_1^2 - v_1^2$$

And
$$\vec{v}_2\vec{v}_2 = v_2^2.$$

Then correspondence (2) can be written differently:
$$m(\vec{v} - \vec{v}_1)(\vec{v} + \vec{v}_1) = M\vec{v}_2\vec{v}_2.$$

Now let's make the substitution from equation (1) and get
$$\vec{v} + \vec{v}_1 = \vec{v}_2,$$ or
$$\vec{v}_2 - \vec{v}_1 = \vec{v},$$

i.e. the relative speed of the hoops after an absolute impact (hereinafter non-central!) does not change.

Thus, it is obvious that the distance at which the hoops will stop from each other (and in this case it depends only on their relative speed) will be equal to
$$L = L_0 \frac{v}{v_0}.$$

F1229. A boiler was lowered into a glass of water, and the water began to gradually heat up. A graph of water temperature versus time is shown in figure 1. After three minutes, the boiler is disconnected from the network. How long will it take for the water to cool to 50 degrees? up to 30?

The initial section of the graph (see Fig. 1) is almost straight; This means that the heat loss here is small. We will assume that they do not exist at all. This gives us the opportunity to estimate the lost thermal power at different water temperatures in shares relative to the heater power. To do this, we need to compare the slopes of the tangents at different points on the graph.

In the temperature region of 60 °C, the tangent of the tangent angle is 8 times less than the tangent of the angle of inclination of the initial straight section. This means that 7/8 of the energy we consume here goes outside. Analogously, we find that at temperatures close to 50 °C, 3/4 of the energy is lost. At lower temperatures it definitely turns out very bad, so the answer to the second question (about cooling to 30 °C) can only be given approximately.

An approximate “remaining schedule,” i.e., a graph of the dependence of water temperature during cooling on time, is shown on figure 2. From this graph it can be seen that the residence time from 60 to 50 °C is approximately 0.3 minutes, and to 30 °C - 2.5-3 minutes.
