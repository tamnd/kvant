An unprecedented match took place during the 1989 Dutch Championship (average lodey!). On one of the free days, all 12 participants of the tour with 12 chess computers at once. Since the strongest Dutch grandmaster J. Timman did not play for “protein” chess, it can be considered fair that “Deep Thought” was not in the team of their opponents.
Before the math, forecasts were collected. The organizer of the event, Van den Herik (a professor at the University of Limburg and one of the leading experts in the field of computer chess), as well as grandmaster G. Rey, predicted the score of 11:1 in the crawl of the masters. D. Levy's forecast was the same. Machines department preference M. Botvinnik - 6.5:5.5.

Thompson, the creator of the Bell program, turned out to be closest to the truth. He showed moderate pessimism, believing that computers would take over 4 outbreaks.
