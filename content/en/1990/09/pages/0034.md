Let us denote the first part of the work by $dA_1$, and the second by $dA_2$. It is easy to report (this follows from the symmetry of space) that
$$dA_1 = 0.$$
The work $dA_2$ can be written as
$$dA_2 = \frac{2Q - q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{R_2 - \delta} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right) dq + \frac{Q + q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_1 - \delta} \right) dq.$$
Here we use the expression for the potential $\varphi(r)$ for the exact charge $q$:
$$\varphi(r) = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}.$$
Having carried out the summation over all charges (interrating along $dq$ from 0 to $q$), we will find the required work $A$. When calculating the work we use that
$$\int_0^Q dq = Q \text{ и } \int_0^Q q dq = \frac{Q^2}{2}.$$
Finally we get
$$A = A_2 = \frac{3Q^2\delta}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{R_2(R_2 - \delta)} - \frac{1}{R_1(R_1 - \delta)} \right).$$
It is always useful to find the answer in a general form, since this allows you to check the analysis of the result obtained. For example, in this case it is obvious that at $\delta \rightarrow 0$ the work should also tend to zero, as follows from our expression. At $R_1 = R_2$ the work must also be zero, and this also follows from our solution.
According to the conditions of the problem, $\delta$ is much smaller than $R_1$ and $R_2$, so we can write the resulting solution in the form
$$A = \frac{3Q^2\delta}{8\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{R_1^2 - R_2^2}{R_1^2 R_2^2}.$$
When substituting the given values ​​$R_1, R_2$ and $\delta$ we get
$$A = -\frac{79}{4484} \frac{Q^2}{\pi\varepsilon_0 R} \approx -\frac{1}{60} \frac{Q^2}{\pi\varepsilon_0 R}.$$
Another possible way to solve this problem is energy. Let's consider it for the approximate case ($\delta \ll R$). Let us write down the energy of our system in the initial state:
$$W_H = W_1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0 E_1^2}{2} 4\pi R_1^2 \delta + W_2 - \frac{\varepsilon_0 E_2^2}{2} 4\pi R_2^2 \delta.$$
Here $W_1$ and $W_2$ are the energies of the electric fields of exact charges $Q$ and $2Q$, $E_1 \approx Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 R_1^2)$ is the electric field strength of charge $Q$ at distances $R_1 - \delta \leq r \leq R_1$, $E_2 \approx 2Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 R_2^2)$ is the field strength of charge $2Q$ at distances $R_2 - \delta \leq r \leq R_2$. After substitution we get
$$W_H \approx W_1 - \frac{Q_2\delta}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 R_1^2} + W_2 - \frac{Q^2\delta}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 R_2^2}.$$
