Here's another way to compare averages. Consider a right triangle $ABC$ with legs $BC = a$ and $AC = b$, and $a > b$. Let $\sigma_M$ be the sum of the distances from some point $M$ lying on the hypotenuse $AB$ to the legs of the triangle.

Exercise 7. Prove that $\sigma_N > \sigma_M$ if and only if the point $N$ lies “above” the point $M$ (Fig. 4).

Indication e. $\sigma_M = KN_1 + KM_2 + KM$, $\sigma_N = N_2N + NK + KN_1 = KN_1 + KM_2 + KN$, but $KN > KM$.

Let the bisector of angle $C$ intersect the hypotenuse at point $H$ (Fig. 5). Then the quadrilateral $CH_2HH_1$ is a square with sides $\frac{ab}{a+b}$, so $\sigma_H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}$ is the harmonic mean

some numbers $a$ and $b$, and $BH/AH = b/a$. If $O$ is the middle of the hypotenuse, then $\sigma_O = \frac{a+b}{2}$, and the point $O$ lies above the point $H$ ($\frac{b}{a} < 1$). Therefore $\sigma_O > \sigma_H$, i.e. $\frac{2ab}{a+b} < \frac{a+b}{2}$.
