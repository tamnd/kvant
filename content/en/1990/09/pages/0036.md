Now you can express any of the flows through the total current and the proportionality coefficient between them - this will be the changed inductance value:
$$\Phi_1 = L_1 I_1 + MI_2 = I_{06\pi} \left( (L_1 L_2 - M^2) / (L_1 + L_2 - 2M) \right).$$
The expression in the outer brackets is the required inductance. Substituting $M = 0,3$ mH, we get $L_{06\pi} = 0,8$ mH; at $M = -0,3$ mH $L_{06\pi} = 0,53$ mH.
An interesting application of the discovered effect: to select the inductance of a coil, you can make it up of two and smoothly adjust the overall inductance, changing the relative position of the coil within certain limits (for this, their leads must be long enough).

F1232. At a great distance from the Earth there is the planet DVK-1, around which the BK-0010SH satellite is orbiting in a circular orbit. The satellite has a radio station that emits a constant frequency signal. This signal is received on Earth, however, interruptions occur - for 45 minutes there is a signal, so for 45 minutes it is absent, so for 45 minutes there is, etc. The matter is not limited to this - the frequency of the received signal changes relative to the average value $f_0 = 1,5 \cdot 10^9$ Hz according to the law shown at figure ($\Delta f = 3 \cdot 10^4$ Hz). Assuming that the Earth-planet segment lies in the plane of the satellite’s orbit, one can find the mass of DVK-1.

The orbital period of the planet's satellite is very much
$$T = 2 \cdot 45 \text{ мин} = 90 \text{ мин}.$$
Half of this time the satellite is hidden by the planet; This means that he flies very close to its surface. The change in the frequency of the received signal is due to the Doppler effect:
$$\frac{\Delta f}{f_0} = \frac{v}{c}.$$
Here $v$ is the speed of the satellite, $c$ is the speed of signal propagation (speed of light).
On the other hand, from the correspondences of mechanics
$$T = \frac{2\pi R}{v} \ln \frac{mv^2}{R} = G \frac{mM}{R^2},$$
where $R$ is the radius, $M$ is the mass of the planet, $G$ is the granite constant.
After simple transformations the mass of the planet is found:
$$M = \left( c \frac{\Delta f}{f_0} \right)^3 \frac{T}{2\pi G} \approx 2,8 \cdot 10^{24} \text{ кг}.$$
M. Gavrilov
