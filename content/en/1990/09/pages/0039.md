Figure from the book by F. Yu. Siegel.
Lomonosov. Where did the version come from that this is a drawing by Leonardo da Vinci?

After diligent attempts, I found an article by V. A. Gurikov “Leonardo da Vinci’s telescope” in the magazine “Earth and Universe” No. 4, 1977 (pp. 70-73). Us. 73 I find the already familiar figure with the caption “Diagram of the telescope (figure Leonardo da Vinci).” I called the author of the article and asked him the same question: where did you get figure? How does it follow that it belongs to Leonardo? V. A. Gurikov reports that he borrowed it from the book by Ya. G. Dorfman “The World History of Physics (from ancient times to the end of the 18th century)” (M.: Nauka, 1974). I find this book on p. 125 I see... the same figure and with the same signature as V. A. Gurikov. Alas, it is no longer possible to ask Y. G. Dorfman: he died in 1974. We need to look further.

First of all: from whom did J. G. Dorfman borrow the description of Leonardo’s trumpet? I see that references are given to the work of S.I. Vavilov, only, of course, to a different one, not the one where he described Lomonosov’s “night-sighting tube”. Wonderful! Such a specialist as S.I. Vavilov, who knows the concepts of the general luminaries of science, could not be mistaken and attribute the Lomonosov pipe diagram to Leonardo. I found the necessary work in the third volume of “Collected Soined” by S. I. Vavilov (M.: AN SSSR, 1956) and read it carefully, at the same time checking the text with the text of Ya. G. Dorfman. Here is what is written in both works:

Vavilov, s. 247-248: There is no doubt that Leonardo not only dreamed of telephone devices, but actually implemented them. Codex A (l. 12) contained the following lines, explained by figure (cf. Fig. 6):

Dorfman, p. 124-125: There is no doubt, says S.I. Vavilov, that Leonardo not only dreamed of telephone devices, but actually implemented them. In Leonardo's notes there is the following indication, explained Fig. 16:

And then both authors cite the same words of Leonardo: “The further you separate the glass from the eye, the larger it will show objects to the eye for 50 years; If the eyes, for comparison, look at one through the spectacle glass, the other outside it, then for one the object will seem large, and for the other small, but for this the visible things must be 200 cubits away from the eye.” Only Dorfman replaced the word “cubit” with “feet” (although the Florentine cubit was equal to two feet (60 cm)).

Dorfman’s “Fig. 16” is the already well-known figure of Lomonosov. But “fit. 6" by Vavilov looks completely different. On it, rays from one object pass through the lens, but not from another. Both of them meet in the eye and are observed. There is no pipe!

Figure from the book by S. I. Vavilov.

So, Y. G. Dorfman made a mistake, and V. A. Gurikov and F. Yu. Siegel repeated his mistake. How such an experienced historian of science as Ya. G. Dorfman could make such a mistake remains a mystery.

In this regard, it is worth saying a few words about the fate of the so-called Leonardo and at the same time to warn some of the readers who might have a “tricky” question: could it not have happened?
