All-round correspondence physics and mathematics optimization at MSTU. N. E. Bauman

Moscow State Technical University named after. N. E. Bauman conducts in the 1990/91 academic year the third All-Union correspondence physical and mathematical optimization (with a technical application) for students of secondary schools, vocational schools and technical schools.
The Olympada is held in two rounds. Conditions of the problems per-
of the next round are published below. The tasks of the second round will be sent to the Optimkada participants admitted to the second round based on the results of the first.

Solutions to the problems of the first round must be sent before December 15, 1990.

Complete all solutions in one school notebook. On the outside cover of your notebook, paste a piece of paper indicating your detailed home address (with postal code), last name, first name, middle name, and place of study. Paste a certificate from your place of study indicating the class (course) on the inside cover.

Notebooks with solutions in a large envelope and send by simple mail to the address: 107005, Moscow, 2nd Baumanskaya st., 5, MSTU im. N. E. Bauman, “Olympada—90/91.”

First round task

1. In a trapezoid with bases 19 and 90, two parallel bases are drawn, dividing this trapezoid into three parts. The area of ​​the first part is 1917 times larger than the area of ​​the second adjacent to it, which, in turn, is 1990 times larger than the third. Find the lengths of these prims.

2. Find solutions to equations for number purposes

$$2x^2 + 1990 + x^2 - y^2 = 2y^2 + y - 1990,$$

satisfying condition $x + y > 1990$.

3. Determine the number of zeros at the end of the decimal notation of 1990!

4. Explain why hydrogen-containing substances (water, paraffin, etc.) are used to replace neutrons.

5. You want to build a plant that uses a daily average pressure drop of $\Delta p = 100$ Pa to produce mechanical energy. Assuming that the temperature in the room is constant, estimate the maximum mechanical energy output per month from an installation with a working volume of $V = 1$ m³.

6. The chamber with a volume of $V_1 = 22,4$ l is filled with air. The pressure in the chamber is $p_1 = 3$ atm, the wall temperature is $T$. At the bottom of the chamber there is spilled water, the volume of which can be taken into account. The volume of the chamber was slowly doubled, leaving the temperature of the walls unchanged. At the same time, the water at the bottom almost disappeared, and the pressure in the chamber became $p_2 = 2$ atm. What will be the pressure ($p_3$) in the chamber if its volume is doubled again (i.e. $V_3 = 2V_2$), keeping the temperature of the walls the same? Determine the mass of water (in liquid and vapor state) and the mass of air in the chamber.

7. Exactly above the resulting gap of an uncharged flat capacitor with a capacity of $C = 1,4$ mKF, a dielectric plate with a dielectric capacity of $\varepsilon = 5$ is suspended in close proximity. The thickness of the plate is equal to the distance between the plates of the capacitor, and the width is equal to the width of the plate. The capacitor is instantly charged to a voltage of $U = 10$ kV, and the source is turned off. In this case, the dielectric is lowered inside the capacitor to a maximum depth of $h = 1$ cm without friction against its plate. Using these data, determine the spring stiffness ($k$), if the body does not stand out during polarization of the dielectric. The height of the condenser plane is $H = 10$ cm.

8. A thin weightless conductive elastic tape, the length of which is $l_0 = 20$ cm and the elasticity coefficient $k = 20$ N/m, is fixed in a straightened, undeformed state in an external magnetic field with an induction of $B = 0,8$ T, perpendicular to it. When passed through the tape, it takes on the shape of an arc of a circle with a radius of $R = 30$ cm. Find the current strength ($I$) and prove that the shape of the tape is really an arc of a circle (i.e., the radius of curvature at any point is the same).
