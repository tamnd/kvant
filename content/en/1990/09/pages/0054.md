If we think about the photon propulsion system, we use the annihilation of matter, then there is still only one problem (storing gigantic reserves of antimatter, protecting photon reflector mirrors from the released energy and from the remnants of unregistered antimatter, acceleration times, dimensions, etc.) and no solutions are visible.

An experienced reader may say: but these estimates were made without relativistic corrections. As for the photon movement, we’ll wait, figure it out, and do it somehow.

A graph of a space flight from the point of view of an astronaut (a) and from the point of view of an earthly one is observed (b). a) The astronaut’s own flight time $t_e$ can be calculated using the formula
$$t_e = 4c/a \cdot \text{arch}(1+a \cdot S/2c^2).$$
(Here arch is one of the inverse so-called hyperbolic functions - hyperbolic arc cosine: arch $x = \ln(x \pm \sqrt{x^2 - 1})$.) Substituting the speed of light $c = 3 \cdot 10^8$ m/s, acceleration $a = 9.8$ m/s² (the usual load for an earthling astronaut), flight range $S \approx 4.7 \times 10^{20}$ m, we get $t_e \approx 42$ years. Speed ​​in the ship system - $v_e \approx \int_a dt.$
b) On Earth, time will pass $t_3 = t_e \cdot \text{sh} \cdot \text{arch}(1+aS/2c^2))/\text{arch}(1+aS/2c^2) \approx 100 000$ years
(sh $x = (e^x - e^{-x})/2$ is the so-called hyperbolic sine).

Let's try to show that taking into account relativistic corrections the picture is no better. Let's imagine a Galactic Phoron ship capable of flying at speeds quite close to the speed of light. The actual flight time of an astronaut back and forth over a distance of the order of half the diameter of the Galaxy with an optimal flight schedule (continuous acceleration and then continuous deceleration) is 42 years. In this case, 100,000 years will pass on Earth (see figure).

Let's assume that we managed to obtain an ideal process in a photon propulsion device, make an ideal construction kit with zero mass of tanks, screens, reflecting mirrors, pumps and other elements, and try to estimate some of the parameters of such an ideal ship.

The ratio of the initial mass of the ship to the final mass will be $\sim 7 \cdot 10^{18}$. This means that with a mass of residential and service premises and equipment (i.e., what the ship carries) of only 100 tons, the launch mass will be $\sim 7 \cdot 10^{20}$ tons - more than the mass of the Moon! Moreover, half of this mass is antimatter.

To provide an acceleration of 9.8 m/s², the propeller must develop a tg $P \sim 10^{24}$ N. To obtain such a tg, it is necessary to place a radiation source with a power of the order of $10^{33}$ W at the focus of the mirror (we obtained this estimate using the formula $W = Pc/2$). To help, the radiation power of our Sun is $4 \cdot 10^{26}$ W, i.e., at the focus of the photon motion of the ship, it is necessary to light up millions of Suns (and maintain this “burning”!) There is a theoretical proposal to carry only antimatter with you. Then these numbers are reduced, but still remain absolutely fantastic - at the focus of the mirror of photon movement it will be necessary to light hundreds, thousands of Suns.
