A simple $V_2$ turns out to be equal to the harmonic mean of the velocities $V_1$ and $V_2$, i.e. $\frac{2V_1V_2}{V_1+V_2}$ (make sure of this).

Widely known and often used in solving inequality problems $\frac{a+b}{2} \geqslant \sqrt{ab}$. It turns out that for any positive $a$ and $b$ the following inequalities hold:

$$\frac{2ab}{a+b} \leqslant \sqrt{ab} \leqslant \frac{a+b}{2} \leqslant \sqrt{\frac{a^2+b^2}{2}},$$

and all inequalities turn into equalities if and only if $a=b$. Each of the inequalities can be proven using the properties of numerical inequalities (do it yourself!).

The definitions of averages naturally extend to the case $n$ of positive numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$:

$$H(a_1, \dots, a_n) = \frac{n}{\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n}}$$

- harmonic mean,

$$G(a_1, \dots, a_n) = \sqrt[n]{a_1 a_2 \dots a_n}$$

— geometric mean,

$$A(a_1, \dots, a_n) = \frac{a_1+a_2+\dots+a_n}{n}$$

— arithmetic mean,

$$Q(a_1, \dots, a_n) = \sqrt{\frac{a_1^2+a_2^2+\dots+a_n^2}{n}}$$

- mean square.

A. GOLDMAN, L. ZVAVICH

Apparently, you have often come across such concepts as the arithmetic mean $\frac{a+b}{2}$ and the geometric mean $\sqrt{ab}$ of two positive numbers. You may have started calculating with other averages: the harmonic mean $\frac{2ab}{a+b}$ and the mean square $\sqrt{\frac{a^2+b^2}{2}}$ of two positive numbers. Thus, the average speed of a tourist who has traveled a certain distance at a speed of $V_1$, and the return journey at a speed of $V_2$, is equal to the harmonic mean of the speeds $V_1$ and $V_2$, i.e. $\frac{2V_1V_2}{V_1+V_2}$ (make sure of this).
