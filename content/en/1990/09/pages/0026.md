M1216—M1220, F1228—F1232

Answer: $\angle A=60^\circ, \angle B=45^\circ, \angle C=75^\circ$.

Let us use the fact that in any triangle (in the notation of the problem) $\angle CAH=\angle BAO$ (and therefore $\angle HAD=\angle OAD$; Fig. 1). Indeed, $\angle CAH=90^\circ-\angle ACB, \angle BAO=90^\circ-\angle AEB$, where $E$ is the second end of the diameter drawn from $A$, and the angles $ACB$ and $AEB$ rest on the common arc $AB$.

Now from the equality $AC=AD$ we obtain that $\angle CAH=\angle HAD$ (AH is the height in triangle ACD). Therefore, AH, AD and AO divide angle A of triangle ABC into equal parts, and if $\angle CAH=\alpha$, then

$$\angle A=4\alpha, \angle B=90^\circ-3\alpha, \angle C=90^\circ-\alpha.$$

The second remark regarding an arbitrary triangle: if $F$ is a point symmetric to the orthogenous $H$ with respect to, say, side $AB$ (Fig. 2), then $\angle AFB=\angle AHB=180^\circ-\angle C$, and hence $F$, lies on the circumcircle.
