We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than November 15, 1990 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant».
Solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are called for in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Task for «Kvant» No. 9—90” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1241” or “F1248”.
In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of the decision review).

Please submit the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Task for «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you will study.

Task M1241 - M1245, F1248 and F1250 were offered at the final stage of the XXIV All-Union Olympiad in Mathematics and Physics.

Exercise

M1241. There are 1990 piles consisting of 1, 2, 3, ..., 1990 stones. In one step, it is cut to select the same number of stones from any set of piles. What is the minimum number of steps in which to throw away all the stones?

N. Adakhanov

M1242. On two sides AB and BC, a regular 2n-gon is taken at points K and N so that angle KEN, where E is the vertex opposite B, is equal to 180°/2n. Prove that NE is a bissertis of the angle KNC.

N. Adakhanov, H. Nechuvgaev, D. Tereshin, D. Fomin

M1243. a) The equation *x²+*x+*=0 is written on the board. The first of the two players names any three numbers, the second places them according to his choice along the stars. Can the first ensure that the resulting equation has different rational roots, or can the second always prevent him?

6) The equation x³+*x²+*x+*=0 is written on the board. The first of the two players names any number, the second puts it in place of any of the stars; then the first one calls out another number, the second one puts it in place of one of the two remaining asterisks; finally, the first one puts any number in place of the last remaining asterisk. can the former ensure that the resulting equation has three distinct integer roots?

A. Verzynysh

M1244. In the Senate, consisting of September 30, everyone is at war or at enmity, and each is at enmity with 6 others. Find the total number of Senate tracks in which either all three are paired, or all three are at enmity with each other.

D. Fomin

M1245. A point O and n vectors are given on the plane, the sum of which is equal to $\vec{O}$. Prove that you can plot these vectors, starting at point O, one after another in such an order that the resulting closed (perhaps self-intersecting) broken line will be entirely located inside or on the boundary of a certain 60° angle with its vertex at point O.

S. Avgustovich, S. Sevayanov

F1248. There is a piece of chalk on a horizontal board. The board is instantly given a horizontal speed V₀ and the board is also left instantly after time t after the first point. The friction coefficient between the chalk and the board is μ. Find the length on the left of the chalk on the board and the complete mixing of the chalk relative to the board.

A. Zilberman
