Task LIII of the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad

The final round of the LIII Moscow Mathematical Olympiad took place on March 4, 1990 at Moscow University. More than 1,000 schoolchildren from grades 8 to 11 took part in it. Below are tasks of this type (4 hours were allotted to solve them).

8th grade
1. Prove that if $0 < a_1 < a_2 < ... < a_8 < a_9$, then
$$\frac{a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_9}{a_3 + a_6 + a_9} < 3.$$
A. Check mark
2. What is the smallest number of different prime divisors that the number $m(n+9)(m+2n^2+3)$ can have, where $m, n$ are natural numbers?
A. Spivak
3. Winners from grades 8, 9, 10 and 11 were invited to the qualifying round of the Olympiad - 11 people in total. Is it possible to seat them at a round table so that among any five schoolchildren sitting in a row there are representatives of all four classes?
S. Konyatsin
4. In a quadrilateral $ABCD$ inscribed in a circle, through the vertices $A, B$ and the point of intersection of the diagonals, a circle is drawn that intersects the side $BC$ at the point $E$. Prove that if $AB=AD$ then $CE=CD$.
B. Protasov
5. The scoreboard, consisting of 64 bulbs, is controlled by 64 konkammies; each light bulb is freshly made. With one turn on, you can simultaneously press any set of switches and record which lights were closed. In what minimum number of switches can you find out about all the lights on the board, which light is turned on by which tram?
S. Gashkov
9th grade
1. In a group of seven boys, each one has at least three brothers among the others. Prove that all seven are brothers.
S. Konyatsin
2. Prove that from 53 different natural numbers, the sum of which does not exceed 1990, it is always possible to choose 2 numbers that add up to 53.
S. Konyatsin
3. In this circle, a point is offset with radius 1. Various paths are drawn through it, and circles of radius 2 are drawn through both ends of the paths. Prove that all the circles drawn touch a certain fixed circle.

B. Protasov
4. There are 8 coins, 2 of which are fake: one is lighter than the real one, and the other is heavier. Is it possible to determine in 3 weighings on a scale without a scale what is heavier: 2 counterfeit coins or 2 real ones, or do they weigh the same?
N. Sergeev
5. The decimal notation of a rational number has a period of length $n$. What is the longest period the decimal notation of the square of this number can have?
S. Gashkov
10th grade
1. Is it possible to cut a square into 3 pairwise different and pairwise similar triangles?
S. Konyatsin
2. Find all prime numbers $p, q, r$ satisfying the equality
$$p^q + q^r = r.$$
B. Kukushkin
3. Prove that for all values ​​of the parameters $a, b, c$ there is a number $x$ satisfying the inequality
$$a \cos x + b \cos 3x + c \cos 9x \geq \frac{|a| + |b| + |c|}{2}.$$
S. Gashkov
4. At what relative position of four points in a circle is the product of all pairwise distances between these points maximum?
A. Spivak
5. The points $A, B, C, D$ are located in space so that the segment $BD$ is visible from the point $A$ and $C$ at an angle $\alpha$, and the segment $AC$ from the point $B$ and $D$ at an angle $\beta$. Find the relation $AC:BD$ if $AB \neq CD$.
H. Ballszin
11th grade
1. Find the greatest value of the expression
$$x \sqrt{1-y^2} + y \sqrt{1-x^2}.$$
N. Sergeev
2. Prove that if the function $f(x)$ is defined on the interval $[0; 1]$, is continuous and satisfies the identity $f(f(x)) = x^2$, then the valid estimates are $x^2 < f(x) < x$, $x \in (0; 1)$. Give an example of such a function.
N. Sergeev
3. In triangle $ABC$ the median $BD$ and bissertrix $BE$ are drawn. Can it be random that at the same time $BD$ is a bissertrix in the triangle $BCE$, and $BE$ is the median in the triangle $ABD$?
B. Kukushkin
4. Prove that for any odd number there is a multiple of it whose decimal notation consists only of odd digits.
N. Sergeev
5. At what location of four points in space can they serve as projections of any point corresponding to the four planes of the faces of a certain tetradir?
B. Protasov
