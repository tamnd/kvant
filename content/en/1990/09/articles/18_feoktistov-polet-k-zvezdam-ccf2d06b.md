Fly to the stars

Doctor of Technical Sciences K. FEOKTISTOV

Almost from the very beginning of space work, it became clear that the space of the solar system, its planet, is within the reach of spacecraft and ships that we can create, and, therefore, people will be able, if not to land, then at least to get to any planet. But at the same time it began to become clearer in our minds that here, “at home,” most likely we would not find anything unusual. It is unlikely that, based on the data obtained from travel through our system, we will make significant progress in understanding the world in which we live.

Naturally, the thought of flying to the stars arose. Yes, it was already implied that flights near the Earth, flights to other planets of the solar system are not the final goal. To pave the way to the stars was what seemed to be the main task of space technology. This seemed very idyllic.

So, it was necessary to start thinking about starships.

What should this ship be like? How to build it? What problems need to be solved to make star flight possible? Reflections and the simplest evaluation calculations have shown that fundamental difficulties arise here.

The first is time. Next, after building a starship that can fly at a speed close to the speed of light (say, at a speed of about 0.7 s), the travel time is

The second difficulty is the dangerous flow of gas and dust. The space between the stars is not empty. There are remnants of gas, dust, and streams of particles. When a starship moves at speeds close to the speed of light, they create a stream of high energy that will affect the ship.

The power of the energy flow from particles colliding with a body moving at a speed of $v$ (without taking into account relativistic energies) can be estimated using the formula

$$W = nv m_q \frac{v^2}{2} = n m_q \frac{v^3}{2}.$$

Here $n$ is the concentration of particles in interstellar space ($[n] = 1/\mathrm{m}^3$), $nv$ is the number of particles incident on 1 $\mathrm{m}^2$ body surfaces in 1 s ($1/(\mathrm{m}^2 \cdot \mathrm{c})$), $m_q$ is the particle mass (kg), $m_q v^2/2$ is the energy of each particle (J) relative to the ship.

Galactic gas consists mainly of hydrogen. Mass.atoma of hydrogen yes $\sim 1,7 \cdot 10^{-27}$ kg. The average concentration of particles in the Galaxy is $\sim 0,5 \cdot 10^6 \mathrm{m}^{-3}$, in the arms of the Galaxy $\sim 10^6 \mathrm{m}^{-3}$, in hydrogen clouds $\sim 2 \cdot 10^7 \mathrm{m}^{-3}$. The mass of dust in interstellar space is approximately 100 times less than the mass of gas.

This flow can be estimated by the following values: the power of the particle and gas flow is $10^4 - 10^5 \mathrm{Bt/m}^2$, the dust flow is $10^2 - 10^3 \mathrm{Bt/m}^2$, the particle flow is $3 \cdot 10^{14}$ particles/(s m²) with the energies of each particle being about 500 MeV. The impact of these flows will lead to the evaporation of any protective shield and to unacceptably high radiation dose rates for a long flight.

The third difficulty is energy. If the most effective thermonuclear reaction is used to propel the ship, then for round trip travel at a speed on the order of the speed of light, even with an ideal ship design, the ratio of its initial and final masses turns out to be large $10^{30}$, which seems unrealizable.

An assessment of the minimum required ratio of masses (initial and final) of a starship with a thermonuclear propulsion engine, propelled at a speed close to the speed of light (but allows relativistic corrections to be ignored as a first approximation), can be carried out in the following way.

The most efficient thermonuclear reaction is

$$2^2 \mathrm{D} + 3^1 \mathrm{T} \rightarrow 4^2 \mathrm{He} + 1^0 n + 17,6 \mathrm{MэВ},$$

moreover, 14.1 MeV of these 17.6 are carried away by neutrals. Thus, to accelerate the gel in motion, it will be possible to use the energy $E \sim 3,5$ MeV per one atom of the gel. The maximum flow rate of the gel in ideal motion can be calculated from the relation $E_q = m_q v^2/n/2$:

$$v_n = \sqrt{2E_q/m_q}.$$

Substituting the mass of the atoma gel $m_q \sim 4 \cdot 1,67 \times 10^{-27} \mathrm{kg}$ and $E_q = 3,5 \cdot 10^6 \mathrm{aB} \approx 3,5 \cdot 10^6 \times 1,6 \cdot 10^{-19} \mathrm{J}$, we obtain $v_n \approx 1,3 \cdot 10^7 \mathrm{m/c}$.

The ratio of the initial and final masses of the starship (without taking into account relativistic corrections) is determined by the Tsiolkovsky formula:

$$\frac{M_0}{M_k} = e^{4v_k/v_n},$$

where $v_k$ is the maximum flight speed of the ship. Adding $v_k \sim 0,7 \mathrm{c}$, we get:

Let's assume that we managed to obtain an ideal process in a photon propulsion device, make an ideal construction kit with zero mass of tanks, screens, reflecting mirrors, pumps and other elements, and try to estimate some of the parameters of such an ideal ship.

The ratio of the initial mass of the ship to the final mass will be $\sim 7 \cdot 10^{18}$. This means that with a mass of residential and service premises and equipment (i.e., what the ship carries) of only 100 tons, the launch mass will be $\sim 7 \cdot 10^{20}$ tons - more than the mass of the Moon! Moreover, half of this mass is antimatter.

To provide an acceleration of 9.8 m/s², the propeller must develop a tg $P \sim 10^{24}$ N. To obtain such a tg, it is necessary to place a radiation source with a power of the order of $10^{33}$ W at the focus of the mirror (we obtained this estimate using the formula $W = Pc/2$). To help, the radiation power of our Sun is $4 \cdot 10^{26}$ W, i.e., at the focus of the photon motion of the ship, it is necessary to light up millions of Suns (and maintain this “burning”!) There is a theoretical proposal to carry only antimatter with you. Then these numbers are reduced, but still remain absolutely fantastic - at the focus of the mirror of photon movement it will be necessary to light hundreds, thousands of Suns.

It is necessary to find a method of travel mechanism that does not involve the need to transport body materials. And here we come to an idea that has long been explored in science fiction literature - about the travel of intelligent beings in the form of a package of information.

Electromagnetic waves propagate virtually losslessly throughout the observable Universe. Perhaps this is the key to interstellar flight.

Without falling into mysticism, we must admit that the personality of a modern organizational person cannot be separated from the body. But one can imagine a specially designed person whose personality and body are separate. Similar to how software can be separated from the design of modern computers.

Personality is an individual complex of characteristics of a given person in his perception of the external world, in his reactions to received information, in his imagination, likes and dislikes, in his knowledge, etc.

Personality is essential in the fields of operations and in storage devices. These fields and devices are our brain, the material medium on which the human intellect lives and operates, on which algorithms for perceiving and analyzing information, synthesizing a picture of the external world, algorithms for assessments and space, intermediate results of “calculations”, and conclusions are recorded (and are periodically replenished, improved, established). This is the field on which our Self is located. It lives as the Self when a person thinks, makes decisions, experiences natural pleasure or disgust, that is, when operations of the intellect take place on this field of operations. These operations are the life of the individual or, if you like, the life of the soul.

If a package of information, which is the complete content of a person, can be rewritten from the fields of operations and storage devices, then this package of information can be transmitted via radio link to the destination receiving station and there rewritten into a standard material medium (either selected by the price list, or...), in which he can live, act and satisfy his curiosity. During the transmission of his packet of information, the person does not live. In order for it to live, a material carrier is necessary.

Such a method of solving the problem of flying to the stars would be the realization of not only the complexes of modern science fiction, but also the complexes of ancient myths about appearances in heaven or instantaneous overthrows into hell. This would be an “experimental solution” to philosophical disputes about the human day, about the frailty of the bodily shell, about the day of existence. What is a person? And what is truth? It is interesting that outstanding philosophers in different eras, from Antinosticism to our time, through logical analysis (not based on knowledge) came to realize the idea of ​​​​the correspondence between the essence and body of a person. The life of a person is the life of his soul, this is the thought about himself (“what am I?”), about the world outside himself and in himself, this is the perception of beauty and the rejection of the primitive and untruth, this is freedom of thought and analysis. We are here, we live, as long as we are able to think, evaluate, process information and generate it. The rest of me, my body, is for maintenance.

The brain is the field of mathematical operations on symbols, numbers, and concepts. But the operations themselves, thoughts, our experience are something that cannot be taken into hand. Man at all times tries to materialize this something in the form of sounds, words, stories, manuscripts, books... But it always turned out to be just a shadow, a weak reflection of this nothing.

More understandable difficulties will appear.

To send a person in the form of a package of information on a galactic journey, it is necessary to create receiving and transmitting stations (for example, in the radio range), and to route (for example, using automatic spacecraft) these stations to the desired destination (not far from any star to provide the stations with energy). If a personality is transmitted over a radio channel over galactic distances, then it will be necessary to create antennas with a size of the order of kliometers, transmitters with a power of the order of $10^8$ kW. The speeds of automatic spacecraft that fly now are ten kilometers per second. Speeds of the order of hundreds and even thousands of cliometers per second seem to be achievable. But this means that the time of transportation throughout the galaxy is millions and even hundreds of millions of years for earthlings. Delivery of stations even to the closest stars, located ten light years away from us, will require tens of thousands. During this time, interest in the enterprise may be lost. Nevertheless, this path is within the limits of the possible.

It is possible to transport not receiving and transmitting stations, but technical tools, robots to create such stations on site.

One can also propose another way to carry out stellar travel, namely, to get in touch with other civilizations, transmit to them information about the construction of a receiving and transmitting station suitable for receiving and returning “our people”, the information necessary to create a standard material carrier, and in this way establish galactic travel (the same idea from science fiction - remember, for example, “Black Cloud”). Here the old problem appears in the discussions: how to get in touch with other civilizations?

The natural way is to create a “mage”, receive a return request and enter into contact. If we proceed from the idea of ​​​​creating a pulse beacon that receives energy from a star, with solar panels with a power of about 10⁹ kW (here the data applies to a radio beacon with a transmission frequency of only 100 Hz), then we can count on subscribers located at distances of up to fifty thousand light years and having receiving antennas with dimensions of up to 10-20 km near their star. The value of 10⁹ kW should not be alarmed. Solar batteries of such power should have dimensions of 100 × 100 km - gigantic, but generally feasible dimensions. Such a structure can be imagined as a light flat truss on which film photovoltaic cells are stretched.

The time frame for the implementation of such a connection will be thousands and ten thousand years. True, it’s not millions anymore, but it’s still a long time.

Could there be a shorter way?

If other civilizations had previously chosen this path of exploration of the Galaxy, then they could have already created their own “mages”. So, look for these beacons. Create receiving antennas capable of receiving signals from galactic beacons.

Radio telexes with antennas of the order of cliometers or more can be created in near-solar space. Where to look? Maybe in the center of the Galaxy? or along the middle lines of the spiral arms? in globular star clusters?

One way or another, this has already been decades, not thousands or millions of years.

Isn't there an even simpler way to get in touch with other civilizations?

Let's assume that representatives of other civilizations were (or are?) already on Earth or in the solar system. What could be the traces of their activities? Where can transmitting and receiving stations (including intermediate ones) be located?

There are two directions of search here. One is space boats. What should we expect them to be like: size, features of their life (maybe, for example, they do not need an atmosphere and organic matter for energy supply, vacuum is their natural habitat...)? Why didn’t they themselves come into contact or why don’t they want to come into contact with us? Another direction is the search for their means of communication, reception and transmission of travelers and information.

The purpose of such reflections on the verge of science fiction is to look ahead in order to understand our data, to determine promising directions for ultra-long-range search, to compare these directions with current gaps in ecology and economics, the arrangement of life on Earth, with today’s interesting scientific problems in the study of the Universe and the Earth. And from this analysis, identify the areas of work on which it is worth spending the total funds, energy and intelligence of people. This is necessary in order to make a balanced and reasonable choice, so as not to waste effort and money.

Reflections on flight to the stars will allow us to highlight several interesting areas of work:
— research into the possibility of creating and developing artificial intelligence;
