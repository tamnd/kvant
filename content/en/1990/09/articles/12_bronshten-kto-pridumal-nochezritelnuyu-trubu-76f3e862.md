“nightly work”?

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. VRONSHTEN

About three years ago I received a letter from a young astronomy enthusiast, Yan Kharitonov, from Kharkov. Here's what he wrote:

“Recently a wonderful book by P. G. Kulikovsky “M. V. Lomonosov - astronomer and astrophysicist”, as well as a new edition of the famous book by F. Yu. Siegel “Astronomical Mozanka”. In the first book he talks about the creative activity of M.V. Lomonosov. Including on p. 82 is given figure “night-visionary works” by Lomonosov. In the book by F. Yu. Siegel... there is a story about Leonardo da Vinci's tele, and on p. 22 is given figure of this work (in the reconstruction of V. A. Gurikov). If we remove the 5 crossing lines figure, we get... figure Lomonosov’s “night-visionary work.”

Figure from the book by P. G. Kulikovsky.

Having removed the books mentioned above from the shelf, I was convinced that Ya. Kharitonov was right. Figure are completely identical. I called both authors and asked them the same question: where did you get this figure? P. G. Kulikovsky answered: from the article by S. I. Vavilov “Nocturnal work of Lomonosov” in volume II of the collection “Lomonosov”, published in 1946. F. Yu. Siegel: from an article by V. A. Gurikov in the magazine “Earth and Universe”, I just don’t remember the year and number.*)

I take a collection with an article by S. I. Vavilov. The article is large and interesting, but figure this is not in it. I take volume IV of the “Complete Works of M.V. Lomonosov,” which contains an article on “night-visionary work,” and there is this figure, even reproduced twice: in the Latin text (Lomonosov wrote in Latin) and in the Russian translation. This is what Lomonosov writes in his marriage to figure:

“For this purpose, I adapted two works in the same way that we used to construct telescopes and microcops. In the lumen of the work ABCD, I placed a biconvex lens, the diameter of which was about 4 London inches [about 10 cm - V.V.]... In the lid of the work EFGH, inside which the first work was played, there was a round hole into which the collecting lenses mn were left, biconvex and having different diameters and foci.”

Note that Lomonosov's text mentions and explains the letter symbols found on figure.

However, Lomonosov did not include this piece of figure in the final text of the article; he crossed out the text and figure and replaced them with a shorter text (without figure). Therefore, in volume IV of the scientist’s “Complete Works,” the old text and the crossed out figure are given in a footnote.

So, it is clear: figure “night-visionary labor” undoubtedly belongs

Figure from the book by F. Yu. Siegel.
Lomonosov. Where did the version come from that this is a drawing by Leonardo da Vinci?

After diligent attempts, I found an article by V. A. Gurikov “Leonardo da Vinci’s telescope” in the magazine “Earth and Universe” No. 4, 1977 (pp. 70-73). Us. 73 I find the already familiar figure with the caption “Diagram of the telescope (figure Leonardo da Vinci).” I called the author of the article and asked him the same question: where did you get figure? How does it follow that it belongs to Leonardo? V. A. Gurikov reports that he borrowed it from the book by Ya. G. Dorfman “The World History of Physics (from ancient times to the end of the 18th century)” (M.: Nauka, 1974). I find this book on p. 125 I see... the same figure and with the same signature as V. A. Gurikov. Alas, it is no longer possible to ask Y. G. Dorfman: he died in 1974. We need to look further.

First of all: from whom did J. G. Dorfman borrow the description of Leonardo’s trumpet? I see that references are given to the work of S.I. Vavilov, only, of course, to a different one, not the one where he described Lomonosov’s “night-sighting tube”. Wonderful! Such a specialist as S.I. Vavilov, who knows the concepts of the general luminaries of science, could not be mistaken and attribute the Lomonosov pipe diagram to Leonardo. I found the necessary work in the third volume of “Collected Soined” by S. I. Vavilov (M.: AN SSSR, 1956) and read it carefully, at the same time checking the text with the text of Ya. G. Dorfman. Here is what is written in both works:

Vavilov, s. 247-248: There is no doubt that Leonardo not only dreamed of telephone devices, but actually implemented them. Codex A (l. 12) contained the following lines, explained by figure (cf. Fig. 6):

Dorfman, p. 124-125: There is no doubt, says S.I. Vavilov, that Leonardo not only dreamed of telephone devices, but actually implemented them. In Leonardo's notes there is the following indication, explained Fig. 16:

And then both authors cite the same words of Leonardo: “The further you separate the glass from the eye, the larger it will show objects to the eye for 50 years; If the eyes, for comparison, look at one through the spectacle glass, the other outside it, then for one the object will seem large, and for the other small, but for this the visible things must be 200 cubits away from the eye.” Only Dorfman replaced the word “cubit” with “feet” (although the Florentine cubit was equal to two feet (60 cm)).

Dorfman’s “Fig. 16” is the already well-known figure of Lomonosov. But “fit. 6" by Vavilov looks completely different. On it, rays from one object pass through the lens, but not from another. Both of them meet in the eye and are observed. There is no pipe!

Figure from the book by S. I. Vavilov.

So, Y. G. Dorfman made a mistake, and V. A. Gurikov and F. Yu. Siegel repeated his mistake. How such an experienced historian of science as Ya. G. Dorfman could make such a mistake remains a mystery.

In this regard, it is worth saying a few words about the fate of the so-called Leonardo and at the same time to warn some of the readers who might have a “tricky” question: could it not have happened?

A parabola has a very important optical property: rays emanating from a light source located at the focus of the parabola, after reflection from the parabola, turn out to be directed parallel to its axis. This property is used in the manufacture of mirrors for projectors, automatic headlights, and televisions.

It is well known that the trajectory of a stone thrown at an angle to the horizon in the absence of air resistance will be a parabola, but little is known that the accessibility zone for stones thrown by us or projectiles fired from a cannon will also be limited by a parabola.

Here we are talking about the envelope curve of the trajectories of stones released from a given point at different angles, but with the same initial speed.
