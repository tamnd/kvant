After receiving data on the successful launch of the landing kit into the ISM orbit, the formation of a rafted interplanetary spacecraft begins in the satellite orbit. Launches of the Energia launch vehicle sequentially deliver to the Mir orbital station packages of blocks for accelerating a set near the Earth with a nuclear propulsion system of the NERVA type, a package braking block for Mars (developed by the USSR) and a rafted interplanetary spacecraft (developed by the USSR with the participation of countries of the world community). Some models are assembled on a special machine at the Mir station. After this, an international crew of 8 people representing different continents is delivered to the station using the Zenit carrier package in transport spacecraft (Soyuz type). Astronauts take jobs in an interplanetary spacecraft. The Mars Expeditionary Complex separates from the Mir station and begins its independent flight in near-Earth orbit. (For the formation of the IEC, 3-4 launches of the Energia launch vehicle and one launch of the Zenit launch vehicle will be required; all operations will take about 5 months.)

But then the propulsion systems turn on, the overload increases, and the expeditionary complex rushes towards Mars. Ahead lies the flight along the Earth-Mars route, braking near Mars, docking with an automatic ship, landing on Mars, two weeks of work on the Red Planet, takeoff from Mars, departure to Earth...

A year and a half after the launch of the rafted interplanetary spacecraft from Earth, it returns to its space home. The lander lands in the Kazakh steppe. The year on the calendar is 2013.

The task is completed. New flights are ahead!
