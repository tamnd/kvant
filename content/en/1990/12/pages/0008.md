Careless combination of object and sign can lead to paradoxes like:
what is written on this line is incorrect.
(This is described in the article “The Priest Had a Dog,” «Kvant» No. 6, 1989.) But it can also lead to wonderful results.
The most famous example of this kind is the meaning of Gödel's theorem on the non-hollowness of an argument.
The question is whether any true argumentative statement can be formally proven, that is, deduced from the axioms of argumentation according to the laws of logic (which are also described by axioms). Imagine that you managed to construct a formula that asserts its own unprovability. If this formula is false, then it is provable, and provable statements are true. Therefore, it is true, and therefore unprovable! So, if such a formula can be constructed in the language of argumentation, then argumentation turns out to be incomplete: it contains true but unprovable statements.
This is exactly what the Austrian people managed to do.
