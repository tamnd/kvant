electrical circuits?

It is impossible to imagine the modern world without the use of various electrical circuits that transmit energy and information. The opportunity to study the laws that govern electric current appeared only at the beginning of the last century after the invention of current sources by Galvani, Volta and Seebeck. The first physicist who tried to figure out these patterns was a modest schoolteacher from Cologne, Georg Simon Ohm. Today his experiments may seem trivial, but at that time conducting reliable experiments with direct current was extremely difficult, and only persistent and bloody changes allowed Ohm to establish his law of signs.
To get a feel for the difficult “elementary” questions that researchers faced as they approached the “age of electricity,” reflect on the objectives of this issue of Kaleidoscon.
