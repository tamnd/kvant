Let us now consider the second type of diffraction by a rod, which gives equal stripes parallel to the axis of the cylinder. The origin of these bands can be described in terms of the well-known Young interference experiment. The regions of the wave front adjacent to the surface of the cylinder are sources of secondary waves, and the result of their addition in the plane is observed and gives the diffraction pattern of interest to us.

To carry out such an experiment (similar to Young’s experiment), the rod was placed in the middle between the knives of the adjustable slit S. The width of the slit was selected such that gaps of 0.1 mm wide remained between the surface of the cylinder and the knives of the slit. When illuminated with laser light, two luminous lines were formed, which gave Young diffraction. From figure 5 it is clear that the diffraction pattern really has the form of equal stripes. The distribution of illumination in the stripes is shown by the corresponding curve in figure 3, b.

So, diffraction by a cylinder can be considered as the addition of the fields of two edge diffractions and Young’s diffraction bands. (When comparing the curves on figure 3, it should be borne in mind that the upper and middle curves were taken at the same chromaticity, and the lower curve at a higher chromaticity.) Generally, Fresnel diffraction on the wire is associated only with a light stripe along the axis of geometric shadows. Experiences with laser light give a more complete picture of diffraction in this case.

The second experiment was carried out with a ball with a diameter of 2.4 mm. The ball is carefully glued with plasticine to a plane-parallel glass plate and installed in a diverging beam of light, as shown in figure 1. During photogistration, a screen with a circular response with a diameter of 0.1 mm is installed in front of the photodetector.

The results of this experiment are reproduced in Figures 6 and 7, from which it is clearly seen that in the case of a ball, two diffraction patterns are also observed.
