6. To determine this, let $h$ be the height of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ drawn from the vertex $A$, and $d$ be the distance between edges $AB$ and $CD$. Let us draw through vertex $B$ a line $l$ parallel to edge $CD$, and through point $A$ a plane perpendicular to edge $CD$ and intersecting lines $l$ and $CD$ at points $E$ and $F$ (Fig.28). Then the heights $AH$ and $FG$ of triangle $AEF$ are $h$ and $d$ respectively. The third height $EK$ of the triangle $AEF$ is equal to one of the heights of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ and, therefore, not less than $h$. Since $AF \cdot EK = FE \cdot AH$, then $AF \leq FE$, and therefore

$$\frac{h}{d} = \frac{AH}{FG} = \frac{AE}{FE} < \frac{AF + FE}{FE} \leq 2.$$

8. Let us prove by induction on four $n$ that if given $n$ coins and it is known how many of them are counterfeit, then for $\left[\frac{3n}{4}\right]$ the weigher can determine all the counterfeit coins. For $n = 0$ and $n = 2$ the statement is true. Let $n \geq 4$.

Let's compare some two coins. If they differ in mass, then both will be immediately identified, and the problem is reduced to $(n - 2)$ coins, since $\left[\frac{3(n - 2)}{4}\right] + 1 \leq \left[\frac{3n}{4}\right]$, and the number of counterfeit coins among the remaining ones is known.

Let the coins weigh the same. Let's compare this pair of coins with another pair. If the pairs differ in weight, then, by comparing them with another pair, we identify all the even coins, and the problem is reduced to $(n - 4)$ coins. If the fours weigh the same, then we compare the entire measure of monet with another coin, and so on. In general, if at some step a group of $2^m$ identical coins differs in weight from another group of $2^m$ coins, then all $2^m$ coins of the first group are completely determined, and the problem is reduced to $(n - 2^m)$ coins, since $\left[\frac{3(n - 2^m)}{4}\right] + (m + 1) \leq \left[\frac{3n}{4}\right]$, $m \geq 2$. If it finally turns out that $2^m > \frac{n}{2}$, then all $2^m$ coins are completely determined.
