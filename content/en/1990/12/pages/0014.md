Let the planets move in a circle. Let us introduce the angular velocity of the planet’s rotation in orbit $\omega = 2\pi/T$. Then Kepler's third law will take the form:
$$\omega_1^2 R_1^3 = \omega_2^2 R_2^3 = \dots = \text{const.}$$

Let us now assume that the force of interaction between the planet and the Sun is proportional to a certain degree of distance:
$$F \sim R^n.$$
The same degree $R$ is proportional to the acceleration of the planet:
$$a = CR^n,$$
where $C$ is some constant. Since $a = \omega^2 R,$
$$\omega^2 = CR^{n-1}.$$
What must the power $n$ be for the product $\omega^2 R^3$ to be constant? Because
$$\omega^2 R^3 = CR^{n-1} R^3 = CR^{n+2},$$
then it is clear that the product $\omega^2 R^3$ does not depend on $R$ if $n = -2$. Then
$$\omega^2 R^3 = C.$$
Newton also suggested that the constant $C$ is proportional to the masses of the gravitating body, i.e. the Sun:
$$C = GM_{\odot}.$$
The constant $G$ is the gravitational constant, which, according to Newton’s assumption, no longer depends on any other quantity. So,
$$a = G \frac{M_{\odot}}{R^2}.$$
Consequently, the force imparting mass $m$ to the planet this acceleration, i.e. the force of interaction of the planet with the Sun, is equal to
$$F = G \frac{M_{\odot} m}{R^2}.$$
In this conclusion, however, the task is too simplified. In fact, it is a significant distance $R$, we reasoned as if the Sun were a material point that has no dimensions. But Newton understood that in order to check the law of gravity, for example, by the movement of the Moon, one must be able to solve the problem for a body of finite size, somehow summing up the contributions from different parts of the Earth and the Moon. The problem was difficult, and its solution was not obtained soon. This was probably the main reason why Newton waited so long to publish his discovery.

Newton presented the results of solving this complex problem in the form of theorems. For bodies of homogeneous density, the formula for the law is gravitated by other bodies and, according to the law of equality of action and reaction, gravitates other bodies itself as if all its mass is concentrated in the center.*) If you imagine a ball complex of spherical layers, then you can see how to calculate the gravitational field of a body with spherically symmetrical mass distribution. These Newton theorems decided the matter. Now, in the expression for the force acting between the Moon and the Earth, $R$ should be understood as the distance between the centers of these bodies. Replacing the real Earth and Moon with bodies with spherically symmetrical mass distributions turned out to be such a good approximation that only in recent years, when calculating satellite trajectories, it was necessary to refine the formulas.**)
