geek Kurt Gödel. To construct the required form, he assigns numbers to all forms and their sequence, according to which the forms are uniquely reconstructed (this is the manifestation of the ideas of object and sign: forms are objects, Gödel numbers are signs). The property of a form to be provable turns out to be recognizable by its number. After this, Gödel constructs forms that express his own unprovability. Of course, this is a very approximate description of Gödel’s work (I recommend on this occasion the replacement book by Yu. I. Manin “Provable and Unprovable”, M.: Radio, 1979). In English there is a book by D. Hofstadter “Gödel, Escher, Bach”, dedicated to Gödel’s theorem and illustrated with reproductions of Escher. (A translation into Russian will be published soon.) Gödel's theorem had the effect of a bomb exploding in the early 1930s, forcing mathematicians to abandon their belief in the omnipotence of the axiomatic method. Of all Escher's works, the best known are his ornaments, i.e., periodic filling of a plane with identical figures. During his trip to Spain, Escher carefully studied and sketched the ornaments in the Alhambra, made during the period of Moorish rule. Arab masters achieved perfection in the art of ornamentation. Islam prohibits the depiction of humans, animals, fish and birds (in accordance with the commandment: “Thou shalt not make an idol!”), so Muslim ornaments are composed of abstract geometric figures. Escher was very interested in the task of composing ornaments using real images as repeating elements. You can judge for yourself the results that he was able to achieve.

Escher's ornaments, of course, respond to the important mathematical idea of ​​periodicity (recently «Kvant» wrote about this: see the article “And the wind returns...”, or Periodicity in mathematics,” No. 4 for this year).
