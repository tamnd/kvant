Design design of the Mars Expeditionary Complex (MEC)

The NASA project (early 70s) proposed to carry out acceleration maneuvers near the Earth, braking maneuvers near Mars and a departure maneuver to the Earth using a nuclear rocket engine with a solid-phase working area, the best designation in the USA is “NERVA”. (In Soviet specialized literature, this nuclear rocket engine is called a nuclear-powered engine of scheme “A”.) The expeditionary complex developed by NASA included three unified large-scale rocket units with a NERVA nuclear-powered engine and liquid hydrogen as fuel. The rocket blocks were placed according to a package scheme (see Fig. 1), with the two outer comparative ones near the Earth, and the central one near Mars. Along the longitudinal axis of the central rocket block, an interplanetary orbital vehicle (IOC) and a Martian landing complex were located sequentially

The projects of the Mars expeditionary complex carried out in the same years in the USSR (NPO Energia, TsNIIMash) contained an electroderive rocket propulsion system (EYARDU), i.e., a “small” propulsion system, as a propulsion system. The layout scheme was proposed as “beam” (Fig. 2) and “T-shaped” (Fig. 3). The latter scheme provided for the creation on board in the living and instrument compartments of artificial gravity models (with a level of 0.2-0.3 of the earth's gravity level) by rotating the entire complex around a continuous axis.

In further design studies, it was proposed to include two electroderive rocket propulsion systems into the IEC, and place the interplanetary orbital ship and landing complex between them (see Fig. 4) in the shadow zone created by a special power supply located near the reactor.

In order to eliminate the possible adverse effects of ionizing radiation from the reactor when ships left the shadow zone in the 80s, NPO Energia was working on a layout diagram with a high-power solar power plant.
