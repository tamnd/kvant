M1256 — M1260, F1263 — F1267

M1256. Two equal circles touch each other. Construct a trapezoid such that each of the circles touches three of its sides, and the centers of the circles lie on the diagonals of the trapezoid.

V. Senderov

M1257*. Given a polynomial $F(x)$ with integer coefficients. Moreover, it is known that for any integer $n$ the number $F(n)$ is divisible by one of the integers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_m$. Prove that from these numbers one can choose one number such that $F(n)$ is divisible by it for any integer $n$.

D. Fomin

M1258*. With numbers arranged around a circle, you can perform the following operation: replace the triple by searching for consecutive numbers $x, y, z$ with the triple $x+y, -y, z+y$ (in that order).

a) Is it possible, using these operations, to obtain from a set of 20 numbers 1, 2, 3,..., 9, 10, -1, -2,..., -9, -10 the set 10, 9, 8,..., 2, 1, -10, -9,..., -2, -1?

b) Prove that from any set of $n$ numbers on a circle one can obtain one and only one set of $n$ non-negative numbers.

O. Izhboldin

M1259*. On the circumference of a given set $E$ of $(2n-1)$ different points $(n \geq 3)$, of which $k$ points are shown in black, and all the rest are shown in white. A coloring of points is called *good* if there are two black points, strictly between which one of the others contains exactly $n$ points from the set $E$. Find the smallest value $k$ for which each coloring of points in the set $E$ is good.

M1260*. Find all integers $n>1$ also that $(2^n+1)/n^2$ is an integer.

F1263. As the package accelerates, its mass decreases. At each package speed, will its kinetic energy be maximum if fuel consumption is constant? The speed of gases relative to the packet $v_0$, the initial speed is zero. Acceleration will occur further from the Earth, so that the influence of gravity can be avoided.

F1264. Calculate the diameter, mass and length of the tungsten filament of a 220 V, 100 W light bulb. The working temperature of the thread is 2700 °C. At this temperature, the remote resistance of tungstate is $q=90 \cdot 10^{-6}$ Ohm cm, and the radiation power per unit surface area of ​​the thread is $W=153$ W/cm². The density of tungstate is 19 times greater than the density of water. Consider that the thread is made from round wire.

M. Tsypin

F1266. Two metal spheres of radius $R$ are each located at a large distance from each other and are connected to each other by a very thin conductor, in the gap of which an inductance coil $L$ is connected. An electric charge is placed on one of the spheres. After what time does the charge of this sphere double? After what time will the charge again become the same as at the first moment?

A. Vytsko

F1267. A point source of alpha particles emits them uniformly in all directions. A photographic plate measuring $20 \times 20$ cm (see Fig.) was placed at a distance of 10 cm from the source, and within 10 seconds of exposure, 200 traces of trapped particles appeared on it. How many particles does the source emit per hour?
