Design design of the Mars Expeditionary Complex (MEC)

The NASA project (early 70s) proposed to carry out acceleration maneuvers near the Earth, braking maneuvers near Mars and a departure maneuver to the Earth using a nuclear rocket engine with a solid-phase working area, the best designation in the USA is “NERVA”. (In Soviet specialized literature, this nuclear rocket engine is called a nuclear-powered engine of scheme “A”.) The expeditionary complex developed by NASA included three unified large-scale rocket units with a NERVA nuclear-powered engine and liquid hydrogen as fuel. The rocket blocks were placed according to a package scheme (see Fig. 1), with the two outer comparative ones near the Earth, and the central one near Mars. Along the longitudinal axis of the central rocket block, an interplanetary orbital vehicle (IOC) and a Martian landing complex were located sequentially

Formation of IEC in satellite orbit
Let us give an example of the formation of an IEC in the installation orbit of an artificial Earth satellite for the service of organizing a rafted expedition according to a two-ship flight scheme.

The start date for transport operations to launch cargo into space is mid-2010. At this time, at the end of the orbit, two manned orbital stations are in orbit - the new generation Mir (USSR) and Freedom (USA), which in this case, in addition to traditional scientific and national economic tasks, are used as assembly and installation orbital complexes.
The first to be formed into the final orbit is an automatic interplanetary spacecraft, which will deliver the landing complex and a rocket unit with fuel for the return flight of the crew or only a tank with fuel into the orbit of the artificial satellite of Mars (ISM). The launch of ALS launch vehicles launches into space the landing complex (developed by the USA), the reentry rocket unit and the EYARDU (developed by the USSR). After receiving data on the successful launch of the landing kit into the ISM orbit, the formation of a rafted interplanetary spacecraft begins in the satellite orbit. Launches of the Energia launch vehicle sequentially deliver to the Mir orbital station packages of blocks for accelerating a set near the Earth with a nuclear propulsion system of the NERVA type, a package braking block for Mars (developed by the USSR) and a rafted interplanetary spacecraft (developed by the USSR with the participation of countries of the world community). Some models are assembled on a special machine at the Mir station. After this, an international crew of 8 people representing different continents is delivered to the station using the Zenit carrier package in transport spacecraft (Soyuz type). Astronauts take jobs in an interplanetary spacecraft. The Mars Expeditionary Complex separates from the Mir station and begins its independent flight in near-Earth orbit. (For the formation of the IEC, 3-4 launches of the Energia launch vehicle and one launch of the Zenit launch vehicle will be required; all operations will take about 5 months.)

But then the propulsion systems turn on, the overload increases, and the expeditionary complex rushes towards Mars. Ahead lies the flight along the Earth-Mars route, braking near Mars, docking with an automatic ship, landing on Mars, two weeks of work on the Red Planet, takeoff from Mars, departure to Earth...

A year and a half after the launch of the rafted interplanetary spacecraft from Earth, it returns to its space home. The lander lands in the Kazakh steppe. The year on the calendar is 2013.

The task is completed. New flights are ahead!
