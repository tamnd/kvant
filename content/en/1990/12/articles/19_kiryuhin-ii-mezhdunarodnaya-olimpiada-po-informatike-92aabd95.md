II International Olympiad in Computer Science

Candidate of Technical Sciences
V. KIRYUKHIN

A year has passed quickly, and now the capital of Belarus, Minsk, Gostinino has opened its doors to the participants of the II International Olympiad in Informatics (MOI). Here, from July 15 to July 21, 1990, schoolchildren from around the world gathered to compare their skills in the field of computer science.

The interest in this Olympics was unusual. If last year 13 countries took part in the International Olympic Games, then this year there were already 24 of them: Argentina, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Greece, Spain, Italy, China, North Korea, Cuba, Kuwait, Morocco, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Romania, USSR, Germany, Czechoslovak Republic, Sweden and Yugoslavia. Observers from Tanland were also present. Each team consisted of no more than 4 participants, although not all teams competed in full.

As the organizing country, the Soviet Union was represented by two teams: the USSR national team and the Belarusian team. Based on the results of their performance at the last All-Union Olympiad in Computer Science and based on the results of participation in winter and summer camps, the following were included in the USSR team: Rein Varblanet, a graduate of the S.S. No. 1 p. New Tarssky district; Georgiy Daguashvili, graduate of the secondary school. No. 25 Tbilisi; Yuri Zaitsev, graduate of the secondary school. No. 57 Kyiv; Dmitry Kozlov, graduate of the secondary school. No. 566 Leningrad.

The Belarusian team included: Serey Linev, a graduate of the school. No. 35 Minsk; Alexander Naumov, 10th grade student at S.S. No. 36 Minsk; Oleg Taborovets, graduate of the secondary school. No. 1 Pinsk; Valery Khamenya, 10th grade student. No. 1 Grodno.

The leader of the USSR team was the author of this article, the leader of the Belarus team was BSU Associate Professor V. M. Kotov.

The organizers of the Olympiad, especially the Ministry of Public Education of the BSSR, did everything possible to ensure that this event remained in the memory of all participants for a long time. The opening of the Olympics was memorable. The first deputy chairman of the USSR State Committee for Public Education V.D. Shadrikov, the chairman of the international jury of the Olympiad, academician of the USSR Academy of Sciences N.N. Krasovsky, and academician of the BSSR Academy of Sciences V.A. Labunov spoke at it. The 2nd International Olympiad in Computer Science was opened by the Minister of Public Education of the BSSR M. I. Demchuk.

The Olympiad differs from other Olympiads in the unusual complexity of its holding. Selection of tasks for improvement and their verification, provision of sites with personal computers, maintenance of computer equipment in working order, and taking into account the fact that sites can use their own software, protection from viruses - all this required the intensive work of all services of the Olympiad. Compared to the 1st MYI, the volume of this work doubled, since the current Olympiad was held in two rounds.

Before each round, the international jury selected one problem from a package of problems prepared by the scientific committee of the Olympiad.

The amount of work turned out to be quite large. For the guys who excelled in this type, the program listing in Pascal was 7-10 pages. According to the results of the first type, participants D. Kozlov and G. Datushvili took 4th and 5th places. In general, the grades for solving the first problem were not high. Only about 20% of participants scored 35 points or higher.

The second type of problem turned out to be more accessible to the participants, but caused a lot of controversy among the members of the international jury both when answering questions from participants in the process of solving it, and when evaluating the results. What is special is the fact that among the three winners of this type was the representative of the USSR national team D. Kozlov, who received the maximum number of points - 95 (the jury did not award five bonus points to the winner).

Based on the results of the official personal test, the participants of the II IOR were awarded 8 first-degree diplomas, 10 second-degree diplomas and 12 third-degree diplomas. Soviet schoolchildren received: G. Datushvili and D. Kozlov (USSR team) - 1st degree diplomas, O. Taborovets (BSSR team) - 2nd degree diploma, Yu. Zaitsev (USSR team) and S. Linev (BSSR team) - 3rd degree diplomas. R. Varbana, the fourth member of the USSR national team, lacked only one point to receive a third degree diploma.

International Olympiads are individual competitions, however, the results of team performances are unofficially summed up. This year, the results of the top five teams were: Bulgaria - 427 points, China - 423 points, USSR - 420 points, Germany - 331 points, Hungary - 307 points.

The BSSR team scored 234 points.

The Olympiad program was very intense. For example, the participants couldn’t cope until summing up the results. And yet, II MY resulted in a wonderful holiday. The colorfully organized closing of the Olympics took place in the city's Palace of the Pinners. And I don’t feel at all
I felt like a loser there, since everyone was united by a love of information and a desire to communicate with peers from other countries.

Many warm words were said at the closing of the International Olympics by both the participants and the organizers and guests of the Olympiad, in particular the official representative of UNESCO E. Jacobson. The results of the Olympiad were summed up and the winner was announced by the First Deputy Minister of Public Education of the BSSR L.K. Sukhnat.

There were a lot of prizes. In addition to the wonderful pictures that the winners of the Olympics received, original recognition was awarded to the winner of individual rounds, recognition for the best result among girls, recognition for the youngest participant and participant, and others. The lucky Belarusian schoolchild (established by the Belarusian branch of the International Combinatorial Club - a monthly scholarship of 200 rubles) received O. Taborovets. At the end of the closing ceremony of the II MY, the representative of Greece invited all countries to take part in the next international Olympiad according to information, which is in September 1991 in Athens.

Soviet schoolchildren have made a significant step forward compared to the previous Olympics. Of course, “houses and walls help,” but one cannot help but notice that the Olympic information movement in our country is gaining strength. We congratulate the Soviet Olympiad participants and their teachers on their successful performance, and wish future Olympians success at the next Olympiads.
