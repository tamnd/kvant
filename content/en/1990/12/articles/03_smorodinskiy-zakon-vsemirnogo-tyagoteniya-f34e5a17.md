In modern physics there are constants that are called Fundamentals. Among them are the speed of light $c$, electron charge $e$, electron mass $m$, gravitational constant $G$, Planck constant $h$. These constants are included in formulas that describe physical processes in the microcosm and in outer space. The fundamentality of these constants is that their values ​​do not change either with time or with place in space.
Wherever we change these constants, we will always get the same values. Physicists know that these constants have not changed in any significant way over the billions of years of evolution of the Universe.

The ancient Greeks created the first theory of planetary motion. This theory was expounded by Ptolemy in his treatise Almagest. But it did not touch at all on the question of why the planet moves. Such a question never arose. Following the teachings of Aristotle, the Greeks believed that planets tend to move in a circle, just as heavy bodies tend to fall, and light ones, like smoke, rise up. Further, in the Middle Ages, discussing the principles of planetary motion meant mixing astronomy with physics, which, according to the ideas of that time, was not permissible; the laws of heaven and earth belonged to different areas, and one should not look for anything common in them.

Nevertheless, Copernicus in 1543 said that heavy is a common property of bodies, “... the desire due to which they, closing in the shape of a ball, form a single whole. And following

Kepler (and before Kepler also Hilbert) compared gravity with magnetic action. At the same time, Kepler was the first to declare the problem of planetary motion a physical problem, and the first to clearly pose the question of why the planets move, while all other astronomers only came up with geometric models that described how the planets move. Physicists of the times of Galileo and Kepler solved physical problems by drawing up proportions. Thus, about the division of bodies on Earth, it was said that if time intervals are arranged in the form of an arithmetic progression, then the segments of the path traversed by the body over successive periods of time will be related as the squares of successive numbers. Galileo knew that all bodies on Earth fall with the same acceleration; he could well explain why a body thrown to Earth flies in a parabola. Describing the flight of a projectile, he said that this movement consists of a uniformly accelerated fall and a uniform near-lightning motion due to inertia, and did not ask the question of why the projectile falls at a uniform acceleration. It was very difficult to come to the conclusion that acceleration depends on altitude. Galileo believed that even the Moon (if its forward motion is left) will fall to the Earth with the same acceleration with which a stone falls on Earth.

True, Galileo believed that the “earthly” law of independence of the acceleration of free fall from mass must also be fulfilled for the Moon. So he understood “half” of the problem correctly! Let the planets move in a circle. Let us introduce the angular velocity of the planet’s rotation in orbit $\omega = 2\pi/T$. Then Kepler's third law will take the form:
$$\omega_1^2 R_1^3 = \omega_2^2 R_2^3 = \dots = \text{const.}$$

Let us now assume that the force of interaction between the planet and the Sun is proportional to a certain degree of distance:
$$F \sim R^n.$$
The same degree $R$ is proportional to the acceleration of the planet:
$$a = CR^n,$$
where $C$ is some constant. Since $a = \omega^2 R,$
$$\omega^2 = CR^{n-1}.$$
What must the power $n$ be for the product $\omega^2 R^3$ to be constant? Because
$$\omega^2 R^3 = CR^{n-1} R^3 = CR^{n+2},$$
then it is clear that the product $\omega^2 R^3$ does not depend on $R$ if $n = -2$. Then
$$\omega^2 R^3 = C.$$
Newton also suggested that the constant $C$ is proportional to the masses of the gravitating body, i.e. the Sun:
$$C = GM_{\odot}.$$
The constant $G$ is the gravitational constant, which, according to Newton’s assumption, no longer depends on any other quantity. So,
$$a = G \frac{M_{\odot}}{R^2}.$$
Consequently, the force imparting mass $m$ to the planet this acceleration, i.e. the force of interaction of the planet with the Sun, is equal to
$$F = G \frac{M_{\odot} m}{R^2}.$$
In this conclusion, however, the task is too simplified. In fact, it is a significant distance $R$, we reasoned as if the Sun were a material point that has no dimensions. But Newton understood that in order to check the law of gravity, for example, by the movement of the Moon, one must be able to solve the problem for a body of finite size, somehow summing up the contributions from different parts of the Earth and the Moon. The problem was difficult, and its solution was not obtained soon. This was probably the main reason why Newton waited so long to publish his discovery.

Newton presented the results of solving this complex problem in the form of theorems. For bodies of homogeneous density, the formula for the law is gravitated by other bodies and, according to the law of equality of action and reaction, gravitates other bodies itself as if all its mass is concentrated in the center.*) If you imagine a ball complex of spherical layers, then you can see how to calculate the gravitational field of a body with spherically symmetrical mass distribution. These Newton theorems decided the matter. Now, in the expression for the force acting between the Moon and the Earth, $R$ should be understood as the distance between the centers of these bodies. Replacing the real Earth and Moon with bodies with spherically symmetrical mass distributions turned out to be such a good approximation that only in recent years, when calculating satellite trajectories, it was necessary to refine the formulas.**)

Now the most important thing could be proven. Prove that from the law of universal gravitation it follows that the trajectories of planets will be ellipses, and the trajectories of comets will be hyperbolas.

The need for such a proof was clearly formulated in 1684 during a conversation between three English physicists - Galday, Wren and Hooke. Ren even announced a prize—a kiniga, which he gave to the one who solved the problem within two months. The prize was never awarded. In May, Halley proposed this problem to Newton, who sent him the necessary proof six months later.

In the Principia, Newton sets out the solution to the inverse problem: he finds what the law of interaction must be for the trajectory to be an ellipse, and shows that it must be the law of “inverse squares”. Moreover, Newton proves that if the law of forces acting between the planet and the Sun deviated even slightly from the inverse square law, then the points of the planet's furthest and furthest distance from the Sun would not be stationary in space. And from the observer it is known that these points (they are called apses) are almost motionless. This Newtonian proof is one of the most brilliant in astronomy.

Let us once again draw attention to what has been said: it can be argued that the inverse square law for attractive forces follows from the mere fact of the immobility of apses; so calculating the force of gravity using Kepler's third law is unnecessary; in fact, the third law is a consequence of the first. For many years, Newton's theory was the basis of celestial mechanics, and although many tried to discover any amendments to the law of universal gravitation, only Einstein was able to find them. Now we know when the law of universal gravitation becomes impure, and we are able to calculate amendments to it. The law of gravity is true as long as the distance between bodies is much greater than the so-called gravitational radius of the attracting body. The gravitational radius of a body with mass $M$ is the quantity

$$R_{\text{rp}} = \frac{2GM}{c^2},$$

where $GM=C$ is the “Kepler constant” already known to us, and $c$ is the speed of light. For the Sun, the gravitational radius is 3 km, and for the Earth - 7 mm. From here we can conclude that the law of universal gravitation on the surface of the Earth may have a relative error equal to approximately $\frac{7 \cdot 10^{-3}M}{6.4 \cdot 10^6M} \approx 10^{-9}$ (radius of the Earth $\approx 6400$ km), and on the surface of the Sun $-\frac{3 \cdot 10^3M}{7 \cdot 10^8M} \approx 0,4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ (radius of the Sun $\approx 700000$ km).
