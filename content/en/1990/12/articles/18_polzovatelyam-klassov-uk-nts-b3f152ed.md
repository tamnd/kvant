$$n_{2k} \leq n_{2k+1} \leq n_{2k}^2.$$

a) A has a winning strategy;
b) B has a winning strategy;
b) neither A nor B has a winning strategy.

In the second problem, it was necessary to change the ratio of magnetic fields (more precisely, magnetic field inductions) in the gaps of two magnets of the same shape. An aluminum disk on an axle, a thread, two weights of different masses, a stopwatch and a ruler were proposed as measuring instruments. The method of change was clear - wind the thread around the axle, attach a load to it and let the disk spin. In this case, the magnet must be positioned so that the edge of the disk falls into the gap - then output currents (Foucault currents) will arise in the disk and a braking torque will appear. The participant must agree (and be sure to give it) that the braking torque is proportional to the square of the magnetic induction. By combining weights and changing the speed of steady motion, it was possible to find the ratio of the magnetic fields. This experiment required great care, but the result was very accurate. The presence of two weights made it possible to take into account the friction force in the axle. This method of estimating the ratio of magnetic fields is quite simple, and this installation can be repeated.
