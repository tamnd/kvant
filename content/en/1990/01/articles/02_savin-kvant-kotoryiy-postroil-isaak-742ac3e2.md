Scientists always have a very important task - to look for talent and help them quickly develop. The first Japanese “tool” in the field of physical and mathematical sciences was school Olympiads. In the 30s, some institutes began to hold them in Moscow, Leningrad, Novosibirsk, and in the 60s, the Olympiads acquired a general scale.

Physics and mathematics school integrators, evening and correspondence schools appeared. And finally, a new stage - the first scientific magazine for schoolchildren. The idea of ​​its publication arose in the 60s and belonged to young scientists who had extensive experience working with schoolchildren. It was a time of a surge of interest in physics and mathematics, caused by successes in space, the rapid development of nuclear physics, solid state physics, and computers.

Why was the magazine created? This can be judged by reading a letter to the CPSU Central Committee from six academicians, outstanding Soviet physicists and mathematicians, who supported the idea of ​​young scientists. The letter is published with abbreviations.

It is known that science develops successfully only when research institutes are replenished with well-selected talented youth. In order for this turnover to be most successful, it is necessary, already from school, to perceive in her those basic traits that are necessary for active scientific work. These traits are: creative imagination, courage and love of discovery. Developing them fully now is an impossible task for our school. Moreover, only a relatively small part of our schoolchildren will require such a perception. In order to cover, as far as possible, all our schoolchildren, it is necessary to create a special magazine. In contrast to our existing good popular science magazines for young people, such as “Technology for Youth”, “Knowledge is Power”, “Science and Life”, the magazine offered should be its main task of developing in schoolchildren a creative interest in science and an active perception of knowledge and the ability to connect theory with practice. Although such a perception should be arbitrary in all areas of the natural sciences, now we are introducing science with the publication of a physical and mathematical journal.

This magazine should be intended to guide and systematically assist students in independent work in physics and mathematics. He must direct the out-of-class work carried out in the subject clubs of the school and Puner houses, establish direct contact with the student...

To achieve this goal, the journal will have to publish general material accumulated in physics and mathematics schools and circles at the leading universities of the country, the most interesting and important tasks of physics and mathematics Olympiads, as well as all the best that appears on these issues in the ruined press. The magazine will also provide an opportunity for schoolchildren to publish their articles, assignments and descriptions of the devices that they themselves build...

A group of young scientists should be created at the journal, carefully interacting both with schoolchildren and with their records, and in this way directing and stimulating the independent work of scientists from school. The magazine may have a group of inspector-consultants who can guide school clubs and guide their work. The journal should be guided

Why did the idea of ​​creating a scientific journal for schoolchildren find support among these scientists? Academicians P. L. Kapitsa, I. K. Kikosh and I. V. Obretsmov are physicists trained at the famous Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute under the leadership of Academician A. F. Ioffe. Their teacher sought to involve young people in science right from their student days. It is not for nothing that his institute was jokingly called “Ioffe’s kindergarten.” This wonderful tradition is described in the article by I. K. Kikoshna “How Soviet Physics was Created,” published in issues 10–12 of our magazine in 1977. (By the way, Kikosh carried out his first gray scientific research while still a student.) A similar tradition developed in Moscow in the scientific school of the outstanding mathematician N. N. Luzin, the famous “Luzitani”, from which academicians P. S. Aleksadrov, A. N. Kolmogorov and M. A. Lavrentiev emerged. P. S. Aleksadrov's story about this school was published in «Kvant» No. 10 for 1977.

The scientists' request was granted - in August 1969, a decision was made to create the journal «Kvant». And at the beginning of 1970, readers received its first issue. Academician Isaac Konstantinovich Kikosh became the editor-in-chief, and academician Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov became the first deputy editor-in-chief. Both of them performed these functions until the last days of their lives. It is difficult to overestimate their contribution to the creation of «Kvant», to which they paid great attention - they determined the structure, selected the editorial team, looked for authors, carefully read the articles, and first wrote for the magazine themselves.

Why did the magazine get this name? The idea that electronic energy is emitted and absorbed in separate portions - quanta - belongs to the famous German physicist Max Planck. She continued on the path to the creation of quantum mechanics, a new field of physics that revolutionized the capabilities of this science. The creators of the magazine hoped that “the appearance of the new magazine will also cause... a noticeable leap forward in bringing schoolchildren closer to modern science.”

I. K. Kikosh.

(this is exactly what is said in the appeal to readers who open the first issue «Kvant»). And the emblem of the solution was to use the symbol h, called “Planck’s constant,” which is invariably present in many branches of quantum physics.

We immediately found out that writing articles for our journal is very difficult - after all, they must be scientifically reliable and at the same time accessible to schoolchildren. If the author of the article is a scientist, he, as a rule, is not sufficiently familiar with school curricula and textbooks, but if he is a teacher, then it is difficult for him to imagine the cutting edge of modern science. Many of our authors are active scientists, but articles in «Kvant» are usually the product of a collective effort, with contributions from members of the editorial collection and the journal's editorial staff.

The modern appearance of the magazine did not immediately take shape, although its main sections - general articles, “Problem «Kvant»”, “Laboratory «Kvant»”, “Practicum for applicants”, “Mathematical circle”, “Olympiads”, “Information” - have been with us since the first issues.

Later, the headings “School in «Kvant»”, “Information and Programming”, “Games and Puzzles”, «Kvant» Smiles” and some others appeared. Last year, two more sections appeared - “R is for Rocket” (talking about the problems of studying and developing outer space) and “Got an idea?!” (dedicated to physics, mathematics and heuristics). Of course, a magazine is not a textbook; it is not at all necessary to read it “from cover to cover,” but many difficult articles and tasks can be returned later.

We strive to ensure that a student who subscribes to «Kvant» has the opportunity not only to read an interesting article, but also to independently work on its pages. As it was said in the very first issue, “problems must be solved, read with paper and pencil in hands, those described by experience must be tried to be reproduced.” Therefore, from the very first issues, many different problems are published in «Kvant». These are problems in sections «Kvant» for younger schoolchildren, “Selected school problems”, “Practicum for applicants”, questions occupied, tasks experienced in “Kaleidoscope «Kvant»”, tasks for university entrance exams... But especially important for us is the competitive section “Problem «Kvant»”, which allows for constant correspondence with readers who are actively working with the magazine. Those who successfully cope with this task are awarded «Kvant» diplomas, and the winners of the competition are immediately invited to the Resource Olympiads. Last year, schoolchildren from among the winners of this competition took part in the Soviet-American summer physics and mathematics schools, which were held in the USA and the USSR. There are similar checks for schoolchildren this year.

Twenty years in a person’s life is a considerable period, and during this time many of our readers have firmly entered into science and have become

This report was read by Academician Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa at the general meeting of the USSR Academy of Sciences in September 1943, 5 years after the discovery of supercurrent liquid helium. At the end of the report, the scientist spoke about some of the problems facing experimenters and theorists studying this phenomenon. These problems have now been resolved. The existence of temperature waves similar to sound ones (second sound) in supercurrent helium has been experimentally confirmed; The study of the critical velocities of current supercurrent helium led to the discovery of an amazing phenomenon - macrosoptic quantum vortices. Some of the numerical data that the author cited in the report have been refined by mid-day. However, we have decided not to make corresponding changes to the original text.
