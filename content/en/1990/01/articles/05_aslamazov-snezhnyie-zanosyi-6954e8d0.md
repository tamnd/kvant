Sections of railways or highways that pass through hollows are covered with snow, even if there is no snowfall. Why is this happening? At first glance, the answer is clear: snow is carried by the wind. However, to understand in detail the mechanism of this process, a whole study was required.

In 1936, the English physicist Bangold studied the transport of sand by wind in an aerodynamic tube.

It turned out that if the wind speed is less than a certain speed $v_1$, then the sand does not move. When the wind speed is greater than $v_1$, not less than some other speed $v_2$, the sand can also remain at rest. But if at the same time there is no caveman in the motionless mass of sand, several other cavemen are blown up by its blow. These sandmen are carried away by the wind, fall, set other sandmen in motion, and as a result the sand is carried along the wind. At a wind speed greater than $v_2$, the sandmen rise, forming a sand-air flow of fairly high density, but decreasing upward. figure 1 gives an idea of ​​the trajectories of the cavemen.

Now we can explain why there is snow in the wind

Well this gets notches. At the point of deepening, the flow expands (this immediately becomes visible from the picture of the linear current at figure 2), and therefore its speed decreases. As a result, the balance between the particles carried upward and the particles falling down is disrupted: more particles fall into the depression than rise from it, and the depression is gradually filled with snow.

Similar processes occur when snow, carried by the wind, encounters some obstacle on its way, such as a tree. In front of the tree trunk, on the side from which the wind blows, an upward movement of air occurs.
