XXIII Vesesoyuzny Olympiad
in mathematics
1. Let $(N, M)$ be a pair of numbers obtained from the pair $(1,1)$ using permitted transformations. We can reconstruct its entire prehistory, since the pair $(a, b)$, where $a \geq b$, can only be obtained from the pair $(a - b, b)$. By performing the reverse process, we will sooner or later obtain the pair $(1,1)$ and can calculate the required number of transformations. The pair $(N, M)$ can be obtained from the pair $(1,1)$ only if $N$ and $M$ are coprime. Otherwise, doing the reverse process, we remain on the pair $(L, 0)$ where $L > 1$. The pairs $(N, i)$ and $(N, N - i)$ give, after inverse transformation, the same pair $(N - i, i)$.
After these replacements, it is clear how the search is carried out: for each pair $(N, i)$, where $1 \leq i < [N/2]$, it is necessary to perform all the inverse transformations and compare the numbers of these transformations.
