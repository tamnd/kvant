In the same way, for the set $R^3$ of all sets $(x, y, z)$ of three real numbers, the distance between the points $(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2, z_2)$ can be given by any of the formulas

$$\sqrt{(x_1 - x_2)^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2 + (z_1 - z_2)^2},$$

$$|x_1 - x_2| + |y_1 - y_2| + |z_1 - z_2|,$$

or

$$\max \{|x_1 - x_2|, |y_1 - y_2|, |z_1 - z_2|\}.$$

In the first case, we get a general three-dimensional space, which is studied by school stepeometry and which is a convenient abstraction of the real space in which we live. All similar $m$-dimensional spaces are also useful - with their help it is convenient to construct a theory of functions of $m$ variables, and sometimes it is more convenient to use one formula for distance, sometimes another.
