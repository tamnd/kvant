It has long been accepted that it is more useful to get acquainted with different solutions to one problem than with similar solutions to different problems. However, among the theorems that, like many other classical theorems of elementary geometry, admit of several respectable proofs, is

Theorem on medians of a triangle. Mediana $AA_1$, $BB_1$ and $CC_1$ of triangle $ABC$ intersect at some point $M$, and each of them is divided by this point in the ratio 2:1, counting from the vertex: $AM:MA_1 = BM:MB_1 = CM:MC_1 = 2$. (1)

In all the proofs given below, except the sixth, we establish only that the median $BB_1$ passes through the point $M$, which divides the median $AA_1$ in the ratio 2:1.

If, in accordance with the reasoning, we replace the segment $BB_1$ with the segment $CC_1$, then we obtain that $CC_1$ also passes through $M$. This will prove that all three medians intersect at some point $M$, and $AM:MA_1 = 2$. Since all medians are equal, we can replace $AA_1$ with $BB_1$ or $CC_1$; sludge flows out (1).
