2. An uncrumpled sheet of paper is a “developing surface”
Since the requirement not to wrinkle the paper is so important, let's see what its mathematical meaning is.

It is easy to understand that the prohibition on crumpling paper significantly limits the ability to manipulate a sheet of paper. For example, a sheet of paper can be rolled into a tube or folded in half without creasing, but cannot be folded into four. You can make a cone (“funtik”) from a sheet of paper without crumpling it, but you cannot make a sphere or even a piece of it: press the sheet of paper against the globe, and folds will appear. As you can see, a sheet of paper cannot be given any shape.

Mathematicians call surfaces that can be made from a sheet of paper by bending it without crushing it, developing it. Of course, in mathematics, developing surfaces are defined differently: in mathematical language there are no words “paper”, “crumple”, “make”. There is a whole theory of developing surfaces, among the achievements of which is a satisfactory answer to the question of what they can be: mathematicians call this “classification” (the answer belongs to Leonhard Euler).
