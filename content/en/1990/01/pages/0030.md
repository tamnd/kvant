In physics, it often happens that it is much easier to study some limiting cases (for us these are $\lambda \rightarrow 0$ and $\lambda \rightarrow \infty$), but the “intermediate region” requires greater conditions. Fortunately, this area is unimportant for us, because, looking at Roerich’s painting, we can reliably say that circular waves have a length clearly greater than $\lambda_*$, and, therefore, their speed is described by expression (2). To find it, you just need to “measure” $\lambda$. We will carry out the calculations below, but for now we will take one more step.

Let us remember that when a water strider beetle runs quickly through water or a bullet flies in the air at the speed of sound, it always forms characteristic “whiskers”, which are more closely pressed to the moving body, the greater its speed $v$ compared to the speed $u$ of the propagation of disturbances caused by the body. On figure 6 three characteristic cases are given: if $v < u$, the waves burn the body and only the dough is skimmed in the direction of movement; if $v = u$, then the wave crests at one point (in the direction of movement) creep onto each other; if $v > u$, then the body is burned by the waves and forms “whiskers”.

Looking at Roerich's painting, we see that it depicts the first case; Moreover, since there is not even a hint of rolling waves in the direction of movement of the ship, we must conclude that its speed is much less than the speed of the waves:

$$v \ll u.$$ (4)

Thus, from the point of view of the surface of circular waves, the ship is simply “sharpening in place.”

Now let's start with the calculations. To do this, we need to “measure” the height of the bow wave $h$, the angle $2\alpha$ and the wave surface length $\lambda$.
