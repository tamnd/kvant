I thought $\lambda = \sqrt{3}$, and it doesn’t surprise me that this cannot be proven.

The point is this. The proof of Theorem 1 leaves unused one important property of our Möbius strip - its lack of self-intersections. A self-intersecting tape cannot be made from paper, but you can imagine it: like a self-intersecting line on a plane, it “passes through itself,” and you can divide it into parts, each of which does not have self-intersections.

Let's assume that, speaking of paper Möbius strips, we allowed them to have self-intersections from the very beginning. Then $\lambda$ takes on a new meaning - the new value $\lambda$ will be less than or equal to the previous one. In this case, Theorem 1 remains true, and not a single word is changed in its proof: the absence of self-intersection is not used in this proof. As for Theorem 2, if self-intersections are allowed, it can be significantly improved. Exactly:

Theorem 3. A Möbius strip with self-intersections can be glued together from a strip of any length greater than $\pi/2$.

It's done like this. Take a sufficiently large odd $n$ and construct a regular $n$-gon inscribed in a circle of diameter 1. Consider, further, $n$ triangles containing the center of the circle, each of which is bounded by a side and two diagonals of the $n$-gon (Fig. 13; here $n = 7$). These triangles cover our $n$-gon, its non-tortic places - several times. Let us now apply these $n$-gons to each other as shown in figure 14, after which we cut off half of the leftmost triangle along the length of the median and apply it to the rightmost triangle (Fig. 14). The result will be a rectangular strip with a ratio of length to width greater than $\pi/2$ and tending to $\pi/2$ with $n$ tending to $\infty$ (the width tends to 1, and the length to $\pi/2$). If you sequentially bend this strip along all the lines drawn on it, through the directions of the stib (Fig. 15), then the triangles will be positioned as in figure 13 (take the bottom ones and paper again and do this). Segments $AB$ and $CD$ will almost coincide - there will only be a few layers of folded paper between them. In this “almost alignment” point $A$ will align with $D$, and point $B$ with $C$, so if we could “pass the tape through ourselves” and glue $AB$ with $CD$, then we would get a Möbius strip. If you take a length of the tape, you can avoid folds, just as we did in the proof of Theorem 2. I tried to depict what happens on figure 16.

Thus, if we wanted (for tapes without self-intersections) to prove that $\lambda \geq \mu$, where $\mu > \sqrt{3}$, we would have to take into account the absence of self-intersections in the proof.
