Sixth proof (grade 10). Let us draw a plane $\alpha$ through the points $A$ and $B$, not containing $C$, and construct a regular triangle $ABC'$ (Fig. 5) in this plane. From the general properties of parallel projection along a line, $CC'$ takes $\triangle ABC$ to $\triangle ABC'$, and the medians of the triangle $ABC$ project into the medians of the triangle $ABC'$. But in a regular triangle, the medians are also bisectors, and therefore intersect at one point. It is also easy to prove (prove!) that for the triangle $ABC'$ their parameters are valid, we obtain that the center of all three masses lies on each of the medians. The center of a system of equal masses placed at some points is called the center of this set of points, therefore the point of intersection of the medians of a triangle is often called its center. More details about the application and justification of the center of mass method can be read in the recently published book by M. B. Balk and V. G. Boltyansky “Geometry of Masses” (M.: Nauka, 1988).
