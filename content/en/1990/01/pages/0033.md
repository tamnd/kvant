Variations on an old theme

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
D. FUCHS

This article brings us back to a wonderful representative of one-sided surfaces - the Möbius strip. It turns out from a narrow strip of paper twisted 180° and glued into a prickly shape. Here we discuss the question of which strip is suitable for making a model of a Möbius strip.

I will not suggest that you run your finger along the Möbius strip or cut it along the midline. We are talking about things less known. I'll start with a question:

1. What formula should you take for a strip of paper to glue a Möbius strip?

Expected answer: the strip should be narrow and long, with the greatest possible length-to-width ratio. Let’s say we can’t make a Möbius strip from a square sheet.

This is true, but with one caveat that is easy to underestimate: size restrictions only matter if the paper is prohibited from being wrinkled. If crumpling the paper is not prohibited, then a Möbius strip can be glued not only from a square, but from a rectangle of any size - the glued sides can even be angularly times the length of the non-glued ones.

You can do it like this (Fig. 1). Fold the straight sheet into an accordion and bend it an even number of times. Then from this accordion, like from a thick strip of paper, we glue a Möbius strip, placing the corresponding parts of the accordion into each other. From figure 1 it is clear that the sheet of paper from which the Möbius strip was glued turned out to be crumpled.

Let us now assume that the paper strip can be bent, but not wrinkled. Moreover, the width of the strip is per unit. It is clear that the longer the strip, the easier it is to glue a Möbius strip from it. Thus, there is a number λ such that a Möbius strip can be glued together from a strip of length greater than λ, but not from a strip of length less than λ (we are not interested in what will happen for a strip whose length is exactly equal to λ). I would really like to find this λ.

Surprisingly, the solution to this problem is still unknown.
