Probably millions of games have already been played there, but a winning strategy has not yet been found. But in a game with pebbles, you can come up with more and more new rules of the game, and finding a winning strategy is no less, if not more, interesting than finding a solution to an intricate chess stage.

Try to find who and how will win if played correctly - the starter or his partner - in the following games:

1. There are two piles of 10 stones each. Two people take turns taking stones from each pile (not necessarily from the same one every time). The one who takes the last stones wins.

2. The conditions are the same as in the previous game, but the player is prohibited from taking such a number of stones that the piles end up with the same number of stones.

3. There are two piles of 9 candies on the table. Each of the two players must first transfer one candy from one pile to another, and then eat two candies from one pile. The one who cannot make his next move loses.

4. There are two piles of peeled nuts on the table, one with 7 and the other with 6 nuts. Two players take turns. The move is that the player eating nuts in one of the piles, and divides the other into two parts (any). If he cannot divide this pile into two parts because there is only one nut in it, then he eats and wins.
