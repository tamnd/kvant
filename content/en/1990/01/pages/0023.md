In the same way, in the general case, we take for the distance between the functions $f_1, f_2$, defined on the segment $[a, b]$, the quantities

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = \max_{a \leqslant x \leqslant b} |f_1(x) - f_2(x)|.$$

Check that the axioms $1^\circ - 3^\circ$ are satisfied here! Moreover, at a distance not greater than $r$ from this function there is also a function whose graphs lie in a strip of width $2r$ around the graph of the function $f$ (Fig. 4).

Distances are also very often used:

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = s(f_1, f_2),$$

where $s$ is the size of the area enclosed between graphs $f_1$ and $f_2$ (Fig. 5), and especially this:

$$\rho(f_1, f_2) = \sqrt{S(f_1, f_2)},$$

where $S(f_1, f_2)$ is the area enclosed between the graph of the function $y = (f_1(x) - f_2(x))^2$ and the axis $Ox$.

Distance (7) is small when the values ​​of the functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ are close for all values ​​of the argument, and distances (8) and (9) show that the functions $f_1$ and $f_2$ are close “on average” (on small segments they can differ significantly from each other). Let, for example, $f_1(t)$ and $f_2(t)$ be the potentials of two specific points of the electric circuit at time $t$; then the power released in the circuit section between these points during the time interval $a \leqslant t \leqslant b$ is proportional to $S(f_1, f_2)$ (if the resistance is constant, the power is proportional to $(f_1(t) - f_2(t))^2$). And if it is important for us that the voltage $f_1(t) - f_2(t)$ does not always exceed some value $V$, then we must estimate the distance using formula (7): it is necessary that the value $\max_{a \leqslant t \leqslant b} |f_1(t) - f_2(t)|$ does not exceed $V$.

Note that we do not give precise formulations here of what “the area between two graphs” means, and we do not discuss for which functions the distance (7)–(9) can be introduced. All these subtleties are removed a lot of space in textbooks of mathematical analysis and functional analysis.

A few definitions from the analysis

Let us present several concepts from mathematical analysis that can be defined in any metric space $X$.

Definition 1. Let $\varepsilon$ be a positive number. A ball of radius $\varepsilon$ with center at point $C$ from $X$ is the set of points $M$ for which $\rho(M, C) \leqslant \varepsilon$. This set is also called the $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the point $C$.
