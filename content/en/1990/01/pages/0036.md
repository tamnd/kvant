The flat point regions are quadrilaterals (which can degenerate into triangles) bounded by two segments of the opposite edges of the tape and two segments passing along the tape. The parts of the tape that do not fall into these areas are elevated by generatrices, the ends of which lie on the edges of the tape. (All this follows from properties 1-3 of developable surfaces given in paragraph 2.) Flat areas can also be replaced by generators, so that the entire tape will be covered by a continuous family of generators (Fig. 5). Generators in identical quadrilaterals can be chosen in the same way, so that the periodicity described above is preserved.

Let's take any generator from our family, say, $AB$. If we cut it symmetrically in the middle line of the strip and then move it in any direction (say, to the right) to $l$, we get the segment $CD$, which is also a generator from our family (Fig. 6). Note (this is important) that $AC + BD = 2l$. When winding our length of tape onto a Möbius strip, the generators $AB$ and $CD$ will take the same position, with the point $A$ aligned with $D$, and the point $B$ with $C$; in other words, the segments $AB$ and $CD$ will make an angle of $180^\circ$ in space. Between $AB$ and $CD$ there will be a continuous family of generators. When moving from $AB$ to $CD$, the magnitude of the angle that these generators make in the space $AB$ continuously changes from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$. (Recall that the magnitude of the angle by the segments $KL$ and $MP$ in space is defined as $KLQ$, where $Q$ is a point such that the segments $LQ$ and $MP$ are equal in length, parallel and directed in the same direction.)

Let's take any $n$ and find between $AB$ and $CD$ such generators $A_1B_1$, ..., $A_{n-1}B_{n-1}$ such that the angle between $AB$ and $A_kB_k$ is equal to $k \frac{180^\circ}{n}$ (points $A_1$, ..., $A_{n-1}$ in this order lie between $A$ and $C$, and the points $B_1$, ..., $B_{n-1}$ in this order lie between $B$ and $D$; see Fig. 7).
