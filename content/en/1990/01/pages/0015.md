This method is important for the experimenter, since until now there has not yet existed a method, even a theoretical one, for approaching absolute zero as close as desired.

On the eve of the war, we began to develop this method in our work and made several successful experiments in this direction. I managed to obtain the concept of temperature by 0.4° using this method. Of course, obtaining temperatures in the immediate vicinity of absolute zero using new methods is a technically difficult task, and it is difficult to immediately count on success. There are many technical difficulties here, and success largely depends on the skill and ingenuity of the experimenter. But all these possibilities of difficulty will not make clear that there are some fundamental prohibitions for approaching absolute zero.

But let us now move on to the theoretical explanation of the mechanism of the phenomenon of liquid gel flowing out of a vessel when it is heated (see Fig. 1). As I already said, initially I explained the phenomenon of filling a vessel with gel by flowing the gel in the opposite direction in a thin layer. I also proposed that the energy state of gel II in this thin layer differs from the energy state of free gel II and thus could explain how great the thermal conductivity of the gel is. It is also possible to calculate the thickness of this layer so that the flow rate of the gel in it does not take on an excessively large value. Further, as stated, I am trying in my experiments to discover the thickness of this experimental layer. To do this, I forced the gel to flow in a very thin layer. Gradually I reached a gel layer thickness of 0.00014 mm, but experience showed that the nature of all phenomena was preserved. Thus, the obligation had to be moved, and this led to the occurrence of new occurrences on the nature of hydrodynamic phenomena in gel II. The first sketches of these ideas were expressed by Tissa, but their scientific development, the provision of theoretical justification for them and the creation of a hydrodynamic theory of the phenomenon belong to our scientist L. Landd.

I will try to give the most general picture of these views. According to this theory, the countercoccus, which I tried to explain by the flow of the gel in one energy state along the wall of another inside the big one, is replaced by the countercoccus of the gel, originating in itself.

The explanation of this phenomenon given by L. D. Landd is as follows.

Liquid gel is like a mixture of two liquids. These two components of the liquid gel are in two different quantum states. Thanks to this, he showed that simultaneous counter flows of the same liquid can exist, which we observe in the hot vessel at figure 1.

If this theoretical position were not so fully supported by experimental evidence, it would sound like an idea that is very difficult to recognize as reasonable.

Landdu's theory well describes the physical existence of those states in which a gel can simultaneously exist at the temperature of the λ-point. As I already said, if the gel continues to be cooled after combustion, then it will be in a state of general liquid up to 2.19 K, i.e., the λ-point. Then, according to Landdu's theory, a gel appears in this liquid as an impurity in a new state. This new state is characterized by the fact that it lacks anger. This gel represents liquid Gel II in the state in which it would weigh at absolute zero.
