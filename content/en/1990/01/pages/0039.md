Positions in three-dimensional space are very difficult. After all, this geometry includes, say, the theory of knots and gears, known for its inaccessibility («Kvant», 1975, No. 7, p. 6); such phenomena as “Alexander’s horned sphere” arose in it («Kvant», 1977, No. 7, p. 22); Its simplest questions, for example, whether it is possible to turn a torus inside out through a small hole, baffle our intuition. And here's what's surprising. It would seem that in a space of more than three dimensions the problems of location should become even more acute. But no; As the dimension increases, these difficulties are smoothed out. However, “smoothing” starts with dimension 5; Four-dimensional space is in a certain sense no simpler than three-dimensional. But let's return to the Möbius strip. Theorem 1, as we have seen, actually applies to self-intersecting tapes. It is unlikely that the condition for the absence of self-intersection will not arise on λ; however, it is not possible to take into account this occurrence, since mathematics does not have adequate technical means for studying self-intersection in three-dimensional space. For example, it is quite likely that Theorem 2 is unimprovable. After all, improving it means coming up with a new tape design. Experience shows that optimal constructions are simple and harmonious, which is exactly what the construction from the proof of Theorem 2 is. It is natural to assume that if the best construction existed, it would have been found - in so many years!

about one similar problem

Make a cylinder from a paper strip, not a Möbius strip (the strip can be any size). Is it possible, please?

Fig. 16.

paper, turn the cylinder inside out? If the cylinder is wide and low, then, of course, it is possible, but if it is narrow and high, then it is impossible. Let us denote by σ such a number that if the ratio of the length of the strip to the width is greater than σ, then the cylinder can be turned out, but if it is less, then it is impossible. I leave the proof of the following three theorems (more difficult than Theorems 1, 2, 3) to you as tasks:

Theorem 1'. $\sigma \geqslant \pi$.

Theorem 2'. $\sigma \leqslant \pi + 2$.

Theorem 3'. Let us assume that self-intersections are allowed during the eversion process. Then any cylinder made from a strip whose strip length ratio is π can be turned inside out (without creasing the paper).

Nothing more is known about this.
