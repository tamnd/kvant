F1219. A rattle in the form of a hollow steel ball with a volume of 0.2 l contains an internal 300 steel balls with a radius of 1 mm. It is shaken so that the balls inside continuously collide with each other and with the walls, making a terrible noise. Assuming the speed of the rattle to be 1 m/s, estimate the number of collisions between the balls in 1 minute. The emitted sound power is 10 W, heat generation during impacts cannot be considered.
A. Zilberman

F1220. A small canister containing the remaining neon for replenishment is connected for a short time to a large reservoir, where the pressure $p$ is twice as high as in the canister. The container will be disconnected immediately after it is filled. What will be the final pressure of the gas in the can? The heat exchange of gas with the walls of the can when filling it is to be neglected.
E. Butikov

F1221. Last century, Russian scientist V.I. Sreznevsky studied the evaporation of liquid droplets in the air. Let this evaporation occur at a constant temperature difference due to the supply of heat to the drop from the environment. Considering the heat flux per unit surface of a spherical drop to be proportional to the temperature difference and inversely proportional to the radius of the drop, find the dependence of the radius of the drop on time. How long will it take to finally evaporate a drop whose radius doubles in 10 minutes?
A. Stasenko

F1222. The inductor with $L=10$ Gn will be connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz network in series with the diode (Fig. 2). Draw a graph of the current in the coil as a function of time. What is the maximum current value? How does the type of graph depend on the moment the circuit is connected to the network?
Z. Rafailov

M1187. We will say that two quadrangles - paper and cardboard - fit together if the cardboard one can be placed on the paper one so that all its vertices fall on the sides of the paper one (one on each) and at the same time,

Let KLMN be a cardboard quadrilateral whose vertices K, L, M, N lie respectively on sides AB, BC, CD, DA of a convex paper quadrilateral ABCD.

Obviously, angles AKN and LKB of two paper triangles with vertex K add up to angle NKL if and only if $\angle NKL=90^\circ$. Thus, if the quadrangles fit together, then the KLMN cardboard quadrangle is necessarily a polygon.
