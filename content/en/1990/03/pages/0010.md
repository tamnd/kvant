At the points where the trajectory intersects with the linear slope, the athlete “shifts” from arc to arc, transferring his center of gravity over the lies. Then the next turn will begin. It would be good to speed up and lie to the pay! Is this possible? Let's look again at the skating move (Fig. 10, a). The center of gravity of a skater or ice skater describes a wave-like trajectory (red line on figure). The athlete's body determines the support lie and moves at an angle to it. At the same time (and not when stepping from foot to foot), work is done and speed is gained.

Wait, but the slalomist also moves along the “snake”! Moreover, his body does not literally follow the lies. At the end of the turn, the athlete’s center of gravity “catches up” with the lies, passes over them and goes forward - inside the next arc. Is it possible to complement this with repulsion? It turns out that it is possible.

True, this one just has little skill with the “horse” and is not at all conspicuous. It consists of two phases: flexion and extension.

First, when approaching the point of intersection (to the point of intersection of the trajectory with the linear slope), when the body catches up with the lie, the athlete bends his legs, as if “damping” the tubercle. This bending (sometimes a very sharp “swallowing” - avalman) allows you to maintain the speed gained and avoid slipping of the lie. Immediately after the conjugation point follows
