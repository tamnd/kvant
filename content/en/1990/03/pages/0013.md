ON THE DIAGRAM...
V. LEV

A familiar task

You have probably encountered the following task*): “Find a ten-digit number, the first digit of which is equal to the number of ones in this number, the second digit is the number of two, ..., and the last, ten digit is the number of zero.”
After some thought and a number of failed attempts, you will, of course, find the answer: 2,100,010,006.
But you might also want to understand the hidden mechanism behind this seemingly simple task. In mathematics it often happens that in order to better understand a problem, it needs to be generalized. Why, for example, did we limit ourselves to ten
