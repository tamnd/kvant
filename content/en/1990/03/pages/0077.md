Option 1
1. In the absence of external forces we have: $-ma_1 + Ma + ma_2 = 0$, whence
$$a = (a_1 - a_2)m/M.$$
2. After connection, on the outer surface of each disk there will be the same charge $q = (q_1 - q_2)/2$, located with the surface plane $\sigma = (q_1 - q_2)/(2\pi R^2)$. There will be no charges on the mating surfaces. After a slight dilution, the charge distribution will remain virtually unchanged. The field intensity created by one disk is
$$E = \sigma/(2\varepsilon_0) = (q_1 - q_2)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0R^2).$$
The field of one disk acts on the charge of another with a force
$$F = qE = (q_1 - q_2)^2/(8\pi\varepsilon_0R^2).$$
3. According to Faraday's law, due to the change in magnetic flux through the entire plane of the triangle, $\Theta D C = \Delta \Phi/\Delta t = \alpha ab$ will appear. According to Ohm's law $\Theta = IR = I \cdot 2\rho(a + b)$, where $\rho$ is the resistance per unit length of the wire. From here
$$I = \frac{\alpha ab}{2\rho(a + b)}.$$
