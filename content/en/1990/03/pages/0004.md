WHAT DOESN'T THINK
SKIER

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. ABRIKOSOV

In the world of winter sports, alpine skiing, sledding and bobsleigh stand apart. When you watch such sports conditions only on TV, some bewilderment arises: is it even possible to compete to see who can slide down the hill the fastest? and isn't the spread of results just a matter of chance? After all, we all solved the problem of a body rolling down an inclined plane (Fig. 1). The system of equations describing this movement makes it possible to find the acceleration of the body at the initial stage, the speed of steady motion, and the time of movement along the track from start to finish. This is where a paradox arises, justifying the bewilderment of the TV fan: the results do not depend on the behavior of the athlete. But we are not trying to take this factor into account in the original equations. What if you try?

It immediately comes to mind to pay attention to the force of friction and the force of air resistance. The friction coefficient is related to the choice of oil. For sledders and sledders (and giants too), the role of arodynamics cannot be underestimated. It is important not only to adopt and maintain the correct stance, but also to select the material and sew the jumpsuit from it well. An example is the legendary victory of the French team in downhill. The French were the first to realize that the fluttering bib number was an unacceptable Russian speed of 100 km/h. Under a hail of jokes, they glued numbers to their costumes with plasters. And we had fun at the finish line. Nowadays, in pursuit of hundredths of a second, even speed skaters wear “super-streamlined” overalls, and ice skaters are sometimes blown in wind tunnels. Curved poles allow you to take a more advantageous stance. New mats and coatings for skis and alloys for sled runners are being tested. Described by L. Cassil in the book “The White Queen’s Move,” the life of a false magician seems like innocent fun when the fight for Olympic gold is going on.

But the equipment of the participants is more or less equivalent, and the individual qualities of the athlete come to the fore - the will to win, physical and special training. We explained how the laws of mechanics allow you to apply your physical data and turn your will to victory in precious seconds of results. To understand the basics of skiing technique, let's take a break from downhill skiing (where aerodynamics, the ability to properly guide skis and successfully set a trajectory are so important) and move on to slalom disciplines. Here the athlete has to fight for success on his own, at times showing acrobatics.

The route through the eyes of a skier

Let's return to the forces that act on the skier on the track. We, ritelli, see the athlete in an inertial reporting system associated with a slope or a television camera. Well, for a skier, a more natural non-personal system associated with himself. It’s not very convenient for calculations, but let’s still try to look at the route through the eyes of a skier.
