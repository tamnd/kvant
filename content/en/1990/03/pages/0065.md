Problem 7. The message about the flat cross-country says that the percentage of the number of group members willing to participate in the cross-country ranges from 96.8% to 97.2%. Determine the minimum possible number of group members.

Solution. The percentage of those who did not participate in the cross ranged from 2.8% to 3.2%. If 1 person did not participate in the cross-country (it is no longer possible to have less), then the number of group members would range from $1 \cdot \frac{100}{2,8} = 35,7$... to $1 \cdot \frac{100}{3,2} = 31,2$... i.e. the minimum number of group members would be 32 people. It is clear that with a smaller number of group members, 3.2% of this number will be less than 1, and according to the condition, at least one person did not participate in the cross.

Problem 8. From points A and B, two trains hit each other at the same time. Which one initially moved at the same speed (the initial speeds of the train are equal to

pullon, accelerated are different), and then, to reach a certain speed, - uniformly. The speed ratio of uniform train movement is 5/4. At some point in time, the speed of the trains turns out to be equal, and by this time one of them has traveled a distance 5/4 times greater than the other. The trains arrived at points B and A at the same time. How much of the journey has which train traveled by the time their speeds are equal?

Solution. Let's look at graphs depicting speed versus time for each train. In this case, we can assume that both trains originated from the same point. For one train the schedule is a broken line OKM, for another - OK₁M₁ (see figure). The length of the process path to a certain moment in time by one of the trains is equal to the area of ​​the figure limited by the segment of the t axis and the corresponding part of the graph of its speed from above. By condition, the areas of the trapezoids OKMN and OK₁M₁N are equal, which means that the figures OKP and PK₁M₁M are equal in size. The area OKPL is equal to 5/4 the area OPL (by assumption). If the area of ​​OPL is 1, then the area of ​​OKP is 1/4; the area PK₁T is equal to 1/16, the plane K₁T = $\frac{1}{4}$PL (by condition, the ratio of the speeds of uniform motion is 5/4, i.e. M₁N = $\frac{5}{4}$PL), and the triangles OPL and PK₁T are similar. Next, from the equal areas OKP and PK₁M₁M, the area of ​​the rectangle TK₁M₁M is found. It is equal to $\frac{3}{16}$. Then the two remaining rectangles are found. The entire path (equal to the area OKMN or OK₁M₁N) is $2\frac{1}{2}$. Since the areas of OKPL and triangle OPL are respectively equal to $\frac{5}{4}$ and 1, then at the moment of equality of speeds (point P) one train has traveled $\frac{1}{2}$ of the path, and the other has traveled $\frac{2}{5}$.
