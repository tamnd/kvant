“Maybe it makes sense to delve into science fiction...” Dan muttered, looking up from the book “Hypnosis and Alpha Waves.” - And even better - fairy tales. What nonsense!

Hayes pushed his books aside and sat down on the edge of the table. In the middle lay a tattered Tsiferon, belly up, with batteries lying separately.

- Why did you destroy him? Hayes asked. — Decided to keep a pirated copy? It won't work. This thing is patented.

- I can't get inside.
- Inside what?
- These are... things. — Dan pointed at the game with a screwdriver. “I wanted to look at her insides.” It is completely unthinkable to take it apart without ruining it. You can only remove the batteries. That's all. Looks like I'll be picking up a hammer soon.

Hayes clucked his tongue.

- Do you have an inferiority complex, or what?

Dan leaned back in his chair and put his hands behind his head.

— Larry, I contacted the toy factory where these things are made. I wanted to talk to the inventor, whoever invented it - devils or demons. Do you know what they answered me? Nobody invented it. It was invented by the computer.

— Did the computer give birth to little computers? - Hayes bowed his head with a significant look. - Well, that's to be expected. Just give it free rein - and before you can blink an eye, they are already placed on sex.

- The main thing is that no one knows who entered the information into the computer so that it would give out... the game. It looks like there is no one to ask for this.

- So what? It's a hell of a lot of fun. They're being snapped up so quickly that stores don't have time to stock up. I went to five stores, wanted to buy Tsiferon for my son, and everything was sold out. They put me on the line, damn it! Can you imagine: there's a queue for the game! They promised to call.

Dan leaned over and slowly replaced the batteries.

“If this is a game,” he said.
- What do you mean?

- Larry... let's assume that you are... well, let's say, a missionary. Your task is to go into the jungle, find the most primitive, savage, sovereign, suspicious and vile pagans on Earth, extract them from the stone age and acquire them. You must educate them, train them, introduce them to modern technology, which is based on their understanding that your very appearance is shooting them to death. But this is your job, your duty. Because in ignorance they will very soon kill each other. They don't know how... to survive in their backward little world. They are mired in their own shit. They are incredibly cruel to each other. Their captive custom is stolwarian, that they kill their own kind out of sheer fear and superstition. Do you get it?

Hayes stood up and sat down in the leather chair.

- Cheerful would have been my best!

- Please be more serious. This is just a hypothesis. Assumption. How would you approach the task?

- Well... to tell the truth, I wouldn't do that. - Hayes shrugged. “In my opinion, it’s better to leave them alone.” I accept the law of natural selection. Maybe this is how it is necessary for them to feel each other. Maybe survival is not for them.

Dan thoughtfully rubbed the Cypheron lying on his lap, as if it were Aladdin’s magic lamp.

- No. Your personal philosophy does not apply in this case. It's your responsibility to save them from themselves. Where to start? Keep in mind that when they see you, they run away. You can't even get close.

- Do I know their language?

Dan frowned.

- Well, you know something about them, because you watched the explorer, secretly studied them... for years. And you know something about their... way of communicating. However, the vocabulary is very poor and limited. It will be of great use if they learn your language. How will you teach them if you cannot approach them openly?
