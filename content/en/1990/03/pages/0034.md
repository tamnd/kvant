F1198. A pipe with radius $R$ is filled with sand to a height $H$ ($H > 200R$). Sand density $ρ$. Find the minimum force of sand pressure on the bottom of the pipe. It is known that this pitch on a horizontal surface forms a hill with a limiting angle at the base of $\gamma_0$ (Fig. 1), and this angle is small ($\gamma_0 \sim 0,1$ rad). The coefficient of static friction between sand and pipe material is rsgen $\mu$.

Let us first find the coefficient of sand-sand friction $\mu'$. Let us consider sand on the slope of a hill with a base angle $\gamma$ (Fig. 2). Three forces act on it: gravity $mg$, normal slope reaction force $N$ and static friction force $F_{tp}$. From the condition that the vector sum of these forces is equal to zero (equilibrium condition), we easily find

$$N = mg \cos \gamma, F_{tp} = mg \sin \gamma = N \text{tg} \gamma.$$
On the other hand, $F_{tp}_{\max} = \mu' N$. Hence,

$$\mu' = \left( F_{tp} / N \right)_{\max} = \text{tg} \gamma_0 \approx \gamma_0,$$

where $\gamma_0 \ll 1$ is the limit (maximum) angle at the base.

Let us now consider a pipe filled with sand. Let us select a thin layer of sand with a thickness of $dz$ (Fig. 3; here the axis $Z$ is directed downward, $z = H$ corresponds to the bottom of the pipe). This layer is subject to gravity forces $dm \cdot g$, friction forces from the walls $F_{tp}$, pressure forces from the upper part of the sand $F_1 = \pi R^2 p(z)$ and pressure forces from the lower part $F_2 = \pi R^2 p(z + dz)$. Here $dm = \rho \pi R^2 dz$ is the mass of the layer under consideration, $p(z)$ is the sand pressure at level $z$, $p(z + dz)$ is the pressure at level $z + dz$. Obviously, the pressure on the bottom will be minimal if the friction is directed upward and is maximum in value.
