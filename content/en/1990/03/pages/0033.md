M1189. There are n straight lines on the plane, no three of which pass through the same point and no two of which are parallel. Prove that in each of the parts into which these lines divide the plane, you can put an integer different from 0 and not exceeding modulo n, so that on any side of any of these lines the sum of the numbers is equal to 0.

Let us give the shortest solution to the problem. It is based on the following fact: each of the parts into which the data straight lines divide the plane can be painted with black or white paint so that the areas that have a common section of the face have a different color. (This coloring can be obtained “by induction”: when adding a line in one of its bounded half-planes, all colors are replaced by opposite ones.)

Each intersection point of a straight line serves as the vertex of four angles - two white and two black. Let's write the numbers +1 (in white) and -1 (in black) in them. Obviously, the sum of these numbers in each half-plane bounded by any of their given lines is equal to 0.

Now, for each area, we sum up the numbers that are written in the corners - these will be the required integers, non-zero: since each convex area has no more than n sides, then it has no more than n corners.

Thus, we have proven the following theorem: if in each region into which n straight lines divide a plane, an integer number is written equal in absolute value to the number of its angles, and the signs of the numbers in neighboring (along a segment or ray) regions are different, then on each side of any of the n straight lines the sum of these numbers is equal to 0.

N. Konstantinov

M1190*. a) Prove that if there are 3n stars in a table of 2n×2n cells, then you can cross out n rows and n columns so that all the stars are crossed out.
b) Prove that in a table of 2n×2n cells you can arrange 3n+1 stars so that after crossing out any n rows and n columns, at least one star remains.

a) Cross out the n lines with the most asterisks. There cannot be less than 2n asterisks in them - this would mean that in one of these n lines there is no more than one asterisk, then in the remaining n lines there is no more than one asterisk, so there are less than 2n+n=3n asterisks in total. So, at least 2n stars have been crossed out; The remaining (no more than n) asterisks can be removed by crossing out the corresponding columns.
b) Place the stars as shown in figure: 2n - diagonally, another n - at the intersection of the i-th row with the (i+1)-th column (i=1, 2, ..., n) and the last one - at the intersection of the (n+1)-th row with the 1st column. Let's assume that we managed to remove all these asterisks by crossing out n rows and n columns. Let k≤n-1 of them rows (and thus n-1-k columns) come to the bottom (n-1) stars, i.e., to the last n-1 rows and columns. Then among the first n+1 lines n-k are crossed out. The remaining lines are divided into several zones: from p1,..., pr lines (r≥1), p1+...+pr=(n+1)-(n-k)=k+1. (We assume here that the first line follows the (n+1)th line.) In these zones there will be (p1+1)+(p2+1)+...+(pr+1)=k+r+1>k+1 stars, standing in different columns. There are n-(n-1-k)=k+1 columns left. Thus, by crossing out columns, all the asterisks cannot be removed.
