3. The capacitor plates are short-circuited, which means that the field inside is zero. Therefore, charges ±q must be induced on the plates, creating a field that exactly compensates the external field inside the capacitor, i.e. $q = \sigma S = E \varepsilon_0 S$. The area of ​​the plates changes due to the rotation of the polydiscs. During time $\Delta t$ the increase in area $\Delta S = r^2 \Delta \alpha / 2 = r^2 \omega \Delta t / 2$. Hence the current $I = \Delta q / \Delta t = E \varepsilon_0 S / \Delta t = \varepsilon_0 Er^2 \omega / 2$.

4. During the flight time $t \sim l / v$ the ball will fall to a depth $h = g t^2 / 2 \sim (g / 2) (l / v)^2$. In accordance with the rules of rebound control (when the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection), if the radius drawn from the center of the descending ball to the vertex of the right angle of the plate forms an angle $\alpha \geq 45^\circ$ with the vertical, then the ball will overcome the obstacles. Otherwise there will be a rebound. Thus, in a critical case, the depth of the ball's descent is $h = R (1 - 1 / \sqrt{2}) \approx 0,3R$. From here
$$v \sim l \sqrt{g / (2h)} \sim 13 \text{ m/c при } R \approx 0,1 \text{ m},$$
$$g = 10 \text{ m/c}^2, l = 1 \text{ m}.$$

5. The thermal conductivity of metal is much higher than foam. It’s easy to feel this by picking up a piece of metal and foam: the metal seems cold (it removes heat well), and the foam feels warm (the body is flat). In a tripa, if you touch frozen metal, the water immediately freezes, and the tripa floats to it. And through the foam plastic, there is practically no heat transfer from the tripe, and it does not freeze.
