In fact, this problem is a general arithmetic problem “in parts”. In each of the new educational ingots, parts of the original ones must be included in the ratio $m:n$. (Think why?) So, a new ingot weighing $m$ kg contains $m$ equal parts from the first ingot (weighing $m$ kg) and $n$ equal parts from the second ingot. The mass of one part is $\frac{m}{m+n}$ kg. The remainder of the first ingot in this new ingot is $\frac{m}{m+n} m = \frac{m^2}{m+n}$ kg, and the cut portion of the second is $\frac{mn}{m+n}$ kg. Problem 6. There are three pipelines in the port for loading tankers. The first of them pumps 300 tons of oil per hour, the second - 400 tons, and the third - 500 tons. We need to load two tankers. If loading is carried out first using two pipelines, connecting the first pipeline to one of the tanks, and the second pipeline to the other tanker, then loading of common tankers with the fastest of two possible connection methods will take 12 hours. In this case, one of the tankers may be filled early, and then the pipeline connected to it is turned off and will not be used for further loading. If the smaller tanker's totality were larger than it actually is, and loading was carried out by the second and third pipelines, then with the fast connection method, loading would take 14 hours. Determine how many tons of oil each tanker contains.

Solution. Obviously, a more productive pipeline should be connected to a tanker with greater integrity. Since one of the two tankers was filled exactly in 12 hours, then either the time is less than 12·300=3600 tons of oil, or more than 12·400=4800 tons. The first case is impossible, since when doubling the capacity of a smaller tanker, we get 7200 tons, and to fill such a tanker even with a third pipeline requires more than 14 hours. Consequently, the larger tanker totals 4800 tons and is filled with the second and, moreover, the third pipeline in less than 14 hours. This means that the tanker is less than $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 14 \cdot 500 = 3500$ tons.
