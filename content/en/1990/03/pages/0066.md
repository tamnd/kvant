Problem 9. The user has a total of 7 rubles. 19 kopecks It is known that any two of them have different amounts of delegates, but one of them has an integer number of times more delegates than the other. How many assignments does each student have?

Solution. Let $x_1$ be the smallest sum, $x_1x_2$ the second largest, ..., $x_1x_2...x_8$ the largest sum. By condition $x_i \neq 1$ at $i > 1$, $x_1 + x_1x_2 + ... + x_1x_2...x_8 = 719$. 719 is a prime number, hence $x_1 = 1$. Next we have $x_2 + x_2x_3 + ... + x_2x_3...x_8 = 718 = 2 \cdot 359$. Thus, $x_2 = 2$. Then we get $x_3 = x_4 = 2$ and $x_5 + x_5x_6 + x_5x_6x_7 + x_5x_6x_7x_8 = 88 \cdot x_5$ - a divisor of 88. If $x_5 = 2$, then $x_6 + x_6x_7 + x_6x_7x_8 = 43$. 43 is a prime number, and $x_6 \neq 1$ means $x_5 \neq 2$. For $x_5 = 4$ we find $x_6 = 3$, $x_7 = x_8 = 2$. Other values ​​of $x_5$ will not work.

Problems to solve independently

1. The vegetable base contained winged mushrooms, the humidity of which was 99%. During storage, its humidity decreased by 1% (it became 98%). By what percentage has the storage mass of the fender base decreased?

2. A car travels from $A$ to $B$ in 1 hour. The car left $A$ and at the same time a pedestrian exited $B$. The car met the pedestrian, took it to $A$ and then landed at $B$, spending 2 hours 40 minutes the whole way. How long does it take for a pedestrian to go all the way from $B$ to $A$?

3. The motor ship travels from $A$ to $B$ in 3 hours, and returns back in 4 hours. How long will it take the raft to travel from $A$ to $B$ at the speed of the current?

4. A train traveling from point $A$ to point $B$ makes several stops along the way. At the first stop, 5 passengers board the train, and at each subsequent stop, 10 more passengers. At each stop, 5 passengers get off the train. Is it possible that point $B$ brings in less than 336 passengers if point $A$ leaves 462 of them?

5. The report on flat cross-country said that the percentage of the number of cross-country participants that did not catch
those living within the standard ranged from 94.2% to 94.4%. What is the smallest number of cross-country participants?

6. The bus on the way from $A$ to $B$ makes 5 stops of 10 minutes every 16 km (the distance from $A$ to $B$ is 96 km), the speed of the bus is 65 km/h. At the same time as the bus, a bicycle comes out from $B$ towards it at a speed of 21 km/h. At what distance from $A$ will the bus meet the bicycle?

7. Passenger train in 6 seconds. How long will it take the fast and passenger trains to pass each other if the speed of the fast train is 3/2 times the speed of the passenger train, and the speed of the passenger train is 4/3 times the length of the fast train?

8. The work began between 9 and 10 a.m. and ended between 3 and 4 p.m. the same day. Determine the duration of the work if the minute and hour hands were perpendicular at the start and end of the work.

9. One worker can produce a batch of parts in 12 hours. One worker began the work, after 1 hour a second one joined him, after another hour - a third, etc., until the work was completed. How long did the first worker work? (The labor productivity of all workers is the same.)

10. There are three slabs weighing 2, 3 and 5 kg with different copper contents. Each strand is divided into three parts, and from the ninth pieces obtained, three strands weighing 2, 3 and 5 kg with comparable copper content are obtained. When rolling parts, should the original slabs be separated to ensure an equal percentage of copper in the resulting ingots, regardless of its content in the original slabs?

11. Three schoolchildren share nuts among themselves. First, the first student divides each of the other two one-quarter of the nuts he has and another half of a nut. Then the second to each of the other two, one quarter of the nuts he had and another half of a nut. Then a third student also followed. As a result, each of them ended up with 30 nuts. How many nuts did each elementary school student have?

12. There are two vessels. One contains 3 liters of 100% acid, and the other 2 liters of water. One glass of acid was poured from the first vessel into the second, and then one glass of the mixture was poured from the second into the first. This operation was repeated three more times. As a result, the second vessel contained acid with a strength of 42%. What percentage of acid is now contained in the first vessel?

13. Point $B$ is located higher upstream than point $A$, at a distance of 4.5 km from $A$. The speed of the river is 3 km/h. Moving in still water, a rower moves at a speed of 5 km/h. The rower came out of $A$, topped up to $B$ and returned to $A$. At regular intervals, the rower separated for 10 minutes (at this time the boat is moving with the current), and in total there were 8 such breaks. After how long did the rower return to $A$?
