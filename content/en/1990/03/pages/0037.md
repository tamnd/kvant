F1201. There is a nonlinear electronic device $\tilde{R}$. figure 1 shows a graph of the dependence of the current through the device on the voltage across it (in sections 1-2 and 3-4 the overlap of the graph is very large). Assemble a circuit consisting of $\tilde{R}$, a coil with inductance $L$ and a battery with an emf equal to $U_0$ (the device is turned on with the “correct” polarity corresponding to the graph). Construct a graph of the current in the circuit versus time and calculate the period of the oscillating current.

Fig. 1.

After the circuit is closed, the electronic device begins to operate in section 1-2 of its characteristics. The equation for the change in current in the circuit over time will have the form
$$LdI/dt = U_0,$$
or
$$LI = U_0 t.$$
Starting from moment $t_1 = LI_2/U_0$, when the state of the device is represented by point 2, a further increase in the current through the device becomes impossible. In this case, the direction on the coil will fall, and on the device it will increase. As a result, the device will very quickly make the transition $2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 2'$, since the state of the system in section 2-3 is unstable. Since the current in the coil cannot change instantly, $I_{2'} = I_2$ (during the transition the current is kept constant due to the so-called parastinous capacitance of the system).

After the system is in state $2'$, the equation for the change in current in the coil will eventually take the form

Fig. 2.

$$LdI/dt = U_0 - 2U_0 = -U_0,$$
or
$$L\Delta I = -U_0\Delta t.$$
After time $\Delta t = L(I_2 - I_1)/U_0$ the system will be in state 3, from which it will very quickly move to state $3'$, after which after the same interval $\Delta t$ it will come to state 2, and the process will repeat.
The repetition period of the process will be equal to
$$T = 2\Delta t = 2L(I_2 - I_1)/U_0.$$
A graph of the current in the circuit versus time is shown in figure 2.

K. Bedov
