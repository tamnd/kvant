“Maybe several light years.”
- Wow!...
- Do you think I'm crazy?
- Absolutely. Listen, Dan...
- I don’t care! I also think I'm crazy. But damn, it all makes sense! They use these things... as tuning devices. When a child's brain emits alpha waves - or anything else - for a long time... for a long time... very intensely... it's like a straight wire...
Only the rich know where. Or maybe this is their means of delivery... They get into children's heads, restore order there and prepare the children for... what is to come...
“Close contacts of a strange kind,” Heyes nodded, rubbing his temples. “You’ve gone crazy, my friend.” You yourself know that this is so. Do you think they send "missionaries" here to teach the savages?
- Something like that.
- Dan, go home. Take a week off. I'll tell Wilson. Everything will be fine...
- I'm not crazy, Larry.
- I’m not even talking. You're just overtired.
Dan sighed and rubbed his eyes.
- Yes. I'm tired. But I'm not pikh.
- Go home.
As Dan entered the house, he heard Cass singing in the kitchen as she chopped celery for a salad. The TV in the living room was on, and sports passions were raging on the screen.
-Where is Jered? Dan asked as he entered the kitchen.
- Oh! You scared me to death. Why so early?

- My head hurts. Where is the boy?
- I think in the living room. What do you want - beets with fried potatoes or green beans?
- Doesn't matter.
- So, beans. Jered can't stand beets.” Cass patted some flour. “Honey, should I give you some aspirin?” You don't look good.
- I'm fine...
He returned to the living room and turned off the phone. From somewhere above, a thin picane “Ziferton” faintly reached out.
“I borrowed it from a friend on our street,” said Cass. Dan rushed up the stairs, stepping through the steps. “He said something about level four.” Dan, don't scold him...
When he reaches the bedroom door, the melodic sounds stop. My head was spinning, adrenaline was raging in my blood. Dan pushed the door. It didn't open.
- Jered! Jered! - he shouted, throwing his whole body at the front. Suddenly it came with clover and ozone - the door swung open. Dan stumbled across the room. A pale blue flicker faded just above the bed. Dan rushed to the “Ziferton”, which had just - a moment ago - accumulated on his son’s lap. A small dent on the bed still contained the body. But there was no child.
Dan sat on the edge of the bed and carefully picked up the multi-colored, caramel-like game. He walked out a little so that his fingers would stop shaking, and whispered:
- Hold on, son. Wait for me, Jered. I'm coming. Dad is coming to see you.
Without haste, he began to play.
