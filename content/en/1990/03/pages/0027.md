Man overboard
(to the 25th anniversary of the Voskhod-2 flight)

When they opened the outer door and I saw myself at the rock of the rocket, I froze and made a convulsive movement that pushed me out of the rocket. It seems that I had attracted a net without support between the walls of this coyote, but when I saw that there was no support under my abyss, that there was no support all around, I felt sick, and I came to my senses only when the chain had already unwound and I was a kilometer away from the rocket.

K. E. Tsiolkovsky. Beyond Earth

On March 18, 1965, that is, almost 25 years ago, the Voskhod-2 spacecraft was launched into orbit of an artificial Earth satellite. It was piloted by P.I. Belyaev and A.A. Leonov. The ship's flight lasted only 25 hours, but two circumstances made it historic.

The first and most important thing is that for the first time a person entered outer space. Of course, today we would never be surprised by the release (however, how can we surprise the general public today?). However, 25 years ago this task did not seem so trivial at all.

What problems needed to be solved? Firstly, the issue of organizing the airlock chamber (SHK). It was, of course, possible to exit directly from the ship’s cabin (somewhat later the Americans did just that on Gemini). But then the requirements for on-board equipment would have to be tightened. In a neutral situation, she should already be working in a depressurized cabin, but this is in a neutral situation...

It was not possible to place a ShK in the small cabin of the Voskhod. So, it was necessary to do it from the outside. But the laws of aerodynamics are inexorable. The optimal decision is made - to make the ShK inflatable.

In addition to organizing the airlock chamber, it was necessary to decide how to ensure the breathing of space during the exit. Supplying air through a hose is not only unprofitable in terms of mass costs, but also unsafe (the hose could be bent or torn somewhere). Therefore, it was decided to remove the space with oxygen cylinders. As for telephone and telecommunications, it is carried out via cable.

A. Leonov’s spacesuit was significantly different from the spacesuits in previous flights: after all, it had to ensure life-activity not just in a vacuum, but also with almost 300-degree temperature changes (on the illuminated and shadowed sides).

Of course, there were still problems that we had not had to deal with before, but the most interesting of them, in our opinion, was not technical, but psychological.
