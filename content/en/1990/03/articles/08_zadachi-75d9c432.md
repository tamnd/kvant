Quests

1. A traveler left A for B. At the same time, a second traveler left B for A. They walked evenly, but at different speeds. At the time of the first meeting, there was still 16 hours left to go, and the second had 9 hours. How many hours after leaving did they meet?

2. Solve the arithmetic puzzle shown at figure. One oblique letter corresponds to the same numbers, different letters correspond to different numbers.

3. Let's write down the numbers from 0 to 9 in a row: 0, 1, 2, 3, ..., 9 and consider the sequence of sums of two neighboring numbers: 1, 3, 5, 7, ..., 17. In this sequence, the numbers increase by 2. Arrange the numbers 0, ..., 9 so that in the resulting sequence of sums, the neighboring numbers increase by 1.

4. Find all two-digit numbers equal to the sum of the cube of the number of ones and the square of the number of tens.

5. There are three identical children's cubes and a ruler. How can you change the larger diagonal of a cube without any calculations?

The first problem belongs to the famous English popularizer of mathematics Lewis Carroll; the remaining problems were suggested to us by A. Savin, N. Avilov, N. Antonovich, I. Nazel.
