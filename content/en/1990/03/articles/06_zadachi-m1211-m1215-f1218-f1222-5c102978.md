Tasks

M1211 — M1215, F1218 — F1222

M1211. Is it possible to arrange a tetrahedron, a sphere and a plane in space in such a way that the cross-sectional areas of the tetrahedron and sphere by any plane parallel to the chosen one are equal?

A. Anjans

M1212. The set of all integers is divided into pairwise disjoint infinite arithmetic progressions with positive differences $d_1, d_2, d_3, \dots$. Could it happen that

$$\frac{1}{d_1} + \frac{1}{d_2} + \frac{1}{d_3} + \dots < 0,9?$$

Consider two cases:

a) the number of progressions is finite;
b) the number of progressions is infinite (in this case, the condition (*) means that the sum of any finite number of terms on the left side (*) is less than 0.9).

A. Tolpygo

M1213. Prove that if some convex hexagon can be cut into $N$ parallelograms of equal area, then a) it has a center of symmetry;
b) the number $N$ is divisible by 3.

B. Free

M1214*. In some straight cells, tables of $n$ rows and $m > n$ columns are arranged with asterisks so that each column has at least one asterisk. Prove that there is an asterisk such that there are more asterisks in its row than in its column.

A. Razborov

M1215*. The number 15 can be decomposed in three ways into the sum of three natural numbers so that all 9 numbers are different:

$$15 = 1 + 6 + 8 = 2 + 4 + 9 = 3 + 5 + 7.$$

For each natural number $n$ we denote by $k(n)$ the most number of a trio of natural numbers that add up to $n$ and consist of $3k(n)$ distinct numbers. Prove that a) $k(n) > n/6 - 1$;
b) $k(n) < 2n/9$;
b) $k(100) = 21$;
r) $k(500) = 110$.

J. Kurlyandchik

F1218. A cylinder of mass $m$ is located between a movable horizontal surface and an inclined plane (Fig. 1) fixed at an angle $\alpha$. The coefficient of cylinder friction on a horizontal surface is $\mu_1$, on an inclined surface - $\mu_2$. A horizontal surface of uniform movement to the left. What is the minimum force required to do this?

M. Qilin
