8. Among the heel of triangles whose vertices are three successive vertices of a pentagon, there is a triangle with the smallest area. Let, for the sake of definition, it be triangle ABC (Fig. 7). Let us show that two of the directed arrows are directed towards vertex B.

Let us drop the redistributions SC and EM from points C and E onto the straight line AB. Since $S_{ABC} \leqslant S_{ABE}$, then $\frac{1}{2}CK \cdot AB \leqslant \frac{1}{2}EM \cdot AB$, i.e. $CK \leqslant EM$.

The equality $CK = EM$ is impossible (if $CK = EM$, then $CE \parallel AB$). So, $CK < EM$. This means that lines EC and AB intersect at point P, which lies on the continuation of AB beyond point B, i.e., the arrow on AB is directed towards point B.

Similarly, it is proved that the arrow on CB is directed towards point B.
