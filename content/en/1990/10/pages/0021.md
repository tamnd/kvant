Racial laws applied to R. Courant, although he was a veteran of the war. Courant began as Hilbert's assistant, and returned to the university from the front after being wounded, he became the successor to Klein's chair. Courant was a brilliant mathematician with a wide range of interests. Maybe you are familiar with his book “What is Mathematics” - it is one of the best popular books on our science. Kurant also had the gift of an organizer. His collaboration with the publisher Ferdinand Springer made it possible to largely solve the problem of publishing mathematical results (now Springer Verlag is the largest publisher of scientific literature).
In the early 1920s, Courant succeeded in realizing Klein's old dream of creating a Mathematical Institute in Göttingen. The time was not the most suitable for such beginnings: in Germany there was evidence of inflation (the huge prize of 100 thousand marks assigned in 1906 for the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem turned into a simple piece of paper). But Kurant, as director of the Institute, managed to successfully overcome numerous financial and administrative difficulties. In 1933, Courant's working conditions became intolerable. He retired and emigrated to the USA. In New York, Courant created a mathematical institute, which now bears his name.
The new director of the Institute was appointed “pure Argentinean” O. Neugebaur. He held this honorary post for one day, as he refused to sign the confirmation in logical mode. The post passed to another outstanding Göttingen mathematician, the successor to Hilbert's chair, Hermann Weyl. Veil was convinced that the mathematical traditions of Göttingen could be saved under the “new order.” However, in the summer of 1933, he also left the country. In the end, Helmut Hasse, a first-class mathematician and a convinced naist, became the director of the Institute.
In 1930, there were 5 full professors of mathematics in Göttingen: F. Bernistein, G. Veillu, D. Hilbert, R. Courant, E. Landdu. Four left the university. Gilbert, who turned 71 in 1933, lost many halls and students (he supported his assistant P. Bernays at his own expense for another year after his dismissal).
