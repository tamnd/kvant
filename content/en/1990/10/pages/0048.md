And now - tasks and questions for independent research.

1. Prove that in an optically homogeneous medium, IR rays propagate rectilinearly.

2. Take a small flat mirror and show that IR rays are reflected from it. Measure the angles of incidence and reflection. Compare the results and draw conclusions.

3. Polish a brass flat 20 mm wide and 80 mm long. Bend it so that you get a cylindrical mirror with a radius of approximately 100 mm - use it to focus the IR rays. Try to obtain an image of an IR source, measure the distances from the mirror to the source and its image and calculate the focal length of the mirror. How is it related to the radius of curvature of the mirror?

4. Direct a beam of IR rays onto one of the faces of a triangular glass panel and show that IR rays are refracted when passing from one medium to another.

5. Prove that a converging lens focuses IR rays. Experimentally determine the focal length of the lens for IR radiation. Should I use diaphragms in this and the previous experiments?

6. Sand a small piece of ebonite with sandpaper so that its thickness becomes no more than 0.5 mm. Find out whether ebonite transmits IR rays.

7. Take a glass or glass jar. Assemble the experimental setup as shown in figure 4. Direct a narrow beam of IR rays to the center of the core and onto its flat face. By stirring the photodiode, show that IR rays are partially reflected and partially refracted at the air-glass interface.
