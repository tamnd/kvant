The easiest way to assemble an IR ray transmitter is according to a standard multiputibrator circuit using transistors of different conductivities (Fig. 1). With the specified nominal details (hereinafter, resistor resistances are given in kiploms, and capacitor capacitances are given in microfarads), the multiputibrator generates rectangular voltage pulses with a repetition frequency of 200-250 Hz. The HL₁ infrared LED carries both direct and alternating current components. As a result, the LED emission is modulated in intensity.

The schematic diagram of the IR radiation receiver is shown in figure 2. The sensor is a photodiodVD₁, the signal from which is fed to a standard audio amplifier and then converted into sound by a micropolephone BF₁ (type TM-2).

The radio components necessary for the manufacture of devices are fully available. Instead of those indicated in the diagrams, you can use any infrared LED and any photodiod (the latter can be easily made from an old transistor by opening its case). The micropolephone can be replaced with a low-power speaker. It seems to us that there is no need to talk about the designs of the transmitter and receiver: each of you is able to think about and implement them independently. Let us only note that it is convenient to connect the LED with the transmitter, and the photodiod with the receiver, using multi-core flexible conductors 30-50 cm long.

After assembling the devices, check the correct installation. Now place the photodiod opposite the LED at a distance of 20-30 cm and turn on the power of the devices. You will achieve a fairly loud monophonic sound, which will gradually weaken if the distance between the LED and the photodiode is increased to 1-2 m. Return the LED and photodiode to their original position and block the beam of IR rays, for example, with your palm. The sound should stop. If everything happens exactly like this, then the devices are working normally. Otherwise, you will have to check the serviceability of the radio components.

To perform many experiments, you will need a narrow beam of IR rays.
