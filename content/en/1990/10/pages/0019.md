Decline of the University of Göttingen

A few large scientific centers play the main role in the development of mathematics. In the first third of this century in Germany, such a center was the University of Göttingen. Its traditions go back to K.-F. Gauss, who entered the university in 1795 and spent time there

D. Gilbert (1862-1943)

Vishchev University on the threshold of the 20th century is associated with the activities of Klein and Hilbert (by the way, Hilbert arrived in Göttingen exactly 100 years after Gauss - in 1895). In the second half of his life, Klein was very actively involved in organizing science. Through his leadership, a number of scientific institutes, a first-class mathematical library, and a collection of mathematical models were created at the university, in which a kind of student club gathered.

On the wall of the wine cellar at the city hall there is a motto: “There is no life outside Göttingen.” These peremptory words, to a certain extent, corresponded to the state of affairs in mathematical life. Numerous mathematicians from America, Hungary, the Dane H. Bohr, the Englishman G. Hardy, the Dutch L. Brouwer and W. Van Der Waerden, the Soviet mathematicians P. Alexandrov, A. Kolmogorov and P. Urysohn visited the university. At times it seemed that an international mathematical congress was taking place in Göttingen.

The Göttingen mathematical school is, first of all, Hilbert's school. His scientific interests cover practically all mathematics: number theory, algebra, functional analysis, geometry, logic. And in each of these areas he achieved outstanding results. Together with G. Minkowski, he led a seminar on physics, in which rapid changes were taking place at that time: the theory of relative mechanics and quantum mechanics were created (Hilbert’s assistant in physics at one time was M. Born). And it was Hilbert’s school that suffered the most losses under Nazism.
