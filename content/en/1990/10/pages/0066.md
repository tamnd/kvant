Attention!
The magazines «Kvant» (USSR) and Quantum (USA - USSR), as well as the All-Russian Youth Aerospace Society "Soyuz" (VAKO) and the International Educational Network (USA) conduct

Soviet-American competition for young lovers of astronomy and cosmonautics

Perhaps this competition is becoming traditional. All students of schools, gymnasiums, technical schools, etc., except for graduating classes (1990/91 academic year), are invited to participate in it. The first competition will be held in three rounds.

Rounds I and II (respectively from October to December 1990 and from January to March 1991) are correspondence and include tasks from the field of cosmonautics, astronomy, physics, mathematics, and logic.

III round (April - May 1991) - very. The winners of the competition will then take part in one of two three-week space schools, which will be held in July - August 1991 in the USA and the USSR.

Classes for schoolchildren are conducted by specialists in physics, mathematics and space technology from both countries.

Below we present the problems of the first round. Responses to them must be sent no later than December 1, 1990 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, magazine «Kvant», marked “KQ Competition - 91”.

1st round tasks

Task 1. At an altitude of 10 km from the surface of Mars, a probe is dropped vertically downward with an initial speed of 1 m/s. After 30 seconds of free fall, the parachute opens, after which the vertical speed of the probe is set to 2 m/s. At what horizontal distance l from the release point will the probe move if during its descent it has a constant stop at a speed of 3 m/s? (The influence of the Martian atmosphere on the initial part of the descent is a claim.)

Task 2. The moon base has the shape of a cube. There are exits on two opposite walls of the base (exactly in the middle). At a distance of 15 m from each exit at the height of a man is within the framework of the International Geophysical Year held in 1957.) Maybe this will be a Mars exploration program. Maybe creating power plants or giant communication kits in orbit... You never know what interesting things we can do together! But in any case, the implementation of such projects will be left to the smallest schoolchildren, who will specialize in the field of astronautics.

That is why the youth program occupies more place within the framework of the IGC. Various contents are also planned, including a space (by analogy with physical, mathematical, etc.) Olympiad.

In issue 7 for this year we published an appeal from the Organizing Committee of the national stage of the International Competition “Together to Mars”. In this issue we begin publishing additional materials that may be useful to its participants.
