F1236. The electromagnetic solution has a winding with an inductance of 1 Gi with a resistance of 10 Ohms. The solution winding is connected to a 20 V source in series with a pair of normally closed contacts, which open when the solution is equalized. Parallel to the solution winding, a diode is connected, as shown in figure (see p. 30). The pickup current of the solution is 0.1 A, the release current is 0.09 A. Why might such a device come across and why is a diode used in it? Calculate the device parameters. At what minimum source voltage can it operate?

After switching on, the current in the coil increases to a value of $I_0=0,1$ A. The circuit resistance is low - even at maximum current the voltage drop is 1 V, so for simplicity we can consider the current increase linear:

$$I=U_0t/L.$$

When the current reaches 0.1 A, the decision will reverse and the contact will open. In this case, the current will be lost through the opened diode. If the circuit resistance were zero, this current would not decrease. However, the presence of voltage across the resistance $r=10$ Ohm (the voltage across the coil is the induced emf) leads to the construction of a current - up to a value of $I_1=0,09$ A (this is the release solution), at which the contact closes again, and the current becomes. The induced emf with increasing current changes from $U_0=I_0r=1$ V to $U_1=I_1r=0,9$ V. It can be seen that the change in this voltage is not large, and for simplicity we can consider it constant:

$$U_{cp}=0,95$$ V.

In this case, the current changes according to an approximately linear law, and the time it falls from $I_0$ to $I_1$ is equal to

$$t_1=L(I_0-I_1)/(rI_{cp})=0,01$$ p.

The rise time of the current from $I_1$ to $I_0$ is determined by the source voltage and is

$$t_2=L(I_0-I_1)/U\approx 0,0005$$ p.
