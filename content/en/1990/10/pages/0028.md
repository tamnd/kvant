F1233. Motorcycle races will take place on a narrow circular track. When starting from a stop, the motorcyclist strives to constantly change the speed. How much of the cruise will he have covered by the time he reaches maximum speed?

Let's say right away that for fast acceleration you need to use the friction force as best as possible.
Let $\alpha$ be the angle between the speed of the motorcycle $\vec{v}$ and the force of friction on the ground $\vec{F}$ at some point in time (see figure). Let us write down the equations of motion of a motorcycle in projections onto the directions of the tangent and normal to the trajectory:
$$mv' = F \cos \alpha, mv^2/R = F \sin \alpha.$$
Differentiating the second equation with respect to time, taking into account the first we obtain
$$2v/R = \alpha'$$
or (since $v/R = \omega = \varphi'$)
$$2\varphi' = \alpha'.$$
From here
$$2\varphi = \alpha + \text{const.}$$
At $\varphi = 0$ (starting point) $\alpha = 0$, so const = 0 and
$$2\varphi = \alpha.$$
At the moment of reaching maximum speed
$\alpha = \pi/2$ and $\varphi = \pi/4$,
which corresponds to 1/8 of a circle.
A. Chernochan
