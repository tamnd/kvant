M1224. A segment is drawn from the vertex of the triangle to a point on the opposite side, dividing the inscribed circle into three equal parts. can this segment be $a$) high; $b$) median; $c$) bisectis of a triangle?

Answer: a), c) cannot, b) can.

Let us introduce the notation as for figure. Note that if the lengths of the two extreme parts of the drawn segment $AD$ are equal to $(AN=DM)$, then the triangle $ABD$ is obviously equilateral to $(AB=BD)$. Consequently, $AD$ cannot be either the height of $(\angle ADB<90^\circ)$ or the bisector of $(\angle CAD<\angle ADB=\angle BAD)$, and it can only be a median when $a=2c$, where $a=BC$, $c=AB$.

Let now condition $a=2c$ be satisfied. Let us show that for some $b=AC$ the incircle

will divide the median $AD=m$ into equal parts. We will need the following three well known and very useful statements:

1) $m^2=(2b^2+2c^2-a^2)/4$;
2) $AE=(b+c-a)/2$,

where $E$ is the point of tangency of the inscribed circle with side $AB$;
3) $AE^2=AN \cdot AM$

(this is true for any ANM secant).

Substituting $a=2c$ into equalities 1) and 2) and into 3) the value $AE$ from 2), $AN=m/3$, $AM=2m/3$ and $m^2$ from 1), we obtain the condition

$$AE^2=\left(\frac{b-c}{2}\right)^2=\frac{2}{9} m^2=\frac{2}{9} \frac{b^2-c^2}{2},$$

or $9(b-c)=4(b+c)$ (reduction by $b-c$ is possible since $a=2c<b+c$, i.e. $b-c>0$). Hence $b=13c/5$. Since $2c-c<13c/5<2c+c$, a triangle with sides $2c$, $13c/5$, $c$ exists. The parts $AN$ and $DM$ of the median in such a triangle are equal, since $BD=c=BA$. The segment $x=AN$ satisfies the equation $x(m-x)=AN \cdot AM=AE^2=2m^2/9$, which has roots $m/3$ and $2m/3$, and the inequality $x<m-x$ ($AN<AM$). Therefore, $x=m/3$, which is what we needed.

V. Senderov
