8. a) Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be these vectors, $S = a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n$ their sum. Let us take an arbitrary set of $p$ vectors $\vec{a}_{i1}, \vec{a}_{i2}, \dots, \vec{a}_{ip-1}, \vec{a}_i$. According to the conditions of the problem $|\vec{a}_{i1} + \vec{a}_{i2} + \dots + \vec{a}_{ip-1}| = |\vec{S}|\vec{a}_{i1} - \vec{a}_{i2} - \dots - \vec{a}_{ip-1} - \vec{a}_i$. It follows that $|\vec{S}|^2 = 2(\vec{S}, \vec{a}_{i1} + \vec{a}_{i2} + \dots + \vec{a}_{ip-1} + \vec{a}_i)$. Similarly, for the set $\vec{a}_{i1}, \vec{a}_{i2}, \dots, \vec{a}_{ip-1}, \vec{a}_j$ the equality $|\vec{S}|^2 = 2(\vec{S}, \vec{a}_{i1} + \vec{a}_{i2} + \dots + \vec{a}_{ip-1} + \vec{a}_j)$ holds. Therefore, $(\vec{S}, \vec{a}_i - \vec{a}_j) = 0$ for any $i, j, t$. i.e. the scalar product $(\vec{S}, \vec{a}_i) = k$ does not depend on $i$. Therefore $|\vec{S}|^2 = (\vec{S}, \vec{a}_1 + \dots + \vec{a}_n) = nk$. On the other hand, $|\vec{S}|^2 = 2(\vec{S}, \vec{a}_{i1} + \vec{a}_{i2} + \dots + \vec{a}_{ip-1} + \vec{a}_j)$. Therefore, $nk = 2pk$, $(n - 2p)k = 0$, $k = 0$, for $p \neq \frac{n}{2}$. Thus, $|\vec{S}| = 0$, $\vec{S} = \vec{0}$.

3. Let's compose the cube $2 \times 2 \times 2$ in an arbitrary way. Let there be $m$ white and $n$ black squares $1 \times 1$ on its surface (we will call them visible). Then $m + n = 24$ and the number $d = m - n$ is even. If $d = 0$, then the problem is solved. Let us show that if $d \neq 0$, then by rotating the cubes $1 \times 1 \times 1$ we can ensure that $d$ becomes equal to zero. Indeed, it is not difficult to verify that the following statements are true:

1) After rotating any cube $1 \times 1 \times 1$ by $90^\circ$ around the straight line connecting the centers of its opposite faces, exactly one invisible square becomes visible and, accordingly, one visible square becomes visible. The number $d$ either does not change at all or changes by 2.
2) For each cube $1 \times 1 \times 1$, you can specify three such successive rotations, after which all its visible faces will become visible, and all invisible ones will become visible.

If you make three rotations with each of the eight cubes $1 \times 1 \times 1$, as indicated in point 2), then the number $d$ will change its sign. Consequently, after one such rotation, $d$ will become equal to zero, which is what needed to be proven.
