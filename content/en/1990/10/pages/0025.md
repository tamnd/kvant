Problem solutions
M1221 — M1225, F1233 — 1237

If the median AD divides angle A of triangle ABC into parts $\angle BAD = \alpha$, $\angle CAD = 2\alpha$ (see figure), then, equating the expressions for the planes of triangles BAD and CAD

$$\frac{1}{2}AB \cdot AD \cdot \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2}AC \cdot AD \cdot \sin 2\alpha$$

we get that cos $\alpha = AB/2AC$. This formula allows us to construct the angle $\alpha$, and with it the triangle ABC, using the given sides AB and AC. From the obvious condition $0 < \alpha < \pi/3$ it follows that the problem has a solution (and, moreover, a unique one) at $1 < AB/AC < 2$.

We also present the number of the geometric solution. If $E$ is a point symmetric to $C$ with respect to the median AD, then, as is easy to see, $BE \parallel AD$ (see figure), therefore, $\angle EBA = \angle DAB = \angle EAB$, i.e. triangle ABE is equilateral, and $BE = EA = AC$. Thus, we can construct a triangle ABE from the sides, drawing $AD \parallel BE$, and then find the point $C$, symmetrically $E$.

V. Prasolov, V. Chuchin

M1222. Let $m > 1$ be a natural number, $s$ the largest integer for which $2^s \leq m$. Prove that a) for any $s + 1$ integers, you can choose several numbers and arrange the signs of the plane and the minute of the thread so that the resulting sum is divisible by $m$;
b) the estimate in part a) is unimprovable: there are also s integers such that no sum of several of them, with any arrangement of signs, is divisible by $m$.

a) Consider all possible “non-algebraic” (i.e., without minutes) sums that can be made from $s + 1$ numbers. Their number is $2^{s+1} - 1 > m - 1$. (Each sum can be specified by a sequence of length $s + 1$ of zeros and ones, in which the $i$th place is 1, if the sum includes the $i$th number; the number of sequences is $2^{s+1}$, the sequence of only zeros must be eliminated.) If some two sums give the same remainders when divided by $m$, then their difference is the desired “algebraic” amount. If all remainders are different, then they take all possible $m$ values ​​$0, 1, ..., m - 1$, and the sum giving remainder 0 is the required one.

b) Here is an example of s integers such that no algebraic sum of some of them is a multiple of $m$: 1, 2, 4, ..., $2^{s-1}$. Indeed, any such sum according to the model does not exceed $1 + 2 + 4 + ... + 2^{s-1} \leq m - 1$. On the other hand, these sums cannot be equal to 0: if $2^t$ is the smallest term of such a sum, then the entire sum will be divisible by $2^t$, but will not be divisible by $2^{t+1}$.

V. Lev
