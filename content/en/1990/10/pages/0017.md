Mathematical ratio

The outstanding German mathematician Ludwig Bieberbach gave a lecture in 1934 on “The Structure of Personality and Mathematical Creativity.” The purpose of the lecture is to show “... using the example of my science, mathematics, the influence of the folk spirit, descent and racial affiliation on the style of creativity. For national socialities, this statement, however, does not require any proof at all...”

Bieberbach divides mathematicians into two types - formalists and intuitives. Using a psychological term, he speaks of the S- and J-types of thinking: “Masterly mastery of the technical apparatus and congruence with understood... is inherent in the unchanging and inorganic S-tine.” This type, according to Bieberbach, is characteristic of French Jewish mathematicians: “... the abst-rationist thinkers of Jewish origin, carriers of the S-type, were so able to reshape the axiomatiku that they turned it into a kind of intellectual cabaret.”

As an illustration, Bieberbach compares the definitions of a complex number in Cauchy and Gauss. Cauchy defines it as an ordered pair of real numbers and introduces arithmetic operations on the set of such pairs. Gauss's definition is more geometric (he was not concerned with problems of justification at that moment). Bieberbach interprets this distinction as a conflict of styles of mathematical creativity, reflecting the confrontation between Germanic and Romanesque
