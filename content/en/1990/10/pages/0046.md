Experiment with IR rays

V. MAYER, S. MAYER

What do a remote temperature meter have in common with a laser scalpel, an electron-optical converter and a gas analyzer? These seemingly completely different devices are united by the use of infrared (IR) rays.

Discovered in 1860 by the English astronomer and optician W. Hermel, IR measurement is now widely used in science and industry, medicine and military equipment, criminology and everyday life. Here are a few devices. Photographing the landscape in IR rays significantly increases the information content of sigma images.

Infrared homing heads are an integral element of combat missiles of various types - from man-portable anti-aircraft missile systems to anti-satellite weapons. IR rays allow you to see in complete darkness. Using infrared absorption spectra, it is possible to conduct a subtle analysis of the state and structure of a substance. IR radiation from powerful lasers rejects and welds metal.

Many years ago «Kvant» already talked about experiments with infrared rays *). However, the modern electronic element base makes it possible to study some properties of IR radiation using simpler means and at a deeper level. You can carry out the proposed experiments on your own or in a physical circle.

As a source of IR rays, it is best to use an LED operating in accordance with the range of electromagnetic waves.

As you well know, a source of IR radiation can be any body whose temperature exceeds the temperature of the surrounding bodies. An accumulation lamp, for example, would be suitable. But it emits a constant-intensity stream of IR rays, which means that the receiver will have to use a rather complex DC amplifier and a corresponding measuring device. A significant benefit can be achieved if the flow of IR radiation is modulated in intensity in one way or another. Then in the receiver it will be possible to use a simple amplifier of the sound part, loaded to a loudspeaker, and the intensity of the radiation can be judged by the volume of the sound.
