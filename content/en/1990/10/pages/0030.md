F1235. A long sigland is made up of identical light bulbs connected to a pair of wires at a distance of 1 m from each other (Fig. 1). The resistance of 1 m of wire is 0.2 Ohm, the light bulb in the siglyand is 100. What current does the siglyand consume from a 2.5 V source?
Let's increase the source voltage by 0.1 V. How much will the power that turns into heat in the sections of wires that connect the source to the first light bulb increase?
The current-voltage characteristic of the light bulb is shown in Fig. 2.

Problems with infinite circuits of resistors are widely known, but in our case everything is more complicated - after all, the further the light bulb is from the source, the less it is accumulated, which means its resistance is less. Nevertheless, with reasonable procedures, this problem can be solved without going beyond the scope of the school curriculum.

Firstly, this circuit can still be considered infinite (make an assessment yourself - just consider the decrease in the resistance of “cold” lamps). Secondly, it is clear that the current of the lamp is many times greater than the current of one lamp at the same voltage; Taking this into account and in the right place, we will use it for the management process.

Let us find the dependence of the sigland current $I$ on the voltage $U$ applied to it. To do this, we will add one more link between the source and the sigland, and increase the source voltage by $\Delta U$ so that the voltage on the sigland remains the same (Fig. 3). Then
$$I(U + \Delta U) = I(U) + i(U),$$
where $i(U)$ is the current of one lamp at voltage $U$. The resistance of a piece of wire is much less than that of the lamp heating, so the value of $\Delta U$ is small, and the change in current can be expressed through an arbitrary function $I(U)$:
$$\Delta I = \frac{dI}{dU} \Delta U.$$
But
$$\Delta U = r(I(U) + i(U)) \approx rI(U),$$
poset can be written
$$rI(U) \frac{dI}{dU} = i(U).$$
The left side of this equality is expressed through the arbitrary squared value $I(U)$:
$$rI(U) \frac{dI}{dU} = \frac{d(rI^2(U))/2}{dU}.$$
