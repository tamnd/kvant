4. As a result of the collision of the upper ball with the wall, it (and therefore the entire “dumbbell”) will receive some impulse $\vec{P}$, perpendicular to the wall (Fig. 17). We will find its value from the law of conservation of energy for an absolutely elastic impact:
$$W_0 = W_1 + W_2$$
or
$$2 \frac{P_0^2}{2m} = \frac{1}{2m} \left( (P - P_0)^2 \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 + \right. \\
\left. + \left( \frac{P}{2} - P_0 \right)^2 \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 \right) + \frac{1}{2m} \left( \frac{1}{2} P_0^2 + \right. \\
\left. + \left( \frac{P}{2} - P_0 \right)^2 \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)^2 \right),$$
where
$$P = 8/3P_0.$$
Here it is taken into account that the impulse component $\vec{P}$ in the direction of the rod is divided equally between both balls, and the component perpendicular to the rod is entirely transferred to the upper ball.

Thus, the “dumbbell” will be reflected from the wall with momentum
$$P' = P - 2P_0 = 2/3P_0.$$
In this case, the center of mass will move with a speed of $v' = 1/3v_0$, and the balls will describe circles around the center of mass, moving along them with a speed of $v = 2\sqrt{2}/3v_0$.

$$l = H + \frac{2H}{n^2} + \frac{2H}{n^4} + \frac{2H}{n^6} + \dots =$$
$$= H + \frac{2H}{n^2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n^2} + \frac{1}{n^4} + \dots \right).$$

The expression in parentheses is the sum of the terms of the geometric progression with value $1/n^2$ equal to $n^2/(n^2-1)$. Then finally
