Hey Biff, let me draw a diagram. Please, can I use these same magnetic waves in my new bird!
These batteries are great. This is the source of energy of the future!

Only Teddy Caper became suspicious when the rocket's flight began. He himself was an amateur magician and immediately figured out the trick. However, he remained silent out of a sense of professional solidarity and only smiled ironically when those present fell silent one after another. The Colonel knew how to perform tricks, and he presented the flight perfectly. He almost managed to convince the audience of the real existence of the Cosmic Waverider before the end of the demonstration. When the model landed and he turned off the current, the spectators crowded around the table.

Thread! - one of the engineers exclaimed with a clear feeling of relief, and everyone laughed.

What a pity! - said the Chief Physicist of the project. “I was hoping that some Cosmic Waves would help us.” Come on, let me try!

First up is Teddy Caper! - Biff announced. “He realized what was going on even when you were all watching the warning light, but he didn’t show it.

Caper put a ring with a black thread on his index finger and began to slowly move back.

“We need to turn on the power first,” Biff reminded.

“I know,” Caper smiled. - But this is the villusion trio - all these tricks and games. First I'll try this trick just for fun, see how to raise and lower the model, and then do the trick with all the attributes.

He began to move his hand back, in a soft and flexible professional movement, almost imperceptible to others. The model rose several feet from the table, then fell down.

The thread snapped,” Caper said.

“You must have been holding it instead of pulling it smoothly,” said Biff, tying up the broken ends of the thread. - Look, I'll show you how it's done.

But when Biff tried to lift the model, the thread failed again, which caused another explosion of the scale and made the colonel blush. Someone reminded me of playing poker.

This was the only mention of poker that evening, for they soon discovered that the thread would only carry all the models when the current was turned on and two and a half volts passed through the jester winding. The model was too heavy when the power was turned off. The thread invariably broke.

“I still thought it was a crazy idea,” said the young man. “We've been off our feet this week showing off toy spaceships to every boy within a thousand miles. Besides, selling them for three dollars a piece when making the model ships cost at least a hundred...

But you still managed to sell a dozen models to people of interest to us? - asked the elderly man.

Please. I managed to sell several of them to BBC offices and to one colonel of the missile forces. Then I gave one to the Bureau of Standards employee. Fortunately, he didn't recognize me. Then to two professors from the university that you showed me.

So now this problem is in their hands, not in ours. Now all we have to do is sit and wait for the results.

What results? When we knocked on the doors of scientific institutes, insisting on demonstrating the effect, scientists showed no interest.
