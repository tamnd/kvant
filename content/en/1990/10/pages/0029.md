Here we have already indicated that gas with density $\rho$ and liquid drops with density $\rho'$ move with the same overall speed; according to the conditions of the problem, $\rho'_0 = \rho_0$.

According to Bernoulli's law (or Newton's second law)

$(\rho + \rho') uS \Delta u = -\Delta p S.$

Let us estimate the change in pressure $\Delta p$ along the pipe. As the droplets evaporate, the density of the gaseous medium will increase, the temperature of the flow will drop, and the speed will decrease (all this will be visible at the end of the solution). Therefore, the maximum change in speed is $|\Delta u|_{\text{max}} = u_0$ (subject to a complete stop of the flow). From the resulting equations we find

$(\rho_0 + \rho') u_0 \cdot \Delta u = -\Delta p,$

where

$|\Delta p|_{\text{max}} = (\rho_0 + \rho') u_0^2 \approx 200$ Pa.

Thus, the most possible change in pressure is three orders of magnitude less than atmospheric pressure. Therefore, the pressure can be considered constant along the flow. Then the temperature of the evaporating drops can also be considered constant (just as the temperature of a boiling kettle at atmospheric pressure is constant; only for liquid nitrogen this temperature is 77 K). Further, from the Mendleyev-Clapeyron law and the condition of constant pressure it follows that the density and temperature of the carrier gas are inversely proportional to each other:

$$\frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \frac{T_0}{T}.$$

Each unit of mass of a substance also carries energy, consisting of the kinetic energy of moving molecules and the potential energy of their interaction. If we consider the gas carrying drops to be ideal, then its specific energy (the energy of the unified mass) is equal to $c_p T$ (for an ideal gas, the interaction energy of its molecules is zero; the index $p$ for the heat capacity indicates constant pressure). The same energy for a gaseous mass that has just evaporated from a drop of temperature $T'$ is equal to $c_p T'$; in the condensed state (in a liquid drop) it is equal to $c_p T' - L$, where $L$ is the specific heat of evaporation, in which the specific potential energy of interaction of molecules. The total energy of a substance crossing any pipe flow per unit time (total energy flow) is the same:

$$c_p T \rho u S + (c_p T' - L) \rho' u S = \text{const.}$$

Here we have ignored the kinetic energy of the macroscopic motion of the mixture $(\rho + \rho') (u^2/2) u S$, since it is significantly less than the thermal energy ($u^2 \ll c_p T, 10^2 \ll 10^3 \times 300 = 3 \cdot 10^5$, where $c_p \approx 10^3$ J/(kg K)).
