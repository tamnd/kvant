Most likely, reader, you have never heard of seafaring. And this is not surprising. Even in the Great Soviet Encyclopedia there is no such word. And this natural phenomenon, although rarer than, say, a tsunami, which has already been recorded about a thousand times, or ball lightning, which has collected more than 1,500 descriptions, deserves attention.

What is seafaring?

This is the name for a strong and short-term disturbance of the sea surface in the zone of an underwater earthquake. Early Portuguese and Spanish navigators used the term maremoto, which can literally be translated as “sea walker,” and Dutch captains called this terrifying phenomenon zeebeben, “terrible sea.” In Russian pilots, the word seasore is used to describe unusual events at sea.

In the World Ocean, at least five so-called massive earthquakes occur every day, i.e. earthquakes of such strength that on land they caused the destruction of buildings, and at sea they lead to sea disasters.

The descriptions of the very people of the sea are full of drama and abound in eloquent details. The Tsunami Catalogs of S. L. Sollovev and C. N. Go, as well as the “Catalogue of Eruptive Volcanoes of the World” by I. I. Gushenko present materials from about two thousand descriptions of geophysical cataclysms in the World Ocean.*) From this Kolyuchev we displayed about two hundred descriptions of sea disasters, in which interesting and important information was discovered. These materials were used as the basis for the reconstruction of the conditional “average” sea basking.

So, we offer the reader synthesized impressions of a generalized eyewitness to the sea disaster.

...The mirror-smooth surface of the sea, with complete lack of wind, became covered with mounds. These bumps didn’t go anywhere, but they didn’t stand still either. They quickly grew to the level of the fingers and also brought in fallen trees, forming a deep funnel in the place of the recent mound.

The vibrations occurred quickly, and my eyes were filled with fish from these extraordinary boiling waves that filled the entire visible space of the sea. The surface of the water bubbled and jumped up, like a boiling bed in a hot saltworks. (Such impressions are observed on the roadstead of Jakarta, 1722)

...The ship tossed and rocked on these bouncing waves. Their steepness reached the steepness of the most severe storm waves, the altitude did not exceed 20 m. The pitching motion was so strong that, after turning several times, the compass card flew off the tip. (Description 1917, Sulawesi Island.)

...All passengers and crew instantly found themselves on deck. The bright sun and complete calm only intensified the direction of this terrifying spectacle of the enraged sea. Less than a minute passed, and there was no longer any strength to resist the monstrous leap, which either weakened or intensified again.

Day oscillations during an underwater earthquake occur predominantly with infrasonic frequencies of the order of 1–20 Hz, the size of the oscillating day area (about 10–100 km) always significantly exceeds the average depth of the World Ocean, equal to 4 km, and the acceleration of day oscillations can reach and even exceed the acceleration of the gravity force.

The pilot plant provides the required range of frequencies and accelerations, and the presence of a movable disk with a diameter of 20 cm in a tank with a diameter of 50 cm at the appropriate water depth made it possible to fulfill the conditions of geometric similarity between the model and the real event.

The effects observed in the experiment were unusual.

Firstly, this is the formation on the surface of the water of a regular structure of unusual building waves, organized into large square cells. Such a structure was observed in the frequency range of oscillations of the day 8-23 Hz at daytime accelerations of the order of g (gravity force acceleration), and the cell sizes, i.e., wavelengths, were 2-13 cm.

Secondly, experiments showed the possibility of a gigantic amplification of oscillations at these frequencies: with an amplitude of oscillation of the additional disk of 0.5-1.5 mm, the amplitude of the building waves reached 20-45 mm, i.e., the amplification factor of the oscillations was no less than 30.

Thirdly, a pattern was discovered of a decrease in cell size (wavelength) b with a decrease in the day oscillation period T.

Fourthly, to obtain the wave structure, a constant correspondence between the wavelength b and its height H was established.

So, the results are clear. But what do they mean?

A theoretical description of the symmetry of the diametric structure of a scale is difficult. However, it was still possible to construct a qualitative theory that satisfactorily describes the results of experiments on modeling seaquakes. Its essence is as follows.

Gravitational waves on water obey the law of dispersion, according to which the square of the wave frequency is figuratively proportional to the wavelength (in other words, as the wave period decreases, its length decreases). The coefficient of proportional service is a constant value $g$ - the acceleration of gravity. In the case of gravity-capillary waves, a term is added to the dispersion law that takes into account surface tension and fluid density.*)

It turned out that when describing oscillatory processes in a liquid, the coefficient proportional $g$ can no longer be considered constant; in addition to $g$, it is necessary to take into account the additional acceleration created by the oscillations of the amide. Since this additional acceleration periodically changes in time, conditions arise for periodic rocking of the surface under the influence of a periodic disturbing force. Such processes are called parametric resonance.**

Moretrising and...
"Flying Dutchman"

There is one more, perhaps the most typical, especially sea-trissania - this is that atavistic inexplicable fear that grips those in distress. And was it not under the influence of such a general psychosis in printing that the sailors of the Middle Ages left their ship in convulsions at sea? And then he wandered across the ocean, abandoned by people and terrifying the crews of oncoming ships. Isn't it a good version of The Flying Dutchman?

Now let's remember the results of one famous experiment in the field of physical acoustics. The popular American physicist Robert Wood performed experiments with a giant pipe that filled the organ pipes, but emitted infrasound. It turned out that vibrations of the impact with a frequency of approximately 7 Hz caused severe anxiety, attacks of incomprehensible fear and even panic in people in the radiation zone. This effect was detected not only in the laboratory, but even outside the building among some street passers-by, dumbfounded by their own emotions. The deleterious effect on the human body of vibrations at infrasonic frequencies has been repeatedly confirmed.

Let's return to sea-triscension. The characteristic current parts of the ground vibrations in the earthquake center are in the range from 1 to 20 Hz. As theory and experiments show, the wave structure on water, generated by fluctuations in the day, is characterized by a frequency that is half the pumping frequency, i.e., the frequency of the driving force. This means that the frequencies of wave oscillations during seatrision should lie in the range from 0.5 to 10 Hz. But it is precisely this range that contains parts that have been well studied for their depressive effect on the human body.

A ship caught in a marine zone can be imagined to be inside a giant vertical pipe with a diameter of about 20 km, which emits powerful infrasound at the most dangerous depressive frequencies. And such terrible “music” continues for ten and even hundreds of seconds. It is clear that such a “sound” of the sea may well cause a collective psychosis among the crew, especially if the reasons for this phenomenon are unknown to them.
