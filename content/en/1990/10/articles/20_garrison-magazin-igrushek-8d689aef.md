TOY STORE
(fantasy story)

G. GARRISON (USA)

Since there were almost no adults in the crowd, and Colonel Biff Houghton was over six feet tall, he could clearly see every detail of the toy on display. The kids—and most of the parents—looked at the counter, eyes wide open. And only Biffa Houghton was too skilled a person to feel awestruck. The only reason he stayed in the store was the desire to find out how the toy works and what makes it fly.

“All this is explained in detail in the instructions,” said the seller, holding high a brightly colored brochure, open to a four-color diagram. “You all know that a magnet can attract different objects, and I’m sure you also know that the Earth itself is a huge, huge magnet - that’s why the compass needle always points to the light.” Well... The Amazing Atomic Cosmic Wavemaker relies on these cosmic waves. The magnetic waves of the Earth are completely invisible to us, and they will arbitrate everything, even ourselves. The amazing Atomic Waverunner floats on these waves, like a ship floats on the waves of the ocean. Now look...

Everyone's eyes were glued to the salesman as he placed the brightly colored model of a rocket ship on the counter and took a step back. The model was made of stamped metal and seemed no more suitable for flight than a can of stew, which it closely resembled in appearance. There were no propellers or rocket nozzles on the multi-colored surface of the model. She dug around on three rubber wheels and a double insulated wire came out of the back of the rocket. This white wire was stretched across the entire black surface of the counter and ended in a small control panel that the seller held in his hand. A signal light, a voltage regulator and a power button are all that are located on the control panel of the remote control.

“I press the button, and the current rushes to the Wave Receiver,” said the seller.

There was a slight click, and the signal light began to send out uniform flashes of light: flashed - went out - flashed - went out. The salesman then began to slowly turn the voltage regulator.

- The Wave Generator must be handled very carefully, since here we are dealing with cosmic forces...

A friendly “Ah!” burst out from the audience as the Cosmic Wavetrooper began to vibrate and then slowly rose into the air. The salesman walked back into the store, and the toy began to rise higher and higher, gently rocking on the invisible waves of magnetic forces supporting it. The direction was then removed and the model slowly lowered onto the counter.

- Only seventeen dollars and ninety-five cents! - the young man announced and put a price tag with large numbers on the counter. - Only 17.95 for a complete set of the Atomic Miracle, including the Volnovet control panel, battery and brochure with instructions and description...

As soon as the price indicator appeared on the counter, the spectators immediately began to leave, and the kids, pushing each other, rushed to the working models of electrical wires. The seller's last words were drowned out by the chatter of the departing spectators, and he plunged into sullen silence. He put the remote control

- Look here, sir... - He first looks at the upper part of the model. “You see, at each end there are Cosmic Wave Coils. “He pointed with a pencil to undeclared plastic rods about an inch in diameter, on which were wound—very randomly—several turns of copper wire. Apart from these coiled rods, the inside of the model was completely empty. The windings were connected to each other, the wires stretched to the control panel and disappeared into its silence.
Biff Houghton looked ironically at the model and then glanced at the face of the salesman, who, very indo, completely ignored this sign of distrust.
“There is a battery inside the control panel,” the young man continued, removing the cover from the remote control and showing an ordinary flashlight battery. — The current flows from the battery through the switching device and the signal light to the Wave Generator.
“You want to say,” Biff said, “that the current from this penny battery passes through this cheap rheostat and ends up in the meaningless vitri winding of the model and that this cannot give absolutely any effect.” Now tell me honestly, what actually makes her rise into the air? If I'm paying eighteen greenbacks for this tin, I want to know exactly what I'm buying.
The seller blushed.
“Sorry, sir,” he muttered, stuttering with embarrassment. “I wasn't trying to hide anything from you, sir.” Like any magical toy, Wavelet has a secret, and this secret cannot be revealed until it is purchased. “Here he leaned forward with a conspiratorial look. - But I will tell you one secret. The price of this model is prohibitively high, and no one buys it. The director said that if there are people willing, we can sell the models for three dollars apiece. If you want to purchase a model for this price...
- It’s coming, my boy! - the colonel did not let him finish and threw three dollar bills on the counter. “I’m willing to pay this price for a toy, no matter how it works.” The guys in the laboratory will be delighted with her,” he added, hitting his chest like a cruise missile. - Well, now how does she fly?
The salesman, looking around with a tanned air, moved closer to the colonel and walked past:
- Twine! Or, more accurately, a black thread. It goes from the bow of the ship, up through a small block in the ceiling and back to my hand - tied to this ring, see? As I retreated, the package rose. That's it.
“All well-illusory tricks are very simple,” the colonel said, following the thread of the display with his eyes. - Especially when the audience is distracted by various tricks.
“If you don’t have a black table at hand, black fabric can perfectly replace it,” the young man noted. “Also, this trick works very well against a doorway, as long as the lights in the back room are turned off, of course.”
- Wrap her up, little guy. I wasn't born yesterday. It’s good to understand such things.

Biff Houghton showed off his purchase during a poker game the following Thursday. In total, the guests were specialists in rocket technology, and the activity did not stop for a minute while reading the instructions.

The thread snapped,” Caper said.

“You must have been holding it instead of pulling it smoothly,” said Biff, tying up the broken ends of the thread. - Look, I'll show you how it's done.

But when Biff tried to lift the model, the thread failed again, which caused another explosion of the scale and made the colonel blush. Someone reminded me of playing poker.

This was the only mention of poker that evening, for they soon discovered that the thread would only carry all the models when the current was turned on and two and a half volts passed through the jester winding. The model was too heavy when the power was turned off. The thread invariably broke.

“I still thought it was a crazy idea,” said the young man. “We've been off our feet this week showing off toy spaceships to every boy within a thousand miles. Besides, selling them for three dollars a piece when making the model ships cost at least a hundred...

But you still managed to sell a dozen models to people of interest to us? - asked the elderly man.

Please. I managed to sell several of them to BBC offices and to one colonel of the missile forces. Then I gave one to the Bureau of Standards employee. Fortunately, he didn't recognize me. Then to two professors from the university that you showed me.

So now this problem is in their hands, not in ours. Now all we have to do is sit and wait for the results.

What results? When we knocked on the doors of scientific institutes, insisting on demonstrating the effect, scientists showed no interest.

We have patented these turns and can prove to anyone that when the winding is energized, the weight of the object in the immediate vicinity of these turns becomes less...
- But only by a very small fraction less. And we don't know what causes it. Nobody shows the slightest interest in this issue - after all, we are talking about a small reduction in an increasingly clumsy model, clearly not enough to lift a current generator into the air. All these scientists, immersed in huge problems, didn’t give a damn about the discovery of some crazy person who managed to find a small error in Newton’s law.
- Do you think they'll do it now? - asked the young man, nervously wringing his fingers.
- Of course they will. The tensile strength of the string is selected so that it will be equal every time you try to lift the full weight of the model on it. But it holds up the model with that slight reduction in weight caused by the action of the coils. This will challenge them. No one is forcing them to deal with this problem or solve it. However, the very existence of such a discrepancy will constantly torment scientists, or they know that this effect is opposed by all law and simply cannot exist. They will immediately understand that our magnetic theory is complete nonsense. Or maybe it's not nonsense? We don't know. But they will constantly think about it and rack their brains. Some people start experimenting in their basement—just a hobby, nothing more—to find an explanation. And someday one of them will find what causes the action of these coils, and maybe they will be able to improve them!
— And all the patents are in our hands...
- Exactly. They will engage in research that will drive the problems of jet propulsion with its monstrous energy costs out of their heads and open up the horizons of real space flights before them.
“And then we will become rich - as soon as it comes to industrial production,” said the young man with a cynical smile.
“We’ll all get rich, son,” the elderly man patted him on the shoulder. “Believe me, in ten years you won’t recognize this old world.”
