Experiment with IR rays

V. MAYER, S. MAYER

What do a remote temperature meter have in common with a laser scalpel, an electron-optical converter and a gas analyzer? These seemingly completely different devices are united by the use of infrared (IR) rays.

Discovered in 1860 by the English astronomer and optician W. Hermel, IR measurement is now widely used in science and industry, medicine and military equipment, criminology and everyday life. Here are a few devices. Photographing the landscape in IR rays significantly increases the information content of sigma images.

Infrared homing heads are an integral element of combat missiles of various types - from man-portable anti-aircraft missile systems to anti-satellite weapons. IR rays allow you to see in complete darkness. Using infrared absorption spectra, it is possible to conduct a subtle analysis of the state and structure of a substance. IR radiation from powerful lasers rejects and welds metal.

Many years ago «Kvant» already talked about experiments with infrared rays *). However, the modern electronic element base makes it possible to study some properties of IR radiation using simpler means and at a deeper level. You can carry out the proposed experiments on your own or in a physical circle.

As a source of IR rays, it is best to use an LED operating in accordance with the range of electromagnetic waves.

As you well know, a source of IR radiation can be any body whose temperature exceeds the temperature of the surrounding bodies. An accumulation lamp, for example, would be suitable. But it emits a constant-intensity stream of IR rays, which means that the receiver will have to use a rather complex DC amplifier and a corresponding measuring device. A significant benefit can be achieved if the flow of IR radiation is modulated in intensity in one way or another. Then in the receiver it will be possible to use a simple amplifier of the sound part, loaded to a loudspeaker, and the intensity of the radiation can be judged by the volume of the sound. Industrially produced IR LEDs are specially designed to produce modified IR radiation. Their use, for example, for remote control of household appliances,

The easiest way to assemble an IR ray transmitter is according to a standard multiputibrator circuit using transistors of different conductivities (Fig. 1). With the specified nominal details (hereinafter, resistor resistances are given in kiploms, and capacitor capacitances are given in microfarads), the multiputibrator generates rectangular voltage pulses with a repetition frequency of 200-250 Hz. The HL₁ infrared LED carries both direct and alternating current components. As a result, the LED emission is modulated in intensity.

The schematic diagram of the IR radiation receiver is shown in figure 2. The sensor is a photodiodVD₁, the signal from which is fed to a standard audio amplifier and then converted into sound by a micropolephone BF₁ (type TM-2).

The radio components necessary for the manufacture of devices are fully available. Instead of those indicated in the diagrams, you can use any infrared LED and any photodiod (the latter can be easily made from an old transistor by opening its case). The micropolephone can be replaced with a low-power speaker. It seems to us that there is no need to talk about the designs of the transmitter and receiver: each of you is able to think about and implement them independently. Let us only note that it is convenient to connect the LED with the transmitter, and the photodiod with the receiver, using multi-core flexible conductors 30-50 cm long.

After assembling the devices, check the correct installation. Now place the photodiod opposite the LED at a distance of 20-30 cm and turn on the power of the devices. You will achieve a fairly loud monophonic sound, which will gradually weaken if the distance between the LED and the photodiode is increased to 1-2 m. Return the LED and photodiode to their original position and block the beam of IR rays, for example, with your palm. The sound should stop. If everything happens exactly like this, then the devices are working normally. Otherwise, you will have to check the serviceability of the radio components.

And now - tasks and questions for independent research.

1. Prove that in an optically homogeneous medium, IR rays propagate rectilinearly.

2. Take a small flat mirror and show that IR rays are reflected from it. Measure the angles of incidence and reflection. Compare the results and draw conclusions.

3. Polish a brass flat 20 mm wide and 80 mm long. Bend it so that you get a cylindrical mirror with a radius of approximately 100 mm - use it to focus the IR rays. Try to obtain an image of an IR source, measure the distances from the mirror to the source and its image and calculate the focal length of the mirror. How is it related to the radius of curvature of the mirror?

4. Direct a beam of IR rays onto one of the faces of a triangular glass panel and show that IR rays are refracted when passing from one medium to another.

5. Prove that a converging lens focuses IR rays. Experimentally determine the focal length of the lens for IR radiation. Should I use diaphragms in this and the previous experiments?

6. Sand a small piece of ebonite with sandpaper so that its thickness becomes no more than 0.5 mm. Find out whether ebonite transmits IR rays.

7. Take a glass or glass jar. Assemble the experimental setup as shown in figure 4. Direct a narrow beam of IR rays to the center of the core and onto its flat face. By stirring the photodiode, show that IR rays are partially reflected and partially refracted at the air-glass interface.

8. Under the conditions of the previous experiment, change the angles of incidence $i_1$ and assumptions $i_2$ of the IR rays. Using formula $n=\sin i_1/\sin i_2$, calculate the value of the stack guess exponent for IR radiation.

9. Direct a bunch of IR rays along the radius of the semi-pillar from the side of its convex surface. Measure the corresponding incidence angles and guesses. Which one is bigger?

10. Show that IR rays can experience total internal reflection when passing from an optically more dense to an optically less dense medium (Fig. 5). Measure the limiting angle of total reflection and compare the resulting result with the tabulated value.

11. In the previous attempt, bring a finger moistened with water to the flat face of the half-pillar in the area where the IR beam, which has experienced total internal reflection, falls. How will the intensity of the reflected beam change? Why?

12. Make a light guide from a polished Ortec glass stick with a diameter of approximately 4 mm and a length of approximately 500 mm. To do this, heat the sticks over an electronic one, give it the desired shape and cool so that the shape is preserved. Attach the LED and photodiode to the ends of the resulting light guide using vinyl chloride tubes. Turn on the power of the device and make sure that the IR rays pass perfectly through the manufactured light guide. What explains this phenomenon? What happens if the bent part of the light guide is immersed in liquid?
