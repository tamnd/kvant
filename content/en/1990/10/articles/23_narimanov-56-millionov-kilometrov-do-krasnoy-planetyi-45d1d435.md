Forward to Mars! Only to Mars!
F. Zander

Mars belongs to the terrestrial planets and occupies an intermediate position in size between the Earth and the Moon. Despite its modest size (diameter is half that of Earth, mass is about ten times less than that of Earth), the surface of Mars is more rugged than that of Earth.

One of the intriguing questions in the geology of Mars is estimating the amount of water in the upper layer of the planet's crust. Due to constant negative temperatures on the surface of the planet, its upper layer froze to a depth of up to one and a half kilometers at the equator and five kilometers at the poles, forming a solid shell - the cryolithosphere. Scientists estimate that the cryolithosphere contains at least $5 \times 10^{16}$ tons of water in the form of ice. A hypothetical ocean of cryolithosphere water, evenly distributed over the surface of Mars, would have a depth of several tens of meters.

A Martian year is equal to approximately 700 Earth days; Martian days are only 40 minutes longer than Earth ones; The change of seasons proceeds in the same way as on Earth.

The atmosphere of Mars is very thin and consists mainly of carbon dioxide. The average density of the atmosphere at surface level corresponds to the density of the earth's atmosphere at an altitude of 35 kilometers. The thickness of the atmosphere reaches 250 kilometers, followed by the outer layer and the hydrogen corona.

Goals of manned flights to Mars

Solving the mysteries of Mars is possible by conducting a variety of broad and thorough scientific research from the orbit of the artificial satellite of Mars, on the surface of the planet and in its interior. Further study of Mars in the main areas - geological history, internal structure, processes, surface formation; climate history, the structure of the atmosphere, its evolution and the processes occurring in it; search for traces of the biosphere - possibly automatically by spacecraft without taking into account the cosmonauts. According to planetary scientists, in the next decade, with the help of automatic spacecraft, global mapping of Mars should be carried out, some of the most interesting areas should be studied in detail using a robotic rover moving on the surface, and soil samples from the studied areas should be delivered to Earth for thorough analysis in laboratories using perhaps the means of world science.

At the further stage of Mars research, it is advisable to include cosmonaut researchers in the work on the surface of the planet. A geologist will perform a preliminary circulation of soil samples, a clay scientist will register and conduct an express analysis of interesting atmospheric phenomena, and a biological scientist will search for life forms in hard-to-reach places in the study area. This dramatically increases the efficiency of work, significantly intensifies research, and increases the possibility of making adjustments to the progress of work on the surface of the planet.

Mars is the only planet on which a colony of earthlings can be created already in the first half of the 21st century on the basis of the developed space technology. In this regard, the first manned experiment on Mars should also be considered as the implementation of the first (reconnaissance) stage of colonization of the planets of the Solar System. Further, the short-term (one to two weeks) stay of astronauts on the surface of Mars will be of great importance for the future fate of people. Studying the surrounding muddy world, maintaining an artificial habitat, ensuring safety, and managing complex technical means will contribute to the improvement of the physical, intellectual and spiritual qualities of the individual, and will form more perfect social relations among the colonists.

At the stage of preparation for the implementation of the first manned experiment on Mars, it will be necessary to unite the efforts of the leading countries of the world, primarily the USSR and the USA. The joint work of scientists and engineers technically on this most complex task, dictated by the requirement of size and progress, will inevitably lead to confrontation, presentation in relations between peoples, and will help to ensure that we become a single family.

The following basic provisions can be accepted as initial data when analyzing (within the national stage of the “Together to Mars” competition) various aspects of the experiment:

1. The duration of the experiment is 500-1000 days.
2. The astronaut's stay on the surface of Mars is 10-15 days.
3. The number of experts at the international building is 4-10 people.
4. The number of astronauts speaking on the surface of Mars is 3-6 people.
