We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The tasks published in it are non-standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we promise to indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than December 15, 1990 to the address: 103006, Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant»” No. 10–90” and the numbers of the problem whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1246” or “F1253”. In the column “...sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of the decision review).

Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in two copies along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you are a teacher.

Problems M1246-M1249 were proposed this year at the Leningrad City Olympiad in Mathematics, problem F1253, F1254 and F1257 - at the final stage of the XXIV All-Union Olympiad in Physics.

M1246. Prove that in any infinite arithmetic progression whose terms are natural numbers, there are numbers with the same sum of digits.

S. Genkin

M1247. Is it possible to cover a plane without overlaps with squares with side lengths 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, ..., using a square of size no more than a) ten times, 6) once?

D. Fomin

M1248. In a segment, finding several of it entirely.
a) Prove that the left halves of these segments cover at least half of the original segment.
b) Prove that if any half of each of these segments is discarded - left or right - then the remaining halves cover at least a third of the length of the original segment.

D. Fomin

M1249. There are n>6 cities in the kingdom of Olympia, each two of which are connected by one one-way road. At the same time, it is not possible to travel from every city to any other without violating traffic rules.
a)* Prove that the king can choose one of the cities and, by changing the direction of movement on all roads entering and leaving it, ensure that it is possible to travel from any city to any other. c) Is this statement true for n=6?

I. Itenberg

M1250*. Let $x_1, x_2, x_3, ..., x_n$ be positive numbers. Prove that

$$\frac{x_1}{x_2+x_3} + \frac{x_2}{x_3+x_4} + \frac{x_3}{x_4+x_5} + ... + \frac{x_n}{x_1+x_2}$$

a) greater than ($\sqrt{2}-1$)n;
b) more than 5n/12;
b) not less than n/2 if the sequence $x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$ is monotonic.

L. Kurlyandchik, A. Faibusovich

F1253. A primordial vessel with water is placed on two sides, spaced at a distance of $L$ from each other. Above the vessel, on a crossbar, a piece of lead with mass $M$ is suspended by threads at a distance $l$ from the center of the vessel (Fig. 1). The reaction forces of the supports are equal to $N_1$ and $N_2$. The thread is extended so that the lead is immersed in water. What will be the reaction forces of the supports after this? The density of lead is $n$ times the density of water.

A. Zilberman

F1255. The surface of the lifeless plane is covered with a thick layer of frozen coal. It is proposed to create an atmosphere of pure oxygen on it, decomposing the coal waste into carbon and oxygen. How long will it take to do this if you decompose $10^6$ moles in 1 second? It is necessary to obtain a pressure of $p=0,2$ atm. Consider that a temperature of $T=200$ K will be established at the surface, at which the evaporation of carbon dioxide can be neglected. The mass of the plans is $M=7,5 \cdot 10^{22}$ kg (approximately equal to the mass of the Moon), radius $R=1750$ km.

D. Molleevtsev

F1256. A coil containing $N=2000$ turns and having an inductance $L=5$ H is wound on a stake core with high magnetic permeability. A resistor with a resistance of $R=200$ Ohm is connected to the ends of the coil. A battery with a voltage of $U_0=1,5$V will be connected between one end of the coil and turn number 300, counting from this end. What points will be accurate on both parts of the coil after a time of $t=0,1$ s after connection? The battery is considered ideal, magnetic flux dissipation is neglected.

Z. Rafailov

F1257. The period of free oscillations of the load, the highest outside, turns out to be equal to $T_0$. The load with the spring was placed on a rough horizontal surface (Fig. 2) and, by mixing the load to the left and right, the width of the region within which the load could be in equilibrium under the influence of the friction force and the elastic force of the spring (the so-called stagnation zone) was changed - it turned out to be equal to $2a$. In the next experiment, the load was displaced from the stagnation zone to a distance significantly exceeding $a$, and oscillations of the load were observed. What is the oscillation period?

Now let's set up the experiment differently. Whenever the load is in the extreme left position when swinging, the load is hit with a hammer (Fig. 3), and the speed of the load becomes equal to $v_0$. What will be the amplitude of the steady oscillations of the load?

Assuming that the amplitude of steady-state oscillations is significantly greater than the width of the stagnation zone, determine how much the period of steady-state oscillations differs from the period of free oscillations $T_0$.

S. Kozel
