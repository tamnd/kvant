XVI All-Russian
Olympics
SCHOOLCHILDREN

According to established tradition, on spring days
final school holidays
standard of the All-Russian Olympics for schoolchildren
in mathematics and physics. This year the composition is
The campaigns were held in the cities of Vladimir, Krasno-
Dare, Penza and Ulan-Ude.

Mathematics
The competition took place over two days, each
Each day the participants were given 4 tasks.
After each problem is formed, the number of points awarded for its complete solution is indicated in parentheses.

9th grade
First day
1. Among twenty five identical coins, 3 are fake and 22 are real. All real coins have equal weights. All counterfeit coins also have equal weights, with the counterfeit coin being lighter than the real one. How can you find six real coins using two weighing cups without weights? (6)
2. The numbers a, b, c, d, p, q are related accordingly
ab + cd = 2pq. Prove that if ac ≥ p² > 0, then bd ≤ q². (7)
3. Given a chessboard will place n×n, n ≥ 3. In one move, select any of the pieces pointing to figure 1 anywhere on the dock and change the color of all its cells to the opposite. Is it possible to change the color of all dock cells to the opposite color in a few moves? (14)
4. There are 1990 points in a square on side 12. Prove that there is an equilateral triangle with side 11 containing at least 498 of these points. (13)

Second day
5. Find all positive values ​​of a for which both roots of the equation a²x² + ax + 1 - 7a² = 0 are integers. (6)
6. One 1×1 corner square is cut from an n×n square. In what direction is the number of equal triangles that can be cut into the resulting figure? (7)
7. Circles S₁ and S₂ intersect at points A and B, the center O of circle S₁ lies on circle S₂. The chord AC of the circle S₁ intersects the circle S₂ at point D as shown in figure 2. Prove that the segments OD and BC are perpendicular. (11)
8. There are no parallel segments among the sides and diagonals of the convex pentagon ABCDE. An arrow is drawn on side AB, directed towards the point of intersection of straight lines AB and CE. An arrow is drawn on side BC, directed towards the point of intersection of lines BC and AD. Arrows are drawn in a similar way on the sides CD, DE, EA. Prove that two of the drawn arrows are directed towards one of the vertices of the pentagon. (16)

$$\sqrt{2b(a+b)} + \sqrt{bc(b+c)} + \sqrt{ca(a+c)} > \sqrt{(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)}.$$ (5)

2. There are 100 points inside the circle, none of which coincides with the center of the circle and no two of which lie on the same radius.

a) Prove that there is a circular sector with angle $2\pi/11$ radians in which 10 of the given points lie. (7)

6) Is it true that there is a circular sector with an angle of $2\pi/11$ radians, in which there are 11 points? (4)

The radii that bound a sector are considered to belong to that sector.

3. Inside a convex quadrilateral there are four circles, each of which touches two sides of the quadrilateral and two circles as shown in figure 3. It is known that a circle can be inscribed in a quadrilateral. Prove that at least two of the given circles are equal. (10)

4. Triples of numbers $(x_n, y_n, z_n)$, $n=1,2,...$ are constructed according to the following rule:

$$x_1 = 2, y_1 = 4, z_1 = \frac{6}{7};$$

$$x_{n+1} = \frac{2x_n}{x_n^2 - 1}, y_{n+1} = \frac{2y_n}{y_n^2 - 1},$$

$$z_{n+1} = \frac{2z_n}{z_n^2 - 1}, n \geqslant 1.$$

a) Prove that the indicated process of constructing tracks can be continued indefinitely. (4)

6) Is it possible at some step to obtain a track of numbers $(x_k, y_k, z_k)$ for which the equality $x_k + y_k + z_k = 0$ is issued? (10)

Second day

5. Several positive numbers are written on the board so that the sum of all their products is equal to 1. Prove that you can erase one number so that the sum of the remaining numbers is less than $\sqrt{2}$. (7)

6. Circles $S_1$ and $S_2$ intersect at points $A$ and $B$, and the center $O$ of circle $S_2$ lies on circle $S_1$. The chord $OC$ intersects the circle $S_2$ at the point $D$ as shown in figure 4. Prove that the point $D$ is the intersection point of the bisserris triangle $ABC$. (9)

7. Is it possible to place the natural numbers from 1 to 21 in the circles shown in figure 5 so that in any row except the first, each number is equal to the modulo difference of the two numbers above it (i.e. $c = a - b$)? (12)

8. Given $n$ different vectors with the following properties: for some natural $p$ the modulo sum of any $p$ of the given vectors is equal to the modulo sum of the remaining $n - p$ vectors.

a) Prove that if $p \neq \frac{n}{2}$, then the sum of these vectors is zero. (8)

6) Is it true that the sum of all given vectors is zero if $p = \frac{n}{2}$? (4)

11th grade
First day

1. Task 1 for ninth grade. (6)

2. Prove that for an arbitrary triangle the inequality $a \cdot \cos A + b \cdot \cos B + c \cdot \cos C \leq p$ is valid, where $a, b, c$ are the sides of the triangle, $A, B, C$ are their opposite angles, $p$ are the half-perimeter. (9)

3. Given are 8 cups with edge 1, arbitrary 24 faces of which are painted white, and the remaining 24 faces are painted black. Prove that these cups can be used to form a cube, on the surface of which there will be equal numbers of white and black squares on side 1. (13)

4. In a circle of radius 1, there is a chord $AB$ whose length is less than $\sqrt{2}$. Taking $l$, trims the given circle (Fig. 6). Using a compass and ruler on the line $l$, construct a point $C$ so that the segments $ED$ and $AB$ are perpendicular. (E and D are the points of intersection of the segments $CA$ and $CB$ with the circle.) (12)

Second day

5. Find all natural numbers $x, y$ satisfying the equation $7x - 3 \cdot 2y = 1$. (7)

6. Points $D$ and $E$ lie on sides $AB$ and $BC$ of triangle $ABC$, points $K$ and $M$ divide segment $DE$ into three equal parts. The primes $BK$ and $BM$ intersect the side $AC$ at points $T$ and $P$. Prove that $TP \leqslant \frac{1}{3} AC$. (8)

7. At the base of the base $SABCDE$ there is a regular pentagon $ABCDE$. It is known that the radii of the sphere described around the tetradirs $SMAB$, $SMBC$, $SMCD$ are equal. Can we say that this prism is correct? (11)

8. The cells of the direct line of the new table contain positive numbers. For each line, the sum of all the numbers written in it is found, and then the product $P$ of all such sums is calculated. Prove that if you rearrange the numbers in each column in non-decreasing order so that the name of the number is in the first row and the largest in the last, then the product $P$ for the new table will not exceed the original value. (14)

Physics

On the theoretical type, each participant of the Olympida was offered 4 tasks (for 4 hours). As the results of the test showed, for ninth-graders the third problem turned out to be the most difficult - only two out of ninety participants were able to solve it. Very few students of the same grade completed their fourth task. The remaining problems were solved correctly by 25 to 70% of the participants.

The experimental round of the Olympics included 2 problems (which also took 4 hours to solve).
