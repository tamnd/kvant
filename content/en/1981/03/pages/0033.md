F634. Rod AB with length $l$ and mass $m$ rests on the wall with its lower end and is held in an inclined position with the help of a thread (see figure). The thread is tied to the wall at point $C$, and to the rod at point $D$ such that $|AD| = \frac{1}{3}|AB|$. The smiles formed by the thread and the rod with the wall are equal to a and β, respectively. Find the possible values ​​of the coefficient of friction between the rod and the wall.

Three forces act on the rod: the thread tension force $\vec{T}$, the tension force $m\vec{g}$ and the reaction force from the wall $\vec{R} = \vec{N} + \vec{F}_{\tau}$ ($\vec{N}$ is the normal reaction force, $|F_{\tau}| \leq \mu |\vec{N}|$ is the friction force). In the equilibrium state of the rod, the sum of the moments of these forces relative to any point is zero. For the condition to be satisfied, the lines of action of the force $\vec{R}$ must pass through the intersection point of the linear action of the force $\vec{T}$ and $m\vec{g}$ (the moments of force $\vec{T}$ and $m\vec{g}$ relative to this point are equal to zero).

Depending on the correspondence between α and β, the intersection point of the linear force $\vec{T}$ and $m\vec{g}$ may lie: a) above the perpendicular $AM_0$ to the wall (point $M_1$ on figure), b) below this perpendicular (point $M_2$), c) on it (point $M_0$). The corresponding friction force is directed either along AC up ($\vec{F}_a$), or along AC down ($\vec{F}_b$), or equal to zero. Let us consider each of these cases separately a) The equilibrium condition of the rod:

$$T \cos \alpha + F_a - mg = 0, \quad N - T \cos \alpha = 0$$

(1)—the sum of the projections of all forces on the $Y$ and $X$ axes, respectively, is equal to zero;

$$d_1 mg = d_2 T,$$ or $$\frac{mg l}{2} \sin \beta = \frac{Tl}{3} \sin (\alpha + \beta)$$

(2) (see figure). From (1) and (2) we have

$$\mu_a \geq \frac{F_a}{N} = \frac{2}{3} \frac{\sin (\alpha + \beta)}{\sin \alpha \sin \beta} - \frac{1}{tg \alpha} = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{2}{tg \beta} - \frac{1}{tg \alpha} \right).$$

(Case a) is carried out under the condition $2tg \alpha > tg \beta$.) b) Having written down the equilibrium conditions, we find:

$$\mu_6 \geq \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{1}{tg \alpha} - \frac{2}{tg \beta} \right).$$

(In this case, the condition $tg \beta > 2tg \alpha$ corresponds.) In this case, the rod is in equilibrium for any value of $\mu_b (2tg \alpha = tg \beta)$.

Thus, with arbitrary correspondence between angles α and β, the rod is in equilibrium at

$$\mu \geq \frac{1}{3} \left| \frac{1}{tg \alpha} - \frac{2}{tg \beta} \right|.$$
