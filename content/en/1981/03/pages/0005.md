To achieve a particular goal, one desire is not enough. You still have strong knowledge, to see things that interest you in the environment, to be able to observe their interrelationships. This is, of course, difficult. But Soviet people, and even more so Soviet scientists, have a good principle: the more difficult, the more interesting. Don’t give in to difficulties, don’t be upset by failures.

Remember, for example, the famous Russian mathematician Alexander Mikhailovich Lyapunov. In his youth, he received from his teacher, another famous mathematician Pafnutya Lvovich Chebyshev, a very difficult problem. This problem is to calculate the stability of the equilibrium figures of a rotating fluid. I wanted to approach the problem of the formation of celestial bodies that make up the Solar System. If the movement of the fiery-liquid ball, as the Sun seemed to be, was unstable, a part could be torn off from it and give rise to new bodies - planets.

Lyapunov could not cope with this task for a long time. His attempts, one after another, were unsuccessful. Only twenty years after he began to think about this question, the scientist finally gave a solution to the problem that brought him worldwide service. In the process of work, he had to create many new ideas and understandable ones, hitherto unknown. He derived a general theory of stability of motion, reconstructed the imperfect theory of gravitational potential that existed at that time, laid the foundations for the future theory of integral equations, and much, much more. This example is a convincing confirmation of how important it is to believe in yourself, to be purposeful, and to strive to keep going.
