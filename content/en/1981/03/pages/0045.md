O. Ovchinnikov

What will the diameter show?

A diameter meter rests on a smooth horizontal surface. A force $F_1=5\text{H}$ is applied to one end of the diameter meter, and a force $F_2=8\text{H}$ (Fig. 1) is applied to the other. What will the diameter show?

Problems of similar content can be found in problem books and textbooks on physics. The answer, as a rule, is given as follows: the diameter will show 5\text{H}. This can be confirmed by the following reasoning. If forces equal to 5\text{H} are applied to both ends of the diameter meter, then the diameter meter shows 5\text{H}. If one of the forces is increased by 3\text{H}, then the balance is disturbed, and the diameter meter, showing 5\text{H}, begins to wonder in the direction of action of the larger force with acceleration $a=(F_2-F_1)/m$, where $m$ is the mass of the diameter meter.

It would seem that everything is obvious. However...

Let's look at this issue in detail. Let's start with this simple task:

A spring with stiffness $k$ lies on a smooth horizontal table. A force $F_0$ is applied to one end of the spring, directed

$$m_x = \frac{m_0}{l_0} x,$$

where $m_0$ is the mass of the spring, $l_0$ is the length of the spring in an unstretched state. The acceleration with which all springs will be surprised under the influence of force $F_0$ is equal to

$$a = \frac{F_0}{m_0}.$$

The section of the spring we are considering with length $x$ will be amazed at the same acceleration. This section is subject to a force from the rest of the spring equal to the mass of the spring $F_x$, arising in the flow at a distance $x$ from the free end of the spring. Therefore, the value of the spring force $F_x$ is equal to

$$F_x = m_x a = \frac{F_0 x}{l_0}.$$

We got an interesting result. Firstly, the spring force that arises in this case is equal to its coordinate with an unstretched spring, or the length of the section cut off by this section. Secondly, the value of this force does not depend on the mass of the spring. The spring mass can take values ​​other than zero. In the case of $m_0=0$ the acceleration acquired by the spring goes to infinity.
