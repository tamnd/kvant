⟦folio 24⟧

Problem
quantum

M671—M675; F683—F687

M671. In a cyclic quadrilateral, one diagonal bisects the other. Prove that the square of the length of the first diagonal is equal to half the sum of the squares of the lengths of all sides of the quadrilateral.

R. Mazov

M672*. Let $a$ be a natural number such that $2^a-2$ is divisible by $a$ (for example, $a=3$). Let us define the sequence $(x_n)$ by the conditions
$$x_1=a, x_{k+1}=2^{x_k}-1.$$
Prove that $2^{x_k}-2$ is divisible by $x_k$ for any $k$.
Walter Janous (Jisbruck, Austria)

M673. Three washers $A, B, C$ lie on the plane at the vertices of the triangle. The hockey player chooses one of them and walks along it so that it passes between the other two and stops at some point.
a) Show how, after heel strikes, puck $C$ can return to its place, and pucks $A$ and $B$ can be swapped.
b) Can all three pucks $A, B, C$ return to their original positions after 25 hits?

A. Razborov

M674. The points $A_1, B_1$ and $C_1$ are taken on the sides $BC, AC$ and $AB$ of the acute triangle $ABC$, respectively. It is known that the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle $ABC$ coincides with the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$. Prove that triangles $ABC$ and $A_1B_1C_1$ are similar.

D. Izaak

M675. A system of weights is a set of natural numbers from which two different sets with the same sum can be extracted (for example, the numbers 24, 23, 22, 20, 17, 11 form a system of weights, but the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 do not form: $2+3+4=1+8$). Prove that from numbers less than 1000 it is possible to select a system of weights consisting of: a) 10 numbers, b) 11 numbers.
