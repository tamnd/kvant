⟦folio 37⟧

Quests

1. In the example shown at figure, some numbers are replaced with asterisks. Recover the missing numbers.

2. In what number system is the number 1111 a perfect square? And the number 11111?

3. From nine digits 1,2,3,...,8,9, make three three-digit numbers that are related as a) 1:3:5; b) 1:2:3.
How many solutions does problem 6) have?

4. The sum of the digits of a three-digit number, all of whose digits are different, is a multiple of 7; the number itself is also divisible by 7. Find all such numbers.

5. The angle given is 54°. Using only a number, divide it into three equal parts (that is, find the points so that the rays passing through the vertex of a given angle and these points divide it into three equal parts).

6. Two circles, each of length 0.5 m, touch side BC of rectangle ABCD (|AB|=1 m, |BC|=2 m) at the same point: one from the outside, the other from the inside (see figure). The circles begin to roll along the sides of the rectangle (being in the same plane as it). How many revolutions will each circle make by the time it returns to its original position?

7. There are seven coins around the circumference, heads up. In one move you are allowed to turn over any five coins lying in a row. Is it possible to ensure that all coins are heads up?

These tasks were offered to us
F. Bartenev, A. Cabrizon, Y. Mett,
A. Shvetsov, L. Yakovleva
