absurdities

One day, mom and Katya went to visit, and Petya, anticipating a pleasant evening, climbed onto the top shelf for a volume of fascinating stories about Sherlock Holmes. While taking out a book, he accidentally knocked a vase off the shelf, which broke into pieces. Petya's good mood was somewhat dampened, but, deciding not to get upset in advance, he swept the shards into a corner and settled comfortably with a book on the sofa. Buddy sat down next to him. As soon as he opened the book, Petya immediately forgot about everything in the world and plunged headlong into the world of mysterious ideas that Sherlock Holmes so deftly unraveled with the help of his detective method. Katya’s indignant exclamation brought him back to reality:

- Mom, look, Petka broke the vase that I gave you!

- Did you see it? - Petya asked out of habit. - Proof that it's me!

- What is there to prove here? - Katya shrugged. - Only you and Buddy were at home. Let's say that it wasn't you who broke the vase; That means Druzhok broke it. But you can’t say that Druzhok was able to get to the top shelf. It would be absurd: Buddy is still a dog, not a cat. So, you broke the vase, there is no one else.

“Yes,” thought Petya, “you can’t argue with her, her logic is like Sherlock Holmes’s; and there’s no point in arguing - I really did break the vase. I’ll go to Mitya and ask him what the math assignment is.

“Stories,” asked Petya (reading a geometry textbook, which they had just begun to study, seemed to him difficult and boring).
