Applying the correspondence (*) defining the sequence $(a_n)$, we obtain

$$1 + a_1 + a_2 + \ldots + a_{2m-1} - a_{2m} = 0,$$

where

$$a_{k+1} = a_{2m} = 1 + 1 + 2 + \ldots + 2^{m-1} = 2^m,$$

— the statement in case a) has been proven.

6) $n = k + 1 \neq 2^m$.

Let us write $k+1$ in the form

$$k+1 = 2^r \cdot s,$$

where $2^r$ is the maximum power of two that divides $k+1$, and $s$ is an odd number greater than one.

According to the induction assumption, the divisors $d$ of the number $k+1$ (other than $k+1$), for which $a_d \neq 0$, are only the numbers 1, 2, $2^2$, ..., $2^r$.

From matching (*)

$$1 + a_1 + a_2 + \ldots + a_{2r-1} - a_{2r} - a_{k+1} = 0,$$

that is

$$a_{k+1} = 1 + 1 + 2 + \ldots + 2^{r-1} - 2^r = 0,$$

- and statement (1) in case 6) is also proven.

V. Abramovich

**M626. Each side of a convex quadrilateral is divided into 8 equal parts. Matching division points on opposite sides are connected to each other, and the resulting cells are colored in a checkerboard pattern. Prove that the sum of the areas of these cells is equal to the sum of the areas of white cells.**

Since the midpoints of a convex quadrilateral are the vertices of a parallel frame, its midlines (red segments at figure) are bisected at the intersection point. Applying this message to the formed quadrilaterals, we obtain the same statement for their midlines, etc., so that each of the segments connecting the division points on opposite sides of a given convex quadrilateral turns out to be divided into eight equal parts.

Let us now consider the “double cell” of the partition (size $2 \times 2$; diagonals at figure are highlighted in green). It is obvious that the sums of the areas of the blue and white fields of this doubled cell are equal (the areas of the corresponding blue and white triangles with a common vertex and pairwise congruent bases are the same). The statement of the problem follows from this remark.
