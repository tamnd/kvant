On a horizontal section of the road, the distance between the machines is 100 m. What would be the minimum distance between the machines when they are moving?

Let's find the speeds of automatic sections I and II. In section I, work is performed only against blinding. In this area, the energy balance can be written as follows:

$$P = |\vec{F}_1| |\vec{v}_1| = \alpha |\vec{v}_1|^3.$$

(1)

In section II, work against blinding is added:

$$P = |\vec{F}_2| |\vec{v}_2| + m |\vec{g}| \frac{h_2}{S_2/|\vec{v}_2|} = \alpha |\vec{v}_2|^3 + m |\vec{g}| |\vec{v}_2| \frac{h_2}{S_2}.$$

(2)

The slope $h_2/S_2$ is determined from the graph: $h_2/S_2 = \frac{4}{30}$.

From equation (1) $|\vec{v}_1| \approx 20,5$ m/s is found. Therefore, $\tau = \frac{l}{|\vec{v}_1|} \approx 4,9$ p. From equation (2) we find $|\vec{v}_2| = 8,5$ m/s. In time t, the first automaton will cover a distance of $l' = |\vec{v}_2| \tau = 41,6$ m. This is the minimum distance between the automaton.

We recommend that you independently analyze the movement of the machines during the transition from horizontal section I to section II and make sure that taking into account the smooth nature of the change in speed from value $|\vec{v}_1|$ to value $|\vec{v}_2|$ does not change the answer to the problem.

F637. In superconducting coils with inductances $L_1$ and $L_2$, connected in parallel, a current is excited. The inductance $L_1$ of one of the coils decreases to zero. How many times does the current in the circuit and the energy of the system change?

Let us write the equation of state of the gas in the cylinder (Boyle-Mariotte law) when the piston is at a certain depth x under water:

$$p_0 V_0 = p V = ph S,$$

(1)

where p is the pressure in the cylinder, and h is the distance from the bottom of the cylinder to the piston. The piston equilibrium condition at depth x is

$$p S + F = (p_0 + \rho_0 g x) S.$$

(2)

The equilibrium condition of the cylinder is

$$p S = [p_0 + \rho_0 g (x - h)] S + Mg,$$

(3)

where M is the mass of the cylinder.

Solving equations (1), (2), (3) together, we obtain:

$$x = \frac{p_0 V_0 \rho_0 g S + Mg F + F^2 - p_0 SF - p_0 SMg}{\rho_0 g S (F + Mg)},$$

(4)

or, if the cylinder is weightless,

$$x = \frac{p_0 V_0 \rho_0 g S + F^2 - p_0 SF}{\rho_0 g SF}.$$

(5)

Relations (4) and (5) are valid, very rarely, only when the cylinder is completely immersed in water.
