⟦folio 60⟧

Recreation and studies

On the southern coast of Crimea, between Yalta and Alushta, there are numerous buildings of the All-Union Pinera camp Artek. Guys from different parts of our country come to this unique Pinera resourcefulness. Here they separate, gain strength and health, acquire the skills necessary for successful work in their school pinning teams and other sciences, develop new songs, learn to dance, and take part in sports competitions.

However, staying in Artek is not only an active holiday, but also the acquisition of new knowledge. For this purpose, all necessary conditions have been created in the camp. There are excellent, informative performances, various museums are in operation, and well-appointed classrooms have been created for the work of scientific and technical circles.

Summer shifts in Artek last 30 days, winter shifts - 40. The life of young Artek residents is full of many interesting everyday activities. In addition, there are excursions, trips to memorable and historical places in Crimea, and boat trips. Under these conditions, it is not possible to allocate much time for circle classes: five one-and-a-half hour classes in the summer and eight in the winter. Naturally, the leaders of the circle work were faced with the question: how to build it? It is impossible to teach radio electronics, electronic embroidery technology, photography, or the method of astronomical observations in a few hours. It is impossible to impart much systematized knowledge to the children in such a short period of time. Is it then worthwhile to carry out circle work at all?

In Artek they firmly decided - it’s worth it. And the purpose of the circle work was defined as follows: to give an idea, to interest, to provide an opportunity to directly experience what constitutes the essence of this field of science and technology.

What does achieving this goal look like in practice? Together with the head of Artek’s circle work department, N.I. Pervukhinny, we go up to the second floor of the House of Pinerskaya Scientists to the computer technology laboratory. Another lesson is underway. There are two people at the table, in front of them are various computing devices. The head of the circle, Candidate of Technical Sciences E.V. Kadushkin, has a doxy with a kusommen in his hand. He willingly introduces us to the program of club classes and the features of their implementation.

They begin with the fact that the children who have decided to study in the computer science club get acquainted with the history of its development, its role in modern science and the national economy, as well as with the main types of computing devices - from tariff meters to electronic embroidery machines.

Then the children are told about the double number system and the basic arithmetic operations carried out in this system. Theoretical classes are carried out in practical ones: study groups learn to independently perform arithmetic operations on various computing devices.

The next stage is familiarization with the operating principles of computers and their main blocks, as well as the order of command execution and various methods of input and output of information. Related practical work - calculations on electronic keyboard computers using memory.

In conclusion, members of the circle are given an idea about algorithms and programming, about machine language. As a practical example, an algorithm and program are compiled to solve the problem of extracting the square root of any number with a given accuracy. This lesson ends with practical work - solving original examples on electronic keyboard computers (ECVMs).

In general, each lesson is called approximately like this: an introductory theoretical part, a game part, when the leader goes through a number of interesting mathematical examples and jokes with the circles, and practical work.

The occupation of the radio electronics circle, which is led by A. M. Pozharsky, is based on a similar goal. Only here even greater importance is attached to independent practical work. This is not accidental - anyone who is always interested in radio electronics should be familiar with a finger gun, have a good knowledge of the basic safety requirements when working with tools, understand various parts, be able to read and assemble modern circuits.

Club members get acquainted with basic information about modern radio electronics and its theoretical foundations, have time to hold various radio components in their hands during the class, assemble a design telephone with a carbon microphone, look first at a detector, then at a transistor example, and then at such electronic devices as a trigger, multimediatror and time relay.

A distinctive feature of all Artek scientific and technical circles is the competitive nature of the final lesson. In particular, members of the computer technology circle participate in a competition for the fastest, correct solution of original examples on a computer, and members of the radio electronics circle participate in the high-speed assembly of a radio-electronic device proposed by the leader. The winners of the competition and active circle members are awarded diplomas and memorable sovereigns.

V. Komarov
