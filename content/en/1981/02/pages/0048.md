Let us depict the process under consideration in coordinates $p, V$ (Fig. 3). In section 1–2, the pressure remains constant, and the volume doubles ($V_2 = 2V_1$). According to Gay-Lussac's law, the temperature at point 2 will be $T_2 = 2T_1$. In section 2-3, the volume of gas remains constant, but the pressure doubles. According to Charles's law, the temperature at point 3 will be equal to $T_3 = 2T_2 = 4T_1$.

Knowing the gas temperatures at points 1, 2, 3 (according to the conditions at point 1, the gas is under normal conditions, that is, $T_1 = 273$ K), it is not difficult to find the amount of heat supplied to the gas:

in an isobaric process 1-2, the gas received an amount of heat

$$Q_1 = C_p(T_2 - T_1) = (C_V + R)T_1;$$

in an isochoric process 2-3 the amount of heat is given to the gas

$$Q_2 = C_V(T_3 - T_2) = 2C_VT_1;$$

the total amount of heat supplied to the gas is equal to

$$Q = Q_1 + Q_2 = (3C_V + R)T_1 \approx 20 \text{ кДж.}$$

Problem 4 (MPEI, 1977). One mole of gas expands so that its volume during the process is proportional to the pressure: $V = \alpha p$. The gas pressure increases from $p_1$ to $p_2$. Find the coefficient $\alpha$ if the heat capacity of a mole of gas at constant volume is $C_V$ and during

process, the gas is given the amount of heat $Q$.

Using the first law of thermodynamics, we write

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Change in internal energy of gas

$$\Delta U = C_V \Delta T,$$

where $\Delta T$ is the temperature change. In the case of a process in which $V = \alpha p$, the equation of gas state has the form $a^2 = RT$. Then

$$\Delta T = T_2 - T_1 = \frac{\alpha}{R} (p_2^2 - p_1^2)$$

And

$$\Delta U = \frac{C_V \alpha}{R} (p_2^2 - p_1^2).$$

To find the work $A$ performed by the gas, it is convenient to use the graphical method (Fig. 4): the work is equal to the area of ​​the protected area, that is

$$A = \frac{p_1 + p_2}{2} (V_2 - V_1) = \alpha \frac{p_2^2 - p_1^2}{2}.$$

Thus,

$$Q = \Delta U + A = \left( \frac{C_V}{R} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \alpha (p_2^2 - p_1^2).$$

Solving this equation for $\alpha$, we obtain

$$\alpha = \frac{Q}{(C_V/R + 1/2) (p_2^2 - p_1^2)}.$$
