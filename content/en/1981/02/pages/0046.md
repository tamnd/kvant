Law of conservation of energy for thermal processes

When solving problems related to the consideration of thermal processes, the most important role is played by the law of conservation and transformation of energy, called thermodynamics:

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Here $Q$ is the amount of heat transferred to the system, $\Delta U$ is the change in its internal energy and $A$ is the work done by the system against external forces. (In the textbook “Physics 9” this work is designated $A'$; for brevity we will omit the “prime”.)

Internal energy $U$ is a function of thermodynamic parameters (pressure $p$, volume $V$ and temperature $T$) that describe the state of the system, but does not depend on how this state arose. For example, for a monatomic ideal gas

$$U = \frac{m}{\mu} \frac{3}{2} RT,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the gas, $\mu$ is its molar mass, $R$ is the universal gas constant equal to 8.31 J/(mol K).

The work $A$ performed by the system depends, of course, on the nature of the thermal process. So, with a small change in volume $\Delta V$ (when the pressure can be considered constant)

$$A = p\Delta V.$$

The thermodynamic parameters of a system are not independent variables; they are related by an equation of state. In the case of an ideal gas, this is the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation

$$pV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

Let's look at some typical problems on thermal processes that were proposed in the introductory copies.

Problem 1 (MPEI, 1978). A thermal process is performed over one mole of an ideal gas, shown in figure 1. How did the temperature of the gas change in sections 1-2, 2-3, 3-1? In which areas did it receive gas and in which areas did it release heat?

For section 1-2, the dependence of pressure on volume is linear: $p = \alpha V$ (α is some constant coefficient). From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation in this case we have

$$\alpha V^2 = RT.$$

This means that the temperature of the gas returns to the volume and its internal energy increases: $\Delta U > 0$. In addition, the gas performed positive expansion work: $A > 0$. From the first law of thermodynamics it follows that the amount of heat

$$Q_{1-2} = \Delta U + A > 0$$

- in section 1-2 the gas received heat.

In section 2-3 $V_2 = V_3 = const$, that is, no work was done by the gas: $A = 0$. Gas pressure dropped: $p_3 < p_2$; meaning $T_3 < T_2$ and $\Delta U < 0$. Therefore, in section 2-3 the gas gave up heat: $Q_{2-3} < 0$.
