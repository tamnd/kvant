1. (K. Fabel, 1949). White's last move was short castling! The most surprising thing is that it involved one king, moving from el to g1. The fact is that White played the game with a two-rook handicap, and the rook on h3 was promoted! Instead of short castling, White now makes a long castling, as a result of which his king gets from el not to g1, but to cl (the rook of al, like h1, participates in castling only symbolically). After 1.0 - 0 the black king is forced to retreat to the corner 1...Kpa1, where he receives mat - 2.Jla3×.
2. (F. Weird, 1910). With the last move, the white king took the black rook Kpg4:Лlf5, which had previously captured the white queen - Rlf1:Qf5. Instead, Black plays 1...Jlh1 and gets mat - 2.Qf2×.
In the conditions, of course, there is a typo (on the board there is not a knight, but a king), but the readers “solved” a lot of the problem with the knight (which will be credited to them when summing up the results).
3. (E. Poghosyants, 1963). The promotion of a pawn to a queen can be divided into two parts: 1) one’s own pawn is removed from the board; 2) the promoted queen is placed on the board. White has already made half a move - he removed his h7 pawn from the board. They end their move by placing the queen on h8! So, White's full move is 1.h7 - h8Q×!
4. (E. Poghosyants, 1980). The move, which consists of promoting a captured pawn, can be divided into three parts: 1) from the board of your own pawn; 2) from the board of the non-spinning queen; 3) the promoted queen is replaced in place of the taken queen. In this position, the third move has already been made - White removed his pawn g7 from the board. All that remains is to remove the black queen (“a third” of the move), and put a white rook or queen in its place (another “third” of the move). Full move 1.g7:h8JL×!
