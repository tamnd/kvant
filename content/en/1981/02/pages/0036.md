⟦folio 34⟧

In the math room, where the participants of the school math club studied, there were the abacus shown on the screensaver on the left. But one day, upon arriving at the next club lesson, the schoolchildren saw another, “spoiled” accounts (they are shown on the screensaver on the right).

- What is this? Why do we need spoiled accounts? - one of the guys asked.

“Don’t be surprised,” said the math teacher. - These are new accounts that we will now learn to read.

First of all, let's look at their design. Each bone of the lower wire, as on your usual abacus, has a “weight” equal to one. But any of the four bones located on the second count replaces not ten bones of the lower wire, as in ordinary abacus, but two, and therefore its “weight” is not ten, but two. It is easy to tell what “weight” any of the bones located on the third and fourth wires has.

“I think I’m finishing the deal,” one of the schoolchildren exclaimed. - Any bone of the third provolochka replaces four bones of the second provolochka, and therefore its “weight” is equal to eighth units, and the “weight” of the bone with the very last provolochka is already equal to forty-eighth units.

“That’s right,” the teacher confirmed. — Thus, the “weight” of the seeds is formed by the sequence 1, 2, 8 and 48.

Acquaintance with non-educational abacus led us to a new number system; Let's call it “spoiled.” Let's see what this system shows, for example, the entry 3021₁.
