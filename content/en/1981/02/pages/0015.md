Unfortunately, the actual process in a thermoelectric battery is not limited to the release and absorption of heat at the junctions. Along the branches of the thermopile itself, a heat flow arises from the warm junction to the cold junction, which carries out the transfer of heat in the opposite direction, accompanying the passage of current. In addition, part of the electric current energy is converted into heat in both branches of the thermal element.

As a result of the presence of these two processes, the use of electric energy is sharply reduced: it comes to adding not 10% of electric energy, but about 60%. But this result is also of significant interest: the energy consumption is only about half of the heat entering the room, the remaining half is delivered by colder outside air or running water. The smaller the difference $T_1 - T_0$ compared to $T_1$, the higher the thermoelectric battery will be compared to the electric resistance furnace.

The thermoelectric battery has another important advantage. If you change the direction of the current to the opposite, then the external junctions begin to release heat $Q_0$, and the heating of the junction junction will optimize the heat $Q_1$, cooling the room. During the hot season, the same thermobattery can cool the air. By adjusting the strength and direction of the current in the battery, you can maintain the same temperature in the room at any outside temperature.

The cooling caused by the thermoelectric battery can, as we have already mentioned, be used to implement a refrigerator. Warm junctions separate the heat they receive $Q_1$ into the surrounding air or tap water; cold junctions located inside the refrigerator separate the heat, maintaining the desired low temperature in it.

In modern technology, the types of refrigeration machines are changing. The fewer necessary processes they have, the more profitable the machines are, the higher their efficiency is relative to the thermodynamic one. There are complex refrigeration machines with greater efficiency than thermoelectric ones, but the latter have the advantages of simplicity and the absence of moving mechanisms.
