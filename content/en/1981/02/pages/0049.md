The internal energy of a certain portion of an ideal gas depends only on its temperature, therefore its change in both processes will be the same and equal to ΔU=vC_VΔT, where v is the number of moles of gas, ΔT is the change in its temperature.

The amount of heat supplied to the gas during an isochoric process is equal to

$$Q_1 = vC_VΔT,$$

and the amount of heat supplied during an isobaric process is equal to

$$Q_2 = vC_pΔT = v(C_V + R)ΔT.$$

Cyclic thermal processes underlie the operation of heat engines, an important characteristic of which is the coefficient of performance (efficiency). Let's consider the problem of calculating this value.

Problem 6 (Physics Department of Moscow State University, 1976). The tell machine has an efficiency of η=40%. What will be the efficiency of the machine if the amount of heat consumed per cycle increases by 20%, and the amount of heat given to the refrigerator decreases by 10%?

By definition, the efficiency coefficient is equal to the ratio of the work A performed by a heat engine per cycle to the amount of heat Q_n received by it. From the law of conservation of energy it follows that the work performed is equal to the value of the amount of heat received and the amount of heat Q_from given by the heat engine to the refrigerator. Thus,

$$\eta = \frac{A}{Q_n} = \frac{Q_π - Q_{от}}{Q_n} = 1 - \frac{Q_{от}}{Q_n}.$$

From here it is not difficult to find that at the beginning, when the efficiency of the machine was 40%, the ratio Q_{from}/Q_n was equal to 0.6. Then, according to the conditions of the problem, the efficiency coefficient is equal to 20%, that is, it becomes equal to Q'_n=1.2Q_n, and the amount of heat transferred to the refrigerator decreases by 10%, that is, it becomes equal to Q'_from=0.9Q_{from}. This means the new efficiency value

$$\eta' = 1 - \frac{Q'_{от}}{Q'_n} = 1 - \frac{0,9Q_{от}}{1,2Q_n} = 0,55.$$

We found, therefore, that the efficiency of the heat engine increased and became equal to 55%.
