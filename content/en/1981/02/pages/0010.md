Dit to new qualitative influence. The efficiency of semiconductor thermocouples is up to 7%, but can be higher. Among the heat engines in circulation, only internal combustion engines that require high-quality liquid fuel - gasoline, kerosene, oil - have an efficiency of about 40-50%. The efficiency of the best steam power plants reaches 30%, and that of low-power steam engines reaches 10%. Considering the extreme simplicity of thermoelectric generators, which do not require complex rotating mechanisms of heat engines or dynamos, and also taking into account the small size and stable operation of thermal elements, we can expect that semiconductor thermopile will occupy an important place in the energy sector of the national economy. It is possible that thermocouples will also open the way to the use of new heat sources in areas where there are significant temperature differences. It is likely that it will be possible to convert the energy of solar rays into electricity using thermoelectric batteries.

The first practical use of thermozements was carried out in the Soviet Union for the purpose of radiofication of areas without electrical energy. The source of heat here is the hot gases coming out of the glass of the kerosene lamp; they pass inside a vertical tube placed above the glass and heat the internal span of the thermocouples located along the radii of the tube. External spans are cooled with room air; To enhance cooling, these spans are contained with metal radiator plates. In such a device, the internal junctions are heated to 300-350°C, while the temperature of the external junctions does not exceed 60°C. A temperature difference of 250-300°C, maintained by the heat supplied by a kerosene lamp, is sufficient to create the electricity needed to power the radio.

Let's consider the device of a semiconductor thermal cement. It consists of two semiconductor branches connected by metal wires, of which one is made of electronic and the other of two semiconductors (Fig. 2). The metal bridge M connecting them is heated by a heat source. The other two ends, cooled by air or running water, are connected to an external circuit used by the electric energy generated by the thermoelectric cement. Such an external circuit in the described case is a radio receiver; in other cases it may be an accumulation lamp or a fluorescent lamp if the task is lighting; electric motor, if mechanical energy is needed, etc. Figure2 shows what has been said. Let us assume that branch I represents electronic semiconductors, and branch II represents double semiconductors.
