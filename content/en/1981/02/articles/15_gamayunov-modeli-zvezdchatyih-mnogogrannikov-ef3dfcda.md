⟦folio 39⟧

Models
star-shaped
polyhedra

On the first and fourth pages of the cover, two colorful drawings (diagrams)* are reproduced, with the help of which one can build models of various polyhedra, in particular stellate ones. Before you start building a model, familiarize yourself with the basic techniques for constructing a polyhedron development described below.

Take the required sheet of tracing paper; the development (or rather, half of it - see Fig. 1) of one of the polyhedra will be drawn on it.

The first step is to select the initial face on one of the diagrams or, more conveniently, on Figures 2, 3 (this choice determines which of the possible polyhedra we will construct); in our case, the central pentagon PSTUR on figure 2 is selected. Let’s intersect this pentagon onto the tracing paper and leave it on figure in the same position.

Let's consider one of the vertices of the PSTUR face, say P. The number of faces of our polyhedron adjacent to this vertex will be equal to the number of lines (in this case 3) passing through it on the diagram, increased by 1. How to construct these 4 faces?

If the vertex is a strip (like P), that is, black arms are drawn near it on the diagram, you need to stick a compass into this vertex, rotate the tracing paper to the angle indicated by the smaller of the arms, and transfer the next one of the same face in a new position to the tracing paper. This way we get a second face adjacent to the first along the edge PS (Fig. 1). By turning the same angle twice more, we get the remaining two faces adjacent to P.

Let us now take vertex U, which is no longer a strip. There are 2 straight lines passing through it, and therefore 3 faces must be adjacent to it.

To find the face adjacent to the edge UT, then the midpoint Q of this edge is a strip with an arc of 180°. In accordance with this, let's rotate the tracing paper about Q by 180° and draw a new position of the face.

There are no positions on edge U anymore; further construction in such cases must be continued on another diagram. To find the place where you should apply the tracing paper, see that the straight line is marked on figure 2 with the Δ sign. Find the same icon on figure 3, attach edge UR along some straight line placed by it, and move the tracing paper along it until a face of the same color touches the PSTUR face (the central hexagonal face on figure 3 - find it on figure 1 too!). Such a combination may not be obtained immediately - there are several straight lines with the Δ sign on figure 3, you will need to find it by trial and error.

Further constructions are carried out in the same spirit. In this case, the tracing paper comes to move from one diagram to another, use the Δ mark, but not thoughtlessly (you must remember that edges of the same length are glued together!). As a result, you should get a scan on your tracing paper that matches figure 1.

Having done this, test yourself by using the same techniques to complete the development of the star polyhedron shown at figure 4. This construction begins with one of the black quadrangular faces at figure 3 (at figure 4 this face is red).

Having understood the development technique, you can move on to the practical construction of a life-size model. To do this, you should translate both diagrams on an enlarged scale onto two sheets of batman. (This can be done with an epidiacol or pantograph; if you don't have these, you'll have to use a compass and ruler) Then take a large sheet of tracing paper, select a starting edge... and go!

There are a lot of initial edges - enough for a whole school class. If you get beautiful models, take pictures and send them to us.

V. Gamayunov
