Emmanuel Lasker, the founder of the psychological approach to chess fighting, was 32 years younger than his great predecessor, and this age difference was a very big head start in their battle.

Consulting: world chess champion, international grandmaster A. Karpov. The page is run by E. Gik, Master of Sports of the USSR in chess, Candidate of Technical Sciences.

Lasker defeats Steinitz
In the previous issue we talked about the first three checkmates for the world championship. Now let's move on...

The second match between W. Steinitz and the Great Russian chess player M. Chigorin (1892) was significantly different from the first. The game went until ten wins. After 23 games, Steinitz was ahead by a point, however, if Chigorin had won the next game, the score would have been 9:9 and, according to the conditions of the match, the game would have continued until three more wins. In this game, a borderline incident occurred, the like of which I have never seen in the struggle for the chess crown.

Chigorin - Steinitz
White has an extra piece, and after 32.R:b7 he won easily (we suggest you see this for yourself). But the perfect 32.Bb4?? followed, and after the response 32...R:h2+ the match was immediately concluded (33.Kg1 Rdg2×).

Lasker - Steinitz
This is the position of my partin match of 1894. Black already had a huge advantage in the opening, and despite the lack of an attempt by the opponent to confuse the game, the situation did not change. By pushing the queen's pawn now (35.Qh6 is not dangerous because of 35...Rg3), Steinitz could gain the upper hand and take the lead. However, his nerves could not stand it, on the next move he made a decisive mistake and soon made a game - the first in a series of five defeats.

34... gf? 35.Qh5+ Ke7 36.Jlg8 Kd6 37.Jl:f5 Qe6 38.Jl:e8 Q:e8 39.Jl:f6+ Krs5 40.Qh6 Le7 41.Qh2 Qd7 (41...Qd8 42.Qf2+ and 43.Rf8) 42.Qg1+ d4 43.Qg5+ Qd5 44.Jlf5 Q:f5 45.Q:f5+ Kd6 46.Qf6+. Black deals.

Having lost the match, Steinitz said three cheers in honor of the second world champion, but two years later he challenged Lasker to a rematch. In all subsequent history, there has never been another occasion when a 60-year-old chess player entered the fight for the world chess crown. Steinitz suffered a curtailment. It is curious that this match took place in Moscow, and the next state of such a high rank took place in our capital more than half a century later. In the second game of the match, Lasker performed the famous mating combination.

Lasker - Steinitz
31.h4 h5 (taking the bishop on f4 led to a quick mate after Kf6+) 32.Cg5 Cd8 33.g4! hg 34.h5 Kf8 35.Kec5+! dc 36.K:c5+ Kpd6 (in case of 36...Kpc7 37.C:e7 C:e7 38.Jl:e7+ Npb6 39.Jl:g7 is enough to win, but now you should form a mate) 37.Cf4+ Kpd5 38.Jle5+ Kc4 (38...Kpd6 39.Jlf5×; 38...Kp:d4 39.Jl1e4×) 39.Jlc1× Kp:d4 (39...Kpb4 40.Cd2+) 40.Kb3+ Kpd3 41.Jle3×. The final matte design of the scale is effective.

Having convinced the chess world that he had conquered it for a reason, Lasker somewhat reduced his chess activity, concentrating his main efforts on mathematics and philosophy. Only years later he played a new match for the world championship. Lasker's advantage over Marshall was undeniable. Here is the end of the third game of the match.
