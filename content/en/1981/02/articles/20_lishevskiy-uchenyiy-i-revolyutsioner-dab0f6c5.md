Scientist and revolutionary

Many scientists have had interesting lives, but few have lived such an exciting life, full of adventures and vicissitudes, as Gaspard Monge.

The son of poor parents, thanks to his talent and hard work, became a great citizen of his country, an academician and a minister, a revolutionary who sent King Louis XVI to his death, was not only a mathematician and mechanic, a chemist and metallurgist, but also an active participant in the Great French Bourgeois Revolution. His life is dramatic and contradictory. In the last years of his life, the revolutionary, Jacobin Monge was a count and personal friend of Napoleon I.

The life and work of the great French scientist, creator of descriptive geometry, Gaspard Monge (1746-1818), is popularly and fascinatingly told by V. P. Demyanov in his book “Geometry and Marseilles” *). The book is written in good literary language, it is very informative, one feels that the author is well acquainted with the material being presented and has the ability to present it. At the latest competition for the best scientific popular book, which is held annually by the Knowledge Society, V. P. Demyanov’s book “Geometry and Marseilles” received an incentive diploma.

The book is read with great interest, and the author can make only one reproach. He described Monge's life more vividly than his scientific activities. And Monge's contribution to science was canceled. Monge was as revolutionary in science as he was in life.

V. Lishchevsky

*) Vladimir Demyanov. “Geometry and Marseilles” (M., “Znanie”, 1979).
