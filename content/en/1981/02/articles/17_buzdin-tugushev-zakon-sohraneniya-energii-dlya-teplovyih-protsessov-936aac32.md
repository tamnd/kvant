Law of conservation of energy for thermal processes

When solving problems related to the consideration of thermal processes, the most important role is played by the law of conservation and transformation of energy, called thermodynamics:

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Here $Q$ is the amount of heat transferred to the system, $\Delta U$ is the change in its internal energy and $A$ is the work done by the system against external forces. (In the textbook “Physics 9” this work is designated $A'$; for brevity we will omit the “prime”.)

Internal energy $U$ is a function of thermodynamic parameters (pressure $p$, volume $V$ and temperature $T$) that describe the state of the system, but does not depend on how this state arose. For example, for a monatomic ideal gas

$$U = \frac{m}{\mu} \frac{3}{2} RT,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the gas, $\mu$ is its molar mass, $R$ is the universal gas constant equal to 8.31 J/(mol K).

The work $A$ performed by the system depends, of course, on the nature of the thermal process. So, with a small change in volume $\Delta V$ (when the pressure can be considered constant)

$$A = p\Delta V.$$

The thermodynamic parameters of a system are not independent variables; they are related by an equation of state. In the case of an ideal gas, this is the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation

$$pV = \frac{m}{\mu} RT.$$

Let's look at some typical problems on thermal processes that were proposed in the introductory copies.

Problem 1 (MPEI, 1978). A thermal process is performed over one mole of an ideal gas, shown in figure 1. How did the temperature of the gas change in sections 1-2, 2-3, 3-1? In which areas did it receive gas and in which areas did it release heat?

For section 1-2, the dependence of pressure on volume is linear: $p = \alpha V$ (α is some constant coefficient). From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation in this case we have

$$\alpha V^2 = RT.$$

This means that the temperature of the gas returns to the volume and its internal energy increases: $\Delta U > 0$. In addition, the gas performed positive expansion work: $A > 0$. From the first law of thermodynamics it follows that the amount of heat

$$Q_{1-2} = \Delta U + A > 0$$

- in section 1-2 the gas received heat.

In section 2-3 $V_2 = V_3 = const$, that is, no work was done by the gas: $A = 0$. Gas pressure dropped: $p_3 < p_2$; meaning $T_3 < T_2$ and $\Delta U < 0$. Therefore, in section 2-3 the gas gave up heat: $Q_{2-3} < 0$.

Problem 4 (MPEI, 1977). One mole of gas expands so that its volume during the process is proportional to the pressure: $V = \alpha p$. The gas pressure increases from $p_1$ to $p_2$. Find the coefficient $\alpha$ if the heat capacity of a mole of gas at constant volume is $C_V$ and during

process, the gas is given the amount of heat $Q$.

Using the first law of thermodynamics, we write

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Change in internal energy of gas

$$\Delta U = C_V \Delta T,$$

where $\Delta T$ is the temperature change. In the case of a process in which $V = \alpha p$, the equation of gas state has the form $a^2 = RT$. Then

$$\Delta T = T_2 - T_1 = \frac{\alpha}{R} (p_2^2 - p_1^2)$$

And

$$\Delta U = \frac{C_V \alpha}{R} (p_2^2 - p_1^2).$$

To find the work $A$ performed by the gas, it is convenient to use the graphical method (Fig. 4): the work is equal to the area of ​​the protected area, that is

$$A = \frac{p_1 + p_2}{2} (V_2 - V_1) = \alpha \frac{p_2^2 - p_1^2}{2}.$$

Thus,

$$Q = \Delta U + A = \left( \frac{C_V}{R} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \alpha (p_2^2 - p_1^2).$$

Solving this equation for $\alpha$, we obtain

$$\alpha = \frac{Q}{(C_V/R + 1/2) (p_2^2 - p_1^2)}.$$

Cyclic thermal processes underlie the operation of heat engines, an important characteristic of which is the coefficient of performance (efficiency). Let's consider the problem of calculating this value.

Problem 6 (Physics Department of Moscow State University, 1976). The tell machine has an efficiency of η=40%. What will be the efficiency of the machine if the amount of heat consumed per cycle increases by 20%, and the amount of heat given to the refrigerator decreases by 10%?

By definition, the efficiency coefficient is equal to the ratio of the work A performed by a heat engine per cycle to the amount of heat Q_n received by it. From the law of conservation of energy it follows that the work performed is equal to the value of the amount of heat received and the amount of heat Q_from given by the heat engine to the refrigerator. Thus,

$$\eta = \frac{A}{Q_n} = \frac{Q_π - Q_{от}}{Q_n} = 1 - \frac{Q_{от}}{Q_n}.$$

From here it is not difficult to find that at the beginning, when the efficiency of the machine was 40%, the ratio Q_{from}/Q_n was equal to 0.6. Then, according to the conditions of the problem, the efficiency coefficient is equal to 20%, that is, it becomes equal to Q'_n=1.2Q_n, and the amount of heat transferred to the refrigerator decreases by 10%, that is, it becomes equal to Q'_from=0.9Q_{from}. This means the new efficiency value

$$\eta' = 1 - \frac{Q'_{от}}{Q'_n} = 1 - \frac{0,9Q_{от}}{1,2Q_n} = 0,55.$$

We found, therefore, that the efficiency of the heat engine increased and became equal to 55%.
