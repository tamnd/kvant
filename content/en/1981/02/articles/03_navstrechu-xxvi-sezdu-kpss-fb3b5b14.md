I will greet the XXVI Congress of the CPSU

On February 23, the Kremlin Palace of Congresses will host participants of the XXVI Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union. The Congress will consider and approve the Main Directions of Economic and Social Development of the USSR for 1981-1985 and for the period until 1990, the draft of which was published in the central press in early December 1980. During the month, our people discussed this important document of our time with extreme interest and openness. At the same time, many specific additions, changes and clarifications were made, taking into account which turned it into the fruit of the collective development of the entire people.

For ten years we will have to live and work on the basis of the decisions that will be made by the 26th Congress of the CPSU. The implementation of the program he outlined will bring our country closer to its cherished goal - communism. And science has a huge role to play in this. Many sections of the Main Directions project are inextricably linked with it. Let's show this using the example of physical and mathematical sciences, which our «Kvant» is related to.

The third section of the draft of the Main Directions of Economic and Social Development of the USSR for 1981-1985 and for the period until 1990 is related to the development of science and the strengthening of technical progress. It says, in part, the following:

“In the field of natural and technical sciences, concentrate efforts on solving the following most important problems:

development of mathematical theory, increasing the efficiency of its use for applied purposes;

development of the physics of elementary particles and the atomic nucleus with the aim of further knowledge of the structure of matter;

development of nuclear and creation of the foundations of thermonuclear energy, improvement of methods of energy conversion and transmission; ...

improvement of computer technology, its elemental base and software, means and systems for transmitting and processing information, increasing the efficiency of automated control systems, developing computer networks and shared computing centers; ...

further study and exploration of outer space in the interests of the development of science, technology and the national economy; ..."

The third section says:

“Based on the use of scientific and technical achievements:

develop production and ensure widespread use of automatic manipulators (industrial robots), built-in automatic control systems using microprocessors and minicomputers, create automated circuits and factories. Expand the automation of design and research work using electronic computer technology; ..."

use electrominic, laser, radiation and other highly efficient methods of processing metals, materials and products in order to significantly improve their properties;...

increase the scale of use of renewable energy sources (hydraulic, solar, wind, geothermal) in the national economy.”

The language of this document is laconic, but behind each of the lines read there are important problems of our national economy that cannot be solved without the enormous help of physical and mathematical sciences.

Many specific tasks, closely related to physics and mathematics, are included in the fourth section of the project of the Main Directions for the Development of Industrial Production; increasing the output of products necessary for the people requires a continuous increase in energy capacity. This poses very difficult challenges for the energy industry. It is necessary to “bring the production of electric energy in 1985 to 1550-1600 billion. kilowatt-hours, including at nuclear power plants up to 220-225 billion. kilowatt-hours and at hydroelectric power plants up to 230-235 billion. kilowatt-hours. To ensure the production of electricity in the European part of the USSR, mainly at nuclear and hydroelectric power plants.

Put into operation 24-25 million kilowatts of new capacity at nuclear power plants. Continue work on the decommissioning of fast neutron reactors and the use of nuclear fuel for the production of thermal energy.”

All this is connected with solving complex scientific and technical problems, primarily in the field of physics.

No less complex problems, the solution of which is simply impossible without the active participation of physical and mathematical sciences, arise in many other sectors of our national economy: in the electrical and instrument-making industries, ferrous and non-ferrous metallurgy, transport, construction and technical communications.

You, our main readers, checked the past ten, five years at school. Someone smelted metal and grew bread for you, laid BAM rails and KAMAZ trusses. You learned everything ready, thanks to the work of your parents, all those older than you. In the eleventh five-year period, you will graduate from school and go into a busy, independent working life. On the 10th, many of you will already become masters of various professions. The plans that our people are discussing today, the XXVI Congress of our native Communist Party, will become plans for your future life. Place a visitor in them and choose. There is a lot of interesting work ahead and there is enough for everyone. The world does not stand still. It is changing quickly. And especially quickly to improve and develop our country, which is following an unknown path towards communism.

Our future life is filled with interesting problems. Many of them are technologies with science, including mathematics, mechanics and physics, and those who choose them for their future profession will never regret it. The romance of scientific research, the pangs of creativity, the joy of victory, the excitement of the social usefulness of your work will make your whole life bright and interesting.
