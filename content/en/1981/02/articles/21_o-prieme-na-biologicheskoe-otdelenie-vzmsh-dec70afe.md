About application to the biological department of VZMSH

In our time, a good knowledge of mathematics and physics is needed not only by specialists in these fields or engineers, but also by biologists. The All-Union Correspondence School of Mathematics has had a biological department for six years. The children who study there have to think not only about purely biological questions. Let us present one of the tasks of the biological department, which requires the ability to approach a biological problem from the point of view of physics and use quantitative messages.

1. The absolute strength of an ant and a human mouse is approximately the same: 8-10 ke per 1 cm² of the cross-section of the mouse. How can we be sure that an ant can carry objects ten times its body weight?

2. In birds and mammals, the heart rate is usually higher, the smaller the animal. How can one explain such a pattern?

3. Come up with a way to measure the frequency with which a mosquito can fly.

4. It is known that the propagation of excitation in the first fibers is carried out using an electric current. In which fiber - only or thin - is the speed of excitation propagation higher and why?

The biology department of the VZMS accepts schoolchildren who are completing the eighth grade. Schoolchildren are sent questions on various sections of biology and manuals on certain difficult issues. Students' answers are checked by students from the Faculty of Biology of Moscow State University. The school accepts both individual students and school groups working under the guidance of an accountant. To enter the school, you must successfully complete a competitive task. Along with the competition work, you must send information about yourself: last name, first name and patronymic; school and class; place of work and position of the parent; full postal address. We hope that the eight classics students who will be completing the competition work will do so by the end of spring break: the deadline for submitting work is April 5. The results of the competition will be announced in the fall. Competition entries are not accepted. Students from Moscow and Leningrad are not accepted into the biological department of the VZMSH.

Although the introductory competition work was compiled for children interested in biology, the answers to its questions take into account a number of physical messages (questions 1, 2 and 4) and some mathematical reasoning (question 5).

Test project questions

1. It is known that some animals (hares, cetaceans) have very fatty milk, while others (monkeys, wolves) do not. Offer an explanation for this fact and, based on your explanation, try to suggest which other competition animals should have fatty milk and which ones should have low-fat milk.

2. In connection with the transition to the terrestrial form of animals, plants developed a number of structures for their purpose: roots, a conducting system, special integumentary tissues, etc. What structures and adaptations do you think should appear first in order to ensure that plants grow on land? The answer must be justified.

3. Some animals, such as a chameleon or an octopus, can change their color to match the color of the surrounding background. Imagine that you are a scientist tasked with studying the physiological basis of this animal response. What competitive questions will you pose to yourself? What experiments will you conduct to answer these questions? How do you interpret this or that result of these experiments?

4. Rank the following animals in order of lower blood pressure: dog, giraffe, chicken, frog. Be sure to attend to the answer.

5. To account for the number of animals, the following method is often used: for some numbers, a significant ratio of the number of local animals in the repeated capture to the total number of animals appearing for the second time. a) Write a formula that will determine the number, and state how you reasoned when deriving it. 6) Indicate what reasons may lead to errors when using this method. For each reason, indicate whether it will lead to an overestimation or an underestimation of the population size.
