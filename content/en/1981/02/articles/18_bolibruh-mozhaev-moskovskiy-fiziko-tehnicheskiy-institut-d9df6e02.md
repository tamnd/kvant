Solution to Problem 1

1. During the action of the force $\vec{F}$, the load performs a harmonious oscillation with a period of $T = 2\pi \sqrt{m/k} \approx 0,3$ s around the new equilibrium position, determined by the condition $kx_0 = |\vec{F}|$, where $x_0$ is the displacement of the new equilibrium position relative to the old one. In this case, the amplitude of the oscillations is also equal to $x_0 = |\vec{F}|/k$, and the right extreme position of the load coincides with its initial position. Obviously, the speed of the load and its acceleration at this moment are equal to zero. Therefore, if the force stops in this position, the load will remain motionless. So, the time $t$ of the force must be equal to $\tau = nT$, where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$.

A typical mistake in solving this problem was an incorrect understanding of the problem. After the force ceases, the load will remain motionless if both the speed and acceleration of the load are zero. This will only be in the far right position. (In the extreme left position, the speed of the load is indeed zero, but its acceleration is non-zero. At the moment when the forces are balanced and the acceleration of the load is zero, its speed is non-zero.)
