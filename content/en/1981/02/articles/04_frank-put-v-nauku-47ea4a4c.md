I. Frank

The way to teach

Trying to look into the future, youth dreams. Happy is the one who does not part with his dream in later life. The closest person to me is the one who, from a young age, strives to study science. Not only in dreams, but also in reality, science is incredibly fascinating. But to become a scientist, you must be sincerely and selflessly devoted to science and not be afraid of difficulties.

The first steps are obviously made easier by teachers, if, of course, they not only know, but also love science. Both the topic of the first work and the method of completing it are often suggested by the leader. This is good, since gaining experience is necessary, and the scientific environment in which a beginner finds himself is important for the future. A beginner can and should be helped to learn, and yet he can only learn by himself. At the same time, the first steps taken independently, which please as some achievements, often lead to disappointment. It often turns out that the result obtained has already been found by someone earlier, and the idea that seems new is in fact not only not new, but may have already been abandoned as erroneous. But you shouldn't be upset. The most important and most valuable thing in science is the element of creativity. Walking a section of the path on your own, even one covered by others, is useful and often necessary. Nevertheless, after several such “failures,” the mistake arises that everything in science is known. In reality the obligation is different. Initial knowledge involuntarily suggests the path along which many have already traveled. The ability to think in your own way does not arise immediately; it is developed through difficult and focused work. It is no coincidence that Newton sees himself as a little one, since it is the little ones, more than adults, who occupy the Japanese “flowery stones”, namely, they are fresher and more easily able to distinguish the unusual. I think that one of the necessary and happy traits of a scientist is curiosity, so characteristic of childhood and so easily lost by adults. Newton was a genius; there are few like him in the history of science. It is not surprising that he found amazing stones where others saw only one-piece placers of sand, and not only found them, but also built buildings of striking beauty from them.

After Newton, many new paths to the ocean of truth were continued and notable pebbles and shells were found, but the ocean of truth was still vast. Everyone who is not devoid of talent and has learned will find something of their own in it.

The dreams of youth are not without illusions. One of them is that a beginner has an unlimited amount of time ahead. They involuntarily think like this: “I don’t know and don’t understand this yet, but I don’t need to rush. I’m young and still have time.” In reality, no matter how long a creative life fate gives us, you still don’t have time to learn even a small fraction of what is necessary for work, much less do everything that you could do. You, of course, know Pavlov’s words: “Remember that science requires a person’s whole life. And if you had two lives, then they would not be enough for you” and “always have the courage to say to yourself: I am ignorant.”

Our young years are the most fruitful. Unfortunately, it is only until you reach old age that you truly understand that not only youth, but all of life flies by uninhabitedly quickly. And yet, it is at a young age that the thirst for knowledge and interest in science makes us work especially hard. Without this, the path to science would have been closed altogether.

Recognition of work contains something significantly greater than a general instruction from parents to schoolchildren that they must study well. Science does not need mechanical completion of materials (this is bad for schoolchildren as well), but genuine creative mastery of knowledge and methods. The ability based on them to pose questions in your own way and answer them, or to see ambiguity where others do not notice it, is what allows you to find a beautiful pebble where others see nothing but sand. There is not just luck here, here, first of all, is work and many failures through which you need to have the courage to go through.

The amount of knowledge in any field of science is enormous, and it is impossible to know everything. It is impossible, for example, to know in detail everything that physicists are working on. However, you need to be aware of the main ideas and events outside your specialization. A scientist must be a widely educated, truly intelligent person. Issues of human culture and problems of social life cannot be alien to him. How to find time for everything? This, of course, is difficult at any age, but in young years there is a lot of time and effort, but the ability to work and the breadth of knowledge comes with age.
