Epilogue. Thus ended a short but very productive period in Galileo’s scientific life, when he was actively engaged in astronomical observations. It lasted for barely more than a year. Galileo did not return to his first work on a treatise on mechanics. He is confident that he has decisive arguments in the attempt to create a gel-centric system for the world, and he devotes all his energy to promoting these ideas.

Galileo was still destined to remember the Medicean stars. From 1636 he negotiated with the States General of Holland to use the eclipse observations of Jupiter's satellites to measure debts on board the ship. It was about solving one of the most pressing problems of the 17th century*). The negotiations ended without result. But in connection with these negotiations, Galileo returned to the problem that had long interested him: calculating the periods of revolution of the satellites he had discovered and unsuccessfully tried, with the help of the monk Renberger, who was experienced in calculations, to compile tables of motion. The task turned out to be difficult, and it caused troubles for astronomers for a long time.
