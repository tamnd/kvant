Botvinnik loses and... wins
The fiftieth years passed under the sign of rivalry between Mikhail Botvinnik and Vasily Smyslov. If in the 1954 match the challenger was limited to an honorable draw, then in 1957 he already managed to climb to the top (score 12.5:9.5). Bot episode from the sixth game of the match.

Smyslov - Botvinnik
23. R:d5! An effective continuation that immediately produces results. 23...ed 24. N:c7 Rds8 25. C:c8 R:c8 26. N:d5 R:c6+ 27. Kd2 Kb 28. Ks3. Black deals.
Botvinnik prepared excellently for the rematch and a year later confidently regained his crown (12.5:10.5). He started the match with three “ordinary” victories, and after the 15th game the score could have become 10:5. The position of Black played by Botvinnik was significantly better, with almost any move maintaining an advantage. The grandmaster plunged into thought in order to outline the most clear plan for winning. Imagine Botvinnik’s surprise when suddenly the arbiter came up to the table and said that Black had run out of time and was clearing the loss. The only service of this kind in the entire history of world championship matches!
An interesting position arose in the 18th game of the match.

At the turn of the 50s and 60s, the chess wizard Mikhail Tal thrilled the most persistent grandmasters with his amazing combinations. He went from master to world champion in just three years!
Having won the match against Botvinnik with a score of 12.5:8.5, the 23-year-old Ryazhin became the eighth and youngest chess king in history. In combination storms, he was clearly superior to his powerful opponent. An episode from the 17th game of the match, won by Tal’s tactical dagger strike, can be found on the “chess page” in «Kvant», 1980, No. 2.
A year later, in a rematch, Botvinnik again shone with his ability to find the “Achilles heel” in the game of his opponents. Having thoroughly analyzed the reasons for his failure to win against the young Ryazhin, he regained the title of the strongest chess player in the world with amazing ease (score 13:8). The right to a rematch was canceled; Botvinnik did not want to start from scratch. In addition, the world expert, who held the chess crown for 15 years with two year-long revoys, soon preferred the work of creating artificial chess to practical play! His entire fascination is with the computer chess playing algorithm.
