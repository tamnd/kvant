Now assemble the entire “setup” as shown in figure 2. These are the so-called linked matrices. You can do many interesting experiments with it.

Lightly rock the heavy (second) matrix and leave the system to its own devices. Repeat the experiment several times, changing the length of the heavy matrix thread. How does the light (first) matrix behave in this case?

When the difference in the length of the threads of both matrices is close, the fluctuations of the second practically do not affect the first. When the lengths of the threads are comparable to each other, the first matrix begins to sway, and it sways first strongly, then weakly, then again strongly and again weakly, and so on. Why?

Obviously, in order to transfer energy from the second matrix to the first, it is necessary that only the movements occur in time with the swings of the first matrix. This is done when the lengths of the matrix threads are equal. In this case, they say that resonance has occurred.

Please note that the amplitude of vibration of the heavy matrix remains almost unchanged. This means that it transfers only a small part of its energy to the lung matrix.

Swap the matrices, that is, initially swing the light matrix. You will see that the resonance will be much less pronounced (the lighter the matrix, the faster its vibrations decay!). Check this again using a matrix with a 20 g weight.

Finally, hang weights of equal weight on identical threads and swing one of them. You will see that in this case both matrices will alternately swing, now strongly, now weakly, until they completely fade out. Moreover, if one is now oscillating with a large amplitude, then the second is with a small amplitude, and vice versa. The vibrations seem to flow from the first weight to the second and back.

It turns out that it's all about the superimposition of vibrations. The matrices are connected by a weak spring, the vibrations of which transfer energy from one matrix to another and, as it were, regularize this transfer. The superposition of such two oscillations is called bcens.

Interestingly, the mathematical equations that describe benzium (and which you will learn about in the second part of the article) have recently come in handy in... neutrino physics. The Soviet scientist academician B. M. Pontecorvo proposed that one type of neutrino (analogous to the first matrix) can transform into another type (the second matrix) if there is a weak interaction in nature (a spring) connecting these varieties. And it may turn out that neutrinos falling to the Earth from the Sun constantly change their nature along the way. The same thing happens as with matrices. Researchers all over the world are now conducting experiments to find out whether oscillations exist. Pontecorvo is the process of transition of one type of neutrino to another.

3. This experience will require some patience.

Make five or six identical matrices and connect them with identical springs (Fig. 3). Set the springs in motion, and you will see how the vibrations will gradually be transmitted to the rest of the matrices - a wave will run along the chain of matrices.

Such a system is the simplest model of a one-dimensional crystal lattice. On it you can, for example, visually illustrate the behavior of the crystal lattice when vibrations arise in some place of the lattice.
