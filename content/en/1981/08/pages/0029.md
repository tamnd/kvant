Galileo's discovery was first mentioned in print in the preface to Kepler's Dioptrics, which was published in 1611.

Two years later, Saturn suddenly stopped being triple. Galileo related this to the movement of Saturn around the Sun and predicted that it would soon be visible again as three stars. The event was predicted, but Galileo did not unravel the secrets of Saturn. The mystery was revealed when, in 1655, Huygens, viewing Saturn with a telephoto lens with 92x magnification, discovered that Saturn was surrounded by a ring, which, at varying magnifications, appeared to be lateral to stars. The ring becomes invisible when it is edge-on towards terrestrial observation. This is a rare phenomenon and Galileo was lucky enough to observe it. Such was the evolution of the critical ones in the impression from Saturn as the telephopes became stronger: from an olive tree to a ball surrounded by a ring. Huygens also discovered Saturn's largest moon, Titan.

Soon after a letter was sent to Kepler with a solution to the anagram, news appeared on other planets. Galileo had been closely observing Venus for a long time. Both the supporters of Ptolemy and the supporters of Copernicus had a lot of trouble with Venus and Mercury. The first could not agree on where their “spheres” were located - inside or outside the “sphere” of the Sun. It was clear to the Copernicans that if these planets were dark bodies, then, since they were located between the Sun and the Earth, incomplete disks of the planets (a phenomenon similar to the phases of the Moon) should be observed at times. This problem will not arise if it is assumed that the planets shine with their own light (apparently, Kepler thought so) or that they are transparent (this possibility was discussed on the server).

Castelli recalls this problem in a letter to Galil dated December 5, 1610. “Since (as I believe) Copernicus’ position is correct that Venus revolves around the Sun,” writes Castelli, “it is clear that it is necessary for us to sometimes observe her horned, sometimes not... if, however, the small size of the horns and the emission of rays does not prevent us from observing these differences in our construction.” But did Galileo need this reminder? Already on December 10, he sent to Kepler in Prague, through the Tokan ambassador Giuliano Medici, an encrypted message about the discovery of the phase of Venus with the accompanying letter: “I am sending you an encrypted message about another of my untrained observations, which leads to the resolution of the most important disputes built in and which contains the most important argument in favor of the Pythagorean and Copernican systems.” Kepler, as always, is eager to find out the answer.
