Oscillations
and pendulums

This is not the first time that Laboratory «Kvant» turns to experiments with pendulums. This is understandable - the theory of oscillations, which is used in many areas of physics and technology, can be simply and clearly presented in such experiments.
This article consists of two parts.
The first will describe some experiments; they do not require any special equipment. Individual experiments and high-quality training see the results obtained by everyone.
In the second part, the article provides a number of explanations for some phenomena. It will be understandable to those who are familiar with the topic “Mechanical Vibrations”, presented at the very beginning of the textbook for grade 10.

Experiments and Observations

Now it is no longer possible to verify the legend about how Galileo, standing at prayer in the cathedral, carefully watched the swinging of the bronze chandeliers. I observed and determined the time spent by the chandelier moving back and forth. This time was later called the period of oscillation. Galileo did not have a watch, and to compare the periods of oscillation of chandeliers suspended on chains of different lengths, he used the frequency of his pulse.
Let's start with similar simple experiments.
1. Take a small weight (preferably a weight for scales) and tie a strong thread or fishing line to it (Fig. 1). You will receive the simplest so-called mathematical matnik. Hang the mat, for example, to a nail, and begin the experiments.

By changing the mass of the weight and the length of the fishing line, you can make sure that the period of oscillation of such a matrix does not depend on the mass of the weight, but depends on the length of the fishing line: with increasing length $l$ of the fishing line, the period of oscillation $T$ increases. Galileo also made similar conclusions.

The formula point for the period of oscillation of the matrix $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$ appears much later. Its derivation is Huygens. Check this formula for your matric.

Observe the matrix for some time and you will notice that the scope (amplitude) of its oscillations gradually decreases - the oscillations die out. Moreover, the attenuation occurs the faster, the smaller the weight of the weight. Pay attention to this. We'll need this a little later.

2. Drive two nails approximately 30 cm apart horizontally and attach the matrices to them. Take the mass of one weight equal to $m_1 = 100$ g, and the mass of the second - $m_2 = 500$ g. Select the length of the thread of the first matrix equal to $l_1 = 1$ m, and the length of the thread of the second matrix will need to be changed, for example, like this: $l_2 = 0,5$ m; 0.7 m; 1 m; 1.2 m; 1.5 m.

Take a copper wire with a diameter of about 0.3' mm and make a soft spring 30 cm long from it (for example, place the wire on a common round pencil and then carefully remove it).
