Task 3 (experimental physics). If the spool is pulled by the thread, holding it low above the ground, the spool will appear towards the puller. And if you raise the thread higher, the spool will appear in the opposite direction. Why?

Look at figure 1. Point A is the instantaneous center of rotation of the coil. Obviously, if the tension of the thread creates a moment that overturns the reel relative to point A, the reel will appear in the direction in which it is being overturned. Thus, the tension of thread 1 causes the spool to roll to the left, the tension of thread 2 to the right, and the tension of thread 3 only lifts the spool.

Task 4 (Earth science). Why is it pointless to indicate the time of sunrise in Moscow on calendars accurate to the second?

It turns out that in different parts of Moscow sunrise begins at different times, and this difference reaches up to two minutes. But this happens not because Moscow is located on seven hills, on the tops of which the sun rises earlier than in the lowlands, and not because it is unclear what is considered a sunrise - when the Sun just appeared or when it completely emerged from behind the horizontal (this can always be agreed upon). The thing is that Moscow has a relatively large extent.

Let's estimate what part of the corresponding parallel Moscow occupies. The length of this parallel will be found by knowing the length of the Paryazh meridian (~40000 km) and the latitude at which Moscow is located (~60°): l~40000 km×0.5~20000 km. Assuming that the diameter of Moscow is d~30 km, we find that the required part is equal to d/l~30/20000~1.5·10⁻³. Consequently, the time of sunrise on the eastern side of Moscow differs from the time of sunrise on its western side by Δt~24 h·1.5·10⁻³~2 min.
