In the meantime, Galileo proposes, through Vinta, to call the satellites of Jupter in honor of Cosimo Medicine Cosmos or Medical stars. The second option was chosen. The number of companions conveniently coincided with the fact that Cosimo had three brothers. “Stellar Vectic” is associated with Cosimo Medicine: “By calling new stars, the discovery by me, the great name of the family of Medicine, I am aware that if those who first entered the starry world served to glorify the rich and powerful, then in this case, on the contrary, the great name of Medicine will ensure an immortal memory of these stars.” Then all four satellites received their own names (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), and in order to distinguish them from the later discovered satellites of Jupter*, they will be called Galilean.

For Easter holidays, Galileo went to Florence. He takes the trumpet with him so that the Duke can see “his” stars himself. Galileo is surrounded by honor, a medal with the image of the Medical Stars should be knocked out in his honor, yesterday he agreed on the terms of the move, the name of Galileo’s position is only being refined. The Emperor is pleased to have his name immortalized in heaven; no royal person can boast of this. On May 14, Galileo receives a letter from France dated April 20, in which he asks him to “discover the possibility of any celestial body that could be given to His Majesty.” We are talking about Henry IV. It is specified that the star should be named “after Henry without the addition of Bourbon.” It turned out that the author of the letter was not in vain in hurrying Galileo: while the letter was being sent, “the one accompanying the sovereign’s happiness” was killed.

Meanwhile, rumors began to persistently spread that the stars “given” to the Duke were the fruit of Galileo’s imagination or the creation

* Currently, 16 satellites of Jupter are known (the last three were discovered during the flight of the American Voyager automatic stations).
