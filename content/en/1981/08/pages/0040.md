Tasks

M696—M700; F708—F712

M696. Is it possible to fill a table of 10×10 cells with 100 different natural numbers so that for any square $k \times k$ cells ($2 \leq k \leq 10$) a) the sums, b) the product of $k$ numbers on its diagonals are the same?

A. Balinsky

M697. Let us determine by the belly of a rectangle the ratio of its smaller side to the larger side (the belly of a square is equal to 1). Prove that, as if cutting a square into rectangles, the sum of their bellies will be at least 1.

S. Fomin

M698. On the sides $a, b, c, d$ of a quadrilateral inscribed “outwards” in a circle, rectangles with dimensions $a \times c, b \times d, c \times a, d \times b$ are constructed. Prove that the centers of these rectangles are the vertices of the $a$) parallelogram, $b$) of the rectangle.

O. Penkin

M699*. A semicircle with diameter $AB$ is cut by a segment $CD$, perpendicular to $AB$, into two curved triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$, in which circles tangent to $AB$ are inscribed at points $E$ and $F$ (Fig. 1). Prove that $|AD| = |AF|$, $|DF|$ are bissertis of angle $BDC$, the value of angle $EDF$ does not depend on the choice of point $C$ on $AB$.

B. Senderov

M700*. Is it possible to divide the set of all finite decimal fractions into $a$) two, $6$) three classes so that two numbers with a difference $10^m$ (for no integer $m = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$) do not fall into one class?

A. Leiderman
