Special crystals - polarizers - convert unpolarized light into linearly polarized light, since they can only transmit light to waves in which the vector $\vec{E}$ is oriented quite specifically with respect to the optical axis of the polarizer. For example, a tourmaline crystal transmits only light polarized along the optical axis of this crystal, while waves with perpendicular polarization are strongly absorbed by it. If two polarizers are placed on the path of the light beam, the axes of which are parallel, then the light (polarized) will pass through such an optical system (Fig. 4, a), and if the axes of the polarizers are crossed, then the light will not be able to pass through this system (Fig. 4, b).

Let us now place two glasses between two crossed polarizers, and a nematic liquid between them, having previously slightly polished the glasses along a certain direction. Such glass polishing is necessary in order to orient the optical axis of the liquid crystal ($\vec{n}$) in a given direction.
