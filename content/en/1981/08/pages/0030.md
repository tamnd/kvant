The support of his Roman colleague played a special role in Galileo's plans, and he hurries to surprise Clavius ​​with his new discovery. Galileo describes his observations of Venus after “her evening appearance”, talks about how unexpectedly her round shape began to distort on the side facing the Sun, until Venus began to resemble a payday; then she “became noticeably horned”; predicts what form Venus will take when observed as a morning star. And here is the conclusion: “So, my lord, it turns out how Venus (and undoubtedly, Mercury will do the same) moves around the Sun, which is, without any doubt, the center of the largest examples of all planets. In addition, we are sure that these planets themselves are dark and shine only when illuminated by the Sun, which, I think, does not happen with fixed stars, according to some of my observations.” Clavius ​​should have had no doubt about where Galileo was going with this.

Thus ended the year of his great astronomical discoveries for Galileo.

Galileo continued his astronomical observations. But he will no longer devote so much time to improving the telescope, and no secret of the world will be revealed to him as in this wonderful year 1610! The achievements of Galileo led to the conclusion that at least half a century would pass before discoveries comparable to those of Galileo (Huygens, Cassini) appeared in observational astronomy.

In the meantime, Galileo begins to worry about other problems, and to solve these problems it is important for him to go to Rome.
