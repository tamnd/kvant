Within the USSR, the entire path of about 7400 km in length will cover the lunar darkness in 101 minutes.

To observe the total eclipse, numerous Soviet and foreign expeditions are sent to the total phase - primarily to Siberia and the Far East, where the eclipse will be the longest and good weather is most likely. Let's wish them success.

On the small map (Fig. 4), a thick line shows the total eclipse band, and parallel lines numbered 0.8, 0.6 and 0.4 (called images) indicate the places where the directing phase of the partial eclipse has the corresponding magnitude. Oblique lines, called isochrones, give the Moscow daylight time of the present direction of the directing phases. So, for example, Moscow is located between images 0.6 and 0.8, but is close to the latter, so the magnitude of the directing phase can be estimated at 0.72. Using isochrones, you can determine the infusion time of this phase - 5 hours 35 minutes. The exact calculation for Moscow is based on summer maternity time:

sunrise 5 hours 32 minutes,
beginning of partial eclipse 5 hours 43.0 minutes,
the greatest phase of the eclipse is 6 hours 34.7 minutes,
end of partial eclipse 7 hours 29.4 minutes

figure 5 shows the view of the Sun in Moscow during the entire eclipse every 10 minutes.

In conclusion, let us remind you that during a partial eclipse, in order to avoid server damage to vision, you cannot look at the Sun without protected dark glass, since the uncovered part of the solar disk has the same brightness as outside the eclipse. Only when the total phase of the eclipse is present can you admire the beautiful spectacle of the chromosphere and corona with the naked eye or through binoculars.
