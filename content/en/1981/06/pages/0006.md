It is not the legend given above, but precisely this real incident associated with the names of two young astrophysicists, that is characteristic of the behavior of our Soviet scientific youth.

Reflecting on the short stories outlined above gives me reason to express some wishes to young people going into science.

First of all, I want to wish her to be brave and ready to face all the changes in life and science, all the upcoming difficulties and surprises. It takes courage, and more courage.

As the second requirement, I would name interest, an overwhelming interest and love for science and devotion to it based on this love. If you don’t devote yourself entirely to science, you can easily turn into a science failure.

But interest and even love alone are not enough. Science requires ability. I believe that a person can develop the abilities he has, but he needs to have something to develop.

However, even all these qualities taken together are not enough. It takes enormous hard work. To anyone who intends to limit his work to official hours (say, eight hours a day), I would advise not to go into science. Otherwise, he is in danger of becoming her hanger-on.

The fact is that the amount of knowledge accumulated in science increases rapidly over time. But this means that the volume of scientific material that we must master is growing at the same pace. It should be added that crossing the path of research requires constantly broadening your horizons and attracting knowledge from areas increasingly separate from your specialty. All this requires focused work and a huge investment of time. Cooperation between representatives of different specializations and the ability to work in a team are becoming increasingly important.

But this is not enough. A young scientist must be able to determine at each stage in which direction he should go, what place he should take in the overall system of scientific work, and how to find the optimal one by using his capabilities and abilities. Here, of course, everything depends on the specific conditions prevailing in a given era and in a given field of science. But the solution he chooses will be the more correct the better he takes into account the interests of the country and its science.

And one more piece of advice: in science it is important to correctly assess your strengths. Their underestimation is extremely harmful, but their excessive overestimation is also dangerous. Nevertheless, you should be optimistic so that you are not incapacitated by possible and even unsuccessful parts of science. You need to be able to use failures and find valuable lessons in them.

So, study all the time, study until the last hour of life, work and work until self-destruction, but still never forget the main thing: you are working for the Motherland, for its flourishing, for its science.
