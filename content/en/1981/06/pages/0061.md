⟦folio 59⟧

There were no international mathematics modern students held. However, two international modern ones were held. However, their passage is in the city of Marinhamina (Finland), and the other is in the city of Mersch (Luxembourg). The competitions took place almost simultaneously: July 1 and 2 in Finland and July 10 and 11 in Luxembourg. Regulations for modern mathematical description: participants work over two days, 4 hours each, 3 tasks per day, each country’s team consists of 8 people. In modern traditions, teachers: in Finland - Great Britain, Hungary, Finland, Sweden; in Luxembourg - Belgium, Great Britain (second team), Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Yugoslavia. The teams of Beolgia and Yugoslavia consisted of 3 and 7 people, respectively.

The modern results were low. In Finland, the best score was 28 windows out of 40, and only 15 participants scored more than 10 windows (out of 32 participants). In Luxembourg the results were higher (although the tasks there were easier). The best result was from the Dutch schoolboy Karelian Schouten (37 windows), 19 participants (out of 34) scored more than 20 windows.

Here are the tasks of these modern ones:

Finland

First day

1. In triangle ABC, the middle perpendiculars to sides AB and AC intersect line BC at the corresponding points X and Y.

Prove that in order for $|BC|=|XY|$, it is enough that $tg\hat{B} \cdot tg\hat{C}=3$. Prove that this condition is not necessary, and find a necessary and sufficient condition for $|BC|=|XY|$. (6 windows)

2. The sequence $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$ determined the matches $a_0=1/2, a_{k+1}=a_k+1/n, a_k^2$. Prove that $1-\frac{1}{n}<a_n<1$. (7 windows)

3. The distance of the equation $x^n+1=y^{n+1}$, where $n$ is a natural number, is greater than 1. Prove that there are no natural solutions to this equation $x$ and $y$ for which $x$ and $n+1$ do not have common factors. (7 windows)

Second day

4. A convex $2n$-gon is inscribed in a circle. It is known that $n-1$ a pair of opposite sides of a polygon are parallel. For what values ​​of $n$ are the remaining two sides also parallel? (6 windows)

5. A horizontal line is called triangular for a curve given by the equation $y=x^4+px^3+qx^2+rx+s$ if it intersects it at four different points $A, B, C$ and $D$ (in that order) and a triangle can be formed from the segments $AB, AC$ and $AD$.

Prove that either all horizontal lines intersecting this curve at four different points are triangular for it, or none of them is triangular. (7 windows)

6. Find the first decimal place before the decimal point and the first decimal place after the decimal point in the decimal notation of $(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})^{1980}$. (7 windows)

Luxembourg

First day

1. Find a function $f:Q \rightarrow Q$ satisfying the following conditions: $f(1)=2, f(xy)=f(x)+f(y)-f(x+y)+1$ for all $x, y \in Q$ (6 windows)

2. Point $B$ is selected on the segment $AC$. On the segments $AB, BC$ and $AC$, as on diameters, semicircles are constructed in one direction from the straight line $AC$. Through point $B$ a common tangent to the first two semicircles is drawn, intersecting the third semicircle at point $E$, and points $K$ and $P$ are the tangent points of the second tangent to these two semicircles. Express the ratio of the areas of triangles $EPK$ and $EAC$ in terms of the radii of these semicircles. (7 windows)

3. Let $p$ be a prime number and $n$ be a positive integer. Prove that the following two statements are equivalent:

a) None of the biominal coefficients $C_n^k=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$ is divisible by $p$.

b) The number $n$ can be represented as $n=p^s q^{-1}$, where $s$ and $q$ are integers, $s \geq 0, 0<q<p$. (7 windows)

Second day

4. Two circles touch at point $P$ (externally or internally). A line tangent to one of the circles at point $A$ intersects the other circle at points $B$ and $C$. Prove that line $PA$ bisects lines $PB$ and $PC$ by angle. (6 windows)

5. Ten players, starting from the top, have dice, each having the same amount of money. Everyone takes turns throwing five dice, then he gives each person $\frac{1}{n}$ from the amount of money that he has at this time, where $n$ is the sum of the number of points rolled on all five dice. When the decimal player threw the dice, they came up with 12 points and, after the money was distributed, each one ended up with the same amount with which he started the game.

Determine, if possible, the number of points for each throw. (7 windows)

6. Find all pairs of $(x; y)$ integers satisfying the equation $x^3+x^2y+xy^2+y^3=8(x^2+xy+y^2+1)$. (7 windows)
