This period, rounded to 6585 1/3 days, or 18 years and 10 1/3 or 11 1/3 days, depending on the number of leap years in this period, is called karos. According to Herodotus, it was known back in the sixth century BC. e. During one karos, 15 partial, 14 collegiate, 2 collegiate-total and 12 total eclipses will occur, and in total there will be 43 solar eclipses, which after the expiration of the karos alternate in the same order. However, such a number of days in Karos leads to the fact that at the end of Karos the Earth turns an additional 1/3 of a revolution, so that eclipses each time move 120° to the west. Therefore, with the help of carosa, one can predict only the day and the general nature of the future eclipse, but without detailed indication of the places of its visibility.

Now eclipses affect many years in advance with an accuracy of a few seconds in time and up to two kilometers in the place of its visibility, which is the result of a huge and long-term work on studying the movement of the Sun and Moon across the starry sky.
