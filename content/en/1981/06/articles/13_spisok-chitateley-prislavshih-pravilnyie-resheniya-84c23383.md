⟦folio 41⟧

F662. To recharge a battery with an emf $\mathcal{E}=12$ V from a powerful voltage source $U=5$ V, a circuit is assembled from a coil with an inductance $L=1$ Gi, a diode D and a resistor K (see figure), which periodically closes and opens for equal periods of time $\tau_1=\tau_2=0,01$ s. Determine the average battery charge current.

In order to find the average battery charge current, you need to determine the charge flowing on the battery during one closing-opening cycle of the key.

When the switch is closed, the coil is directly connected to the source and the resulting self-induction emf is equal to $U$. This means that the current through the coils changes according to a linear law. Considering that at the moment the key is closed, the current through the coils begins to kill linearly according to the law

$$I = \frac{U}{L} t.$$

By the time the key opens, the current will be equal to

$$I_0 = \frac{U}{L} \tau_1.$$

After opening the key, the diode will open and the self-induction EMF $\mathcal{E}_{c,n}$ becomes the difference in direction of the battery and the battery - $\mathcal{E}_{c,n}=U-\mathcal{E}$ - and changes sign. This means that the current through the coils begins to kill linearly according to the law

$$I = I_0 - \frac{\mathcal{E}-U}{L} t.$$

The rate of current decrease (with the switch open) turns out to be greater than the rate of current increase (with the switch closed). This means that even before time $\tau_2$ expires, after time $\tau_3$ (from the moment the switch opens) the current will drop to zero and at this moment the diode will close. The time $\tau_3$ during which the battery is recharged will be found from the condition

$$\frac{\mathcal{E}-U}{L} \tau_3 = I_0 = \frac{U}{L} \tau_1,$$

where

$$\tau_3 = \frac{U}{\mathcal{E}-U} \tau_1.$$

The charge that will flow through the battery during this time is equal to

$$\Delta q = I_{cp} \tau_3 = \frac{1}{2} I_0 \frac{U}{\mathcal{E}-U} \tau_1 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{U^2 \tau_1^2}{L(\mathcal{E}-U)}.$$

The average battery charge current is

$$I_{cp} = \frac{\Delta q}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} = \frac{U^2 \tau_1^2}{2L(\mathcal{E}-U)(\tau_1 + \tau_2)} \approx 8,9 \text{ мА.}$$

A. Zilberman
