⟦folio 44⟧

G. Topadze

In the magical world of numbers

In the world of numbers there are amazing equalities and ratios. We would like to introduce the reader to some of them.

Symmetrical product

Take a look at the following equalities:
$$12 \cdot 42 = 24 \cdot 21,$$
$$102 \cdot 402 = 204 \cdot 201,$$
$$1002 \cdot 4002 = 2004 \cdot 2001,$$
$$10002 \cdot 40002 = 20004 \cdot 20001.$$

Wonderful symmetry - isn't it? This series of equalities can be continued; it is only necessary to note that
$$(10^n + 2) \cdot (4 \cdot 10^n + 2) =$$
$= (2 \cdot 10^n + 4) \cdot (2 \cdot 10^n + 1).$

(At $n=1, 2, 3, 4$ our equalities are obtained.)

Standing square numbers

Who doesn't know that 121 is a “square number”: it is equal to $11^2$. It is interesting that this number has some “persistence” - it is square not only in decimal, but also in any other number system whose base is greater than two.

Indeed, the empty base of the number system is equal to $a$ and $a > 2$:
$$121_a = 1 \cdot a^2 + 2 \cdot a + 1 = (a + 1)^2,$$
that is, $121_a$ is the square of the number obtained by adding one to the base of the system.

Are there other stable square numbers? It turns out that it exists. For example, the numbers 144 and 441 are square numbers in any number system with base $a > 4$.

We invite the reader to find other similar numbers.

Squaring

See how you can square the numbers 44, 55, 66 in an original way:

$$\begin{array}{c c c}
44^2 & 55^2 & 66^2 \\
16 & 25 & 36 \\
+1616 & +2525 & +3636 \\
16 & 25 & 36 \\
1936 & 3025 & 4356
\end{array}$$

$77^2, 88^2, 99^2$ can be found in a similar way. And everything here comes down to equality
$$11^2 = 121 = 10 + 101 + 10.$$

Indeed, for example, for $44^2$ we have
$$44^2 = (4 \cdot 11)^2 = 16 \cdot 121 =$$
$$= 16 \cdot (10 + 101 + 10) =$$
$$= 160 + 1616 + 160.$$

The result obtained can be presented in the form of the first entry above.

Similarly, if we use the equality
$$111^2 = 12321 =$$
$$= 100 + 1010 + 10101 + 1010 + 100,$$
You can establish a unique way of squaring some three-digit numbers. For example,
$$555^2 = (5 \cdot 111)^2 = 25 \cdot 12321 =$$
$$= 25(100 + 1010 + 10101 + 1010 + 100) =$$
$$= 2500 + 25250 + 252525 + 25250 + 2500,$$
$$666^2 = (6 \cdot 111)^2 = 36 \cdot 12321 =$$
$$= 36(100 + 1010 + 10101 + 1010 + 100) =$$
$$= 3600 + 36360 + 363636 + 36360 + 3600.$$

Based on the obtained equalities, we can write:

$$\begin{array}{c c c}
555^2 & 666^2 \\
25 & 36 \\
2525 & 3636 \\
+252525 & +363636 \\
2525 & 3636 \\
25 & 36 \\
308025 & 443556
\end{array}$$

This original method can also be used to find the squares of four-digit, five-digit,... numbers of the type considered.
