In the name of the flourishing of science in our Motherland

One of the modern legends speaks of a young man who was an aspiring researcher in the field of theoretical physics. He came to his supervisor A, a scientist distinguished by his enormous scientific initiative and original thought, and received from him a topic for his first research and instructions on where to begin work on the proposed topic.

The young man boarded a plane and flew to the city where another prominent scientist lived, V., who became famous for his profound ability to use mathematical apparatus in theoretical physics. The young man described to him the essence of his topic and the direction in which he was going to begin its development. B really liked the topic, he highly appreciated its originality and the correct choice of the direction of the first steps. Moreover, possessing impeccable technical research, he advised exactly how best to carry out this first part of the work. Having transferred all this to paper, the young man wandered off to the third city, where he lived with theoretical phenomenal erudition in the same area. The theoretical one was delighted with both the topic and the implementation of the first steps and, telling about the mistakes that were made in previous times by other scientists working on similar problems, gave a number of valuable advice. The young man returned to A and reported on the results achieved. Very satisfied with the success of a real young man, And with satisfaction he gave advice on how to overcome the next difficulties and move forward.

The enterprising young man repeated the advice of his consultants several times. The result was an original work, brilliant in technical execution and erudition, for which he was awarded all the degrees and distinctions that a physicist can receive.

Academician Viktor Amaazpovich Ambartsumyan - twice Hero of Social Labor, laureate of State Prizes, chairman of the Academy of Sciences of the Armenian USSR, director of the Bioprascan Astrophysical Observatory.

The article is reprinted with slight abbreviations from the collection “Lenin. Science. Youth", published in 1980 by the Nauka publishing house.

The legend adds only one detail. In a conversation with each of the consultants, the young scientist did not talk about the help received from the other two. From this detail, the authors of the legend drew a conclusion that fulfilled its hero.

Fully joining this moral realization, I would like to note at the same time that the young man essentially showed some qualities that in themselves not only do not contain anything positive, but would be useful for many organizers of science.

Indeed, how good it would be if there were more people who knew how to impoverish the scientific services of scientists of different types, accustomed to working in isolation from each other, and would do this not out of some “private” or careerist interests, but out of duty as organizers of science.

Obviously they believe that the organizer of science must choose the subject of research, support the necessary personnel, distribute work among them, supply them with apparatus and find ways to apply the completed work in life. Of course, an organizer of science needs to be able to do all this.

But, it is believed, it is necessary that he be able to actively compare the thoughts of different authors with each other, impoverish their knowledge and, delving deeply into the essence of their thoughts, build scientific brains that impoverish the diverse creativity of different scientists.

At the same time, there will be many creative elements in the work of such an organizer.

This means that if young people with such ability as the hero of the legend are sent along the right path, then there will be a gain in the organization of science.

I learned about the above legend just before the commissioning of the new large telephone line of the Bioprakan Astrophysical Observatory, at a time when I myself was in dire need of scientific advice. After the first tests, it was necessary to begin the first series of photographic symbols of the sky using the new instrument. Knowing that two young employees who do not have any academic degrees, Armen Gyulbudaghyan and Tigran Magakyan, had just successfully completed Japanese maps of the famous Palomar sky atlas of very faint cometary nebulae that had not yet been discovered by other astronomers, I turned to them with a request to display the most interesting objects from among the discovered nebulae for inclusion in the Nagorago Telephone program. And they showed me an object in the atlas, the appearance of which was completely atypical for cometary nebulae. The latter is promised to attract attention by the presence of a tail, or “fan”, resting its apex on the star. Here there was only a star, there was no fan, and only at a considerable distance from the star there was a small weak luminous “stub”, the connection of which with the star seemed problematic. Disappointed at first with my impression, I began to object, but, feeling the conviction of my employees in the need to study this object, I gave in and agreed to include it in the program. In the second half of the day, after they left, I reproached myself for my softness and compliance.

Two or three days later I received a SIM card of this object, received on a large phone. The glowing “stub” was no longer there. In the two decades following the Palomar Atlas, it disappeared, but the star was adjacent to a bright Russian fan, which had never existed before and the details of its structure were clearly visible. The star itself has also changed. Its brightness increased greatly, and the color of the head became red. For our Observatory and for all of our astronomy, this was a happy discovery, since our interest is increasingly concentrated around the phenomenon of object variability and their non-stationality. Although variability has also been observed in some cometary nebulae (over the past hundred years, perhaps four or five

It is not the legend given above, but precisely this real incident associated with the names of two young astrophysicists, that is characteristic of the behavior of our Soviet scientific youth.

Reflecting on the short stories outlined above gives me reason to express some wishes to young people going into science.

First of all, I want to wish her to be brave and ready to face all the changes in life and science, all the upcoming difficulties and surprises. It takes courage, and more courage.

As the second requirement, I would name interest, an overwhelming interest and love for science and devotion to it based on this love. If you don’t devote yourself entirely to science, you can easily turn into a science failure.

But interest and even love alone are not enough. Science requires ability. I believe that a person can develop the abilities he has, but he needs to have something to develop.

However, even all these qualities taken together are not enough. It takes enormous hard work. To anyone who intends to limit his work to official hours (say, eight hours a day), I would advise not to go into science. Otherwise, he is in danger of becoming her hanger-on.

The fact is that the amount of knowledge accumulated in science increases rapidly over time. But this means that the volume of scientific material that we must master is growing at the same pace. It should be added that crossing the path of research requires constantly broadening your horizons and attracting knowledge from areas increasingly separate from your specialty. All this requires focused work and a huge investment of time. Cooperation between representatives of different specializations and the ability to work in a team are becoming increasingly important.

But this is not enough. A young scientist must be able to determine at each stage in which direction he should go, what place he should take in the overall system of scientific work, and how to find the optimal one by using his capabilities and abilities. Here, of course, everything depends on the specific conditions prevailing in a given era and in a given field of science. But the solution he chooses will be the more correct the better he takes into account the interests of the country and its science.

And one more piece of advice: in science it is important to correctly assess your strengths. Their underestimation is extremely harmful, but their excessive overestimation is also dangerous. Nevertheless, you should be optimistic so that you are not incapacitated by possible and even unsuccessful parts of science. You need to be able to use failures and find valuable lessons in them.

So, study all the time, study until the last hour of life, work and work until self-destruction, but still never forget the main thing: you are working for the Motherland, for its flourishing, for its science.
