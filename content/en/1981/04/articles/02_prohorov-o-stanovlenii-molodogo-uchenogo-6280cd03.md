It must be said that the role of a manager is extremely important precisely at the initial stage of a young scientist’s work. It does not matter whether the young person is called a graduate student, a research student, a graduate student or a junior researcher. The formulation of the research problem is of particular importance at this stage. It is in it that the experience accumulated by the leader, his understanding of the day of the matter is concentrated, his intuition and, if you like, his creativity are manifested. At the time of setting the problem and in the first steps of its research, the manager, in addition, has a greater amount of specific knowledge on the topic being developed than a young researcher. But if the matter is done correctly, if the leader cultivates independence in the young man, and he shows it, then by the time the problem is solved, it is he who has the most powerful specific knowledge on this problem. The duty of the manager is to take an active interest in the progress of the research, carefully observe the style and nature of the work of the young researcher and carefully direct his efforts.

Here again the question of authority and independence arises. You cannot follow the original task. Research, the process of understanding the laws of the objective world, the process of discovering new phenomena has its own logic. When conducting fundamental research, both an experienced leader and a creative, gifted young researcher must follow this Pozanin logic, not turn away new opportunities that unexpectedly arise during the research, and boldly follow new paths that would seem to lead away from the first similar goal.

An excellent example of the correct combination of the tact of a leader and the perseverance of a researcher is the discovery by S. I. Vavilov and P. A. Cherekov of a phenomenon that later became known as the Cherekov effect. The task was set by S.I. Vavilov in terms of studying the logistics of liquids under the influence of fast electrons. Very soon it was found out that it has a weak glow, which is not a logistic impurity, and in general is not a logistic substance under the influence of electrons. It would seem that the study should have been stopped. From the point of view of the task at hand, the work has reached a dead end. But P. A. Cherekov also pursued independence of thinking. Thus, a new weak phenomenon was transformed into a strong effect, which, as is well known, plays an extremely important role in modern physics. It must be said that all this happened with the full support of Academician S. I. Vavilov, the head of P. A. Cherekov’s graduate work.

Very often, a researcher in his work has to overcome a psychological phenomenon that can be called an authority of public opinion, which plays a conservative role. This is the most difficult transformation. Only a scientist who can think independently, who has developed in himself, including under the influence of an experimental and tactical teacher, the ability to form his own opinion and the ability to defend this opinion, can successfully overcome such a transformation.

A great example is the creation by Academician P. L. Kapitsa and his introduction into production of a turbocharged coolant. All the specialists in deep cooling machines, all the ruined companies working in this area, responded to the case of P. L. Kapitsa simply as unserious. Only perseverance, authenticity, talent as an experimental physicist and engineer, and a deep understanding of the physics of processes that play an extremely important role in the expectation of gases allowed P. L. Kapitsa to completely solve the problem posed.

These striking examples refer to works awarded Nobel Prizes in physics. This was the highlight of the work, and that is why they are used as illustrative examples for using a simple thought: fruitful scientific work is unthinkable without independent thinking, and this must be learned from a young age.

It must be said that the role of a leader does not exhaust the task and direction of the young researcher’s work. In the same way, a young person must not only work hard at the task at hand, absorb the experience of his elders and develop only his independence. Modern research work is largely collaborative. Research work is carried out in large laboratories, often work is carried out on many different topics, connected, however, by a common big “overarching goal”. And here it is extremely important to develop goodwill and comradely mutual assistance, to be able to please each other’s successes, to support and rip each other off, to criticize each other, having the benefit of the matter first in mind, to be able to serve criticism and perceive it correctly. All this needs to be learned.

The role of an authoritative leader in creating and maintaining such a truly creative atmosphere in a team cannot be overestimated. On the other hand, it is young researchers who make up a large and, due to their youth, promising part of the team. Therefore, their moral level largely determines the moral climate of the team. In matters of moral education of the team, the authority of a true leader must be indisputable.

It must be said that collectivism and the correct system of evaluation of education at scientific seminars are extremely effective. The leader should conduct the seminar in such a way, doing it not intrusively, but strictly, so that the youth see goodwill and support for the new, principled and objectivity, high demands and constructive criticism. On the other hand, it is at seminars that young scientists should learn not to confine themselves only to their task, only to themselves; they should learn to acquire a broad outlook and outlook, and should enrich their experience with product experience.

The above applies to those aspects of the development of a scientist’s personality that are directly connected by the teacher-student relationship. In these relationships, the authority of the teacher and the independence of the student are more important.

In short, the authority of the teacher and the independence of their students is necessary for both seniors and juniors, for the successful work of the research team as a whole.
