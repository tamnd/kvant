**M631. Two-digit numbers from 19 to 80 are written out in a row. Is the recipient's number 192021...7980 divisible by 1980?**

Since 1980 = 99 · 20, and 99 and 20 do not have common divisors, it is enough to find out whether the number A = 192021...7980 is divisible by 20 and 99. The divisibility of A by 20 is obvious. Next, note that 100 to any power when divided by 99 leaves a remainder of 1, so the number

$$A = 19 \cdot 100^{61} + 20 \cdot 100^{60} + \dots + 79 \cdot 100 + 80$$

when divided by 99 gives the same remainder as the number

$$B = 19 + 20 + \dots + 79 + 80 = \frac{19 + 80}{2} \cdot 62 = 99 \cdot 31.$$

So, it turns out that the number B is divisible by 99; therefore, the number A is divisible by 99, and at the same time by 1980.

F638. While giving lectures in Strasbourg in 1908, Academician L.I. Mandelstam amazed the employees with the following red drink. Two tuning forks on resonant boxes with resonant parts of 500 Hz and 505 Hz were placed side by side and one of the tuning forks was excited. The second tuning fork practically did not respond to the vibrations of the first. But as soon as the experimenter began to periodically close and open the box of the tuning fork with his hand, the second tuning fork immediately began to pound. Explain the result of this experiment.

Waves of frequency $f=500$ Hz emanate from the box of a tuning fork. Periodically closing the box, L.I. Mandelstam changed the amplitude of the oscillations, we come to the second tuning fork. What are oscillations with changing amplitude - amplitude-modified oscillations?

Let's consider the simplest case when the modulation is sinusoidal, that is, the amplitude $A$ changes according to the sine (cosine) law with frequency $F$:

$$A = A_0 \cos 2\pi F t.$$

In this case, the change in pressure (or density) of air, which is a eucus wave, occurs according to the law

$$\Delta p = (A_0 \cos 2\pi F t) \cos 2\pi f t.$$ (*)

Let's transform the right side of the equality (*), using the well-known formulas of trionometry:

$$\Delta p = \frac{A_0}{2} [\cos 2\pi(f-F)t + \cos 2\pi(f+F)t].$$

Thus, oscillations of frequency $f$, the amplitude of which changes with frequency $F$, can be represented as a sum (superposition) of harmonic oscillations (that is, oscillations of a sinusoidal shape with a constant amplitude) of two frequencies:

$$f_1 = f - F$$ and $$f_2 = f + F.$$ If $F=5$ Hz, then the frequency $f_2$ coincides with the resonant frequency of the receiver tuning fork, the corresponding oscillation component swings this tuning fork, and it begins to hum.

With an arbitrary, non-sinusoidal, periodic modulation of oscillations, their representation as a sum of harmonic components (their spectrum) will contain more than two terms (harmonics) - saying that the spectrum of oscillations will be rich. But if the modulation period is equal to 1/F, then the spectrum will necessarily have a component with frequency $f+F$ (as well as with frequency $f-F$). We considered the sinusoidal modulation law only because in this case the decomposition into harmonics looks the simplest and can be carried out in an elementary way.

Taking the coordinate of the load at the initial moment as zero (see Fig.ok). Then the dependence of the elasticity forces acting on the load on the coordinate $x$ of the load will be as follows:

$$F_1(x) = -(l_1+x)k_1,$$
$$F_2(x) = (l_2-x)k_2.$$

Here $(l_1+x)$, $(l_2-x)$ are algebraic values ​​of the length of the first and second springs, respectively. At the moment the load passes the equilibrium position, the total force $F$ acting on the load from the springs is zero. Let us denote $x_0$ the coordinate of the load at this moment. Then

$$F(x_0) = F_1(x_0) + F_2(x_0) = -(l_1+x_0)k_1 + (l_2-x_0)k_2 = 0,$$

where

$$x_0 = \frac{l_2k_2-l_1k_1}{k_1+k_2}.$$

$$A = |x_0| = \frac{|l_2k_2-l_1k_1|}{k_1+k_2}.$$

$$F = F_1 + F_2 = -x(k_1+k_2) + (l_2k_2-l_1k_1) = -x(k_1+k_2) + x_0(k_1+k_2) = -(k_1+k_2)(x-x_0)$$

((x-x_0) - deviation of the load from the equilibrium position). It can be seen that the desired dependence is the same as if one spring with a stiffness of $k=k_1+k_2$ was attached to the load. Therefore, the period of the oscillating load is equal to

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k_1+k_2}}.$$

F641. A wall clock with a matrix has a mass of $M=5$ kts. The mass of the load at the end of the light matrix is ​​$m=150$ kts. What error in the clock readings will accumulate over the course of a day if the clock is suspended from the ceiling on two long parallel cords? Believe that the clock attached to the wall is accurate.

Clocks suspended on cords, unlike clocks mounted on the wall, can swing. If the cords are parallel, then when swinging the movement of the watch case will be translational, that is, all points of the watch (except for the matrix) will move equally. This means that the entire mass of a clock without a matrix ($M-m$) can be considered concentrated at one point, for example at the point $A$ of the matrix suspension (Fig. 1).

The movement of the watch body when it swings is not VELKI - much less than the amplitude of oscillations of the watch matrix (this will be shown below). Therefore, long cords will remain almost vertical at all times. The longer the cords, the better this condition will be fulfilled.

Thus, the cords acting on the watch can be considered directed vertically upward. Gravity forces acting on the body and matrix are directed vertically downwards. There are no other external forces. Consequently, in the horizontal direction on the system under consideration (watch case + matrix) no external forces act, and the center of mass of this system will not move horizontally.

It is now clear that when the matrix oscillates, the watch body (point $A$) will oscillate in antiphase - so that the center of mass (point $C$ on figure 2) remains on the same vertical all the time. The mixtures of the matrix weight $s_1$ and the watch body $s_2$ at any moment will be inversely proportional to the corresponding masses:

$$\frac{s_1}{s_2} = \frac{M-m}{m}.$$ (1)

From formula (1) it is clear that at $m \ll M$ the amplitude $S_2$ of the body swaying is much less than the amplitude $S_1$ of the clock matrix oscillations ($S_2 \ll S_1$).

It remains to find out what the period $T_1$ of matrix oscillations will be. Looking at figure 2 and remembering that the point $C$ does not move horizontally, it is easy to report that the movement of the weight matrix $m$ can be approximately represented as free oscillations of a mathematical matrix of length $l_1$. (Strictly speaking, point $C$ moves vertically; however, with a small amplitude of matrix oscillations, these movements are columnar, which does not affect the oscillation period.)

From the similarity of protections on figure 2 triangles it is clear that

$$\frac{l}{l_1} = 1 + \frac{s_2}{s_1}.$$ (2)

Substituting here $s_2/s_1$ from correspondence (1), we find

$$\frac{l_1}{l} = 1 - \frac{m}{M}.$$ (2)

The period of oscillation of a mathematical matrix is ​​proportional to the square root of its length: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$. Since $l_1 < l$, the period $T_1$ of matrix oscillations in a clock suspended on long cords is less than the period $T$ of matrix oscillations in a clock mounted on the wall. This means that the clock suspended on cords will be in a hurry.

Taking into account correspondence (2), we find the relation $T_1/T$:

$$\frac{T_1}{T} = \sqrt{\frac{T_1}{l}} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{m}{M}} \approx 1 - \frac{m}{2M}.$$ (3)

(Here we taught that according to the conditions of the problem $m/M=0,03 \ll 1$, and we used the approximate formula $\sqrt{1+x} \approx 1+x/2$, valid for $|x| \ll 1$.)

Now from (3) it is easy to find the decrease in period $\Delta T = T-T_1$:

$$\Delta T = T \frac{m}{2M}.$$

Substituting the values ​​$m$ and $M$ from the condition, we find: $\Delta T = 0,015T$. Over the course of a day, a clock suspended on cords will move forward by 21 minutes 36 seconds.

In this case, alternating current will flow through the series connection of the capacitors

$$i = I_0 \sin \omega t = \left( U_0 \omega \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \right) \sin \omega t,$$
