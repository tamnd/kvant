F642. figure shows the idealized voltage-voltage characteristic of the diode. A capacitor emkocti $C = 100$ uF, charged to a voltage of $U = 5$ V, is connected through a diode to a resistor with a resistance $R = 100$ Ohm. What amount of tell will be released on the resistor when the capacitor is discharged?

While the discharge current exceeds $I_0 = 10$ mA, the voltage across the diode remains constant and equal to $U_0 = 1$ V. By the time the current drops to a value of $I_0$, the voltage across the capacitor is equal to

$$U_1 = U_0 + RI_0.$$

The charge that has flowed through the circuit by this time is equal to

$$q = C(U_0 - RI_0).$$

According to the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{CU^2}{2} - \frac{C(U_0 + RI_0)^2}{2} = U_0q + W_1,$$

where $W_1$ is the tello allocated on the resistor up to this moment. Further, the diode is simply equivalent to a resistor with a resistance of $r = U_0/I_0$. With further discharge, a tello is released on resistor $R$.

$$W_2 = \frac{CU^2}{2} \frac{R}{R+r} = \frac{C(U_0 + RI_0)^2}{2} \frac{R}{R + \frac{U_0}{I_0}}.$$

The total amount of tell that will be released on the resistor when the capacitor is discharged is equal to

$$W = W_1 + W_2.$$

Substituting the values ​​$W_2$ and $W_1 = \frac{C}{2} \left[ U^2 - (U_0 + RI_0)^2 \right] - U_0q$, we find

$$W = \frac{C}{2} \left\{ (U - U_0)^2 + RI_0U_0 \right\} = 8,5 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ Дж.}$$

F643. Two rockets are simultaneously launched from the southern and northern stripes of the Earth with the same initial speeds, directed horizontally. After a time of $\tau = 3$ and 20 minutes, the missiles were at their maximum distance from each other. Determine the maximum distance between missiles. The acceleration of free fall on Earth is considered known. The radius of the Earth is $R_3 = 6400$ km.

The rockets will move in identical elliptical orbits (see figure), which touch the Earth in the North and South lanes. The missiles reach their maximum distance from each other through the flow of the circulation period. Thus, $T = 2\tau$. The maximum distance $h$ rockets from the center of the Earth (at apogee) can be found based on Kepler’s second law, from which it follows that with equal periods of revolution in different orbits (including steep ones), more stripes of orbits are equal to each other. Let us find the radius $r$ of a steep orbit with an orbital period $T$. The linear speed in such an orbit is $2\pi r/T$. Equating centripetal acceleration to the acceleration of gravity, we obtain

$$\left( \frac{2\pi r}{T} \right)^2 \frac{1}{r} = G \frac{M_3}{r^2} = \frac{gR_3^2}{r^2}.$$

From here

$$r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{gR_3^2T^2}{4\pi^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{gR_3^2\tau^2}{\pi^2}} \approx 18 \cdot 10^6 \text{ м}.$$

Based on Kepler's second law $h + R_3 = 2r$. Thus, the maximum distance $L$ between missiles is

$$L = 2h = 2(2r - R_3) = 5,92 \cdot 10^6 \text{ м} = 59200 \text{ км}.$$

It is interesting to note that the problem also allows for other solutions, since the rockets find themselves at the maximum distance from each other not only through rotation periods, but also through $\frac{3}{2}T$, $\frac{5}{2}T$, etc. In the general case, the conditions of the problem are satisfied by all orbits with rotation periods $T_n = \frac{2}{n}\tau$ ($n$ is any odd number), for which $h_n \geq R_3$. The last conditions can be rewritten in the form $r_n \geq R_3$, where $r_n$ is the radius of a steep orbit with an orbital period of $T_n$. The solution we find $r$ corresponds to the following n=1.
