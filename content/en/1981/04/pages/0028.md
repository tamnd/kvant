Knowledge $\varphi = \varphi_0$ is characterized by the fact that the segments $OH$ and $OE$ coincide, which corresponds to the chord $BD$ perpendicular to $AC$; for this knowledge

$$\cos \frac{\varphi_0}{2} = \frac{a}{R}.$$

So, it remains to find the maximum value of the area of ​​the triangle $OBD$ at $\varphi_0 \leqslant \varphi < \pi$. There are two possible cases:

1) If $\varphi_0 \leqslant \frac{\pi}{2}$, then the maximum is achieved at $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$. In this case

$$\frac{a}{R} = \cos \frac{\varphi_0}{2} \geqslant \cos \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},$$

$$a \geqslant \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}}.$$

and the desired chord $BD$, contracting the arc to $90^\circ$, must be absent from the center at a distance $R/\sqrt{2}$, that is, it must touch the circle with center $O$ of radius $R/\sqrt{2}$.

2) If $\varphi_0 > \frac{\pi}{2}$, which will happen at $a < \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}}$, then the maximum area is achieved at $\varphi = \varphi_0$ - the desired chord $BD$ must be perpendicular to the diameter $AC$.

Let us prove that for any $m \in \mathbb{N}$ at least one of the numbers $m, m+1$ is equal to $S_n$ for some $n$. Since $S_1 = 2$, for $m=1$ and $m=2$ the statement of the problem is true. If $m > 2$, then there is $S_n$, less than $m$ (for example, $S_1$), and at the same time for $n \geqslant m$ $S_n > n \geqslant m$. Consequently, among $n$ such that $S_n < m$, one finds its most general through $N$. Then $S_{n+1} \geqslant m > S_n$, so $N$ cannot be on the number 9 and, therefore, $S_{n+1} = S_n + 2$. Therefore, $m \leqslant S_{n+1} < m+2$, therefore the number $S_{n+1}$ is equal to $m$, or $m+1$ - the required is proven.
