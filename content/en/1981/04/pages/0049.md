Geometric extremum problems have become familiar to applicants. Indicative in this regard is the problem proposed by E. Gotman (Arzamas).

Example 7. A triangle ABC is fixed, in which
$$|AC|=b, |AB|=c, \widehat{AC}=\alpha,$$
and $\alpha<\pi/2$. In the plane of this triangle, all possible rectangles ALMN are considered, so that vertex B of the triangle lies on the side LM of the rectangle, and vertex C of the triangle lies on the side MN of the rectangle. What is the maximum area of ​​a rectangle that has the indicated property?

From figure 4, which shows rectangles “circumscribed” around triangle ABC, it is clear that as a parameter characterizing the choice of a rectangle, we can take the values ​​of the angle $x$ between the sides AL of the rectangle and the sides AB of the triangle. Then it is obvious that the area of ​​the rectangle ALMN $S=|AL|\cdot|AN|=$
$$=c \cos x \cdot b \cos \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x - \alpha \right)=$$
$$=bc \cos x \sin(x+\alpha).$$

However, it is also necessary to indicate, based on the geometric meaning of the problem, the domain of definition of the functions $S=S(x)$. For brevity, let's put ABC=$\beta$, $\widehat{ACB}=\gamma$.

Let ALMN be some “circumscribed” rectangle; then it is not difficult to check (make sure of this) that the following relationship holds:
$$x \geqslant \begin{cases} 0, & \text{ если } \beta < \pi/2, \\ \beta - \pi/2, & \text{ если } \beta \geqslant \pi/2, \end{cases} \quad (4)$$
$$x + \alpha \leqslant \begin{cases} \pi/2, & \text{ если } \gamma < \pi/2, \\ \pi - \gamma, & \text{ если } \gamma \geqslant \pi/2, \end{cases} \quad (5)$$
$$x \leqslant \beta, \pi/2 - (x+\alpha) \leqslant \gamma. \quad (6)$$

It is very important that the converse statement is also true: if $x$ simultaneously satisfies all inequalities (4) - (6), then the rectangle corresponding to this

value of the angle $x$, has the connection specified in the conditions.

Now you can finish solving the problem. To determine this, let us assume that in this triangle the angle ABC is acute (that is, $\beta < \pi/2$; other possibilities are left to the reader to consider). In this case, the task is obviously to find the most important functions
$$S=bc \cos x \sin(x+\alpha)$$
at $0 \leqslant x \leqslant \min(\beta, \pi/2-\alpha)$. (7)

Since (check!) $S'(x) = bs \cos(2x+\alpha)$, the equation $S'(x) = 0$ has root $x_0=\pi/4-\alpha/2$ (we cannot be interested in the other roots). The specification form of the domain of definition of functions (7) leads to the following final result:
$$\alpha+2\beta > \pi/2, \text{ то } x_0 \text{ является критической точкой функции (7)},$$
and it reaches its greatest significance there:
$$S_{\max} = bc \cos^2(\pi/4-\alpha/2) = \frac{1}{2} bc(1+\sin \alpha);$$
— if $\alpha+2\beta \leqslant \pi/2$, then function (7) has no critical points and is recurrent, taking on the most values ​​at $x=\beta$:
$$S_{\max} = bc \cos \beta \sin(\alpha+\beta) = \frac{1}{2} c^2 \sin 2\beta.$$

In conclusion, we present an inequality sent to the editor by S. Mayzus (Zaporozhe).
