F645. The loudspeaker has a diffuser with a cross-sectional area $S = 300 \text{ cm}^2$ and a mass $m=5 \text{ e}$.

Let us assume that the circuit connects to the source at time $t=0$. In this case, the capacitor $C_2$ will quickly charge through the diode $D_1$ to a voltage equal to the amplitude value $U_0$ of the alternating voltage. Capacitor $C_2$ will receive a charge

$$Q_0 = U_0 C_2.$$

In subsequent moments of time, diodes $D_1$ and $D_2$ will be closed, that is, they will not pass current, since the potentials of the cathodes will always be greater than the potentials of the anoids and the diodes are ideal (their resistance in the closed state is equal to infinity). The circuit will behave as if diodes $D_1$ and $D_2$ are disabled; therefore the charge $Q_0$ will be conserved.

In this case, alternating current will flow through the series connection of the capacitors

$$i = I_0 \sin \omega t = \left( U_0 \omega \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \right) \sin \omega t,$$

where $I_0 = U_0 \omega \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$ is the current amplitude, $U_0$, $\omega$ are the amplitude and frequency of the alternating voltage. The alternating current will produce an alternating voltage across each capacitor:

$$u_{c1} \sim = \frac{I_0}{\omega C_1} \cos \omega t = U_0 \frac{C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \cos \omega t,$$

$$u_{c2} \sim = \frac{I_0}{\omega C_2} \cos \omega t = U_0 \frac{C_1}{C_1 + C_2} \cos \omega t.$$

In addition to this alternating voltage, a constant voltage component will be present on the capacitors due to the charge of capacitor $C_2$ to voltage $U_0$. To find this constant component of voltage $U$, we write the charge conservation law:

$$C_2 U_0 = C_1 U + C_2 U.$$

From here

$$U = \frac{C_2 U_0}{C_1 + C_2}.$$

Thus, the total voltages on the capacitors will be equal

$$u_{c1} = u_{c1} \sim -U = U_0 \frac{C_2}{C_1 + C_2} \cos \omega t - \frac{C_2 U_0}{C_1 + C_2},$$

$$u_{c2} = u_{c2} \sim +U = U_0 \frac{C_1}{C_1 + C_2} \cos \omega t + \frac{C_2 U_0}{C_1 + C_2}.$$

Plots of these dependent constants for $C_2 = 2C_1$ are shown in Fig. 2.

Note that if the connection of the circuit to the source occurs at time $t \neq 0$, then the circuit acquires the established resistivity after some time, when the diode $D_1$ opens and the capacitor $C_2$ charges to voltage $U_0$.
