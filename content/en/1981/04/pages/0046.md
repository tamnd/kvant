⟦folio 44⟧

There are equations and systems of equations in the solution of which the main role is played not by standard transformations, but by special logical reasoning (see «Kvant», 1978, No. 4, p. 50; 1979, No. 4, p. 50). Here is one of such problems - it was proposed by G. Shishkin (Glazov):

4. Solve the equation

$$\sin^{11} x + \cos^{11} x = \frac{1}{\sin^4 x + \cos^4 x}.$$

* *

“I recently paid attention to the proposal to send to «Kvant» short and clear solutions to problems offered during the entrance exams,” writes 10th grade student Andrei Sazonov (Moscow) in his letter. “I want to talk about one interesting problem.”

Example 1 (KSU, Mechanics and Mathematics, 1979). Solve the equation

$$\frac{2 \mathrm{tg} \frac{x}{2}}{1 + \mathrm{tg}^2 \frac{x}{2}} = y^2 - 4y + 5.$$ (1)

The solution to this problem, published in «Kvant», 1980, No. 6, is based on some estimates of the left and right sides of equation (1). Meanwhile, another way of reasoning quickly leads to the goal.

Since the left side of equation (1) at $x \neq \pi + 2\pi k$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$) is equal to $\sin x$, this equation can be rewritten as

$$y^2 - 4y + (5 - \sin x) = 0$$

and treat it as square with respect to $y$. This equation has roots for those and only those values ​​of $x$ for which the discriminant is $D = \sin x - 1 \geq 0$. It follows that $\sin x = 1$. The following solution is very useful.

Example 2 (KSU, 1975). The lateral edges of a triangular paramid have the same length $l$. Two flat angles at the vertex of the para-

the first ones have magnitudes $a$, and the third one has magnitudes $b$. Find the volume of the paramid.

Let $SABC$ be a given paramid in which $A\widehat{SC} = B\widehat{SC} = a$, $A\widehat{SB} = b$; it is easy to show that $|AC| = |BC|$. If we use the “standard drawing” (S is the top of the paramid, $ABC$ is its base), then to solve it we need to separately consider three different cases: $A\widehat{CB} < 90^\circ$, $A\widehat{CB} = 90^\circ$, $A\widehat{CB} > 90^\circ$.

However, nothing prevents us from “putting the paramid on the other face” - considering the point $C$ as the vertex, and $ASB$ as the base (Fig. 1). Since the plane angles $ASC$ and $BSC$ are congestive, the vertex $C$ projects to some point $P$ lying on the bissertrix $SM$ of the angle $ASB$ (prove it!). Using the formula from §37 of the textbook “Geometry 9-10”, we can write that

$$\cos P\widehat{SC} = \frac{\cos B\widehat{SC}}{\cos B\widehat{SM}} = \frac{\cos a}{\cos \beta}.$$

To complete the solution, all that remains is to calculate the length of the height CP of the paramid (from the triangle $PSC$) and calculate the area of ​​the base $ASB$ (carry out the necessary calculations).
