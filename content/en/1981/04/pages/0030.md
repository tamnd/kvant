F639. Two springs with stiffnesses $k_1$ and $k_2$ are connected at one end to a vertical wall, and at the other to a mass $m$ lying on a horizontal table. At the initial moment, a spring with stiffness $k_1$ stretched by values ​​$l_1$, and a spring with stiffness $k_2$ stretched by values ​​$l_2$. The load is released. Find the amplitude and period of the oscillating load. Neglect the training.

Taking the coordinate of the load at the initial moment as zero (see Fig.ok). Then the dependence of the elasticity forces acting on the load on the coordinate $x$ of the load will be as follows:

$$F_1(x) = -(l_1+x)k_1,$$
$$F_2(x) = (l_2-x)k_2.$$

Here $(l_1+x)$, $(l_2-x)$ are algebraic values ​​of the length of the first and second springs, respectively. At the moment the load passes the equilibrium position, the total force $F$ acting on the load from the springs is zero. Let us denote $x_0$ the coordinate of the load at this moment. Then

$$F(x_0) = F_1(x_0) + F_2(x_0) = -(l_1+x_0)k_1 + (l_2-x_0)k_2 = 0,$$

where

$$x_0 = \frac{l_2k_2-l_1k_1}{k_1+k_2}.$$

The amplitude of the oscillating load is obviously equal to

$$A = |x_0| = \frac{|l_2k_2-l_1k_1|}{k_1+k_2}.$$

To find the oscillating period, we write down how the restoring force $F$ depends on the deviation of the load from the equilibrium position:

$$F = F_1 + F_2 = -x(k_1+k_2) + (l_2k_2-l_1k_1) = -x(k_1+k_2) + x_0(k_1+k_2) = -(k_1+k_2)(x-x_0)$$

((x-x_0) - deviation of the load from the equilibrium position). It can be seen that the desired dependence is the same as if one spring with a stiffness of $k=k_1+k_2$ was attached to the load. Therefore, the period of the oscillating load is equal to

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k_1+k_2}}.$$

A. Bereman

F640. Fig.x 1 and 2 show the boundaries of the wave region generated by the ship on two sections of the route. Red arrows indicate the direction of the ship's speed. In the first section (Fig. 1) there is no flow. The direction of flow in the second section (Fig. 2) is shown by the blue arrow. Determine the speed of the current if the speed of the ship relative to the shore is in general cases the same and equal to 18 km/h.

Let's combine figure 1 and 2 (Fig. 3). From an arbitrary point $A$ on the ship's course, we will carry out a redistribution to the boundary of the wave region in the absence of a current. The length of this redistribution ([AB] or [AB']) determines the distance traveled by the wave during the time after which the ship passed the distance $AO$. The distance $BC$ (or $B'C'$) determines the way the region is bordered by waves and currents over the same period of time. Therefore, the ratio [BC]/[AO] (or $B'C'$/[AO]) determines the ratio of the speed $v$ of the current to the speed of the ship. From the drawing is

$$\frac{v}{v_0} = \frac{1}{5} \Rightarrow v = 3,6 \text{ km/ч}.$$
