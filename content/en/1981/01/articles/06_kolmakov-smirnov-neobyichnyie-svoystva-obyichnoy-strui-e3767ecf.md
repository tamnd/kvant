5. Formation of droplet fog

Take a thin tube (3-5 mm in diameter) and use it to get a thin stream of preferably cold water. Observe the stream and you will see that in its lower part it seems to be collapsing and at the same time becoming cloudy. This is due to the fact that there the jet actually ceases to be monolithic, it breaks up into separate drops. Due to the scattering of light, the eye does not perceive the fall of individual drops, which is why the destroyed section of the jet appears opaque (Fig. 5).

It is interesting that, in addition to relatively large drops, a kind of fog of very small drops appears on the sides of the broken stream. Sometimes they form a wave-stable configuration. Looking at the jet from above, you can see a clearly defined inner line. If you look at the jet from the side, its sinucondal outline immediately catches your eye.
