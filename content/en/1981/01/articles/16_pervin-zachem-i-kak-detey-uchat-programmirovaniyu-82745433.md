Why and how are children taught programming?

In the “Art of Programming” section we continue to publish lessons from our Correspondence School of Programming and articles on information, programming languages ​​and computer technology. Although it is now quite difficult to connect to the second course of the ZShP (on this subject, see «Kvant», 1980, no. 9), new readers of «Kvant» will hopefully be interested in many other articles in this section.

Last summer, the Summer School of Young Programmers was held in the Novosibirsk Academic Town (see «Kvant», 1980, No. 12), in which the best ZShP students and winners of the Programming Olympiad («Kvant», 1980, No. 3) participated. As part of the Summer School, a series of lectures with the title in the title was read for teachers, educators and parents accompanying junior schoolchildren. However, there was no notice on the door of the auditorium in which the series was read, prohibiting entry for “children under 16 years of age.” Below is an excerpt from these lectures.

Tri kateori programmers
Every year there are more and more computers. The number of problems solved using a computer is increasing even faster. It would seem that the prospect is clear: soon everyone will have to write programs in algorithmic languages.

Let's not argue - you need to learn programming. But to whom and how?

From the first steps of the development of computer technology, a division of labor emerged among programs, which has become especially clear today. Programming systems are located closest to computers. These are programming professionals. They create programs that have the main purpose of facilitating communication with the machine for all other computer users: transports from high-level languages, dialog systems, database management programs. Software systems usually receive a university education.

The second “layer” of software is application programs. These are specialists in various subject areas - economics, geology, biology, meteorology, medicine, etc., who, without knowing either the theory of programming or the features of programming on specific computers, have to describe tasks from their subject areas in one of the most suitable programming languages ​​for this purpose. Application programs are prepared by “specialized” universities.

Through the efforts of system and application programmers, solving problems on a machine can be reduced to the level of preparing parameters into ready-made procedures. It is in this way that access to the machine is opened to a wider range of parametric users who may not know either the computer structure, the algorithm for solving the problem, or the language in which the program is written. A parametric user must only be able to correctly pass parameters to solve a problem. A typical representative of parametric computer users is an Aeroflot cashier who turns to the Sirena automated system for information about available seats.

The number of system and application programmers is relatively small, and it increases over time not only significantly. But the number of “simple” parametric computer users is growing and will continue to grow rapidly. Programming will come to the rescue

The importance and general nature of the skills listed requires that people master them in high school. On the other hand, secondary school curricula do not provide for teaching students these skills. And the point is not only that the need for these skills arose relatively recently, along with the rapid manifestations of the modern scientific and technological revolution. Another thing is important: none of the school disciplines - neither physics, nor literature, nor even mathematics - has (in full) the necessary tools to teach a student the skills that are essential for him.

But in programming these tools exist! Indeed, the ability to plan is easy to learn for someone who understands what a procedure, conditions, operator, and cycle are. For information modeling, those means of describing data that are available in modern programming languages ​​- files, sets, files, databases - are well suited. In the same way, to organize a search, various search mechanisms are used (catalogues, polygonal indexes, arranging functions, etc.), and to organize communication processes, various syntactic descriptions and macro means are used (part of the terminological paragraph may not yet be familiar to the reader of the section “The Art of Programming”; some of them will be discussed in the following issues «Kvant»; others will become known in the recommended books).

It is precisely because today we need to learn to think “programmerically”, that is, to firmly free up the skills associated with programming (and not at all because there are a lot of machines), that is why we can confidently answer the first question “Why are children taught programming?”

When and how?

One can say even more precisely about the place of programming in school. The ability to plan, model, search, and communicate are so fundamental that teaching these skills should begin as early as possible - in the lower grades of primary school. The experience of Soviet teachers and programmers who successfully teach programming in the Novosibirsk academic town to 2nd grade schoolchildren, and the experience of American scientists who integrate computers into the 3rd grade curriculum, confirms the correctness of these conclusions.
