Through the pages of school textbooks

M. Gelfand, V. Berman

Ten tasks on the use of arbitrary

The proposed tasks are addressed to ninth graders. Solving them requires an understanding of the geometric and physical meaning of an arbitrary one, as well as the ability to judge the properties of functions from its graph.

Problem 1. Each of figure 1, a—i shows graphs of the time $t$ coordinates $x(t)$ and speed $v(t)$ of a material point moving according to the quadratic law $x(t) = pt^2 + qt + r$ along the coordinate line. Eight speed graphs are incorrect. Determine which ones exactly. What are the mistakes made? Find the correct figure.

Note. Take advantage of the fact that $v(t) = x'(t) = 2pt + q$, so the speed graph is a straight line, the tangent of the angle of inclination of which to the axis $t$ is equal to $2p$. The point $t = -\frac{q}{2p}$, at which this line intersects the axis $t$, is the extremum point of the function $x(t)$.
