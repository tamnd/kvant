Laboratory «Kvant»

2. Jet wrapping around a spoke
Place the knitting needle into the stream as shown in figure 2. By wetting the knitting needle, the stream will flow down it. Now carefully turn the knitting needle around its axis - the stream, as if following the knitting needle, will wrap around it (see Fig. 2,a). Continuing to turn the knitting needle, we managed to get two or three revolutions of the jet (see Fig. 2.6).

Unusual properties of an ordinary jet

It turns out that a lot of interesting and unexpected things can be seen by watching the most ordinary stream of water from a tap. Here are some examples of our experiments and observations. We hope they will interest you too.

1. Ripples on the surface of the jet
Open the water tap so that it flows. A thin, calm stream of water. Take a knitting needle (or needle) with a diameter of 1-2 mm and insert it into the stream at a distance of 5-7 cm from the tap (Fig. 1). You will see that in a small area above the spoke the surface of the jet becomes wavy and ripples form *). Pay attention to the distances between the humps of the wave - the further from the spoke, the larger they are.

What happens if you move the knitting needle down or up the stream? As the needle lowers, the pockmarks will become smaller and smaller, and finally the eye will stop spotting them altogether. And if you bring the knitting needle to the very base of the stream, there will be no ripples, but the stream will bend a little.

*) You can find an explanation of this phenomenon, for example, in the article by E. Kuznetsov and A. Rubenchik “On waves on the sea and fish in puddles” («Kvant», 1980, No. 9) or in the article by S. Soskin “Capillary waves in a jet” («Kvant», 1976, No. 10).
