This figure shows the principle design of the relief ceiling found in some modern buildings. In the upper left corner there is a slightly open rod frame on which the shoulder covering the ceiling is mounted.

The design is based on the amazing correspondence between the divisions of the plane into squares and into regular triangles. You can see the division into squares (with black sides) in the upper left corner; four rods are attached to the tops of the squares (blue dots); they are fastened (at the red dots) so that a semi-ktazdron is formed under each square. A geometric film closing with a rod system is formed as follows: first, a flat sheet of film is divided into equilateral triangles, they are combined in pairs into parallelograms (these parallelograms form the ceiling faces visible on figure) and then the sheet is folded in a “double accordion”.

What’s surprising here is this: it turns out that if you take the side of the triangle equal to the side of the square, the folded film will just fit to the rod system without gaps - all sides of the parallelograms will be pressed against the edges of the semi-ctazors.

It is not difficult to make such a double accordion from a thick sheet of paper. You can read more about such ceilings on cl8.
