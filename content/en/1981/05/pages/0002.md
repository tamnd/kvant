The “encompassing” curve drawn on this cover page has long been known in mathematics - this is the so-called Pascal’s snail. It is obtained as follows: for each right angle based on the horizontal diameter of the “generaison of a circle” (at figure all these angles are blue), we plot on the straight line containing its bisector, from the vertex of the angle in both directions, segments of a given length (the same for all angles!) (the shape of the curve depends on this length); the set of ends of these segments is the required curve.
(By the way, all the described straight lines intersect at one point on the circle; this point is called the pole of Pascal’s cochlea.)

And the “asterisk”, symmetrically composed of two Pascal snails, has the following remarkable property: it can rotate inside any of the described right angles so that its border will always touch the side of the angle and passes through the point of intersection of the bisector of this angle with Pascal’s snail.

You will find out why this is so by reading the article by A. Babichev “On one of Kolmogorov’s problems.”
