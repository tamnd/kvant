It is not difficult to tell what will happen when the following atoms are added: for three atoms each, the original level will be distributed into three (see Fig. 3). Nine electrons will be located like this: six on the lower triad of levels arising from the level closest to the nucleus of the inner filled shell of the atom; two more electrons are at the lower level of the next triad; the remaining electron is at the middle level of the same triad. One more space on this level remains vacant, and the upper level is completely empty. If we take $n$ atoms ($n \gg 1$), then each level will be distributed into $n$ closely spaced levels, forming, as they say, a band or zone of allowed energy values. In the lower band, the entire state will be occupied, and in the second - only half, and precisely those whose energy lies lower. The next strip is completely empty.
