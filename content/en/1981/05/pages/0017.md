It was already noted that when rotated by values ​​from 0 to 2π/5, our angle will rest on the diagonal BE; therefore, its vertex will slide along the circular arc shown in figure 3.

Point O is located on the bisector of the angle and is removed from its vertex at a given distance. The bisector of an angle when the vertex of the angle moves along the larger arc BE of the circle being considered will pass through the midpoint S less than the other BEs of this circle. Thus, the method for constructing the desired curve is clear: it is necessary to draw a straight line through each point of the greater length BE of our circle and the middle of S less than a length, and from the point of the greater length lay off a segment equal to the distance from point O to the vertex of the angle; then the ends of these segments will describe the desired curve.

Then the same procedure must be done with the diagonals AD, EC, DB and CA of the pentagon, rotating our angle respectively by values ​​from 2π/5 to 4π/5, from 4π/5 to 6π/5 and from 6π/5 to 8π/5 and from 8π/5 to 2π.

The resulting curve (see Fig. 3) has long been known in mathematics, «Kvant» has already written about it on his pages. It is called Pascal's snail (see «Kvant», 1977, No. 5).
