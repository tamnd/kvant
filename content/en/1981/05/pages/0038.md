Thus, the impossibility of constructing regular heptagons, 9agons, decagons, and thirteenagons using a compass and ruler was proven.

The theory of construction using compasses and rulers was widely developed at the end of the 19th century. For example, it was shown that any construction performed using a compass and a ruler can be performed using only one ruler, if a certain circle is specified in the construction plane and its center is indicated.

All these studies made a huge contribution to the development of mathematics. However, in practical constructions nothing limits us in the set of tools. For large constructions, a ruler with divisions and a protractor are sufficient.

Some approximate construction methods are quite interesting. For example, an approximate square circle is obtained if the side of the square is taken to be a chord passing through the end of one of the diameters of the circle and the middle of the radius perpendicular to it (Fig. 4). This construction corresponds to the value π=3.2.

A method for approximate trisection of an angle using a compass and ruler

suggested by our reader A. Padalko. Here is his method: “To trisection an angle with vertex O₁ (see Fig. 5), we describe a friend AmB with a center O₁ of arbitrary radius and draw a chord AB; let O be its midpoint. With a radius equal to |AO|, we describe the resulting AnB with center O. From point B we make on this the resulting notch C with radius |AO|; it is easy to see that BC = \frac{AmB}{3}. Then we find a point D such that |BD| = \frac{|AB|}{3}. By connecting points C and D and from the middle E of the segment CD we restore perpendicular to [CD]. Let Y be the point of intersection of this perpendicular with the continuation [AB]. Radius |DY| draw from Y friend; let X be the intersection of this arc with another AmB. Then BX = \frac{AmB}{3}, that is, BO₁X ≈ \frac{AO₁B}{3}.

We obtained an estimate of the accuracy of this construction from a computer. The machine produced the following result: the construction accuracy is at least 0.1°, the worst result is at an angle of about 78°, the relative error, that is, the ratio of the deviation to the angle itself, does not exceed 0.01 and the maximum is at an angle of about 158°.
