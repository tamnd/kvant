⟦folio 8⟧

The resistance begins to increase, while for glass, for example, it decreases. This means we need to monitor what happens during cooling. And here we will finally see qualitative differences. Look at figure 2: at the temperature of the liquid gel, below absolute zero, the substances separated into two groups. For some, the resistance remains small; for alloys or not very pure metals, Q almost does not change when cooled; for pure metals, the resistance decreases greatly. The number and perfectness of the crystal, the more significant this change. Norda Q at a temperature close to absolute zero is hundreds of thousands of times less than at room temperature. For other substances, for example semiconductors, as the temperature drops, the resistance begins to increase rapidly, and the lower the temperature, the greater it is. If it were possible to get to absolute zero, then Q would become infinitely large. Moreover, it is enough that the resistance actually becomes so large that no modern device can change it.

So, we get to the answer: metals are substances that conduct electricity at any temperature.

In contrast, dielectrics conduct current when cooled to absolute zero. If we use this definition, then both graphite and molybdenum dioxide turn out to be metallic. Where do semiconductors belong? If we are talking about pure, perfect crystals, then, strictly speaking, they are dielectrics. But if they contain many impurities, then they can become metallic, that is, they remain conductive at the lowest temperatures.

What do we have left in the end? We managed to identify the only essential feature, guided by which we can, if not in advanced practice, then at least in principle, always distinguish a metal from a non-metal. And since this sign is the only one, then both conditions are automatically satisfied, the fulfillment of which we demanded at the beginning of the article.
