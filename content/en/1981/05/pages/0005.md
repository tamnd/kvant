I think a person who wants to become a scientist should develop the ability to work hard as soon as possible. We must learn to work even during rest.

So, cultivating in yourself a great capacity for work. To this I would add one more quality, especially important for a scientist, the absolute part. A person who is inclined to distort facts, to attribute to himself an idea that does not belong to him, will never be able to become a real scientist...

So, hard work and purity go beyond competition. This is clear without words. It’s more difficult with abilities. When a boy or girl is selected for ballet, a singing school, or a conservatory, this is done relatively simply. People show their abilities in these areas openly. Everyone sees them, everyone places them, and our fairly developed system of amateur performances also helps to show them. But even here, of course, a great sign of art is needed to determine whether a young man will become a great musician or artist. In science, the situation is many times more complicated.

In the United States, they proposed different ways of selecting people for creative scientific work. There, in general, for each profession they try to find its own “test”, a characteristic task-exercise, by performing which a person tests himself, which things he is better at. Unfortunately, such “tests” do little for selection in science. They touch only superficially on that part of the abilities that is needed for creativity. How is life? What methods to use, what specific principles to draw from? We cannot wait for pedagogy to give us these methods and “signs” in decades. We need special ones today. And therefore, Siberian scientists generalize their personal experience and act collectively when it comes to selecting young people who show an aptitude for, for example, mathematics and physics.

I All-Siberian Physics-Mathematics Olympid for high school students was not fun and not just a “social burden” for scientists. Everyone who held this Olympiad thought about the future of Soviet science, about the replenishment that should come to replace us. It is gratifying that most of the Olympiad winners were from villages, small towns, and distant regions. About 120 children who showed an aptitude for mathematics and physics, participating in a special, accelerated program in a specially created boarding school. It is open here, in the Academic Town of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

We need theorists who specialize in different areas of science. And they are not born ready. They need to be identified and raised from a young age. We are not looking for “prodigies”, but people with certain inclinations. And we proceed from the fact that most people are gifted with abilities. We just have to come to the youth and determine what a person’s soul is about. And then everything depends on the school, on the scientists who undertake to help such a school, and, of course, on the young person himself.

I didn’t mention one sign that our scientists use when looking for talented guys. So far this sign has not let us down. It is not just sitting down, not memorizing (even brilliant) ready-made standard solutions, but independent thinking that is interesting to a scientist in a young person. A boy or girl is assigned to do an experiment, solve a problem, and is asked to explain a natural phenomenon. There is nothing unlearning about the task itself or the experience. The experience can be extended, the problem can be solved, and the “unlearning” may not appear. But the young man offers his own solution. Another teacher who is so uneducated can let him go. And it makes scientists happy. As long as the conclusion is correct, the path to the conclusion may be different. And the more the path a young man takes, the freer he finds the shortest, the most economical, the most rational, I would add, the most beautiful, the more confidence we have in what will come of it.
