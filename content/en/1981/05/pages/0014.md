The atoms themselves in the crystal are not only identical, but also located in exactly the same way relative to their neighbors (with the exception, of course, of those that fall on the surface of the crystal). And all electrons now have different energies. This can only happen if the electrons no longer belong to individual atoms, but each electron is “shared” among all the atoms. In other words, electrons move freely inside an ideal crystal, forming a kind of liquid that fills the entire volume of the sample. And the electronic current is a directed flow of this liquid, similar to water flowing through pipes.

To make water flow through a pipe, it is necessary to create a pressure difference at the ends of the pipe. Then, under the influence of external forces, the molecules will gain directional speed - the water will flow. The appearance of precisely directed speed is very important here, because the molecules themselves move at enormous speeds - at room temperature, the average speed of thermal movement of a molecule is of the order of $10^3$ m/s. So the additional energy acquired by the molecule in the flow is small compared to the energy of thermal motion.

The additional energy that must be imparted to the electron in order for it to participate in the general directional movement of electrons in the crystal (and this is the current) is also small compared to the “own” energy of the electron. This is not difficult to verify. We have already said that the energy of an electron is equal in order of magnitude to $1\exists B=1,6 \cdot 10^{-19}$ J. If we remember that for a free electron $E=mv^2/2$ and $m=9,1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg, then it is easy to find the speed: $v \sim 10^6$ m/s. Let us assume that all electrons participate in the current, and there are $1\ m^3$ conductors $n \sim 10^{28}$ Z (Z is the nuclear charge). Then in a wire with a cross section $S=10^{-6}$ m$^2$ at a current $I \approx 10$ A (at a higher current the wire will pay off) the directional speed of electrons is $v_H=1/neS \approx 10^{-2} \div 10^{-3}$ m/s. This means that the energy of the electron participating in the current is greater than the energy of the $E$ free electron by only $10^{-8}$ E, that is, by $1,6 \cdot 10^{-27}$ J.

And here we are faced with an amazing fact: it turns out that electrons, which are located in the lower band, usually called the valence band, cannot change their energy by a small amount. After all, if an electron increases its energy, this means that it must move to another level, and all neighboring levels in the valence band are already occupied. There are free places only in the next zone. But in order to get there, the electron must change its energy by several electron volts at once. This is how the electrons in the valence band are divided and the pie in the sky is created - an energetic quantum. And quanta of the required energy come from electromagnetic waves of high frequency - visible light or ultraviolet light.

But solid helium is truly an insulator, so we can already congratulate ourselves on some success: we have not yet explained why current can flow in metals, but we understand why dielectrics, where there are a lot of electrons and they are all “smeared” throughout the crystal, do not conduct current.

What about lithium? Why, he has a second zone, which is only half filled. The energy separating the occupied and free levels of the inner zone is called the Fermi energy $E_\Phi$. As we have already said, the energy difference between the levels in the zone is very large. An electron that is located in a zone near the Fermi level only needs to slightly increase its energy - and it is free, where the state is not occupied. Nothing prevents electrons from the prepared strip from increasing their energy under the influence of an electronic field and acquiring a directed speed. But this is current!
