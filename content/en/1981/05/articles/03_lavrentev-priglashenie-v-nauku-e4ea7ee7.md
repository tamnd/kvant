⟦folio 2⟧

M. Lavrentiev

Invitation
into science

What qualities distinguish a scientist, what character traits should a young man who decides to devote himself to science cultivate in himself?
It is not easy to answer this question briefly.

Let's look at scientists who have done a lot in science. It is characteristic that, regardless of the specialty and talent, each of them invested enormous personal work in science. In certain periods of life (and these periods lasted for years), directed labor reaches the top. Research, forgetting about finishing, works 14-16 hours every day. So words about work in science are not a phrase. This is the law. It remains valid for our time.

What else is typical for research? Complete disconnection from “extraneous” matters that would prevent him from realizing the completed idea. Such “disconnection periods” last, as life shows, for several minutes. From the siege of the fortress, the scientist moves on to its shtum. This is a very important stage, and here a person’s ability to complete the task is tested. During such periods, the researcher trains himself again and again, learns to overcome emerging difficulties, quickly solve puzzling problems that always surround the problem with an invisible at first glance, but very stable fortress wall. Every stage that the wall has fallen, you are already in the fortress, but in front of you there is only a new one with a fortified one, which was not visible before. Like in war: fish, wire, off-road... This happens in mathematics, in engineering - in any field. Therefore, a young scientist needs to prepare himself for Great Patience, to quickly navigate and change tactics in time, call for help or build new “siege weapons” himself.

Academician Mikhail Alekseevich Lavrentiev (1900-1980) - State Prize laureate, honorary chairman of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

⟦folio 4⟧

Once, when I was about ten years old, a friend of our family, academician Nikolai Nikolaevich Luzin, told me a funny story about how Socrates discovered Plato, his best student. Luzin was such a good storyteller that it was impossible to distinguish where his truth was and where his fiction was. Listening to him, I imagined how Socrates, taking a walk, clicked around his hometown. So he approached the construction site and suddenly stopped. Something unusual caught his eye. All the stones built walls using the same movements. And only one of them caml stones in a special way. Socrates took a closer look. Young worker shore of strength. He had everything figured out. Where others made two movements, he made do with one. And his wall grew faster, and he worked with less stress. Socrates called the worker and took him to live with him. And a few years later, the mason worker turned into a scientist, philosopher, whose name and identity are still alive today.

Nikolai Nikolaevich died long ago. But I often remember this story, which I first heard from him.

Legends are legends, but business is business. Now scientists and educators have had to seriously face the problem: how to determine whether a person has an internal desire to unravel phenomena that are new to him, to reveal the large and small secrets of nature? When teachers begin to do this everywhere, it will be much easier to update schools, and the quality of education will improve.

I foresee the question: aren’t we asking too much from the guys? Will they be overloaded?

No, if two conditions are met.

First. Children should go to one or another specialized school according to their inclination, and not questions about it. And then they absorb the material twice as quickly and easily. A favorite activity is satisfaction, not tedious, obligatory work.

Second. An experienced leader, the environment itself, the environment in which the children were raised should help them quickly become scientists. The full development of a sense of purpose and the acquisition of sufficiently broad knowledge should not come at the expense of overwork. Among mathematicians, there are especially many cases when a young man overexerts himself so much that he is incapacitated for a long time. Continuity of work must be combined with reasonable rest and “switching” of consciousness to other areas.

You have to work hard, but do it wisely. It is necessary to create a new system for educating young scientists, but within this system we must abandon standards and templates.

The senior comrade worker, the leader, is obliged from the very beginning to cultivate in his young wards the qualities characteristic of the majority of our leading Soviet scientists. Do we need to talk about them again here? They are known all over the world. We want the most stimulating creativity for future, growing scientists to be the desire not only to make a discovery, but also to quickly put this discovery in the service of the Fatherland and the people. Inextricably linked with this quality is a feeling of camaraderie and satisfaction not only with personal success, joy not only for oneself, but also for the success of a neighbor, for the success of one’s institute.
