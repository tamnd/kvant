The International Mathematical Olympiad is not only about problems and medals. The organizing committee of the Olympics, which did a lot of work and rightly earned the gratitude of the participants of the Olympics, provided a cultural program for the community. The guys went on a number of excursions (around New York and Washington), visited the National Aerospace and Space Museum, the Washington National Gallery, visited the Capitol, and attended theatrical performances.

The Olympiad means a lot of interesting meetings and conversations. True, the participant's vocabulary to speak other languages ​​was, to put it mildly, limited in many cases; however, the desire to understand each other was so great that language problems during communication receded into the background. Each participant received a lot of interesting impressions during these two weeks, and made many new friends in different parts of the world.

Cube development

We all probably encountered cube developments, that is, flat figures (shaped whole faces of a cube) from which the surface of the cube can be composed. However, few people know that the number of different such scans is equal. figure 1 shows the cube itself, and Figures 2-4 show all its developments. The numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are written on the faces of the cube.

On figure 1 we see only the first three numbers, and how the remaining numbers are located on the cube can be understood from figure 2, where these numbers are depicted on one of the scans of the cube. If we take another scan (Fig. 3), then the numbers there are arranged in a completely different order; in addition, they turn out to be superficial.
