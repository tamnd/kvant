F689. On the main horizontal table there is a stack of paper containing 500 sheets. The hundredth sheet (counting spiza) is larger than the others. This sheet is carefully pulled in a horizontal direction. What maximum acceleration can be imparted to the pack so that it moves without breaking up? What is the maximum acceleration with which the center of mass of the pack can move? With what acceleration should the column move in this case? Friction coefficient on paper μ=0.2.

Let us find the kinetic energy of a hoop rotating with angular velocity ω around the $O'O''$ axis in the position as indicated in the conditions.

The linear velocities of different points on the hoop are different; Accordingly, the contributions made by individual sections of the hoop to the total kinetic energy are different. Let us find the kinetic energy of a pair of small sections $A_1$ and $A_2$ of the hoop, located so that $A_1OA_2 = \pi/2$ (Fig. 2). The size of each section is $R \cdot \Delta\varphi$, the mass is $m = \frac{M}{2\pi R} R \cdot \Delta\varphi = \frac{M}{2\pi} \cdot \Delta\varphi$ (the size of $R \cdot \Delta\varphi$ of the present is small, which varies in the radii of the circles along which the points of the sections rotate, you can run). The linear speeds of sections $A_1$ and $A_2$ are equal, respectively,

$$v_1 = \omega |O_1A_1|, \quad v_2 = \omega |O_2A_2|,$$

where $|O_1A_1|, |O_2A_2|$ are the radii of the circles along which the points of sections $A_1$ and $A_2$ rotate around the axis $O'O''$. As can be seen from figure 2,

$$|O_1A_1| = \sqrt{|OA_1|^2 - |OO_1|^2} = R \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \alpha \cdot \cos^2 \varphi},$$

$$|O_2A_2| = \sqrt{|OA_2|^2 - |OO_2|^2} = R \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \alpha \cdot \sin^2 \varphi}.$$

Thus, the kinetic energy of the pair of sections $A_1$ and $A_2$ is equal to

$$E_n = \frac{1}{2} m (v_1^2 + v_2^2) = \frac{M}{4\pi} \cdot \Delta\varphi \cdot \omega^2 R^2 (1 + \sin^2 \alpha).$$

Considering any pair of sections whose radius vectors form an angle π/2, we obtain the same expression for their kinetic energy. The number of such pairs is obviously equal to π/Δφ. Therefore, the total energy of the spinning hoop is

$$E = \frac{\pi}{\Delta\varphi} E_n = \frac{M}{4} \omega^2 R^2 (1 + \sin^2 \alpha).$$

According to the law of conservation of energy, the work that must be done to spin the hoop to angular velocity ω is equal to

$$A = \frac{M}{4} \omega^2 R^2 (1 + \sin^2 \alpha).$$

For the special case α = π/2 (the hoop rotates in a horizontal plane around a vertical axis)

$$E_r = \frac{M}{2} \omega^2 R^2.$$

For α = 0 (the hoop rotates around the vertical diameter)

$$E_b = \frac{M}{4} \omega^2 R^2 = E_r/2.$$
