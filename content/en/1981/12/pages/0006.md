MILKY WAY

Democritus (5th - 4th centuries BC) said that the Milky Way, a whitish stripe stretching across the sky, is “a combination of the light of many stars.” The history of astronomy knows many other assumptions about the nature of the Milky Way, which were discussed long before it became possible to verify them with astronomical observations; The debate about the Milky Way has continued for centuries. The solution - through the Treaty of Democritus - came in 1610, when Galileo pointed the telescope he had just created to the sky.

In just a few hours, many remarkable astronomical discoveries and intermediate ones were made - the discovery of the stars of the Milky Way. As Galileo wrote, stars became “visible to the eye that had never been visible before, and the number of which is at least ten times greater than the number of stars known from ancient times. This is a fact of great importance, which put an end to the controversy about the Milky Way and revealed its nature to sense and reason."
