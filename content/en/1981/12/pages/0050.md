⟦folio 48⟧

The leader of each team received official solutions to the problems proposed by the jury, and ORMATIVE ratings of the solutions in points. Therefore, the leaders, together with the children, could check all the work themselves and make sure that the jury’s assessments were correct.

The selection of winners was carried out according to the following principle. First, the winner was identified - the participant who received the maximum number of points. It turned out to be the Soviet schoolboy A. Gutin, who scored 47 points out of 50 possible. The first prize, in addition to the overall winner, was also awarded to the participant who scored at least 90% of the points from the best result. There were six of them: A. Wirenbach (Germany), V. Tudose (Romania), I. Solodovnikov (USSR), M. Lehn (Germany), V. Derevianko (USSR) and V. Lerz (Poland). The second prize was won by participants who scored at least 80% of the points from the best result; among them is the Soviet schoolboy A. Mushinsky. The third prize was given to schoolchildren who scored at least 65% of the best score; among them is a member of our team I. Shubenin. And finally, all those participants who scored at least 50% of the best result were awarded certificates of appreciation. In addition, special awards were awarded for the best solutions to theoretical problems: from the Academy of Sciences of the NRB - to S. Muller (Germany), from the journal "Physics" - to A. Gutin (USSR), from the Society of Physicists - to A. Klumper (Germany). A special prize for the best solution to the experimental problem was given to the Soviet schoolchild A. Mushinsky.

During the Olympiad, in one of the halls of the International House of Scientists named after Frederic Jollu-Curie, a round table discussion was held on the topic “The role of physical optimization in the development of interservations and creative abilities of youth.” Representatives of all countries present at the Olympics, as well as UNESCO representative Dr. Samadi, took part in the conversation.

Dr. Samadi spoke about the work carried out by UNESCO in connection with the problems of teaching science, in particular, participation with the children themselves as participants in teaching science.

The reports of Soviet scientists were listened to with great interest.
