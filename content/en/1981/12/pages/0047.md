At first glance, there is nothing terrible about this result. But remember that the average depth of the oceans is about 4 km; This means that the time for their complete evaporation is about $10^6$ s, which is a little more than 10 days!

What's the matter? Why do oceans exist?

There are several circumstances that prevent their disappearance.

Let's start with the less important ones. First, relative humidity is not zero. But even if we take it to be 90%, this will only lower the evaporation rate by an order of magnitude. Secondly, not all vapor molecules that strike the surface of water remain in the liquid—those that do not bounce off and are reflected. Consequently, we overestimated the rate of condensation, and therefore the equal rate of evaporation. Let a tenth of the molecules “float” (a number close to the real one, but already underestimated). Let's add some more order. We have extended the life of the oceans to 100 days, to 1001 days. For Scheherezda (and world literature) this is an era, but for the ocean with its billion-year history, three years is a moment.

But let's return to our glass. Further, with the correction we introduced by 100 times, water from a full glass should evaporate completely in less than half an hour. The figure is clearly unrealistic. In reality, a layer of saturated vapor quickly forms above the surface of the water, or more precisely, a layer of air with a relative humidity of 100%. And only before the formation of this layer does the water level drop at a rate of 4.7 mm/s. Once the layer has formed, the rate of condensation becomes almost equal to the rate of evaporation, and a further decrease in the water level is determined by the slow process of diffusion of vapor from the surface of the liquid into the surrounding space.

However, it is not these factors that replace evaporation that play the main role in the fate of the oceans; the oceans would have enough time to completely evaporate. The fact is that we considered equilibrium or almost equilibrium (quasi-equilibrium) systems. And the earth’s atmosphere is an essentially nonequilibrium system. Warm, moist air rises into the cold layers of the atmosphere, where water condenses, and on the way to the Earth's surface the droplets do not have time to evaporate. Ocean waters are replenished, the water cycle in nature continues.
