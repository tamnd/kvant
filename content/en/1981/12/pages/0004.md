The task is still the same - to study!

At one of the most critical moments for the fate of the Soviet state, 60 years ago, V.I. Lenin, speaking at the III All-Russian Congress of the RKSM, said: “... the tasks of youth in general and communist youth unions and all other organizations in particular could be expressed in one word: the task is to learn.” 60 years have passed. The first social state emerged and strengthened, the educational development of socialism in the USSR, and socialism won in many countries. From the seeds sown in the first years of Soviet power, Soviet science grew. It solved a number of historical problems - from the electronicization of the country to the creation of atomic science and technology, space exploration, etc. Today, a third of the world's scientists work in the USSR.

The development of the scientific and technological revolution has led to the fact that the product of science - knowledge - has become one of the main national wealth.

The USSR has created not only powerful advanced science, scientific schools, experimental and/material bases of science, but also one of the most powerful and effective education systems. And at the same time, the main task of young people - to study - remains as relevant as it was 60 years ago.

Each new generation of Soviet youth encounters new tasks set by various societies - from the restoration of the national economy after the civil war, the construction of the first five-year plans to the development of virgin lands, the construction of the Baikal-Amur Mainline, patronage of the nuclear energy industry, and the oil industries of Tyumen. An equally titanic task is faced in education: each new generation must accept and digest everything that was created by the previous ones. In the 20th century, the growth of scientific and technical information will occur like an avalanche. Further, teachers sometimes do not have time to keep up with modern science. However, the progress of our society depends on the success of this process - learning.

Academician Evgeny Pavlovich Velikov is a State Prize laureate, Vice-President of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Chairman of the Section of Physical, Technical and Mathematical Sciences of the Predium of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Deputy Director of the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I.V. Kurchatov.
