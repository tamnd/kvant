This year marks the ninetieth anniversary of the birth of Academician Sergei Ivanovich Vavilov (March 12, 1891—January 25, 1951), an outstanding Soviet physicist and organizer of science, Chairman of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Director of the P. N. Lebedev Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences. S. I. Vavilov made a very important contribution to the development of physical optics, primarily to the study of the luminescence period. Together with his student Pavel Alekseevich Cherenkov (sung by the academician) S.I. Vavilov discovered the famous “superluminal electron” effect, which later received the name of the authors of the discovery.
S.I. Vavilov was an active promoter of natural scientific knowledge, the author of several excellent popular science books. We place here a chapter from his book “On “tell” and “cold” light,” published in 1949. The text is given with minor editorial changes.

It is well known that the light source that remained dominant until his time - the electronic incandescent lamp - was preceded by many thousands of years of development and improvement. And yet, from a fundamental point of view, an incandescent lamp differs little from a primitive fire. Let us first examine the usual sources of “solid light”. We flash the match, giving a yellowish flame and light for a short time. The flame temperature is very high, above 1500°C. At the same time, if you monitor the burning of a match more carefully, at first (at least in some types of matches) a peculiar sharp yellow glow is clearly noticeable, the same as when introducing table salt into a seedless gas flame. When the flame is established, part of it, near the barrel of the match, has a faint blue color. However, the main part of the flame glows with the familiar yellow-orange, “body” light, depending on the incandescence of the sahka dust particles in the flame.
