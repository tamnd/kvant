A pentomino is a figure that is a polygon made up of five identical squares *); the complete pentomino collection is depicted at figure before the title. Of course, in all the problems discussed below, pentominoes can be inverted.

Pentominoes make wonderful puzzles. So, from twelve pentominoes you can form a rectangle $4 \times 15$, $5 \times 12$, $6 \times 10$,

A regular chessboard with four open squares can also be made from pentominoes in many ways. This “incomplete” board looks quite elegant (three assembly options are shown at figure 1).

But the figure depicted on figure 2 cannot be folded from pentominoes: at the edges it is limited by 22 squares, and if you count how many squares of each pentomino can be on the edge of this figure, then the total for all pentominoes will be 21. The figures depicted on figure 3 cannot be folded from pentominoes, but a short and elegant proof of this fact has not yet been found.
