Appendix I
Let us prove that the attractive forces acting on the star from the side of “external” stars balance each other if the external distribution of the spherical sphere is symmetrical.

Let us first consider the gravitational effect of a thin spherical layer of radius $r$ and thickness $l \ll r$ on the region inside this layer (Fig. 4). To do this, let us calculate the sum of forces acting from the individual elements of the layer on the mass $m$ placed at an arbitrary point $A$. Let us select a pair of elements “cut out” in the layer by a two-sheet cone with its apex at point $A$ (see Fig. 4). We consider the cone to be very narrow, so $a \ll \pi$. The layer elements formed by such a section can be approximately considered flat disks, neglecting the curvature of their surface. In this approximation, the diameters of the disks are equal
$$d_1 = r_1 a, \quad d_2 = r_2 a,$$
where $r_1, r_2$ are the distances of the disks from the point $A$.

From the side of disk 1, the force $\vec{F}_1$ acts on the mass $m$ at point $A$, and from the side of disk 2 - the force $\vec{F}_2$.

Due to the small size of the disk ($d_1 \ll r_1, d_2 \ll r_2$), the forces $\vec{F}_1$ and $\vec{F}_2$ can be determined by considering that the entire mass of each of the disks is concentrated at a point located, respectively, at distances $r_1$ and $r_2$ from the point $A$. Then
$$|\vec{F}_1| = \frac{G m_1 m}{r_1^2}, \quad |\vec{F}_2| = \frac{G m_2 m}{r_2^2},$$
where $m_1, m_2$ are the masses of the disks. To find $m_1$ and $m_2$, we take into account that the disks have columnar thickness, that the change in density $\rho$ inside them can be neglected, so these masses are equal

Appendix II
Let us prove that if the total mass $M$, contained inside a sphere of radius $r$, returns proportionally to $r$, then the density $\rho(r)$ (spherical symmetric distribution) according to the law $\rho \sim r^{-2}$.

Let us select a thin spherical layer of thickness $l$ with an outer radius $r$. The mass of such a layer is equal to $\Delta M = M(r) - M(r-l) = Kr - K(r-l) = Kl$, where $K$ is the proportionality coefficient. The volume of such a layer is equal to $\Delta V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 - \frac{4}{3} \pi \cdot (r-l)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (3r^2 l - 3rl^2 + l^3)$ (the volume of a sphere of radius $r$ is equal to $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$). The average density in the layer is
$$\rho_{cp} = \frac{\Delta M}{\Delta V} = \frac{3Kl}{4\pi(3r^2 l - 3rl^2 + l^3)} = \frac{K}{4\pi r^2} \sim r^{-2},$$
Q.E.D.
