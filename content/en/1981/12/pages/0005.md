⟦folio 3⟧

The tasks here are daunting, but the reserves are not used. Oddly enough, we still know the process of thinking and learning very poorly, and therefore it is ineffective to use the abilities of the human mind. Teaching is still an art that can sometimes work wonders. From our own school experience, we know that most of us do not master a foreign language in 10 years, which, if necessary and using the appropriate methodology, can be effectively mastered in six months. The same applies to all other disciplines. A schoolchild who has studied the principles of higher mathematics in grades 6-7 looks like a child prodigy, although this is no less natural than learning to swim at the age of one.

Modern debate forces us to pay much attention to the development of man to the limits of his physical capabilities. In sports, it is considered normal for schoolchildren to achieve world championships and for ice skating to reach the North Pole. In the area of ​​learning we are much more timid. And do we attach comparable social significance to the corresponding human efforts in the intellectual sphere? Only those who have ever had an argument with a real teacher can imagine to what extent the routine process of transferring knowledge from generation to generation, developed by man, is ineffective. Given society's modern need for human intelligence, how many Einsteins, Mendleyevs, Tolstoys are we losing! Important factors for success in education are the desire, internal need and self-augmentation of every young person. And, of course, these properties should be stimulated primarily by society, including Komsomol and other public organizations.

No less strange is the fact that we artificially separate two processes - study and creativity. In fact, it is one process. If learning is not a creative process for a person, then it is almost useless. And vice versa, only by maintaining the ability to learn and be surprised by new things, a person retains the ability to create, regardless of age. This process is determined by individual efforts and needs and the social incentives that shape them.

The modern world, with its “well-trodden roads”, small daily problems and observances, contributes to the AMERization of the individual. The very process of education, and then the beginning of work, sometimes also suppresses the individual desire for every young person to be a bright individual with a strong, independent, creative character, regardless of what profession he chooses and where he works. In industry, in agriculture, in the service sector, especially in science and technology in a socialist society, intelligence is required more and more - this is the general line of our development. The formation of such a personality requires early hardening and training - mental and physical.

The most important task of the entire Soviet society, including young people, is to ensure the proper scientific and technical level and progress in continuous progress with capitalism. We have reason to be confident that this task will be successfully solved.
