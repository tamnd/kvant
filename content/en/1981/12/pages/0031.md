M680. Two svyazsta tseraot in such a tserra. There are n telephone nodes, and the communication nodes take turns connecting cables to two of them of their choice.

The call is made, after which it will be possible to call any other node from any node (maybe through several intermediate ones).

a) Find out who wins when n=4, 5, 6, 7,8 - the beginner or his partner?
b) What is the answer for arbitrary n?

Let's also consider two modified versions of the cerra:

c) Let the cerrok, tied all the knots, break. Answer questions a) and b) for this new cerra.

d) Let all the nodes be first connected in pairs by cable, and the connections are removed in turn, one connection at a time. The hierok, who has broken the connection in the scheme, breaks down. The question is the same as in points a) and b).

a) When n=4, 7, 8, the first player wins, when n=5, 6 - the second; this will follow from the solution of general problem 6).

b) We will call a group of nodes connected if any two nodes included in it are connected (maybe through several other nodes). We will call a connected group of nodes a component if none of the nodes included in it are connected to any of the nodes not contained in it. It is clear that any eraf (a system of somehow connected nodes) consists of one or more components.

In any game of hastanet opponents, the moment when the set of all nodes turns out to be divided into three components A1, A2, A3. Let a1, a2, a3 be the number of nodes in these components. It is clear that the player who is the first to connect any two of these components loses. A critical situation will arise when all possible connections within components A1, A2, A3 have been made.

It is clear that from the beginning of the party until such a moment comes, it will be done

$$N = \frac{a_1(a_1-1)}{2} + \frac{a_2(a_2-1)}{2} + \frac{a_3(a_3-1)}{2} = \frac{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2 - n}{2}$$

moves. If the number N is even, then the first player loses, but if it is odd, then the first player wins. The parity of the number N, very/no, is determined by the remainder, which gives the number $a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2 - n$ when divided by 4. Since the remainder of the number $a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2$ when divided by 4 is equal to the number of odd numbers among the numbers a1, a2, a3 (make sure of this), the first player must be added so that the number of odd numbers among a1, a2, a3 does not coincide with the remainder from the division of n by 4. The second, on the contrary, it must strive for their coincidence.
