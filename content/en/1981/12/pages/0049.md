**Theoretical problem**

1. A test tube of mass $M$ is in a vacuum. A partition of mass $m$ with a relatively small thickness divides the volume of the test tube into two equal parts. The closed part of the test tube contains $n$ moles of an ideal monatomic gas with a molar mass of $μ$ at a temperature of $T$. The septum releases and, moving without friction, flies out of the test tube. Then the gas flows out. What will be the final speed of the test tube if it was stationary at the moment the partition began to move? The universal gas constant $R$ is known.

The gas pulse before the partition leaves, as well as the heat exchange between the gas, on the one hand, and the test tube and the partition, on the other, can be avoided. Earthly acceptance does not take into account.

2. An electric lamp with a resistance of $R_0=2$ Ohm at a rated voltage of $U_0=4,5$ V is supplied with current from a battery with an emf of $\mathcal{E}=6$ V, the internal resistance of which can be overcome.

a) Let the rated voltage be supplied to the lamp through a rheostat with the motor turned on as a potentiometer. What should be the resistance of the rheostat and what maximum current should it be designed to achieve maximum efficiency?

b) What is the maximum possible efficiency of the lamp-battery circuit at the rated voltage across the lamp and how should they be connected through a suitable rheostat to achieve maximum efficiency?

3. The receiver of radio waves for radioastronomical support is located on the seashore at an altitude of $h=2$ m above sea level. When a radio star rises above the horizon, emitting electromagnetic waves with a length of $\lambda=21$ cm, the receiver registers maxima and minima through the ears. It is known that the receiver registers only those electromagnetic waves in which the electric vector $\vec{E}$ oscillates parallel to the water surface. The intensity of the recorded signal is proportional to $E^2$.

a) Determine the heights of the star above the horizon, changed in angles along the celestial sphere, at which registration maxima and minima (in general terms).

b) Will the signal intensity at the receiver increase or decrease spontaneously after the radio star rises? Explain why.

b) Find the ratio of the intensity of the signals at the first maximum and the following minimum.

When an electromagnetic wave is reflected from water, the ratio of the amplitudes of the electric field of the reflected ($E_{\text{от}}$) and incident ($E_{\text{пад}}$) waves is described by the law

$$\frac{E_{\text{от}}}{E_{\text{пад}}} = \frac{n - \cos \varphi}{n + \cos \varphi},$$

where $n$ is the refractive index and $\varphi$ is the angle of incidence of the electromagnetic wave. For the air-water boundary at $\lambda=21$ cm of magnitude $n=9$.

d) Will the ratio of signal intensities received at adjacent maxima and minima increase or decrease as the star rises above the horizon?

When solving the problem, consider the sea surface to be smooth.

**Experimental task**

An elastic rubber cord is available, suspended vertically on a stand. The initial length of the cord is 150 mm, and the mass of the scale cup is 5 g. In addition, these are a set of weights weighing from 10 to 100 g, a stopwatch, a measuring ruler, a pattern and graph paper. Take the free fall acceleration to be 10 m/s². You can run over the mass of the cord.

a) Load the rubber cord with successive loads weighing from 15 to 105 g. Enter into the table and draw graphically on a suitable scale the experimental dependence of the elongation $\Delta l$ of the cord on the tension force model $F$.

b) Using the results of the measurements carried out in point a), calculate and create a table of cord volume values ​​for loads from 35 to 95 g. Perform the calculations sequentially for each of two adjacent load values ​​from the specified interval. Write the formulas you used in your calculations. Express a similar solution to the assumption about the dependence of volume on load.

Assume the Young model value is constant and equal to $E=2 \cdot 10^6$ N/m². When discussing the results, it should be borne in mind that for the indicated loads, Hooke's law for rubber is approximate, so deviations from it can reach up to 10%.

b) Determine the volume of the rubber cord using a stopwatch and placing a 60 g weight on the scale. Indicate the formulas used.
