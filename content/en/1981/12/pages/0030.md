Prove that in any $(2m+1)$-digit number you can cross out one of the digits so that the resulting 2-digit number is fair.

Now we prove the required statement by indication of $m$. For $m=1$ it is true, since in a three-digit number you can always cross out a digit so that the remaining two have the same parity. Let us assume that the statement of the problem is true for some $m$, and prove that it is true for $m+1$.

In any $(2m+3)$-valued number, it will always find two digits of the same parity, either next to each other, or one at the beginning and the other at the end of the number. Crossing out these two digits for a while, we get a $(2m+1)$-digit number, in which, according to the indication assumption, one can cross out one digit so that the resulting 2-digit number will be valid. Having restored two deleted digits of the same parity in it, we obtain, according to the remark made, a fair $(2m+2)$-digit number.
