State diagram

As is known, any substance, depending on external conditions, can be in a solid, liquid or gaseous state.

If the pressure is low and the temperature is high, the substance usually exists as a gas. At low temperature and high pressure, all substances turn into solids. At some intermediate values ​​of the parameters, liquid exists. This is shown schematically in figure 1. It remains to check the boundaries between the regions of different states - and we will obtain the so-called state diagram of matter in $p - T$ coordinates. Let us recall a few well-known facts that will help us in this matter.

1) Saturated vapor pressure increases with temperature; Accordingly, the boiling point increases with increasing pressure. This means that the liquid-gas (L-G) boundary goes in the following direction - from bottom to right - to top.

2) The melting point of weakness from pressure - the solid-liquid boundary (S - L) goes almost vertically.

3) There is a special point - critical ($T_{кр}, p_{кр}$). As you approach it along the L-G boundary, the properties of the liquid and gas (primarily the densities) differ less and less; at the critical point the differences disappear. the boundary W - G ends at this point.

4) For all substances (with the exception of helium) there is a point - a set of parameters ($T_{тр}, p_{тр}$) at which all three phases (liquid, solid and gaseous) exist. If $T < T_{тр}, p < p_{тр}$, then the liquid state is impossible, only the solid and gaseous states are possible. Consequently, there is a solid-gas boundary ($T - \Gamma$). And since at low temperatures, far from the critical temperature ($T \ll T_{кр}$), the distribution between the liquid and solid state of the city is less than between it and the gas, the behavior of the T - G boundary is similar to the behavior of the F - G boundary.
