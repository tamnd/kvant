ROOK ENDGAME ALGEBRA

Mathematical ideas are applied in the analysis of many types of endgames.

Thus, in the latticed endings there are the rules of the triangle, the square, and even a whole theory of the corresponding fields (see «Kvant», 1980, No. 10, 11). Here we will look at the rook vs pawn vs rook endings.

In the next diagram, White has an extra pawn, but he cannot win.

To evaluate such positions in which the black king is cut off from the white pawn, and the rook attacks the pawn “from the front,” there is the following simple arithmetic rule.

Heel rule. If the number of the rank occupied by the white pawn and the number of ranks separating the black king from it add up to a number less than or equal to the heel, then positional; if this number is greater than five, then White wins.

According to this rule, the given position is a draw. Indeed, the pawn is on the third rank, and the king is cut off into two ranks, 3+2=5. The game can go like this: 1.Kpc3 Rs8+2.Kpb4 Rd8 3.Kpc4 Rs8+4.Kpb5 Rd8 5.Jld1 Kpf6 6.d4(6.Kpc6 Kpe5) 6...Kpe7 7.Kpc6 Rs8+ and the black king manages to take place in front of the pawn.

If on the same diagram you move the white pawn and the king one rank up (Kp d3, p. d4), then White wins (4+2=6>5): 1.Kpc4 Rs8+ 2.Kpb5 Rd8 3.Kpc5 Rs8+ 4.Kpb6 Rd8 5.Jld1 Kpf6 6.Kpc7! Rd5 7.Kpc6 Rd8 8.d5 and the pawn continuously follows to the last rank.

If in the original diagram the black king and white rook are moved one vertical to the right, then again white wins (3+3=6>5). Solves 1.Kpc3 Rs8+2.Kpd4 Rd8+3.Kpe4 Re8+4.Kpf5 Rf8+5.Kpe6 Rd8 6.Jld1 Jld4 7.Kpe5 Jd8 8.d4 etc.

In our positions, White's pawn was central. However, the heel rule also applies in the case of a bishop or knight pawn.

In this position, according to the heel rule, there should be a draw. It is achieved by 1.Kpb3 Rb8+2.Kpa4 Rc8 3.Kpb4 Rb8+4.Kpa5 Rc8 5.Jlc1 Kpe3 6.c4 Kpd2 or 1.Jle6 Kpf5 2.Jlb6 Kpe5 3.Kpb3 Kpd5. The result will not change if the last position is shifted by one vertical either to the right or to the left.
