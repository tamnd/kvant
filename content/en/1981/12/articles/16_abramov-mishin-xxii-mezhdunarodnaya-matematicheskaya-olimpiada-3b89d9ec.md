XXII International Mathematical Olympiad

The XXII Olympiad brought together a record number of participating countries - 27. These are Australia (8*), Austria (8), Belgium (8), Bulgaria (8), Brazil (8), Great Britain (8), Hungary (4), Venezuela (8), Greece (8), Israel (6), Canada (8), Colombia (8), Cuba (8), Luxembourg (1), Mexico (5), the Netherlands (8), Poland (8), Romania (4), USSR (6), USA (8), Tunisia (2), Finland (8), France (8), Germany (8), Sweden (8), Czechoslovakia (5), Yugoslavia (8).

For the first time, the Olympiad was not held on the European continent: this year the participants met in the United States of America.

As before, our team’s meat training camp took place in the village of Chernogolovka, Moscow Region, on the basis of the experimental school of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR. At the training camp, which required quite intensive work from the participants, Olympiad problems were solved, including problems proposed at international Olympiads. Many of the teachers who worked at the training camp had previously participated in international competitions themselves (I. Zakharevich, B. Ivlev, S. Konyagin, V. Lyashko, A. Razborov). Much attention was paid to sports - football, swimming, basketball, table tennis and even diving from a five-meter high platform (for the bravest) helped keep team members in good physical shape; this turned out to be very important: in the United States it was thirty-degree heat with one hundred percent humidity.

The Soviet team sang in the USA on July 7th. The work schedule for the Olympics was as follows:

July 7-8 - team gathering at Rutgers University (New Brunswick in the vicinity of New York).

July 9-12 - preparatory work of the international jury: selection of problems for the Olympiad, evaluation of problems in points, coordination of translations from English into other languages.

The jury's work took place at the Mary Washington College in Fredericksburg (near Washington). At this time, schoolchildren and pedagogical team leaders remained at Rutgers University, and on July 12 they moved to Georgetown University (Washington).

July 13-14 - solving problems of the Olympiad.

On each of these days, three tasks were offered, for which 4.5 hours were allotted (the first 30 minutes were for preliminary familiarization with the texts; during this time, participants could contact the jury with questions about the wording). For complete solution of each problem 7 points were given.

July 15-17 - checking the work of team leaders; coordination of assessments.

Preliminary assessments and presentations by managers were clarified when discussing the work with the coordinating commission; For each problem, this commission included three American mathematicians. The leaders of the USSR team (as well as the leaders of teams from other countries that submitted problems displayed for the Olympiad) coordinated the work of American schoolchildren on the problem proposed by the USSR.

July 18 – final jury meeting.

It was decided to award the first prizes to participants who scored 41 or 42 points. Second prizes were awarded for scores between 34 and 40 points; finally, the third prizes received participation, scoring between 26 and 33 points. The jury decided not to award a special prize “for the best solution.”

On July 21, the Soviet team returned to their homeland.

Five problems of the Olympiad were published in the Problem Book “Kvaンタ” of this issue (see p. 24). Problem M716 was proposed by the UK, M717 by Germany, M718 by the Netherlands, M719 by Belgium, M720 by Finland*). Let us give the formulation of the sixth problem proposed by the USSR: Through the point O lying inside a given triangle, three circles of equal radii are drawn, each of which lies inside the triangle and touches two of its sides. Prove that point O, the center of the circle circumscribed about the triangle, and the center of the circle circumscribed into it lie on the same straight line.

The selection of problems for the Olympiad is carried out as follows: the organizing committee of the Olympiad (the president of the Olympiad is Professor R. Anderson, the proposer of the organizing committee is Dr. A. Wilokos) selected 19 problems from the problems sent by representatives of the participating countries. The international jury (chaired by Professor S. Greitzer, proponent of the task selection committee by Professor D. Barlas) selected 6 problems from these problems for the Olympiad. In doing so, the jury sought to satisfy the following requirements:

1) the problems should reflect various branches of mathematics, 2) the number of Olympiad problems should be simple, medium difficulty and complex. If the first requirement was met, then the same cannot be said about the second.
