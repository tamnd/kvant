What date was the book given to me?

The puzzle is based on the following two properties of the calendar:

I. Every day, three months begin on the same day of the week: in a leap year - January, April and July, in a non-leap year - February, March and November; in particular, 1/1, 1/17 and 1/17-80 are Tuesdays, 1/11, 1/11 and 1/11-81 are Sundays, and the remaining days of the week are the first numbers no more than twice a year.

II. Every day, except the 31st, is any of the seven days of the week during the year; The 31st day falls on six days of the week every day, and never falls on any one day of the week for the entire year (this feature of the 31st day is due to the fact that out of seven months of the year, numbering 31 days, two months end on the same day of the week: in a leap year, 31/1 and 31/VII fall on one day of the week, in a non-leap year - 31/1 and 31/X); in particular, in 1980, the 31st was not a Tuesday in any month, and in 1981 it was not a Wednesday (and - compare with I, this is important for what follows - it was a Sunday).

To verify the validity of properties I and II, it is very necessary to check them for one leap year and one non-leap year (for example, for 1980 and 1981).

What date did they give me the book? By convention, they never fall on a particular day of the week during the year. This means, by property II, this is the 31st number.

The book was probably given to me after it was printed, but it was started before the publication of this issue «Kvant», that is, either in 1980 or 1981. Which one? According to the conditions, three months in it (including the one when I started writing the note) begin on a day of the week that does not fall on the 31st. According to I and II, this is 1980 (and mention three months - January, April and July).

Uncertainty remains (let's say, I could get book 31/III and attend the writing of the note in April, or take book 31/V, and start writing the note in July, etc.). Information about “tomorrow” and “the day before yesterday” helps eliminate uncertainty.

Let's call the middle day when I started writing the note, and we will move from it back - into the past: yesterday, the day before yesterday, "the day before yesterday." This very “day before yesterday” is the day for which the “day before yesterday” was completed. This is “the day before yesterday” the 31st; that means the day before yesterday - 1st, yesterday - 2nd, today - 3rd. So, today is either 3/1, or 3/17, or 3/17-80. “The day before yesterday” for these three days is 31/XII-79, 31/III-80 and 30/VI-80, respectively. The first of them dates back to 1980, the third is not the 31st. So, my friends gave me a book by Loyd 31/III-80. Cow, goat and goose

45 days after the cow and goat are released onto the pasture, there is no grass left on it - neither what was there before the feast began (let's denote its quantity by T), nor what will grow in 45 days:

$$45(x+y) = T + 45t,$$
$$45(x+y) = T.$$

Likewise,

$$60(x+z-t) = 90(y+z-t) = T.$$

So,

$$3(x+y-t) = 4(x+z-t) = 6(y+z-t) = \frac{T}{15}.$$

Since $x=y+z,$
$$3(2y+z-t) = 4(y+2z-t) = 6(y+z-t) = \frac{T}{15},$$

from where $z=t,$ $y=2t,$ $x=3t,$ $T=180t.$

So, one of the questions is clear: the grass grew in the pasture for 180 days before the feast began. If you release all three - a cow, a goat and a goose - onto the pasture, then the cow and goat will eat the first initial supply of grass in 36 days, and the goose allows the destruction of its growth:

$$36(x+y+z-t) = 36(x+y) = 36 \cdot 5t = 180t.$$
