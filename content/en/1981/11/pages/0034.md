Following the Pythagorean Theorem

In the current geometry textbook, the Pythagorean theorem “The square of the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is equal to the sum of the squares of its legs” is proved “algebraically” by calculation (“Geometry 6-8”, paragraph 67).

Euclid proves it "geometric". He constructed squares on the sides of a right triangle and proved that the sum of the areas of the squares on the sides is equal to the area of ​​the square on the hypotenuse. We will now present two proofs of the Pythagorean theorem that use this idea, and then present many other figures for which the same “Pythagorean correspondence” holds as for squares.

Story squares

At figure 1, $a$ squares are constructed inside on the legs of the selected right triangle; the square on the hypotenuse is built outward. Wherever one square overlaps another, the sides of the squares are continued. Equal-sized figures*) are painted the same color. From figure it is clear that the triangle formed from a red trapezoid and a yellow triangle (the “red-yellow” triangle) is equal in size to the triangle formed from a green trapezoid and a gray triangle (the “grey-green” triangle). At figure 1, $6$ on the legs of the original triangle (white telerb), the squares are constructed externally, and in one of them the gray-green triangle is replaced by an equal red-yellow one. Once again replacing the green trapezoid with a gray triangle with a red trapezoid with a yellow one.

*) That is, figures that have the same area.
