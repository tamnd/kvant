⟦folio 44⟧

XV All-Union
Schoolchildren's Olympiad

V. Vavilov, A. Zemlyakov

Olympida
in mathematics

From April 15 to 22, the final stage of the XV All-Union Schoolchildren Olympiad in Mathematics took place in Almaty. 148 schoolchildren took part in it: 39 eighth-graders, 47 ninth-graders and 62 tenth-graders. All these guys are winners of the Resurublikan and zonal Olympiads, as well as schoolchildren who received I and II degree diplomas at the final stage of the XV All-Union Olympiad.

This year the Olympics was dedicated to the XXVI Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union.

The final stage of the Olympics took place in two rounds, which took place on April 17 and 19. On each day, the student proposed four problems, which were allotted 5 hours to solve. When selecting the task, the methodological commission adhered to the same principles as last year («Kvant», 1980, No. 11, p. 45). Below we present the conditions of all tasks; in parentheses next to the conditions of each task it is indicated who proposed it. At the end of the article you will find the solution to all problems not included in Problem «Kvant», 1981, No. 7).

Tasks
First day
8th grade

1. Two identical chessboards (8x8 squares) have a common center, and one of them is rotated relative to the other by 45° near the center. Find the total area of ​​all intersections of black cells of these two boards if the area of ​​one cell is A, B, M and N. From point M the chords MA1 and MB1 are drawn, perpendicular to the straight lines NB and NA, respectively. Prove that lines AA1 and BB1 are parallel. (If A=A1, then line AA1 is tangent to the circle at point A; similarly for B=B1.) (A. Slinko)

3. We will say that a number has property (K) if it can be decomposed into the product of K consecutive natural numbers greater than 1.

a) Find a K such that the neotorgore number has both the properties (K) and (K+2).

6) Prove that a number that has both properties (2) and (4) does not exist. (B. Fedotov)

4. The table has 4 rows. The first of them contains arbitrary natural numbers, some of which may be identical. The second line is filled in as follows: the numbers of the first line are looked through from left to right and the number k is written under the number a, if a appears in the first line for the kth time. Similarly, the third is written on the second line, and the fourth on the third. Prove that the second and fourth lines are always the same. (A. Kuznetsov)

9th grade

1. Find the smallest area of ​​a rectangle with sides parallel to the coordinate axes Ox and Oy, containing the figure defined by the system of inequalities (a∈R)

$$\begin{cases} y \leq -x^2, \\ y \geq x^2 - 2x + a. \end{cases}$$

(A. Savin)

2. See problem number 4 for 8th grade.

3. Regular triangles ABC, CDE and EHK (vertices are indicated counterclockwise) with pairwise common vertices C and E are located on the plane so that AD=DK. Prove that the triangle BHD is also regular. (A. Kuntsov)

4. In some successive 1000 inhabitants. Every day, each of them shares the news they learned yesterday with all their friends. It is known that any news is learned by the entire village resident. Prove that you can select 90 residents so that at the same time you tell them all some news, then after 10 days it becomes known to all residents of the village. (N. Kartatsov, A. Savin)
