⟦folio 45⟧

10th grade

1. About the numbers $a$ and $b$ it is known that the inequality
$$a \cdot \cos x + b \cdot \cos 3x > 1$$
has no solutions. Prove that $|b| \leq 1$.
(Yu. Nesterenko)

2. The points $K$ and $M$ are the midpoints of the side $AB$ and $CD$ of the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$, the points $L$ and $N$ are located on the other two sides so that the quadrilateral $KLMN$ is a rectangle. Prove that the area of ​​the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is twice the area of ​​the rectangle $KLMN$.

3. Find all sequences of natural numbers ($x_n$) such that the following two conditions are satisfied:
a) $x_n \leq n\sqrt{n}$ for any $n$;
b) the difference $x_m - x_n$ is divisible by $m - n$ for any different $m$ and $n$.
(Yu. Nesterenko)

4. Is it possible to color all the cells of any rectangular table white and black so that there are equal numbers of white and black cells, and in each row and in each column there are more than 3/4 of the cells of the same color? (S. Konyatsin)

Second day

8th grade

5. Prove that if the quadrilaterals $ACPH, AMBE, AHBT, BKXM, CKXP$ are parallelograms (the vertices of all quadrilaterals are listed in the same direction), then the broken line $ABTE$ has no self-intersections and bounds the parallelogram.
(B. Fedotov)

6. Solve the equation
$$x^3 - y^3 = xy + 61$$
in natural numbers $x$ and $y$. (S. Konyatsin, A. Slinko)

7. In a football tournament, 18 teams played 8 rounds with each other - which team played with eight different teams. Prove that there are three teams that have not yet played a single match.
(S. Konyatsin)

8. The points $C_1, A_1, B_1$ are taken on the sides corresponding to $AB, BC$ and $CA$ of triangle $ABC$ such that $|AC_1|:|C_1B| = |BA_1|:|A_1C| = |CB_1|:|B_1A| = 1:3$. Prove that the perimeter $P$ of triangle $ABC$ and the perimeter $P_1$ of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ are related by the inequalities
$$\frac{1}{2}P < P_1 < \frac{3}{4}P.$$

(B. Turchinnov)

9th grade

5. Prove that if positive numbers $x, y$ satisfy the equation $x^3 + y^3 = x - y$, then $x^2 + y^2 < 1$. (G. Galperin)

6. The student needs to copy a convex polygon that fits in a circle of radius 1. First, the student split off the first side, from its end he drew the second - third, etc. After completing the construction, he discovered that the polygon was not closed, and the first and last vertices he drew were at a distance of $d$ from one another. It is known that students plot angles accurately, and the relative error when plotting the length of each side does not exceed the number $p$. Prove that $d \leq 4p$. (N. Kartashov)

7. Each vertex of the cube contains a number. In one step, one is added to two numbers placed on one (any) edge. Is it possible to make all eight numbers equal in a few steps like this if numbers like figure 1 were placed at the beginning? As in figure 2? As in figure 3? (N. Vasiliev)

8. Find at least one natural number $n$ such that each of the numbers $n, n+1, n+2, \dots, n+20$ has a common divisor greater than one with the number 30 030 = 2·3·5·7·11·13. (Yu. Nesterenko)

10th grade

5. Natural numbers from 100 to 999 are each written on their own card. The cards are placed number side down on the table, moved and then placed into one pile. We will open sequential cards from this pile, sort them, arrange them into cubes in numbers in accordance with the lowest digit of the written number. The first cube will contain all numbers ending in 0, the second cube will contain all numbers ending in 1, and so on. Let's put all the cubes in one pile, placing the second on top of the first cube and finally the ten. The resulting stack of cards will be sorted again, but now, when opening the card, we will arrange them into cubes in accordance with the highest digit. In what order will the cards be arranged in the table, assembled as indicated earlier, after this sorting? (Yu. Nesterenko)
