Monastic task
It is impossible to fulfill the paradoxical conditions of the problem at first glance (especially if it is solved by selection). Let us, however, call upon elementary algebra to help. Let's draw a diagram (Table 1) of the dwelling floors: with the letter A we mark the corner cells, with the letter B - the middle cells facing west and east, with the letter C - the middle cells with windows to the north and south. Let there be 2n nuns on the top floor, n on the bottom floor, and 3n in total. In 6 keloys on the south side there live 11 nuns, in 6 keloys facing the north - the same 11, together - 22. This means that in 4 keloys B there live 3n - 22 nuns. There are the same number of them in 4 cells C. This means that in 8 cells A there live 3n-2(3n-22)=44-3n nuns. Since every cell is occupied, then
3n — 22≥4,
44 — 3n≥8,

whence 26≤3n≤36 and, therefore, since n is an integer,
9≤n≤12,
27≤3n≤36,

So, at the beginning there were no more than 36, and at the end there were no less than 27 nuns left. If 9 nuns disappeared, then there were exactly 36, and there were 27 nuns left.
