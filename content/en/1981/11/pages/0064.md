Fig. 4.

Tom, son of Tubular

Let (Fig. 4, a) Tom run at speed $v_T$ along the chase line from point $(0; -r)$, and let the pig run at speed $v_c$ along the x-axis from point $(0; 0)$. The speeds are constant in magnitude. Tom is homeless fast pig: $|v_T| = v$, $|v_c| = \omega v$, $\omega < 1$.

Let us prove that in a finite time $\tau$ Tom will catch up with the pig and that the distance $R_T = v\tau$ that he will cover in this case is expressed in terms of $r$ and $\omega$ according to the formula

$$R_T = \frac{r}{1 - \omega^2}.$$

To find the speed of convergence of Tom and the pig, let's go to the reporting system, where the pig is motionless and located at the origin of coordinates $O$, the new x-axis $OX$ is parallel to the old one and directed to the right (Fig. 4, b). Let at moment $t$ Tom be at point $A = A(t)$. Let's find its speed $u(t)$ in the new system: from point $A$ we plot the vector of length $v$ in the direction $AO$ and the vector of length $\omega v$ to the left; then there are $u(t) = \cos \theta$ vectors (in other words, $u(t) = v_T - v_c$; note that both the directions $u$ and $|u|$ depend on $t$). Let's find the speed $u_{AO}(t)$, with which the distance $AO$ (the speed of merging of Tom and the pig), and the speed $u_{OX}(t)$, with which Tom moves along the axis $OX$. To do this, we project vector $u(t)$ onto directions $AO$ and $OX$. The projections are easy to calculate, since $u(t)$ is the difference vector of $v_T$ and $v_c$, which have exactly these directions. Denoting by $\varphi(t)$ the angle between $AO$ and $OX$ and projecting $v_T$ and $v_c$ onto $OX$ and $AO$, we obtain (Fig. 4, c)

$$u_{OX}(t) = v \cdot \cos \varphi(t) - \omega v, \quad (1)$$
$$u_{AO}(t) = v - \omega v \cdot \cos \varphi(t). \quad (2)$$

Since $u_{AO}(t) \geq v(1 - \omega)$ and since the initial distance between Tom and the pig is $r$, the chase time

$$\tau \leq \frac{r}{v(1 - \omega)}$$

Since Tom starts and ends the chase at points where $X = 0$, we get

$$\int_0^t u_{OX}(t) dt = 0,$$

that is, according to (1),

$$\int_0^t v \cdot \cos \varphi(t) dt = \omega v \tau = \omega R_T.$$

According to (2),

$$r = \int_0^t u_{AO}(t) dt = v \tau - \omega \int_0^t v \cdot \cos \varphi(t) dt,$$

so, taking into account (3),

$$r = R_T(1 - \omega^2),$$

or

$$R_T = \frac{r}{1 - \omega^2}.$$

Let us recall the correspondences $R_1 = \frac{r}{1 - \omega}$, $R_2 = \frac{r}{1 + \omega}$, $R_c = \omega R_T$ (here $R_1$ and $R_2$ are the distances that Tom would run if he chased the pig not from point $(0; -r)$, but from points $(-r; 0)$ and $(r; 0)$, respectively), we find

$$R_T = \frac{1}{2}(R_1 + R_2),$$
$$R_c = \frac{1}{2}(R_1 - R_2).$$

Using the same method, a more general formula is obtained: if Tom is still chasing the pig along the chase line and the pig is moving along the x-axis from point $(0; 0)$, and Tom at the initial moments is at point $(x_0; y_0)$, where $x_0 = r \cdot \cos \theta$, $y_0 = r \cdot \sin \theta$, then the distance that Tom will run before chasing is equal to

$$R = \frac{1}{2}[R_1(1 - \cos \theta) + R_2(1 + \cos \theta)];$$

$$R_1, R_2$$ and $R_T$ are obtained from this formula at $\Theta = \pi$, $\Theta = 0$ and $\Theta = -\frac{\pi}{2}$.
