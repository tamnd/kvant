In the story of color vision, the characteristic features of Maxwell's scientific method are clearly visible. Almost all of his research contained mandatory stages: the creation of a visual geometric model and the use of simple analogies.

Here are the words of Maxwell about what a physical analogy means, what it means to build a model: the task is to “find a physical analogy that would allow the mind to embrace the results of previous research, or the latest of any theory based on the physical principles of the science from which the analogy was borrowed, so as not to be distracted from the very subject of the study, breaking down the analytical subtleties, and not to be clear of the truth because the hypothesis you like.”

Such a formula, full of only warnings, is completely equivalent in the Japanese solutions to Maxwell's main problem - the theory of the electromagnetic field. It was Maxwell who managed to see in Faraday’s works what no one else could see. In Faraday's power lines, he saw a new physical object - a field. But for this it was necessary to go through a difficult stage of research - the stage of a primitive model.

Again the story begins with the works of the Great Physicist. Faraday suggested that the action at a distance between charges and currents is transmitted using lines of force connecting different charges (electric lines) or rings surrounding points (magnetic lines). These lines tend to move along their length (attraction) and push away from their neighbors. By the movement of such lines, Faraday was able to explain the results of a huge number of his own and other people’s experiments.

Maxwell really liked Faraday's idea: the visual picture of elastic threads turned out to be a successful analogy. It was necessary to find a mathematical apparatus to describe such a mechanical model. Maxwell recalls the work of Thomson (future Lord Kelvin) on how to fit together the equation for the gravitational potential and the equation for heat distribution. Both phenomena can be described using a system of surfaces on which there is potential or temperature. Thus, the temperature in the space around a heated ball drops according to the same law as the potential of the gravitational field of a massive ball.

Based on a similar analogy, Maxwell begins by describing the electric and magnetic fields using a system of lines, arbitrary at each point in space and indicating the direction of action of forces. In order to describe the magnitude of the forces, Maxwell takes one more step, which must be indicated, like in a chess game, with the sign “!!”. He replaces drain lines with tubes, and in order to make the tubes elastic, he assumes that liquid is tapping along them.

The concept of electric and magnetic fluids was not new. Thus, even before Faraday, he tried to describe the phenomena occurring with charges and magnets. But no one tried to treat them as objects worthy of mathematical description, and no one combined them with Faraday force lines. Faraday realized that in the presence of fields, the body goes into some “electric state” (this is how he imagined the action of the field). Maxwell tried to find mathematical laws that describe this state. In his first work on electromagnetism, “On Faradian Lines of Force” (made in 1855-1856, and published in 1861), he still sadly writes: “...
