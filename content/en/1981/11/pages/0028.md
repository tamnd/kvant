F685. Between the wall and the cube of mass $M=10$, a ball of mass $m=0,1$ g flies on a smooth control table. This speed at the beginning, when the cube accumulated, was $v_0=100$ m/s. Find the speed of the cube at the moment when it is 2 times farther from the wall than at the beginning.

We will assume that with each impact of the ball on the cube, the speed of the cube changes by a very small amount and remains many times less than the speed of the ball.

Let $x$ be the distance from the cube to the wall, $u$ the speed of the cube, $v$ the speed of the ball. Then the time between two successive collisions is $\tau=2x/v$. With each collision, the cube receives an impulse of $\Delta p=2mv$ (we taught that $u\ll v$). The speed of the ball per blow changes by $\Delta v=-2u$ (this is very/no, if we consider the movement in the frame of reference associated with the cube). To simplify the calculations, we assume that as a result of collisions, the velocities $u$ and $v$ change not jumpwise, but smoothly. Then the “average” acceleration of the ball is $v'=\frac{\Delta v}{\tau}=-\frac{uv}{x}$. Considering that $u=x'$, we rewrite this equality:

$$v'x+x'v=0.$$

Note that the following expression is equal to the product of the product $vx$; the fact that this product is zero means that the quantity $vx$ remains during construction. Hence,

$$vx=v_0x_0,$$

where $x_0$ is the distance between the cube and the wall at the beginning of the process. At $x=2x_0$ the speed of the ball is $v_0/2$. We find the speed of the cube at this moment from the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv_0^2}{2}=\frac{m(v_0/2)^2}{2}+\frac{Mu^2}{2} \Rightarrow u=\frac{v_0}{2} \sqrt{\frac{3m}{M}} \approx 0,27 \text{ м/с.}$$

F686. Thin hoops of mass $M$ and radius $R$ are placed on a horizontal plane. Along a smooth channel passing inside the hoop, a small puck of mass $m$ slides from the top point without an initial speed. Determine the speed of the center of the hoop at the moment when the puck is at the point of the hoop $A$, the radius vector of which makes an angle $\varphi$ with vertical. At the initial moment, the hoops will accumulate. There is no friction between the hoops and the plane.

The forces acting on the hoop-puck system are the gravity force and the normal reaction force from the plane. Both of these forces are directed along the vertical. Consequently, the center of mass of the system does not move in the horizontal direction.

Since there is no connection between the hoop and the plane, the hoop moves forward. According to the law of conservation of momentum, at any moment in time

$$M|u|=m|v_x|,$$

where $u$ and $v_x$ are horizontal projections of the velocities of the hoop and puck (and $u=|\vec{u}|$). Since $v_x$ periodically changes sign, then $u$ “synchronously” changes sign. The general nature of the movement of the hoop is as follows: the puck in sections $BC$ and $BE$ (Fig. 1) - the center of the hoop moves to the right; the puck on sections $CD$ and $DE$ - the center of the hoop moves to the left.

The speeds of the puck $\vec{v}$ and the hoop $\vec{u}$ are related by the law of conservation of energy:

$$mgR(1+\cos \varphi)=\frac{mv^2}{2}+\frac{Mu^2}{2},$$

where $\varphi$ is the angle that the radius vector of the point at which the puck is located at a given moment (for given values ​​$\vec{v}$ and $\vec{u}$) makes with the vertical.
