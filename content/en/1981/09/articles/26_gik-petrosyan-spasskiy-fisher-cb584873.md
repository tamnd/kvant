Petrosyan - Spassky - Fischer

In 1954, as a seventeen-year-old boy, Boris Spassky first participated in the selection for the World Championship and immediately before the Candidates' Tournament. However, the next two cycles turned out to be dramatic for him - in the USSR championship, which is a zonal tournament, both times he lost the decisive games of the last round and dropped out of the fight. The fourth attempt was more successful - Spassky emerged victorious in the claims matches. However, his finest hour has not yet come. In the 1966 match, Petrosyan played weaker, more subtly and, despite the minimal advantage in the score (12.5:11.5), confidently retained the crown.

In the early 70s, American grandmaster Robert Fischer amazed the world with his fantastic victories - he beat Taimanov and Larsen in pre-claim matches with a score of 6:0, and Petrosn - 6.5:2.5. The chess world took the birth of the eleventh champion for granted. Fischer's lead in the 1972 match for the world crown was wrong (12.5:8.5). But it is fair to note that the American launched a number of psychological attacks on his opponent, and not only at the chessboard. This clearly affected Spassky's performance. Unfortunately, after this match, fans of the ancient game were bitterly disappointed - the new king left chess. What are the reasons for this strange behavior? Here is one point of view.

The champion's crown secured for Fischer, in his opinion, the role of the chess messiah on Earth. As a result, he considered that he now had no right to perform unsuccessfully in tournaments and generally lose at least one game. In short, the champion took on such a burden of obligations that he simply agreed to chess - not any specific chess, but the Pieces and the boards themselves.

Fischer's telegram in 1975, on the eve of the match with Karpov, that he was refusing the title of FIDE world champion, brought an end to his reign.
