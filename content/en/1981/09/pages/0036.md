Chains are useful, of course, but less beautiful than Borromean rings. However, if you try to straighten the links of the chain so that they become flat again, the chains unfold into beautiful rosettes.

Fig.x 4,a and 4,b show sockets obtained accordingly.

Vienna from a 5-link and 12-link chain like the one on figure 3, a. Use colored wires, try to make the transition from weave 3, a to 4, b. Try to get sockets from chains 3,b and 3,c.

The “rug” obtained at figure 4,b is woven not from one or two threads, but from 12 rather large rings: removing any of them leads to trivial entanglement - the weight of the “rug” crumbles. From the last figure it is clear that the number of rings that make up the “rug” can be as large as possible, and yet it is enough to break one (and any!) ring for the “rug” to break up into separate rings.
