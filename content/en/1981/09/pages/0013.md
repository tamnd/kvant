When determining the concentration of a solution, a crystal lattice appears, periodic in two directions. In this case, diopole molecules are collected into liquid columns or “threads”, which, in fact, form such a lattice, similar to a stack of pencils (Fig. 5, a). Such partially solid crystals exist not only in rasters. They can also be individual substances that change their state with changes in temperature. In this case, as the temperature decreases, the states usually change in the following sequence: ordinary liquid - nematic liquid or cholesteric - smectic - solid crystal. For a long time, liquid crystalline substances with lattices periodic in two directions were not found, but they have recently been discovered. figure 5.6 shows such a lattice formed by liquid stacks of disk-shaped molecules. It is interesting that in the latter case there is also an orientational order: the planes of the disks in the stolnik are parallel to each other, although the centers of the disks are located completely chaotically along the axis of the liquid stolnik.

The structure of liquid crystals - rasters - is of great importance for the life of an organization, for example, for blood circulation, the transfer of its oxygen, the functioning of brain cells, and for the work of various cellular membranes. Such membranes often have a structure like that shown in figure 4.6; defects in this structure lead to illness in the organization. The formation of cholesteric and especially smectic liquid crystals in the blood causes cardiovascular diseases. When there is an unfavorable concentration of various components in the bile, first incompletely solid crystals and then “stones” are formed. These examples could be continued. Here are two more.

The first example is associated with the expression “cholesterol in the blood,” which is used by both doctors and non-specialists to indicate a disease expressed in blockage of blood juices. This is exactly the case when a “good” (that is, with a perfect structure) cholesteric liquid crystal formed as a result of a disease is a flat symptom. Indeed, we have already noted that cholesteric has good fluidity only along the nematic layers, while in the transverse direction it is more like a solid body. Therefore, when in the circulatory state pieces of cholesteric helices are arranged so that the blood flow must flow through the nematic elephants, the resistance to this flow increases greatly - the blood vessel becomes clogged.
