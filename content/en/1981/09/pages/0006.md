⟦folio 4⟧

N. Semenov

Bolder,
don't back down
before difficulties!

Every year thousands of young men and women enter Soviet science.

When educating young people starting scientific work, the most important thing is the consistent and steady development of their initiative and independence.

Of course, this does not mean that we, scientists, should leave young people to their own devices: a path, they say, that attracts independence, a path of self-determination in science. With such a “gravity” method, the very first steps of young people could lead to a chain of mistakes and deep disappointments. However, even worse is the second extreme - the transfer of a huge theoretical and experimental heritage in a ready-made form.

It is necessary for young people to achieve a lot on their own, looking for their own, not yet the best, but independent solutions. This, after the first hesitant steps, will allow young scientists to feel their strength, their ability, even with difficulty, to step independently in science. Our duty is to give them tasks not with very good answers, but those that would require thoughtful reflection, fundamental changes and improvements in work methods and deep independent analysis.

We try in no case to impose “unconditional” judgments and solutions and do not demand that the results of the decision strictly correspond to the manager’s point of view - and no other.

Academician Nikolai Nikolaevich Semenov - twice Hero of Socialist Labor, Nobel, Lenin and State Prize laureate, director of the Institute of Chemical Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

The article is reprinted from the collection “Lenin. Science. Youth", published in 1980 by the Nauka publishing house.
