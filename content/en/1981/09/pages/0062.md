⟦folio 60⟧

Where did the puzzle come from in which Miss Pocahont is 24 years old? As you probably already guessed, this is a typo in the answer to problem No. 154.

Alas, this is not the only problem that is insignificant in this way when preparing a book for printing. An equally annoying misunderstanding occurred with Mary (Ann’s sister), whose age Lloyd asks to calculate from the confusing conditions of problem No. 127:

“You see,” said Grandpa, “the combined age of Mary and Ann is 42 years, and Mary is twice as old as Ann was when Mary was half as young as Ann will be when Ann is the second older than Mary was when Mary was second older than Ann.” How old is Mary?

We consider it necessary to warn the reader who got out of these intricacies: do not look back - Mary is not 27.5 years old.

It’s a pity, of course, for both poor Pocahont and Mary, who was married to tears, but their unenviable fate is nothing in comparison with the fate of the great Lou Dillon and the oddities of problem No. 215, the main of which, of course, is the answer to this problem, printed on p. 324:

The horse ran successive quarter miles in 27 1/4, 27, 27 1/8 seconds, respectively, and a mile in 1 minute 48 1/2 seconds.

So that we can evaluate it, here are the conditions:

No. 215. On the run

One day, at a race with racetrack queen Lou Dillon, a rather strange problem arose, which turned out to be too difficult for the judges who were not very skilled in mathematics. The first judge timed the time (81 3/8 sec) that Lou took to run only the first 3/4 mile, and the second judge timed (81 1/4 sec) Lou took to run only the last 3/4 mile. Let's assume that Lou ran the first half mile in the same time as the second. How many sums can you predict how many seconds it took Lou to run just miles?
