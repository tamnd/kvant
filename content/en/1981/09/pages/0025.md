3°. $\vec{a} \wedge \vec{b} = 0$ if the vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ are collinear follows from the fact that $\vec{a} \wedge \vec{b}$ is equal to the scalar product of the vectors $\vec{b}$ and $\mathbf{R}^{90^\circ}(\vec{a})$.

It is convenient to introduce “symmetrical” notation: let $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ be a given even angle, $H_1, H_2, H_3$ and $H_4$ the intersection points of the altitudes of triangles $A_2A_3A_4, A_3A_4A_1, A_4A_1A_2$ and $A_1A_2A_3$, respectively, and $\vec{a}_i$ and $\vec{h}_i$ the vectors going from a fixed point $O$ to $A_i$ and $H_i$ ($i=1, 2, 3, 4$).

Let us prove that the triangles $A_1A_2A_3$ and $H_1H_2H_3$ are equal in size (have the same area) and are equally oriented. Since twice the area of ​​$\triangle A_1A_2A_3$ (taking into account orientation) is equal to $\vec{A}_1\vec{A}_2 \wedge \vec{A}_1\vec{A}_3 = (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) \wedge (\vec{a}_3 - \vec{a}_1) = \vec{a}_1 \wedge \vec{a}_2 + \vec{a}_2 \wedge \vec{a}_3 + \vec{a}_3 \wedge \vec{a}_1$, we must prove the equality

$$\vec{a}_1 \wedge \vec{a}_2 + \vec{a}_2 \wedge \vec{a}_3 + \vec{a}_3 \wedge \vec{a}_1 = \vec{h}_1 \wedge \vec{h}_2 + \vec{h}_2 \wedge \vec{h}_3 + \vec{h}_3 \wedge \vec{h}_1.$$

(1)

To do this, we only use the fact that $[A_iH_j] \parallel [A_jH_i]$ for all $i \neq j$. Let's say $[A_1H_2] \parallel [A_2H_1]$ since they are perpendicular to $[A_3A_4]$; therefore $(\vec{a}_1 - \vec{h}_2) \wedge (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{h}_1) = 0$. Adding three equalities:

$$\vec{a}_1 \wedge \vec{a}_2 - \vec{h}_1 \wedge \vec{h}_2 = \vec{a}_1 \wedge \vec{h}_1 - \vec{a}_2 \wedge \vec{h}_2,$$
$$\vec{a}_2 \wedge \vec{a}_3 - \vec{h}_2 \wedge \vec{h}_3 = \vec{a}_2 \wedge \vec{h}_2 - \vec{a}_3 \wedge \vec{h}_3,$$
$$\vec{a}_3 \wedge \vec{a}_1 - \vec{h}_3 \wedge \vec{h}_1 = \vec{a}_3 \wedge \vec{h}_3 - \vec{a}_1 \wedge \vec{h}_1.$$

we get (1).

Of course, it is also proved in general that the triangles $A_iA_jA_k$ and $H_iH_jH_k$ are equal in size and equally oriented (for all $i \neq j \neq k$); in particular this applies to triangles $A_3A_4A_1$ and $H_3H_4H_1$. This implies the equality of the areas of the quadrilaterals $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ and $H_1H_2H_3H_4$.

Moreover, both of these quadrilaterals will be simultaneously either (a) convex, or (b) non-convex but not self-intersecting, or (b) self-intersecting: if all the quadrilaterals $A_1A_2A_3, A_2A_3A_4, A_3A_4A_1, A_4A_1A_2$ have the same orientation, then (a), if one differs in orientation from the other three - (b); if the “score is tied” 2:2 - (c).

If we tried to translate this solution into an elementary geometric language, we would get a gramodic picture of many parallelograms, the very correspondences between the areas of which are confused due to the peculiarities of their location. A more elegant geometric solution (requiring, however, non-circularity, in part, it uses the formula $tg\alpha + tg\beta = (1 - tg\alpha tg\beta) tg(\alpha + \beta)$) is based on the useful relationships shown in figure 2, where $H$ is the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangles $ABC$, $O$ is the circumcenter of the circumcircle, $K$ is the midpoint of the side $AB^*$). On this path, it is immediately clear that for a quadrilateral $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ inscribed in a circle, the “orthocentric” quadrilateral $H_1H_2H_3H_4$ will not only be equal in size to it, but also congruent (in the general case, as follows from the equality of the areas of triangles $A_iA_jA_k$ and $H_iH_jH_k$, these quadrilaterals are afficino equivalent, that is, one is obtained from the other by a linear transformation of coordinates).

**M665. The light display consists of several light bulbs, each of which can be in two states (corresponding or not containing). There are several keys on the path, pressing each of them simultaneously changes the state of a certain set of light bulbs**

Let the number of lamps be $N$, the number of buttons be $n$. Let us denote by $A_i$ the subset of the light bulb that the $i$th button switches, that is, the pattern that is obtained from the initial state $B_0$—“all the lights are off”—when the $i$th button is pressed; we will also talk about “operation $A_i$”, meaning by this pressing the $i$ button.
