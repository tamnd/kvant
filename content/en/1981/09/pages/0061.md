Let's consider two auxiliary problems:

Let Tom and the pig run in one straight line, once - Tom follows the pigs, another time - towards each other. Then (check), let's grab the pig, Tom will run the corresponding distance $R_1 = r/(1-\omega)$ and $R_2 = r(1+\omega)$.

In the solution to problem No. 145, Loyd, without explanation or evidence, reports the rules: $R_T = (R_1 + R_2)/2$.

As soon as they look at this incredibly simple formula, the majority of artificial mathematicians (who have never studied the chase problem) will exclaim: “This cannot be!” - and will be surprised how the editor of the book, Martin Gardner, and the translator, Yu. The most amazing thing is that the rule is... correct! Oddly enough, $R_T$ is real equal to $r/(1-\omega^2) = (R_1 + R_2)/2$, which gives us an incredibly simple formula for $R_C$: $R_C = r(\omega)/(1-\omega^2) = (R_1 - R_2)/2$.
