various machines in agriculture... Can all this be successfully used in the future by someone who is studying hard today, getting from two to three? Of course, we still have professions that do not require deep knowledge and special training, but every year the need for them will be less and less.

Guys sometimes ask: “Why should everyone know mathematics and physics - after all, not everyone will need these sciences in their future work?” Of course, in order to count change in a store, knowledge of basic arithmetic is enough. Then algebra and geometry for the future cook, cutter or assembly line worker? You can work in different ways. You can day after day, without thinking about anything, put the same products into the cauldron, cut fabrics according to standard templates and tighten nuts with a primitive tool. And today you can already calculate the nutritional value of products, create optimal and inexpensive food rations, look for the most economical methods of cutting fabric, and improve tools and work techniques. This is where you can’t take a step without a good knowledge of school disciplines!

Studying mathematics does not give a person narrow professional knowledge and skills, preparation for each one profession, but develops, trains and improves his mind - teaches him to reason correctly, analyze what he read or hear, justify and convincingly prove his judgments, make the right decisions in difficult life situations. Mathematics teaches a person to think and think correctly, and this is necessary for everyone.

Physics is no less important. It gives a person knowledge about the basic laws of nature, which are the theoretical foundation of all modern technology. And if the driver wants not only to turn the steering wheel and get his feet on the pedals, but even to understand a little about his car, he certainly needs a good knowledge of school physics. And elektrichesvo - after all, we are inextricably linked with it both at home and at work. How can one not know its basic laws!

The school does not train professional physicists, mathematicians or historians. To become a scientist, you need to work hard and hard. But centuries of human experience have made it possible to select and introduce into school curricula what is needed by all members of society, regardless of their future profession.

Our readers have a deeper interest in mathematics and physics, otherwise they would not be subscribers to our magazine. Many of you are passionate about this science, work with satisfaction on our articles and solve the problems we propose. But we would like even those who are truly proficient in mathematics or physics not to neglect other school subjects, but to try to look for basic patterns and causal relationships in them, to analyze the course of evidence, in a word, as our great poet said, to verify harmony with algebra. And we would also like your interest in these sciences not to remain within you, not to be only your personal property. Try to get your products interested in this, involve them in setting up the experiments that we talk about in the “Laboratory «Kvant»”, in solving problems, puzzles, crosswords, rebuses. Let them too feel the “joy of modest victories”, believe in their strength, trust their abilities.

Speaking at the third Komsomol congress, V.I. Lenin called on young people to actively share their knowledge with others and help their comrades study successfully. “Only in such work does a young man or girl turn into a real communist,” said Vladimir Ilyich. We are building communism, and every schoolchild can successfully and more usefully participate in this construction today.
