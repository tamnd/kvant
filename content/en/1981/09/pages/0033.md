⟦folio 31⟧

Exercise

1. Flour was brought to the store in bags. It is known that the first, second and third bags contain at least 60 kg of flour, the first, second and fourth - no more than 50 kg of flour, the first, third and fourth - no more than 40 kg of flour, and the second, third and fourth - no more than 30 kg of flour. How much flour was in each bag?

2. Decipher the rebus TWO × TWO, shown at figure. Identical letters correspond to the same numbers, different ones - different numbers. The dots can be any numbers.

3. Once upon a time, the court Mathematician, having received his wish for a year in middle thalers, arranged the coins into nine unequal columns (see figure) so that the numbers of coins in the columns formed a magic square of a third order *); At the same time, some of the columns turned out to be quite high, but still there was no column that would contain more than three hundred coins. The King then liked Mathematics; he only regretted that all the numbers turned out to be composite. “If Your Majesty,” said the Mathematician, “added 9 more thalers to my wish, I would put another coin in each column, and then the magic square would be preserved, but all the numbers of coins in the columns would already be prime.” The King was just about to increase the desire of Mathematics, as the Jester talked together. He took a coin from each column, and the new numbers all turned out to be prime (and the square, of course, remained magical!) How did the Mathematician add up his coins?

These tasks were offered to us
V. Berezhnoy and B. Rublev
(students of the 9th grade of Physics and Mathematics School
at KSU, Kyiv),
A. Shvetsov, G. Shishkina

*) A 3×3 square of nine numbers whose sums in rows, columns and diagonals are the same.
