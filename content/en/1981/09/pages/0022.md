Tasks

M701 — M705; F713 — F717

M701. Lyuda, Marina and Natasha drew the acute triangle LMN. Then Luda constructed her own triangle, in which the lengths of two sides are equal to $|LM|$ and $|LN|$, and the angle between them is 60° greater than the angle $L$ of the triangle LMN. In the same way, Marina constructed her triangle from the sides of length $|ML|$ and $|MN|$, the angle between them is 60° greater than $\hat{M}$, and Natasha built hers, in which the angle between the sides $|NL|$ and $|NM|$ is equal to $\hat{N} + 60^\circ$. Prove that the third (new) sides of the triangles are the same for all three girls.

A. Kaplan

M702. Let us denote by $S_n$ the sum of the first $n$ primes: $S_1 = 2, S_2 = 2 + 3 = 5, S_3 = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10, S_4 = 17$, etc. Prove that for any $n$ between $S_n$ and $S_{n+1}$ an exact square occurs.

I. Zhuk

M703*. Solve the system of equations

$$\begin{cases} 3(x + \frac{1}{x}) = 4(y + \frac{1}{y}) = 5(z + \frac{1}{z}), \\ xy + yz + zx = 1. \end{cases}$$

A. Fedorov

M704*. A parallelogram is described around a square (the vertices of the square lie on different sides of the parallelogram). Prove that the perpendiculars dropped from the vertices of the parallelogram to the sides of the square form a new square (Fig. 1).

N. Vasiliev

M705. On a rectangular sheet of checkered paper there are several rectangular cards, the sides of which lie on linear grids. The cards cover the sheet in two layers (that is, each cell of the sheet is covered with exactly two cards). a) Let each card have dimensions $1 \times 2$ cells. Prove that you can select some of the cards so that they cover the sheet in one layer. (You cannot move the cards.) Will this remain true if the cards 6) can have arbitrary sizes, c*) have dimensions of $1 \times 3$ cells?

G. Galperin, V. Proizvolov
