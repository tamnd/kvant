This year marks the 140th anniversary of the birth and 70th anniversary of the death of the famous puzzle master, inventor of the famous "game of 15", one of the pioneers of the mathematician, the classic of this genre, Sam Loyd. In 1980, the publishing house Mir published Loyd's Mathematical Mozik, a collection of his best mathematical problems and puzzles.

The article published below, evident from reading this unconventional collection, is not a solution in the generally accepted sense of the word. Its author, a professional mathematician, prefers to retell Loyd in waves and examine specific puzzles in detail, proposing new ones along the way, instead of a general discussion of the content, advantages and disadvantages of the book. Despite the numerous absences from the letter of Loyd's problems (and perhaps thanks to them), the article, it seems to us, better than the promised solution conveys the festive spirit of wave invention, wit and fun, characteristic of Loyd's book.

One Dane with a goat on the rope and a goose under his arm met a milkmaid who was leading a cow. Suddenly the girl screamed in fear.

“So you wanted to kiss me against my will,” answered the modest woman.
