Tasks

M706 — M710; F718 — F722

M706. From the center of each of the two given circles, tangents are drawn to the other circle. Prove that the chords connecting the points of intersection of the tangents with the circles (at figure 1 these chords are shown in red) have the same lengths.

A. Savin

M707. Each of the students in the class is involved in no more than two clubs, and for any pair of students there is a club in which they study together. Prove that there is a club where at least 2/3 of the students in this class participate.

A. Sidorenko

M708. On the sides of a convex quadrilateral of area $S$, squares are constructed outside it, the centers of which serve as the vertices of a new quadrilateral of area $S_1$. Prove that
a) $S_1 \geq 2S$;
b) $S_1 = 2S$ if and only if the diagonals of the original quadrilateral are equal in length and mutually perpendicular.

P. Gusyatnikov

M709. The floor of the room, shaped like a regular hexagon with sides 10, is filled with tiles in the shape of a rhombus with sides 1 and an acute angle of 60°. It is allowed to take out three tiles that make up a regular hexagon with sides 1, and replace their arrangement with another (Fig. 2). Prove that
a) from any arrangement of tiles of such operations any other can be obtained;
b) this can be done in no more than 1000 operations;
c) from the arrangement of tiles figure 3, and it is impossible to obtain the arrangement figure 3.6 in less than 1000 operations.

A. Smirnov

M710. Is there a sequence of distinct natural numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$, none of whose terms is equal to the sum of several others, such that (for all $n=1,2,\dots$)
a) $a_n \leq 2(\sqrt{3})^n$;
b) $a_n \leq 10(1,5)^n$;

⟦folio 33⟧

d) $a_n \leq 1000n^{7/2}$; e) $a_n \leq 1000n^{3/2}$

S. Konyatsin

F718. On the bank of the river, the flow speed of which is $\omega$, at point $A$ there is a boy. He can be along the shore at a speed of $v$ and swim along the river at a speed of $u$ (relative to the water), and $u < \omega$. Determine at what distance $x$ from point $A$ there is that point $C$ of the shore from where the boy must start swimming in order to get to the buoy $B$ in the shortest time. The distance $BD$ from the buoy to the shore is $h$, the distance $AD$ is $l$ (Fig. 4).

S. Krotov

F719. A small heavy ball of mass $m$, having a charge $q$, is given an initial velocity $v_0$, directed vertically upward. The ball is in a uniform horizontal electric field, the direction of which is $E$. Neglecting air resistance and the dependence of free pressure acceleration $g$ on height, determine the minimum speed of the ball during its movement.

B. Bukhovtsev

F720. A homogeneous beam of mass $M$ of length $l$ begins to move down an inclined plane making an angle $\alpha$ with the horizon. The initial section of length $l$ of the inclined plane is occupied by closely spaced rollers in the form of tubes of mass $m$ and radius $r \ll l$ (Fig. 5), which rotate without friction in the bearings. The rest of the inclined plane is smooth. Find the dependence of the acceleration of the load on its movement along the plane.

A. Stasenko

F721. A dielectric sheet uniformly charged over the surface and shaped like an equilateral right angled triangle was folded in half. In this case, work $A$ was done against the forces of the power plant field. How much work must be done to fold a triangle in half?

O. Savchenko

F722. The Plock capacitor is in a magnetic field that is perpedicular to the plane of the plates (Fig. 6). The distance between the plates is $d$, the magnetic induction vector is $B$. Inside the capacitor, near the negatively charged plate, there is a source of slow electrons that emits electrons in different directions. At what direction between the plates will the electrons be focused onto the positively charged plate? What determines the size of the “spot”?

A. Trubachev
