- Dear Baron! Our first question concerns Vachero’s story “By the Hair.” Should the statement that a person is able to lift himself up by his own hair really be considered your main physical discovery?

Baron: - I think not. The ability to lift yourself by your hair is not the most important thing, not physical at all, and, in fact, not a discovery.
—Are you joking, as usual?
Baron: - Not at all.
— To bring our readers up to date, would you tell us again how this was an accident?
Baron: - With great satisfaction.
This was during the war with the Turks. In general, during this war I had many adventures.
Once, fleeing from the Turks, I tried to jump over a swamp on horseback. But the horse did not jump to the shore, and we immediately ran into the living mud.
They stumbled and began to drown. There was no escape.
The swamp sucked us deeper and deeper with terrible speed. Now the entire body of my horse was hidden in the stinking mud, and now my head began to sink into the swamp, and only the braid of my wig sticks out from there.
What was to be done? We would certainly have perished if not for the amazing strength of my hands. I'm a terrible strong man. Grabbing myself by this pigtail, I held it up with all my might and without much difficulty pulled both myself and my end out of the swamp, which I squeezed tightly with both legs, like thorns.
But let me ask you too. Why do you call this a physical discovery?
- First of all, because it contradicts the known physical law. And then - no one except you continued this.
Baron: - You are wrong. Isn’t this what a cyclist does when he rides onto the sidewalk from the roadway? The moment when

In conclusion, we will consider one interesting theorem of planimetry and try to find a similar theorem of stereometry.

Theorem 5. Through an arbitrary point $O$ taken inside the triangle $ABC$, lines are drawn parallel to its sides. If $S_1, S_2, S_3$ is the area of ​​the triangles formed in this case (Fig. 5), and $S$ is the area of ​​the given triangle, then

$$\sqrt{S} = \sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}.$$

Proof. The formation of a triangle is similar to the triangle $ABC$, therefore

$$\frac{\sqrt{S_1}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|MR|}{|AB|}, \quad \frac{\sqrt{S_2}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|OQ|}{|AB|}, \quad \frac{\sqrt{S_3}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|OP|}{|AB|}.$$

Adding these equalities, we get

$$\frac{\sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|MR| + |OQ| + |OP|}{|AB|} = \frac{|MR| + |BR| + |MA|}{|AB|} = 1,$$

where

$$\sqrt{S} = \sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}.$$
