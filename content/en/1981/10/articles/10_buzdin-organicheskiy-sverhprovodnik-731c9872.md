Organic superconductor

As is known, the phenomenon of superconductivity consists in the complete disappearance of electrical resistance at a temperature below a certain critical $T_k$, called the superconducting transition temperature. Almost all currently existing superconductors are metals or their alloys with $T_k$ not exceeding 25K. In other words, there are currently no materials that are superconductors at temperatures above 25K.

The need to operate at very low temperatures greatly limits the use of superconductors. In this regard, the study of the possibility of creating a superconductor with a high value of $T_k$ is of great interest from both experimental and theoretical points of view. About twenty years ago, reports were made about the possibility of a significant increase in $T_k$ for certain types of organic compounds in which electrons move along individual stacks of organic molecules, and transitions of electrons to neighboring stacks are difficult. However, so far all attempts to obtain an organizational superconductor have not been successful, and only recently a group of Danish researchers succeeded in synthesizing an organizational superconductor for the first time. The resulting compound has a rather complex name - diethragmethyl-tetraselenfort) terraoxylorate and is designated by the first letters of its atoms and radicals - $(\text{TMTSF})_2\text{ClO}_4$. The transition to the superconducting state occurs at normal pressure and temperature $T_k = 1,2 \div 1,4$ K. The temperature of the superconducting transition is limited by the organization of the superconductor, and, as can be seen, is quite low. In compound organizations, replacing one or another radical or introducing other molecules into them leads to the creation of a large number of new compounds with a wide variety of properties. It is this circumstance that determines the practical importance of obtaining organizational superconductors. It can therefore be hoped that targeted research in this area will lead to the synthesis of superconductors with high $T_k$. The importance of such a discovery is simply difficult to overestimate.
