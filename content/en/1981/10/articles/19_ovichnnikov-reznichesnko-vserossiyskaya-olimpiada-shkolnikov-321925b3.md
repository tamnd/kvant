All-Russian
Olympics
SCHOOLCHILDREN

According to established tradition, the All-Russian
Olympids for schoolchildren in mathematics and physics are held in four stages: the first stage - school olympids; the second - districts, urban; third - resursikansky (ASSR), regional, regional; fourth - zonal olympids.

This year, the final, fourth stage of the Olympics took place during the spring school holidays in four cities of the RSFSR: Kuibyshev, Ryazan, Novosibirsk and Ordzhonikidze.

Schoolchildren who took 1st-3rd places were awarded diplomas and memorable prizes, and those who took 4th place were awarded certificates. Original solutions to individual problems were awarded with special prizes. The Ministry of Education of the RSFSR also awarded certificates to the teachers who prepared the winners and prizes of the Olympiad.

Below are the problems in mathematics and physics of the final stage and the names of the prizes of the All-Russian Olympiad.

Mathematics

8th grade

1. Prove that the number $(1+\sqrt{2})^{1981}$ can be represented as $a+b\sqrt{2}$, where $a$ and $b$ are coprime integers.

2. Given a triangle $ABC$. On the extensions of the sides $AB, CB$ and the median $MB$, the points $K, L, N$ are taken as the point $B$, respectively, so that $|BK|=3|AB|$, $|BL|=5|CB|$, $|BN|=4|MB|$.

Find the ratio of the areas of triangles $ABC$ and $KLN$.

3. The sequence $(a_n)$ is given in a recurrent way: $a_1=m$, $a_{n+1}=a_n+2^{a_n}$, $n\geqslant1$, $m$ is a given natural number. Prove that among the terms of this sequence there are infinitely many numbers divisible by 3.

4. The coefficients $a, b, c$ of the equation $x^3+ax^2+bx+c=0$ modulo do not exceed the number 1980. Can this equation have a root greater than 1981?

5. Two regular triangles $ABC$ and $A_1B_1C_1$ (vertices pointing counterclockwise) are located on the plane so that $\vec{AB}_1=2\vec{BA}_1$. Point $D$ is the middle of segment $AB_1$. Find the angles of triangle $CDC_1$.

9th grade

1. Is it possible to write the numbers 1, - 1 and 0 into a table of $n\times n$ cells so that all the sums - in each row and on each of the two diagonals - are different?

2. Two squares $ABCD$ and $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ (the vertices are listed against the parts) are located on the plane such that $\vec{AA}_1=2\vec{BD}_1$. Point $E$ is the middle of segment $AA_1$. Find the angles of triangle $CEC_1$.

3. In a nine-wall primid, the lateral ribs and diagonals of the base are painted: the neoctors are red, the rest are blue. Prove that among these segments you can identify three that form a triangle and are colored the same color*).

4. Two sequences $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ are defined in a recurrent manner: $a_1=a>0$, $b_1=b>0$, $a_{n+1}=\frac{a_n+b_n}{2}$, $b_{n+1}=\sqrt{\frac{a_n^2+b_n^2}{2}}$, $n\geqslant1$. Prove that for any $n$ the following inequality holds:

$$|b_{n+1}-a_{n+1}| \leqslant \frac{|b-a|}{2^n}.$$

5. Find all 200-digit numbers starting with 99 nines that are perfect squares.

10th grade

1. Is it possible to arrange the numbers from 0 to 9 in a circle so that the sum of any three-row numbers does not exceed: a) 14, b) 15?

2. The sequence $(a_n)$ is given in a recurrent manner: $a_1=1$, $a_2=1$, $a_{n+2}=a_{n+1}+\frac{a_n}{2^n}$, $n\geqslant1$. Prove that for all $n$ the inequality $a_n<3$ holds.

3. Find all solutions to the $(x; y; z)$ system of equations:

$$\begin{cases} \cos x+\cos y+\cos z=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}, \\ \sin x+\sin y+\sin z=\frac{3}{2}. \end{cases}$$

Physics

Theoretical tour
8th grade

1. Points A and B are located on the same horizontal line on a mountain slope with a slope α at a distance l from each other (Fig. 1). A car starting from A must reach point B moving along straight AB. Find the minimum time in which this can be done. Friction coefficient between wheels and soil μ > tgα. Consider that the weight is automatically distributed between the wheels and all wheels are driven.

2. On the wedge forming an angle α with the horizontal plane lies a weight A, connected by an inextensible thread to point B on the vertical plane (Fig. 2). The wedge begins to move to the right with constant acceleration $\vec{a}$. What is the trajectory of the weight? How does he move along this trajectory?

3. Using three resistors with resistance R1, R2 and R3, assemble the circuit shown in figure 3. If a voltage U0 = 1.5 V is applied to input 1 - 1′, and an ampere is connected to terminals 2 - 2′, it will show a current strength of I = 60 mA. If you connect a voltmeter to terminals 2 - 2′, it will show voltage U = 1 V. If voltage U0 is applied to terminals 2 - 2′, and the voltmeter is connected to terminals 1 - 1′, it will again show U = 1 V. Determine the resistance values ​​of the resistors. Consider the resistance of the amperator to be very small, and that of the voltmeter to be very large compared to the resistance of the resistors.

4. See problem F718 in Problem Book «Kvant».

9th grade

1. The conveyor (Fig. 4) lifts the sand, which is uniformly loaded onto the belt from the hopper with a feed speed μ (kg/s). The length of the transport is l, the angle of inclination is α, the radius of the drive wheel is R. At what minimum torque does the conveyor move that will still be able to uniformly convey sand upward? What is the speed of the belt? Consider that the initial speed of the sandbags is zero, and the length of their movement along the belt is sufficiently small, so that at the upper edge of the transport the sandbags are motionless relative to the belt.

2. See problem F719 in Problem Book «Kvant».

3. A box containing an ideal monatomic gas is instantly set in motion with a speed of $\vec{v}$. Find the change in gas temperature. Gas atom mass m0. The heat capacity and thermal conductivity of the box can be measured.

4. On the horizontal surface there is a small washer of mass M (Fig. 5).

Another puck of mass m hits it with speed $\vec{v}_0$. As a result of the impact, the washer m stops, and the washer M reaches the height h1 of the overhead plane. In the second experiment, the washers were placed in places: a puck M hits a stationary puck m with a speed of $\vec{v}_0$. Find the height ratio h2/h1, where h2 is the height at which the puck m rises in the second experiment. Central blow; there is no training on the horizontal section and on the inclined plane; washers mass ratio M/m = 3.

Experimental tour

8th grade

1. Determine the density of the stack from which the mayonnaise jar is made.

Equipment: mayonnaise jar, beaker with a volume of $V \approx 250$ mm³, a glass vessel, a strip of soft metal measuring $2 \times 20$ cm, a vessel with water (water tap).

2. Determine the specific heat of vaporization of water. Consider the specific heat capacity of water known.

Equipment: thin-walled metal vessel, electric stove (burner), piece of ice of unknown mass, watch.

9th grade

1. Determine the surface tension coefficient of the soap solution. Estimate measurement errors for different ways of doing work.

Equipment: capillary tube, vessel with water, vessel with soap solution, pressure gauge in the form of a U-shaped tube, bulb for pumping air, glass tee, rod with coupling and foot, ruler.

2. Determine the work that needs to be done to pull back the bow string by 5 cm. Make a bow with a string that is 40 cm long from a ruler of strings. Determine the work required to pull back the string of this bow by 5 cm. Make the same measurements with a string length of 37 cm. Explain your results.

Equipment: two student wooden rulers 40 cm long, holes are drilled at the ends of one of them, a 0.5 m long thread, two Bakushinsky diameter gauges, shatin-wet with a foot, graph paper measuring $20 \times 25$ cm.

10th grade

1. Determine the density of the glass rod.

Equipment: glass rod, large diameter vessel with water, ruler.

2. Plot a graph of the power released at the external resistance versus the value of this resistance. Explain your results.

Equipment: direct current source, two ammeters, voltmeter, rheostat, set of wires, key.
