To determine the average density of the Earth, Cavendish carried out seventeen series of measurements. The average relative density calculated from these measurements

Earth (average density of the Earth) to the density of water turned out to be 5.48.

The scientist's experiments were highly appreciated by his contemporaries. In 1820, the outstanding French mechanic and mathematician Laplace wrote: “Having scrupulously studied Cavendish’s apparatus and all the experiments carried out with the accuracy and insight that are characteristic of this outstanding physicist, I could not discern a single objection to the value he gave for the average density of the Earth, 5.48.”

In ours, the average density of the Earth is taken to be 5.517 g/cm³. Note that the modernized large Cavendish balances are still used for physical measurements today.
