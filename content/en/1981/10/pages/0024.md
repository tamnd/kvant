In the last works of the scientist, some of his experiments were carried out anew. It turned out that in the vast majority of cases, Cavendish's results are very accurate.

Interest in the scientist’s scientific heritage did not wane. In 1927, at the initiative of the University of Cambridge, a new edition of Cavendish's work was proposed, which included previously unpublished works. And once again the scientific world was delighted: long-term (1782-1809) changes in the characteristics of the Earth’s magnetic field placed invaluable material about the “magnetic history” of the Earth in the hands of geophysicists. It turned out that Cavendish expressed the idea of ​​conservation of energy and considered the quantity corresponding to potential energy. And once again, it was agreed that Cavendish's scientific results remained unknown for more than a hundred years. One has to regret not only the oblivion of the results, but also the fact that his contemporary was not aware of the problems that interested Cavendish. They often constituted an entire scientific program developed during the 19th century. An example can be a list of problems in the field of magnetism: the study of permanent and “changing” magnets; the influence of the body on the magnetic properties of the body; dependence of the “strength” of magnets on the cross-section; control bending of magnetized needles in the Earth's magnetic field; errors in the study of terrestrial magnetism caused by this bending.

So, admiration and bewilderment are the feelings that arise when getting acquainted with the scientific work of Henry Cavendish. And, despite the fact that many of his results were obtained anew by other scientists who are rightfully considered the authors of the discoveries, the value of Cavendish’s works is enduring. And, above all, it is in the scientific depth with which Cavendish thinks through his experiments, and in the scrupulous care with which he carried them out. It is these requirements that are fundamental in modern experimental physics.

Experimental determination of the law of electric force

The best way to get acquainted with the work of an outstanding scientist is by reading his works. Celebrating Cavendish's anniversary, we are publishing an abridged translation of his work devoted to establishing the dependence of the forces of electrical attraction and repulsion on distance. Cavendish's work on electrical problems appears to have begun in 1771. The results he obtained represent a very complete and, most importantly, accurate solution to the problem posed. In his research, the scientist identified Coulomb for more than a decade, who published the results of his experiments in 1785. It is interesting that Cavendish was able to answer the question posed, despite the fact that he held misconceptions about the nature of electricity. He believed that there was a certain electrical fluid, the excess of which manifests itself in positive electrification of the body, and the deficiency - in negative.
