In conclusion, we will consider one interesting theorem of planimetry and try to find a similar theorem of stereometry.

Theorem 5. Through an arbitrary point $O$ taken inside the triangle $ABC$, lines are drawn parallel to its sides. If $S_1, S_2, S_3$ is the area of ​​the triangles formed in this case (Fig. 5), and $S$ is the area of ​​the given triangle, then

$$\sqrt{S} = \sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}.$$

Proof. The formation of a triangle is similar to the triangle $ABC$, therefore

$$\frac{\sqrt{S_1}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|MR|}{|AB|}, \quad \frac{\sqrt{S_2}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|OQ|}{|AB|}, \quad \frac{\sqrt{S_3}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|OP|}{|AB|}.$$

Adding these equalities, we get

$$\frac{\sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{|MR| + |OQ| + |OP|}{|AB|} = \frac{|MR| + |BR| + |MA|}{|AB|} = 1,$$

where

$$\sqrt{S} = \sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2} + \sqrt{S_3}.$$

Let us now formulate a similar theorem in space.

Theorem 6. Through an arbitrary point taken inside the triangle, four planes parallel to the grammetry are drawn. If $V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4$ is the volume of the triangles formed in this case, and $V$ is the volume of the given triangle, then

$$\sqrt[3]{V} = \sqrt[3]{V_1} + \sqrt[3]{V_2} + \sqrt[3]{V_3} + \sqrt[3]{V_4}.$$

To prove this theorem, which we provide to the reader, one should take advantage of the fact that the tetrahedrons discussed in the conditions are similar to the original one, as well as the fact that the volume of such polyhedra is the cube of the lengths of their edges.
