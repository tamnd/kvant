The structure of a real quantum magnetometer is actually much more complicated. Thus, it usually uses not one, but several weak connections connected in parallel - the peculiar interference of superconducting currents leads to an increase in the accuracy of measurements (which is why the devices are also called interference devices). The sensitivity element of the device is inductively coupled to the coil of the oscillating circuit, where flux surges are converted into voltage pulses, which are then damped. But talking about all these subtleties is beyond the scope of this article.

Note that today ultrasensitive magnetometers that measure magnetic fields with an accuracy of $10^{-15}$ T are already industrial products that are widely used in measuring technology. For example, that day is not far away

In this case, the rate of change of current $\Delta I/\Delta t$ must be such that the self-induced emf $\mathcal{E}_{is} = -L\Delta I/\Delta t$ compensates for the external emf $\mathcal{E}$ (the resistance of the superconductor, and therefore the voltage drop across it, is zero):

$$\mathcal{E}_{is} = -\mathcal{E}.$$

When the charge $\Delta Q = I\Delta t$ passes through the circuit, the source does work

$$\Delta A = \mathcal{E} \Delta Q = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t} I \Delta t = L I \Delta I.$$

The product $LI$ is the magnetic flux $\Phi$ through the area of ​​the bulb. In order to more clearly present the result obtained, for example, a graph of the dependence of $\Phi$ on $I$ (Fig. 7). As can be seen from figure, the elementary work $\Delta A$ is equal to the area of ​​the protected rectangle. The total work $A$ when the current changes from 0 to $I_0$ is equal to the sum of the areas of rectangles with heights equal to the corresponding value of the magnetic flux, that is, the area of ​​a triangle with sides $I_0$ and $LI_0$:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} (LI_0) I_0 = \frac{1}{2} LI_0^2.$$

This work is exactly equal to the current energy in the ring. Of course, the resulting expression does not depend on whether the current flow is superconducting or normal.
