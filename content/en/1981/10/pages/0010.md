Let's first discuss the case where metal films are in a normal (non-superconducting) state. Can electrons from one metal film (Fig. 3,a) move to another? It would seem not - the dielectric layer interferes with them. In order to leave the metal, an electron must have an energy greater than the work function, and at low temperatures there are practically no electrons with such energy. At figure 3.6 there is a graph of the dependence of the electron potential energy W on the x coordinate (the X axis is perpendicular to the sandwich plane). In a metal, an electron moves freely and its potential energy is zero. To enter the dielectric, it is necessary to perform the work function W_B, which is greater than the kinetic, and therefore total, energy of the electron W_9*). Therefore, they say that electrons in metal films share a potential barrier, the height of which is W_B-W_9.

If electrons obeyed the laws of classical mechanics, then such a barrier would be insurmountable for them. But electrons are microparticles, and in the microworld there are special laws that allow much that is inaccessible to large bodies. For example, a person with such energy cannot climb a hill, but an electron can pass through it! It is as if he is growing a tunnel under the hill and pierces, even if his energy is not enough to climb the hill. Of course, all this should not be taken literally. The tunnel effect (as this phenomenon is called) is explained by the wave properties of microparticles, and you can truly understand it only by studying quantum mechanics. But the fact remains: with some certainty, electrons can penetrate the dielectric from one metal film to another. This confidence is greater, the smaller the height W_B-W_9 of the barer and the smaller its width a.

So, a thin film of dielectric turns out to be permeable to electrons - the so-called tunnel current can flow through it. However, the total tunnel current is zero: how many electrons move from the lower metal film to the upper one, and how many, on average, move, on the contrary, from the upper film to the lower one.

How can we make the tunnel current different from zero? To do this, it is necessary to break the symmetry, for example, to connect metal films to a current source with voltage U (Fig. 4,a). Then the films will play the role of capacitor plates, and in the case of a dielectric, an electric field with intense E=U/a will arise. The work done to move a charge e a distance x along the direction of the field is A=Fx=eEx=eUx/a, and therefore the graph of the electron's potential energy takes on the form shown in figure 4.6.
