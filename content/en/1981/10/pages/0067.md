CHESS COMPETITION

Until now, chess competition «Kvant» has offered combinations of outstanding grandmasters. This time, the authors of the problems are the magazine’s readers—mostly, of course, schoolchildren.

G. Zhukov. Checkmate in 3 moves.
The author of the problem graduated from school this year, but he sent his problem last year, when he was in ninth grade (Gena lives in the city of Shumerle). The position is somewhat rough (too many pieces fell on the poor black king), but the introduction to the three-move Libra is spectacular: 1.Kd5! (preventing the enemy king from escaping through the f4 square)
1...Kp:d5 2.Jlh4! d3 (2...e4 3.Jlh5×) 3.b4× - this is where the bishop on a2, lurking in the back, came in handy. 1...d3 2.Jlh4+Kp:d5 3.b4× doesn’t change things either.

A. Bekoryukov. Checkmate in 3 moves.
Muscovite Alyosha Bekoryukov, the youngest author of the proposed positions, is only 10 years old. Solves 1.Jlh2!! gh+2Kpf1! (first White got rid of his rook, and now invites the pawn to queen) 2...h1Q+ 3.Q:h1×; 1...g2 2.Jlh1! ghФ+ 3.Ф:h1×.

V. Leonov. Checkmate in 2 moves.
It is not difficult to prove that Black's last move could only be b7-b5, so the solution is 1.a5:b6 (on pass) a3 2.b7×.

This task for a 7th grade student at Moscow School 370 is, as you can see, reproanalytic (requires study of the previous game). It is said that he is a student of the international, master of chess composition E. Poghosyants.

Oleg Molozhavtsev, 14 years old, came up with a whole series of red problems, of which two were chosen. The secret of one is revealed, and the other you are asked to solve for yourself.

O. Molozhavtsev. Checkmate in 3 moves.
1.Qf6! Q:f6 2.Ch2+ Qe5 3.C:e5×; 1...Kd4 2.Q:d4+ Qd5 3.Ch2×; 1...Kpd5 2.C:e6+! Ke4 3.Kc5×.

It remains to make a replacement regarding two more competition problems. The three-move problem belongs to S. Pelagimov, a student at the Moscow Technical Instruments Institute, and the cooperative mat problem belongs to mathematics teacher S. Abrahamyan.

The deadline for sending the decision is November 30, 1981 (with a note on the envelope “Chess Competition No. 10 - 81”).

1. V. Leonov. White starts and gives mat in 6 moves.

2. O. Molozhavtsev. White starts and gives mat in 4 moves.

3. S. Pelagimov. White starts and gives mat in 3 moves.

4. S. Abrahamyan. Black starts and gets a cooperative mat in 2 moves.
