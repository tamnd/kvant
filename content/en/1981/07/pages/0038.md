Goat - pose - sex - regiment - wolf

The author of the chain game is apparently Lewis Carroll. Carroll's uses - did you understand them from the examples given? - you can turn night into day, a Witt into a bolt or a Kurd into a Persian, you can make a night out of a hand (or a hand out of a nose - as you prefer), make a pound from the last penny (and even from an exotic yuan) or from a single vessel (be it a large, fast-moving breeze or a small yawl) get a whole fleet...

True, true! Check it out for yourself.

You can even - although this is more difficult - make a molehill out of a molehill.

Why more difficult? Due to the different placement of the oculars in these words. You will encounter the same difficulties if you want to turn a Neer into an Arab or obtain uranium from sulfur.

Elephant, Arab and Uranus belong to one group of words, muxa, neer and sulfur to another: in each of these two groups the words turn into each other relatively easily, and from one group to another, as far as I know, there is only one transition. The last three of the problems proposed above come down to finding it.

Once he finds it, playing “Carroll's chains” becomes, frankly speaking, not particularly interesting.

New rules

To breathe life into the ancient game, we are introducing new rules for converting words. While still changing one letter at a time, we will also allow you to change the order of the letters on each move:

(k - m) (a - o)
