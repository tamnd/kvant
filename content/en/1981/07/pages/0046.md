⟦folio 44⟧

B. Kordemsky

Seventeen
tasks
on your wits

Among the tasks offered by the manuals for applicants to university, there are also those that can be solved with resourcefulness and ingenuity.

Of course, as a rule, these problems can be solved using standard methods, but solutions using non-standard, island communications often turn out to be shorter and more straightforward than traditional ones.

We offer several such tasks to our readers.

Problem 1. Having walked 3/8 of the length of place $AB$, a person heard behind him the horn of a machine gun approaching the place at a constant speed of 60 km/h. If this person runs back, he will meet the machine gun at $A$; if he runs forward, the automatic will catch up with him in $B$. How fast is this person running?

Problem 2. From point $A$ of the river, the ball was simultaneously floated along the current and against the current. After 10 minutes, the swimmer turned back and caught the ball under a place located 1 km from $A$. It is known that the swimmer did not change his conditions throughout the entire movement. Find the speed of this river.

Problem 3. There is a salt solution in the flask. A 1/n part of the solution is poured from the flask into a test tube and evaporated until the percentage of salt in the test tube doubles. After this, the resulting solution is returned to the flask and mixed with the solution that remained there. As a result, the salt content in the solution increased by p%. Determine the percentage of salt in the original solution.

Problem 4. 27 identical mechanisms can perform a certain task in 35 hours of continuous operation. But after 11 hours, several more such mechanisms were connected to this task, and the work was completed 6 hours earlier. How many additional mechanisms were connected?

Problem 5. A piece of equal mass was cut from two pieces of an alloy of the same mass, but with different percentages of copper. Each of the cut pieces was fused with the remainder of the other piece, after which the percentage of copper in the total pieces became the same. How many times is the cut piece smaller than the whole piece?

Problem 6. There are two alloys of silver and gold: in one quantity these metals are in the ratio 2:3, in the other in the ratio 3:7. How much of each alloy must be taken to obtain 8 kg of a new alloy in which silver and gold would be in a ratio of 5:11?

Problem 7. An arbitrary point $E$ is taken on the side $BC$ of the square $ABCD$. The bissertis of the angle $DAE$ intersects the side $CD$ at the point $F$, $|AE| = a$. Find $|BE| + |DF|$.

Problem 8. Given $\triangle ABC$. a) Prove that there exists a triangle $A_1B_1C_r$, the side lengths of which are equal to the lengths of the medians of the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$, similar to the triangle $ABC$.

6) Prove that triangle $A_2B_2C_2$, the lengths of which sides are equal to the lengths of the medians of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$, is similar to triangle $ABC$.

c) Find the area of ​​triangle $A_2B_2C_2$ if the area of ​​triangle $ABC$ is $S$.

Problem 9. Let the point $K$ be the midpoint of the median $AM$ of the triangle $ABC$, $L$ the intersection point of the line $(BK)$ with the sides $AC$. Find the area of ​​the quadrilateral $LKMC$ if $S_{ABC} = 1$.
