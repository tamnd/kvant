⟦folio 18⟧

"head" does not play any role. In the lower part of the “tulvish” there is a massive body in the form of a spherical segment with a height of $h$. The segment is limited by part of the surface of the “tulvish” and a plane perpendicular to the axis passing through the centers of the sphere and the point of their contact.

If you try to put the doll on a horizontal surface and imagine it to itself, it immediately gets up. Why? It is obvious that the vertical position of Vanka-Vstanka is a position of stable equilibrium. There is a rule in mechanics: in a state of stable equilibrium, the center of gravity of a body should be in the lowest possible position for it. This means that the value of potential energy caused by gravity must be the smallest possible.

Let's find out under what conditions the potential energy of Vanka-Vstanka will be minimal when he occupies a vertical position. Let's move the doll out of its equilibrium position by deflecting its axis by an angle $\alpha$ from the vertical. Let $h_c$ be the height of the center of gravity of a massive body when the axis is vertical, $M$ its mass and $m$ the mass of the “head”. The mass of the tulvish shell does not matter, since the height of the center of gravity of the shell, and therefore its potential energy, does not change when Vanka-Vstanka is tilted.

As can be seen from figure, the height of the center of gravity of a massive body when the axis is tilted is $h_c = |CD| + |BL| = h_c \cos \alpha + R - R \cos \alpha$, and the height of the center of gravity of the head is $h_2 = |LO| + |KO_1| = R + (R + r) \cos \alpha$.

The total potential energy of the massive body and the "head" is $E_\pi = Mgh_1 + mgh_2 = (M + m)gR + (m(R + r) - M(R - h_c))g \cos \alpha$.

It follows that the potential energy of Vanka-Vstanka with a vertical position of the axis, that is, at $\alpha = 0$, will be minimal if the expression by which $g \cos \alpha$ is multiplied is negative:

$$m(R + r) - M(R - h_c) < 0,$$

or

$$m < M \frac{R - h_c}{R + r}.$$

Remaining, the rules, the unknown quantity $h_c$, but it can be expressed through the known quantities $R$ and $h$ (this is accepted without proof):

$$h_c = h \frac{8R - 3h}{12R - 4h}.$$

Thus, we have found an exact mathematical condition showing to what extent Vanka-Vstanka must be “light-headed” in order for him to assume a vertical position as soon as he is left to his own devices.
