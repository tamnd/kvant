CREATORS OF NON-EUCLIDIAN GEOMETRY

In the photo you see 4 stamps with portraits of outstanding mathematicians. These are Carl Friedrich Gauss (1777-1855), Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky (1792-1856) and Janos Bolyai (1802-1860). What unites them is that all three, independently of each other, were engaged in the development of non-Euclidean geometry (see the article “Creator of the New Geometry” on page 7).

Gauss did not publish a single work on non-Euclidean geometry. However, after his death, materials related to non-Euclidean geometry were found in his diaries, which revealed that he had come to the idea of ​​​​the possibility of constructing non-Euclidean geometry. However, he feared that these ideas would not be understood, and not only did he not publish them, but he also forbade others familiar with his work from publishing anything on these issues. He wrote: “It is even possible that I will not dare to do this all my life, because I am afraid of the cry of the Bettians, which will rise when I express my views in full.”

Further, after familiarizing himself with the works of Bolyai and Lobachevsky, Gauss did not express his attitude towards them, although he was the initiator of the election of Lobachevsky as a corresponding member of the Göttingen Scientific Society.

Nibollier fully developed the non-Euclidean geometry of Lobachevsky. He published the first works on non-Euclidean geometry three years before Bolyai (in 1829), and in addition to the physics and mathematics department of Kazan University even earlier (1826).

By 1825, Bolyai had already arrived at the basic principles of non-Euclidean geometry. However, it took him six years to bring these results to completion.

Bolyai published his research in 1832 in the appendix “Appendix” to the 1st volume of the works of his father, a professor of mathematics.

A stamp with a portrait of Gaus was issued in 1955 in Germany on the 100th anniversary of his death.

Lobachevsky has 2 Soviet stamps associated with him. The first was published in 1951 in the series “Scientists of our Motherland”, and the second in 1956 on the 100th anniversary of the death of the great mathematician.

A stamp with a portrait of Bolyai was published in the series “World Cultural Figure”, published in the Romanian People's Republic in 1960.
