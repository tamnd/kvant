Problem 5. At the bottom of the reservoir there is a U-shaped structure of three identical beams connected to each other (Fig. 9). How does the force of pressure of this structure on the bottom depend on the water level in the reservoir? There are two distances: 1) we sweep water under the supports; 2) the supports are in close contact with the bottom. The beams have a square section with side square a; beam length l=2a. The density of the material of the beams is ρ₀, the density of water is ρ.

The pressure force on the bottom N is determined by the difference between the weight of the structure P=6ρ₀ga³ and the buoyancy force F. In the first case, when water flows under the supports (for example, if the bottom of the reservoir is covered with pebbles - figure 9, a), Archimedes' law is valid. The dependence of the buoyancy force on the height of the water level h is given by the formulas: F=2ρgha² at h≤a, F=2ρga³+4ρga²(h-a) at a≤h≤2a, F=6ρga³ at h≥2a. The corresponding graph for strength N is shown at figure 9, in (it is designated by the number 1).

In the second case, there is no water pressure on the supports (Fig. 9, b), and it is no longer possible to use Archimedes’ law. To determine the force F, it is necessary to find the equivalent of the pressure forces: F=0 for h≤a, F=2ρga²(h-a) for a≤h≤2a, F=2ρga²(h-a)-4ρga²(h-2a) for h≥2a. The last expression vanishes at h=3 a and becomes negative at larger h. This means that when h>3 a the pressure force does not push the structure out of the water, but, on the contrary, presses it to the bottom. The dependence of the pressure force N on the bottom on the height of the water level h is shown in the second graph figure 9, c.
