An exponential function with base $e$ is called an exponent. It has a special designation: Expr $t$. The graph of the exponential $y=e^t$ is depicted in figure 2. Its tangent at point $t=0$ is superimposed on the axis $Ot$ at an angle $45^\circ$, so it is given by the equation $y=1+t$.

It can be shown that all points of the exponential lie above the tangent, so that for all values ​​of $t$ the inequality $e^t>1+t$ holds. From this inequality we can obtain estimates for the number $e$. To do this, note that for $t=\frac{1}{n}: \frac{1}{n}>1+\frac{1}{n}$; from here, raising to the power $n$, we get

$$e > \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^n.$$

For $t=-\frac{1}{n+1}: e^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}>1-\frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n}{n+1}$, whence $\frac{1}{n+1}<\frac{n+1}{n}=1+\frac{1}{n}$, and raising to the power $n+1$, we get

$$e < \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n+1}.$$

Since at the base $a=e$ by definition $tg\varphi=1$, then from formula (3) the approximation equality follows (for $t=0$ and small $\Delta t$)

$$\Delta e^t = e^{\Delta t} - e^0 = e^{\Delta t} - 1 \approx \Delta t.$$ (4)

Now let's introduce the proportionality coefficient. To do this, let us substitute $k \cdot \Delta t$ together with $\Delta t: e^{k \cdot \Delta t} - 1 \approx k \cdot \Delta t$ into formula (4) (for small $\Delta t$).

This means that for the function $e^{kt}$ the increment $\Delta y$ (for any $t$) has the form

$$\Delta y = e^{k(t+\Delta t)} - e^{kt} = e^{kt} \left(e^{k \cdot \Delta t} - 1\right) \approx ky \cdot \Delta t.$$

Thus, the function $y=e^{kt}$ satisfies conditions (1). It is obvious that the function $y=Ce^{kt}$ for any constant factor $C$ satisfies the same condition, since its increment is equal to

$$\Delta y = Ce^{k(t+\Delta t)} - Ce^{kt} = Ce^{kt} \left(e^{k \cdot \Delta t} - 1\right) \approx ky \cdot \Delta t.$$

In specific examples, we can tellе $C$ allows us to take into account the so-called initial condition - the value of the quantity $y$ at the initial moment of time $t=0$. For $t=0$ we get $y(0) = Ce^0 = C$. The final process described can be written as follows:

$$y(t)=y(0)e^{kt}.$$ (5)

We started with exponential functions, but any exponential function can be expressed in terms of an exponent using the equality

$$a^t = e^{kt},$$

where $k=\log_e a$. The logarithm to base $e$ is called the natural logarithm and is denoted by the symbol $\ln a$, that is, $\ln a=\log_e a$. The choice of exponents together with another exponential function is explained only by a simple consideration of the proportionality coefficient $k$.

Natural logarithms are related to decimals by

$$\ln a = \frac{1}{\lg e} \cdot \lg a,$$

which is obtained by taking the base 10 logarithm of the identity $e^{\ln a}=a$ and moving $\lg e$ to the right side.
