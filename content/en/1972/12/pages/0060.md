The part of the surface of the upper face of the cube, “enclosed” in a tube, is not affected by water pressure: $F = \rho g a^3 + \rho g h S$, where $S = \pi d^2/4$ is the cross-sectional area of ​​the tube. Thus, the gravity of the bob is $P = F - P_0 = (\rho - \rho_0) g a^3 + \pi g h d^2/4 \approx 12 \text{ h}$. Weight of the weight $m = P/g \approx 1,2 \text{ kg}$.

The buoyant force acting on the cube can be found in another way. Let us consider the cube with the tube as a single body displacing a volume of water $V = a^3 + Sh$. Then, according to Archimedes' law, the cube with the tube is acted upon by a buoyant force $F = \rho g V = \rho g a^3 + \rho g h S$, which is equal to the buoyant force acting on the cube, since the equilibrium force of water pressure on the tube is zero.

Problem 7. A small cube of density $\rho_0 = 7,8 \cdot 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$ floats on the boundary of mercury with water (Fig. 11). Determine what part of its volume is in mercury. Density of mercury $\rho_1 = 1,36 \cdot 10^4 \text{ kg/m}^3$, density of water $\rho_2 = 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3$.

The pressure on the lower face of the cube $p_2$ is greater than the pressure on its upper face $p_1$ by the magnitude of the force of gravity acting on a column of water with a height of $x$ and a column of water with a height of $a - x$, having a unit cross-section: $p_2 = p_1 + \rho_1 g x + \rho_2 g (a - x)$, where $x$ is the depth of immersion of the cube in the pt. Therefore the buoyant force is $F = (p_2 - p_1) S = [\rho_1 g x + \rho_2 g (a - x)] a^2$. From this formula it is clear that the buoyant force, as one would expect, is equal to the sum of the gravitational forces displacing the liquid. For a body of arbitrary shape, this statement can be proven using the principle of branching.

The cube will float if the buoyant force is equal to the force of gravity $P = \rho_0 g a^3$ acting on it. From the condition of equality of forces $F$ and $P$ we find: $\frac{x}{a} = \frac{\rho_0 - \rho_2}{\rho_1 - \rho_2} \approx 0,54$. Thus, 0.54 of the volume of the cube is in ptru.

Let us now study the equilibrium of a liquid in a vessel moving under acceleration. According to Newton’s II law, in this case, the vector sum of all forces acting on any selected element of the fluid should be equal to ma, where m is the mass of the selected fluid, a is the acceleration of the vessel. But the selected element of the liquid is acted upon by forces and pressure forces from the surrounding liquid. Their equivocal and must be equal to ma.

Problem 8. A vessel with a liquid of density $\rho$ is accelerated a. Determine the pressure of the liquid on the lubbine h and the force of pressure on the bottom of the vessel. The height of the water level in the vessel is H, the area of ​​the bottom of the vessel is S.

Let us select a column of liquid of height h with base area s (Fig. 12). It is acted upon by the forces of gravity $P = \rho g h$ and the pressure force ps directed upward. Equivalent to these using the acceleration of the column. According to Newton's II law: ma = $\rho g h$ -.ps, where m = $\rho h$ is the mass of the column. For pressure p at depth h, this gives p = $\rho(g - a)h$. The pressure force on the bottom of the vessel F = $\rho(g - a)HS$ will be less, the greater the acceleration of the vessel a. At a = g (free fall), the pressure force of the liquid becomes zero - a compound of weightlessness occurs. When a > g, the liquid will fall freely with acceleration g, and the vessel will fall with greater acceleration, and water will flow out of the vessel.
