⟦folio 11⟧

But the devastating review of Nikolai Ivanovich Fuss did not frighten Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky. He continues to develop his ideas and in subsequent years he takes a step that is brilliant in its simplicity: he abandons the fifth approach, which had dominated the minds of almost two thousand years and did not raise any doubts among either his predecessors or his contemporaries.

Lobachevsky assumed that through a given point outside a line one can draw at least two lines in their plane that do not intersect with the given line $AB$.

From this assumption, which was completely stupid at first glance, Lobachevsky began to draw further conclusions. And I didn’t come to any contradiction! Moreover, he received a harmonious, logical geometric system, a complete whole, which he called “imaginary geometry” - by analogy with the minimic ones, which he also called imaginary; if the minimum numbers are the most general, for which the laws of general arithmetic are valid, then “imaginary geometry” is the most general geometric system in which all Euclid’s axioms are satisfied, except for the axiom of parallels. And just as the real numbers are a special case of the set of complex numbers, so the traditional geometry of Euclid is a special case of the general geometric system.

“Imaginary geometry” differs significantly from the conventional geometry of Euclid.

Let's start with the fact that the sum of the angles of a triangle in it is always less than 180°; that the set of all points of the plane lying in one half-plane and equidistant from a line given in it - the “base” - is not a straight line at all, but a special line called an equidistantor; that through three points that do not lie on a straight line, one can draw either a circle, or an equidistant line, or an intermediate line between them - a “horocycle”.
