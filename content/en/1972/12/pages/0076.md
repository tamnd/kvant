5. Let the initial speed of the balls be $v$. During an inelastic impact, the velocity component of the ball perpendicular to the plane disappears, while the velocity component parallel to the plane remains. Therefore, the inelastic ball will fly off from the inclined plane at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizon (Fig. 3, b), at $\alpha > \frac{\pi}{4}$ it will fly back at an angle $\pi-2\alpha$ (Fig. 3, c).
In the first case, he will fly the distance
$$S_1 = \frac{2v^2 \cos^2 \alpha \cdot \cos \alpha \cdot \sin \alpha}{g}.$$
With an elastic impact, two cases are possible: at $\alpha < \frac{\pi}{4}$ the ball will fly forward at an angle $2\alpha$ to the horizon (Fig. 3, b), at $\alpha > \frac{\pi}{4}$ it will fly back at an angle $\pi-2\alpha$ (Fig. 3, c).
In the first case, he will fly the distance
$$S_2' = \frac{2v^2 \cos 2\alpha \sin 2\alpha}{g},$$
in the second:
$$S_2'' = \frac{2v^2 \cos (\pi-2\alpha) \sin (\pi-2\alpha)}{g} = -\frac{2v^2 \cos 2\alpha \sin 2\alpha}{g}.$$
From condition $S_1 = S_2'$ we obtain:
$$\alpha_1 = \arccos \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}};$$
In the second case ($S_1 = S_2'$):
$$\alpha_2 = \arccos \sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}.$$
