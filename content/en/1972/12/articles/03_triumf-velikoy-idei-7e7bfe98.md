TRIUMPH OF THE GREAT IDEA

Most citizens of our great Motherland, especially young people, have been accustomed since childhood to the fact that they were born and live in the Soviet Union.

Occasionally we pronounce the full name of our country, born 50 years ago: the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics. We do not always think about the deep meaning of this name and the greatness of the very idea of ​​​​forming this Union. Meanwhile, the idea of ​​​​forming the Soviet Union is a great idea.

The author of this idea is Vladimir Ilyich Lenin.

Readers «Kvant» know that in the field of physical and mathematical sciences, great ideas are those that transform the foundations of science (for example, quantum theory, the theory of relativity). What can we say about the scientific idea that was essential to transform the world?

Its existence led to a fundamental solution to one of the world's most pressing problems, which is called the national question.

For many centuries the world has been divided, and the capitalist world and the Pong are divided by nationalist contradictions. A scientific analysis of this problem, carried out by V.I. Lenin on the basis of world experience of social development and the experience of the Great October Socialist Revolution, showed that there are deep fundamental principles for hostility between individual national netniks.

National discord is the product of a reactionary social system, be it an equal division, federal or capitalist system.

The capitalist system serves as a breeding ground for the development of ultra-reactionary “theories” of national or racial exceptionalism. It was these “theories” that were adopted by German fascism.

Lenin's theory of the national question, as opposed to Bourgeois-nationalist ideology, proceeds from the fact that there is no national or racial exclusivity. The great physicist of the 20th century, creator of the theory of the structure of atoma, Niels Bohr, was deeply interested in the absence of national exclusivity with the following example. If, N. Bohr argued, a Russian child at the breast influences permanent residence in France, then, having grown up there, he will not differ in his spiritual appearance, habits and behavior from a native Frenchman.

In the same way, a French child raised in Russia will not differ from a native Russian.

Nationality has nothing to do with it - everything is determined by the social environment in which a person is raised.

Lenin resolutely defended the idea of ​​equal distribution, showing at the same time that the implementation of this kind of equal approach in a socialist society.

The formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was a practical matter of this idea.

The popular experience of the existence and development of the Soviet Union is a triumph of Lenin’s teaching on the national question.

One of the most striking illustrations of the successful solution of the national question can be the development of science in the resource areas of the Soviet Union.

In place of the backward federal and received outskirts of Tsarist Russia, where the overwhelming majority of the population was illiterate, numerous cities grew up and became major scientific centers. All allied resource centers now have scientific centers. Many scientific institutes have nuclear reactors for resource use. One of the world's best atomic reactors designed for physical research was built in the Georgian SSR. One of the best electron accelerators in the world is located in Armenia.

Research important for science and the national economy is being carried out in physical institutes of Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan.

All allied resource agencies have resource academies of sciences, uniting major scientists.

A number of allied resource forces have created world slavery for themselves. Many international unions have learned
head the Soetsk scientists. According to Nyantono, all this led to the fact that Soviet science in general had a huge influence on the development of science throughout the world.

Evidence of this is, for example, the fact that more Soviet scientific journals are translated into foreign languages.

Readers «Kvant» know that all-Union optimization of schoolchildren is taking place in the cities of a number of allied resource centers. Parts of these optimizations were carried out on our own experience regarding our products of different nationalities.

We are accustomed to such situations, and we take it all for granted.

But we must remember that the path to this “should” was not easy and simple. On this path, the Soviet people and the Communist Party had to offer considerable difficulties associated with economic and cultural stability, to fight against the attempt of the counter-revolution to use the legacy of former national enmity for their own purposes. And in this struggle, the Soviet people emerged victorious.

Now the Soviet experience of creating a multinational social state has received scientific recognition and serves as an example for fighters for socialism and national liberation around the world.

That is why the 50th anniversary of the Soviet Union will be inflated not only by the people of our country, but also by all progressive humanity of the world.

“Each of them made a revolution in scientific ideas,” said the English scientist Vilym Clifford about Copernicus and Lobachevsky, “and both of these revolutions have the same meaning.” These are revolutions in our understanding of space.

Non-Euclidean geometry, discovered by Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky, dealt the first blow to the age-old ideas of the inviolability of geometric laws formulated by Euclid. Then came a new blow: Michelson's famous experiment in determining the speed of light. And finally, Einstein’s relative theory finally destroyed scientists’ ideas about the absolutism of space. There is no absolute space that is the same for everyone, Einstein said, just as there is no absolute time that is the same for everyone.

And just as the Copernican solar system announced the riddle of the creation of the world, which tormented astronomers, so did Lobachevsky’s geometry of solving problems, over which geometers were at their best.

“The problem that Lobachevsky solved was a problem that was put on the agenda by mathematicians and physicists of that time,” said Professor A.V. Vasiliev, a representative of the Copernican Physics and Mathematics Society. “To complete this task, we needed the generality of Gauss and Lobachevsky, we needed the authenticity and hard work of our famous compatriot. It has always remained a matter of high patriotic pride for us that this problem was solved by a scientist who lived in Kazan, far from the centers of intellectual life.

To the 180th anniversary of the birth of N. I. Lobachevsky

On December 1, 1792, a son was born to a minor Nizhny Novgorod official, Ivan Maksimovich Lobachevsky, “who was named Nicholas at holy baptism.” Ten years later, Nikolai, together with his brothers Alexander and Alexei, was enrolled in the Kazan gymnasium for government raznochinsky maintenance.

Already in the gymnasium, Nikolai’s extraordinary abilities for science in general, mathematics and physics in particular, were evident. The young teacher G.I. Kartashevsky played a significant role in the development of Lobachevsky’s talent.

Since 1807, Nikolai has been a student at Kazan University. The profession is surprising, the ease with which he perceives science, their order, the abilities of a fifteen-year-old boy, his original thinking. Nikolai becomes “the pride of the university.” But the boy remains a boy: either he launches rockets, or rides through the city garden on a cow, or, on a dare, burps over the overweight and shortness of breath Professor Nikolysky. It "shows signs of burnlessness." This server presentation is discussed at the university council, the Volvonovets gather separately, Professors Littrov and Bartels remove the talented young man from the soldier's overcoat.

In 1811, after brilliantly graduating from the university, Lobachevsky became a master *), from 1814 he was an adjunct *), and from 1816 he was a professor.

*) Master, adjunct - junior academic degrees at Russian universities (until 1863).

In November 1820 he was elected dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics, and in 1827 - rector of the university. Lobachevsky remained in this post for almost 20 years.

For many years, Lobachevsky was in charge of the university library, selecting and purchasing educational and scientific literature. Since 1822 he was a member, and since 1825 - chairman of the building committee. Not content with inviting the best architects of the time, Lobachevsky himself seriously studied architecture. Under his leadership, many educational buildings, a library, an astronomical observatory, a clinic, and a printing house were built and reconstructed.

In difficult times, Lobachevsky came to the leadership of the university. Many professors have taken the position of a resurrected co-worker of the world.

- ... From holy scripture it is revealed that the light of the sun is a reflection of the face of God. But how it spreads, through excitement or in any other way, Holy Scripture does not clearly say. It is more consistent with Holy Scripture to think that light is an orran, through which one living in an inaccessible world sends his spirit into the sublunary world... - written in the notebook of one of the professors.

Lectures were given to students using this notebook!

“The hypotenuse, by God’s mill,” Professor Nikolsky began his lectures on geometry. That’s how the students pronounced it: “Hypotenuse of the Divine Millia.”

The recent trustee of the educational district, the obscurantist M. L. Magnitsky, who tried (but, fortunately, failed) to achieve the closure of the university, drew up a special instruction obliging his position to deal with any “sedition”; 9 leading professors were expelled.

Lobachevsky believed that schools and universities should give young people knowledge, certain moral principles, and make them useful and valuable members of society. A year after taking office, on July 17, 1828, Rector Lobachevsky delivered his significant speech “On the most important subjects of education.”

“We have entered this completion, youth will not accept empty words, without any thought. Here they teach what actually exists, and not what is invented by an idle mind... Ask nature! She keeps everything true and will answer your questions consistently and satisfactorily.

The new rector managed to rid the university of incompetent teachers who considered sunlight to be a reflection of the face of God. Soon a printed publication was established - “Scholarly Notes of Kazan University”, which is still published to this day. Kazan University is becoming a first-class educational institution.

The archives of the university preserved L. N. Tolstoy’s decision to allow him to take the entrance exams. The famous chemists Zinin and Butlerov studied here. Ilya Nikolaevich Ulyanov graduated from the University. And at the end of the 19th century, Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov-Lenin became a student at Kazan University.

In the hot summer of 1830, cholera was approaching Kazan. Even as I learned about it, many residents left the city; those who remained stocked up on food and tried not to go out. But the danger was already close.

The first cases of illness convinced Lobachevsky. He ordered:

- both gates are locked;
- along the fences - security;
- let in - only with his permission and after fumigation with a bang;
- chemists - prepare cotton and cotton lime.

Then Lobachevsky went to the next street to see the governor, Baron Pirkh.

- What, cholera? - The Baron was surprised. — From Simbirsk? But we pressed it, Mr. Lobachevsky, from above, from Nizhny Novgorod.

“I consider it necessary, Your Excellency, to immediately assess the piers, set up cordons, isolate the city...” And Lobachevsky unfolded before the governor a detailed plan for combating the impending danger.

- What are you talking about? - Pirkh did not listen to the end. - Do you want a cholera riot? Isolate the city? And who will collect taxes? And who will recruit soldiers, who will fight?

Lobachevsky could only say goodbye.

Having urgently met, the university council transferred all power to the rector. Lobachevsky declared quarantine. All students, teachers, and employees moved to the territory of the university. Yet 12 people died in the first four days. By order of the rector, groups of the dead were doused with cotton slurry; clothes, bedding and other personal belongings were immediately burned. On the fifth day the illnesses stopped. Lobachevsky personally observed the delivery and slamming of water. And although in the city cholera wiped out part or half of the population, out of the 560 people who lived on the territory of the university, no one else was injured.

The rector's energy did not change in August 1842, when a fire raged in Kazan. Entire neighborhoods in the city burned down, and many buildings were left in piles of firebrands. The university library and the newly built observatory caught fire. Lobachevsky was not absent from the fire; he himself supervised the rescue efforts. The observatory burned down, but valuable equipment was saved; library from fire. However, the house belonging to Lobachevsky burned to the ground; The university apartment where Nikolai Ivanovich lived also burned down; The Lobachevskys' property and priceless manuscripts were lost in the fire.

Lobachevsky's services to Kazan University can hardly be overestimated. His discussion “on the most important subjects of education” turned out to be a valuable contribution to pedagogical science. But boldness of judgment and direct, independent character do not suit everyone. Mischief arose against Lobachevsky.

In 1846, Nikolai Ivanovich was appointed assistant trustee of the Kazan educational district, and the trustee soon became the poorly educated Cossack general Molostvov, who, without any embarrassment, declared to the Minister of Public Education Nosov:

- My assistant has absolutely nothing to do...

Removal from active administrative and teaching activities caused Lobachevsky to experience a sharp decline in strength. At 55, he felt weak and sick. Soon he lost even the nominal position of Molostvov’s assistant, and his salary. Financial difficulties arose.

Lobachevsky began to take ill: the excessive full-time vigils in his youth took their toll. In the last years of his life, Nikolai Ivanovich could not recover on his own. But his belief in the great future of his discovery did not leave him: being weak and sick, seeing almost nothing, he wrote his scientific testament - “Pan Geometry”, which was published shortly before his death.

Even in his youth, Lobachevsky became interested in the foundations of geometry and criticized Euclid's Elements. Particular attention was drawn to the so-called V postulate:

- Any two straight lines which, when intersecting with a third, form internal one-sided angles, giving a total of less than two

For many centuries, geometers have tried to prove the fifth postulate as a theorem. But each of them implicitly introduced some “obvious” property:

... The geometric locus of points on a plane that are equidistant from a straight line and lie on one side of it is a straight line, believed Posidonius (Rome, 1st century BC).

... Two approaching straight lines necessarily intersect,” said Omar Khayyam (Central Asia, 11th century).

... For any figure you can build a similar one,” John Valis was convinced (England, 17th century).

Alexis-Claude Clairaut (France, 18th century) built a theory of parallel lines based on the existence of a rectangle; Farkas Boyan (Hungary, late 18th - early 19th centuries) proved the fifth postulate, based on the fact that a circle can be drawn through any three points that do not lie on the same straight line.

But all these extensions turned out to be equivalent to Euclid’s fifth postulate or even a stronger postulate: through a point outside a certain line one can draw in their plane one and only one line that does not intersect the given one. And the last statement is equivalent to

Back in 1823, Lobachevsky prepared for publication the first geometry course he compiled. Trying to break away from Euclid, he constructed in the first half of the book “absolute geometry” - independent of the postulate of parallels. He was convinced that geometry, in which all Euclidean axions are true, except for the parallel axions, is not contradictory. The most important role in this conclusion was played by Lobachevsky’s critical attitude to the conviction that our geometric knowledge is unique: after all, the uniqueness of the geometric system would follow from this conviction.

A student of the great Euler, academician Nikolai Ivanovich Fuss, did not understand the revolutionary system of Lobachevsky, and gave a sharply negative review of the work. The academician was especially outraged by ... the introduction of the meter as a unit of measurement of length and the division of a circle into 400 degrees, not 360.

“This division was invented during the French Revolution,” Fuss writes in the decision, “but radiance was not accepted anywhere due to further inconveniences.”

This review is an erroneous introduction: Lobachevsky’s genius turned out to be inaccessible not only to Fuss, but also to many talented minds of that time, and the meter firmly won the rights of citizenship.

But the devastating review of Nikolai Ivanovich Fuss did not frighten Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky. He continues to develop his ideas and in subsequent years he takes a step that is brilliant in its simplicity: he abandons the fifth approach, which had dominated the minds of almost two thousand years and did not raise any doubts among either his predecessors or his contemporaries.

Lobachevsky assumed that through a given point outside a line one can draw at least two lines in their plane that do not intersect with the given line $AB$.

From this assumption, which was completely stupid at first glance, Lobachevsky began to draw further conclusions. And I didn’t come to any contradiction! Moreover, he received a harmonious, logical geometric system, a complete whole, which he called “imaginary geometry” - by analogy with the minimic ones, which he also called imaginary; if the minimum numbers are the most general, for which the laws of general arithmetic are valid, then “imaginary geometry” is the most general geometric system in which all Euclid’s axioms are satisfied, except for the axiom of parallels. And just as the real numbers are a special case of the set of complex numbers, so the traditional geometry of Euclid is a special case of the general geometric system.

“Imaginary geometry” differs significantly from the conventional geometry of Euclid.

Let's start with the fact that the sum of the angles of a triangle in it is always less than 180°; that the set of all points of the plane lying in one half-plane and equidistant from a line given in it - the “base” - is not a straight line at all, but a special line called an equidistantor; that through three points that do not lie on a straight line, one can draw either a circle, or an equidistant line, or an intermediate line between them - a “horocycle”.

Lobachevsky also considered the geometry of space. By rotating a circle, oricle and equidistant around from straight lines orthogonal to them, one can obtain in the first case a sphere, in the second - an orisphere, in the third - an equidistant surface, that is, the geometric locus of points in space equidistant from the plane. It turned out that the geometry on the sphere coincides with the geometry of the eclidean sphere, the geometry on the horosphere coincides with the geometry of the eclidean plane, and the geometry on each of the two cavities of the equidistant surface is of the same type as the geometry of the Lobachevsky plane.

Lobachevsky found trigonometric relations in plane triangles in the geometry he discovered. Further, he showed that these relations can be obtained from the formulas of spherical trigonometry.

Lobachevsky geometry also has other properties similar to spherical geometry; in particular, the area of ​​a triangle on the Lobachevsky plane is expressed through its angles (in radian measure) according to the formula; coinciding with the formula

$$S = r^2 (A + B + C - \pi)$$

for spherical triangles, if we consider the radius of the sphere $r$ to be purely minimal.

Euclidean geometry can be considered as a limiting case of either spherical geometry or Lobachevsky geometry as $r$ tends to infinity. Lobachevsky subjected the more general nature of the geometry he discovered to the name “Pangeometry” - “universal geometry”, which he gave to it in his last work.

Having established the foundations of new geometry and trigonometric relationships in the triangles of this geometry, Lobachevsky introduced coordinates into this geometry and solved a large number of problems of analytical geometry and problems of calculating areas and volumes, with the help of which he first

Lobachevsky is trying to establish experimentally which geometry takes place in the real world - Euclidean or “imaginary”. To do this, he calculated the sum of the angles of a triangle, the vertices of which were two diametrically opposite points of the earth's orbit and one of the fixed stars. Having found that the deviation of these sums from l does not go beyond the limits of observational error, Lobachevsky came to the conclusion that the geometry of the real world can be considered Euclidean, but he believed that the deviation of the sum of the angles of triangles from l could be detected when considering cosmic triangles of large sizes.

At the same time as Lobachevsky, several other mathematicians discovered non-Euclidean geometry. Even in the last years of the 18th century, Gauss came to the idea of ​​non-Euclidean geometry with SEL, who continued to write down these questions for several decades, but did not publish his results. Somewhat later, to Lobachevsky's geometry with SEL, Janos Bolyai. He outlined his discovery in an appendix to the mathematical work of his father, Farkas Bolyai, published in 1832. Since Gauss did not publish his discovery, and Bolyai published it later than Lobachevsky, priority is recognized for Lobachevsky.

Those present at the meeting were unable to understand the work; A commission of three specialists was appointed to review the submitted essay in detail. However, neither associate Nikolai Dmitrievich Brashman, later a prominent scientist, one of the founders of the Moscow Mathematical Society, nor professor Aleksandr Yakovlevich Kupfer, nor Nikolai Ivanovich’s old friend and classmate, a prominent scientist of that time, Ivan Mikhailovich Simonov, could understand the deepest physical significance of the new geometry. Month after month passed, year after year, no feedback was received from the scientific commission, and the invaluable work was archived.

Only in 1829, the first presentation of Lobachevsky’s discovery appeared in the university magazine “Kazansky Vestnik” - the article “On the principles of geometry”. Several more articles and topics presenting Lobachevsky's main results were published in the 30s. In 1840, Lobachevsky published in Berlin in German a systematic presentation of his discovery - the book “Geometric Studies on the Theory of Parallel Lines.” This book was replaced by Carl Friedrich Gauss, the "king of mathematicians" of the time. He was the only authoritative person in the world who could appreciate the ingenious discoveries of Lobachevsky - and appreciated them - at their true worth.

“As I was told,” Gauss wrote to one of his students in 1841, “the works of Kazan University, written in Russian, contain a lot of his works... Many people have mastered the ability to read the large works of this ingenious mathematician.

At the age of 63, the “king of mathematicians” begins to study Russian.

“I begin to read Russian with some fluency and derive greater comfort from it,” writes Gauss.

On the initiative of Gauss, Lobachevsky was elected a corresponding member of the Royal Society of Goettigen. In the Diploma sent to Kazan, he is called “one of the most excellent mathematicians in the Russian state.” However, the “king of mathematicians” did not appear in print with public recognition of Lobachevsky’s merits, or he was afraid of being incomprehensible to his contemporaries. During Lobachevsky’s lifetime, only one of his colleagues—Kazan University professor P.I. Kotelnikov—publicly announced the great scientific and educational significance of his discovery.

Recognition came only a few years after the death of Nikolai Ivanovich, thanks to the works of Eugenino Beltrami (Italy) and later, Felix Klein (Germany) and Anir Poincaré (France).

Lobachevsky was the author of a number of works in other areas of mathematics. Some works were related to its geometry - for example, a work on the calculation of certain intervals, a work on a reliable estimate of what is observed when measuring the sum of the angles of a triangle with very large sides. Other works relate to algebra and analysis: “Algebra or finite calculus” (1834), “On the disappearance of trigonometric strings” (1834), “On the convergence of infinite series” (1841), etc. Lobachevsky was one of the first to give a general definition of a function, formulate the concept of continuity and differentiability of a function and establish the difference between these concepts, which before him were often confused.

But Lobachevsky’s main merit, his most valuable contribution to the treasury of world science, is the completion of traditional and intuitively irrefutable ideas - the rejection of the fifth postulate and the creation of a generalized geometry, in which Euclid’s geometry is only an extreme special case.
