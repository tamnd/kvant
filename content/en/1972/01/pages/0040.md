Constructing a correct 17-gon

Richmond gave a simple construction of the seventeenth triangle $P_0P_1 \dots P_{16}$ (see figure).

Let's connect point $P_0$ with point $J$, lying on radius $OB$ at a distance $1/4OB$ from the center. On the diameter passing through the point $P_0$, we choose points $E$ and $F$ so that $\Rightarrow OJE$ is equal to four times the angle $OJP_0$, and $\Rightarrow FJE$ is equal to $45^\circ$. Let a circle built on $FP_0$ as a diameter intersect

$OB$ at point $K$, and let a circle with center $E$ and radius $EK$ intersect $OP_0$ at points $N_3$ (between $O$ and $P_0$) and $N_5$. Let us restore the perpendiculars to $OP_0$ at these two points until they intersect with the original circle at points $P_3$ and $P_5$. Then the arc $P_3P_5$ (and the equal arc $P_1P_3$) is equal to the $2/17$ circle. The proof uses several times the fact that the roots of the equation

$x^2+2x\text{ctg}, 2\alpha-1=0$ are equal to tg$\alpha$ and - ctg$\alpha$.

(G. S. M. Coxter, Introduction to Geometry, M., “Hayka”, 1966, p. 49.)
