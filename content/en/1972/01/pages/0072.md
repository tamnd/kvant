To the article “Irrational equations”
1. No solutions.
2. $x = 2$. Note. Rewrite the equation in the form $\sqrt{x + 2} = \sqrt{3x + 2}$ and raise to the sixth power; At the same time, take into account the additional condition $x \geq -\frac{2}{3}$.
3. $x_1 = 12/127, x_2 = -4/43$. Note. Bring the left side to a common denominator, raise the equation to the seventh power, and then take the root from both sides; the result is equation $|x + 12| = 128 x$.
4. $x_1 = 3, x_2 = 5$. Note. The numerator of the fraction on the left side is the sum of the cubes $(\sqrt{5} - x)^3 + (\sqrt{x} + 3)^3$.
5. No solutions. Note. Put $y = \sqrt{x - 2}$.
6. $x = 5$. Note. Put $y = \sqrt{x + 4} + \sqrt{x - 4}$.
7. $x_1 = 1, x_2 = -6$. Note. Convert the equation to the form $\sqrt{3(x - 2)^2} + \sqrt{3(x - 2)(x + 7)} + \sqrt{3(x + 7)^2} = 3$ and multiply both sides by $\sqrt{3(x + 7)} - \sqrt{3(x - 2)}$.
8. $x = a + \frac{1}{2} - \sqrt{a + \frac{1}{4}}$ at $a \geq 0$; for $a < 0$ there are no solutions.
