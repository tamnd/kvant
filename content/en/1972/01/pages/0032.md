The first matrix will oscillate with less and less amplitude and finally stop. At this time, the amplitude of vibrations of the second matrix will be the most magnificent. Then the first matrix will roll out, and the second will stop, and so on.

The complex oscillatory motion of a system consisting of two matrices can be represented as the result of the addition (superposition) of two oscillations with frequencies $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. One of the frequencies is equal to the frequency with which both matrices will oscillate if they are simultaneously deflected in one direction and then released (Fig. 10). Let's denote this frequency by $\omega_1$. Another frequency is $\omega_2$. This is the frequency with which both matrices will vibrate if they are first simultaneously deflected in opposite directions and then released (Fig. 11).

Note that the angular frequency $\omega_2$ is greater than the angular frequency $\omega_1$. These two vibrations are called normal vibrations of two coupled matrices.

Let's write the equation of normal vibrations. First

$$\begin{cases} x_1' = a \cos \omega_1 t, \\ x_2' = -a \cos \omega_1 t, \end{cases}$$

second

$$\begin{cases} x_1'' = a \cos \omega_2 t, \\ x_2'' = a \cos \omega_2 t. \end{cases}$$

Let's add the mixtures $x_1'$ with $x_1''$ and $x_2'$ with $x_2''$. We get:

$$\begin{cases} x_1 = x_1' + x_1'' = a \cos \omega_1 t + a \cos \omega_2 t, \\ x_2 = x_2' + x_2'' = -a \cos \omega_1 t + a \cos \omega_2 t. \end{cases}$$

Using the formula for adding cosines, we get:

$$\begin{cases} x_1 = A \cos \Omega t, \quad \Omega = \frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}, \\ A = 2a \cos \left( \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{2} t \right). \end{cases}$$

Due to the fact that $\omega_1$ differs little from $\omega_2$, the expression $\cos \left( \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{2} t \right)$ is close to 1 (if $\omega_1$ is equal to $\omega_2$, then this expression is equal to 1). Therefore, it has little effect on the value of $x_1$. Therefore, the factor $2a \cos \left( \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{2} t \right)$ in the expression for $x_1$ can be considered as slowly changing the amplitude (red point on Fig. 12, a).
