Disorder is a physical quantity

The words “order” and “disorder” that we used here seem at first glance to be somehow unfamiliar, taken from everyday life. In fact, behind these words lies the physical concept of the present, which is important, that it is expressed by a special quantity. This quantity, which expresses the degree of disorder or order (order is simply the absence of disorder), is called entropy. It is denoted by the letter $S$, and it is defined as follows: if a certain amount of the body $\Delta Q$ is supplied to or removed from the body, and the absolute temperature of the body is equal to $T$, then the change in entropy $\Delta S$ is determined by the equality

$$\Delta S = \frac{\Delta Q}{T}.$$

In all processes that happen to bodies, it is not entropy that is important, but rather the change in entropy. (In this respect, entropy is similar to the potential energy of a body. In all problems, it is not the potential energy itself that is important, but its change.)

Of particular interest to us is the case when the process occurs in such a way that the body is not supplied to or removed from the body: $\Delta Q = 0$. Such conditions can be ensured by a well-heat-conducting body or by quickly carrying out the process, when the body does not have time to move from one body to another. Such processes are called adnabate. From expression $\Delta S = \frac{\Delta Q}{T}$ it follows that during adnabate processes the entropy does not change.

Adnabate change in volume

In the technique of artificially producing cold, we are mainly interested in the processes of changing the volume of the body, the processes of their expansion and rolling. They play a particularly important role here.

Let's imagine that we have a body that we expand or compress adnabatally, that is, without supplying or withdrawing the body. We already know that increasing the volume of a body with a given number of particles leads to an increase in the degree of disorder, that is, to an increase in entropy. But our process is adnabate, in which $\Delta Q = 0$ and entropy do not change. How to ensure the constancy of entropy, provided that it returns a noble increased volume? If the second law of thermodynamics is correct, then this is only possible if, when the volume of a body changes, some other process occurs, due to which entropy decreases by exactly the same amount as it increases due to an increase in volume. We know that entropy changes when body temperature changes. Therefore, we can expect that with an adnabate increase in the volume of a body, its temperature will decrease. This is what actually happens.

Thus, we immediately have a way to obtain body temperature. It is necessary to expand this body adnabatally, that is, to increase its volume. Adnabate expansion is precisely one of the main ways to obtain cold.
