Physics and mathematics school
at MIIT

A. L. Sadovsky

In January 1968, the Moscow Institute of Transport Engineers had an organizational evening physics and mathematics school. Initially, the Physics School accepted everyone who wanted to study mathematics and physics in depth both within the school curriculum and beyond. However, the level of those attending FMS turned out to be very varied. Some of the applicants did not expect that they would also have to work at evening school, and therefore quickly lost interest in it.

I had to turn to a tried and tested method: in the spring and fall, when the 1970 intake was formed, exam competitions were held. These exams consisted of three stages: written work (two problems in physics, three in mathematics) and oral exams in physics and mathematics. As a result of the selection, out of 320 candidates, 250 students were accepted (110 in the ninth and 140 in the tenth grades), from which two streams were compiled, one in nine and the other in tenth grade. In 1971, two streams of tenth graders were formed (who studied in the previous year in the ninth grade and from new admissions), a stream of ninth graders and a stream of eighth graders.

Classes at Physics School are conducted as follows: on Wednesdays there is a lecture in physics and a seminar in mathematics, and on Fridays there is a lecture in mathematics and a seminar in physics. The duration of lectures and seminars is two academic hours. Lectures are given by professors, teachers and graduate students of the institute; classes are taught by students of the Faculty of Automation and Computer Science (specialty “Applied Mathematics”) and other faculties of MIIT. Among the leaders of the busy group there are many graduates of specialized physics and mathematics schools.

There are two main directions in the work of the school and its program. The first of them is a more detailed study of the traditional school course and sections of mathematics and physics very close to it (elements of combinators, complex numbers, polynomials and algebraic equations, and others). The second direction is the development of mathematical culture and broadening the horizons of students. For this purpose, series of lectures are given on special issues, lectures outside the school curriculum: geometric transformations, the concept of Lobachevsky geometry, elements of set theory, the theory of active numbers, elements of the theory of probability and the theory of ingredients.

It is known that the problems facing Physics and Mathematics School students are different: tenth-graders strive to prepare for competitive exams at a university or technical college, ninth-graders have not yet set themselves such a task. In this regard, the programs of the oldest ones are more utilitarian, that is, within the limits of their material, we adhere mainly to the first direction. A completely different approach to ninth grade students. Taking into account the available time reserve, the physics and mathematics school creates a base consisting of such functional theories as set theory and the foundations of mathematical analysis, so that later, relying on this basis, it would be easier for the teacher to explain an elementary mathematics course, and for schoolchildren to understand.

It is somewhat more difficult for our physicists, since in an ordinary school physics there is often insufficient attention to some information on the entire Physics School program in physics, which, in practice, does not provide for the study of many interesting problems and tasks that are not included in the standard school course.

The organization of work at the Physics School is carried out in two sections: physics and mathematics. The governing body of the school is the Physics and Music School Council, headed by the institute's chairman for educational work, Professor I.P. Isaev, deputy. Chairman for General Issues Associate Professor V.I. Malakhov and Head of Student Viktor Matveychkom. The FMS Council consists of four students each - representatives of the physics and mathematics sections.
