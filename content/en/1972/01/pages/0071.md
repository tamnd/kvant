Every week the sections hold a methodological seminar for student teachers, at which the topics of the lessons, the displayed tasks, the reasons for their solution and other issues are discussed. These seminars are conducted in direct contact with lecturers. Associate professors V.I. Korovin, A.S. Drobat, T.A. Shulman, Professor E.S. Ventzel and many teachers from the Department of Applied Mathematics provide great assistance in this physics and mathematics school.
The MIIT Publishing House provides teaching aids for school students. However, one of the goals of the functioning of the school is to prepare applicants for MIIT, which practically turns into a modern polytechnic institute.

Physics and Mechanics School teachers also participate in traditional MIIT Olympiads in mathematics and physics. In 1971, the Department of Computational Mathematics held the V Olympiad in Mathematics, and the Department of Physics held the IV Olympiad in Physics. Below are some of the Olympiad problems in mathematics.

Quests

1. Two lines and a point are given on a plane. On one of the lines, find a point equidistant from the other line and the given point.

2. A train of n steps is sent from A to B, some of which are loaded, into the gorons NUMerated in order, the sum of the numbers loaded into the gorons is a multiple of k, where k>n. During the haul, the train may be attacked by robbers, who can clear no more than one carriage. In point B the numbers n and k are known. After checking all the cars, how can you find out whether there was a restriction and which car was restricted?

3. Prove that for a polynomial P(n)=a1968n1998+...+a1n+a0 with natural coefficients, find a natural n0 such that P(n0) is not a prime number.

4. The polynomial f(x)=a_nx^n+...+a_1x+a_0 when divided by x-a gives remainder a^2, when divided by x-b leaves remainder b^2, and when divided by (x-c) gives remainder c^2. Find the remainder when dividing the polynomial f(x) by (x-a) (x-b) (x-c), where a, b, c are pairwise unequal numbers.

5. Given a square with side 1. Prove that no matter how large the number N is, it is possible to construct a system of non-overlapping circles that lie entirely inside the square and such that the sum of their lengths will be greater than N, and the sum of their areas less than $\frac{1}{N^2}$.

6. Given 2n digits a1, a2, ..., an, an+1, ..., a2n. The number A, formed by the first n digits, is 2 times larger than the number B, formed by the remaining digits. The sum of the first n digits is equal to the sum of the second n digits. Prove that the sum of the digits is divisible by 9.

7. Monkey Chi-chi-chi owns bananas. I'll give her 100 bananas. When Doctor Aibolit weighed these bananas, they found out that they weighed 10 kg, but not a single banana weighed more than 200 g. Prove that with these bananas

8. The graph of the function y=x^n+a1x^n+1+...+a_n(n>1) intersects the line y=a at points A1, ..., An, and the line y=b at points B1, ..., Bn. Straight lines AiBi relate angles αi to the abscissa axis. Prove that $\sum \text{ctg} \alpha_i = 0$.

9. In a convex n-convex all diagonals are drawn. On each side there are arrows in such a way that there are no cycles. A cycle is a closed broken line, on each link of which there is an arrow so that the beginning of each arrow is aligned with the end of the previous one (an example of a cycle is shown in figure 2).

10. A secant is drawn from a point outside the circle so that it bisects the circle.

11. Prove that between n and n! there is at least one prime number.

12. A set of segments is given on a line, each pair of which has at least one common point. Prove that all segments have at least one common point.
