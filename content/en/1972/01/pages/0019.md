⟦folio 17⟧

Throttling leads to gas cooling. This is explained by the fact that gas molecules interact with each other. When, during the expansion of a gas, the molecules move away from each other, the arbitrary work against the molecular forces connecting the molecules, and the kinetic energy of the molecules, and, therefore, the temperature of the gas, will decrease. We can say that here too, work is done, but not against external forces, as in detand, but against the internal forces of cohesion between molecules.

The cooling effect of gas during throttling is called the Joule-Thomson effect.

For the Joule-Thomson efficiency, it is important that the gas is not ideal, that is, its molecules interact with each other at a distance, and not just in a direct “head-on” collision. An ideal gas would not cool when throttling. It could be cooled, but it uses detaner.

As already indicated, the cooling during throttling is stronger, the lower the temperature. At room temperature, which is where you start, it is not great. In order, despite this, to achieve significant cooling, resort to a constructive technique, the existence of which is clear from figure 2.

The pipe through which gas flows to the throttle is placed in another, wider pipe. When the gas cools slightly after throttling, it returns from the refrigeration chamber to the compressor through a wide pipe, cooling the oncoming gas “on the way.” Therefore, the next portion of gas will suit the throttle colder. Having passed through the throttle, the gas will cool even more, and returning through the outer pipe, it will cool the oncoming gas even more. After some time, the gas will approach the throttle so cold that throttling will lead to its liquefaction, and the first portion of liquid will appear in chamber X. Now the cold vapor of this liquid will escape through a wide pipe, continuing to cool the oncoming gas.

The cold liquid accumulated in the refrigeration chamber can be immediately used to cool other bodies. Sometimes this “other” body is a salt solution, which then goes to pipes installed in store counters, under an artificial skating rink, etc. But you can do it differently: drain the compressed gas through tap B into special vessels prepared for storing and transporting cold liquids (Dewar vessels). The liquid thus obtained can be used to cool other bodies anywhere and at any time. Common machines that use cooled gas or liquid as a refrigerant in the refrigerating chamber are called refrigerators. A machine designed to dispense a liquid product is called a cooling machine.

Throttling is the most commonly used cooling method. It is, in particular, used in home refrigerators.
