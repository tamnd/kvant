4. By Bezout's theorem
$$f(a)=a^2; f(b)=b^2; f(c)=c^2.$$
Dividing $f(x)$ by $(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)$ means representing $f(x)$ as
$$f(x)=(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)Q(x)+p_1x^2+p_2x+p_3.$$
Substituting $x=a, x=b, x=c$ into this equality successively, we obtain a system of equations for the unknowns $p_1, p_2, p_3$.

$$S=N^4\frac{\pi}{4N^6}<\frac{1}{N^2}.$$

6. On the one hand, $A$ and $B$ give the same remainder when divided by 9. On the other hand, $A$ gives twice the remainder. From this it is easy to deduce that both $A$ and $B$ are divisible by 9.
