If a parameter salt is placed in a constant magnetic field, then all the magnets will line up parallel to the confluent line of the magnetic field. We will bring order to the orientation of the ions. By removing the field, we will create chaos again. This is completely analogous to the rolling and expansion of gas.

To obtain low temperatures proceed in the following way. A paramagnetic salt in an ampoule $A$ filled with a gaseous gel and located in a vessel with a liquid gel, the temperature of which is about $1^\circ\text{K}$, is placed in a magnetic field between the electromagnetite strips (Fig. 3, $a$). The helium gas in the ampoule provides thermal contact with the liquid gel, so that the temperature of the magnetized cols matches the temperature of the surrounding liquid helium. Helium gas is then pumped out of the ampoule, providing heat-conducting salt from the environment (Fig. 3, $\delta$). After this, the magnetic field is turned off, that is, adiabatic gas formation cols (Fig. 3, $\theta$) is produced. In this case, misorientation of the magnets occurs -

For almost forty years now, the described method has been used to obtain so-called ultra-low temperatures, that is, temperatures below those that can be obtained using only liquid helium. The lowest temperatures obtained in this way are thousandths of a degree.

Not only atoms, but also atoms cores are magnetic, albeit 1000 times weaker. Nevertheless, if you start not from $1^\circ\text{K}$, but from $0,01^\circ\text{K}$, you can, by adiabatic demagnetization of not atoms, but nuclei, obtain a temperature of about a millionth of a degree.
