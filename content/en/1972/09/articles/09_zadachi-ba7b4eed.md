TASKS

M161. The lake has the shape of a small $n$-gon. Prove that the set of points of the lake from which all its shores are visible is either empty or fills the interior of a convex $m$-gon, where $m \leq n$.

I. N. Bernishtein

M162. Sequence of natural numbers
$a_1 < a_2 < a_3 < \dots < a_n < \dots$ (A) is such that every natural number is either included in the sequence (A), or is represented as the sum of two numbers from the sequence (A), perhaps identical.
Prove that $a_n \leq n^2$ is for all $n=1, 2, \dots$.

Yu. G. Eroshkin, 9th grade student

M163. Prove that if the diagonals of a convex quadrilateral are mutually perpendicular, then their intersection points on all four sides (or their extensions) lie on the same circle.

I. A. Kushinr

M164. On the white cells of an infinite chessboard filled with a half-plane (Fig. 1) at the top, some numbers are written so that for each black cell the sum of the numbers in the two cells adjacent to it - to the right and to the left - is equal to the sum of the other two numbers located in the cells adjacent to it - above and below. You know the number in one cell of the $n$th line (blue cross on figure 1), and you need to find out the number above it in the $(n+2)$th line (red question mark on figure). How many more numbers in the bottom two lines (blue dots on figure) do you need to know?

M. L. Gerver

M165.* There is a set of $F$ points on a circle, consisting of 100 arcs. It is known that with any rotation $R$ of the circle, the set $R(F)$ has a common point $c$. (In other words, for any $\alpha$ from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$ in the set $F$ one can specify two points $\alpha$ apart from each other.) What is the smallest sum of lengths that the 100 arcs forming the set $F$ can have? What will be the answer if the arcs are not 100, but $n$?

Yu. P. Lysov

F163. In a uniformly charged sphere of radius $R$ there is a spherical cavity of radius $r$, the center of which is located at a distance $a$ from the center of the sphere (Fig. 1). Find direction

Fig. 2.

electric field at different points of the cavity if the charge density is $\sigma$.

V. D. Krivtsenkov

F164. Find the heat capacity of an ideal gas in a process in which the temperature of the gas is a) proportional to the square of its volume; 6) inversely proportional to its volume.

I. Sh. Slobodetsky

F165. Estimate the time of an elastic collision of two steel or two rubber balls with identical radii $R=1$ cm. For steel, Young's modulus is $E_c=2,1\cdot 10^{11}$ n/m², steel density is $\rho_c=7,8\cdot 10^3$ ke/m³. Young's modulus of rubber is $E_p\approx 10^6$ n/m², its density is $\rho_p\approx 10^3$ ke/m³. The balls move towards each other at speeds of $v=1$ m/s.

What is the average force between the balls?

G. L. Komkin

F166. Why is it easier to poke a hole with an awl if the awl is twisting? Why do you need to rotate a nail to get it out of the wall? Why is it that when you cut bread or meat, you move the knife back and forth, but when you cut cheese, you just press down on the knife?

A. G. Kosourov

F167. The frequency of the vibrating string depends on its length, tension and linear density - the mass of units of string length. Determine the type of these dependencies.

I. A. Zaitsev
