To describe circular motion, along with linear velocity, the concept of angular velocity is introduced. If a point, when moving along a circle in time $\Delta t$, describes an arc whose angular measure is $\Delta \varphi$, then the angular velocity is $\omega = \frac{\Delta \varphi}{\Delta t}$.

Angular velocity $\omega$ is related to linear velocity $v$ by the relation $v = \omega r$, where $r$ is the radius of the circle along which the point moves (Fig. 1). The concept of angular velocity is especially convenient for describing the rotation of a rigid body around an axis. Although the linear velocities at points located at different distances from the axis will not be the same, their angular velocities will be equal, and we can talk about the angular velocity of rotation of the body as a whole.

Problem. The disk of radius $r$ rolls without slipping along the horizontal plane. The speed of the disk chain is constant and equal to $v_{\pi}$.

Fig. 1. Fig. 2.

At what angular velocity is the disk being healed?

Each point of the disk participates in two motions - in translational motion with a speed $v_{\pi}$ together with the center of the disk and in rotational motion around the center with a certain angular velocity $\omega$.

To find $\omega$ we use the absence of slippage, that is, the fact that at each moment of time the speed of the point of the disk in contact with the plane is zero. This means that for point $A$ (Fig. 2) the speed of translational motion $v_{\pi}$ is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the linear speed of rotational motion $v_{BP} = \omega r$. From here we immediately get $\omega = \frac{v_{\pi}}{r}$.

$$\frac{mv^2}{R}h = -\frac{mgl}{2},$$

To find the speed $v_D$, you can use the law of conservation of energy, as if the car were moving only under the influence of gravity. The fact is that the reaction force of the road at each moment is directed perpendicular to the movement of the car, and therefore its work is equal to zero (recall that the work $\Delta A = F \Delta s$ cos $\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the force $F$ and the direction of movement $\Delta s$). The friction force between the wheels of the car and the road when driving with the engine turned off can be neglected. Therefore, the sum of the potential and kinetic energy of the car when driving with the engine turned off does not change.

Let us equate the energy values ​​of the car at points $A$ and $D$. In this case, we will count the height from the level of point $D$, that is, the potential energy of the car at this point will be considered equal to zero. Then we get

$$\frac{mv_D^2}{2} = \frac{mv_A^2}{2} + 2mgR.$$

Substituting here the value $v_A = \sqrt{gR}$ for the exclusive speed $v_D$, we find:

$$v_D = \sqrt{5gR} \approx 70 \text{ m/c} \approx 260 \text{ km/ч}.$$
