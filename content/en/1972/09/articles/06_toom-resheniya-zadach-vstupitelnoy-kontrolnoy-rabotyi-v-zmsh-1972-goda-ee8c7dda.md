A. L. Toom

The conditions of these problems and the rules for admission to the correspondence mathematical school at Moscow State University and Leningrad State University were published in the first issue «Kvant» for this year. Here we provide solutions and directions to some of these problems.

1. In a triangle, the ceptra of the rising and circumscribed circle are symmetrical about one of the lines. Find the corners of the triangle.

Let us denote the ends of the side relative to which the symmetries are centers by A and C, and the third vertex by B (see Fig. 1). Let O and D be the centers of the corresponding extruded and circumscribed circle. If we omit the redistributions to AC from them, then they will fall to one point M - the middle of the segment OD (this means that O and D are symmetrical with respect to AC). We need to find the angles of the triangle: $\angle BAC = \alpha$, $\angle ABC = \beta$, $\angle BCA = \gamma$.

Let us first prove that $\alpha = \gamma$. Indeed, $AD = DC$ (as radius),

poset $AM = MC$, open $\triangle AMO = \Delta CMO$. Therefore, $\angle OAM = \angle OCM$. But $\angle OAM = \frac{\alpha}{2} \Rightarrow OCM = \frac{\gamma}{2}$ (the center of the circle extending into the triangle is located at the point of intersection of its bisectors), poset $\alpha = \gamma$.

It follows that $AB = BC$, then the median $BM$ is also the height, which means O lies on BM.

Posibly, points B, O, M and D lie on the same straight line, and since $BD = AD$ (as a radius), then $\angle BAD = \angle ABD = \frac{\beta}{2}$.

On the other hand, $\triangle AOM = \Delta ADM$. That's why

$\angle MAD = \angle MAO = \frac{\alpha}{2}$.

Then

$\angle BAD = \angle BAM + \angle MAD = \alpha + \frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{3\alpha}{2}$.

So,

$\frac{\beta}{2} = \frac{3\alpha}{2}, \beta = 3\alpha$.

To Posny, the sum of the angles of the triangle $ABC$ is equal to $5\alpha$, whence $\alpha = 36^\circ$, $\beta = 108^\circ$.

Answer: $36^\circ, 108^\circ, 36^\circ$.

2. Two cerrates into the cerra. In front of him, several disadvantages are written in a chain on paper. Each person in turn transfers one or two adjacent minuses to the plane. We call mom, who will forward the last minus. Who do we call for a right tseregna: the beginner or his partner, and how does he need to tserrat for this, if at the beginning it is written: a) 7 minuses; b) k minuses?

For all n, the beginner wins. Let's describe a strategy, using which he will surely win. The first move must be made in the middle so that the remaining minuses form two separate “pieces” of equal length (figure 2 shows the position after the first move for n=7)

and n=8). After this, the beginner, with each of his moves, must forward minuses symmetrical to those that the second forwarded before. So, if the second one forwarded the k-th (or k-th and k+1st) minus on the right, then it is necessary to forward the k-th (or k-th and k+1st) minus on the left. The second, each move, will transport one or two minuses, the symmetrical ones of which have not yet been transported; therefore, these minuses cannot be the last ones, and the second one cannot win.

4. Is there at least one number a such that both numbers $\frac{1}{a} - \sqrt{15}$ and $a + \sqrt{15}$ are integers?

Let $a + \sqrt{15} = m$, $\frac{1}{a} - \sqrt{15} = n$.

Let's express a from the first equality and substitute it into the second:

$$\frac{1}{m - \sqrt{15}} - \sqrt{15} = n.$$

Let's transform:

$$16 - mn = (m - n) \sqrt{15}.$$

To fulfill this equality it is sufficient that:

$$\begin{cases} 16 - mn = 0, \\ m - n = 0. \end{cases}$$

(In fact, this is necessary, since m, n are integers, but you may not know this: after all, we only need to find at least one value of a.)

The resulting system can be easily solved:

$$m_1 = n_1 = 4, m_2 = n_2 = -4.$$

Answer: such a exists: for example, $4 - \sqrt{15}$ (in reality there are only two such numbers).

5. One of the direct grambel window. Andrey said: “It’s either Vitya or Tolya.” Vitya said: “It wasn’t me or Yura who did this.” Tolya said: “You both are telling lies.” Dima said: “Nim, one of them did the truth, and the other did the dumb.” Yura said: “Hey, Dima, we’re wrong.” Their father, who, of course, can be trusted, is sure that at least three years ago the truth was told. Who broke the window?

Let's represent the grambel statements in the form of a table of 5 rows and 5 columns

(Fig. 4). The rows and columns are named by the first letters of the brothers' names. The statement of each brother is recorded in a column under the first letter of his name. In this column we will arrange pluses and minutes according to the following rule: plus if the statement assumes that the brother after whom the line is named broke the window, and minute if the statement does not allow this.

So, in the column under the letter A, the plus is only opposite the letters B and T, since Andrei said: “This is either Vitya or Tolya.” In the column under the letter B, minutes appear only in rows B and S.

To fill out column T, you need to figure out in which case Andrey and Vitya are both wrong. For example, if Andrei broke the window, then Vitya will be right, since he allowed this, and in column B there is a plus against the letter A. In general, Andrei and Vitya will both be wrong only if Yura broke the window, since only in the first two places in Yu’s line is minute. This means that in column T you need to put a plus only against Y.

Dima will turn out to be right only if exactly one of the first two brothers - Andrei and Vitya - turns out to be right and the other is wrong. This means that in column D, a plus is placed only in those rows in which there is one minus and one plus in the first places.

Yura simply denies what Dima said; Where there is a plus in Dima’s column, there is a minus for him, and vice versa. The table is full. We know that at least three brothers told the truth. This means that you need to look for a line that contains at least three pluses. There is only one such line - T.

Answer: Tolya broke the window.

6. Think of four non-negative integers also, so that each number from 1 to 81 can be represented as the sum of four numbers - one from each triple.

The following fours are suitable:

1, 2, 3
0, 3, 6
0, 9, 18
0, 27, 54.

Let's explain how they were invented. It is easier to solve this problem in a more general form: come up with k triples of non-negative integers such that each number from 1 to 3^k can be represented as a sum of k numbers - one from each triple.

For k=1, the solution is very indo: one triple 1, 2, 3. Let the problem be solved for k triples. How to choose another triple to get a solution to the problem for k+1?

The first number must be taken equal to zero. Then, adding it to all the sums of k numbers taken from one of the first triplets, we get the same numbers from 1 to 3^k.

Let's take the second number equal to 3^k. Adding it to the numbers from 1 to 3^k, we get all the numbers from 3^k+1 to 2.3^k.

Let's take the third number equal to 2.3^k. Adding it to the same numbers, we get all the numbers from 2.3^k+1 to 3.3^k = 3^k+1.

So, the problem is to solve for k+1 as well. This is how fours and threes are built. It has been proven that they are suitable for every k, including k=4.
