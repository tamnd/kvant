⟦folio 30⟧

D. Borodin

The beginning of this story is similar to a detective novel. One spring day in 1970, there were two passengers on board a plane flying around the world. One of them was the American physicist Hafel, the other occupied as many as two seats and was registered at the airport as Mr. Klock. Mr. Klock was actually a clock*), a very accurate atomic clock that was missing 13-digit time**).

The journey was undertaken in order to demonstrate the efficiency of clock movement predicted by relative theories.

The experiment was staged in such a way that it actually involved not one, but two planes, each of which carried two copies of atomic clocks. (Mr. Klock was accompanied on the second plane by another physicist, Keating).

One of the planes was flying from west to east, the other from east to west. Their route ran at an altitude of about 10 km and ran along the earth's parallel. The speed of the planes was about 1000 km/h, so they completed their flight in about two days (including stops along the way).

When the clock readings were compared with the clock at the airfield, it turned out that the clock that was flying on the plane from west to east was behind by 5 hundred-millionths of a second (as they say now, 50 nanoseconds). The clock, which flew around the Earth from east to west, moved ahead by 16 hundred millionths of a second (160 sec). Although there were many noises in the experiment that are difficult to account for accurately (for example, plane landings at intermediate airports), nevertheless, the efficiency of the clock was demonstrated very convincingly.

Physicists who study elementary particles have been observing the efficiency of time for a long time. They are well aware that a particle flying at high speed, for example, a μ-mezon, “lives” longer than a stationary μ-mezon. The lifetime of a $T$ μ-meson flying at a speed of $v$ is related to the lifetime of a $T_0$ μ-meson at rest by the formula

$$T = \frac{T_0}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

($c = 3 \cdot 10^{10}$ cm/s is the speed of light, $T_0 = 2$ microseconds = $2 \cdot 10^{-8}$ s).

Only thanks to this observation of the decay of μ-mez does it manage to pass through the entire thickness of the Earth’s atmosphere.
