$$T_{\max} = mg \left( 1 + \frac{v_0^2}{gl} \right).$$

The maximum speed of the load $v_0$ is found at the angle α at which the thread is deflected, from the law of conservation of energy: $$\frac{mv_0^2}{2} = mgh = mgl (1 - \cos \alpha).$$

Using this relationship, for the maximum value of thread tension we obtain the formula: $$T_{\max} = mg (3 - 2 \cos \alpha).$$

According to the conditions of the problem $T_{\max} = 2mg$. Equating these expressions, we find cos α=0.5 and, therefore, α=60°.

Let us now determine the thread tension at β = α/2. The speed of the load at this moment is also found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv_1^2}{2} = mgl \left( \cos \frac{\alpha}{2} - \cos \alpha \right).$$

Substituting the value $v_1$ into the formula for the tension force, we find:

$$T = \frac{mv_1^2}{l} + mg \cos \frac{\alpha}{2} = mg \left( 3 \cos \frac{\alpha}{2} - 2 \cos \alpha \right) \approx 1,6 \text{ mg.}$$
