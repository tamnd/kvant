2. There are two possible cases here (Fig. 1). If the angular speed of the disk is $\omega$, and the speed of its center is $v$, then the velocities of the points in contact with the slats will be correspondingly equal in case a) $v_1 = v + \omega R, v_2 = v - \omega R$; in case b) $v_1 = v + \omega R, v_2 = \omega R - v$. (We took $v_1 > v_2$ for definition). Solving these systems, we find:
a) $v = \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}; \omega = \frac{v_1 - v_2}{2R}$;
b) $v = \frac{v_1 - v_2}{2}; \omega = \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2R}$.
3. The speed of any point $M$ lying on the segment $OB$ (see Fig. 2) is found by the formula $v_M = v + \omega r_M$, where $r_M$ is the distance from point $M$ to the center of the disk $O$. For any point $N$ belonging to the segment $OA$, we have: $v_N = v - \omega r_N$, where $r_N$ is the distance from point $N$ to the center. Let us denote by $p$ the distance from any point of diameter $BA$ to the point $A$ in contact of the disk with the plane. Then it is obvious that $r_M = \rho - R$ and $r_N = R - \rho = -(\rho - R)$, where $R$ is the radius of the disk. Therefore, the speed of any point on the diameter $BA$ is found by the formula: $v_\rho = v + \omega (\rho - R)$. Since the disk rolls without slipping, then $\omega = \frac{v}{R}$ and for speed $v_\rho$ we get $v_\rho = \omega$. It follows that the ends of the velocity vectors are on a straight line emerging from point $A$ and inclined to the diameter $BA$ at an angle proportional to the angular velocity of rotation of the disk $\omega$.
