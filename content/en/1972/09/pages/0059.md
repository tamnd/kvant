$v_A = \sqrt{gR}$ the reaction force of the road becomes zero only at the top point of the loop. In fact, the speed of the car in other sections of the loop will be greater, and as is easy to see from the solution to the previous problem, the reaction force of the road will also be greater than at point $A$. Therefore, if the car at the top point of the loop has a speed of $v_A = \sqrt{gR}$, then it will not break away from the loop anywhere.

Now we determine what speed the car must have at the bottom point of the loop $D$ so that at the top point of the loop $A$ its speed is $v_A = \sqrt{gR}$. To find the speed $v_D$, you can use the law of conservation of energy, as if the car were moving only under the influence of gravity. The fact is that the reaction force of the road at each moment is directed perpendicular to the movement of the car, and therefore its work is equal to zero (recall that the work $\Delta A = F \Delta s$ cos $\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the force $F$ and the direction of movement $\Delta s$). The friction force between the wheels of the car and the road when driving with the engine turned off can be neglected. Therefore, the sum of the potential and kinetic energy of the car when driving with the engine turned off does not change.

Let us equate the energy values ​​of the car at points $A$ and $D$. In this case, we will count the height from the level of point $D$, that is, the potential energy of the car at this point will be considered equal to zero. Then we get

$$\frac{mv_D^2}{2} = \frac{mv_A^2}{2} + 2mgR.$$

Substituting here the value $v_A = \sqrt{gR}$ for the exclusive speed $v_D$, we find:

$$v_D = \sqrt{5gR} \approx 70 \text{ m/c} \approx 260 \text{ km/ч}.$$

If a car enters a loop at such a speed, it will be able to complete it with the engine turned off.

Let us now determine with what force the carILITY will press on the road at point $B$. The speed of the car at point $B$ is again easily found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv_D^2}{2} = \frac{mv_B^2}{2} + mgR(1 + \cos \alpha).$$

Substituting the value $v_D = \sqrt{5gR}$ here, we find that the speed is $v_B = \sqrt{gR(3 - 2\cos \alpha)}$.

Using the solution to the previous problem, using the given speed, the pressure force at point $B$ is found:

$$N_B = \frac{mv_B^2}{R} - mg\cos \alpha = 3mg(1 - \cos \alpha) \approx 2 \cdot 10^3 \text{ N}.$$

Similarly, you can find the pressure force at any other point of the “dead loop”.
