TASKS

M161. The lake has the shape of a small $n$-gon. Prove that the set of points of the lake from which all its shores are visible is either empty or fills the interior of a convex $m$-gon, where $m \leq n$.

I. N. Bernishtein

M162. Sequence of natural numbers
$a_1 < a_2 < a_3 < \dots < a_n < \dots$ (A) is such that every natural number is either included in the sequence (A), or is represented as the sum of two numbers from the sequence (A), perhaps identical.
Prove that $a_n \leq n^2$ is for all $n=1, 2, \dots$.

Yu. G. Eroshkin, 9th grade student

M163. Prove that if the diagonals of a convex quadrilateral are mutually perpendicular, then their intersection points on all four sides (or their extensions) lie on the same circle.

I. A. Kushinr

M164. On the white cells of an infinite chessboard filled with a half-plane (Fig. 1) at the top, some numbers are written so that for each black cell the sum of the numbers in the two cells adjacent to it - to the right and to the left - is equal to the sum of the other two numbers located in the cells adjacent to it - above and below. You know the number in one cell of the $n$th line (blue cross on figure 1), and you need to find out the number above it in the $(n+2)$th line (red question mark on figure). How many more numbers in the bottom two lines (blue dots on figure) do you need to know?

M. L. Gerver
