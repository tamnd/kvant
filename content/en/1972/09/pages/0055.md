At a certain speed, the moment of friction force will exceed the moment of reaction force and the LIB machine will tip over.

Task a. At what speed can a car moving along a circle of radius $R = 130m$ overturn? The center of gravity of the car is at a height of $h = 1m$ above the road, the width on the left of the car is $l = 1,5m$ (Fig. 4).

At the moment the car rolls over, both the road reaction force $N$ and the friction force $F_{\text{тр}}$ are applied

to the "outer" wheel. When a car moves in a circle with a speed $v$, a friction force $F_{\text{тр}} = \frac{mv^2}{R}$ acts on it. This force creates a moment about the center of gravity of the car $M_{\text{тр}} = \frac{mv^2}{k}h$. The maximum moment of the road reaction force $N = mg$ relative to the center of gravity is equal to $mg\frac{l}{2}$ (at the moment of overturning, the reaction force passes through the outer wheel). Equating these moments, we find the equation for the maximum speed at which the LIB machine will not overturn:

$$\frac{mv^2}{R}h = -\frac{mgl}{2},$$

where

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{glR}{2h}} \approx 30m/c \approx 110km/u.$$

In order for the LIB machine to move at such a speed, a friction coefficient of $k \geq \frac{v^2}{gR}$ is necessary (see the previous problem).

A similar situation occurs when turning a motorcycle or bicycle. The frictional force created by space acceleration has a moment relative to the center of gravity, tending to overturn the motorcycle. Therefore, to compensate for this moment, the moment of road reaction force is applied to the motorcyclist in the direction of the turn (Fig. 5).

Task a. Momoshikst drives along a horizontal road at a speed of $v = 70km/u$, making a turn of radius $R = 100m$. At what angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal should it tilt so as not to fall?

The frictional force between the motorcycle and the road is $F_{\text{тр}} = \frac{mv^2}{R}$, since it imparts centripetal acceleration to the motorcyclist. Road reaction force $N = mg$. The condition for the equality of the moments of friction force and reaction force relative to the center of gravity gives the equation:

$$F_{\text{тр}} \cdot l \sin \alpha = Nl \cos \alpha,$$

where $l$ is the distance $OA$ from the center of gravity on the left of the motorcycle (see Fig. 5).
