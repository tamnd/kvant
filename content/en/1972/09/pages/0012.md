WAVES ON THE WATER
AND “OVERSEAS GUESTS” BY N. ROERICH
A. L. Stasenko

“Deep, as cold as spring, feel the fish in the river. Ornate painted boats with colorful sails sail merrily along it. Their bows rose up dashingly, ending with the heads of the Drakovs. Their bright colors glow in the sun...
The path of the Varangians is far and dangerous.”*).

We are talking about the painting by the wonderful Russian artist Nicholas Roerich “Overseas Guests” (1901), which can be admired in the Tretyakov Gallery. It is reproduced on the cover of our magazine.

Let's use this bright, positive canvas to think about some physical topics: the law of conservation of energy, the theory of allocated physical quantities and statistical processing of experimental results. And what does this picture have to do with it? Just to make it more fun.

In fact, isn’t it interesting to try to determine, looking at this picture, at what speed the overseas guests are swimming? Let it be relative to the flowing water, and not relative to the shores. What in the picture can give us the necessary information? First of all, there is a bow wave, forming on both sides at the foreline, ending with a dragon. Far away, the circular waves that run along the surface of the water from the ship; qualitatively they are similar to circles with a common center lying somewhere in the flatness of the rook.
