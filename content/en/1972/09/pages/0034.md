⟦folio 32⟧

Solutions to the problems of the introductory test in 3MSH 1972
A. L. Toom

The conditions of these problems and the rules for admission to the correspondence mathematical school at Moscow State University and Leningrad State University were published in the first issue «Kvant» for this year. Here we provide solutions and directions to some of these problems.

The new introductory work to the All-Union Correspondence School of Mathematics will be published in the first issue «Kvant» 1973.

1. In a triangle, the ceptra of the rising and circumscribed circle are symmetrical about one of the lines. Find the corners of the triangle.

Let us denote the ends of the side relative to which the symmetries are centers by A and C, and the third vertex by B (see Fig. 1). Let O and D be the centers of the corresponding extruded and circumscribed circle. If we omit the redistributions to AC from them, then they will fall to one point M - the middle of the segment OD (this means that O and D are symmetrical with respect to AC). We need to find the angles of the triangle: $\angle BAC = \alpha$, $\angle ABC = \beta$, $\angle BCA = \gamma$.

Let us first prove that $\alpha = \gamma$. Indeed, $AD = DC$ (as radius),

poset $AM = MC$, open $\triangle AMO = \Delta CMO$. Therefore, $\angle OAM = \angle OCM$. But $\angle OAM = \frac{\alpha}{2} \Rightarrow OCM = \frac{\gamma}{2}$ (the center of the circle extending into the triangle is located at the point of intersection of its bisectors), poset $\alpha = \gamma$.

It follows that $AB = BC$, then the median $BM$ is also the height, which means O lies on BM.

Posibly, points B, O, M and D lie on the same straight line, and since $BD = AD$ (as a radius), then $\angle BAD = \angle ABD = \frac{\beta}{2}$.

On the other hand, $\triangle AOM = \Delta ADM$. That's why

$\angle MAD = \angle MAO = \frac{\alpha}{2}$.

Then

$\angle BAD = \angle BAM + \angle MAD = \alpha + \frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{3\alpha}{2}$.

So,

$\frac{\beta}{2} = \frac{3\alpha}{2}, \beta = 3\alpha$.

To Posny, the sum of the angles of the triangle $ABC$ is equal to $5\alpha$, whence $\alpha = 36^\circ$, $\beta = 108^\circ$.

Answer: $36^\circ, 108^\circ, 36^\circ$.
