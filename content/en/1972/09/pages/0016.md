Looking at Roerich's painting, we see that it depicts the first case; Moreover, since there is not even a hint of rolling waves in the direction of movement of the ship, we must conclude that its speed is much less than the speed of the waves:

$$V \ll u.$$

Thus, from the point of view of surface circular waves, the ship is simply “sharpening in place.”

Now let's start with the calculations. To do this, we need to “measure” the bow wave height $h$, the angle $2\alpha$ and the surface wave length $\lambda$.

Measurements

We could construct and measure the quantities we need from the picture more precisely, taking into account perspective, projections, angles, etc. But, first of all, this is quite difficult and tedious; secondly, we would find ourselves in a funny position: indeed, it is not wrong to approach the artist’s fairy-tale invention as an experimental photograph. Besides, we don’t know how tall the nose was, for example.

What size are the painted shields of the ship shown? There is, however, one characteristic size in the picture, which, apparently, has changed little since the time of the Varangians - this is the size of the head.

Before reading further, try yourself, using at least this “standard”, to estimate $h$ and $\lambda$. This will be your personal “dimension”. We must not forget, however, that the picture is perceived by each viewer very individually. Show this picture to all the children in your class and ask everyone what he thinks about the values ​​of $h$, $\lambda$, $\alpha$ (it’s good if the person being asked has not heard the answers of other goods; at the “reading” stage, the readings of all students will be similar to the independent readings of the device). Write down the answers you receive, then build a graph where you plot the values ​​$h$ on the horizontal axis, and the number of people $n$ who named this value $h$ on the vertical axis.
