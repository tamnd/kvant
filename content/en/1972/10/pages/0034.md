How to throw a returning boomerang? Typically, you take it with your right hand by one of the ends and hold it vertically, turning the convex side slightly to the left. In this case, both ends of the boomerang are directed either forward (as Australians throw) or backward. You can choose any method. Then, swinging their right hand, they throw the boomerang forward in a horizontal direction or slightly upward. For success, it is necessary, firstly, that the plane of the boomerang when thrown is almost vertical or slightly tilted to the right, but not horizontal. Secondly, the boomerang needs to be made to spin quickly. This can be achieved if, at the moment of the throw, you sharply delay the forward movement of your right hand. By inertia, the boomerang immediately begins to rotate around the point at which metadata held it, and, therefore, acquires simultaneous translational and rotational speeds.

At first it seems that the boomerang is flying away, but soon its trajectory deviates to the left, and partly upward. Then he makes a wide, more or less rounded petlio and falls somewhere at the feet of the thrower, sometimes, however, describing another small petlio before this. The second loop is curved in the other direction, so that all the trajectories in this case have the shape of a figure eight (Fig. 3). It’s great when the boomerang, having described the petlio and losing speed, floats above your head for some time and then slowly descends.

Each boomerang is characterized by the ease of throwing, the type of trajectory and the ability to hover. In addition, any boomerang can describe different orbits depending on the method of throwing.
