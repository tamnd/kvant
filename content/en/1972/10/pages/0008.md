By Fermat's Little Theorem, $a^{p-1} = 1$ in $Z_p$ for $a \neq 0$, which is equivalent to the comparison $a^{p-1} \equiv 1$ (mod $p$) for $a$, is not divisible by $p$. Euler noted that Fermat's theorem can be generalized to the case of composite $m$. Let $a$ be coprime with $m$. Just like for the simple $p$, it shows that $a^k - 1$ for the new $k$ is divisible by $m$. Let $l$ be the smallest exponent that has these properties; it is customary to say that $a$ belongs to the exponent $l$ modulo $m$.

food $\varphi(m)$ is the number of elements in $Z_m$ coprime to $m$.

The quantity $\varphi(m)$ is called the Euler function (Table 3).

The proof exactly repeats the reasoning for the prime $m = p$. We need to write down on the cards $\varphi(m)$ the numbers coprime to $m$ and less than $m$, and then at each step, together with one of the remaining cards with the number $b_j$, we will select cards on which the remainders from the division of $b_j, b_j a, \dots, b_j a^{l-1}$ by $m$ are written. In this case, at each step exactly $l$ cards will be selected, and as a result $\varphi(m)$ must be divided by $l$.
