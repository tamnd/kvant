Let's represent $P(x)$ as $P(x) = (x - r_0)Q(x) + P(r_0)$, where $Q(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $l - 1$ and $P(r_0)$ is divisible by $p$. Since $r_j - r_0$ cannot be divided by $p$, then $Q(r_j)$ is divisible by $p$, and then, by the induction hypothesis, $Q(r)$ will be divisible by $p$ for all $r$. Therefore, $P(r)$ is divisible by $p$ for all $r$.

Consequence. The comparison $x^l \equiv 1 (\bmod p)$ is satisfied by at most $l$ elements $Z_p$.

By virtue of the lemma, it is sufficient to find at least one $x \in Z_p$ that does not satisfy the comparison. You can take $x = 0$.

Gauss's theorem. Let $p - \text{просмоте число}, l - \text{делимель (p - 1)}$. Then the exponent $l$ belongs to $\varphi(l)$ elements $Z_p$.

Consequence. For any projection $\varphi(p - 1)$ of primitive roots (see Table 4).

Proof of Gauss's theorem. Let the exponent $l(l - \text{делитель p - 1})$ belong to some $a \in Z_p$. Let's look at the numbers $1, a, \dots, a^{l-1}$ and their remainders $b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{l-1}$. It has already been shown that all $b_j$ are different. It is clear that for any $m$ we have:

$$(a^m)^l \equiv (b_m)^l \equiv 1 (\bmod p),$$

that is, $b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{l-1}$ is a solution to comparison $x^l \equiv 1 (\bmod p)$, and by virtue of the corollary to the lemma, this comparison has no other solutions. In particular, among $b_0, \dots, b_{l-1}$ there are all residues belonging to the exponent $l$. Obviously, if $m$ and $l$ have a common divisor $d \neq 1$, then $(a^m)^{l/d} \equiv 1 (\bmod p)$, so $b_m$ cannot belong to the exponent $l$, and therefore the exponent $l$ can belong to at most $\varphi(l)$ elements of $Z_p$.

Each of the $p - 1$ nonzero elements $Z_p$ belongs to some exponent $l$, which is a divisor of $p - 1$. Let $d_1, \dots, d_k$ be divisors of $p - 1$ and the exponent $d_s$ belong to $\psi(d_s)$ elements of $Z_p$. Then $\psi(d_1) + \dots + \psi(d_k) = p - 1$ and $\psi(d_s) \leqslant \varphi(d_s)$ for all $s$. By Gauss's formula, $(4) \varphi(d_1) + \dots + \varphi(d_k) = p - 1$, therefore $\psi(d_s) + \dots + \psi(d_k) = \varphi(d_s)$ and, since $\psi(d_s) \leqslant \varphi(d_s)$, must $\psi(d_s) = \varphi(d_s)$ for all $s$. The theorem is proven.
