In order to study the processes that arise in this case, one must be able to study the elementary actions of individual particles. However, until recently, physics was only able to monitor the final results of numerous collisions of a large number of interacting particles and monitor the directions of their movement.

Two decades ago, a group of Leningrad physicists began to solve this extremely difficult problem. First of all, they developed experimental methods that make it possible to monitor the state of individual particles during inelastic collisions. These methods make it possible to measure the energies, masses, charges of interacting particles and monitor the directions of their movement.

Using these methods, many years of systematic research were carried out, which unusually expanded and deepened our understanding of the inelastic interaction of atoms and molecules. It turned out that the previous theory of such processes was incorrect. A new theory was needed. It was developed by the participants in these studies. In the experiments carried out, multi-electron insertion was discovered, in which an atom loses several electrons at once in one collision. It was found that the energy losses of interacting particles during collisions are discrete in nature. The energy transferred during a collision may not acquire any values ​​within the permissible range, but only some strictly defined values. It turned out that the melting atoms form, for an insignificant fraction of a second, systems similar to molecules. And although such a “molecule” exists for a very short time (~10⁻¹⁵ s), during this time a deep restructuring will occur in the electronic shells of its atoms, leading to the loss of many new physical phenomena, which were discovered during experiments.

Knowledge of the mechanism of elementary acts of inelastic collisions is of great importance for radio electronics, the study of processes occurring in gas lasers, magnetohydrodynamic generators, as well as in the Earth’s atmosphere and distant outer spaces. They are of great value for the development of high temperature chemistry and solving problems of controlling thermodermic reactions. In particular, thanks to the research described above, it was possible to create a method for reliably determining the main characteristics of hot plasma - the so-called “corpuscular diagnostics” of plasma.

Rapid development is presented about the nature of the Universe, associated with the creation of new fields of astrophotics (radio astronics, RENTenian astrophotics, etc.), as well as with the manifestation of man in Space, an extremely detailed interest in the problems of gravity. These problems are studied by the general theory of the relative theory of space, time and gravity. The general relative theory has deepened our understanding of the nature of space, time and gravity and their interaction. She showed that heavy bodies, by their attraction, bend the space around them, just as a stone bends the stretched canvas on which it lies. Heavy bodies notice the passage of time in their surroundings.

The general theory of relative weight is still far from complete and continues to be successfully developed today.

A very significant contribution to the development of this theory was made by the work of A.Z. Petrova. His main scientific achievements are concentrated in two monographs: “Einstein Spaces” and “New Methods in the General Theory of Relative Theory”, in the first work of A.Z. Petrov examined all possible variants of gravitational fields (various types of curvature of space by heavy bodies), allowed by the general theory of relative theory, and created their mathematical classification structure. At the same time, he managed to find new solutions to the basic control of this theory.

In the second work A.Z. Petrov investigated the permissible types of symmetrical gravitational fields. These types of symmetries are associated with the laws of conservation of various physical quantities. Thanks to the research of A.Z. Petrov, we now know which of the conservation laws take place in a system with different gravitational fields.
