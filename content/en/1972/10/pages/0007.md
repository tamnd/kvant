In part, if $a$ is divisible by $m$, then $a \equiv 0$ (mod $m$). Consistency (1) is equivalent to the fact that $a$ and $b$ have the same remainders when divided by $m$. Let us recall that by the remainder of the division of $a$ by $m$ we mean $r$, $0 \leq r < m$ such that $a = mq + r$, where $q$ is an integer. This condition $r$ is uniquely determined. Residues are also called modulo residues $m$. We denote their totality $\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}$ by $Z_m$.

Comparison by one modulo can be obtained by adding and variable, if $a \equiv c$ (mod $m$), $b \equiv d$ (mod $m$), then $a + b \equiv c + d$ (mod $m$), $ab \equiv cd$ (mod $m$). This is easy to check directly. If $a - c$ and $b - d$ are divisible by $m$, then their sum $a - c + b - d = (a + b) - (c + d)$ is divisible by $m$, as well as the expression

$$ab - cd = ab - cb + bc - cd = b(a - c) + c(b - d).$$

In the language of remainders, this means that the remainder of the sum $a + b$ depends only on the remainders $r_1$ and $r_2$ and coincides with the remainder of the number $r_1 + r_2$; similarly, the remainder $ab$ depends only on $r_1$ and $r_2$ and is equal to the remainder $r_1r_2$. As a result, operations arise in the set $Z_m$ that will be called the corresponding addition and multiplication modulo $m$ (or addition and multiplication in $m$-arthmetic).

So, to perform one of these operations on $Z_m$, you need to perform these operations in the usual sense and take the remainder when dividing the result by $m$. Subtraction in $Z_m$ (the remainder of the ordinary difference) is defined in a similar way. It is immediately verified that in $m$-arthmetic subtraction is the inverse action of addition, that is

$$a + b = c \iff c - b = a;$$

$$a, b, c \in Z_m$$ (proof).

Note that

$$0 - a = m - a \in Z_m;$$

$$a - b = a + (m - b).$$
