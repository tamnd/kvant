Signs of divisibility

The results obtained on the divisibility of powers of 10 by $p$ have another interesting application. It turns out that for any prime number $p \neq 2,5$ there is a test for divisibility that generalizes the test for divisibility by 3. Let us recall the reasoning that applies to $p=3$. Let $A$ be a natural number, $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_k$ its digits, numbered from right to left. Then $A=a_0+a_110+\dots+a_k10^k$. Since $10^s$ for all $s$ when divided by 3 gives a remainder of 1, then $A$ gives the same remainder when divided by 3 as $a_0+a_1+\dots+a_k$. In particular, $A$ will be divisible by 3 if and only if $a_0+a_1+\dots+a_k$ is divisible by 3.

Let now $p=11$. Then, as we have seen, $l=2$ and all $10^{2k}$ when divided by 11 gives a remainder of 1. The digit $A$ is divided into pairs from right to left, with only one digit falling into the last group if the number of digits is odd: $b_0=a_1a_0, b_1=a_3a_2, \dots$. The number $A$ should be denoted by $b_j: A=b_0+b_110^2+b_210^4+\dots+b_m10^{2m}, 0 \leq b_j < 100$. It is clear that $A$ when divided by 11 will give the same remainder as the sum of two-digit numbers $b_0+b_1+\dots+b_m$.

In general, if $p \neq 2,5$ is a prime number and $10^l-1$ is divisible by $p$, we split $A$ into $l$-digit blocks from right to left (the first block from the left may have fewer digits) so that $A=c_0+c_110^l+c_210^{2l}+\dots+c_n10^{nl}$, $0 \leq c_j < 10^l$.

Then $A$ when divided by $p$ gives the same remainder as the sum of $l$-digit numbers $c_0+c_1+c_2+\dots+c_n$; in part, $A$ is divided into $p$ moeda and molko moeda, when ema the sum is divided into $p$. This sign is very simple at $p=3$; We have seen that at $p=11$ we have to split into two-digit blocks, at $p=37$ into three-digit blocks, but at $p=7$ into six-digit blocks, and generally speaking, we can only guarantee that the division into blocks of length $p-1$ will lead to a goal.

Comparison theory

When studying the divisibility of integers, it is convenient to use the language of comparison theory introduced by Gauss. The integers $a$ and $b$ are said to be modulo comparable $m$ (m is a natural number) if $a-b$ is divisible by $m$. It means this:

$$a \equiv b \pmod m.$$ (1)
