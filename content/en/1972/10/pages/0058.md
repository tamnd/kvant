Let us have two bodies $M_1$ and $M_2$, which are gravitated to each other, and the body $M_2$ is fixed motionless, and the body $M_1$ moves from point $B$ (at which it was motionless) to point $A$ (Fig. 7). But body $M_1$ did not have a reserve of kinetic energy and did work due to interaction with body $M_2$. Therefore, they say that body $M_1$ has energy, which is determined relative to the position of bodies $M_1$ and $M_2$. This energy is called potential energy. If a body has kinetic energy, then potential energy can no longer be attributed to a specific body. A system of bodies has potential energy. Often, however, when the displacement of one of the bodies and the change in its kinetic energy is small compared to the changes in the movement of the second body, they say that the potential energy refers to one body - the one that makes large movements. For example, they talk about the potential energy of a body interacting with the Earth, instead of talking about the potential energy of the body system - the Earth.

Potential energy possessed by a body has an important difference from kinetic energy. If the body is motionless ($v=0$), then its kinetic energy is zero and the body cannot do work by changing its kinetic energy - after all, kinetic energy is equal to $$\frac{mv^2}{2}$$ and cannot be negative. If it is equal to zero, then it cannot decrease. At the same time, if the body is at a height $H$ above the surface of the Earth, then, falling, it can do work equal to the work done by gravity:

$$A = mgH.$$

Potential we can say that the body has potential energy $mgH$. But the body can fall into a shaft with a depth of $h$. then it can do work $mg(H+h)$ and we need to assume that the potential energy is equal to $mg(H+h)$. So what is it really equal to?

The fact is that potential energy is determined if we set the position of the body at which, as we believed, the potential energy is equal to zero. This, however, does not mean that, moving from this position, the body cannot do work. The simple potential energy of the body will decrease, becoming negative. Which position of the body is taken as the position of “zero potential energy” does not matter: all laws and equations include a change in potential energy, and it does not depend on the choice of “zero potential energy”. For kinetic energy, “zero”—the “zero state”—is the state in which the body is motionless.
