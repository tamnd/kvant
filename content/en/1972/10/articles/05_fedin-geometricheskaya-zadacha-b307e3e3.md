The Earth's crust is composed primarily of SiO$_2$ ($A = 60$) and its core is composed primarily of iron ($A = 57$). These two substances have approximately the same atomic weight, and therefore the same atomic sizes. If the radii of atoms are considered equal to $a$, then the volume of the Earth will be equal to
$$\frac{4}{3} \pi R_3^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi \frac{N_3}{A} a^3,$$
where
$$R_3 = a \left(\frac{N_3}{A}\right)^{1/3}.$$

$$g = \gamma \frac{M_3}{R_3^2} = \gamma \frac{N_3 m_p}{R_3^2} = \gamma N_3 \left(\frac{A}{N_3}\right)^{2/3} \frac{m_p}{a^2}.$$

ABOUT ELECTRICITY

In Glasgow (Scotland) and Moscow there was an anecdote that the English attributed to the famous English physicist Lord Kelvin, and the Muscovites attributed it to Moscow University professor Nikolai Alekseevich Umov.

During the exam, Umov, Jacob, asked the student: “What is electricity?” After thinking for a long time, the student replied: “Sorry, professor, I forgot. I did it this morning, but now I forgot.”
“Here, gentlemen,” Umov addressed the other students, “is the greatest tragedy of physics of the 19th century: the only person in the world knew what electricity was, and even he forgot!”
