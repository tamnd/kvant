NEW BOOKS

In this issue we continue to publish summaries of books published in 1972 that are of interest to our readers.

The following books will be published in the third and fourth quarters of 1972 (orders can be sent through the Book by Mail stores).

MATHEMATICS

Publishing house "MIR"

1. Ebbin house G., Yakos K., Man F., Hermes G., Tyurin machines and recursive functions. (Volume 12 l., circulation 40,000 copies, price 86 kopecks.)

This collective monograph of German mathematicians contains an elementary presentation of the theory of Tyurin machines and recursive functions - an important section of modern mathematical logic, which has found wide application in cybernetics. In addition to the foundations of this theory, the book contains a number of significant results, including recent discoveries (in particular, Kolmogorov’s results on the connection of Tyurin’s machines with the basic theories of probability). The presentation is strict, but accessible, and contains many examples and explanations.

The monograph will be read with interest by readers of different categories, starting with high school students of mathematical specialization and final employees and teachers of higher education.

2. Davis M. Theory of cer. (Volume 12 l., circulation 75,000 copies, price 60 kopecks.)

Game theory is one of the young and rapidly developing areas of modern mathematics. Its subject is the formal study of conflict situations in the broadest sense of the word. The ideas and methods of game theory find application in economics, military affairs, biology and other branches of knowledge.

M. Davis's book is a fascinating popular study of game theory. The most striking feature of the book is that the author, starting with the basic, primary concepts of game theory, brings the reader to the scale of modern and subtle ideas, insignificantly highlighting a generally accessible style of presentation that does not require mathematical training from the reader. The book's six sections cover the correspondence of one-person games, finite zero-sum games, utility theory, two-person non-zero-sum games, and polychlus games.

The book is of interest to a wide range of readers who want to get acquainted with the basic ideas of game theory. Mathematicians will also read it with satisfaction.

3. Sawyer W. The Path to Modern Mathematics. (Volume 11 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 55 kopecks).

Professor W. Sawyer is known to Soviet readers for his book “Prelude to Mathematics” and the article “Algebra” in the collection “Matematika in the Modern World.”

In the book “The Path to Modern Mathematics,” discussing ideas for reform of mathematics education, the author makes attempts to highlight new issues of modern mathematics for inclusion in the school curriculum.

Considering individual topics in mathematics (maps, matrices, vector spaces, etc.), which are becoming important in scientific and engineering practice, the author shows how many of these ideas and understandable natural ones arise and develop from the topics of traditional mathematics.

Sawyer addresses his book to young people, but it will be read with interest not only by students and schoolchildren, but also by teachers, especially in connection with the prospect of school curricula.

Publishing house "HIGH SCHOOL"

4. Zorin V.V. Mathematics manual for university applicants. (Volume 12 l., price 59 kopecks.)

The manual explains the basic concepts of elementary mathematics. All concepts are given as applicable to the requirements for applicants to universities.

The manual is intended for individuals who are ready for competitive university exams.

5. Solomonik V. S., Milov P. N. Collection of questions and problems in mathematics. (Volume 15 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 67 kopecks.)

The book is compiled in accordance with the program of application copies for applicants to universities and secondary specialized educational institutions of higher education. Examples and problems in arithmetic, algebra and geometry are included.

Almost all sections begin with brief theoretical material. There are also questions that make it possible to understand how thoroughly the theory has been accepted.

PHYSICS

Publishing house "MIR"
6. Lipson G. Great experiments in physics. (Volume 10 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 72 kopecks.)
The book by the famous English physicist G. Lipson contains a description of experiments that marked giant steps in understanding the surrounding world. It covers practically all the most important sections of physics—from simple mechanical motion to the structure of the atom, as well as those experiments that lead to a revision of the presented classical physics.
The book is written vividly and emotionally. It is extended to a wide range of audiences - from schoolchildren and students to scientists.
7. Serre E. Enrico Fermi - physicist. (Volume 18 sheets, circulation 50,000 copies, price 1 ruble 50 kopecks)
The biography of the world-famous scientist, one of the founders of nuclear physics and nuclear energy, Enrico Fermi, was written by his scientist and close collaborator Emil Segre. The book reveals the image of E. Fermi as a scientist: it talks about the style of his work, the problems he dealt with, and his relationships with collaborators and students. The high scientific level of the book is combined with a popular presentation.
The book is of interest to a wide variety of readers - from specialists - physicists to students and high school students.
8. Gregory R. Intelligent climber. (Volume 11 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 55 kopecks.)
Autograph of bionics at the University of Edinburgh, one of the world's leading specialists in vision psychology.
Answering the question: “how could he extract information about the outside world from a certain pattern of spots of light on the retina?” and many others, Gregory talks about the connection between the most important factors of perception.
The material is richly illustrated with drawings that allow the reader to check the information presented to him.
The book is of interest to lovers of popular science literature.
9. Kempfer F. Fundamentals of modern physics. (Volume 16 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 80 kopecks.)
Every year it becomes more and more difficult for physics teachers of both secondary and higher schools to “keep up” with the rapid growth of their science. The only possible way out is to rebuild the structure of the presentation of physics, bringing it as close as possible to the structure of modern science.
The proposed book by Professor Kaempfer, known to Soviet readers for his functional monograph “Fundamentals of Quantum Mechanics” (Mir, 1967), is a successful attempt to solve this difficult problem. Using a compromised mathematical apparatus, the author systematized, critically comprehended and elegantly presented the basic concepts on which physics is built.
The book is addressed to a very wide range of readers, primarily junior students and schoolchildren, for whom it is quite accessible.

Publishing house "ENLIGHT"
10. Grinbaum M.I. Homemade devices in physics. (Volume 10 l., circulation 40,000 copies, price 35 kopecks.)
The book contains descriptions of various physics instruments that can be made in a school environment. Recommendations are given for the use of these devices in the practice of school physics experiments.
11. Kogan B. Yu. Za-
dachas in physics. (Volume 15 l., circulation 100,000 copies, price 60 kopecks.)
The collection consists of problems of increased difficulty that the teacher can use in extracurricular classes, during competitions, in club work, and also recommend to students when preparing for entrance exams in.by.
The first part of the collection contains the conditions of the problem and basic theoretical information on the course of elementary physics.
The second part contains solutions to all problems, and at the end of the collection - answers to all problems.

Publishing house "HIGH SCHOOL"
12. Milkovskaya L. B. Let's repeat the physics. (Volume 27.5 l., circulation 200,000 copies, price 88 kopecks).
This manual is intended for young people preparing to enter foreign courses. Its content corresponds to the physics program for applicants to.by. They differ from this year's manuals in that each chapter contains a systematic presentation of the topic, questions for repetition, examples of problem solving and problems for independent exercises.
The manual includes some questions with modern advances in physics.
