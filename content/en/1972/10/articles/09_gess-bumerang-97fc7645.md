QUANTUM LABORATORY

BOOMERANG

F. Hess

Apparently, it is difficult to find a person who would not be surprised by the trajectory described by the boomerang. And, of course, many would really like to make a boomerang and figure out why it comes back. But the boomerang is not just a toy. A heavy boomerang is quite dangerous. Therefore, we invite our readers to make a boomerang from lightweight materials, for example, cardboard.

BELOW we publish an abbreviated version of the article published in the November 1968 issue of Scientific American magazine. The article was translated and prepared for publication by V. I. Bakhmin.

Imagine that a piece of wood that you threw flies in a circular path, then returns and calmly lies at your feet. Absurd! However, this is exactly how a boomerang behaves, of course, if you make it correctly and throw it correctly.

The boomerang was first made by the native inhabitants of Australia. The reader will probably be a little disappointed to learn that most Australian boomerangs do not return. Boomerangs can be roughly divided into two types: military and returning. Boomerangs of the first type are used as weapons for war or hunting. A good military boomerang flies very far, but does not return. Returning boomerangs are used almost exclusively for gaming.

In fact, understanding all the existing types of boomerangs is not as easy as it might seem. There are many types of native weapons in Australia. Different tribes have different boomerang shapes (Fig. 1).

It is not easy to determine from its appearance whether a given boomerang is returning. As a rule, the boomerang returns less massive, and the angle between its shoulders is more acute. In general, they range in length from 25 to 75 centimeters, width from three to five centimeters and thickness from half to one and a half centimeters. The angle between the arms of the boomerang can vary from 80 to 140 degrees. Everything reaches 300 grams.

The banana-shaped shape of most boomerangs has little effect on its ability to return. Boomerangs in the shape of beech X, V, S, T, H, Y (apparently, other shapes are possible) can be made so that they will return. Important,

How to throw a returning boomerang? Typically, you take it with your right hand by one of the ends and hold it vertically, turning the convex side slightly to the left. In this case, both ends of the boomerang are directed either forward (as Australians throw) or backward. You can choose any method. Then, swinging their right hand, they throw the boomerang forward in a horizontal direction or slightly upward. For success, it is necessary, firstly, that the plane of the boomerang when thrown is almost vertical or slightly tilted to the right, but not horizontal. Secondly, the boomerang needs to be made to spin quickly. This can be achieved if, at the moment of the throw, you sharply delay the forward movement of your right hand. By inertia, the boomerang immediately begins to rotate around the point at which metadata held it, and, therefore, acquires simultaneous translational and rotational speeds.

At first it seems that the boomerang is flying away, but soon its trajectory deviates to the left, and partly upward. Then he makes a wide, more or less rounded petlio and falls somewhere at the feet of the thrower, sometimes, however, describing another small petlio before this. The second loop is curved in the other direction, so that all the trajectories in this case have the shape of a figure eight (Fig. 3). It’s great when the boomerang, having described the petlio and losing speed, floats above your head for some time and then slowly descends.

Each boomerang is characterized by the ease of throwing, the type of trajectory and the ability to hover. In addition, any boomerang can describe different orbits depending on the method of throwing. The accuracy of the return largely depends on the skill of the thrower. The flight range can be 40 meters, or maybe half that, the flight altitude is about 15 meters, although it may be

The phenomena that occur with a boomerag can only be explained by considering its interaction with the impact. After all, in a vacuum he cannot describe anything other than a parabola. However, this task is very complex, so let's take a simpler look at the problem.

If you throw a boomerang horizontally, forcing it to rotate in a vertical plane, then each of its arms will dissipate the impact of the force F with which the boomerag's arms are affected in the direction from the flatter to the more convex part (Fig. 4). This force is similar to the lift acting on an airplane wing. In addition, from the side of the influence of Q. When throwing with the right hand, the force Q will be directed from right to left. But this force alone is not enough to make the boomerang turn left.

The speed of the boomerang arm relative to the impact is not constant.

But a gyroscope (which is a rapidly rotating maximum) has the following properties: under the influence of a force trying to rotate it, the gyroscope turns not in the direction of the action of this force, but around an axis perpendicular to both the axis of rotation and the axis along which the turning moment is directed. In our case, the boomerang begins to turn to the left. This movement is called for the first time.

So, the boomerang turns to the left, so that its plane would gradually make an increasingly greater angle with the direction of movement, if not for the rapidly increasing gyroscopic forces, which again direct the flight parallel to the plane of the boomerang. As a result, the trajectory is curved to the left, and the angle between the plane of the boomerang and the direction of its movement is maintained very small.

Often the plane of the boomerang, almost vertical at the beginning of the flight, eventually becomes almost horizontal, the boomerang seems to “lay down”. Let's look at this issue in more detail.

We will assume that there is no movement perpendicular to the boomerang plane. In a cross-shaped boomerang, the center of gravity lies at the intersection of the blades, but in a regular boomerang this is not the case. Now we move on to an important question: how will the boomerang move under the influence of all these forces and moments (and gravity, of course)?

As mentioned earlier, the average moment $M_1$ causes gyroscopic precession of the boomerang. Let's take a closer look at the gyroscope. If the gyroscope is rotated around its axis at a speed $\omega$ and a moment $M$ acts on it, directed along an axis perpendicular to the rotation axis, then the gyroscope begins to prepress, and the precession axis is perpendicular to both the rotation axis and the moment axis $M$ (Fig. 9). The angular velocity of precession is denoted by $\Omega$. There is a very strong lift force between $\omega$, $\Omega$, $M$ and the gyro moment of inertia $I^*$. Therefore, if you want to boom-

$$\Omega = M/I\omega.$$

For a boomerang, the moment $M$ is proportional to $\omega V$, so the precession speed $\Omega$ must be proportional

$$\frac{\omega V}{I\omega} = \frac{V}{I}.$$

Consequently, the speed of precession does not depend on the speed of rotation of the boomerang $\omega$.

An even stranger conclusion can be drawn. The rate of precession is proportional to $V/I$; the proportionality coefficient depends on the shape of the boomerang. Therefore, we can write

$$\Omega = CV,$$

where $C$ is the characteristic parameter of the boomerang. Now let the speed of the boomerang be doubled.

Consequently, the position of its plane will also change twice as fast. This shows, among other things, that the boomerang will fly along the same curve.

Thus, the diameter of the boomerang’s orbit, roughly speaking, does not depend on either the speed of rotation of the boomerang or its translational speed. This means that the length of the boomerang path is more or less constant.
