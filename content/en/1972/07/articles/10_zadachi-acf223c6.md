⟦folio 33⟧

TASKS

In this issue we present the problems proposed at the final rounds of the All-Round Mathematical and Physical Optimization in April of this year.

Solutions to the problems in this issue can be sent no later than September 15 to the address: 117071, Moscow V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka publishing house, journal editorial office «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, indicate the solutions to which problems you are sending (for example: “Problem «Kvant», M151, F153.)

Send solutions to problems in mathematics and physics, as well as new problems in each subject, in separate envelopes. At the beginning of your letters, please indicate your last name, first name, patronymic, home address (as well as the class and school you attend).

M151. Each of the nine prims divides the square into two quadrangles, the areas of which are in the ratio 2:3. Prove that at least three of these nine primors pass through the same point. (8th, 10th grade).

B. M. Ivlev

M152. Let $a, b, m, n$ be natural numbers, and let $a$ be coprime with $b$ and $a > 1$. Prove that if $a^m + b^m$ is divisible by $a^n + b^n$, then $m$ is divisible by $n$ ($9^*$), grade 10).

D. Yu. Grigoriev.

M153. The two play the next game. One names the number, and the other places it according to his own discretion along with one of the asterisks in the following difference:

* * * *

* * * *

Then the first one calls out another number and so on 8 times until all the asterisks are replaced with numbers.

*) In 9th grade this problem was proposed for the special case $b=1$

The one who names the numbers strives to make the difference as large as possible, and the second - to make it as small as possible. Prove that

a) the second can arrange the numbers so that the resulting difference becomes no more than 4000, regardless of what numbers are called;

b) the first can name the numbers so that the difference becomes at least 4000, regardless of where the second places the numbers (8 grades).

Yu. I. Ionin.

M154. There are 50 segments on a straight line. Prove that at least one of the following statements is true:

a) some general possibilities;

b) there is a possibility, no two of which have a common point (8, 9 cells).

A. G. Kharazishvili.

M155. Given several squares, the sum of their areas is 1.

⟦folio none⟧

Prove that they can be placed without overlaps in a square of area 2 (9, 10 cells).

G. A. Galperin.

F163. According to one of the first models of the hydrogen atom (Thomson's model), it is a uniformly charged electric ball with an electron in the center. In general, atom is neutral. Find the radius of such an atom if it is known that the minimum energy that must be imparted to an electron to remove it from the atom over a large distance is equal to W. The charge of the electron is e. (10 grades)

Fig. 1. Fig. 2.

F164. A foam cube of mass $M=100$ e lies on a horizontal stand. The height of the cube is $a=10$ cm. From below the cube is pierced by a vertical flying bullet with a mass $m=10$ e (see Fig. 1). The speed of the bullet when entering the cube is $v_1=100$ m/s, when leaving it is $v_2=95$ m/s. Will the cube bounce? (8th, 9th grade)

A. R. Zilberman

F165. Determine how many times the illumination of the image of the Sun obtained by a flat lens will change if the lens is cut along the diameter and folded with flat sides. (10 grades)

V. N. Listvin

F166. A horizontal cylinder on one side of a fixed piston contains 1 mol of ideal gas. There is a vacuum in the other part of the cylinder. The spring located between the piston and the cylinder wall is in an undeformed state (Fig. 2). The cylinder is thermally conductive from the environment. The piston is released and after equilibrium is established, the volume occupied by the gas doubles. How will the temperature of the gas and its pressure change? The heat capacity of the cylinder, piston and spring is negligible. (9 grades)

E. I. Butikov, A. A. Bykov, A. S. Kondratev

F167. An ideal ammeter is connected to the output of the “black box”. If a battery with emp is connected to the input. $E$ and internal resistance $r$, then the current through the ammeter is exactly 2 times less than in the case when two such batteries connected in series are connected to the input of the box. Draw the simplest possible diagram of the internal structure of a “black box”. (8th, 9th grade)

A. R. Zilberman
