⟦folio 33⟧

TASKS

In this issue we present the problems proposed at the final rounds of the All-Round Mathematical and Physical Optimization in April of this year.

Solutions to the problems in this issue can be sent no later than September 15 to the address: 117071, Moscow V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka publishing house, journal editorial office «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, indicate the solutions to which problems you are sending (for example: “Problem «Kvant», M151, F153.)

Send solutions to problems in mathematics and physics, as well as new problems in each subject, in separate envelopes. At the beginning of your letters, please indicate your last name, first name, patronymic, home address (as well as the class and school you attend).

M151. Each of the nine prims divides the square into two quadrangles, the areas of which are in the ratio 2:3. Prove that at least three of these nine primors pass through the same point. (8th, 10th grade).

B. M. Ivlev

M152. Let $a, b, m, n$ be natural numbers, and let $a$ be coprime with $b$ and $a > 1$. Prove that if $a^m + b^m$ is divisible by $a^n + b^n$, then $m$ is divisible by $n$ ($9^*$), grade 10).

D. Yu. Grigoriev.

M153. The two play the next game. One names the number, and the other places it according to his own discretion along with one of the asterisks in the following difference:

* * * *

* * * *

Then the first one calls out another number and so on 8 times until all the asterisks are replaced with numbers.

*) In 9th grade this problem was proposed for the special case $b=1$

The one who names the numbers strives to make the difference as large as possible, and the second - to make it as small as possible. Prove that

a) the second can arrange the numbers so that the resulting difference becomes no more than 4000, regardless of what numbers are called;

b) the first can name the numbers so that the difference becomes at least 4000, regardless of where the second places the numbers (8 grades).

Yu. I. Ionin.

M154. There are 50 segments on a straight line. Prove that at least one of the following statements is true:

a) some general possibilities;

b) there is a possibility, no two of which have a common point (8, 9 cells).

A. G. Kharazishvili.
