All atoms are in this stable state under normal conditions. When energy is absorbed, the atom goes into one of the emerging states $E_2$, $E_3$, $E_4$, ... The way an atom appears can be very different: irradiation with fast electrons, thermal exposure, exposure to light quanta or electric discharge. In all cases of occurrence, energy is transferred to the electrons of the atom, therefore they often speak not about transitions of the atom from one level to another, but about transitions of the electron. The excited states of the atom are unstable. During the time $\tau \approx 10^{-8}$ s, the atom goes into the ground state, emitting energy in the form of quanta of the electromagnetic field. Quantum energy $E = 2\pi hv = E_2 - E_1$.

Consider a hydrogen atom, calculated from atoms, consisting of a proton and an electron associated with it. Energy of any level can be found from the following formula:

$$E_n = -\frac{13,59}{n^2} \text{эв},$$

where $n=1, 2, \dots$ is the so-called principal quantum number, characterizing the energy of the level. In the unexcited state of the atom, the electron is at the level c $n=1$. When excited, the electron may end up at the c $n=2, 3, \dots$ level. It is clear from the formula that with increasing excitation energy, the distance between adjacent levels (that is, the energy difference) decreases. A negative energy value $E_n$ corresponds to bound states of the electron. Consequently, as long as the electron is held in the field of the atom, the spectrum has a discontinuous (discrete) character. By giving the hydrogen atom an energy greater than the value $E = 13,59 \text{эв}$, we will lower it.

When an electron moves from a higher level to a lower one, a quantum of light is emitted. Its energy is equal to the energy difference between the levels:

$$E_{n_i n_k} = E_{n_i} - E_{n_k} = 13,59 \left( \frac{1}{n_k^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2} \right) (\text{эв}).$$

Knowing the energy of the quantum, we can find the wavelength of the emitted light observed in the spectroscope:

$$\lambda = \frac{2\pi hc}{E} (\text{см}).$$
