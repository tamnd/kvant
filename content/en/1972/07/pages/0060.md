COLLECTOR'S CORNER

ATOMIC ENERGY
IN THE SERVICE OF PEACE

The use of atomic energy for peaceful purposes began in July 1954, when the nuclear power plant of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Obchinsk went into operation in the Soviet Union. This is actually associated with three stamps issued in January 1956. You can see one of these stamps, which shows the appearance of this power plant, in the background.

Particular benefits come from installing nuclear reactors on ships. Thus, a little more than 80 kg of atomic fuel is enough for the Lenin icebreaker to sail all year round without visiting a port for refueling. In the background is a stamp with an image of the nuclear-powered icebreaker "Lenin".

In 1962, the Soviet Union issued a series of two stamps “Atomic Energy in the Service of Peace.” You see these stamps in the background. On one of them, in the center, there is a siligent of a high-rise university building, and at the edges there are image conditions, symbolizing the use of atoms in metallurgy, medicine, agriculture and other areas. On the second stamp there is an outline map of the USSR with conditions depicting atoms.

In 1963, a stamp was issued in our country on the theme “Atom for the World”, with the image of atoms against the background of power lines. This brand is shown in the background. Stamps symbolizing the use of atomic energy for peaceful purposes were issued in many countries around the world. For example, a stamp of Czechoslovakia (see photo in the upper right corner), issued in 1954 in the “Czechoslovak Mechanical Engineering” series, is of interest. It depicts factory buildings, and in the foreground is a steam engine and a diagram of the atoms structure. The text on the stamp reads: “From the steam engine to the nuclear power plant.”

The photo also shows stamps from Indonesia, Poland, Yugoslavia and a United Nations stamp with the symbolic image of atoms.

A. V. Alitys
