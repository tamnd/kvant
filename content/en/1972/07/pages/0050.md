Solving problems in electrostatics
(electric capacity)

G. Ya. Myakishev

Electrical capacity is the last topic in the Electrostatics section. When solving problems on this topic, all the information obtained from the study of electrostatics may be required: conservation of electric charge, the concepts of field strength and potential, the behavior of conductors in an electrostatic field, changes in field strength in dielectrics, the law of conservation of energy. Therefore, the tasks are obviously figurative. However, if the previous concepts are well understood, then you should not encounter any particular difficulties when solving problems on electrical capacity.

The introduction of the concept of electrical capacity is based on the fact that all points of a conductor, when a charge Q is imparted to it, acquire the same potential φ. The ratio of charge to potential does not depend on the amount of charge and is called the electrical capacitance of a single conductor:

$$C = \frac{Q}{\varphi}.$$

The capacity of a single conducting ball (in vacuum) in the SGSE system is equal to its radius:

$$C = R.$$

The capacitance of a sphere in a dielectric in the SI system is equal to:

$$C = 4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R.$$

The most interesting is the capacitance of a system of two conductors, the charges of which are equal in magnitude and have opposite signs. Such a system is called condenser

sator, and its capacity is equal to:

$$C = \frac{Q}{\Delta \varphi},$$

where Q is the charge of one of the capacitor plates, and Δφ is the potential difference between the plates. The capacitance of the capacitor is practically independent of surrounding bodies.

For parallel plate capacitor

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon S}{4\pi d} \left( \Gamma C \partial\right); \quad C = \frac{\varepsilon \varepsilon_0 S}{d} \left( \mathrm{C I}\right).$$

Here S is the area of ​​the plates, and d is the distance between them.

The energy of a capacitor can be calculated using one of three formulas:

$$\Pi = \frac{Q \Delta \varphi}{2} = \frac{Q^2}{2C} = \frac{C (\Delta \varphi)^2}{2},$$

that is, for any pair of values ​​out of three, the values ​​are Q, C, Δφ. When connecting capacitors in parallel, their capacitances are added up:

$$C = \sum_i C_i.$$

When connected in series, the reciprocal of the battery capacitance is equal to the sum of the reciprocals of the capacitance of the individual capacitors:

$$\frac{1}{C} = \sum_i \frac{1}{C_i}.$$

Let us recall the basic concepts and formulas, and now move on to solving specific problems.
