Electromagnetic induction

L. G. Aspamazov

The solution to problems on electromagnetic induction is based on the use of Farad's law: if the magnetic flux, an arbitrary circuit, changes by values ​​$\Delta \Phi$ during the time $\Delta t$, then an electromotive force $E_{\text{инд}} = k \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$ is induced in the circuit (k is the number of lentic coefficients, depending on the choice of system of units). In this form, we denote by $\Delta \Phi$ the abscultaneous magnitude of the change in magnetic flux, considering $E_{\text{инд}}$ to be always positive.

The direction of the current induced in the circuit is induced according to Lenz's rules: the current is directed so that its magnetic field counteracts the change in the magnetic flux.

The magnetic flux is equal to the product of the magnetic field induction component $B_1$, perpendicular to the plane of the circuit, and the area of ​​the circuit $S$ (Fig. 1): $\Phi = B_1 S = BS \cos \alpha$. Consequently, a change in magnetic flux can occur due to a change in the magnetic field induction, the area of ​​the circuit or its orientation.
