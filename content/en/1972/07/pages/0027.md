Thus, it is clear that by changing the correspondence of the amplitudes, different ellipses can be obtained.

Let now $\omega_1 = 2\omega$, $\omega_2 = \omega$, $\varphi'_1 = 0$ and $\varphi'_2 = 0$. then the system of equation (3) takes the form

$$x = A_1 \cos 2\omega t,$$
$$y = A_2 \sin \omega t.$$

Let us transform the equation for $x$ as follows:

$$x = A_1 \left( \cos^2 \omega t - \sin^2 \omega t \right) =$$
$$= A_1 \left( 1 - 2 \sin^2 \omega t \right) = A_1 \left( 1 - 2 \frac{y^2}{A_2^2} \right).$$

This curve is part of a parabola with its axis along the $X$ axis and its vertex at $x = A_1$ (Fig. 5). Thus, we have an open curve.

Let us now consider the influence of frequencies on the shape of the trajectory, and take the amplitudes of the transverse and longitudinal oscillations described by the system of equation (3) to be the same.

Let us construct, for example, curves corresponding to the equations

$$\begin{cases} x = A \cos \omega t, \\ y = A \sin 2\omega t, \end{cases}$$

The easiest way to do it is this way. Take a circle of radius $A$ (Fig. 6), mark on it the points corresponding to angles $\omega t$ equal to 0, $\frac{\pi}{8}, \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{3\pi}{8}, \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{5\pi}{8}, \frac{3\pi}{4}, \frac{7\pi}{8}, \pi, \dots, 2\pi.$

*) That the system of equations

$$\begin{cases} x = A_1 \cos \omega t, \\ y = A_2 \sin \omega t \end{cases}$$

describes an ellipse, can be shown in analysis:

$$\frac{x^2}{A_1^2} + \frac{y^2}{A_2^2} = \cos^2 \omega t + \sin^2 \omega t = 1,$$

that is, the point with coordinates $x$ and $y$ lies on the ellipse. (See I. N. Bronstein’s article “Ellipse”, published in «Kvant» No. 9, 1970)
