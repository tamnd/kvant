If we express the creatures of the story in the modern language of mechanics, then it has properties to the following.

Descartes believed that a body that has a greater value of $mv$ has a greater “force” of motion or a greater amount of motion. And here's why: if we take two positive bodies with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ and subject them to the action of forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ for the same period of time $t$, then the ratio of forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ will be equal

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{m_1 a_1}{m_2 a_2}.$$

Let's multiply and divide the right side of this equation by $t$. Then we get

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{m_1 a_1 t}{m_2 a_2 t}.$$

But $a_1 t = v_1$, and $a_2 t = v_2$. That's why

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{m_1 v_1}{m_2 v_2}.$$

Since the ratio of forces is equal to the ratio of the magnitude $mv$, then $mv$ is the amount of motion (“silla” of motion).

Leibniz reasons as follows: if we take two bodies with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ and subject them to the action of forces $F_1$ and $F_2$ so that under the action of these forces both bodies travel the same distance $S$, then the ratio of the forces will be equal

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{m_1 a_1}{m_2 a_2}.$$

Multiplying and dividing the right side of this equality by $S$, we get

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{m_1 a_1 S}{m_2 a_2 S}.$$

But, as is known, $a_1 S = \frac{v_1^2}{2}$, and

$$a_2 S = \frac{v_2^2}{2}.$$

$$\frac{F_1}{F_2} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2}{\frac{1}{2} m_2 v_2^2} = \frac{m_1 v_1^2}{m_2 v_2^2}.$$

Since the ratio of force is equal to the ratio of magnitude $mv^2$, then $mv^2$ expresses the “force” of movement, or the amount of motion.

For the Cartesians, therefore, the “force” of motion is the magnitude $mv$, and for Leibniz and his side - the magnitude $mv^2$. In 1695, during a dispute, Leibniz proposed the name “living force” for the quantity $mv^2$ (more precisely, for $\frac{mv^2}{2}$), and it has not yet fallen out of use. In the language of modern mechanics, $\frac{mv^2}{2}$ is the kinetic energy of a body. A force applied to a body changes the kinetic energy of the system, and this change is equal to the work of the force, that is, equal to FS. The value $mv$ is the momentum of the body. It also changes under the action of a force applied to the body, and its change is equal to the impulse of the force, that is, equal to $Ft$.

Who was right in this dispute?

Strange as it may seem, Urgent.net should not be victorious in this. Both sides were right and wrong. They are right because both quantities - $mv$ and $mv^2$ are important characteristics of movement. The value of the quantity $mv$ determines the time during which the initially suspended body under the influence of a given force changes speed from zero to $v$. The value $mv^2$ (more precisely, the value $\frac{mv^2}{2}$) determines the distance that the body must travel under the influence of a given force while its speed changes from zero to $v$.
