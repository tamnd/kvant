Solution. This problem can be solved using planimetrics, but we, being true to the theme, will analyze the solution to this problem using trigonometry (we recommend the reader to try to find a planimetric solution). Let us denote by $\alpha$ one of the angles of the triangle, $2\alpha$ the other angle, and the opposite sides, respectively, $x$ and $x+2$ (Fig. 8).

By the theorem of sines $\frac{x}{\sin \alpha} =$
$$\frac{5}{\sin(180^\circ - 3\alpha)} = \frac{x+2}{\sin 2a};$$ from here $x = \frac{5\sin \alpha}{\sin 3\alpha},$
$x+2 = \frac{5\sin 2\alpha}{\sin 3\alpha}$.

The Teler problem is reduced to solving the trigonometric equation $\frac{5\sin 2\alpha}{\sin 3\alpha} - \frac{5\sin \alpha}{\sin 3\alpha} = 2$. Applying the formula $\sin 3\alpha = 3\sin \alpha - 4\sin^3\alpha$, we obtain $\frac{10\cos \alpha}{3-4\sin^2\alpha} - \frac{5}{3-4\sin^2\alpha} = 2$ and further $8\cos^2\alpha - 10\cos \alpha + 3 = 0; \quad (\cos \alpha)_1 = \frac{3}{4},$
$(\cos \alpha)_2 = \frac{1}{2}$. But according to the meaning of the problem, $\alpha < 60^\circ$, therefore, $\cos \alpha > \frac{1}{2}$. Therefore, from the values ​​found, $\cos \alpha = \frac{3}{4}$ is suitable.

We further have $\sin \alpha = \sqrt{1-\cos^2\alpha} = \frac{\sqrt{7}}{4}, \quad x = \frac{5}{3-4\sin^2\alpha} = 4, \quad x+2 = 6$.

Exercises

1. In triangle $ABC$, angle $A$ is equal to $\alpha$, side $BC$ is equal to $\alpha$. Find the length of the bisector $AD$ if the angle between $AD$ and the height $AE$ is $\beta$.

2. The base of the rectiline is an equilateral trapezoid, the acute angle of which is equal to $\alpha$, and the area is equal to $S$. All side faces are inclined to the base plane at an angle $\beta$. Find the volume of the straight line.

3. The generatrix of the cone is equal to $l$ and makes an angle $\alpha$ with the height. A plane is drawn through two generatrices of the cone, the angle between which is equal to $\beta$. Find the distance from this plane to the center of the ball inscribed in the cone.

4. The altitude of the triangle, equal to $6\text{ cm}$, divides the angle of the triangle in the ratio $2:1$, and the side into segments, the less of which is equal to $3\text{ cm}$. Determine the sides of the triangle.

5. A regular triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in a circle. An arbitrary point $M$ is taken on arc $BC$ and connected by chords to the vertices of the triangle. Prove that $MA = MB + MC$.

6. Prove that the sum of the squares of the diagonals of a trapezoid is equal to twice the product of its base added to the sum of the squares of the lateral sides.

7. Plane angles of a triangular angle are equal to the corresponding angles of a triangular angle.

8. Given a triangle $ABC$, on side $AC$ a point $E$ is taken so that $AE:EC = a$, on side $AB$ a point $D$ is taken so that $AD:DB = b$. Segments $CD$ and $BE$ were carried out. Find the ratio of the area of ​​the resulting quadrilateral to the area of ​​the given triangle (MIET, 1970).

9. Two mutually perpendicular diameters $AB$ and $CD$ are drawn in a circle. The chord $AE$ intersects $CD$ at the point $K$ such that $CK:KD = 2:1$. The chord $EC$ intersects $AB$ at point $M$. Prove that $AM:MB = 3:1$ (Fig. 9).
