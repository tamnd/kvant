The transfer of electricity along the circuit is accompanied by the release of a Joule body. Let's consider, for example, the following problem.

Task 5. A conductor of length $l$ (Fig. 4) slides along horizontal rails located in a vertical magnetic field with indication $B$. The ends of the rails are closed to resistance $R$. Determine the power of heat loss through resistance if the conductor moves at a constant speed $v$. Neglect the resistance of the rails and conductor.

Let us find the change in magnetic flux through the circuit formed by a moving conductor, rails and resistance during time $\Delta t$. When the conductor moves, the area of ​​the circuit increases by values ​​$\Delta S = l v \cdot \Delta t$. Change of flow $\Delta \Phi = B \cdot \Delta S = Bl v \cdot \Delta t$. That's why

$$E_{\text{инд}} = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = Bl v$$

The heat released at the resistance $R$ is determined according to the Joule-Lenz law: $Q = I^2 R t$. Heat loss power

$$W = \frac{Q}{t} = I^2 R = B^2 l^2 v^2 / R.$$

It is useful to understand due to what energy heat is released at the resistance.

As you know, a force (Ampere’s law) acts on a conductor carrying current in a magnetic field. In our case (the magnetic field is perpendicular to the conductor) this force is equal to $Bl I$ and is directed opposite to the speed. According to the conditions of disassembly at a constant speed $3\Omega$, this means that a force $F$ is applied to it, balancing the braking force from the magnetic field. The work of force $F$ turns into heat, which is released at the resistance. The power of this force is equal to the power of heat loss:

$$N = F \cdot v = Bl I v = B^2 l^2 v^2 / R = W.$$

From the obtained formulas it follows that as the resistance $R$ increases, the current in the counter will decrease ($I = Bl v/R$). However, the voltage drop across the resistance, equal to the potential difference at the ends of the conductor, remains unchanged ($U = IR = Bl v$).
