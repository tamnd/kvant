Exercises

1. Prove that no convex polygon (cannot be expressed as a line segment) can be covered by two polygons equal to the given one and smaller than it.

2. Let us denote the smallest number of a polygon M and the smallest number of its polygons that can be completely covered by M by s(M); then s(T)=4, where T is a tetrahedron, and s(K)=8, where K is a cube.

Also indicate three polygons A, B and Γ, such that

s(A)=5, s(B)=6, s(Γ)=7.

3. Prove that no (not expressed in a flat polygon) convex polygon M can be covered by a third of a polygon M, ready for a given M and less than M.

4. Prove that every convex polygon can be covered by the following convex polygon M:

a) a triangle with sides a, b and c (the required number, of course, may depend on the values ​​of a, b and c);

b) rectangular with sides m and n;

b*) parallelogram with sides m and n and angle α≤90°?
