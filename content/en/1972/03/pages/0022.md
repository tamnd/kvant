figure 4 shows a square with four equal right triangles highlighted on it. It was from this figure that the Indian mathematician Bhaska-Acharna based his proof in the 12th century.

Let the side of the large square (also the hypotenуza of the right triangle, colored here in yellow) be equal to $c$. Let also two of its katat be equal to $a$ and $b$, respectively. Then, in accordance with the drawing, $(a-b)^2 + \frac{4ab}{2} = c^2$, that is, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. Consequently, if the triangle is right-angled, then the sum of the squares of its real catatas is equal to the square of the hypotenуzy.

At figure 5 one of the three given right-angled triangles is enclosing. All three triangles are pairwise similar. This is the key to the proof, either the areas of similar figures, built respectively on the catatas and hypotenуze of a given right triangle, are in the same ratio as the areas of the squares built on these catatas and hypotenуze. In other words, with the help of figure we obtain the equality $ka^2 + kb^2 = kc^2$, where $a$ and $b$ are the catata of the enclosing triangle, $c$ is its hypotenуza, $k$ is a number equal to the ratio of the area of ​​the enclosing triangle to the area of ​​the square built on its hypotenуze. Reducing both sides of the equality by $k$, we obtain, as a consequence, the Pythagorean theorem.
