⟦folio 24⟧

Geskanma environment

A. P. Savin

Playing tic-tac-toe on checkered paper has long become one of the favorite pastimes of schoolchildren and students, since they always have a pen or pencil and a piece of checkered paper at hand.

In problem M91 a new version of this game was proposed.

M91. Two people play tic-tac-toe on an endless sheet of checkered paper. The beginner puts a cross in any cell. Each of his next moves must place a cross in any free cell; any cell adjacent to one is considered to be any cell that has a common side or a common vertex with it. The second player, with each turn, can place three polykkas at once in any three free cells (not necessarily next to each other). Prove that, no matter how the first one plays, the second one can “lock him up”: make sure that the first one has nowhere else to put a cross.

Explore similar games in which the second player is allowed to place not three, but only two or only one polyk in one turn. What will be the result here if the partners play correctly: will the players be able to “lock” the crosses (and what is the cleanest move that the crosses can “hold out”) or can the game continue indefinitely?

Try to study other variants of this game: when only cells that have a common side with it are considered adjacent to a given one; when the plane is divided not into squares, but into regular hexagons; when the first one is allowed to put p crosses at once, and the second one - q crosses.

The actions of the crosses are very similar to the actions of the ten trying to avoid being surrounded, which is why we called this game “surrounding the ten.”
