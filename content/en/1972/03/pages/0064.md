We can write the law of conservation of momentum:

$$\frac{m}{2} u_1 \cos \alpha + \frac{m}{2} u_2 \cos \beta = mv$$

for pulse components along the $x$ axis,

$$\frac{m}{2} u_1 \sin \alpha - \frac{m}{2} u_2 \sin \beta = 0$$

for pulse components along the $y$ axis.

From these equations we find

$$u_2 = \sqrt{u_1^2 + 4v^2 - 4vu_1 \cos \alpha},$$

$$\sin \beta = \frac{u_1}{\sqrt{u_1^2 + 4v^2 - 4vu_1 \cos \alpha}} \sin \alpha.$$

If the system is not isolated and a certain force $F$ acts on it, then the total momentum of the system is not conserved. However, the impulse component in the direction perpendicular to the force $F$ is preserved. This is the basis for solving a large number of problems. Let's solve, for example, the following problem.

A gun is mounted on a railway platform moving at speed $v$. The gun barrel is directed towards the movement of the platform and raised above the horizon. The gun fired, after which the speed of the platform decreased by $n$ times. Find the speed $u$ of the projectile (relative to the ground) if it leaves the barrel at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal. The mass of the projectile is $m$, the mass of the platform with the gun (without the projectile) is $M$.

The weapon-platform-projectile system is not isolated: it is affected by gravity and the reaction force of the Earth. However, in the horizontal direction, external forces do not act on the platform with the cannon and the projectile. This means that the horizontal component of the system’s impulse should not change during the shot. That's why

$$mu \cos \alpha + M \frac{v}{n} = (M + m) v.$$

From here

$$u = v \frac{mv + M \frac{n - 1}{n}}{m \cos \alpha}.$$

If the system of a part or bodies is isolated and its momentum does not change, then the speed of the center of mass of the system (center of gravity) does not change. In part, if at some moment the system was moving in such a way that the speed of the center of mass was equal to zero, then this speed remains equal to zero all the time. Therefore, the position of the center of mass does not change. Let's look at an example.

A man of mass $m$ is at the stern of a boat of mass $M$, standing in the ore. Boat length $L$. How much will the boat move relative to the shore if a person moves from the stern of the boat to the bow?

Since no forces act in the horizontal direction on the boat-man system, the position of its center of mass must be preserved. But the position of the center of mass of the system is determined by the position of the center of mass of the boat and the person (Fig. 9). Let initially the distance between the center of mass of the system ($O_c$) and the center of mass of the boat ($O_\pi$) be equal to $x$.
