The neutrons produced in the reactor have significantly different energies. However, a pulsed reactor makes it possible to easily isolate neutrons with a certain energy value, that is, to carry out so-called neutron electrometry. To do this, it is enough to turn on the recording equipment at the moment when neutrons of the energy we need approach it (faster neutrons will pass earlier, slower neutrons will pass later).

To release neutrons with a given energy in Dubna, a 1000-meter metal tube was laid from the IBR reactor to the hall where the experiments are carried out, from which the air was evacuated. The long path length results in the separation of neutrons by energy (near the reactor, neutrons of different energies travel very close to each other), and the labor transforms the scattering of neutrons.

With an average power of about 25 kilowatts, the IBR reactor in many experiments replaces a conventional continuous reactor with a power of up to 100,000 kilowatts, and in some cases up to 1,000,000 kilowatts.

The IBR reactor has carried out valuable research in the field of atomic nuclear physics and solid state physics, which has gained wide international fame.

Problems involving the interaction of several bodies present a huge difficulty for theoretical physics. Such problems are especially difficult in the field of quantum mechanics, where the very nature of interacting particles is much more complex than that of bodies studied by ordinary Newtonian mechanics. Such problems do not have exact solutions, and their approximate solutions require extensive computational work. Leningrad theoretical physicist L.D. Faddeev, in a series of works “Correct formulation and study of the quantum problem of three parts” (“Faddeev’s Equations”), found ways to accurately solve such problems. The method he developed makes it possible to solve many complex problems in the field of atomic and nuclear physics, as well as elementary particle physics. This method is used to calculate such systems as an atom and two electrons interacting with it, three interacting nuclei (prototypes or neutrons), a nucleus and two nuclei of parts interacting with it, etc. Recently, this new method of calculation was able to be extended to the case of interaction of four or more parts. For the successful solution of a whole range of problems in quantum mechanics, L.D. Faddeev was awarded the State Prize.

A group of Soviet astronomers consisting of E. P. Aksenov, V. G. Demin, G. N. Duboshin, E. A. Grebennikov and M. D. Kislik was awarded the State Prize for “a series of works on modern problems and methods of celestial mechanics, published in 1958-1968.”

When solving various problems about the motion of planets and artificial satellites in celestial mechanics, they usually proceed as follows. As a first approximation, the trajectory of the celestial body under study is considered elliptical. Then corrections and clarifications are made related to the influence of various odd stationary objects (or, as astronomers say, disturbances). These include: asymmetry of the central gravitational body (for example, the Earth), the influence of neighboring planets, the gravitation of the Sun, the own rotation of the cosmic body under study, etc. In this case, the initial trajectory of movement is distorted: the ellipse begins to show, rotate in the orbital plane, which, in turn, also changes its spatial orientation. Taking into account disturbances gives a complex trajectory of motion.

However, these approximate methods for calculating the trajectory of celestial objects are suitable only on the condition that disturbances in the state of other objects, as well as deviations in the distance between shapes and the uneven distribution of the masses of their internal ones, are insignificant. They are unable to take into account the interaction between the translational and rotational motion of the distance of bodies. In reality, even when studying the motion of the long-lasting satellites of the Earth and the Moon, it is impossible to calculate their orbits for a long period using methods either extremely difficult or completely impossible. It was not possible to obtain reliable results for the satellites of Mars, Jupiter, the so-called “resonant” astrojudes, comets passing close to Jupiter.

The authors of the award-winning series of works proposed a completely new method for solving such problems. They worked away from Keller's elliptical orbits and found more non-elliptical orbits, used as a first approximation. The models of the celestial granitic surface he constructed describe the granitic fields of the Earth, Moon and other celestial bodies more accurately than in classical mechanics. The perturbation theory created by the authors allows us to take into account the mutual influence of the simultaneous occurrence of translational and rotational movements of celestial bodies.

Using this theory, it is possible to calculate over long periods of time the exact orbits of artificial satellites of the Earth, taking into account the inhomogeneities of its gravitational field, the disturbing influence of the Moon and the Sun, the light pressure of solar rays and a number of other factors. It was also possible to obtain fairly accurate solutions to the problems of the motion of the satellites of Mars Phobos and Deimos, one of the closest satellites of Jupiter, “resonant” astrojudes, and comets that come close to Jupiter.
