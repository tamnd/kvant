The branch of geometry, to which the problems considered in this note relate, arose relatively recently - just some 15-20 years ago. This section is called combinatorial geometry; it is characterized by the simplicity of the conditions of large_instances of the problem under consideration (often understandable to any schoolchild!), but, unfortunately, the solutions to these problems are, as a rule, quite complex. Today, dozens of books and hundreds, if not thousands, of scientific articles are devoted to combinatorial geometry. Some books available to schoolchildren are listed at the end of this note.

All the problems posed below relate to combinatorial geometry; they are relevant to the topic of this note. Asterisks are removed from problems whose solution is not known to the author of the note; Here I would especially like to draw attention to problem 6, which seems to me very interesting (but perhaps also very difficult!).

Exercises

1. Prove that no convex polygon (cannot be expressed as a line segment) can be covered by two polygons equal to the given one and smaller than it.

2. Let us denote the smallest number of a polygon M and the smallest number of its polygons that can be completely covered by M by s(M); then s(T)=4, where T is a tetrahedron, and s(K)=8, where K is a cube.

Also indicate three polygons A, B and Γ, such that

s(A)=5, s(B)=6, s(Γ)=7.

3. Prove that no (not expressed in a flat polygon) convex polygon M can be covered by a third of a polygon M, ready for a given M and less than M.

4. Prove that every convex polygon can be covered by the following convex polygon M:

a) a triangle with sides a, b and c (the required number, of course, may depend on the values ​​of a, b and c);

b) rectangular with sides m and n;

b*) parallelogram with sides m and n and angle α≤90°?

6*). From the result of Problem 4 it follows that the minimum number less than a convex polygon M and similar polygons that can cover it is always equal to 2 or 3. Characterize the class of those polygons M for which this number is equal to 2.
