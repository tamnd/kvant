In tabletry it is a meorem, in stereometry it is an unsolved problem.

I. M. Yaglom

Issue 6 of the magazine «Kvant» for 1971 contained the following problem (problem M89):

Prove that in any convex polygon, except a parallelogram, you can also choose three sides, which, when extended, form a triangle that encloses the given polygon. (For example, at figure 1, where the polygon is outlined with black lines, the three red lines satisfy the required condition, but the three blue lines do not.)

This problem has many different solutions. However, before we tell her decision, let's talk about why she is interesting.

On figure 2 you see triangle ABC and three smaller triangles $Ab_1c_1$, $Ba_1c_2$ and $Ca_2b_2$, similar to ABC and resulting from ABC by slopes to points A, B and C - the vertices of the original triangle. Triangles $Ab_1c_1$, $Ba_1c_2$ and $Ca_2b_2$ are not only similar to ABC - they are ABC-ready, that is, similar to ABC and located parallel to it. (I later learned that the segments corresponding to each other, say, in similar triangles ABC and $Ab_1c_1$ are figuratively parallel to each other.) It is clear that if the triangles $Ab_1c_1$, $Ba_1c_2$ and $Ca_2b_2$ are slightly smaller than the original triangle ABC, then they completely cover triangle ABC.
