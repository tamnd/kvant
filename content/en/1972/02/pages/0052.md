F95

The surface of the river forms an inclined plane. Is it possible for a word to swim down a river at a speed exceeding the speed of the current?

If the body does not move relative to the water, then the friction force acting on it from the side of the water is zero. Therefore, the component of the gravity force, parallel to the surface of the water (Fig. 14), will straighten the body until it is balanced by the friction force on the water (which at low speeds is proportional to the speed of the body). In this case, it will flow at a speed greater than the speed of the current.

Now imagine that we, having left a thin, rigid, weightless shell, removed the body and poured water into the shell so that its volume was equal to the volume of the immersed part of the body (Fig. 15). Since the body was floating, the mass of the poured water is equal to the mass of the body. Therefore, the shell with water is equivalent to the body that we removed. But the water poured into the shell is no different from the water in the river. The friction in a fluid is related only to the thickness of the fluid itself and does not depend on the surface of the body. This is explained by the fact that a thin layer of water molecules “floats” to the surface of the body and the formation is determined by the interaction of water molecules with each other. This means that you can completely remove the shell, the friction force will not change. It turns out that the body must flow at a speed equal to the speed of the current.

What's really going on?

Let’s first understand what “flow speed” means. Due to friction, the speed of the river flow varies at different points in the river. It increases from day to surface and from the shore to the middle of the river. Therefore, the speed of the flow in the place where the plush is located along the body river is also different. This water in the shell differs from the surrounding water, because the speed of the water in the shell is the same at all points.

Let us assume that the speed of the body is equal to the speed of the current at the edge of the body, which is close to the shore. Then the speed of the flow of water passing through the body at other points is greater than the speed of the body. Because of this, the water should carry the body, increasing its speed. On the other hand, when the speed of the body increases, it should slow down due to the fact that the speed of its point, located close to the shore, is greater than the speed of the current and the friction force is converted into movement of the body. As a result, the body must flow at a speed greater than the minimum, but less than the maximum of the flow rates throughout the body.
