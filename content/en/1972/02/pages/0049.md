M88

What condition must the coefficients of the equation $x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ satisfy in order for its three roots to form an arithmetic progression?

Let's give the answer right away. The desired match is $c = \frac{ab}{3} - \frac{2}{27} a^3$ (or, what is the same, one of the roots must equal $-\frac{a}{3}$). Indeed, let $x_1, x_2$ and $x_3$ be the roots of our equation. Then, according to Vieta’s theorem,

$$x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = -a.$$

On the other hand, in order for $x_1, x_2, x_3$ to form an arithmetic progression, it is necessary and sufficient that

$$x_1 + x_3 = 2x_2.$$

Pose $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 3x_2$, opening $x_2 = -\frac{a}{3}$. Try to derive the conditions under which equations of the fourth degree form an arithmetic progression.

L. I. Moscoviome

F92

The mass hoop $m$ stands on the mass board $M$ (Fig. 10). The friction coefficient between the board and the hoop is $k$. The board lies on a smooth table. With what acceleration will the board move if the hoop is pulled with a force of $F$?

Let's write down the equation of motion of the hoops and boards (equation II of Newton's law).

In the horizontal direction, two forces act on the hoop - the force $F$ and the tread force $F_{TP}$ between the board and the hoop (see Fig. 10). The center of gravity of the body—in this case, the center of the hoop—moves as if all the forces acting on the body were applied to it. Therefore we can write that

$$ma_1 = F - F_{TP}$$

($a_1$ is the acceleration of the center of the hoop).
