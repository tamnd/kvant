Let $P$ and $N$ be two distinct points of a circle with center $O$; $A$ is the base of the perpendicular dropped from the point $O$ to the given line $l$, and $f$ is the mapping that assigns to each point $X$ the line $l$ the point $f(X)$ of the same line according to the following rule: if $X'$ is the point of intersection of the line $PX$ with a circle, $X''$ is the point of intersection of the straight line $NX'$ with $l$, then $f(X)$ is a point symmetrical to $X''$ relative to $A$. then the mapping $f$ coincides with its inverse, that is, $f(f(X))=X$ for all $X$ (Fig. 4).

For those who are familiar with the basic concepts of project geometry, we suggest trying to find a simple “projective” proof of this theorem (note that even in its formulation it is necessary to consider $l$ an extended line, that is, add an infinite remote point to $l$ - then the mappings in question will be one-to-one). For a particular case, the solution is given in I.M. Yaglom’s book “Geometric Transformations” (GITTL, 1956; part II, problem 177), but it only applies if the straight line $l$ intersects the circle, and uses the formula of “double relations”. A blueprint for another solution, “without computing,” appears on page 71 of this issue of the journal.
