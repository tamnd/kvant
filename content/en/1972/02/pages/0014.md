We will plot the pressure under which ice, water or steam is located along the vertical axis, and their temperature along the horizontal axis. Each point on the plane bounded by the coordinate axes $P$ and $t^\circ$ corresponds to a certain state of water (ice, water or steam).

Let's start with ice. The blue curve shows the dependence of the pressure under which ice melts on temperature. The higher the pressure, the lower the melting point. Let's move away from the blue curve a little to the right: at the same pressure we will increase the temperature. The ice will completely melt - the points of the plane to the right of the blue curve correspond to water. If you move to the left from the blue curve and reduce the temperature, the water will freeze and turn into ice. At the blue points of the curve (and only at these pressures and temperatures) ice and water are in equilibrium with each other - how much ice melts, how much water turns into ice. Therefore, the blue curve is called the equilibrium curve of ice and water.

If we increase the temperature of water at a constant pressure, we will find ourselves in a region of temperatures at which only steam can exist. This region of temperature and pressure is to the right of the red curve - the equilibrium curve of water and steam, or, in other words, the curve of the dependence of the boiling point of water on pressure. This curve, as can be seen from figure, shows that the lower the pressure under which water is, the lower its boiling point. We know this well - for example, high in the mountains, where the air pressure is low, it is difficult for a climber to boil an egg in boiling water, so we have to fry the eggs. At the top of Elbrus, where the pressure is equal to the atmospheric atmosphere, water boils at $82^\circ\text{C}$.
