⟦folio 28⟧

V. I. Kuznetsov

One day, builders are paving a road and find an ancient cave filled with volcanic rocks. It contained a warehouse of shoes - three hundred pairs of sandals made of woven grass. When, in what building was this amazing warehouse founded? There was no answer. It was obtained only a few years later, when a method was developed for determining the age of a product made of wood, bone and other materials containing carbon.

To understand how the time of creation of such products is determined, let us turn to the nuclear processes occurring in the upper layers of the atmosphere (at an altitude of about 15 kilometers).

The earth's atmosphere is continuously bombarded by cosmic rays. The bulk of cosmic radiation comes from fast-moving protons—the nuclei of hydrogen atoms. Their energy is enormous - it reaches billions of electron volts. Passing through the atmosphere, protons collide with the nuclei of atoms that make up the air. When atomic nuclei collide, they release neutrons and other particles. The resulting neutrons, in turn, interact with atmospheric matter, most often with nitrogen. In some cases, neutrons knock a proton out of the nitrogen nucleus (consisting of 7 protons and 7 neutrons) and take its place. As a result, nitrogen turns into an isotope of carbon, the nucleus of which contains 6 protons and 8 neutrons. The nuclear reaction that occurs can be written as follows:

$$\frac{14}{7}N + \frac{1}{0}n \rightarrow \frac{14}{6}C + \frac{1}{1}p.$$

The new atom has two more neutrons than the nucleus of the most common isotope of carbon, $^{12}$C. Heavy carbon $^{14}$C is called radiocarbon. Radiocarbon nuclei emit electrons (beta particles) and turn into nitrogen: $^{14}$C $\rightarrow^{14}$N. The rate of this process is characterized by the time during which half of the first initial number of atoms $^{14}$C is converted into nitrogen. This time is called the half-life and is usually designated $T_{\frac{1}{2}}$. For the isotope $^{14}$C $T_{\frac{1}{2}} = 5600$ years.
