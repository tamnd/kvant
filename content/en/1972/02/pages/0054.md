What is the maximum potential difference that can be obtained with a source with emf at its disposal? c.E and n identical capacitors with capacitance C each?

Let us first examine the simpler case with n=2.

By charging both capacitors from the source to a potential difference E and connecting the source and both capacitors together in series, a potential difference of 3E can be obtained. However, this is not the maximum potential difference. Let us prove that having two capacitors and a source, you can obtain a potential difference as close as you like to 4E.

We connect the source in series with one of the capacitors, and connect the second capacitor to this pair: connect the “—” of the capacitor to the “—” of the source, and connect the “+” of the capacitor to the “+” of the second capacitor (Fig. 17). In this case, the charge on the capacitors is redistributed. Denoting the charges on the condensates after redistribution through q₁ and q₂, and the voltage drop across the condensates through u₁ and u₂, we can write

$$E + u_1 = u_2.$$ (1)

The total charge of the right plates of the capacitor is 2CE. This charge is redistributed between the plates, but cannot change. That's why

$$q_1 + q_2 = 2CE.$$ (2)

Solving equations (1) and (2) together, we find

$$u_2 = \frac{3}{2} E.$$

Let's charge capacitor 1 again to potential difference E and connect the same circuit. Now u₂ becomes equal to $\frac{4}{7} E$.

By repeating this operation many times, you can charge capacitor 2 to a potential difference arbitrarily close to 2E. Now connecting the source and both capacitors in series, we get the difference

figure 18a shows V-T diagrams of two circular processes. In which of them does the gas do more work: in the process 1-2-3-1 or in the process 1-3-4-1?

Let us redraw the diagrams of the processes shown on figure in coordinates P→V.

Points 1 and 3 lie on the same isotherm, and point 1 corresponds to a smaller volume than point 3. Therefore, having drawn the isotherm, we select two points on it - points 1 and 3, as shown in figure 18, 6. Now it is not difficult to construct cycle diagrams. Line 1-2 is an isobar, and the volume at point 2 is greater than the volume at point 1. Line 2-3 is an isochore. On a P-V diagram it should be vertical. The diagram of the second process is constructed in exactly the same way.

We know that the work done by the gas per cycle is equal to the area of ​​the figure limited by the cycle graph. But from figure it is clear that the area of ​​the “red” process is larger than the area of ​​the “gray” process. This means that more work is done by the gas during the 1-2-3-1 process.
