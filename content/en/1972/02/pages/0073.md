To problem M84

It is natural to imagine a project prim, an additional infinite point at a distance, as a circle (when point X runs through the prim, then X' goes through a full revolution around the circle).

If point X moves in one direction, then Y=f(X) moves in the opposite direction, therefore the mapping f has two fixed points Z₁ and Z₂ such that f(Z₁)=Z₁; f(Z₂)=Z₂.
(This is true for any continuous one-to-one mapping f of a circle onto itself, changing the direction of the traversal.

If point L is an infinite distant point of line l, then it is easy to check that f(f(L))=L, and f(L)≠L. The mapping X−f(f(X)) is projective and has three fixed points: Z₁, Z₂ and L, so f(f(X))=X for all X.
