keep only the $(1/2)^n$ part of the $^{14}\text{C}$ atoms:

$$N = N_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n = N_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{T_{1/2}}}.$$

(1)

Here $N_0$ is the number of radiocarbon atoms at the initial moment, $N$ is their number at time $t$.

The half-life of radiocarbon is a value of the same order as the age of a person in a culture. History has reliable data only within 5000 years. Knowing the number of radiocarbon decays per unit volume or weight per unit time in the atmosphere, water and living materials, it is not difficult to calculate how much of it decays per year and how much of it is contained on Earth. Calculations have shown that there are only about 60 tons of radiocarbon on Earth.

For every thousand billion atoms of earth's carbon there is one atom $^{14}\text{C}$. Radiocarbon atoms, together with common carbon atoms, are produced by plants, and from there they enter the organisms of animals and humans. In any animal organism, radiocarbon is continuously decayed and replenished. In every gram of carbon in a living organism, approximately 14 radiocarbon atoms disintegrate per minute. The concentration of isotone $^{14}\text{C}$, as a rule, is the same in all carbon that is part of living organisms.

If an object is made from a tree cut down approximately 5,600 years ago, then in a gram of carbon released from it, the radioactive radiation counter will register not 14, but about 7 decays per minute. Based on the number of decays, the age of objects made of wood, as well as animal bones and plant remains can be calculated. To do this, you need to use formula (1). From this formula it follows that

$$\frac{N}{N_0} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{T_{1/2}}};$$

$$\lg \frac{N}{N_0} = \frac{t}{T_{1/2}} \lg \left(\frac{1}{2}\right);$$

$$t = T_{1/2} \frac{\lg(N/N_0)}{\lg(1/2)} = T_{1/2} \frac{\lg(N_0/N)}{\lg 2}.$$

The number of decays per minute $A$ is proportional to the number of atoms $N$. So, at the initial moment of time $A_0 = kN_0 = 14$, and at the moment of time $t$, when the number of atoms is $N$, $A = kN$. Hence,

$$N = A/k, N_0 = A_0/k = 14/k,$$

And

$$N_0/N = 14/A$$

$$t = T_{1/2} \frac{\lg(14/A)}{\lg 2} = 18400 \lg(14/A).$$

Here $t$ is age in years.
