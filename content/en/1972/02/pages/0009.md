The question naturally arises: is not any of the indicated properties 1-3 logical a consequence of the other two (in this case we would not have to check all three properties)?

It turns out that this is not so: the properties of reflectivity, symmetry and transnitivity are independent. To prove that a certain property does not depend on the other two, it is enough to give an example of a relation that has the last two properties, but does not have the one in question.

For example, the relation ≤ to the set of real numbers is reflective and transnitive, but not symmetric. This means that symmetry is not a consequence of reflectivity and transnitivity. The property of transnitivity is also not derived from the other two. For example, for lines in a plane, the relation “line $x_1$ coincides with line $x_2$ or perpendicular to $x_2$” is reflective and symmetrical, but not transnitive. (Check!)
