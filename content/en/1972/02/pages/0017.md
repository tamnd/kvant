Are rivers possible on the Moon?

At first glance, no. If water is in a solid or gaseous state, then how can it flow? However, upon closer analysis of the state diagram (Fig. 2), such a simple answer turns out to be incorrect.

Let's imagine that there is a stream of water flowing across the surface of the Moon. Water will evaporate intensely from the surface. Evaporation wastes a lot of heat. This removes heat from the still uncorrected water and it begins to freeze. The ice that forms is lighter than water. It will float on the surface and creates additional pressure on the surface of the water with its weight. When the pressure of the ice layer $P = \rho gh$ exceeds the pressure at the triple point $P_0 = 0,006 \text{ am}$, the water will stop evaporating.

On the Moon $g = 1,16 \text{ m/cm}^2$, the density of ice is $0,9 \text{ e/cm}^3$, that is, the minimum thickness of the ice layer that protects water from boiling at $0^\circ \text{ C}$ is equal

$$h = \frac{P_0}{\rho g} = \frac{0,006 \cdot 10^5 \text{ m/m}^2}{0,9 \cdot 10^3 \text{ kg/m}^3 \cdot 1,6 \text{ m/cm}^2} \approx 0,4 \text{ m}.$$

Are there grooves on the surface of the Moon similar to the beds of earthly rivers? It turns out yes. The winding grooves were replaced on the Moon as early as 1788. They usually originate in craters, on high ground, and end in lowlands. Recently, American scientists Lingenfelter, Peel and Schubert proposed that the winding grooves are the former beds of lunar water rivers flowing under a protective layer of ice. Indeed, in their structure and size, the winding furrows are similar in appearance to the beds of earthly rivers. The subglacial layer can serve as a source of water. A large meteor is able to break through the lunar crust to the water layer and open a path for water on the surface.

But there are other theories of the origin of winding furrows, for example, from lava flows. Whether this or that theory holds true must be shown by further research. In “earthly” laboratories,

Attempts have been made to model “lunar rivers.”

It was placed in a vacuum chamber under the nose with dimensions $30 \times 42,5 \text{ cm}$, up to which it was filled with a layer of crushed rock with particles from 100 microns to 4 mm (in most experiments, the diameter of the particles of this “rheolite” is 100-500 microns). The thickness of the porda layer varied from 0 to 5 cm. The tray was placed obliquely. Water was introduced through a tube at the end of the tray, raised upward, at a speed of up to 800 milliliters per minute. The pressure in the chamber is maintained at about 0.0013 am, that is, five times lower than the pressure at the triple point.

The results of the experiment were as follows. At atmospheric pressure, the flow of water actually broke through a system of channels in the “rheolitta”. True, the meanders characteristic of terrestrial rivers were not observed, but these channels also branched out and changed direction.

During experiments in vacuum, a completely different picture was observed. The flow of water intensively closed along its entire length, dispersing particles of “lunar dust” and forming ice to the sides. The boiling continued for several seconds until the water source was covered with an ice crust. The water continued to flow under the sheet of ice, but did not necessarily flow downwards, in the direction of the slope of the tray with soil. It seeped through the “rheolite,” breaking through the surface in one place or another. This process reveals the character of an explosion on the surface—a jet of steam carried away particles of the rheolate, and then an ice crust formed. At the end of each, a layer of ice covered the entire tray of soil. Then he evaporated. No channel formations were observed, and the soil surface became cold.

So the hypothesis about “lunar rivers” was not found experimentally confirmed.

It is more likely that water is released from the depths of the Moon by volcanoes. Volcanic activity (the release of gases from the depths of the Moon) was discovered by the Leningrad astronomer Processor Kozyrev. If there is still water in the depths, then when gases erupt, water vapor should also be released.
