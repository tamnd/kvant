These stars are the basis of snowflakes. At temperatures several degrees below zero, the crystals fuse together to form snowflakes.

All this suggests that the shape of crystals is determined mainly by the temperature at which they grow. This was confirmed by experiments on growing ice crystals in the laboratory. Ice crystals are grown in a special chamber in which the temperature and amount of water vapor are strictly controlled. A thin thread was used as a seed. The temperature in the chamber was different in different areas along the thread. figure 3 shows a photograph of a section of thread overgrown with crystals. The size of this area is about 6 cm. On the right at figure the temperature in the chamber is indicated. In the upper part of the thread, mostly tiny hexagonal plates were grown. Below are hexagonal plates, branched stars and again hexagonal plates. At temperatures below - 25° C, hexagonal features are formed again.

Experience has shown that it is the temperature that determines the shape of the crystal. The amount of water vapor affects the growth rate. However, the exact nature of snow crystal growth still remains unclear.

It is very interesting to study the growth of snow crystals on earth. Often in winter, when there is a sharp drop in temperature, tree branches and house walls become covered with frost. The clouds where snowflakes originate are difficult to reach. Frost is easily achieved and can be observed during its formation.
