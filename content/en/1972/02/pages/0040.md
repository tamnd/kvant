8. Prove that the area of ​​a polygon circumscribed about a circle is equal to the product of the radius of this circle and the perimeter of the polygon.

In these problems, you can bypass the “cut and fold” technique. Note that, in principle, this technique can always be used when you need to prove the equality of the areas of two polygons; The following theorem is true: if two polygons are equal in size, then one of them can be cut into parts from which the other polygon is composed.

This theorem is not difficult to prove *). But practically, in order to establish equality of areas or find the ratio of areas, it is not at all necessary to “cut and add”. For example, you can very quickly compare the areas of two triangles using the formula we called A4. It immediately follows, in particular, also correspondences for areas:
