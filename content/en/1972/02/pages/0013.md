We know the age of the Moon's rocks from the relative abundance of radioactive isotopes and their decay products in lunar samples. This age is about 4.5 billion years. The calculation shows that with $t = -30^\circ\text{C}$ and the physical properties and structure of lunar rocks known to us, not all of the led from the lunar crust evaporated over these 4.5 billion years.

The hypothesis about permafrost and a deep layer of water on the Moon was envisioned in 1961 by the American astrophysicist Gold. However, his conclusion about the existence of ice and water deep in the lunar crust is based on some assumptions.

Firstly, it is believed that the total amount of juvenile water in 1 kg of matter of the Earth and the Moon was the same, and this total amount of juvenile water is estimated by the average depth of the world's oceans (layers of water obtained by dividing the volume of water of the oceans, seas, ice of the Arctic and Antarctic, ice of mountainous regions on the surface of the Earth). On Earth this depth is ~1 km.

Secondly, it is believed that a significant part of the juvenile water comes to the surface after the lunar crust has cooled and therefore froze in the led, which evaporated slowly - through the pores.

If vapors of juvenile water managed to emerge on the surface of the lunar crust, in order to reliably calculate the process of evaporation of ice from it.

There are also subsurface ices on Earth. In those areas where the average pressure temperature is below 0°C, a layer of permafrost soil remains under the Earth's surface, which only partially thaws in the summer. Permafrost areas make up 15% of the Earth's land mass (and another 10% of the landmass is occupied by glaciers). In our country, permafrost covers the common areas of Siberia and the Far East.
