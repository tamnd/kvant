Here are some problems related to the concept of area of ​​a polygon. We give an axiomatic definition of the area of ​​a polygon, listing those properties that we need.

A1. The area of ​​a polygon is a positive number.
A2. The areas of equal (congruent) polygons are equal.
A3. If a polygon is cut into several parts, then its area is equal to the sum of the areas of these parts.
A4. The area of ​​a triangle is equal to half the product of its base and height. (We consider the unit of scale to be given; it is easy to show that this product does not depend on which side and the corresponding height we take.)
The letter A was chosen because the word “Axiom” begins with it. From these four properties of the axioms you can derive all the theorems about the areas of polygons that you study in school.

It is much more difficult to prove that, indeed, it is possible to associate with each polygon $M$ in the plane the area number $S(M)$, its area, so that properties A1–A4 are satisfied. This is true, but it is usually not proven in a school course and is considered very dynamite. Instead of A4, we can consider the main property from which A4 is derived:

A4'. The area of ​​a square with side 1 is 1.

For example:

b) The area of ​​a trapezoid is equal to the product of half the sum of the bases and the height.
