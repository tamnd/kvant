M88

What condition must the coefficients of the equation $x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ satisfy in order for its three roots to form an arithmetic progression?

Let's give the answer right away. The desired match is $c = \frac{ab}{3} - \frac{2}{27} a^3$ (or, what is the same, one of the roots must equal $-\frac{a}{3}$). Indeed, let $x_1, x_2$ and $x_3$ be the roots of our equation. Then, according to Vieta’s theorem,

$$x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = -a.$$

On the other hand, in order for $x_1, x_2, x_3$ to form an arithmetic progression, it is necessary and sufficient that

$$x_1 + x_3 = 2x_2.$$

Pose $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 3x_2$, opening $x_2 = -\frac{a}{3}$. Try to derive the conditions under which equations of the fourth degree form an arithmetic progression.

L. I. Moscoviome

F93

In the side wall of the vessel directed to liquid with a refractive index $n$, a hole of small radius $r$ continues. A thin beam of light is directed horizontally from the vessel along the axis of the hole. To what level $h$ must there be liquid above the hole so that a ray of light emerges from the jet without ever experiencing internal reflection?

Neglect the change in the cross section of the jet; the refractive index of the liquid is assumed to be sufficiently large.

At the greatest angle to the surface of the water, a ray of light falls at the point $A$ scab (Fig. 12). The angle of incidence $\alpha$, at which the beam at this point does not experience total internal reflection, must be such that

$$\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{n}.$$

This follows from the law of light refraction ($\frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \beta} = \frac{1}{n}$) and the condition that the angle is $\beta = 90^\circ$. The tangent to the jet at point $A$ makes an angle $\gamma = 90^\circ - \alpha$ with the horizontal. Thus, a ray of light will emerge from the scab without ever experiencing internal reflection if the angle formed by the tangent to the jet at point $A$ makes an angle $\gamma$ with the horizon such that cos $\gamma = \frac{1}{n}$.

But the tangent to the surface of the liquid is the tangent to the trajectory of the particle emerging at the upper edge of the hole. Therefore the direction of the tangent

$$\cos \gamma = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{\sqrt{4gr}}{\sqrt{2gh}} = \sqrt{\frac{r}{h}}.$$

Now expressing cos $\gamma$ in terms of tg $\gamma$ —

$$\cos \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + tg^2 \gamma}}$$

and taking into account that cos $\gamma = \frac{1}{n}$, we get

$$\frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 2\frac{r}{h}}}.$$

From here

$$h = 2r/(n^2 - 1).$$

F95

The surface of the river forms an inclined plane. Is it possible for a word to swim down a river at a speed exceeding the speed of the current?

If the body does not move relative to the water, then the friction force acting on it from the side of the water is zero. Therefore, the component of the gravity force, parallel to the surface of the water (Fig. 14), will straighten the body until it is balanced by the friction force on the water (which at low speeds is proportional to the speed of the body). In this case, it will flow at a speed greater than the speed of the current.

Now imagine that we, having left a thin, rigid, weightless shell, removed the body and poured water into the shell so that its volume was equal to the volume of the immersed part of the body (Fig. 15). Since the body was floating, the mass of the poured water is equal to the mass of the body. Therefore, the shell with water is equivalent to the body that we removed. But the water poured into the shell is no different from the water in the river. The friction in a fluid is related only to the thickness of the fluid itself and does not depend on the surface of the body. This is explained by the fact that a thin layer of water molecules “floats” to the surface of the body and the formation is determined by the interaction of water molecules with each other. This means that you can completely remove the shell, the friction force will not change. It turns out that the body must flow at a speed equal to the speed of the current.

What's really going on?

Let’s first understand what “flow speed” means. Due to friction, the speed of the river flow varies at different points in the river. It increases from day to surface and from the shore to the middle of the river. Therefore, the speed of the flow in the place where the plush is located along the body river is also different. This water in the shell differs from the surrounding water, because the speed of the water in the shell is the same at all points.

Let us assume that the speed of the body is equal to the speed of the current at the edge of the body, which is close to the shore. Then the speed of the flow of water passing through the body at other points is greater than the speed of the body. Because of this, the water should carry the body, increasing its speed. On the other hand, when the speed of the body increases, it should slow down due to the fact that the speed of its point, located close to the shore, is greater than the speed of the current and the friction force is converted into movement of the body. As a result, the body must flow at a speed greater than the minimum, but less than the maximum of the flow rates throughout the body.

Two identical balls connected by a thread. Find the height of rise of this system if one of the balls is thrown upward with a speed of $v$.

The momentum of the entire system is $mv$, and its mass is $2m$. Therefore, at the initial moment

⟦folio 51⟧

the speed of the center of mass of the system is $\frac{mv}{2m} = \frac{v}{2}$.

Let us write down the law of conservation of energy for the entire system. In this case, we can consider the movement not of individual balls, but of the center of mass of the system, considering that the entire mass of the system is concentrated in it. If the maximum height at which the center of mass of the system rises is $h$, then

$$2mgh = \frac{2m\left(\frac{v}{2}\right)^2}{2}.$$

From here

$$h = \frac{v^2}{8g}.$$

We received many solutions to this problem, but most of the letters had the same error. The authors of the solution believed that at the moment when the center of mass of the system reaches the maximum height of the boom, the balls are motionless and their kinetic energy is zero. This, however, is not true. The balls are moving and their kinetic energy at this moment is equal to

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - 2m\cdot gh = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{mv^2}{4} = \frac{mv^2}{4}.$$
