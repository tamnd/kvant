STAMPS DEDICATED TO INTERNATIONAL GEOPHYSICAL SCIENTIFIC PROGRAMS

Geophysics - the science of physical processes occurring on Earth - also includes the study of various phenomena observed in various layers of the atmosphere, in the water shell, the solid crust and the interior of our planet. Geophysical phenomena often span most or all of the globe. Therefore, even at the dawn of the development of geophysics, it became clear that it was necessary to pool the efforts of scientists from different countries to conduct research according to a single program in different parts of the globe.

In October 1951, the International Council of Scientific Unions decided to hold an International Geophysical Year. From July 1, 1957 to December 31, 1958, scientists from many countries, under a special program, carried out a huge amount of coordinated research in all regions of the globe. The International Geophysical Year was the largest scientific event in which more than 50 thousand scientists from 60 countries took part.

The newest and most difficult task at this time is the launch of artificial Earth satellites. Therefore, the emblem of the International Geophysical Year depicts a globe with an artificial satellite flowing around it.

The Soviet Union issued three series of stamps dedicated to the International Geophysical Year.

They reflect the most important research that was carried out in the USSR under the international program. One of the stamps of the first series (issued in 1957) depicts a chromospheric-photospheric telephone for studying solar activity, another shows an observatory and a radiar installation for observing meteorites, and the third shows launch packages for studying the upper layers of the atmosphere.

The second series (issued in 1958) is devoted to the study of geomagnetism, auroras and meteorology.

In the third series you see a stamp with an image of the Soviet research vessel "Vityaz", studying various phenomena in the oceans (at the top of the stamp the route of the "Vityaz" in the Indian Ocean is shown), a stamp dedicated to glasiology (the study of the Earth's ice cover), as well as a stamp dedicated to Antarctica with a diagram of the location of Soviet Antarctic stations. In the same series a stamp was issued dedicated to batch research. All four stamps feature the International Geophysical Year emblem.
