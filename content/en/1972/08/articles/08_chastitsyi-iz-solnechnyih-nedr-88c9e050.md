It is much easier to change the speed of a drop near the Earth's surface $v_0$.

$$v_0 = \frac{f}{\alpha} = \frac{Mg}{\alpha} = \frac{g}{\tau},$$

and from here we find the time constant $\tau = \frac{g}{v_0}$.

You, of course, noticed: for strength we used the expression for the force of gravity; In addition, we assumed that the motion was already established near the Earth. This assumption needs to be tested. A very simple way to change the speed of djede drops is to observe the traces of these drops left on the windows of a moving tram. If the speed of the tram is, say, $V = 20 \text{ km/ч}$, and the traces of drops on the glass make an angle $30^\circ$ from the vertical, then the speed of the drops is

$$v_0 = \frac{V}{\text{tg} 30^\circ} = 20 \text{ km/ч} \approx 9,4 \text{ m/c.}$$

It follows that the time constant is approximately equal to one second. Let the drop passing through during the transition regime, the duration of which can be taken equal to 5-10 s, therefore does not exceed 50-100 m (we taught that the speed of the drop in the transition regime is not greater than the established speed). The fact that the path length in the transition mode turns out to be so small confirms our assumption: the drop moves near the Earth at a set speed. But this conclusion can be reached without calculation, watching the djed from the open window of the tram or, even simpler, the slanted djed.

In the article by V.I. Kuznetsov “Radioactive Memory” (see «Kvant» No. 2, 1972) a method was discussed for determining age by the concentration of radioactive carbon $^{14}$C in the objects under study. Recent comparisons of tree ages determined by $^{14}$C concentration and by counting annual rings on media have shown good agreement of results until time results are equal to 1000 BC. But the age data, determined by $^{14}$C, turned out to be less than the actual one, as can be seen from the table.

The reasons for this discrepancy are not clear. Apparently, the intensity of cosmic rays was greater in ancient times. This could be a consequence of a change in the Earth's magnetic field, allowing more of it to pass through from space. Or perhaps this is due to changes in solar activity. Such conditions in the near-Earth space could lead to an increase in the content of the $^{14}$C isotope in the atmosphere, and, consequently, in plants. Be that as it may, for those times the radioactive parts show the wrong time.

“PARTICLES FROM THE SOLAR INTERIOR”

In No. 6 «Kvant» for 1972 the note “Particles from the solar interior” was published. Unfortunately, new experiments did not confirm the results. Therefore, the question of how many solar neutrinos come to Earth again remains open.

By the way, we note that in the mentioned note the layer “antineutrino” and the symbol “v” must be replaced with the word “neutrino” and the symbol “v”.
