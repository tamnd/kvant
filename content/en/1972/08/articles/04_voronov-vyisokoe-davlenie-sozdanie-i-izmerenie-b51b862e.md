HIGH GAVENESS-CONSTITUTION AND DIMENSION

The properties of a substance depend on the distances between its atoms. As the mechanical distances decrease, the density of the substance increases, the electrical, optical, mechanical properties, and crystal structure change. By studying the properties of liquid substances, we learned more deeply about the nature and nature of the forces acting between atoms.

How to combine atoms in matter?
Let's take a cube of the substance and begin to compress it under pressure. As a result of compression along one axis, we will cause deformation in this cube, the distance between its atoms will decrease. However, we will not get significant deformations. Once the limit is reached, destruction will set in firmly.

How can we combine atoms in matter even more strongly? To do this, you need to compress the substance from all sides, that is, apply equal forces simultaneously to all sides of our cube, subjecting it to all-round high pressure. In this case, we can obtain atoms in a substance without destruction, change its physical properties, and force chemical processes to proceed differently.

The pressure range in nature is enormous. At the bottom of the ocean of air we live at a pressure of one atmosphere. A column of water presses on the diver's eardrums, and special diving suits are needed for deep-sea diving. According to calculations by geophysicists, the pressure in the center of the Earth is about 4 million atmospheres.

In the morning you open the water tap and water flows into the washbasin with a strong stream. The water pressure in the water supply is 1.5-2 atmospheres. Breakfast was prepared on a stove, to which gas was supplied at a pressure of 1.01 amp.

On the mismo street, trolley buses and buses rush past you. In their tires the air capacity is up to 2.5-4 amps. In the engine cylinders of the fast-moving Volga, the pressure reaches 30-50 amps. Power plant turbines are driven by steam at a pressure of 200-500 amps. At chemical industry plants, polyethylene is produced by skimming ethylene gas to a pressure of 1000 amps, and ammonia is synthesized from nitrogen and hydrogen at a pressure of 1500 amps. When turning graphig into diamond, the pressure reaches 100,000 amps and the temperature reaches 2000 degrees.

Let us define pressure as the force with which a gas or liquid enclosed in a tank acts per unit area of ​​the wall of this tank:

$$P = \frac{F}{S}.$$

Using this simple formula, you can set the units for measuring pressure. Putting the force equal to one dyne and the area equal to one square centimeter, we get the unit of pressure in the CGS system - $1 \frac{\delta \ln}{cm^2}$, which is called barney. However, this unit is too small for measuring high pressures, and the cup uses a million times more: $1 \text{ бар} = 10^8 \frac{\delta \ln}{cm^2}$, and its derivatives: kilobar, equal to $10^3 \text{ бар}$ or $10^9 \frac{\delta \ln}{cm^2}$; medabar equal to $10^6 \text{ бар}$. Let us determine what the pressure of one physical atmosphere corresponds to in these units. As the name suggests, this unit is related to atmospheric air pressure. Let a surface of area S be pressed by a liquid or gas (in our case, air) of height h and density ρ. then the volume of this column is V=h·S, and its weight G=h·S·ρ·g (under the acceleration of the gravity force g), and the pressure P = $\frac{G}{S} = h \cdot \rho \cdot g$. Assuming the altitude of the atmospheric air to be 200 km (2.10⁷ cm) and its average height density ρ=5.16·10⁻⁵ e/cm³, we obtain that at g=980 $\frac{cm}{c^2}$ P=1 amm=1.01·10⁶ $\frac{\partial \ln}{cm^2}$.

The airy ocean of our planet is restless. Air currents and cyclones change the height and density of the atmosphere. Atmospheric pressure fluctuates over a wide range and, of course, cannot be taken as an etapone. We agreed to take as the “physical atmosphere” the pressure produced on a horizontal plane by columns (equivalent of atmospheric air) with a density of 13.595 e/cm³, a height of 760 mm at 0°C and an acceleration force of 980.665 cm/s². Compressed gas has a huge amount of energy. When fired, the maximum pressure of powder gases in Ordinary mode reaches 5-6 thousand atmospheres. Pressure measurement

The simplest way to measure pressure is to balance the measured pressure with the weight of a column of liquid. The heavier the liquid, the lower the height of the column will be needed to balance the same pressure. However, even using the heaviest liquid, rutin (density 13.6 $e/cm^3$), to measure a pressure of hundreds of atmospheres would lead to a column height of hundreds of meters (remember that 0.76 m $pm. cm.=1$ atmosphere). The original solution to this problem was proposed by D.I. Mendeleev in 1872. His manometer consists of several elbows filled with rutin and water (Fig. 9). The pressure measurement will be equal to the sum of the height of the rutin columns ($H_1+H_2+H_3$) multiplied by the density of the rutin, minus the sum of the height of the water columns ($h_1+h_2$) multiplied by the density of the water. But despite the significant gain in size, an accurate manometer of such a design for 200 atmospheres is an internal structure of 9 elbows 18 meters high.

At such pressures, the pistons of pressure gauges deform very strongly: the best steels and hard alloys become plastic, and it is impossible to talk about exact dimensions. In such cases, pressure values ​​are determined by changes in the physical properties of liquid substances.
