STAMPS DEDICATED TO THE INTERNATIONAL YEAR
GOOD SUN

The sun sends more than just visible light to us on Earth. It is a source of various invisible electromagnetic radiation and flows of charged particles. These radiations have a huge impact on the state of the atmosphere, causing auroras, magnetic storms, fading of radio waves and many other effects. Solar activity changes periodically at intervals of 11 years. The International Geophysical Year, the stamps in honor of which we introduced to readers in the second issue of Quat for 1972, took place during a period of exceptionally high activity. In order to compare the information obtained then about the Sun and the earth's atmosphere with similar information obtained from observations during a period of low activity, scientists decided to repeat an extensive program of international research. In 1964-1965, the International Year of the Quiet Sun (YYYS) was held. Many countries participating in this program have issued special postage stamps dedicated to it.

To the note “Described trapezoid and among”
(see see 49)

The lateral side of the trapezoid is the average arithmetic base - this follows from the fact that in the circumscribed quadrilateral the sums of the lengths of the opposite sides are equal.

Let's express the height $BH$ through the bases
$$BH^2 = AB^2 - AH^2 = \left[ \frac{1}{2} (AD + BC) \right]^2 - \left[ \frac{1}{2} (AD - BC) \right]^2 = AD \cdot BC = a \cdot b.$$
Finally, we express the segment $BG$ through the bases of the trapezoid. To do this, note that the triangles $ABH$ and $HBG$ are similar, hence $\frac{BH}{BG} = \frac{AB}{BH}$. Hence,
$$\frac{1}{BG} = \frac{AB}{BH^2} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} (AD - BC)}{AD \cdot BC} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} \right).$$

To problem M116

The ratio of perimeters $P'/P$ for each $n>3$ can take any value from the interval $(1/2, 1)$. We will not prove this strictly, but will limit ourselves to describing examples when this ratio approaches $1/2$ and 1. If the polygon $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ “degenerates into a segment” so that two neighboring vertices - say $A_1$ and $A_2$ - approach one end of the segment, and the rest - to the other, then $P'/P$ approaches 1; if it "degenerates into a segment" such that $A_1$ and $A_2$ serve as the ends of this segment, and all other vertices approach the middle of the segment, then $P'/P$ approaches $1/2$.

The area ratio $S'/S$ for each $n>4$ can be as close as possible to $1/2$: it is enough to imagine that the polygon “degenerates into a triangle” so that $A_1$ and $A_2$ serve as two vertices of the triangle, and all other vertices approach the third vertex of the triangle. For each $n>5$ the ratio is easily brought closer to 1, causing the polygon to degenerate into a triangle so that $A_1$ and $A_2$ approach one vertex, $A_3$ and $A_4$ to another, and the remaining vertices (at least two of them!) to a third. But for $n=5$ it was not possible to get close to 1. And indeed, as you can prove, the ratio $S'/S$ for the fifth square does not exceed $3/4$.
