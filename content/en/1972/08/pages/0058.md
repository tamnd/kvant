⟦folio 56⟧

Tasks

In this issue we present problems proposed at the final rounds of the All-Union Mathematical and Physics Olympiad, held in April of this year.

Solutions to the problems in this issue can be sent no later than September 15 to the address: 117071, Moscow, V-71, Leninsky Prospekt, 15, Nauka publishing house, journal editorial office «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, indicate the solutions to which problems you are sending (for example: “Problem «Kvant», M156, F157”).

Send solutions to problems in mathematics and physics, as well as new problems in each subject, in separate envelopes. At the beginning of your letters, please indicate your last name, first name, patronymic, home address (as well as the class and school you attend).

M156. In a rectangle ABCD, point M is the midpoint of side AD, N is the midpoint of side BC. On the continuation of the segment DC, point D is taken to be point P. Let us denote the point of intersection of the lines PM and AC by Q. Prove that $\Rightarrow QNM = \Rightarrow MNP$ (8th cell).

Yu. V. Mikheev

M157. The sum $n$ of positive numbers $x_1, x_2, x_3, \ldots, x_n$ is equal to 1. Let $S$ be the largest of the numbers

$$\frac{x_1}{1+x_1}, \frac{x_2}{1+x_2}, \frac{x_n}{1+x_n}, \ldots, \frac{x_n}{1+x_n}$$

Find the smallest possible value of $S$. At what values ​​of $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ is it achieved (10 cells)?

Yu. I. Ionin

M158. The triangular table is constructed according to the following rule: the natural number $a > 1$ is written in the top line, and then under each number $k$ there is a trace of $k^2$, and on the right - the number $k + 1$. For example, with $a = 2$ the table is obtained

Prove that in each row of the table all numbers are different (9, 10 cells).

M159*. Is it possible to arrange the numbers 0, 1, 2 in the cells of a sheet of cellular paper of size $100 \times 100$ in such a way that in each rectangle $3 \times 4$, the lines of which go along the sides of the cells, there would be three zeros, four ones and five twos (9 cells)?

V. E. Lapitsky

M160*. When the hockey tournament ended (in one round), it turned out that for any group of teams you can find a team (maybe from the same group) that scored an odd number of points in games with teams from this group. Prove that the number of teams participated in the tournament. (Loss - 0 points, draw - 1 point, win - 2 points.) (10 grades).

G. A. Kasparov
