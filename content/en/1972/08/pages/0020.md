⟦folio 18⟧

A measly hundred thousand, but any amount - of course, not too wild -. Additionally, I offer you health and happiness for the rest of your life. If I answer your question, well, you know the consequences. That's all I can offer you.

He took the air-burnt cigarette and started smoking.

A determined silence reigned.

Simon looked ahead, seeing nothing. Large drops of sweat appeared on his forehead. He knew very well what conditions the devil could face. The muscles of his face turned... No, he is ready to lay down his soul that no one - neither man, nor beast, nor devil - will answer his question in 24 hours.

- Include my wife in the clause about health and happiness - and get a deal! - he said. - Let's sign.

The devil nodded. He took out the glass, looked at it with disgust and touched it with his finger. The cigarette butt instantly turned into a pink mint cake, which the devil began to suck loudly and with obvious pleasure.

“As for your question,” he continued, “there must be an answer to it, otherwise our agreement is invalid.” In the Middle Ages, people loved to ask riddles. People often came to me with paradoxes. For example: in the village there lived only one barber who shaved everyone who did not shave himself. Who shaved the barber? - they asked. But, as Russell noted, the word “all” makes such a question meaningless, and there is no answer to it.

“My question is pure and contains no paradox,” Simon assured him.
- Great. I will answer it. Why are you grinning?
“I... nothing,” Simon answered, agreeing with the expression on his face.
“You have strong nerves,” said the devil in a gloomy but approving tone, producing a parchment from the air. - If I presented before you in the form of a monster combining the billions of your gorillas with the gracefulness of the monster turned on Venus, you would hardly retain your aplomb, and I’m sure...

“There’s no need for that,” Simon said hastily.

He handed him the contract, made sure everything was in order, and opened the pocketknife.

- Just a minute! - the devil stopped him.
- Let me disinfect it. “He brought the blade to his lips, blew lightly, and became heated to an outer red color. —

Here you go! Now touch the tip of the knife...um...to the ink, and that's all... Please, the second line is gray, the last one is mine.

Simon placed it, looking thoughtfully at the hot tip of the knife.
“Sign,” the devil hurried, and Simon, straightening his shoulders, signed his name.

Having signed his name with a magnificent flourish, the devil rubbed his hands, looked at Simon with an openly possessive look and said cheerfully:

- Well, post your question! As soon as I answer it, we'll leave. I have one more client to visit today and I'm running out of time.

“Okay,” Simon said and took a deep breath. — My question is this: is Fermat’s Last Theorem true or false?
