The prism carves a convex polygon in the picture plane; it will play a major role in all further discussions - let's call it the fundamental polygon. So, there is a picture plane $K$ and in it a fundamental polygon $M$; How to geometrically describe reflections in the facets?

Let's start with a single reflection. If we look into a kaleidoscope from point $T$ (Fig. 5a), and the ray emitted from point $A$ was reflected from mirror l at point $O$ and then hit our eye, then it seems to us that point $A$ is located behind the mirror on the continuation of straight line $TO$, and if we denote by $A_1$ is the image of the point $A$, then $OA_1=OA$, because our eye has to estimate the distance to the object. A simple geometric reasoning shows that the point $A_1$ lies in the same picture of the plane $K$ and is obtained as a geometric reflection of the point $A$ in the edge $R$; Carry out this reasoning yourself.

Task 1. Take the point $A_1$, which is obtained as a geometric reflection of the point $A$ in the edge $R$ (see Fig. 5a), $AP \perp R$, $AP=PA_1$. Prove that the point $A_1$ satisfies the physical concept of “reflection of the point $A$ in the mirror l”. This means the following. For any observation point $T$ we draw a straight line $TA_1$. Let it intersect the plane l at point $O$. Let's connect the points $O$ and $A$. It is necessary to prove that: $TOS=POA$ (the distance is equal to zero reflection); $OA=OA_1$ (lobe $A_1$ is the same distance from $T$ as $A$ “taking into account the mirror”).

The statement of problem 1 is valid for any point of the fundamental polygon, therefore, with a single reflection in the mirror, the image $M'$ of the entire fundamental polygon $M$ lies in the picture plane $K$ and the reflection is obtained
