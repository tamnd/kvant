Task 4. Prove that two mirrors placed at an angle $120^\circ$ do not form a mathematical regular kaleidoscon.

Task 5. Prove that two mirrors placed at an angle $\alpha = \frac{180^\circ}{n}$ and $n$ are a natural number.

Task 6. Prove that if a kaleidoscon is mathematically correct, then the elements at its vertices are its functional set and what elements at its vertices all these elements have the form $\frac{180^\circ}{n}$ (n is a natural number correspondence; for different vertices these $n$ can be different).

The difference between tasks 5 and 6 is not great: in task 5 the mirrors are unlimited, but in task 6 they have finite sizes. Both problems are solved by mail in the same way. But Problem 6 gives a necessary condition for the mathematical correctness of a kaleidoscon: the angles at the vertices of its functional polygon must have the specified form.

Once we have one condition that every mathematically regular kaleidoscope must satisfy, we can try to find all kaleidoscopes that satisfy this condition. In any case, among them there will be all the original kaleidoscopes, which is a significant essence of our concepts.

Task 7. Find all kaleidoscopes that satisfy the necessary condition of correctness (task 6). This means the following: we need to find for what number of sides of the functional polygon and what lines at its vertices all these lines have the form $\frac{180^\circ}{n}$. Checking that all receiving kaleidoscopes are mathematically correct, that is, that the necessary conditions for the mathematical correctness of a kaleidoscope, formed in problem 6, is sufficient.
