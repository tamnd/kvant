Let us determine what the pressure of one physical atmosphere corresponds to in these units. As the name suggests, this unit is related to atmospheric air pressure. Let a surface of area S be pressed by a liquid or gas (in our case, air) of height h and density ρ. then the volume of this column is V=h·S, and its weight G=h·S·ρ·g (under the acceleration of the gravity force g), and the pressure P = $\frac{G}{S} = h \cdot \rho \cdot g$. Assuming the altitude of the atmospheric air to be 200 km (2.10⁷ cm) and its average height density ρ=5.16·10⁻⁵ e/cm³, we obtain that at g=980 $\frac{cm}{c^2}$ P=1 amm=1.01·10⁶ $\frac{\partial \ln}{cm^2}$.

The airy ocean of our planet is restless. Air currents and cyclones change the height and density of the atmosphere. Atmospheric pressure fluctuates over a wide range and, of course, cannot be taken as an etapone. We agreed to take as the “physical atmosphere” the pressure produced on a horizontal plane by columns (equivalent of atmospheric air) with a density of 13.595 e/cm³, a height of 760 mm at 0°C and an acceleration force of 980.665 cm/s².
