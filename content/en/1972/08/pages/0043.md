MATHEMATICAL CIRCLE

KALEIDOSCOPES

A. N. Vilenkin

Probably, in childhood, each of you took apart at least one kaleidoscope, and you know how it works. However, let us remind you that when you took it apart, pieces of colored glass fell out of it, and inside there were three strips of mirror (or glass), located at an angle of 60° to each other, so that a regular triangle was formed.

Why the kaleidoscope is designed this way and how else it can be made, we will tell you in this article. The tasks included in the text (their results are used in calculations) will allow you not only to passively read the article, but also to independently “have a hand” in building a theory. However, all problems in the article begin with the words “Prove that...”, so during the first reading they can be replaced with the words “It is proven that...”, and then come back and take on the proof. At the end of the issue there are instructions for all tasks.

Kaleidoscope device

Kaleidoscopes that are sold in stores are made in such a way that it is impossible to change, say, the angle between the mirrors. We will make a somewhat homemade kaleidoscope, this one is adapted for experiments.

Glue the glass strips to a sheet of paper, leaving small gaps between the strips (so that you could bend the sheet into a shape). It’s good if the sheet is opaque - no excess light will get in.
