If the matrix cannot withstand it, throw it away and replace it with something that can withstand such pressures - similar pistons, which should also be moved with hydraulic presses. This is how the six-axis installation (Fig. 7) was created. A cube made of plastic material, inside of which the sample under study is located, was pressed with pistons - punches, each of which pressed on one side of the cube.

Between the pistons - punches, as in the finishing apparatus, gaskets were placed, when they rolled down, a supporting pressure arose on the side surfaces of the punches. In such an installation, the limit of achievable pressure arises due to the destruction of the material from which the device is made.

Higher pressures can be obtained by using a cascade principle or inserting the device into the device according to the “matryoshka” principle. So, if a confining chamber operating up to a pressure of 100,000 atmospheres is placed in the same confining chamber, also at 100,000 atmospheres, but larger in size, then in the inner chamber you can very well get 200 thousand atmospheres. Thanks to the fact that the strength of materials under pressure increases, even greater gains can be expected.

Even more pressure can be obtained in thin layers of the substance by pressing it between anvils (Fig. 8). The sample under study in the form of a thin disk is placed in a ring of limestone - catlinite. Limestone has a high coefficient of friction and does not allow the substance being skimmed to leak out of the apparatus. In such devices, made from the very materials, scientists obtain pressures of 0.5 million atmospheres. Even more pressure - up to 10-15 million atmospheres - is obtained using dynamic methods: exploding the substance with an explosion. If the area with the test substance is surrounded on all sides by an explosive substance, for example, tol, and this stable substance is undermined from the surface, then the front of the explosion will spread to the center and the blast wave from all sides will create the area for a fraction of a second to enormous pressure.
