Why do materials resist external loads? I will buy that mixtures occur in them. There are no and cannot be absolutely rigid materials in nature; all materials have flexibility to one degree or another. In many bodies the mixtures are very small. “If I step on a common building brick,” says Gordon, “then its height will decrease by approximately (1/20,000) cm. And any two neighboring atoms in the brick will become closer to each other by (1/500,000) Å, that is, by 2·10⁻¹⁴ cm.” Should such small mixtures be taken into account? It turns out they are not so small! “The strong ropes that extend across Forth Bay in Scotland,” Gordon gives an example, “are always elastically growing by about 0.1%, but with a total length of about 3 km, this stretch is already 3 meters! These small, invisible mixtures sometimes lead to the fact that a steel place can suddenly collapse, taking with it tens or hundreds of human lives, and an ocean steam code suddenly and instantly breaks in half. In the first 60 years of the 20th century, 27 ocean-going steamships were lost when they unexpectedly broke in half on the open sea.”
