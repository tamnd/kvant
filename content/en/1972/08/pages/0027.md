The circle contains an infinite number of rational points, and their description is precisely given by the “Formula of the Hindus”. An infinite set of rational points on several so-called elliptic curves, defined by equations of the third level, for example, on the curves $y^2 = x^3 + 1$, $x^3 + y^3 = 7$.

As for the equation of the stage above the fourth, then on the curves defined by them it has so far been possible to discover only finite sets of rational points, which further led L. J. Mordell to hypothesize that this is the case for all curves above the fourth stage *). Proof of Mordell's conjecture (still stubbornly unsolvable) would mean that the number of solutions to Fermat's equation $x^p + y^p = z^p$ is finite. As with respect to Kummer's methods, one can say about the geometric approach to Fermat's theorem that, regardless of the conditions in solving the original problems themselves, this whole direction as a whole promises interesting new results in number theory.

Finally, there is an approach to Fermat’s conjecture that is fundamentally different from all previous ones: it does not follow from anything that its positive or negative solution can generally be deduced from the axioms of the arithmetic of natural numbers! In other words, the statement contained in Fermat’s Last Theorem may turn out to be algorithmic, and successes in proving the algorithmic unsolvability of a number of number-theoretic problems**) force us to think more and more seriously about this possibility (although a direct path to its identification is also not yet visible)...

*) Of course, here we are not talking about “expressions” of curves of higher stele, obtained by simply raising an equation of the form $y=x+1$, etc., to the obihace stele.

**) See, for example, the article by F.L. Varpakhovsky and A.P. Kolmogorov “On the solution of Hilbert’s tenth problem” («Kvant» No. 7, 1970).

What is the situation with Fermat’s problem today?*) Despite the fact that Fermat’s conjecture has been proven for a large number of special cases (for example, for all $n < 619$), it also takes its full form from the solution, just like three hundred years ago. The experience of the development of mathematics since then has clearly shown the complete futility of relying on an “elementary” proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem - a hope that, alas, is tempting for people who love not only mathematics, but success in mathematics. True, the “Fermatists” are not spoiled by success; They are, as a rule, not private people, and we, with all our servers, would like to warn our young readers against such a career.
