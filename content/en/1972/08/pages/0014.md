Compressed liquids have less energy and have less skimmability. However, at high pressures, the growth of the liquid increases greatly, which complicates the operation of compressor clans and the transmission of pressure through connecting pipes - capillaries. At pressures of the order of 30-40 thousand atmospheres, liquids solidify. Therefore, to create a pressure of 50 thousand atmospheres and higher, plastic solids are used as a working medium.

The simplest apparatus for obtaining high pressures in solids is the pyzometer, shown in figure 5. Cylindrical pistons made of hard alloy are pushed using a hydraulic press into a high-pressure vessel called a matrix. The matrix contains a soft plastic substance

The pressure in such a pressure meter is determined by the magnitude of the applied force per unit cross-sectional area of ​​the pressure gun channel. The maximum pressure that can be obtained in such a device is determined by the tensile strength of the pistons during rolling and usually does not exceed 40-50 thousand atmospheres.

To protect the pistons from destruction, it is necessary to “support” them: to create a pressure-supporting surface on their side surface. This is easily achieved in the Konch apparatus (Fig. 6). When pushing the conch pistons into the matrix with a press, not only the working medium and the sample in the matrix are removed, but also the gasket between the conch entry into the matrix and the piston (it is shown in black in figure). The resulting forces exert supporting pressure on the side surface of the piston.
