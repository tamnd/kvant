Point $A_2$ turns out like this. Since the ray was first reflected in the edge $R_1$, we reflect the point $A$ in the edge $R_1$, we get the point $A_1$ (Fig. 6). Then the ray, now coming as if from the point $A_1$, was reflected in the edge $R_2$ - let us also reflect the point $A_1$ in the edge $R_2$, we get the point $A_2$ - the image of the point $A$ with reflections in the edges $R_1$ and $R_2$.

But you can think differently. Behind the edge $R_2$ we see the image of a fundamental polygon - the polygon $M'$, in which there is a point $A'$ - an image of the point $A$ in the edge $R_2$. $M'$ has an edge $R_1'$ - the image of the mirror (edge) $R_1$ in the mirror $R_2$. And now it turns out that this image $R_1'$ of the mirror $R_1$ can be considered a real mirror and, by reflecting the point $A'$ in $R_1'$, one can obtain the same image $A_2$. To see this, notice that figure 6 is symmetrical with respect to $R_2$.

So, the polygon $M'$ turned out to be equal to the fundamental polygon $M$, and the edge $R_1'$ is a full-fledged mirror. By reflecting $M'$ in the edge $R_1'$, we obtain a polygon $M''$ corresponding to the reflection in the mirror $R_1$ and $R_2$. It is not difficult to understand that we can make reflections in the edges of the polygon $M''$ and thus obtain a subsequent image of the fundamental polygon $M$.

Now we can worry not only about space, but also about mirrors and describe the image in a picture of a purely geometric plane.

**Geometry by plane picture**

There is a (fundamental) polygon $M$ with edges $R_1$, $R_2$, ... A picture is placed inside the polygon (we consider the polygon as a flat figure). In the edges $R_k$ reflections of the polygon $M$ (Fig. 7) are produced, which results in polygons with edges $R_1'$, $R_2'$, ... Reflections are also produced in these edges, resulting in new polygons. By moving in this way, we pave the entire picture plane.
