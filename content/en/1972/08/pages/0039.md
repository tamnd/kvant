Conservation laws

It is well known that conservation laws apply in Newtonian mechanics. They are most simply formulated for a closed system. This is the name of a system of bodies that interact with each other and do not interact with any other bodies. Frankly speaking, closed systems do not exist in nature. The gravitational and electromagnetic fields, the flows of cosmic particles do not allow any system of bodies to “close in on itself.” There are, however, almost closed systems. The forces acting on the bodies entering them from foreign bodies are very small, and such systems behave almost indistinguishably from closed systems. Therefore, it seems important to become familiar with the basic properties of closed systems.

It turns out that the seven magnitude energy of the system, the three components of its momentum and the three components of its angular momentum do not change over time (“conserved”) if the system is closed.

It is easy to find the conservation law in Aristotle's mechanics. For this purpose, let us consider some “closed” system of bodies. The word closed implies that from the point of view of Newtonian mechanics, not a single system of bodies in Aristotle’s mechanics is closed - which body interacts with the environment is responsible for the friction force.

Let $r_1(t), r_2(t), \ldots, r_n(t)$ be the radius vectors of the bodies forming a “closed” system. Let's build a vector

$$R = \frac{\sum_i \frac{1}{\alpha_i} r_i(t)}{\sum_i \frac{1}{\alpha_i}}$$

and above it the main vector of the system. We invite the reader to independently prove the following statements: 1) the main vector of the “closed” system is preserved - does not change over time; 2) if external forces act on bodies, then the end of the main vector of the system (the body is no longer closed) moves as a body with mobility

$$\alpha = \frac{1}{\sum_i \frac{1}{\alpha_i}}$$

under the action of the sum of external forces applied to the system. This statement is not difficult to prove, using Aristotle’s Law and the definition of the main vector of the system.
