Fig. 8.

As the item $R$, you can use the glowing filament of an electric lamp. The lamp should be placed in a housing, and a hole with a diameter of 40-80 mm should be made in one of the walls of the housing. At a distance of a meter from the lamp, you need to place an opaque screen (for example, a sheet of plywood) with a hole with a diameter of about 10 mm. This hole should be covered with a piece of foil with a hole pierced in it (screen $\partial_1$). For the experiment, you can use a lens $L$ with a focal length of 5-10 cm. The white screen $\partial_2$ (distance of thick white paper) is located at a distance of 1-2 meters from the screen $\partial_1$.

Fig. 9.

At the beginning of the experiment, unscrew the lens $L$ from the screen $\partial_1$ to a distance exceeding the sum of the focal lengths of the hole and the lens, and then attach it to the screen $\partial_1$. On the white screen $\partial_2$ you will observe the image of the object created by the hole $R_1$ (Fig. 9, a), then a sharp image of the hole in the screen $\partial_1$

(Fig. 9, b), and, finally, the image of the minimum image $R_2$ of the object created by the hole (Fig. 9, c). In the first case, on the screen $\partial_2$ you will receive a direct image of the object $R$, in the latter - inverted.

Instead of moving lens $L$ closer to screen $\partial_1$, you can shuffle screen $\partial_2$.

The experiment showed that the hole gives a real and a minimal image of an object, that is, it acts simultaneously as both a coinciating and a diverging lens. Is it possible to take advantage of this amazing ability of the hole in practice?

Exercises

1. For an ideal lens the following formula is valid:

$$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{f},$$

where $a$ is the distance from the object to the lens, $b$ is the distance from the lens to the image, and $f$ is the focal length of the lens. Having obtained the Rayleigh criterion, derive a similar formula for a circular hole. (The point light source is on the main optical axis at a distance of $a$ from the hole.)

2. The object is at a finite distance from the hole. Where is the image of the object created by the hole, and what does it represent?

3. The length of the “hole” tube (the distance between its “lens” and the front focal plane of the eyepiece) is 500 mm. What is the maximum diameter of the “lens” hole if the pipe is to be used as a telescope?

4. The “hole” labor criterion suggests using it as a microchip. The length of the tube is 500 mm, and the distance from the object to the “lens” is 50 mm. What is the maximum diameter of the lens opening can also be used as a telescope.

5. It is very difficult for you to carry out the satisfied experience described in the article in order to confirm the existence of a simultaneous active and minimal image of an object. Come up with (and perform) a simpler experiment.
