The use of telescopes significantly expands the possibility of detecting multiple stars.

The angular dimensions of the diffraction patterns are generally small; To see them, it is necessary to use eyepieces that provide such magnifications that the angular dimensions of the details of the observed picture would be greater than the angle of resolution of the human eye.

Thus, a telescope with an ideal lens with a diameter of 6 cm allows one to obtain a diffraction pattern with an angular size of the radius of the central disk $\alpha = 14:6 = 2,3''$. In order for the eye to see this radius at an angle of $3' = 180''$, it is necessary to use an eyepiece that gives a magnification of $180'':2,3'' \approx 80$ times. With increasing magnification of the telescope, the visible angular dimensions of the diffraction images of stars increase, but at the same time the intensity of the diffraction patterns themselves decreases, and in faint stars the rings only disappear - there is not enough light energy to damage the retina over an increased area.

Experience shows that for stars of the same brightness, indifference occurs when the angular distance between the centers of the components of the double star is equal to

$$\psi_0 = 0,85\alpha = \frac{12''}{D(cm)}.$$

Therefore, the angle $\psi_0$ characterizes the resolution of a good telescope.
