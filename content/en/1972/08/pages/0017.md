Convenient for remote measurement is an electric pressure gauge, which uses the property of mangiin wire *) to linearly increase its resistance with pressure. If the resistance of a coil of mangiin wire at atmospheric pressure was $R_0$, and at $P_x$ it became equal to $R_x$, then

$$P_x = k \frac{R_x - R_0}{R_0},$$

where $k$ is the coefficient ($k \approx 0,5 \times 10^6$ bar), known from preliminary calibrations using an abcylot pressure gauge. A mangiin pressure gauge is used to measure pressure in the range of 1000-100,000 atmospheres. How to change pressure over 100,000 atmospheres? At such pressures, the pistons of pressure gauges deform very strongly: the best steels and hard alloys become plastic, and it is impossible to talk about exact dimensions. In such cases, pressure values ​​are determined by changes in the physical properties of liquid substances.

*) Mangiin is an alloy of 86% Cu, 11% Mn, 3% Ni.
