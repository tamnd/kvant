Solution. The potential at the center of the flask is equal to the sum of the potential created by individual elementary charges $\Delta Q_i$ on the flask. Since the charge $\Delta Q_i$ can be considered as exact, then

$$\varphi = \sum_i \frac{\Delta Q_i}{R} + c = \frac{Q}{R} + c.$$

The potential energy of interaction of charge $-q$ with charge $+Q$ is equal to

$$\Pi = -q\varphi = -\frac{qQ}{R} - qc.$$

Total energy at the initial moment

$$E_H = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} - \frac{qQ}{R} - qc.$$

In the final state, the kinetic energy of the particle is $\frac{mv^2}{2}$ and the potential energy is $-qc$, since the distance-dependent term of the potential energy vanishes. According to the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{qQ}{R} - qc = \frac{mv^2}{2} - qc.$$

From here

$$v = \sqrt{v_0^2 - \frac{2Qq}{mR}}.$$

As you can see, the result does not depend on the value of the constant $c$.
