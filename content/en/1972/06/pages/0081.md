3. When the radius of the shell increases by a small amount $\Delta x$, the electron forces perform work $\Delta A = 4\pi R^2 f \Delta x$, where $f$ is the force per unit area. Work is done due to the loss of electronic energy:

$$\frac{Q^2}{2R} - \frac{Q^2}{2(R + \Delta x)} = \frac{Q^2 \Delta x}{2R(R + \Delta x)}.$$

According to the law of conservation of energy: $4\pi R^2 f \Delta x = \frac{Q^2 \Delta x}{2R(R + \Delta x)}$. Considering that $\Delta x \ll R$, we obtain $f = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi R^4} = 2\pi \sigma^2$, where $\sigma = \frac{Q}{4\pi R^2}$ is the surface charge density.

4. The potential of the droplet is $\varphi_1 = \frac{q}{r}$, where $q$ is the charge and $r$ is the radius of the droplet. The potential of a large drop is $\varphi = \frac{Nq}{R} = \frac{N\varphi_1 r}{R}$. Obviously $\frac{4\pi}{3} r^3 N = \frac{4\pi}{3} R^3$. Hence $\frac{r}{R} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}$ and $\varphi = \frac{N\varphi_1}{\sqrt{N}} = N^{2/3}\varphi_1$.
