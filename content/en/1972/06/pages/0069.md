At the very end of 1970 and in January 1971, two volumes of M. E. Podtyagin’s books “Elementary Mathematics” (theory and practice) * appeared on bookstore shelves. Almost immediately the entire circulation was sold out: books with such a promising title do not linger on bookstore shelves. What did the 20 thousand buyers of M. E. Podtyagin’s two-volume book spend their 25 thousand rubles on?
Let's open the first volume. Already on the 7th page (from which, in fact, the book begins), the eye stumbles upon the phrase: “Only a number can be used in practice.” How do you define the concept of this deep-thinking concentration? That everything that is not directly related to mathematics (let's say air, water, sun, bread...) cannot be used in practice? Or that in mathematics itself, all sorts of sets, functions, graphs and others again have no practical significance? - Don't rack your brains. This phrase doesn't mean anything.
