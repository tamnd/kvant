⟦folio 44⟧

We'll tell you in the oral exam

E. B. Vakhovsky, A. B. Volinsky

What did you do in the oral exam in mathematics? This question is especially often asked by those receiving calls where increased requirements are imposed (Moscow State University, Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology and others). The standard answer, which is that you need to clearly answer the tickets and know all the points of the program, satisfies few people. “We know how to answer a ticket and what kind of tickets are available from the final school exams,” say applicants, “but we don’t know what the student has to do after answering the ticket.”

To a certain extent this question is justified. While the literature now available can give applicants an almost complete understanding of the written examinations, the materials for the oral examinations exist only in the form of pamphlets published by various institutes for internal use.

Currently, state authors have made an attempt to highlight the specifics of the oral exam using several examples.

The article is divided into two parts. The first part will contain formulations of problems without solutions (the most complex problems will be given brief instructions first); in the second part there will be given decisions or instructions. It is assumed that the reader will first try his hand on his own, then look at the instructions, if there is one, and only then look at the solution if all the conditions are ineffective.

If we consider all the questions (tasks) asked during the oral exam, then their conditions can be divided into two levels.

For questions of the first (simpler) level, you can answer those questions that an applicant who thoroughly knows the program can and should answer almost immediately - in a few minutes. If an applicant answers incorrectly or makes it difficult to answer such a question, then this means that he has gray problems in the basics of elementary mathematics.

The second level tasks are more complex. They will, of course, require the applicant to have active rather than simple mastery of the school curriculum. It should, however, be noted that such questions can only be encountered in institutes with increased requirements in mathematics.

First level questions

1. Solve the equation
$$\frac{x^2 + 2x + 1}{x^2 + 2x + 1} = 1.$$

2. If in the equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$, where $p, q$ are real numbers
a) $q > 0$, then is it true that its roots are of the same sign?
b) $q < 0$, then is it true that the roots of it are different signs?
