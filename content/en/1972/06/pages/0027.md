Let us first prove this lemma.

Lemma. The projection of a vector onto the axis does not depend on from which point in space the vector is plotted.

Proof. Let $C'D'$ be the projection of the vector $CD$ onto the axis $l$, that is, $CC'$ and $DD'$ are the perpendiculars dropped from the point $C'$ and $D$ to $l$ (Fig. 4). Let $E$ be the fourth vertex of the parallel $CC'D'E$, then $CE = C'D'$, $CC' || ED'$ and therefore $ED' \perp l$.

Since $ED' \perp l$ and $DD' \perp l$, then $DE \perp l$ and therefore $DE \perp CE$. Thus, we have proven that the projection of the vector $CD$ onto the axis $l$ is equal to its projection onto the axis parallel to $l$ and passing through the origin of the vector - point $C$:

$$C'D' = CE.$$

Now the statement of the lemma is very convenient (if $C_1D_1 = CD$, then the corresponding right triangles $CDE$ and $C_1D_1E_1$ are equal in acute angle $\varphi = \angle ECD = E_1C_1D_1$ and hypotenuse $CD = C_1D_1$).
