SCHOOLCHILDREN CLEANING THE RINK
(see page 35)

Work is spent moving the snow outside the skating rink. It is best to move each lump of snow to a close point on the boundary of the skating rink (along the radius). Consequently, the work that schoolchildren must do in this case is proportional to the distance from the lump of snow to the edge of the skating rink, therefore it is proportional to the volume of the column above the piece of snow, the height of the column is equal to the distance from the piece of snow to the edge of the skating rink. Let's build all such columns; then the work of clearing the skating rink will be numerically equal to the sum of the volumes of all the columns. For a round skating rink, the columns will fill a circular cone with a right angle at the apex (Fig. 1). If $h$ is the radius

roller, then the volume of the constructed cone is equal to $\frac{\pi}{3} R^3$, that is, proportional to the cube of the radius of the roller. Consequently, schoolchildren will clear a skating rink with a radius of 20 m in 8 hours. Schoolchildren will be able to clear the hockey rink much faster. Columns built over the hockey rink fill a body shaped like a “barn roof” (Fig.2).
