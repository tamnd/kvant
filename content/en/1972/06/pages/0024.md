Leibniz correspondence and the distribution property of the scalar product of vectors

Z. A. Skopets

Recall that the scalar product of two vectors is a number equal to the product of the length (modules) of these vectors and the cosine of the angle between them:

$$a \cdot b = |a| \cdot |b| \cdot \cos(a, b).$$

(1)

This definition is suitable for vectors both on the plane and in space. Scalar multiplication is related to the addition of distribution vectors (distributive) law:

$$a(b + c) = ab + ac.$$

(2)

This property is convenient to use in many cases, especially when proving stereometric theorems about perpendicular lines and planes*). But the trouble is that usually property (2) itself is proven using some of these theorems**). In this note we will show that there is a way free from this shortcoming: we will derive property (2) from the Leibniz correspondence for the elements of a triangular paramide, and then prove this correspondence without using theorems of stereometry.
