In the gravitational field

Even in a school physics course, it is pointed out that there is a deep analogy between gravitational and power fields, resulting from the formal mathematical similarity of Newton’s law of gravity and Coulomb’s law. Both in the gravitational field and in the power field, the work of moving bodies or electric charges does not depend on the shape of the path, but is determined only by the potentials of the points of the beginning and end of the movements and the magnitude of the masses or charges. From here, in turn, it follows that the work along a closed loop, both in one and the other field, is equal to zero.

The potential of any point in the power plant field $\varphi = \frac{kQ}{r}$, where $Q$ is the exact charge that formed the field, $r$ is the distance from this charge to the point at which the potential $\varphi$ is determined, and $k$ is a constant, depends on the choice of the system of units. In the GHS system $k = 1$; in SI $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$; $k$ is the proportionality coefficient in Coulomb’s law.

The work done to move the exact charge $q$ is equal to

$$A = q \left( \frac{kQ}{r_2} - \frac{kQ}{r_1} \right),$$

where $r_1$ corresponds to the initial point at which the charge $q$ is located, and $r_2$ to its final position.

From the analogy between the gravitational and power fields, we conclude that the work $W$ in the gravitational field, for example, of the Earth is equal to

$$W = -m \left( \frac{\gamma M}{r_2} - \frac{\gamma M}{r_1} \right),$$

where $\gamma$ is the gravitational constant (the coefficient of proportionality in the law of gravitation), $M$ is the mass of the Earth, $m$ is the mass of the exact body, transferable in the field of the Earth’s gravitational force, and $r_1$ and $r_2$ are, respectively, the initial and final distances from this body to the center of the planet.

We put a “—” sign in this formula, since two bodies always bend down. This corresponds to the interaction of positive and imprinted charges. In order to increase the distance between the bodies $(r_2 > r_1)$, positive work must be done. When the distance between bodies decreases, the direction of movement of the body coincides with the direction of the force acting on it. Therefore, in this case the work is structural.

Let us now determine the potential energy of the body at a distance $R$ from the center of the Earth. Since the change in potential energy is equal to the work expended on moving the body, then, assuming that the potential energy is equal to zero infinitely far from the Earth $(r_1 = \infty)$, we find

$$U = -\frac{\gamma M}{R} m.$$
