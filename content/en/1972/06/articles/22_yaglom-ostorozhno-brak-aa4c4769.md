At the very end of 1970 and in January 1971, two volumes of M. E. Podtyagin’s books “Elementary Mathematics” (theory and practice) * appeared on bookstore shelves. Almost immediately the entire circulation was sold out: books with such a promising title do not linger on bookstore shelves. What did the 20 thousand buyers of M. E. Podtyagin’s two-volume book spend their 25 thousand rubles on?
Let's open the first volume. Already on the 7th page (from which, in fact, the book begins), the eye stumbles upon the phrase: “Only a number can be used in practice.” How do you define the concept of this deep-thinking concentration? That everything that is not directly related to mathematics (let's say air, water, sun, bread...) cannot be used in practice? Or that in mathematics itself, all sorts of sets, functions, graphs and others again have no practical significance? - Don't rack your brains. This phrase doesn't mean anything. Those readers «Kvant» who have younger brothers or sisters will easily be convinced by looking at their textbooks that such pseudo-definitions have long ceased to be stuffed into first-graders. True, M.E. Podtyagin provides his “definition” with the following: “There are no rules for adding single-digit numbers,” but, unlike the tabological “definition,” it is simply wrong! (We all remember the “addition table” - although we usually don’t use this term - even before the multiplication table - and it is this that allows us to add any multi-digit numbers.) And how do you like the “rule”:

“Subtraction of natural numbers is possible only under the condition $c \geq b$”
(t. I, p. 13), not accompanied by any erroneous meanings of the designations $c$ and $b$. (True, his errors will not save him: anyway, the non-structured inequality would have to be replaced with a strict one, since the natural series in the book begins not from zero, but from one...) The following “rule” is also mysterious:

“To divide a part by a number, it is sufficient to divide by the product of the divisor and the quotient”
(t. I, p. 21) - it’s easy to remember, but we wouldn’t recommend answering this way on the exam to anyone... The author defines the combinatory property of adding numbers (vol. I, p. 12) based on the concept of the sum of several numbers, completely forgetting that this concept itself is introduced on the basis of the combinatory law for addition! Likewise, the chapter on equations (Vol. I, p. 137) begins with the definition:

“Two numbers or two expressions are considered equal if the difference between them is zero.”
— thus, the author defines the concept of “right” through the concept of “right”. On page 22, volume I, the author declares the tanistic “property of moving a row of divisions”, without even trying (in our opinion, however, quite reasonable!) to dictate what it (“property”) could consist of... The trusted reader finds himself in a funny position when he decides to prepare for university with the help of M.E. Podtyagin! So if you are unlucky enough to buy these two amazing books, then don't be stubborn and waste your money: throw these books away immediately!

Isn't this advice a bit hasty? No way! After all, the “guide” is literally books and contains the most basic mistakes. A few examples. On page 60, volume I, the condition of irreducibility is not stipulated by the fact that these segments “are legs of unequal triangles” (a typical case of an incomprehensible difference between the direct and inverse theorem!)
