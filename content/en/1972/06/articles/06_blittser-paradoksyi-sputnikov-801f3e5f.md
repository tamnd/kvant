PARADOXES
SPUTIIKOV

The law of inertia is well known. And although on Earth we have never seen a uniformly moving body on which no forces acted, nevertheless, a puck on ice can serve as a good model of such a body: the friction force is small, and the force of gravity is compensated by the reaction force of the ice. In space, where artificial satellites fly, nothing is compensated by gravity. Therefore, there bodies that are not acted upon by any forces (except for the force of gravity) move along ellipses, parabolas or hyperbolas. This can be considered the “law of inertia” for satellites *).

What does “Newton’s second law” look like for a satellite, that is, how does the speed of a satellite change if any force acts on it other than the force of gravity towards the Earth? This is described in the article, which we reread from the “American Journal of Physics” for 1971. The text of § 2 is slightly modified in order to consider motion not along an ellipse, but along a circle, since formulas for such motion can be easily obtained by using a school physics textbook. The ellipticity of the satellite's orbit in this case is not significant.

The article was prepared for publication by N. Ya. Smorodinskaya.

I. Introduction

What do you think happens to the speed and kinetic energy of an artificial Earth satellite when braking in the atmosphere? You will certainly be mistaken if you are guided only by what your everyday “earthly” experience tells you. It turns out that the speed and kinetic energy of the satellite are returned when braking in the atmosphere!

This amazing phenomenon was truly unexpected that it was called a paradox. We have discussed here three seemingly perfect developments, the explanation of which is associated with this paradox.

1) Reducing the size of the satellite's orbit and increasing its speed in the earth's atmosphere.

2) Oscillation of the Earth's equatorial satellite relative to a stable equilibrium position.

3) Change in the continuity of an earthly place.

The first of these phenomena is explained by braking in the atmosphere, the reason for the second lies in the “three-dimensionality” of our planet, and the third is due to the fact that tidal protrusions form on the Earth’s surface.

II. Unperturbed Keplerian orbit

Let's remember the simplest equations of motion that obey Kepler's law. If the Earth were spherically symmetrical and if there were no atmosphere or other disturbing factors around it, then it is well known that the orbit of the earth’s satellite would be an ellipse, one of the foci of which would lie at the center of the Earth (Fig. 1).

When the satellite's orbit is close to a circle with radius $a$, then its period of revolution around the Earth is equal to

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}},$$

where $\mu = \gamma M$ (γ is the gravitational constant and $M$ is the mass of the Earth).

According to Kepler's third law, the period of revolution of the satellite in an elliptical orbit with a semi-major axis equal to $a$ will also be the same.

It can be shown that when moving in a circular orbit, the potential energy $U$, kinetic energy $K$ and total energy $E$ of the satellite are expressed in terms of $a$, and the mass of the satellite $m$ as follows:

$$U = -\frac{\mu m}{a}*),$$

$$K = \frac{\mu m}{2a},$$

$$E = -\frac{\mu m}{2a}.$$

*) See the article by N. M. Speransky “Potential energy of bodies in a gravitational field” on page 20.

Fig. 1. Keplerian ellipse. $F$—disturbing force.

From this it is clear that these quantities are related by the relation

$$U = -2K = 2E.$$

For an elliptical orbit, correspondence (5) and formulas (2)–(4) remain true, but instead of kinetic and potential energy, we need to talk about the average (per one revolution around the Earth) kinetic and average potential energy of the satellite.

III. Explanation of the paradox

Let us now consider what happens when some arbitrary additional force acts on the satellite. Obviously, if this force is greater than the gravitational force ($\frac{\mu}{r^2}$), then it should play the main role. This condition is met, for example, at the moment of satellite launch. If the disturbing force is small compared to the gravitational forces, then the orbit must be Keplerian (that is, an ellipse or circle.) It is this case that we will study.

Any continuously acting disturbing force $F$ can be replaced by a sequence of infinitely small impulses. As a result of the work done by force $F$ during an arbitrary small time interval $\Delta t$, the energy of the satellite returns to values ​​$\Delta E$:

$$\Delta E = F_T \Delta S = F_T v \Delta t,$$

where $F_T$ is the tangential component of the force $F$, directed tangentially to the orbit, and $v$ is the speed of the satellite.

An infinitely small increment of energy $\Delta E$ corresponds to an increase in the radius of the satellite’s orbit by an infinitely small amount $\Delta a$.
