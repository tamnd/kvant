Particles from the solar interior

The only source of energy that can support the activity of the Sun is the nuclear reactions occurring in its depths. Modern physicists and astronomers think so. This hypothesis can be tested experimentally. The chain of nuclear reactions that take place in the Sun begins with the collision of two protons, which at the same time emit a positron (e⁺) and an antineutrino and turn into a heavy hydrogen nucleus - deuteron:

$$p + p \rightarrow d + e^+ + \bar{v}.$$

No matter how deep in the bowels of the Sun such a reaction takes place, atineutrinos must fly out of the Sun, since they interact very weakly with matter and therefore only a very small part of these parts will be absorbed inside the Sun.

For many years, physicists have been preparing to detect on Earth atyneutrinos flying from the Sun. This turned out to be a very difficult matter, and recently doubts have even arisen about the very existence of the solar atineutrino flow. At least it was reported that in any case there are significantly fewer of them than was required by theory to ensure the entire flow of energy generated in the bowels of the Sun.

Finally, as the summer passed, the efforts of the physicists increased in success. American physicist Davis, who worked for many years on methods for registering such particles, reported that he was able to register atineutrinos flying from the Sun.

It was recorded using a counter filled with the isotope of chlorine - chlorine-37. (In fact, the counter contained approximately 500,000 liters of liquid carbon tetrachloride CCl₄.) The chlorine nuclei were converted into argon-37 nuclei by the reaction

$$\bar{v} + Cl^{37} \rightarrow Ar^{37} + e^-.$$

In the fires, the amount of argon formed was measured by its radioactive decay, which was calculated according to the scheme

$$Ar^{37} \rightarrow Cl^{37} + e^+ + \bar{v}.$$

This method was proposed many years ago by academician B. M. Pontecorvo.

In its huge details, there is only one Ar³⁷ decay every two days. However, this is exactly how many of them there should be, if we take into account that, as in the Sun, the majority of antineutrinos are shed in total. Based on their calculations, physicists expected that the number of antineutrinos would be 6 times larger, but the theory is not so accurate that such a discrepancy would cause server concern. Be that as it may, atineutrinos, which indicate thermonuclear reactions that supply the Sun (and therefore our Earth) with energy, have finally been registered.
