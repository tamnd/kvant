We encounter various waves everywhere, from gamma rays with a very short wavelength to huge wave movements in the region of fine dust scattered between stars. But since all waves carry energy, it is not surprising that their behavior is largely the same.

In a homogeneous medium, waves propagate in straight lines at a constant speed, changing direction and speed in places where the physical properties of the medium change. If a sound wave in the atmosphere hits a solid object, such as a brick wall, then part of it returns, like an echo, to the source.

Studying one type of wave allows one to explore what can be expected from other types of waves. For example, some acoustic properties of an audience can be judged by observing the behavior of waves in a flat bath of water.

A wave bath can be done at home (Fig. 1). Its main part consists of a wooden frame 6 with a glass house. The frame thickness is about 5 cm, the glass bottom area is ≈ 0.2 m². To prevent water from leaking, it should be attached to the frame with mastic or plastic-LIN. The bathtub is installed on four legs at a height of about 0.5 m from the floor. A matte lamp 5 with a power of 100 W is used as a light source to cast shadows from fish onto screen 9 located under the glass. The lamp is placed in a box made of fireproof cardboard 2 and installed at a height of about half a meter above the bathtub on a wooden frame. In the box you need to do answer 3 to get an accurate light source.

A wooden crossbar 11 is fixed to two brackets 10. A block is suspended from this crossbar with elastic bands, which serves to excite waves. The distance between the bar and the water can be adjusted by changing the position of the metal brackets and shortening or lengthening the rubber hangers.

In the middle of the block (Fig. 2) the battery for the toy motor is secured with a white attachment 4. Several beads 5 made of glass or plastic are attached to the bar using a rigid wire bent at a right angle; the distance between them can be changed if you make responses in the bar every 5 cm. A motor 2-3 cm long is attached to the shaft, which performs

A soft aluminum mesh 8 is passed along the inner edge of the bathtub (Fig.3), bent at a right angle along the entire length and covered with one layer of gauze 9. This combination of gauze and mesh absorbs the energy of incoming waves. This prevents reflections from the edges of the bath, which could cause interference.

The device is leveled, the bath is filled with water to a depth of 2 cm, the lamp is turned on, the moto terminal is connected to the steel spring and the height of the wooden block is set so that one of the glass balls touches the water. The rotation of the eccentric weight causes the rectangular bracket to vibrate, and the ball rises and falls in the water. The amplitude of the waves is adjusted by changing the position of the loose nut on the mounting screw. The wavelength, determined by the distance between the crests of adjacent waves, varies depending on the speed of the moto. The contrast between light and shadow of the wave pattern is projected onto the screen and adjusted by turning the lamp. The device must have at least two balls, that is, two precise wave sources. To obtain waves with straight fronts (an analogue of plane waves propagating in three-dimensional media), the ball holder is turned upward, and the block itself is lowered into water.

Install the device so that it excites plane waves with a distance between the crests of about 5 cm. The waves will diverge from the wave source to simulate waves.

Place a paraffin barer in the bath at an angle of 45° and watch how the waves are reflected (Fig. 4). In particular, we can verify that the angle $\Theta_i$ between the direction of wave propagation and the line perpendicular to the barer is equal to the angle $\Theta_r$ between the direction of propagation of the reflected waves and the same perpendicular.

Set the barer at a different angle than 45° and change the wavelength and amplitude. It is easy to verify that, for any location of the barer, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. This is the law of reflection for waves of any type.

Replace the paraffin barer with a glass plate 15 cm wide and 30 cm long. Install it so that the top surface is 1 cm from the bottom of the bath. Fill the bath with water so that the distance from the glass to the surface of the water is 2-3 mm, and create a series of plane waves. See how the waves change as they cross the edge of the glass and hit shallow water. Due to the change in speed, the waves will spread in a new direction over the glass, just like a line of soldiers moving from a land location onto a dirt road (Fig. 5). In this mode, the waves are deflected from the first direction due to refraction, a phenomenon observed in waves of all types when they travel at an angle from one medium to another, in which they propagate at a different speed. Waves on water are distinguished by the fact that they change the speed of propagation as the depth of the medium changes. Approximately, we can assume that the ratio of wave propagation velocities in shallow and deep water is proportional to the ratio of water depth. Both phenomena are due to the fact that moving wave sources can cancel out and sometimes even overpower the waves they emit. To demonstrate the Doppler effect in a bath, take a small tube, which, using a membrane, driven by a solenoid, directs portions of air into the bath at regular intervals, simultaneously moving through the bath at a given speed. (You can look at several sections of the toy railroad at the edge of the bathtub and install the tube in the toy trailer). When the tube moves at a speed that changes the speed of the waves, the crests in front of it are located more often, and behind them - more rarely.

The Doppler effect is observed in various types of waves, including radio waves. Having obtained relatively few equipment, it is possible to determine the magnitude and direction of the satellite’s speed using radio signals from an artificial satellite, taking into account the Doppler effect.

All these experiments are just a few of the things that can be done with a few devices in a wave bath. Anyone who builds and equips such a bath will be convinced that there will be enough interesting experiences for more than one bad day.
