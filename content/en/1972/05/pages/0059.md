Two electrons are introduced into a cylindrical jar and connected to an electronic machine. The jar is filled with smoke through a rubber tube. When the electronic machine is “turned on,” the smoke disappears very quickly. How is this phenomenon obligatory?

All schoolchildren know how a thermal ammeter works. An electronic current passes through the wiring, the wiring heats up, lengthens, and the ammeter needle attached to it “creeps” upward. The wiring is heated to white potassium, the current is turned off. The ammeter needle begins to return to zero. At some point (that is, at certain wiring temperatures), a jump occurs - the arrow again “creeps” up, and then returns in the same direction to zero. What does this oblige?

An interesting experiment was demonstrated with a helium-neon gas laser. In the dark, a laser beam is projected onto the screen. A dot of light is visible on the screen. Then the experimenter places a plate in the path of the beam, and the picture on the screen changes: instead of the dots, a system of luminous dots appears, forming a square grid. In this case, the brightest points are located in two mutually perpendicular directions, forming a cross. What kind of plate was placed in the path of the beam?

Everyone really liked the report by *Nikolai Makarov* (10th grade, 239 school, Leningrad) “On the issue of free padenistre.” It reported the results of measurements of the magnitude of the acceleration of free padding.
