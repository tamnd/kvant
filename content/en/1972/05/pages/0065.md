From the similarity of triangles $SQA$ and $SPE$ we have
$$\frac{AQ}{PE} = \frac{SA}{SE},$$
(8)

and from the similarity of triangles $SAC'$ and $SEC$
$$\frac{SA}{SE} = \frac{AC'}{EC}.$$
(9)

Since $AC' \perp SC$, then $AC' < AC$, therefore from (9) it follows
$$\frac{SA}{SE} < \frac{AC}{EC}.$$
(10)

From (8) and (10) we obtain
$$\frac{AQ}{PE} < \frac{AC}{EC} \text{ или } \frac{AQ}{AC} < \frac{PE}{EC}.$$
(11)

Triangle $ABC$ is correct, so
$$\frac{AQ}{AC} = \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Then from (11) it follows
$$\sin \theta = \frac{PE}{EC} > \frac{1}{2},$$

that is, $\theta = 60^\circ$. It follows that points $A_1$ and $B_1$ lie on the segment $EF$, $P$ is the midpoint of $A_1B_1$ and $A_1B_1 < EF$.

Now it is not difficult to make figure (Fig. 3). Base side $AB$ accepted

Fig. 3.

intersects the recognized faces $A_1A_2SC$ and $B_1B_2SC$ at points $M$ and $N$.

Let us denote $x = MN$, $a = AB$, $b = A_1B_1$, $h$ as the straightness height. Let us find the volumes of the straight line $SABC$ and its part lying inside the straight line:

$$V_{SABC} = \frac{1}{6} ah \cdot QC,$$
$$V_{SMNC} = \frac{1}{6} hx \cdot QC,$$

where
$$\frac{V_{SABC}}{V_{SMNC}} = \frac{x}{a}.$$
(12)

From the similarity of triangles $MSN$ and $B_1SA_1$ we find
$$\frac{x}{b} = \frac{SQ}{SP}.$$
(13)

Consider the triangle $SPC$ (Fig. 4). Let's draw a straight line $QL \parallel PC$. From the similarity of three

Fig. 4.

triangles $SQL$ and $SPC$ we have
$$\frac{SQ}{SP} = \frac{QL}{PC}.$$
(14)

Next, $PC$ is the height of the base of the straight lines, $PC = b \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $QL = QC \cdot \sin \alpha$.

Angle $\alpha$ is the angle between edge $SC$ and height $QC$ of the base of a properly straight line. If $SF$ is the straightness height, then
$$FC = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$
By condition, $SC = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} a$, whence $\cos \alpha = \frac{1}{2}, \alpha = 60^\circ$, and, therefore,
$$QL = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = \frac{3a}{4}.$$

From (13) and (14) we find
$$\frac{x}{b} = \frac{SQ}{SP} = \frac{QL}{PC} = \frac{\sqrt{3}a}{2b},$$

whence $\frac{x}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. This, as follows from (12), gives the desired volume ratio.

Answer. $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
