The front cover features a sundial. They were made from silver, platinum and bronze in 1620 in Augsburg (Germany) by the Frenchman Nicola Rougan. From the second half of the 18th century, portable clocks (Rugan clock ring diameter 100 mm) were also widely used to set the true solar time and time.

The needle, the shadow of which falls on the hour ring, is placed in the center of the ring perpendicular to its plane. In its working position, it points to the North Star, that is, it is located along the axis of the world. The ring is equipped with hour divisions every 15°. When adjusting, the plate - the base of the clock - is installed in a horizontal plane with the help of the plumb line and compass given to it, so that the compass needle and the silt are in the same vertical plane (meridonal). Then, using the dial, the plane of the hour ring is rotated to its working position. The shadow then moves almost uniformly along the kolyz scale, since the apparent daily movement of the Sun occurs uniformly along a circle, the plane of which is perpendluklar to the axis of the world, and is located within the limits indicated at figure.

The octagonal plate at the base of the clock on the front cover contains the solar calendar.
