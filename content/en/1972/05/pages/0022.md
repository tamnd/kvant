⟦folio 20⟧

We encounter various waves everywhere, from gamma rays with a very short wavelength to huge wave movements in the region of fine dust scattered between stars. But since all waves carry energy, it is not surprising that their behavior is largely the same.

In a homogeneous medium, waves propagate in straight lines at a constant speed, changing direction and speed in places where the physical properties of the medium change. If a sound wave in the atmosphere hits a solid object, such as a brick wall, then part of it returns, like an echo, to the source.

Studying one type of wave allows one to explore what can be expected from other types of waves. For example, some acoustic properties of an audience can be judged by observing the behavior of waves in a flat bath of water.

A wave bath can be done at home (Fig. 1). Its main part consists of a wooden frame 6 with a glass house. The frame thickness is about 5 cm, the glass bottom area is ≈ 0.2 m². To prevent water from leaking, it should be attached to the frame with mastic or plastic-LIN. The bathtub is installed on four legs at a height of about 0.5 m from the floor. A matte lamp 5 with a power of 100 W is used as a light source to cast shadows from fish onto screen 9 located under the glass. The lamp is placed in a box made of fireproof cardboard 2 and installed at a height of about half a meter above the bathtub on a wooden frame. In the box you need to do answer 3 to get an accurate light source.

A wooden crossbar 11 is fixed to two brackets 10. A block is suspended from this crossbar with elastic bands, which serves to excite waves. The distance between the bar and the water can be adjusted by changing the position of the metal brackets and shortening or lengthening the rubber hangers.
