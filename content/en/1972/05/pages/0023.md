The speed of the moto can be controlled by a conventional rheostat, consisting of a thin steel spring and an alligator clip. One end of the spring is connected to the battery, and the clamp is connected to one of the moto terminals. The required speed is selected by connecting the moto output to various points on the spring.

A soft aluminum mesh 8 is passed along the inner edge of the bathtub (Fig.3), bent at a right angle along the entire length and covered with one layer of gauze 9. This combination of gauze and mesh absorbs the energy of incoming waves. This prevents reflections from the edges of the bath, which could cause interference.

The device is leveled, the bath is filled with water to a depth of 2 cm, the lamp is turned on, the moto terminal is connected to the steel spring and the height of the wooden block is set so that one of the glass balls touches the water. The rotation of the eccentric weight causes the rectangular bracket to vibrate, and the ball rises and falls in the water. The amplitude of the waves is adjusted by changing the position of the loose nut on the mounting screw. The wavelength, determined by the distance between the crests of adjacent waves, varies depending on the speed of the moto. The contrast between light and shadow of the wave pattern is projected onto the screen and adjusted by turning the lamp. The device must have at least two balls, that is, two precise wave sources. To obtain waves with straight fronts (an analogue of plane waves propagating in three-dimensional media), the ball holder is turned upward, and the block itself is lowered into water.

Install the device so that it excites plane waves with a distance between the crests of about 5 cm. The waves will diverge from the wave source to simulate waves.
