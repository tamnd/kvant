Both phenomena are due to the fact that moving wave sources can cancel out and sometimes even overpower the waves they emit. To demonstrate the Doppler effect in a bath, take a small tube, which, using a membrane, driven by a solenoid, directs portions of air into the bath at regular intervals, simultaneously moving through the bath at a given speed. (You can look at several sections of the toy railroad at the edge of the bathtub and install the tube in the toy trailer). When the tube moves at a speed that changes the speed of the waves, the crests in front of it are located more often, and behind them - more rarely.

The Doppler effect is observed in various types of waves, including radio waves. Having obtained relatively few equipment, it is possible to determine the magnitude and direction of the satellite’s speed using radio signals from an artificial satellite, taking into account the Doppler effect.
