When solving problems using Coulomb's law, one must keep in mind that this law relates to the interaction of precise charged bodies and is not applicable to bodies of arbitrary shape. Bodies can be considered accurate in the case when their geometric dimensions are significantly less than the distance between them. Problem 2. A thin wire spike of radius $R$ carries an electric charge $q$. In the center of the ring there is a charge of the same name as $q$, $Q$, and $Q \gg q$. Determine the tensile force of the stab.

Since $Q \gg q$, the interaction between individual elements of the ring can be neglected. Let us select a small ring element of length $R\alpha$ (Fig. 3). The sides of the charge $Q$ act on it by a force $F = \frac{Q\Delta q}{R^2}$, where $\Delta q = \frac{q\alpha}{2\pi}$.

The tension force of the ring $T$ is balanced by $F$. From the equilibrium condition, taking into account that $\alpha$ is small, we have $F = 2T \sin \frac{\alpha}{2} = T\alpha$.

The required tension force is $T = \frac{Qq}{2\pi R^2}$.

The problem was solved in the SGSE system. In the SI system

$$T = \frac{Qq}{8\pi^2\varepsilon_0 R^2}.$$
