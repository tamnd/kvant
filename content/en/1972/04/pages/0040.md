2. It is easy to check the following points:
$$a^2(b-c) + b^2(c-a) + c^2(a-b) = -(b-c)(c-a)(a-b).$$
From this point it follows that $|b-c| |c-a| |a-b| \leqslant |a^2| |b-c| + |b^2| |c-a| + |c^2| |a-b|$ or (Fig. 7)
$$BC \cdot CA \cdot AB \leqslant OA^2 \cdot BC + OB^2 \cdot CA + OC^2 \cdot AB.$$ (8)
Corollary 1. If $ABC$ is an equilateral triangle, then $OA^2 + OB^2 + OC^2 \geqslant d^2,$ (8')
rede $d$ is the length of the side of the triangle.
So, the sum of the squares of the distances of any point on the plane to the vertices of an equilateral triangle is not less than the square of its side.
Corollary 2. If $O$ is the center of a circle circumscribed about an arbitrary triangle, then from (8) it follows
$$AB \cdot BC \cdot CA \leqslant R^2(AB + BC + CA)$$
or
$$\frac{AB \cdot BC \cdot CA}{AB + BC + CA} \leqslant R^2.$$ (8'')
Thus, the ratio of the arbitrariness of the three sides of a triangle to their sum does not exceed the square of the radius of the circle circumscribed about the triangle.
