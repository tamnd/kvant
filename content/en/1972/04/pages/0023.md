This mesh divides the plane into equal hexagons. Inside each of them lies exactly one node, which is its symmetrical center and the center of its circumscribed circle.

We claim that the resulting hexagons are Dirichlet clouds. In fact, the distance, direction, is a hexagon with a center at node O. Each of its sides is a perpendicular to the segment connecting the node with one of its neighboring nodes. Moreover, this perpendicular passes through the middle of the corresponding segment (Fig. 6). Therefore, the points of the hexagon are located from point O at a distance no greater than from the grid nodes surrounding point O. (Here we use the well-known fact: if we draw a perpendicular through the middle of the segment AB, then it splits the plane into two parts: one of these parts consists of points that are closer to A than to B, and the other consists of points that are closer to B than to A.)
