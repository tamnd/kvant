Task 4. A mathematical mathematician represents a ball of mass $m=1$ e, suspended on a silk thread of length $l=36$ cm. How will the period of the flask matrix change if, by imparting a positive or negative charge to the ball $q=\pm 20$ units. charge of the SGSE, place the matrix in a uniform electric field with a direction of $E=10$ units. direction SGSE, plum linear direction vertical down.

Without an electric field, the period of the flask matrix is ​​$T_0 = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} \approx 1,2$ sec. In an electric field, the acceleration of the matrix will be equal to $g + \frac{q}{m} E$. Hence,

$$T_1 = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g + \frac{q}{m} E}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{lm}{gm + qE}}.$$

If $q=+20$ units. SGSE charge, then $T_1 \approx 1,1$ sec and $T_1-T_0 \approx -0,1$ sec.

In the case of $q<0$ $T_2 \approx 1,35$ sec and $T_2-T_0 \approx 0,15$ sec.

Task 5. In a strong uniform electric field, directed $E$ on one plum line at points 1 and 2, located at a distance $l_0$ from each other, finding a proton and an electron (Fig. 4). The initial speed of the driver is zero. What will be the distance between the particles after a time $t$ after the start of motion?

Let us choose the origin at point 2, where the electron is located, and take the direction of the electric field as the positive direction. Neglecting the interaction of particles with each other (strong field), we can consider the motion of the electron and proton to be equally accelerated. Then, according to the well-known kinematic formula, the coordinate of the proton at the moment of time $t$ will be equal to

$$x_1 = l_0 + \frac{e}{m_\pi} E \frac{\tau^2}{2},$$

where $e$ is the charge, $m_\pi$ is the proton mass. Electron coordinate

$$x_2 = -\frac{e}{m_\pi} E \frac{\tau^2}{2},$$

where $m_\pi$ is the electron mass. Required distance

$$l = x_1 - x_2 = l_0 + \frac{eE\tau^2}{2} \frac{m_\pi + m_\pi}{m_\pi m_\pi} \approx \approx l_0 + \frac{eE\tau^2}{2m_\pi},$$

since $m_\pi \gg m_\pi$.

Conductors in an electric field

When solving a large number of problems, you need to know how conductors behave in an electrostatic field. It can be shown that the direction of the field inside the conductor is zero, and on its surface it is directed perpendicular to the conductor. The charge inside the conductor is also zero; it is all concentrated on the surface of the conductor.

The surface charge is most dense at the sharp points, where the curvature of the surface is maximum. A visual confirmation of this can be obtained by solving the following problem.
