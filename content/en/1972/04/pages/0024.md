Indeed, $f_0(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$. All our arguments remain the same (we can assume $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and the coordinate system $Oxy$ turns out to be rectangular (see Fig. 7)), only together with hexagons we get squares and the estimate $\bar{f}_0(x, y) \leq \frac{1}{2}$. The case of $a=2$, when $f_2(x, y) = (x+y)^2$, needs to be analyzed separately, here $\bar{f}_2(x, y)$ is equal to the square of the difference between the number $x+y$ and the nearest integer, so the maximum of $\bar{f}_2(x, y)$ is equal to $\frac{1}{4}$.

So, the estimate (*) $\bar{f}_a(x, y) \leq \frac{1}{a+2}$ is also true for $a=0, a=2$.

It remains to consider the case $a>2$. Let us show that for each point $(x, y)$ the smallest number $f_a(x-m, y-n)$ does not exist.

Indeed, let $a=2+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon>0$. Let's fix the point $(x, y)$ and consider $f_{2+\varepsilon}(x-k, y+k)$, where $k$ is some integer:

$$f_{2+\varepsilon}(x-k, y+k) = (x-k)^2 + 2(x-k)(y+k) + (y+k)^2 + \varepsilon(x-k)(y+k) - \varepsilon k^2 + \varepsilon(x-y)k + (x+y)^2 + \varepsilon xy.$$

We have obtained a quadratic trinomial with respect to $k$, the first coefficient of which is less than zero. Therefore, with $k$ tending to $+\infty$, this quantity will tend to $-\infty$, that is, it can be made less than any negative number. Thus, $\bar{f}_a(x, y)$ is undefined at $a>2$.

The more natural constraint on $a$ in the problem conditions was not $a \geq 0$, but $-2 \leq a \leq 2$.

For negative $a(-2 < a < 0)$ the solution is transferred without any changes: we follow $a=2$ cos $\varphi$ and take the angle $\frac{\pi}{2} < \varphi < \pi$.

Since the sieve obtained in this case is the same as for the angle $\psi = \pi-\varphi$, we also obtain the same estimate as for acute angles, that is, $\bar{f}_a(x, y) \leq \frac{1}{2+|a|}$.
