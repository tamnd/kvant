The long flight of cosmonauts A. G. Nikolaev and V. I. Sevastyanov on the Soyuz-9 spacecraft confirmed the possibility of successful long-term human work outside the Earth’s atmosphere. Cosmonauts have gone into outer space more than once; control systems for group flights have been debugged; spacecraft were docked; Space welding methods have been tested. On the surface of the day, the question has been raised about the creation of orbital observatories, where periodically changing groups of scientists will conduct scientific research on a broad program. The first raftable orbital scientific station was created in our country on June 7, 1971, after the successful orbital scientific station Salyut and the Soyuz-11 spacecraft.

Lunar exploration also progressed successfully. A particularly large contribution to them was made by the flights of the automatic stations “Luna-16”, “Luna-17” and “Luna-20”. The first and third ones delivered samples of lunar soil to Earth. The second lowered the mobile research laboratory “Lunokhod-1” onto the surface of the Moon, which for many months carried out a complex scientific program. (One of the panoramas of the lunar surface taken by the Lunokhod is shown in the photograph shown here.)

The Soviet Union is far advanced in the study of the planets of the solar system, primarily Venus and Mars. Our four landers are on the surface of Venus. They provided more detailed information about the temperature, pressure and chemical composition of the atmosphere of this mysterious planet. On December 2, 1971, for the first time in history, the descent module of the Mars-3 automatic station made a soft landing on the surface of Mars. The Mars-2 and Mars-3 stations became artificial satellites of Mars.

Our space science and technology will go a long way in a decade and a half. And ahead is the unknown road to more distant planets, to the stars and the world inhabited by intelligent beings. And who knows what amazing discoveries she will give us in the near future.
