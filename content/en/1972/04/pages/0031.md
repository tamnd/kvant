LET'S CALCULATE THE MAXIMUM VOLUME OF THE CONE. FOR THIS, we substitute the value of sin $\frac{\alpha}{2}$ found in (4) into (2):

$$V_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{3} \pi L^3 \left( \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} \right)^2 \sqrt{1 - \left( \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} \right)^2} = \frac{1}{3} \pi L^3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{2 \pi L^3 \sqrt{3}}{27} = 0,4030 L^3$$

It is interesting to note that such cones are found in nature, of course, with a certain approximation. Here is one physical example.

Task 3. Let an electronic light bulb be able to move (for example, on a block) along a vertical straight line OB (Fig. 3). At what distance from the horizontal plane OA should it be placed in order to obtain the most illuminated light at point A?

Solution. It is known that illumination $E$ is proportional to the cosine of the angle of incidence of the rays ($\Rightarrow BAN = \frac{\alpha}{2}$) and inversely proportional to the square of the distance ($AB = L$), that is

$$E = C \cdot \frac{\cos \frac{\alpha}{2}}{L^2},$$

where $C$ depends on the light intensity of the lamp.

Taking into account that $L = \frac{R}{\sin \frac{\alpha}{2}}$, we reduce formula (8) to the form

$$E = \frac{C}{R^2} \sin^2 \frac{\alpha}{2} \cos \frac{\alpha}{2}.$$

We note that $E$ is a proportional function (3), that is, this problem has been reduced to problem 2.

Thus, if a light bulb is placed at the top of a cone of maximum volume, then the circumference of its base will receive the most illumination (!).

In the textbook on organizational chemistry by L. A. Tsvetkov (Enlightenment, M., 1969, p. 16) there is:

“All valence bonds of atom carbon are directed to the vertices of the terrahedron, equally distant from each other, the angle between them is 109°28′.”

Let's check that the most illuminated illumination of the cone appears here too?

Let there be a carbon atom in the center $O$ of the correct terrahedra $SABC$ (Fig. 4), and the four valences are directed to its vertices. Let us prove that the triangles $AOS, BOS$ are the main sections of the cone of maximum volume, that is, that to the right,

$$\sin \frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}.$$

Let us denote $\Rightarrow AOS$ by $\alpha$. Let's do $ON \perp AS$, then $\Rightarrow NOS = \frac{\alpha}{2}$. Next, $\triangle AO_1S \sim \triangle ONS$ (obviously). Hence $\Rightarrow SAO_1 = \Rightarrow NOS = \frac{\alpha}{2}$.

If we denote the edge of the terrahedron by $a$, then $AO_1 = \frac{a \sqrt{3}}{3}$,

$$SO_1 = \sqrt{AS^2 - AO_1^2} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}.$$

Hence,

$$\sin \frac{\alpha}{2} = \sin \Rightarrow NOS = \sin \Rightarrow SAO_1 =$$

$$= \frac{SO_1}{AS} = \frac{a \sqrt{6}}{3a} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3},$$

which corresponds to formula (4).
