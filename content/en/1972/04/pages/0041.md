Task 3. Prove that $AB \cdot CD + BC \cdot DA \geq CA \cdot BD$, where $A, B, C, D$ are arbitrary points of the plane.

Task 4. Prove that $AB \cdot AM \cdot BM + BC \cdot BM \cdot CM + CA \cdot CM \cdot MA \geq AB \cdot BC \cdot CA$.

Task 5. Derive from the destvo point $\frac{a}{(a-b)(a-c)(a-d)} + \frac{b}{(b-c)(b-d)(b-a)} + \frac{c}{(c-d)(c-a)(c-b)} + \frac{d}{(d-a)(d-b)(d-c)} = 0$ the inequality $OA \cdot BC \cdot BD \cdot CD + OB \cdot CA \cdot CD \cdot AD + OC \cdot AB \cdot AD \cdot BD \geq OD \cdot AB \cdot BC \cdot CA$, where $O, A, B, C, D$ are arbitrary (different) points of the plane.

Directions

1) The equality holds for points $O, A, B$ located on the same line.

2) Take advantage of the fact that if $H$ is the orthocenter of triangle $ABC$, and the center of the circle circumscribed about the triangle coincides with the origin of coordinates, then $h = a + b + c$ ($a, b, c, h$ are complex numbers corresponding to points $A, B, C, H$). Note that $h - a = k_1(b + c)$, $h - b = k_2(c + a)$, $h - a = k_3(a + b)$, where $k_1, k_2, k_3$ are real numbers. From this system it follows that $k_1 = k_2 = k_3 = 1$.

3) Consider the identity $(m - a)(b - c) + (m - b)(c - a) + (m - c)(a - b) = 0$.

4) Consider the identity $(m - a)(m - b)(a - b) + (m - b)(m - c) \times (b - c) + (m - c)(m - a)(c - a) = (a - b)(b - c)(c - a)$.

THE HISTORY OF PERFECT NUMBERS CONTINUES

In «Kvant» No. 8, 1971, a story was published about the amazing history of perfect numbers. Let us recall that a natural number is called perfect if it is equal to the sum of all its divisors. The first such number is 6, then comes 28. In total, until recently, 23 perfect numbers were found.

And then the next number became known. According to the Scientific American magazine (June 1971), the American mathematician B. Thackerman obtained the largest prime number known today, namely

$$2^{19937} - 1$$

(the full record of this number contains 6002 digits). However, a new perfect number is also indicated for time:

$$2^{19936} (2^{19937} - 1)$$

(this number is written using 12,003 digits). The calculations took 40 minutes of computer time on one of the largest advanced computational compilations.

However, this discovery does not follow any patterns in the appearance of perfect numbers. In particular, the resulting perfect number, like all the previous ones, is even.

N. Rozov
