Cosmonautics Day

In 1972, it will be 15 years since the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite and 11 years since the first manned flight into space. Both of these events took place in our country. The Soviet Union will forever remain in the memory of people as the country that first paved the way in outer space.

A little more than ten years will separate us from these events. Over these years, science has learned more about the Moon, Venus, Mars, the Sun and outer space than in the entire previous centuries of persistent observations and research.

To date, more than a thousand different devices have been launched into space, half of which were made by the hands of Soviet scientists, engineers, and workers. Our space program generates the world through its consistency. It clearly distinguishes three main directions: the study of near-Earth space and the Earth itself, which have not only scientific but also greater practical significance; study of the moon; exploration of the planets of the solar system.

Soviet artificial satellites of the Meteor series made it possible to create a permanent service for monitoring meteorological processes in the atmosphere, ice sheets, ocean surfaces, etc. The Molniya series satellites unusually expanded the capabilities of information transmission. They are a reliable basis for the creation of the international space communications system “Intercosmos”.

Satellites provide invaluable assistance in studying the water regime and drawing up maps of hard-to-reach areas, searching for minerals and protecting forest resources, facilitating the habitat conditions of ships and small ships, and controlling pests in agriculture.
