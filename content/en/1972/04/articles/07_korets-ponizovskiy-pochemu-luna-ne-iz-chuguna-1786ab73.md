Why isn't the Moon made of cast iron?

As you know, Laputian scientists told Gulver that Mars has two satellites. The great Laputans reported at what distances from Mars these satellites move. Terrestrial astronomers discovered them a hundred years later, and many details of the Laputans’ message were confirmed. How did Swift know about the moons of Mars?

For a long time they saw it as a mere coincidence, until the fashion came to blame such “miracles” on aliens from outer space. According to some people, the aliens communicated a number of astronomical information to the Lods in coded form. This information was preserved in different ways - in fairy tales, songs, myths.

Obviously, a dialogue passed down from generation to generation by children: “Why isn’t the Moon made of cast iron? “Because there wouldn’t be enough cast iron for the Moon,” is also an excerpt from information about the surface of the Moon reported by aliens. Consequently, sooner or later, science had to confirm the idea contained in this dialogue.

Indeed, a study of the Moon showed that the Moon contains 15% iron (and the Earth 35%). In addition, the absence of a magnetic field near the Moon is explained by either the absence or a much smaller volume of a molten, iron-enriched core. Namely, it is the convection movements in this core of the Earth that now explain the magnetic field. And finally, the specific gravity of the Earth is 5.5, and the Moon is only 3.34 e/cm³, which corresponds to the density of a substance consisting mainly of silicates of the earth’s crust. According to Professor S. Runcorn (England), it can be assumed that 4.5 billion years ago, during the revolution of the Earth and its satellite, the Moon had already received a significant amount of iron. Why? The answer to this question is no. Perhaps the aliens knew this, but the continuation of the dialogue is assured with endless repetitions.

What is the Moon made of, anyway, since there is not enough cast iron for it? To answer this question we have to look for other sources. As you know, Baron Munchausen discovered an island on Earth consisting of cheese. From his words, this wonderful and reliable message was published by G. A. Berger back in 1786. Since then, proposals have repeatedly appeared that the Moon consists of the same materials. In general, the question of what type of cheese Luna is made of has caused heated debate.

The famous American geophysicist Professor O. Anderson from Columbia University was tasked with changing the speed of sound propagation in lunar rocks and finding analogues among terrestrial rocks. Naturally, the results were eagerly awaited.

On New Year's Eve, O. Anderson sent out a postcard to his imcom sign.

Here is a wave translation of the poem at the top of the card:

Illumination has descended upon us,
And all doubts disappeared
We inform the whole world,
That the Moon is a head of cheese.

BELOW PROFESSOR O. Anderson gives the speed of sound propagation $v_{3B}$ (km/sec) in the studied samples:

| Moon rocks and cheeses | Speed ​​of sound $v_{3B}$ (km/sec) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Moon rock sample 10 017 | 1,84 |
| Norwegian cheese | 1,83 |
| Italian cheese | 1,75 |
| Italian cheese (Primsky) | 1,75 |
| Vermont cheese | 1,72 |
| Swiss cheese | 1,65 |
| Wisconsin cheese | 1,57 |
| Moon rock sample 10,046 | 1,25 |

As can be seen from the table, for this physical parameter the indicators for cheeses coincide with the indicators for samples of lunar rocks. Thus, we can assume that cheese is the terrestrial analogue of lunar soil. Unfortunately, from the table above it is still unclear which type of cheese plays this role. However, it was possible to solve this riddle.

The seismic vibrations of the lunar soil after the package was dropped onto the Moon died down not in seconds, as was expected by all the rules, but in ten minutes. I suggest that this may be caused by the existence of some tannic cavities, the origin of which is unknown. But cheese (at least in some varieties) must have holes! Recalculate to the appropriate sizes, and the incomprehensible effect will be fully explained. Moreover. Since the largest holes are found in Swiss cheese, the question of the type of cheese is finally resolved.

So, the Moon is not made of cast iron, but of Swiss cheese. Hence the advice of scientists - to carefully review all folklore and all children's calculations. It is best to entrust this to a computer.
