ON THE RELATIVITY OF MOTION

When Huygens was thinking about the laws of collision of balls, he came very close to one important principle of modern mechanics - the principle of relative motion, that is, the independence of the laws of impact from the uniform motion of the system (we are talking about Galileo’s principle, but Galileo did not use it to solve problems).

Suppose that an experiment with a stolnov of identical balls is carried out by a person standing on a boat that floats along the mismo current of another person standing on the shore (figure taken from the book by W.I. Frankfurt and A.M. Frank “Christan Huygens”, M., publishing house of the USSR Academy of Sciences, 1962). The man in the boat clasps his hands like this

that the balls begin to move uniformly with a speed equal to, say, $\pm v$, where $v$ is the speed of the boat.

The ball in hand $A$ will move relative to the observable on the shore before the collision, and the ball in hand $B$ moves relative to the observable at a speed $2v$. After the collision, the poly balls will change: ball $B$ will remain, and ball $A$ will move along the motion of the boat with double speed $2v$. Thus, from the problem of the impact of identical balls moving towards another, the solution to the problem of the impact of a moving body on a body that is initially at rest is derived.
