STAMPS DEDICATED TO THE DAY OF COSMONAUTICS

On April 12, 1961, an outstanding event took place in the history of science and technology - man first ascended into outer space. The first cosmonaut was the Soviet communist Yuri Alekseevich Gagarin.

Since then, April 12 has been celebrated as a national holiday - Cosmonautics Day. For this day, special postage stamps are issued telling about outstanding achievements in the field of space exploration and exploration.

The first such stamp with the authorship of Yu. A. Gagarin was issued in 1962. She is depicted in the upper left corner. Nearby are the first stamps with the inscription “Cosmonautics Day”. They were published in 1963. The top one depicts the Vostok-1 spacecraft, and the bottom one shows a rocket launcher.

In 1964, a series of three stamps was issued for Cosmonautics Day. One of them depicts Yu. A. Gagarin, the other shows the first Soviet artificial satellites of the Earth, and the third shows the Mars-1 spacecraft.

One of two stamps issued in 1965 shows a spaceport (see bottom left). Two 1966 stamps commemorate our successes in lunar exploration. The first shows the flight path of the automatic station "Luna-9", which on February 3, 1966, floated down on the surface of the Moon in the area of ​​​​the Ocean of Storms. The lower stamp symbolically depicts some of the many possibilities for using space research in the interests of the national economy.
