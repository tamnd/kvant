The application of algebraic points is to obtain geometric inequalities

Z. A. Skopets

Algebraic points, valid with certain exceptions for the set of complex numbers, can be successfully applied to obtain interesting geometric inequalities connecting linear and angular elements of plane figures. Below, several geometric inequalities are obtained in this way. Note that it is quite difficult to find the number of geometric proofs of these inequalities. We will denote complex numbers by small letters of the Latin alphabet $a, b, c, \dots$. Recall that a complex number is the expression $x+iy$, where $x$ and $y$ are real numbers and $i$ is the minimum unit ($i^2=-1$). If a rectangular (Cartesian) coordinate system $Oxy$ is given on the plane, then each complex number $a=x+iy$ can be associated with a point $A$ of the plane with an abscissa $x$ and an ordinate $y$. Let us give some definitions related to complex numbers.
