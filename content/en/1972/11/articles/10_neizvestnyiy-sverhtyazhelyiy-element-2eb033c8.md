UNKNOWN SUPER HEAVY ELEMENT?

Until recently, it was believed that the xenon discovered in meteorites is formed only as a result of the fission of pulotonium 244. In 1969, it was proposed that xenon could also arise from the fission of a still unknown LET SUPERHEAVY Element, which had already decayed by the time the meteorite fell.

What do scientists now know about this still elusive hypothetical element? Its heat of evaporation, according to Professor E. Anders and J. Larimer (USA), is $(54 \pm 3)$ kcal/mol, and its boiling point $(2500 \pm 400)^\circ\text{K}$. The most suitable “candidates” for this element would be elements numbered 111 (eka-gold) and 115 (eka-bismuth) in the periodic table. It is followed in terms of probability by elements 113 (eka-thalium), 114 (eka-lead), 112 (eka-mercury) and 116 (eka-polony). Elements 105 (eka-tantalum) to 110 (eka-platinum) are not volatile enough and should very much be excluded from this list.
