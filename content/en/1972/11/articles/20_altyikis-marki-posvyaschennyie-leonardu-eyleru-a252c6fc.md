BRANDS DEDICATED
LEONARD EULER

Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) was the largest and most productive mathematician and mechanic of the 18th century. His research covered all areas of mathematics and its applications. Along with mathematics, the main sphere of his work was mechanics. Many theorems, formulas and methods perpetuate the name of Euler. During his life he wrote about 800 seminal works. Among his works there are many works devoted to astronomy, optics, calculations of the most correct designs of telecopes, microfalcons and even ordinary glasses (see article on pp. 31-33).

The first stamp with a portrait of Leonhard Euler was issued in the German Democratic Republic in 1950 in a series dedicated to the 250th anniversary of the Berlin Academy of Sciences. Then three stamps with Euler's portrait were issued in 1957 on the 250th anniversary of his birth. One of them, released in Euler's homeland in Switzerland, depicts his portrait, and on the left side is one of the formulas he derived. On another, released in the Soviet Union, Euler's portrait is depicted against the backdrop of the St. Petersburg and Berlin Academy of Sciences, of which he was a member. The third stamp was issued by the German Democratic Republic in the “Outstanding Scientists” series. You see all these brands in the photo.
