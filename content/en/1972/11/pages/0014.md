It was based on the remarkable phenomenon of freezing of pieces of ice brought into contact, observed by Faraday. Such freezing occurs with the weakest contact when the pieces are in water (Fig. 3). Faraday said that water molecules pass from a liquid to a solid much more easily when they are surrounded by a significant already solidified mass. A thin layer of water near the places where both pieces come into contact hardens and holds them together, even if it could not freeze on its own, being away from the mass of ice. According to this view, freezing depends not only on temperature, but also on environmental conditions. Likewise, water vapor inside a crack, that is, between two surfaces, will condense and turn into ice much more easily than in an open place, where contact occurs with only one surface of the ice.

Ice under tension will crack. Tyndall and Huxley proposed that the cracks were "healed" by the deposition of vapor or liquid emanating from the surrounding parts of the ice. Therefore, the ice mass can adapt to the shape of its bed and the glacier as a whole, like a flow.
