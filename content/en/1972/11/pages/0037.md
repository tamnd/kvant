There is another way: if someone in the family smokes, ask him to blow smoke onto the reflected beam. Particles of dust or smoke in the air reflect light towards the eye, and the beam becomes visible.

If you put your palm out near the place where the beam of light hits the water, a bright spot will appear on it - this means that the reflected beam of light passes there. Raising our hand in between to follow the beam, we will eventually touch a bright spot on the wall.

Let's take a closer look at what angle the beam hits the surface of the water, and then try to determine at what angle it is reflected. For this purpose, it is best to use the beam detection method using talc or smoke. What can be said about the angle at which the beam hits the water and the angle at which it is reflected? They are clearly the same.

Let's try to change the tilt of the beam. figure 2 shows some typical cases of reflection. If a light beam enters the water at a small angle, as in figure 2 a, reflection from the surface occurs at the same small angle. If the angle between the light beam and the water surface is greater, as in figure 2.6, the reflected beam also departs from the water at a large angle. If the beam goes almost vertically downwards, as at figure 2.8, it is reflected almost vertically upwards.

Now let’s direct the beam of light obliquely towards the water so that a light spot forms on the wall. In this case, the surface of the water should be slightly uneven (touch it several times with your finger). What will happen to the four REM reflected spot on the wall? Look, it expands, it becomes blurry, it distorts, it trembles and sways.

Many rays (very narrow beams) of light sent by a flashlight strike the water at almost the same angle. If the surface of the water is calm and level, then it will reflect all the rays of light at the same angles, that is, all the rays are reflected accordingly and the beam of light will remain collected as it was. That's why, when the surface of the eye, a small bright spot forms on the wall.

When we “disturbed” the water, ripples formed on its surface. And although the reflected rays still depart from the surface at the same angle at which the incident rays arrived at each section of the water, now each section of the surface changes its position all the time (Fig. 3). Because of this, the angles of each individual beam of light towards the water are constantly changing, and the beam is reflected each time in a different place on the wall. The beam of light jumps, expands - it becomes smaller and larger, depending on how disturbed the surface of the water is.

So, only the eyes of the surface can give a concentrated reflection. On a smooth surface, reflections diverge in all directions.
