⟦folio 27⟧

Pris. 2. Flight diagram with the return of astronauts to Earth.

So far we have only been interested in flight time. Let's think now about the required power. Let the ship move in the optimal manner described above with constant acceleration to the halfway point and subsequent deceleration for the remaining section. On the way back, everything is repeated in the same order (Fig. 2). Let the acceleration of the ship be $g$, the final mass 10 tons, the further flight 1,000 light years (very close compared to the Andromeda nebula!). What is the initial mass of the ship? What is the energy equivalent of this mass? What is the driving power?

Since the flight range is 1,000 light years, the length of the acceleration section is 500 light years. Polling $x=500$, from formula (3) we find $\mu=10^3$. Consequently, if $M_0$ is the initial mass of the package, then at the end of the acceleration section its mass is $M_1=10^{-3} M_0$. Since the length of the deceleration section is also 500 light years, and the acceleration (deceleration) is still $g$, then the mass of the package at the end of this section is $M_2=10^{-3} M_1=10^{-6} M_0$. On the way back, after braking, the mass of the package will be equal to $M=10^{-6} M_2=10^{-12} M_0$. This is the useful mass of the ship, equal to 10 tons. From here we get $M_0=10^{13}$ tons, which is equal to the mass of the Martian satellite Phobos. The energy equivalent of this mass is $E=M_0 c^2 \approx 10^{40} \text{эр}$. This is $10^{13}$ times the current annual energy consumption around the globe.

Let us now determine the power of motion, which from the photon rocket theory is equal to

$$P=M_0 \text{ ca.}$$

(5)

Substituting the value $M_0$ here, we find $P=3 \cdot 10^{25} \text{ бт}$. This is only 10 times less than the solar radiation power. But the mass of the Sun is $2 \cdot 10^{33} \text{ e}$, and the mass of our package is only $10^{19} \text{ e}$. Consequently, the specific power of the Sun (calculated per 1 mass) is about $10^{-7} \text{ бт/е}$, and the specific power of the motion of a photon packet in our example should be equal to $3 \cdot 10^6 \text{ бт/е}$, that is, 13.5 orders of magnitude higher. This is, of course, a fantastically large value. If the mass of the Bratsk hydroelectric power station was 1 kW, it would have a similar specific power. For comparison, we note that modern ship reactors have a power density of the order of $10^{-2} \text{ бт/е}$.

The difficulties we encountered in the field are understandable. In an effort to reach our “brothers in mind,” we are trying to accelerate a heavy body (a ship with astronauts) to near light speed. But in nature there are particles without rest mass (photons, neutrinos), which do not need to be accelerated, or can only exist by moving at the speed of light. Should we use their services? In high life, we constantly do this when the opportunity arises, watching TV or talking on the phone. Electromagnetic waves will faithfully transmit information to our subscribers. So let's try to use it to communicate with other civilizations.

Let us assume that we have energies of the order of $10^{40} \text{ эр}$ and can build either a photon rocket with a propulsion power of $3 \cdot 10^{25} \text{ бт}$ or a transmitter of the same power. Which path should we choose? For a given propulsion power, a photon rocket, as we have seen, can travel to a distance of 1,000 light years and return back. IN
