ABOUT THE STRUCTURE OF ICE

W.G.Bragg

The article by the famous English physicist William Bragg “On the structure of ice” appeared in 1938. In 1939, this article, translated by N. A. Shishakov, was published in the journal “Success of Physical Sciences”. Below we present a shortened version of this article.

The publication was prepared by I. Sh. Slyubodetsky.

In recent years, studies of the crystalline nature of ice and snow have revealed some facts that provide new explanations for old problems. However, one of them is the movement of ice and snow on the mountains, which has always been a subject of surprise. To determine the nature of the majestic movements of the Alpine glaciers, attempts were made at systematic measurements in the middle of the 19th century. It turned out that the ice flows, adapting to the curves and slopes in the valleys, like water. It was then established that the middles of glaciers move faster than the edges. At that time, I encountered great difficulties in explaining this curious plate-like structure of ice, which is at the same time a very brittle substance, but now this problem can be considered solved.

It turns out that there is an amazing connection between the majestic pictures of the movement of glaciers and the smallest details of the structure of water molecules and ice crystals.

The subject of previous attention is such an amazing regularity in the structure of snowflakes, which is especially striking when observing them in countries of the world. However, only at the end of the century this fact was subjected to systematic observation, which was then the subject of classification. At the same time, the perfect state between all six rays in any snowflake and at the same time the huge difference in the structure of various snowflakes seemed extremely interesting and puzzling.
