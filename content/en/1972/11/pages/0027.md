How long will it take to fly to the center of our Galaxy? To the Andromeda nebula?

Well, it's quite simple. The distance to the center of the Galaxy is approximately 34,000 light years, therefore, if you fly at the speed of light, it will take 34 thousand years. Really simple, but disappointing: again too long. The situation for each is hopeless - after all, we are flying at the maximum possible speed. Where is the way out? However, stop! There is an inaccuracy in our reasoning. We have not taught the pace of time on Earth and in our package. For an observer who remained on Earth and follows our flight with his watch, the actual time will pass 34,000 years. But on a moving ship, according to the theory of relative time, time flows differently. The closer the speed is to the speed of light, the slower time flows on it. This means that if we get close enough to the speed of light, we will be able to fly anywhere and almost never get together.

Let the ship move with a constant speed $v$. You have been given a task: to fly to the Andromeda nebula in 10 years. What should the ship's speed be? What is its ratio to the speed of light?

The distance to the Andromeda nebula is approximately two million light years. To fly this distance in 10 years, the relativistic reduction of time, that is, the ratio of the proper time $t$ of the spacecraft to the time $t$, changed by the clock of the earthly observer, must be equal to

$$\frac{\tau}{t} = \frac{10}{2000000} \frac{v}{c}.$$

But at a constant speed of the ship this value

$$\frac{\tau}{t} = \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{v}{c}\right)^2}.$$

From here

$$\frac{v}{c} \approx 1 - 1.25 \cdot 10^{-11} = 0.999999999875c,$$

So, if the ship moves at a speed of 0.999 999 999 987 5 $c$, it can fly to the Andromeda nebula in 10 years.

But in order to achieve such speed, the ship must first be accelerated using some kind of movement. Consequently, it is also necessary to take into account the time it takes to boost the ship. The efficiency of the packages is higher, the closer the flow rate of the working fluid (“fuel”) is to the final speed of the packages. Therefore, the most effective means of achieving near-light speeds is the use of photon packets, which operate through the angulation reaction of matter and antimatter. The end product of this reaction is γ radiation. So, the speed of outflow of the working fluid is equal to the speed of light.
