⟦folio 60⟧

During the Olympics, children from different countries were connected by the technology of friendship. Despite the fact that we spoke different languages, we had a common language - the language of mathematics. In the process of getting to know students from other teams, we exchanged tasks and told each other how the national Olympids were going. He wants to give us the most interesting problems.

1. Let us assume that $a_n$ is the sum of the digits of the number 1972$^n$ in decimal notation. Prove that
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{a_n} = 0.$$
This problem was told to us by user members. At the All-User Olympiad, only a few people solved it.

2. Prove that there exists a sequence of integers $\{a_n\}$: $a_0 = 0$, $a_1$, $a_2$, ..., $a_n$, ...; $a_n < a_{n+1}$, for which the following conditions are met:

1°. For every natural number $k$ there are $a_i$ and $a_j$ from the sequence $\{a_n\}$ such that $k = a_i + a_j$ (i can equal $j$). 2°. $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{a_n} = 0.$
This problem was proposed by English schoolchildren.

3. $p + q = 1$, $q \geq 0$; $p \geq 0$. Prove that for any natural numbers $m$ and $n$ the inequality $(1 - p^n)^m + (1 - q^m)^n \geq 1.$ holds

(Veneria)

4. Given $n$ real numbers $a_1; a_2; ...; a_n$. Prove that
$$\min_{i \neq j} \left[ \left( a_i - a_j \right)^2 \right] \leq \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2}{n(n-1)(n+1)}.$$

(Veneria)

5. Prove that
$$\left( 1 - \frac{1}{2^3} \right) \left( 1 - \frac{1}{3^3} \right) \cdots \left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^3} \right) > \frac{1}{2}.$$
(Czechoslovakia).

6. Given a trapezoid $ABCD$, $BC \parallel AD$. On $CD$ the point $K$ is taken, on $AB$ the point $L$ is taken so that $AK \parallel CL$. Prove that $DL \parallel BK$.

(Holland)

7. The number $x$ is complex or negative if and only if there are positive real numbers $a_0, a_1, ..., a_n$ such that
$$a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_0 = 0.$$
(Czechoslovakia)

8. Prove that
$$\sqrt[3]{a_1 a_2 a_3 + a_2 a_3 a_4 + a_3 a_4 a_1 + a_4 a_1 a_2} \leq \sqrt[3]{a_1 a_2 + a_2 a_3 + a_3 a_4 + a_1 a_3 + a_1 a_4 + a_2 a_4};$$
(all $a_i > 0$).

This problem was proposed by GDR team member P. Kröger.

9. Prove that
$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{1}{\sin^2 \frac{\pi(2k+1)}{2n}} = n^2.$$
(Bulgaria)

We hope that the mathematical contacts established with our peers from different countries will be of mutual benefit to all.

S. Belkin, V. Schwartz
