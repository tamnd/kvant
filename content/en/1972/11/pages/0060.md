XIV INTERNATIONAL MATHEMATICS OLYMPIAD FOR SCHOOLCHILDREN

From July 5 to July 18, the XIV International Mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren will take place in Poland. The zhorny's work began in Warsaw, and they themselves made passages in the ancient city of western Poland - Torun. Teams from 14 countries took part in the oplitpad: Australia, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, East Germany, Cuba, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, the Soviet Union, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia. The leaders of the teams of these countries made up the international jury, which was represented by Professor of the University of Torun Stanislav Balcezyk. All of the countries listed above took part in the previous Olympics.

Each team consisted of 8 people (there were only three participants from Cuba). In each country, the team was formed based on the results of various national competitions. According to the multi-leader team, the international Olympiad is a server incentive for increasing the level and popularity of national Olympiads. In some countries, additional qualifying competitions are held among the winners of the national Olympiad to finalize the composition of the team. It will be interesting for Quat readers to know that in England, at this final selection, a problem from Quat is presented (see No. 4, 1971, problem M76). The Kvata problem is also used in the Netherlands and in many other countries.

The Soviet team was formed based on the results of the VI All-Union Mathematical Olympiad. It included Sergey Belkin, a student at school No. 7 in Moscow (previously studied at school No. 556 in Moscow), Vladimir B. Urkov, a student at a boarding school at Moscow State University. Lomonosov (previously studied at the 25th school in Vladimir), Andrey Golberg - a student of the 2nd Moscow School, Yuri Kolmakov - a student at the boarding school at Moscow State University. Lomonosov (previously studied at the 1st school in Rasskazovo, Tambov Olympiad), Sergey Konyagin - a 9th grade student at school No. 9 in the city. Saratova, Merkuvev Alexander - a student at a boarding school at Leningrad University, Shapovalov Alexander - a student at a boarding school at Moscow University (previously studied at a village school

Chemolgan, Alma-Ata region), Vladimir Schwartz - student at a boarding school at Leningrad University. The reserve members of the team were: Lando Sergei - student of the 19th school. Perm and Leshiner Dmitry - a 9th grade student of the 2nd Moscow school.

The Soviet team of 8 people approached Warsaw on July 7th. Sovetnova took place in Torun on July 10 and 11.

The maximum number of points that an accountant could score is forty. 8 accountants scored 40 points and were awarded first degree diplomas. They were received by Gregos Andrejczyk - Poland, Vladimir Burkov - USSR, Vilmos Komornik - Hungary, Sergey Konyagin - USSR, Pavel Kroeger - GDR, Martin Mirka - Romania, Solt Tuza - Hungary, Zoltan Furedi - Hungary.

Registrants who scored from 30 to 39 points were awarded a second degree diploma. From our team, Sergei Belkin (39 points), Alexander Merkuvev (38 points), Andrei Golberg (33 points), Vladimir Schwartz (30 points) received second-degree diplomas.

Diplomas of the third degree, which were awarded to accountants who scored from 19 to 29 points, were received by Yuri Kolmakov (28 points) and Alexander Shapovalov (22 points) in the Soviet team.

Thus, all 8 members of the Soviet team became recognized at the Olympiad.

Overall, the Soviet team scored the highest (270 out of 320 possible). Next come the teams of Hungary (263 points), East Germany (239 points), Romania (206 points), Great Britain (179 points).

At the closing ceremony of the Olympiad, which took place in Warsaw on July 17, the Minister of Education and Education of the Polish People's Republic Jerzy Kuberski presented diplomas and recognitions to the winners.
