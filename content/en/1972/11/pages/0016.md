led him to the conclusion that the secret of the laminarity of ice can only be explained by studying the properties of ice single crystals. Most types of ice, especially artificial led and glacial mass, are a conglomerate of individual small crystals (Fig. 5). Single crystals of fairly large size were grown by McConnell from ice formed on the surface of a lake or in an open vessel. McConnell's experiments studied elastic and plastic deformations under the following conditions:

1. The sample was taken in the form of a plate 10-15 centimeters long and approximately 2-3 centimeters wide and thick and placed upward on two edges of the natural ice surface.

2. The same sample was rotated at a right angle so that the “surface of the lake” became vertical.

3. As a sample, we took the length that was vertical in the original array.

In the first case, the sample turned out to be bent like an ordinary rod (Fig. 6). In the second case, the deformation turned out to be the same in characteristics as in the first case, but significantly less in magnitude. In the third case, the deformation acquired a completely unique character (Fig. 7), showing that this time there is movement along some slip planes.
