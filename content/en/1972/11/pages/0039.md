Now let's try to answer the question: why is the image in water not as clear as in a mirror? This is due to several reasons. Let's take the flashlight again and illuminate the bathtub filled with water. Is all the light reflected? No, part of it, and a larger part, passes through the water and illuminates the bottom of the bath.

The proportion of light energy reflected from water is small—about 10 percent. As for the mirror, the opposite picture is observed there. A layer of metal located on the back of the mirror reflects most of the light falling on it—about 95 percent. This is why the images we see in an ordinary mirror are much brighter than the images on the surface of the water.

The second reason why the image in the water appears darker to us is that when we look into the bath, we are blocking the light with our heads. The light from a lamp hanging from the ceiling or sunlight pouring in through a window can only fall on the face in a roundabout way - it first comes from the walls, from the floor or from the walls of the bathtub. Needless to say, since the face is illuminated, the image turns out dim. When we hang a flashlight on our face, part of the light is reflected directly from the face, then goes into the water, and as a result the image becomes brighter.

Curved straight stick

What happens to light that is not reflected from the surface of the bath water? To answer this question, we continue with the following experiment. Let's pour a bath, as usual for bathing, and lower a straight stick into the water, tilting it slightly. Now, if we look at the stick, it no longer appears straight to us: the part of it that is in the water seems to be bent upward (Fig. 5).

Well, what if you take the stick out of the water? Then the broken part will again be completely straight, and the part immersed in water still seems to be curved upward.

Now let's take the stick completely out of the water - it is perfectly straight, just as it was. The bend has completely straightened.

It is probably clear to you that the stick does not bend at all when it is immersed in water and does not straighten when we take it out of the water. If anyone doubts this, let him send the “bent” part of the stick that is in the water. To the touch it is completely straight. There is clearly some kind of optical illusion taking place here, due to which we see something that is not really there. A mirror image is also a deception of this kind. Objects that seem to us to be behind the mirror are actually located in a different place.

Let's explore this phenomenon deeper. To do this, we will use a flashlight with a tube, which we made earlier when we were talking about mirrors.
