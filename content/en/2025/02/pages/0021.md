In Japan, the launch of a magnetic levitation railway using the levitating property of superconductivity is scheduled for 2034. Such trains are called “maglev” (magnetic levitation). The 286 km long railway will connect the largest cities of Tokyo and Nagoya. The estimated speed of the train is 500 km/h. Small maglevs without the use of superconducting materials are already used in the USA, China and Australia. They test magnetic devices that accelerate the train and lift it above the track. The use of magnets with superconducting windings should significantly reduce the cost of building and operating maglevs. It will be enough to use liquid nitrogen and high-temperature ceramic superconductors in them, since there are not wires, but conductor bars. It is promising and realistic to create electric motors of small size and high power. Such electric motors do not heat up, but, on the contrary, must be very cold. They have already found application in the external mechanisms of spacecraft - there is no need for cooling with liquid refrigerants, since it is cold enough without them. Quite recently, there was a message about the development in China of powerful magnetic devices (like maglevs) for throwing lunar minerals from the Moon to Earth. But these are just dreams for now! And the creation of a general theory of superconductivity (if possible) will mean a powerful leap in the knowledge of the laws of nature that are still unknown to us. More than 30 years ago, Alexei Alekseevich Abrikosov said prophetic words: “Research is being carried out with very large forces, and it is possible that they will be crowned with success. However, one important thing has already been done - the discovery of high-temperature superconductors: it has destroyed the long-standing prejudice that superconductivity necessarily requires low temperatures. This inspires people to further search, and even if it is not possible to “tame” superconducting ceramics, other classes of superconductors with higher critical temperatures and more suitable for practical use will certainly be found.”
