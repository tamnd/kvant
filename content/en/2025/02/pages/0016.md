Let’s try to at least somehow decipher the terms “phonons” and “Cooper pairs”. Phonons are similar to photons. And this is no coincidence. If photons can be considered as quanta of light waves, then phonons are quanta of energy of the coordinated vibrational motion of atoms of a solid body, forming an ideal crystal lattice. Like light, sound vibrations can be considered as a wave, or as a discrete quantum of energy and momentum of vibrational motion. Taking into account inharmonicity (non-sinusoidality) and damping of vibrations, the concept of phonons turns out to be useful when it is necessary to consider the forced vibrational motion of particles of a crystal lattice. For longitudinal vibrations in a wave, describing it using phonons is not so important. But it is suitable for transverse waves and for wave interference. In these cases, the term “virtual phonons” is often introduced. These are exactly what are considered in the BCS theory. “On the fingers,” the interaction of electrons with phonons can be described as follows (of course, for the sake of clarity in describing the phenomenon, one has to sacrifice its completeness and accuracy). At a sufficiently low temperature, when the vibrational motion of ions becomes very weak (phonon energy is low), an electron flying in the thick of ions attracts ions located on the sides of its path, they receive impulses towards it. But ions move much slower than electrons. The electron that caused the ions to approach flies away. And in its place another electron appears, which experiences attraction from the approaching ions. Its added speed is thus indirectly related to the speed of the passing electron.
