Dependence of metal resistance on temperature. After all, with the help of liquid helium he had already achieved a decrease in temperature to values ​​​​less than 1 K. Preparation of the experiment with pure mercury took a long time. The most important thing was the installation of more accurate equipment that measured electrical resistance. And now everything is ready, the decisive experiment is scheduled for April 8, 1911. Rods were obtained from frozen mercury. They are connected in series so that the total length of the conductor is half a meter. The rods are immersed in a vessel with helium. Cooling with the cooling hydrogen liquid begins. The instruments show 10 K, 8 K, 5 K. Finally, at 4.2 K, helium becomes liquid. On the graph of the dependence of the resistivity of mercury on temperature, a rectilinear dependence is clearly visible, directed towards T = 0. Suddenly... (in the history of physics this “suddenly..." often happens). At a temperature of 3 K, a device that measured the current in a circuit with mercury resistance showed a value that was off the scale. The first thought is that the device is faulty. Replaced. We took a device with a different scale. And the new device showed such a large current that could only exist if the resistance of the mercury conductor was practically zero. This completely unexpected result prompted another series of experiments in which the temperature was recorded with greater accuracy. And what happened? If at 4.2 K the resistance of mercury was still of some small value, then at 4.15 K

it almost completely disappeared. With a further decrease in temperature, the resistance remained zero. And when the temperature increased to 4.15 K, it was restored to its previous value. Thus, a phenomenon was discovered that Kamerlingh Onnes himself called superconductivity. To test the phenomenon using an alternating magnetic field, an induction current was excited in a ring of mercury conductor in a superconducting state. When the magnetic field was turned off, the current did not disappear and its strength remained constant. An hour passed, then another, a day passed, a week passed, and the current continued to exist, without decreasing at all. The strength of the current was recorded by the magnetic field that arose from this current on the axis of the ring. Of course, it was necessary to ensure that the superconducting ring was always at a temperature below 4.15 K - liquid helium was added all the time. Later, when liquid helium became much cheaper (it began to be produced not from air, but from natural gas, where its content is noticeably higher), such an experiment lasted a year and a half. He made it possible to assert that the value of resistivity in a substance in a superconducting state differs from zero by no more than $10^{25}$ Ohm m. In his articles of 1911 and 1912, Kamerlingh Onnes formulated the conclusion that at a temperature of 4.15 K a phase transition of crystalline mercury occurs from one solid state to another. In 1913, he discovered a transition to the superconducting state in pure lead (at 7.26 K) and in pure tin (at 3.69 K). Please note the high accuracy that was achieved in measuring the critical phase transition temperature.
