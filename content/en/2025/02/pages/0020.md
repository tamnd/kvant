KVANT 2025/№2

a false sensation was the “discovery” by Korean scientists of superconductivity at atmospheric pressure at 127 q C in a substance with a complex composition $Pb_{9} Cu_{2} (PO_{4} )_{2}$ O. A publication about this appeared in July 2023, at the height of summer, and in many laboratories around the world, including in Russia, physicists, interrupting their vacations, rushed to check. And they found nothing, no sensation. And chemists even proved that such a substance cannot exist at all. So today the record temperature for the superconducting transition (at atmospheric pressure) remains 135 K (since 2003). And although the term CTSC has already appeared instead of HTSC, in which CT means “room temperatures” instead of HT, i.e. “high temperatures”, there is less and less hope for the discovery of this very QTS, room-temperature superconductivity. And, unfortunately, all HTSCs are low-tech, they are fragile, chemically unstable, etc. The most disappointing thing is the lack of a theory of superconductivity in ceramic and other materials, except for the now “classical” BCS theory for metallic superconductors and the work of Abrikosov and Ginzburg on type II superconductors. It seems that for each type of superconductor you need to create your own theory. Or is there still one general theory about which nothing is known yet? In any case, new superconductors are being sought purely empirically. There are several such laboratories in Russia, mainly in university research centers. But, regardless of these searches, the use of superconductors operating at helium temperatures is becoming increasingly widespread. The main application today is the creation of powerful magnets for scientific purposes. But out of the entire multi-hundred list of superconductors that would be technologically suitable for this purpose (i.e., would allow the production of wire windings), there are only a few. And they all only work at helium temperatures (below 4.2 K). This is already mentioned

ground bimetals niobium-tin, niobium-titanium and niobium-germanium with critical temperatures of 18.5 K, 9.2 K and 23.9 K, respectively. Superconductivity in them is not destroyed in magnetic fields with an induction of up to 25 Tesla and a current strength in the wires of at least 50,000 A. In 2022, after a technical pause, the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) began operating again in Switzerland. In 2025, the NICA collider in Dubna near Moscow and the world's largest accelerator FAIR in Darmstadt, Germany, are due to come into operation. The main goal of research at these accelerators is to “look” into the very beginning of the emergence of our world, when the nuclei of future atoms were born in the quark-gluon plasma. The international experimental thermonuclear reactor ITER is scheduled to be launched in France in 2025, for which most of the superconducting windings have already been produced in Russia. But even now there has been a sharp increase in the use of superconductivity for human needs. This is due to the use of strong magnetic fields primarily for medical diagnostic purposes: magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and other tomographic methods of diagnosis and treatment. Over twenty years, the annual number of MRI sessions in Russia has increased several dozen times and continues to grow. It is attractive to use superconductivity to transmit electricity without heating losses (atmosphere). The AC cable contains three (since three-phase current is transmitted) superconducting wires with pumped liquid refrigerant. Such a cable is quite heavy; it is not suspended from masts, but is laid at a shallow depth underground. The first such power line went online in New York in 2008. And, for example, in South Korea the total length of such power lines already exceeds 1000 kilometers. Despite the cost of manufacturing the cable and the cost of refrigerants, the operation of such lines is cheaper than conventional ones
