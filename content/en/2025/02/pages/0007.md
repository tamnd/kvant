It is easy to see that this requires solenoids with a current in the windings of tens of thousands of amperes, but their Joule heat will melt the metal of the wires and burn the insulation. When P.L. Kapitsa created strong magnetic fields in Cambridge a hundred years ago, he used short-circuit currents that destroyed electrical circuits. In this case, a strong magnetic field existed, although for a very short time, but sufficient for studying intra-atomic processes. The only way to create strong magnetic fields of long-term rather than pulsed action is to use the phenomenon of superconductivity, in which Joule heat is completely absent. And this phenomenon occurs in electrical metal materials (wires) at normal pressure only at ultra-low temperatures (several Kelvin). And only with the help of liquid helium can such a temperature be created and maintained. Without liquid helium, it would be impossible to create modern powerful accelerators. But even now there has been a sharp increase in the production of liquid helium and the use of superconductivity not only for scientific purposes, but also for human needs.
