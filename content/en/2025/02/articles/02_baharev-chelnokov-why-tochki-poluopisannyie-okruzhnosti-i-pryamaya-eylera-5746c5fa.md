Why-points, semi-circles and Euler's straight line

F. BAKHAREV, G. CHELNOKOV

The appearance of the Why-dot

Over the past few decades, Olympiad geometry has acquired terminology, a significant part of which cannot yet be called generally accepted. Thus, some names are common only in the Russian-speaking space (lemmas about sparrows [1], problem 255 [2], lemma about cyclists [3]), others - mainly in English-language literature (Queue point, Iran lemma, Shooting Death Star lemma [4, 5]). In this article we will talk about a point whose name has not yet caught on. Sometimes it is referred to as ISL $2011 G4 point^{1}$, and more recently there is a not so unsightly, but still not very successful name: Why-dot (see, for example, [6]). Without pretending to make a final decision on the name, we will talk about the constructions associated with these points, some of which are direct observations of the authors of the article. Before moving on to definitions, we will analyze two problems proposed at the final stages of the All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren. The names of the points have been changed compared to the original wording.

1 ISL – IMO Short List – selected problems from which the final version of the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) is compiled. The number G4 means that the problem is number 4 on the list of geometric problems. The 2011 shortlist offered 8 geometry problems. Problem G4 was proposed by Russia, author – Mikhail Isaev.

Relation to semi-circles

Circles passing through two vertices of a triangle and touching the incircle internally are called semicircles. It turns out that Why points are closely related to the indicated tangent points of semicircles.
