On Poncelet invariants and pencils of circles

P.KOZHEVNIKOV

Around Poncelet's theorem

Poncelet's Theorem is one of the geometric theorems that you can admire endlessly! Let us present one of the “classical” versions of its formulation (of course, variations and generalizations are known). Poncelet's theorem. Let two circles be given on a plane, and the second circle lies inside the first (Fig. 1).

⟦figure⟧

Fig. 1

Suppose there is a closed n-link broken line, all of whose vertices lie on the first circle, and all of whose links touch the second. Then there are infinitely many such closed n-link broken lines and any point on the first circle can be the vertex of such a broken line. Thus, the construction of a polyline can be started from any point of the first circle, drawing a tangent to the second circle and obtaining a new intersection point on the first circle. The step-by-step construction of such a broken line is called the Poncelet process for a pair of given circles, and the broken line itself (inscribed in the first circle and circumscribed around the second) is called the Poncelet broken line or Poncelet trajectory. Essentially, Poncelet's theorem

states that if the Poncelet trajectory, started at some point on the first circle, turns out to be periodic, i.e. closed after some step, then this closed trajectory can be “rotated” between the circles. Let us ask ourselves: what interesting properties do Poncelet trajectories have? An interesting property may be an invariant, i.e. a quantity or characteristic that does not depend on the choice of trajectory. An example of a metric invariant is given in problem M2817 from the “Problem Book «Kvant»” (for its solution, see this issue of the journal). This problem considers a pair of circles for which there is a family of Poncelet inscribed (2n)-gons, and states that the ratio of the area to the sum of the lengths of the main diagonals is invariant, i.e. the same value for any (2n)-Poncelet triangle. Solution problem M2817 uses a remarkable geometric invariant of the Poncelet family of (2n)-gons: their main diagonals intersect at one fixed (independent of the choice of (2n)-gon) point.
