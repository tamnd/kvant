Surface waves

If the gaseous medium is heterogeneous and a sharp interface is observed, for example, separating from each other areas with different densities of the same gaseous substance (at different temperatures, but the same pressure) or areas with different compositions of gas mixtures, which also have different densities, then in the gravitational field the substances are located so that the less dense component is located above the more dense one. In such a situation, the formation and propagation of so-called surface waves along this interface is possible. (An analogue is gravitational waves on the surface of water.) The speed of their propagation depends on the densities above $U_{в}$ and below $U_{н}$ of the interface, as well as on the magnitude of the acceleration of gravity g. And, most interestingly, the speed of propagation of such waves depends on both the wavelength and amplitude. Of course, in the atmosphere at different altitudes, air densities differ and such surface waves are sometimes observed in the form of a specific form of clouds, but the frequency of vibration in such waves is so small that they do not fall into the frequency range that we can hear. In other words, such waves cannot be called sound waves.
