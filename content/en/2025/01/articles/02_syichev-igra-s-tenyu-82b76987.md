On a sunny summer day, children (and adults) love to play in the shadows. The most observant people probably paid attention to this fact. If you stand, for example, under the roof of a house and bring your shadow closer to the shadow of the house, then your shadow begins to stretch in the direction of the shadow of the house (Fig. 1). What is the reason for this shadow distortion? We do not consider diffraction - not its scale, everything here is macroscopic. Let's use the laws of geometric optics and draw a diagram of this phenomenon (Fig. 2). Apparently, the point is primarily that the light source has non-zero angular dimensions. For simplicity, let us consider only the outer rays coming from the disk of the Sun (Fig. 3,a). At a large distance between screens 1 and 2, the shadow from the first screen is not distorted (left photo on figure 1). When the shadows from the head and roof are sufficiently close to the body closer to the ground (screen 1), light begins to fall not from the entire surface of the light source, but only from that part that is not yet blocked by a more distant body (screen 2).
