Basic Fact

Is it possible to solve a complex Olympiad problem with knowledge that does not go beyond the curriculum of the first half of the 8th grade? And without knowing special “Olympiad” facts and techniques? It turns out that in many cases it is enough to have a good command of ordinary school material. Let's consider a simple problem. How can you use this fact? The fact is that it indicates the location of the center of the circle circumscribed about the triangle. In school courses it is usually proved that the circumcenter of a triangle is equidistant from its vertices, i.e. lies at the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors drawn to the sides of the triangle. But if, for example, angle B of triangle ABC is fixed, then the center of the circumscribed circle also lies on a straight line that makes a fixed angle with side AC. Let's consider the following problem. Problem 1. The bisector AL intersects the circumcircle of the isosceles triangle ABC (AB = BC) at point W; point I is the center of the inscribed circle of triangle ABC.
