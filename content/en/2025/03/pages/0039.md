Since human vision is based on the existence in his eyes of three receptors sensitive to colors in different wavelength ranges and the sensitivity ranges of these receptors overlap, we see in the same wavelength range 380 nm - 760 nm (Fig. 2). In fact, there are “cones” that are sensitive to colors, and “rods” that simply sense light without distinguishing colors, but we will not discuss such details here. The most effective for creating a visual sensation of brightness is radiation with a wavelength of 0.555 microns. For such radiation, two sensitive receptors “work” simultaneously - green and red. They together provide the maximum sensation of brightness (or luminous intensity) compared to any other wavelengths, provided that the power of radiation entering the eyes for these different wavelengths is visual.
