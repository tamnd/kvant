KVANT 2025/№3

Why is the first direction called unconscious effort? By receiving external energy to maintain vital functions, the body itself tries to slow down aging. Sometimes - adapting to the problems associated with aging, sometimes - restoring impaired functions, literally “clinging” to life. A decrease in the body's safety margin as age increases is associated with reasons that arise at all levels from cells to the body: DNA damage, cell death, the destructive effects of free radicals and toxic substances, inaccurate regulation of functions, diseases, stress. In fact, the body tries to fight each of these causes: by repairing DNA, removing damaged parts of cells from the body with the lymph flow, regulating the activity of enzymes and metabolic processes. As some of the cells die, the remaining ones switch to a more intensive mode of operation, increase in size and even change structure (forming up to several nuclei in the cell). Similar processes occur with organs (liver, kidneys, lungs). In a young body, these organs contain both working cells and reserve cells. The latter are used under intense loads. As we age, there are fewer and fewer reserve cells and more and more working cells. The supply is decreasing, and those remaining are increasingly working to the limit. And here it’s time to make conscious efforts of the person himself and help the body, which is trying to continue to work in conditions of decreasing reserves with age. Use acquired skills and accumulated experience that allow you to complete work with less effort. And in general, lead an active lifestyle, which is extremely important for slowing down the rate of aging. Let us touch upon the topic of such influence of an active lifestyle using the example of changes in the activity of the human brain with age. It would seem that the nerve cells are not restored, the brain gradually “dries out”, decreases in volume, work

It’s getting worse, memory and attention are weakening... But modern research has shown that new brain cells regularly appear, new connections are formed between neurons. Brain volume after 80 years decreases by 5–10% (if no effort is made), but an active lifestyle allows you to restore this volume. When solving complex problems, the brains of older people use both hemispheres, rather than just one, as in younger people. Brain training can improve memory and attention, and slow down the aging process. It is important to train all organs. Not excessive, but sufficient for gradual fatigue to set in, followed by recovery. The views of Hans Selye, the founder of the doctrine of stress, are also close to this position. According to him, moderate, not very long-term stress (those in which the expenditure of the body’s reserves is balanced and there is no depletion yet) leads to stimulation and training of the body’s defenses, improves its physical and mental health and promotes longevity. In this case, the body also manages to adapt to changing conditions. Most centenarians have fairly simple rules of life: physical activity, fresh air, sleep, hobbies, favorite activity or hobby, positive attitude, communication, moderation in everything, especially in nutrition. However, is it even possible to extend life beyond the current limit of 120 years and make this not a rare exception, but the rule? Or is it better to live not so long in the end, but actively, leaving behind a good memory and, perhaps, followers and descendants?
