It's interesting that...

... from Pascal and Fermat, who gave clear and simple examples of the applications of probability theory, Huygens took the baton, setting out his results in 1657 in the book “On Calculations in Gambling” - the first treatise in a new scientific direction. ...in response to an appeal about new dice games, Newton had to solve a problem to find out which one was more profitable. As far as is known, this was the scientist’s only work on probability theory. ... while studying the probability of judicial verdicts, the French physicist and mathematician Poisson established in 1837 one of the primary laws - the so-called “distribution”, named after him and which became a research tool for scientists in solving many diverse problems. ... On September 21, 1859, at a meeting of the British Association for the Advancement of Science, Maxwell first reported on his work, which laid the foundation for a statistical, or probabilistic, picture of the world. ...physicists who studied Brownian motion or gas diffusion at the beginning of the 20th century, for example Einstein and Smoluchowski, turned to the study of probabilistic processes before this problem attracted the attention of mathematicians. ...the answer to the question of why heat is transferred from hot objects to cold ones, and not vice versa, was proposed by Boltzmann: because it is more probable. The idea, initially received with hostility by scientists, later received universal recognition. ...quantum physics, unlike classical physics, denies the possibility of establishing the exact coordinates or trajectories of a particular particle, for example an electron, but operates only with the probabilities of its location at a given point in time at a particular point in space. ...the law of radioactive decay is fundamentally statistical in nature due to the probabilistic laws of behavior of microparticles; Only after the creation of quantum mechanics was it possible to construct a theory of radioactive transformations of atomic nuclei.
