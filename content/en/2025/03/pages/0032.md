Well, we figured out the main fact and looked at the first examples. What's next? Which angle also complements the angle of the triangle to 90q? Of course, the angle between the side of the triangle and the altitude drawn to the other side. Based on this, we can formulate the following. Corollary 1. Let CC c be the altitude of the acute triangle ABC $(рис.^{9).}$ Point O is the center of the circumscribed circle. Then ACO BCC c, i.e. the lines CO and CC c are symmetrical with respect to the bisector of angle C of triangle ABC.

Prove that if ACM BCH, then ACB 90 q. Solution. Let us prove that M is the center of a circle circumscribed about triangle ABC, from which the statement of the problem will follow. Let O be the center of the circle circumscribed about triangle ABC. Then ACO BCH, i.e. O lies on the ray CM. On the other hand, O lies on the perpendicular bisector of side AB. Since the triangle is not isosceles, they cannot coincide and the center O of the circumscribed circle lies at their intersection - at point M. The problem is solved.
