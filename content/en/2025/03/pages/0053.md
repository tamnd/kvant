Problem 3. Black smoker's bubble In the oceans at great depths near mid-ocean ridges, heat sources such as black smokers can be found. They spew out high temperature geothermal water. After one of these emissions, a layer of warm water appeared in the ocean, the temperature t of which varied with depth h as shown in figure 12. The products of black smokers also often contain various gases that form bubbles. 1) Find all possible depths at which a bubble in water will be in equilibrium. 2) Determine whether the found equilibrium positions are stable. Consider that there is no current in the water and it is at rest; the bubble always has the shape of a ball and the pressure inside it is equal to the external pressure of water; the gas inside the bubble can be considered ideal; the temperature inside the bubble is equal to the temperature of the surrounding water; the gas does not dissolve in water. The molar mass of the gas is M = 222 g mol, the density of water is constant and equal to $U_{в} 1020$ kg $м^{3}$, the universal gas constant R = 8.3 J (mol K), the acceleration of gravity g = 10 m $с^{2}$, the atmospheric pressure $p 0 10^{5}$ Pa.
