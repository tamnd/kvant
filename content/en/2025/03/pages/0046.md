OLYMPICS

XLVI Tournament of Cities

Objectives of the autumn tour

Basic option

8–9 grades

$1 (4)^{1}$. Given a circumscribed pentagon ABCDE. The center of its incircle lies on the diagonal AC. Prove that AB + BC > CD + DE + EA. E. Bakaev

2 (4). There are 100 stones in a row: black, white, black, white, ..., black, white. In one operation, either choose two black stones, between which there are only white stones, and repaint all these white stones black, or choose two white stones, between which only black stones lie, and repaint all these black stones white. Is it possible, after several such operations, to obtain a row in which there are first 50 black stones, and then 50 white ones? E. Bakaev

3 (4). See problem M2818 of the Problem Book «Kvant». 4 (5). Mom and son are playing. First, the son cuts a 300 g piece of cheese into 4 pieces. Then mom distributes 280 g of butter onto 2 plates. Finally, the son places the pieces of cheese on the same plates. He will win if there is at least as much cheese on each plate as there is butter (otherwise mom will win). Which of them can win, no matter how the other acts? A. Shapovalov

5 (5). The set consists of identical three-cell corners, the central cells of which are stained with paint. The rectangular board was covered in one layer with corners that did not extend beyond the board, and then the corners were removed. The dirty squares left marks on the board. Is it always possible to find out from these traces exactly how the corners were laid? A. Gribalko

Each polynomial has an odd degree and is equal to the number of its distinct real roots. The product of the values ​​of the first polynomial at the roots of the second is 2024. Find the product of the values ​​of the second polynomial at the roots of the first. S.Yanzhinov

3 (5). There are 5 natural numbers written in a row. Each of them, except the first, is the smallest natural number that is not divisible by the previous one. Can all five numbers be different? B.Frenkin
