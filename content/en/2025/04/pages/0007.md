For example, such figures as a square, an equilateral triangle, a domino rectangle, a corner of three cells and a sphinx composed of six equilateral triangles (Fig. 14) are self-similar. Let us define the following inflation-deflation process. One step of this process is that we first break the given figure into similar parts according to the given diagram, and then “inflate” them so that in the end each of the parts is equal in size to the original figure (Fig. 15). You can not limit yourself to one step, but continue to break the figures obtained in the previous steps into smaller parts, “inflating” them to their original size, and then repeating everything again and again. It turns out that if we do this in a certain way, in the limit we will get a tessellation of the entire plane with copies of the original figure. Without going into details and without providing strict definitions and evidence, we note, however, that the process of moving to the limit is much more subtle than it might seem at first glance. So, a lot depends on how exactly to place the “inflated” figures on the plane relative to the cluster obtained in the previous step

The tiling obtained by passing to the limit is self-similar.
