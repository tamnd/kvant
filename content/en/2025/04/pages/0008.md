This means that they cannot be combined by any similarity transformation. The resulting macrotiles can also be combined into even larger tiles, and so on. Thus, enlargement is possible at any step, and tiles of the kth level, being grouped in a certain way, form tiles of the (k + 1)th level. Thus, an infinite hierarchy of tilings arises, corresponding to tiles of different levels. For example, figure 19 shows the first three steps of such a hierarchy for self-similar tiling with corners. Hierarchies of self-similar tilings differ depending on how many ways the enlargement can be carried out at each level. If there are several such possibilities, then the hierarchy is called weak. These are, for example, tiling the plane with squares or equilateral triangles. For example, figure 20 shows four possible ways to group square tiles in groups of four to make macrotiles.

If each time the enlargement can be carried out in a unique way, then the hierarchy of tilings is called strict - these are self-similar tilings with dominoes, corners and sphinxes (see Fig. 14). It turns out that self-similar tilings with a strict hierarchy mostly have a number of typical properties. Thus, they are non-periodic, but any finite fragment occurs in each such tiling infinitely many times. Let's look at why this happens, using the example of tiling with corners. Let us assume that some of the self-similar tilings by corners is periodic, i.e. remains unchanged with some shift. Then the kth level tiles must go into the kth level tiles for every k. However, if k is large enough, then the kth level tile is so large that it overlaps with itself at the shift in question. For self-similar tilings with a weak hierarchy, this is not a problem, as can be seen in the example of square tilings. But for strict hierarchies, we immediately come to a contradiction, since enlargement of tiles at each level is possible in a unique way, which means that any two tiles of the kth level do not intersect. Thus, a self-similar tiling with corners, and indeed any self-similar tiling with a strict hierarchy, is non-periodic. Now consider some finite fragment of a self-similar tiling with corners.
