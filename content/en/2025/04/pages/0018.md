KVANT 2025/№4

Title page of the book “Encyclopedia, or Explanatory Dictionary of Sciences, Arts and Crafts”

It was quite expensive, since it had to be published not in France, but mainly in Switzerland. Diderot and D'Alembert recruited prominent scientists of the time to write articles and were themselves the authors of many articles. It can be considered that this era received one of the names in history “the age of enlightenment”, including thanks to this encyclopedia. In 1765, the last, seventeenth volume of the main text of the encyclopedia was published and the eleven-volume publication of illustrations (engravings) continued. Subsequently, seven more volumes of articles were published, mainly devoted to new achievements of science and technology, which were already developing rapidly at that time. Despite the high cost, Ampere's father was one of the most loyal subscribers and had all 35 volumes.

⟦column⟧

Let us note by the way that in Russia at that time only Empress Catherine II could afford to have the entire encyclopedia. So, Andre Ampere, who had this encyclopedia in his home library, at the age of 14, read all seventeen volumes volume by volume. And I didn’t just read, but studied and memorized a lot of them. So the words often found in Ampere’s biographies that he had an encyclopedic education have a very specific basis. But his greatest interest was in mathematics and natural sciences. At the age of 12, a teacher from Lyon College became his home mathematics teacher. He asked the boy if he knew how to extract square roots. Andre replied that no, but he knows how to integrate. Soon the classes stopped because the teacher said that his knowledge was not enough to teach such a student. Ampere's serene youth ended when he was 14 years old. The Great French Revolution broke out, which was later called the “bourgeois” revolution. But at the beginning it was not like that, since on its banners, along with “freedom” and “brotherhood,” there was also “equality,” which could not please rich people. And in 1793, a rebellion broke out in Lyon against the Jacobin dictatorship. The rebellion was brutally suppressed. As a participant and one of the leaders of the rebellion, Ampere's father was guillotined, and all the family's property was confiscated. The eighteen-year-old boy had a hard time with the death of his father and was forced to think about his livelihood. He became a private teacher of mathematics, and a few years later - a teacher of physics and chemistry at a small town gymnasium. From that time on, Ampere began his teaching career, which continued throughout his life. The main goal of this activity was the restructuring of the teaching of physics in secondary and higher schools.
