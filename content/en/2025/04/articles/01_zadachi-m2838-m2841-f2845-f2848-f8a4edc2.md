PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Problem M2838–M2841, F2845–F2848

M2838. There are jugs of juices in a circle, not necessarily of the same size. From any jug you are allowed to pour any part of the juice (perhaps none or all the juice) into the adjacent jug on the right, so that it does not overflow and the sweetness of the mixture in it becomes equal to 10%. It is known that at the initial moment such a transfusion could be done from any jug. Prove that it is possible to make several such transfusions in some order (no more than one from each jug), so that the sweetness of the mixture in all non-empty jugs becomes equal to 10%. A. Shapovalov

M2839. Given a natural number k. In a country whose currency is the Tugrik, only banknotes of two integer denominations are circulated. Both the buyer and the seller have quite a lot of both bills, but for each payment they can use no more than k bills together (including change). It is known that this way you can make a payment for any integer amount from 1 to n tugriks. What is the largest possible value of n? A. Shapovalov

F2847. A cylindrical vessel installed vertically in a gravity field contains a monatomic ideal gas under a massive horizontal piston of mass m = 100 kg. There is a vacuum above the piston. Then the piston is released. With further movement of the piston with the weight, its minimum distance $h_{min}$ to the bottom of the vessel is half the initial distance $h_{0} = 1,0$ m. The heat capacity of the vessel and piston is negligible, and there is no heat transfer to the environment.
