TUNNEL IN ANIYE

And 20 million Kelvin is exactly the temperature that the plasma of the “fireball” of a uranium or plutonium nuclear explosion has. Therefore, in every hydrogen bomb, the hydrogen “charge” is surrounded by layers of uranium or plutonium. We can figuratively call this uranium charge a “match” for “igniting” the main charge – hydrogen isotopes. But not with a simple match, but with a “quantum” one, using the quantum tunneling effect. Now the most gigantic (in terms of scale and cost) scientific “device” - ITER, the international thermonuclear experimental reactor, is nearing completion. According to the project, in pulsed mode for 400 seconds, it should release an energy of 500 MeV while spending 50 MeV on a “match”, which is 10 times less. The “match” in this case is jets of deuterium-tritium plasma accelerated by an electric field - these are the so-called gyrotrons, invented and manufactured in Russia, which is a full participant in this grandiose experiment (10% of the cost of manufacturing individual elements). And the products of the fusion reaction, the nuclei of helium atoms and neutrons will give up their energy to the coolant. Moreover, in 2021, serious defects were discovered in the materials of the pipes through which liquid helium circulates, creating a low temperature in the windings of electromagnets for their superconducting state. This will likely delay the launch of ITER by several years. We emphasize that without the tunnel effect the reactor would not be able to operate. But it is becoming increasingly clear that the tunnel effect plays a significant role not only in nuclear physics. For example, its role has proven to be very important in the kinetics of many chemical reactions. The role of this effect is also great in semiconductor phenomena and devices - tunnel diodes, gate photoelectric effect, LEDs, Josephson effect and others. And the tunnel effect cannot be understood and applied without quantum mechanics. The year 2025 is the centenary year of this science, which, without exaggeration, can be considered one of the most significant scientific achievements of the twentieth century. This is exactly how the Nobel laureate, Russian theoretical physicist Lev Landau described the significance of quantum mechanics in his interview in 1962.
