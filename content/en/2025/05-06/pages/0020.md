Problems in mathematics and physics

PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with the solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2842 was offered at the regional stage of the XVII Olympiad named after L. Euler, problem M2844 a, b and M2845 - at the XLIV Tournament of Cities, and problem M2846–M2849 - at the final stage of the LI All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren in mathematics. Problem F2849–F2856 were offered at the final stage of the LVIII All-Russian Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics.

Problem M2842–M2849, F2849–F2856

M2842. Several (more than one) consecutive natural numbers were written on the board. Could it happen that the sum of all even numbers written out is equal to the square of a natural number, and the sum of all odd numbers written out is equal to the square of a natural number? A. Kuznetsov

M2843. Let A c, B c, C c be the midpoints of sides BC, CA, AB, respectively, in regular triangle ABC (Fig. 1). On the continuation of the segment A c B c, point D is taken as B c, and on the continuation of the segment A c C c, point E is taken as C c so that the segment DE passes through point A. The segments B c E and C c D intersect at point X.

⟦column⟧

a) Prove that points X, A c, B c, C c lie on the same circle. b) Prove that the segments A c X, CE, BD and B c C c intersect at one point. K. Bayrak, 11th grade student

M2844. The owner took out a piece of meat from the refrigerator, and the kittens gathered around her. Once a minute, the housewife cuts off a piece of meat and feeds it to one of the kittens (of her choice), and each piece should be the same proportion of the piece from which it is cut. After some time, the housewife puts the rest of the meat in the refrigerator. Can the housewife feed the kittens equal amounts of meat if there are a) two kittens in total; b) three; c*) four? A. Kushnir, V. Retinsky

M2845. In three-dimensional coordinate space, consider the set of all cubes with integer vertex coordinates. Prove that this set contains an infinite subset K such that any two different cubes from K do not have parallel edges. M. Malkin, M. Meerov
