L. BELOPUKHOV

SO IN THEORETICAL PHYSICS it is customary to call the effect, which is called tunneling. The encyclopedic definition of the tunnel effect is as follows: the penetration of a microparticle into the region of a potential barrier when the total mechanical energy of the particle is less than the height of the potential barrier. In the one-dimensional case, on the graph of the dependence of energy on the coordinate, it will look as if there is a mountain in the path of the particle, which it overcomes in a “tunnel”, passing through the mountain and ending up on its other slope. In reality, of course, there is no mountain. It only appears on the chart. But in reality, in experiments where such an effect is detected, the particle in its motion approaches the point where it is acted upon by a force that throws it back, and, according to the laws of classical physics, stops and then changes the sign of its speed. In a word, the particle is reflected as if from an invisible wall. Science fiction writers called this invisible wall a force barrier. But it turned out that in the microcosm this is not so, or rather, not entirely so. The flow of microparticles in this situation behaves in such a way that part of the flow is actually reflected. But part of the flow appears in some mysterious way in the area of ​​the force barrier and even behind the barrier if it has a finite extent.

the tunnel effect simply does not exist in classical physics. Explanation refers to the creation and discussion of a visual model of a phenomenon. But the phenomenon itself exists! It was noticed and began to be studied independently of the birth of quantum physics. At the beginning of the twentieth century, due to the rapid development of radio electronics, physicists paid a lot of attention to the detailed study of gas discharges. And in 1901, the young scientist Robert Earhart in the laboratory of J. J. Thomson in Cambridge obtained unexpected results in the behavior of ions during a gas discharge between two closely spaced electrodes. Thomson suggested that this was not an experimental error, but some new phenomenon. Practical work on creating radio tubes distracted physicists from studying an incomprehensible phenomenon. These studies were continued only in 1911 by Thomson's graduate student Franz Roser. In 1914, he discovered the release of electrons (emission) from the cathode when emission could not occur due to the presence of another negatively charged electrode nearby (under high vacuum conditions). The phenomenon was called field emission, and no explanation was given for it at that time. Physicists at the Cavendish Laboratory became interested in creating a planetary model of the atom and the first nuclear reactions. But there were also inexplicable phenomena in nuclear physics. One of them is the probabilistic nature of radioactive transformations of nuclei. A theoretical explanation for these incomprehensible phenomena associated with the presence of a potential barrier appeared only after the creation of quantum mechanics in 1925. German physicist Friedrich Hund, Russian physicists Leonid Mandelstam, Mikhail Leontovich and Georgiy Gamow, American physicists Ronald Henry and Edward Condon almost simultaneously and independently applied wave quantum mechanics, then just created by Edwin Schrödinger, to problems with a potential barrier. The harbinger of quantum mechanics was Louis de Broglie's hypothesis about the wave properties of matter, expressed by him in 1923. By this time, Einstein’s hypothesis about the quantum properties of radiation, formulated by him back in 1905, had already gained universal recognition, especially after its brilliant application by A. Compton to explain the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with free electrons. According to this hypothesis, an electromagnetic radiation wave can also be considered as an object that has corpuscular (quantum) mechanical characteristics - momentum p and energy E, related to the wavelength O by the relations $p h O,^{E} hc O$, where h is Planck’s constant, c is the speed of light in vacuum. Thus, electromagnetic radiation has a dual nature (dualism). In some phenomena, wave properties are more significant, in others – corpuscular (quantum) properties. But the fact that such dualism is characteristic not only of electromagnetic radiation, but of every

In order for two nuclei of hydrogen isotopes (for example, a deuteron, i.e., a deuterium nucleus, with a triton, i.e., a tritium nucleus) to combine, they need to approach at a distance of 2 fm (2 $10^{15}$ m). Only then will the powerful nuclear forces “turn on” and, with their powerful attraction, give the particles a speed of the order of 5 $10^{6}$ m s. When there are many particles and they have such an average speed, this means that the temperature of the entire mass of particles will be on the order of hundreds of millions and even billions of kelvins. Apparently, this is exactly the temperature of the inner layers of the Sun. But on the path of the collision of particles, two deuterium nuclei or a deuterium nucleus with a tritium nucleus, there is a potential barrier of Coulomb repulsive forces. Calculations have shown that if the nuclei of hydrogen isotopes have an average energy corresponding to a temperature of 20 million degrees, then this barrier can be partially overcome. The probability of the tunnel effect will already be sufficient for the particles to come closer to 2 fm and the fusion of nuclei to occur with the appearance of a helium nucleus and a neutron. But their average energy will be much higher - it will correspond to a billion kelvins.
