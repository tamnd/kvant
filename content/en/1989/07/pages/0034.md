M1148. Prove that for any $a > 1$, $a \neq \sqrt{q}$ ($p$ and $q$ are integers) and natural $n$ the equality $[\log_a 2] + [\log_a 3] + \dots + [\log_a n] + [a] + [a^2] + \dots + [a^k] = nk$ holds, where $k = [\log_a n]$ ($[x]$ is the integer part of $x$).

For any positive $z$, the number $[z]$ is equal to the number of natural numbers not exceeding $z$, so $[\log_a m]$ is the number of points of the form $(m; y)$, where $y$ is a natural number not exceeding $\log_a m$, and the sum $[\log_a 2] + [\log_a 3] + \dots + [\log_a n]$ is the number of points with natural coordinates lying below straight $y = k$ and to the left of the graph $y = \log_a x$. These two sets of points do not intersect, since by condition there are no integer points on the graph itself (with the exception of $(1; 0)$). And the union of these sets obviously gives all the integer points $(x; y)$ in the right angle $1 \leqslant x \leqslant n$, $1 \leqslant y \leqslant k$, the number of which is $kn$.

R. V. Alekseev, L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1149. Two loops $p, q$ with vertices at points $P$ and $Q$ of the corresponding one are given on the plane. Two circles—one centered on lunar $p$, passing through point $P$, and the other centered on lunar $q$, passing through $Q$—touch each other externally at point $M$. Find the set of points $M$.

Let us denote by $l$ and $m$ the lines perpendicular to the rays $p$ and $q$, passing through their ends $(P$ and $Q)$; through $\Pi_l$ and $\Pi_m$ are the corresponding half-planes filled by our circles with centers at $p$ and $q$. It is obvious that when one of the circles touching each other increases, the other decreases so that the point of contact $M$ describes a certain line lying at the intersection $\Pi_l \cap \Pi_m$; its ends correspond to the extreme position when one of the circles goes either to the point $(P$ or $Q)$, or to the straight line $(l$ or $m)$; the ends of the line do not belong to the exclusive set. We will prove that this line is an arc of a circle passing through the points $P$ and $Q$; To do this, you just need to check that the angle $PMQ$ is constant.
