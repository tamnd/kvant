Tzadka (gallery), where tourists are allowed access. It has long been replaced that soft speech at point A (Fig. 1) is clearly audible at point B if the speaker looks along the gallery wall. But if the speaker looks in the direction of point B, then at the same volume the speech at point B cannot be heard. Why is this happening?
At point A, a non-directional sound source emits a fairly loud pulse of digestion $\tau_1$. What will be the digestion of the impulse received at point B? Gallery diameter d.

If at point A there is a non-directional emitter of a sufficiently loud sound, then this sound can get to point B in many ways. The shortest of them is straight from A to B. The signal traveling this way will arrive first, and it will take time

$$\tau_0 = \frac{d}{v},$$

where $v$ is the speed of sound. Then two signals will arrive (one on the right, the other on the left - see Fig. 3), once reflected from the gallery wall. Then the signals will be reflected twice, three times, and so on many times. The last to come are two signals, coming from A almost tangentially to the gallery wall in two opposite directions. Each of these signals, having been reflected from the wall a very number of times, will travel a path almost equal to half the circumference of the gallery. This will take time

$$\tau = \frac{\pi d}{2} = \frac{\pi d}{2v}.$$

The difference in propagation time of the first and last signals is

$$\Delta \tau = \tau - \tau_0 = \frac{d}{v} \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \right).$$

It is for this time that the sound pulse emitted at point A will be delayed in relation to point B. Consequently, the digestion of the pulse received at point B will be equal to

$$\tau_2 = \tau_1 + \Delta \tau = \tau_1 + \frac{d}{v} \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \right)$$

It is interesting to note that with any other location of the points of emission and reception of sound pulses, the difference in their digestion will be even greater (see this for yourself).
