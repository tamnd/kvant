Lepiped of length $\Delta l$ with base area of ​​lateral faces $\Delta s$. The force $p_1 \cdot \Delta s$ acts on the left side of the parallelepiped, and the force $p_2 \cdot \Delta s$ acts on the right side. The total force acting on the parallelepiped is equal to

$$F = p_1 \cdot \Delta s - p_2 \cdot \Delta s = (p_1 - p_2) \Delta s.$$

Since $p_2 = p_1 + \frac{dp}{dx} \Delta l$, then

$$F = -\frac{dp}{dx} \Delta l \cdot \Delta s = -\frac{dp}{dx} \Delta V,$$

where $\Delta V = \Delta l \cdot \Delta s$ is the volume of the parallelepiped. The work done by the pressure when moving the parallelepiped along the $x$ axis is determined by the $\int F dx$ integral. If there is a movement from a point with coordinate $x = a$ to a point $x = b$, then

$$A = \int_a^b F dx = -\Delta V \int_a^b \frac{dp}{dx} dx = \Delta V (p_a - p_b).$$

This work is equal to the change in the kinetic energy of the parallelepiped, i.e. $A = K_b - K_a$ (we neglect the work of friction forces):

$$\Delta V (p_a - p_b) = \Delta V \cdot \rho \left( \frac{v_b^2}{2} - \frac{v_a^2}{2} \right).$$

From here we get

$$p_a + \rho \frac{v_a^2}{2} = p_b + \rho \frac{v_b^2}{2},$$

i.e.

$$p + \rho \frac{v^2}{2} = \text{const.}$$

“Okay,” I said, “we’ve sorted out Bernulin’s equation.” But how can we still determine the ratio $v_0/v_{01}$?

- I said - using this equation. This is true for both liquid flow and air flow. We will, as before, write down quantities relative to air with the index “1”. So, in the water film formed on the surface of the wing,

$$p + \rho \frac{v^2}{2} = \text{const},$$

and in the air flow

$$p_1 + \rho_1 \frac{v_1^2}{2} = \text{const}_1.$$

To compare $v$ and $v_1$, we need to establish some correspondence between const and const$_1$, $p$ and $p_1$.

Do you remember, comparing the levels of LOB resistance when flying in dry weather and in rain, we came to the conclusion that they are practically no different. This means that the pressure $p_c$ at point $C$ (look at figure 1) can be considered the same in general cases. And the velocities of a particular liquid and air at this point are equal to zero. Taking all this into account, we can write:

for the fluid flow around the wing,

$$p + \rho \frac{v^2}{2} = p_c,$$

for air flow

$$p_1 + \rho_1 \frac{v_1^2}{2} = p_c,$$

and therefore

$$p + \rho \frac{v^2}{2} = p_1 + \rho_1 \frac{v_1^2}{2}.$$

Now let's deal with $p$ and $p_1$. The parallelepiped we have selected moves with the flow practically horizontally, and in the transverse direction (i.e. in the vertical direction) its speed is zero. This means that the drains acting on it from above and from below are obviously the same. But the leakage acting from above is in both cases (both in the presence of a water film and without it) a leakage of pressure from the side of the “external” air flow.
