Here are several problems from the Canadian Mathematical Olympiad.

1 (1969). Let $f$ be a function with the following properties:
1) $f(n)$ defined for any natural $n$;
2) $f(n)$ — integer;
3) $f(2)=2$;
4) $f(mn)=f(m)f(n)$ for any $m$ and $n$;
5) $f(m)>f(n)$ when $m>n$.
Prove that $f(n)=n$.

2 (1970). Let a polynomial $P(x)=x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+...+a_{n-1}x+a_n$ with integer coefficients $a_1, ..., a_n$ be given. It is known that there exist four distinct integers $a, b, c$ and $d$ such that $P(a)=P(b)=P(c)=P(d)=5$. Prove that for any integer $k$ $P(k)\neq 8$.

3 (1971). Each of $n$ knows exactly one piece of news, and all $n$ news are different. Every time $A$ calls $B$, $A$ reports $B$ all he knows, then $B$ doesn't say anything. What is the minimum number of calls required for everyone to know all the news?

4 (1972). Prove that the equation $x^3+11^3=y^3$ has no solutions in natural numbers.

5 (1973). Let for every natural number $n$
$$h(n)=1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\dots+\frac{1}{n}.$$
Prove that
$$n+h(1)+h(2)+...+h(n-1)=nh(n)$$
at $n=2, 3, 4, ...$.

6 (1974). Let $n$ be a given natural number. Let's make the following sum $T_n=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_i-x_j|$, where $x_i\in R(i=1, 2, ..., n)$ and $0\leq x_i\leq 1(i=1, 2, ..., n)$. Let us denote by $S(n)$ the maximum possible value $T_n$. Find $S(n)$.

7 (1975). Let $k$ be a natural number. Find all polynomials $P(x)=a_0+a_1x+\dots+a_nx^n$ with real coefficients the same as $P(P(x))=(P(x))^k$.

8 (1976). Let for every natural number $n(n\geq 1)$
$$n(n+1)a_{n+1}=n(n-1)a_n-(n-2)a_{n-1}.$$
Find $\frac{a_0}{a_1}+\frac{a_1}{a_2}+\frac{a_2}{a_3}+\dots+\frac{a_{50}}{a_{51}}$ if $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$.
