Below we use simple approximation (4). In this case, the energy of the rope distances $W = F^2 L / 2 S_0 E_{\text{cp}}$ (see formulas (2), (3)). Equating this expression to the kinetic energy of the vessel, we find that at current speed $v < v_{\text{max}}$ the maximum tension of the rope is
$$F_v = F_{\text{max}} \frac{v}{v_{\text{max}}}.$$
(10)

Well, now let’s find out what the role of the water resistance force is. This force is maximum when the ship is stopped, and at flow speed $v$ it is $F_c = M v^2 / l_{\text{эф}}$.

Let us find the ratio of this addition to the tension of the rope $F_v$. Taking into account relations (3), (8) and (10),

$$\frac{F_c}{F_v} = \frac{M v^2}{l_{\text{эф}} F_v} = \frac{M v^2}{l_{\text{эф}} F_{\text{max}} (v / v_{\text{max}})} \cdot \frac{M v_{\text{max}}^2 / 2}{M v_{\text{max}}^2 / 2} = \frac{v^2}{l_{\text{эф}} (v / v_{\text{max}})} \cdot \frac{2 L F_{\text{max}} \varepsilon_{\text{max}} / 2}{v_{\text{max}}^2 F_{\text{max}}} = \frac{L \varepsilon_{\text{max}}}{l_{\text{эф}}} \cdot \frac{v}{v_{\text{max}}}.$$

Thus, the role of water resistance is described by the parameter
$$\beta = L \varepsilon_{\text{max}} / l_{\text{эф}}.$$

Typically, with this value, a rope no longer than 20 m is used. With $\varepsilon_{\text{max}} = 0,15$ this gives a total rope extension of 3 m, and with $l_{\text{эф}} \gtrsim 10$ m
$$\beta \lesssim 1/3.$$

So, when the flow speed is less than $v_{\text{max}}$, the value $F_c$ does not exceed $F_{\text{max}} / 3$. Therefore, the addition due to flow pressure plays a small role, and the limiting speed is close to $v_{\text{max}}$.
