From old experiences

For our readers' seasonal inspiration, we offer excerpts from J. Perry's book "The Rotating Drawbar", published in Russian in 1935. The book is based on a lecture given by the author, English professor of physics John Perry, to the public, containing a popular presentation of the Voluk theory and accompanied by beautiful demonstrations. The material is published with minor editorial cuts and changes.

Rotating die

Think carefully - and you will admit that the behavior of the common wolf is extremely surprising. If he doesn’t believe it, then, as you see, he immediately topples, and I am not able to keep him balanced on the tip. But it has completely different properties when rotating: not only does it not fall, but on the contrary, when I push it, it shows amazing resistance and even takes on a more and more vertical position.

As soon as we awaken our interest in scientific observation, we will notice that nature will overcome phenomena of this kind for us in large numbers.

Those of you who have had to spin a rope quickly know that fast movement imparts a kind of “stiffness” to flexible and even liquid bodies. Here, for example, is a circle made of very thin paper (Fig. 1); if I bring it into rapid rotation, it resists behind my hand or the blow of my fist, as if it were a circle of steel. You can hear what it sounds like when I hit it with a stick. Where did his flexibility go?
