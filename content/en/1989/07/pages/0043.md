⟦folio 41⟧

The lines in a triangle connecting its vertices with the points of tangency of the outer circles intersect at one point, which is called the Nagel point in honor of the German mathematician August Nagel (1821-1903) who discovered it.

If you draw a circle through the bases of the altitudes of a given triangle, then it will pass through the midpoints of the sides of this triangle and through the midpoints of the segments of the altitudes of the triangle from the point of their intersection to the vertices. This circle is called the circle of the ninth point. The circle of the ninth point touches the outer circle of this triangle.

It is curious that the segments connecting the center of the inscribed circle in the triangle with the centers of the outer circles are divided in half by the circle described in the circular triangle.

It is easy to prove that an isosceles triangle has equal medians, altitudes and bisectors extending from the ends of the base. It is not much more difficult to prove that if two altitudes or two medians of a triangle are equal, then the triangle is isosceles. This statement is also true for bisectors, but its proof is quite complicated. This statement itself is called the Steiner-Lemus theorem.
