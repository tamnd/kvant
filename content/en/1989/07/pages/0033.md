F1180. The light bulb connected to the battery stays on for three hours, after which the battery is completely discharged. We made a copy of this battery with twice the linear dimensions from the same materials. How long will the same light bulb stay on when connected to such a copy? The internal resistance of a battery is much less than the resistance of a light bulb.

K. S. Bedov

F1181. A rigid thin conducting ring lies on a non-conducting horizontal surface and is in a uniform magnetic field, the induction lines of which are horizontal. Ring mass $m$, radius $R$, induction values ​​$B$. How much current must be passed through the ring for it to begin to rise?

S. S. Krotov

F1182. When carrying out radar detection of the Moon, which rose above the horizon, to ensure the correct direction, the emitter of high-frequency radio signals was paired with an optical telephone. However, even though there was an optical image of the Moon, there was no reflected radio signal. At the same time, when it was possible to obtain a reflected radio signal, there was no optical image. Explain this phenomenon.

A. R. Zilberman

M1147. Given several points connected by segments of two cents: some pairs of points - we get segments, several segments, several even segments. Prove that all points can be divided into two sets so that each red segment connects points from different sets, and each blue segment connects points from the same set.

Let's take one of the given points $a$ and consider all the points connected to $a$ by a path from the given segments. If such a path contains an even number (possibly zero) of red segments, then we assign the corresponding point to the set $A$, in particular, $a \in A$; if the number of red segments on such a path is odd, then we assign its end to the set $B$ (see figure). This rule breaks down correctly: if one could come to some point $x$ from $a$ along two different paths, and one of them would have an even and the other an odd number of red segments, then they would form a closed path with an odd number of red segments, which is impossible by condition.

It is clear that any segment connecting points of different sets is red, and any segment connecting two points $y$ and $z$ of set $B$ is also blue - if it were red, then, when connecting it to a path from $a$ and $b$ with an odd number of red segments, we would get a path from $a$ to $z$ with an even number of red segments.
