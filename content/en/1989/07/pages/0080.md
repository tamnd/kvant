9. In decimal notation of the number $p$ the only numbers that can be found are 1, 3, 7 and 9; Let's assume that they all occur. Consider the numbers $a_1=1973, a_2=1937, a_3=1397, a_4=1739, a_5=3719, a_6=1793$ and $a_7=1379$. It is easy to check that the remainder when $a_k$ is divided by 7 is 7-k. By rearranging the digits, this number $p$ can be reduced to the form $b \cdot 10^4 + a_k$, where $k$ is the remainder of dividing $b \cdot 10^4$ by 7. The number $b \cdot 10^4 + a_k$ is divisible by 7. A contradiction is obtained.

Moreover. It can be proven that in the decimal notation of the number $p$ there are no more than two different digits.

10. It is enough to consider the case when the last digits other than 1, 3 and 7 occur in the permutation sequence. If the numbers 1 and 7 are not permuted, then since 7 when divided by 3 leaves a remainder of 1, the sequence will have infinitely many numbers divisible by 3. Let us now assume that $a_k = p$ is a prime number and $a_{k+n} = p \cdot 10^n + b_n$, where $b_n = 33 \dots 3 (n$ is a triple). Among the numbers $b_1, \dots, b_{p+1}$ find the numbers $b_i$ and $b_j$ that give the same remainder when divided by $p$. The number $b_i - b_j = 33 \dots 300 \dots 0 = b_{i-j} \cdot 10^j$ is divisible by $p$, so $a_{k+i-j}$ is divisible by $p$.

11. Let $M$ be the center of a given circle, $O$ be the center of a circle inscribed in a triangle $ABC$ with an obtuse angle $C$, $D$ be the point of intersection of line $CO$ with a given circle. In triangle $ADO$ the angles at vertices $A$ and $O$ are equal, so $DO = AD$, and therefore $MO \geqslant DO - R = AD - R > R \sqrt{2} - R$. The desired GMT is given by inequalities $R(\sqrt{2} - 1) < MO < R$.

12. Suppose first that the circumcircles of the triangle $APR$ and $BPQ$ intersect at the point $O$, which lies inside the triangle $ABC$. Since $\angle QOR = 360^\circ - \angle QOP - \angle POR = \angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ - \angle C$, the points $O$ also lie on the circumcircle of triangle $CQR$. Let $O_a, O_b$ and $O_c$ be the circumcenters of triangles $APR, BPQ$ and $CQR$. Since $O_aO_b$ and $O_aO_c$ are bissertrices of the angles $PO_aO$ and $RO_aO$, then $\angle O_bO_aO_c = \angle PO_aR/2 = \angle A$. The case when the point $O$ lies outside the triangle $ABC$ is treated similarly.

13. For $p = 2$ the statement is obvious, so we will assume that $p$ is odd. Let us prove that $(-3)^i = 2^i + 5z_i$. For $i = 0$ this is obvious, but for $(-3)^i + 1 = 2(-3)^i - 5(-3)^i = 2^{i+1} + 5(2z_i - (-3)^i)$. Therefore $2^p - (-3)^p = (2 - (-3)) \times \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} 2^{p-1-i}(-3)^i = 5 \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} 2^{p-1-i} \cdot 2^i + 25 \times \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} 2^{p-1-i} \cdot z_i = 5p2^{p-1} + 25z$. A number that is divisible by 5 but not divisible by 25 cannot be a power of a natural number.

14. The last digit of the number $a$ can only be one of the following digits: 1, 3, 7 and 9. The case where the last digit of the number $a$ is 1 is very long. Now suppose that the last digit of the number $a$ is 3, 7 or 9. Then $a + a^2 + a^3 + a^4$ ends with a zero, which means $a^k + a^{k+1} + a^{k+2} + a^{k+3}$ also ends with a zero. It remains to check that the numbers $a + a^2$ and $a + a^2 + a^3$ cannot end in 1. But the number $a + a^2$ is even, and $a + a^2 + a^3 = (1 + a + a^2 + a^3) - 1$.
