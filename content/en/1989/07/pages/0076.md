On my first visit to Brazil, I once had lunch in a restaurant. It was not lunch time (for some reason I always ended up in the restaurant at the wrong time), and I was the only client among four office workers.

While serving my favorite steak and rice, I saw a Japanese man enter the restaurant. I met him before - he traded accounts. And now I hear his conversation with the officeants:

“You bet I can add two numbers faster than all of you?”

The waiters, not wanting to lose face, said:

— There’s a client sitting there, why don’t you compete with him?

- But I don’t know Portuguese! - I was indignant.

- Nothing, numbers are easy! - they laughed.

They brought paper and pencil. The Japanese asked the office workers to name the terms. Of course he won: while I was writing down the numbers, he immediately got the answer. I suggested that they write us two identical lists of terms and give them to us at the same time. The result changed little - he still counted faster.

My opponent got a little wound up and wanted to make his victory easier. "Multiplicação!" - he said. Someone wrote us a multiplication problem. He won again, but not by such a margin. And then he made a mistake - he offered to take the part. He didn't assume that the harder the task, the better my chances.

- Raios cubicos! - he declared. Cube roots! He wants to extract cube roots on an abacus! It is difficult to imagine a more complex problem for arithmetic methods. This was probably his signature number.

I still remember this number - 1729.03. He started counting at an alarming rate, muttering to himself. It gave off a straight stream of steam.

Meanwhile I just sat.

- Why are you sitting? - asked one of the office workers.

- Think! - I answered with dignity. Then he took his hand and wrote: 12. A little weather I got 12,002.

- No way! - I said. Next numbers please!

I knew that with each successive digit the amount of arithmetic work increased.

The waiters were very friendly:

- Please note: he is already calculating, but you need an abacus. And he got more numbers!
