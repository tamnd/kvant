With the change $w_{\text{max}}$ everything is simple. Take a short piece of vervik, length $L$. Rigidly fasten one end of the rope, tie a weight to the other end, lift it vertically up (as far as possible), and then allow the weight to fall freely. Repeat the experiment, gradually increasing the mass of the load. If the rope breaks at a load mass of $m$, then $w_{\text{max}} = 2mg(1+a/L)$ J/m, where $a$ is the distance from the point where the rope is secured to the load to its center of gravity. Here, we do not take into account the addition to the change in potential energy associated with the extension of the rope, $mg \cdot \Delta L = mgL\varepsilon_{\text{max}}$.
