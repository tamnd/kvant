WHY
PLANES DO NOT FLY
IN HEAVY RAIN?

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. K. BETYAEV

“Nature has no flat weather,” says a popular song. This provision is very often refuted by Aeroflot, which delays flights citing flat weather conditions. I was convinced of this when, on a rainy autumn night, my friend and I continued to board the flight we needed at Moscow Domodedovo Airport.

— Why are flights delayed? — I began to think dryly. — The time of thunderstorms has already passed, the loss of wings in warm weather is impossible, the current state of navigation technology allows flights in conditions of complete lack of visibility, in complete fog...

The speaker rustled again: “... flight departure... postponed... weather conditions...”. Outside the windows, lanterns blurred in a continuous blur...

“It’s clear why flights are prohibited,” I said after a long silence, “aircraft are not designed to operate in conditions of high humidity.” An airplane propeller is not a ship's propeller.

“Our plane is a tourist plane,” the friend corrected, “the presence of water in the engine does not have a strong effect on its efficiency.”

- So what's the matter? It turns out that the weather conditions are just an excuse?!

- Don't rush to conclusions. Let's draw how a flow containing water droplets flows around an airplane wing.

The friend pushed away the half-drunk glass of tea, took out a sheet of paper, a fountain pen with multi-colored stubble, and scribbled figure 1.

— We will consider the movement in the reference frame associated with the aircraft, assuming that it is already in flight. In this system, the aircraft wing is stationary, and air and droplets fly at it. And their speeds far from the wing are the same and equal in abscissa to the aircraft's flight speed.

- What about gravity? - I asked, looking at figure.
