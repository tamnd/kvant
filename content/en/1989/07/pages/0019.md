AMAZING EQUALITY

V. D. KOTLYAR

Perhaps a coincidence... What is the value of l - the ratio of the circumference of a circle to its diameter? Reader «Kvant» has the right to be offended by such a question. Of course l ≈ 3.14; it is possible and more precise: l ≈ 3.14159; it can be even more precise... This means π² ≈ 9.87. What is g, the acceleration due to gravity, equal to? Who doesn’t know this - whoever studied physics will say that g ≈ 9.81 m/s². Means:

"So what? - the reader will shrug his shoulders. - You never know how many coincidences there are in the world! And it’s not a real coincidence: the difference between π² and g is not so small - about 0.06.”

“There’s nothing to talk about here at all,” another reader will enter into the conversation, “the equality π² ≈ g itself does not make any sense, because on the left side there is a number, and on the right side there are quantities that have dimension. If you change the distance not in meters, but, for example, in centimeters, then g ≈ 981 cm/s². And in England at all: g ≈ 32.2 ft/s². So there is nothing promised between π² and g!”

And yet, let's try to figure out what the nature of the guessing equality π² ≈ g is. To do this, as the second reader usually noted, you need to understand in what units physical quantities are measured.

How to change the length? From ancient times to the present day, people have had to make measurements - determine length, time, weight, speed and other quantities. And the question of uniform measurements has long been raised. Everyone knows, of course, that in the International System of Units (SI) the main units are length - meter, mass - kilogram, time - seconds. The people of Edine are good, we are used to them, we cannot imagine our daily life without them. When we come to the store, we ask you to answer 2 kilograms of steepness, when we go out onto the path of the herd, we run 100 meters, from bell to bell in the lesson 45 minutes pass, or 2700 seconds. We don’t have time; we need a scale to determine the mass; we measure the distance with a graduated ruler or tape measure. What if you don’t have a tape measure at hand? How to change the length in this case? It would be nice if the unit of measurement was always at hand; I wanted to and made my own “ethailon”. That's how it goes with measuring angles! To open an angle of 42°, we use a transor, but the standard of “1 degree” angles is not needed. If tomorrow all the angles and transorts disappeared on Earth, we could easily restore this unit by simply dividing a full angle into 360 equal parts. It is desirable for other quantities to have reproducible units of measurement. When the first systems of measures spontaneously arose, this reproducibility was built into them from the very beginning - units of length were associated, for example, with the sizes of individual parts of the human body. This is how “fathom” arose - the distance between the ends of the outstretched arms of an adult (“measured fathom” ≈ 176 cm) and “elbow”, the name of which speaks for itself - it is equal to the distance from the elbow to the outstretched fingers.
