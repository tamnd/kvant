At the top of the column, we want the mortar to absorb moisture from the air, which may be as low as 20% relative humidity. The solution must therefore be quite saturated: the relative increase in saturated vapor pressure $p$ above the solution (in our case 80%) is proportional to its concentration (Raoult’s law). That's why

$$\frac{p}{p_0} = \frac{80}{100} \approx \frac{n}{(N+n)},$$

where $p_0$ is the saturated vapor pressure above pure water. This condition is satisfied starting from $n=4N$, whence the minimum height of the liquid column is

$$h = \frac{RT}{g} \frac{4N}{(NM+4N\mu)} = \frac{4RT}{(M+4\mu)g}.$$

Take $T=300$ K and find $h$ for the three above-mentioned compositions. The molecular weight of water is 18, i.e. $M = 0,018$ kg/mol. For sulfuric acid $\mu = 0,098$ kg/mol, for sugar syrup $\mu = 0,342$ kg/mol, for liquid polyethylene oxide $\mu = 5$ kg/mol. Corresponding height of the liquid column is 2430 m (sulfuric acid); 720 m (sugar syrup), 50 m (polyethylene oxide). It is clear that the choice should be polyethylene oxide pastes.
