I give her a quick rotational movement on this drum and let her hit the floor, she hits across the entire table just as if it were a hard stake, and when this chain falls on the floor, it bounces up like a toy hoop.

The water inside the glass vessel (Fig. 3) is in a state of rapid movement, rotating along with the vessel. Look at a piece of paraffin A submerged in water, and you will notice that it trembles if I push it with a stick, exactly as if it were surrounded by a thick jelly. Seeing now this experience of William Thomson, as proposed by Professor Fitzgerald. Let's attach circle B to the end of the stick. When I slide circle B into the water, you see that, although it does not touch piece A, it still pushes it away. And then you can notice that if I quickly rotate circle B, it kind of feels like a piece of paraffin A.

By striking the elastic surface of the back wall of the box (Fig. 4), a small amount of air near the round hole on the front side can be brought into rapid rotational motion; to make this air visible to the eye, it is mixed with smoke, and thus a stick of smoke is obtained. This shot moves over a considerable distance without changing, almost like a solid body, and I don’t know for sure whether it would be possible to send a large shot of poisonous smoke so far away that it would destroy or kill an army several kilometers away.

Do not forget that during the existence of the ring, the air particles that make it up remain the same. You may notice that the two smoke rings coming out of the two boxes have a knocking effect on each other.

Already from the experiments that have been shown to you, you see that movement imparts, with a small amount of liquid, the amazing properties of elasticity, acceptance and repulsion; that each of the whole parts of a substance resists being divided into two parts; that you can’t even come close to the smoke
