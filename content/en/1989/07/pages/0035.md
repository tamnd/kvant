R is the point of intersection of straight lines l and m, t is the common tangent to the circle at point M, α and β are the angles formed by it with straight lines l and m. Then α + β = 180° – ∠PRQ and ∠PMQ = (90° – α/2) + (90° – β/2) = 180° – (α + β)/2 = 180° + ∠PRQ.

Conversely, through any point M with the head of the arc there pass two circles touching it, satisfying the conditions of the problem (if a straight line is drawn through M, making angles α = 180° – 2∠MPR and β = 180° – α – ∠PRQ, then they form two equilateral triangles with bases MP and MQ).

A similar proof can be found for any other arrangement of rays: the quantity α + β (and, therefore, ∠PMQ) is always expressed in terms of the angle between the lines l and m (i.e., through ∠PRQ), since the angles α and β between the tangents (l and t, t and m) are located on opposite sides of the line t.

Note that the center O of the arc head is the midpoint of the arc PQ of the circle passing through the points P, Q, R: for example, for the arrangement shown in figure 1, ∠POQ = ∠POM + ∠MOQ = 2(180° – ∠PMQ) = 180° – ∠PRQ and, of course, OP = OQ.

Note that if in the conditions we replace the rays with straight lines containing them and consider both the external and internal tangency of the circles, then the excluded set will be the union of circles ω1 ∪ ω2 (without points P and Q).
