So, we are interested in the following problem: it is necessary to stop a vessel of mass $M$ in the flow using a rope of length $L$. At what speed is this possible?

But before moving on to the main calculations, let’s talk separately about the rope and the tourist boat.

Rope. A fairly detailed calculation about ropes used in tourism and mountaineering is contained in the article by A. B. Geller “Does a climber need physics” («Kvant», 1988, No. 1). But we have a little more to say.

Naturally, we are primarily interested in reading the ropes, the maximum tension that it can withstand.

Let us denote by $L_0$ and $S_0$ the length of the rope and its cross-sectional area ($S_0 = \pi d^2/4$, where $d$ is the diameter of the rope). It is well known that with relatively small sections $F$ the elongation of the rope $\Delta L$ is proportional to $F$ (Hooke’s law):

$$F = E \frac{S_0}{L_0} \Delta L = ES_0 \varepsilon,$$

(1)

where $E$ is the modulus of elasticity (it is the same for commonly used ropes of the same weave), $\varepsilon = \Delta L/L_0$ is the relative elongation of the rope. Correspondence, potential strain energy of a stretched rope -

$$W = \frac{k(\Delta L)^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} ES_0 L_0 \varepsilon^2.$$

(2)

This energy is proportional to the length of the rope $L_0$, so it is useful to introduce the concept of energy per unit length of rope $w$:

$$w = \frac{W}{L_0} = \frac{ES_0 \varepsilon^2}{2} = \frac{F \varepsilon}{2} = \frac{F^2}{2S_0 E}.$$

(3)

Note that for a given value relative to the extension $\varepsilon$, the values ​​$F$ and $w$ are proportional to $S_0$ and do not depend on $L_0$.

So, under small conditions $\varepsilon$ from $F$ is linear -

$$\varepsilon = \frac{F}{ES_0}.$$

(1')

Under greater conditions, the problem depends on the violation - with increasing force, the elongation begins to increase than according to law (1'). When the tension force reaches a certain value $F_{\max}$, the rope begins to stretch. The values ​​$F_{\max}$ and $\varepsilon_{\max}$, as well as the exact type of load characteristic, can be found in special reference books. Knowing them, we need to determine $w_{\max}$ - the maximum deformation energy per unit length of the rope (it is described by the plane under the red curve at figure 2). In general, this is done numerically (often on a computer).
