⟦folio 61⟧

Anyone who thinks that the body should rotate in exactly the direction in which I spun it from the very beginning will find this phenomenon extraordinary. Meanwhile, you can easily verify that almost every rounded stone, when rotated, rises in this way vertically along the longest axis, if only the rotation is fast enough; In exactly the same way, the rotating fiber strives to rise as much as possible.

***

I hung several glasses here (see the intro to the article): one (a) filled with sand, another (b) with syrup, a third (c) with oil, four (d) with water, and a fifth (e) let. Look now: I twist the wires on which the glasses are, and then I leave them to their own devices - an oscillatory movement begins, similar to the swinging pendulum of a pocket watch. Notice now that the glass of water moves very quickly and the duration of the oscillation is almost the same for this glass and for the empty one, that is, the water does not seem to move with the glass. You will also notice that this glass continues to oscillate for a significant period of time.

On the contrary, a glass filled with sand oscillates slowly; in this case, the pitch and the glass together represent a kind of solid body, and the vibration lasts a long time.

In glasses filled with oil and syrup, the periods of oscillation are longer than in a glass with water or empty, but shorter than would be the case if the oscillating bodies were completely solid, since the oil or syrup only partially participates in the movement. But since the liquid only partially participates in oscillations, tremors appear inside the liquid itself, which lead to the fact that the oscillations die out faster than if the glass is empty or filled with sand.

A boiled egg (g) and an unboiled egg (f), which are suspended in the same way on a wire, will show the same difference in the properties of oscillatory motion, as do two bodies, one of which is solid inside and the other liquid; you see how much slower the vibrations of a boiled egg are than those of an unboiled egg.

Further here, on this table, you can easily detect the difference between a boiled and unboiled egg. If you twist both eggs, you see that the unboiled egg stops much earlier than the boiled one, since the first one comes to a state of rest earlier due to internal tremors.

Now watch carefully and understand the completely missing sign by which you can tell whether an egg is boiled or not. I twist the egg or rotate it around a vertical axis, and then hold it with my finger for just one moment, just long enough to stop the movement of the shell. You see that the boiled egg has only made the movements of the shell, but the liquid contents not only continue to move, but even resume the movements of the shell as soon as I remove my finger from it.
