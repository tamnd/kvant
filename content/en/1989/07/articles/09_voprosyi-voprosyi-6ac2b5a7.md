If this explanation is correct, then the piece of paper should be pressed against the glass with a very strong atmosphere (0.99 atm). Metal or glass plastic weighing several tens of grams does not hold at all at the edges of the glass - it falls off under the influence of weight. 11. Candle in a closed jar. Here is a description of an experiment borrowed from a children's (foreign) magazine, the purpose of which is to demonstrate atmospheric pressure:

“The burning stub of a candle is secured at the bottom of a glass jar and, when the flame has burned for some time, cover the jar with a lid, continuing to moisten the rubber ring between their edges. The flame dims and soon goes out (Fig. 3). Then try to tear off the lid from the jar - you will only succeed when you have had a lot of time.

The reason is easy to understand. The flame consumes oxygen, the supply of which in a hermetically sealed jar is limited. When the weight is consumed, the flame goes out. The remaining part of the air, occupying a larger volume, is cut and presses less. Excess external pressure is what presses the lid so tightly against the jar.”
