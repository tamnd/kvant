Sprinkler
for the sake of it

Daedalus proposes a new method for separating water vapor from the air in the desert, which is based on the fact that sulfuric acid or corn syrup, left in an open container, actively absorbs moisture from the air. In general, any solution whose saturated vapor pressure is lower than the water vapor pressure in the surrounding air must absorb moisture from the air. But how to extract this moisture from the solution? The most natural thing would be to use “reverse osmosis” for this, i.e. simply “squeeze” water from the solution through a semi-permeable MEMbrane*). Daedalus began to develop a water press

for desert travelers, which allows you to “squeeze” water out of sulfuric acid through a semi-permeable MEMbrane, and after removing the external pressure, the sulfuric acid again squeezes moisture out of the air. But then Daedalus came to the idea that hydrogrowth pressure could well play the role of a press. A tall column of sulfuric acid will continuously collect moisture from the air in its upper part, as a result of diffusion, the water is evenly distributed throughout the entire column and in its lower part under the influence of enormous pressure hydrogrowth of water through a semi-permeable MEMbrane. Daedalus, however, does not always like fiddling around with sulfuric acid - fortunately, thanks to its unhealthy molecular weight, corn syrup is even better suited for these purposes. If a column of sulfuric acid 2.4 km high is required to separate moisture from air with a relative humidity of 20%, then under the same conditions a column of corn syrup should only be (!) 720 m high. Of the liquids miscible with water, liquid polytylene acid seems to have the most convenient molecular weight; The height of the column of this liquid required for our purposes is only 50 m. The energy required to separate the liquid is ultimately provided by the water molecules themselves, descending downwards, so that the whole process proceeds continuously and does not require human intervention. From Daedalus's notebook

Suppose the dissolver contains $N$ moles of water with molar mass $M$ (total mass $NM$) and $n$ moles of dissolved substance with molar mass $u$ (total mass

mass $n\mu$). The mass of the raster in this case is equal to $M=NM+n\mu$, and its volume is $V=M/\rho$, where $\rho$ is the density of the raster.

The osmotic pressure, under the influence of which the solution absorbs clean water, is located on the other side of the semi-permeable_membrane, equal to

$$p_{\text{oc}}=nRT/V=nRT\rho/M.*$$

If the solution is poured into a vessel with a height of $h$, then the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of the vessel is equal to $\rho gh$. In order for pure water to be forced out of the solution through the semi-permeable membrane, the hydrostatic pressure must exceed the osmotic pressure:

$$\rho gh>nRT\rho/M,$$

$$h>nRT/gM=\frac{RT}{g}\frac{n}{(NM+n\mu)}$$

(For the end see p. 62)

*) Pay attention to the formula analogy with the universal gas law of Mendeleev - Clapeyron.
