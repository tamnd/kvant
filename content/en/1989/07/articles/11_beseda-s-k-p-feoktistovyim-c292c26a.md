Conversation with
K. P. Feoktistov

— Konstantin Petrovich, 20 years have passed since the day when man first set foot on the moon. For most of our readers, this day is distant history, it was still “before them,” and can they imagine what the cleansing of people on Earth was like at that time. How would you express them?

— On July 20, 1969, we looked at the Moon with unusual, new purifications. It seems as if he always sees in the sky either an image or an actual celestial body. But now there are people walking there - Armstrong and Aldrin... How strange - we are here, and they are there, in this golden sparkling country, at a fantastic distance of 400,000 kilometers from us! This is precisely the moment of self-extraction of a person. Well, and the Americans, naturally, in particular.

The self-perception of people as individuals (both believers and atheists) equally stimulates them to discover something new, to expand the sphere of understanding, action and life. True, more often this manifests itself in a purely “geometric” sense. Here, usually, a psychologically understandable, logical, not convincing idea: differences in the achievable world, we get the opportunity to learn more about the world and, perhaps, something new about our place in it and about ourselves. For most, this idea was the driving force behind the Lunar Project. And the event of July 20, 1969 was perceived, in Armstrong’s words, as “a huge leap for man.”

Impressive scope and impressive results. Complete success.

The natural euphoria of the project developers and, probably, the majority of Americans at that time is also understandable: “We are on the Moon!... It’s us on the Moon, and not these Russians who are forever lagging behind, producing in their pillboxes and in their administrative-hierarchical structures... The natural position in military research has been restored (and ours will represent it!)... What we all previously perceived as some kind of abstraction, some colorful and unchanging detail in the sky, turned out to be the same a world in which you can walk, ride, you can

I don’t remember any special emotions that I experienced at that moment, except that I wanted to be careful, I wanted to make sure that it was safe to step on this surface...

Neil Armstrong

Emotionally, this worldwide show, of course, meant a lot for everyone: to feel like a participant in this extraordinary journey and adventure, to feel the Moon under your feet...

- But?

- But there is also something complacent, some kind of dissatisfaction. The landing of N. Armstrong and E. Aldrin on the Moon marked the beginning of the implementation of the Lunar Project. From 1969 to 1972, Americans delivered 6 expeditions to the Moon. What should be included in the Lunar Project plan? The 12 people who walked on the Moon, in total, all together, walked and drove on its surface about 100 kilometers. About 400 kilograms of moon rocks were delivered to Earth. But these stones themselves have never (except, perhaps, to selnologists and geochemists) provided fundamentally new and valuable information.

Perhaps some other significant information was received? Apparently not.

Positive emotions and images of the USA - yes, of course. But “25 billion at a time”—that’s exactly how much the United States promised the first flight of astronauts to the Moon—sounds a little funny. And sad. After all, those space programs that could have been implemented with these enormous funds can also be written off as murders...

— How did it happen that “practical Americans left their choice on the Moon?” Who made this choice?

- Here we need to go back and remember the late 50s - early 60s.

1957 - The Soviet Union launched the first international rocket and the first artificial Earth satellite.

1961 - The Soviet Union carried out the first manned flight into space.

Why did this situation arise? Why are we ahead, despite the enormous technical and technological potential of the United States?

I would not want to create the same impression as in the Fox and the Grapes database, but today I see no reason to be upset that we did not invest more effort and resources in order to end up on the Moon.

— And yet, what place does the Lunar Expedition occupy from the point of view of the development of space technology and technologically?

— Work on the Lunar project began in the early sixties. Over these years, the Americans, as a result of enormous and well-copied work, created not only the Apollo spacecraft and the Saturn 5 package, but also a gigantic experimental base, fire walls for testing package engines, equipment for preparing the package and spacecraft for launch, and flight control facilities. This entire work was an outstanding technical achievement. But some parts of this work are a remarkable success. Here the assessment may be subjective. I would note the following:

the hydrogen-oxygen engine of the second stage of the Saturn 5 carrier package, approximately 1.3 times more efficient than liquid engine packages running on oxygen and kerosene or other components;

a descent vehicle in which the cosmos return to Earth. Achievements here include the creation of protection for the device when it returns to the Earth’s atmosphere at the second cosmic speed (about 11 km/s). This speed greatly complicates the task of combating the flows of tellum coming from the high-temperature plasma onto the body of the device;

vertical package landing system on the lunar surface;

a system for autonomous release of the lunar cabin and the main block in orbit of the Lunar satellite (during the return, the lunar experiment started from the surface in the lunar cabin, entered lunar orbit, rendezvoused with the main block and transferred to it);

electrical generators running on hydrogen and oxygen;

technical means, on-board flight control system, which became a turning point in the complete set of space flight controls. They provide prompt processing of information coming from ships, the ability to quickly analyze, develop recommendations and quickly transmit them on board ships in the form of radio commands, instructions or advice to the crew. Probably, other achievements should be noted (materials, production of liquid hydrogen, etc.).

All of these are undoubtedly outstanding results. And yet...

- And yet?

- As for the Lunar program - the train has left - it has been implemented. But there is a lesson here for the future.

Was the US space goal chosen correctly in the early 60s? Was it done on time or too early? Let's put it together, why exactly today do we find it necessary to create an electric light bulb, discover the structure of the nucleus, fly into space, land on the Moon? Or bring about a revolution? The answer seems to be very clear - “because today it turned out to be possible!”

This is not always the case. Especially in this case. Especially if you take into account the gigantic funds allocated for the Lunar program. For me, it is not only an achievement, but also one of the examples of an unsuccessful or incorrectly pursued goal.

After all, the problems of choosing a target are transmitted by entire nations. We can recall an example closer to us from the field of choosing political goals. For example, what was the whole of the October Revolution, and what was the means...

— What if we return it to space? The press is now considering the issue of a vision of about 100 billion dollars or rubles for the implementation of a joint Soviet-American expedition to Mars...

But when making a decision, choosing a goal, you need to think hard!

Why Mars? Are there any significant chances of finding something completely unexplained and very valuable on Mars?

Actually, maybe. If, for example, we find living organisms on Mars, at least at the level of fungi or bacteria. Be able to bring them alive to Earth and establish a mechanism for their reproduction. Understand what mechanism this is. The same as those of terrestrial organisms, or completely different? Find the answer to the question about the origin of life on Earth - “spontaneous generation” or “seeding”? Or find traces of intelligent beings on Mars. Extremely interesting. But what are the chances of finding living organisms or traces of civilization on Mars? I'm afraid they are small, to put it mildly.

Why Mars? Aren't there other really sharp, intriguing universal secrets that could be “unearthed” for 100 billion?

For example, to create a complex of astrophysical instruments in space (large telescopes, including radio telescopes), which would allow us to examine the center of our Galaxy, the closest to the Universe, or even the northern methodical search for signals from other civilizations.

Or take part in the solution of a major global economic problem - the creation of a network of orbital power plants and corresponding receiving ground stations, which should be an opportunity to obtain an environmentally friendly energy supply system for the Earth. To solve this problem, it would be necessary to create a real solution for launch vehicles, efficient space tugs, film solar panels, giant antennas for transmitting energy to Earth in the radio range, complex equipment for orbital solar power plants...

Returning to today, the twenty-year anniversary of the first lunar expedition, I would say the following. One of the main results of the implementation of the lunar landing program is another content that when choosing goals for large, expensive enterprises, a very careful weighing of options is necessary, studying them, elaborating them. In order to most successfully and without harming yourself find the correct match, the goal is the possibility that the money spent and the successful match with the result.
