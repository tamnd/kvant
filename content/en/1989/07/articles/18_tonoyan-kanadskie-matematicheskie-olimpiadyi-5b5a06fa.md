Mathematical Olympiads of Germany

Mathematical Olympiads for schoolchildren in Germany have been held since 1970. Anyone can participate in the first correspondence Olympiad: posters with the conditions of the problem are displayed in all schools, and students must submit their solutions within two months. The number of students of the first type in different years fluctuated from 500 to 3000 people. Winners of the first type are invited to the second round. In the first and second rounds, 4 tasks are proposed. The winners of the Olympiad are determined after the third type - their own.

Below we present several problems from the first two rounds.

1 (1971, I typ). On a chessboard of size $n \times n$ cells, in one move you are allowed to move to one of the following cells: 1) the one adjacent from above; 2) next to the right; 3) the adjacent one at the bottom left. Prove that from each cell it is impossible to go to the cell adjacent to it on the right by raising exactly once in each cell.

2 (1972, I typ). Let $f(n) = [n + \sqrt{n} + 0, 5]$, where $n$ is a natural number. Prove that $f(n)$ takes all natural values ​​except perfect squares. ($[x]$ denotes the most integer number not exceeding $x$.)

3 (1973, I typ). Given a number $a = 0$, $a_1 \dots a_n$, the decimal notation of which contains $n$ decimal places. Is there always a natural number $N$ such that the first $n$ digits of $\sqrt{N}$ after the decimal point coincide with $a$?

4 (1974, I typ). The lengths of the sides of a school quadrilateral are $a, b, c$ and $d$. What is the necessary and sufficient condition for the fact that in one of its positions this quadrilateral is a trapezoid or a parallelogram?

5 (1975, I typ). Which quadrilaterals with perpendicular diagonals are both vertical and circumscribed?

6 (1975, II typ). a) The ratio of the arithmetic mean of two natural numbers to their geometric mean is a natural number. Prove that both of these numbers are equal.

6) Refute the statement of problem a) in the case of the ratio of natural numbers to their geometric mean.

7 (1978, I typ). The sum of remainders $r(n)$ of a natural number $n$ is the sum of remainders from the division of $n$ by all natural numbers from 1 to $n$. Prove that the sums of the remainders for the numbers $2^n - 1$ and $2^n$ are equal.

8 (1978, I typ). Let $A_1, B_1$ and $C_1$ be the images of the points $A, B$ and $C$ under symmetry with respect to the points $B, C$ and $A$, respectively. Construct triangle $ABC$ given triangle $A_1B_1C_1$.

9 (1978, II typ). The prime number $p$ has the property that any number obtained from $p$ by rearranging its digits is prime. Prove that the decimal notation of the number $p$ contains no more than three different digits.

10 (1979, II typ). In the sequence of natural numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$, ..., each last term is obtained from the previous one by assigning to it on the right (i.e. at the end) a number other than 9. Prove that there are infinitely many composite numbers in this sequence.

11 (1979, II typ). Given a circle of radius $R$. Find the locus of the center of the circle inscribed in all possible triangles inscribed in the given circle.

12 (1980, II typ). On sides $AB, BC$ and $CA$ of triangle $ABC$ points $P, Q$ and $R$ are taken, respectively. Prove that the circumcenters of triangles $APR, BPQ$ and $CQR$ form a triangle similar to triangle $ABC$.

13 (1981, I typ). Prove that if the number $p$ is prime, then $2^p + 3^p$ is not a power of a natural number.

14 (1981, I typ). Let $S = a + a^2 + \dots + a^n$, where $a \in \mathbb{N}$. Prove that the last digit of the number $S$ is equal to 1 if and only if the last digits of the number $a$ and $n$ are equal to 1.

Canadian Math Olympiads

The National Mathematical Olympiads in Canada have been held since the fall of 1968. Before this, in all the provinces of this country, mathematical perfection of schoolchildren was carried out for many years, so that the general olympiads became the completion of this work.

Participants in the first four Olympiads fought back from the impoverished provincial Olympiads. This method was later expanded upon. Now the director of secondary schools has decided to personally nominate students

Here are several problems from the Canadian Mathematical Olympiad.

1 (1969). Let $f$ be a function with the following properties:
1) $f(n)$ defined for any natural $n$;
2) $f(n)$ — integer;
3) $f(2)=2$;
4) $f(mn)=f(m)f(n)$ for any $m$ and $n$;
5) $f(m)>f(n)$ when $m>n$.
Prove that $f(n)=n$.

2 (1970). Let a polynomial $P(x)=x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+...+a_{n-1}x+a_n$ with integer coefficients $a_1, ..., a_n$ be given. It is known that there exist four distinct integers $a, b, c$ and $d$ such that $P(a)=P(b)=P(c)=P(d)=5$. Prove that for any integer $k$ $P(k)\neq 8$.

3 (1971). Each of $n$ knows exactly one piece of news, and all $n$ news are different. Every time $A$ calls $B$, $A$ reports $B$ all he knows, then $B$ doesn't say anything. What is the minimum number of calls required for everyone to know all the news?

4 (1972). Prove that the equation $x^3+11^3=y^3$ has no solutions in natural numbers.

5 (1973). Let for every natural number $n$
$$h(n)=1+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\dots+\frac{1}{n}.$$
Prove that
$$n+h(1)+h(2)+...+h(n-1)=nh(n)$$
at $n=2, 3, 4, ...$.

6 (1974). Let $n$ be a given natural number. Let's make the following sum $T_n=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_i-x_j|$, where $x_i\in R(i=1, 2, ..., n)$ and $0\leq x_i\leq 1(i=1, 2, ..., n)$. Let us denote by $S(n)$ the maximum possible value $T_n$. Find $S(n)$.

7 (1975). Let $k$ be a natural number. Find all polynomials $P(x)=a_0+a_1x+\dots+a_nx^n$ with real coefficients the same as $P(P(x))=(P(x))^k$.

8 (1976). Let for every natural number $n(n\geq 1)$
$$n(n+1)a_{n+1}=n(n-1)a_n-(n-2)a_{n-1}.$$
Find $\frac{a_0}{a_1}+\frac{a_1}{a_2}+\frac{a_2}{a_3}+\dots+\frac{a_{50}}{a_{51}}$ if $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$.
