I. A. Kushinr from Kyiv draws attention to segments in a triangle that can be characterized by the same words (for example, heights or medians). Such segments are called homonymous. Segments of the same name sometimes turn out to be equal. Here are two examples found by the author.

1. Each of the bases of the altitudes of the triangle is projected onto its sides. Then the one-dimensional segments connecting these projections are equal.

Proof. The quadrilateral $AF_1HF_2$ (Fig. 1) is inscribed.

nom. $AH$ is the diameter of the circumscribed circle. Therefore $F_1F_2=AH/\sin A=2S/BC \sin A=S/R$.

So, the length of the segment $F_1F_2$ does not depend on which side the height is omitted.

2. Equal segments of the same name are also segments: their ends are the midpoint of a side of the triangle and the midpoint of a segment whose ends are the opposite vertex of the triangle and its orthoterra.

Proof and try to find other examples of equal one-dimensional segments.

G. M. Smetanok from Lozovaya reports an interesting property of trisketris. Let $OM$ and $ON$ be the trisketrices of the angle $AOE$ (see Fig. 2). Then $ND=DA=AM$. Try to prove these equalities yourself.
