In bees, as can be seen from the table, there is a wide spread in the frequencies of wing vibrations. This deserves special mention. The fact is that the frequency of oscillations of a bee’s wings depends on whether it flies with a bribe (~300 Hz) or whether it is unloaded (~440 Hz). So, by the sound, as well as by a photograph taken with a short shutter speed (~1/1000 s), you can determine whether the bee comes home with the addition or not. The sound of a flying bee is not just tonal vibrations of the air at a carrier frequency equal to the frequency of the wings. He's much richer. Unlike many other insects, bees can produce sounds of varying pitches and different timbres, and the source of these sounds is not only the wings, but also parts of the bee’s body that vibrate due to frequent muscle contractions.

These data were obtained based on high-speed photography of the flight of bees.

How do birds fly?

A photograph of a flock of sparrows basking on a March morning in Chertanovo (one of the new districts of Moscow) was taken from approximately a distance of 10 m using a Jupiter-37 A object with a focal length of 135 mm on photographic film with a sensitivity of 250 units. GOST at shutter speed 1/500 s. Don't you think that these birds are somewhat reminiscent of fish in an aquarium? The only difference is that their “fin” wings are comparatively larger than those of fish. Such external similarities do not serve any purpose: there are many similarities in the movement of fish and birds, although birds are significantly heavier than air, and fish in water are almost “weightless”.
And one more detail is clearly visible in the photo: sparrows are able to rotate the plane of their wings almost 90° relative to their position in the general gliding flight (see the group of birds in the center of the photo). This helps the sparrows to take off vertically upward, abruptly change the direction of flight and remain motionless in the air.

Once I saw such a picture. A sparrow accidentally fell into the gap between the window and the closet on our balcony. The shelf was narrow, and the sparrow could not get out of captivity. I had to help him. While I was taking the sparrow out, its wings were touching the window glass. A pair of sparrows flew in from the street at the sound. To be honest, I did not expect such actions from them, which were clearly not caused by simple curiosity.
