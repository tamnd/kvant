Relative movement
As is known, the movement of any body is relative, i.e. its movement, speed, type of trajectory depends on the reporting system (s.o.) in which this movement is considered. When describing motion, you can use different reporting systems, stationary or moving.
To move from one system to another part, it is enough to write down the law of addition of displacements or

speed addition con:
$$\vec{s}_1 = \vec{s}_{12} + \vec{s}_2, \quad \vec{v}_1 = \vec{v}_{12} + \vec{v}_2.$$ (1)

Here $\vec{s}_1(\vec{v}_1)$ is the displacement (velocity) of the body relative to the first s. o., for example, at rest, $\vec{s}_{12}(\vec{v}_{12})$ - displacement (velocity) of the body relative to the second s. o., for example moving, $\vec{s}_2(\vec{v}_2)$ - movement (speed) of the second s. O. relative to the first one. Thus, the speed of the aircraft relative to the ground is the speed of the aircraft relative to the air plus the speed of the impact itself:
$$\vec{a}_1 = \vec{a}_{12} + \vec{a}_2,$$ (2)

where $\vec{a}_1$ is the acceleration of the body relative to rest c. o., $\vec{a}_{12}$ - acceleration of the body
