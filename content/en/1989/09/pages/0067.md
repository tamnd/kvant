All the data about Halley's Comet was collected by the Chinese. The first reliable mention in Chinese chronicles gives the date May 25, 240 BC. e. However, there is even a date: March 7, 1057 BC. e.! And between these two dates there are eight centuries of silence. What's the matter?

But the point is in unbridled human nature. Archangel Stratilatovich Interkhvat-Zalikhvatsky, according to Saltykov-Shchedrin, only “entered Glupov on a white horse, snowed the gymnasium and ruled the sciences,” as a result of which “history stopped flowing,” but, apparently, only on the scale of the city of Glupov. “The ideal ruler” (according to Chairman Mao), Emperor Qin Shi Huang decided that history should begin with him. And so that there was no doubt, he showed all the 460 scientists he discovered and sekg all the historical books. And since the historical chronicles mentioned events that took place before the rise of the Qin dynasty, the chronicles also went to the fire. Miraculously, individual books were taken into account, passages like the one that mentions the comet of 1057 BC. e. The “Great” Qin Dynasty ruled the Celestial Empire... for 14 years. Two millennia passed, and already in the center of Europe a “Millennium Empire” arose, where bonfires of books also burned and scientists were warned. She was answered with a story of 12 years...

But let's return to the Comet. If the time is from May 25, 240 BC. e. until February 9, 1986 e. divided by the 29 revolutions that Halley’s comet made during this time, you get an average of just 76.7 years per revolution. And all deviations are jokes, mainly from Jupiter. In general, Jupiter, which is more than twice as massive as all the other planets taken together, greatly distorts the orbits of polyhomets. It even has its own comet family. The Jupiter family is a group of comets whose aphelions lie close to the orbit of Jupiter. This giant captured them and does not allow them to return to their homeland - to the far reaches of the solar system. Introduces us to one of these comets

Problem 5. Comet Griese - Skjellerup belongs to the Jupiter group, its aphelion is located at a distance of 5.0 AU. e. from the Sun. The comet's orbital period is 4.9 years. can this comet cross the earth's orbit?

The calculation (which we provide to the reader) gives for the minimum distance of the comet from the Sun a value of 0.77 AU. e., i.e. the comet approaches the Sun closer than the Earth. So, in principle, comet Grieg-Skjellerup can cross the Earth’s orbit.

However, as already mentioned, the orbits of comets can have a noticeable inclination to the ecliptic plane. In this case, the intersection of the projections of the orbits of the planet and the comet onto this plane does not guarantee the intersection of the orbits themselves.

Thus, Comet Halley has perihelions at a distance of 0.59 AU. e., and meanwhile the minimum distance between the orbit and the orbit of the Earth is currently approximately 0.064 a. e., i.e. about 10 million km. The shortest distance at which Halley's comet passed from the Earth was 0.04 AU. e.=6 million km. This was in 837. Of course, there is no hard evidence of this, and there cannot be any. It is more or less clear to imagine the orbits of the comet after Halley.
