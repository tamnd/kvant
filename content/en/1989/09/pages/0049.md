Formulas (1) and (2) can be written in a more symmetrical and convenient form to directly express the relative movement (velocity, acceleration) of two bodies:

$$\vec{s}_{12} = \vec{s}_1 - \vec{s}_2, \quad \vec{v}_{12} = \vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2,$$

$$\vec{a}_{12} = \vec{a}_1 - \vec{a}_2.$$

For example, the speed of a boat relative to the water is the speed of the boat minus the speed of the water (current speed):

$$\vec{v}_{\pi B} = \vec{v}_{\pi} - \vec{v}_{\pi B}.$$

Note that everything said is true for the translational motion of one s. O. relative to another (the coordinate axes of motion of the coordinate system are always parallel to the coordinate axes of the stationary system). In addition, the speeds of all bodies must be small compared to the speed of light $c = 300\ 000$ km/s, otherwise the laws of the relative Eischstein theory come into force, which differ from those presented above.
