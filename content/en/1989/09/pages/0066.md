The comet appeared at the beginning of 1453, when the Christians were defeating the Muslims, and the pope considered it a herald of victory. By the way, this was, as we found out much later, the most famous comet - Halley's Comet.

The fame of this comet has many reasons. But the main thing is that it was through her example that the nature of cometary orbits was clarified. After all, even Kepler and Halley believed that the paths of comets were straightforward. But in 1680, the English astronomer Edmund Halley noticed the curvilinearity of the path of one of the comets. Of course, it was precisely the comet that soon received his name. More specifically, it was the appearance of Halley's Comet in 1682.

So what year is this comet: 1680 or 1682? The fact is that comets are often double dated: by the time of first observation and by the time of passage of perihelion. For periodic comets, which include Halley’s comet, the second system is more often used, which is why Halley in 1680 saw the comet of 1682, and Calixtus III in 1453 first blessed, and then cleared, and then cleared the comet of 1456.

Fortunately, Halley did not limit himself to stating the curvilinearity of the comet's path. He agreed that the comets of 1607 and 1531 were the same comet of 1682, and predicted its appearance in 1758.
