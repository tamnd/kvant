⟦folio 61⟧

And in scientific matters, Brahe and Kepler did not agree. Brahe was a proponent of a compromise theory of the structure of the solar system: the Sun revolves around the stationary Earth, and the planets revolve around the Sun. The Copernican era is treated with disdain. Brahe had a violent temperament: it is known that in a duel, the cause of which was scientific disagreement, he lost his nose. Therefore, one can imagine how the debate between the ardent Brahe and the stubborn and proud Kepler proceeded.

Kepler endured a difficult struggle with himself, with his own views. After all, everything went well at first! In the sphere on which the (circular) orbit of Saturn lies, we inscribe a cube. Let's inscribe a sphere into this cube - the orbit of Jupiter lies on it. We inscribe a tetrahedron into the sphere of Jupiter, and a sphere into it - we get the sphere of Mars, and so on, until the regular polyhedra and at the same time planets are exhausted. The “Cosmogtical Mystery” (this is the title of Kepler’s book!) The Harmony of the Spheres has been revealed! Pythagoras was envious!

But... eight angular minutes of discrepancy between the representative and observed positions of Mars forced Kepler to open up from the idea of ​​uniform motion of planets in circular orbits, illuminated not even by thousands of years. The role of Tycho Brahe cannot be underestimated: he was the second in the world - after Ulugbek - to achieve exactly the changes in two minutes, practically the limit for the naked eye. If the accuracy of his changes had been lower, Kepler would not have been able to distinguish an ellipse from a circle.

These eight minutes... give us the means to transform astronomy.

(I. Kepler)

And the “harmony of the spheres” collapsed. KEPLER'S FIRST LAW was born: planets move in ellipses, with the Sun at one of the foci.

And besides - KEPLER'S SECOND LAW - a line connecting the Sun and the planet describes equal areas in equal periods of time.

Johannes Kepler published these two laws in 1609. However, the work “New Astronomy”, in which they were proclaimed, was published by a “happy” accident. Strictly speaking, the work belonged to the Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire of the German Nation, Rudolf II, by whose order it was executed. But the emperor was unable to pay the costs of printing the book, and it came into the hands of the author. But Kepler was once again left without a livelihood - books in those days did not bring in income, especially if they proclaimed the heretical teaching of Keprnik. Such books could lead to indirectness for the author. Kepler, as a Protestant, was persecuted by the categorical church; in protesting countries he was persecuted by his “co-religionists” as a heretic-conner.
