Note that four points A, B, A₁ and B₁ always lie in the same plane, because a ray of light passing through A and B, after refraction on the straight line l, will pass through A' and B' (Fig. 4, 6); therefore the lines AB and A₁B₁ also intersect. (If line AB is parallel to l, then, as is easy to understand, line A₁B₁ is also parallel to l.) Consequently, lines AA₁ and BB₁ are always either parallel or intersect. Let us assume that not all such lines are parallel, for example AA₁ intersects with BB₁ at point P. For any point C outside line AB, line CC₁ lies in the same plane with AA₁ and in the same plane with BB₁ (Fig. 5). Therefore, it is the line of intersection of these planes, and therefore passes through their common point P. For points lying on AB, the proof is carried out similarly, only the pair of points A, B must be replaced by A, C, where C does not lie on AB.

The proven statement can be reformulated as follows: the mapping p₁, the mapping of a point A to a plane α corresponding to a point A₁ to a plane α₁, is a parallel or central projection.
