4. Kolladan's task. Since the surface of the river is an inclined plane, one might think that moving a vessel upstream requires the same amount of work as sliding a body up an inclined plane. But one should not lose sight of the fact that the gravity of a ship floating in the water is balanced by the buoyant force of the water, so lifting the floating ship to a higher level does not require any force. The work of such a lift is zero; it should not be taken into account.

It is curious that of the students at the academy where the problem was proposed, only one gave the correct solution, who later became a famous railway engineer in France.

5. Water in an overturned glass. It is a mistake to believe that the glass contains only water and no air at all, since the paper is adjacent to the water closely. There is certainly air there. If there were no layer of air between two flat objects in contact, we could not lift a single thing from the table that rests on the table with a flat base: we would have to overcome atmospheric pressure. By covering the surface of the water with a piece of paper, we always have a thin layer of air between them.

Let's see what happens when the glass is turned upside down. Under heavy water, the paper protrudes slightly downward, so that some space under the bottom of the glass is freed up for the small amount of air that was between the water and the paper. This space is larger than the initial one; The air, therefore, becomes rarefied and its pressure drops.
