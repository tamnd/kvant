Zoy. It is clear, for example, that the image of any straight line will be a straight line (try to prove this using the non-presidential construction figure 8!). True, strictly speaking, this property is satisfied with one exception: the straight line passing through the focus $F$ perpendicular to the main axis is not depicted in any way, because the rays emanating from any of its points are converted by the lens into parallel ones. We will return to this important remark later.

How are the distances $r=OA$ and $r'=OA'$ related from the center of the lens $O$ to the point $A$ on the main axis and to its image $A'$? And here the answer is easily obtained using the central projection. From figure 9 it is clear that

$$\frac{OF}{OA} + \frac{OF'}{OA'} = \frac{OF}{OA} + \frac{OF_1}{OA_1} =$$

$$= \frac{A_1P}{A'A} + \frac{PA}{A'A} = \frac{A'A}{A'A} = 1,$$

i.e.

$$\frac{f}{r} + \frac{f'}{r'} = 1.$$

In particular, at $f=f'$ the so-called “lens formula” $1/r+1/r'=1/f$ is satisfied. These equalities remain in force for the point $A$ on the segment $OF$ and on the ray $OF'$, if we consider all segments to be directed, and the quantities $r$ and $r'$ are the coordinates of the points $A$ and $A'$ on the main axis with the beginning at the point $O$. In this case, a positive result was obtained
