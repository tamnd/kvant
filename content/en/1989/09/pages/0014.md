The Admiral's Riddle

Let's consider a problem that, at first glance, has nothing to do with the topic of our conversation.

It is known that the flight of many insects is accompanied by some kind of sound. Remember the characteristic rustle of the wings of a leaking dragonfly, the buzzing of bees, the peak of a mosquito that does not evoke pleasant emotions, etc. Sounds arise from the flapping flight of insects, and the source of sound is most often the vibration of insects’ wings in the air. He asks: why do many butterflies (but not all!) fly silently, although they also “flap” their wings?
