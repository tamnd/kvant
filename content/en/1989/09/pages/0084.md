On this page we provide two ways to fold a cube from a paper square without stripping and glue. The first methods are presented by the author of origami already familiar to us - the model of terrahedra and octahedron (see «Kvant» No. 7 and No. 8) by Professor K. Haga. A distinctive feature of his model is the absence of ssyb lines on their surface. Figure 1 is the initial “marking” of the square, on which, as in previous publications, all the ssyb lines are indicated, with the red color meaning “ridges” and the blue color meaning “uchelya”. In the first step, the square turns into a rectangular angle (Fig. 2). At further stages, the model alters the color lines of each successive figure. For control, those squares of the original size that form the surface of the cube in the test are marked with asterisks. Squares with diazonal folds turn into triangular flaps (Fig. 4, 5), left in line with the pockets.

The second model was invented by S. Fujimota. We present only the “marking” (Fig. 7) and the final form of the cube (Fig. 8). We are sure that you will appreciate what you have learned about this design if you manage to restore the folding sequence. (Hint: the first fold is along the middle line AA, then along lines 1 and then along lines 2, after which line DABCD is folded into a square. At the last stage, triangular flaps are folded from the squares of the bottom row, which, intertwined, form the base of the cube.)
