Physics and technical creativity

All our lives we have been dealing with physics. Already in early childhood, we are attracted to rainbows, frozen rays, frosty patterns on glass, steam from the mouth, etc. All this causes surprise, admiration and a lot of “why?” Then comes the endless “how?” How to make a birdhouse? How to fix a bicycle? How are houses built? How are cars made? Over time, we have learned that consistent answers to “why?” natural sciences, in particular physics, give answers to the questions “how?” technical answers.

In engineering, as in physics, the same problem can be solved in different ways. And often the solution to a technical problem is suggested by “number” physical laws. We will call the search for optimal principles of completely new technical solutions technical creativity. (In this sense, we do not consider building a model of the famous icebreaker or assembling a receiver from ready-made parts technical creativity.)

Perhaps at this point the reader will decide: “I am not denunciating technical creativity (in this definition). When I finish school, let’s see then.” Don't rush to conclusions! Many creative tasks that should be “too tough” for people with diplomas can be solved with only school knowledge. The fact is that, according to academician S.P. Kapitsa, knowledge related to various disciplines lies in a person’s head, as if in different boxes. Using this analogy, we can say that the successful solution of a problem is largely determined by the ability to use knowledge from different boxes at the same time. And this skill does not come on its own; it must be developed, added. One of the ways to “do it” is to read popular articles and books about the application of physics in technology. But this is not enough. Many such publications suffer from “problemlessness” - they immediately provide a ready-made answer, and learning from them how to apply physics in technology is as difficult as learning to solve problems by reading solutions. You have to solve problems yourself. That’s why in the ruble notes “Any idea?!” we will “post” questions and answers. A number of problems will be offered for independent solution. Under no circumstances should you start searching for a solution by randomly searching through various options or inventing cumbersome devices. First of all, you need to understand what needs to be done, and then talk about what physical effect you know can be used to achieve the desired result. The problem is considered solved if the basic idea is found, i.e. you have found the same physical coefficient.

This is clearly stated by the example of a problem with a solution. Once upon a time, a young man came to the great American inventor Thomas Alev Edison who wanted to work in his laboratory. Edison took the example of new employees very seriously: he examined them, asked them about their interests. So, it turned out that the young man dreams of inventing a universal disintegrator, that is, a substance capable of dissolving everything in the world. “Where are you going to store your rastorigel?” - Edison was surprised.

Would such a remark “sober” you? But a reader of Pionerskaya Pravda, which published this problem several years ago, found a solution:

You need to freeze the universal rastorigel and store it in a frozen state.
