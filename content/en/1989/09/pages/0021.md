"Stop! - the captain ordered, and the submarine immediately stopped in place... - Aim! - Sound!...

In the first minute, nothing changed in the appearance of the cresser. The ultrasonic gun worked only at five-tenths of its power. Suddenly... the middle of the underwater part of the cresser began to stretch, spread apart, like clay. Only one minute after the start of the ultrasonic attack, the middle of the side of the ship facing the submarine unexpectedly and immediately pressed inside it, then suddenly, like a huge bubble, it burst, and a giant stream of water rushed into the triomes, the engine room, and the artillery magazines.”

We have given an excerpt from some popular science fiction novel by Gr. Adamov "The Secret of Two Oceans". (Perhaps some of our readers will better remember the two-part adventure film with the same name.) The hero of this work traveled around the world on board the experimental submarine “Pioneer” - a miracle of Soviet military technology. Both the submarine and the divers in autonomous suits are armed with ultrasonic weapons, which have more than once helped them out in critical situations. Using powerful ultrasound, the aquanauts tried to destroy the rock that blocked the exit from the underwater cave; immediately a huge sperm whale, which almost drowned the whalers who harpooned it; destroyed the enemy cresser and even... fantastic sea wonders, similar to one of the crew members of the submarine...

Perhaps the reader will find the attention that the author paid to the guards and the pursuit of veneers unnecessary, but let us remember that the novel was published shortly before the start of the war. And then one could still think that the use of ultrasound as a weapon was real, that it was a technical issue that could be resolved over time. However...

In this article we will introduce the reader to some readers of physics.

Adamov’s novel is a purely fantastic work). As for the “peaceful” use of powerful actic waves, the situation is different: non-destructive testing methods, parametric emitter and antennas, ultrasonic cleaning of surfaces, drilling holes, breaking kidney stones are just some of their applications.

Over the past twenty years, there has been a colossal leap in the understanding of how large amplitude waves in general and actic waves in particular behave. What do we know about powerful action waves today?

The magazine «Kvant» has already talked about interesting phenomena accompanied by the propagation of large waves on the surface of the ocean (see article by L. A. Ostrovsky in No. 8, 1987). Let us recall: effects that appear only for very strong waves and depend on their amplitude are called nonlinear effects. A field of science has already emerged that studies these phenomena—the physics of nonlinear wave processes. The branch of physics that studies powerful actic waves is called “nonlinear actics.” This science occupies a special place in physics in terms of the breadth of objects studied. Its waves studied arise in gases, liquids, solids, and plasma. They exist in natural environments - in the atmosphere, in the ocean, in soils, in objects of cosmic scale; examples include thunder, underwater explosions, seismic waves from earthquakes, and many more.

The reader may have questions. The first question: which sound should be considered strong (powerful) and which weak, where are the number of boundaries of this division?
