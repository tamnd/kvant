KEPLER'S THIRD LAW: the squares of the times of formation of the planets are related as the cubes of their distances from the Sun (more precisely, as the cubes of the semi-major axes of the orbits).

So, we remembered all three of Kepler's laws. The first law talks about the orbits of all planets, the second compares the passage of different parts of the orbit by one planet, the third gives a correspondence between the orbits of different planets. Now let’s try to use these laws.

The next two tasks will be associated with Mars. Tycho Brahe studied the motion of this planet in the most detail, and it was for Mars that Kepler first established his first two laws. In general, Mars attracts people's attention more than other planets. And when, during the Great Confrontation of 1877, the Italian astronomer Schipaprelli discovered “canals” on Mars, the still unresolved question always arose: is there life on Mars? The Englishman Lovell suggested that this is a real artificial structure - channels broken through by intelligent inhabitants of Mars. A great enthusiast of searching for evidence of the existence of life on Mars, albeit only plant life, was the Soviet astronomer G. A. Tikhov. Sim cards received from artificial satellites of Mars have finally dispelled the myth about the existence of canals. The study of Martian soil did not give a definite result - there are no organisms, but the body seems to have substances. It is no coincidence that recently the possibility of joint compatibility on Mars has been widely discussed.
