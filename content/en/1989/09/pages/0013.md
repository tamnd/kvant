If the film is developed under standard conditions, then it is possible to find the absolute value of the light flux from the radiation source at a given point (albeit with a certain error). By selecting the film sensitivity, the developer, the frame exposure time, the aperture size and additional light filters, you can photograph and study a very wide class of objects (for example, carry out a night panoramic light of an area or take a photograph of the dazzlingly bright sun).

Common amateur photographic film is a relatively sensitive light receiver. With exposures of the order of 0.01-0.1 l/s or more, it is already possible to photograph. Therefore, night photography on highly sensitive photographic film for long durations (say, ~100 s) can replace observations using a so-called night vision device, i.e., a very complex device - an electronic axis of literal rage.

N. Niens received the first photographic positive (around 1826). Having placed a plate coated with bitumen “emulsion” in a pinhole camera, he exposed it for a day and then processed it in lavender oil. The record captured the view from the workshop window.

J. Daser developed the first practical photographic process ten years later. The essence of Daser's image is based on his discovery of the effectiveness of the manifestation of a cardiac plate coated with cardiac iodide in a pair of others, previously exposed in a camera obscura.

F. Talbot depicted a negative-positive photograph around the same time. He obtained an image in a camera obscura on a paper plate impregnated with a light-sensitive solvent.
