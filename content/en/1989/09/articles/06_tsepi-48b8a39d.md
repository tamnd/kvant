The principle of “clinging” a subsequent element to a previous one is often encountered in mathematics, for example, in “continued fractions”. Any positive fraction can be written, and in a unique way, in the form:

$$\frac{p}{q} = a_0 + \frac{1}{a_1 + \frac{1}{a_2 + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n}}},$$

where the numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are natural numbers, and $a_0$ is a non-negative integer. For example,

$$\frac{13}{7} = 1 + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{6}};$$

$$\frac{22}{7} = 3 + \frac{1}{7},$$

$$\frac{355}{113} = 3 + \frac{1}{7 + \frac{1}{16}}.$$

All irrational numbers are also expressed as whole fractions. So,

$$\sqrt{2} = 1 + \frac{1}{2 + \frac{1}{2 + \dots}}$$

$$\frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} = 1 + \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1}{1 + \dots}}$$

$$\pi = 3 + \frac{1}{7 + \frac{1}{15 + \frac{1}{1 + \dots}}}.$$
