And in scientific matters, Brahe and Kepler did not agree. Brahe was a proponent of a compromise theory of the structure of the solar system: the Sun revolves around the stationary Earth, and the planets revolve around the Sun. The Copernican era is treated with disdain. Brahe had a violent temperament: it is known that in a duel, the cause of which was scientific disagreement, he lost his nose. Therefore, one can imagine how the debate between the ardent Brahe and the stubborn and proud Kepler proceeded.

Kepler endured a difficult struggle with himself, with his own views. After all, everything went well at first! In the sphere on which the (circular) orbit of Saturn lies, we inscribe a cube. Let's inscribe a sphere into this cube - the orbit of Jupiter lies on it. We inscribe a tetrahedron into the sphere of Jupiter, and a sphere into it - we get the sphere of Mars, and so on, until the regular polyhedra and at the same time planets are exhausted. The “Cosmogtical Mystery” (this is the title of Kepler’s book!) The Harmony of the Spheres has been revealed! Pythagoras was envious!

But... eight angular minutes of discrepancy between the representative and observed positions of Mars forced Kepler to open up from the idea of ​​uniform motion of planets in circular orbits, illuminated not even by thousands of years. The role of Tycho Brahe cannot be underestimated: he was the second in the world - after Ulugbek - to achieve exactly the changes in two minutes, practically the limit for the naked eye. If the accuracy of his changes had been lower, Kepler would not have been able to distinguish an ellipse from a circle.

These eight minutes... give us the means to transform astronomy.

(I. Kepler)

And the “harmony of the spheres” collapsed. KEPLER'S FIRST LAW was born: planets move in ellipses, with the Sun at one of the foci.

And besides - KEPLER'S SECOND LAW - a line connecting the Sun and the planet describes equal areas in equal periods of time.

Johannes Kepler published these two laws in 1609. However, the work “New Astronomy”, in which they were proclaimed, was published by a “happy” accident. Strictly speaking, the work belonged to the Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire of the German Nation, Rudolf II, by whose order it was executed. But the emperor was unable to pay the costs of printing the book, and it came into the hands of the author. But Kepler was once again left without a livelihood - books in those days did not bring in income, especially if they proclaimed the heretical teaching of Keprnik. Such books could lead to indirectness for the author. Kepler, as a Protestant, was persecuted by the categorical church; in protesting countries he was persecuted by his “co-religionists” as a heretic-conner.

KEPLER'S THIRD LAW: the squares of the times of formation of the planets are related as the cubes of their distances from the Sun (more precisely, as the cubes of the semi-major axes of the orbits).

So, we remembered all three of Kepler's laws. The first law talks about the orbits of all planets, the second compares the passage of different parts of the orbit by one planet, the third gives a correspondence between the orbits of different planets. Now let’s try to use these laws.

Let's draw a picture of such a satellite (Fig. 1). The next two tasks will be associated with Mars. Tycho Brahe studied the motion of this planet in the most detail, and it was for Mars that Kepler first established his first two laws. In this solution we assumed that the orbital planes of the Earth and Mars coincide. This is quite justified, since in reality the corresponding angle is less than $2^\circ$. So what year is this comet: 1680 or 1682? The fact is that comets are often double dated: by the time of first observation and by the time of passage of perihelion. For periodic comets, which include Halley’s comet, the second system is more often used, which is why Halley in 1680 saw the comet of 1682, and Calixtus III in 1453 first blessed, and then cleared, and then cleared the comet of 1456.

Fortunately, Halley did not limit himself to stating the curvilinearity of the comet's path. He agreed that the comets of 1607 and 1531 were the same comet of 1682, and predicted its appearance in 1758.

Problem 5. Comet Griese - Skjellerup belongs to the Jupiter group, its aphelion is located at a distance of 5.0 AU. e. from the Sun. The comet's orbital period is 4.9 years. can this comet cross the earth's orbit?

The calculation (which we provide to the reader) gives for the minimum distance of the comet from the Sun a value of 0.77 AU. e., i.e. the comet approaches the Sun closer than the Earth. So, in principle, comet Grieg-Skjellerup can cross the Earth’s orbit.

However, as already mentioned, the orbits of comets can have a noticeable inclination to the ecliptic plane. In this case, the intersection of the projections of the orbits of the planet and the comet onto this plane does not guarantee the intersection of the orbits themselves.

10. Rainbow. A rainbow is visible only when the height of the Sun above the horizon does not exceed 42°. Meanwhile, the altitude of the midday Sun at the latitude of Moscow on the day of the summer solnarium (July 22) is 57.5°. This means that the Sun was higher in the sky than necessary for a rainbow to appear.

11. Candle in a closed jar. The explanation of the experience is incorrect. Instead of the oxygen consumed during combustion, carbon dioxide appears - one molecule of carbon dioxide for every molecule of oxygen. However, the same number of molecules always occupies the same volume at equal pressure (Avogadro's law). This means that oxygen consumption by itself cannot change the volume of gas in the jar.

The true cause of the observed phenomenon is different - not chemical, but physical. The air inside the jar actually becomes rarefied when the candle burns, but not due to oxygen consumption, but due to heating. Part of the expanding gas is removed outside until the pressure of the cold outside air and the warm inner can is equal. When the candle goes out due to lack of oxygen, the air in the jar cools, its pressure decreases, and excess external pressure presses the lid.
