THREE RARE GENRES

Among the unusual tasks and puzzles related to fairytale chess, there are quite rare, but very witty genres. Let's get acquainted with the crown of them.

Tsiree

The peculiarity of this original fairy-tale genre is the following: after capturing an enemy piece, it is not removed from the board, but returns to the place it occupied in the original arrangement of pieces. Rooks and knights return to the squares of the color on which they were captured, and pawns return to the original square of the vertical on which they were captured. However, if the square to which the captured piece should move is occupied, then it leaves the board as usual.

N. McLeod, 1978
Checkmate in 2 moves (circe).

It seems that White mates in one move, and in two ways - 1. Q:b3× or 1. Cg4×. But not everything is so simple...

1. Q: b3 (knight b3 leaves the board, since the g8 square is occupied by the black rook) - checkmate? Defense 1... K:d2 is impossible - the captured bishop returns to c1, and the black king is in check when White moves. However, Black has another clever defense - 1... N:c7!, and the white pawn appearing on c2 closes the a4 - d1 diagonal. It’s as if White is still adding to his goal - 2. c2 - c3 with checkmate, but after 2... b4: c3! A white pawn appears on c2 again.

1. Cg4 does not solve either. The refutation is 1... C:e3! On e2 the white pawn is restored and there is no checkmate either on the first move or on the second, since the e2 pawn cannot move.

The quiet move 1. c7 - c8Л leads to the goal! Now the c-pawn has disappeared from the board and is threatening 2. Q:b3×. Black responds with 1... Rxc8, while a white rook appears on the h1 square. Capture 2. Q:b3 is countered with 2... R:c5!, and again a white pawn appears on c2. But now 2 is mate. Cg4× - unexpectedly the h1 rook comes into play: the black bishop is pinned and there is no answer 2... B:e3. But doesn't Black collapse with 1... B:e3 2. Q:b3 B:c5!? In this case, the effective solution is 2. Rg7 - g1× - Black cannot take the rook g1 with either the bishop or the rooks - then a white rook appears on square a1, and the black king is in check, again when White moves. By the way, immediately 1. Rxg1+ does not work due to the return of the bishop to f8, and the white king is already in check.

It remains to say that 1. c8Q(K) gives nothing because of 1... Rxc8 and 1. c8C? because of 1... B:e3!

Lattice board

In this interesting genre of fairy-tale chess, the docks are divided into 16 squares of four fields each. The rules are simple: a piece loses its properties within the square in which it stands (it cannot walk and does not attack enemy pieces), it only acts when moved to another square of the board.

E. Visserman, 1955
Checkmate in 2 moves on a lattice board.

In this position, the black king cannot move to h5 or h6 (these squares are in the same square as the king), and the b2 pawn is promoted to the left when capturing a piece on the c1 square (connected square!).

1. Cd7! The threat is 2. Cf6× and there is no protection. On 1... Cf3 solves 2. Ke4×! — the bishop f3 is in the same square as the knight and has no right to capture it. On 1... Qe4, connecting with the womb of d4, follows 2. Kf3× (the queen blocked the path to the bishop b7 - an idea that is not feasible in a general two-move problem).

Other options: 1... Lis6 2. Rg2×!; 1... Qf3(c6) 2. h4×; 1... Rg4 2. Rf5×. The knight on g6 was left unprotected in normal play, but on the lattice board it is unsound.

Frankfurt chess

In this variation of fairy chess, the piece that captures transforms into the piece that captures (without changing color).

N. Bakke, 1986

Cooperative checkmate in 2 moves (Frankfurt chess).

As you know, in a cooperative problem, black starts and helps the opponent checkmate. 1. 0—0—0! fe. The e5 square is now occupied by the white bishop. 2. Ke7+ de×! — an effective mat was announced by the knight, placing e7 on the square.

It’s interesting that this problem has a very attractive sibling. When moving the rook from a8 to h8, the short move already decides: 1. 0-0! hg - white rook on g3, 2. Qh5+! gh× - there is a white queen on h5, and the black king is mate. It's funny that the white pawns in the last diagram are transformed into all possible pieces, and at quite a large distance from the extreme rank.
