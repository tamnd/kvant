Relative movement
As is known, the movement of any body is relative, i.e. its movement, speed, type of trajectory depends on the reporting system (s.o.) in which this movement is considered. When describing motion, you can use different reporting systems, stationary or moving.
To move from one system to another part, it is enough to write down the law of addition of displacements or

speed addition con:
$$\vec{s}_1 = \vec{s}_{12} + \vec{s}_2, \quad \vec{v}_1 = \vec{v}_{12} + \vec{v}_2.$$ (1)

Here $\vec{s}_1(\vec{v}_1)$ is the displacement (velocity) of the body relative to the first s. o., for example, at rest, $\vec{s}_{12}(\vec{v}_{12})$ - displacement (velocity) of the body relative to the second s. o., for example moving, $\vec{s}_2(\vec{v}_2)$ - movement (speed) of the second s. O. relative to the first one. Thus, the speed of the aircraft relative to the ground is the speed of the aircraft relative to the air plus the speed of the impact itself:
$$\vec{a}_1 = \vec{a}_{12} + \vec{a}_2,$$ (2)

where $\vec{a}_1$ is the acceleration of the body relative to rest c. o., $\vec{a}_{12}$ - acceleration of the body

relative to the moving s. o., $\vec{a}_2$ is the acceleration of this s. O.

Formulas (1) and (2) can be written in a more symmetrical and convenient form to directly express the relative movement (velocity, acceleration) of two bodies:

$$\vec{s}_{12} = \vec{s}_1 - \vec{s}_2, \quad \vec{v}_{12} = \vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2,$$

$$\vec{a}_{12} = \vec{a}_1 - \vec{a}_2.$$

For example, the speed of a boat relative to the water is the speed of the boat minus the speed of the water (current speed):

$$\vec{v}_{\pi B} = \vec{v}_{\pi} - \vec{v}_{\pi B}.$$

Note that everything said is true for the translational motion of one s. O. relative to another (the coordinate axes of motion of the coordinate system are always parallel to the coordinate axes of the stationary system). In addition, the speeds of all bodies must be small compared to the speed of light $c = 300\ 000$ km/s, otherwise the laws of the relative Eischstein theory come into force, which differ from those presented above.

Let us now pose the following question: why do we need to change the reporting system at all? Isn’t it more convenient, in order to avoid confusion, to once and for all choose one? It turns out that this is not the case, and several reasons can be given for this.

Firstly, in many cases we are simply forced to involve the second s. O. - without this, it is impossible to solve the problem. Let's take an example of an airplane flying in windy weather. Instruments that record the course chosen by the pilot show how the axis of the aircraft body is located in relation to the magnetic needle of the compass, and the speed of the aircraft is changed along the object plane by the air flow. The meaning of these changes becomes clear and unambiguous if you go to the reporting system associated with the impact; they determine the direction and magnitude of the aircraft’s speed in this sec.
