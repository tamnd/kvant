M1173*. Three lines are drawn through one point inside a triangle of area S so that each side of the triangle is intersected by two of them (Fig. 1). Prove that the areas $S_1, S_2, S_3$ of three educational triangles satisfy the inequality

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{9}{S}$$

We will prove two inequalities that strengthen the inequality of the problem in different ways.

1. Let us show that the inequality of the problem is valid for any three triangles with a common vertex O inside a given triangle ABC, the bases of which lie on its sides (Fig. 2).

It is clear that for a given point O, the smallest value of the left side of the inequality will be achieved for triangles OAB, OBC and OCA. Then $S = S_1 + S_2 + S_3$ and due to the inequality $a + 1/a \geq 2$ at $a > 0$

$$\frac{S}{S_1} + \frac{S}{S_2} + \frac{S}{S_3} = 3 + \left( \frac{S_2}{S_1} + \frac{S_1}{S_2} \right) + \left( \frac{S_3}{S_1} + \frac{S_1}{S_3} \right) + \left( \frac{S_3}{S_2} + \frac{S_2}{S_3} \right) \geq 3 + 3 \cdot 2 = 9$$

Equality is achieved when $S_1 = S_2 = S_3$, i.e. O is the point of intersection of the medians.
