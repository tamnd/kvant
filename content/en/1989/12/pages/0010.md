Anyone who wishes to use a hyperbolide can follow the path of composing an equation; the equation of our hyperbolod in a suitable coordinate system has the form $x^2+y^2=r^2+a^2z^2$, where $r$ and $a$ depend on the size of the object, the lengths of the surfaces and the angle of rotation. For those who do not want to bother with proofs, I will say another thing, one that does not need proof, does not need existence: our hyperbolid is also a surface of rotation of a straight line relative to another straight line intersecting with it (Fig. 7).

Two families. We already know that our hyperbolide consists of straight lines. By the way, in mathematics, a straight line that contains its entirety in a non-flat surface is called its *rectilinear image* (or simply *image*). A surface through each point of which a rectilinear image passes is called *ruled*. In particular, our hyperbolid is a ruled surface, but in fact more is true: it is a *double-faced* surface, i.e., two different rectilinear shapes pass through each point.
