strong inequality:

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{18}{S}.$$

Let's connect the ends of these segments so that three more triangles are formed (Fig. 3); let their areas be equal to $T_1, T_2, T_3$. Expressing the areas of all six triangles in terms of their sides extending from the vertex $O$ and the angles between these sides, we easily obtain that $S_1S_2S_3 = T_1T_2T_3$. Now let’s take advantage of the fact that the arithmetic mean of several numbers is not less than their geometric mean:

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{3}{\sqrt[3]{S_1S_2S_3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt[6]{S_1S_2S_3T_1T_2T_3}} \geq \frac{3 \cdot 6}{S_1 + S_2 + S_3 + T_1 + T_2 + T_3} \geq \frac{18}{S}$$

(the total area of ​​all six triangles is, of course, no more than $S$). Equality here is sufficient when $S_1 = S_2 = S_3 = T_1 = T_2 = T_3 = S/6$, that is, when the given segments are medians of the triangle (Fig. 4).

**M1174*. The sequence of integers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots$ is given by the conditions $a_1=1, a_2=12, a_3=20, a_{n+3}=2a_{n+2}+2a_{n+1}-a_n$ ($n=1, 2, \dots$).

Prove that for any number $n$ the number $1+4a_na_{n+1}$ is the square of an integer.

| $a_n$ | $4a_na_{n+1}+1$ | $q_n$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 49 | 7 |
| 12 | 961 | 31 |
| 20 | 5041 | 71 |
| 63 | 39409 | 197 |
| 154 | . . . | . . |

By writing out the first few terms of the sequence, as done in the margin, we can prove that $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$, where $q_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n$; for example, with $n=1$ and $n=2$ the following equalities are obtained: $1+4 \cdot 12=(20-12-1)^2, 1+4 \cdot 12 \cdot 20=(63-20-12)^2$.

Let us use the induction method: we will prove that the assumption $1+4a_{n-1}a_n=q_n^2$- implies the equality $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$ (for $n=2, 3, \dots$). As follows from the problem conditions, $q_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n=a_{n+1}+a_n-a_{n-1}=q_{n-1}+2a_n$. That's why

$$1+4a_{n-1}a_n=q_{n-1}^2=(q_n-2a_n)^2=q_n^2-4a_n(a_{n+1}+a_n-a_{n-1})+4a_n^2=q_n^2+4a_na_{n-1}-4a_na_{n+1},$$

whence $1+4a_na_{n+1}=q_n^2$.

From our solution it is clear that for any initial terms for the sequence with the concurrent condition $a_{n+2}+a_n=2(a_{n+1}+a_n)$ the value $(a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}-a_n)^2-4a_na_{n+1}$ is constant and does not depend on $n$.

N. V. Vasiliev
