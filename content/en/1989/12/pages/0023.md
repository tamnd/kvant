In the end, if all this happens very slowly, quasi-statically, we are actually at each moment simply holding the swing in the position in which it is currently located. A complete analogy with the statistical model of tides is a deviation in phase with an external force.

But if we try to “grow” the swing so that the direction of the force changes five times per second, everything will be different. The swing was just about to move to the left, where we pulled it in the previous moment, but we only moved it to the right. It is not possible to immediately move them to the right, we must first slow down their “left bias”, then they will pick up the “right” speed, and we are already pulling them to the left again. Here it is more difficult to monitor the correspondence of phases with force and deviation, but it is very likely that now these two quantities are in antiphase.
