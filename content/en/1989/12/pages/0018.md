Sturm's theorem

The time has come to fulfill the promise made at the beginning of the article and talk about how to determine the number of roots of any polynomial $f(x)$. In order to enumerate the roots of a polynomial, we construct a kind of counter. As the variable $x$ moves along the number line, this counter will equalize each time $x$ passes through the root of the polynomial. Let us construct a sequence of polynomials of killing degree $p_0(x), p_1(x), p_2(x), \dots, p_n(x)$. This sequence will have the third properties:

1) $p_0(x)=f(x), p_1(x)=f'(x)$;
2) if $p_k(x)=0$ for some $x$, then the numbers $p_{k-1}(x)$ and $p_{k+1}(x)$ are different from zero and have different signs;
3) the last polynomial $p_n(x)$ has no roots at all.

Such a sequence of polynomials is called *Sturm series**. We see how to build the Sturm row below. For now, let’s assume that we already have such a series.

We want to determine the number of roots of the polynomial $f(x)$ on the interval $(a; b)$ (it is possible that $a=\infty$ or $b=\infty$). Let's start moving from point $a$ to point $b$ and (using Descartes' rules!) we will monitor the changes in signs in the sequence of numbers

$p_0(x), p_1(x), p_2(x), \dots, p_n(x)$.

The signs can change only if $x$ passes through the root of one of the polynomials $p_i(x)$. Let's see how such an event affects the number of variables in sequence (3). If $x$ is the root of the original polynomial $f(x)=p_0(x)$, then, as can be seen from figure 3 and 4, one of two things happens: at the beginning of the sequence, the signs $(- +)$ change to $(+ +)$ or $(+ -)$ to $(- -)$. In both cases, the number of sign changes decreases by one. If $x$ passes through the root of one of the polynomials $p_k(x)$, where $0<k<n$, then, in accordance with property 2 of the Sturm series, the signs of the numbers $p_{k-1}(x)$ and $p_{k+1}(x)$ are different. Therefore, changing the sign of $p_k(x)$ does not change the number of sign changes in sequence (3). It looks like this:

$(- + +) \leftrightarrow (- - +)$ or $(+ + -) \leftrightarrow (+ - -)$.
