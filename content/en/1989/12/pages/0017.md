Descartes' rule

The statement in the previous paragraph that the $k$-term has at most $2k-1$ roots becomes too general if we are only interested in positive roots (for example, any polynomial with non-negative coefficients has no positive roots at all!). More accurate is the following Descartes rule:

the number of positive roots of a polynomial is not greater than the number of alternating signs in the sequence of its (non-zero) coefficients.

In particular, a polynomial that has $k$ nonzero coefficients cannot have more than $k-1$ positive roots - after all, in the sequence of its $k$ nonzero coefficients, of course, there are no more than $k-1$ sign changes.
