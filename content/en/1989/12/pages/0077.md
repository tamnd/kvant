Task 1
a) Boiling occurs when the saturated vapor pressure is equal to atmospheric pressure: $p_i = p_0 \Rightarrow \ln(p_i/p_0) = 0$. From formula $\ln(p_i/p_0) = a_i/T + b_i$ for the boiling point we obtain

$$T_{\kappa i} = -a_i/b_i.$$ (*)

The numerical values ​​of $a_i$ and $b_i$ can be obtained from the table:
$$a_A = -3748 \text{ K}, a_B = -5122 \text{ K}, b_A = 1072, b_B = 13,73.$$

Finally

$$T_{\kappa A} \approx 350 \text{ K}, T_{\kappa B} \approx 373 \text{ K}.$$

b) Initially (the first slope on the original graph), the liquids heat up. There is no evaporation from the surface. The distance of the bubble formed is directed by fluctuations, either the internal liquid $A$, or the internal liquid $B$, or at their boundary. If it arises inside $A$, then the pressure in it is $p = p_A(T)$, if inside $B$, then $p = p_B(T)$, and if at the border, then $p = p_A(T) + p_B(T)$. Let at some temperature $T_x$
$$p_A(T_x) + p_B(T_x) = p_0.$$

In this case, $p_A(T_x) < p_0$ and $p_B(T_x) < p_0$, i.e., the bubble in $A$ and $B$ will disappear, but not at the boundary. This bubble begins to rise, should reach the surface of the liquid liquid, and borderline boiling will be observed. Temperature $T_x$ is found from the condition

$$p_A(T_x) + p_B(T_x) = p_0 \Rightarrow e^{a_A/T_x + b_A} + e^{a_B/T_x + b_B} = 1.$$

Solving this equation accurately is quite difficult. But since the conditions require an accuracy of 1 K, this equation can be solved by the positional division method using a calculator. We get $T_x = 340$ K. During such boiling, $T = \text{const}$, which means that the first horizontal section and $T_1 = T_x = 340$ K correspond to borderline boiling. A bubble with a volume of $V$ carries away a mass of $m_A$ substance $A$ and $m_B$ substance $B$:

$$\frac{p_A V}{T_x} = \frac{m_A}{M_A} R, \frac{p_B V}{T_x} = \frac{m_B}{M_B} R \Rightarrow \frac{m_A}{m_B} = \frac{p_A}{p_B} \frac{M_A}{M_B}.$$

Substituting $T_x$ into equation (*), we obtain the value of $p_A/p_B$ and find $m_A/m_B = 22,4$, i.e., the mass of $A$ decreases 22.4 times faster than the mass of $B$. When the substance $A$ runs out, part of the substance $B$ will boil away, and part remains:

$$\Delta m_B = m_B/m_A, m'_B = m(1 - m_B/m_A) = 95,5 \text{ г}.$$

Now the boundary boiling has ended, and the next inclined section corresponds to the cutting of the substance $B$ to its boiling temperature. The amount of substance $B$ does not change, and thus at the moment $\tau_1$ $m_A = 0, m'_B = 95,5 \text{ г}$. It is clear that $T_2$ is the boiling point of the substance $B$, i.e. $T_2 \approx 373$ K.

Task 2
Let us consider the movement of these bodies in the inertial reporting system associated with the $o$ axis (it exists because the system of bodies is isolated). Since the configuration of the bodies does not change, according to the law of conservation of moment momentum $\omega = \text{const}$. Let us denote the vectors $\vec{P}_1\vec{P}_2 = a_{12}, \vec{P}_1\vec{P}_3 = a_{13}, \vec{P}_1\vec{O} = \vec{R}_1$, where $O$ is the center of mass (Fig. 9). By definition of the center of mass

$$\vec{R}_1 = \frac{1}{M} (m_3\vec{a}_{13} + m_2\vec{a}_{12}),$$

where $M = m_1 + m_2 + m_3$. Let's consider the mass $m_1$. Its acceleration is

$$a_1 = \omega^2 \vec{R}_1 = \frac{\omega^2}{M} (m_3\vec{a}_{13} + m_2\vec{a}_{12}).$$

The forces acting on $m_1$ from $m_2$ and $m_3$ are equal to the corresponding

$$\vec{F}_{12} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{a_{12}^2} \vec{a}_{12}, \vec{F}_{13} = G \frac{m_1 m_3}{a_{13}^2} \vec{a}_{13}.$$

According to Newton's second law

$$\vec{F}_{12} + \vec{F}_{13} = m_1\vec{a}_1,$$

i.e.

$$\frac{m_2}{a_{12}^2} \vec{a}_{12} + \frac{m_3}{a_{13}^2} \vec{a}_{13} = \frac{\omega^2}{GM} m_2\vec{a}_{12} + \frac{\omega^2}{GM} m_3\vec{a}_{13}.$$

Since the points $P_1, P_2$ and $P_3$ do not lie on the same line, the vectors $a_{12}$ and $a_{13}$ are non-clipnear, and the previous equality implies

$$\frac{m_2}{a_{12}^2} \vec{a}_{12} = \frac{\omega^2}{GM} m_2\vec{a}_{12}, \frac{m_3}{a_{13}^2} \vec{a}_{13} = \frac{\omega^2}{GM} m_3\vec{a}_{13},$$

where

$$a_{12} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{\omega^2}}, a_{13} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{\omega^2}}.$$

Considering the points $P_2$ or $P_3$, by analogy we obtain

$$a_{23} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{\omega^2}}.$$

So, the necessary condition is the following equality:

$$a_{12} = a_{13} = a_{23} = \sqrt{\frac{G}{\omega^2}} (m_1 + m_2 + m_3).$$
