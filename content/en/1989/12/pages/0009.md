STRAIGHT ON CURVED SURFACES

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
D. V. FUCHS

There are surfaces that look like curved scales, but nevertheless contain solid straight lines. In this article we will talk about those surfaces on which there are a lot of these straight lines.

The article consists of two parts; the first is in front of you, and the second will be published in one of the upcoming issues of the magazine. In the first part we will perform several interesting geometric experiments, the second is addressed to lovers of more serious mathematics: in it, among other things, we will try to survey all surfaces through each point of which two straight lines lying in them pass.

Part one:
hyperbolide of the Master Homemade Man
Surface made of shoe cords.
Take two identical objects and tie them with a dozhina of cords of the same length, as shown in figure 1. If you lift the resulting structure by one hoop, holding this hoop horizontally, then the cords will form a surface - a straight circular cylinder. If after this, holding the cords taut, you turn one hoop relative to the other (at an angle not very small, but not very large either - say, about four turns), then the cords will form a new surface - see Fig. figure 2. We will now discuss in detail the structure of this surface, but we will immediately notice the main thing: it is so curved that it is simply difficult to believe that it is made of perfectly straight cords.

This surface is called *zunep-bolouodom*, and here's why. If we look at it from the side (it’s better said if we project it onto a plane), then we will see a figure enclosed between two winds of the hyperbola (Fig. 3). In addition, our surface can be obtained by rotating the hyperbola around one of its symmetry axes (the one that does not intersect it) - see figure 4. By intersecting our surface with different planes, we can also obtain a hyperbola (Fig. 5, a, b), as well as an ellipse (Fig. 5, c), paraboly (Fig. 5, e), a pair of intersecting lines (Fig. 5, e). Nevertheless, our surface has a lot in common and deserves the name “hyperbolide”. However, it is referred to as a one-sheet hyperboloid to distinguish it from a two-sheet hyperboloid
