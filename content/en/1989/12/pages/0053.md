What was striking was not even the number of books, which corresponds to the resources of our regional libraries, but the organization of the work itself. Each student, in order to find the necessary material, can use one of the many computers located here. Documentation is entered using 2 central computers. There you can also get information about periodicals, which are supplied to the library even in the summer.

And one more interesting point: each student has his own metal cabinet, closed with a lock, which, oddly enough, cannot be opened either with a lock or a hairpin, so each student can put any valuable thing inside this “safe” with full ability.

It is difficult to end our story with words full of hidden envy. Therefore, we will tell you a secret that the Americans have no fewer, and maybe even more, those who are forced to study even in the summer than we have.

Voloda Baranovsky (Omsk, s.sh. No. 115). What I liked most about walking around the Capitol was the significance of the US congress in Washington (by the way, the dome of the Capitol is crowned by an American Indian). In one of the halls I saw statues of the most famous tribunes from different states of America. It was also interesting to look at the room in which the Supreme Court sat in the past. It’s just a pity that the Congress begins work in September; we were not able to watch the congress of their “deputies.”

Diar Chokin (Almaty, RFMSH). I would like to share my impressions of the physics lectures at the University of Maryland. If you please, they seemed to me (and maybe I’m not the only one) not very interesting. The lectures talked about quite complex things, which I would not have understood even in Russian, not to mention English. Despite all this, we went to bed very late and had to get up very early. And therefore, most of the energy that should have gone to digesting lectures went to fighting sleep. But when the lecturer began to show weak or using other equipment from the rich arsenal of auxiliary means, the hall was noticeably expected. Yes, the equipment of university auditoriums is enviable. This includes a computer that displays data on a huge screen, automated boards, and rotating stages. Of course, he really wants us to have all this. And I really liked the monument to Albert Einstein in Washington. I thought that this was a masterpiece of contemporary art.

Yulia Velikina (Dnepropetrovsk, s.sh. No. 45). Before leaving for the States, we were all told that Americans are very recipe people, and we were able to verify this for ourselves. Their piety, however, is completely invisible at first glance. Religion is not called anyone here; people themselves come to God, many of them already in adulthood. By chance, we met members of one of the Christian communities. They come to us several evenings in a row, read the Bible, and talk about themselves. These people did not try to convert us to their faith, they simply talked to us about God and people, about good and evil.

Boris Dubrov (Minsk, no. 107). The final point of our program - a visit to NASA - turned out to be very interesting. We were taken through a modern science center, shown the latest equipment for research, lectures about the work of NASA, the use of the ocean, the useful American spacecraft Voyager, and the work of one of the world's largest telecops, which is located in low-Earth orbit. Over lunch we met with cybernetics specialists and had a long conversation about computer science.

Katrin Kaarli (Tartu, no. 2). If you ask me what struck me most in America, I will answer - lodi. Thanks to these boats, our trip was wonderful and unforgettable. And as we asked, we said to each other: “We'll surely meet soon!” (“We will definitely meet soon!”).
