Observation 2. Let $l_1$, $l_2$, $l_3$ be pairwise crossing lines. Then there is a unique line parallel to $l_3$ and not crossing with any of the lines $l_1$, $l_2$.

Proof (Fig. 12). Let us draw through $l_3$ a plane $P_1$, parallel to $l_1$, and a plane $P_2$, parallel to $l_2$. The intersection line of planes $P_1$ and $P_2$ is the desired straight line.

Proof of Theorem 3. We already know that the generator of the second family does not cross with any generator of the first family (Theorem 2). The theorem will be proven if we are convinced that each of the lines not crossed with $l_1$, $l_2$ and $l_3$ is a generator of the second family. We know that through each point of the generator $l_3$ there passes exactly one generator of the second family and one more generator of this family is parallel to $l_3$. But at the same time, through each point of the generatrix $l_3$ there passes exactly one straight line that does not cross either $l_1$ or $l_2$ (observation 1), and there is also one straight line parallel to $l_3$ and not crossing either $l_1$ or $l_2$ (observation 2). Consequently, the straight lines referred to in observables 1 and 2 are generators of the second family.
