Triangulation of a sphere and a ball
Before we begin to present the results of Slator, Tarjan and Thurston, a few definitions are necessary.

Triangulation of a sphere will be called the division of a sphere into several regions, obtained as follows: several points are placed on the sphere - they are called triangulation vertices - and some of them are connected by non-self-intersecting curves that can intersect each other only at the vertices. In this case, each of the areas that is divided by a sphere must have exactly three sides, i.e., it must be bounded by exactly three curves.

Thus, during triangulation, the sphere is divided into several curved triangles. An example is shown at figure 3.
