3. Let us assume the opposite, i.e. that

$$k > \frac{1}{2} + \sqrt{2n}.$$

Let $P \in S$. By condition, there are at least $k$ points equidistant from $P$. From them it is possible to form at least $C_k^2$ pairs of points A and B such that $AP = BP$. Since this statement is true for any point $P \in S$, then in $S$ there are at least $nC_k^2$ pairs of points A and B for which at least one point from S lies on the perpendicular bisector to the segment AB.

We have:

$$nC_k^2 = n \frac{k(k-1)}{2} \geq \frac{n}{2} \left( \sqrt{2n} + \frac{1}{2} \right) \left( \sqrt{2n} - \frac{1}{2} \right) = \frac{n}{2} \left( 2n - \frac{1}{4} \right) = n \left( n - \frac{1}{8} \right) > n(n-1) = 2C_k^2.$$

Since $C_k^2$ is the number of all pairs of distinct points from S, it follows from the proven inequality that there exists a pair of points A and B and points $P_1$, $P_2$, ..., $P_m$, $m > 2$ such that $AP_i = BP_i$, $i = 1, 2, ...$, $m$. So the points

$$P_1, P_2, ..., P_m, m > 2$$ lie on the same line, and this contradicts the conditions.

4. Let the data of a quadrilateral $ABCD$ and a point $P$ satisfying the conditions as shown in figure 7. 5. Let $N = p_1p_2 \dots p_k$, where $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_k$ are all prime numbers not exceeding the given $n > 2$. Let's look at the numbers $N! + 1, N! + 2, ..., N! + n$. Suppose there is $1 \leq j \leq n$ and a prime $p$ for which $N! + j = p^m$. Since $N > n$, $N!$ is divisible by $j$, but then $j = p^\alpha$ ($\alpha < m$), and $p < n$. Shortening by $j$ we get $p^{m-\alpha} = 1 + 2 \cdot 3 \dots (j-1)(j+1) \dots N$, which is impossible.

6. Divide the number 1, 2, ..., 2n into pairs: $(1; n+1), (2; n+2), ..., (n; 2n)$. The permutation will be convenient if the numbers of at least one of the pairs are adjacent. Let $A_k$ be the set of permutations in which the numbers $n$ and $n+k$ are nearby.

Let $|x|$ be the number of elements in the set $X$. We must prove that

$$\bigcup_{k=1}^{n} A_k > \frac{1}{2}(2n)!.$$

Note that $\bigcup_{k=1}^{n} A_k \geq \sum_{k=1}^{n} |A_k| - \sum_{1 \leq i < j \leq n} |A_i \cap A_j|$. In a permutation of 2n elements, pairs of connected elements can be chosen in 2n-1 ways, and the numbers $k$ and $n+k$ can be placed in these places in two ways. Therefore $|A_i \cap A_j| = 8(n-1)(2n-3)! = 4(2n-2)!$.

So, $\sum_{i,j} |A_i \cap A_j| = 4C_k^2(2n-2)! = \frac{n-1}{2n-1} \times (2n)! < \frac{1}{2}(2n)!$. We finally get that $\sum_{k=1}^{n} |A_k| - \sum_{i,j} |A_i \cap A_j| > \frac{1}{2}(2n)!$.
