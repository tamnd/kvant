The tortuous history of studies of the electrical properties of a wide variety of media, when it was difficult to recognize that current flowing through a metal wire and in liquids passing through gases and vacuum, are close “relatives,” necessarily led to the electric theory. This theory makes it possible to use many - and not only electrical - properties of materials, and was a step towards a more subtle understanding of the structure of matter and the interactions that control its behavior. And this, in turn, allows for solid technical discoveries and fundamental changes in the appearance of the world around us.

Superconductors and solar cells, MHD generators and semiconductor elements of computer technology are just examples from those areas of physics and technology where it is fundamentally important to know how current flows through a particular substance. This question is the main one in today's Kaleidoscope.
