⟦folio 45⟧

The shape of the discharge depends in a rather interesting way on voltage and frequency. Observe how it changes as the generator warms up and the voltage gradually increases. Often the “flame” turns out to be unstable (photo at figure 5). Sometimes, in order for the discharge to ignite, it must be “ignited” by touching the tip with the blade of a screwdriver with an insulated handle.

At low frequencies (less than 1 MHz), the electron discharge near the oscillator looks like a diffuse cold glow (the so-called corona discharge), accompanying hissing and the formation of ozone. But our discharge is compact and hot, which is easy to verify by holding the end of a wooden ruler to it. The discharge can even penetrate the bulb of a neon bulb if it is brought too close.

We will continue our discussion of wholesalers with high-frequency generators in the next issue of “Laboratory «Kvant». In the meantime, here are a few questions for independent research.

— Estimate the inductance of the coil $L_1$.

— Estimate the capacitance between the top end of the coil $L_3$ and your hand holding the neon bulb. Estimate the current flowing through the arm. Why shouldn't you keep your hand too close to the reel for too long?

— A neon light glows next to the generator, although it is not connected to anything. Where does energy come from? How does it get into the light bulb?

— If you remove coil $L_3$ from the circuit, the neon bulb still glows. True, it now needs to be brought close to $L_1$. By changing the inductance of the coil and the capacitance of the capacitors, you can change the frequency of the generator. Does the color of a neon light bulb depend on frequency?
