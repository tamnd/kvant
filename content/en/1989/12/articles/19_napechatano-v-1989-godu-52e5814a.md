Tivist formulas. This is not necessary for a proton (511 keV≤938 MeV).
a) For a particle in a magnetic field

$$\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = \vec{F}_L,$$

where $\vec{P} = m_0 v / \sqrt{1 - v^2 / c^2}$ is the impulse, $\vec{F}_L = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}(x)$ is the Lorentz force, $\vec{B}(x)$ is the magnetic field induction (depends only on the coordinates).
Force is perpedicular to velocity, so the velocity and momentum models and the energy of the particle do not change:

$$\vec{P} \frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{d\vec{P}^2}{dt} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{dP^2}{dt} = \vec{F}_L,$$

$$\vec{P} \frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = \frac{ds}{dt} \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = v \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = \vec{F}_L,$$

$$v \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}(x).$$

Let $s$ be the path traveled by the particle, then

$$\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt} = \frac{ds}{dt} \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = v \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = \vec{F}_L,$$

$$v \frac{d\vec{P}}{ds} = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}(x).$$

Let's divide both sides by $Pv$:

$$\frac{d}{ds} \left( \frac{\vec{P}}{P} \right) = \frac{q}{P} \left( \frac{\vec{v}}{v} \right) \times \vec{B}(x),$$

or $\frac{d}{ds} \vec{r} = \frac{q}{P} \vec{r} \times \vec{B}(x),$

where $\vec{r}$ is the unit vector of the velocity direction. The last equation sets the trajectories of particle motion to be identical if

$$\frac{q_e \vec{B}(x)}{P_e} = \frac{q_p \vec{B}'(x)}{P_p},$$

or $\vec{B}'(x) = -\frac{P_p}{P_e} \vec{B}(x).$

The field $\vec{B}(x)$ is the geometric sum of the fields of the coil, therefore the field of each coil, and therefore the currents in them, must be changed by $-P_p / P_e$ times:

$$i'_j = -\frac{P_p}{P_e} i_j.$$

For electron $P_e = \sqrt{2m_e U e + U^2 e^2 / c^2}$, for proton $P_p = \sqrt{2m_p U e}$. Then

$$\frac{P_p}{P_e} = 35, \quad i'_j = -35 i_j.$$

b) Each particle corresponds to a de Broglie wave with length $\lambda = h / P$. The resolution angle, determined by the ratio $\lambda / d \sim 1 / P$, will decrease by a factor of $P_p / P_e = 35$. Consequently, the resolution will increase by 35 times.
