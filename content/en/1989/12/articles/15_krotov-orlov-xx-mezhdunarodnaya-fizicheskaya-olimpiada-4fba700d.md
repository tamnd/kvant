Problem 1

Liquid $A$ and $B$ are mutually insoluble. Their saturated vapor pressures $p_i$ ($i=A$ or $B$) obey the law with good accuracy

$$\ln \left( \frac{p_0}{p_1} \right) = a_i/T + b_i,$$

where $p_0$ is the normal atmospheric pressure, $T$ is the abscilotic temperature of the vapor, $a_i$ and $b_i$ are non-octorian constants, depending on the liquid (the symbol ln denotes the natural logarithm, i.e. the logarithm to the base $e=2,7182818...$). The values ​​of the (exact) ratios $p_i/p_0$ for liquid $A$ and $B$ at temperatures $40^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $90^{\circ}\text{C}$ are given in the table:

| $t,^{\circ}\text{C}$ | $p_i/p_0$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| $i=A$ | $i=B$ |
| 40 | 0,284 | 0,07278 |
| 90 | 1,476 | 0,6918 |

a) Find the boiling point of liquid $A$ in liquid $B$ at pressure $p_0$.

b) Liquid $A$ and $B$ are poured into a vessel, where they spread out as shown in figure 1. The surface of liquid $B$ is covered with a thin layer of non-coalescing liquid $C$, which does not spread out in liquid $A$ and $B$ and prevents free evaporation from the surface of the liquid $B$. Determine the temperatures $t_1$ and $t_2$ (accurate to an integer number of boundaries), corresponding to the horizontal segments of the diagram, and the masses of the liquid $A$ and $B$ (with accurate to tenths of a gram) at time $\tau_1$.

Problem 3

Explore the possibility and consequences of converting an electron microbraid with magnetic control of an electron beam accelerated by a voltage of $U=511$ kV into a proton microbraid in which the proton beam is accelerated by a voltage of $U$. To do this, answer two questions.

a) An electron, after acceleration by voltage $U$, enters a region with a low-grade magnetic field $B$, created by a system of stationary coils $L_1, L_2, \dots, L_n$. The current strengths in the coils are equal to the correspondence $i_1, i_2, \dots, i_n$. In this region, the electron moves along a certain trajectory $T$. What must be the current strength $i_1', i_2', \dots, i_n'$ in coils $L_1, L_2, \dots, L_n$ so that a proton, accelerated by voltage - $U$, after entering a region with a magnetic field, can move along the same trajectory $T$ (and in the same direction) along which the electron moves?

Note. Find the conditions under which the equation describing the trajectory is the same in general cases. Match can be used

$$\bar{P} \frac{d\bar{P}}{dt} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{d\bar{P}^2}{dt} = \frac{1}{2} \frac{dP^2}{dt},$$

where $P$ is the frequency pulse.

b) By how many times will the resolution of the microscope under consideration (i.e., the minimum distance between two precise objects whose images in the form of disks are still different) increase or decrease if the electron beam is replaced by a proton beam? Assume that the resolution of the microscope is determined only by the wave properties of the materials. It is assumed that the velocities of electrons and protons before their acceleration are equal to zero. To simplify, we also assume that the interaction of the intrinsic magnetic moments of electrons and protons with an external magnetic field is negligible and that these particles, moving in a magnetic field, do not emit electromagnetic radiation.

Notes. In physics, 1 electronvolt (eV) and derivative units of 1 keV or 1 MeV are often used as a unit of energy measurement; 1 eV equals the measurement of the energy of an electron producing a potential difference of 1 V. In the calculations, use the following values: electron rest energy $E_e = m_e c^2 = 511$ keV, proton rest energy $E_p = m_p c^2 = 938$ MeV.

Note that all three theoretical problems are beyond the scope of the general education physics course in our high school. The purpose of the experiment was to determine the speed of propagation of ultrasonic waves in water and glycerin, using the method of physical shift of reference signals and signals transmitted through the medium. The basis of the physical content of the experimental problem was the piezoelectric effect in dielectrics.

To complete the task, students were offered modern physical equipment that was not common to the school physics classroom: a two-beam electronic oscilloscope, a standard signal generator, and piezoelectric sensors. It should be noted that over the past five years there has been a fairly stable trend of transition from home-grown (sometimes very witty) equipment to real, modern equipment. The USSR team training strategy clearly does not take this into account.

The success of solving an experimental problem depends not only on the depth of knowledge of physics, but on the performance of painstaking and laborious observations, competent presentation of the experimental results, including thorough calculations of errors. Unfortunately, this is not typical for experimental rounds of weighty physical optimization, which affected the results. To this end, in the near future it is necessary to make fuller use of the experimental bases of Moscow State University and MIPT, which are actively involved in the preparation of the team, and in the future, to create an independent experimental base.
