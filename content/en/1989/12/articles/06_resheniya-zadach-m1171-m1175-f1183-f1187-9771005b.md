M1173*. Three lines are drawn through one point inside a triangle of area S so that each side of the triangle is intersected by two of them (Fig. 1). Prove that the areas $S_1, S_2, S_3$ of three educational triangles satisfy the inequality

$$\frac{1}{S_1} + \frac{1}{S_2} + \frac{1}{S_3} \geq \frac{9}{S}$$

We will prove two inequalities that strengthen the inequality of the problem in different ways.

1. Let us show that the inequality of the problem is valid for any three triangles with a common vertex O inside a given triangle ABC, the bases of which lie on its sides (Fig. 2).

It is clear that for a given point O, the smallest value of the left side of the inequality will be achieved for triangles OAB, OBC and OCA. Then $S = S_1 + S_2 + S_3$ and due to the inequality $a + 1/a \geq 2$ at $a > 0$

F1185. A uniformly charged cube creates an electric field in the direction $E_0$ at its vertex. A cube half the size will be removed from the cube (see Fig.ok). What will now be the direction of the field at point A?

Let us find how the direction of the field created by a uniformly charged cube at its vertex depends on the linear dimensions of the cube. From the symmetry messages it is clear that this directionality is directed along the corresponding major diagonal of the cube. Therefore we can write

$$E = \sum_i \frac{\rho \Delta V_i}{r_i^2} \cos \alpha_i,$$

where $\rho$ is the charge density, $\Delta V_i$ are small volumes into which the entire cube is mentally divided, $r_i$ is the distance from the $i$th volume to the vertex of the cube under consideration, $\alpha_i$ is the angle between the direction from the volume to the vertex and the large diagonal of the cube.

For a cube with edge $l$

$$\Delta V_i \sim l^3, \quad r_i^2 \sim l^2,$$

and $\alpha_i$ are the same for all cubes; hence,

$$E \sim \frac{l^3}{l^2} = l$$

— the direction of the field at the top of the cube is directly proportional to its linear size.

Obviously, a cube of half the linear dimensions creates a directionality that is also half as large. Therefore, if at first the directionality at point A was equal to $E_0$, then after removing the small cube the directionality will become half as large:

$$E_A = E_0 - \frac{E_0}{2} = \frac{E_0}{2}.$$

The direction of the direction will not change.
