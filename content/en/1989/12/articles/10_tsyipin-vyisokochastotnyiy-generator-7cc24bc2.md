HIGH FREQUENCY
generator

M. M. TSYPIN

We hear about many physical phenomena or read about them in books, but we almost never see them. Meanwhile, nothing helps the understanding of the physical world in which we live as much as direct experience of communicating with it. An experiment carried out with your own hands, a phenomenon seen and studied, reflections on what really happens in nature - all this usually turns out to be more diverse and more complex than what is written in books.

This article will describe a high-frequency oscillation generator that you can make yourself at school or at home. The generator allows you to carry out many interesting experiments related to electrical discharges, to get acquainted with the properties of alternating current and the electromagnetic field.

The generator, the circuit of which is shown in figure 1, creates a high voltage (3-4 thousand volts) of high frequency (about 5 MHz). The oscillatory circuit, which determines the frequency of the generator, consists of a coil $L_1$ and a capacitor $C_1$ (the capacitance of the capacitors is indicated in picofarads). The maintenance of insignificant oscillations in it is ensured with the help of an additional coil $L_2$ and an amplifier assembled on a lamp $J_1$: part of the circuit energy is taken from this coil, fed to the lamp grid, amplified and through a capacitor $C_2$ fed back into the circuit. Chain $R_1C_3$ provides (think of it as) the imprinted DC voltage on the lamp grid, necessary for its normal operation. Another coil - due to its role in the circuit, called a choke (Dr) according to radio engineering tradition - separates

sections of the circuit where there is high-frequency voltage (the anode of the lamp) and where it is not (“plus” of the power source). This occurs due to the high resistance of the inductor to high frequency alternating current. The second grid of the lamp is connected to the anode, as a result the lamp operates as a triode. Coil $L_1$, $L_2$ and $L_3$ are inductively coupled to each other and form a high frequency transformer.

It would be a mistake to think that only a highly experienced experimenter can assemble such a generator and make it work. The generator requires virtually no setup, and the necessary parts can be easily purchased or obtained from an old tube receiver or TV.

As for the reels, they are quite easy to make yourself. The coil $L_1$ and $L_2$ is wound onto a common cylindrical frame with a diameter of 3.5 cm and a length of 7 cm, which is easiest to glue together from thick paper (Fig. 2). The first coil consists of 6 turns of copper wire with a diameter of 2-3 mm (in any insulation), the distance between the turns is 1 cm. The second contains 10 turns of wire (in enamel insulation) with a diameter of 0.5 mm, wound close turn to turn. (Question for the theoretical: how should the winding directions $L_1$ and $L_2$ be coordinated so that the feedback contributes to the occurrence of oscillations in the circuit, and not vice versa? Otherwise, the generator will not work.) The generator requires a power source that provides two voltages: direct 200-250 V and alternating 6.3 V. You can take a suitable ready-made power supply available in the school physics classroom, use the power supply of any tube receiver, or assemble your own according to the diagram shown in figure 3. You can use it, for example, a commercially available drain transformer for the Record-353 radio and "Record-354". figure 4 shows the power supply control diagram. With it, the generator will only work for the second half-cycle, when the voltage at the anode is positive. Its average power will be halved, and this is even good: the lamp and coil will heat up less.

Everyone can decide on the design of the generator to their own taste. A person with a practical mind will come up with and make a case and a board to accommodate the parts; theoretically, by laying out the parts on the table and attaching them to each other. Fortunately, the “form” of the generator does not affect its “content”.
