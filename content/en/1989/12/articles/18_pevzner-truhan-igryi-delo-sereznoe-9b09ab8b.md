Games are a serious business

- this is the conclusion reached by mathematicians, historians, physiologists and simply declared games, having gathered at the first congress of the International Association of Intellectual Games in Warsaw in the summer of 1989.

How many logic games do you know?

The first thing that comes to mind is, of course, chess. The list continues with checkers, dominoes, tic-tac-toe, some mention Go, Renju. But in the collection of the Chairman of the International Association, scientific director of the Warsaw Center for Logic Games, Tadedus Urbanov, there are hundreds of games, and this list is constantly growing. Moreover, completely new and still largely unknown games in the USSR have firmly entered among the most popular games in the world in recent years.

Historical, military or commercial games (one of the first commercial games, Monopoly, is distributed in the USSR) is an ideal way to fill a family arc, because they are accessible and interesting for both adults and children, and the difference in skill is no less satisfying from the game. In addition, by playing a historical game, you simultaneously learned about reigning persons, prominent positions, diplomats, government structure, major battles, etc. - the games are called, for example, “Peter I”, “Alexander Nevsky” or, say, “World War II”.

What games will we enter the 21st century with? What games did our ancestors play? Will our generation be able to come up with a game that, like chess or Renju, lasts for thousands of years? Will the development of commercial games lead to the extinction of traditional ones - with a board and pieces (chips)? What is the role of mathematics in the analysis of games and games in the development of mathematics («Kvant» wrote more than once about games, the analysis of which led to the emergence of entire mathematical directions)? These and other questions were the subject of heated discussions at the congress. It was decided to significantly expand the exposition of the International Association, the scientific director of the Warsaw Center for Logic Games Tadedusz Urbanov - hundreds of games, and this list is constantly growing. Moreover, completely new and still largely unknown games in the USSR have firmly entered among the most popular games in the world in recent years.
