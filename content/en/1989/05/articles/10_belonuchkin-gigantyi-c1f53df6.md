The first scientist known to us by name is Thales of Miletus, whose scientific activity began at the turn of the 7th and 6th centuries BC. e., contributed to the history of astronomy. According to legend, he predicted a solar eclipse on May 28, 584 BC. e. However, there is reason to believe that he used a method developed in ancient Babylon. The Vyvilonians' method was purely empirical: long-term observations allowed them to capture patterns in the repetition of astronomical phenomena. The first “theoretically” established fact, with the recognition of which the construction of the diagram of the Universe began, should, apparently, be considered the sphericity of the Earth.

Two concepts lay at the basis of the first scientific systems for constructing the world: the obvious immobility of the Earth and the uniform movement in a circle around the Earth of the Sun, Lunya, and planets. The injustice of the second dotma was replaced by ancient astronomers, but it was precisely this that remained unshakable for two thousand years - right up to Kepler.

Eudoxus of Cyd (c. 406 - c. 355 BC) - ancient Greek mathematician and astronomer. He imagined the movement of the planets as a combination of 27 concentric spheres rotating uniformly around the Earth.

Aristotle (384-322 BC) - ancient Greek philosopher and scientist. Aristotle's discussion covers all branches of knowledge of that time. To calculate the speed of a star, you must at least determine its position twice. The accuracy with which the coordinates of luminaries can be measured using a telescope is determined by its resolution, and it is fundamentally limited by diffraction phenomena. The minimum angle that can be measured, applied by diameter D, is approximately λ/D, where λ is the wavelength of the radiation at which the observations are made. For yellow light, for example, λ≈6·10⁻⁷ m, which means the maximum accuracy of measurements on the VTA is approximately 10⁻⁷ radian. Roger Bacon in the 13th century, Nicholas of Cusa in the 15th century, Francis Bacon in the early 17th century laid the foundations of modern scientific methodology, the main thesis of which can be expressed in the words “experience is the criterion of truth.”
Some may ask: isn’t it enough to have one thesis in four centuries? Let us remember that for more than a thousand years agreement with the Bible and Aristotle was considered the criterion of truth.
