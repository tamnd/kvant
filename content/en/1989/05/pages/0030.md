Let's list some of them:

1) xenon was pumped out of the chamber - there was no signal;
2) the xenon pressure in the chamber changed (the number of Xe atoms changed) - the signal appeared as the pressure increased and decreased as it decreased;
3) the voltage to the capacitor was turned off - there was no signal;
4) the voltage on the capacitor changed - it changed in the time interval between the laser pulse and the signal on the oscilloscope, and as the voltage decreased, the signal delay increased (the acceleration reported by ionam decreased);
5) The intensity of the laser radiation decreased significantly - there was no signal.

All this convincingly indicated that ionization of Xe atoms occurs under the action of laser radiation. However, experimenters wanted to obtain irrefutable evidence of the fact that this is a process of changing seven quanta. What kind of convincing experiment can be staged?

Such an experience immediately comes to mind if we recall the correspondence that describes the dependence of the probability of a multiquantum transition on the intensity of radiation. This dependence must be a power law, with an exponent equal to the number of absorbed quanta. The discovery of such a dependence is that irrefutable evidence that should satisfy the most severe and demanding critics!
