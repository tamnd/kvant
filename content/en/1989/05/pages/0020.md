The sum of the curvatures of the angles of a polyhedron. Let us construct a dual angle at each vertex of a convex polyhedron. Figure 10 shows that the sum of the curvatures of the angles of a convex polyhedron is equal to 4π.

This statement is the spatial analogue of the fact that the sum of the exterior angles of a polygon is 2π. Aren't figure 2 and 10 similar?

Let us denote by B the number of vertices of the polyhedron, by P the number of its edges, and by Γ the number of faces. Let's calculate the sum of all plane angles in two ways. The sum of plane angles at the i-th vertex is equal to 2π-Ki, where Ki is the curvature of this vertex. Therefore, the sum of all plane angles is equal to 2πB—ΣKi=2πB—4π. On the other hand, the sum of the angles of an n-gon is π(n-2). This means that the sum of the plane angles of the jth face is equal to π(nj-2), where nj is the number of edges of this face. Therefore, the sum of all plane angles is also equal to πΣnj—2πΓ. Each edge belongs to exactly two faces, so Σnj=2P. This means that the sum of all plane angles is equal to 2πP - 2πΓ. So, 2πB—4π=2πP—2πΓ or

B—P+Γ=2.

This formula is called Euler's formula, and the number B - P+Γ is called the Euler characteristic of the polyhedron. The Euler characteristic of a convex polyhedron is 2,
