Eudoxus of Cyd (c. 406 - c. 355 BC) - ancient Greek mathematician and astronomer. He imagined the movement of the planets as a combination of 27 concentric spheres rotating uniformly around the Earth.

Aristotle (384-322 BC) - ancient Greek philosopher and scientist. Aristotle's discussion covers all branches of knowledge of that time. In the field of astronomy, Aristotle's interests focused mainly on questions of the creation of the world. He also made observations of celestial phenomena, comets and falling stars.

Ginnapkh (c. 180-190-125 BC) - ancient Greek astronomer, one of the founders of astronomy. He determined the distance to the Moon, the length of the year, and compiled a catalog of 850 stars, in which he divided them into 6 classes by brightness.

Claudius Ptolemy (c. 90 - c. 160) - ancient Greek astronomer, creator of the geocentric system of the world. He developed a mathematical theory of the movement of planets around the Earth, which made it possible to pre-calculate their positions in the sky.

Nikolai Konernik (1473-1543) - user astronomer, creator of the geocentric system of the world. He made a revolution in natural sciences, abandoning the doctrine of the central position of the Earth, which had been accepted for many centuries. He explained the apparent movement of the celestial bodies by the rotation of the Earth around its axis and the revolution of the planets (including the Earth) around the Sun.

Juodani Kepler (1571-1630) - German astronomer, one of the creators of modern astronomy. He discovered the laws of planetary motion (Kepler's theory), on the basis of which he compiled planetary tables.
