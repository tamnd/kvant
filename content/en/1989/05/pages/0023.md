This is significant Gauss-Bonnet geometry - one of the fundamental theorems of differential geometry.
As you can see, parallel transfer on a surface differs from parallel transfer on a plane: its result depends on the path along which the transfer occurs, and if this path is closed, then the result of parallel transfer turns out to be a rotation. Parallel transport owes these unusual properties to the curvature of the surface. If the curvature is zero, that is, the surface is developable, then parallel transfer is “arranged” exactly as on a plane.

Exercise 5. At what angle will the tangent plane of the sphere rotate during parallel translation along its parallel with latitude φ?

Geodetic lines. Having the definition of parallel translation, we can determine the curvature of the curve on the surface. This definition is very similar to the definition of curvature of a plane curve. Let us take on the surface a small section of the curve AB of length l and parallelly transfer along it the tangent vector of the curve from point A to point B. This vector will no longer necessarily touch the curve at point B; let us denote by α the angle between it and the tangent at point B. The limit of the ratio α/l as l→0 is called the geodesic curvature of the curve on the surface.

Exercise 6*. Consider a closed curve on a surface. Prove that the integral of its geodesic curvature complements to 2n the integral of the curvature of the surface over the region bounded by this curve.

Among the lines drawn on the surface, a special role is played by lines whose curvature at each point is zero. These lines are called geodesics. They have the highest direction (in the sense that the road leading directly to the top of the hill is straight, as opposed to a winding serpentine road going up the mountain). Geodesic lines on a plane are, of course, straight lines; geodesics on a sphere are arcs of great circles. One geodesic emerges from each point on the surface in each direction - like the earth's meridians emerging from the pole. Geodesics can also be described as curves along which you need to twist in order to get from one point of the surface to another as quickly as possible (this is not surprising - in order to do it faster, you need to walk without turning).

Exercise 7. Draw several geodesics on a cone and a cylinder. Hint: turn these surfaces flat.

Recall that parallel translation along a curve on a curved surface is the quality of the tangent plane along that curve. Let's go to the reporting system associated with the tangent plane. Then it will seem to us that the surface is rolling along a stationary plane. Due to the fact that rolling occurs without slipping, the geodesic curvature of the curve on the surface along which rolling occurs is equal to the curvature of the trace that remains on the plane. In particular, if you roll the surface along the geodesic, the trace on the plane will be a straight line.

Let's imagine that a straight line on a plane is drawn with sticky paint. When rolled, this straight (or rather, narrow strip of paint) will adhere to the surface and will be located along the geodesic. A practical method for constructing geodesics is based on this: you need to cut out a narrow rectangle from paper and press it tightly to the surface - you get a geodesic line. Experiment with different surfaces (for example, a balloon or an inflatable ring).
