Why doesn't the bag slide?

During one of the lessons, the teacher places a matchbox on the table and places a glass of water on the box (Fig. 1).
— How to take out the box without touching the glass? Pull? No, the glass will follow the box. Now look.
The teacher picks up a heavy ruler, moves it to the side and

Mahu hits the box. The matches fly into the far corner, the zagostan stands on the table, almost unmoved by the blow!
Why didn't the glass move? To avoid misunderstandings, let us remind you that the teacher does not hit the glass, but the box.
On the glass in the horizontal direction, only the friction force from the side of the box $F_{\text{тр}} = \mu m_{\text{ст}} g$ acts.
Of course, with the help of such a force you can impart a noticeable speed to the glass by moving the box across the table with a slight acceleration (think about the limits within which this should lie)
