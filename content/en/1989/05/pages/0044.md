About curvature

(See beginning on p. 14)

Let's roll the ball. Take a tennis ball. A groove of a narrow shape is made on its surface. Mark some point on the groove and place the ball on the table so that this point is on the table. Now carefully roll the ball along the table without slipping along the groove until the placed point is again on the table. As a result of this rolling, the ball will rotate around its axis through a certain angle. Which one?

The rotation of a ball when rolled along a closed curve is equal to the rotation of its tangent plane when it intersects parallel along this curve. By the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, it is also equal to the integral of curvature over the region limited by the groove. Look at a tennis ball: the two areas into which the groove divides it are exactly the same. As we know, the integral of curvature over the area limited by the groove is half as large, i.e., equal to 2n. So, as a result of rolling, the ball will turn a full angle, or, what is the same, it will not turn at all. Try checking this result experimentally.
