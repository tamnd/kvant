To calculate the speed of a star, you must at least determine its position twice. The accuracy with which the coordinates of luminaries can be measured using a telescope is determined by its resolution, and it is fundamentally limited by diffraction phenomena. The minimum angle that can be measured, applied by diameter D, is approximately λ/D, where λ is the wavelength of the radiation at which the observations are made. For yellow light, for example, λ≈6·10⁻⁷ m, which means the maximum accuracy of measurements on the VTA is approximately 10⁻⁷ radian.
