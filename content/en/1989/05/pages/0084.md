In "Kaleidoscon" these numbers at figure 7 show a replacement puzzle "tangram" - a square cut into 7 parts, of which various siligers are made. It appeared in China at the end of the 18th century. Her first image (1780 2.) was found in the acidography of the Lon artist Utamaro, where two girls put together the figures of “chi chao tu” - the so-called “tangram” in his homeland (translated as “mental puzzle of seven parts”). The extraction of tangrams originated in Europe most likely from the word "tan" (Chinese in Cantonese) and the common Greek root "grama" (letter). However, the authors of many books on mathematics attribute the invention of the tangram to the scientist Tang, who allegedly lived 4000 years ago in China. This elaborate legend is dreamed up from start to finish by inventive puzzle writer Sam Loyd.
Top row figure reproduces three of the nine tangram postcards issued in New York in 1900. It’s easy to put these figures together. It is more difficult to fold all the simple shapes.
