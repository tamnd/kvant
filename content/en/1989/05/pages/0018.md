a section of the curve of length $l$ and denote by $\alpha$ the angle between the tangents to the curve at its ends. Curvature is the limit of the ratio $\alpha/l$ at $l \to 0$. Figure 4 shows that curvature is the magnitude of acceleration when moving along a curve with unit speed. Curvature can be assigned a sign (according to whether the curve turns clockwise or counterclockwise (Fig.5)).

Let's go around the closed curve at unit speed and return to the starting point. The end of the velocity vector will make a revolution of the unit circle and cover a path equal to 2π. If the speed of movement of the end of the vector were constant, then the path would be equal to the product of its speed and time. In the general case, the path is equal to the speed interval in time. The speed at which the end of the vector moves is the curvature of the curve. So, the integral of curvature over a closed non-self-intersecting curve is equal to 2π.

This theorem is analogous to the statement that the sum of the exterior angles of a polygon is 2π. For curves that have self-intersection points, the curvature integral can take other values ​​(Fig. 6).

a triangle with angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$, the sides of which are arcs of great circles. What is its area? Figure 7 shows that this area is given by a simple formula:

$$S = \alpha + \beta + \gamma - \pi.$$

An important consequence: the sum of the angles of a spherical triangle is always greater than $\pi$ and the larger its area, the greater it is.

How to find the area of ​​a spherical $n$-gon with angles $\beta_1, \beta_2, \dots, \beta_n$? Just like on a plane: divide it into triangles by diagonals (you, of course, understand that both sides and diagonals are arcs of great circles). You will also get the formula:

$$S = (\beta_1 + \beta_2 + \dots + \beta_n) - (n - 2)\pi.$$

An unexpected consequence: the area of ​​a spherical polygon depends on the size of its angles and does not depend on the length of its side.

Let us denote by $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$ the external angles of the spherical polygon equal to $\pi - \beta_1, \pi - \beta_2, \dots, \pi - \beta_n$. The formula for areas can be rewritten as follows:

$$S = 2\pi - (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \dots + \alpha_n).$$
