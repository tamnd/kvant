Using emission spectra, you can carry out not only a qualitative analysis, but also a quantitative one—to find out how many atoms of a particular isotope are present in a sample. The fact is that the intensity of an isotope’s radiation is directly proportional to the number of its nuclei. Having standards (i.e. samples with a previously known content of the analyzed element), it is possible, by activating them, to construct a graph of this dependence. And by measuring the emission intensity of a given isotope in the test sample, which was activated together with the standard, it is easy to determine the content of this isotope from a graph. (After what we said about the half-life, you should understand why the activation of the sample and the standard and the change in their spectra are carried out simultaneously. If this condition is not met, then to determine the amount of the isotope from the standard graph, it is necessary to make corrections for the half-life.)

The method of qualitative and quantitative analysis we described is called neutron activation analysis - HAA.
