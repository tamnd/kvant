ABOUT CURVATURE

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. L. TAVACHNIKOV

Anyone who has ever studied elongated figures from wire or drawn curved lines on a piece of paper is familiar with the concept of curvature of a plane curve - figure 1.
What is surface curvature?
Which of the two surfaces - a cylinder or a sphere - has greater curvature? Similar questions are dealt with by differential geometry, a branch of mathematics created in the 18th and 19th centuries through the efforts of many scientists, and first of all, L. Euler, K.-F. Gauss and W. Riemann.

Plane polygons and curves.
We will teach you about the curvature of a surface with... flat polygons. You remember that the sum of the exterior angles of a convex polygon is 2π (Fig. 2). The curvature of the angle α is the number π - α, i.e., the size of its external angle (the sharper the angle, the greater the curvature). Then the sum of the curvatures of the angles of a convex polygon is equal to 2π.
In fact, this statement is true not only for convex polygons, but also for non-self-intersecting polygons. The case of self-intersecting polygons is more complicated.

How can you determine the curvature of a main curve that has no corners? Let's draw a curve and go around it at unit speed. We will plot the velocity vector from some fixed point O. Then its end will move along a unit circle with center O. Let us call the curvature of the curve at a given instantaneous point the speed of movement of the end of the vector along the unit circle. The faster a curve turns, the greater its curvature.

Exercise 2. Find the curvature of a straight line and a circle of radius R.

By the way, straight lines and circles are the only curves whose curvature is the same at all points.
