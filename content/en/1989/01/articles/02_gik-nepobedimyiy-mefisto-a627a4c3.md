⟦folio 28⟧

INVICIBLE
"MEPHISTO"

The next, seventh World Intermediate Microcomputer Chess Championship took place in Rome. For the first time, the computer crown was determined in a match tour. One team was represented by three copies of the famous three-time world champion “Mephisto” (author of the program P. Lang), the other - three copies of the less famous “Sphinx” (author of the program D. Levy). The composition lasted 6 rounds: three “Mephisto” (A, B and C) played two games with each of the three “Sphinks” (the same A, B and C) - white and black. The result turned out to be sad for the “Sphinks” - “Mephisto” won all six games, and won the championship crown for the fourth time in a row.

Simultaneously with the main compilation, another event took place in Rome, like a “small world championship”. Seven personal computer programs tested the round robin. The winner was the program “Psion Chesn”, the author of which is also P. Lang, the creator of “Mephisto”. It is curious that, despite his high scientific and computer level, Richard Lang does not belong to the category of “absent-minded professors” - in his projects between compiling a computer program, he successfully participates in marathon tasks.

Let us now present several examples of computer games in Roman tournaments, paying attention to the most intriguing episodes of the game.
