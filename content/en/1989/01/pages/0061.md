XIX
International Physics Olympiad

Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences
O. F. KABARDIN,
Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
V. A. ORLOV

This year, the International Physics Olympiad for Schoolchildren was held from June 23 to July 2 in the city of Bad Ischl (Austria). The number of pages of the Olympiad increases daily (this year by two countries). Schoolchildren from 27 countries came to Austria: Australia, Australia, Belgium, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Iceland, Italy, Canada, Cyprus, China, Colombia, Cuba, Kuvet, Inderland, Norway, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, Finland, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia.

A representative of UNESCO and correspondents from magazines for schoolchildren from Belgium, Great Britain, Hungary, Yugoslavia, and Czechoslovakia took part in the Olympiad. It’s a pity that there was no representative of the magazine «Kvant» at the Olympics.*

The grand opening of the Olympiad took place in the building of the Higher School of Personnel Training in the Field of Tourism. The schoolchildren were led by the director of this school, Master R. Ginzel, the mayor of Bad Ischl, K. Saller, the representative of the International Commission, Professor of the Vienna Technical University P. Skalitsky, and the head of the section of the Ministry of Education, Art and Sports, Master L. Leitner. Professor of the University of Vienna R. Dobrozemsky read a bright report on the topic “Physics in Australia”, from which it clearly followed that Australia gave the world not only brilliant artists, musicians, poses, but also no less brilliant physicists: H. Doppler, I. Loschmidt, L. Boltzmann, W. Hecca, E. Schrödinger, W. Pauli, E. Mach (“Mach cone” was used as an emblem Olympics).

The competition took place, as always, in two rounds. At the theoretical round, schoolchildren were given three tasks, and at the experimental round, two tasks (they were given 5 hours to complete each task). Here are the conditions for these tasks.

Theoretical tour

Task 1. Determining speed using effective data.

The processes of emission and absorption of photons are reversible, therefore, even after the process of absorption of a photon by an atom, its spontaneous emission (fluorescence) can be observed. This phenomenon is used in modern methods of particle detection and identification, as well as for spectrosynthesis of atomic particle velocities.
