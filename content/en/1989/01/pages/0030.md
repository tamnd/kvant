F1132. It has recently been established that the extinction of animal species on Earth is particularly intense during periodic periods of time lasting $T = 6,2$ million years. This pattern is explained by the hypothesis proposed by the existence of the star Nemesis, which is a satellite of the Sun. This faint and therefore invisible star moves in an orbit that is located within the so-called “Oort belt”, containing a supply of comets (Fig. 1). By disturbing the movement of comets, Nemesis causes a “comet house” on Earth, the duration of which practically coincides with the time of Nemesis’s stay inside the Oort belt.

Determine the greater half a of the orbit of Nemesis and the period of its revolution around the Sun, if it is assumed that the perihelion of the orbit is at a distance of 0.15a from the Sun. The area of ​​the ellipse is $S = \pi ab$, where $a$ and $b$ are the ellipse.

Over a time of $T = 6,2$ million years, the radius vector of Nemesis occupies the area protected by figure 2. This is the half of the area of ​​the ellipse minus the area of ​​the triangle with base $2b$ and height 0.85 $a$. What part of the period is the time of Nemesis inside the Oort belt (this follows from Kepler’s second law). The total period of Nemesis' orbit around the Sun is therefore $T_x = 27$ million years.

Comparing, with the help of Kepler's third law, the motion of the Earth and Nemesis, we obtain a greater semi-presence of the invisible satellite of the Sun:

$$a = \left( T_x/T_0 \right)^{2/3} R_0 = \left( 27 \cdot 10^6 \right)^{2/3} a.e. = 9 \cdot 10^4 a.e. \approx 13,5 \cdot 10^{12} \text{km.}$$

Here $T_0$ is the Earth year, and $R_0$ is the radius of the Earth’s orbit, equal to one astronomical unit (1 AU), or 150 million km.
