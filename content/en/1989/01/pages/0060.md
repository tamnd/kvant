Of the 275 students who participated in the MMO, approximately 100,000 students were awarded gold, silver or bronze medals (approximately 1:2:3 ratio). In the USSR team, N. Filonov (42), S. Ivanov (41), D. Tulyakov (37) and D. Ivanov (36) were awarded gold medals, and Y. Khokhlov (31) and S. Berlov (30) were awarded silver medals; The number of points scored by participants (out of 42 possible) is indicated in parentheses.

In the unofficial (as well as at sports Olympiads) team competition, the USSR team took first place (217 points out of 252 possible). This is followed by: China (201), Romania (201), Germany (174), Vietnam (166), USA (153), East Germany (145), Bulgaria (144), France (128), Canada (124). Let us especially note that the teams of Bulgaria and the GDR competed with incomplete teams - 5 schoolchildren each. The Australian team scored 110 points, one of the participants, Terence Tao, won a gold medal (34 points) and one, Jeffrey Bailey (20 points), won a silver medal. Terence Tao is competing for the third time in the national team - last year in Cuba he won a silver medal, and in 1986 in Warsaw - a bronze medal. And the most important thing is that during this year’s Olympics he turned only 13 years old and during lunch all the MMO participants gave him a loud shout, thereby expressing their admiration and respect for him.

Mathematical tests took place over two days, and all the remaining days of the stay were devoted to getting to know the country, its attractions, culture and art, nature, simple and well-organized individual sports events and mutual communication between members of various teams. One can talk endlessly about extraneous experiences from the excursion, about acquaintances that arose during the Olympic period, the inverted place and the creation of the Southern Cross, left-hand traffic, a kangaroo, a koala - a small bear cub the size of a pillow, a cockatoo, flying squirrels, etc., etc....

We will end with stories from the speech of the Prime Minister of Australia, Roberta Hawke, at the very solemn and moving closing ceremony of the XXIX IMO: “In areas such as debate and the arts, there is much opportunity for the encouragement and early discovery of talent, but in the intellectual field this is less common. The real Olympics represents just such an opportunity, and in a context where the pursuit of excellence is extremely important not only for specific individuals, but also for countries as a whole. Encouraging excellence in mathematics can only promote industrial and economic development, which in turn can lead to a better life for the people of all our countries...

As important as the mathematical accomplishment itself is, I believe that the cultural exchange that took place here is equally important.
