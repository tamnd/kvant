Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. L. GUTENMACHER

It often happens that the same mathematical problem naturally arises in modern situations - from puzzles to applied calculations. Today we are almost talking about one of these tasks.

Arithmetic problem

Find all quadruples of natural numbers (p, q, r, s) for which the equality on the right is

$$\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{r} + \frac{1}{s} = 1.$$

We will assume that $p \leq q \leq r \leq s$. Then the smallest number - $p$ - is not more than 4 (otherwise $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{r} + \frac{1}{s} < 1$) and, of course, not less than 2. Therefore, we need to consider three cases: $p=2$, $p=3$, $p=4$.

Let $p=2$. Then $\frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{r} + \frac{1}{s} = \frac{1}{2}$. Let's estimate the smallest number again - $q$. It cannot be more than 6 (otherwise $\frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{r} + \frac{1}{s} < \frac{1}{2}$) and must be at least 3.
