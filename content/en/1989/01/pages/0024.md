F1152. Behind the lens at a distance of $l=4$ cm (large focal length), a flat mirror is located perpendicular to the main optical axis. Problem solutions
M1116—M1120, F1128—F1132

Answer: a) 74; b) 6) $2S+2$.
A tract of size $m \times n$ cells contains $f=(m+1)(n+1)$ nodes. For a given area $mn=S$ the value

$$f=S+m+n+1=S+m+\frac{S}{m}+1$$

takes the most value $2S+2$ for $m=1$ or $m=S$ (for a rectangle of size $1 \times S$). For other $m$ ($1<m<S$), the value of $f$ is less than $2S+2$. This follows from inequality $m+\frac{S}{m}<S+1$, which is equivalent to the next inequality $(m-S)(m-1)<0$. Note that a more general statement is true: given $S$ (S is an integer or the resulting number), of all polygons with vertices at checkered paper nodes, the polygon that has no internal nodes contains the largest number of nodes. This follows from Pick's formula for cell $S$ of such polygons*: $S=i+\frac{r}{2}-1$, where $i$ is the number of nodes lying inside the polygon, and $r$ is the number of nodes on its boundary: the total number of nodes is $i+r \leq 2i+r=2S+2$. (Inequality turns into equality only at $i=0$.)

V. L. Gugenacher
