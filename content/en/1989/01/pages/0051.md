to the stars. A star is a giant cloud of gas and dust that tends to shrink under the influence of gravitational attraction. As a result of this decline, the temperature in the core of the star increases, and at some point a thermonuclear reaction is ignited: hydrogen nuclei merge and turn into helium. The energy released in this case further transforms the star into scatia, and the star stabilizes (it is at this stage of stellar evolution that the Sun is located). But gradually the hydrogen burns out, and tell us the possibility. The colossal pressure that arises in this case crushes the atoms. A state arises in which electrons seem to float freely in the field of bare nuclei. If the mass of the star is not too large (less than 1.25 solar masses), then the specific scaling that exists between the electrons transforms it further into compression (this repulsion is not associated with electrical forces, but is purely quantum in nature). As a result, a completely special state arises with enormous density (about 60 t/cm³). Stars arranged in this way are called white dwarfs (due to their light blue glow and small size). If the mass of the star is greater (more than 1.5-2 solar masses), then electrons cannot resist gravitational shrinkage. As a result, they (electrons) move into nuclei and, merging with protons, form neutrons. A substance appears, consisting not of atomic nuclei, but of neutrons, with a very fantastic high density (≈2·10⁹ t/cm³). These are neutron stars.
