Czech communications officer Prokop Diviš according to the rules of the unofficial tipula “magus of electricity” (magus electricus). In 1754, he built the first lightning rod in Europe, which he invented independently of Franklin. But let's fast forward a few years ago.

In 1750, Viennese professor Joseph Franz decided to show Divish his electrical experience. Franz had a static electricity generator typical of that time - a doctorable glass ball with a conductor (outlet) in the form of a metal tube or rod suspended on silk cords. To test any electrical experiment, it was necessary to rotate a glass ball, which was then electrified by friction and transmitted to the parquet.

Divish came to Franz in a wig, as fashion demanded. But the wig had a secret: it was covered all over with scraps of wire. It has long been known that the absence of metal “takes away electricity” (this is one of the phenomena on which the action of a lightning rod is based). By laying his head on the conductor, Divish thereby diverted the electric train. Probably, Divish experienced unpleasant mistakes, sending a couple into the ground through his body, but why not endure it for the sake of a good joke!
