2. The state of 1 mole of an ideal gas on the right side of the vessel at the initial moment is described by the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation:

$$p_1 V_1 = RT_1.$$

Here and henceforth, the index “1” will refer to the gas parameters on the right side of the vessel, and “2” - on the left. When we supply a small amount of heat $\Delta Q$ to the right side of the vessel, the parameters of the gas change, but will still satisfy the equation of state:

$$(p_1 + \Delta p_1)(V_1 + \Delta V_1) = R(T_1 + \Delta T_1).$$

Subtracting one equality from the other term by term, we get:

$$p_1 \Delta V_1 + V_1 \Delta p_1 + \Delta p_1 \Delta V_1 = R \Delta T_1.$$

As usual, assuming that in the process we are conducting all gas parameters change by small values ​​($\Delta T_1 \ll T_1$, $\Delta p_1 \ll p_1$, $\Delta V_1 \ll V_1$), we can neglect the small term $\Delta p_1 \Delta V_1$ compared to the others and write:

$$p_1 \Delta V_1 + V_1 \Delta p_1 = R \Delta T_1.$$

We can obtain a similar expression for 1 mole of gas on the left side of the vessel:

$$p_2 \Delta V_2 + V_2 \Delta p_2 = 0.$$

By definition of heat capacity

$$C = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}.$$

Using the first law of thermodynamics, for the gas on the right side of the container we can write:

$$\Delta Q = C \Delta T_1 = \Delta U_1 + p_1 \Delta V_1,$$

or

$$C \Delta T_1 = \frac{3}{2} R \Delta T_1 + p_1 \Delta V_1.$$

Since the pressures in both parts of the vessel remain equal all the time: $p_1 = p_2 = p$ and $\Delta p_1 = \Delta p_2 = \Delta p$, and the changes in volumes are related by the estimating relation $\Delta V_1 = -\Delta V_2$, from equalities (1) and (2) it is easy to obtain that

$$p_1 \Delta V_1 = \frac{R \Delta T_1}{1 + V_1/V_2},$$

and therefore

$$C = \frac{3}{2} R + \frac{R}{1 + V_1/V_2}.$$
