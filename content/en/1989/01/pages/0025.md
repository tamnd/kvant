druga touching each other in pairs (at points K, L, M); b) if through the middle of each arc KL, LM, MK lying inside the triangle, draw a tangent to it, then four triangles are formed, the area of ​​one of which (the central one) is equal to the sum of the areas of the other three.

The caital line drawn through the midpoint $C'$ for KL (with center C) cuts off from $\triangle ABC$ an equilateral triangle equal in area to the quadrilateral $CKOL$: the height $CC'$ cuts this equilateral triangle into two right triangles on the left, equal to $\Delta KCO = \Delta LCO$. Here it should also be noted that this tangent (at figure is red) always intersects sides CA and CB (angle COB is obtuse, since the half-sum of two angles B and C of triangle ABC is less than $90^\circ$, therefore the hypotenuse CO of triangle COK is less than CB; similar is $\angle COA > 90^\circ$, and therefore $CO < CA$). A similar rearrangement for the other two red tangents turns the equilateral triangles with altitudes $AA'$ and $BB'$ into quadrilaterals $ALOM$ and $BMOK$. Thus, the sum of the areas of three equilateral triangles with red bases is equal to the area of ​​triangle $ABC$. This implies the last statement of the problem: the area not covered by triangles is equal to the area covered by them twice.

A. A. Gorbachev

M1118. a) Prove that the equation $(x+y+z)^2=x^2+y^2+z^2$ (*) has infinite multisolutions in four numbers $(x, y, z)$ that do not have a common divisor greater than 1.
b) How many solutions are there for which $z=1988$?

a) Let us rewrite equation (*) in the form
$$xy+yz+zx=0.$$
Note that if one of the unknowns is 0, then some others are also 0; In what follows we will not consider such trivial solutions. Let $d$ be the most common divisor of $x$ and $y$, $x=da$, $y=ab$. then $z=-xy/(x+y)=-dab/(a+b)$, where $a, b$ and $a+b$ are pairwise coprime, so $d$ is divisible by $a+b:d=k(a+b)$. From here we obtain a general formula for nontrivial solutions:
$$x=ka(a+b), y=kb(a+b), z=-kab,$$
where $a, b, k$ are integers, and $a$ and $b$ are coprime. For $k=1$ we obtain all possible different solutions from numbers that do not have a common divisor greater than 1.
b) Answer: 16. The question boils down to how many ways can one represent 1988 as the product $-ab$, where $a$ and $b$ are coprime integers. Since 1988 = $4 \cdot 7 \cdot 71$, in the role of $a$ we can take the product of some numbers - 1, 4, 7, 71 (then $b$ will be the product of the rest; the product of the “empty set” can naturally be considered 1.)
S. G. Mamikonyan, N. V. Vasiliev
