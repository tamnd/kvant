XXIX
International Mathematical Olympiad

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. V. VAVILOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. FOMIN

The XXIX International Mathematical Olympid was held from July 9 to 21, 1988 in Australia in Sydney and Canberra and was part of the state program dedicated to celebrating the 200th anniversary of the founding of the Union of Australia.

Of course, we were all really looking forward to this Olympiad and prepared for it diligently throughout the year. Our country was represented by Sergei Berlov, Sergei Ivanov, Nikolai Filonov, Yuri Khokhlov (all four from Leningrad), Dmitry Ivanov (Dolgoprudny, Moscow region), Dmitry Tulyakov (Zhdanov). Sergei and Dmitry Ivanov now participated in the tenth grade and have a real chance of being included in the USSR team at the XXX Olympiad, which is in Germany in 1989; In addition, for Sergei Ivanov, the Olympid in Australia was already the second International Olympid, and in both he added substitute results. The leaders of the team's training camp and the team at the Olympics itself were the authors of this article. Members of the editorial board of the journal «Kvant» provided great assistance in preparing the team.

At the opening ceremony, Union Member of Parliament for Employment, Education and Training John Downis said: “Participation in this important international event shows what can be achieved when countries around the world come together to provide an enabling environment for the education of young people... By participating in this competition you are affirming the core principles that underpin education systems around the world: the principle of striving for excellence in all we do; the principle of cooperation both in the classroom and in all areas of working life; and the simple, but so important, principle of hard work..."

A record number of countries took part in the XXIX IMO - 58, of which 50 countries were represented by teams of schoolchildren, and 8 countries were represented only by observed ones. The Olympiad was held at a very high scientific and organizational level. It was successfully facilitated by extensive preparatory work, led by Prime Minister of Australia Robert Hawke, Minister of Employment, Education and Training John Downis, Chairman of the Academy of Sciences David Curtis, Director of the Canber College of Higher Education Roger Scott, Chairman of the Legal Public Mathematics Teacher Patrick Costello, Director of IBM Australia Brain Finn and many others.

According to the established tradition, international Olympids are held in two rounds, each of which offers three tasks. The round will take 4.5 hours of work time, and each task (regardless of its difficulty) is worth 7 points.
