⟦folio 44⟧

Every minute the cherished “five o’clock” was getting closer and closer.
At four I told the guys “to witness.”
“Are you leaving already, Chris?” Well, what about in the evening?
“Come in at eight,” I said. - Let's go see the new picture with Sally Ghiberti!
- That's right.
- Key to start!
And Ralph and I went home.
Mom was not at home, but on my bed there was an audio film on which she left a part of herself - her smile, her voice, her words. I inserted the video into the projector and put it on the wall. Soft brown hair, mother’s white face, her quiet voice:
- I don’t like to beg, Chris. I'll go to the laboratory and work there. Happy birthday to you. Hugged tightly. When I see you again... you will be a man.
That's all.
Priory waited outside the door, and I played the video for the fourth time.
- I don’t like to beg, Chris. I'll go... work... happily. Tightly... hugged...
I also recorded a video the night before. Now I put it in the projector and left it - two or three stray words.
Priory kept me waiting until halfway through. It’s impossible for him to go with me to the Spaceport. At the monorail station I shook his hand firmly and said:
- We had a great day today.
- Yeah. Now, what, until next Saturday?
“I wish I could answer yes.”
- Answer “yes” anyway. Next Saturday - forest, gang, packages, old Wickard with his trusty parapistol. We laughed at each other.
- Agreed. Next Saturday, early in the morning. And you take care... take care of our mother, okay, Priory, do you promise?
“What a stupid question, you idiot,” he said.
- Exactly, you idiot.
He took a sip.
— Chris.
- Yes?
- I'll be waiting. Just like you waited, and now you don't have to wait anymore. Will wait.
“I don’t think you’ll have to wait long, Ralph.” I hoped it wouldn't be long.
I lightly hit him once on the arm. He responded in kind.
The monorail door closed. Kabi rushed forward and Priory remained behind.
I got off at the Kosmoport stop. The control building was about five hundred meters away. I walked this stretch for ten years.
"When I see you again, you will be a man..."
“Not a word to anyone...”
"I'll be waiting, Chris..."
All this is like a cork in the heart, and you just don’t want to leave, and it floats before your eyes...
I thought about my dream. Moon rocket. Now she will no longer be a part of my soul, my dream. Now I will become a part of it.
I walked and walked and walked, feeling completely insignificant.
The very minute I approached the controls, the evening London rocket took off. She slipped onto the ground, and glided in and filled my heart with sweet trembling.
And I immediately began to grow terribly fast.
I followed the rocket with my eyes until someone's heels clicked in greeting next to me.
I'm petrified.
— K. M. Christopher?
- Yes, sir. Called, sir.
- This way, Christopher. These questions...
The fence to which a week ago we pressed our faces, and felt the wind getting hot, and pressed ourselves even harder against it, forgetting who we are, where we come from, dreaming only about who we could be and where we could go...
The fence, against which a week ago we pressed our faces, who liked being boys, liked living in a small Florida town, and certainly liked school, and liked playing football, and they liked dads and mothers too...
Boys who, every week, every hour, minute, incessantly thought about the flame, and the stars, and the vastness beyond which all this awaited them... Boys who liked more.
Mom, Ralph, we'll see you. I'll come back.
Mother! Ralph!
And I went through the gate and entered inside the fence.
