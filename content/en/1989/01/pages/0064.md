However, if the metal is heated, then the electrodes, due to their thermal movement, can overcome the energy threshold $W$ and leave the cadan. The number of electrons released per unit time depends only on the materials of the cadan and its temperature. The direction of $U$ determines whether all electrodes reach the anode or not. For a given kadan, the dependence of the saturation current on temperature is shown in figure 9. For the saturation current (the charge carried away by electrons per unit time), the Richardson equation is valid:

$$I_H = CT^2 \cdot \exp(-W/kT),$$

where $W$ is the electron work function, $C$ is a constant, $T$ is the Cadan temperature on the Kelvin scale, $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant.

The task is to change the work function of the $W$ electron from a given material. To do this, you are provided with the following devices: an AZ41 electronic tube (a two-hand vacuum rectifier tube with a knurled direction of no more than 4 V), a universal electronic measuring device (its internal resistance when the voltage changes is 10 MΩ), a 1.5 V battery, 4 9 V batteries (batteries can be contained in series), three resistors, the resistance of which can be determined by colored stripes (the resistance of the resistors is 1000 Ohm ± 2 %, 100 Ohm±2% and 47.5 Ohm±1%), 4 resistors of about 1 Ohm each (they are numbered), 12 screw terminals, 6 connected wires about 20 cm long each, 2 plug connections (suitable for a 9V battery), screwdriver, logarithmic paper, graph paper.

A graph of the resistivity of kadan materials versus the kadan temperature is shown in figure 10. The room temperature is known.

1) Determine the resistance values ​​of the numbered resistors. Do not use an ohmmeter.

2) Determine the saturation current at different incandescent temperatures, i.e., at different incandescents. Use a 1.5 V battery for the nakadaba current and control it using numbered resistors. By saturation current you should remember the current that is obtained by constantly pumping 36-40 V between the anomer and the cathode. Create this arrangement using four 9V batteries in series. Briefly explain how you determined the temperature of the pot.

3) Determine the work function $W$ from equation (*). Explain how you did it.
