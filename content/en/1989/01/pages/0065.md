malbio the authors of the problems “obeying the letter of the law,” since the topics of all problems are outlined in the program of the International Physics Olympiad, adopted by the International Commission in 1985 *). But the depth of interpretation of the topic outlined in the program can be any. The line in the program “Polarization of electromagnetic waves” corresponded to an experimental task, the successful implementation of which required a deep understanding of the phenomenon of polarization, birefringence, the phenomenon of integration of polarized light, and the existence of the phenomenon of elliptical polarization. Two words from the program - “Uncertainty relation” - allowed the author to include quantum mechanical problems both in theory and in experiment. The situation is similar with the interpretation of the words of the “Doppler Effect” program. This, of course, needs to be taken into account by schoolchildren and their teachers when preparing for subsequent Olympiads.

How did our schoolchildren cope with the proposed tasks?

The Soviet team scored 41 points out of 50 for the first problem (each problem in the theoretical and experimental rounds of evaluation is worth 10 points), for the second - 43 points, for the third - 25.5 points, for the fourth - 14 points, for the fifth - 32.13 points. Comparing these scores with the scores of other teams, we can conclude that the result for the first two problems is good, and for the fifth - absolutely the best. Our guys did a little worse on the third theoretical and first experimental tasks (for example, the Romanian team managed to score 39.5 points for the third task, and the Polish team received the maximum number of points - 23.5 - for the fourth task).

In terms of total points (155.63), the USSR team was second after the Romanian team (166.75). Teams
