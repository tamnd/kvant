$$T(m) \leftrightarrow (\ldots y_2 y_1 x_1, x_2 x_3 x_4 \ldots).$$

We see that double notation of the coordinates of a point allows us to represent the transformation $T$ in a very simple form - as a shift to the left of an infinite sequence of zeros and ones in both directions:

$$T: (\ldots z_{-1}, z_0 z_1 z_2 \ldots) \mapsto (\ldots z_{-1} z_0, z_1 z_2 \ldots).$$

In this notation, the numbers $z_n$ have a clear geometric meaning: if $z_n = 0$, then the transformation $T$ takes the point responsible for this sequence to the left half of the square in $n$ steps, and for $z_n = 1$ to the right.

5. Prove that the area $P$ of the set $M$ described in Exercise 4 is equal to $(1/2)^n$.

Heads or tails? We will interpret the sequence $(\ldots z_{-1}, z_0 z_1 \ldots)$ as a record of the results of a series of coin tosses with equal probabilities of getting “heads” and “tails”. In this case, $z_n = 0$ means that in the $n$th toss the result was “heads”, and $z_n = 1$ means that the result was “tails”. Experience tells us that the probability of the correct result of $n$ independent tosses is $(1/2)^n$, i.e.

the probability that $\{z_{i_1} = a_1, \ldots, z_{i_n} = a_n\}$ is equal to $(1/2)^n$, whatever the numbers $a_k$ and pairwise distinct numbers $i_k$. Thus, from Exercise 5 it follows that the probabilities of certain results of the toss game, written in the form of a set of sequences of zeros and ones, coincide with the area of ​​the set consisting of the corresponding points of the square.
