Its last launch took place in 1975.

The first Soviet long-term orbital station, Salyut, was launched in 1971. Following it, several more stations were launched into orbit. The United States limited itself to one space station (Skylab), made on the basis of the upper stages of the Saturn 5 launch vehicle (LV) (1973).

In the sixties, a number of launch vehicles of different classes were created in the USSR and the USA. Among them are the Soviet launch vehicles Cosmos, Soyuz and Proton. The last rocket is capable of launching a load of up to 20 tons into orbit. The most powerful US launch vehicle was the Saturn-5 launch vehicle created specifically for the lunar program (it launched more than 130 tons into low-Earth orbit). The rockets following it (of the Titan family) launched a cargo that was an order of magnitude inferior in mass to that of the Saturn 5.

As for the return of cargo and pilots from orbit, the practically only means were the manned ships themselves, or rather, their descent vehicles.

In the early seventies, in connection with the end of the Lunar research program, the United States stopped production of the Saturn 5 launch vehicle and the Apollo spacecraft and began developing the reusable space transport system (MTKS) Space Shuttle *). According to the original plan, the orbital stage included in its composition should be to deliver cargo and people to the new orbital station. Later, due to financial reports, production of the station was abandoned, and the Shuttle became “edin in two persons.”

The Soviet Union focused on the creation of orbital stations. The first generation stations have already been tested in flight. The next one was being developed (with two joints, with increased energy, with the possibility of refueling). This was a step towards the creation of permanent nursery stations. The delivery of crews and cargo was carried out sufficiently for decontamination, and also by serial, and therefore relatively cheap, means, so there was no need to create an MTKS similar to the American one. Moreover, the cost of launching the Space Shuttle turned out to be more than an order of magnitude higher than preliminary calculations. What was needed was a qualitative step that would solve the problems not only of the next decade, but also for many years to come.

To some extent, this step was the creation of the “Energy” - “Buran” kit. According to its characteristics, “Buran” is in many ways reminiscent of the American man. And the point here is not only in aerodynamics, the laws of which are the same. Many of the decisions made by American specialists were reasonable, and it is quite natural that our designers also came to similar decisions.
