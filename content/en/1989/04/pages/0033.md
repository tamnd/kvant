Let us compare the motion of a bead with the motion of a body thrown at an angle to the horizontal. In this case, we will choose the parameters of this movement such that the trajectory of the body is a parabola symmetrical to the trajectory of the bead (see Fig. 2). Then

$$u_x = \frac{OK}{t} \text{ и } t = \sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}},$$

where $OK = \sqrt{L^2 - h^2}$ and $H = (L + h)/2$.

Normal acceleration of the body at point $N'$

$$a'_n = g \cos \alpha = \frac{u^2}{R}.$$

Considering that $u^2 = u_x^2 + u_y^2$, where $u_y^2 = 2g(H - y)$, we obtain

$$R = \frac{u^2}{a'_n} = \frac{2(L - y)}{\cos \alpha}. \tag{3}$$

Let's combine equations (1) - (3) into a system and find the tension of the thread along which the bead slides:

$$T = \frac{mgL}{2(L - y)}.$$

The thread will break if $T = T_0$, i.e. when

$$y = L \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)$$

note that

$$v = \sqrt{2gy} = \sqrt{2gL \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)}.$$

$$0 \leq y \leq \frac{L + h}{2},$$

i.e. $$0 \leq L \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right) \leq \frac{L + h}{2}.$$

Thus, if the condition is met

$$2 \geq \frac{mg}{T_0} \geq 1 - \frac{h}{L},$$

then at the moment the thread breaks, the speed of the bead is

$$v = \sqrt{2gL \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)};$$

if $mg/T_0 > 2$, the sink will break at the initial moment; if $mg/T_0 < 1 - h/L$, then during the movement of the bead the thread will not break at all.
