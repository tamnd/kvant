⟦folio 33⟧

Zeke will fall off the platform?
(Assume that this time is much longer than the period of platform oscillations.)

The period of oscillation of the velocity component $v_x$ in the direction of the load rolling does not change. Moreover, during most of the period the horizontal velocity component is $v_y \gg v_x$. This means that the friction force is also directed horizontally, and the movement of the body along the $X$ axis will occur only under the influence of the rolling force:

$$v_x = at \quad \text{и} \quad t_x = \sqrt{2l/a}.$$

At the same time, friction will certainly introduce a small (due to the smallness of $c^{-1}$) correction. Let's evaluate it. The small magnitude of the friction force and the corresponding acceleration component along the axis $X$ are determined by the relation $v_x/\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}$:

$$b_x = b \frac{v_x}{\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}} = b \frac{v_x}{\sqrt{v_x^2+\omega^2 L^2 \sin^2 \omega t}}.$$

In this case, we believe that the motion along the $Y$ axis is purely harmonic. In fact, due to the presence of friction force, this movement will differ from the harmonic one, but the order of the correction will be such that it can be neglected (this will be discussed in more detail below).

Let's calculate the average value $b_x$:

$$\bar{b}_x = \frac{2\omega}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi/(2\omega)} bv_x/\sqrt{v_x^2+\omega^2 L^2 \sin^2 \omega t} \, dt.$$

For $t$, for which $\omega L \sin \omega t \gg v_x$, the velinium $v_x$ can be neglected, therefore

$$\bar{b}_x \approx \frac{2\omega}{\pi} \int_{v_x/(\omega^2 L)}^{\pi/(2\omega)} \frac{bv_x}{\omega L \sin \omega t} \, dt =$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi\omega} \frac{bv_x}{L} \int_{v_x/(\omega L)}^{\pi/2} \frac{d\varphi}{\sin \varphi} = -\frac{2bv_x}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{v_x}{2\omega L}.$$

More accurate calculations show that the value under the logarithm sign should be half as large; however, with the precision we chose, this difference is not important. Let us substitute here the expression $v_x=at$ we found as a first approximation and obtain

$$\bar{b}_x(t) = -\frac{2bat}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}.$$

Hence the total acceleration of movement

$$x''(t) = a - \bar{b}_x(t) = a + \frac{2bat}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}.$$

Interpreting this equation, we get

$$v_x(t) = x'(t) = at + \frac{8b}{\pi} \left( \frac{at}{4\omega L} \right)^2 \left( \frac{\ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}}{2} - \frac{1}{4} \right).$$
