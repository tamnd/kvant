⟦folio 18⟧

10. Show that the Conway polynomial is not a complete invariant, i.e., show that there are different nodes with the same Conway polynomials.

Note. The simplest example is a trefoil and its mirror image. To prove their non-equivalence, you need to model the Conway polynomial, replacing it with the Jones polynomial, which is defined in the same way as the Conway polynomial, only Axiom III changes:

$$\text{III}' \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} P_{L^+} - \sqrt{x} P_{L^-} = \frac{1 + \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x}} P_{L^0}.$$

Let's sum it up

Further, the necessary number of calculations performed shows that the Conway polynomial is a fairly subtle tool that allows you to distinguish between nodes and links and establish their non-trivial properties. By calculating, for example, the polynomials of trefoil, vosperka, and Borromean rings (and making sure that these polynomials are not equal to 0 or 1), we have proven that they cannot be untangled. Or, by calculating the corresponding polynomials, we proved that the links in Figures 7,δ,e are not equivalent, which is actually not very obvious.

Of course, these proofs are valid only if the fact of the existence and uniqueness of the Conway polynomial for each node (and link) has already been established. After all, simply constructing axioms does not guarantee the existence of an object that satisfies these axioms (what if the axioms are contradictory!). How can I get such a guarantee? Only by proving the existence and uniqueness of the Conway polynomial. But the elementary proof of this fact known to me is very cumbersome, and it will not be given here.

And finally, one last question:

So what should we do?

I hope the reader enjoys the articulate and elegant constructions described above. It must be said that in modern mathematics there are rarely intensively developing areas about which a student can be told anything in essence. Living mathematics, as a rule, describes a huge body of knowledge that goes far beyond the scope of the school (and even platoon) curriculum. Here everything is almost elementary, although quite new (the construction of the Conway polynomial, for example, dates back to the 70s). But we shouldn’t delude ourselves. What we managed to describe above could not, despite its attractiveness, arouse that increased interest in the theory of knots, which I spoke about at the beginning of the article.

The point here is different. Firstly, in the deep connections found by the English mathematician W. Jones between knot polynomials, statistical physics (more specifically, the so-called “Potts model” for ice) and quantum physics (the so-called “quantum groups”). Secondly, in the appearance of knots in molecular biology (knotting of circular DNA molecules *)). And thirdly, in the recent solution of the main problems of knot theory (the problems of comparison and disentanglement), based on the deep geometric work of the German mathematicians W. Haken and G. Waldhausen.

Currently, in the mathematical literature there is a description of algorithms for comparing and disentangling knots. However, these algorithms are still too complex for computer implementation. There is also a complete invariant for knots, invented by the Soviet mathematician S.V. Matveev. But its practical calculation is also inaccessible to a computer. Work in these directions continues.

*) Details can be found in the replacement book by M.D. Frank-Kamenetsky “The Most Important Molecule” (series “Vibloteka «Kvant»”, M., Nauka, 1983). Its author mistakenly believes that the Alexander polynomial is a complete invariant of a knot (see p. 109 of this book). However, in other places the author states that he is not a mathematician (p. 106).
