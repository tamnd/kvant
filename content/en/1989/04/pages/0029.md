From formula (*) it follows that $f(m)=m$ for numbers $m$, the double notation of which is symmetrical. To get all such numbers with $2k-1$ or $2k$ double signs, you need to put $a_1=1$ in the first place of the notation, fill in the next $k-1$ places $a_2$, ..., $a_k$ arbitrarily, and fill the remaining places symmetrically with the first $k$. This gives $2^{k-1}$ numbers. Since $2^{10}<1988<2^{11}$ and the only two 11-digit "binary symmetric" numbers greater than 1988=$\overline{11111000100}$ are $\overline{11111011111}$ and $\overline{11111111111}$, the exception number is $2(1+2+2^2+2^3+2^4)+2^5-2=92$.

M1133. Prove that the set of solutions does not intersect intervals whose sum of length is 1988.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} \frac{k}{x-k} \geq \frac{5}{4}$$

is a merging disjoint of intervals whose sum of length is equal to 1988.

The domain of definition of the inequality is the union of the half-lines $(-\infty; 1)$, $(70; \infty)$ and the intervals $(1; 2)$, $(2; 3)$, ..., $(69; 70)$. Obviously (see the example graph in the margin), on each of the intervals the lying part of the inequality is continuous and monotonically kills: from 0 to $-\infty$ and from $\infty$ to 0, respectively, on half-lines and from $\infty$ to $-\infty$ on the intervals. Consequently, on every interval except $(-\infty; 1)$, it takes the value 5/4 at exactly one point. Let us denote these points in ascending order by $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{70}$, then the set of solutions to the inequality is the union of semi-pitterles $(k; x_k)$, $k=1, 2, \dots, 70$. The numbers $x_k$ are the roots of the polynomial

$$\prod_{k=1}^{70} (x-k) - \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k \prod_{i \neq k} (x-i)^*.$$

According to Vieta’s theorem (see «Kvant», 1987, No. 4, p. 40), their sum is equal to the coefficient of $x^{69}$ with a minus sign, i.e.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} k + \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k$$

therefore, the sum of the half-pitter lengths is equal to

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} (x_k-k) = \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k=1988.$$

V. V. Vavilov
