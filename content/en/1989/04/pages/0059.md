And again - general stroboscopy

Let's consider another interesting manifestation of stroboscopy. When you watch TV, try moving your pointed finger up and down between the screen and your eyes, as shown in figure 2. You will notice the appearance of frozen, “standing” fingers. Moreover, these fingers, when moving the hand from top to bottom, will be wider and lighter than when moving the hand from bottom to top. Why is this so?

The image on the TV screen appears due to the fact that an electron beam is constantly scanning across the logoimaging screen from left to right and from top to bottom, which forms the image. When the beam hits any area of ​​the screen, a short-term glow will appear in that place. Running across the screen, the electron beam leaves behind a luminous line. After a moment, these lines fade out, so we can say that luminous frames are running across the screen. When we look at the screen, we do not see any migraines caused by the scanning beam. The fact is that the frequency of these migraines is very high - 50 times per second, and the dark gaps that appear on the screen between frames speed up our appearance.

Let your finger move mismo the screen so that it takes about 0.2 seconds to pass in front of the screen. This means that at a frame rate of 50 frames per second, 10 frames will run across the screen in 0.2 seconds. Moreover, if the finger moves from top to bottom, then the finger moves slower relative to the frames than when moving from bottom to top. Therefore, the long-term illumination of the finger by the frame in this case is greater, which means that the shadow from the finger remains longer. Consequently, in the case of a finger moving from top to bottom, the shadow is more spread out across the retina, so that the fingers appear wider and lighter. By adjusting the speed of finger movement, we can regulate the number of individual running frames illuminating the finger, and therefore the number of shadows formed by the finger on the retina. The speed of the finger can be selected such that the shadows from the finger will be resolved by the eye. So, in 0.2 seconds, 10 frames pass across the screen from top to bottom, and we can see 10 frozen fingers.
