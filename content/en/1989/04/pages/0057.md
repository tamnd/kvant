The miracle of the twentieth century - cinema - owes its existence entirely to the strokosoppic effect. This happens as follows. A film with sequential frames is pulled by successive jolts of the mismo lens of a film projector at a speed of 24 carats per second. Images depicted on two adjacent frames differ little from one another, and, thanks to the inertia of perception of our visual system, it seems to us that the depicted pictures are continuously moving across the screen.

Now we have to understand the “work” of our visual system.

A little physiology

Rays of light from an object, having passed through the optical system of the eye, consisting of a convex cornea, lens and semi-liquid vitreous body, are then collected on a kind of screen of the eye camera - on the retina. The retina is a complex structure consisting of several layers of nerve cells.

figure 1 shows a schematic section of the retina. The arrow shows the direction of light falling on the retina. In the outer layer 1, directly adjacent to the choroid, there are cells stained with black pigment. Then there are the basic elements of visual perception 2, called (by appearance) rods and cones. Layers 3-5 correspond to the upper fibers, suitable for the rods and cones. Behind these layers are the so-called granular layers 6 and 7, also connected by upper fibers. Layer 8 is the ganglion cells, each of which is connected to the superior fibers located in layer 9. Layer 10 is the internal limiting membrane. Each upper fiber turns out to be either a cone or a group of rods. (Figure 1 and the marriages to it are taken from the book by S. I. Vavilov “The Eye and the Sun”.)

The human head has about 7 million cones and about 130 million rods. The study shows that rods are more sensitive to light than cones, but tanners are capable of color vision. In what follows, when we say “rods,” we will mean both rods and cones.

Light hitting the photosensitive substance of the rod—rhodopisin—causes its breakdown into retinene and opsin. As a result of this, one rod is excited, and an excitation impulse travels along the optic nerve to the brain. And retinene and opsin are converted through a chain of chemical reactions into rhodopisin.
