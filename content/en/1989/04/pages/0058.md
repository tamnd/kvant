The inertia of vision, which is used, in particular, in cinema, manifests itself in the fact that the visual system is able to “remember” an image for a short time - about 0.2 seconds. During this time, she “remembers” the previous signal, but is not yet able to perceive the next one. If a time interval passes between the signals, changing the “memorization” time, then successive images merge, and the observed ones seem to be continuous. This is why we cannot distinguish the dark spaces between frames on a movie screen. In general, the visual system is capable of confidently, without special modifications, feeling the impact of approximately 10 backgrounds and registering a flash in the dark lasting 0.000001 seconds.

Note that the semantic “stitching” of an image and the conclusion that changing images are the result of the movement of the same object are carried out unrepresented in the human brain, and its visual system does not play any role here.

Amazing “eye-voice” effect

The first report of this interesting effect appeared in 1967. It consists in the fact that it turns out to be possible to “stop” a rotating object with the help of... your voice. Moreover, if for ordinary strokosoppia a tower lamp is needed, then for “ocular-vocal” strokosoppia the ordinary sun is completely suitable.

The idea behind the effect is as follows. Let's imagine that we are looking at black and white stripes running from top to bottom. The retina of our eye will show images of these stripes. It is possible to force our eye to move in the plane of movement of the stripes, and with such an amplitude and frequency that during most of the period of eye selection the image of the stripes on the retina remains motionless. Under these conditions, the illusion of stopped stripes will arise.

It turns out that you can set the necessary parameters for eye vibration using your voice. The vibrations of the vocal cords are transmitted through the bones of the ceren to the eye, and by choosing the volume and timbre of the voice, you can “stop” the bands. Instead of the voice, you can use vibrations of the tongue when exhaling and its beating against the palate, but the result will not be effective. In principle, for these purposes you can use a loudspeaker connected to a signal generator, a motor whose frequency can be adjusted, or a simple vibrating electric massager. By pressing your head against it, you can achieve the desired effect.

Using the method described, you can “stop” the rotating ventilation of an aircraft, the spokes of a bicycle wheel, the fan blades and other wheel or rotating systems. Some people have been known to be able to use the hum to cause stripes to appear and change the picture quality on their TV screen. True, such experiments require patience and debate and therefore not everyone succeeds.
