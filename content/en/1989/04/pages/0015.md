Operations of the Reidemeister

Let's analyze how we solved the problem of untangling the knot $Y_1$. From figure 3 it is clear that during the process of unraveling, the appearance of the knot fundamentally changes only at those moments when the number or nature of the arrangement of double points changes. One double point can disappear when we unwind a small loop (Fig. 3, $\delta$), a pair of adjacent double points can disappear when one arc slides off another (Fig. 3, $a$), and finally a pair of double points can jump over a third (Fig. 3, $\delta$). Situations before this operation on figure 3 are highlighted with yellow circles; all three types of operations are reproduced separately on figure 4. There is a special name for them - the Reidemeister operation, named after the German mathematician who isolated them and proved the following in his early years.

Reidemeister operation: $\Omega_1^{\pm}$ - unwinding (and twisting) of loops; $\Omega_2^{\pm}$ - slipping of one branch from another and overlapping of one branch on another; $\Omega_3$ - transfer of a branch through a double point.
