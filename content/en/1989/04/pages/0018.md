⟦folio 16⟧

Figure 6. Main relation (axiom III) for Convel polynomials.

answer polynomials related relation

$$P_{L^+}(x) - P_{L^-}(x) = x \cdot P_{L^0}(x)$$

We will not discuss why for each $L$ the polynomial $P_{L}(x)$ exists and is uniquely determined by these axioms, but we will learn, based on the axioms, to calculate this polynomial.

Let's look at figure 7,a. It shows a diagram (labeled $L^+$) of a trivial knot with one double point. From axioms I and II it follows that the polynomial $P_{L^+}(x)$ is equal to 1. If we replace the double point of the diagram $L^+$ with the opposite one, and then destroy the double point (in accordance with Axiom III), then we obtain another diagram of the trivial knot $L^-$ and a pair of unlinked circles $L^0$. Applying relation III, we obtain

$$P_{L^+}(x) - P_{L^-}(x) = x \cdot P_{L^0}(x),$$

i.e. $1 - 1 = x \cdot P_{L^0}(x)$, whence $P_{L^0}(x) = 0$, i.e. a pair of unlinked circles has a zero Convel polynomial.

Now let's calculate the Convel polynomial of a pair of linked circles. On it with replacement: since we are considering oriented lines, such pairs come in two varieties: right and left. To explain the difference between them, consider one of the circles as the first, and the other as the second. A line piercing the plane of the first circle inside this circle is directed according to the “reverse” gimlet rule; if the second circle has exactly this orientation, we consider the linked pair to be left, otherwise - right (Fig. 8).

Fig. 7. Convel polynomials: a) for a pair of unlinked circles $L^0(P_{L^0}(x) = 0)$; b) for a pair (see Fig. 8) of linked circles $L^+(P_{L^+}(x) = x)$; c) for the trefoil knot $L^+(P_{L^+}(x) = x^2 + 1)$; d) figure eight knot $L^+$; e) pierced figure eight for engagement; f) for other variants of the pierced figure eight.
