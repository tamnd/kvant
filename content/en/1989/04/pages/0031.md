F1143. There are a very large number of vessels with water, piled one into the other so that each next one floats in the previous one. The day area of ​​the smallest vessel is $s_0$ and much less than the day area of ​​the largest. A volume of water $v_0$ is added to the smallest vessel. How far will this vessel descend relative to the ground? (After adding water, all the vessels continue to float.)

Let us consider the equilibrium condition of any of the floating vessels: the force of gravity of this vessel is balanced by the difference in water pressure forces from the outside and from the inside. Thus, both before and after adding water to any of the vessels, the difference in water levels outside and inside each vessel remains the same. This means that relative to the ground, the position of the water levels in all vessels remains unchanged.

So, relative to the ground, the water level in the smallest vessel does not change after adding water to it. Consequently, this vessel will drop by amounts

$$h = \frac{v_0}{s_0},$$

i.e. to the height of the thin layer of water.

S. S. Krotov

F1144. There are $N$ identical large balls on the plane. One ball was pushed, and, having experienced several vessels, it remained at the same point from which it began to move. At what minimum $N$ is this possible? The collisions are considered to be absolutely elastic.

First of all, we note that after an absolutely elastic collision of two identical balls, one of which was previously dug, the balls spread out at right angles. Indeed, according to the law of conservation of momentum (Fig. 1; here $v_0$ is the speed of the incoming ball before the impact, $v_1$ and $v_2$ the speeds of the balls after the impact)

$$m\vec{v}_0 = m\vec{v}_1 + m\vec{v}_2,$$

and according to the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{m v_0^2}{2} = \frac{m v_1^2}{2} + \frac{m v_2^2}{2},$$

it follows that the velocities $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ of the balls after the impact are perpendicular to each other: $\vec{v}_1 \perp \vec{v}_2$.

Now we will show that, having experienced one vessel, the ball deviates from the direction of its motion by an angle $\beta < \pi/2$. According to figure 2, $\beta = \pi/2 - \alpha$, where $0 \leq \alpha \leq \pi/2$. If $\alpha = 0$, the first ball is abandoned after the hit (and the second ball starts moving at a speed of $\vec{v}_2 = = \vec{v}_0$). Therefore, $\beta < \pi/2$.

According to the conditions of the problem, one of the balls (we will call it the first), having experienced several vessels, must describe a closed broken line. As is known, the sum of the external angles of such a broken line is greater than or equal to 2π (the equal sign corresponds to a convex polygon).
