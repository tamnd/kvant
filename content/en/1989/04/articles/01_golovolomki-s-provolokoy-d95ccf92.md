How to thread thick wire through a narrow gap? Anyone who wants to calculate the figures shown will have a hard time racking their brains over this. But the reader «Kvant» should also be interested in a purely geometric question: at what smallest width l is the calculated gap possible if the wire diameter is equal to d?

At figure A is a cross-section of a puzzle with figures of animals (Fig. 1) in the place of the gap at the time of calculation. Obviously, l here l=($\sqrt{2}-1$)d≈0.41d. For the nails puzzle (Fig.2) the calculation is more complicated. If you mentally cut out an area near the gap from one nail, you get two cylinders with crossing axes. When spread apart, these cylinders slide screw-wise along two sections of other nails that intersect the cylinders. At the critical moment, the axes of all four cylinders can be represented as the dizonals of the lateral faces of the right-hand quadrangular prism (Fig. B). By setting the dimensions of the prism a and b, you can find the distance d between adjacent dizonals, and it is equal to the largest (given a and b) diameter of the nails: $d=ab/\sqrt{a^2+2b^2}=a/\sqrt{2}+tg^2(\alpha/2)$, where $\alpha$ is the angle at which the nails are aligned. figureB shows the projection of our auxiliary prism onto the base and two cylindrical pieces of a single nail. Obviously, the width of the gap between these pieces is l=a-d=($\sqrt{2}+tg^2(\alpha/2)-1$)d. In particular, at $\alpha=90^\circ$ there should be no less than ($\sqrt{3}-1$)d≈0.73 d. The gap is calculated similarly for the two remaining puzzles.
