One shuttle, two shuttle...

V. V. NIKOLAEV

And the flame is brighter, faith is gone,
How simple is the deceptive shuttle.
V. Ropshin, 1911

For a long time, any reputable enterprise has had to rely on three pillars. Human exploration of near-Earth space in this sense is no exception. “Whales” of astronautics are package-space systems that provide payload launch into orbit, means for its return to Earth and orbital facilities.

In the first years of the space age, the USSR and the USA adhered to similar concepts in building their space programs, including manned ones, but over the years, differences began to appear more and more often.

In 1961, the world's first spacecraft, Vostok, was launched in the USSR. A ship similar (in terms of tasks) - "Mercury" - was created in the same year in the USA. They were followed by Voskhod and Gemini. The next generation of ships - Soyuz and Apollo, although they appeared at approximately the same time (1967-1968), differed not only in design, but also in their purpose. The Soyuz spacecraft was conceived as a vehicle to work mainly with orbital stations and (subject to conditions) is in operation and complete. The number of launches reached six tens! The Progress cargo ship was also made on its basis (forty more launches). The Apollo spacecraft was built as part of the American expedition to the Moon and, after its brilliant completion (in 1972), was used several times for flights to the American Skylab station and under the program of the Soviet-American project Soyuz - Apollo.

Its last launch took place in 1975.

The first Soviet long-term orbital station, Salyut, was launched in 1971. Following it, several more stations were launched into orbit. The United States limited itself to one space station (Skylab), made on the basis of the upper stages of the Saturn 5 launch vehicle (LV) (1973).

In the sixties, a number of launch vehicles of different classes were created in the USSR and the USA. Among them are the Soviet launch vehicles Cosmos, Soyuz and Proton. The last rocket is capable of launching a load of up to 20 tons into orbit. The most powerful US launch vehicle was the Saturn-5 launch vehicle created specifically for the lunar program (it launched more than 130 tons into low-Earth orbit). The rockets following it (of the Titan family) launched a cargo that was an order of magnitude inferior in mass to that of the Saturn 5.

As for the return of cargo and pilots from orbit, the practically only means were the manned ships themselves, or rather, their descent vehicles.

In the early seventies, in connection with the end of the Lunar research program, the United States stopped production of the Saturn 5 launch vehicle and the Apollo spacecraft and began developing the reusable space transport system (MTKS) Space Shuttle *). According to the original plan, the orbital stage included in its composition should be to deliver cargo and people to the new orbital station. Later, due to financial reports, production of the station was abandoned, and the Shuttle became “edin in two persons.”

The Soviet Union focused on the creation of orbital stations. The first generation stations have already been tested in flight. The next one was being developed (with two joints, with increased energy, with the possibility of refueling). This was a step towards the creation of permanent nursery stations. The delivery of crews and cargo was carried out sufficiently for decontamination, and also by serial, and therefore relatively cheap, means, so there was no need to create an MTKS similar to the American one. Moreover, the cost of launching the Space Shuttle turned out to be more than an order of magnitude higher than preliminary calculations. What was needed was a qualitative step that would solve the problems not only of the next decade, but also for many years to come.

To some extent, this step was the creation of the “Energy” - “Buran” kit. According to its characteristics, “Buran” is in many ways reminiscent of the American man. And the point here is not only in aerodynamics, the laws of which are the same. Many of the decisions made by American specialists were reasonable, and it is quite natural that our designers also came to similar decisions.

However, there are also significant differences. This especially applies to all the “Energy” - “Buran” sets. The main thing here is the presence of a launch vehicle capable of functioning independently of the Buran. "Energy",

A year later, they completely “trusted the automation,” and it did not disappoint—it landed the plane from a height of 4 km.

Another two years passed before Buran “received certification” for orbital flight. By this time, a landing strip about 5 km long and 80 m wide had been prepared for him. An airfield was established with all the necessary radio equipment to ensure all-year landing, including automatic.

So, on November 15, 1988, the universal rocket launch vehicle Energia, which was attached to the Buran orbital ship, lifted off from the launch pad. We have completed the allotted time for the “side beam”, the second stage...
The time has come for Buran to separate.
Eat! The orbiter's engines are turned on. (A feature of the Buran launch scheme is that the launch vehicle at the end of the active phase creates the conditions for the Buran itself to launch it into orbit. The entire launch phase of the launch vehicle takes about 8 minutes.) Now, by launching the propulsion system twice, the ship is launched into the reference orbit. Half of the task—injection into orbit—has been completed. Telemetry already tells us how the glider behaves in an unfamiliar environment, where there is no air flow or weight. One turn, the second... The landing program is launched - the most important part of the test. The ship turns forward with its engines to create a braking impulse, and before entering the atmosphere it again assumes its normal position. The signal from the ship has disappeared - Buran is flying in plasma. At an altitude of approximately 40 km, 400 kilometers from the landing strip, the interaction of on-board systems with airfield facilities begins...

Buran is approaching the landing site. Radio beacons-relays

In the last, pre-landing section, the microwave radio-technical automatic landing system forms a “radiopath” that exactly corresponds to the specified descent line. So, the first space flight of Buran was completed. Having launched from the Baikonur Cosmodrome at 6 a.m. Moscow time, he returned there 205 minutes later.

What to do, or rather, what will the creation of a Soviet reusable ship give us? (Today, experience has already been accumulated in operating the American Shuttle - as of January 1, 1989, in almost 8 years from the start of its operation, only 26 successful flights of four of its models took place, i.e., on average, one flight of each spacecraft per year: this is much less than planned. For launching conventional cargo into orbit, this system turned out to be not so effective. The ship is simply not suitable for long-term work in orbit, and it makes no sense to turn it into a station - it would be too expensive a convenience.) The ability to deliver failed satellites from orbit?

They say that the most interesting thing for me is the person who is interested in me. Therefore, let’s listen to the designer of “Buran” - Yu. P. Semenov.

“Returning satellites that have reached their end of life to Earth for re-launch or simply delivering them from orbit due to unforeseen failures is just one of the tasks that can be solved by the Buran spacecraft.” It’s hard to expect any special “gain” here, if we take into account... that over time the obsolescence of satellites will occur... But in some cases, the return to Earth of a unique and very expensive research complex may be extremely important... But still, we consider the task of return as being solved along the way, and the main one is the launch of expensive objects based on unique scientific instruments - for example, a large optical telescope with complex electronic equipment.*)

Another direction, says Yu. P. Semenov, is the implementation of expensive projects involving the use of manipulators and robots. In other cases, unambiguous means of deduction still cost less. Therefore, the reported space program is supposed to be built on a reasonable combination of means. An example is the currently discussed project of a ship (weighing more than 450 tons) for a Mars expedition. The interplanetary ship is supposed to be assembled in satellite orbit from blocks launched by Energey, with the participation of Buran.

There are other ideas for using both “Buran” and “Energia”, and yet I believe that here, as often happened (and starting under S.P. Korolev), supply is driven by demand. The time will come, and customers will appear both within the country and abroad. Thus, with the introduction of the Energia-Buran complex into examination, the Soviet cosmonautics will have quite strong whales: LV - with a payload capacity of several tons (with prospects for increasing to several hundred tons); the Buran spacecraft, capable of launching 30 tons into orbit and returning approximately half of this mass; a permanent orbital complex based on the Mir station and models of the «Kvant» type.

No one has such a large set of funds. And here it is very important not to miss the opportunity that our achievements in the multi-characteristics of astronautics provide...
