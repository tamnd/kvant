M1132. The function $f$ defines the set of positive integers and satisfies the following conditions:
$$f(1) = 1, f(3) = 3, f(2n) = f(n),$$
$$f(4n+1) = 2f(2n+1) - f(n),$$

$$f(m) = a_k a_{k-1} \dots a_2 a_1.$$

$$f(\bar{11}) = f(3) = 3 = \bar{11}.$$

1) $m$ is even: $m=2n = a_1 \dots a_{k-1} 0$. Then $n = a_1 \dots a_{k-1}$ and

M1133. Prove that the set of solutions does not intersect intervals whose sum of length is 1988.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} \frac{k}{x-k} \geq \frac{5}{4}$$

is a merging disjoint of intervals whose sum of length is equal to 1988.

The domain of definition of the inequality is the union of the half-lines $(-\infty; 1)$, $(70; \infty)$ and the intervals $(1; 2)$, $(2; 3)$, ..., $(69; 70)$. Obviously (see the example graph in the margin), on each of the intervals the lying part of the inequality is continuous and monotonically kills: from 0 to $-\infty$ and from $\infty$ to 0, respectively, on half-lines and from $\infty$ to $-\infty$ on the intervals. Consequently, on every interval except $(-\infty; 1)$, it takes the value 5/4 at exactly one point. Let us denote these points in ascending order by $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{70}$, then the set of solutions to the inequality is the union of semi-pitterles $(k; x_k)$, $k=1, 2, \dots, 70$. The numbers $x_k$ are the roots of the polynomial

$$\prod_{k=1}^{70} (x-k) - \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k \prod_{i \neq k} (x-i)^*.$$

According to Vieta’s theorem (see «Kvant», 1987, No. 4, p. 40), their sum is equal to the coefficient of $x^{69}$ with a minus sign, i.e.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} k + \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k$$

therefore, the sum of the half-pitter lengths is equal to

$$\sum_{k=1}^{70} (x_k-k) = \frac{4}{5} \sum_{k=1}^{70} k=1988.$$

M1135. Let a and b be positive integers such that $a^2+b^2$ is divisible by ab+1 without remainder. Prove that $\frac{a^2+b^2}{ab+1}$ is the square of an integer.

Let a and b be natural numbers and

$$\frac{a^2+b^2}{1+ab}=k.$$

Then b is the root of the equation

$$x^2-kax+a^2-k=0.$$

If c is the second root of this equation, k is an integer and a<b, then

1) $(a^2+c^2)/(1+ac)=k$ (follows from the equation);
2) c is an integer, since c=ka-b (Vieta’s theorem);
3) c<a, т. к. bc=a^2-k<a^2 и b>a;
4) c≥0, because

$$\left(\frac{1}{a}+c\right)\left(\frac{1}{b}+b\right)=\left(-\frac{1}{a}-c\right)\left(-\frac{1}{b}-b\right)=$$

$$=\left(-\frac{1}{a}\right)^2-ka\left(-\frac{1}{a}\right)+a^2-k=\frac{1}{a^2}+a^2>0,$$

and therefore the integer c satisfies the inequality $c>-1/a$.

If c=0, then b=a^3. This is the “main series” of pairs (a, b) satisfying the conditions; for them k=a^2. If c>0, then the pair (a, b) can be replaced by a pair (c, a) with the same ratio k, to which our reasoning is again applicable, etc. In the process of “descent”, the most number of the pair decreases each time, so it must end on some pair (0, n), which precedes the pair (n, n^3) of the “main series”; then k=n^2. Finally, if the numbers of the original pair (a, b) are equal to each other, then the ratio k can be an integer only when a=b=1; then k=1.

Our reasoning allows us to construct an infinite sequence of pairs that satisfy the conditions, starting from any pair (0, n). For example, with n=2, k=4 we get pairs (0, 2), (2, 8), (8, 30), (30, 4 30−8)=(30, 112)...

F1143. There are a very large number of vessels with water, piled one into the other so that each next one floats in the previous one. The day area of ​​the smallest vessel is $s_0$ and much less than the day area of ​​the largest. A volume of water $v_0$ is added to the smallest vessel. How far will this vessel descend relative to the ground? (After adding water, all the vessels continue to float.)

Let us consider the equilibrium condition of any of the floating vessels: the force of gravity of this vessel is balanced by the difference in water pressure forces from the outside and from the inside. Thus, both before and after adding water to any of the vessels, the difference in water levels outside and inside each vessel remains the same. This means that relative to the ground, the position of the water levels in all vessels remains unchanged.

So, relative to the ground, the water level in the smallest vessel does not change after adding water to it. Consequently, this vessel will drop by amounts

$$h = \frac{v_0}{s_0},$$

i.e. to the height of the thin layer of water.

S. S. Krotov

F1145. One end of an inextensible weightless thread, threaded through a small bead of mass m, is fixed at point A, and the other is attached to a weightless ring, which can slide freely along a horizontal rod (Fig. 1). At the initial moment, the bead is held by the ring, the thread is straight and not stretched. The bead is released. Find the speed of the bead at the moment the thread breaks, if it is known that the thread can withstand the maximum tension $T_0$. The length of the thread is L, the distance from point A to the rod is h. Neglect the friction in the system.

Fig. 1.

To determine the trajectory of the bead, we introduce a coordinate system as shown in figure 2 (the X axis is directed along the rod to the right, and the Y axis is directed vertically downward and passes through the point of fastening the thread A). Let the bead be at point N. Since the ring is weightless and slides freely along the rod, the upper section of the thread BN is vertical. Then

$$BN = y \text{ и } PN = x.$$

From triangle APN we have

$$AP^2 + PN^2 = AN^2,$$

or

$$(h - y)^2 + x^2 = (L - y)^2.$$

From here we get

$$y = \frac{L + h}{2} - \frac{x^2}{2(L - h)},$$

that is, the bead moves along a parabola.

At point N of the trajectory, the tension forces $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$ act on the bead from the side of the thread, and their sum $\vec{F}$ is directed perpendicular to the tangent to the parabola at this point. (Indeed, the tension force of the thread alone cannot cause the movement of the bead.) Now, since the thread is ideal and there is no friction of the bead on the thread, $T_1 = T_2 = T$, and, therefore, the force $\vec{F}$ is directed along the bisectrice angle between the vectors $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$.

Let us write down Newton’s second law for the bead in projections onto the direction perpendicular to the tangent to the trajectory:

$$ma_n = 2T \cos \alpha - mg \cos \alpha.$$

On the other hand, normal (centripetal) acceleration is

$$a_n = v^2/R,$$

where $v = \sqrt{2gy}$ is the speed of the bead and $R$ is the radius of curvature

we have parabolas at point $N$. To solve the problem, we just need to find $R$.

Let us compare the motion of a bead with the motion of a body thrown at an angle to the horizontal. In this case, we will choose the parameters of this movement such that the trajectory of the body is a parabola symmetrical to the trajectory of the bead (see Fig. 2). Then

$$u_x = \frac{OK}{t} \text{ и } t = \sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}},$$

where $OK = \sqrt{L^2 - h^2}$ and $H = (L + h)/2$.

Normal acceleration of the body at point $N'$

$$a'_n = g \cos \alpha = \frac{u^2}{R}.$$

Considering that $u^2 = u_x^2 + u_y^2$, where $u_y^2 = 2g(H - y)$, we obtain

$$R = \frac{u^2}{a'_n} = \frac{2(L - y)}{\cos \alpha}. \tag{3}$$

Let's combine equations (1) - (3) into a system and find the tension of the thread along which the bead slides:

$$T = \frac{mgL}{2(L - y)}.$$

The thread will break if $T = T_0$, i.e. when

$$y = L \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)$$

note that

$$v = \sqrt{2gy} = \sqrt{2gL \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)}.$$

$$0 \leq y \leq \frac{L + h}{2},$$

i.e. $$0 \leq L \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right) \leq \frac{L + h}{2}.$$

Thus, if the condition is met

$$2 \geq \frac{mg}{T_0} \geq 1 - \frac{h}{L},$$

then at the moment the thread breaks, the speed of the bead is

$$v = \sqrt{2gL \left(1 - \frac{mg}{2T_0}\right)};$$

if $mg/T_0 > 2$, the sink will break at the initial moment; if $mg/T_0 < 1 - h/L$, then during the movement of the bead the thread will not break at all.

On a platform inclined at a small angle α to the horizontal, at a distance l from its lower edge lies a small weight, the mass of which is much less than the mass of the platform (see figure). The platform performs a harmonious oscillation along the OO' axis; the oscillation frequency is ω, the amplitude is L. The third coefficient between the weight and the platform is k. How long will it take a Georgian to solve a problem in a non-inertial system associated with a fluttering platform? For example, the Y axis is along the vibration axis OO', and the X axis is perpendicular to this axis. In the plane of the platform, three forces act on the weight - the so-called rolling force (projection of the force of gravity on the X axis) mg sin α, the friction force kmg cos α and the inertial force mω²L cos ωt (if the platform oscillates according to the law y=L cos ωt).

Since the oscillations of the weight plate are fast, we can assume that the inertial force is greater than all other forces. Let us denote characteristic quantities with the dimension of acceleration by a, b and c:

$$a = g \sin \alpha, \ b = kg \cos \alpha, \ c = \omega^2 L.$$

Then c>>a and c>>b. In this case, you need to think about what

Zeke will fall off the platform?
(Assume that this time is much longer than the period of platform oscillations.)

The period of oscillation of the velocity component $v_x$ in the direction of the load rolling does not change. Moreover, during most of the period the horizontal velocity component is $v_y \gg v_x$. This means that the friction force is also directed horizontally, and the movement of the body along the $X$ axis will occur only under the influence of the rolling force:

$$v_x = at \quad \text{и} \quad t_x = \sqrt{2l/a}.$$

At the same time, friction will certainly introduce a small (due to the smallness of $c^{-1}$) correction. Let's evaluate it. The small magnitude of the friction force and the corresponding acceleration component along the axis $X$ are determined by the relation $v_x/\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}$:

$$b_x = b \frac{v_x}{\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}} = b \frac{v_x}{\sqrt{v_x^2+\omega^2 L^2 \sin^2 \omega t}}.$$

In this case, we believe that the motion along the $Y$ axis is purely harmonic. In fact, due to the presence of friction force, this movement will differ from the harmonic one, but the order of the correction will be such that it can be neglected (this will be discussed in more detail below).

Let's calculate the average value $b_x$:

$$\bar{b}_x = \frac{2\omega}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi/(2\omega)} bv_x/\sqrt{v_x^2+\omega^2 L^2 \sin^2 \omega t} \, dt.$$

For $t$, for which $\omega L \sin \omega t \gg v_x$, the velinium $v_x$ can be neglected, therefore

$$\bar{b}_x \approx \frac{2\omega}{\pi} \int_{v_x/(\omega^2 L)}^{\pi/(2\omega)} \frac{bv_x}{\omega L \sin \omega t} \, dt =$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi\omega} \frac{bv_x}{L} \int_{v_x/(\omega L)}^{\pi/2} \frac{d\varphi}{\sin \varphi} = -\frac{2bv_x}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{v_x}{2\omega L}.$$

More accurate calculations show that the value under the logarithm sign should be half as large; however, with the precision we chose, this difference is not important. Let us substitute here the expression $v_x=at$ we found as a first approximation and obtain

$$\bar{b}_x(t) = -\frac{2bat}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}.$$

Hence the total acceleration of movement

$$x''(t) = a - \bar{b}_x(t) = a + \frac{2bat}{\pi\omega L} \ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}.$$

Interpreting this equation, we get

$$v_x(t) = x'(t) = at + \frac{8b}{\pi} \left( \frac{at}{4\omega L} \right)^2 \left( \frac{\ln \frac{at}{4\omega L}}{2} - \frac{1}{4} \right).$$

Discarding the terms compared to ln $(at/(\omega L))$, we get

$$x'(t) = at + \frac{4b}{\pi} \left( \frac{at}{4\omega L} \right)^2 \ln \frac{at}{\omega L} \cdot \frac{4\omega L}{a},$$
