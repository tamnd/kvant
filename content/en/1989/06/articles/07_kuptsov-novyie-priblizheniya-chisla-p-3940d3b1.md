⟦folio 29⟧

The performance of silver halide sunglasses is measured in minutes. With such a reaction speed, the change in the surroundings of the photochromic suit would not keep up with the changes in illumination that occurred during movement. But in more advanced photochromic systems—for example, in glasses used to protect the eyes from the flash that accompanies a nuclear explosion—the reaction time is reduced to microseconds. Systems with similar properties would be perfect for a photochromic suit.

The role of light and shadow in visual perception has been discussed in detail by J. Beck (Scientific American, Aug. 1975, p. 62); an object that does not obey the general law of distribution of light and shadow is very difficult, and sometimes impossible, to recognize, no matter how closely we look at it. The eye is not able to determine the surface texture and shape without the usual light and shade transitions. Now imagine a room whose walls, floor and ceiling are covered with photochromic wallpaper. No matter how bright the lighting, any photochromic object placed in this room will appear invisible because there will be no contrast between it and the background. What scope for illusion!

New approximations for pi

The reader «Kvant» is well aware that the problem of squaring a circle, that is, constructing a square equal in size to a given circle, is unsolvable using a compass and a ruler. This means that, given a unit segment, it is impossible to construct a segment of length π. Therefore, the problem of constructing segments whose lengths well approximate the number π≈3.1415926... has long been of interest to mathematicians.

Here are some classical approximations for π:

22/7=3.1428571... (Archimedes, 287-212 BC);
355/113=3.1415929... (A. Mecius, Holland, 1543-1620);

$$\sqrt{\frac{40}{3}-2\sqrt{3}}=3,1415333$$

(A. Kochanski, Poland, 1631-1700);

$$\sqrt{9-3\sqrt{6}}+\sqrt{1+\sqrt{6}}=3,1423991$$

(J. Mascheroni, Italy, 1750-1800).

Evaluate for yourself the accuracy of these approximations and think about how to construct the corresponding segments of a compass and ruler.

Here are some approximations of π found by the author:

$$\sqrt{145}-8,9=3,1415945$$;

$$5\sqrt{2}/2-13/33=3,1415945$$;

$$\sqrt{7\sqrt{2}}=3,1463462$$;

$$\sqrt{2+\sqrt{3}}=3,1462643$$;

$$10\sqrt{2}-11=3,1421356$$

And the next two approximations of π have “record” accuracy:

$$\sqrt{399550}-22=3,1415926$$

$$\sqrt{97+9/22}=3,1415926$$

Despite the “terrifying” appearance, the corresponding segments are not difficult to construct. A hint for constructing the first segment is in the equality:

$$\sqrt{399550}=\sqrt{5}\sqrt{11^2+1^2}\sqrt{11^2+3^2+1^2}$$

(J. P. Kuptsov
