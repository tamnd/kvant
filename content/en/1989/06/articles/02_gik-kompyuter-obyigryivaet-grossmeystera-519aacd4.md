COMPUTER
SURVEYS
GRANDMASTER

Time flies quickly. Just recently we wrote in «Kvant» that chess computers play better because they are candidate masters, but now new information has arrived: chess robots have reached the level of strong masters and are even “taking aim” at grandmasters. We are talking about two computers (programs) - “Hitech” and “Deep South”, created at Carnegie Mellon University (USA) and developed by groups of researchers. It is believed that “Hitech” is the most advanced machine in the world in terms of the volume of accumulated chess knowledge, its game is more reminiscent of a human being than others, and “Deep South” surpasses its competitors in the speed and depth of search.

The 1988 US Open brought together more than 600 chess players, among whom were “Deep South”. The computer's performance turned out to be sensational - Shashina took 18th place, and her success in the game against I. Ivanov, who was one of the 20 strongest American chess players, was the computer's first victory in Turin over an international master. Before the start of the game, having no experience with machines, the master asked knowledgeable people: how to cope with a computer? He was advised: the main thing was to take it off the beaten path, to falsify the theory in order to “disconnect” the machine from its powerful opening library. And this is what came out of it.

On New Year's Eve 1989, at a festive international tournament in Long Beach (California), “Deep South” became a real chess star - he confidently beat grandmaster B. Larsen, sharing the first prize with other famous grandmaster E. Miles. Bot is the first computer victory over the grandmaster.

A preliminary calculation of the results of the tournament in California showed that the “Deep South” rating is between 2400 and 2500, i.e., in terms of its strength, the computer is somewhere between a grandmaster and an international master.

The already familiar “Hitech” does not lag behind “Deep South” (see «Kvant», 1987, No. 5). Recently, the computer won the Pennsylvania pants championship and scored the first tour victory over an international master - E. Formanek fell victim. The machine has already had victories over skilled masters before, and the score in office meetings with “non-masters” is 15:0 in its favor!

Can “Hitech” boast success in immediate grandmasters? Surprisingly, thanks to this machine, in 1988, two-time US champion, 75-year-old A. Denekr, entered the Guinness Book of Records as the first grandmaster to lose a service match to a computer. The four-game duel with Hitech was concluded with a devastating score of 0.5:3.5. Denekr very LECTO spoke about the game of his electronic offender, especially noting the brilliant opening preparation of the EVM and its extraordinary ingenuity in acute situations.

One way or another, machines already pose a great danger to strong chess players.

E. Ya. Gik
