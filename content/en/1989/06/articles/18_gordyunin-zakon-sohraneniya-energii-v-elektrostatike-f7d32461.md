Law of conservation of energy in a power plant

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. A. GORDYUNIN

The law of conservation of energy defines in its most general form the energy balance for all kinds of changes in any system. Let's write it as follows:

$$A_{\text{внешш}} = \Delta W + Q,$$

(1)

where $A_{\text{внешш}}$ is the work done on the system under consideration by external forces, $\Delta W$ is the change in the energy of the system, $Q$ is the amount of heat released in the system. Let's agree that if $A_{\text{внешш}} > 0$, then positive work is done on the system, and if $A_{\text{внешш}} < 0$, positive work is done by the system; if $\Delta W > 0$, then the energy of the system increases, and if $\Delta W < 0$, the energy decreases; finally, if $Q > 0$, then heat is released in the system, and if $Q < 0$, heat is absorbed by the warm system.

In this article we will look at how the law of conservation of energy “works” in a power plant. In the general case, a power plant system contains charges that interact with each other and are located in an electric field.

Let us consider which term in equation (1) separately.

Let's start with energy. The energy of interaction of charges is expressed through the characteristics of the electric field of this system of charges. So, for example, the energy of a charged capacitor with capacity $C$ is given by the well-known expression

$$W = \frac{q^2}{2C} = \frac{CU^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} qU,$$

(2)

where $q$ is the charge of the plates, $U$ is the voltage between them. Let us remind you that

A capacitor is a system of two conductors (plates, plates), which has the following property: if the charge $q$ is changed from one plate to another (that is, one plate is charged with a charge $+q$, and the other $-q$), then all the drain lines create fields in this way that will begin on one (positive charging) plate and end on the other. The field of a capacitor exists only within it.

The energy of a charged capacitor can also be represented as the energy of a field localized in the space between the plates with energy density $\varepsilon_0 E^2/2$, where $E$ is the field strength. In essence, it is precisely this fact that gives grounds to speak of a field as an object that really exists - this object has an energy density. But we must remember that this is simply an equivalent way of determining the energy of interaction of charges (which we now call electric field energy). Thus, we can calculate the energy of the capacitor both by formula (2) and by formula

$$W = \frac{\varepsilon_0 E^2}{2} V,$$

where $V$ is the volume of the capacitor. The last formula is easy to use, of course, only in the case of a uniform field, but the representation of energy in this form is very clear and therefore convenient.

Of course, in addition to the energy of interaction of charges (energy of the electric field), the energy of the system can also include the kinetic energy of charging bodies, and their potential energy in the gravitational field, and the energy of springs attached to the bodies, etc.

Now about the work of external forces. In addition to the usual mechanical work $A_{\text{max}}$ (for example, moving the capacitor plates apart), for the electrical system we can talk about the work of an external electric field. For example, about the operation of batteries that charge or recharge a capacitor. The task of batteries is to create a fixed potential difference inherent in a given source between the bodies to which it is connected. She does this in the only possible way - she takes a charge from one body and transfers it to another. The source does not create charges in any way, but only moves them. The overall charge of the system is conserved - this is one of the most important laws of nature.

In sources of different designs, the electric field necessary to move charges is created by different “mechanisms”. In batteries and accumulators these are electrochemical reactions, in dielectric machines they are electromagnetic induction. It is important that for the chosen system of charges (charged bodies) this field is external, on its side. When a charge $\Delta q$ flows through a source with an EMF from the imprinted pole to the positive poles, the sides of the force do work

$$A_{\text{бат}} = \Delta q \varepsilon.$$

Moreover, if $\Delta q > 0$, then $A_{\text{бат}} > 0$ - the battery is discharged; if $\Delta q < 0$, then $A_{\text{бат}} < 0$ - the battery is discharged and chemical (or magnetic) energy accumulates in it.

Finally, about the isolation of the body. Let us only note that this is a Joule tell, i.e., a tell associated with the current protection through the resistance.

Task 3. In the circuit at figure 2, a capacitor with capacitance C is charged to voltage U. How much chemical energy will be stored in the battery with emf E after the switch is closed? How much heat will be released in the resistor?

The initial charge on the capacitor is $q_1=CU$. After recharging is completed, the charge on the capacitor becomes equal to $q_2=C\mathcal{E}$. The charge flowing through the battery in the case when a negatively charged capacitor plate is connected to the minus of the battery will be equal to

$$\Delta q=q_2-q_1=C\mathcal{E}-CU.$$

Otherwise $\Delta q=C\mathcal{E}+CU$, in which case the battery will be discharged ($\Delta q>0$). And in the first case, at $U>E$ the battery is charged ($\Delta q<0$), and the amount of chemical energy stored in the battery after closing the key is equal to the battery's total:

$$\Delta W_{\text{хим}}=A_{\text{бат}}=C\mathcal{E}(U-\mathcal{E}).$$

Now let’s write down the law of conservation of energy (1) -

$$\mathcal{E}(C\mathcal{E}-CU)=\left(\frac{C\mathcal{E}^2}{2}-\frac{CU^2}{2}\right)+Q$$

- and find the amount of heat released:

$$Q=\frac{C}{2}(\mathcal{E}-U)^2>0.$$

Task 4. A plate capacitor is in an external uniform field with a voltage $\vec{E}_0$ perpendicular to the plates. Charges $+q$ and $-q$ are distributed on plates of area S. Distance between plates d. What is the minimum amount of work that must be done to swap the plates? Place it parallel to the field? Take it out of the field?

The work will be minimal when the process is done very slowly - no heat is generated. Then, according to the law of conservation of energy, $A_{\text{min}}=\Delta W$.

To find $\Delta W$, we use formula (3). The field between the plates is a superposition of the field $\vec{E}_1$ of a given parallel-plate capacitor -

$$E_1=\frac{q}{C}\frac{1}{d}=\frac{q}{\varepsilon_0 S}$$

— and external field $\vec{E}_0$.

When the plates are swapped, the field $\vec{E}_1$ changes to $-\vec{E}_1$, but the field outside does not change, i.e. the change in the energy of the system is associated with a change in its density between the capacitor plates:

$$\Delta W=\frac{\varepsilon_0(E_0-\pm E_1)^2}{2}Sd-\frac{\varepsilon_0(E_0\pm E_1)^2}{2}Sd=$$

$$=\mp 2\varepsilon_0E_0E_1Sd.$$

If the directions of the vectors $\vec{E}_0$ and $\vec{E}_1$ were the same, then the energy density between the plates decreased after the plates were swapped, and if the directions were opposite, then the energy density increased. Thus, in the first case $A_{\text{min}}=\Delta W=-2qE_0d$.

— the capacitor wants to unfold itself and must be removed ($A<0$), and in the second case $A_{\text{min}}=2qE_0d>0$.

When the capacitor plates are aligned with the field, $\vec{E}_0$ and $\vec{E}_1$ are perpendicular to each other. The field energy of the internal capacitor in this case is equal to $\varepsilon_0(E_0^2+E_1^2)Sd/2$. Then

$$A_{\text{min}}=\Delta W=\frac{\varepsilon_0(E_0^2+E_1^2)}{2}Sd-$$

$$-\frac{\varepsilon_0(E_0\pm E_1)^2}{2}Sd=\mp qE_0d.$$

Problem 5. A capacitor with capacity $C$ without a dielectric is charged with charge $q$. What amount of heat is released in the capacitor if it is filled with a substance with a dielectric substance $\varepsilon$? The same, but the capacitor is connected to the battery with emf $\varepsilon$.

When the dielectric was filled with the capacitance of the capacitor, it increased by $\varepsilon$ times.

In the first case, the charge on the plates is fixed, there are no external forces, and the law of conservation of energy (1) has the form

$$0 = \Delta W + Q.$$

From here

$$Q = -\Delta W = -\left( \frac{q^2}{2C\varepsilon} - \frac{q^2}{2C} \right) = \frac{q^2}{2C} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\varepsilon} \right) > 0.$$

Heat is released due to a decrease in the energy of interaction of charges.

In the second case, there is battery operation and the voltage on the capacitor is fixed:

$$A_{6ат} = \Delta q\varepsilon = (\varepsilon C\varepsilon - C\varepsilon)\varepsilon = C\varepsilon^2(\varepsilon - 1),$$

$$\Delta W = \frac{\varepsilon C\varepsilon^2}{2} - \frac{C\varepsilon^2}{2} = \frac{C\varepsilon^2}{2}(\varepsilon - 1).$$

Then from equation (1) it follows

$$Q = A_{6ат} - \Delta W = \frac{C\varepsilon^2}{2}(\varepsilon - 1).$$

Problem 6. Two plates of area $S$ connected by a conductor are located at a distance $d$ from each other (this distance is small compared to the dimensions of the plates) in an external uniform field with a voltage $\vec{E}$, perpendicular to the plates (Fig. 3). What work must be done to salt them to a distance $d/2$?

The plates are equivalent and there is no field between them. The result of the fusion work is the creation of a field with voltage $\vec{E}$ in volume $S(d-d/2)$. Then, in accordance with equations (1) and (3),

$$A = \Delta W = \frac{\varepsilon_0 E^2}{4} Sd > 0.$$
