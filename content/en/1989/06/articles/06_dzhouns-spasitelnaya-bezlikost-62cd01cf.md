Thankful facelessness

Photochromic glasses used in sunglasses have an interesting property - they darken in light. Under the influence of light, the flakes of silver introduced into the glass composition decompose, and opaque grains of silver are formed. This reaction is processed - at low light levels the glass becomes transparent again; thus, this glass automatically adjusts its transparency. In this regard, Daedalus recalls the basic rule of camouflage: avoid contrasts. Many animals, for example, have a dark back and a light belly, but since the back is well lit and the belly remains the same, their tones practically merge. Photochromic animals - frogs and chameleons -

But they are also not able to vary the color of individual parts of their body so as to completely merge with the surrounding background. This method of camouflaging the present is effective, and nature has undoubtedly already used it; it is quite possible that animals with such camouflage have so far successfully managed to avoid meeting humans.

Daedalus is trying to transfer this principle to human society. He had long wondered why the once fluffy men's dress became very dull in the Victorian era and remains so to this day, at least in everyday and business life. According to Daedalus, this is explained by the desire of people not to attract special attention to themselves from the outside: an eccentrically or flashily dressed subject seems to be asking for attention.

Commentary by Daedalus

In photochromic glass the classical photographic reaction occurs: AgCl $\xrightarrow{hv}$ Ag+Cl. The chlorine atom, released in the photographic emulsion, immediately binds irreversibly with the iron, and the silver atom becomes the center of development. In glass, chlorine cannot move further from the silver atoms, and therefore the reaction is reversible. The equilibrium of the reaction depends on the illumination of the glass.
