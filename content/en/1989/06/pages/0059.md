Bubbles in the onion
(For the beginning, see p. 22)

But we would like to pay attention to the process of evaporation of liquid from the walls of the bubble. You are probably familiar with the popular saying “bubbles in puddles mean prolonged rain.” Precisely because during heavy rain or when it is cloudy, the relative air humidity is close to 100%, the evaporation of water from the walls of the bubble is insignificant. This circumstance “extends” the life of large bubbles, which are rich in good summer juice. When the air humidity is low, the water quickly evaporates. In dry air, the thin layer of the wall of the water bubble would disappear almost instantly. You can carry out quantitative measurements of the rate of moisture evaporation yourself if you have a room hygrometer. All you have to do is drink water in a saucer and watch how quickly it evaporates. The result will depend on the readings of a hygrometer installed away from the saucer of water.
