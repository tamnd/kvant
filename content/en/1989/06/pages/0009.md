Possibility of statistical description

So, the movement of ball-molecules due to random deviations leads to molecular chaos. However, in the emerging chaos, its own patterns appear. Let us remember that the state of a gas can be characterized using average values ​​- temperature, pressure. And if the gas is in a state of equilibrium, then these quantities remain unchanged. This means that, despite many random factors, the system exhibits a certain constancy. And macroscopic parameters - such as temperature, pressure - are associated with the most probable values ​​of quantities that characterize the behavior of individual elements of the system.

The method of probabilistic (statistical) description of a system is the so-called distribution function. We will get acquainted with one of them using the following computer experiment.

We will mark on the plane with coordinates $v_x, v_y$ the points corresponding to the velocities of the balls at different times. These points form a “cloud”, the concentration of point $n(v_x, v_y)$ in which is determined more and more accurately as the number of point $M$ in the “cloud” increases. Function

$$f(v_x, v_y) = \frac{1}{M} n(v_x, v_y)$$

for very large $M$ it ceases to depend on $M$ at all. It is called the distribution function in velocity space. When changing the number of balls $N$ and their total energy $E$, new distribution functions are obtained.

In particular, for two balls the points uniformly fill on the plane $v_x, v_y$ a circle of radius $\sqrt{2E/m}$ (Fig. 6).

To determine the distribution function in a computer experiment

Change the volume of the plane $v_x, v_y$ into equal small cells and count the number of points that fall in each of the cells. The result is clearly presented in figure 7.

Another type of distribution function is based on the velocity component $v_x$. It is obtained by dividing the plane $v_x, v_y$ into strips of width $\Delta v_x$ and counting the number of points in each strip (Fig. 8).

Exercise

1. Find the time after which balls of radius $a$ will collide if their initial positions are $\vec{r}_1 = (x_1, y_1), \vec{r}_2 = (x_2, y_2)$ and their velocities are $\vec{v}_1 = (v_{1x}, v_{1y}), \vec{v}_2 = (v_{2x}, v_{2y})$.
2. Two identical elastic balls moving along the same straight line with speeds $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ are collided. Find the speed of the balls after the collision.
3. Two identical smooth elastic balls of radius $a$ collide. The velocities before the collision are $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$, the coordinates at the moment of collision are $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$, with $(\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2)^2 = (2a)^2$.
