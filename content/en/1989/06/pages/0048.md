Center lines

V. N. VAGUTEN

This article discusses a whole series of theorems that cover the entire school geometry course - from the 6th to the 10th grade. It starts with the theorem about the midlines of a triangle and leads to interesting properties of the tetrahedron and other polyhedra.

Triangle, quadrilateral, parallelogram

To any triangle KLM, three equal triangles AKM, BLK, CLM can be attached, each of which, together with triangle KLM, forms a parallelogram (Fig. 1). In this case, AK = ML = KB, and the vertex K is adjacent to three angles equal to those different angles of the triangle,

We can get the same picture by starting with a large triangle ABC.

Theorem 1. If we connect the middle side of any triangle, we get four equal triangles, and the middle one is a parallelogram with each of the other three.

This formulation involves all three middle lines of the triangle at once. Obviously in the textbook there is a theorem where we are talking about one center line.
