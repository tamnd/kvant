Let's take each one as one. Let's find the center of mass of this load system.

Let us first consider two weights located at the vertices A and B: their center of mass is located in the middle of the segment AB, so these weights can be replaced by one weight of mass 2 placed in the middle K of the segment AB (Fig. 8, a). Now you need to find the center of mass of a system of two loads: one with mass 1 at point C and the second with mass 2 at point K. According to the rules of the lever, the center of mass of such a system is at point O, dividing the segment CK in the ratio 2:1 (closer to the load at point K with a larger mass - Fig. 8, b).

Of course, we could first combine the loads at points B and C, and then combine the resulting load of mass 2 in the middle L of segment BC with the load at point A. Or first combine the loads A and C, and then add B. In any case, we should get the same result. The center of mass is thus located at point O, dividing each of the medians in a ratio of 2:1, counting from the vertex.
