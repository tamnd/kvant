What is a complex diffuser?

As is known, the reflection of light from the interface between two media can be specular or diffuse. All definitions relate the size of the interface irregularities to the wavelength of the incident light.

If the irregularities are small compared to the wavelength, specular reflection occurs. When the irregularities are commensurate with the light wavelength or exceed it and the arrangement of the irregularities is random, diffuse reflection occurs. In this case, the interface between the media scatters light in all possible directions. This surface is called a diffuser.

Now consider an optical system of two flat and parallel diffusers I' and I'' (Fig. 3). Let us assume that these diffusers, consisting of a variety of chaotic distributed unequal scattering centers, are identical, i.e., the distribution and properties of the scattering particles of the second diffuser ideally repeat the distribution and properties of the particles of the first. In other words, each scattering center of the second diffuser has its own twin on the first, and the diffusers are located so that their identical centers are located opposite each other. Such a system is combined with a complex diffuser.
