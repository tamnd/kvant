⟦folio 38⟧

Linear inequalities and problem M1085

Problem M1085 “Problems” dealt with the interlacing of lines on a plane. Some of the wreaths are a projection of a system of non-intersecting lines in space (Fig. 1, a), and some correspond to “impossible” objects (Fig. 1, b). We received a letter from the Canadian mathematician Walter Whiteley, who, as part of his scientific work, comes to LOB to study such tangles. His approach is based on the use of linear inequalities.

We will assume that the plexus is located in the horizontal (x, y)-plane, and none of the lines passes through the origin. Let's direct the OZ axis vertically upward. How to set the position of the i-th line in space? Its projection onto the (x, y)-plane is already given, so the line lies in the vertical plane passing through this projection. To determine the position of a line in space, it is enough to specify another plane in which it lies - for example, a plane passing through the origin. This plane is given by the equation z=px+qy. So, lines in space are given if for each line of the plexus its own coefficients p_i and q_i are chosen.

Let (x_i,j_y) be the coordinates of the intersection point of the i-th and j-th plexus lines. Then the height of the point of the i-th line in space lying above the point (x_i,j_y) is z=p_i x_i+q_i y_i, and for the corresponding point of the j-th line is z=p_j x_j+q_j y_j. If the intersection is the same as at figure 2, a, then p_i x_i+q_i x_j>p_j x_j+q_j x_i.

and if it is the same as in figure 2, b, then the inequality is the opposite: p_i x_i+q_i x_j<p_j x_j+q_j x_i.

The set of inequalities (1) and (2) for all pairs of indices (i, j) forms a system of linear inequalities with unknowns p_1,...,p_n, q_1,...,q_n. If this system has a solution, then the interweaving is a projection of spatial lines, and if the system is inconsistent, then the interweaving describes an “impossible” object.

W. Whiteley also offers another - physical - approach to this problem. Let's imagine that straight plexuses are made of rigid wire that can be slightly bent. Then, based on any picture, it is possible to make a wire plexus that “almost” lies in a horizontal plane.

Let us denote by f_i_j the magnitude of the elastic force acting on the j-th wire straight from the i-th side. These forces are applied at the points of tangency of the lines and are directed along the vertical axis Oz. For an intersection such as at figure 2, a, f_i_j<0 (3)
(the force is directed downward), and for such as on figure 2, b, f_i_j>0. (4)

In addition, the action force is equal to the reaction force:

f_i_j=-f_ji. (5)

Each straight line of the weeding plexus will be in balance. This means that the total force acting on the i-th line is zero:

f_1i+f_2i+...+f_ni=0 (i=1, 2,..., n). (6)

The total moment of force acting on the i-th straight line relative to the OX and OY axes is also equal to zero:

f_1i x_1i+f_2i x_2i+
+...+f_ni x_ni=0 (i=1, 2,..., n). (7)

f_1i y_1i+f_2i y_2i+
+...+f_ni y_ni=0 (i=1, 2,..., n). (8)
