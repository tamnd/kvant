However, one special case is known when interference fringes are formed when the interlayer is very thick. How to see the color of the top layers?

One of the experiments on interference in diffuse (scattered) light can be tested with a so-called dusty mirror. To carry out the experiments, very modest equipment is required: a 4.5 V flashlight battery, a 3.5 V light bulb, a common household mirror (preferably thin) about 1 dm² in size and a piece of telephone wire.

To turn a light bulb into a suitable light source for experiments, do the following. Solder two wires to the light bulb circuit; during the experiments you will connect their opposite ends to the battery electrodes. Cut out a small strip of black wrapping paper, roll it up into a small bag, put it on the light bulb from the base side and glue it so that a small black lampshade is formed.

When preparing a mirror for experiments, it is necessary to apply a scattering coating to its surface. The easiest way to do this is to use diluted milk. Mix one spoon of milk with 4-5 spoons of water, then pour 0.5-1 teaspoon of the solution onto a mirror lying on the table and add even distribution of liquid over the entire surface of the mirror. After this, leave the mirror to dry for a few minutes.

If it is possible to supply a mirror with a metal coating (such mirrors are available in the school physics classroom), then a thin “airy” mirror can be made. To do this, apply a scattering coating to one of the surfaces of photographic plastic (for example, 9x12 cm in size) or a piece of window glass, washed from the emulsion. Then, from common terdan paper to the size of the mirror, cut out a rectangular frame-spacer 0.5-1 cm wide, place this frame on the mirror and cover it with a glass plate so that the milk film faces the mirror surface. As a result, you will get an “air” mirror, the thickness of which corresponds to the thickness of the paper sheet (spacer), i.e. ~0.1 mm.

When conducting experiments, the mirror is placed horizontally. In the case of a relatively thick mirror (3-6 mm thick), it should be placed directly on the floor. A thinner mirror (1-2 mm) can be placed on a chair. If experiments are performed with a thin “air” mirror (~0.1 mm), it is more convenient to place the mirror on the table. The best observations are random in a dark room.

Test 1. Connect the light bulb to the battery, take the battery in one hand, and the wires near the light bulb in the other. Place the light bulb between the eye and the mirror, close to the eye, so that the light beam is directed towards the mirror, and the light bulb itself and the eye are approximately at the same normal to the mirror.
