"Unpredictable behavior" of balls

So, the study leads to the conclusion that the movement of steel billiard balls is extremely unstable. It is clear that the point here is not in the approximation of the calculations, but in the very characteristics of the motion of the balls. The roughness of the estimate allows us to extend it to the movement of molecules of a real gas. Only for molecules the initial uncertainties arise from all sorts of perturbations, which in all other respects can be neglected. (Say, the influence of the electric field of lightning striking in another city, or a man leaking there...) We come to the conclusion that the movement of balls (and molecules) is quite natural over relatively short time intervals and turns out to be random over a long period.

The difference in the characteristics of the ball's motion during collisions with only walls and during collisions with other balls can be considered qualitative. In the first case, the best accuracy of the initial direction is a million times, you can get a million times larger section of the correct path. In the second, it is larger by only 5-10 hundred years. If the motion of the planets were unstable, then under the influence of some astroid invisible to us it would change in a few years beyond recognition.

However, the movement of the planets is calculated thousands of years in advance. Calculations of movement in the past are in excellent agreement with observations of, say, ancient variants. Such conditions of mechanics formed the basis of the views on the world in general that developed in the last century and were best expressed by the remarkable French astronomer, mathematician, and physicist Laplace: “A mind that would, for any given moment, determine all the forces that animate nature, and the relative position of all its component parts, if the additions were general enough to subject these data to analysis, would declare in one formula the movements of the magnitudes of the bodies of the Universe along with the movements the lightest atoms, there would be nothing left that would be unworthy for him, and the future, as well as the past, would appear before his gaze.”

Science and practice have shown that such a perfectly ordered world is unreal. And the reason for this is the existence of objective randomness. The example we examined of the movement of balls (with the understanding that this example is not at all exceptional) is one of the proofs of the decisive role of chance in the life of a complex (consisting of many particles) system.
