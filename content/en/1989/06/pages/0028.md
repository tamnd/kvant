In 1985, the publishing house "Mir" published a translation of David Jones's book "The Inventions of Daedalus." We will not talk about this book - firstly, because it is difficult to tell what this book is about, and secondly, because it is much more interesting to read the book than to tell it. But he is afraid that doing this - reading a book - is no longer so easy today. It's good if you find it in the library...
And we decided to give our readers the opportunity to at least partially become acquainted with the “inventions of Daedalus” - we will post short excerpts from D. Jones’ book. We present the first such publications with excerpts from the author’s proposal for the book.
“This book collects (and expands) 129 “inventions of Daedalus,” published at different times on Arsadna’s “page” in the New Scientist diary. All the inventions of modern Daedalus are not inferior in the boldness of invention to the wax wings of his ancient predecessor: they are original and witty and provide the reader with rich food for thought. These ideas lie on the border between strict reality and obvious fantasy.
...The inventive fantasy of Daedalus always responds to scientific reality. And oddly enough, approximately 17% of inventions in one form or another were accepted without any consequences, patented, implemented, and some, as it turned out, had already been implemented before!...
For those who want to take a closer look at the scientific background of these “inventions”, I have added a section “From Daedalus’s notebook”, where some calculations are given and the scientific side of the issue is discussed... Other comments, including mention of the implementation of Daedalus’s projects, are depleted under the heading “Commentary of Daedalus”...
David Jones

Thankful facelessness

Photochromic glasses used in sunglasses have an interesting property - they darken in light. Under the influence of light, the flakes of silver introduced into the glass composition decompose, and opaque grains of silver are formed. This reaction is processed - at low light levels the glass becomes transparent again; thus, this glass automatically adjusts its transparency. In this regard, Daedalus recalls the basic rule of camouflage: avoid contrasts. Many animals, for example, have a dark back and a light belly, but since the back is well lit and the belly remains the same, their tones practically merge.
