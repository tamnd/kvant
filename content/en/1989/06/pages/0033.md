Like other simple geometric problems, this problem has many solutions. Let's list three of them.

The first two use the inequality m≥2r, where m is the midline of the trapezoid, and r is the radius of the inscribed circle, t. e. 2r is the height of the trapezoid (Fig. 1).

If the diagonal makes an angle greater than 45° with the base, then its projection onto the base is less than 2r of the trapezoid. The sum of the projections of both diagonals, very/no, is equal to the sum of the bases, i.e. 2m. Therefore, if the statement of the problem is not true, then 2m<2r+2r=4r, which contradicts the inequality m≥2r.

Another reasoning: the area of ​​a trapezoid, equal to 2rm, does not exceed d1d2/2, where d1 and d2 are its diagonals. If d1≥d2, then d1²/2≥d1d2/2≥2rm≥4r². Consequently, 2r/d1≤√2/2, which means that the diagonal d1 makes an angle of no more than 45° with the base (see Fig. 1).
