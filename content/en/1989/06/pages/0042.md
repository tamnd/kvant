Is it so familiar to you?
electromagnetic induction

Faraday devoted his entire life to proving that not a single electrical or magnetic process occurring in nature occurs in isolation. Faraday's deep belief in the interconnection of all the forces of nature led him, after many years of failure, to a unique discovery.

A new effect, as often happens, was then discovered in a variety of apparently different phenomena, united by a single-component conclusion: alternating magnetic fields excite electric fields. It is on this basis that the operation of all existing electric machines is based. It was Faraday's discovery that provided the opportunity to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy, transfer energy over a distance, and thereby formed the foundation of modern technical civilization.

The work of Faraday and his outstanding contemporaries made it possible, step by step, to create a unified picture of electromagnetism.

When studying this section of physics, you will not only explain the facts and observations you know, but will also be able to understand electromagnetic phenomena on both cosmic and microscopic scales.

Questions and tasks

1. How to move a magnet to rotate

the arrow with the north pole towards is observed?

2. A horizontal round frame is located in a magnetic field directed vertically upward. What will be the direction of the induction current when observing the frame from above if the field decreases with time?

3. At what positions of the frame, rotating at a constant speed in a straight conductor with current, will the emf generated in it be the greatest?

4. A magnet is first quickly and then slowly inserted into a short-circuited coil. Is the same charge transferred by the induction current? Is the same amount of heat generated in the coil?

5. How will a magnet fall in a long copper tube? Neglect air resistance.

6. The ends of the folded wire are connected to the galvanometer. Why does the instrument needle remain at zero when the wire crosses the magnetic field line?

7. A metal coin lies on a vertically located reel. Why does it heat up when alternating current flows through the coil, but remains cold when it is constant?

8. A high frequency current flows through a straight conductor. How will the resistance of this conductor change if it is shaped like a solenoid?

9. The conductor AB moves so that current flows through it from point A

to point B. At which of these points is the potential higher?

10. Two identical planes fly horizontally at the same speeds, one near the screen, the other near the pole. Which of them will have a larger potential difference at the ends of the wings?
