From the theorem on the midlines of a triangle follows the property of the midline of a trapezoid (Fig. 2), as well as the theorems on segments connecting the midpoints of the sides of an arbitrary quadrilateral.

Theorem 3. The midpoints of the sides of the quadrilateral are the vertices of the parallelogram. The sides of this parallelogram are parallel to the diagonals of the quadrilateral, and their lengths are equal to half the length of the diagonals.

Indeed, if $K$ and $L$ are the midpoints of the sides $AB$ and $BC$ (Fig. 3), then $KL$ is the midline of the triangle $ABC$, therefore the segment $KL$ is parallel to the diagonal $AC$ and equal to its half; if $M$ and $N$ are the midpoints of sides $CD$ and $AD$, then the segment $MN$ is also parallel to $AC$ and equal to $AC/2$. Thus, the segments $KL$ and $MN$ are parallel and equal to each other, which means that the quadrilateral $KLMN$ is a parallelogram.

As a consequence of Theorem 3 we obtain the following fact.

Theorem 4. In any quadrilateral, the segments connecting opposite sides are divided in half by the intersection point.

Indeed, in these segments one can see the diagonals of the parallelogram (see Fig. 3), and in a parallelogram, as we know, the diagonals are divided in half by the point of intersection (this point is the symmetry center of the parallelogram).

Note that Theorems 3 and 4 and our reasoning remain true both for a non-convex quadrilateral and for a self-intersecting quadrilateral closed broken line (Fig. 4; in the latter case it may turn out that the parallelogram $KLMN$ is “degenerate” - the points $K, L, M, N$ lie on the same line).

Let us show how from Theorems 3 and 4 we can derive the main theorem on the medians of a triangle.

Theorem 5. The medians of a triangle intersect at one point and divide it in the ratio 2:1 (counting from the vertex from which the median is drawn).

Let us draw two medians $AL$ and $CK$ of triangle $ABC$. Let $O$ be their intersection point. The midpoints of the sides of a non-convex quadrilateral $ABCO$ are points $K, L, M$ and $N$ (Fig. 5) - the vertices of a parallelogram, and the point of intersection of its diagonals $KM$ and $LN$ for our configuration will be the point of intersection of the medians $O$. So, $AN=NO=OL$ and $CM=MO=OK$, i.e. the point $O$ divides each of the medians $AL$ and $CK$ in a ratio of 2:1.

Instead of the median $CK$, we could consider the median drawn from the vertex $B$ and see in the same way that it divides the median $AL$ in the ratio 2:1, i.e., passes through the same point $O$.
