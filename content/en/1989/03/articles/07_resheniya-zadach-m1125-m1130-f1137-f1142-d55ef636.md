Let us denote the $k$-th word by $w_k$. According to the rule specified in the conditions, the word $w_{k+1}$ is obtained from the word $w_k$. Using this, we can prove by induction that for $k > 1$ the word $w_{k+1}$ consists of the words $w_k$ and $w_{k-1}$ repeated twice (for $k = 2$ and $k = 3$ this is shown in the margin):

$$w_{k+1} = w_k w_{k-1}.$$

In fact, applying our rule to the correspondence (*), we obtain $w_{k+1}$ from $w_k$, and $w_k$ from $w_{k-1}$, so if this correspondence holds for some $k$, then a similar correspondence holds for $k+1$:

$$w_{k+2} = w_{k+1} w_{k+1} w_k.$$

Thus, the correspondence (*) is proven for all $k > 1$.

From here, in particular, follows statement a): each word $w_k$ is the beginning of the word $w_{k+1}$; thus all $w_k$ are the initial segments of the infinite word $w = AABAABAAAB$...

This proof can be presented without the help of checkered paper and broken paper. M1126. In a trapezii ABCD with base BC and AD on sides AB and CD, points K and M are chosen. Prove that if $\angle BAM=\angle CDK$ then $\angle BMA=\angle CKD$.

Let $O$ be the intersection point of the extensions of the lateral sides $AB$ and $CD$ of the given trapezoid (see figure). Let us carry out the sequential symmetry $S$ with respect to the bisector of the angle $AOD$ and the preparation $H$ with the center $O$ and the coefficient $OD/OA$. In this case, the point $A$ will go to $D$ (see figure), and the point $B$ - to $C$ (since $OC/OB=OD/OA$), the angle $OAM$ - to the angle $ODK$, which is equal to it by condition. Consequently, the point $M$, lying at the intersection of the lines $AM$ and $OD$, will go to the point of intersection of the images of these lines - $DK$ and $OA$, i.e. to the point $K$. So, the points $A$, $M$, $B$ go, respectively, to $D$, $K$, $C$, and therefore $\angle AMB=\angle DKC$.

Another solution is based on the fact that a circle can be described around the quadrilateral $AKMD$, and therefore also around $BKMC$.

M1130. A convex n-gon is given on the plane, the length of the kth side is equal to $a_k$, and the length of the projection of the polygon onto the line containing this side is equal to $d_k$ (k=1,2,...,n). Prove the inequality
$$2 < \frac{a_1}{d_1} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{d_n} \leq 4.$$

Fig. 1.

Obviously, the length of the projection of a convex polygon onto any line is equal to two less than the sum of the lengths of the projections of its side onto this line (Fig. 1), and therefore less than half of its perimeter $p$. That's why
$$\frac{a_1}{d_1} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{d_n} > \frac{2a_1}{p} + \dots + \frac{2a_n}{p} = 2 \cdot \frac{a_1 + \dots + a_n}{p} = 2.$$

This estimate cannot be improved, as the example of a “flat” polygon shows, which has $d_i=p/2$ for all $i$.

To prove the upper bound, we construct some auxiliary polygon. Let us plot from one point $n$ pairs of opposite vectors, each of which is equal in length and parallel to one of the sides of the given $n$-gon $M$, and number these vectors clockwise: $\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_{2n}$ (Fig. 2); rated byindo, $\vec{v}_i = -\vec{v}_{i+n}, i=1, 2, \dots, n$. From the end $B_1$ of the vector $\vec{v}_1$ we disconnect the vector $\overrightarrow{B_1}B_2 = \vec{v}_2$, from its end $B_2$ we disconnect the vector $\overrightarrow{B_2}B_3 = \vec{v}_3$, etc. (Fig. 3). The last point $B_{2n}$ coincides with the beginning of $\vec{v}_1$. It is easy to see that the resulting $2n$-gon $N=B_1B_2...B_{2n}$ is convex, has a center of symmetry $O$ and each two of its opposite sides are equal and parallel to one of the sides of the polygon $M$. By virtue of the statement given at the beginning of the solution, the length of the projection $N$ onto any line is equal to the sum of the lengths of the projections of the vectors $\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_n$ onto this line, i.e. twice

F1138. Accelerating with the maximum possible acceleration on a straight section of the highway, the condenser increases speed from $v_1 = 10,0$ m/s to $v_2 = 10,5$ m/s in a time of $\Delta t_1 = 0,1$ s. How long would it take him to do the same thing on a ring road section of the highway with a radius of $R = 30$ m? At what radius of the ring would he generally not be able to increase the speed above 10 m/s? The highway plane is horizontal.

Maximum possible acceleration

$$a = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\Delta t_1} = 5 \text{ м/c}^2$$

is determined by the friction force and remains the same for both straight and ring sections of the highway. When moving in a circle, the total acceleration $a$ consists of the tangential acceleration $a_K(a_K = \Delta v/\Delta t)$ and the normal acceleration $a_H(a_H = v^2/R)$:

$$\vec{a} = \vec{a}_K + \vec{a}_H,$$

or

$$a^2 = a_K^2 + a_H^2,$$

where

$$a_K = \sqrt{a^2 - a_H^2}.$$

In our case, the speed changes little, and the tangential acceleration can be considered constant. Then the required time

$$\Delta t_2 = \frac{\Delta v}{a_K} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\sqrt{a^2 - (v_1^2/R)^2}} = 0,15 \text{ с.}$$

Minimum turning radius at speed $v_1$

$$R_{\min} = \frac{v_1^2}{a} = 20 \text{ м.}$$

$$p_1 = p_0 \frac{T_1}{T_0} \frac{h_0}{h_0 - \Delta h}, p_2 = p_0 \frac{T_2}{T_0} \frac{h_0}{h_0 + \Delta h}.$$

As can be seen from the problem conditions, the induction $B$ of the magnetic field takes the maximum value of the value of the internal core of the cable. It should not exceed a given value $B_{\max}$, otherwise the superconductivity of the internal core will be destroyed. This condition determines the maximum current $I_{\max}$ that can be passed through the cable:

$$B_{\max} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \frac{I_{\max}}{d/2} = \frac{\mu_0 I_{\max}}{\pi d},$$

$$I_{\max} = \frac{\pi d B_{\max}}{\mu_0}.$$

Thus, for a given value $B_{\max}$ the maximum current is proportional to the diameter of the internal cable core:

$$I_{\max} \sim d.$$

The voltage $E$ of the electric field in the space between the cylindrical conductors varies inversely proportional to the distance $r$ from the axis of the cylinders:
