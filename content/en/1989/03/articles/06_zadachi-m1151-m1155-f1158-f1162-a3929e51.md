M1151 — M1155, F1158 — F1162

M1151. a) Prove the equality (where $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot n$):

$$\frac{1 \cdot 2!}{2} + \frac{2 \cdot 3!}{2^2} + \dots + \frac{n(n+1)!}{2^n} = \frac{(n+2)!}{2^n} - 2.$$

6) Find the amount

$$\frac{1 \cdot 3!}{3} + \frac{2 \cdot 4!}{3^2} + \dots + \frac{n(n+2)!}{3^n}.$$

V. N. Zhokha

M1152. Let $h$ and $l$ be the height and notch drawn from one vertex of the triangle, $R$ and $r$ the radius of its circumscribed and inscribed circle. Prove her equality $h/l \geq \sqrt{2r/R}$.

Cornel Berceanu, Istvan Varda (Romania)

M1153. What is the maximum number of turns that can be contained in a closed rook route that goes around all $8 \times 8$ cells of the chessboard once?

M. V. Khovanov

M1154*. Prove that if a quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle and circumscribed by another circle, then a line drawn through the centers of these circles passes through the intersection point of the diagonals of the quadrilateral.

V. Yu. Protasov

M1155. A point moves in a triangle, reflecting from its sides according to the law “the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.” Periodic is also the name given to the trajectory of this point, which is a closed broken line and does not pass through the vertices of the triangle. (Examples of periodic trajectories with 3 and 6 links in a triangle are given in figure 8 on p. 12. a) Prove that no triangle with different sides contains a four-link periodic trajectory.

Is there an acute triangle with a periodic trajectory 6) of 5 links? c) from 7 links?

G. A. Galperin, A. M. Stgenin

F1158. An all-terrain vehicle can use either front or rear wheels as drive wheels. The driver wants to tow a three-lane load. What is the maximum traction force $T$ (without jerk) that an automobile can exert if the friction coefficient of the wheels on the road is $\mu=0,4$, the mass of the automobile is $M=2$ t, the distance between the centers of the wheels is $l=4$ m, the radius of the wheels is $R=0,3$ m? The front and rear axles are located in the same horizontal plane; the center of mass of the automobiles lies in this plane at an equal distance from the axes; the cable lies in the same plane. Which wheels should be driving? Does $T$ depend on this?

A. I. Buzdin

F1159. Imagine that you are in an accelerating train and walking at a constant speed relative to the car forward along the train. A very significant force is pushing you back, and you are undoubtedly doing work against this force. What is your work spent on? Apparently, she cannot go for additional acceleration of the train - after all, you are pushing her backwards. What's the matter here? Isn't the law of conservation of energy violated?

A. N. Korotkov

F1160. A cylinder of radius $R$ and height $H$ contains $N$ electrons. A constant magnetic field of induction $B$ is applied parallel to the cylinder axis. It is assumed that all electrons have the same speed $v$, lying in a plane perpendicular to the magnetic field, and the impacts of electrons on the walls of the cylinder are absolutely elastic. Estimate what is equal and how the pressure on the walls of the cylinder, which creates such an “electron gas”, depends on the magnetic field (the pressure can be found up to a constant coefficient, does not depend on the magnetic field). Electron charge $e$, mass $m$. Consider that $(mv/eB) \ll R$ (“strong” field $B$). Neglect the interaction of electrons with each other.

D. A. Kuptsov

F1161. In architectural acoustics, it is well known that galleries are “sparse”. In large cathedrals (for example, in St. Peter's Basilica in Rome), a fenced area (gallery) is built around the circumference of the base of the dome, where access to tourists is limited. It has long since been replaced that the soft thrumming at point $A$ (Fig. 1) is clearly audible at point $B$ if the speaker is looking along the gallery wall. But if the speaker looks in the direction of point $B$, then with the same speech volume at point $B$ nothing is heard. Why is this happening?

At point $A$, a non-directional sound source emits a fairly loud pulse of duration $\tau_1$. What will be the duration of the pulse, taken at point $B$? Gallery diameter $d$.

V. I. Klyachin

F1162. Underwater at a depth of $h$ there is a precise light source. Where is the image of this source for an observer looking along the surface of the water (Fig. 2)? The refractive index of water is $n$.
