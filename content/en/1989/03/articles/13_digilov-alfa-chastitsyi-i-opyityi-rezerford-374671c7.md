Alpha particles and Rutherford's experiments

In the fall of 1903, Ernest Rutherford, a thirty-year-old physics professor, sailed from Europe to Canada. He took with him a small metal box with a free load for him - thirty milligrams of radium salt.

The pig box caused concern among customs officials in New York - at that time there were no laws regarding the import of radium. Is it a jewel or a chemical? Should it be taxed and how? Officials are always the same, and American customs officials decided to forward the foreign cargo to their superiors. But researchers are also everywhere and always the same. And a report appeared in which the officials informed their superiors that Dr. Rutherford absolutely refused to part with his treasure. And only the professor's commitment to checking the box through the territory of the United States intact (that is, without speculating on the substance) allowed American officials to shift the solution to the duty problem to the films of their Canadian colleagues. Perhaps it was thanks to these milligrams of radium that many wonderful discoveries were made in physics.

Rutherford's name appears in school physics courses in connection with the establishment of the planetary model of the atom. But Rutherford is the author and multi-artist of discoveries no less valuable for physics. These include, in particular, experiments to study the nature of α-rays. Let's talk about some of them.

Back in 1899, while working at the Cavendish Laboratory (at the University of Cambridge), Rutherford established that the radiation of radioactive elements is not uniform. Here is one such experiment.

Experiment 1. Two zinc plates were placed horizontally next to each other. One plate was connected to a strip of a grounded battery, and the other to a grounded galvanometer. A thin layer of radium salt was poured onto the bottom plate. The radiation of the salt generated onns in the air, the air between the plates ceased to be an insulator, and an electric current arose, which was recorded by the device.

If radium salts are covered with a thin metal sheet, part of the radiation is absorbed, and the current becomes more than two times weaker. If the radiation is shielded by two sheets, the current will weaken by almost six times, if three - by the same time. It would seem that further on the current should gradually decrease (according to the exponential law). But it is surprising that the experiments did not confirm this - starting from the fifth sheet, the current practically did not decrease.

It was obvious to assume that oniization of the air is caused by at least two reasons. Or, in other words, that radiation consists of two different types: one radiation generates strong insulation, but is well absorbed by the metal, the other - ionizes the effect of the metal, but is more penetrating. Rutherford's first radiation is called α-rays, and the second - β-rays. Now scientists are faced with the problem of investigating the nature of these rays.

Very quickly and quite easily it was possible to establish that β-radiation is a flow of free electrons. In any case, in electric and magnetic fields β-rays are affected in exactly the same way as electrons.

As for α-rays, their deviation in a magnetic field could not be detected for some time - even in a strong field the deviation turns out to be small. Finally, in 1903, Rutherford added positive results and showed that α-radiation must consist of positively charged particles moving at high speeds.

The next task was to determine the charge values ​​of the α particle.

Experiment 2. To determine the charge of one α-particle, we experimentally determined the total amount of electrical energy transferred by all radiation from radium in a certain time, and the number of α-particles emitted by radium during this time. The hardest part was registering one part. For this purpose, Rutherford and Geiger in 1908 developed a special method for counting α-particles, based on their initiating effect, and created a special device (the well-known Geiger counter).

This tube was placed inside a wider tube T, to the top of which was attached a small vacuum tube V with electrodes melted into it. Finally, from experiments on the deflection of α-particles in a magnetic field, their mass can be determined.

Experiment 4. A cloud chamber, in which the trajectories of α-particles were observed by their scinitations, was placed in a very strong magnetic field. Since the radius of the steep orbit of an α-particle is proportional to the mass of the particle multiplied by its speed, and inversely proportional to its charge, the mass of the α-particle could be calculated from known values. It turned out to be equal to 6.62·10⁻²⁴ g.

So, thanks to the experiments of Rutherford and his colleagues, the nature, charge and mass of the α particle became known. In addition, physicists now have a powerful and fundamentally new tool for studying the structure of the atoma itself. Bombarding atoms with α-particles made it possible to establish the structure of the atom.

Experiment 5. Thin plates of the substance under study were bombarded with α-particles and their deflection was studied. On figure 2 a schematic diagram of the experiment on the scattering of α-particles is shown. Bombardment particles, flying out of the radioactive substance, passed through the collimator, and hit the target, which was a very thin gold foil, in a narrow beam. Using a screen coated with a scinitation substance, the scattering of α-particles was observed. The scattering angles of most particles were small - on the order of 1°, but a small number of particles were scattered at large angles, and there were even particles moving in the opposite direction.

After analyzing the results of the experiments, Rutherford came to the conclusion that the strong deflection of α-particles in the column occurred only if the inside of the atom had an extremely strong electric field created by the charge associated with a larger mass (the nucleus of the atom). Rutherford also developed a theory of scattering of α-particles, which established the distribution of particles according to scattering angles. In this regard, this fact is curious.
