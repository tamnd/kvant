⟦folio 43⟧

Quests

1. Seryozhka added three consecutive natural numbers, then the next three numbers, and then the resulting sums of the variable. Could he have come up with the number 111,111,111?

2. 35 matches were placed on the table as shown in figure. The result is a spiral, “twisted” clockwise. Arrange four matches so that you get the same spiral, twisted counterclockwise.

3. With the number written on the board, the following operations are cut: either replace it with a double one, or erase its last digit. How can you use these operations to get the number 14 from the number 458?

4. Can equality be true?
$$\text{Ж·У·Р·Н·А·Л} = \text{К·В·А·Н·Т},$$
if instead of letters you substitute the numbers from 1 to 9? In this case, the same letters must correspond to the same numbers, and different numbers must correspond to different ones.

5. In a regular eight-pointer, two parallel diagonals were drawn (see figure). Prove that the area of ​​the best straight beam is half the area of ​​the eight-arm.

These tasks were suggested to us by S. A. Genkin, A. M. Domashenko, D. V. Fomin, A. P. Tonkikh, V. V. Proizvolov.
