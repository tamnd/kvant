1. Balls that initially have charges of different signs: $q_1$ and $-q_2$, after settling down, carry the same charge $q=(q_1-q_2)/2$. The relationship between the forces of repulsion and attraction -

$$k \frac{q^2}{l^2} = \frac{1}{8} \frac{q_1 q_2}{l^2},$$

from here we come to the equation

$$x^2 - (5/2) x + 1 = 0,$$

where $x=q_1/q_2$.
Solving this equation, we get two answers:

$$q_1/q_2 = 2 \text{ и } q_1/q_2 = 1/2,$$

that is, the charge of one ball is twice as large as the other.

2. The critical condition for a bullet to pass through a board (both fixed and unsecured) is the release of thermal energy $Q=mv_0^2/2$ in the board. In the case of an unsecured board, the final velocities of the board and the bullet are the same. Let this speed be equal to $u$. Then from the law of conservation of momentum

$$mv=(m+M)u$$

and the law of conservation of energy

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{(m+M)u^2}{2} + \frac{mv_0^2}{2}$$

we get

$$v=v_0 \sqrt{(m+M)/M}.$$
