Wednesday. At what distance $l_2$ from the entry point would the particle stop if the field were half as strong?

F1141. Currently, in connection with the discovery of the phenomenon of high-temperature superconductivity, the question of creating direct current transmission lines without energy loss on a Joule body is being studied. It is proposed to use a coaxial cable consisting of an internal cylindrical shell made of a superconductor to transmit direct current. The electric and magnetic fields in such a system are represented by figure. It is known that the inductive magnetic field at the surface of a superconductor cannot exceed $B_{\max}$, otherwise the superconductivity is destroyed, and the electric field voltage should not exceed $E_{\max}$, otherwise an electrical breakdown will occur in the insulating process of the cable. Determine how many times does the maximum direct current power transmitted through such a cable change if the diameters of the inner and outer sheath are doubled?

As can be seen from the problem conditions, the induction $B$ of the magnetic field takes the maximum value of the value of the internal core of the cable. It should not exceed a given value $B_{\max}$, otherwise the superconductivity of the internal core will be destroyed. This condition determines the maximum current $I_{\max}$ that can be passed through the cable:

$$B_{\max} = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \frac{I_{\max}}{d/2} = \frac{\mu_0 I_{\max}}{\pi d},$$

$$I_{\max} = \frac{\pi d B_{\max}}{\mu_0}.$$

Thus, for a given value $B_{\max}$ the maximum current is proportional to the diameter of the internal cable core:

$$I_{\max} \sim d.$$

The voltage $E$ of the electric field in the space between the cylindrical conductors varies inversely proportional to the distance $r$ from the axis of the cylinders:

$$E(r) \sim \frac{1}{r}.$$

This dependence can be easily understood qualitatively by reducing the electric field lines to figure (remember that the strength of the electric field characterizes the density of the field lines, i.e., the number of lines, arbitrary units of the plane). Quantity dependency $E(r)$ try to get using the field.

So, the strength of the electric field, as well as the induction of the magnetic field, is most important to the size of the internal core of the cable.
