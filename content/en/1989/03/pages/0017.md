In addition, in an arbitrary right triangle there are periodic trajectories with an angularly large number of links. This is unknown for acute-angled triangles, although it has been proven that for any directional number $N$ there is an acute-angled triangle in which there are periodic trajectories with more than $N$ links.

In conclusion, we repeat that periodic trajectories in triangular billiards always exist in the shape of a triangle. The reason for the destruction or separation of a periodic trajectory during the deformation of a triangle is the passage through the corner point of the boundary. This obstruction explains the intricate nature of the yellow figure at figure 13.6.

We invite those readers who have studied the theory of mathematical billiards to think about problem M1155 (see p. 27), as well as find some other typical triangles with stable periodic trajectories. In this regard, we offer you the following task:

Problem 7. Construct a periodic trajectory of 15 links in a typangular triangle with angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ satisfying the conditions:

$$\frac{\pi}{8} - \frac{1}{1000} < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{8}, \frac{\pi}{3} < \beta < 3\alpha.$$
