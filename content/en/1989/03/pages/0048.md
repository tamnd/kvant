Lorentz force and Hall effect

When studying the topic “Magnetic Field” you became acquainted with two forces. One of them is called the Lorentz force, the other - the Ampere force. The Lorentz force acts from the magnetic field on a moving charged particle. If there is not just one moving charge in a magnetic field, but a conductor with current, then the Ampere force acts on it.

The Ampere force can easily be reduced to the Lorentz force if we remember that the electric current in a conductor is nothing more than the direction of movement of free charges, for example, electrons. Each electron is acted upon by the Lorentz force, and the sum of all such forces is precisely the Ampere force.

At first glance, everything is simple. But upon closer examination, questions arise. For example, it may seem unclear how the force acting on free electrons is “transmitted” to the entire conductor. If electrons are free, then they do not interact with the crystal lattice, and therefore cannot have any effect on it. Let's try to figure it out.
