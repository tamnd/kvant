But in Europe, after the fall of ancient science, the named digits of numbers following a thousand were not filled in. European mathematicians could still read the number 999,999, but they could not count further. In 1271-1275, the Venetian merchant Marco Polo made a journey unprecedented for that time. Having passed the northern coast of the Black Sea, he crossed the Volga, the endless Asian steppes and reached China along the Great Silk Road. Here he lived for many years, observing what the Europeans of that time had no idea about: the flight of gunpowder rockets, book printing, porcelain making.

When he returned to Venice, there was no end to the stories. And most often in the stories of Marco Polo the word “million” was repeated - a large thousand. That's what he called the thousand thousand. The distrustful Venetians visited Marco Millione and thought that he was deceiving them. Only a few hundred years later, when the Venetians became better acquainted with China, did they learn that Polo's stories were true.

In the 15th century, the French mathematician Nicolas Chuquet coined the word “billion,” which means a million million. To write a billion, you need to put 12 zeros after the one. The prefix “bi” in Latin meant “second” (in theaters they shout “encore” when they want a repetition). Therefore, “billion” can also be read as follows: “second million.” A million billions were called a trillion, and a million trillions were called a quadrillion (from the Latin word quart, meaning four).

In the USA, England and Germany, a different system of named numbers has been adopted. According to this system, a thousand million is called a billion or billion, a thousand billion is called a trillion, and a thousand trillion is called a quadrillion. These systems are also used in our country. Here are the names of some huge numbers with the number zero after one in two systems:

They don’t study these things at school, and they aren’t really needed. We often read in newspapers that this or that factory has produced so-and-so millions of meters of fabric or such-and-such hydrosanitizer for so-and-so billions of kilowatt-hours of electric grain. Trillions are found in newspapers when they write about the budgets of the largest states. But the name “quadrillion” does not appear in the newspapers - there is hardly anything on the globe that would be calculated in quadrillions.
