Bombarding atoms with α-particles made it possible to establish the structure of the atom.

Experiment 5. Thin plates of the substance under study were bombarded with α-particles and their deflection was studied. On figure 2 a schematic diagram of the experiment on the scattering of α-particles is shown. Bombardment particles, flying out of the radioactive substance, passed through the collimator, and hit the target, which was a very thin gold foil, in a narrow beam. Using a screen coated with a scinitation substance, the scattering of α-particles was observed. The scattering angles of most particles were small - on the order of 1°, but a small number of particles were scattered at large angles, and there were even particles moving in the opposite direction.

After analyzing the results of the experiments, Rutherford came to the conclusion that the strong deflection of α-particles in the column occurred only if the inside of the atom had an extremely strong electric field created by the charge associated with a larger mass (the nucleus of the atom). Rutherford also developed a theory of scattering of α-particles, which established the distribution of particles according to scattering angles. In this regard, this fact is curious.
