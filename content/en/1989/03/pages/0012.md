Perimeter minimization

If the boundary of a billiard table has break points, then Birkhoff’s method crosses the “work” - the vertices of the inscribed polygon of the smallest perimeter can fall into the corner points of the boundaries. For example, among the triangles inscribed in a given triangle $ABC$, $\triangle ABC$ itself has the smallest perimeter. How to find periodic billiard trajectories in a triangle?

For an acute triangle, the solution is to replace the maximum perimeter with the minimum. Let us inscribe in this triangle $ABC$ the triangle $XYZ$ of the smallest perimeter with vertices on the sides $AB, BC$ and $CA$ (figure 4). We claim that $XYZX$ is a billiard trajectory. However, you can read about the evidence for this fact below in the section “Mechanical Interpretation”, and another is the subject of the first of the following problems.

Task 3 shows how to construct a three-link billiard trajectory in an acute triangle. The only such trajectory will be proven below.

The idea of ​​considering an inscribed triangle of the smallest perimeter is also applicable to figures bounded by several smooth curves. Unfortunately, this method does not work for obtuse triangles - for them, the inscribed triangle of the smallest perimeter degenerates into the height lowered from the vertex of the obtuse angle. Moreover, billiards in an obtuse triangle does not have three-link periodic trajectories (try to prove this). However, this does not mean that the idea of ​​minimizing the perimeter is useless for constructing periodic billiard trajectories in obtuse triangles; it only needs to be combined with the straightening method, which is outlined in the next section.
