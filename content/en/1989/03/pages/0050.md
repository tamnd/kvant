Then finally

$$\Delta \varphi_X = \frac{d}{enS} I B.$$

Looking at this formula, it is not difficult to imagine the main possibilities for using the Hall effect. Let's look at some of them.

1. The Hall effect can be used to measure magnetic field induction. To do this, a small conductor is made (it is called a Hall sensor) and the proportionality coefficient between $\Delta \varphi_X$ and the product $IB$ is determined for it, using some known (reference) magnetic field. Then, by placing the Hall sensor at various points in the field under study, the current and cold potential difference are measured, and $B$ is calculated from these data.

2. The results of cold potential difference measurements are easily reproduced, so the Hall effect is used to create a reference voltage.

3. The Hall effect plays an important role in studying the physical properties of conductive materials. And this is its most important use. Indeed, by measuring $\Delta \varphi_X$, $I$ and $B$, it is possible to calculate such an important characteristic as the concentration of free charge carriers in a substance under various conditions. I expect that this concentration will be the same order of magnitude as the concentration of atoms, since it is from the atoms of the crystal lattice that free electrins are “torn off.” This expectation is determined for multihot semiconductors. Their concentration of free charges turns out to be many orders of magnitude lower and, moreover, strongly depends on temperature. It seems like one free electron per hundred or even per million atoms! But the most necessary choice is that, according to the experimental results, the charge of free carriers in multihot semiconductors should be positive!

How did you manage to find out? It is easy to notice that all the manifestations of electric current known to you (tell, magnetic, etc.) are completely independent of the sign of the charge carriers, i.e., everything is determined only by the magnitude of the current, and only in the Hall effect is this not the case. Look at figure 2, $a$. If the charge carriers were positive, then with the same direction of current, the speed of the charges would have the opposite direction. But since both the sign of the charge and the direction of the velocity have changed, the Lorentz force will again be directed upward. This means that in this case, not a negative, but a positive charge will accumulate on the upper face, and $\Delta \varphi_X$ turns out to be of the opposite sign (Fig. 2, 6).

This, as it is called, the aomal Hall effect, was discovered experimentally. An error has arisen that the current is created by positive electrons! In fact, as it turned out later, the aomal Hall effect in semiconductors corresponds to the case of hole conduction.
