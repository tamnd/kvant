1 degree in a well-insulated room measuring 4 m×5 m×5 m = 100 m³. The approximate calculation is very simple. Gas energy

$$E = \frac{3}{2} NkT,$$

where $N$ is the number of gas particles, and the change in energy is

$$\Delta E = \frac{3}{2} Nk \cdot \Delta T.$$

The number of gas particles in a room is determined by several factors. Each mole of gas under normal conditions occupies a volume $V_0 = 22,4$ l = 22.4×10⁻³ m³; this means the room contains 100/(22.4·10⁻³)≈5×10³ moles. And the number of molecules in each molle (Avogadro’s number) is $N_A \approx 6 \cdot 10^{23}$ mol⁻¹. This means there are $N \approx 3 \cdot 10^{27}$ molecules in the room, and $\Delta E \approx 6 \times 10^{11}$ ergs. Now let’s calculate the required mass using the formula $\Delta E = Mgh:M \approx 6 \times 10^6$ g, or 6 tons(!). The answer made me triple check the calculation.
