For. What is the maximum power that can be transmitted through a cable with sheath diameters $D=8$ cm, $d=3$ cm, if $E_{\max}=20$ kV/cm and $B_{\max}=5 \cdot 10^{-2}$ T? Note: the magnetic field induction in the space between the cylindrical conductors coincides with the field of a straight conductor with current I:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi} \frac{I}{r}$$

($\mu_0=4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} H/A^2$ is the magnetic constant).

F1142. The formation of the cometary family of Jupiter is described by the following scheme. The comet falls from a great distance without initial speed onto the Sun and flies a short distance from Jupiter (see figure). After the noticeable influence of the gravitational field of Jupiter ceases, the comet again moves in the field of the Sun, and its speed turns out to be directed counter-directionally with the speed of Jupiter, and the new orbit of the comet is located greater than the orbit of Jupiter, i.e. at a distance of $R=5,2$ a. e. from the Sun. At what distance from the Sun will the entire orbit of such a comet be located?

Equal to $E_{\max}$, then in the space between the cylindrical conductors

$$E(r) = E_{\max} \frac{d/2}{r}.$$

From here you can find the maximum voltage $U_{\max}$ between the electrodes:

$$U_{\max} = \int \frac{d/2}{d/2} E_{\max} \frac{d/2}{r} dr = \frac{E_{\max} d}{2} \ln \frac{D}{d}.$$

If the cables compared in the problem are geometrically similar, i.e. $D/d=$const, then the maximum voltage is proportional to the diameter of the inner core:

$$U_{\max} \sim d.$$

Therefore, the maximum power transmitted through the cable is

$$P_{\max} = U_{\max} I_{\max} \sim d^2.$$

When the diameters of the inner core and outer sheath of the cable increase by 2 times, the maximum transmitted power increases by 4 times. For a cable whose parameters are specified in the task conditions, the maximum power is equal to

$$P_{\max} = U_{\max} I_{\max} = \frac{\pi d^2 E_{\max} B_{\max}}{2\mu_0} \ln \frac{D}{d} \approx 10^8 \text{ Вт.}$$

Note that the Sun is more than $10^3$ times as massive as Jupiter, so the size of the region where Jupiter's gravity is comparable to the influence of the Sun is $10^6$ times less than the radius of the orbital plan. At the same time, the time of significant interaction of the comet with Jupiter is incomparably short compared to the periods of revolution of Jupiter and the comet around the Sun, therefore, the meaning of the comet during this time is negligible. Therefore, we can divide the movement of the comet into three independent stages: 1) movement of the comet from a distant position towards the Solar system under the influence of the Sun’s gravity, 2) an “instant” turn in the field of Jupiter, 3) movement in an elliptical orbit around the Sun (and at this stage the influence of Jupiter is not taken into account).
