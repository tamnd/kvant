Alpha particles and Rutherford's experiments

In the fall of 1903, Ernest Rutherford, a thirty-year-old physics professor, sailed from Europe to Canada. He took with him a small metal box with a free load for him - thirty milligrams of radium salt.

The pig box caused concern among customs officials in New York - at that time there were no laws regarding the import of radium. Is it a jewel or a chemical? Should it be taxed and how? Officials are always the same, and American customs officials decided to forward the foreign cargo to their superiors. But researchers are also everywhere and always the same. And a report appeared in which the officials informed their superiors that Dr. Rutherford absolutely refused to part with his treasure. And only the professor's commitment to checking the box through the territory of the United States intact (that is, without speculating on the substance) allowed American officials to shift the solution to the duty problem to the films of their Canadian colleagues. Perhaps it was thanks to these milligrams of radium that many wonderful discoveries were made in physics.

Rutherford's name appears in school physics courses in connection with the establishment of the planetary model of the atom. But Rutherford is the author and multi-artist of discoveries no less valuable for physics. These include, in particular, experiments to study the nature of α-rays. Let's talk about some of them.

Back in 1899, while working at the Cavendish Laboratory (at the University of Cambridge), Rutherford established that the radiation of radioactive elements is not uniform. Here is one such experiment.

Experiment 1. Two zinc plates were placed horizontally next to each other. One plate was connected to a strip of a grounded battery, and the other to a grounded galvanometer. A thin layer of radium salt was poured onto the bottom plate. The radiation of the salt generated onns in the air, the air between the plates ceased to be an insulator, and an electric current arose, which was recorded by the device.

If radium salts are covered with a thin metal sheet, part of the radiation is absorbed, and the current becomes more than two times weaker. If the radiation is shielded by two sheets, the current will weaken by almost six times, if three - by the same time. It would seem that further on the current should gradually decrease (according to the exponential law). But it is surprising that the experiments did not confirm this - starting from the fifth sheet, the current practically did not decrease.

It was obvious to assume that oniization of the air is caused by at least two reasons. Or, in other words, that radiation consists of two different types: one radiation generates strong insulation, but is well absorbed by the metal, the other - ionizes the effect of the metal, but is more penetrating. Rutherford's first radiation is called α-rays, and the second - β-rays. Now scientists are faced with the problem of investigating the nature of these rays.
