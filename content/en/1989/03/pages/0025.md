Statement of the problem
and necessary simplifications

Let us formulate the conditions of the problem:

A man is standing on the street. Suddenly it starts to rain. The person goes to the nearest shelter, located at a distance l from him. At what speed should he beat in order to get as little wet as possible?

Fig. 2.

The task has been set, but in this form, please, we cannot solve it. Indeed, a person is a creature of complex shape, and when he moves, he also continuously changes his shape (rearranges his legs, waves his arms...). So making an accurate calculation is an incredibly difficult task. Therefore, there is nothing left but to solve its approximation, and therefore we reformulate the problem:

A suitable paralleled object whose face areas are $S_B$, $S_B$ and $S_J$ (from the words “side”, “top” and “front”) moves with speed and perpedicular to the face $S_J$. At the same time, it is raining, each drop of which has a speed v (Fig. 1) (the vector v is not necessarily directed vertically downwards - the rain can also be oblique). The number of raindrops per unit volume is k. The question is: how many drops N will fall on the paralleled object during the time that it moves a distance l, and at what value of speed i will this value N be the smallest?
