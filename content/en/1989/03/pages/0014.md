According to Birkhoff's theorem, in a convex figure with a main figure, there are periodic trajectories with any number of links. But for traekolniks the situation is of a modern significance: there are no two-link periodic trajectories in traekolniks at all, and four-link trajectories exist only in equal-adjusted trajectories. Moreover, for any natural number $n$ one can construct a trajectory in which each periodic trajectory has more than $n$ links. To do this, in the traekolnik $ABC$ it is enough to take the angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ at the base $AC$ to be very small and incommensurate with $\pi$ (i.e. $k\alpha+l\beta \neq m\pi$ for any integers $k, l, m$). For a periodic trajectory in such a trajectory, it is necessary to find the value from one side to the other (we leave the proof of this fact using inequality $k\alpha+l\beta \neq m\pi$ to the present reader). Due to the smallness of the angles, this link is almost parallel to the base $AC$ and therefore the number of reflections of our periodic trajectory from the side $\triangle ABC$ is greater than the smallest number $\left[\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right], \left[\frac{\pi}{\beta}\right]$. And these numbers, for sufficiently small $\alpha$ and $\beta$, are greater than any direction given $n$.

If the vertices of the trajectory are aligned, then in the resulting figure (according to Birkhoff’s theorem) there is a “low-link” periodic trajectory. However, with a decrease in smoothing, this trajectory will tend to a broken line, passing through the top of the trajectory, i.e., in the limit of permutation, the periodic trajectory.

Mechanical interpretation

Let's put a small ring on each side of the acute-angled traekolnik $ABC$ and pass a stretched rubber band $XYZX$ through them (see Fig. 4). The rubber band tends to roll off, so the rings will take positions at the vertices of the smallest perimeter of the traekolnik $XYZ$ inscribed in $ABC$. Let's look at the ring on the $AB$ side. Since it does not move along the side of the traekolnik, the equileading tension forces $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$ are perpendicular to this side. In addition, the vectors $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$ have the same length, since the tension along the elastic band is constant. Consequently, the vectors $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$ form mutually complementary angles with the segment $AB$. This means that $XYZX$ is a billiard trajectory. So, we have proven that the inscribed trajectory is the smallest periodic trajectory in $\triangle ABC$.
