We will solve the problem in the reference system associated with the parallelepid, i.e. we will consider the parallelepid motionless: then we must be计 on this reference system will receive a speed $\bar{w} = \bar{v} - \bar{u}$. The projections of the velocity vector $\bar{w}$ on the coordinate axes will be $w_x = v_x - u, w_y = v_y, w_z = v_z$ (u is the length of the vector $\bar{u}$). It is required to determine how many drops $N$ will fall on the parallelepid in time $\tau = l/u$ and at what $u$ the value $N$ will be minimal.

It is clear that in time $\tau$ all the drops will fall onto the parallelepid, from here from the sides of the parallelepid to a distance no greater than $\tau \cdot |\bar{w}|$, in the direction opposite to the vector $\bar{w}$, i.e. all the drops located inside the volume of the body outlined in red lines on figure 3. How to find the volume of this body? It is easy to notice that this body consists of three prisms, the areas of the bases of which are equal to $S_\pi, S_B$ and $S_B$, and the heights are the abscille values ​​of the projections of the vector $\tau \cdot \bar{w}$ on the axes $Ox, Oy$ and $Oz$, respectively. Therefore, the volume of this body is equal to

$$\tau(|w_x|S_\pi + |w_y|S_B + |w_z|S_B) = \tau(|v_x - u|S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B),$$

and the required number of drops $N$ is equal to $\tau k(|v_x - u|S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B)$.

Considering that $\tau = l/u$, we obtain the dependence of $N$ on $u$:

$$N = kl \frac{|v_x - u|S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B}{u}.$$

Now let’s find a $u > 0$ for which $N$ is minimal.

Let's continue with the solution

Since the quantities $k$ and $l$ are constant, for convenience we will operate with the quantities

$$\psi = \frac{N}{kl} = \frac{|v_x - u|S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B}{u}.$$

Let's consider two cases:

1. $v_x \leq 0$ (oncoming death).

In this case, $v_x - u < 0$, and the function $\psi$ takes the form:

$$\psi = \frac{(u - v_x)S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B}{u} = S_\pi + \frac{-v_x S_\pi + v_y S_B + v_z S_B}{u}.$$

Since $v_x \leq 0$, the numerator of the fraction is positive, and the function $\psi(u)$ kills on the interval $(0; +\infty)$. The graph of function $\psi$ versus $u$ is shown in figure 4. We see that although $\psi$ kills as $u$ increases, it is still always $\psi > S_\pi$, and $\psi \rightarrow S_\pi$ at $u \rightarrow \infty$.
