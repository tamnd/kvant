M1130. A convex n-gon is given on the plane, the length of the kth side is equal to $a_k$, and the length of the projection of the polygon onto the line containing this side is equal to $d_k$ (k=1,2,...,n). Prove the inequality
$$2 < \frac{a_1}{d_1} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{d_n} \leq 4.$$

Fig. 1.

Obviously, the length of the projection of a convex polygon onto any line is equal to two less than the sum of the lengths of the projections of its side onto this line (Fig. 1), and therefore less than half of its perimeter $p$. That's why
$$\frac{a_1}{d_1} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{d_n} > \frac{2a_1}{p} + \dots + \frac{2a_n}{p} = 2 \cdot \frac{a_1 + \dots + a_n}{p} = 2.$$

This estimate cannot be improved, as the example of a “flat” polygon shows, which has $d_i=p/2$ for all $i$.

To prove the upper bound, we construct some auxiliary polygon. Let us plot from one point $n$ pairs of opposite vectors, each of which is equal in length and parallel to one of the sides of the given $n$-gon $M$, and number these vectors clockwise: $\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_{2n}$ (Fig. 2); rated byindo, $\vec{v}_i = -\vec{v}_{i+n}, i=1, 2, \dots, n$. From the end $B_1$ of the vector $\vec{v}_1$ we disconnect the vector $\overrightarrow{B_1}B_2 = \vec{v}_2$, from its end $B_2$ we disconnect the vector $\overrightarrow{B_2}B_3 = \vec{v}_3$, etc. (Fig. 3). The last point $B_{2n}$ coincides with the beginning of $\vec{v}_1$.
