M1126. In a trapezii ABCD with base BC and AD on sides AB and CD, points K and M are chosen. Prove that if $\angle BAM=\angle CDK$ then $\angle BMA=\angle CKD$.

Let $O$ be the intersection point of the extensions of the lateral sides $AB$ and $CD$ of the given trapezoid (see figure). Let us carry out the sequential symmetry $S$ with respect to the bisector of the angle $AOD$ and the preparation $H$ with the center $O$ and the coefficient $OD/OA$. In this case, the point $A$ will go to $D$ (see figure), and the point $B$ - to $C$ (since $OC/OB=OD/OA$), the angle $OAM$ - to the angle $ODK$, which is equal to it by condition. Consequently, the point $M$, lying at the intersection of the lines $AM$ and $OD$, will go to the point of intersection of the images of these lines - $DK$ and $OA$, i.e. to the point $K$. So, the points $A$, $M$, $B$ go, respectively, to $D$, $K$, $C$, and therefore $\angle AMB=\angle DKC$.

Another solution is based on the fact that a circle can be described around the quadrilateral $AKMD$, and therefore also around $BKMC$.

V. N. Dubrovsky, A. S. Merkuryev

M1127. The "Cheburashka" microcalculator can add, subtract and find the reciprocal number $1/X$ from a given number $X$. Is it possible to use this microcalculator to obtain a unit, given the initial number $a)$ $\sqrt{19}+88$; b) $\sqrt{19}+88$; c) $\sqrt{19}+88$? (It is prohibited to enter numbers into the microcalculator that differ from the original ones or those obtained as a result of calculations on it.)

a) Answer: yes. First let's calculate

$$a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{19}+88} = \frac{88-\sqrt{19}}{88^2-19},$$

then we obtain the sequential numbers $88-\sqrt{19}$ (as the sum of $88^2-19$ terms $a$), $176=(88+\sqrt{19})+(88-\sqrt{19})$, $1/176$ and $1$ (the sum of $176$ terms $1/176$).

b) Answer: yes. Let us use the identity

$$\frac{1}{x} - \frac{2}{\frac{1}{1+x}+\frac{1}{1/x-y}} = xy^2.$$

Its left side can be calculated as "Cheburashka". Therefore, from the number $x$ one can obtain $x^3$ (with $y=x$), $x^5=x^3 \cdot x^2$, $x^7=x^5 \cdot x^2$, ..., $x^{19}$, i.e. from $\sqrt{19}88$ one can obtain $88$, then $1/88$ and $1$.

b) Answer: no. Applying the permitted operations to numbers of the form $a\sqrt{19}+b\sqrt{88}$ with dissimilar numbers $a$ and $b$, we will again obtain numbers of the same type (in particular, $1/(a\sqrt{19}+b\sqrt{88})=(a\sqrt{19}-b\sqrt{88})/(19a^2-88b^2))$. Therefore, it is impossible to obtain one from the number $\sqrt{19}+88$.
