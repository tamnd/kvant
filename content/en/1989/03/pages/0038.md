F1139. The U-shaped tube is partially filled with water (see figure). The upper ends of the tube are closed and the right elbow of the tube is heated to a temperature of $t_2 = 100^\circ\text{C}$, and the left one - to $t_1 = +99.5^\circ\text{C}$. Determine the steady-state difference in water levels in the tube bends. Right: At 23 floors (70 m above ground), the boiling point of water is 0.25 degrees lower than at ground level. The Tellian extension of the stack should be ignored when calculating.

Each of the tube bends contains air above the water (which was there before heating) and saturated water vapor. We will assume that during heating, very little water evaporated (this can be easily checked later), i.e. when the water level in one of the elbows rose by $\Delta h$, in the other it should also drop by $\Delta h$, and we will write down the condition of the water in the tube after heating:

$$p_1 + p_{\text{н1}} + \rho_{\text{воды}} g(2\Delta h) = p_2 + p_{\text{н2}},$$

where $p_1$ and $p_2$ are air pressures, $p_{\text{н1}}$ and $p_{\text{н2}}$ are pressures of saturated water vapor.

Air pressure is easily expressed in terms of atmospheric pressure $p_0$, modified by volume and temperature:

$$p_1 = p_0 \frac{T_1}{T_0} \frac{h_0}{h_0 - \Delta h}, p_2 = p_0 \frac{T_2}{T_0} \frac{h_0}{h_0 + \Delta h}.$$

From here we find the difference in air pressure:

$$\Delta p_{\text{возд}} = p_1 - p_2 = p_0 \left( \frac{T_1}{T_0} \frac{1}{1 - \Delta h/h_0} - \frac{T_2}{T_0} \frac{1}{1 + \Delta h/h_0} \right) =$$

$$= \frac{p_0}{T_0} \frac{T_1 - T_2 + (T_1 + T_2)\Delta h/h_0}{1 - (\Delta h/h_0)^2} \approx \frac{p_0}{T_0} \left( T_1 - T_2 + (T_1 + T_2)\frac{\Delta h}{h_0} \right)$$

(here we taught that $\Delta h/h_0 \ll 1$).

According to the conditions of the problem, the difference in saturated vapor pressure in the bends of the tube is equal to the pressure of a column of water with a height of $H = 140$ m:

$$\Delta p_{\text{н}} = p_{\text{н2}} - p_{\text{н1}} = \rho_{\text{возд}} g H = \frac{p_0 M_{\text{возд}}}{RT_0} g H.$$

Now from the equilibrium condition we find the relation

$$\frac{\Delta h}{h_0} = \frac{T_2 - T_1 + M_{\text{возд}} g H/R}{T_1 + T_2 + 2 \rho_{\text{возд}} g h_0 T_0/p_0} \approx 7 \cdot 10^{-3}$$

and the established difference in water levels

$$2\Delta h \approx 8,4 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ м} = 0,84 \text{ см}$$

(here we took $T_0 = 273$ K, $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa, $h_0 = 60$ cm).
