Were these numbers enough to solve the problem posed by Archimedes? It turns out that all that was needed for this was the numbers from the first period. We now know that the Universe is immeasurably larger than Archimedes thought, and that there are particles (such as neutrinos) that are wilder in size than the smallest gerbil that the ancient Greek scientist could imagine. And yet, if you try to calculate how many neutrinos can fit in a ball, the center of which coincides with the center of the Sun, and the radius is equal to the distance to the most distant nebula, then it turns out that the numbers of the first period are more than enough here too.

But although Archimedes already had names for huge numbers, he still did not know how to designate them. What he missed was the smallest thing, such that it couldn’t be changed: Archimedes, one of the most brilliant mathematicians in the entire history of man, didn’t get to the bottom of... zero! Nowadays, schoolchildren are introduced to zero in the first grade and, of course, are aware that this is one of the most important inventions in mathematics. Only after people learned to indicate the missing digits in the recording of numbers, to indicate that counting is carried out not in units, but in millions or hundreds of thousands, did they get their hands on the powerful ordine of nature - the positional number system. Without zero, all modern mathematics would not exist; there would not be such achievements of the human mind as spaceships, electronic computers, or atomic energy.

The most surprising thing is that, despite the importance of this discovery, the discoverer does not know its author. For more than three thousand years, Babylonia used its positional number system without using zero. Because of this, they could not say for sure that they would read the entry ▼▼▼▼ - whether it was the number 60 + 24, i.e. 84, or whether it was 60 60 + 24, i.e. 3624, or whether it was 60 60 + 24 60, i.e. 5040. And if you consider that they also used sexagesimal fractions, then the same entry could denote some fraction, for example 84/60 or 3624/3600.
