Phase rule for multicomponent systems

Pure water, pure bismuth, or any other pure substance is relatively rare. Significant value comes precisely up to various multi-component systems. For example, with rasters. The simplest example of a solution is water, which consists of two components - sugar and water, each of which is a type of molecule.

To describe the state of the solution, it is necessary, in addition to temperature and pressure, to indicate the relative content of all components, i.e. their concentration. It is clear that in a system consisting of K components, the value (K - 1) of concentration should be indicated.

For phases in equilibrium, temperature and pressure are common (identical) parameters. But equality of concentration does not follow. Moreover, it is easy to give an example of the opposite sense. If you put too much sugar in tea, it will not be placed even with long and intensive work with a spoon. A two-phase equilibrium will be established between the liquid trail tea and the solid sugar lying at the bottom. It is clear that the concentrations of the components in each of the two phases are completely different.

Taking this circumstance into account, let's try to describe the situation mathematically.

Let us assume that in a system of K components the phases are in equilibrium. Now the equilibrium conditions should be written down for each component:

$$q^{(1)}_I = q^{(1)}_{II} = q^{(1)}_{III} = \dots = q^{(1)}_{\Phi},$$

$$q^{(2)}_I = q^{(2)}_{II} = q^{(2)}_{III} = \dots = q^{(2)}_{\Phi},$$

$$q^{(K)}_I = q^{(K)}_{II} = q^{(K)}_{III} = \dots = q^{(K)}_{\Phi}$$

(the lower index indicates the phase number, and the upper index indicates the component number). This system of equality is a non-representational generalization of equilibrium conditions in a one-component system.

Each of the values ​​of $q$ must depend on the general pressure and temperature - on $p$ and $T$ - and on (K-1) concentration in its phase. Thus, the total number of parameters that must be specified to describe the equilibrium is $2 + (K-1)\Phi$. Now let's clean up the total number of the equation in the last system. For each component it is equal to (Ф - 1), and for the total components K. So, we get K(Ф - 1).
