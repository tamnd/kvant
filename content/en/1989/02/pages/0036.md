⟦folio 34⟧

Quests

M1146—M1150, F1153—F1157

M1146. Point $K$ is the midpoint of side $AB$ of equilateral triangle $ABC$. On the sides $AC$ and $BC$ the points $M$ and $N$ are taken so that $\angle MKN=60^\circ$. Prove that the perimeter of triangle $MCN$ is equal to half the perimeter of triangle $ABC$.

E. G. Gotman

M1147. Several points are given, connected by segments of two chains: some pairs of points - by head segments, some others - by paint. It is known that in any closed path consisting of several segments, the number of beautiful segments is even. Prove that all points can be divided into two sets so that each colored segment connects points from different sets, and each blue segment connects points from the same set.

M1148. Prove that for the non-integer $a>1$ (and $a\neq \sqrt[p]{q}$, where $p$ and $q$ are natural numbers) and the natural number $n$ the equality holds

$$[\log_a 2]+[\log_a 3]+...+[\log_a n]+[a]+[a^2]+...+[a^k]=nk,$$

where $k=[\log_a n]$ ([x] is the integer part of the number $x$).

R. V. Alekseev, L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1149. Two loops $p, q$ are given on the plane with vertices at points $P$ and $Q$, respectively. Two circles—one centered on lunar $p$, passing through point $P$, and the other centered on lunar $q$, passing through $Q$—touch each other externally at point $M$. Find the set of point $M$.

V. V. Shabunin

M1150. Prove that for any positive $a_1, a_2, a_3, ..., a_n$ the following inequality holds:

$$\frac{(a_1+a_2+...+a_n)^2}{2(a_1^2+a_2^2+...+a_n^2)} \leq \frac{a_1}{a_2+a_3} + \frac{a_2}{a_3+a_4} +...+\frac{a_n}{a_1+a_2}.$$

E. G. Moiseev, 10th grade student

F1153. The liquid solution of betan was poured into the body of the dump truck to the top. Estimate what proportion of the solvent will remain in the body after sudden braking. It is assumed that you have a good understanding of the phenomenon, can set the quantities necessary for the solution, choose their sufficiently reasonable numerical values ​​and obtain a numerical result.

G. V. Meledin

F1154. Two identical cubes of mass $m$ each lie on a smooth horizontal table. One of the cups is glued to the table (Fig. 1). A cube is lifted off the table if a horizontal force $F$ is applied to it. Between the cups there is a non-slip free spring with a stiffness of $k$. The loose cup was given a speed of $v$. At what speed will the cubes fly apart after a collision?

G. V. Fedogovich
