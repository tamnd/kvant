where A. N. Kolmogorov and his assistants are directly involved with him. At the last school they were all enrolled in a boarding school:

I still have Gena Arkhipov’s test paper. Now he is a Doctor of Physics and Mathematics, a well-known specialist in number theory. He was always in love with mathematics, and he showed so much creativity and wit while solving this physics test!

Our boarding school began operating in December 1963. You should have seen the interest with which the guys carried out laboratory work, made cosmic ray counters - of course - when the part was carried out, the light bulbs lit up. Theoretical physics courses enjoyed tremendous success. I would like to wish today's teachers and students the same fun work as it was in the early years.

A. B. Sosinsky. I started working at the boarding school in 1964 at the same time as such wonderful mathematicians as V. M. Alekseev, V. I. Arnold, V. M. Tikhomirov, D. B. Fuks and others - their mere presence at the school set an extremely high level of teaching. Both the children and the teachers loved one of the best mathematics teachers in Moscow, A. A. Shershevsky. Younger mathematicians who worked there in the first years of its existence also played a major role in the formation of the boarding school: E. V. Gaidukov, D. I. Gordeev, A. A. Egorov, Yu. A. Ilyashenko, I. G. Zhurbenko, A. S. Mishenko, V. A. Skvortsov. Physics was presented in an extremely interesting and lively manner. The classes were taught by young teachers, graduate students and physics students from Moscow State University and Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. The first director of the boarding school, P. A. Kuznetsov, did a lot during his short directorship (a little over a year later he moved to work as a director at another school). He was replaced by R. A. Ostraya, a front-line soldier, a historian by profession (Ransa Arkadyevna graduated from the Institute of Physics, Literature and Art), an excellently educated person, intelligent in the highest sense of the word. She immediately fell in love with and accepted the idea of ​​the school’s creators: in a boarding school one should acquire not only the exact sciences, but also cultural values.

When recruiting donors, Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov considered it important not only knowledge of mathematics or physics, but also knowledge, understanding of music, history, and some sports sciences. It’s not for nothing that FMS sometimes stands for “football-mathematics school” - this refers to heated matches between teams of students and teachers in football, basketball, volleyball and other competitions.

The team of teachers of the first years - people not much older than their students - managed to involve them in an interesting, intense intellectual life. Musical evenings and collective readings by Pasternak, Bulgakov, Khlebnikov, Zaboltsky, and Akhmatova were organized.

This period of the boarding school can be described from the wonderful poem “October 19” by the now famous post-bard Yu. Kim, who taught literature and history at the boarding school. There are these lines:

Under your heartless shadow
I wish I could learn to wave
I am happy for the lucky one,
I trust the vescous one,
Freethinking depth...

However, many did not like the “reckless cheerfulness”, the “mournless trust” caused distrust, and the “free-thinking depth” seemed completely poisonous. In the 70s, the development of the boarding school went in the wrong direction. It increasingly turned into something like a preparatory course for schoolchildren from the provinces, and social origin played an increasingly important role in entering the boarding school, rather than real abilities for science, so that from the 70s there was a decline in the scientific intensity of boarding school life.

Now new people have come to the boarding school, and I believe that the boarding school is a wonderful phenomenon of our culture.
