$$C = 2 + (K - 1)\Phi - K(\Phi - 1) = 2 - \Phi + K.$$
(For $K = 1$ (one-component system) we obtain the already known equality $C = 3 - \Phi$).

Since the number of degrees of freedom in meaning must be non-negative, it immediately follows from the phase rule that the maximum number of phases that can be in equilibrium in a K-component system is 2+K.

The phase rule was derived for a closed system. But, strictly speaking, this almost never happens. The system is usually in contact with the external environment, with which an exchange of atoms or molecules can occur. Sometimes this has to be taken into account. However, in the vast majority of cases, this circumstance can be painlessly challenged.

A lot of interesting conclusions follow from the phase rules. You can probably now easily explain why the temperature of the water-ice equilibrium is fixed. But if we are not dealing with pure water, but with some kind of aqueous solution, then the water-ice equilibrium (not pure!) at a fixed external pressure will “capture” a whole range of temperatures. Why this is so is left to explain on the basis of the phase rule.
