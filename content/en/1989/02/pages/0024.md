⟦folio 22⟧

In the spectrum of electromagnetic noise there are no visible maxima corresponding to the natural frequencies of the global electromagnetic resonance Earth-ionosphere.

Thousands of thunderstorms are raging around the globe at any given moment, with an average of about 100 lightning flashes every second. After each discharge, the Earth-ionosphere resonator “rings” like a bell at its own frequencies. But before this “ringing” has time to subside, the next discharge occurs, and the vibrations arise again. Since lightning flashes occur randomly, their continuous sequence excites electromagnetic noise in the resonator, which can be easily detected using a simple receiving device consisting of an antenna, an amplifier (without a detector) and a recording device. As the latter, a spectrum analyzer is used, which allows one to determine the amplitudes of electromagnetic oscillations at different frequencies (from units to tens of Hz). In the electromagnetic noise spectra obtained in this way (see figure), there are no visible maxima at natural frequencies close to those calculated above.
