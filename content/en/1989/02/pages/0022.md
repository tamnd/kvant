The thickness of the atmosphere is very small compared to the radius of the Earth, so formula (2) can be used to estimate the natural frequencies of the global atmospheric resonance. Of course, this is just an order of magnitude estimate, but it follows from it that for resonance to occur, a source is needed that can vibrate the entire thickness of the atmosphere with a period of the order of ten hours. Tidal forces acting on the atmosphere in the states of Lunya and the Sun are possessed in this way.*) Everyone knows about tides in the seas and oceans, but this is observed not only in expanses of water. There are ebbs and flows both in the solid cortical ball, although the height of the tidal waves here is very small (at the latitude of Moscow it is ~40 cm), and in the air shell, where, on the contrary, the amplitude of the weight is significant.

The Moon also acts on the atmosphere in a similar way, but due to its own rotation around the Earth, a slightly different period of tidal deformations will arise - $T_2 \approx 12,4$ hours. Since the Moon is much closer to the Earth than the Sun, the tidal forces associated with it should be, according to calculations, 2.5 times stronger than the solar ones.

For ocean tides, this conclusion is well confirmed in practice. However, observed tides in the atmosphere (they manifest themselves, in particular, in the form of regular periodic fluctuations in atmospheric pressure in the tropics with an amplitude of ~2 mm Hg) show that the amplitude of “solar” tides (with a 12-hour period) is approximately 16 times greater than that of “lunar” tides. Consequently, the observed ratio of the amplitudes of two tidal disturbances of the atmosphere (solar and lunar) differs from the calculated one by 40 times! Such a large discrepancy shows that something essential is missing in the theoretical reasoning.
