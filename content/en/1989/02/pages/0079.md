Selected School Problems
1. {{0, 0}; (1, 2)}. Solution. Since $1+x+x^2+x^3=(1+x)(1+x^2)$, then $1+x$ and $1+x^2$ are powers of two. Let $1+x=2^m$; then $1+x^2=2^{2m}-2^{m+1}+2$. If $m>1$, then the last expression when divided by 4 has a remainder of 2 and is not a power of two. Therefore $m=0$ or $1$ and $x=0$ or $1$.
2. $x_1=x_3=\ldots=x_9=-1$, $x_2=x_4=\ldots=x_{10}=1$. Solution. Dividing each equality by the following (the last by the first), we get: $x_1=-x_4=x_7=-x_{10}=x_3=-x_6=x_9=-x_2=x_5=-x_8$.
