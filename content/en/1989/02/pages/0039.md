M1123. The right angle is divided into an infinite number of square cells on the unit side. We will consider rows of cells, parallel sides of the lot (“vertical” and “horizontal” rows). Is it possible to write a natural number in each cell so that each vertical and each horizontal row of cells contains all the natural numbers once?

This question has been answered. However, from the possible arrangements it is constructed by an indication in powers of 2 (Fig. 1). Let a square $A_n$ of size $2^n \times 2^n$ (at the intersection of the first $2^n$ rows and $2^n$ columns) contain numbers from 1 to $2^n$ so that each of them appears once in each row and each column. figure 2 shows how from $A_n$ a square $A_{n+1}$ of size $2^{n+1} \times 2^{n+1}$ is made, in which the numbers from 1 to $2^{n+1}$ are located and the same condition is satisfied; the notation $A_n+2^n$ here means that $2^n$ is added to all numbers of the square $A_n$.

M1124. The sides, diagonals and extensions of the bases of the trapezoid intersect line l at six points, i.e., five segments are cut out on line l. a) Prove that if the extreme (1st and 5th) segments are equal, then the adjacent ones

Let the straight line l intersect the lateral sides AB and CD of the trapezoid at points K and N, the diagonals AC and BD at points L and M, the continuations of the bases AD=a and BC=b at points P and Q; PA=x, CQ=y (see figure). Then, considering pairs of similar triangles whose bases lie on lines AD and BC, and whose common vertex is one of the points K, L, M or N, we obtain:

$$\frac{PK}{KQ} = \frac{x}{b+y}, \frac{PN}{NQ} = \frac{x+a}{y}, \frac{PL}{LQ} = \frac{x}{y}, \frac{PM}{MQ} = \frac{x+a}{y+b}.$$
