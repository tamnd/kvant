Exercise 2. Prove that $S_i^e S_j^\delta = S_j^e S_i^\delta$ for $\varepsilon$, $\delta \in \{1, -1\}$, $|i - j| \geq 2$.

Let's look at one more example: we prove the relation $K_3 = 1$ (established geometrically on figure 3) by direct calculation. We have:

$$K_3 = S_2(S_1 S_3^{-1}) S_1^{-1} S_3 S_2^{-1} S_1(S_3 S_1^{-1}) S_3^{-1} = S_2 S_3^{-1}(S_1 S_1^{-1}) S_3 S_2^{-1}(S_1 S_1^{-1})(S_3 S_3^{-1}) = S_2 S_3^{-1} \cdot 1 \cdot S_3 S_2^{-1} \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = S_2(S_3^{-1} S_3) S_2^{-1} = S_2 S_2^{-1} = 1.$$

Here we first took advantage of distant commutability, and then everything “self” contracted (due to trival relations).
