⟦folio 44⟧

The moving reporting system (circle with radius $r$) and the trace that the coin draws on the moving platform (circle with radius $\rho$). If in a stationary reporting system a coin moves counterclockwise (like the platform), then relative to the platform its movement occurs clockwise. With the “fast” movement of the platform, when $\omega^2 R/(\mu g) \gg 1$, the coin in the stationary reporting system practically stands in place $(r \ll R)$, and the platform below it describes circles with a radius $R$, so that $\rho \approx R$. With the “slow” movement of the platform, when $\omega^2 R/(\mu g) \gtrsim 1$, the coin almost keeps up with the platform, describing circles of almost the same radius in the stationary reporting system: $r \approx R$, so $\rho \rightarrow 0$.

E. I. Butikov

F1136. In the circuit shown in figure 1, the amperators show points 0.2 A and 0.3 A. After the two resistors in the circuit were placed in places, the ampere readings did not change. What current is flowing through the battery? Assume the battery voltage remains constant. The resistances of the amperators are quite small.

In the original circuit, the voltage across each resistor is the same. Since after rearranging the resistors, the ampere readings have not changed, which means that the resistances of the rearranged resistors are the same.

Let's consider two possible options for rearranging the middle resistor and one of the extreme ones (Fig. 2):

$$R_1 = R_2 \text{ и } R_2 = R_3.$$

Option $R_1 = R_3$, i.e., rearranging the extreme resistors, is impossible, since otherwise the amperators would initially have to show the same points.

So (see Fig. 2):

Fig. 1. Fig. 2.

1) $R_1 = R_2 \Rightarrow I_1 = I_2 = \frac{I_1 + I_2}{2} = 0,1 \text{ A},$
$$I_{6ат1} = I_1 + (I_2 + I_3) = 0,1 \text{ A} + 0,3 \text{ A} = 0,4 \text{ A}.$$

2) $R_2 = R_3 \Rightarrow I_2 = I_3 = \frac{I_2 + I_3}{2} = 0,15 \text{ A},$
$$I_{6ат2} = I_3 + (I_1 + I_2) = 0,15 \text{ A} + 0,2 \text{ A} = 0,35 \text{ A}.$$

A. R. Zilberman
