F1155. A heavy piston with mass $M$ can move freely inside a vertical heat-conducting cylinder with cross-section $S$, the upper end of which is closed and the lower end is open to the atmosphere (Fig. 2). Inside the cylinder there is a horizontal partition with a small hole that cuts off one mole of air from the atmosphere, occupying a volume of $V$ and having an atmospheric pressure of $p_a$. The piston, which initially pressed the force against the partition, is released. Assuming that the internal energy of the gas is $cT$, find how much the piston will lower.

G. L. Kotkin

F1156. Two identical electromagnets $L_1$ and $L_2$ are connected in series to a direct current circuit (Fig. 3). Using the key $K_1$, a diode $D$ can be connected in parallel to one of them in a non-conductive direction. When the key $K_2$ is closed, there is a nice iron plate to the electromagnets. If the key $K_1$ is open, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate breaks away from the common magnets at the same time and falls, maintaining a horizontal position. If the key $K_1$ is closed, then when $K_2$ is opened, the plate first breaks away from the magnet $L_1$, and then from $L_2$, which leads to its rotation. Explain the difference in the behavior of the plate in the first and second cases.

V. V. Golubev

F1157. There are two translucent mirrors, each of which, as measured, transmits approximately 1/5 of the light flux, and reflects the rest of the light. If both mirrors are installed on the path of a parallel beam of light so that their planes are perpendicular to the beam, then it would seem that they should transmit 1/25 of the incident light flux, whereas in fact the light is attenuated not by 25 times, but noticeably less (about 10 times). Explain the phenomenon.

V. V. Golubev

Problem solutions

M1121—M1124, F1133—F1136

I. One of the most straightforward solutions is based on dropping from the point $B$ the perendiculars $BH$, $BA_1$, $BC_1$ to the straight lines $AC$, $AK$, $CK$ (they are equal according to symmetry messages) and considering the receiving angles with the vertex $B$. Triangles $KA_1B$ and $KC_1B$ are equal, and $\angle A_1BK = \angle KBC_1$ (their sum is $2\angle B$, since $\angle A_1BA = \angle ABH$, $\angle HBC = \angle CBC_1$). On the other hand, the radius $OB$ of the circumscribed circle in any triangle $ABC$ also makes the same angles on the side $AB$ and $BC$ as the height $BH$ on the side $BC$ and $AC$, respectively:

$$\angle ABO = |90^\circ - \angle AOB/2| = |90^\circ - \angle C| = \angle HBC,$$
$$\angle CBO = |90^\circ - \angle COB/2| = |90^\circ - \angle A| = \angle HBA.$$

Therefore, $\angle A_1BO = \angle OBC_1 = \angle B$, i.e. $OB$ and $KB$ lie on the same straight line - the bisserp of the angle $A_1BC_1$.
