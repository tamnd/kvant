Experiment 5. Using a glass lens and a flat mirror, it is not difficult to determine the refractive index $n_{ж}$ of an arbitrary unknown liquid, and, unlike the method discussed in the second experiment, for this purpose only a few drops of liquid are sufficient, which, moreover, may not be transparent.

Let's apply 3-4 drops of the liquid under study to the mirror $3_1$ and cover it with lens $J_1$ (Fig. 4). A thin concave-planar liquid lens $J_2$ is formed between the surface of the lens and the surface of the mirror. The first surface along the bow is concave - it is in contact with the second surface of the glass lens, i.e. $r_{ж} = -r,$ and the second surface of the liquid lens is flat, i.e. $r_{ж} \to \infty,$ and formula (2) for $J_2$ takes the form

$$\frac{1}{F_2} = -(n_{ж} - 1)\frac{1}{r}.$$

Lens $J_2$ together with glass lens $J_1$ form an optical system $J_3,$ whose focal length $F_3$ can be changed in the manner described in the third experiment. For an optical system $J_3,$ consisting of two adjacent thin lenses, we have (this expression is not difficult to obtain on your own)

$$\frac{1}{F_3} = \frac{1}{F_1} + \frac{1}{F_2}.$$

Let us express from here the optical power of a liquid lens through easily variable focal lengths $F_1$ and $F_3$:

$$\frac{1}{F_2} = -\frac{F_3 - F_1}{F_1 F_3}.$$

Then for $n_{ж}$ we get

$$n_{ж} = 1 + \frac{d_1(F_3 - F_1)}{F_3(F_1 - d_1)}.$$

If the surfaces of the lens have different radii of curvature, then, touching the liquid with different surfaces of the lens, we will change different pairs of values ​​$d_1$ and $F_3.$ But, regardless of the orientation of such a lens, both calculated values ​​$n_{ж}$ for a given liquid should be the same (within the possible error of the result). Therefore, in the case of an asymmetric lens, you can conduct two experiments with the same liquid and obtain two values ​​of the desired quantity $n_{ж}.$

Experiment 6. In conclusion, consider a case that is a combination of previous experiments. Let's take glass from glasses for further use - a positive meniscus lens $J_m$ of small optical power of 1.5-2 diopters. Let us carry out three experiments with this glass in succession: No. 1, No. 3 and No. 3 + No. 2 (the experimental diagrams are shown in figure 5) and change the corresponding values ​​$R_1, F_1$ and $F_3.$ Then, based on formulas (2) and (5), we obtain the following expression for calculating the refractive index on the surface of the lens $J_m$ liquid:

$$n_{ж} = 1 + \frac{R_1(F_1 - F_3)}{F_1 F_3}.$$
