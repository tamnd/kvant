Our very rough estimates of $T_1$ are not suitable for testing the resonance hypothesis, so we should turn to experimental data. The period of natural atmospheric oscillations was determined based on observations of those around-the-world pressure waves that arose after the explosion of the Krakatoo volcano and the fall of the Tunguska meteorite, which we have already mentioned. The period $T_1$ found in this way turned out to be equal to 10.5 hours. This value does not contradict our rough estimate $T_1$ in terms of the order of magnitude, but is completely inconsistent with the assumption of resonance. It would seem that the resonance hypothesis should be discarded. But at the end of the 30s of our century it was revived again, in a more complex version.

The main feature of the new theory is the assumption of two periods of natural oscillations of the atmosphere: $T_{1\text{н}}=10,5$ h for the lower layers and $T_{1\text{в}}=12$ h for the upper ones (above ~50 km). If this is really the case, then one can imagine that explosions on the Earth’s surface are caused by short-period atmospheric oscillations, and tides that cover the entire thickness of the atmosphere are caused by long-period oscillations. However, how are the lower and upper bishops isolated from each other? The role of a barrier between them can be played by a layer of air with an elevated temperature, which weakly transmits IGW upwards, and does not transmit those waves that originate above.

Calculations show that a 12-hour period of oscillations in the upper atmosphere can occur if the air temperature at an altitude of 50-60 km reaches 320-340 K. After this maximum, the temperature decreases, and starting from an altitude of 80-100 km it increases again. The accepted changes that began to arise with the advent of meteorological packages confirmed the presence of the predicted maximum! However, the increase in temperature turned out to be not as significant ($T_{\text{макс}} \approx 270—280$ K) as follows from the resonance theory.

Thus, the theory is observed periodically due to changes in atmospheric parameters until it is completely completed.

Global electromagnetic resonances

Under the influence of solar radiation in the upper layers of the atmosphere, atoms and molecules are ionized, free charges appear, and the electrical conductivity of the air, starting from a height of 50-60 km, increases greatly. This is where the ionosphere begins, which at large distances from the Earth “closes” with interplanetary space. The lower layers of the atmosphere, several tens of kilometers thick, have very low electromagnetic waves (radio waves). What are the natural frequencies of this spherical resonance? The answer is expressed using formula (2), but with the speed of sound $v$ replaced by the speed of light $c=3 \cdot 10^8$ m/s. The result is the following set of natural frequencies of global electromagnetic resonance:

$$f_n = \frac{3 \cdot 10^8 \cdot \sqrt{n(n+1)}}{2 \cdot 3,14 \cdot 6,4 \cdot 10^6} \approx 10,6; 18,3; 25,9; \dots$$
