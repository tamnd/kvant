⟦folio 31⟧

Let's open the source...

...which is the book by N. G. Pomyalovsky “Essays on the Bursa”. We will talk about a seemingly simple problem that arose while reading a book.

In the first of the essays, entitled “Winter Evening at the College,” one of the students, whose name is Tsapley, during a boring lesson performs the following actions (quoted): “Heron writes something with all his might; From the outside looking in, he is a most diligent student, but meanwhile he does this: he writes a number, then another one under it, then multiplies them, under the product he writes the first number again, multiplies the numbers again, etc.; works, wanting to know what will come of it.”

So let’s ask ourselves a simple question: “So what will come of this?” This question bleibt another: “What exactly did the student do, how to track his actions?” The author asked his friends to explain what Tsaplya does. Each interviewee stated that everything was crystal clear to him and gave an explanation that differed from those offered by the others. Nevertheless, after a series of exercises, the author managed to reduce all the tracks to those that offer attention to the individual.

Interpretation first, the most uninteresting

By the way, the majority of respondents chose it. The sequence of numbers $x_n$ is constructed according to the following rule: $x_0$ and $x_1$ are specified in advance and $x_n=x_{n-1}x_0$ with $n\geq 2$.

There is no problem with this interpretation. Since each subsequent term is obtained by multiplying the previous one by the same number, the usual geometric progression will arise: $x_n=x_1x_0^{n-1}$. Sequences of this type are so well studied that there is simply nothing to add here: everyone can easily determine not only the values ​​of an arbitrary term, but also easily calculate, for example, the sum of the first $n$ terms, or even the entire sequence (if $x_0<1$).

The interpretation is the same and, unfortunately, the most interesting

This interpretation was given by only one person - unfortunately, not the author of this article. Its essence is this. Each time, under a work, the student writes down the first number of this work and performs the next arrangement. For example:

$$9.8=72; 72.7=504; 504.5=2520$$

etc.
