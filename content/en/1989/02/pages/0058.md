⟦folio 56⟧

Narika. (The illuminator screen will be discussed in more detail below.) We fix the light bulb so that its filament is in the plane of the screen. Let's place the illuminator-screen $\partial$ above the concave spherical mirror $\partial$ lying on the table in such a way that the thread $S$, playing the role of an accurate light source, is close to the main optical axis of the screen (the line connecting the top of the screen with the center of its spherical surface), and the plane of the screen is perpendicular to this axis (Fig. 1).

By moving the screen in height and observing it from above, we can easily find a position in which a sharp and bright image $S'$ appears on the screen next to the source $S$. Achieving this position means that the center of curvature of the screen is also located on the surface of the screen - only in this case, the rays from the source go almost normally to the spherical surface of the screen and, changing their direction to the opposite when reflected, are focused near $S$. To determine the radius of curvature $R$ of the screen, all that remains is to change the distance from the screen $\partial$ to the stripe (vertex) of the screen $\partial$.

Routine 2. Let’s set up the experiment by pouring a small amount of the test liquid, for example water, onto the screen $\partial$. Then a thin liquid line $J_{\pm}$ (Fig. 2, a) is formed on top of the screen, and the first line along the ray of the surface of this line is flat, i.e., has a radius of curvature $r_1 \rightarrow \infty$, and the second is convex - it is in contact with the surface of the screen, i.e. $r_2 = R$.

If the water does not cover the entire mirror, then the image $S'$ on the screen $\partial$, distant from the screen $\partial$, is preserved - it creates a free part of the screen surface. But, lowering the screen by about 0.3 $R$ (this value is different for different liquids), we get a second color and bright image $S''$, forming that part of the screen that is covered with liquid. For this image to appear in the plane of the screen, it is necessary that, after refraction on a flat liquid surface, the line falls on the screen again almost normally to its surface. Only under such reflection conditions do the rays change their direction by 180° and form an image $S''$ adjacent to $S$.

Let's assume that we have achieved the appearance of a clear image $S''$. Let us denote the corresponding distance from the screen plane to the screen stripe by $d$. Let us now separate the mental liquid line $J_{\pm}$ from the screen (Fig. 2, b). In the absence of a screen, the rays, which, as we have established, fall normally on the lower surface of the line, would pass this surface without deflection and form a mini-image $S'$ at the point of intersection of the continuation of the rays, i.e. at the center of curvature of the spherical surface at a distance $f = R$. Therefore, the thin line formula for our case looks like:

$$\frac{1}{d} - \frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{F},$$

where $F$ is the focal length of the line.
