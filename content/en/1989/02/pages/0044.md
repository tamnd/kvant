F1135. A horizontal platform with a coin lying on it makes a circular translational motion in the horizontal plane so that all its points describe circles of radius $R$ with angular velocity $\omega$. The friction coefficient between the coin and the pad is $\mu$. What will be the steady-state movement of the coin? What trace does she “draw” on the site?

The general nature of the established movement of the coin is best understood from the messages of symmetry. With the circular translational motion of a horizontal platform, all its points and all directions in the horizontal plane are physically equivalent. Therefore, whatever the initial conditions are in the steady state in a stationary (laboratory) inertial reporting system, the coin will move along the circle translationally (without rotation) and with the same angular velocity $\omega$ as the platform. Only the position of the center of this circle depends on the initial conditions. Any other mental trajectory does not have this property - the absence of identified directions.

Symmetry messages allow us to draw another conclusion: relative to the platform (i.e., the moving non-personal reporting system), if the coin slips, it also moves in a circle.

Now that we imagine the nature of the movement of the coin, all that remains is to find the radius of the circles drawn by the coin in one and the other reporting system.

In the horizontal plane, the only force acting on the coin is the friction force from the platform. Let's consider first with a platform, i.e. it does not slip. In this case, in the inertial reporting system, the coin moves along a circle of radius $R$ with acceleration $\omega^2 R$, directed towards the center of this circle. Since this acceleration is imparted to the coin by the friction of rest, the model of which cannot exceed the value $\mu m$, then the movement together with the platform (without slipping) will occur under the condition $\omega^2 R \leq \mu g$, i.e. at $\omega^2 R / (\mu g) \leq 1$.
