If you love puzzles, you may be familiar with the name Jerry Slocum, a researcher and collector of puzzles from California, to whom Martin Gardner devoted a chapter in one of his popular books. In 1986, Slocum, along with Danish puzzle inventor Jack Botermans, published a book about what was probably the world's largest collection: Puzzles Old and New. This basilically illustrated book presents all kinds of mechanical puzzles, that is, “objects consisting of one or more parts that must be brought into a given state by manipulating with hands, using logic, intuition, luck and LOB.” Introduce one puzzle from this book that attracted us because it was easy to do, but not easy to solve.

To make it, you will need a good stand and 5 identical matchboxes with a 3:2:1 size match (such boxes can be found, although they differ from our most common standard). Glue them into 5 figures, as shown in figure. Now the challenge: close all the boxes!
As the authors of the book write, three different solutions are known. Here is the appearance of one of them. And the secret of the assembly will be revealed in the next issue of the magazine.
