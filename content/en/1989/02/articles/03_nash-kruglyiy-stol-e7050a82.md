OUR ROUND TABLE

25 years ago, on the initiative of the country's leading scientists, academicians A. N. Kolmogorov, I. G. Petrovsky, P. S. Aleksandrov, I. K. Kikon and M. A. Lavrentiev, specialized physics and mathematics boarding schools were created in Moscow, Leningrad, Kyiv and Novosibirsk. The goal of this school was set as follows: to provide schoolchildren living far from scientific centers with the same opportunities for learning science as students in university cities.

Recently, our editorial office hosted a meeting dedicated to the twentieth anniversary of the physics and mathematics boarding school at Moscow State University. We present its participants: D. L. Abarov - director of the school, V. V. Vasilov - associate professor of Moscow State University, member of the editorin «Kvant», V. N. Dubrovsky - teacher at the boarding school at Moscow State University, member of the editorin «Kvant», A. A. Egorov - researcher at the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR, teacher at the boarding school at Moscow State University, O. N. Naida - quick-willed scientist schools, now a researcher, candidate of physical and mathematical sciences, A. V. Sosinsky - associate professor at MIEM, member of the editorin «Kvant», G. Sh. Fridman - associate professor at Omsk State University, graduate of the Physics and Mechanics School at NSU.

D. L. Abarov. The main result of any school is its graduates. Over the 25 years of its existence, our boarding school has graduated more than 4,000 people. Almost all of them entered universities (mainly Moscow State University and Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology); many completed graduate studies and completed their dissertations.

Among our graduates there are more than 400 candidates and 20 doctors of science, there are laureates of the Lenin and State Prizes, prizes of the Council of Ministers of the USSR and the Lenin Komsomol.

Our graduate Yu. A. Matiyasevich solved Hilbert’s 10th problem; outstanding work was done by A. N. Varchenko, E. V. Shepin, I. M. Krichever, V. V. Nikulin, etc. Now the maximum age of graduates is 43 years, they successfully work in various scientific institutes, computer centers, and educational institutions.

Among the boarding school teachers there are 25 candidates and 3 doctors of science. This allows you to conduct educational courses in a non-standard way, develop and maintain interest in science. We are pleased with the success of our students in optimization at all levels - from Moscow to international.

Back in the 60s, a tradition emerged: graduates of the boarding school, having become students, returned to the school as a teacher. It is impossible to list them all. I will name only A.P. Veselov, V.N. Dubrovsky, A.N. Dranishnikov, B.M. Ivlev, T.N. Trushanina, E.V. Shepina - this is a leading teacher who has been working in the boarding school for a long time and creatively.

O. N. Naida. In 1962-63, on behalf of the Komsomol committee, a physicist at Moscow State University, I helped I.K. Kikon in his work with schoolchildren. Literally before my eyes, in the spring of 1963, I.K. Kikon wrote a letter to the government with a proposal to open a boarding school in Moscow, similar to the one in Novosibirsk, which was already operating at that time, thanks to the initiative of M.A. Lavrentiev. The letter was signed by the rector of Moscow State University I. G. Petrovsky, the resident of the USSR Academy of Sciences M. V. Keldysh, as well as the heads of graduate schools, education and leading industry representatives. The result of their authenticity was a resolution of the Council of Ministers of the USSR on the organization of boarding schools at Kiev, Leningrad, Moscow and Novosibirsk universities.

Then there was a huge amount of work to carry out the first enrollment at the school. The first 19 students (11th grade) were recruited from among the winners of the III All-Russian Mathematical Olympiad. They were invited to Letnyushka in Krasnovidovo near Mozhaisk,

The team of teachers of the first years - people not much older than their students - managed to involve them in an interesting, intense intellectual life. Musical evenings and collective readings by Pasternak, Bulgakov, Khlebnikov, Zaboltsky, and Akhmatova were organized.

This period of the boarding school can be described from the wonderful poem “October 19” by the now famous post-bard Yu. Kim, who taught literature and history at the boarding school. There are these lines:

In the 70s, the development of the boarding school went in the wrong direction. It increasingly turned into something like a preparatory course for schoolchildren from the provinces, and social origin played an increasingly important role in entering the boarding school, rather than real abilities for science, so that from the 70s there was a decline in the scientific intensity of boarding school life.

Now new people have come to the boarding school, and I believe that the boarding school is a wonderful phenomenon of our culture.

BRAIDS AND KNOTS

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. V. SOSINSKY

Braiding hair and tying knots - is this really mathematics?

An earnest student clearly takes away from school the following point of view: mathematics deals with abstract concepts, and everyday objects, like braids and knots, have nothing to do with mathematics.

But that's not true. Mathematicians in our time deal not only with emerging problems - number theory, calculations of space flights, the study of poetic meters - but also with earthly, everyday issues: economic calculations and even... the theory of queues.

And braid theories. This is a realizing and living science that arose in the 20s of our century; is not yet completed and has not exhausted its applications. And in beauty, the theory of braids is not inferior to the classical, but frozen in its magnitude, mathematics of antiquity and the 17th century, which is only studied in school.

Our story begins with examples of braids (Fig. 1). You can imagine a braid like this: n nails are driven into the upper and lower edges of a vertical board (n can equal 1, 2, 3,...) - each of the nails on the upper base is connected by a thread to one of the nails on the bottom; the threads do not intersect in pairs and must always go down (the thread has no right to turn around or start going up: the figures on figure 2, for example, are not braids).

Two braids are considered equivalent (i.e., the same) if one can be turned into an exact copy of the other by moving the threads (without breaking or gluing) so that each point of each thread moves only in the horizontal plane. Such a movement is shown in figure 3.

At figure 1 at the top at the beginning of each thread, the artist indicated its serial number. Below again the number of each thread is indicated - but here the numbers are not required and are in order: each braid corresponds to a permutation of the numbers of its threads. Thus, the braids $K_1$, $K_4$, $K_5$ and figure 1 correspond to permutations

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Among the braids at figure 1, the painted braid $K_4$ stands out; it is called so not at all because the artist painted its legs in different colors: any braid that corresponds to an identical permutation is called dyed

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & \dots & n \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & \dots & n \end{pmatrix},$$

i.e., a braid that preserves the order of thread numbers. A trivial braid, all of whose threads are vertical, is a special case of a dyed braid.

By the way, figure 1 shows not one, but two trivial braids. Two? — Yes, exactly two: the braid $K_3$ is trivial, or it easily turns (see Fig. 3) ​​into a braid of four vertical threads.

Among all the braids, except for the dyed ones, it is worth highlighting the cyclic braids that are in a sense opposite to them: these are braids that rearrange all the thread numbers in a single cycle, as the braid $K_5$ does:

$$1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 1.$$

Braids are one of the simplest geometric objects that can easily be “algebraized”: braids with the same number of threads can be multiplied. This is done quite simply (see Fig. 4): you need to attach one circle, gluing the corresponding threads, and remove the studs that have become unnecessary (the lower studs of the first braid and the upper ones of the second). Such multiplication has a number of properties of general multiplication of numbers: the associative law is satisfied

$$K_1(K_2K_3)=(K_1K_2)K_3,$$

$$1 \cdot K=K \cdot 1=K.$$

There is also an analogue of division: each braid $K$ has a reverse braid $K^{-1}$; for her

$$K^{-1} \cdot K=K \cdot K^{-1}=1.$$

The last fact is not very clear, and we invite the reader to figure out how to construct a braid inverse to this one. Braid multiplication, however, is not commutative: $K \cdot L$ is not necessarily equal to $L \cdot K$ (an example is given below).

I. Trival relations

$$S_i S_i^{-1} = S_i^{-1} S_i = 1,$$

I'll try to answer. The fact is that braid theory, as already mentioned, has many applications both in mathematics and beyond. I have focused here on only one application that is especially valuable - the application to the theory of knots.

Let's first look at a few examples of nodes (Fig. 9). A knot is a closed line in space, smooth or broken, which can be twisted and intertwined as desired. It is useful to imagine that the knot is made of a thin, flexible and extensible thread; we consider that two knots are the same (equivalent) if one can be turned into an exact copy of the other by moving, bending, stretching and skimming this thread in space without breaking it. Among the knots, a trivial knot stands out (actually, not a knot at all) - a common, insignificant circle: $Y_0$ on figure 9. In conclusion, for readers who do not think beauty is a sufficient basis for studying the subject, we will try to answer the question: “Why is all this necessary?” Part of the answer lies in the very history of the emergence of the theory of braids and knots.

The theory of braids was created in the twenties of our century by the still young German algebraist E. Artin at the request of... a weaving factory: he performed, as we would say today, management work for this enterprise.

The origins of the theory of knots are more ancient, and the interesting circumstances of its immediate emergence are almost forgotten. And it was like this.
