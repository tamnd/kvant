$a_2 \cdot 2^k$ has remainder $5^k - 1$ when divided by $5^k$, and if $x - 1 = a_2 \cdot 2^k$ then $x$ is divisible by $5^k$.

We are convinced that in each of the four random numbers the need $x$ exists and is unique, i.e. there are only four such numbers. It is also clear that in case (3) the number $x$ ends in 6, and in case (4) in 5. The theorem is proven.

We will carry out the second proof, assuming that it is already known that there are no more than four numbers with the indicated property (we know this from the first proof, but it can easily be proven directly). In addition, we apply induction on $k$: we will assume that the numbers $x_{k-1}$ and $y_{k-1}$ have already been found, which turn out to be five and six, respectively, such that $0 \leqslant x_{k-1} < 10^{k-1}$, $0 \leqslant y_{k-1} < 10^{k-1}$, and the differences $x_{k-1}^2 - x_{k-1}$ and $y_{k-1}^2 - y_{k-1}$ are divisible by $10^{k-1}$. Our task is to find the numbers $x_k$ and $y_k$ with similar properties.

To find $x_k$, we square $x_{k-1}$ and take the last $k$ digits of that square; thus, $x_{k-1}^2 = a \cdot 10^k + x_k$. Let us show that $x_k^2 - x_k$ is divisible by $10^k$:

$$x_k^2 - x_k = (x_{k-1}^2 - a \cdot 10^k)^2 - (x_{k-1}^2 - a \cdot 10^k) = x_{k-1}^4 - 2ax_{k-1}^2 \cdot 10^k + a^2 \cdot 10^{2k} - x_{k-1}^2 + a \cdot 10^k = (x_{k-1}^2 - x_{k-1})(x_{k-1}^2 + x_{k-1}) + 10^k(a^2 \cdot 10^k + a - 2ax_{k-1}^2).$$

No $x_{k-1}^2 - x_{k-1}$ is divisible by $10^{k-1}$, and $x_{k-1}^2 + x_{k-1}$ is divisible by 10 (because $x_{k-1}$ and $x_{k-1}^2$ happened to be 5); that means $(x_{k-1}^2 - x_{k-1})(x_{k-1}^2 + x_{k-1})$ is divisible by $10^k$.

The construction of $y_k$ is a little more complicated: you need to take the last $k$ digits of the number $y_{k-1}^5$. The proof that $y_k^2 - y_k$ is divisible by $10^k$ is almost the same:

$$y_k^2 - y_k = (y_{k-1}^5 - b \cdot 10^k)^2 - (y_{k-1}^5 - b \cdot 10^k) = y_{k-1}^10 - y_{k-1}^5 + 10^k \cdot c = (y_{k-1}^2 - y_{k-1})(y_{k-1}^8 + y_{k-1}^7 + y_{k-1}^6 + y_{k-1}^5 + y_{k-1}^4 + 10^k \cdot c,$$

where $c = b^2 \cdot 10^k + b - 2by_{k-1}^5$. But the expression $y_{k-1}^8 + \dots + y_{k-1}^4$ is divisible by 10,

because it is the sum of five digits ending in 6, and the difference $y_{k-1}^2 - y_{k-1}$ is divisible by $10^{k-1}$; that means $y_{k-1}^2 - y_{k-1}$ is divisible by $10^k$. The proof is over.

What can be seen from these two pieces of evidence? From the first proof it is clear that the sum of the third and fourth numbers found is equal to $10^k + 1$ (indeed, $25 + 76 = 101, 625 + 376 = 1001$). The second proof demonstrates perfect ordering weis: four $k$-digit endings that are preserved when squared by adding one digit to the left! (Indeed, the last $k$ digits of the number $x_k^2$ is $x_{k-1}$ and only the $k$th digit from the end of the number $x_k$ is new; similarly for $y_k$.) We could already notice this in our numerical examples:

0—00—000—...
1—01—001—...
5—25—625—...
6—76—376—...

Thus, in the sequence 5, 25,..., $x_{k-1}$, $x_k$,...... there comes an infinite increase in numbers on the left, and similarly in the sequence 6, 76,..., $y_{k-1}$, $y_k$,...... If we do not leave this process, we will get two infinite “numbers” (we write them with 10 digits):

$X = \dots 8212890625,$
$Y = \dots 1787109376.$

We put the word “numbers” in quotation marks, but, as we will now see, such an endless sequence can give the status of real numbers, although they are somewhat unusual.
