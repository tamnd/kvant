Thus, among the supernumbers there are all ordinary natural numbers (and 0), but not only them. Supernumbers can be added and multiplied (in a column) - see figure 1, a and 1, b, and for ordinary numbers this addition and multiplication coincides with ordinary addition and multiplication. It is interesting that supernumbers can always be subtracted in any order - also in a column - figure 2 (for supernumbers there is no concept of “larger - smaller”). In particular, any supernumber can be subtracted from zero (from the supernumber ...000). This means that among the supernumbers there are all ordinary negative numbers; for example, on figure 3 is the number -132=...99868.

By the way, it is very interesting that “ordinary negative numbers” are exactly supernumbers in which, starting from a certain point, all the digits are nines.

Addition and multiplication of supernumbers (as well as their subtraction) have the usual properties: $a+b=b+a$, $ab=ba$, $a+(b+c)=(a+b)+c$, $a(bc)=(ab)c$, $a(b+c)=ab+ac$, etc. In particular, for any supernumber $x$ one can evaluate the expression $x^2-x$, and our Theorem immediately follows.

Consequence. The equation $x^2=x$ has 4 supernumber solutions: 0,1,X,Y.

So we have proven this result.

How so? After all, if operations on supernumbers have the usual properties, then the usual proof of the fact that the equation $x^2-x$ has two solutions must pass. Let's see. As before, $x^2-x=x(x-1)$ has not been canceled. This means that if $x^2-x=0$, then either $x=0$ or $x-1=0$. But why? Who said that if the product of $ab$ supernumbers $a$ and $b$ is equal to 0, then one of these supernumbers is equal to 0? This is wrong! Moreover, we know very well that $XY=0$.

Indeed, $x_k$ is divisible by $5^k$, and $y_k$ is divisible by $2^k$; This means that the product $x_k y_k$ is divisible by $10^k$, i.e. it ends with $k$ zeros. This means that multiplying $X$ and $Y$ by a column, we will get one zero as a result. For those who doubt, we present figure 4.

If $a\neq0$, $b\neq0$ but $ab=0$, then $a$ and $b$ are called zero divisors. Among the numbers there are no zero divisors, but among the supernumbers there are, and that’s the whole point.

By the way, it is now clear why $X$ and $Y$ are solutions to our equation: after all, $X+Y=1$ (remember, $x_k+y_k=10^k+1$) and $X(X-1)=-X(1-X)=-XY=0$, and similarly $Y(Y-1)=0$.

Equation $x^m=x$. If $x^2=x$, then $x^3=x^2 \cdot x=x \cdot x^2=x$, $x^4=x^3 \cdot x=x \cdot x^2=x$ and similarly $x^5=x$, $x^6=x$, ... Thus, solutions to equation $x^2=x$ are solutions to equation $x^m=x$ for any $m$. Does equation $x^m=x$ have other solutions?
