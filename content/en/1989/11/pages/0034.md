It turns out that straight line $A'C'$ is inclined to the left, i.e. angle $\angle A'DC$ is acute: $\angle A'DC = \pi - (2\alpha + (\pi - 2\beta)) = 2(\beta - \alpha) < 4\alpha < \pi/2$. Let us prove that in the constructional triangle $AND$ formed by the lines $AD$, $AF'$ and $DA'$, the base of the height $H_1$ lowered to $AD$ takes the segment $AC$. The effective one, as we have already seen, is $\angle A'DA < 4\alpha = \angle F'AD$, so it is enough to note that $CD < AC$. (The last inequality holds because in the triangle $A'CD$
$$\angle A'DC = 2\beta - 2\alpha >
> 2\pi/3 - 2\alpha > \pi/2 - 2\alpha >
> 4\alpha - 2\alpha = 2\alpha = \angle CA'D,
$$
hence $AC = A'C > CD$.)
Let $H_2$ and $H_3$ be the bases of the altitude omitted by the sides of the triangle $AND$. Each of the triangle angles $AND$ at vertices $A$ and $D$ differs from $\frac{\pi}{2}$ by less than 0.004 (check!), so segment $H_2H_3$ will pass below point $B$. The figure $\Phi$, made up of 7 triangles equal to $\triangle ABC$ (see Fig. 4), will be placed “accordion-style” on $\triangle ABC$ (as in step 6) of problem M1155). In this case, the periodic trajectory $H_1H_2H_3$ in $\Phi$ will turn into a periodic trajectory $l$ in the triangle $ABC$, consisting of 15 links, as required.
Note that the constructed trajectory $l$ is stable: this means that with a slight deformation of the triangle $ABC$ it turns into a 15-link periodic trajectory close to $l$.
The described construction can be generalized. Let's start with an auxiliary triangle $\Delta_0$ with a base $AC$ and acute angles $\alpha$, $\beta$ at the base, for which a chain of triangles is constructed
$$\Delta - 2l, \Delta - 1, \Delta_0, \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{2k},
$$
intersecting the horizontal line $AC$ and the upper half-plane, and the neighboring triangles of the chain have a common side and symmetries with respect to it. Since the result of applying two consecutive segments with respect to non-parallel axes is a rotation, then the triangle $\Delta - 2l$ is rotated relative to $\Delta_0$ by some angle $n_1\alpha + n_2\beta + n_3\pi$; similarly, triangle $\Delta_2k$ is rotated relative to triangle $\Delta_0$ by angle $m_1\alpha + m_2\beta + m_3\pi$. Suppose that the pairs of chain numbers ($n_1, n_2$) and ($m_1, m_2$) are disproportionate, the triangles $\Delta - 2l$ and $\Delta_2k$ have sides perpendicular to the line $AC$ and there are no other vertices of the chain triangles on this line except $A$ and $C$. Then it turns out that by no matter how small a change in the angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ one can obtain triangles $ABC$ with stable periodic trajectories. In particular, if $\Delta_0$ is a right angle triangle with $\alpha = \pi/8$, $\beta = 3\pi/8$, then triangles are obtained from distance notation, and for $\alpha = \pi/2k$, $\beta = \pi/2l$ - triangles are obtained from stata in «Kvant» No. 3 (see Fig. 12 in this article).
