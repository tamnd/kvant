⟦folio 7⟧

Every new figure needs a new rock.

Optical disks for repeated rewriting of information - they are also called reversible, erasable disks - have not yet received increased industrial distribution, but several suitable physical effects that make their production convenient and profitable have already been selected.

The cutest coastal magnetoptic disks so far. Some ferromagnetic films, such as manganese-bismuth alloys or the amorphous iron-cobalt-gadolinium-terbium composition, change their magnetic properties (as they sometimes say, change magnetic polarity) when heated by a laser beam. As a result, the “transformed” areas—the recording marks—change the polarization of the reflected light beam differently than the rest of the surface. If in the circuit shown in figure 1, a polarizer is placed at the laser output, and an analyzer is placed in front of the photodiode, then when reading, the rays reflected from the mark and the untouched surface will cause a different reaction of the photodiode. The difference is very minor, logical “1” and “0” are very “similar” to each other, but modern electronics manage to distinguish them. For a page, the entire disk is either placed in a magnetic field or heated and cooled. It seems that the “write - read - erase” cycle can be repeated as many times as necessary - magnetoptic disks do not “get tired”, and this is their main advantage. But this storage time does not exceed only two years - the advantage of optical disks is lost and the defect of magnetic disks is “acquired”. And one more thing. For magnetoptic disks, ordinary laser electrophones designed for disks with high-density rejections or microdrives are not suitable—new players are needed.
