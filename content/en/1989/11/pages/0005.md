Already at the end of the 60s, discomfort began to occur; In the 70s, they started talking loudly about the disharmony between semi-current microprocessors and magnetic 3Us as a very problem. Computer technology has become very supportive of the emergence of alternative directions in the development of the memory environment. Optical memory occupied the most important place among them, promising more promising prospects for information. The first optical 3D devices to appear in industry in a number of developed countries in 1984 were optical disk drives (ODS). Their idea is simple.

Who among us in early childhood was not interested in burning on plywood, focusing sunlight with a lens? If the burnt point is set aside with logical unity (“1”), and the untouched surface with logical zero (“0”), then the necessary information can be written on the plywood, and the recording process is, as they say, “a technical matter.” Of course, in a filled device, it does not use light from the sun (it cannot be collected into a small spot - the focus is always blurred, rainbow rings spread around the periphery of the spot), and plywood is not suitable as an information carrier. We need a powerful monochromatic emitter with a narrow radiation pattern, the intensity of which can be easily and quickly modulated, the emitter is miniature and durable. The development of technology has prepared such an emitter - this is a half-power injection laser (see «Kvant», 1988, No. 11-12). And the media had to be specially created - more about these “optical disks” below.

In the block diagram of ODN (Fig. 1), the central zeno is the optical disk on which the laser beam leaves memory marks. To record, the beam is focused on the surface of the rotating disc; The beam power is selected such that the mark is formed in a few tens of nanoseconds. In this case, the mark does not smear along the track, but retains the shape of a circle, and provides a very high recording speed. The pulse excitation source receives a sequence of command pulses from the computer, and according to this program, the laser either gives a bright flash or goes out for a moment - this is how the recorded information is “introduced” into the light beam and transmitted to the rotating medium. After each revolution of the disk, the laser beam is transferred to the next track, and recording continues. In addition to information marks, auxiliary marks are also applied - road tracking marks and reading marks. The first are known to “keep” the laser on the track: if it stumbles a little, then the feedback mechanism returns it to the “true path.” Labels of the second type speed up the search for the desired array; they form a key.

When reading, the modulation is turned off, the radiation is supplied continuously. The laser power was reduced so much that the beam could neither create new marks nor distort existing ones. “Feeling” the rotating disk, the beam is reflected differently from the marks and the untouched surface. Thus, the recorded information passes into the reading beam, then enters the photoidode, where it is converted into electronic pulses, which, after successfully arriving at the output of the drive.

The most important parameter of the ODN is the minimum diameter of the mark created by the laser - it determines the density of recording information on the medium (bits/cm²). It is known that no matter what we try, the light flux cannot be reduced to a geometric point - the phenomenon of diffraction leads to the fact that the minimum diameter of the focused spot is close to the wavelength λ of the using radiation.
