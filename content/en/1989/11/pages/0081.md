M1. Let $P_{2n}$ be the number of desired “lucky” $2n$-digit numbers; $S_i(k)$ is the number of $i$-digit numbers with a sum of digits of $k$. Then

$$P_{2n} = \sum_{k=0}^{9n} (S_n(k))^2,$$
$$S_{n+1}(k) = \sum_{j=0}^{min(k,9)} S_n(k-j).$$

The first formula reflects the partitioning of $2n$-digit numbers into groups with the required digit sums equal to 0, 1, ..., $9n$; the second is the division of $n+1$-digit numbers with a sum of digits $k$ into groups of numbers with the first digit 0, 1, ..., $9$ (or $k$ if $k<9$). The second formula allows you to construct table $S_n(k)$. These numbers grow quickly in BASIC at $n>7$, and in Pascal at $n>6$ they go beyond the range of integers allowed by the computer, so you need to use special programs to work with large numbers.
