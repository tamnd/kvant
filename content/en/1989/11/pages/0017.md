ABOUT THE FOUR SOLUTIONS OF EQUATION $X^2 = X$

A. V. ZHIGLEVICH,
Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
N. N. PETROV

Before convincing the reader that the equation $x^2 = x$ has not two solutions, as everyone thinks, but four, as stated in the title of the article, we turn to the following confusing fact. If a natural number is marked (in decimal notation) with the number 0, 1, 5, 6, then its square is marked with the same number. The same applies to two-digit endings 00, 01, 25, 76, i.e. if it ends with one of these pairs of numbers, then its square ends with the same pair of numbers (for example, $176^2 = 30976$, $225^2 = 50625$, etc.). The three-digit endings 000, 001, 625, 376 have a similar property. At the same time, no other single-digit, two-digit or three-digit endings have a similar property. What's the matter? The answer is given by Theorem. For any $k$, there are exactly 4 sets of $k$ digits - 00...00, 00...01 and two more, ending with a pager and a six, respectively - which have the following property: if a natural number ends up with one of these sets of digits, then its square ends with the same set of digits.

To be convincing, we give two proofs of this theorem. However, first let’s figure out what we have to prove. We are looking for an integer $x$, $0 \leqslant x < 10^k$ such that for any integer $a \geqslant 0$ the square of the number $10^k a + x$ (this is how an arbitrary number is written whose $k$-digit ending coincides with $x$) has the form

$$10^k b + x, \quad b \text{ — целое число. Но } (10^k a + x)^2 = 10^k (10^k a^2 + 2ax) + x^2; \text{ поэтому наше условие состоит попросту в том, что } x^2 - x \text{ делится на } 10^k. \text{ Итак, наша задача формулируется предельно просто: найти все целые } x, 0 \leqslant x < 10^k, \text{ такие, что } x^2 - x \text{ делится на } 10^k. \text{ Мы должны убедиться в том, что задача имеет 4 решения и что эти решения обладают указанным в формулировке теоремы свойствми.}$$

First proof. So the difference $x^2 - x = x(x - 1)$ must be divisible by $10^k = 2^k 5^k$. But the numbers $x$ and $x - 1$ cannot be divisible by either 2 or 5 at the same time. This leaves us with four possibilities:

(1) $x$ is divisible by $10^k$;
(2) $x - 1$ is divisible by $10^k$;
(3) $x$ is divisible by $2^k$ and $x - 1$ is divisible by $5^k$;
(4) $x$ is divisible by $5^k$ and $x - 1$ is divisible by $2^k$.

Since $x < 10^k$, the first possibility is realized only at $x = 0$; similarly, the second possibility is realized only for $x = 1$.

In case (3) the number $x$ (and in case (4) the number $x - 1$) is one of the numbers $a \cdot 2^k$, where $0 \leqslant a < 5^k - 1$. All these numbers have different remainders when divided by $5^k$: if $b \cdot 2^k$ and $c \cdot 2^k$ ($0 \leqslant b \leqslant c < 5^k$) have the same remainders when divided by $5^k$, then $(c - b) \cdot 2^k$ is divisible by $5^k$ and therefore by $10^k$, which is only possible with $c = b$. But the remainder is possible $-5^k$, which means which of them is represented once in our set of numbers.
