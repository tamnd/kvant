F1199. It is known from experience that the speed of waves on the surface of the ocean, the length of which is $\lambda = 10$ m, is equal to $v = 4$ m/s. Suppose that in the ocean at great depth there is an interface, above which there is less salty water, and below which there is more salty water, so that the difference in the density of water is $\Delta \rho = 1$ kg/m$^3$. There may be waves along this boundary (so-called internal waves). Find the speed of such waves with a length of $\lambda = 10$ m. Consider the amplitude of the waves to be small.

V. V. Geojavev

F1200. There are two identical glasses on the table, one of which contains hot tea. Tea temperature $t_0$. The tea needs to be cooled to a temperature of $t_k$. This can be done in two ways:
1) immediately pour the tea into a second glass and wait until it cools to a temperature of $t_k$;
2) wait until the tea cools down to a certain temperature $t_1$ such that after pouring into the second glass the temperature of the tea immediately turns out to be $t_k$.

Which way is the fastest? It is known that the heat transfer of a glass with something is proportional to the temperature difference between the glass and the environment; heat exchange between the glass and the glass occurs very quickly. The heat capacity of a glass is $C_0$, the heat capacity of tea is $C$.

K. S. Vedoye

F1201. There is a nonlinear electronic device $\tilde{R}$. Fig. 2 shows a graph of the dependence of the current through the device on the voltage across it (in sections 1-2 and 3-4 the overlap of the graph is very large). The states are assembled from $\tilde{R}$, a coil with inductance $L$ and a battery with an emf equal to $U_0$ (the device is turned on with the “correct” polarity corresponding to the graph). Plot a graph of the current in the circuit versus time and find the period of current oscillations.

K. S. Vedoye

F1202. During an electrical discharge in rarefied neon (Ne) at room temperature, a very small fraction of neon atoms decays into electrons and ions (the mass of $M$ neon atoms is $4 \cdot 10^4$ times the mass of $m$ electrons). The free path length of electrons (i.e., the average distance that an electron travels without collision) is $l = 0,1$ mm. The gas is in an electric field with a voltage of $E = 10$ V/cm. Estimate the “temperature” of electrons corresponding to their average kinetic energy. Large constant $k = 1,38 \cdot 10^{-23}$ J/K, electron charge $e = -1,6 \cdot 10^{-19}$ Cl.

D. A. Kuptsov
