Once again about intertwined straight lines

Problem M1085 (see «Kvant» No. 1 for the conditions, and see «Kvant» No. 5 for 1988 for the solution) aroused the interest of many of our readers. We published one of the postcards for her in «Kvant» No. 6 for this year. Let us recall that the problem deals with how to determine from the projection of a set of straight lines onto a plane whether their corresponding arrangement in space is possible or not.

A 10th grade student at boarding school No. 18 in Moscow, A. Skopenkov, discovered a sequence of weavings $L_n$ that have the following interesting property: each of the weavings cannot be realized in space, but when one straight line is removed from the weaving, it becomes realizable. The $n$th plexus $L_n$ ($n=2, 3, 4, \dots$) consists of the $(2n+1)$ line; the law of its construction is clear from figure 1-3, which depict the interlaces corresponding to $n=2, 3$ and 4. Please note that the intersections of each of the lines of the interlaces $L_n$ from the other devices are the same: first the line passes $(n-1)$ times under the lines intersecting with it, then once above them, then again under, and then $(n-1)$ times above them (Fig. 4).

The proof of the necessity of the interlacing $L_n$ can be obtained in two solutions to part a) of problem M1085. Let's see what happens if we remove one straight line from $L_n$, for example $l_i$ to figure 4.

Let us assume that the interlace $L'_n$ formed by the remaining lines cannot be realized in space. After removing $l_i$ straight lines $l_{i-1}$ and $l_{i+1}$ can be rotated in the vertical plane without changing their horizontal projections, made vertical and “pulled out” from the plexus. Then the remaining interweaving will remain unrealizable. But now we can remove straight lines $l_{i-2}$ and $l_{i+2}$ by turning in the vertical plane in the same way, preserving the unrealizability of the remaining interweaving, and so on. As a result, the remaining interweaving consists of only two straight lines, which, obviously, is realized in space. This contradicts the assumption that $L'_n$ is unrealizable.

The process of disintegration of the tangle $L_n$ after removing one straight line of Borromean wheels after removing any of them - figure 5, taken from the article by A. T. Kalinin (see «Kvant», 1981, No. 9).
