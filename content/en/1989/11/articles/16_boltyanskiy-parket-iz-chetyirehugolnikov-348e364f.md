Parquet made of quadrangles

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. G. BOLTYANSKY

At the VI Moscow Mathematical Olympiad (in 1940), the following task was proposed: to lay a parquet with a given quadrilateral of any shape, that is, to fill the entire plane without gaps and overlapped. Its solution can be obtained using central symmetry. Let us reflect the quadrilateral ABCD symmetrically about the middle of side AB. On figure 1 the original quadrilateral is placed with the number 1, and the symmetric one with the number 2. Now we reflect quadrilateral 2 symmetrically relative to the middle of its side BC, and the resulting quadrilateral 3 - relative to the middle of its side CD. Quadrilaterals 1, 2, 3, 4 are adjacent to their common vertex by angles A, B, C, D. And since the sum of the angles of a quadrilateral is 360°, then these four quadrilaterals will be located around their common vertex without gaps and overlapped. The same construction can be carried out around each vertex of each of its new quadrangles, which gives parquet on the entire plane (Fig. 2).

The constructed parquet floors make it possible to solve a number of problems related to the properties of quadrangles. The solution to each of the problems considered below can be presented without using parquet: consider only those quadrilaterals that are needed for the solution, and the remaining “traces” are parquetasters. However, our solutions are using parquet.

Task 1. Two identical convex quadrangles were made from paper. One was cut along one diagonal, the second - along the other. Prove that the four triangles obtained can be used to form a parallelogram.

Problem 2. In a convex quadrilateral, midlines are drawn, i.e., segments connecting the midpoints of opposite sides. Prove that the resulting four parts can be used to form a parallelogram.

Solution. At figure 5, the parquet fragment is divided into parts by center lines (the center lines MN and NP lie on the same straight line, since they are symmetrical about the point N; the same applies to other center lines). Parts m, n, p, q make up

Problem 3. Prove that if the midline connecting the midpoints of two opposite sides of a quadrilateral is equal to half the sum of the other two opposite sides, then this quadrilateral is a trapezoid (or parallelogram).

Solution. On figure 6 $MN$ and $NP$ are the middle lines; the segments $AQ$ and $MP$ are equal and parallel (since $AM$ and $QP$ are equal and parallel). If $MN = \frac{1}{2}(AB+CD)$, i.e. $AQ=MP = AB+BQ$, then the point $B$ belongs to the segment $AQ$. Therefore, $AB \parallel CD$, i.e. $ABCD$, is a trapezoid.
