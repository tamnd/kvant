XXIII National Olympiad in Physics

A. R. ZILBERMAN

This year the Olympics were hosted by Chisinau. The grand opening of the final stage of the All-Union Physics Olympiad took place on April 13, and on April 14 the participants solved problems of a theoretical type.

As usual, 8th graders were given 4 problems (for 4 hours), and 9th and 10th graders were given 5 problems each (for 5 hours). Here are the conditions of these problems (most of them are included in the “Quantum Problems” and published in the 9th and 10th issues of the journal for this year):

8th grade
1. A cube (Fig. 1) moves in space. At this moment, the ABVG face is horizontal, and the velocities of points A and B are directed vertically downward and are equal in modulus v. It is known that the speed of point B at the same moment is equal in magnitude to 2v. What is the maximum absolute speed that other points of the cube can have at this moment?

2. Between two high smooth wheel walls (Fig. 2) there is a wheel pair - two heavy disks of mass M each, mounted rigidly on a light axle of length L (L≈R₂−R₁). By applying force to the horizontal direction, I force the wheelset to move in a circle without distortions (so that the axis is always directed along the radius). What is the minimum amount of work that needs to be done to make the circle tall? The friction coefficient of wheels on the ground is μ. The wheels are considered narrow.

3. In winter, a constant temperature of tₖ=+15 °C is maintained in the large room with the help of three central heating radiators connected in series, through which hot water is pumped. In this case, the temperature of the first radiator is t₁=+80 °C, and the temperature of the last - third - t₃=+30 °C. What is the temperature of the second radiator? Consider that heat exchange, both between the radiator and the room, and between hot water and the radiator, is proportional to the corresponding temperature difference.

4. The operating voltage of the light bulb, the current-voltage characteristic of which is shown in figure 3, is 3.5 V (the curve ends at a voltage of 4 V - the light bulb burns out). Two such light bulbs are connected and connected to a powerful 5 V voltage source. How much current will this consume in the circuit? What resistor should be connected in parallel with one of the bulbs so that the voltage on the other is 3.5 V? Will any of the light bulbs burn out if this resistor is replaced with another one of the same light bulb?

9th grade
1. During the landing on the surface of a small spherical asterid, distant from all other cosmic bodies, the astronauts fired a salute, shooting vertically upward. Exactly 20 years later, the grail fell on the asterids. The average density of the asteride is 5 g/cm³, the initial speed of the bullets is 600 m/s. Using these data, determine the mass of the asteride.

2. The capillary is made of two thin glass tubes with internal diameters d₁ and d₂, having a common axis (Fig. 4). A large drop of water with mass M was introduced into it. When the capillary was placed horizontally, all the drops “crept” into the thin part, and when it was placed vertically, all the water flowed out of it. Under what conditions will the drop be located partly in a thick tube and partly in a thin tube? Water surface tension coefficient σ, water density q. Wetting is considered complete.

10th grade

1. At a separate point in the Universe around a star, very similar to the Sun, a planet, almost the same as the Earth, rotated in a circular orbit. Physicists there conducted a terrible experiment, sending a powerful beam of protons from the planet to their star, and some of these protons reached the chain. After the experiment, which lasted several hours, the charges of the planet and star no longer changed, but the planet’s orbit changed - now the minimum distance to the star was halved. How many times has the length of the year changed? Previously, the charges of celestial bodies were negligible.

A sample was cut from the plate in the form of a strip of length $L$ and width $d$ ($L \gg d$) at an angle $45^\circ$ to the direction of axes $X$ and $Y$, and at the end of the strip a source with direction $U_0$ (Fig. 6). In 8th grade, task 3 turned out to be the most difficult - both in the opinion of the jury members and from the point of view of the participants. (The opinion of the participants can be judged by the results of the survey - approximately every third in each class filled out a questionnaire, where they rated the tasks according to difficulty and “interestingness”.) In the 9th grade, the children identified tasks 3, 4 and 5. In the 10th grade, the selection of tasks turned out to be relatively even. In general, this year the tasks were more difficult than before, especially in grades 8 and 9. This was also noted by the team leaders, but their opinions on whether this was good were divided.

After a day of rest, an experimental round was conducted. Each participant solved two problems; 4 hours were allotted for work. In 8th grade, mechanical and electrical problems were proposed. To solve the first problem, the participants received a rather meager set of instruments. A permanent triangle was made from stubble of various masses. It was necessary to find the mass using a weight of known mass, a ruler and a pencil. (Hint: a pencil (round) is a good support for a lever.) In the second task, it was necessary to determine the resistance of a resistor using an apermeter, a battery, and a piece of low-carbon wiring (such wiring is used to make power supply coils). A caliper was given, with which it was possible to measure the required dimensions, and the resistivity of ichchrome was brought into the conditions of the problem.

In the first problem for 9th grade, it was necessary to measure the mass of a cylinder made of an unknown material using a block, a thread, a set of weights and millimeters of paper. The difficulty was that even the total mass of the weights was less than the mass of the cylinder. Think for yourself how to solve the problem. (Hint: use equilibrium conditions, do not forget about friction forces - a reasonable solution allows you to bypass this problem.) If the work is done carefully, it is possible to achieve an accuracy of no worse than 1-2%.

The second task required determining the power of the heater using a calorimeter, thermometer and stopwatch (and, of course, water). This task is quite simple in concept, but requires a competent approach that allows taking into account heat exchange with the environment and all the “extra” heat capacities.

In the 10th grade, problems were proposed on electricity and the mechanics of oscillatory motion. In the first task, it was necessary to find the capacitance of an electric capacitor (a capacitor of a fairly large capacity was given - 5000 μF, such capacitors are generally intended to work in prirogiter filters, so their capacitance can differ from the nominal one upward - up to 80%) using a resistor with a known resistance and a stopwatch. The source of battery life, and the difficulty was created artificially - along with a voltmeter, it was proposed to use a threshold indicator - an LED (the voltage-tampere characteristic of an LED, like any semiconductor diode, is very steep, and therefore it lights up brightly when a certain voltage is reached - for use in the operation of AL-307 LEDs, this voltage is approximately 1.7-1.9 V). All experimental problems were traditionally prepared by the owners of the Olympida - scientists from Chisinau. In the questionnaires, experimental problems were rated even higher than theoretical ones.

The next day, April 17, a server analysis of the participants’ problem was carried out, detailing the criteria for evaluating solutions to both theoretical and experimental problems. This approach allows participants to compare the position of their assessment with the expected ones and, if they do not match, appeal to the jury. This year, the jury accepted appeals on both theoretical and experimental trams, which somewhat stipulated the work of the commission, but there were many questions. For those who did not participate in the final stage of the Olympiad, it may seem that even the statements of the problems can be “defended” without difficulty for some points. I assure you that this is not so, although all sorts of things can happen during appeals.
