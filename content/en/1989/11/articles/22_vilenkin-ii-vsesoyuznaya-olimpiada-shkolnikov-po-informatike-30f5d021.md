The II All-Russian Information Olympiad for schoolchildren was held from April 15 to April 22, 1989. 82 schoolchildren who won in the previous rounds of the Olympiad or in the 1st All-Union Olympiad arrived in the capital of Belarus, Minsk, as part of a 19th team (from the union republics, the Ministry of Railways, Moscow, Leningrad, Minsk).

The opening ceremony of the Olympiad took place at the Republican Boarding School for Music and Fine Arts named after. I. O. Akhremchik, which became the main base of the Olympiad. The Jorri Olympiad was headed by Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the BSSR V. A. Labunov. On the same day, the Olympiad participants began to get acquainted with computer technology. Minsk residents provided an available number of Yamahas and EC1840s. Most schoolchildren went to Yamaha, but some preferred the more powerful EC1840 personal and professional computers.

The first round of the Olympiad (theoretical) took place on April 17, the second (practical - on a computer) - on April 19. Unlike the first All-Olympiad, when evaluating problems, a coefficient was not introduced to characterize the difficulty of the problem (depending on the number of participants who solved this problem). The solution to each problem was scored between 0 and 10 points, and up to 5 points were additionally awarded for special achievements. Let us present the conditions of the problems.

Theoretical type problems

T1. Based on the results of the work, two lists were compiled to assess the labor participation of the same N employees (1≤N≤6): the first - by decision of the work collective, the second - by opinion

M2. There is a game cube on the table with side $i_0$ towards us, side $j_0$ up. Write a program that determines the sequence of “turnings” of the cube (forward, backward, right, left), after which the cube ends up in the same place, but with the $i_k$ face towards us and the $j_k$ face up.

Application: turning is understood as rolling a cube over an edge in contact with the table without sliding. Other methods of moving the cube are prohibited.

The numbering of the faces of the cube is such that if you put it on the face with the number “5”, then the side faces will have the numbers “1”, “6”, “4”, “3” when going around clockwise, and the top will have the number “2”.

The tasks turned out to be unequal. 1168 points scored by all 82 Olympada participants based on the results of solving problems were distributed according to the given ones as follows: T1 - 337.5 points; T2 - 379; T3 - 172.5; M1 - 254; M2 - 25. (Instructions for tasks and evaluation criteria for each task are given at the end of the magazine.)

The analysis of problems was carried out on April 20 in the afternoon, then April was considered.

On the evening of April 21, the winner was awarded and the Olympics were closed. 6 schoolchildren received I degree diplomas (they scored 49.5-30.5 points), II degree diplomas - 9 schoolchildren (27-19.5 points), III degree diplomas - 10 schoolchildren (19-16.5 points). Many schoolchildren and teams were awarded prizes from public and scientific organizations, including prizes from the magazine «Kvant» (for high mathematical work culture) A. Savchenko (Mogilev, school No. 1, 10th grade), I. Dogolyatsky (Leningrad, school No. 239, 10th grade) and D. Kozlov (Leningrad, school No. 566, 9 class). The Leningraders showed the best results. They, like the owner of the Olympic pad, received an MK-90 computer as a reward.

It is gratifying that this year the winners of the All-Union Olympiad went to the International Olympiad in Informatics (although the team was only three people). We plan to talk about this Olympics in one of the following issues.
