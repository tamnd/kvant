Solution. On figure 8 points E, F divide side AB, and points G, H - side DC into three equal parts. The segments CM, GN, KP, LQ are equal and parallel to each other. The area of ​​the CMQL parallelgram is equal to 4S, where S is the area of ​​the quadrilateral ABCD (this is established in the same way as when solving the previous problem). Further, the area of ​​the parallelgram KGNP is equal to 2(p+r), where p, q, r are the areas of the parts into which the segments GE and HF divide the area S of the quadrilateral ABCD. Since the segment KG is parallel to the segment LC and the second is smaller than it, the area of ​​the parallelgram KGNP the second is less than the area of ​​the parallelgram CMQL, i.e. 2(p+r) = $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 4S$, and therefore p+r = $\frac{2}{3} S$. Therefore, q = $\frac{1}{3} S$ (since p+q + r=S), which is what needed to be proved.

Let us now present several problems for you to solve on your own.

6. Prove that using centrally symmetric hexagons (of arbitrary shape, even non-convex ones) it is possible to cover a plane with parquet so that any two hexagons are populated from each other by parallel translation.

7. Prove that a parquet of quadrangles can be obtained as follows: a parquet of hexagons is laid, as indicated in task 6, and then parallel diagonals are drawn through the centers of all hexagons.

8. Prove that if in a convex quadrilateral one of the midlines divides its area in half, then this quadrilateral is a trapezoid. If the middle lines divide the area in half, then the quadrilateral is parallel.

9. In a convex quadrilateral, the diagonal does not pass through the point of intersection of the middle lines and is divided by the middle lines into three segments, the outermost of which are equal. Prove that the second diagonal passes through the point of intersection of the midlines.

10. Prove the converse statement: if in a convex quadrilateral one diagonal passes through the point of intersection of the middle lines, then the second diagonal, if it does not pass through this point, is divided by the middle lines into three segments, the outermost of which are equal.

11. Prove that if in a convex quadrilateral the sum of the lengths of the middle lines is equal to its half-perimeter, then this quadrilateral is parallel.

12. Prove that if in a convex quadrilateral the sum of the distances from the point of intersection of the midlines to the vertices is equal to the sum of the lengths of its diagonals, then this quadrilateral is parallel.
