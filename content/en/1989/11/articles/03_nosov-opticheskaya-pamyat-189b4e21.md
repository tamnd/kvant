Memory, undoubtedly, is a certain attribute of human existence. The developed ability to memorize, store, and recall a wide variety of information sets humans apart from all representatives of the animal world. It is memory that makes a person a Human. Those of you who have read “The Storm Stop” by Chingiz Aitmatov probably remember the gloomy legend about the mankurs. People’s memory was “killed”, and they became reliable, natural bodyguards of their rulers, capable of killing their own mother without thinking. A man who lost his memory retained his human appearance, but became a monster.

The cumulative memory of individual individuals, the collective memory of the entire human community, materialized in books, paintings, sweat, photographs, drawings, films, records and much, much more, constitutes essential values.

With the advent of computer technology, fundamentally new means of memory appeared and began to develop—electronic ones. The functional purpose of storage devices (memory) is to record, store and non-destructively read recorded information. Characteristics of computer memory usually speak of the non-rachtic principle of its construction as the most appropriate. Operating memory devices work next to the processor; they are very fast, but not very capacious; outside the computer - archival storage devices, which have enormous capacity, but are significantly slower. And - numerous varieties of intermediate type memory. Figuratively, the entire “knowledge” of the computer memory is represented in the form of a paramid, the sharp top of which is made up of operational memories, and the massive base is made up of archival ones.

One of the definite tendencies of the computer program is the operating system, an accelerated means of memory - modern computers do not so much “compute” as manipulate data stored in storage devices. It is estimated that to enter the so-called “information society” - a society in which computerization will cover all spheres of production, management, science, education, culture, everyday life, entertainment - the daily increase in storage capacity per person should approach 1 billion bits (such information is contained in approximately a hundred book volumes).

It has been noticed that the “memory pyramid” becomes “deformed” over time, becoming more and more “sharpened” at the top and ugly “spreading” at the base. And this is understandable. The operational memory units occupying the upper floors of the paramida have found a completely suitable element base for themselves - these are semiconductor intergal circuits, similar to those used in the process. That is why the computer processor and its RAM go into the future “hand in hand”, in complete agreement with each other.

In the lower floors of the paramid, harmony is destroyed. The “building material” of archival storage devices—magnetic tapes and disks—also continuously programs, but they cannot keep up with semiconductor assemblies. And the more productive the computer becomes, the more manifested is the “tyranny” of archival storage devices, before they require for their size

Already at the end of the 60s, discomfort began to occur; In the 70s, they started talking loudly about the disharmony between semi-current microprocessors and magnetic 3Us as a very problem. Computer technology has become very supportive of the emergence of alternative directions in the development of the memory environment. Optical memory occupied the most important place among them, promising more promising prospects for information. The first optical 3D devices to appear in industry in a number of developed countries in 1984 were optical disk drives (ODS). Their idea is simple.

Who among us in early childhood was not interested in burning on plywood, focusing sunlight with a lens? If the burnt point is set aside with logical unity (“1”), and the untouched surface with logical zero (“0”), then the necessary information can be written on the plywood, and the recording process is, as they say, “a technical matter.” Of course, in a filled device, it does not use light from the sun (it cannot be collected into a small spot - the focus is always blurred, rainbow rings spread around the periphery of the spot), and plywood is not suitable as an information carrier. We need a powerful monochromatic emitter with a narrow radiation pattern, the intensity of which can be easily and quickly modulated, the emitter is miniature and durable. The development of technology has prepared such an emitter - this is a half-power injection laser (see «Kvant», 1988, No. 11-12). And the media had to be specially created - more about these “optical disks” below.

In the block diagram of ODN (Fig. 1), the central zeno is the optical disk on which the laser beam leaves memory marks. To record, the beam is focused on the surface of the rotating disc; The beam power is selected such that the mark is formed in a few tens of nanoseconds. In this case, the mark does not smear along the track, but retains the shape of a circle, and provides a very high recording speed. The pulse excitation source receives a sequence of command pulses from the computer, and according to this program, the laser either gives a bright flash or goes out for a moment - this is how the recorded information is “introduced” into the light beam and transmitted to the rotating medium. After each revolution of the disk, the laser beam is transferred to the next track, and recording continues. In addition to information marks, auxiliary marks are also applied - road tracking marks and reading marks. The first are known to “keep” the laser on the track: if it stumbles a little, then the feedback mechanism returns it to the “true path.” Labels of the second type speed up the search for the desired array; they form a key.

When reading, the modulation is turned off, the radiation is supplied continuously. The laser power was reduced so much that the beam could neither create new marks nor distort existing ones. “Feeling” the rotating disk, the beam is reflected differently from the marks and the untouched surface. Thus, the recorded information passes into the reading beam, then enters the photoidode, where it is converted into electronic pulses, which, after successfully arriving at the output of the drive.

The most important parameter of the ODN is the minimum diameter of the mark created by the laser - it determines the density of recording information on the medium (bits/cm²). It is known that no matter what we try, the light flux cannot be reduced to a geometric point - the phenomenon of diffraction leads to the fact that the minimum diameter of the focused spot is close to the wavelength λ of the using radiation. Let us now see what an optical disc is. The most common method for recording optical pulses has become burning, the melting of microholes in a thin metal film.

In order for this process to proceed in the best possible way, a four-layer film structure (Fig. 2) is produced on the highly polished surface of the glass blank. The laser beam is focused on the film of the working metal (5) and in a small area of ​​heating it until it melts. Surface tension forces stretch the melt, and a through hole opens in the film. The thickness of the film is negligible, it does not exceed 0.02-0.05 microns, so the shape of the response follows the cross-section of a laser bow, and the diameter of the response may even be slightly smaller than the previously indicated diffraction limit. It is possible to obtain responses with a diameter of 0.3-0.5 microns; in industrial 3U, the response sizes are usually close to 1 micron, and the distances between adjacent responses and between tracks are about 2 microns.

With other complex structures, it is also important which one “does the job.” The dielectric pad (4) is made of materials with a high reflection coefficient - this has a difference of “0” and “1” when read. The aluminum screen (3) returns rays that, without being absorbed, passed through the metal film, and thereby increases the efficiency.

Optical disks for repeated rewriting of information - they are also called reversible, erasable disks - have not yet received increased industrial distribution, but several suitable physical effects that make their production convenient and profitable have already been selected.

The cutest coastal magnetoptic disks so far. Some ferromagnetic films, such as manganese-bismuth alloys or the amorphous iron-cobalt-gadolinium-terbium composition, change their magnetic properties (as they sometimes say, change magnetic polarity) when heated by a laser beam. As a result, the “transformed” areas—the recording marks—change the polarization of the reflected light beam differently than the rest of the surface. If in the circuit shown in figure 1, a polarizer is placed at the laser output, and an analyzer is placed in front of the photodiode, then when reading, the rays reflected from the mark and the untouched surface will cause a different reaction of the photodiode. The difference is very minor, logical “1” and “0” are very “similar” to each other, but modern electronics manage to distinguish them. For a page, the entire disk is either placed in a magnetic field or heated and cooled. It seems that the “write - read - erase” cycle can be repeated as many times as necessary - magnetoptic disks do not “get tired”, and this is their main advantage. But this storage time does not exceed only two years - the advantage of optical disks is lost and the defect of magnetic disks is “acquired”. And one more thing. For magnetoptic disks, ordinary laser electrophones designed for disks with high-density rejections or microdrives are not suitable—new players are needed.
