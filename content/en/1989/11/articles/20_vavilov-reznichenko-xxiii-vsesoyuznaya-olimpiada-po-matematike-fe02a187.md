XXIII National Olympiad in Mathematics

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. V. VAVILOV,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. V. REZNICHENKO

In the final stage of the XXIII All-Union Mathematical Olympiad, held from April 15 to April 22, 1989 in Riga, 30 teams took part: 14 allied resource teams, one team from each of the four zones of the RSFSR, teams from Moscow, Leningrad, Riga and the Riga region and 8 teams of specialized boarding schools at the leading universities of the country - 164 schoolchildren (48 eighth-graders, 62 ninth-graders and 54 tenth-graders).

As usual, the Olympiad participant was given 4 problems in each of the two rounds, which were given 5 hours to solve. The assessment of the complexity of the proposed tasks in points is given in Table 1.

In Table 1, scores characterize the relative complexity of tasks within one class. In general, as the test results allow us to conclude, the task for eighth-graders turned out to be too difficult; The assignments for grades 9 and 10 turned out to be well balanced both in complexity and topic; the students liked them, as evidenced by the test results. How the participants coped with the tasks of the Olympiad can be found in Table 2, which shows the number of schoolchildren who solved each problem.

At the end of the inspection, each Olympiad participant could familiarize himself with his work and discuss with the jury members his mistakes, assessment, and performance of the work by the inspectors. At the same time, sometimes the evaluation of the work changed. Table 3 shows data on the number of winners in each class.

Here are the problems for the final round.

First day
8th grade
1. Each of the seven boys went to the ice cream book 3 times on Sunday. It is known that every two of them met near the knisk. Prove that at some point at least three boys met near the knisk at the same time.

A. Anjans
2. There are 77 straight bars measuring 3x3x1. Can all these bars be placed in a 7x9x11 square box (box with lid)?

A. Berzinysh
3. Let M be the tangency point of the circle inscribed in triangle ABC on side AB, T be an arbitrary point on side BC other than the vertex. Prove that three circles inscribed in triangles BMT, MTA and ATC are tangent to one straight line.

A. Anjans
4. The natural number N has exactly 12 divisors (including 1 and N). Let's put them in ascending order: $d_1 < d_2 < ... < d_{12}$. It is known that the divisor with number $d_4$ - 1 is equal to the product $(d_1 + d_2 + d_4)d_8$. Find the number N.

A. Berzinysh
9th grade
1. There are 2000 coins, of which two are counterfeit: one is lighter than the real one, and the other is heavier. How can you determine, after repeated weighings on a scale without weights, which is greater: the total weight of two counterfeit coins or the total weight of two real coins; or are these weights equal?

S. Avgustovich
2. Prove that if a, b, c are side lengths of a triangle and a+b+c=1, then the inequality is true
$$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 4abc < \frac{1}{2}.$$

D. Tereshin
3. Points K and M are taken on sides AB and CD of a convex quadrilateral ABCD, respectively. Let L be the intersection point of the segments AM and KD, N the intersection point of the segments KC and BM.

a) Prove that if K and M are the midpoints of AB and CD, then $S_{KLMN} < \frac{1}{3} S_{ABCD}$.

6) Prove that if AK:KB=CM:MD=m:n, then
$$S_{KLMN} < \frac{mn}{m^2 + mn + n^2} S_{ABCD}.$$

D. Tereshin

4. Squares $1 \times 1, 2 \times 2, 3 \times 3$ were used to create square $23 \times 23$. What is the smallest number of squares $1 \times 1$ that could be used?

N. Agakhanov

10th grade

1. For what smallest natural number $n$ the equation

$$\left[ \frac{10^n}{x} \right] = 1989$$

has an integer solution, $[a]$ denotes the integer part of the number $a$, i.e., the largest integer without exceeding it $a$?

S. Gashkov

2. On sides $AB, BC$ and $AC$ of triangle $ABC$ the points $D, E$ and $F$ are taken respectively, so that $DE=BE$ and $FE=CE$. Prove that the center of the circumscribed circle of triangle $ADF$ lies on the bissertrix of angle $DEF$.

B. Protasov

3. On one of the two given intersecting spheres, points $A$ and $B$ are taken, on the other, points $C$ and $D$. The segment $AC$ passes through the common point of the sphere. The segment $BD$ passes through another common point of the sphere and is parallel to the line containing the centers of the sphere. Prove that the projections of the segments $AB$ and $CD$ onto the line $AC$ are equal.

H. Ballszin

4. Two tourists are at the same height (in the air) at points $A$ and $B$, located on opposite sides of the mountain range. The path along the pass connecting points $A$ and $B$ has the shape of a broken line, all of whose vertices are above its ends $A$ and $B$. Can both hikers travel across the entire pass while remaining at the same altitude at any given time?

E. Abakumov, D. Fomin

Second day

8th grade

5. There are 8 pieces placed on a chessboard so that there is one piece in each horizontal and each vertical row of cells. Prove that there are an even number of pieces on the black squares of the chessboard.

B. Protezovol

6. On the sides $AB, BC$ and $CA$ of the triangle $ABC$, the points $C_1, A_1$ and $B_1$, different from the vertices of the triangle, were marked in green and red, respectively. It turned out that $\frac{AC_1}{C_1B} = \frac{BA_1}{A_1C} = \frac{CB_1}{B_1A}$, and $\angle BAC = \angle B_1A_1C$. Prove that the triangle with green vertices is similar to the triangle $ABC$.

H. Ballszin

7. In some positions there are $n \geq 3$ birdhouses, and all distances between birdhouses are different. Each birdhouse contains a starling. At each moment, several of them left their birdhouses and flew to others so that again there was a starling in each birdhouse. Moreover, if the distance between each pair of starlings was less than the distance between another pair of starlings (one starling can read in different pairs), then after the flight the distance between the pair of starlings became greater

f(n) = f(f(n-1)) + f(f(n+1))?

E. Barabanoov, I. Voronovich

8. Given a convex polygon. The length of any segment connecting the vertex of this polygon with some point on its boundary and its area is bisected, not exceeding 1. Prove that the area of ​​the polygon is less than $\frac{\pi}{4}$.

S. Anisov, D. Tulyakov
