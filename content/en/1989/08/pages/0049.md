⟦folio 47⟧

Criminal geometry, or the Case of Priitspa

(methodological manual in one act)

D. V. Fomin

The stage is dark. The melody sounds quietly (see intro). The light comes on. Living room at 221-6 Baker Street. Sherlock Holmes sits looking through the evening newspaper. Enter Batcon.

Holmes. Hello my friend. I see that you have decided to forget about medicine for a while and occupy yourself with geometry.

Batcon. But how...?

Holmes. Sticking out of your pocket is a copy of yesterday's Daily Joke with a geometric puzzle competition. It's immediately obvious that you spent a lot of ink trying to overcome at least one of the tasks.

Batcon. However, where did you get the idea, Holmes, that I haven’t solved a single problem? True, that’s how it is... (Sits down).

Holmes. Don't be harsh, dear Batcon. By the way, all these problems can be solved in practically the same way, if, of course, you approach them correctly. True, I haven’t seen the problems of this competition yet, but... However, let’s take a look.

Problem 1. Point O is located inside square ABCD, and angles OCB and OBC measure 15 degrees.
