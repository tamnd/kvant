Problem 4. ABC is an equilateral triangle with vertex angle $C$ equal to 20 degrees. The points $M$ and $N$ are taken on the sides $AC$ and $BC$ so that the angle $NAB$ is 50 degrees, and the angle $MBA$ is 60 degrees. Prove that the angle $NMB$ is 30 degrees.

Watson. I tried to calculate this angle using trigonometry...

Holmes. My friend, take care of your health! The best ones are to read the newspapers, and I will devote a few minutes to this task.

For five or six minutes, Watson reads the newspaper, Holmes thoughtfully studies figure. Listen to quiet music.

Holmes. So, Watson, listen up. The task is indeed not an easy one. Let us consider on the side $BC$ the points $P$ such that the angle $PAB$ is 60 degrees. It is clear that the line $PM$ is parallel to the line $AB$ and that the triangle $PDM$ (and $D$, Watson, is the point of intersection of the segments $PA$ and $BM$) is equilateral. Since the triangle $BNA$ is equilateral, the lengths of the segments $BN$, $BA$ and $BD$ are equal and the angles $BND$ and $BDN$ are equal to 80 degrees. From here we can easily deduce that the angle $NDP$ is equal to 40 degrees. But the angle $NPD$ is also equal to 40 degrees. This means that the triangle $NDP$ is equilateral, and, therefore, $MN$ is the bisector of the angle $BMP$. Therefore, the value of the angle $NMB$ is equal to half the value of the angle $DMP$ and is equal to 30 degrees. That's it.

Watson (necessarily). But how?! How did you manage to come up with this solution?
