and there is mechanical power:

$$P_M = P_{\text{от}} - P_T = IU - I^2R.$$

The efficiency of a motora is by definition equal to the ratio of mechanical power to the power consumed from the source:

$$\eta = \frac{P_M}{P_{\text{от}}} = \frac{IU - I^2R}{IU} = 1 - \frac{IR}{U}.$$

As you can see, the thermal power increases with increasing current according to a quadratic law, the power received by the motor from the source increases according to a linear law, and the efficiency decreases with increasing current - the power of heat losses in the winding increases. Conducting arguments for mechanical power similar to those carried out in Problem 2 for $P_{\text{вн}}$, we conclude that there is a certain value of current at which $P_M$ is maximum. Differentiating the expression for $P_M$ with respect to the current and equating the derivative to zero, we find that this current strength is equal to $U/(2R)$.
