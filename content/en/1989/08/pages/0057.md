turbulent flow (from the Latin turbulentus - disorderly).
Such a remarkable discovery was made in 1893 by the famous English physicist O. Reynolds (1842-1912). His experiments were documented not with photographs, but with drawings. However, the experimental setup itself was preserved at the University of Manchester, and on it in our time, that is, almost a hundred years later, the classical experiment of Reynolds was repeated and photographs of the flow of liquid were obtained. They are shown at figure 4.

In the topmost picture you can see that the shavings of tinted water introduced into the glass tube move in a straight line - the flow is laminar. The next picture shows the transition from laminar flow to turbulent flow - at increased speed. The other two images show a clearly defined turbulent flow.

The following fact is interesting. In modern Manchester, city traffic is more intense than in the past. So, this tulin noise influenced the critical speed at which the transition from laminar to turbulent occurs
