⟦folio 23⟧

AMAZING
ADVENTURES
PERIODIC FIREWOOD

From the editor. Amazing thing. Our magazine has existed for 20 years and has never written to us on this topic: about the properties of periods of decimal fractions. And suddenly - three articles at once! One was written by V. G. Stolyar, another by E. A. Kuraev and Z. K. Silozadze, and the third by G. A. Galperin and A. V. Korlyukov. In the end, we decided to combine these articles into one, using V. G. Stolyar as a basis. This is what came out of it.

Let's look at examples...

The reader probably knows (and if not, even better: he learned this from our article) that every ordinary fraction is represented by a periodic decimal fraction (we can consider the final decimal fraction to be periodic with a period of 0 or 9). But hardly many people realize how many surprises this periodic fraction contains. Let's look at three examples:

$$\frac{1}{7} = 0,142857142857...$$
$$\frac{1}{12} = 0,08333333333...$$
$$\frac{1}{13} = 0,076923076923...$$

We see that for the numbers $1/7$ and $1/13$ the period begins immediately after the decimal point and consists of six digits (142857 and 076923, respectively), and for the number $1/12$ it begins in the third position after the decimal point and consists of a single digit: 3. A careful examination of the periods of the numbers $1/7$ and $1/13$ allows us to notice another circumstance. Namely, we put $N=142857$ (period of the fraction $1/7$) and then multiply $N$ by 2, 3, 4, ..:

$$2N=285714, 3N=428571,$$
$$4N=571428, 5N=714285,$$
$$6N=857142, 7N=99999.$$

We see that the first five of these numbers are obtained from the number $N$ by a “circular permutation” of the digits: how many digits from the end of the number move to the beginning; and the number $7N$ consists of only nines. Now let's do the same with the period of the fraction $1/13$ ($N=076923$):

$$2N=153846, 3N=230769,$$
$$4N=307692, 5N=384615,$$
$$6N=461538, 7N=538461,$$
$$8N=615384, 9N=692307,$$
$$10N=769230, 11N=846153,$$
$$12N=923076, 13N=99999.$$

Here the situation is somewhat different, but still interesting: five of the written numbers ($3N, 4N, 9N, 10N, 12N$) are obtained from the number $N$ by circular rearrangement of the digits, the other six numbers ($2N, 5N, 6N, 7N, 8N, 11N$) are obtained by circular rearrangement of the digits from each other, and, finally, the number $13N$ consists of only nines.

Another thing you can notice is this. If you take any of the six number values ​​written above, except for the number 999999, “break” it into two or three number values ​​and calculate the sum of these halves, you will get 999; for example, $142+857=999$, etc.

As you can see, there are many mysteries associated with periodic decimal fractions. Some of these mysteries remain unsolved in seconds, despite numerous attempts made over several centuries by mathematicians from different countries, both great and more “modest”. Still, we can tell you something about this.
