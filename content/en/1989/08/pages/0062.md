⟦folio 60⟧

However, with the “thermonuclear Eldorado” everything is not so simple yet. Research into the thermonuclear fusion control program has been going on for about 35 years, but is still far from being realized. The cost of experimental thermonuclear installations of the current generation exceeds 300 million dollars, and at the next stage it will increase by an order of magnitude. According to American experts, the re-commercial thermonuclear reactor will begin powering up no earlier than 2020. At the same time, despite the long period of research, there is still no convincing evidence of the economic competitiveness of thermonuclear power plants of the future.

So where can we look for a way out of this tangle of complex energy and economic problems? There is one promising direction that, for some reason, is undeservedly little talked about. This direction is space power systems. Even at the beginning of this century, the founder of astronautics, K. E. Tsiolkovsky, dreamed about them, who wrote that people would be able to master “solar energy, two billion times greater than that which humanity has on Earth.” His work will develop a detailed scenario for mankind’s industrial exploration of outer space, including the construction of orbital power systems of various types and purposes.

To transmit concentrated energy flows to the Earth's surface, reflective solar radiation located in near-Earth orbits can be used. For the first time, Yu. V. Kondratok, one of the pioneers of rocket technology in the USSR, drew attention to this possibility in his handwritten work (1918-1919).

In 1943, the famous Soviet electrical engineer Professor G.I. Babat proposed and substantiated a method of transmitting energy on board an aircraft using a good fused path of microwave radiation.

In 1960, another step was taken: engineer N.A. Varvarov came up with a project to use an orbital gel power station to supply energy to the Earth. And in 1968, the American engineer P. Glaser, working independently, combined the ideas of Babat and Varvarov - he put forward a project for the construction of a solar power station in geostationary near-Earth orbit to supply the Earth with electricity.

Since then, a lot of research has been devoted to this project. The authors of this article also came to this area, making several specific proposals. In particular, it was shown that with the help of such stations it is possible to launch cargo into space. What are the features and advantages of the space solar power plant (SSP) project?

To put it very briefly, the following is proposed. A satellite with a mass of 5·10⁴ tons is placed in a geostationary orbit at an altitude of 36,000 km. Solar panels with an area of ​​100 km² are installed on the satellite and convert the energy of solar radiation into electric current, which, in turn, is converted into microwave radiation. A well-focused transmitting antenna beam of this radiation is directed to Earth, caught there by a receiving antenna, the diameter of which is 20 km, and then again converted into electric current for transmission to the consumer. The parameters of the microwave radiation beam - frequency 2.45 Hz, energy flux density 10 mW/cm² - are selected based on safety requirements and minimal absorption in the atmosphere. These are the features of the project in the most general form. Of course, there are numerous options, but let’s not dwell on specific issues here.
