Of course, there are no projects that have these advantages and no disadvantages. KSE also have them, and they are Serbez ones. Firstly, this is the high cost of launching columnar mass structures into space. Secondly, the high cost of the energy converters themselves. Thirdly, there is the danger of harmful effects on the atmosphere from emissions from propulsion systems of space transport systems. Undoubtedly, all these are serious problems, but they are of a technical nature. And therefore, in principle, solvable.

In what ways can solutions to these problems be found? There is reason to expect that with the development of the package-space and electronics industry, by the beginning of the 21st century, new space transport systems will be created and highly efficient low-cost photoconverters will be developed. According to American experts, in the future, the cost of launching payloads weighing 300-500 tons into low-Earth orbit will drop to hundreds and ten dollars per 1 kg, and the cost of solar panels will drop to 100 dollars per 1 kW of produced electrical power. Taking these figures into account, CSPs with a capacity of 5-10 GW will acquire a sixth advantage - economic competitiveness.

What about the danger of air pollution, which arises from the launch of a large fleet of package carriers? There is a way out here too: Professor of Princeton University in the USA J. O’Neill proposed using mineral resources added on the Moon to build a network of near-Earth CSPs. O’Neill figuratively described his idea as the “boot-lacing” method: the first CSP is built based on earthly resources, and then uses it as an orbital base to create a mining and industrial complex on the satellite of our planet.

Have we moved too far from modernity? — perhaps an attentive reader will ask us. “Isn’t it better to leave these questions for consideration by our descendants, who will live in the 21st century?” And we have enough of our own problems...

The time for restructuring the energy sector to new energy resources, as experience shows, is quite significant - about 50 years. Think about these numbers and judge which decisions can be left to posterity and which we have to make ourselves.
