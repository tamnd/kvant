2. The distance circle $C_1$, specified in the rectangular coordinate system of the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 2xsec b + 1 = 0$, where $b$ is a real number such that $0 < b < \pi/2$.

Find the center $A_1$ and radius $r_1$ of circle $C_1$.

The distance is then the circle $C_2$, given by the equation $x^2 + y^2 - 2ytg a - 1 = 0$, where $0 < a < \frac{\pi}{2}$.

Express the distance $A_1A_2$ in terms of $r_1$ and $r_2$.

3. When different points $B, C, D$ lying on the plane are determined by the vectors $b$, $\vec{c}$ and $\vec{d}$ with a common origin $O$.

Prove that $B, C$ and $D$ lie on the same line if and only if $\alpha \bar{b} + \mu \vec{c} + \vec{d} = 0$ for some numbers $\alpha, \mu, v$ not equal to the same number and satisfying the conditions $\alpha + \mu + v = 0$.

Next, prove that the points $B, C$ and $D$ lie on the same line and the point $D$ divides $BC$ in the ratio $\gamma: \beta$ if and only if $\vec{d} = (\beta \bar{b} + \gamma \vec{c})(\beta + \gamma)^{-1}$.

The fourth point $A$ of the plane is determined by the vector $OA = \vec{a}$ with the same origin $O$ and does not lie on the line $BC$. Prove that every point $P$ belonging to triangle $ABC$ and lying on $AD$ is given by the vector $\overline{OP} = \vec{p} = (\alpha \bar{a} + \beta \bar{b} + \gamma \vec{c})(\alpha + \beta + \gamma)^{-1}$,

where $\alpha$ is some positive number.

If the point $Q$ corresponds to a vector $\vec{q} = OQ$ such that $\vec{q} = (-\alpha \bar{a} + \beta \bar{b} + \gamma \vec{c})(-\alpha + \beta + \gamma)^{-1}$,

then the point $Q$ lies on the line $AP$.

4. Let $a$ and $c$ be real numbers, and $b$ be a complex number such that $ac < bb$ ($\bar{b}$ is the number, complex conjugate of $c$ $b$).

Prove that the set of points in the complex plane $z = x + iy$ satisfying the conditions

$$az\bar{z} + \bar{b}z + b\bar{z} + c = 0,$$

are a circle at $a \neq 0$ and a straight line at $a = 0$.

In the case of $a = 0$, prove that if the line is tangent to the unit circle $zz = 1$, then $b$ and $c$ satisfy the corresponding conditions $c^2 = ub\bar{b}$.

Prove that under the transformation

$$z \rightarrow w = \frac{1}{\bar{z}}$$

a) the points of the unit circle are fixed;

b) a straight line given by the equation $\bar{b}z + b\bar{z} + c = 0$ and tangent to the unit circle,

5. A light ladder of length $l$ is leaned against the wall at an angle $\theta$ from the vertical.

A person weighing $W$ stands on a ladder at a height $Y$ from the floor.

Prove that at the moment when the ladder begins to slip, the normal reactions $R_1$ and $R_2$ of the floor and wall are equal to the corresponding ones.

$$R_1 = \frac{W}{1 + \mu_1\mu_2}; R_2 = \frac{\mu_1W}{1 + \mu_1\mu_2},$$

where $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ are the friction coefficients of the ladder on the field and the wall, respectively.

Prove that the ladder will slide only if

$$Y > \frac{\mu_1l\cos \theta(\mu_2 + \text{ctg} \theta)}{1 + \mu_1\mu_2}.$$

From here show that a person can climb to the very top of the ladder if

$$\text{tg} \theta < \mu_1.$$

6. A tennis player located at point $x = 0$ on axis $Ox$ throws the ball into a wall perpendicular to axis $Ox$ at point $x = X$. Let him, with his $n$th hit, send the ball from a height $h_n$ with a speed $v_n$ at an angle $\theta$ to the horizon. At what height above the $Ox$ axis will the ball hit the wall (dwelling air resistance)? When reflected from the wall, the vertical component of the ball's velocity does not change, but the horizontal component is multiplied by the recovery coefficient $e$. Prove that the height $h_{n+1}$ at which the ball will end up when returning to the tennis player satisfies the corresponding ones.

Prove that a tennis player can maintain a “stationary mode” (i.e. $h_{n+1} = h_n$ for all $n$) if he sends the ball at a speed

$$v = (g(1 + e)X/esin 2\theta)^{1/2}.$$
