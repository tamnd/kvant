School of Cosmonautics

In 1986, a visiting School of Cosmonautics (SC) was organized at Krasnoyarsk State University. It is held 3-4 times a year, usually in school books.

SC is a temporary creative team consisting of high school students (8-10) grades, students, graduate students, teachers and researchers. The task of this temporary creative team is professional development: for experienced teachers and researchers - improving pedagogical skills; for undergraduates and graduate students - deepening knowledge of the subject being studied and acquisition for teaching activities; for schoolchildren - choosing a profession and initial preparation for it.

The basic principles of organizing the work of the ShK are as follows: staggered deadlines in accordance with the maximum load; creating conditions for informal communication among schoolchildren, students, teachers and researchers in an intensive work environment; creating conditions for the free choice of activities by each cadet and for the development of collective forms of work “epicages”.

The tenth ShK became the first all-Union school cosmonautics with the participation of schoolchildren from Bulgaria.

It took place from January 30 to February 12, 1989 on the basis of the Higher Komsomol School at the Komsomol School in Moscow. The All-Union Youth Aerospace Society “Soyuz” took the most active part in the organization of the school, organizing and excursions to Zvezdny and the Mission Control Center, and meetings with interested people, and assistance in establishing business contacts.

For each visiting school, which generally lasts about two weeks, its own program is created, including lectures, seminars, work in the classroom or in laboratories, problem tasks, tours, a marathon, expert surveys, electives based on interests, discussion and amateur clubs.

For example, it includes an opshem program of ten ShK. The lectures were as follows: Newton's laws, gravity, conservation laws, near-space physics, thermodynamics, cosmic rays, space optics. These lectures were accompanied by seminars on exercises. Popular lectures were also given and lecture-meetings were held with leading space industry specialists on the following topics: classification of spacecraft and space systems, environmental systems, medical systems, medical process space technology, cosmology, fine arts and space, etc.

There were also organized excursions to the Kremlin, VDNKh, the Pushkin Museum, and the Korolev House Museum, where a meeting with space artists took place. The trip to Zvezdny included mathematical lectures and conversations with cosmonauts and leading space exploration specialists. In the room of maintenance simulators for training methods for cosmonauts for future flights, in the overload test room, the range of questions was the widest - from purely technical, related to the test stand, to medical-biological, maintenance

In addition to lectures and seminars, compulsory classes included work in the terminal class, in the physical experiment laboratory and classes in the swimming pool.

A form of educational work called “Roulette” is very popular among students. Roulette is carried out by crew between the lecture and the seminar session. The task of Roulette is to clearly record the main information points (new words, ideas, facts, etc.) that were communicated to the cadets during the lecture. To work, the crew stands in a circle, with an impromptu roulette spinning in the center. Whoever the arrow points to must answer the next question. The time spent answering is recorded, as is the total time spent on Roulette. The result is taken into account in the general competition between crews. For example, here is a set of Roulette questions after the lecture “Physics of Near Space”:

1. What are the characteristic dimensions of the Earth's magnetosphere?
2. What is the Lorentz force?
3. What trajectories do charged particles move in a uniform magnetic field?

4. How does a magnetic trap work?
5. How does a particle move in a magnetic trap?
6. What is the drift speed?
7. What conclusion follows from the conservation of the magnetic moment of a charged particle?
8. What are the Earth's radiation fields and how are they structured?
In addition to compulsory classes, the school had a number of electives: space optics, basics of digital technology, history of astronautics, microcalculators, cosmology, probability theory, PAL-SEECAM video club, English club, etc. Each cadet was required to attend one of the electives.

For those who did not have enough workload, correspondence tours were held: a list of 5-6 tasks was posted, for which four days were given to solve. The results were assessed in points, personal perseverance was determined and at the same time this was taken into account in the modernization between the crews.

Each working day in the school begins with the assignment of a problematic task for the day. The crew worked on the task throughout the day, and before lights out, they submitted the solution in writing to the training department. The best works were celebrated the next day, and the scores were taken into account in the general competition between the crews.
