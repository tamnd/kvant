REWARD

In this issue we offer our “younger” readers a story by the famous Soviet popularizer of mathematics and physics Ya. I. Perelman, which we reprinted from his book “Living Mathematics” (M.: Nauka, 1959).

Y. I. PERELMAN

This is what, according to legend, happened many centuries ago in ancient Rome.*)

The commander Terence, by order of the emperor, made a victorious victory and returned to Rome with trophies. Arriving in the capital, he asked to be allowed to see the emperor.

The emperor kindly received the commander, thanked him heartily for his military services to the empire and promised to give him a high position in the cenate as a reward.

But that’s not what Terentz needed. He objected:

“I won many victories in order to exalt your power, sir, and surround your name with weakness.” I was not afraid of death, and if I had not one, but many lives, I would sacrifice them all to you. But I'm tired of fighting; My youth has passed, the blood runs slower in my veins.

*) The story in the wave transmission is borrowed from an ancient Latin manuscript belonging to one of the private book depositories in England.

The time has come to relax in the house of my ancestors and enjoy the joys of home life.

- WHAT would you like from me, Terence? - asked the emperor.

- Listen with condescension, sir! Over the long years of military life, day after day staining my sword with blood, I did not manage to arrange financial well-being for myself. I'm poor, sir...

- Continue, brave Terence.

“If you want to give a breast to your humble servant,” continued the encouraged commander, “then your empty generosity will help me to wait peacefully in abundance for the year near the hearth.” I do not seek honor or high position in the all-powerful Senate. I would like to retire from power and from public life in order to rest in peace. Sir, give me money to provide for the rest of my life.

The emperor, says legend, was not distinguished by widespread generosity. He loved to save money for himself and spent it sparingly on others. The commander's request made him think.

“What amount, Terence, would you consider sufficient for yourself?” he asked.

- A million denarii, sir.

The emperor thought again. The commander waited with his head down. Finally the emperor spoke:

- Valiant Terence! You are a great warrior, and your glorious exploits deserve a generous reward. I will give you wealth. Tomorrow at noon you will hear my decision here.

Terence bowed and left.

The next day, at the appointed hour, the commander appeared at the emperor's palace.

- Hello, brave Terence! - said the emperor.

Terence humbly bowed his head.

“I have come, sir, to listen to your decision.” You promised to reward me a million times.

The Emperor replied:

“I don’t want such a noble warrior like you to receive a pitiful reward for your exploits.” Listen to me. There are 5 million copper brasses in my treasury*). Now listen to my words. You will enter the treasury, take one coin in your hands, return here and lay it at my feet. The next day you will go to the treasury again, take a coin equal to 2 breasts, and put it here next to the first. On the third day you will bring a coin worth 4 breasts, on the fourth - worth 8 breasts, on the fifth - 16, and so on, increasing the value of the coin. I will order that coins of proper value be made for you every day. And as long as you have the strength to lift the coins, you will carry them out of my treasury. Nobody has the right to help you; you must use only your own strength. And when you notice that you can no longer lift the coin, stop: our agreement will end, but all the coins that you managed to carry will remain yours and will serve as your reward.

Greedily has the right to help you; you must use only your own strength. And when you notice that you can no longer lift the coin, stop: our agreement will end, but all the coins that you managed to carry will remain yours and will serve as your reward.

“I am pleased with your mercy, sir,” he answered with a joyful smile. - Your reward is truly generous!

Terence began daily visits to the state treasury. They were located not far from the emperor's reception hall, and the first transitions with coins did not cost Terenz any favors.

On the first day, he took only one breaststroke from the treasury. This is a small coin, 21 mm in crossbar and 5 g in weight**).

The second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth transitions were also easy, when the commander brought out coins of double, 4-fold, 8-fold, 16-fold and 32-fold weight.

*) Small coin, fifth of a denarius.
**) Weight of a five-kopeck coin of modern mintage.

The seventh paddle coin measured 320 grams in our modern measures and had a diameter of $8^{1/2}$ cm (more precisely 84 mm *).

On the eighth day, Terentsyu had to withdraw a coin from the treasury, corresponding to 128 single coins. She oared 640 g and had a head of about $10^{1/2}$ cm.

On the ninth day, Terenqu brought a coin of 256 single coins to the imperial hall. It was 13 cm wide and oared over $1^{1/4}$ kg.

On the twelfth day, the coin reached almost 27 cm in diameter and weighed $10^{1/4}$ kg.

The Emperor, who had until now looked at the commander admiringly, now did not hide his triumph. He saw that 12 transitions had already been made, and only a little over 2,000 copper coins had been taken out of the treasury.

The thirteenth day delivered to the brave Terentsy a coin equal to 4096 individual coins. It was about 34 cm wide and weighed $20^{1/2}$ kg.

On the fourteenth day, Terentsyu heavily carried out from the treasury a coin weighing 41 kg and with a face of about 42 cm.

“Aren’t you tired, my brave Terentsyu?” - the emperor asked him, holding back a smile.

“No, my lord,” the commander answered gloomily, wiping sweat from his forehead.

The fifteenth day has arrived. This time it was hard for Terence. He slowly walked towards the emperor, carrying a huge coin made up of 16,384 individual coins. It reached 53 cm in width and weighed 80 kg - the weight of a tall won.

On the sixteenth day, the commander staggered under the neck lying on his back. It was a coin equal to 32,768 unit coins and weighing 164 kg; its diameter reached 67 cm.

The commander was exhausted and breathing heavily. The Emperor smiled...

When Terenqu appeared at the emperor's reception hall the next day, he was greeted with loud laughter. He could no longer carry his shoe in his hands, but rolled it forward of him. The coin was 84 cm in diameter and weighed 328 kg. It corresponded to the weight of 65,536 individual coins.

The eighteenth day was the last day of Terence's enrichment. On this day his visits to the treasury and his pilgrimage to the emperor’s hall ended. He had to deliver this time a coin corresponding to 131,072 unit coins. It was more than a meter across and had oars of 655 kg. Using his stake as a lever, Terentsyu with the greatest direction of his strength barely rolled her into the hall. With a roar, a gigantic coin fell at the feet of the emperor.

Terentsyu was completely exhausted.

“I can’t take it anymore... That’s enough,” he said.

The Emperor could hardly suppress a laugh of pleasure, seeing the complete success of his cunning. He ordered the treasurer to count how many breast strokes Terentsyu carried into the reception hall.
