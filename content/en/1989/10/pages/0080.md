11. $\frac{p^3-p^2}{2}$. Solution: For any $m$, the sum of $m^p+(p-m)^p$ is divisible by $p^2$ (see below for proof). Since for $m=1$, ..., $p-1$ the numbers $m^p$ and $(p-m)^p$ themselves are not divisible by $p^2$, the sum of the remainders from dividing these numbers by $p^2$ is equal to $p^2$. This means that the sum of the remainders from division by $p^2$ of the numbers $1^p$, $2^p$, ..., $(p-1)^p$, i.e. $1^p$, $(p-1)^p$; $2^p$, $(p-2)^p$; ... (total $\frac{p-1}{2}$ pairs), equal to $p^2\frac{p-1}{2}$. As for the above fact, it is a consequence of Newton's binomial formula. For those who do not know this formula, we will provide a more detailed proof. When opening the brackets in $(p-m)^p=(p-m)...(p-m)$, the set $p$ will appear less than twice in $p+1$ terms: $-m^p$ and $p$ times $pm^{p-1}$. Thus, $m^p+(p-m)^p=m^p-m^p+p\cdot pm^{p-1}+(\text{число, делиеся на } p^2)$; that means $m^p+(p-m)^p$ is divisible by $p^2$.

12. $\{(0, 2), (0, -2), (-2, -2), (-2, 2)\}$. Solution. Since the number $x+1$ is coprime to the number $x^2$, our equality can only hold for $x^2=x+1$, $x^2=-(x+1)$, $x+1=1$, $x+1=-1$ or $x^2=0$. The first two equations do not have integer solutions. So, $x=0$ or $x=-2$.

$$\cos \left( \arcsin \frac{x^2-8}{8} + \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = -\sin \arcsin \frac{x^2-8}{8} = \frac{8-x^2}{8},$$

$$\cos \left( 2\arcsin \frac{x}{4} \right) = 1-2\sin^2\arcsin \frac{x}{4} = 1-2\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{8-x^2}{8}.$$
