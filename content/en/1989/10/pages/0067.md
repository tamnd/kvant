Opportunities for creating coal-fired power plants

From the editor. The following article is reprinted from the Royal Institution Energy Diary 40905, page 1001.

In connection with the acute crisis caused by the threat of depletion of uranium and thorium deposits on the Earth and the Moon, the editors consider it useful to acknowledge the widest possible dissemination of the information contained in this article.

Introduction. Coal (black, fossilized remains of ancient plants) recently discovered in several places at once opens up interesting opportunities for the creation of non-nuclear energy. Some burning sites bear traces of their exploitation by acceptable people, who apparently used coal to make jewelry and cherries for their faces during funeral ceremonies.

The possibility of using coal in the energy sector is associated with the fact that it is easily described, and creates a high temperature with the release of specific energy close to 0.0000001 metawatt-day per gram. This is, of course, very small, but the coal reserves are apparently large, possibly amounting to millions of tons.

The main advantage of coal should be considered its very small critical mass compared to fissile materials. Nuclear power plants are known to produce self-sustaining and controlled reaction reactions of fuel cells. The kinetics of this reaction is much more complex than the kinetics of nuclear fission and is still poorly understood. True, a differential equation that approximates this process has already been obtained, but its solution is possible only in the simplest special cases.
