M1186 — M1190, F1193 — F1197

M1186. We will say that two quadrangles - paper and cardboard - fit together if the cardboard one can be placed on the paper one so that all its vertices fall on the sides of the paper one (one on each) and at the same time, if you bend the four small paper triangles formed onto the cardboard quadrangle, they will cover it all in one layer.

a) Prove that if the quadrilaterals fit together, then the paper has either two opposite sides parallel or the diagonals are perpendicular.

b) Prove that if a paper quadrilateral is a parallelogram, then you can make a suitable cardboard quadrilateral.

N. V. Vasiliev

M1187. Prove that if $m$ is even, then all integers from 1 to $m-1$ can be written in such an order that no sum of several in a row is divisible by $m$.

F. G. Shleifer

M1188. a) Given 101 rectangles with integer sides not exceeding 100. Prove that among them there are three rectangles $A, B, C$ that can be placed inside each other: $A \subseteq B \subseteq C$.

b) Prove that among 1989 rectangles with integer sides not exceeding 100, there are 40 rectangles such that the first can be placed in the second, the second in the third, ..., the 39th in the 40th.

N. M. Sedrakin

M1189*. There are $n$ lines on the plane, no three of which pass through the same point and no two of which are parallel. Prove that in each of the parts into which these lines partition the plane, you can put an integer different from 0 and not exceeding modulo $n$, so that on any side of any of these lines the sum of the numbers is equal to 0.

D. V. Fomin

M1190*. a) Prove that if there are $3n$ stars in a table of $2n \times 2n$ cells, then you can cross out $n$ rows and $n$ columns so that all the stars are crossed out. b) Prove that in a table of $2n \times 2n$ cells it is possible to place a $3n+1$ asterisk so that after crossing out any $n$ rows and $n$ columns, at least one asterisk remains.
