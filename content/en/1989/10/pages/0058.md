About enlarging an image

V. A. BODIC, F. V. SHEINERMAN

One of the characteristics of the image of a luminous object in a lens (or in a more complex optical system) is its magnification. Longitudinal and transverse increases are reasonably distinguished. Let, for example, a lens give an image of an object in the form of a right angle ABC (Fig. 1). Then the longitudinal increase

$$\beta = \frac{f_B - f_A}{d_A - d_B},$$

and transverse -

$$\Gamma = \frac{B'C'}{BC} = \frac{H'}{H},$$

where $d$ is the distance from the luminous point to the lens, $f$ is the distance from the lens to the image of the point, $H$ is the transverse size of the luminous object, $H'$ is the transverse size of its image.

If an object is located perpendicular to the optical axis of a collecting lens and the lens creates its actual image (Fig. 2), then the transverse magnification

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{d - F} = \frac{f - F}{F}. \tag{1}$$

If a converging lens creates a mini-image (Fig. 3), then

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{F - d} = \frac{f + F}{F}. \tag{2}$$

In the case of a diffuse lens (Fig. 4)

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{F + d} = \frac{F - f}{F}. \tag{3}$$
