Let's build a “model” of the design plane. First, all points of the upper hemisphere without the equator are glued to the corresponding points of the lower hemisphere; Now we also need to glue the diametrically opposite points of the equator - the boundaries of the hemispheres (Fig. 1). We transform the equator into a quadrilateral ABCD (Fig. 2, 3), glue its antifiber vertices A and C, B and D, and then its sides - AB with CD, AD with CB (Fig. 4); check that the rule for gluing equatorial points will be observed). When gluing the second pair of sides, self-intersection of the surface cannot be avoided. A line of self-intersection arises, each point of which must be represented as a double: one instance of the point belongs to one entity, the other to another. If, moving along the design plane, we pass through the line of self-intersection, then we will put the outer side of the surface on the inner one. Thus, in essence, this surface does not have any two sides - like the Möbius strip, it is one-sided.

If we glue each point of the sphere with a diametrically opposite point, we obtain a surface equivalent, up to continuous deformations, of the design plane (see pp. 10-11). We show two methods of such gluing.
