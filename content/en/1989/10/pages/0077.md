3. Let us denote by K, M, P, T (Fig. 14) the bases of the heights lowered from point C to straight lines AB, AE, DE and BD, respectively. It is clear that the rays CK and CP lie between the rays CT and CM and the quadrilateral KMPT is convex. Therefore, by virtue of the theorem on the raised angle, to prove the statement of the problem it is enough to show that $\angle TKM + \angle MPT = 180^\circ$. Let $\alpha = \angle DBC, \beta = \angle EAC$. Obviously, $\angle DCT = \alpha$ (recall that CT ⊥ DB) and $\angle MCE = \beta$. Four points C, T, K, B lie on the same circle, since the angles $\angle CTB$ and $\angle CKB$ are right angles. Therefore $\angle TKC = \angle TBC = \alpha$ (these angles are equal as they rest on the same arc). Similarly, $\angle CKM = \angle CAM = \beta$ (points C, M, K, A lie on the same circle), $\angle DPT = \angle DCT = \alpha$ (points C, P, T, D lie on the same circle), $\angle EPM = \angle ECM = \beta$ (points E, M, P, C lie on the same circle). Therefore, $\angle TKM + \angle TPM = (\alpha + \beta) + (180^\circ - \alpha - \beta) = 180^\circ$. This implies the required statement.

4. From the inequality between the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean, each of the ratios $\frac{a}{1 + a^2}, \frac{b}{1 + b^2}, \frac{c}{1 + c^2}$ does not exceed $\frac{1}{2}$. This implies a left inequality. Let's prove the right inequality. Let $x = 1 + a, y = 1 + b, z = 1 + c$. Then $x + y + z \leq 6$. Dividing this inequality by x, y, z and adding the resulting inequalities, we obtain the inequality

$$\left(1 + \frac{y}{x} + \frac{z}{x}\right) + \left(\frac{x}{y} + 1 + \frac{z}{y}\right) + \left(\frac{x}{z} + \frac{y}{z} + 1\right) \leq 6 \left(\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{z}\right),$$

whence the required inequality follows, since $\frac{x}{y} + \frac{y}{x} \geq 2, \frac{y}{z} + \frac{z}{y} \geq 2, \frac{z}{x} + \frac{x}{z} \geq 2$.
