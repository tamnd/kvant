There will be an amorphous body, characterized by a completely random arrangement of atoms. This arrangement, you say, is not symmetrical at all - any rotations (not multiples of 360°) will give different pictures. But all the pictures will be equally disordered and in this sense no different from the original one. Disorder evens out (uniforms) the properties of the environment in all directions. It makes all directions equivalent. This means, paradoxically, the directional symmetry in an amorphous body is higher than even in a cubic crystal! Any direction is an axis of symmetry. In light of this, Ohm's law (3) for manufactured media of each simple elementary truth.

Yes, directional symmetry is good. But what about life if it is not there at all (for example, the crystal at figure 5 does not have a single axis of symmetry)? We will have to turn to Ohm's law in its most general form. Let's try to install it.

Let us arbitrarily choose three mutually perpendicular directions $x, y, z$ (coordinate axes). Let us decompose the field $\vec{E}$ into components $E_x, E_y, E_z$. We will also look for Tok $\vec{j}$ in the form of three components $j_x, j_y, j_z$.

What does $j_x$ consist of? Coordinate axes are not yet axes of symmetry! When tova, generated by the three components of the field, not necessarily whose exactly along the axes $x, y$ and $z$. This means that each current will have $x-, y-$ and $z$-components, i.e., not only the $x$-component of the field, but also the $y-$ and $z$-components of the $\vec{E}$ vector make a proportional contribution to $j_x$. Let us denote the correspondence of the proportionality coefficients by $\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{xy}, \sigma_{zx}$.

What does $j_y$ and $j_z$ consist of? The answer is almost verbatim the same as the previous one, only the coefficients will be different.

So:

$$\begin{align*}
i_x &= \sigma_{xx} E_x + \sigma_{xy} E_y + \sigma_{zx} E_z, \\
j_y &= \sigma_{yx} E_x + \sigma_{yy} E_y + \sigma_{yz} E_z, \\
j_z &= \sigma_{zx} E_x + \sigma_{zy} E_y + \sigma_{zz} E_z.
\end{align*}$$
