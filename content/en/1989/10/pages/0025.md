Zeta function

A reader familiar with the classic proof of the prime number theorem will say with disappointment that the new proof is no simpler

the one that Euclid came up with more than two thousand years ago. That's right, but it turned out that from Euler's proof one can also learn things about prime numbers that cannot be deduced from ενClide's proof. To do this, you need to form a series

$$1 + \frac{1}{2^2} + \frac{1}{3^2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n^2} + \dots$$

and study its behavior for all possible values ​​of $z$. Due to the exceptional importance of the sum of series (7), it should be designated $\zeta(z)$ and called `дзета-функцией`.

It turned out that the question of how often prime numbers occur among natural numbers depends precisely on the behavior of the zeta function $\zeta(z)$ for complex values ​​$z$. Of course, we can talk about these values ​​only if we reduce the numbers to a complex degree. And this is done using the formula derived by Euler

$$e^{ix} = \cos x + i \sin x,$$

which we will talk about later.

The main question, the solution of which depends on the assessment of problems in number theory, is the following: at what values ​​of $z$ does the zeta function vanish? Back in the second half of the 19th century, the remarkable German mathematician W. Riemann (1826–1866) proposed that all these values ​​(they are called `нулями` zeta functions) are complex and have the form $z_k = \frac{1}{2} + iy_k$. Mathematicians have made a lot of successful efforts to prove this statement. But so far no one has succeeded, although some claim to have found proof. To test Riemann's assertion, high-speed computers were introduced. With their help, it was possible to find several million zeros of the zeta function. They all have the same appearance that Riemann predicted. If I had decided on any issue in physics or biology, no one would have doubted that Riemann was right - a hypothesis, confirmed by millions of experiments, becomes an inevitable statement in these studies. But mathematics is not like that - it deals with an infinite number of natural numbers, and suddenly the Riemann hypothesis turns out to be false!
