⟦folio 55⟧

Ship waves have a completely different nature - they are formed as a result of vibrations of the sea surface under the influence of gravity forces. A photograph taken with an impact above the ship (Fig. 7) shows that the disturbances induce from the ship and the turbulence formed behind it on the left fill a wedge-shaped region with an angle of $\alpha \approx 19,5^\circ$. This angle corresponds to the Mach number

$$M = \frac{1}{\sin \alpha} \approx \frac{1}{\sin 19,5^\circ} \approx 3.$$

It turns out that any ship (as opposed to an airplane), regardless of its shape and size, moves as if its “effective” Mach number was 3.

10. Sphere in supersonic flow

The sound wave we just talked about is generated far from the aircraft. The situation is different near the plane. Here the wave is compressed, turning into a shock wave - a surface on which the velocity component normal to it suffers a break. Supersonic before the shock wave, the speed becomes subsonic after the shock wave.

Shock waves are easy to observe in wind tunnels. figure 8 shows a photograph of a flow around a sphere moving at a speed that corresponds to the Mach number $M=1,5$. Beyond the section of the bow shock wave that is located directly in front of the ball, the flow will be subsonic up to the angle $45^\circ$ (the angle of absence from the direction of flight of the ball). Then the flow accelerates and becomes supersonic. At an angle of approximately $90^\circ$, the laminar boundary layer breaks away from the ball, creating an oblique shock wave emanating from it, as if hanging above the turbulent wake.
