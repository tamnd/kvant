This is how the phenomenon of superconductivity was discovered - the complete disappearance of metal resistance. If a current is created in a superconducting ring and the power sources are turned off, then there should be no noticeable attenuation of the current, and distant flows were found: the corresponding time is much greater than the age of the Universe. To paraphrase Sholem Aleichem, we can say: “Resistance is like money - if it is there, it is, and if it is not, it is not.”

Over time, it turned out that superconductivity is applicable to a wide range of substances, each of which has its own critical temperature - the temperature at which resistance disappears. As new superconductors were discovered, the value of this value grew, but very slowly: over 75 years it grew from 4 K to 23 K - by about 0.2 K/year.

The situation changed radically in 1986, when Swiss physicists A. Moller and G. Bedorz began studying a new class of materials containing oxygen, copper and other metals - the so-called oxocuprates. These materials do not look like ordinary conductors (outwardly they resemble carbon tablets produced in pharmacies), and their promise in terms of BC seemed very competent. However, Moller and Bedorz believed in success and were not deceived in their expectations: the critical temperature immediately moved back to about 40 K.

This success became the discovery of BC (more precisely, the discovery of a new class of materials that are extremely promising from the point of view of further increasing the critical temperature). A short time later, at the beginning of 1987, the American P. Chu synthesized oxocuprate with a critical temperature of about 100 K, and today’s absolute record is approximately 125 K. This is, so to speak, “a bird in the hand.” As for the “pie in the sky”, there are reports (which, however, require confirmation) of the observation of superconductivity at room and even higher temperatures. In any case, the existence of superconductors that do not require any specialization and retain their ordered character. To understand the nature of this “insensitivity” of the electrons of a superconductor, you will have to believe in one fundamental property of the particles from which the matter is built. They consist in the fact that in the microcosm there are two forms of behavior of particles in relation to each other (and, accordingly, two classes of particles) - “individualistic” and “collective”.

Particles that are “individualized” (fermicized) are necessarily in different states, preventing the accumulation of more than one particle in each of them. For example, “collective” particles (Bose particles) tend to accumulate in the same state, “attracting” other Bose particles into it and, conversely, they will leave this state. This tendency is expressed especially strongly if a noticeable fraction of all Bose particles have already accumulated in a given state. Such a cluster is called a Bose condensate (the particles included in it are absolutely not different from each other).

The Bose condensate is not supported by external influences; it turns, as already noted, into attempts to align individual particles from it and transfer them to another state. It is a monolith, tightly welded together by the “moral and political unity” of its members and resisting attempts to destroy this unity. The reader can easily obties corresponding analogies in history or in everyday life.
