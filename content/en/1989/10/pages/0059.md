Now let's look at a few specific tasks. Many of them are taken from entrance exam tickets of different years to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology.

Task 1. Using a thin lens, an image of an object with a fivefold magnification is obtained on the screen. Without changing the position of the lens, the screen was moved along the optical axis by $l=30$ cm. In order to obtain a sharp image, the object also had to be moved. In this case, the image turned out to be the second larger than the object. How far did the object move?

In both cases, the image of an object is real (since it is obtained on the screen), and it is created by a converging lens. Let's write down the lens formula for the first case -

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}$$

and for the second (Fig. 5) -

$$\frac{1}{d+x} + \frac{1}{f-l} = \frac{1}{F}.$$

Here we taught that the screen was moved towards the lens, and the object was moved away from it. Only under these conditions is the increase in the second case less than in the first.

From the written equations, taking into account the increase in $f/d=5$ and $(f-l)/(d+x)=3$, we obtain

$$\frac{5}{f} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{3}{f-l} + \frac{1}{f-l}.$$

From here

$$f=3l, \quad d=\frac{3}{5}l, \quad x=\frac{l}{15}=2\text{ см}.$$

If the screen were not moved closer to the lens, but moved away from it, then from the magnification

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F} \quad \text{и} \quad \frac{1}{d-x} + \frac{1}{f+l} = \frac{1}{F}$$
