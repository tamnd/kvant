How is the projective plane structured?

Let's consider a sphere with a center at point P. Any straight line of our path (“point”) intersects it at two diametrically opposite points. Let us note the lines to these pairs of points. Then the projective plane is applied to the sphere (Fig. 3), in which the ends of each diameter are considered to be one point - identified, and the lines of the projective plane are represented by circles with the identification of diametrically opposite points. Each pair of such points can be “glued” together by replacing them with one common point - this was done at figure 4 (in this case continuous deformations come to be performed). What happened in the end? Circle! So, by adding an infinitely distant point to the general line, i.e., turning it into a projective line, we, as it were, close it into a circle.

Now we will try to carry out the same gluing for the entire plane. Let's cut out a ring strip along the screen from our sphere - two spherical caps will remain, which after gluing turn into one (Fig. 5). Let's cut the belt along two opposite meridians (Fig. 6, a; the cut lines are marked the same way, because they must be identified) and glue one half to the other.
