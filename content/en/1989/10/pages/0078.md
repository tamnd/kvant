7. Let $O$ be the center of the circle. If $k=0$, then the statement is obvious. In the case of $k \neq 0$, we lower the perpendiculars $OM, OP, OT$ from the center $O$ onto the chords $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ (Fig. 16). Let $\alpha = \angle OKT$. Then

$$KM = KO \cdot \cos(60^\circ - \alpha),$$
$$KP = KO \cdot \cos(60^\circ + \alpha),$$
$$KT = KO \cdot \cos \alpha.$$

Since $\cos(60^\circ - \alpha) + \cos(60^\circ + \alpha) = \cos \alpha$, we get that $KM + KP = KT$. But $KM = \frac{1}{2} (KA - KA_1)$, $KP = \frac{1}{2} (KB - KB_1)$, $KT = \frac{1}{2} (KC_1 - KC)$. Substituting these expressions into the resulting equality $KM + KP = KT$, we arrive at the required equality.

Comment. A special case of this problem—the point $K$ lies on a circle—is well known.
