8. Tray Hill-Show

In 1898, the English hydrodynamicist Hill-Shaw created an original installation, which was later named after him. Between two parallel glass plates he passed a clear liquid (water, glycerin) and introduced a red substance (Fig. 1) at several points in the inlet part of the tray. Tinted structures, flowing around the barrier inserted between the plates - into a hydro-

in dynamics it is called a model - they reproduced the picture of the lines along which the particles of the liquid moved. The distance between the plates $d$ was much smaller than the characteristic size of the model $l$.

Hill-Shaw got an amazing result. Inside the tray, the fluid flow is normal - the influence of friction (viscosity) is significant, the velocity profile $u(z)$ has the shape of a parabola, the flow “sticks” to the walls, i.e. $u(\pm d/2)=0$ (Fig. 2). But the picture of the flow around the obstacle as a whole is unusual, the flow is as if the fluid is completely devoid of friction: there is no viscosity, no boundary layers, no turbulence, no flow separation. In one word - “superfluidity” at room temperature (this is, of course, a joke)!
