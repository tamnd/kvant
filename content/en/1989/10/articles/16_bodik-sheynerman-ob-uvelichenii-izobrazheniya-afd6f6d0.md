About enlarging an image

V. A. BODIC, F. V. SHEINERMAN

One of the characteristics of the image of a luminous object in a lens (or in a more complex optical system) is its magnification. Longitudinal and transverse increases are reasonably distinguished. Let, for example, a lens give an image of an object in the form of a right angle ABC (Fig. 1). Then the longitudinal increase

$$\beta = \frac{f_B - f_A}{d_A - d_B},$$

and transverse -

$$\Gamma = \frac{B'C'}{BC} = \frac{H'}{H},$$

where $d$ is the distance from the luminous point to the lens, $f$ is the distance from the lens to the image of the point, $H$ is the transverse size of the luminous object, $H'$ is the transverse size of its image.

If an object is located perpendicular to the optical axis of a collecting lens and the lens creates its actual image (Fig. 2), then the transverse magnification

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{d - F} = \frac{f - F}{F}. \tag{1}$$

If a converging lens creates a mini-image (Fig. 3), then

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{F - d} = \frac{f + F}{F}. \tag{2}$$

In the case of a diffuse lens (Fig. 4)

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{F}{F + d} = \frac{F - f}{F}. \tag{3}$$

Let us show how the transverse magnification of a system of two thin lenses is equal. By definition $\Gamma = H'/H$. Multiply the numerator and denominator by $H_0$ -

$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} \frac{H_0}{H_0} = \frac{H_0}{H} \frac{H'}{H_0} = \Gamma_1 \Gamma_2,$$

Now let's look at a few specific tasks. Many of them are taken from entrance exam tickets of different years to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology.

Task 1. Using a thin lens, an image of an object with a fivefold magnification is obtained on the screen. Without changing the position of the lens, the screen was moved along the optical axis by $l=30$ cm. In order to obtain a sharp image, the object also had to be moved. In this case, the image turned out to be the second larger than the object. How far did the object move?

In both cases, the image of an object is real (since it is obtained on the screen), and it is created by a converging lens. Let's write down the lens formula for the first case -

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}$$

and for the second (Fig. 5) -

$$\frac{1}{d+x} + \frac{1}{f-l} = \frac{1}{F}.$$

Here we taught that the screen was moved towards the lens, and the object was moved away from it. Only under these conditions is the increase in the second case less than in the first.

From the written equations, taking into account the increase in $f/d=5$ and $(f-l)/(d+x)=3$, we obtain

$$\frac{5}{f} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{3}{f-l} + \frac{1}{f-l}.$$

From here

$$f=3l, \quad d=\frac{3}{5}l, \quad x=\frac{l}{15}=2\text{ см}.$$

If the screen were not moved closer to the lens, but moved away from it, then from the magnification

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F} \quad \text{и} \quad \frac{1}{d-x} + \frac{1}{f+l} = \frac{1}{F}$$

Problem 2. Two thin collecting lines with focal lengths $F_1$ and $F_2$ are pressed tightly against each other. On the main optical axis, a line perpendicular to it stands with height H. The distance from the line to the first line is $F_1$. Find the height of the image.
First of all, let's find the focal length of a system of two thin lines pressed tightly against each other. Let the light source be at the focus of the first line. Then the rays emerging from it will be parallel to the main optical axis and will converge at the focus of the second line. Applying the thin line formula to the system
$$\frac{1}{F_1} + \frac{1}{F_2} = \frac{1}{F},$$
we get the required focal length:
$$F = \frac{F_1 F_2}{F_1 + F_2}.$$
Using this formula, we find the lateral magnification of the candle:
$$\Gamma = \frac{H'}{H} = \frac{F}{F_1 - F} = \frac{F_2}{F_1},$$
where is the height of the image from?
$$H' = H \frac{F_2}{F_1}.$$
Problem 3. A flat mirror is pressed closely to a thin collecting face with a focal length F. Iso-

the broken object is at a distance of 2F from the line. What notice is the item depicted with?
Note that the rays projected onto the line, after being reflected from the mirror, pass through the line a second time. Since the line is thin and tightly pressed to the mirror, we replace the line-mirror system with a system of two pressed lines, the focal length of which is equal to F/2. For the line equivalent to this system we have
$$\Gamma = \frac{2F - F/2}{F/2} = 3.$$
Problem 4. The positive line creates an image of the Moon with a diameter of l. The same size image of the Moon is created by a negative line. What size will the image of the Moon be if these two lines are placed next to each other (with the negative line in front) at a distance equal to the focal length of the positive line?
Let α be the angular size of the Moon. Then the image of the Moon is in the focal plane line and has dimensions l = F_+ tan α in the positive line and l = |F_-| tan α in negative (Fig. 6). From here we get
$$F_+ = |F_-|.$$

Problem 5. Two converging thin lenses - L₁ with focal length F₁=5 cm and L₂ with focal length F₂=3 cm - are located one behind the other at a distance L so that their main optical axes coincide. An object is located in front of the lens L₁ at a distance d₁=15 cm. This system creates a direct magnification of the image of the object. At what L is this possible?

In order for the image of an object to be direct, it is necessary that L−f₁>F₂, where f₁ is the distance between the first lens and the image of the object in it. From here

$$L > F_2 + F_1 \frac{d_1}{d_1 - F_1} = 10,5 \text{ см.}$$

The second constraint on L is imposed by increasing:

$$\Gamma = \frac{F_1}{d_1 - F_1} \frac{F_2}{L - f_1 - F_2} > 1,$$

whence L<12 cm.

Finally

$$10,5 \text{ см} < L < 12 \text{ см.}$$

Problem 6. Using a thin lens, an image of the three-tube ABCEDG (Fig. 7) was obtained. The base of the trub AC lies on the main optical axis of the lens, AB=BC. Segment AB is depicted with magnification β₁=6, and segment BC with magnification β₂=3. At what magnification is segment BD displayed?

By definition

$$\beta_1 = \frac{f_A - f_B}{d_B - d_A}.$$

Let's express f_A and f_B from the lens formula:

$$f_A = F \frac{d_A}{d_A - F}, \quad f_B = F \frac{d_B}{d_B - F}.$$

Substituting the expressions for f_A and f_B into the formula for β₁, we obtain

$$\beta_1 = \frac{F}{d_A - F} \frac{F}{d_B - F} = \Gamma_{AG} \Gamma_{BD}.$$

Likewise,

$$\beta_2 = \Gamma_{BD} \Gamma_{CE},$$

$$\beta_3 = \Gamma_{AG} \Gamma_{CE},$$

where β₃ is the magnification with which the segment AC is drawn. Let us denote AB=BC=a, then

$$\beta_3 = \frac{\beta_1 a + \beta_2 a}{a + a} = \frac{\beta_1 + \beta_2}{2}.$$

Variable β₁ and β₂, taking into account the equality β₃=(\beta₁+\beta₂)/2 we obtain

$$\beta_1 \beta_2 = \Gamma_{BD}^2 \frac{\beta_1 + \beta_2}{2},$$

where

$$\Gamma_{BD} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \beta_1 \beta_2}{\beta_1 + \beta_2}} = 2.$$

Note that if, for example, AB→0, then β→Γ². Thus, the longitudinal and transverse magnification of an object are interconnected.

Exercises

1. The coin is examined at the moon. The apparent diameter of the coin is twice the true diameter. How did the thickness of the coin change?

2. Using a thin lens, an image of an object is obtained on the screen with a magnification of Γ₁=2. The object was moved by l=1 cm. In order to get a sharp image, the screen had to be moved. In this case, the increase turned out to be equal to Γ₂=4. How far will the screen be moved?

3. Using a thin lens, an image of an object is obtained on the screen with a fivefold magnification. The screen was moved by l=30 cm along the optical axis of the lens. With the lens position unchanged on the screen, an image of the same object was obtained with a threefold magnification. Determine the focal length.

4. Using a negative lens, an image of an object ABCD was obtained; segment AC lies on the main optical axis, AB=BC (Fig. 8). Segment CB is depicted with magnification β=0.3; segment BD - with an increase Γ=√3/8. Determine the magnification of segment AB.
