XV All-Russian Olympiad in Mathematics and Physics

Below are the conditions of the problem in mathematics and physics of the final stage of the All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren.
After forming a math problem, the maximum number of points awarded for complete solution of the problem is indicated in parentheses.

Mathematics

8th grade

First day
1. Find three different natural numbers forming an arithmetic progression whose product is a perfect square. (5)
2. Find the roots of the equation $x^2+px+q=0$ if they are known to be integers and $p+q=198$. (6)
3. Twelve numbers 1, 2, ..., 12 are written in each way around a circle. One move is allowed to rearrange two adjacent numbers if the model places them on the sides CD and CB respectively. Prove that these lines and line AC have a common point. (10)
4. The diagonals of a convex quadrilateral ABCD are mutually perpendicular. Straight lines are drawn through the midpoints of sides AB and AD, perpendicular to the opposite sides CD and CB, respectively. Prove that these lines and line AC have a common point. (10)
Second day
5. Prove that the number $4^{545}+545^4$ is composite. (7)
6. Give an example of a triangle that can be cut: a) into 12 equal triangles (4); b) into 5 equal triangles. (6)
7. On the bookshelf, in some order, are the books of the 20-volume collected works. Libraries wants to arrange these volumes in multi-order order - from 1st to 20th from left to right. In one example, libraries swap any volume that is out of place with the volume that occupies its place. Prove that the number of such operations required to organize the volumes does not depend on the sequence of actions of the library. (9)
8. Inside the circle there is a convex pentagon ABCDE with equal sides. Each side of the pentagon is extended until it intersects with the circle. The extensions of sides AB, BC, CD, DE, EA beyond points B, C, D, E, A are colored red, and the extensions of the same sides beyond points A, B, C, D, E are colored blue. Prove that the sum of the lengths of all red segments is equal to the sum of the lengths of all blue segments. (10)

9th grade
First day
1. Prove that the sum $1\cdot 2\cdot 3\cdots 2000\times 2001+2002\cdot 2003\cdots 4001\cdot 4002$ is divisible by 4003. (4)
2. In the squares of the board of size $9\times 9$ there are beetles, one in each cell. At a signal from each beetle, it crawls into one of the adjacent (diagonally) cells. In this case, each cell may contain several beetles, and some cells may not be eaten. Find the smallest possible number of unseedable cells. (6)
3. Point A is fixed on one of the sides of this angle. All possible circles are considered that touch this side at point A and intersect the other side at two points B and C (each circle in its own set of points, denoted by B and C). Prove that the centers of the inscribed circles of triangle ABC lie on the same straight line. (8)
4. Four numbers are written one after another on the board: 7956, 3923, 5857, 9725. The remainder of their sum divided by 10,000 is written after them. Then the first number is removed. The same thing continues with the remaining four numbers. And so on. Could there be four numbers on the board one day: 1989, 1989, 1989? (12)

Second day
5. Seven hexagonal cells (see Fig. 1, a) are painted in two colors: white and blue. In one move, you can choose an arbitrary cell and repaint it and all the cells adjacent to it in a different color. Prove that in a finite number of moves from the coloring indicated on figure 1, a,
a) you can get the coloring indicated at figure 1, b; (2)
b) it is impossible to obtain the coloring indicated on figure 1, p. (5)
6. Prove that for any values ​​of the variables x, y from the interval (0; 1) the following inequality holds:
$$x(1-y)+y(1-z)+z(1-x)<1.$$ (9)
7. There is a fixed arc BC on a circle. Consider all possible triangles ABC whose vertices A lie on the arc BC. For each such triangle ABC, an inscribed circle is constructed and a straight line is drawn through the tangent points of this circle on the sides AB and AC. Prove that all lines constructed in this way touch the same circle. (8)

10th grade

First day

1. Find the natural numbers $a_1 < a_2 < ... < a_{2n+1}$ forming an arithmetic progression such that their product is equal to the square of the natural number. (5)

2. The numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., $n$ are written around the circle in some order. You are allowed to swap places of any pair of adjacent numbers in one move if the model of their difference is greater than 1. Prove that in a finite number of moves all numbers can be arranged in a natural order. (5)

3. Points D and E are taken on sides AC and CB of the primangular triangle ABC, respectively. Prove that the bases of the perpendiculars dropped from vertex C onto lines DE, EA, AB and BD lie on the same circle. (10)

4. Let $a \ge 0$, $b \ge 0$, $c \ge 0$ and $a+b+c \le 3$. Prove the inequalities

$$\frac{a}{1+a^2} + \frac{b}{1+b^2} + \frac{c}{1+c^2} \le \frac{3}{2} \le \frac{1}{1+a} + \frac{1}{1+b} + \frac{1}{1+c}$$

(left inequality - 3 points; right inequality - 7 points).

Second day

5. Prove that for any value of $c$ the equation

$$x(x^2-1)(x^2-10)=c$$

cannot have a path of integer roots. (6)

6. On the chessboard there is a rook on the lower left square a1. In one move, the rook moves one square vertically or horizontally. Can a rook go around all the squares of the board, having been on some pole exactly 1 time, on another square exactly 2 times, on the third square exactly 3 times, ..., on the 64th square exactly 64 times, and:

a) return to the original field a1? (4)

b) will finish the route on square a2? (4)

(The initial position on field a1 is counted as a one-time “visit” to this field.)

7. Three chords of the circle $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ have a common point $K$ and intersect at an angle $60^\circ$ (Fig. 2). Prove that

$$KA+KB+KC=KA_1+KB_1+KC_1.$$ (10)

8. Can a cube with edge 1 accommodate three right-hand disjoint tetrahedra with edge 1? (Tetrahedrons are allowed to touch their boundaries.) (12)

Physics

Theoretical tour

8th grade

1. At some point in time, from a height $H$, a small spring ball is dropped on the surface of a wedge with an angle $a$ at the base (Fig. 3). At the same moment, the wedge begins to move in the horizontal direction with constant acceleration. What is the time interval between two successive impacts of the ball on the surface of the wedge, if it is known that these impacts occurred at the same point on the surface?

2. The pig and algorithm weights are balanced in the air on unequal-armed lever scales. If you swap the weights and lower them into water, the balance of the scales will not be disturbed. Determine the ledev length ratio. Lead density $Q_1 = 11,3 \text{ г/cm}^3$, algorithm density $Q_2 = 2,7 \text{ г/cm}^3$. Neglect the density of air compared to the density of water. The balance beam is considered very light.

3. The end of a piece of steel wire weighing $m=20$ g is heated in the flame of a gas burner. Dependence of wire temperature on distance $x$ (in fractions of wire length $l$)

9th grade

1. A massive coil (Fig. 5) is turned on without piercing at a constant speed on an inclined plane with an angle $\alpha=30^\circ$, acting on the end $A$ of a thread wound on a small cylinder of the coil, with a constant force twice the gravity force of the coil. It is known that in this case, section $AB$ of the thread always occupies a horizontal position. At what speed does the reel roll if the speed of the end $A$ of the thread is $v=0,1$ m/s?

2. In a heat-conducting cylinder under a weightless piston there is saturated water vapor, the mass of which is $m$. External pressure is normal. An amount of water at a temperature of $t_0$ was also introduced into the cylinder. Neglecting the heat of the cylinder and the friction of the piston, find the work done by the surface pressure. What table quantities are needed in order to solve the problem numerically if the mass $m$ and temperature $t_0$ are known?

3. Two infinite parallel half-planes are charged uniformly with the same absolute charge, but opposite in sign, surface charge density (Fig. 6). It is known that the direction of the field at point $A$, located in autumn far from the edge of the plates $(l \gg h)$, is equal to $E_0$. What is the direction of the field at point $B$, located at the edge of the plates, if it is known that the merging field line passing through point $B$ makes an angle $\alpha$ with the plane of the plates?

4. A constant voltage of $U=7$ V is applied to a circuit consisting of a light bulb and an ammeter connected in series. The ammeter shows a current of $I_1=120$ mA. After shunting the ammeter with a resistor, its reading became $I_2=50$ mA. Find the resistance of the resistor. The current-voltage characteristic of the light bulb is shown in figure 7.

10th grade

1. A trolley with a mass $M$ is driven by a rigidity spring $k$, which at one end is mounted on a bracket, rigidly connected to the trolley, and at the other is connected to a thread wound on a small cylinder with a radius $r$ of the drive wheel (Fig. 8). In the initial position the spring is calculated at values ​​$\Delta l_0$. Find the acceleration of the cart immediately after it is released. Assume that the wheels of the cart are not removable and will not puncture the floor. The major radius of the wheel is $R$.

2. Find the maximum possible efficiency of the water mill (Fig. 9). Assume that the plane of the water wheel poppets turns out to be perpendicular to the stream of water supplying them.

3. At the initial moment in the circuit (Fig. 10), the switch is open and the capacitor is not charged. The switch is periodically closed for a time of $t=8$ ms and opened for the same time. What will be the direction to the capacitor after the first “close-open” cycle? After the fourth? What is this direction after a sufficiently long time if the insulation resistance of the capacitor is $R=5$ MOhm? All other elements of the circuit are counted
