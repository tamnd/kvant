In the mid-60s in England, the Education Group at the Institute of Physics and Physical Social Testing experimented, the purpose of which was to compare the American and English experiments in physics at an advanced level. In this experiment, 600 English schoolchildren took not only their own exam, but also an example of an American exam of the same difficulty, the text of which was compiled at Princeton University.

In England, passing the Advanced Certificate exams gives students the right to go to university. In the United States, a relatively small group of students admitted to some universities will take the advanced physics exam. The results of the exams determine whether a first-year student needs to take the entire preparatory course in physics or only part of it.

The pilot exam consists of two parts. The first part includes 44 questions that must be answered within one hour using the American examination technical "multiple-choice" type. For each question, the machine offered 5 different answers, from which you had to choose the correct one. The second part is more similar to a traditional English exam at an advanced level. It took two hours to solve ten problems. Different numbers of points were given for correct solutions to different problems. Tasks 1 and 2 (you had to solve one of these problems by choice) were worth 25% of the total points, task 3 was given 20%, task 10 - 25%, and questions 4-9, which required short answers, were worth 30% together.

Below is the text of the exam, or more precisely, its second part. Try your hand at solving these problems.

1. The internal check of a cylinder of radius 1.5 m is smooth, except for a small section BC (Fig. 1). A body of mass 4 kg is held in position A by a thread that is perpendicular to the radius of the cylinder passing through the center of the body. a) Indicate the forces acting on the body in position A.

The thread is removed and the body slides down, reaching position E, which is the turning point. 6) What forces act on the body in position D? c) Determine the energy loss in section BC. d) What is the reaction force of the cylinder on the body in position D?

2. A small body of mass m is held in a liquid at rest. At time t = 0 the body moves away. Assuming that the drag force of the flowing fluid is proportional to the speed of the body, obtain the time dependence of one of three quantities: a) position, b) velocity, b) acceleration of the body, and explain how to determine the other two quantities. The position of the body at time t = 0 is taken as the beginning of the report.

6. A boy sits on a chair rotating at an angular velocity of $\omega$ and holds two identical dumbbells on his outstretched arms. At some point in time, the boy drops the dumbbells without lowering his arms. a) What will happen to the angular velocity of rotation? b) Is the moment of impulse of the system preserved? Explain.

7. Two spectroscopes $A$ and $B$ are used when studying the same light source. The dispersion element in $A$ is a diffraction grating, in $B$ it is recognized. Describe three differences in the spectra observed by instruments $A$ and $B$.

8. The solid ball rolls down first along the inclined plane $A$, and then along the inclined plane $B$ (Fig. 5). Will it take longer to roll down one slope than another? Explain. Consider that the ball begins to move from a state of rest and that there is no slipping.

10. This requires you to think and apply your knowledge to solve the problem. Your imagination and ability to scientifically substantiate your idea will also influence the final grade.

Iron wiring about 2 m long hangs on two vertical supports. The ends of the wires are connected to the electrical network, so that the current in the wire can be increased at will from 0 to a higher value. a) List five or more wiring parameters that will change as the current increases. b) Choose any three of these quantities that can be changed independently of each other.
