⟦folio 23⟧

Sweeping. Below, for brevity, we omit the words “local”. In proving the formulated “reason for the extremal” (i.e., minimum or maximum) path for billiard trajectories, we will use the common convention of studying maximum and minimum problems; its description in the general case can be found in the book by N.B. Vasilyev and V.L. Gutenmacher “Straights and Curves”, § 7, or in the article by V.G. Boltyansky and I.M. Yaglom “Geometric problems for maximum and minimum” in volume V of the “Encyclopedia of Elementary Mathematics”.

Proof of Theorem 2. For each number $c > |AB|$, consider the set $\Gamma_c$, consisting of those points $M$ for which $|AM| + |MB| = c$. As is known (see, for example, the above-mentioned book “Straights and Curves”, § 5, or the article by I. N. Bronstein “Ellipse”, «Kvant» No. 1 for 1975), the set $\Gamma_c$ is an ellipse with formation points $A$ and $B$; they form a family of confocal ellipses $\{\Gamma_c\}$. It is easy to see that if the broken line $APB$ is minimal or maximum, then the point $P$ is the point of tangency of the curve $\Gamma$ with one of the ellipses of the family $\{\Gamma_c\}$ - prove this using figure 10, a and b. (Note that not all points of tangency give a maximum or minimum - see Fig. 10, c.) At such a point $P$ the curve $\Gamma$ and the ellipse $\Gamma_c$ tangent to it have a common tangent $MN$. According to the optical property of the ellipse *), the segments $PA$ and $PB$ connecting the point $P$ with the shapes of the ellipse $\Gamma_c$ form equal angles with the tangent $MN$ to $\Gamma_c$ at the point $P$ (Fig. 11). Since straight line $MN$ is also tangent to curve $\Gamma$ at point $P$, broken line $APB$ is a billiard trajectory going from $A$ to $B$ after reflection from $\Gamma$ at point $P!$ Theorem 2 proven.

Appeal to priority readers. If our reasoning seemed too lax to you - for example, due to the lack of precise definitions of $P$.

*) See, for example, the article by V. G. Boltyansky in «Kvant» No. 12 for 1975.
