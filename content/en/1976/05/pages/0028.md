Let us now consider points of the sequence $P_n$ with numbers $n, n+l, n+2l, \dots$ (Fig. 14). They form a subsequence $\{P_n, P_{n+l}, P_{n+2l}, P_{n+3l}, \dots\}$, each subsequent point of which $P_{n+(r+1)l}$ is obtained from the previous point $P_{n+rl}$ by rotating through an angle $l\alpha$, i.e., in exactly the same way as point $P_{n+l}=P_m$ is obtained from point $P_n$. Thus, neighboring points of the selected subsequence are separated from each other by an arc of radian measure $\delta$. Since $\delta < \varepsilon$, at least one of the points $P_n, P_{n+l}, P_{n+2l}, \dots$ must fall on the arc $\Delta$. Thus, Jacobi's theorem is proven.

(B) DERIVATION OF THEOREM 1 FROM Jacobi’s THEOREM.

Let $\alpha$ be a number incommensurable with $\pi$, $\Gamma_1$ be a circle that is touched by the links of each billiard trajectory corresponding to angle $\alpha$ inside the circle $\Gamma$. Let $K$ be the ring between $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma$, and $D$ be an arbitrary circle inside the ring $K$. Let us draw through its center a chord $MN$ of the circle $\Gamma$ tangent to the circle $\Gamma_1$ (Fig. 15). Obviously, if we take a sufficiently small arc $\Delta$ of the circle $\Gamma$ with the midpoint at point $M$, then for any other point $M'$ of the arc $\Delta$ the tangent to the circle $\Gamma_1$ drawn from the point $M'$ on the same side of $\Gamma_1$ as $MN$, necessarily intersects the circle $D$ (explain!).

Let us now consider any trajectory $P_0 P_1 P_2 \dots$ of the billiard inside the circle $\Gamma$ corresponding to the angle $\alpha$. We need to prove that at least one of its links intersects the circle $D$. According to Jacobi's theorem, the points $P_0, P_1, P_2, \dots$ densely fill the circle $\Gamma$, and therefore at least one of them, say $P_n$, lies on the arc $\Delta$ chosen above. Due to the choice of this arc $\Delta$, one of the two links of the trajectory leaving the point $P_n$ necessarily intersects the circle $D$. Thus, Theorem 1 is proven.

In addition to Theorem 1, Jacobi's theorem has many other interesting consequences*). We formulate two of them as problems.

Task 16. Prove that if the number $d$ of the player, then for any number $a$ the sequence of fractional parts of the terms of the arithmetic progression $a, a+d, a+2d, \dots$, i.e. the sequence of numbers $a_n={a+nd}$, densely fills the segment $0 \leq x \leq 1$ on the number axis everywhere. ($\{x\} = x-[x]$).

Task 17. Prove that if the decimal logarithm of the number $c$ is playable, then the decimal notation of the natural powers $c$, i.e. the numbers $c, c^2, c^3, \dots, c^n, \dots$, can begin with any predetermined set of digits. (For example, there is a natural power of the number 17 that begins with seventeen consecutive sets of digits 1976!)

*) Some applications of Jacobi’s theorem can be found in the article by A. A. Egorov “Lattices and regular polygons”. «Kvant», 1974, No. 12.
