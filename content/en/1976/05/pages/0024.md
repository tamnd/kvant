§ 3. Extremal properties of billiard trajectories

The method of constructing a billiard trajectory leading from one given point A to another given point B after one reflection from a straight side MN follows from the law of elastic reflection: move point B′, symmetrically to point B relative to straight line MN, and connect A to B′ (Fig. 9); the point P of the intersection of straight lines AB′ and MN will be the point of collision of the ball with the side MN, and the broken line APB is the desired trajectory (explain). From the construction it is very clear that the billiard trajectory APB is the shortest of all broken lines of the form AP′B with point P′ on the straight line MN leading from A to B! (Give a detailed proof.) This statement is part and the simplest case of the fundamental principle of classical mechanics - the principle of least action (in optics it is called Fermat's principle)*. In our situation, this principle can be formulated as follows: the billiard ball is “smart” and chooses the shortest of all possible paths. So far, we have established the “principle of the shortest path” only in the case of one reflection from a straight side. In problems 5-7 it is proposed to check it in cases where the ball is reflected not once, but several times, but from straight sides.
