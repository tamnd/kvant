⟦folio 67⟧

In the mid-60s in England, the Education Group at the Institute of Physics and Physical Social Testing experimented, the purpose of which was to compare the American and English experiments in physics at an advanced level. In this experiment, 600 English schoolchildren took not only their own exam, but also an example of an American exam of the same difficulty, the text of which was compiled at Princeton University.

In England, passing the Advanced Certificate exams gives students the right to go to university. In the United States, a relatively small group of students admitted to some universities will take the advanced physics exam. The results of the exams determine whether a first-year student needs to take the entire preparatory course in physics or only part of it.

The pilot exam consists of two parts. The first part includes 44 questions that must be answered within one hour using the American examination technical "multiple-choice" type. For each question, the machine offered 5 different answers, from which you had to choose the correct one. The second part is more similar to a traditional English exam at an advanced level. It took two hours to solve ten problems. Different numbers of points were given for correct solutions to different problems. Tasks 1 and 2 (you had to solve one of these problems by choice) were worth 25% of the total points, task 3 was given 20%, task 10 - 25%, and questions 4-9, which required short answers, were worth 30% together.

Below is the text of the exam, or more precisely, its second part. Try your hand at solving these problems.

The position of the body at time t = 0 is taken as the beginning of the report.
