(A) Jacobi's theorem.
Let us recall how it is formulated.

Let $\alpha$ be a number incommensurable with $\pi$, $\{P_0, P_1, P_2, \dots\} = \{P_k\}$ be a sequence of mokas of the circle $\Gamma$, implying that each subsequent moka sequentially $P_{k+1}$ is obtained from the previous moka $P_k$ by repeating by an angle $\alpha$ radians. Then for any arc $\Delta$ of the circle $\Gamma$ at least one moka sequentially $\{P_k\}$ lies on this arc $\Delta$.

Proof. Let $\Delta$ be an arbitrary arc and $\varepsilon$ its radian measure. Let us choose a natural number $N$ such that $2\pi/N < \varepsilon$. Dimension of the circle $\Gamma$ into $N$ equal in length to the arc $\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \dots, \Delta_N$; the radian measure of each of them is $2\pi/N$ and less than $\varepsilon$. Let's consider the first $N+1$ points of the sequence $\{P_k\}$, i.e. points $P_0, P_1, P_2, \dots, P_N$. According to the Dirichlet principle, at least one of the $N$ arcs $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_N$ contains at least one of these points. Let, for example, the points $P_n$ and $P_m$, where $m=n+1>n$, lie on the arc $\Delta_i$. Since $\alpha$ is incommensurable with $\pi$, then $P_m \neq P_n$ (explain!). Let the radian measure of the arc $P_nP_m$ be equal to $\delta$; then $\delta < 2\pi/N$ and even more so $\delta < \varepsilon$.
