⟦folio 2⟧

In the center of Oslo, in the Royal Park, there is a monument to the outstanding Norwegian mathematician Niels Henrik Abel. But Abel himself erected a more impressive monument to himself. Abel's theorems, Abelian integrals, Abelian equations, Abelian groups, Abelian transformations have forever entered various branches of mathematics, and Abel obtained all his results in seven years of creativity: he died when he was not yet twenty-seven years old.

Abel was born on August 5, 1802 in the small village of Finneina in the south of Norway, in the family of a pastor. Niels was the second son; He is a rude sick boy.

In the fall of 1815, Pastor Abel sent his two eldest sons to Christanya (as Oslo was then called), to the Cathedral School. Until then, he taught them himself.

The school did not live up to Nils' expectations. Back in 1811, the best teachers left for the newly organized university. The new teachers were not very knowledgeable. They often beat students. The mathematics teacher Bader was especially cruel. It is not surprising that Nils soon lost interest in his studies and stopped getting excellent grades even in mathematics. Only visits to the theater, which Abel loved dearly, playing chess and conversations with friends brightened the teenager’s life.

Everything changed one day in 1818. Bader beat one student so badly that he died a few days later. Bader was fired, and a new mathematics teacher, Bernt Mikel Holmboe, joined the school. Holmboe himself did almost nothing in mathematics, so he was the first to notice and nurture the mathematical talent of Niels Abel.
