On the vertical axis (up from point O) we will plot the path taken by the ship, and on the horizontal we will mark the days of the week (this axis is called the time axis, Mon means Monday, Tue means Tuesday, etc.).

What will the ship traffic look like on the chart? At noon on Friday the ship will be 700 km from Liverpool. We place point A at the intersection of the vertical passing through Friday noon and the horizontal and souls at the height of “700”. By Saturday afternoon the ship will have covered 1,400 km. On figure 1 this is represented by point B. Points C, D, etc. are constructed similarly. They show the position of the vessel in the subsequent length (at noon). The ship is moving uniformly, and therefore all these points will lie on the same straight line. By the way, each point on this line depicts the position of the ship at a certain point in time. For example, point R shows that the ship will be at a distance of 2450 km when Big Ben is full and Sunday begins in Liverpool.

The traffic schedule for ships leaving Rio de Janeiro on Mondays is constructed in the same way.

Now let’s depict the movements of all ships on one graph (see Fig. 2). Then each point of intersection of two straight lines will correspond to the meeting of two ships.

Consider, for example, a KL flight from Liverpool to Rio de Janeiro. This segment intersects the segments depicting the movements of oncoming ships 4 times. This means that the answer to the first question of the problem is 4 ships.

Further, from the graph it is clear that before the first meeting at point M, the same amount of time will pass as it will take the oncoming ship to sail from the meeting point to Liverpool. It follows that the first meeting will take place at noon on Saturday, the second - at full time from Tuesday to Wednesday, etc., and the distances from the meeting place to Liverpool will be 1400 km, 3850 km, etc.

The hands go around the dial

At exactly 12 o'clock in the afternoon, the minute and hour hands of the clock coincide. Then the minute hand rushes forward and after a while, having gone around the hour hand in a complete circle, it returns to it again. At what point will this happen? At what moments between 12 o'clock in the afternoon and 12 o'clock at night do the arrows turn: a) turn the corner; b) right angle; b) an angle of 120°?

Gentlemen on a walk

Two gentlemen simultaneously went for a walk along a 100-meter-long alley. Mr. Smith walks 1 kilometer in an hour, Mr. Jones walks indoors - only 600 meters per hour. Having reached the end of the alley, each of them turns and goes back at the same speed. When meeting, gentlemen each time sigmoid their hats and bowed. How many times do they bow during the first 25 minutes of the walk? How many minutes (out of these 25) did they walk in one direction?

Isaac Newton's problem

Two postmen A and B, who share a distance of 59 miles, leave in the morning to meet each other. A travels 7 miles in 2 hours, and B travels 8 miles in 3 hours, while B sets off an hour later than A. Find how many miles A will travel before meeting B.
