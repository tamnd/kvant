Problem 8. The statement inverse to the extremal path principle states: the billiard trajectory APB going from point A to point B after reflection from curve G is either minimal or maximal. Give an example showing that for small curves Г this statement is not true. Will it be true for great curves Г (that is, for curves lying entirely on one side of any of their tangents)? Prove this “inverse principle” in the case when the curve G is convex and faces points A and B with its convexity (as at figure 10, a).

Let us now apply the principles of extremal path to Birkii’s theorem mentioned in §2:

if Q is a convex region bounded by a curve Γ without a break point, then for arbitrary n≥3 there is at least one periodic billiard trajectory in the region Q, consisting of exactly n links.

Fig. 12.

Proof. Let us consider all possible non-self-intersecting closed n-link broken lines P_1P_2...P_nP_1 inscribed in the domain Q (that is, with vertices P_i on the curve Γ). Let's choose the longest broken line among them (think about why such a broken line exists?).

Problem 9. Using the extremal path principle, prove that this longest broken line is a billiard trajectory in the domain Q.

From Problem 9 it follows that the “longest” broken line P_1P_2...P_nP_1 will be a closed periodic trajectory, and Birkoff’s theorem is proven (we do not want to deprive the reader of the satisfaction of solving Problem 9 on his own).
