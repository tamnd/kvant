⟦folio 31⟧

We agreed to neglect the direct interaction of conduction electrons with the units of the crystal lattice. However, in the process of braking, forces come into play that will still slow down the electrons. These are the forces of the vortex electric field.

When a current occurs in the ring, a magnetic field is induced. Because the current varies, this field is not constant. Consequently, it generates an eddy electric field in the metal of the ring. The forces acting on the charges from the vortex field prevent the change in current in the ring, i.e., they tend to reduce the relative speed of electrons and ions (see (8)). Consequently, these forces slow down the electrons and accelerate the ions of the crystal lattice. The work of these forces to move a single positive charge along the ring is represented by e. d.s. induction (recall that the work of an eddy field on a closed path is not zero).

In order to more clearly imagine the dynamics of the movement of the crystal lattice and conduction electrons (electron gas), it is convenient to mentally separate them and consider them separately. On figure 5, a, all the movements of the crystal lattice related to the diamnic are shown separately (blue ring), and on figure 5, b - all the movements of the electron gas related to the diamnic (red ring). Now we can easily write down Newton's law as applied to the crystal lattice and electrons. Let the masses of the blue and red rings be equal to the corresponding $M$ and $m$, and their accelerations $a_i$ and $a_e$. Then

$$Ma_i = QE - F, \quad (9)$$

$$ma_e = -QE,$$

where $E$ is the intense vortex electric field. Let us calculate the value of this tension.

Since the work done by the vortex field forces to move a single charge along the ring is equal to e. d.s. self-induction, i.e. $El = \mathcal{E}_{is}$, then

$$E = \frac{\mathcal{E}_{is}}{l} = -\frac{1}{l} \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = -\frac{L}{l} \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t} = -\frac{QL}{l^2} \left( \frac{\Delta v_i}{\Delta t} - \frac{\Delta v_e}{\Delta t} \right) = -\frac{QL}{l^2} \left( a_i - a_e \right).$$

Thus, equation (9) of the motion of the crystal lattice and electron gas will take the following:

$$Ma_i = -\mu \left( a_i - a_e \right) - F, \quad (10)$$

where for brevity we introduce the notation $\mu = \frac{Q^2 L}{l^2}$. Solving system (10) with respect to $a_i$ and $a_e$, we find the braking acceleration of our rings:

$$a_i = -\frac{F}{M + \frac{m\mu}{m + \mu}}; \quad a_e = \frac{\mu}{m + \mu} a_i. \quad (11)$$
