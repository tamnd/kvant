Using the problem just solved, it is not difficult to prove the validity of Simpson's formula, which is used to calculate the volume of polytopes of a special type.

Given a polyhedron, all the vertices are in two parallel planes, located at a distance of $h$ from each other. Let $S_1$ be the area of ​​a facet in one plane, $S_2$ be the area of ​​a face located in the second plane, $a$ $S_{cp}$ be the cross-sectional area of ​​a polyhedron to a plane located at a distance of $\frac{h}{2}$ from each of these planes. Then the formula for the volume of the polyhedron is valid:

$$V = \frac{h}{6}(S_1 + S_2 + 4S_{cp}).$$

To prove, check that this formula is correct if the polyhedron is a tetrahedron, and then split an arbitrary polyhedron, the vertices of which are located in two parallel planes, into tetrahedra with vertices in the same planes: the areas of the faces of tetrahedra located in one plane will add up to $S_1$, those located in the second plane will add up to $S_2$, and the sum The product of cross sections of tetrahedra is equal to $S_{cp}$ polyhedron.
