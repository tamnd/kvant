To the article “Pop-up air bullet and Archimedes’ law”
1. From the law of conservation of energy
$$\frac{m + m'}{2} v_1^2 = \frac{m + m'}{2} v_2^2,$$
where $m = V\rho_0$ (V is the volume of the rod),
$m'_1 = V\rho \frac{r^2}{R_1^2 - r^2}$ (ρ – water density),
$m'_2 = V\rho \frac{r^2}{R_2^2 - r^2}$, we find
$$v_2 = v_1 \sqrt{\frac{\rho_0(R_1^2 - r^2) + \rho r^2}{\rho_0(R_2^2 - r^2) + \rho r^2}}.$$
2. $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m + 2/3\pi R^3\rho}{\pi r^2\rho g}}$. (For the solution to the problem, see, for example, «Kvant», 1974, No. 6, p. 36. You just need to replace m with $m + m' = m + \frac{1}{2}V\dot{\rho}$.)
3. Let's write down Newton's second law for the rod, for the mass $m_1 = \pi(R^2 - r^2)l\rho$ of water moving between the rod and the walls of the tube, and for the remaining, stationary water mass $m_2 = \pi R^2(L - l)\rho$:

$$ma = F_{BbT1} + F_{BbT2} - mg,$$
$$-m_1a_1 = -m_1g - F_{BbT1} - F_1,$$
$$0 = -m_2g - F_{BbT2} + F_1 + F.$$

Here $F_{BbT1}(F_{BbT2})$ is the force with which moving (motionless) water acts on the rod, $F_1$ is the force of interaction between moving and motionless water, $F$ is the force with which the bottom of the tube acts on motionless water. Having added all three equations, we eliminate the forces $F_{BbT1}, F_{BbT2}$ and $F_1$. Note that $\frac{a_1}{a} = \frac{v_1}{v} = \frac{r^2}{R^2 - r^2}$. Then after simple transformations we get:

$$F = (m + m_1 + m_2)g - a(V\rho - m) = \pi(R^2L - r^2l)\rho g + mg - \frac{(V\rho - m)^2}{m + \frac{V\rho r^2}{R^2 - r^2}}.$$

This shows that the force of water pressure on the bottom (numerically equal to the force F) is less than the total weight of the water and the rod. Moreover, this is true both when the rod floats ($V\rho > m$) and when it sinks ($m > V\rho$).
