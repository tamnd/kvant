It is enough to go beyond the boundaries of this area or find yourself beyond it at the beginning of the period, and the achievement of a global extremum becomes difficult to guarantee. The chances of hitting the local (*local*) extreme increase.

There are many ways to combat multi-extreme behavior, but none of them guarantee success. These methods are of a non-rigorous, as mathematicians say, *heuristic* nature. Proponents of three different multi-extreme methods met in the Skeptics group. We do not cite them specifically: the front of scientific research in this area has recently expanded so much and has increasingly entered into the fight against multi-extremeness that the indicated methods are difficult to associate with someone’s specific names.

Some tried to jump out of the extremum if they were already in it, and by the success or slowness of these attempts they judged the achievement of the global extremum. Others attempted to descend from several randomly selected points. Finally, others (the height of skepticism!) came to the conclusion that the wisest thing to do was not to direct the mind, but to simply fight around the island, looking at the altimeter.

All three of these courses of action find adherents among scientists, although the third is still less common. Special programs for computers have been compiled that implement each of these methods and many others. Nevertheless, not only inveterate skeptics, but also cheerful optimists do not risk today considering the problem solved. And the first impulse when analyzing any function for an extremum is an attempt to prove its uniqueness. Often this achievement is revered no less than the correctness of the mathematical model in general. For example, the absence of local extrema in linear programming problems is an important argument for trying linear models, outweighing many of their shortcomings.

There are all kinds of sophisticated ways to combine different methods. For example, all three skeptics could be united into one team. You say there is little kinship between them? And you don’t need much. It is enough that they have little faith in their own methods. You can send them to search for a lowland in a general group of three people, deciding that at every step of the pope the one on which the lot will fall will act. Let's say all three will pull rolled up pieces of paper from a hat, one of which has a cross on it. And, imagine, there are reasons for attempting such a course of action.
