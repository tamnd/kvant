⟦folio 40⟧

Physics is an experimental science. Every physical study begins with observation and study of a phenomenon and ends with a comparative theory with experimental results.

Many laws and natural phenomena are not so complex at their core, and their characteristic features can be traced even in relatively simple experiments. Galileo discovered the laws of falling bodies by watching them fall from the top of the Leaning Tower of Pisa. Newton discovered that sunlight was a complex mixture of different colors using a simple glass detector. Many of Faraday’s experiments were amazingly simple, but incredibly deep in content and conclusions.

Of course, today there is very little hope that with the means at hand one can discover a new law of nature. Physics these days requires very complex experimental installations - charged particle accelerators, atomic reactors, electron and proton microscopes, etc. But much of what science already knows can be tested in simple home experiments. The movement of magnets, crystal growth, diffraction and interference of waves, surface tension or dry friction - all this can be studied with simple improvised means. Wooden cubes or balls, a piece of paper, a sewing needle, a soap bubble or a stream of water from an open tap, not to mention a compass and a flashlight, can all be used for a variety of interesting physics experiments. Such experiments are the first step to independent research. They help to gain a deeper insight into the essence of various physical phenomena, develop observation and communication skills.

The section “Kwana Laboratory” belongs to the systematic sections of our magazine. In it we strive to give our readers the opportunity to carry out simple but powerfully educational experiments that do not require special laboratory equipment.

From the letters of our readers, we see that many of them willingly carry out the experiments we propose and even strive to creatively visualize them (vary the conditions - change the temperature, for example, or other physical quantities involved in the experiment, select other substances and materials, etc.). At the same time, they have many unexpected and interesting questions, from which they contact our editors.

When reproducing the experiments we describe, it is necessary to pay special attention to technical precautions and strictly follow the recommendations in the articles.

We hope that “Kwana Laboratory” will help our readers better understand their abilities and develop the skills of future nature researchers.
