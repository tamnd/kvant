Do transcendental numbers exist?

The question naturally arises: are there transcendental numbers at all?

A positive answer to it was given in 1844 by the French mathematician Lie Houville (1809-1882). In 1873, Hermite proved the transcendence of the number $e$, in 1882 Lindeman - the transcendence of the number $j$. In 1900, at the PM International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris, D. Hilbert (1860-1940), among the 23 problems he posed and became famous, formulated the following problems: are numbers of the form $\alpha^\beta$ transcendental, where $\alpha$ is an algebraic number described from zero to one, and $\beta$ is an irrational algebraic number?

A positive answer to this question was given in 1934 by the outstanding Soviet mathematician A. O. Gelfond (1906-1968).

Liouville results

In the future, we will propose that algebraic and transcendental numbers are real, although everything remains true for complex numbers (see exercises at the end of the article).

Theorem 1. Let $\alpha$ be an irrational number that is the root of the equation

$$f(z) \equiv a_0 z^n + a_1 z^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0.$$ (2)

Using the coefficients of the equation and an arbitrary $A > |\alpha|$, one can find a number $M$ such that for any fraction $\frac{p}{q}$, $\left|\frac{p}{q}\right| \leqslant A$, is not a root of equation (2), the inequality is true

$$\left|\alpha - \frac{p}{q}\right| \geqslant \frac{1}{M q^n}.$$ (3)

Proof. According to the conditions of the theorem we have:

$$f(\alpha) = a_0 \alpha^n + a_1 \alpha^{n-1} + \dots + a_{n-1} \alpha + a_n = 0,$$

$$f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = a_0 \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^n - \alpha^n + \dots + a_{n-1} \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0.$$

From here

$$f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) - f(\alpha) = a_0 \left[ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^n - \alpha^n \right] + a_1 \left[ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{n-1} - \alpha^{n-1} \right] + \dots + a_{n-1} \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{n-1} + \dots + a_n = 0.$$

$$\times \left\{ a_0 \left[ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^n - \alpha^n \right] + \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{n-2} \alpha + \dots + \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{n-2} + \alpha^{n-1} \right] + \alpha_1 \left[ \left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^n - \alpha^n \right] + \dots + a_n = 0.$$

Since $|\alpha| < A$, $\left|\frac{p}{q}\right| \leqslant A$, then

$$f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) \leqslant \left|\frac{p}{q}\right| - \alpha |M|,$$ (4)

Where

$$M = |a_0| n A^{n-1} + |a_1| \times (n-1) A^{n-2} + \dots + |a_{n-1}|.$$ (5)

But

$$\left| f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\right| = \frac{\left| a_0 p^n + a_1 p^{n-1} q + \dots + a_n q^n \right|}{q^n}.$$ (6)

The numerator of the fraction (6) $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n, p, q$ are integers, so the numerator (6) is an integer, and since $f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) \neq 0$, it is not less than one.

Hence,

$$\left| f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\right| \geqslant \frac{1}{q^n}.$$ (7)

From (4) and (7) follows (3). The theorem has been proven.
