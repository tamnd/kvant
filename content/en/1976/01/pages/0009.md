A hexagonal prism has 12 vertices, 8 faces and 18 edges, and the bipyramid obtained from it has 8 vertices, 12 faces and 18 edges. Thus, the numbers of vertices and faces of a prism and a bipyramid are, as it were, “rearranged,” but the number of edges remains unchanged. Make sure that the same correspondence is observed for the remaining pairs of polyhedron distances. The established pattern follows from the principle of duality, which consists of the following: for any sentence regarding lobes, straight lines and planes, it is always possible to formulate a sentence dual to it by replacing the words “lobe” with the word “plane”, and the word “plane” with the word “lobe”, leaving the words “straight” unchanged.

The principle of duality applies not only to polyhedra: it extends to all figures and statements that are considered in projective geometry. This principle is justified there, but we will not prove it here.

It was indicated above that there are 14 equilateral half-right polyhedra. Each of them corresponds to a certain equilateral semi-right polyhedron dual to it. On figure 11 you see three Archimedean solids (in the left column) depicted together with their equilateral half-right “doubles” (in the right column). Readers can familiarize themselves with a complete list of all these polyhedra in the article by V. G. Ashkinuse, published in the Encyclopedia of Elementary Mathematics, book four (M., “Nauka”, 1963).

Check whether there is an equilateral semi-right polyhedron corresponding to the body found by Ashkinuse. Think about what properties of equilateral semi-right polyhedra ensure the existence of “dual” or equilateral semi-right polyhedra.

Exercises

1. What is an antiprism, the bases of which are regular triangles? Determine its dual polyhedron.

2. Calculate the volume of an antiprism with a hexagonal base (Fig. 1), assuming that the edge length is $a^*$).

3. The polyhedron shown at figure 9 and 10 can be obtained in another way. It is enough to place a regular triangular paramid on each face of a regular tetrahedron. Calculate the height of this paramid and the plane angle at its vertex if the edge of the tetrahedron is $a$.

4. Under the conditions of Exercise 3, calculate the volume of an equilateral polyhedron.

5. Calculate the plane angle $\psi$ at the vertex of the face of the polyhedron shown in figure 7 and construct its development.

6. By cutting off the corners of a regular octahedron, construct a semi-right polyhedron with 8 faces - regular hexagons and 6 faces - squares, and find its volume.

7. Determine the polyhedron dual to the truncated ocmahedra (Exercise 6). Find the plane angle $\varphi$ at the vertex of its face.
