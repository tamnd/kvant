Completing the tetrahedron

One of the beautiful prims that can be used in solving geometric problems is to replace the geometric figure being studied with another, in some sense more convenient. So, for example, if the conditions of the problem include a triangle in which the median is drawn, it is often useful to construct this triangle to the parallelgram, extending the median to a distance equal to itself. In this country, several problems are being considered about a triangular figure - a tetrahedron, which it turns out to be possible to solve quite simply.

To sew, just tetrahedron to another polyhedron (usually a parallelepiped).

The first method of constructing a parallelepiped is depicted in figure 1. Here $AA_1BD$ is this paramid, and the planes of the faces $DCC_1D_1$, $CBB_1C_1$ and $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ of the parallelepiped pass through one vertex of the paramid and are parallel to the face of the paramid opposite this vertex.

Problem 1. (Item a) of the problem was given at the entrance exam at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University.) Given a triangular paramide $AA_1BD$, in which the edges $AA_1$, $AB$ and $AD$ are pairwise repredicular, and their lengths correspond to a, b, c.

a) Prove that the vertex A of the paramid, the intersection point of the median $A_1BD$ and the center of the circumscribed ball lie on the same straight line.

b) The name of the radus of the ball is described near this paramid.

Let's build the paramid $AA_1BD$ up to a parallelepiped (rectangular!), as shown in figure 1. Then the ball circumscribed around this paramid is simultaneously a circumscribed ball for the entire parallelepiped. The radius of this ball is equal to half the diagonal of the parallelepiped, namely $\frac{1}{2} (a^2 + b^2 + c^2)^{1/2}$, this is the answer to point 6).

To prove the statement in point a), consider the rectangle $AA_1C_1C$ (we advise you to make a separate drawing for it). The center O of the ball is on the diagonal $AC_1$, the median $A_1O_1$ of the triangle $A_1BD$ intersects $AC_1$ at the point M, and if we prove that $\frac{|A_1M|}{|MO_1|} = 2$, then this will mean that M is the intersection point of the median of the triangle $A_1BD$; Thus we will prove the statement in point a).
