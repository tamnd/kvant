4. Build the tetrahedron to a parallelepid using the second method. Express the quadruple square of the length of each parallelepid edge using the cosine theorem in terms of the lengths of the diagonals of the corresponding face and the angle between them (one face per edge). Then, to the edges of the parallelepid, apply the theorem on the sum of the squares of the lengths of the diagonals of a parallelogram and the sum of the squares of the lengths of its sides. Adding all the resulting equalities, you get the required result.

1. Let us denote the speeds of the three skiers by the letters $v_k$ ($k=1, 2, 3$) ($m/\mu m$), and the entire distance by $S$ ($m$). The third skier after $m$ ($\mu m$) remains to cover the distance ($S-mv_3$) ($m$). According to the conditions of the problem, the first skier will cover this distance in $n$ minutes, and the second one in $p$ minutes.

Taking into account these conditions and the correspondence between speeds, we obtain a system of equations:

$$\begin{cases}
\frac{S-mv_3}{v_1} = n, \\
\frac{S-mv_3}{v_2} = p, \\
v_1+v_2=2v_3.
\end{cases}$$

From system (1) it is required to find the relative $\frac{S}{v_k}$ ($k=1, 2, 3$). Eliminating $S-mv_3$ from the first two equations of the system, we obtain

$$V = \frac{1}{3} BD \cdot Q + \frac{1}{3} AD \cdot Q = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{a}{2} \cdot Q,$$

where $Q$ is the area of ​​triangle $CDS$, $BD = DA = \frac{1}{2} a$ (by construction). Thus, to find the volume of the prim, it is enough to calculate the area of ​​the triangle $CDS$.

$$Q = \frac{1}{2} CS \cdot DE = CE \cdot DE.$$
