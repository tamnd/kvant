It turns out that the problem of a bubble is very close to the problem of the movement of weights connected by a thread thrown over a stationary block (Fig. 1). It is not difficult to see the analogy between them. Indeed, one of the weights (with mass $m$) seems to play the role of a bubble, the other (with mass $M$) plays the role of water, and the tension of the bottom $T$ plays the role of a buoyant force.

Newton's second law applied to the mass loader $m$ can be written as follows:

$$ma = T - mg. \tag{2}$$

If the mass weight $m$ holds, then the weight of the weight $T$ turns out to be numerically equal to the weight of the weight $Mg$ (all the “displaced” water). Substituting $T = Mg$ into equation (2), we get:

$$a = \frac{M - m}{m} g \quad (\text{неверно!}). \tag{3}$$

At $M \gg m$ it turns out to be $a \gg g$. This conclusion is not entirely similar to the conclusion about the enormous acceleration of the bubble (see (1)). The reason for the confusion is the same: it is necessary to take into account the movement of the mass weight $M$ and the movement of the “displaced” water. Let us recall that to correctly solve the problem of weights, we also need to write down the equation of Newton’s second law for a weight of mass $M$

$$Ma = Mg - T \tag{4}$$

and solve the system of equations (2) and (4). From here

$$a = \frac{M - m}{M + m} g, \tag{5}$$

$$T = \frac{2mM}{M + m} g.$$

At $m \ll M$ it turns out $a \approx g$, $T \ll Mg$, which is quite true.

You can solve this problem in other ways - use the law of conservation of energy. When moving a weight of mass $m$ up (and, accordingly, a weight of mass $M$ down) at a distance $h$, the potential energy of the system will decrease by values ​​$Mgh - mgh$. The kinetic energy will become equal to $\frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{Mv^2}{2}$, where $v$ is the speed of the loads (we consider the initial speed to be equal to zero). Equating the values

$$Mgh - mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{Mv^2}{2},$$

we find

$$v^2 = 2 \frac{M - m}{M + m} gh,$$

or (see (5))

$$v^2 = 2ah. \tag{6}$$

Such a connection between speed and displacement is of the nature for motion with constant acceleration $a$. (In this case $a = \frac{M - m}{M + m} g$.)
