Task 5. In a metrahedron, the areas of two faces are $S_1$ and $S_2$, the two-dimensional angle between is $\alpha$, the areas of the other two faces are $Q_1$ and $Q_2$, the angle between $\beta$. Prove that
$$S_1^2 + S_2^2 - 2S_1S_2 \cos \alpha = Q_1^2 + Q_2^2 - 2Q_1Q_2 \cos \beta.$$
Let us first prove that if the area of ​​one side face of a triangular feature is $S$, the other two faces have areas $S_1$ and $S_2$, and the two-dimensional angle between $\alpha$, then
$$S_1^2 + S_2^2 - 2S_1S_2 \cos \alpha = S^2.$$
In fact, let the plane $ABC$ (Fig. 5) be perpendicular to the side edge of the surface, $\angle BAC = \alpha$. Let us write down the cosine theorem for the triangle $ABC$
$$|BC|^2 = |AB|^2 + |AC|^2 - 2|AB| \cdot |AC| \cos \alpha.$$
It remains to multiply the last equality by $l^2$, where $l$ is the length of the side edge of the recognition.

Let us now move on to solving Problem 5. Let $ABCD$ be the given triangle, $S_{\triangle ABD} = S_1$, $S_{\triangle ADC} = S_2$, $S_{\triangle ABC} = Q_1$, $S_{\triangle DBC} = Q_2$, the two-dimensional angle at edge $AD$ is equal to $\alpha$, and at edge $BC$ - $\beta$. Let us consider a triangular feature with a base $ABC$, one of the side edges of which is $AD$ (Fig. 6). Let us denote by $S$ the area of ​​the parallelogram

$$BB_1C_1C,$$ then according to the formula we just proved
$$4S_1^2 + 4S_2^2 - 8S_1S_2 \cos \alpha = S^2,$$
Moreover, $S$ can be easily expressed in terms of the lengths of the edges $BC$ and $AD$ and the angle $\varphi$ between them: $S = |AD| \cdot |BC| \cdot \sin \varphi$.
If we consider another triangular feature with base $ACD$ and side edge $BC$, we get
$$4Q_1^2 + 4Q_2^2 - 8Q_1Q_2 \cos \beta = S^2.$$
This implies the statement of Problem 5.
