Another common way to build a tetrahedron to a parallelepiped is as follows. Let us draw a plane through each edge of the tetrahedron parallel to the opposite edge. These planes will limit a certain parallelepiped (Fig. 2), the diagonals of the faces of which will be the edges of the original tetrahedron.

A little practical advice: it is more convenient to start a drawing with an image of a parallelepiped.

Task 2. Naimpi radius of a ball touching all edges of a regular tetrahedron, the length of the Comorozo edge is $a$.

As is easy to see from figure 2, the parallelepiped constructed in the described way will be a cube with edge $a/\sqrt{2}$; the ball inscribed in this cube will be the desired one. Answer: $a/2\sqrt{2}$.

The first of the proposed methods of completing a tetrahedron to a parallelepiped is convenient when the given angles are flat at one of the vertices of the tetrahedron (especially if they are all straight), the second is used in problems in which crossing edges of the tetrahedron appear.
