Semiregular polyhedra

Reader of the magazine «Kvant» have encountered regular polyhedra more than once*). Regular polyhedra have faces that are equal regular polygons, and all polyhedral angles are equal. But there are also polyhedra in which all polyhedral angles are equal, and the faces are equal polygons of several types. They cannot be classified as regular - they are called equiangular semiregular polyhedra.

The simplest of the semiregular polyhedra is the regular $n$-gonal prism ($n = 3, 5, 6, \dots$), all of whose edges are equal and whose lateral faces are squares. The bases of, for example, a hexagonal prism are regular hexagons: it has 12 vertices, 8 faces and 18 edges.

Another representative of equiangular semiregular polyhedra is the so-called antiprism. Imagine, for example, two regular hexagons located in parallel planes, one of which is rotated around the center relative to the other by $30^\circ$. Each vertex of both the lower and upper hexagon is connected to two close vertices of the other.

For regular polygons, the hexagons (bases) are also selected so that the lateral boundaries are regular triangles (Fig. 1) - in the case of a hexagonal antiprism, this distance (height) is equal to $H = a\sqrt{3} - 1$ (check that this is the case). A hexagonal antiprism has 12 vertices, 14 faces and 24 edges, all edges and all tetrahedral angles are equal to each other.

Using all regular polygons for prisms and antiprisms, we obtain an infinite series of semiregular polygons. However, these series do not exhaust all equiangular semiregular polygons: there are other semiregular polygons.

The simplest of them can be obtained if the corners of a regular tetrahedron are surrounded by planes, each of which cuts off a third of the edges emerging from one vertex (Fig. 2); this polyhedron has 12 vertices, 8 faces and 18 edges, and is called a truncated methradron. All its trihedral angles are equal to each other - each of them contains two plane angles in $120^\circ$ and one plane angle in $60^\circ$. A truncated tetrahedron has tetrahedral faces that are regular hexagons, and four more that are regular triangles.

*) See, for example, the article by V. N. Berezin and “Regular polygons”, «Kvant», 1973, No. 5.
