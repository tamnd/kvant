M. Golovey
"Water Snail"
Archimedes

How great this is, I leave it to those who understand it to the end to decide.

Galileo Galilei

The initial stage of the development of physics is closely connected with the invention of various mechanisms that enable human labor. By mastering the use of means and materials, learning in practice in the process of work the basic laws of geometry, mechanics, hydraulics, scientists long before our era created machines that to this day amaze us with the depth of design thought, ingenuity and mastery of execution.

One of the most outstanding inventors of antiquity was Archimedes.

And although at present his name is primarily associated with the law of buoyant force acting on a body immersed in a liquid, Archimedes is responsible for a huge number of ingenious inventions. And the most outstanding of them is the lifting witt, which is still called the Archimedes witt. Galileo wrote in one of his works that this invention “is not only magnificent, but simply wonderful, since we see that the water rises in Witt, continuously descending.”

And indeed, when first getting acquainted with this device, it is even difficult to imagine how water, falling along the Vitta blade or Vitt channel, can actually rise up the pipe. In one of his works, Galileo gives a detailed description of the operating principle of this machine, calling it “Archimedes’ water snail.” From a distance, this car actually somewhat resembles a snail.
