M321. There is a rectilinear table of area 1. Prove that for any ε>0 it is possible to indicate a system of rectilinear napkins covering this table (the edges of the napkins are parallel to the edges of the table), assuming that any of its subsystems consisting of non-overlapping napkins has an area less than ε.

We will say that the napkin system $A$ is a subsystem of the system $B$ if every napkin from $A$ is a napkin from $B$. Let us introduce the following notation $S(A)$ - the area of ​​the system $A$ is the area of ​​the table part covered with napkins from $A$.

Before we begin to solve the problem, we will describe the construction of two napkin systems that will be useful to us in the future.

1. Construction of the $A^k$ system.

System $A^0$ consists of one napkin with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (a, 0), (0, b), (a, b)$.

System $A^0$ is obtained from system $A^{k-1}$ by adding two napkins: (see figure 1): napkins with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (2^k a, 0), (0, 2^{-k} b), (2^k a, 2^{-k} b)$, and napkins with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (2^{-k} a, 0), (0, 2^k b), (2^{-k} a, 2^k b)$.

Each of these napkins has an area $ab$ and exactly half comes out of the system $A^{k-1}$, therefore $S(A^k) = S(A^{k-1}) + ab$ and $S(A^k) = (k+1) \cdot ab$, that is

$(nлощадь каждой салфетки) = \frac{S(A^k)}{k+1}.$ (*)

Note that any two napkins from the system $A^k$ overlap.

2. Construction of the $B^k$ system.

System $B^0$ coincides with the already constructed system $A^k$, in which the parameters $a$ and $b$ are selected so that $2^k a$ and $2^k b$ are the lengths of the sides of the table, and the coordinate system $xOy$ is connected to the table in the following way: the origin of coordinates $O$ is an arbitrary corner of the table, and the coordinate axes are directed along sides of the table.

System $B^k$ is obtained from system $B^{k-1}$ like this. It is necessary to mentally cut the frequency of the table, not covered by the napkins of the system $B^{k-1}$, into rectangular tables and build “your own” system $B^0$ on each of them. Then you need to add the resulting new napkins to the napkins of system $B^{k-1}$ - this will be system $B^k$.
