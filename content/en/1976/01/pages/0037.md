## Science reads invisible traces

No one will blame a historian if he does not know the first law of thermodynamics. But a physicist should certainly know it. It will not surprise anyone if a botany specialist has a poor understanding of the laws of mathematics, just as it will not surprise anyone if a mathematician does not understand the laws of living nature. As they say, to each his own.

But there are laws that everyone must know. These are the legal norms of the state in which we live. People of different professions, ranks and titles, of different ages - both a teenager and a gray-haired old man - must obey them.

You can often hear, especially among teenagers, a reference to ignorance of the laws. “I didn’t know I couldn’t do that.” “I didn’t think that such and such entailed criminal liability.” Is it possible to go unpunished in this case? No! The rules of law stipulate that ignorance of the laws does not excuse one from responsibility. That is why, quite recently, by order of the Minister of Education of the USSR, starting from the new school year, the course “Fundamentals of the Soviet State and Law” was introduced in the eighth grade of the country’s general education schools. Law is also studied at vocational schools. However, mastery of this subject is still ahead. In the meantime... For now, let's talk about how mathematics and physics help fight crime.

The material published below was prepared at our request by the editors of the popular science magazine of the USSR Ministry of Justice “Man and the Law”.

Probably, many readers «Kvant» will be sincerely surprised to learn that M.V. Lomonosov was engaged in forensic examinations. Meanwhile, this is true. His participation in a large number of expert works on cases of counterfeiting gold and silver items, the production of counterfeit coins, etc. at one time played an important role in establishing the truth.

Quite often, investigators and judges turned for help to such scientists as D. I. Mendeleev, A. M. Butlerov, N. N. Zinin, N. D. Zelinsky, N. I. Pirogov, A. N. Krylov and others, whose names are known throughout the world. The examinations they carried out allowed lawyers to solve a number of serious crimes and find the true culprits.

However, the list of such names can be continued, and many interesting stories can be told about each of them related to detective research that was carried out long before the birth of truly scientific criminology. Nowadays, in the scientific institutes and laboratories of the USSR Ministry of Justice, which are located in all union republics, in the scientific and technical departments of the internal affairs bodies, physicists, mathematicians, chemists, and biologists work side by side with lawyers. Higher mathematics and electronic computer technology, lasers and invisible rays, microbiology and the finest chemical analysis are put to the service of justice.

Here we will only talk about what is closest to the readers of «Kvant» - the application of the achievements of physics and mathematics in forensic research.

Luminescent analysis has found wide application in forensic science, the founder of which is rightfully considered to be the Soviet physicist S.I. Vavilov, whose contribution to the study of the nature of luminescence is undeniable. The name of the American physicist R. Wood is associated with photography in infrared and ultraviolet rays, which is also used for forensic purposes.
