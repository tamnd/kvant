Let us now briefly explain where our marked points and angles come from. We have already said that in each of the planes of the faces of an isohedral regular or semiregular polyhedron, as a result of its intersection with the other plane-faces, they form congruent systems of lines (this is obtained due to the fact that the polyhedron is equihedral semiregular, and there is a movement of space that transfers one of its faces to any direction, so that the polyhedron itself turns into itself). Let us fix the plane of some face of our polyhedron and the resulting system of intersection lines with the other plane-faces. Let's take another face and mark the line of its intersection with the first selected face in our drawing - let it be straight line $l_1$. Let us now rotate our polyhedron around a certain axis so that the second face goes into the first (this person of rotation either intersects with the line of intersection of the two selected faces, or is parallel to it - try to prove this yourself). In this case, the first selected face will go into some other face, and their intersection line will go into the line of intersection $l_2$ of the first face with the face into which it went as a result of rotation. Let us now find the straight line corresponding to the line $l_2$ on our “flat” drawing - let it be the straight line $l_2'$. The point of intersection of straight lines $l_1$ and $l_2'$ is the offset point, and the angle between these straight lines is the offset angle.
