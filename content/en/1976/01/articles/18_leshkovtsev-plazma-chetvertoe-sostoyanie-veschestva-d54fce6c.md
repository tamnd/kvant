Plasma is the fourth state of matter

This is the name of a popular science book written by Professor D. A. Frank-Kamenetsky and published in the fourth edition in 1975 by the Atomizdat publishing house *). David Albertovich Frank-Kamenetsky was one of the best popularizers of modern physics. Unfortunately, he died in the same year when “Kvant” was born, and we managed to publish only one of his articles on the pages of our magazine (“Electrical resistance is a quantum phenomenon”, “Kvant”, 1970, No. 9). Many people still consider it one of the best articles on physics published in our journal over all the years of its existence.

The idea of ​​creating this book was proposed by the author, Academician I.V. Kurchatov. He asked D. A. Frank-Kamenetsky to “explain the fundamentals of plasma physics without unnecessary mathematics, but with sufficient clarity.” Being by this time a major specialist in the field of plasma physics, the author nevertheless faced a very difficult task. After all, this young and unusually rapidly growing field of physics practice

*) D. A. Frank-Kamenetsky. Plasma is the fourth state of matter. 4th edition. M., Atomizdat, 1975. Circulation 80,000 copies, price 26 kopecks.

I didn’t yet have my own popular science literature. And many processes and phenomena occurring in plasma are completely different from the processes and phenomena occurring in gases, liquids and solids. And the author had to carefully think through the entire physics of plasma in order to explain all the basic processes in it without using complex mathematical apparatus.

The book is small in size - it only has one and a half pages. But it does cover all the basic processes occurring in both low-temperature and high-temperature plasmas.

Having talked about how plasma is produced and studied, the author continues to characterize various types of instability, which still do not allow maintaining a hot plasma for a period of time sufficient to carry out controlled thermonuclear reactions in it. He talks about hydrodynamic instability, the instability of the so-called “pinch”, and groove instability, due to which the plasma is calculated through the magnetic traps that isolate it and ends up on the walls of the vessel in which it is hardened. The book also examines a variety of oscillatory processes occurring in plasma, the propagation of shock waves in it, and various options for interacting parts of plasma with each other. Several paragraphs are devoted to the practical applications of plasma. They talk about electromagnetic pumps, plasma resonants and waveguides. In short, the book being solved is a small popular science encyclopedia on plasma physics.

In creating this book, the author tried to make it accessible even to readers with a general secondary school education. The book does not assume any knowledge of plasma physics in its future readers. The mathematical apparatus used in the main text of the book does not go beyond the scope of school algebra, and its physical part is based on the amount of knowledge in the field of molecular physics, electrical engineering and magnetism, which is completely included in the school physics course. The author does not avoid technical scientific terms - he talks to the reader in the language of modern science. However, each new term is explained in detail and highlighted in the course so that, if necessary, it can be easily found in the text. Places that are more difficult for initial familiarization are highlighted in the book with petit.

So, the book is quite accessible even for a reader who is completely unfamiliar with this area of ​​physics and has a school course in physics and mathematics. But you need to read it slowly and thoughtfully, with a pencil and paper in your hands. The book will certainly bring true satisfaction to such a reader, enriching him with an understanding of Intersea phenomena that are not at all reflected in school textbooks.

The book is written very vividly, imaginatively, accessible and exciting. The author uses unexpected comparisons, deep physical analogies, and a high appreciation of the main aspects of the phenomena under consideration.

The undoubted merits of this excellent book allowed the author of the postscript to the fourth edition, Academician R. Z. Sagdeev (by the way, one of the students of D. A. Frank-Kamenetsky) to tell me that it can be considered among the golden background of popular science literature.
