M321. There is a rectilinear table of area 1. Prove that for any ε>0 it is possible to indicate a system of rectilinear napkins covering this table (the edges of the napkins are parallel to the edges of the table), assuming that any of its subsystems consisting of non-overlapping napkins has an area less than ε.

We will say that the napkin system $A$ is a subsystem of the system $B$ if every napkin from $A$ is a napkin from $B$. Let us introduce the following notation $S(A)$ - the area of ​​the system $A$ is the area of ​​the table part covered with napkins from $A$.

Before we begin to solve the problem, we will describe the construction of two napkin systems that will be useful to us in the future.

1. Construction of the $A^k$ system.

System $A^0$ consists of one napkin with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (a, 0), (0, b), (a, b)$.

System $A^0$ is obtained from system $A^{k-1}$ by adding two napkins: (see figure 1): napkins with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (2^k a, 0), (0, 2^{-k} b), (2^k a, 2^{-k} b)$, and napkins with vertex coordinates $(0, 0), (2^{-k} a, 0), (0, 2^k b), (2^{-k} a, 2^k b)$.

Each of these napkins has an area $ab$ and exactly half comes out of the system $A^{k-1}$, therefore $S(A^k) = S(A^{k-1}) + ab$ and $S(A^k) = (k+1) \cdot ab$, that is

$(nлощадь каждой салфетки) = \frac{S(A^k)}{k+1}.$ (*)

Note that any two napkins from the system $A^k$ overlap.

2. Construction of the $B^k$ system.

System $B^0$ coincides with the already constructed system $A^k$, in which the parameters $a$ and $b$ are selected so that $2^k a$ and $2^k b$ are the lengths of the sides of the table, and the coordinate system $xOy$ is connected to the table in the following way: the origin of coordinates $O$ is an arbitrary corner of the table, and the coordinate axes are directed along sides of the table.

System $B^k$ is obtained from system $B^{k-1}$ like this. It is necessary to mentally cut the frequency of the table, not covered by the napkins of the system $B^{k-1}$, into rectangular tables and build “your own” system $B^0$ on each of them. Then you need to add the resulting new napkins to the napkins of system $B^{k-1}$ - this will be system $B^k$.

Now it's easy to solve the problem. Let $\varepsilon > 0$. Let us choose $k$ so that $\frac{1}{k+1} < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}$. Note that

There is a magnetic field with induction $B$ (Fig. 7). The maximum potential between the middle and the end of the wiring.

Let us first consider a conductor $OA$ of length $R$ attached to a disk along its radius. When the disk rotates, Lorentz forces will act on the free electrons of the conductor, as a result of which a potential difference will arise between the conductor. It can be calculated by imagining that the moving end $A$ of the conductor slides along a semi-angled contact wire connected to the end $O$ through a voltmeter (Fig. 8). Then, in a short period of time $\Delta t$, the area of ​​the circuit formed by moving and resting conductors will change by $\Delta S = R^2\omega \Delta t/2$, and the magnetic flux through the circuit will change by $\Delta \Phi = B\Delta S$. Consequently, the absolute value $\Delta c$ and the induction in the circuit, equal to the modulo potential difference at the ends of the conductor, will be equal to $|\mathcal{E}_1| = \Delta \Phi/\Delta t = BR^2\omega/2$, and the potential of the fixed end $O$ of the conductor will be higher than the potential of the end $A$.

If the length of the conductor were equal to $a$, then the potential of the end $O$ would be higher than the corresponding one by values ​​$\mathcal{E}_2 = Ba^2\omega/2$.

F335. A cylindrical vessel of radius $R$, the axis of which makes an angle $\alpha$ with vertical water-filled water. A well-sized piston is lowered into the cylinder; the material component is permeable to air, but impermeable to water. At what minimum weight of the piston will its lower surface always touch water?

Let's consider the forces acting on the piston. This is the gravitational force $mg$, equal to the acting $Q$ water pressure forces and the reaction force $N$ from the side of the cylinder walls (Fig. 10).

According to the conditions of the problem, the weight of the piston should be minimal. This means that at the highest point of contact between the piston and the water (point 0 at figure 10), the water pressure is zero. With an increase in $x$, the pressure $p$ increases in proportion to $x$:

$$p = \rho gh = \rho gx \sin \alpha$$

(ρ is the density of water). In order to find the equal $Q$ pressure force on the piston, we divide its contact surfaces with water into narrow strips so that the pressure on each of them can be considered constant. Let us select two identical strips $A$ and $B$ (with an area of ​​$s$ each), located on opposite sides of the center of the piston at equal distances $a$ from it (Fig. 11), and calculate the force $\Delta Q$ acting on both strips together. The water pressure at the points of the strip $A$ is $p_A = \rho gx_A \sin \alpha = \rho g(R - a) \sin \alpha$, and at the points of the strip $B - p_B = \rho gx_B \sin \alpha = \rho g(R + a) \sin \alpha$. That's why

$$\Delta Q = p_A s + p_B s = 2s\rho gR \sin \alpha.$$

From this we can see that the force $\Delta Q$ does not depend on the distance $a$, that is, it does not depend on the position of the strips. It is determined only by the total area of ​​the selected strips. Therefore, the pressure force acting on the entire piston will be equal to

$$Q = \rho gR \sin \alpha \cdot \Sigma s_i,$$

where $s_i$ is the area of ​​the $i$-th strip. But $\Sigma s_i$ is the area of ​​the piston, i.e. $\Sigma s_i = \pi R^2$, and

$$Q = \pi \rho g R^3 \sin \alpha.$$

We will assume that there is no friction between the piston and the cylinder walls; then the reaction force $N$ is directed perpendicular to the walls of the cylinder.

When the piston is in equilibrium, the sum of the projections of all forces on any direction, and therefore on the $Y$ axis, is equal to zero. Then

$$Q - mg \cos \alpha = 0, \quad \text{или } mg = \pi \rho g R^3 \mathrm{tg} \alpha.$$

Since in our case the weight of the piston (the force $P$ with which the piston acts on the support - on the water and the walls of the cylinder) is equal in abscissa to the gravitational forces, then

$$P = mg = \pi \rho g R^3 \mathrm{tg} \alpha.$$

F337. When observed through a melescope, bright stars are visible even during the day. Explanation why.

When viewed with the naked eye, stars are not visible against the background of a bright daytime sky due to the weak contrast sensitivity of the eye. The construct can be dramatically enhanced by observing the sticks with a telescope. Let's look at this in more detail.

The eye evaluates the brightness of objects by the illumination of their image on the retina. The brighter the source, the greater the illumination of its image. A measure of the contrast of a star’s image against the sky can be the ratio of the illumination of their image on the retina, i.e. $E_{3B}/E_\phi$. This ratio depends both on the parameters of the blue sources (star and background) and on the optical instruments with which observations are made.

Let us first consider the picture that is processed on the retina of the naked eye. Let $d$ be the screen diameter, $f$ be the focal length of the eye optics. Illumination of location as the ratio of luminous flux to the area of ​​the image. It should be borne in mind that the image of a star (the exact source) has the form of a diffraction path. The size of this path cannot be obtained using the formulas of geometric optics. The theory of diffraction shows that the linear size of the diffraction path at the focus of the lens is of the order of $\frac{\lambda}{d} f$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength $^*$).

Let's select a small element of the sky (background). The luminous flux from this distant light source entering the eye is very/no, proportional to $d^2$. The image area of ​​such an extended source is proportional to $f^2$. Hence the background illumination $(E_\phi)_1 \sim \frac{d^2}{f^2}$.

The coefficient of proportionality is determined by the rage of the source and does not change when the observation method changes.

The luminous flux from the star is also proportional to $d^2$, and the area of ​​the diffraction image on the retina is proportional to $\left(\frac{\lambda}{d} f\right)^2$. Thus, $(E_{3B})_1 \sim \frac{d^2}{\left(\frac{\lambda}{d} f\right)^2} = \frac{d^4}{\lambda^2 f^2}$. It is important to note that the illumination of the diffraction image of the star turns out to be proportional to the image of the star and the background on the retina of the naked eye $d^2$.

$$\left(E_{3B}/E_\phi\right)_1 \sim \frac{d^2}{\lambda^2}.$$

As a result of the daytime viewing condition, this ratio $(E_{3B}/E_\phi)_1$, which is a measure of the contrast of the image, is such that the naked eye practically does not see the stars against the bright sky.

Let the eye now be armed with a telescope. The telescope consists of two lenses: the objective and the eyepiece. The path of rays from an infinite distant object located on the optical base of the telescope is shown at figure 14. The first image of the object is obtained in the focal plane of the image $O_1$. The eye views this image through the eyepiece $O_2$ as through a magnifying glass. The image that is produced on the retina is similar to the image in the focal plane of the image. Therefore, to solve our problem, it is enough to consider the ratio of the illumination of the images of the star and the background in this plane. Repeating the reasoning given above, we get:

$$\left(E_{3B}/E_\phi\right)_2 \sim \frac{d^2}{\lambda^2},$$

where $D$ is the diameter of the telescope object. Since the proportionality coefficients in expressions (1) and (2) are the same (they depend only on the light sources themselves - the star and the background), then

$$\frac{(E_{3B}/E_\phi)_2}{(E_{3B}/E_\phi)_1} = \frac{d^2}{d^2}.$$

Thus, the telescope increases the contrast of the star image against the sky by $\left(\frac{d}{d}\right)^2$ times.

For large astronomical telescopes, this ratio reaches values ​​of the order of $10^5 - 10^6$. With the help of such telescopes you can even see very stars during the day.
