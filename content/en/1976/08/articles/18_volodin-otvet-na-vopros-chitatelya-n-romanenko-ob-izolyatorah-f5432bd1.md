Ask - we answer

Reader N. Romanenko asks: can metals be insulators under some conditions? Is it possible for insulators to conduct current?

Consultant of the physics department A. Volodin answers these questions.

Substances that we call metals have a number of properties that distinguish them from other substances - non-metals. The most characteristic of them are high electrical conductivity combined with good thermal conductivity. The opposite of metals in these properties are dielectrics, or insulators. It should, in addition, be noted that sometimes a substance can be classified as a metal based on one of these properties, but not on the other. Therefore, it is not possible to verify the absence of boundaries between metals and non-metals.

Under the influence of external factors, such as changes in temperature, pressure, etc., both reverse and non-reverse metal-insulator and dielectric-metal transitions can occur. Let us give a number of examples.

Such a typical metal as tin, when heated at high temperatures (and the presence of gray tin seeds) can transform into another, non-metallic modification - gray tin.

Some types of metals undergo so-called Mogta transitions: upon reaching a certain temperature, they practically jump from typical dielectrics into a good conductor - metallic.

Recently, at the Institute of High Pressure of the USSR Academy of Sciences, transitions to the conducting state of such perfect insulators as quartz (SiO₂) and aluminum oxide (Al₂O₃) were discovered. These transitions occur at very high pressure - on the order of a million atmospheres. And, of course, any metal that is transferred to a vapor state at not too high pressures becomes a typical dielectric.
