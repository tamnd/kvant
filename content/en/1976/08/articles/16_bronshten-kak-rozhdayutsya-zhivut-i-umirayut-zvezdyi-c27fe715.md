How stars are born, live and die

Which of you, young readers, looking into the night sky strewn with stars, has not thought about how and from what stars are born, how they go through their life path, what kind of end awaits them...
Scientists and specialists in stellar physics have repeatedly asked themselves these questions. And the answers did not come immediately, but only after many years of persistent concepts. Unusual issues of stellar wickedness have not yet been resolved.

The book by corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. S. Shklovsky *) introduces the reader to the current state of this difficult problem. Its author is a leading astrophysicist, widely known for his work in the USSR and abroad. At the same time, I. S. Shklovsky is an excellent popularizer of science. His book “The Universe, Life, Mind” was received with interest by a wide circle of readers and was published in several editions**).

An interesting book that tells about the amazing world of stars and nebulae, about pulses and supernovae, about black holes and gravitational waves. But it’s even more interesting to read this book if you know that its author himself “had a hand” in solving many of the problems described in it, that we receive information, as they say, first-hand.

Let's leaf through the book. A short introduction. In it, the author shows us how the development of astrophysics methods (the creation of radio telescopes, the use of spectral analysis, etc.) leads to the expansion of our knowledge about the Universe and its interactions. But, for example, the vast picture of the development of the Universe, the author suddenly puts an end to this and turns to a completely different question, which is the topic of his book: the evolution of stars. Why? “Well, if only because,” the author appears, “that 97% of the matter in our Galaxy is concentrated in stars.”

And here we have the first part of the book: “Stars are born.” First, the author introduces us to the physical properties of stars, their main types. We learned that there are capable stars (stationary), and there are pulsating and even flaring stars. This is followed by an introduction to the interstellar medium—the material from which stars are formed. But how does star formation occur? Where, under what conditions? The author leads the reader through the clouds of the interstellar continent to gas-dust complexes, which he calls star pens.

Now let's look into the second book. In the study of stars - the meaning of their existence. This is the theme of this chapter, which is called “The Stars Radiate.” If stars were not studied, they would not be stars. The study of stars makes them visible, it gives us all the information we have about these celestial bodies. On the other hand, the study of stellar radiation has greatly benefited from the physics of radiation under new conditions, and scientists are not yet able to reproduce stellar matter in the laboratory.

So, a star is a ball of gas in a state of equilibrium. What kind of balance are we talking about? It turns out that when applied to stars, there are several concepts of equilibrium. Let's give some examples. The pressure in the outer layers of the star, caused by gravitational forces, is balanced by the sum of the gas pressure and the pressure created by radiation. Radiation is in equilibrium with a substance: the amount of energy emitted and absorbed by a substance is the same. Each visible volume of a star receives approximately the same amount of energy as it gives out; this is called local thermodynamic equilibrium.

Where does the energy emitted by stars come from? Answering this question, the author talks about thermonuclear reactions and leads the reader to one of the most difficult problems in balancing astrophysics - the problem of neutrino radiation from the Sun. This radiation could tell us about reactions in the interior of the Sun, but its power turns out to be much less than necessary.

The third part of the book is called “Stars Explode.” It deals with so-called supernovae, flashes, or rather, explosions of which are associated with the final stage of stellar wickedness. First (as in previous parts) a general introduction is given, then an overview of the latest supernova explosions: the formation of radio nebulae, the generation of cosmic rays.
