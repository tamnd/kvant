And the author, who has made a significant contribution to solving the problems of radio emission of supernova remnants and the causes of their explosion, will familiarize the reader with the current state of our knowledge about both.

And finally, the last part of the book: “Stars Die.” Yes, stars, like people, having lived more or less the southern debt (the duration of which varies by hundreds of millions and billions of years), come to the end of their life journey. Nuclear fuel supplies are running out: hydrogen is burned out, helin is burned out. Not all stars give reaction energy for long, taking into account carbon and oxygen. Energy sources research. The star is collapsing - the pressure caused by radiation in its depths has dropped, and gas pressure alone is not able to overcome the granitization slope. Then before the star there are three paths, three possible fates. No, a star is not a wave, making a choice between them, it all depends on its mass, although you have to study some other factors (for example, the rotation of the star, the presence of a magnetic field).

The first way - for stars with a mass less than the Sun (more precisely, less than 1.2 solar masses) - turns into a white dwarf, “dropping” a significant part of its mass, and then gradually grows until the glow completely stops (the author calls this state a “black dwarf”).

A different fate awaits stars with masses between 1.2 and 2.4 solar masses. The star experiences a catastrophic decline to a very small size (several tens of kilometers in diameter) with the simultaneous “shedding” of the shell. This is a supernova explosion. The final stage of such an explosion is the formation of a neutron star (the matter of such a star consists of densely “packed” neutrons). Rapidly rotating neutron stars, which also have a powerful magnetic field, send radio signals in the form of a narrow directed beam of rays. Once for each revolution of the star (and the period of this revolution is about a second), the beam can eat the Earth. And then the earthly radiotelexes will receive a short pulse. The periods between pulses are maintained with an accuracy of billionths of a second. How much excitement and controversy their discovery generated in 1967! The sources of these radio pulses, called pulsarms, were soon deposited with long-predicted but until then unobserved neutron stars.

The stars of the third destiny are the most massive. They cannot stay at the stage of even a neutron star: the granitization of the force that overthrows the star is so great. The star splits itself and turns into a so-called “black hole”. Want to know what it is? Read Shakespeare. Yes, yes, William Shakespeare, the wonderful English playwright. His words are given as an episode to the four parts of the book by I. S. Shklovsky. Here they are: “To be called into a large sphere and not to be seen how you move there - this is a hole!” ("Antony and Cleopatra").

The reader will find a lot of interesting things in the book by I. S. Shklovsky. But it is not easy to read. The author does not hesitate to give formulas expressing certain physical laws or relationships, or to discuss complex physical issues. The presentation does not contain the slightest signs of simplification, which is a common sin of some popularizers. Shklovsky does not hide the difficulties facing science in this or that issue. Perhaps the tormentor will have to read some passages twice at a time or turn to senior comrades, teachers, and specialists for help. But much will be clear even upon first reading. Of course, the brothers for this book should only be those who sincerely love the science of the Universe and strive to find out how it lives and suffocates in our days.

Interested in space exploration

Nowadays, it is difficult to find people who are indifferent to reports of new space flights. But have you wondered what colossal work scientists, engineers, workers and the cosmonauts themselves did before the cherished moment of launch arrived? Who among you is not interested in learning how to calculate the speed of movement of a package that launches a spacecraft into a given orbit, understand the design characteristics of the package, determine the burn-up time of the package, calculate the required amount of fuel and oxidizer, and build the flight trajectories of spacecraft? The new book “Fundamentals of Cosmos Sciences,” written by Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences, Associate Professor A. Marlensky *, can help you with this.
