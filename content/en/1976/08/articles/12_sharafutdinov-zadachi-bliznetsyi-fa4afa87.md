Tannstevny Island
Of course, you recognize the illustration for Jules Verne’s novel “Tannestvennaya Isle”? The pegs in the sand are hammered out at the end of the shadow from the pole by the all-knowing and all-smart engineer Cyrus Smith. The journalist Gedon Spilett is standing with a watch... But here is a detail that you might have forgotten: the island where the events described in the novel took place was located south of 30° south latitude.
The engraver J. Barban and the artist P. Ferat, the illustrated novels of J. Verne, as a rule, coped with their task perfectly.
But in this case...
Try answering the following questions:
1. Are the shadows depicted correctly in the illustration?
2. In which direction should the convex covering, which can be drawn through the base of the pegs, be placed - from the pole or towards the pole?
3. Is it true that the pegs went from left to right?
Try drawing a shadow that describes the end of the pole's shadow during the day at different latitudes. The pole is directed parallel to the Earth's rotation axis.

V. Tikhonov

Twin problems
Chess players are familiar with the so-called “twin problems”, the conditions of which are slightly different, and the solutions are completely different. Here's a fun example of two twin math problems.
The following dialogue took place between A and B.
A: Do you have children?
B·(1): Yes, there is.
B·(2): Yes, three.
A: What age?
B: The product of their ages is early 36, and the sum is the number of windows in that house.

A (after thinking): I can't determine their age.
B: Let me add: the eldest is a boy.
A: Now everything is clear.
It is required to determine the number and age of children for two answer options to the first question ((1) and (2)). The most surprising thing is that although the answers are different, in both cases there are three children.

V. Sharafutdinov
(Novosibirsk)
