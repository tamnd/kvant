From this marriage he had eight children—two sons and six daughters. None of them inherited his profession.

Contemporaries in their memoirs depicted Avogadro as a man with very sons and six daughters. They note his friendliness and sincerity in dealing with other people. “Highly educated without pedagogy, wise without a person, despising luxury, not caring about wealth, not striving for honors, indifferent to his own merits and his own fame, modest, knowledgeable, benevolent” - this is how one of his contemporaries characterizes Avogadro. In his indifference to honors, he represented a rare temptation among scientists of that time.

After leaving the university, Avogadro briefly held the position of senior inspector of the Chamber of Control, as well as a member of the Higher Statistical Commission, the Higher Council of Public Education and the representation of the Commission of Weights and Measures. Despite his advanced age, he continued to publish his research in the proceedings of the Turin Academy of Sciences. His last work was published three years before his death, when Avogadro turned 77 years old. He died in Turin on July 9, 1856 and was buried in the family crypt in Vercelli. The year after Avogadro's death, in recognition of his services to science, his bronze statue was installed at the University of Turin.

How did this wonderful man enrich science, in particular physics and chemistry, during his life?

These questions were at the forefront of science at the time, and it was natural that young Avogadro decided to try his hand here. Avogadro's works, devoted to various problems of electricity, appeared until 1846. He also paid great attention to research in the field of electrochemistry, trying to find a connection between electrical and chemical phenomena, which led him to create his electrochemical theory. In this regard, his research came into contact with the works of the famous chemists Davy and Bercellius. But Avogadro entered the history of physics as the discoverer of one of the most important laws of molecular physics.
