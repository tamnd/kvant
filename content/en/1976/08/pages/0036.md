They begin to move their finger over the tooth powder on the conductor paper, and then, without stopping the movement of the finger, they burn the thread with a match. Along the trajectory of the finger movement, an area with distinct time marks is formed. By counting the number of marks received, the time of the body's fall is determined, and the acceleration of free fall is calculated using the formula (*).

In our experiment, the path traversed by the body when falling was 27 cm. The number of marks turned out to be 12. Consequently, the body fell for 0.24 seconds. Based on these data, the acceleration of gravity is $g \approx 9,4 \text{ m/sec}^2$. Considering the simplicity of the experiment, this result should be accepted quite well. The accuracy of changes in the acceleration of gravity can be significantly improved by increasing the height from which the body falls.

To successfully carry out the experiment, it is necessary, using a voltage divider, to select such a potential difference so that when the circuit is open, marks do not appear (remember that area insulation leads to the actual closure of the circuit, and marks are formed even when the contacts are open). It is not at all necessary to use a tripod in the experimental setup: at home, you can attach it to a support with contacts to a wooden rail or to the back and leg of a chair.
