Tasks

M396—M400; F408—F412

Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than November 1, 1976 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: “Problem «Kvant», M396” or “...F408”.
Solutions to problems in each of the subjects (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems should be sent in separate envelopes. Please also send assignments from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. In the letter, include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking your decisions). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).
In this issue, “Problem «Kvant»” is composed mainly of problems proposed at the last All-Union Olympiad. In parentheses (after the problems of the final type of the Olympiad) the class for whose students the task is proposed is indicated.
More difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk.

M396. A triangle whose sides are all larger than 1 cm is called “large.” Given a regular triangle ABC with a side of 5 cm. Prove that a) 1000 “large” triangles can be cut from triangle ABC; b) triangle ABC can be cut into 1000 “large” triangles; b)* triangle ABC can be triangulated into 1000 “large” triangles, that is, it is divided so that any two triangles either have no common points, or have only a common vertex, or have a common side; d) do steps 6) and b) for a regular triangle with a side of 3 cm. (8-9 cells)

S. Fomin

M397. There are three circles of the same radius on the data plane.
a) Prove that if they all intersect at one point, as shown in figure 1, then the sum of the marked arcs AK, CK, EK is equal to 180°.
b) Prove that if they are located as shown in figure 2, then the sum of the marked arcs AB, CD, EF is equal to 180°. (8 grades)
