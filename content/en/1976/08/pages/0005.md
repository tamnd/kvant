On February 9, 1665, Pierre Fermat's obituary was published in the Journal of Scientists, which stated:

“He was one of the most remarkable minds of our century, such a universal genius and so versatile that if all scientists had not arisen due to his necessary merit, it would be difficult to believe all the things that need to be said about him, so as not to miss anything in our honorable word.”

And although during his lifetime Pierre Fermat was recognized as the first mathematician of his time, and after his death his words increased even more, we know very little about him.

Here's the little that is known. Pierre was born in August 1601 in the south of France in the small town of Beaumont-de-Lomagne, where his father, Dominique Fermat, was the “second consul,” that is, something like an assistant to the mayor. Pierre's mother, Claire de Long, came from a family of lawyers. So, Pierre Fermat belonged to the “third estate”.

Dominique Fermat gave his son a very solid education. In the college of his native city, Pierre acquired well-known languages: Latin, Greek, Spanish, Italian and French. Subsequently, he wrote poetry in Latin, French and Spanish "with as much grace as if he had lived in the time of Augustus and spent a large number of private residences at the court of France or Madrid."

However, Fermat directed all the power of his genius towards mathematical research. And yet he is not a professional mathematician. Scientists of his time did not have the opportunity to devote themselves entirely to their favorite science.
