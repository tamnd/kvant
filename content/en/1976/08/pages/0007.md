Analytical geometry

One of Fermat's first mathematical works was the restoration of two approved books of Apollonius "On flat places". We would now classify the methods that Apollonius used as analytical and design geometry. However, at the time of Apollonius there was no letter symbolism, so he had to write down algebraic formulas and equations of geometric curves. For example, our formula $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ was written down (and proven!) by the ancients using a drawing (Fig. 1).

The main difference between modern methods for solving geometric problems and Apollonius’ methods is the use of letter algebra. The first person to understand how the new algebra should be applied to problems in geometry was Fermat.

In 1636, his essay “Introduction to the Study of Plane and Spatial Places” **) appeared in manuscript, in which analytical geometry on the plane was consistently constructed. Even when restoring the books of Apollonius, Fermat appreciated the application of the coordinate method and realized that an equation with one unknown completely determines a certain number, an equation with two unknowns - a set of points on a plane (curve), an equation with a third unknown -
