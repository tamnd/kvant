Guess with obstacles

To solve the problem, 29 questions are enough. First, ask about the first 15 digits contained in the double entry of the telephone number (questions like: “Is it true that the kth place in the double entry is 0?”). Then he asks: “Do you agree on one of the 15 answers?”

If “yes,” then either one of the 15 answers or the last one is false. The “halving” method uses four questions to find out which of the 16 answers is false, and then nine questions find out the remaining numbers.

If the answer to question 16 is “no,” then... Indeed, I have never lied, and the 15 numbers you received are correct (otherwise the lie would be contained in two answers: one of the first 15 and the 16th, and this contradicts the conditions). Next, ask for the next seven numbers and again ask the question: “Do you agree with one of the last seven answers?”

If the answer is “yes”, then you will find out with three questions where the lie is, and two more - the last two digits; if the answer is “no,” then ask about the remaining two numbers and “were you in charge in these two answers?” Two questions are enough to find out where the lie is.
