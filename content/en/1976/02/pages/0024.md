Lobachevsky and physics

Mathematics has always served as the physicist's ordinem research. Kepler would not have been able to formulate his laws if he did not know the theory of finite sections. In order to solve problems in mechanics, Newton had to create a new branch of mathematics: differential and integral calculus. But even against the background of these great discoveries, the discovery of non-Euclidean geometry stands out due to the influence it had on our understanding of the physical picture of the world.

In those years when Nikolai Lobachevsky worked, no one doubted the correctness of Newton’s mechanics, and questions about the nature of space and time, although they were of interest to scientists, seemed completely abstract and had no practical significance.

Lobachevsky is compared to Copernicus. Only the Earth, the position of the center of the world, Copernicus opened the Universe for exploration. Lobachevsky, having shown that the ενClidean geometry is not the only one, made natural scientists think that the space in which the Universe develops should also be a subject of study. Geometry after Lobachevsky was characterized by its absolute infallibility and was processed by experience for confirmation or denial of its laws.

Lobachevsky understood that the possibility of changing the laws of geometry could entail the possibility of changing the laws of mechanics. “... some forces in nature,” he wrote, “follow one; other properties of geometry... forces all produce one: motion, speed, time, mass, even distances and angles.”

Physicists ignored these warnings for a very long time. Only after the discovery of the special (SRT) and general relative theory (GRT) it became clear what deep meaning is contained in these words of Lobachevsky. Physicists should consider Lobachevsky one of the great creators of their science. The history of non-Euclidean geometry still serves as a striking example of how the most abstract theories can lead to a complete shift in views on our real world.
