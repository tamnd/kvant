Either statement $B$ is true, or its negation (that is, the statement: “Statement $B$ is not true”). All Hockens players are poor learners (they have the $A$ property). If this is false, then at least one Hokkenst is a good student (does not have the $A$ property).

So here's our diagram:

Statement
1. All items from $M$ have the property $A$.
2. Some items from $M$ have the $A$ property.
3. His denial
1. At least one item from $M$ does not have the property $A$.
2. All items from $M$ do not have the $A$ property.
Note that the statements “some objects have the property $A$” and “at least one object (and perhaps all) have the property $A$” mean the same thing in our scheme.

18. Find all natural numbers $n$ for which the following inequalities are valid: $486 < n \leq 501$ - and, in addition, one and only one of the following conditions is true:

d) $n$ is not divisible by 3, but divisible by 4;
