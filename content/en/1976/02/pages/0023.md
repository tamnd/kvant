The Edens of the great Russian geometer received worldwide recognition only after his death. To understand how this happened, you need to go back. In 1828, Gauss’s book “Studies on Curved Surfaces” was published, in which “surface geometry” or its internal geometry was constructed. Gauss gave formulas that make it possible to calculate the lengths of other curved lines on a surface, determine the angles between curves or planes of limited immi regions, find geodesics (i.e., the shortest) lines on the surface and determine the so-called Saussian curvature - a value characterizing the deviation of the internal geometry of the surface from the geometry of the plane.

A student of Gauss, F. Mindin (1806-1885), who worked in Dorpat (now the city of Tartu), studying the so-called * pseudosphere *, i.e., surfaces of constant Gaussian curvature, received back in the 30s centuries some formulas for the trigonometry of geodesic triangles on this surface. In 1868, the Italian geometer E. Beltrami drew attention to the fact that Minding’s formulas coincide with the formulas of Lobachevsky’s hyperbolic geometry.
