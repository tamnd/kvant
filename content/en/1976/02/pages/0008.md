However, the very first, generally very flattering review that the university inspector gave to Lobachevsky when confirming him as a chamber student, makes one feel the lively, expansive and playful character of the young student, essentially still a boy (let’s not forget that Lobachevsky was not yet 17 years old in the spring of 1809!).

In July 1811, by resolution of the Council of Kazan University, Lobachevsky, apparently for bad behavior, was not awarded the title of candidate, but at the next meeting of the Council he was elevated directly to the degree of master (he was confirmed in this degree on August 3, 1811).

By this time, Lobachevsky was already a mathematician - if in June 1810 (when Lobachevsky was not yet fully 17 years old!) Littrov *) began to sign up with him as an astronomer “for preliminary preparation for doing observations,” then in October 1811 Bartels *) had already studied mathematics with Lobachevsky at home for four hours a week, having recently completed Gauss’s “Arithmetic” with him and the first the volume of Laplace's “Celestial Mechanics” are great works of mathematical thought, which have become classics from their very appearance. At the same time, Bartels writes an enthusiastic letter about Lobachevsky to the trustee of the educational district, Rumovsky. Finally, at the same time, Bartels involves Lobachevsky in pedagogical work with students as his, so to speak, private assistant: “... moreover, Mr. Lobachevsky will explain to his listeners, Mr. Professor, what they misunderstand”...

Bartels’ private assistantship was not followed by the beginning of Lobachevsky’s official teaching activity: it dates back to 1812, and is modestly high.

*) Iosif Antonovich Littrov is a famous astronomer, the first professor of astronomy at Kazan University, then director of the Vienna Astronomical College.

*) Martin Fedorovich Bartels - an outstanding mathematician, Doctor of Physology at the University of Jena; since 1805 - professor at Kazan University in the department of pure mathematics.
