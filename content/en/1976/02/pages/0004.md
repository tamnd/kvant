Towards the XXV Congress of the CPSU

February 1976 will forever go down in the history of our country. The XXV Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union will take place in this place. The congress will sum up the extensive work of our people to implement the ninth five-year plan and approve the main directions for the development of the national economy of the USSR for 1976-1980.

The main task of the tenth five-year plan in the field of development of the national economy is a sharp increase in the quality of products and the efficiency of production activities in all spheres of labor. It is not for nothing that this five-year plan is called the “five-year plan for quality.”

Having successfully completed the ninth five-year plan for the development of the national economy, the Soviet people took a major step towards communism. Physics and mathematics played an important role in these achievements. The role of these sciences in the new five-year plan will be even more significant.

We live in an era that is often called the era of scientific and technological revolution. Science in our day has turned into a force of direct productivity, the power of which is constantly growing. Over the past three decades, we have witnessed the turbulent conditions of physics and mathematics, as a result of which nuclear energy emerged, replacing traditional chemical energy. Rocket and space technology was born. Electronic computers appeared. They made available solutions to problems that previous generations could only dream of being able to solve. Broad production of semiconductors, electronic equipment, various automation equipment. At the same time, science is preparing profound revolutionary changes that will subsequently be significantly reflected in metallurgy and mechanical engineering, light and food industries, construction and agriculture. The meaning of these changes is an incredibly rapid increase in productivity, production efficiency and product quality.

In the near future, most enterprises will turn into complex automated production, in which a person will no longer be tied to a machine or conveyor. All production operations will be taken over by machines and control mechanisms. A person will create these complex sets of automatic machines, prepare work programs for them and monitor their execution, and continuously improve production. In such factories, labor productivity will make a huge leap. But it is precisely the unprecedented growth of productive labor that, according to V.I. Lenin, is the decisive factor in the struggle for communism.

Integrated automated production leads us to an abundance of materials and benefits. Nuclear and thermonuclear energy opens the way for humanity to an abundance of energy. Big chemistry creates an extraordinary wealth of starting materials and raw materials for industry. Electronic computers make it possible to timely and accurately process gigantic amounts of information, which a person deprived of these means is powerless to deal with.
