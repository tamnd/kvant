⟦folio 61⟧

"All", "some" and negation

I recently had an interesting conversation with sixth-grader Alik, the centerpiece of our yard’s football team.

- What, Alik, are you running to training? In winter, perhaps, you will change your football to hockey?

“No way in the world!” Alik exclaimed passionately. “In winter, you need to borrow money, and not run after the puck.” All hockey players study poorly, but football players study much better!

- Why did you decide that?

- And what? Everyone on our team thinks so, I’m not alone.

- Isn’t it true that everyone you are talking about now is like the infamous monkeys from Kipling’s book “Muggly”? Do you remember, they shouted to the whole forest: “We are great! We are free! We deserve praise! We all say that, so it’s true!..."

“Who is this monkey?!” Alik was indignant.

“I didn’t want to offend you, Alik, but you can’t prove anything by citing the opinions of many.” The earth is round, but in ancient times almost everyone thought it was flat. The Indians of the Snu tribe, as their leader Mato Nazhin writes, even believed that the Earth was smooth, and this was already in the 19th century... So why are all hockey players poor students?

- Well, here is Seryozha Kukushkin. He plays hockey all the time and gets bad marks all the time.

“You talk like one Frenchman who said: “All Englishmen are short, plump and black,” only because the only Englishwoman he met looked like that. Both you and this Frenchman are violating the laws of logic.

Alik thought about it, and I decided to help him. To begin with, I told this story.

“In one city I saw a sign on a house: “There are no double rooms in our house.” There was no such table on the neighboring house. In your opinion, Alik, does this mean that everyone in the neighboring house was double?

“N-no,” Alik muttered uncertainly. Not necessarily. This only means that there are bad students there... At least one bad student... Or maybe everyone there is a bad student. No, I don’t know, I wasn’t there!

— What does this phrase mean: “It is not true that among the guys there are doubles”?

- It’s like in the first house - everyone studies without failing.

“So, it’s one of two things,” I said with satisfaction. “Either everyone studies without a bad grade, or there is at least one bad student in the house.” What about puck lovers?

And then they found out that Alik could, after thinking, reason correctly.

- One of two things is true: either all hockey players study poorly, or not all - there are hockey players who study well.

- But even in the creative class, Alik, I know three excellent students who love to kick the puck. This means that the second is true: there are hockey players who study well.

- Well, of course!

- What then does that example with Seryozha Kukushkin mean?
