Such a qualitative consideration allows us to obtain the reasons for the appearance of the vibration moment and establish the correct dependence of this moment on the vibration amplitude of the suspension, vibration frequency, matrix mass and deflection angle $\varphi$, but does not allow us to find the value of the proportionality coefficient. A more rigorous analysis carried out by P. L. Kapitsa leads to the correspondence
$$M_B = \frac{m\omega^2 x_0^2}{4} \sin 2\varphi. \quad (1)$$

Using the understandable vibration-moment, we will now write the conditions for the equilibrium of the matrix in the field of gravity. We will assume that vibrations of the suspension occur in the vertical

direction. In addition to the vibration moment $M_B$, a gravitational moment $M_T = mgL \sin \varphi$ also acts on the magnet. In the equilibrium position these moments are equal:
$$mgL \sin \varphi = \frac{m\omega^2 x_0^2}{4} \sin 2\varphi,$$
or
$$\sin \varphi \left( \frac{2gL}{\omega^2 x_0^2} - \cos \varphi \right) = 0. \quad (2)$$

The values ​​of $\varphi$ that satisfy this equation are the equilibrium positions. They are:
$$\varphi_1 = 0, \quad \varphi_2 = 180^\circ, \quad (3)$$
$$\varphi_3, 4 = \pm \arccos \frac{2gL}{\omega^2 x_0^2}.$$

Here $\varphi_1 = 0$ is the upper position of the weight, $\varphi_2 = 180^\circ$ is the lower position of the weight.

Having considered the correspondence between the magnitude of the vibration moment and the moment of gravity near the equilibrium positions, we can be convinced that $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ are stable positions, and $\varphi_3$ and $\varphi_4$ are unstable. Thus, if the magnet is in the area $A$ (Fig. 4), then it will oscillate around the position $\varphi_1$, and if in the area $B$, around the position $\varphi_2$. From (3) it is clear that if $\frac{2gL}{\omega^2 x_0^2} = 1$, then $\varphi_1 = \varphi_3 = \varphi_4$ and the position $\varphi_1$ becomes the case when the direction of vibration makes a certain angle with the vertical. But it turns out that a stable position of the inverted matrix is ​​possible in any direction of vibration. True, the parameters of the matrix cannot be arbitrary.
