Finding the velocity v' comes down to finding, using the correct Lobachevsky geometry, the side d of the triangle BAD using two known sides a and b and the straightness of the angle between them *).

Angle β of triangle BAD is the angle that velocity v' makes with the horizon. The rule by which side d is constructed is the one used for straightening a triangle. However, the formulas for determining the length of side d are different in Lobachevsky geometry than in Euclidean geometry (for example, in the problem we are considering, the Pythagorean theorem is not applicable). But in the case when the velocities u and v are much less than c, these formulas practically coincide with the formulas of Euclidean geometry.

We can say that SRT is in the same relationship to Newton's classical mechanics as Lobachevsky's geometry is to Euclid's geometry.

In the problem we have considered, we can give a “physical” representation of the geometric concept of parallelism. The greater the speed of the packets v_i, the greater the angle α, which makes the speed v' with the horizon.

In classical mechanics, the speed v_i can be as high as you like.

According to SRT, the packet speed v_i cannot be greater than the speed of light c. In the case where |v_i| = c - light (photon) is released from the ship - the magnitude of the speed v' is also equal to c. Indeed (see (**)),

$$(v'_i)^2 = u^2 + v_i^2 \left(1 - \frac{u^2}{c^2}\right).$$

Substituting v_i = c into this expression, we get v'_i = c.
