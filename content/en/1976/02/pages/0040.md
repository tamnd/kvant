Let us denote the members of the company by points on the plane (no three of which lie on the same straight line). The fact that A is corrected by B will be denoted by a segment with an arrow directed from point A to point B. Then exactly k arrows will come out of each point, and there will be kn arrows in total. We need to find the number k₀ - the smallest value of k at which at least one segment must have arrows in both directions.

There are $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ segments without arrows connecting n points. If there is no more than one arrow on each segment, then there are no more arrows than $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$. So, if $kn > \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, then $k \geq k_0$. From here

$$k_0 \leq \begin{cases} \frac{n}{2}, & \text{ если } n \text{ четное,} \\ \frac{n+1}{2}, & \text{ если } n \text{ нечетное.} \end{cases}$$

Let us prove that inequality (1) can be replaced by equality. Let us put an equal sign in formula (1) and show that n points for any n can be connected by segments with arrows in such a way that each segment has exactly k₀ - 1 arrows, and no segment contains two arrows. Here's one way to do it. It is necessary to place n points at the vertices of the correct n-pleasure and from each point direct arrows to k₀ - 1 vertices following it clockwise. Since k₀ - 1 < $\frac{n}{2}$ for all n, no two arrows end up on the same segment.
