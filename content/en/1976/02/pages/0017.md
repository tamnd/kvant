⟦folio 15⟧

Lobachevsky case: on the proposal of Gauss, Lobachevsky was elected in 1842 as a corresponding member of the Gottingen Royal Society of Sciences.

The right to the free and difficult years of the 19th century was a period of increased development of both the creative, scientific, pedagogical and organizational activities of Lobachevsky, then from the mid-forties, it completely contributed to the present period of inaction for Lobachevsky. The event that brought with us this tragic turning point in his entire life was his dismissal from the post of rector on August 14, 1846. This was due to the wishes of both Lobachevsky himself and the University Council. However, Lobachevsky was temporarily dismissed from his position as a professor in the department of pure mathematics. Lobachevsky himself, perhaps, gave some reason for the last act, since, fulfilling his obligations as trustee of the district since 1845, together with Musina-Pushkin, who was transferred to St. Petersburg, he, transmitting to the ministry the resolution of the University Council on electing him for a new five-year period to the department of mathematics, added that “he is ready to refuse the position in favor of a worthy young man, such as Doctor of Mathematics Popov.”

Lobachevsky's dismissal had all the features of a gross disqualification from service, bordering on direct insult. Such was the official gratitude of the reign of Emperor Nicholas I not only to the greatest Russian scientist of the nineteenth century, but also to our largest university activity.

It is quite understandable that Lobachevsky, for whom his activities in the area of ​​university education, and indeed public education in general, were a large and irreplaceable part of his entire life, perceived his resignation as a heavy, irreparable blow. This blow was especially severe, of course, because it broke out in that era of Lobachevsky’s life, when his scientific work was basically already legal and, therefore, university activities in the natural world became the main content of his life. If we add to this the exclusively activity of Lobachevsky’s character, which means his habit, created over decades, of being a leader in organizing affairs, and not an ordinary participant (a habit to which Lobachevsky truly had the right!), then the size of the καταstrophistantus that broke out over him is quite clear. Lobachevsky was unable to rule due to this καταstrophy. From 1847 onwards, he practically completely withdrew from participation in the affairs of both the educational district and the university. Old age began - premature, but all the more depressing, with progressive signs of paradoxically early decrepitness. His health was declining. He began to become lazy, and by the end of his life he was completely asinine. His last scientific work, “Pangeometry,” was written out of curiosity. Added to all this was the death of his son. Broken by life, a big, blind old man, Lobachevsky died on February 12, 1856.
