“Two thousand years ago, geometry froze in its size and beautiful forms, like an enchanted beauty in a folk tale. But a hundred years ago three knights came: one from the German (Gauss), another from the Hungarian (Bolyai), the third from the Russian Land (Lobachevsky). They sprinkled her with dead and living water. Dead water washed away the autocracy of Euclidean geometry, forced it from the absolute dominance with which it ruled in spatial relations; living water gave it, Euclidean geometry itself, eternal existence.” (V. Kagan)
