Towards the XXV Congress of the CPSU

February 1976 will forever go down in the history of our country. The XXV Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union will take place in this place. The congress will sum up the extensive work of our people to implement the ninth five-year plan and approve the main directions for the development of the national economy of the USSR for 1976-1980.

The main task of the tenth five-year plan in the field of development of the national economy is a sharp increase in the quality of products and the efficiency of production activities in all spheres of labor. It is not for nothing that this five-year plan is called the “five-year plan for quality.”

Having successfully completed the ninth five-year plan for the development of the national economy, the Soviet people took a major step towards communism. Physics and mathematics played an important role in these achievements. The role of these sciences in the new five-year plan will be even more significant.

We live in an era that is often called the era of scientific and technological revolution. Science in our day has turned into a force of direct productivity, the power of which is constantly growing. Over the past three decades, we have witnessed the turbulent conditions of physics and mathematics, as a result of which nuclear energy emerged, replacing traditional chemical energy. Rocket and space technology was born. Electronic computers appeared. They made available solutions to problems that previous generations could only dream of being able to solve. Broad production of semiconductors, electronic equipment, various automation equipment. At the same time, science is preparing profound revolutionary changes that will subsequently be significantly reflected in metallurgy and mechanical engineering, light and food industries, construction and agriculture. The meaning of these changes is an incredibly rapid increase in productivity, production efficiency and product quality.

In the near future, most enterprises will turn into complex automated production, in which a person will no longer be tied to a machine or conveyor. All production operations will be taken over by machines and control mechanisms. A person will create these complex sets of automatic machines, prepare work programs for them and monitor their execution, and continuously improve production. In such factories, labor productivity will make a huge leap. But it is precisely the unprecedented growth of productive labor that, according to V.I. Lenin, is the decisive factor in the struggle for communism.

Integrated automated production leads us to an abundance of materials and benefits. Nuclear and thermonuclear energy opens the way for humanity to an abundance of energy. Big chemistry creates an extraordinary wealth of starting materials and raw materials for industry. Electronic computers make it possible to timely and accurately process gigantic amounts of information, which a person deprived of these means is powerless to deal with. Thus, during the scientific and technical

The problem of a sharp increase in product quality and labor efficiency can be successfully solved only with the help of modern science. Physics and mathematics should make a major contribution to its solution. They are still widely used in determining product quality. Modern methods of mass product quality control are based on probability theory and mathematical statistics. Achievements in optics, RENT ray physics, ultrasound physics, magnetism, and nuclear physics are the scientific basis for a variety of control and measuring equipment. Optical and RENT spectral analysis, magnetic and ultrasonic flaw detection, gamma radiation from radioactive sources have long been successfully used in production to measure product thickness and detect hidden defects.

Modern physics provides industry with fundamentally new methods of influencing materials. Concentrated beams of fast electrons, ions, a laser beam, powerful neutron fluxes created by atomic reactors, ultra-strong magnetic fields and ultra-high pressures make it possible to influence the entire thickness of the product, simplifying the crystal lattice and giving the product incredible strength, wear resistance, reliability and durability. Physics opens the way to new technological processes and methods of production, changing the thickness of a product and detecting hidden defects.

Let's take as an example the creation of nuclear engines for maritime transport - icebreakers, submarines. The Soviet nuclear-powered icebreakers "Lenin" and "Arktika" conduct caravans of ships in winter in such high Arctic seas that are completely insufficient for conventional icebreakers at this time of year. Soviet nuclear submarines successfully circumnavigate the world without ever surfacing the ocean surface throughout their entire journey.

Thanks to the outstanding achievements of radio electronics and reactor technology, the possibility of creating new mechanisms for a radio relay and television communication system has opened up, which makes it possible to transmit television programs of any state to all other countries of the world. To implement it, it will be necessary to launch several synchronous (formed around the Earth at the same speed with which the Earth rotates around its axis) artificial Earth satellites in the equatorial regions, equipped with powerful relay devices. This will create a qualitatively new system of information transmission.

Nuka reveals to a person great prospects for the development and improvement of all types of labor. And these possibilities can be realized most fully only under socialism.

The lectures on geometry given by Lobachevsky in the 1816-1817 academic year are of particular interest to us, since in these lectures he apparently for the first time came close to the question, the solution of which was the glory of his entire life - the question of the axiom of parallels. True, this first contact with the main subject of all his theoretical activity was still completely traditional - Lobachevsky, as well as his numerous ideas in the field of non-Euclidean geometry, began with attempts to prove the signs of Euclid’s axiom. Lobachevsky's youth is over. A period of full disclosure of a rich and diverse personality began in all ways of interaction with the world around her: scientific creativity began, exceptional in its abstract mathematical power, his multifaceted educational work began and quickly developed, using the unyielding energy and passion for the work of the professor - soon in all respects the first professor of Kazan University, his ardent participation in all aspects of the work, organization and construction of the university began, which then turned into a twenty-year full and sole leadership of all university life.

II.

As already mentioned, Lobachevsky has been a professor at Kazan University since 1816; two years later, his administrative and organizational activities began, various forms of which accompanied him further during the three decades of his life: in August 1818, Lobachevsky was appointed a member of the school committee. During these years, dark clouds were gathering over Kazan University, and over the entire Russian education in general; The reactionary aspirations of the last decade of the reign of Alexander I were heard every year and became more and more oppressive. The coming era in the history of Russian culture is known as the “Magnitsky era”, named after the trustee of the Kazan educational district, famous for his obscurity. Kazan thus becomes the center and breeding ground of the darkest reaction, gripping all areas of cultural life. All the more difficulties befell Lobachevsky, who precisely at this

Meanwhile, as we will now see, his work in this field is developing rapidly: in December 1819, Lobachevsky, together with Professor Verderamo, received an assignment to put in order the university library, which was in an extremely neglected state. At the end of 1820, Lobachevsky was given even more responsible responsibilities: on November 19, he was elected dean of the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics. However, this activity of his ends in the summer of 1821, approximately simultaneously with his library activities.

Lobachevsky's professorial activity in these years received significant new content: he was entrusted with the departments of physics and astronomy. In this regard, Lobachevsky did not teach mathematics for two academic years, 1819-1820 and 1820-1821, devoting himself entirely to teaching physics and science.

Lobachevsky understood his unity as a rector very broadly - from the ideal leadership of the life and work of the university as a whole to his personal involvement in the increased needs of the university. As examples of the energy and activity shown by Lobachevsky for the benefit of the university, we mention his role during two tragic events that befell the population of Kazan in the middle of the last century. The first of these events was the cholera epidemic of 1830; This epidemic (which was part of the cholera epidemic of 1829-1832) raged among us, especially in the Volga region, and claimed thousands of victims. When cholera reached Kazan in its spread, Lobachevsky immediately took more severe measures against the university: the university was actually isolated from the rest of the city and turned, as it were, into a fortress. Accommodation and meals for students were organized on the university territory itself, all this with the very participation of rector Lobachevsky.

The success was great - the epidemic passed through the university; there were no, or almost no, cases of cholera among university students and employees. Lobachevsky’s energetic and selfless work in the fight against cholera made a painful impression on all public opinion at that time, and office institutions considered it necessary to note it: Lobachevsky was expressed “the highest favor” “for his diligence in protecting the university and other educational institutions from cholera.”

Another natural disaster that struck Kazan during Lobachevsky’s rectorship was the fire of 1842. During this terrible fire, which destroyed part of the city, Lobachevsky again took extremely energetic measures to save university buildings and equipment. In particular, thanks to his stewardship, the library and astronomical instruments were preserved.

It is interesting to note that even as rector, Lobachevsky did not consider it possible to refuse the mandatory librarian duties for eight whole years: only in 1835, when the work of the library could be considered already fully established, Lobachevsky left the position of librarian.

It is also necessary to point out Lobachevsky’s production activities (since 1823 he has been a member of the product committee). He created the “Uchenyzapiski” of the Kazan University, which replaced the reactionary and insignificant “Kazansky Vestik”, organized by Magnitsky.

Lobachevsky's election to the post of rector of the university was only a few months separated from the change of trustee of the educational district: on May 6, 1826, Magnitsky was removed from the post of trustee for monetary settlements. The removal of Magnitsky, and even with such a plausible motivation, could not help but defuse the atmosphere both in the district and at the university, and this is obligatory for Lobachevsky’s position, especially since the new trustee, Musin-Pushkin, had great respect for Lobachevsky and took him into account to the highest degree. Problems of education are of interest to Lobachevsky in all their scope and in the most ardent way. Lobachevsky himself has the full diversity and wide life of interests included in his ideal of the harmonious development of a person's personality. And he demanded a lot from the young man who came to the university to study: he first of all demanded from him that he be a citizen who “with high knowledge forms part and glory of his fatherland.” He emphasizes that “mental education alone does not complete education.”

Let us note, by the way, that Lobachevsky paid attention to the physical development of youth, in particular, he introduced the introduction of gymnastics in high school.

Lobachevsky can serve as an example, probably, of the greatest person nominated by our almost two hundred years of university life. And even if he did not write a single line of independent scientific research, we should remember him as one of our most significant university activities. But the fact of the matter is that Lobachevsky, in addition, was also a brilliant scientist.

The geometry of “world regions” of average size is, of course, Euclidean geometry - in the sense that Euclidean geometry describes with sufficient accuracy everything that we actually observe in these regions. If you go beyond their limits, then, as you discover in modern physics, you may come across systems, cities that are more complex than even non-Euclidean geometry, in the sense that Lobachevsky defined it. Moreover, it is impossible to talk about a “single”, motionless geometry, since it forever embraces the entire variety of spatial relationships that can be abstracted by our cognition from the material world around us.

Lobachevsky's immortal merit is that he was the first to break through the perception of geometry as the only conceivable logical system.

It is quite understandable that when he came to his conclusions, Lobachevsky could not count not only on recognition, but even on a simple understanding of his ideas - it took half a century for these ideas to enter mathematical science and become its integral part. Therefore, during his life, Lobachevsky found himself in the difficult position of an “unrecognized scientist.” His contemporary, the Hungarian scientist Janos Bolyai, found himself in the same situation, who came to non-Euclidean geometry independently of Lobachevsky, although he published his results somewhat later than him. But what a difference between these two scientists!
If Bolyai was, so to speak, oppressed by the lack of princedom of his ideas and never found any way out of the truly deeply dramatic situation created for him by virtue of this lack of princedom, then Lobachevsky found this way out in varied, vigorous activity. The strength of Lobachevsky’s personality triumphed not only over all the difficulties of the time in which he lived, it also triumphed over what is perhaps most difficult for a scientist to survive: over the idea of ​​isolation, over a complete and comprehensive misunderstanding of what was dear to him and most needed - his scientific discoveries and ideas. Having not discovered any contradiction in the geometric system he created, and having received full confidence that there is no contradiction in it and cannot be, Lobachevsky did not give proof of consistency and thereby opened the possibility for the development of subjective assessments of the revolution he had made in geometry. He died without the main work of his life being understood and appreciated.

The right to the free and difficult years of the 19th century was a period of increased development of both the creative, scientific, pedagogical and organizational activities of Lobachevsky, then from the mid-forties, it completely contributed to the present period of inaction for Lobachevsky. The event that brought with us this tragic turning point in his entire life was his dismissal from the post of rector on August 14, 1846. This was due to the wishes of both Lobachevsky himself and the University Council. However, Lobachevsky was temporarily dismissed from his position as a professor in the department of pure mathematics. Lobachevsky himself, perhaps, gave some reason for the last act, since, fulfilling his obligations as trustee of the district since 1845, together with Musina-Pushkin, who was transferred to St. Petersburg, he, transmitting to the ministry the resolution of the University Council on electing him for a new five-year period to the department of mathematics, added that “he is ready to refuse the position in favor of a worthy young man, such as Doctor of Mathematics Popov.”

Lobachevsky's dismissal had all the features of a gross disqualification from service, bordering on direct insult. Such was the official gratitude of the reign of Emperor Nicholas I not only to the greatest Russian scientist of the nineteenth century, but also to our largest university activity.

It is quite understandable that Lobachevsky, for whom his activities in the area of ​​university education, and indeed public education in general, were a large and irreplaceable part of his entire life, perceived his resignation as a heavy, irreparable blow. This blow was especially severe, of course, because it broke out in that era of Lobachevsky’s life, when his scientific work was basically already legal and, therefore, university activities in the natural world became the main content of his life. If we add to this the exclusively activity of Lobachevsky’s character, which means his habit, created over decades, of being a leader in organizing affairs, and not an ordinary participant (a habit to which Lobachevsky truly had the right!), then the size of the καταstrophistantus that broke out over him is quite clear. Lobachevsky was unable to rule due to this καταstrophy. From 1847 onwards, he practically completely withdrew from participation in the affairs of both the educational district and the university. Old age began - premature, but all the more depressing, with progressive signs of paradoxically early decrepitness. His health was declining. He began to become lazy, and by the end of his life he was completely asinine. His last scientific work, “Pangeometry,” was written out of curiosity. Added to all this was the death of his son. Broken by life, a big, blind old man, Lobachevsky died on February 12, 1856.
