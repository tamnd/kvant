Look at the seven shells depicted on these stamps. How many of them are “right” and how many are “left”? “Right” and “left” shells are shells that are twisted like a right-handed or left-handed witt, respectively. Do not forget that the right Witt remains right, even if it is turned upside down. How many “right” and how many “left” shells are there?

The asymmetry of right and left in nature is a very interesting and very strange phenomenon. The shells are twisted mostly in one direction, although there are types of shells in which both types of spiral occur equally. But even in this case, individuals live in some areas, and left ones live in others. In nature, the asymmetry of left and right is sharply expressed. This is visible in the animal world, in the plant world, and even in microbes. The famous deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), which transmits hereditary characteristics of organization, has the symmetry of a right-handed helix.

Among elementary particles there are also “left” and “right” particles. “Right” and “left” are determined by how the particles “spin in flight.” Both types of neutrinos (muon and electron) turned out to be “left-handed,” and both types of antineutrinos turned out to be “right-handed.” Non-neutrinos seem to be “screwed” into space as a left-handed witt, and antineutrinos as a right-handed one. Quanta of light—photons—are also “right” and “left.”

So, somewhat unexpectedly, shells acquire something in common with neutrinos and quanta.

Ya. S., V. L.
