New about gravity

The forces of gravity are natural and present. But science has not yet been able to answer many questions related to the nature of these forces.

Why do all the bodies always just bend towards each other? Why is it not possible to isolate ourselves from the action of squashing forces and create a screen that does not allow them to pass through? What is the nature of the invisible “springs” that bend even very distant bodies? Are there so-called gravitational waves, which A. Einstein theoretically predicted half a century ago? What do “black holes” look like - amazing “stellar tunnels” with completely unusual properties?

Science is persistently attacking this fortress, expanding and deepening our understanding of the nature of gravity and the structure of the Universe. The theory is being improved, amazing experiments are being carried out, and knowledge about this interesting physical problem is being accumulated step by step. The story about these achievements is contained in a small popular science brochure by Professor Ya. A. Smorodinsky “Gravitation”, published by the publishing house “Znanie” at the end of 1975 *).

*) Ya. A. Smorodinsky. Gravity. M., “Knowledge”, 1975. Circulation 53,300 copies, 64 pp., price 11 kopecks.

In the first half of the brochure, the author provides basic information from the field of general relative theory and describes a number of classical experiments to test this theory. The second half of the brochure is devoted to the most modern problems in the theory of gravitation.

The author talks in it about Japanese gravitational waves, an unfinished report on the registration of which was made by the American physicist V.B. Braginsky at Moscow State University) did not confirm this discovery. Thus, gravitational waves still remain a mysterious object of modern physics.

The formula of the law of universal gravitation contains a violation with the masses of the bodies being pressed down and the distance between them and also the gravitational constant γ. Until recently, physicists considered this quantity to be as unchanged as the speed of light in vacuum, the charge of an electron, or Planck's constant. But relatively recently, the famous English theoretical physicist P. Dirac put forward a hypothesis that the value of the gravitational constant decreases over time by approximately 0.5·10⁻¹⁰ of its value per year. At the beginning of 1975, the first experimental confirmation of this very strange circumstance was obtained.

The brochure also describes recent experiments to measure the so-called “red shift” of the spectral lines of light emitted by very massive stars, predicted by general relativity. It turned out that to verify this prediction with an unusually high degree of precision, it is enough to compare the energy of gamma radiation quanta emitted by artificially radioactive iron on the surface of the Earth and at an altitude of 20 m above the Earth! The theory predicts that the energy of these gamma quanta will differ by values ​​of the order of 2·10⁻¹⁵. It is precisely this difference that was discovered in the experiments of the American physicists Pound and Rebka.

In another experiment, using incredibly precise atomic clocks placed on two passenger planes, one flying from west to east and the other in the opposite direction, it was possible to change the clock rate change predicted by general relativity by billions of fractions of a second.

The brochure ends with a description of various theoretical models of “black holes” and a story about these amazing astronomical objects in Japan. The forces of gravity near them are so strong that the light emitted by them cannot move beyond the immediate vicinity of these bodies. It would seem that we are fundamentally deprived of the opportunity to learn about the existence of such objects. But they found out that if a black hole forms a binary system together with any promised star, then by some features of the emission spectrum of this star it is possible to establish the presence of a black hole associated with it. The first candidate for the role of a black hole was star XI in the constellation Cygnus.

The author also talks about hypothetical expressions of black holes and even more amazing objects - “black abysses”, the gravitational fields of which can tear apart stars, cause catastrophic destruction and release incredible amounts of energy.
