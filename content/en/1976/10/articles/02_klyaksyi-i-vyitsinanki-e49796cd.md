BLOTS AND SQUEEZES

All over the world, Polish lace “vycinanki” (single-color and multi-color cut-outs) are famous, which are used as interior decoration, and as motifs for artists in the graphic arts and textile industry. Probably no less famous all over the world are mugs, which, before the advent of ballpoint pens, caused grief among many schoolchildren, writers and business executives.

Let's try to approach the issue of mathematical circles and exercises.

Both the shirt depicted here and the mug on the first page of the symmetrine cover - apparently everyone will agree with this. Which one is “more symmetrical”? Is it not possible to describe this concept of quantity, to introduce a “measure of symmetry” of a figure?

The blot was made like this: ink was splattered onto a sheet of paper, the sheet was folded in half, and then unfolded. The fold line is the axis of symmetry of the blot; when reflected in this axis, the blot will turn into itself.

The pie was made in a similar way, only a sheet of paper was prepared several times, a piece was cut out from the resulting “pie”, and then the sheet was unfolded. Therefore, the print will turn into itself when reflected in any fold line of a sheet of paper.

It turns out that it is precisely the number of such fold lines that describes the degree of symmetry of the figure.

This is discussed in detail in the articles on p. 2-12.
