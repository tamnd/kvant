To the article “In the Focus of the Lens”

1. Integrity at the focal point of the lens
$$I_F = I_0 K.$$
Integrity $I_0$ before focusing kills in inverse proportion to the square of the distance to the source. Therefore, when the Sun moves away to stellar distance $R$, it will be equal to
$$I_{0R} = I_0 \left( \frac{R_0}{R} \right)^2,$$
where $I_0 = 0,14 \text{ вм/cm}^2$ is the integisity of radiation at a distance equal to the radius of the Earth’s orbit $R_0 = 1,5 \cdot 10^{11} \text{ m}$. Substituting $R = 4 \cdot 10^{16} \text{ m}$, we find
$$I_{0R} = 2 \cdot 10^{-12} \text{ вм/cm}^2.$$
The angular size of the Sun, when removed by a distance $R = 4 \cdot 10^{16} \text{ m}$, decreases to $\theta_0 = 1,75 \cdot 10^{-8} \text{ pa/d}$ (focus of the Sun $\approx 7 \cdot 10^8 \text{ m}$). Assuming that the radiation length is $\lambda \approx 4,5 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ cm}$, we find the value of the diffraction blur angle: $\theta_d \approx 2,25 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ pa/d}$.
Thus, $\theta_0 \ll \theta_d$, and therefore, for the emissivity one should use the diffraction formula
$$K = K_d \approx \frac{a^4}{\lambda^2 f^2} \approx 8 \cdot 10^7.$$
Final result:
$$I_F = I_0. R K \approx 1,6 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ вм/cm}^2.$$
2. The radius of the focal spot is
$$r_F \approx f (\theta_0 + \theta_d) \approx f \left( \theta_0 + \frac{\lambda}{a} \right).$$
Focal spot area $S_F = \pi r_F^2 \approx \pi f^2 \left( \theta_0 + \frac{\lambda}{a} \right)^2$. Because
$$K = \frac{S_0}{S_F} \left( S_0 = \pi a^2 - \text{ площадь апертуры} \right),$$
then in general case
$$K \approx \frac{a^2}{f^2 \left( \theta_0 + \frac{\lambda}{a} \right)^2}.$$
3. Integrisity of star radiation before focusing $I_{0R} = 2 \cdot 10^{-12} \text{ вм/cm}^2$ (see Problem 1). Therefore, to obtain integisity $I_F = 100 \text{ вм/cm}^2$ in the focal spot, the emissivity of the lens must be equal to
$$K = \frac{I_F}{I_{0R}} = 5 \cdot 10^{13}.$$
Since the radius of the lens (and therefore the correspondence between $\theta_0$ and $\theta_d$) is unknown, the general formula should be used for the emissivity (see Problem 2)
$$K \approx \frac{a^2}{f^2 \left( \theta_0 + \frac{\lambda}{a} \right)^2}.$$
Substituting the known quantities ($\frac{a}{f} = 0,2, \theta_0 = 1,75 \cdot 10^{-8} \text{ pa/d}, \lambda = 4,5 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ cm}$) into this formula, we obtain
$$5 \cdot 10^{13} \approx \frac{1}{25 \left( 1,75 \cdot 10^{-8} + \frac{4,5 \cdot 10^{-5}}{a} \right)^2}.$$
From here we find the radius of the lens:
$$a \approx 43 \text{ m}.$$
