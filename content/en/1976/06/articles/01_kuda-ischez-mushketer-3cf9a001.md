## Where did the musketeer go?

These brave musketeers, who started exhibition fights in front of de Treville's house, will play a funny joke on you, if you want, of course. Transfer figure to tracing paper, cut out the circle along the dotted line and rotate it a little around the center [or press play. — *Note. ed. site*] - one of the musketeers will disappear without a trace!

Where did he go? The following figure will help you figure this out. Instead of each musketeer, a straight line segment is drawn on it. All segments are congruent, but $[B_1B_2]$ is $\dfrac1{12}$ part of its length closer to the center than segment $[A_1A_2]$; $[C_1C_2]$ is as much closer to the center than $[B_1B_2]$, etc.

⟦figure⟧

Points $A_1$, $B_1$, $\ldots$, $P_1$ lie on a curve called *Archimedes' spiral*. Imagine that a snail $\textit{У}$ is crawling along a rotating gramophone record at a constant speed from the center $T$. Then, relative to the table, it moves exactly along the Archimedes spiral.

Let us now rotate the circle by figure so that point $P_2$ is on the same radius as $O_1$, point $O_2$ is on the same radius as $N_1$, etc. The number of segments will be reduced by 1, but they will all be congruent again, only the length of each of them will increase by $\dfrac1{12}$.

Now do you understand where the musketeer went?
