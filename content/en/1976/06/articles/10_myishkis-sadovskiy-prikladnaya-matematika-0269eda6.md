Applied Mathematics

1. What is applied mathematics?

It is difficult to point out a subject that would have caused columnar debates among people related to mathematics in recent years. While these debates are going on, extensive training of specialists in the field of applied mathematics has begun in all industrialized countries; in particular, in our institutes the specialty “applied mathematics” is becoming one of the most popular. At the same time, many prominent experts claim that there is no applied mathematics at all. . .

What is this area that is not? And what do specialists in this field (or non-field) do?

Matematika began to be applied even before it became a science. Proctype arithmetic and geometric concepts and patterns penetrated into all areas of human activity. During the heyday of the English world, mathematics was formalized as a science with its characteristic deductive method, according to which all its statements are deduced according to strict logical rules from a few initial provisions, accepted without proof, as axioms. From this period the construction of the great edifice of mathematics began.

Along with the development of mathematics, he expanded to the range of its applications. Many important mathematical concepts and methods were created specifically for solving applied problems and only then were analyzed, developed and generalized in a “purely mathematical” way. Certain disciplines - celestial mechanics, theoretical electrical engineering, strength theory, theoretical physics and some others - turned out to be literally “stuffed” with mathematics.

However, until recent decades, relatively complex sections of mathematics were still used only in a small number of traditional fields of science and technology; and even there I often fail to bring complex problems to a practical solution.

In our country, electronic digital computers have radically changed the idea of ​​the possibility of using mathematics. With the help of computers, many previously posed mathematical problems of an applied nature were solved, as well as new problems and problems related to traditional areas of application, as well as to new areas where mathematics had not previously found application. It turned out that not only specific mathematical results, but also the very structure of mathematical thinking brings invaluable benefits in various fields of science, technology, economics, and all human activity. A qualitatively new period in the development of mathematics is beginning—the period of “universal mathematization.”

And so it became very clear that mathematics, in the process of its applications, acquires a number of characteristic features, features that are related to various areas of application and at the same time, sometimes significantly different from the traditional features of “pure” mathematics. As a result, it turned out that specialists in the field of “pure” mathematics were often unable to use mathematics effectively. There is a real need for a new type of specialization.

Applied mathematics is recognized to create, study, develop and improve methods of applying mathematics to problems arising beyond its boundaries. Thus, with a fairly broad view of mathematics, applied mathematics is an integral part of “mathematics in general.” At the same time, one should not imagine in a simplified manner that mathematics can be clearly divided into “pure” and “applied” or that applied mathematics is a mathematical discipline such as algebra or geometry. A wide variety of branches of mathematics can be used, and a huge number of mathematical concepts and methods are both “pure” and “applied” (or “mostly pure”, “mostly applied”, etc.), that is, they can be included in both purely mathematical and applied research. Therefore, it is more correct to talk about pure and applied mathematics not as branches of mathematics, but as its aspects, approaches to it, corresponding to the corresponding themes “mathematics as a whole” and “mathematics as a means.” And it turns out that many concepts, methods, statements in these two approaches not only play a significantly different role, but are sometimes filled with different content (see paragraph 2)!

While there were few disciplines based on the systematic application of mathematics, and the methods of this application themselves were not too complex, there was no need for a large number of specializations - applied mathematicians. Representatives of these disciplines themselves coped with easy mathematical problems, and more difficult and fundamentally new problems were also studied by great scientists such as W. Riemann, A. Poincaré, A. M. Lyapunov and others (who were both pure and applied mathematicians!). However, in the period of general mathematization, when applied mathematical problems become more and more complex and diverse, such a combination of conditions is not enough: there are not enough great scientists to solve all problems!

At the same time, a significant contribution to the solution of such problems from the most diverse areas of human activity can now be made only by specialists with a broad mathematical image, who are proficient in the methods of applying mathematics and who have relevant interests and skills. These are applied mathematicians. Depending on their temperament and exhaustion, they can specialize either in a particular area of ​​application of mathematics, or, being primarily mathematics, they move from one area to another; can work as part of a group or independently.

And finally, we note that, despite the centuries-old history of the application of mathematics and the vast experience of such application to specific problems, the study of the principles and general methods of this application is just beginning. Perhaps some of our readers will take part in the study, systematization and improvement of these applications and methods, that is, in the formation of their own discipline - general applied mathematics.

a) Existence of a solution

The question “does this problem have a solution?” not as simple as it might seem at first glance; otherwise, “pure” and “applied” mathematicians give directly opposite answers to it. Without going into physical and logical jungle, let us illustrate what has been said with the following example.

American students invented the game of hex. Two players play on a four-sided board made of regular hexagons (for example, a tiled floor can be used as a board) with two-colored players: “black” and “striped.” Typically the size of the board is 11x11 hexagons - see figure. Two opposite sides of the board are declared “black”, the other two are “striped”. Players take turns laying out their hexagon tiles: one is “black”, the other is “striped”, and the date can be classified into any free field. Each player is assigned a pair of sides of the board - the same color as his chips. The goal of each player is to connect the sides with their chips in a coherent way.

It is natural to ask the question: is there a winning strategy for the first or second player (“strategy” consists of indicating a move in any already created position)? This question was answered by the famous American mathematician J. Nash: he proved that there is a winning strategy for the first (beginner) player and does not exist for the second. 3. Mathematical models

An applied mathematician deals with mathematical models all the time. Models can be geometric figures, numerical sets, various equations and systems of equations, etc., describing any properties of the studied implementation of an object or phenomenon.

Let's look at a simple example. Let us be interested in the volume of liquid, which, together with a mathematical model, makes it possible to obtain the answer:

4. How to become an applied mathematician?

The training of applied mathematicians in our country is carried out at universities and a number of large higher technical educational institutions. At the same time, there is a certain merging of university and technical education, mainly due to a significant increase in the general theoretical potential of university graduates specializing in applied mathematics (specialty 0647).

At the same time, some features will be preserved in higher educational institutions - a focus on the application of mathematical methods in practical problems that have arisen in accordance with the fields of economics, technical, transport, management, etc. Therefore, along with mathematical courses of a general theoretical and applied nature (1700-2050 hours), the curriculum (4700 hours in total) of the specialty 0647 includes disciplines related to computers (300-600 hours), automated control systems, control theory, physics, mechanics, electrical engineering and radio electronics. In the last semesters, courses related to specialization are taught: methods of applied mathematics, numerical methods, algorithmization of control processes, systems theory, system modeling and some others. Applied mathematics has three main specializations:

1) application of computer technology to solving problems of the national economy - technology, economics, management, planning;
2) ACS software;
3) computer software.

5. What to do at school?

Of course, master mathematics, or applied mathematics - this is primarily mathematics. It is necessary to master the practical method of reasoning, learn to give precise definitions, and train in solving problems of various types, in particular problems that develop logical and algorithmic thinking.

Do not neglect “test” problems, the solution of which requires representative management. Unfortunately, text problems in a school mathematics course, as a rule, have little in common with the problems faced by an applied mathematician. However, the important habit is not to be shy in front of non-mathematical texts!

To fall in love with calculations, starting with the roughest estimates of the order of magnitude and the roughest calculations and ending with the most accurate calculations using all the methods known to you and available computing tools.

Masters physics and certain sections of other disciplines (including those not included in the school curriculum) in which mathematics is used. Learn to think in “physical” language, conduct informal reasoning, learn to use heuristic definitions, conduct heuristic proofs typical of applied disciplines.
