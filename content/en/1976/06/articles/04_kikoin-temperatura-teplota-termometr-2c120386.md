Temperature is one of those not very many physical quantities that a person learned about not only before he learned to study physics, but also before he learned to read and write. Already in early childhood, we learned that the words hot, warm, cold, reflecting our sensations, correspond to different temperature values. We hear that the temperature is high in summer and low in winter, that a healthy person has a body temperature of 36.6 degrees, and if it is higher, then you need to call a doctor...

Because of the attractiveness of the concept of temperature, we usually do not understand how unique this quantity is, how different it is from other attractive quantities, such as length, mass, volume. And the difference here is very significant. This is what it consists of.

If you connect ten rods each 1 m long, placing them one next to the other, you will get a rod 10 m long. Ten masses of 1 kg each will add up to a mass of 10 kg, etc. But if you connect ten bodies, each of which has a temperature of 20 degrees, then we will get a body whose temperature is 20 degrees, not 200. The temperatures of the bodies when they are connected do not add up, as their lengths add up, volumes, masses, etc. A length of 100 m is the sum of one hundred lengths of 1 m, but a temperature of 100 degrees is not the sum of one hundred temperatures of 1 degree each, just as a person aged 15 is not the same as 15 year old children! Temperature, as they say, is not an additive quantity. This is one of the most important features of this quantity.

The method of temperature change is also associated with this feature. To change the length of a body, you need to compare it with another body, the length of which is taken as one. Determining the mass of a body means comparing it with another mass taken as a unit. After all, both the length and mass of a body are equal, respectively, to the sum of the length and masses of its parts.

But the temperature cannot be changed that way. But this means that the temperature value cannot be changed at all, since it cannot be compared with a temperature standard. How does the temperature always change?

A little history

An instrument for measuring temperature, the thermometer, was first invented by Galileo around 1592 (the word “thermometer” itself first appeared in literature in 1624). Way

Of course, the described device does not yet have temperatures. It cannot be used to calculate the numerical value of temperature. Therefore, it should not be called a temperature, but a thermoscope. But if a thermoscope is equipped in one way or another in a cabinet, then it will become a thermoscope. It took almost 150 years to solve this problem. For now, it is important for us that Galileo’s device already contains the principles of temperature change, and this principle has not had to be changed up to the present day: temperature does not change in any way. Temperature-dependent quantities are measured. In Galileo's thermoscope, there was a volume of gas of this magnitude. In a modern hand-held thermometer, the temperature-dependent quantity, by the change of which the change in temperature is judged, is also the volume, but not of gas, but of rutui. Along with the volume of gas of this magnitude, there may be gas pressure (at constant volume), the length of the solid rod, the electrical resistance of the conductor, etc.

A law of nature that cannot be discovered without a thermometer

Already the first imperfect thermometers and even thermoscopes made it possible to discover one of the most important laws of nature - the law of thermal equilibrium. This law seems and seemed so obvious to many that not a single scientist claims to have discovered it and no one can indicate the date of its discovery. This law is that any isolated group (system) of a body by itself, over time, comes to a state in which the temperatures of all bodies in the system are the same. This state is called the state of thermal equilibrium.

It is clear that the law of thermal equilibrium could only be discovered after the invention of the thermometer. On the other hand, the very invention of the thermometer using a thermometer is based on the law of thermal equilibrium. After all, a thermometer is also a body that has a certain temperature. And it changes precisely its own temperature. And if we want to use it to invent a thermometer for some other body, it must be in thermal equilibrium with the thermometer, because in this case the thermometers of the body and the thermometer are the same. That is why, when changing body temperature using a thermometer, it always comes down to waiting for some time - waiting for thermal equilibrium to be established between the body and the thermometer.

A little more history

So, the thermoscope allows at the end of the 16th century. It became a thermometer

approximately in the middle of the 18th century. But what exactly does the thermometer change? What is temperature? The correct answer to this question was a hundred years after the thermometer appeared.

Temperature is a quantity that characterizes the thermal state of the body. We say about cold and hot bodies that they have different temperatures. Consequently, the question of what temperature is is a witness to the question: how does a cold body differ from a hot one?

The first answer to this question was given by Galileo himself. From the easily observed fact that when a cold body is near a hot body, the hot body cools and the cold body heats up, Galileo made a natural choice that something passes from a hot body to a cold one (with the same right one could assume that something passes from a cold body to a hot one!). Galileo believed that this “something” was a special thermal substance. And most researchers of the 17th-18th centuries adhered to the same point of view and called this substance caloric.

According to the theory based on the concept of caloric, a hot body differs from a cold one in that it contains more caloric than a cold one. The establishment of thermal equilibrium according to these ideas consists in the fact that caloric moves from a hot body to a cold one. This means that every body consists, as it were, of two substances - the substance of the body itself (water, copper, iron, glass) and caloric. Each body is a mixture of the substance of the body and the substance of heat (teloride). The word temperature just means mixture. And for many years it was believed that if we change the temperature, we change the concentration of caloric in the body. Hence the name for the units of temperature - degrees. It was in these units that, for example, the water concentration of rasters was measured.

This view of temperature lasted for a very long time - until the end of the 18th century. That's what they said - degrees of warmth.

But at the same time that the “material” theory of heat exists, the tree lights up with flame; when silicon is removed from the fire, a spark appears; iron heats up from being forged in parts and by strong blows...》 From here it was concluded that heat is not a substance, but the movement of small particles from which all bodies are composed (“insensitive particles,” as they were then called).

For over two years there was a struggle between these two theories. For a long time the first theory prevailed, but in the end the second prevailed.

Already in the 18th century, experiments were performed that forced many physicists to change the idea that temperature is the concentration of heat (teloride) in a body.

In 1760, the English physicist and physician Black showed that the same amount of heat supplied to equal masses of different substances leads to different temperature changes. But if temperature really represents the concentration of heat in a body, then the receipt of the same amount of heat should cause equal masses of any substances to produce the same change in temperature. In these experiments, Black discovered, as we now know, that different substances have different heat capacities. But this is incompatible with the caloric theory.

In 1764, the same Black showed that if ice is caught it absorbs

What exactly is temperature, a quantity whose meaning has remained unclear for so long?

This became clear only after the kinetic theory of the structure of matter appeared. And we will understand the meaning of temperature from a comparison of two seemingly completely different things - one of the results of the kinetic theory and the method of measuring temperature.

About molecular chaos and its laws

The basis of the kinetic theory of the structure of matter is the statement that every substance consists of small particles - molecules, continuously and randomly moving. Molecules operate by complex forces of attraction and repulsion. But in gases at ordinary pressures these forces are small. And one can imagine a gas in which there are no interaction forces between molecules at all. Since such a gas can only be imagined, it is called an ideal gas.

An ideal gas is an accumulation of a huge number of molecules moving randomly in all directions at a speed of hundreds of meters per second, constantly copying among themselves and with the walls of the vessel. In this unimaginable chaos (it is possible that the word gas itself comes from the ancient word chaos), however, strict and inviolable laws operate. Due to the fact that in an ideal gas there is no need to take into account the interaction forces between molecules, these laws can be established theoretically. In particular, you can use the laws of mechanics to calculate the gas pressure, that is, the force with which the gas acts on a unit wall of the sauce. This force is the result of impacts of moving molecules on the walls.

The calculation shows that if there are $N$ gas molecules in a state with a volume of $V$, then the pressure exerted by the gas on the walls of the sauce is equal to

$$p = \frac{2}{3} \frac{N}{V} \bar{E},$$

where $\bar{E} = \frac{mv^2}{2}$ is the kinetic energy of xaotic motion, arriving on average per 1 gas molecule. Formula (1) shows that gas pressure is equal to $\frac{2}{3}$ the average kinetic energy of the xaotic movement of molecules contained in a unit volume (after all, $\frac{N}{V}$ is precisely the number of molecules per unit volume).

For the sale of gases, the calculation of pressure is quite complex, but under certain conditions, formula (1) is quite accurate. The smaller the values ​​of $N/V$ and $\bar{E}$, the more accurate it is. In practice, this formula can be used at pressures of about 1 ampere and less.

But what does all this have to do with temperature? After all, temperature is not included in formula (1)!

To understand, let's return to the illegal method of measuring temperature we considered.

The French scientist Romur, like Celsius, attributed the temperature of the melting ice to 0, but to Romur's thermometer water boils at a temperature of 80 degrees.

What is temperature?

So, according to the accepted method for measuring temperature, the pressure $p$ of an arbitrary gas mass $M$, that is, an arbitrary number $N$ of gas molecules, in a vessel with a volume $V$ is proportional to its absolute temperature $T$.

This can be seen from equation (4), which can be rewritten as

$$\frac{p}{T} = \frac{p_0}{T_0}.$$

Relation (5) shows that at a constant volume, the ratio of gas pressure to its absolute temperature is a constant value. On the other hand, gas pressure, as we have seen, is determined by the formula (see (1))

$$p = \frac{2}{3} \frac{N}{V} \bar{E}.$$

Substituting this value $p$ into expression (5), we obtain

$$T = \frac{2}{3} \frac{N}{V} \frac{T_0}{p_0} \bar{E}.$$

Equation (6) refers to a gas in a closed vessel of constant volume. Therefore, the number $N$ of gas molecules remains constant; the relation $\frac{T_0}{p_0}$, as we have seen, is also constant. Consequently, the coefficient for $\bar{E}$ in formula (6) is a constant value for any gas, that is, moles of gas is equal to $\frac{M}{\mu}$; and since there are $N_A$ molecules in each molega ($N_A$ is Avogadro’s number), then the number of $N$ molecules in a mass of $M$ gas is $N = \frac{M}{\mu} N_A$. So, the value of the gas mass is $M$, its molar mass is $\mu$, the volume of the vessel is $V$ and the gas pressure is $p_0$

This is the correspondence between degrees Kelvin and joules per molecule.

In conclusion, we still have to find out what is the connection between temperature and heat - two concepts that for centuries were considered almost synonymous.

It is known that heat is the energy of thermal random movements transferred from one body to another (during heat transfer). It is clear, therefore, that heat is not a quantity characterizing the state of a body. It cannot be said to be contained in the body. temperature characterizes the state of the body, because it is determined by the average kinetic energy of its molecules. It is clear that there is no connection between heat and temperature. We can only say that if two bodies have different temperatures, then the higher is the temperature of mine, which we transfer to the other method. The temperature of a body is a quantity that determines whether a given body will give heat to other bodies or receive it from them. Such a definition of temperature in its time for Maxwell.

Do we need a quantity called temperature?

Temperature as a concept and as a physical quantity appears at the beginning before its true meaning could be understood. But now that it is known, is it worth preserving this seemingly archanic magnitude? Isn’t it better, wherever we are used to talking about temperature, about degrees Kelvin, about degrees Celsius, to replace them with what they really are - the average kinetic energy of a particle, and change it in joules?

It is not difficult to see that there is no reason to abandon temperature and degrees.

Firstly, it would hardly be convenient, for example, for a doctor to consider a patient sick on the basis that the average kinetic energy of his molecule is $6,64 \cdot 10^{-21} \text{ дж}$, instead of talking about a temperature of $38^\circ\text{C}$.

Secondly, replacing degrees with joules can also give rise to misunderstandings. After all, energy, for example, in $100^\circ\text{дж}$, generally speaking, means that work in $100^\circ\text{дж}.$ can be obtained at its expense. Between the temperature of a body is $100 \text{ дж/mолекулу}$ (for temperature this is a fantastic value), this does not mean at all that the same work can be obtained at its expense.
