§ 1. Mini-Euclidean, or rather, finite affine plane

The elements from which the mini-plane is built are “points” and “straight lines”. (This is just a name, which, as the reader will see, is far from the original meaning of these words. D. Hilbert said that “points”, “straight lines” and “planes” could just as well be called “chairs”, “tables” and “circles”.) Point A can lie on line a; the same can be expressed differently: point A belongs to line a, line a passes through point A, line a contains point A. Any other statements about points and lines are valid that can be expressed through this basic relation: the statement “lines a and b intersect” means that there is a unique point that lies on both line a and line b; “lines a and b are parallel” if they do not intersect, etc.

We will call a finite collection of points and lines with the relation “lie on” or “belong to” a finite affine plane if the following three axims are satisfied:

Axima A1. Through any two points of the plane we pass a single straight line.

Fig. 1. a) “Degenerate” geometry (Axim A3 is violated!).

Aksima A2. Through any point not lying on a line we pass a unique line parallel to the given line.

Aksima A3. There are three points that do not lie on the same line.

Explanations of axioms A1–A3

1. The first axiom intersects here Euclidean geometry without changes, the second corresponds to the axiom of parallel Euclidean geometry (the famous Fifth Postulate), the third axiom saves us from expressed geometries, such as the one depicted on Fig. 1, a).

2. In our mini-planimetrine, the following statements have no meaning: “point A belongs to segment BC”, segment AB is congested to segment CD”, “point A lies inside angle BCD”, “angles ABC and DEF are congested” and many other concepts and statements that have been taught to us.

3. All information about any line is contained in the list of points that lie on this line; Therefore, the wave is allowed (although not at all obligatory) to have this view of things: a plane is a finite set of points, straight lines are different sets of these points.

The minimal mini-plane satisfying our three axims contains four points and six lines; its conventional image is shown in Fig. 1, b).
