To the article “Temperature, heat, thermometer”
1. The ratio of the absolute temperatures of water boiling and ice melting according to experimental data is equal to
$$\frac{T_K}{T_0} = 1,3661.$$
On the Fahrenheit scale, the interval between the temperatures corresponding to $T_K$ and $T_0$ is divided into 180 parts-degrees. That's why
$$T_K - T_0 = 1,3661 \cdot T_0 - T_0 = 0,3661 \cdot T_0 = 180,$$
where
$$T_0 = \frac{180}{0,3661} \approx 491,67.$$
In other words, the temperature range from absolute zero to the melting temperature of ice on the Fahrenheit scale must be divided into 491.67 parts-degrees. Since the melting temperature of ice on this scale corresponds to +32°F, then absolute zero should correspond to -459.67°F.
Using similar calculations, we find that on the Reomiopra scale the absolute zero temperature corresponds to -218.52°R.
