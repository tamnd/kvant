Where has the mancatcher gone?

These brave scavengers, who started exhibition matches in front of de Treville's house, will play a funny joke on you, if you want, of course. Transfer figure to tracing paper and cut out the circle along the dotted line. and turn a little around the center - one of the musketeers will disappear without a trace!

Where did he go? The following figure will help you figure this out. A straight line segment is drawn on it together with some kind of man. All segments are congruent, but $[B_1 B_2]$ is $1/12$ part of its length closer to the center than segment $[A_1 A_2]$; $[C_1 C_2]$ is as much closer to the center than $[B_1 B_2]$, etc.

The points $A_1, B_1, ..., P_1$ lie on a curve called the Archimedes spiral. Imagine that a snail $Y$ is crawling along a rotating gramophone at a constant speed from the center $T$. Then, relative to the table, it moves exactly along the Archimedes spiral.

Let us now rotate the circle by figure so that point $P_2$ is on the same radius as $O_1$, point $O_2$ is on the same radius as $N_1$, etc. The number of segments will be reduced by 1, but all of them will be loaded again, only the length of each of them will increase by $1/12$.

Now do you understand where the musketeer went?
