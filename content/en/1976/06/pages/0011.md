What are considered straight lines of a plane? Of course, any horizontal or vertical row of nodes. In addition, any set of (at least two) nodes lying on the same Euclidean line within the square $n \times n$. Let us only agree to “return” any such straight line as shown in figure 8.

Problem 6. Prove that after a certain number of such “jumps” we will again get to the original line and that the number of nodes through which we will “pass” will not exceed $n$.

We will consider the set of nodes that we have “traversed” along one “jumping” line to be a straight line.

This idea actually allows us to construct a plane of order $n$ when $n$ is a prime number. We will consider the arithmetic version of this definition of the plane, leaving the reader to carefully carry out the geometric construction we have outlined.

Problem 7. Show that if $n$ is an arbitrary natural number (not necessarily prime), then in this way we can determine $\varphi(n) + 1$ families of parallel lines, where $\varphi(n)$ is the Euler function (!) ($\varphi(n) =$ the number of natural numbers less than $n$ and coprime to $n$).

So let $n = p$ be a prime number. Obviously, for any integer $m$ there is a unique number from the set of numbers $F_p = \{0, 1, 2, \dots, p-1\}$ equal to the remainder of $m$ divided by $p$; let's denote this number by $\{m\}_p$. Now for any two numbers $a$ and $b$ from the set $F_p$ we define two operations: $p$-addition and $p$-multiplication by putting:

$$a \oplus_p b = \{a+b\}_p, \quad a \otimes_p b = \{a \cdot b\}_p,$$

where $a+b$ and $a \cdot b$ are the total sum and product of the numbers $a$ and $b$. Obviously, for any two numbers $a$ and $b$ from the set $F_p$, the $p$-sum and $p$-product again belong to $F_p$.
