§ 5. Coordinate method

Let $n$ be an integer greater than one. We will try to construct a finite affic plane of order $n$ by introducing coordinates on our set. In doing so, we imitate the great example of Rene Descartes, who systematically reduced precisely geometric problems on a folded plane to corresponding algebraic problems.

So, let's arrange $n^2$ points in the form of a square $n \times n$ in the nodes of the checkered paper, as was done on figure 7 for $n = 5$. Let us renumber the vertical and horizontal rows of nodes with integers from 0 to $n - 1$ and assign to each point in the future of the finite plane a pair of coordinates $(x, y)$, where $y$ is the number of the horizontal, and $x$ is the number of the vertical of the two rows at the intersection of which this point lies.
