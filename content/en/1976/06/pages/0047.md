Such reasoning is typical for all disciplines (physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, etc.), except pure mathematics; so in this respect, applied mathematics is, as it were, at the intersection of mathematics with these disciplines.

Heuristic reasoning can include analogues, numerical and physical experiments, general conclusions based on the analysis of typical cases (this is the so-called “incomplete induction”) and other similar methods of reasoning. 3. Mathematical models

An applied mathematician deals with mathematical models all the time. Models can be geometric figures, numerical sets, various equations and systems of equations, etc., describing any properties of the studied implementation of an object or phenomenon.

Let's look at a simple example. Let us be interested in the volume of liquid, which, together with a mathematical model, makes it possible to obtain the answer:

$$V = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 H - \text{без эксперимента, но и без учета несовершенства реализации страны, или поверхностного натяжения, и т. п.}$$
