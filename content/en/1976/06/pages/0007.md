Theorem A9. Any two lines e and e' of a finite afficine plane contain the same number of points.

□ Each of the lines e and e' intersects at least two of the lines a, b and c Theorem A5. Therefore, one of these lines, say a, intersects both line e and line e'. Let us now establish, using this line a, a one-to-one correspondence between the points of the lines e and e'.

To do this, to each point A on e we associate the point A' on e', which is obtained from the intersection of e with the line a' (A2)*), passing through A and parallel to a. In this case, different points on e will correspond to different points on e' (A2); It is also obvious that any point B' on e' will correspond to some point B on e (see Fig. 3a).

Definition. The order of a finite affine plane is the number of points on any straight plane.

Order is the most important numerical characteristic of the affinity plane. As we will see, it is not difficult to construct a plane; the order is equal to any one specified in advance.

Theorem A10. Through any point of the plane there are the same number of straight lines: for a plane of order n this number is n+1.

□ Let a be an arbitrary straight line; and let point A not belong to line a (A3).
