⟦folio 20⟧

In order not to break with the hundred-degree Celsius scale that has become established over two hundred years, it is still believed that

$$T - T_0 = 100.$$

(3)

The pressure difference at the boiling point of water and its ice is divided into 100 equal parts - degrees. This means that now we assume in advance that the temperature depends linearly on the gas pressure at constant volume. Moreover, we can assume that the temperature of a gas at propio is proportional to its pressure. This assumption, of course, cannot be verified for the same reason that in the above anecdote it is impossible to check the correctness of a clock by a cannon shot, or the timeliness of a shot by a clock. Simply, the temperature change itself is based on the fact that the gas pressure and its temperature are considered proportional to each other.

It is now necessary to assign a value of zero to the temperature of such ice. It can be simply calculated. In fact, if the temperature of a gas is directly proportional to pressure, then the ratio of the gas pressure at the temperature of boiling water and its ice is equal to the ratio of these temperatures, that is

$$\frac{p}{p_0} = \frac{T}{T_0}.$$

(4)

But the ratio on the left side of this equation is 1.3661. Therefore, the right part is equal to this number:

$$\frac{T}{T_0} = 1,3661.$$

From here we get

$$T = 1,3661T_0.$$

Substituting this value for $T$ into equality (3), we find

$$1,3661T_0 - T_0 = 100,$$

and we immediately get

$$T_0 = \frac{100}{0,3661} \approx 273,15.$$

Fig. 4.

This is what distinguishes the new scale from the old Celsius scale: the melting temperature of ice on this scale is not zero, but 273.15 degrees. And the zero temperature is 273.15 (273 for short) degrees below the melting temperature of ice. This is the temperature at which the pressure of an ideal gas would become zero if such a temperature were reached and if the gas still remained a gas at this temperature. Since the gas pressure cannot be less than zero, the temperature on such a scale cannot be negative (variable zeros).

The temperature scale just described (we omit some subtleties in its definition, practically non-existent) is called the absolute temperature scale or Kelvin scale. And the temperature itself, which is absent on this scale, is called absolute temperature. It is designated by the letter $T$ and is expressed in degrees Kelvin (abbreviated °K), so the melting temperature of ice is 273.15 °K, the boiling point of water is 373.15 °K, etc.
