Problem 4 (MSU, Faculty of Geography, 1968). The height of a straight prism is 1, its base is a rhombus with side 2 and acute angle $30^\circ$. A secant plane is drawn through the side of the base, inclined to the plane of the base at an angle $60^\circ$. Find the area of ​​the cutting plane.

On the multigeometric exam, having completed figure 6, for the following approximate “solution” of this problem: “Let $MN$ be the line of intersection of the secant plane $\alpha$ with the face plane $DCC_1D_1$; lowering the perpendicular $MK$ to $AB$, by the theorem on three perpendiculars we obtain that $KD \perp AB$. Posny $\Rightarrow MKD$ is the linear angle of the dihedral angle between the $\alpha$ plane and the base plane, so $\Rightarrow MKD = 60^\circ$. Then

$$MK = \frac{KD}{\cos 60^\circ} = \frac{AD \sin 30^\circ}{\cos 60^\circ} = 2.$$

There is a significant gap in this reasoning: the drawing on which it is based was made under the simple assumption that the plane $\alpha$ intersects the rectangle $DCC_1D_1$. Meanwhile, given the given numerical data, this is not at all obvious; moreover, it is incorrect: in reality, the plane $\alpha$ “emerges” from the given prism through the top of the face, and the point $M$ lies on the continuation of the edge $DD_1$.
