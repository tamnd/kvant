The result of both experiments can be formulated as follows: crystals of rock salt, which are blown into a cylindrical or spherical shape that is not typical for them, tend to restore the shape of a cube - their natural limit, or, as crystallographers say, “natural gas”. The high temperature in these experiments is needed only to give activity to some mechanism of crystal mass transfer necessary for the formation of “natural gas”. crystals, of course, will “prefer” the one of the mechanisms that gives them the opportunity to quickly get rid of the air of a given shape and restore their natural shape.

Why does the crystal strive for natural limitation? It turns out that, among a myriad of other ideas, it is precisely this that provides the lowest surface energy of a crystal for a given volume *). Namely, the minimum energy is characteristic of the equilibrium, i.e., the most natural, state of the crystal. Mathematically, this behavior of the crystal is described by the Curie-Wolfe rule. It can be formed as follows: if a crystal has a volume of $V$, is bounded by $n$ edges, its $i$-th edge has a surface area of ​​$s_i$ and a surface energy of $\alpha_i$, then

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} s_i \alpha_i \rightarrow \text{minimum при } V = \text{const.}$$

In this notation, the arrow indicates the tendency of the crystal to make its total surface energy minimal, provided that the volume is constant. Lukirsky's experience made visible what the symbolic arrow denotes.

The wise Curie-Wolfe rule may seem contradictory to the no less wise statement of geometry, according to which, of all bodies of a given volume, the sphere has the minimum surface. Therefore, if a spherical single crystal tends to reduce the surface energy, it would seem that it should not be limited, since in this case its surface will only increase. The surface will actually increase - the geometry is right. But the energy will decrease, because during limitation, surface areas that have a large specific surface energy (i.e., surface energy relative to a unit surface area) disappear, and surfaces presented in a natural limitation, which have a lower surface energy, develop. And this effect plays a larger role than the energy of increasing the surface. The surface area increases, but the energy decreases!
