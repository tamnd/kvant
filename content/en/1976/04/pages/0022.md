Awesome experience with cumulative flow

We have already talked about how one can observe the formation of a thin, strong scab caused by the concentration of energy in a certain direction (see the article by G. Pokrovsky “Hydrodynamic mechanism in a falling test tube”, «Kvant», 1974, No. 3). This effect is called cumulative (from the Latin situlo - collect), and the formation of a scab is cumulative.

Now we are publishing an article by V. Mayer, in which he talks about another experiment with a cumulative scab, and also proposes several experimental tasks related to the formation of a scab in a falling test tube.

In a simple and elegant experiment by Professor G.I. Pokrovsky, a scab is formed when a vertically falling test tube (from a height of several centimeters), filled with water, hits a hard surface of the table. As water wets the glass, the surface of the water in the test tube forms a rippled meniscus. When the test tube hits the table and the water is in it, it sharply slows down, very large accelerations occur, the liquid becomes as if very heavy and its surface is leveled. The edges go down, and from the central part a small amount of water is thrown into the air in the form of a narrow short-term fountain.
