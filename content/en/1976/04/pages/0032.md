⟦folio 30⟧

Tasks
M376—M380; F388—F392

Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than June 1, 1976 to the address: 113035, Moscow, Zh-35, st. B. Ordynka, 21/16, editorial office of the magazine «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, write the solutions to which problems you are sending, for example: “Problem «Kvant» M376” or “...F388”.
Solutions to problems in each of the subjects (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, please send in separate envelopes. Problems from different issues of the magazine are also assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking your decisions). The conditions of the original problems proposed for publication are assigned in duplicate along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).
After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. The more difficult problems are marked with an asterisk.

M376. a) There are 30 cells in a row. On the very right square there is a white chip, on the very left there is a black one. Each of the two players takes turns moving their piece one space - forward or backward. (You cannot skip a move.) The loser is the one who does not have a move. Who wins: the beginner or his partner?
6) Solve the problem by replacing 30 with N in the conditions.
A. Talalay

M377. Given triangle ABC. Find a point D on side AC such that the perimeter of triangle ABD is equal to the length of side BC.
C. Ochitin

M378. Prove that there are an infinite number of natural numbers that cannot be represented in the form
a) $x^3 + y^3 + z^3$, where x, y, z are integers;
b) $x_1^n + x_2^n + \ldots + x_n^n$, where $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ are integers.
b)* Prove that any rational number can be represented as $x^3 + y^3 + z^3$, where x, y, z are rational numbers.
B. Kolosov

M379. On each of several pieces of randomly shaped paper there is a mark (of arbitrary shape). Let's find a blotter suitable for a given piece if it can be placed inside this piece so that it covers the blotter. Let a set of blotters in the shape of circles of different radii have the following property: for any two given pieces there is a blotter suitable for each of them. Prove that then in this set there is one blotter suitable for all the pieces.
B. Provozvolov

M380* a). A convex figure is given on the plane and inside it there is a point O. To each straight line l passing through the point O, a perpendicular is drawn at the point O and on it, on both sides of the point O, two segments are opened, the lengths of which are equal to the length of the segment obtained when this figure intersects the straight line l. Combine all these segments.
