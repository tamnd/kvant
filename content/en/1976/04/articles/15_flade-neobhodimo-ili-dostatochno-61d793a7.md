Necessary or sufficient?

Frank had long dreamed of having his own railway. And on his birthday, rails, switches, a rheostat and an electric locomotive with four cars were laid out on the table. There was also a house with a sign “Station”. After lessons, Frank invited his friend Misha to visit. First, the guys built the track shown in figure 1. Frank adjusted the speed of the train using a rheostat and moved needle No. 1 (C₁). Misha moved the remaining arrows. The guys noticed that if the C₁ arrow is set to the “left” position (as the train moves), then the train cannot pass the station (red arrows on figure 1). This dependence can be formulated in the form of “if-then”:

(1) if the C₁ arrow is set to the “left” position, then the train arrives at the station.

Or else:

(2) always, if C₁ is set “to the left”, then the train arrives at the station;

(3) position C₁ “to the left” until you see o, so that the train arrives at the station.
