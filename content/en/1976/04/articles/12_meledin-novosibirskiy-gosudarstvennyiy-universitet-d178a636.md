In this issue we present samples of options for a written exam in physics at the Faculty of Physics of NSU in 1975.

At the physics exam, each applicant was offered 6 problems, of which one was qualitative - to explain the physical phenomenon shown by the examiner on the demonstration table. 5 hours were allotted to solve all problems.

Solutions to all problems (except the first) were scored in points, depending on the degree of complexity of the problem (part of the points was given for incomplete solution of the problem). The maximum points are as follows: for task No. 2 - 3 points, No. 3 - 4 points, No. 4 - 5 points, No. 5 - 7 points, No. 6 - 9 points. To receive a positive grade, you had to score at least 4 points. An “excellent” rating was given for scores of 19–21 or higher.

In the options below, after the formation of each problem, the share (in percent) of correct solutions to this problem is indicated in parentheses. The first number is the percentage of correct solutions among the values ​​at the university, the second is among the insignificant ones, and the third number is the average percentage. When calculating percentages, a task was considered solved if it received at least half the maximum score.
