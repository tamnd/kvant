EVOLUTION OF THE TEACHING ABOUT THE STRUCTURE OF ATOMS AND MOLECULES

We present here part of the report that Academician D.S. Rozhdestvensky made on November 15, 1932 at the anniversary meeting of the Academy of Sciences, dedicated to the fifteenth anniversary of the Great October Socialist Revolution. The publication was prepared by V. Leshkovtsev.

There was no era in the history of physics when its development proceeded as quickly and decisively as in the last 15-20 years. At this time, a new and wonderful structure of scientific thought was erected, the solution was the riddle of the structure of atoms...

The construction of the atom began quickly due to the fact that huge reserves of materials had already been brought in and lay in mountains in order, awaiting the builder. The periodic properties of atoms, after centuries of work by Khimki, were put into a complete system by Mendeleev, which still retained almost all of its original appearance, required theoretical justification. Tens of thousands of works on spectral analysis, precise patterns in the spectra seemed to clearly indicate where and how construction should be carried out. But the builder was late. And now we know why.

The best minds of the time certainly tried to put their hands on laying the foundation. Rayleigh wrote about his powerlessness. Ritz, who devoted his entire short life as a deep researcher to the theory of spectral analysis, did not find a way. Undoubtedly, the theorists who became famous at the turn of the century changed their minds much more than was written, since the flatness of attempts was revealed from the very first steps.

Everyone knows the last lecture by J. J. Thomson. He managed to build a model of the chemical atom. Inside a wide, positively charged sphere throughout the volume, electrons are correctly distributed. Their forces of attraction and repulsion are mutually balanced, they are motionless. This is required by classical electrodynamics, since any movement is equivalent to radiation, loss of energy, and the energy reserve in the atom cannot but be built up. This model of the atom successfully emphasized Mendeleev's eightfold periodic system and therefore enjoyed great success. But even she was powerless before the laws of spectral analysis. And Rutherford's experiments on the scattering of α-particles ... showed a familiar picture of light electrons surrounding a heavy nucleus in a number equal to the number of the element.

But why don’t electrons fall onto the nucleus, neutralizing its charge, if their movement is prohibited? Niels Bohr had to cut this dado knot. In July 1913, he proclaimed two principles that not only firmly established the meaning of the atom, but also made a shift in the foundations of physics.

To do this required courage, almost audacity.

Looking ahead, we can say that such a train of thought is natural for us now (in 1932), but then it was not in the minds of even Einstein. On the contrary, he writes: “... the wave theory of light has been overwhelmingly justified in the description of optical phenomena that it, of course, will in no way be replaced by another theory.” And, undoubtedly, the ground had not yet been prepared; The very concept of a quantum of light was resolutive. As is known, the quantum combustor or quantum needle was not immediately accepted in science. They aroused doubt, but did not evoke agreement. It was too difficult to attribute the properties of a wave—interference, diffraction—to a lump or a particle. Bordeaux did not accept the idea of ​​a quantum of light as a particle...

But in the first stage of quantum theory, even the very concept of a light quantum as a particle was of little interest to researchers. All thoughts were occupied with studying the structure of the hydrogen atom and analyzing the effect of electric and magnetic fields on it. In particular, there have been many successful studies of elastic and inelastic collisions of electrons with all kinds of atoms (famous experiments initiated by Frank and Hertz already in 1913). At first they served as one of the main confirmations of Bohr's principles. Here, in particular, it became clear to concreteness how each atom can exist in different states and how an electron incident from the side throws the electron of the atom from level to level, always in a separate jump...

When we observe the absorption and emission of monochromatic light of the same wavelength, then such a process is interpreted by foreign optics as resonance. Light synchronously swings electrons, and they release radiation. Otherwise in quantum theory: an absorbed quantum of light transfers an electron from level to level, from state 1 to state 3 (see Fig.). Having been in state 3, the electron spontaneously jumps back to state 1, losing the same monochromatic quantum that was previously absorbed. This quality is not a successful re-phasing of the old view. But it turns out that there are atoms - and there are many of them, and almost all molecules - where the return comes from state 3 not only to state 1, but also to a completely new state 2. If we add that with a new suitable quantum one can catch an electron in state 3 or 2 and transfer it to state 4 and then in this way throw the electron, like a football, from state to state, then it is clear that the experimenter has completely mastered the concept of the numerous states of atoms: quantum sides of the issue...

Looking ahead a little, we can say that the experiences of the present have concretized Bohr’s actions, that the very purpose of the action has lost its meaning; Bohr's statement is now a clear and necessary conclusion from experiment. This applies especially to the exchange of energy by quanta, the second process, the fruitfulness of which was and remains amazing.
