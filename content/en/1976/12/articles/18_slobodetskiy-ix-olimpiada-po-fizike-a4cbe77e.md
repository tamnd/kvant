Problems of the XVIII International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren
1st day
1. The area of ​​a flat convex quadrilateral is 32 $cm^2$, and the sum of the lengths of two opposite sides and one diagonal is 16 $cm$. Indicate all the values ​​that the line of the other diagonal can take. (Czechoslovakia, 5 points.)
2. Let $P_1(x) = x^2 - 2$, $P_k(x) = P_1(P_{k-1}(x))(k=2, 3, \dots)$. Prove that for any natural number $n$ all roots of the equation $P_n(x) = x$ are real and distinct. (Finland, 7 points.)
3. A Primogols box can be completely filled with single cubes (the edges of the cubes are parallel to the edges of the box). If we fill the box with cubes of volume 2 with edges parallel to the edges of the box, then the maximum number of such cubes will fill only exactly 40% of the volume of the box. Indicate the internal dimensions of all boxes for which this is the case ($\sqrt[3]{2} \approx 1,2599$). (Netherlands, 8 points.)
2nd day
4. Find the maximum value that the product of several natural numbers can take, the sum of which is 1976. (USA, 6 points.)
5. In a system of $p$ equations with $q=2p$ unknowns
$$\begin{cases} a_{11}x_1 + \dots + a_{1q}x_q = 0, \\ a_{p1}x_1 + \dots + a_{pq}x_q = 0 \end{cases}$$
coefficients $a_{ij} \in \{ -1, 0, 1 \}$. Prove that there is a solution $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_q)$ of this system such that all $x$ are integers, for some $j (1 \leq j \leq q)$ $x_j \neq 0$ and for all $j (1 \leq j \leq q)$ $|x_j| \leq q$. (Netherlands, 7 points.)
6. The sequence $\{u_n\}$ is defined as follows: $u_0 = 2$, $u_1 = 5/2$, $u_{n+1} = u_n(u_{n-1}^2 - 2)^{-1}u_1 (n \geq 1)$. Prove that for $n \geq 1$
$$[u_n] = 2^{(2^n - (-1)^n)/3},$$
where $[x]$ is the largest integer not exceeding $x$. (UK, 7 points.)

IX Olympiad
in physics
From July 1 to July 7, the next IX International Physics Olympiad among schoolchildren was held in Budapest. Such Olympiads began to be held in 1967. The first International Olympiad was organized on the initiative of the Polish People's Resource Society. Schoolchildren from five socialist countries took part in it: Bulgaria, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Czechoslovakia. Already in the next 1968, students from the German Democratic Resource Center, the Soviet Union and Yugoslavia joined the participants in the Olympiad from these countries. Since then, our country’s team has been participating in all Olympiads. And in 1970, Moscow hosted students of the IV International Physics Olympiad.
The Olympiad includes solving three theoretical problems and performing one experimental work. The theoretical and experimental tours are held on different days. You are given 5 hours to solve the problem and complete the experiment. All problems are prepared by the country hosting the Olympics. This country also provides verification of the work, but the results of the verification are finally approved by the International Commission, which consists of the leaders of all teams. Let us present a brief solution to the experimental problem.

In the course of performing the work, it is necessary to construct graphs of the temperature of the pure liquid (heated heated) and the liquid together with the crystalline material $X$ (see figure) as a function of time. From figure it is clear that the second graph has an almost horizontal section; this indicates the presence of a phase transition,

i.e., melting of the crystal $X$. Using these graphs, you can determine the melting temperature of the crystal, the specific melting temperature of the crystal, and the specific melting temperature of the crystal. Since the mass of the crystal was small, it was natural to assume that when the test tube was heated, the heat conductor (taking into account the heating of the test tube itself) was the same in all cases. The influx of heat from heating cannot be changed, of course.

The melting point of the crystal is determined directly from the graph.

The specific heat of the crystal had to be calculated by drawing up a heat balance equation for a pure liquid and for a liquid with a crystal. When a pure liquid is heated in a test tube, the equality

$$c_0 m_{\text{ж}} \Delta T = \Delta W t. \tag{1}$$

Here $m_{\text{ж}}$ is the mass of the liquid, $\Delta W$ is the difference between the power of the source (heat influx) and the power of losses (solid removal), $t$ is time and $\Delta T$ is the change in temperature of the liquid during this time. Similarly when heating a liquid with a crystal

$$c_0 m_{\text{ж}} \Delta T_1 + c_{\text{ж}} m_{\text{ж}} \Delta T_2 = \Delta W t_1, \tag{2}$$

where $c_{\text{ж}}$ is the specific heat of the crystal, $m_{\text{ж}}$ is its mass, $t_1$ is the new time and $\Delta T_1$ is the new temperature change.

We denote $\Delta T/t$ and $\Delta T_1/t_1$ by $\alpha$ and $\alpha_1$ respectively. At figure $\alpha$ and $\alpha_1$ are the tangents of the slopes of the graphs of temperature in a test tube versus time (naturally, until the start of melting). Equations (1) and (2) can be rewritten as follows:

$$c_0 m_{\text{ж}} \alpha = \Delta W, \tag{1'}$$
$$c_0 m_{\text{ж}} \alpha_1 + c_{\text{ж}} m_{\text{ж}} \alpha_2 = \Delta W. \tag{2'}$$

Dividing the second equation by the first, we get

$$1 + \frac{c_{\text{ж}}}{c_0} \frac{m_{\text{ж}}}{m_{\text{ж}}} = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha_1},$$

where

$$c_{\text{ж}} = c_0 \frac{m_{\text{ж}}}{m_{\text{ж}}} \frac{\alpha - \alpha_1}{\alpha_1}.$$

Having determined $\alpha$ and $\alpha_1$ from the graphs, it is not difficult to find $c_{\text{ж}}$.

The specific heat of the melt can be determined similarly.

In order to find the specific heat of melting of the crystal $\lambda$, it was necessary to create another heat balance equation:

$$\lambda m_{\text{ж}} = \Delta W t_2, \tag{3}$$

where $t_2$ is the melting time of the crystal. Dividing this equation by equation (1'), we get

$$\frac{\lambda m_{\text{ж}}}{c_0 m_{\text{ж}}} = \alpha t_2,$$

where

$$\lambda = c_0 \frac{m_{\text{ж}}}{m_{\text{ж}}} \alpha t_2.$$

All these calculations could have been carried out more accurately. Firstly, due to uneven heating, the temperature of the liquid and crystal increased slightly during melting (see figure). Therefore, the left side of equation (3) must contain terms $c_0 m_{\text{ж}} \Delta T_2$ and $c_{\text{ж}} m_{\text{ж}} \Delta T_2$ ($\Delta T_2$ - the corresponding change in temperature during time $t_2$). True, it is not entirely clear what would happen in the second term - when a crystal melts, its specific temperature changes. However, they could absolutely be complained about, which did not introduce a big mistake. Secondly, it was possible to explicitly take into account the heat of the test tube itself. To do this, it would be enough, for example, to plot the temperature change over time for a test tube with a different amount of liquid.

When determining the thermal parameters of the crystal $X$, an accuracy of up to 20% was considered satisfactory. In principle, there were several ways to get it. However, many areas of the olympid were unable to do this.
