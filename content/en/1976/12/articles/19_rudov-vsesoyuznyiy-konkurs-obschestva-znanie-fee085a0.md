## All-Union competition of the “Knowledge” society

The Knowledge Society annually holds an All-Union competition for the best popular science book and brochure, in which central and republican publishing houses take part. In 1976, 191 books and 282 brochures published in 1975 were entered into the competition. Among them were 42 books and brochures devoted to various problems of physics, mathematics, astronomy and astronautics. The competition jury, chaired by Academician A.L. Yanshin, awarded some of them with honorary diplomas and cash prizes.

The second degree diploma was awarded to the popular science book by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. S. Shklovsky “Stars: Their Birth, Life and Death”, which we have already described in the review by V. Bronshten “How stars are born, live and die”, published in the eighth issue of our magazine.

The second similar diploma was awarded to the popular science book by Professor N. Ya. Vilenkin “Popular Combinatorics”, published by the publishing house “Nauka” (see the review by M. Smolyansky “Combinatorics - what is it?” in this issue of the magazine).

Another second degree diploma was awarded to a collection of popular science articles entitled “For schoolchildren about modern physics. Physics of Solid State", published by the publishing house "Prosveshcheniye". We spoke about this collection in detail in the twelfth issue of our magazine for 1975 (see the review by B. M. Yavorsky “For schoolchildren about solid state physics”).

Also very interesting is the book by R. S. Guter and Yu. L. Polunov “From the Abacus to the Computer” from the series “The Life of Wonderful Ideas”, published by the publishing house “Znanie”. It tells the history of computing from ancient times to the present day. But not only technology, but also its creators - Moreland, Barrows, Stanhope, Babbage, Aiken and many other mathematicians and mechanics. This book also received a second degree diploma.

The book “Search and Discovery of Planets”, written by doctors of physical and mathematical sciences E. A. Grebenikov and Yu. A. Ryabov (Nauka publishing house), received an incentive diploma. A story about her was published in the previous issue of our magazine. The same award was given to the book by L. A. Levinov and G. V. Sapgir “The Adventures of Kubarik and Tomatik, or Fun Mathematics” (Part 1), designed for preschoolers. The book was published by the Prosveshcheniye publishing house.

The jury also noted several popular science brochures on physics, mathematics and astronomy published by the Znanie publishing house.

The first degree diploma was awarded to the brochure “Modern Culture and Mathematics” written by Academician V. M. Glushkov, Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B. V. Gnedenko and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences A. I. Koronkevich.

The brochure contains three articles on general problems of mathematics. The first article is called “Mathematics in the history of mankind”, the second - “On the sources of new things in mathematics” and the third - “The role of mathematics in modern science”. It was published in the series “Mathematics. Cybernetics".

A second degree diploma was awarded to the brochure of Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences G. T. Zatsepin and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V. S. Berezinsky “Neutrino Astrophysics”. In an unusually lively, almost artistic form, it talks about the complex problems of searching and recording neutrinos - these seemingly completely elusive elementary particles.

Neutrino astrophysics is a new field of modern science. It allows you to solve problems that seem completely impossible. Until recently, astronomers recorded electromagnetic waves and particles produced on the surfaces of celestial bodies. Now, thanks to neutrino astrophysics, they are able to look into the depths of these bodies. After all, neutrinos are born inside stars as a result of thermonuclear reactions occurring there. Since neutrinos are able to pass through enormous thicknesses of matter without collisions, they carry with them undistorted information about the processes occurring inside stars. We still don’t know what the Earth’s interior looks like at a depth of more than two tens of kilometers. And neutrino telescopes will bring us information about the state of the stellar interior.

The same diploma was awarded to the brochure of a member of the editorial board of our journal «Kvant» Professor Ya. A. Smorodinsky “Gravitation”, which we talked about in the tenth issue of our journal (see review by V. Leshkovtsev “New about gravity”). It was published in the Physics series.

Three brochures were awarded incentive diplomas.

The brochure of Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences A. G. Postnikov is called “The culture of doing mathematics (from the notes of a scientist).” It represents a phenomenon that is extremely rare in our book world. Using his rich personal experience, as well as the experience of scientists known to him, the author explores numerous problems in the psychology of mathematical creativity. How to correctly assess your abilities, how to develop and improve them, how to better organize your studies - this brochure awakens many interesting thoughts even in those readers who do not intend to connect their lives with mathematics. Well, those who love mathematics should definitely read it. It was released in the series “Mathematics. Cybernetics".

The second brochure - “Variable Stars” - was written by a member of the editorial board of our journal, Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences Yu. N. Efremov. It was published in the series “Cosmonautics. Astronomy".

The third brochure - “Liquid Crystals” - was written by Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences I. G. Chistyakov and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences L. K. Vistin. It was published in the series “New in Life, Science, and Technology.” It talks about the unusual state of many organic substances, in which the fluidity characteristic of liquids is combined with the ordering of molecules inherent in solid crystals. The authors describe the physical properties of liquid crystals, as well as their practical applications.
