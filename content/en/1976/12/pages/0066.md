Weight-bearing competition of the Knowledge Society
The Knowledge Society annually holds a weighty competition for the best popular science book and brochure, in which central and republican publishing houses take part. In 1976, 191 books and 282 brochures published in 1975 were entered into the competition. The average included 42 books and brochures devoted to various problems of physics, mathematics, astronomy and astronautics. The jury of the competition, chaired by Academician A.L. Yanshin, awarded some of them with honorary diplomas and prizes.

The second degree award was awarded to the popular science book by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences I. S. Shklovsky “Stars: Their Birth, Life and Death,” which we have already described in V. Bronshten’s decision “How stars are born, live and die,” published in the recent issue of our magazine.

The second similar diploma was awarded to the popular science book by Professor N. Ya. Vilenkin “Popular Combinatorics”, published by the publishing house “Nauka” (see the decision of M. Smolyansky “Combinatorics - what is it?” in this issue of the journal).

Another second degree diploma was awarded to a collection of popular science articles entitled “For schoolchildren about modern physics. Physics of the creative body”, published by the publishing house “Prosveshcheniye”. We described this collection in detail in the 2nd issue of our magazine for 1975 (see the decision of B. M. Yavorsky “For schoolchildren about the physics of the creative body”).

Also very interesting is the book by R. S. Guter and Yu. L. Polunov “From the Abacus to the Computer” from the series “Life of Great Ideas”, published by the publishing house “Znanie”. It tells the history of computing from ancient times to the present day. But not only technology, but also its creators - Moreled, Burrows, Stanhope, Babbage, Aiken and many other mathematical sciences, E. A. Grebenkov and Yu. A. Ryabov (Nauka publishing house). A story about her was published in the previous issue of our magazine. The same award was awarded to the book by L. A. Levinov and G. V. Sapgir “The Adventures of Kubarika and Tomatik, or Fun Mathematics” (Part I), intended for preschoolers. The book was published by the Prosveshcheniye publishing house.

Dill of the first degree was awarded to the collection “Modern Culture and Mathematics”, written by Academician V. M. Glishkov, Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B. V. Gnedenko and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences A. I. Koronkovich.

The brochure contains three articles on general problems in mathematics. The first articles are called “Mathematics in the history of man”, the second - “On the sources of new things in mathematics” and the third - “The role of mathematics in modern science”. It was published in the series “Matematika. Cybernetics".

A second degree diploma was awarded to the brochure of Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences G. T. Zatseplina and Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences V. S. Berezinsky “Neutrino Astrophysics”. In a necessarily lively, almost artistic form, it talks about the complex problems of searching for and registering neutrinos - these seemingly completely elusive elementary particles.

Neutrino astrophysics is a new field of modern science. It allows you to solve problems related to completely elusive elementary particles. An astrophysicist's phone gives them the opportunity to look into the depths of these bodies. After all, neutrinos are born inside stars as a result of thermonuclear reactions that occur there. Since neutrinos are able to penetrate enormous thicknesses of matter, they carry with them undistorted information about processes occurring in the interior.
