F385. A large thin conducting plate of area S and thickness d is placed in a uniform electric field E, redistributing the plate. How much heat will be released in the conductor if the field is turned off?

When a conducting plate is introduced into an electric field, under the influence of this field it produces a redistribution of free charges in the plate. On opposite sides of the plate, perpendicular to the direction of the external field, charges of opposite signs accumulate. Inside the plate the field strength is zero. This means that free charges distributed over the surfaces of the plate create a field, the intensity of which is equal to -E on the inside of the plates and zero on the outside.

Immediately after the disappearance of the external field of the internal ones, only the field of surface charges remains, which has the energy

$$W = \frac{E^2}{2} \varepsilon_0 Sd.$$

Under the influence of this field, it produces a “reverse” redistribution of charges throughout the entire volume of the plate. In this case, all the energy of the electric field will be released in the form of heat:

$$Q = \frac{E^2}{2} \varepsilon_0 Sd.$$

F386. At the vertices of an isosceles right triangle, the leg of which is equal to a, there are identical metal balls of radii r (r≤a). The balls are each charged with charge q. Balls 1 and 2 connect the conductor, then the conductor is removed. Then do the same with balls 2 and 3 and, finally, with balls 3 and 1. What charges are placed on the balls?

The solution to the problem depends on which of the balls is at the vertex of the right angle.

It is more convenient for Pozny to solve two auxiliary problems first. The first problem is about the distribution of the total charge Q of two small balls located at the ends of the leg. The balls connect the conductor in the presence of the same ball with a charge Q0 located at the third vertex of an isosceles right triangle (Fig. 5). Let the charges of the balls become Q1 and Q2 after connection. The connected balls will have the same one, which can be defined according to the principle of superposition as the sum of the potentials created by each charge separately. The condition r≤a allows you not to worry about exactly how the charges Q1, Q2, Q0 are distributed over the surface of the balls.

Control system

$$\frac{Q_1}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon r} + \frac{Q_2}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon a} + \frac{Q_0}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon a} = \frac{Q_2}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon r} + \frac{Q_1}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon a} + \frac{Q_0}{4\varepsilon_0\varepsilon a\sqrt{2}},$$

$$Q_1 + Q_2 = Q$$

leads to a solution

$$Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2} - \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})r}{4(a-r)} Q_0 = \frac{Q}{2} - kQ_0,$$

$$Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2} + \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})r}{4(a-r)} Q_0 = \frac{Q}{2} + kQ_0,$$

Where

$$k = \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})r}{4(a-r)} \approx \frac{(2-\sqrt{2})r}{4a}$$ (since r≤a).
