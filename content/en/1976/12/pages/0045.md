For the body to be in equilibrium, it is necessary and sufficient that the vector sum of the forces applied to the body and the algebraic sum of the moments of these forces relative to any point $O$ of the plane be simultaneously equal to zero:

$$\sum_i F_i = 0,$$
$$\sum_i M_O(F_i) = 0.$$

When the first condition is met, the acceleration of the body's center of mass is zero; when the second condition is met, the angular velocity of rotation of all points of the body remains unchanged and, in particular, if the body is buried, the angular velocity of its points remains equal to zero *).

Vector equality (*) can be represented as two folding ones:

$$\sum_i F_{ix} = 0,$$
$$\sum_i F_{iy} = 0,$$

where $F_{ix}$ and $F_{iy}$ are respectively projections of forces $F_i$ on the coordinate axes $X$ and $Y$, lying in the plane of action of the forces.

When solving problems to obtain an equation in the simplest form, it is recommended to draw one of the coordinate axes perpendicular to the largest possible number of unknown forces, and to locate the moments of the forces relative to the point at which the largest possible number of unknown forces intersect.

If the forces acting on the body located in the plane are mutually parallel, then the number of the equilibrium equation will be reduced to two. Problem 2

Ten balls are strung on a weightless rod divided into 10 equal parts, the masses of which are successively equal to 1, 2, 3, ..., 9, 10 $z$ so that their centers coincide with the division points (Fig. 4). Determine where the rod should rest on the support in order to be in balance.

Solution. To satisfy equation (*) for the equilibrium of the rod, it is necessary that at the point of support the rod is acted upon by a support force directed upward and equal in abscissa value

$$R = \sum F_i = \sum m_i g = 55 \ g \ (\delta u n).$$

In order for equation (**) of equilibrium to be satisfied, the fulcrum must be at such a distance $x$ from point $O$ of the rod that $\sum(m_i g \cdot x_i) = -Rx = 0$, where $x_i$ is the distance from point $O$ to the ball with mass $m_i$:

$$a \cdot m_1 g + 2 a \cdot m_2 g + \dots + 10 a \cdot m_{10} g = Rx.$$
