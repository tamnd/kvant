⟦folio 13⟧

True to the conditions unfavorable for the pursuit of science, and even in complete isolation from all world science, D. S. Rozhdestvensky found the basic ideas for the further development of Bohr's theory. In a scientific report on the topic “Spectral analysis and structure of the atom,” which he read in 1919, at the celebration of the first anniversary of the State Optical Institute, Rozhdestvensky clearly formulated the main principles, thanks to which Bohr’s theory was applied first to the spectra of alkali metal atoms with one electron in the outer shell, and then to the spectra of any atom.

As they later found out, the same ideas were used by Arnald Sommerfeld, Erwin Schrödinger and a number of other outstanding foreign physicists in the development and improvement of Bohr's theory.

Implementing his ideas, Rozhdestvensky carried out a classical analysis of the spectrum of nanomagnesium, the lines of which are close to those of the neutral atom of the alkali metal sodium. This work served as a model for numerous analyzes of the ion spectra of various elements carried out in subsequent years.

D. S. Rozhdestvensky made a major contribution to the theory of microcospa. Invented back in the 17th century, the microcosop soon found many applications. However, the theory of this important optical device was created only at the beginning of our century. Geometric optics allows you to calculate the magnification of a microscope. But she cannot answer the question of what are the maximum body sizes that can still be seen using a microscope. (After all, we know, for example, that an ordinary microscope does not allow us to see atoms and molecules.) It also cannot tell us anything about the characteristics and origin of diffraction and interference fringes,

To do this, we need a theory that takes into account the interaction of light waves reflected by objects viewed in the microcosmos. The author of the first such theory was the German optical physicist Abbe. But Abbe's theory applied to a special phenomenon when the object in question is illuminated by coercive rays. Such rays have the same wavelength and a constant oscillation phase difference. In practice, this servant is usually not realized. Another limiting concept in the microcosm theory was considered by the Soviet physicist Academician L.I. Mandelstam. In this case, the object being studied itself glows with incoherent light. Both theories took into account only diffraction phenomena. D. S. Rozhdestvensky created the theory of a microcospa operating under ambient lighting conditions. It also took into account interference effects. At the same time, he found practical ways to increase the efficiency of microcosmoscopic research.
