It should be regretted that the length and nature of the book did not make it possible to give a general proof of this remarkable theorem.

Next, we examine the theorem about various representatives (or, as it is also called, the theorem about village weddings), which is given with a complete proof. This theorem gives the author a reason to talk about graphs and combinatorial problems in graph theory. The chapter ends with the more well-known material of graph theory - Euler routes (a traversal of a graph in which one edge is not traversed twice), Hamlutonian paths (closed paths passing through each vertex of the graph once), the problem of four colors, etc. Like all subsequent chapters, it contains many varied problems (in total, the book contains more than 400 problems). Unfortunately, most of the problems in the second chapter are of an optimization nature. They may give the impression that the tasks of subsequent chapters will be difficult, although this is not at all the case - many of the tasks of the third, fourth and fifth chapters are quite simple.

The third chapter outlines the “classical combinatorics” - placements, permutations and combinations, both with and without repetitions. In presenting these questions, the author systematically uses the language of set theory and the concept of tuple. This allowed him to define basic concepts. Although, as in the entire book, the presentation is carried out by analyzing more or less intricate problems, in fact this chapter is a textbook on combinatorics, written quite clearly and systematically. The transition to a theory-theoretic language allows us to analyze the inclusion and exclusion formula (overlapping formula), which allows us to find the number of elements in the union of several sets, if the number of elements in each set is known, as well as in the intersection of any set of these sets.

The next chapter is called “Combinatorics of layouts and partitions.” These issues were discussed in the author’s previous book, but now the presentation has become clearer and more compact. New material has appeared, for example, it was told about Bell numbers, that is, about the number of decomposing n different objects into m indifferent boxes so that all the boxes are non-empty.

There was also a chapter in the previous book “Combinatorial problems with restrictions”. But now, together with an analysis of individual, almost unrelated problems, a presentation of general concepts and formulas is given. In particular, the author gives a general formula for calculating the so-called rook multiprices. He restores historical justice, noting that dade numbers (the number of ways to put n rooks on the board of a given form so that no pair of rooks can take each other) were introduced and studied by the Soviet mathematician S. E. Arshon ten years before the American mathematicians Kaplansky and Riordan began to borrow these numbers. The chapter ends with the problem about King Arthur's maxim and the queue at the cash register.

The last, sixth, chapter of the book talks about combinatorial problems, connecting the entry with group theory - about the combinatorics of orbits. A typical problem of this kind is to find the number of ways to paint the vertices of a cube in k colors. The general method for solving such problems is based on some concepts of group theory*). Although the author managed to find a simpler presentation of this method than was found in most books on combinatorics (this method, as far as we know, has never been presented in popular literature), the end of the chapter is not proven in the book.

Thus, overall it turned out to be a very interesting and lively written book on combinatorics. However, we have to admit that the problems given in the book are given not only without solutions, but also without answers.

N. Ya. Villenkon's new book on combinatorics will be of interest to high school students interested in mathematics, teachers and everyone who deals specifically with combinatorial problems. In 1976, she was awarded second prize at the All-Union competition for the best work of popular science literature.

Currently, the book's circulation is completely sold out. We hope that the Nauka publishing house will release a second edition of this useful book, expanding its scope, which makes it possible to include solutions to the problems given in it and other material, as well as based on King Arthur and the line at the cash register.

*) You can read about groups in «Kvant», 1976, No. 10.
