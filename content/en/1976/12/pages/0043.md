In rigid body statics, two main problems are considered:

1. Determination of the conditions under which a body under the influence of forces can be in equilibrium.
2. Finding the forces acting on the body (in most cases, coupling reactions) when the body depends on being in equilibrium.

We will limit ourselves to considering only such systems in which all the forces acting on the body lie in one plane - the so-called plane systems of forces.

Any motion of a rigid body can be represented as a superposition of two types of motion - translational and rotational (around a certain axis). The body will remain at rest if there is no cause leading to translational motion or rotation.

When a body moves forward, we can consider the movements of one point of the body—its center of mass.
