d) If the function $f(x)=x^2+3x+17$ is periodic and $T\ne0$ is its period, then for any $x\in D(f)=\mathbb R$ the relation $f(x+T)=f(x)$ must be satisfied, i.e.

$$
(x+T)^2+3(x+T)+17=x^2+3x+17,
$$

or

$$
2Tx+T^2+3T=0.
$$

Substituting $x=0$ and $x=1$ here, we get two equations that the number $T$ must satisfy:

$$
T^2+3T=0\quad\text{и}\quad T^2+5T=0.
$$

Subtracting the first equation from the second, we obtain: $2T=0$, and $T=0$, in contradiction with the condition $T\ne0$. This means that $f$ is non-periodic.

e) The function $f(x)=x^2+\sin x$ vanishes at $x=0$ and cannot be equal to zero at $|x|>1$ (because then $x^2>1$ and $\sin x\geqslant-1$, whence $f(x)=x^2+\sin x>1+(-1)=0$). Consequently, $f$ is non-periodic [if $f$ were periodic with the period $T$, then $f(x)=0$ for $x=nT$ (explain!), i.e. $f$ would vanish for arbitrarily large values ​​of $x$, and this contradicts the above].

f) The function $f(x)=\sin\sqrt{|x|}$ is defined everywhere, therefore, of course, the periodicity condition (A) is satisfied. Let us assume that $f$ is periodic with period $T$. Let us consider the zeros of the function $f$, i.e. $x$ such that $f(x)=0$. We have:

$$
\sin\sqrt{|x|}=0\Leftrightarrow\sqrt{|x|}=\pi n,
$$

where $n\in\mathbb Z,\ n\geqslant0$,

therefore $|x|=\pi^2n^2$, that is, $x=\pm a_n=\pm\pi^2n^2,\ n=0,1,2,\ldots$ Note that the distance along the number axis between adjacent zeros $a_n$ and $a_{n+1}$ (or $-a_n$ and $-a_{n+1}$) is

$$
a_{n+1}-a_n=\pi^2(n+1)^2-\pi^2n^2=\pi^2(2n+1)
$$

and tends to infinity as $n$ grows.

On the other hand, from the assumed periodicity $f$ it follows that

$$
f(nT)=f(0)=0
$$

And

$$
f(\pi^2+nT)=f(\pi^2)=f(a_1)=0,
$$

that is, the function $f$ has infinitely many zeros $nT$ and $\pi^2+nT$ at the same distance $\pi^2$ from each other. Obviously this contradicts the above (explain!). Therefore, the function $f$ is non-periodic. [Try to prove the non-periodicity of the function $f(x)=\sin(x^2)$ in a similar way.]
