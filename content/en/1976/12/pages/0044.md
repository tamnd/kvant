Problem 1

A block lying on a smooth horizontal plane has two forces applied at its two points, equal in magnitude and directed in opposite directions (Fig. 2). (Such a system of forces is called a pair of forces.) Relative to what point will the block rotate?

Solution and e. Olt suggests that the block will rotate. But since the sum of the forces acting on the body is equal to zero, its center of mass will remain at rest, and a pair of forces will cause the bar to rotate around an axis passing through the center of mass and perpendicular to the plane in which the forces lie.

In the general case, when the sum of the forces applied to the body is equal to zero ($\sum F_i = 0$), and the lines along which the forces act do not intersect at one point, the center of mass maintains the state of motion unchanged, in particular, it will end, but the body itself will rotate under the force passing through the center of mass.

To characterize the rotational action of force in statics, a new concept is introduced - moment of force.

Consider a rigid body capable of rotating in a vertical plane around a horizontal axis passing through the point $O$ (Fig. 3). Let us assume that forces $F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4$ are applied to the body, the lines of action of which lie in the vertical plane. Olt shows that the rotational effect of each of these forces depends not only on the magnitude of the force, but also on the distance from the axis to the line of action of the force. This distance is called the leverage.

The moment of force relative to the axis is called the algebraic quantity

In the case of a plane system of forces, instead of the moment of force relative to an axis perpendicular to the plane of action of the forces, we can talk about the moment of force relative to a point, meaning the point of intersection of this axis with the plane.

External forces, for example $F_1$ and $F_4$ on figure 3, can rotate the body around an axis in opposite directions, so the moment of force is assigned the sign “+” or “-”. It is conventionally accepted that the moments of forces tending to rotate the body counterclockwise are with the “+” sign, and clockwise moments are with the “-” sign (in accordance with the rules for reporting angles).

The moment of a pair of forces is equal to the product of the model of one of the forces taken with the corresponding sign and the distance between the lines of action of the forces (arm).

A pair of forces, without changing its rotational action on a given rigid body, can be transferred and arbitrarily rotated in the plane of action of the force.

By introducing the moment of force, it is possible to formulate general equilibrium conditions for a plane system of forces.
