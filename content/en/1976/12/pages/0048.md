6) From (3) it follows that the force $T$ will be minimal when the value of the denominator ($\cos \alpha + \mu \sin \alpha$) is maximum. Denoting $\mu$ by $tg \varphi$, we can transform the denominator as follows:
$$\cos \alpha + tg \varphi \sin \alpha =$$
$$= \frac{1}{\cos \varphi} (\cos \alpha \cos \varphi + \sin \alpha \sin \varphi) =$$
$$= \frac{1}{\cos \alpha} \cos (\alpha - \varphi).$$

This expression is maximum at $\alpha - \varphi = 0$, hence $\alpha = \varphi = \arctg \mu$.

Considering that $\sin \alpha = \frac{tg \alpha}{\sqrt{1 + tg^2 \alpha}}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + tg^2 \alpha}}$, substituting the found value $tg \alpha$ into (3), we obtain
$$T_{\text{min}} = \frac{\mu mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}.$$

Problem 6

The staircase rests on a vertical wall and a horizontal floor (Fig. 8). The center of gravity of the staircase is in the middle of its length. The friction coefficients at points $A$ and $B$ are respectively equal to $\mu_1 = 0,5$, $\mu_2 = 0,4$. Determine the smallest angle of inclination of the ladder to the horizon at which it can remain in equilibrium.

Solving equation (1)–(5) jointly, we obtain for $\alpha_{\text{min}}$:
$$\alpha_{\text{min}} = \arctg \frac{1 - \mu_1 \mu_2}{2 \mu_1} = 38,6^\circ.$$
