75 YEARS OF NOVELEVSKY
PRIZES

Every year since 1901, on December 10, the Swedish Copol in a solemn ceremony presents the Nobel Prize winners. These international prizes got their name from their founder Alfred Nobel, a Swedish chemical engineer, inventor and industrialist, who brought all his capital for these purposes. Five Nobel Prizes are awarded annually for work in physics, chemistry, physiology or medicine, literature, as well as for activities to strengthen peace between nations. In addition, in 1968, the Swedish Bank established an annual Nobel Memorial Prize for work in the field of economic sciences. The Nobel Prizes in the field of science have the highest prestige. Among the laureates in physics are the most prominent scientists from different countries, such as Roentgen, Lorentz, Becquerel, Pierre and Marie Curie, Planck, Einstein, Bohr, de Broglie, Heisenberg, Dirac, Fermi. In 1958, the Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Soviet scientists I. E. Tamm, I. M. Frank and P. A. Cherenkov for the discovery and announcement of the reduction phenomenon known as the “Cherenkov effect.” For research on the theory of condensed matter, especially liquid gel, the 1962 Physics Prize was awarded to L. D. Landay. N. G. Basov and A. M. Prokhorov, together with the American scientist C. Townes, received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1964 for functional research in the field of quantum radiophysics.
