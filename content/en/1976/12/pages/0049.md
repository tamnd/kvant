Let us construct the lines of action of the total reaction forces $R_A$ and $R_B$ at points $A$ and $B$ (Fig. 9). To do this, let us set aside the angle $\varphi_1 = \arctg \frac{F_A}{N_A} = \arctg \mu_1$ (friction angle) from the normal to the floor at point $A$
in a counterclockwise direction (direction of possible rotation under the action of force $R_A$).
This angle is normal to the floor at point $A$ along the line of action of force $R_A$. In a similar way we obtain the line of action of the force $R_B$.
Since the ladder is in equilibrium under the action of three forces:
$mg, R_A, R_B$, then the lines of action of these forces intersect at one point (point $C$). The point of intersection of the lines of action of forces $mg$ and $N_B$ is designated $D$. Consider triangle BDC. From this triangle we have
$$\frac{CD}{BD} = \tg \varphi_2 = \mu_2.$$ (6)
But $BD = l \cos \alpha, CD = CE - DE = CE - BO$. From triangle CEA there is
$$CE = EA \text{ сtg} \varphi_1 = l \cos \alpha \text{ сtg} \varphi_1 = \frac{1}{\mu_1} l \cos \alpha.$$
From triangle BOA
$$BO = 2l \sin \alpha.$$

Therefore, $CD = \frac{1}{\mu_1} l \cos \alpha - 2l \sin \alpha$. Substituting the values ​​$CD$ and $BD$ into (6), we obtain
$$\frac{1}{\mu_1} l \cos \alpha - 2l \sin \alpha = \mu_2,$$
or
$$\frac{1}{\mu_1} - 2 \tg \alpha = \mu_2.$$
From here
$$\alpha = \arctg \frac{1 - \mu_1 \mu_2}{2 \mu_1}.$$
