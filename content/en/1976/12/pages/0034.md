Solving two auxiliary problems allows you to quickly find a solution to the three main ones. Distance, for example, the case when ball 1 (Fig. 6) is at the vertex of a right angle. Then connector $q_1 = q, q_2 = q, q_3 = Q_0 = q$ to all.

The total charge of balls 1 and 2 is $Q = 2q$. The charge at the third vertex is $Q_0 = q$. Therefore, after connecting balls 1 and 2 it will have:

$$Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2} - kQ_0 = q - kq, Q_2 = q + kq, q_3 = q.$$

Now let's remove the conductor between balls 1 and 2 and connect balls 2 and 3. The total charge of balls 2 and 3 before connection 2-3 is

$$Q = (q + kq) + q = 2q + kq.$$

These balls are located at the ends of the hypotenuse. Therefore, after connection 2-3, the charge distribution will be as follows:

$$q_1 = q - kq, q_2 = \frac{Q}{2} = q + \frac{k}{2} q; q_3 = q + \frac{k}{2} q.$$

The total charge of balls 3 and 1 after removing the conductor is equal to

$$Q = \left( q + \frac{k}{2} q \right) + \left( q - kq \right) = 2q - \frac{k}{2} q,$$

the charge at the last vertex (2) is $Q_0 = q_2 = q + \frac{k}{2} q$. Therefore, after connecting 3-1, the charges will be distributed like this:

$$q_1 = \frac{Q}{2} - kQ_0 = \left( q - \frac{k}{4} q \right) - k \left( q + \frac{k}{2} q \right) = q \left( 1 - \frac{5}{4} k - \frac{1}{2} k^2 \right),$$

$$q_2 = Q_0 = q \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} k \right),$$

$$q_3 = \frac{Q}{2} + kQ_0 = q \left( 1 + \frac{3}{4} k + \frac{1}{2} k^2 \right).$$

Substituting the value $k \approx \frac{(2 - \sqrt{2}) r}{4a}$ and neglecting terms containing the values ​​$\frac{r}{a}$ to a power higher than the first, we finally obtain:

$$q_1 \approx q \left( 1 - \frac{5(2 - \sqrt{2}) r}{16a} \right),$$

$$q_2 \approx q \left( 1 + \frac{(2 - \sqrt{2}) r}{8a} \right),$$

$$q_3 \approx q \left( 1 + \frac{3(2 - \sqrt{2}) r}{16a} \right).$$
