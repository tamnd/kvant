A similar phenomenon is observed when light passes through sodium vapor (Fig. 1) and other substances whose absorption lines lie in the visible region of the spectrum. This phenomenon is called anomalous dispersion. The theory of anomalous dispersion was created by the German physicist Sellmeyer back in 1871, but for a long time there was no reliable experimental verification. Determining the refractive index near absorption lines has proven to be extremely difficult due to the strong absorption of light.

D. S. Rozhdestvensky developed an original method for accurately determining the refractive index not only near absorption lines, but even inside the lines themselves. The data he obtained on changes in the refractive index near the absorption lines of sodium vapor confirmed the validity of Selmeyer's theory. But this work had an even more important result. Rozhdestvensky's method makes it possible to accurately determine the intensity of double spectral lines (doublets), or, as they said at that time, the ratio of the numbers of selectors emitting each of these lines. It turned out that for many substances the ratio of the intensities of neighboring lines in dublets is expressed in small integers. Rozhdestvensky determined the values ​​of these ratios for most doublets of whole metals and showed that they do not depend on either temperature or vapor pressure, but are determined by the properties of the atoms themselves and the substances under study. In his doctoral dissertation, he wrote about it this way: “The simple relationships found must, very much so, correspond to some very simple feature in the structure of atoms and molecules. But what this simplicity consists of is still impossible to decide with modern data on atoms.” Let us remind readers that this was written at the very beginning of 1915, when the atom theory proposed by Niels Bohr was still taking its first steps.

As quantum mechanics later showed, the intensity ratio characterizes the probabilities of electron transition from one orbit of an atom to another and is a very important characteristic of an atom.
