⟦folio none⟧

The intricate pattern on the magazine cover consists of four identical curves with a common origin located in the center. These curves are drawn using a computer. The beginning of one curve is reproduced here. The rule by which this curve is constructed is quite simple: starting from a segment $AB_1$ of length $d$ (on the first page of the cover $d=1$ mm), the following procedure is sequentially carried out: the already constructed part of the line $AB_k$ is rotated relative to the point $B_k$ by $90^\circ$ clockwise.

This creates a new piece of line $B_k B_{k+1}$; then the right angle with vertex $B_k$ is rounded - replaced by another circle of diameter $d$. (Points $AB_1, B_2, \dots$, on figure are marked in red.) The curve constructed in this way—depicted mathematically by Knuth and Davis—is called the “Dragon Curve.” It has a number of amazing properties: it never intersects itself, and even curves issued from one point, with unlimited continuation, fill the entire plane with a uniform pattern. Some properties of this curve are described in «Kvant» No. 2 for 1970.
