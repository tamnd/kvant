The young mathematicians sent telephony greetings to Kyiv and Minsk, where the final stages of the All-Union Olympiad of Chemists and Physicists opened that day.

The next day, April 16, was the first participant with 41 participants in the eighth grade, 50 in the ninth grade and 65 in the tenth grade. It is interesting to note that two participants: Oleg Okunev, a tenth grader from Kazan, and Vilayat Huseynov, a tenth grader from Nakhichevan, belonged to the All-Union Olympiad for the fourth time; 19 people came to the Olympiad three times each.

Pupils in grades 8 and 9 were offered 4 problems, and students in grades 10 were given five problems (an analysis of non-ctoral problems is given in the article by L. Limanov; see p. 60).

A few words about the selection of the proposed tasks. Selecting problems for the All-Union Olympiad is, apparently, one of the most difficult moments of the jury’s work. It is not so easy to come up with a problem that would be new for all the guys who came to the final round, many of whom participated in specialized physics and mathematics courses, are well acquainted with popular mathematical literature, and regularly read the magazine «Kvant». In addition, the Olympiad task must satisfy an indispensable condition: it should not highlight those who know more, those who are better “technically” equipped, it should reveal the ability to analyze a new unfamiliar situation, the acuity of logical thinking, and the ability to quickly orientate.

It is worth noting that when selecting problems, the jury tried to satisfy another important requirement - the relevance of the proposed problems. At the same time, behind the external occupancy there should be some server-side mathematical facts, which, perhaps in a simple situation, students should come to discover. Almost half of all the problems proposed at the X Olympiad were compiled by 3rd year student at Leningrad University, jury member S. V. Fomin.
