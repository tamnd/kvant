What is $\sqrt[5]{4}$ equal to?

In a number of textbooks on mathematics for applicants and in some other publications, an interpretation of the solution of one type of equation has become widespread, which contradicts the definition accepted at school. We consider it necessary to provide the reader with some confidence in the erroneous statements of the authors of these books.

Consider equation No. 7.053 from the “Collection of problems in mathematics for specific exams in colleges” edited by M. I. Skanavi (M., “HIGH SCHOOL”, 1973):

$$\sqrt[5]{2^x \cdot \sqrt[3]{4^x \cdot \sqrt[1]{0,125}}} = 4 \cdot \sqrt[3]{2}.$$

The answer gives two values ​​as roots of this equation:

$$x_1 = 3, \quad x_2 = -\frac{1}{5}.$$

Apparently, the compilers of the “Collection” proceeded from the following solution: equation (1), after detailed transformations using the properties of powers and roots, is rewritten in the form

$$\sqrt[5]{2^5x^2 - 3} = 2^{7/3};$$

then we have

$$6x \cdot \sqrt[5]{2^5x^2 - 3} = 2^{7/3},$$

where

$$\frac{5x^2 - 3}{6x} = 2^{7/3};$$

finally, equating the exponents, we arrive at a quadratic equation with roots (2).

However, the indicated solution cannot be considered exhaustive, since the calculation formulas are not yet clear at all whether the found values ​​(2) are actually the roots of the original equation (1). This issue requires special verification.

Before carrying out such a check, let us recall that in a school mathematics course, when determining the expression $n$a (the arithmetic root of the $n$th power of the number $a$), it turns out that $n$ is a natural number greater than one (see “Algebra 8”, paragraph 24). Thus, from the point of view of the school definition of the expression

$$0.5\sqrt[5]{4}, -\sqrt[5]{2/5}, \sqrt[5]{9}$$

are as meaningless as expressions

$$\frac{1}{0}, \sqrt{-4}, (-3)^{\sqrt{2}},$$

$$\log_2(-3), \arcsin \pi.$$

Positive number $x$ cannot be recognized as the root of equation (1); substituting it into the left side of the equation gives the meaningless expression $-1/5\sqrt{0,125}$. In other words, answer (2) is incorrect; in fact, equation (1) has a single root $x$ 3.
