You became acquainted with the operation of a siphon - the simplest device for pumping liquid - back in the sixth grade. They told us what the American physicist Robert Wood did when he was still a boy, starting his fascinating experiments with the siphon.

“There was more than a foot of water around the river, and everyone knew very well that the water did not flow uphill. Rob laid the hose on the ground, told one of the boys to plug the end with his finger, and he began pouring water into the other until the entire hose was full. Even then, being a demonstrator by nature, Rob took this end and, instead of simply putting it on the ground, threw the hose over the high fence that separated the road from the ditch. Water flowed through the siphon. This, it is believed, was Wood’s first public scientific victory.”*).
