The system described in terms of conditions is a physical matrix. Finding the period of oscillation of a physical matrix is ​​a rather difficult task. The fact is that the period depends on the shape of the physical matrix, its configuration. But for any physical matrix it is possible to select a mathematical matrix whose oscillation period will be equal to the oscillation period of the given physical matrix. This is what we will do: we will select a mathematical matrix “equivalent” to the system depicted in figure 11.

The period of small oscillations of a mathematical matrix depends only on its length. So we need a fairly long “equivalent” mathematical matrix - the so-called reduced length of the physical matrix.

figure 11 shows the equilibrium position of our system. In this case, the center of gravity of the system (point $M$) is located on the vertical passing through the center of rotation $O$; $d$ is the radius vector of point $M$ relative to $O$. Let $l$ be the reduced length of the physical matrix. If during oscillations the angular velocities $\omega$ and $\omega'$ of rotation of the vectors $d$ and $l$ around the point $O$ are the same, then the periods of oscillation of the matrices are the same. Let's find $\omega$ and $\omega'$.

Let $\varphi$ be the maximum angle of deviation of the radius vector $d$ from the vertical. Let us find the angular velocity of a point in the system at the moment when the radius vector $d$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical.
