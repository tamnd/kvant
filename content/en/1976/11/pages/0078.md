$A \rightarrow K$, $B \rightarrow D$, $C \rightarrow E$.

In this case, the mappings of the points $A$ and $B$ were mapped onto the points $K$ and $D$, but $|AB| \neq |KD|$, i.e. the distance was not preserved. Consequently, the figure $F_1$ can be mapped onto the figure $F_2$ without preserving the distance, i.e. “according to Stepa” $F_1$ and $F_2$ are not constructive.

Now let’s see which figures “according to Stepa” are constructive in themselves. Let the figure $F$ consist of two points: $F = \{A, B\}$. This figure can be mapped onto itself in only two ways:
1) $A \rightarrow A$, $B \rightarrow B$;
2) $A \rightarrow B$, $B \rightarrow A$.

In both mappings the construction is preserved: $|AB| = |AB| = |BA|$. Thus, for any mapping of a given figure $F$ onto itself, the distance is preserved.

Let now $F_1 = \{A, B, C\}$ be the figure of three points depicted on figure 3. Let us map it onto ourselves like this:
