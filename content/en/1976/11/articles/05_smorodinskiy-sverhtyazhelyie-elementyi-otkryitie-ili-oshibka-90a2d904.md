Superheavy elements - discovery or mistake?

In mid-June 1976, reports appeared in newspapers in different countries that a group of American scientists, which included physicists, chemists and geologists, had discovered traces of superheavy elements in micronodules of monzaite crystals, which should appear in 116, 124 and 126 places in the periodic table! The correspondents referred to the speech of Paul Dirac, one of the creators of quantum mechanics, during a scientific conference. On July 5, a scientific article about this discovery appeared in the American journal Physical Review Letters.

But can this message be trusted right away? After all, flawless research into superheavy elements has been going on for a long time, and it is necessary to very carefully check all possible interpretations of the results obtained in order for the discovery to be true.

The story that is unfolding now before our eyes illustrates very well how difficult it is to prove the reliability of a scientific discovery that was made at the very border of the flowering of modern physical equipment.

It will take some more time until the question “discovery or error?” can be finally answered. For now, we can only talk about what the procedure itself consists of and what allows experimenters to suspect the existence of new elements in their rock samples.

End of periodic table

In the periodic table, cell number 92 is the last element that can be found in nature - uranium.

Physicists received many new elements in laboratories - their list now extends more than 10 cells beyond uranium. Elements 93 (neptutic) and 94 (plutonic), which are obtained from uranium after neutron capture and subsequent β-decay, were obtained in the early years of the development of nuclear energy. Subsequent elements were obtained either after repeated capture of neutrons by samples, placed in atomic reactors, or by bombarding heavy elements with lighter nuclei using accelerators. Physicists will find that in the coming years it will even be possible to see what happens when two uranium nuclei collide.

Why is the periodic table announced? Heavy nuclei have too much electrical pollution; they have too many protons. The electrical repulsive forces are so strong that the nucleus spontaneously divides. Of course, nuclear forces act between all particles of the nucleus, which keep them from collapsing, but still, for a nucleus with a large number of protons, the electrical repulsion turns out to be stronger.

"Magic numbers"

It has long been discovered that when the number of neutrons or protons in a nucleus is equal to one of the so-called “magic” numbers, for example, 20, 56, 82, then such nuclei turn out to be more stable than their neighbors. From theoretical messages

It followed that the “magic” numbers should also be 114 and 126. Therefore, some scientists considered it possible for very pure nuclei with such contaminants to exist, despite the fact that nuclei with less contaminants decay very quickly.

Of course, the stability of such nuclei is not absolute, like, for example, the nuclei of iron or gold. But if the physicist increases it and the lifetime of such nuclei turns out to be about a billion years, they can even end up in nature, for example, in some old minerals.

But how can they get there?

Our views on the origin of bond elements with the idea of ​​the gradual emergence of heavier and heavier elements as a result of neutron capture. Such processes occurred at an early stage in the development of the Universe and, perhaps, will occur now in the cores of the galaxy and in the interior of stars. But the sequential capture of neutrons cannot transform a uranium nucleus into a superheavy nucleus with a charge of 114 or 126 - in this way it is impossible to jump over the region of elements with serial numbers 100-110, it is impossible to bridge the gap between the “mainland” of ordinary nuclei and the “island of stability”. If, however, nuclei from the island of stability are discovered, then the question of how such an island arose will become very relevant.

Experiment

That is why the existence of superheavy elements in nature is met with skepticism from many scientists. However, it cannot prove that they do not exist in nature. Experience is needed.

In the experiments mentioned at the beginning, superheavy elements were searched for in very old mica samples containing inclusions of monazite microcrystals. These microcrystals contain nuclei of radioactive thorium and uranium. The alpha particles released from these nuclei were forced into the mica, creating spherical gallos (dark spheres), passing through places where they caused the most damage to the mica atoms. Some galloes had a radius larger than normal, indicating the emission of high-energy alpha particles (up to 14 million electron volts). It was in these places that populist new elements were introduced.

A very thin beam of protons with energies of 4.7-5.7 million electron volts, with a diameter of ~ 30 microns, was created in the cyclotron. This beam fired at the place where they hoped to detect atoms of superheavy elements - the center of the gallo. Protons must knock electrons out of the inner shells of these atoms. In this case, electrons from layers further away from the nucleus would move to the vacated places, emitting X-rays. Knowing the energy of X-rays, it was possible to calculate the charge of the nucleus.

The X-rays obtained in this way indicated the presence of elements 116*) and 126 (and less than 124) in the samples. But the intensity of the X-rays was extremely low, and new, even more accurate experiments are needed, “which should confirm or refute the indication of superheavy elements” (this is a quote from the article).

If we consider the interpretation of the experiments correctly, then we can estimate that the samples contain several hundred picograms (1,ne=10⁻¹² e) of superheavy elements. And then we will soon learn about the properties of these new elements.

*) And not 114, as theorists predicted.
