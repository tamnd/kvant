Plant-technical college at the Moscow Automobile Plant named after. I. A. Likhacheva

Factory-technical college at the automobile plant named after. I. A. Likhacheva is a new type of higher education institution, organizing a combination in its program of theoretical and derivative training of special qualifications. The Institute trains engineers for industrial training at ZIL, AZLK, 1-GPZ and their branches located in 15 cities of the Soviet Union.

The plant-technological college at ZIL has three faculties: automotive, mechanical and technological and evening. The Faculty of Automotive trains mechanical engineers in the design and research of automobiles, automobile piston engines and automobile bodies, and the training of engineers in the specialty of automobile bodies is the only one in the Soviet Union. The automotive faculty includes the following specialties:

1. Car and tractors with special car bodies.
2. Internal motors.
3. Machines and technological processing of metals by pressure.
4. Machines and technological cooking production.
5. Economics and organization of the machine-building industry.
The Faculty of Mechanical Technology trains mechanical engineers in the following specialty:

1. Metal science, equipment and technological terminology of metal processing.
2. Mechanical engineering technology, machines and tools with specialties:
   a) technological equipment;
   b) tool production;
   c) repair and modernization of metal-cutting equipment;
   d) electronic and electronic methods of metal processing.
3. Machinery and technological foundry production.

4. Automation and complete set of mechanization of machine building.

The evening faculty trains mechanical engineers in the above-mentioned specialties.

The preparation of highly qualified specialties is carried out by highly qualified teaching staff with the involvement of leading specialties in basic subjects.

The institute has a military department, where upon completion of training young men are assigned an officer. All young men receive a professional driver's license.

Students of the institute will take advantage of all the benefits established for full-time students, as well as benefits provided for workers of such large factories as ZIL, AZLK, 1-GPZ.

For the entire duration of their studies, students receive a monthly salary of 0.5 months for professorial time, and 0.5 months of a stipend for study time, increased by 15% compared to the stipends of full-time students.

What is the peculiarity of training engineers in the “plant-technical college” system?

In the 1st year of full-time faculties of the institute, classes are conducted as in regular institutes. Starting from the 2nd year of study, derivative activities are carried out with and without interruption with a weekly alternation, i.e., during the academic week, students study in the classrooms of the institute, in the next working week, students work at base factories (ZIL, AZLK, 1-GPZ), participating in the production of the main products of items.

The institute's curriculum in the subject industrial training section states that in the second and third courses, students work at workplaces as work depending on the chosen specialty (assemblers, mechanics, repairmen, machine operators, stampers, etc.). Starting from the fourth year, students are transferred to engineering technical positions, where they will perform the functions of the technical departments of the institute; students conduct independent research work related to solving problems facing the base plants.

The general translation program for the training of engineers, despite the participation in the production process of base plants, provides for the study of general translation disciplines in a volume equivalent to the volume in a general higher technical educational institution. In Zila alone, more than 10 workshops were proclaimed by graduates of the plant-technical college.

Students of the plant-college school have the opportunity to take an active part in the sports life of one of the largest sports clubs in the country “Torpedo” Zil, sports clubs “Moskvich” and “Podshipnik”.

Students involved in sports regularly participate in activities with college teams and production teams in Moscow and other cities. A large group of students is part of the combined teams of base factories, MGS and CS DSO "Trud". A number of students who are members of the USSR national teams in certain sports study at the plant-college school.

Students from the plant-technical college participated in the USSR national team in the Olympic Games in Montreal.

The full-time department of the institute accepts persons working at base factories (ZIL, AZLK, 1-GPZ), Moscow schoolchildren sent for training by these factories, and non-resident applicants sent by the administration of films at the base factory. Students sent to record films from the base plants of the Avto-Zil, AZLK association, as well as from KAMAZ, are presented with a pleasant community.

Those entering the 1st year of the plant-technical college at Zila will take entrance exams within the scope of the high school curriculum in the following subjects: mathematics (written and oral), physics (written) and socialization.

Entrance exams are taken in three terms: July (for evening and daytime faculties), August (for daytime faculties) and September (for evening faculties).

A few words about the selection of the proposed tasks. Selecting problems for the All-Union Olympiad is, apparently, one of the most difficult moments of the jury’s work. It is not so easy to come up with a problem that would be new for all the guys who came to the final round, many of whom participated in specialized physics and mathematics courses, are well acquainted with popular mathematical literature, and regularly read the magazine «Kvant». In addition, the Olympiad task must satisfy an indispensable condition: it should not highlight those who know more, those who are better “technically” equipped, it should reveal the ability to analyze a new unfamiliar situation, the acuity of logical thinking, and the ability to quickly orientate.

It is worth noting that when selecting problems, the jury tried to satisfy another important requirement - the relevance of the proposed problems. At the same time, behind the external occupancy there should be some server-side mathematical facts, which, perhaps in a simple situation, students should come to discover. Almost half of all the problems proposed at the X Olympiad were compiled by 3rd year student at Leningrad University, jury member S. V. Fomin.

On the second day of the compound, April 18, an interesting experiment was carried out. A prototype of such an experiment was first conducted five years ago at the All-Union Olympiad in Rita for the tenth grade. Students in each class were offered three complex tasks, each of which was divided into several points, arranged in order of increasing difficulty. In the preface to the problems it was stated that the complete solution of each of these problems

presents a short mathematical study and that the jury recommends that in one (maximum two) of them (of their choice) each participant should go as far as possible. These tasks were divided by nature so that everyone could find a task to their liking. It must be said that preparing such tasks is a very difficult task, since it is necessary to compose tasks of approximately the same level, quite complex and varied in topic. The jury received a number of interesting papers in which there was fairly complete progress in solving one or two problems. In general, the jury found the experiment useful and believes that tasks of such a research plan are desirable not only at the final type of Olympiad, but also at various stages of work with schoolchildren.

The participants' work was checked by class commissions. Several jury members participated in the discussion of each work. Then an analysis of the problem was organized for the Olympiad participants and team leaders. Participants had the opportunity to familiarize themselves with the results of checking all works and, if necessary, appeal the decision of the jury.

All this required a lot of stress from the jury members, who sometimes had to work until 3-4 o’clock in the morning.
