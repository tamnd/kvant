⟦folio 21⟧

How does automatic siphon work? Let us turn again to figure 1. When the ball is lowered into a glass of water, water begins to enter inside it through hole 2. At the same time, water rises through the glass tube, entering it through the open end of the tube. The rate at which water rises in the tube is greater than in the balloon. A column of water, rising through the tube to hole 1 in its wall, seems to block it. As the balloon fills with water, the air pressure in the balloon will increase. At some point, a small air bubble is “pushed” into hole 1 of the tube. He cuts off a small column of water and lifts it up. The water rising through the tube again blocks hole 1, and again compressed air is pushed in the form of a bubble into this hole and cuts off a new portion of water. Thus, an air-water column is formed in the elbow of the tube with the ball, the average density of which is less than the density of water. Under the influence of hydrostatic pressure, this column rises to the bend of the tube, descends along the second bend and, when the ball is completely filled with water, a continuous stream of water is “stretched” behind it. The siphon starts working.

Exercises

1. Show experimentally that water should flow into the ball more slowly than into the glass tube. Explain why this happens.

2. To make sure that the reason for the operation of the automatic siphon is correct, replace the opaque ball with a small glass vial with a rubber stopper. In general, the entire device with a bubble should be exactly the same as when using a ball. Insert the glass tube into the vial through the hole in the rubber stopper. The transparent walls of the bubble allow you to observe the process of formation of an air-water column in a glass tube.

3. Find out whether the height of the rise of the air column depends on the depth of immersion of the knee with the ball in the water.

4. Make an automatic siphon by replacing the glass tube with a rubber one.
