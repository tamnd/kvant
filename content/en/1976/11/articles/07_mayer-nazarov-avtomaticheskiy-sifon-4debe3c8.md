You became acquainted with the operation of a siphon - the simplest device for pumping liquid - back in the sixth grade. They told us what the American physicist Robert Wood did when he was still a boy, starting his fascinating experiments with the siphon.

“There was more than a foot of water around the river, and everyone knew very well that the water did not flow uphill. Rob laid the hose on the ground, told one of the boys to plug the end with his finger, and he began pouring water into the other until the entire hose was full. Even then, being a demonstrator by nature, Rob took this end and, instead of simply putting it on the ground, threw the hose over the high fence that separated the road from the ditch. Water flowed through the siphon. This, it is believed, was Wood’s first public scientific victory.”*).

We will tell you about the automatic siphon *). Bend a glass tube about 60 cm long and an internal diameter of 3-4 mm above the flame so that two elbows are formed, one of which is about 25 cm long (Fig. 1). In this knee

at a distance of 33-35 mm from its end, use the edge of a file (moistened with water) to carefully cut a small hole (1). Its area should be no more than 0.5-1 mm². Use an awl to make a hole in the wall of the ping-pong ball and use a round file to widen it until the glass tube fits into it with friction. Pass the tube into the hole made so that its end rests against the diametrically opposite point of the ball wall. In this case, the hole in the glass tube should be inside the ball near its surface (see Fig. 1). The connection between the glass tube and the ball must be sealed. If you made a slight mistake and made a hole in the ball of too large a diameter, coat the joint with plasticine. In the ball near the end of the tube that rests against its wall, pierce another hole (2). Its initial diameter should be approximately 1 mm.

Quickly lower the siphon elbow with the ball at its end into a glass of water. Almost immediately, rising water appears in this knee, separated by air bubbles. It reaches the point where the siphon bends, descends down the second bend (Fig. 2) and after a short time a continuous stream begins to flow from the hole in the second bend!

If the experiment does not work, you just need to carefully debug the device. The operation of the automatic siphon depends on the correct selection of the fruit opening in the glass tube and ball. Poor placement of the glass tube relative to the ball or insufficient tightness of the connection between the ball and the tube can also lead to flat operation of the siphon. The diameter of the hole in the ball can be gradually increased with a needle file, adding better results. After setting up the device, the ball can be glued to the glass tube with kleem BF-2.
