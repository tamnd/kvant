However, in Poincaria there is another sign of the congruence of triangles: triangles are congruent with try to prove it; see figure 5 and problem 4.) This means that the area of ​​a triangle in Poincaria (like the triangle itself) is determined by the values ​​of its angles $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. In Lobachevsky geometry, the sum of the angles of a triangle is less than $\pi$. The quantity $\pi$—($\alpha+\beta+\gamma$) is called the triangle defect. It can be observed that a triangle defect behaves in the same way as an area; more precisely: if a given triangle is cut by a straight line passing through its vertex, then its area will be equal to the sum of the areas of the resulting triangles; the same will be true for the defect of any triangle: it is equal to the sum of the defects of the formed triangles (Fig. 6). From here we can conclude that the area of ​​the triangle in Lobachevsky geometry is proportional to the defect $\pi$—$\alpha$—$\beta$—$\gamma$.
