Let us find the most possible value of the number $A = \sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k$ such that the last inequality is still true. It is not difficult to prove (we will do this below) that

$$\sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k^2 \geq \frac{1}{7} \left( \sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k \right)^2 = \frac{1}{7} A^2;$$

then from inequality (**) it follows that

$$\frac{1}{7} A^2 - A \leq \sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k^2 - \sum_{k=1}^{7} a_h \leq 42,$$

or $$\frac{1}{7} A^2 - A \leq 42,$$ or $$A^2 - 7A \leq 294,$$ that is, $(A - 21)(A + 14) \leq 0$, whence $A \leq 21$.

The proof for the square $13 \times 13$ cells is carried out in a similar way, and we advise readers to do all the calculations on their own.

Now we will prove (for any $n \geq 1$, $a_k \geq 0$, $k = 1, 2, \dots, n$) the inequality

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 \geq \frac{1}{n} \left( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \right)^2.$$

For convenience of calculations, we put $na = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k$ and prove that $\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 \geq na^2$. Let us denote the difference between the number $a_k$ and the average value $a$ of the numbers $a_k$ by $\varepsilon_k: \varepsilon_k = a_k - a$. Then

$$na = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a + \varepsilon_k) = na + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon_k,$$

and therefore

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon_k = 0.$$

It follows from this:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a + \varepsilon_k)^2 =$$

$$= \sum_{k=1}^{n} a^2 + 2a \sum_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon_k + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon_k^2 = na^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} \varepsilon_k^2 \geq na^2.$$
