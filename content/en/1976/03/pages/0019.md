A solid body is characterized by the fact that when moving in a medium with a constant temperature, the distance $r(A, B)$ (Euclidean) between any two of its points $A$ and $B$ is preserved. But if a body moves from a region with temperature $T_1$ to a region with temperature $T_2$, then the distance between its points will decrease by $T_2/T_1$, in other words, the ratio $r(A,B)/T$ remains the same. What will happen if the body immediately finds itself in an area with different temperatures?

Fig. 12.

What value will be conserved under these conditions? Let, for example, a sufficiently large thermode body move in a medium where on one side of some straight line $m$ the temperature is $T_1$, and on the other - $T_2$; let $A$ be a point of the body located in a region with temperature $T_1$, and $B$ be a point of the body located in a region with temperature $T_2$. Take a broken line with ends at points $A$ and $B$ and vertex $C$ on line $m$. Let's denote $|AC|=r_1$, $|CB|=r_2$ and the distance $r_1/T_1+r_2/T_2$. It turns out that when moving in such a temperature environment, the smallest value of the quantity $r_1/T_1+r_2/T_2$ is preserved, taken along all broken lines with vertices on the straight line $m$ and with ends at two given points $A$ and $B!$ Then we can exactly repeat the same discrepancies as when applying Fermat’s principle, for example, to the derivation of the Sneglius law of refraction, and we obtain that the smallest value will be drawn by a broken line for which $\frac{\sin \alpha_1}{T_1} = \frac{\sin \alpha_2}{T_2}$, where $\alpha_i$ is the angle of the corresponding link of the broken line with the normal to the straight line $m$.

Let now in Poincaria at point $(x; y)$ the absolute temperature $T(x, y)=y$ be constantly maintained. then, due to the chosen temperature regime, when solid (in our sense!) bodies move, they will no longer maintain ενClidean distances, but n-distances, and from the point of view of Poincaria (after all, they do not feel the temperature difference!) the size of a body moving in such a medium is preserved, that is, it is n-solid. All that remains to be taken care of is that all objects have low thermal capacities and move so slowly that they are in thermal equilibrium, and that the change in temperature is imperceptible to Poincaria. As a result, Poincarie not only will not see the border of the world, but will also not be able to get to it: when approaching the border, the temperature tends to absolute zero, and then the layout of objects will tend to zero, without changing the proportions between objects.
