It is easy to see that the cycle $1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 1$ consists of the process of isochoric heating $1 \rightarrow 2$, isothermal distribution $2 \rightarrow 3$ and isobaric cooling $3 \rightarrow 1$. In coordinates $(p, V)$ it takes the form shown in figure 7.

The cycle $3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3$ consists of the processes of isochoric heating $3 \rightarrow 4$, isobaric cooling $4 \rightarrow 2$ and isothermal distribution $2 \rightarrow 3$. Since the processes $2 \rightarrow 3$ for both cycles coincide, it is reasonable to display the dependence graphs $p(V)$ for them on the same coordinate axes (Fig. 7).

It is quite obvious that the area of ​​the figure $1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 1$ is less than the area of ​​the figure $3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3$. It immediately follows that, according to the gas work model, $A$ in the first process is less than in the second, i.e. $|A_{1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3}| < |A_{3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3}|$. But we must not forget that the work done by the gas is an algebraic quantity: if the process occurs with an increase in volume, then $A > 0$, and if the volume of the system decreases, then $A < 0$. For circular processes, one can formulate the following rule for determining the sign of the work done by the gas: if the circuit of the circular process is traversed clockwise, then $A > 0$; if the process of traversing the contour proceeds counterclockwise, then $A < 0$. Applying this rule to this problem, we get

$$A_{1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3} > 0 \text{ и } A_{3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3} < 0.$$

The final answer to the problem can be given as follows:

$$A_{1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3} > A_{3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3},$$

but at the same time

$$|A_{1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3}| < |A_{3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3}|.$$

Task 3. We perform a gas cycle $1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 1$ (Fig. 8), consisting of two isotherms ($1 \rightarrow 2$ and $3 \rightarrow 4$) and two adιαbas ($2 \rightarrow 3$ and $4 \rightarrow 1$). Which of these processes occur when mellot is supplied to the system, and which require the removal of mellot?

Students rarely give the correct answer to this question. The most common mistake is that two different concepts are identified: temperature and amount of heat. It is stated that if the temperature is constant (processes $1 \rightarrow 2$ and $3 \rightarrow 4$), then heat does not need to be supplied or removed. This is, of course, not true.

To solve the problem, we will use thermodynamics:

$$Q = \Delta U + A.$$

Here $Q$ is the amount of heat transferred to the system, $\Delta U$ is the change in the internal energy of the system and $A$ is the work done to the system.

The internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature (of course, provided that $m = const$ and $\mu = const$). Therefore, for an isothermal process $1 \rightarrow 2$ the change in internal energy is $\Delta U = 0$, and equality (2) takes the form

$$Q = A.$$
