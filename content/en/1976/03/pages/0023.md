⟦folio 21⟧

Task 2. Prove that for arbitrary three points $A, B$ and $C$ the “triangle inequality” holds:

$$\rho (A, C) + \rho (B, C) \geq \rho (A, B).$$

Thus, the distance $\rho$ behaves like a normal one.

Task 3. Prove that for any line $AB$, any point $C$ on it and any positive number $t$ one can find two distinct points $D$ and $D_1$ on the line $AB$ such that $\rho (C, D) = \rho (C, D_1) = t$. In other words, prove that a segment of any length can be drawn from a point.

We will call two figures (for example, triangles, segments, angles) congruent segments of equal length.

Task 4. Prove the existence and uniqueness of the midpoint of the segment.

Task 5. Prove that segments of the same length are congruent.

All that remains is to enter the angle values.

Let there be a neoctor angle with vertex at point $C$, angle $C$. Let us take any displacement that takes the point $C$ to the center of a given circle, and set the vertex of the angle $C$ equal to the Euclidean vertex of the angle (with the vertex in the center of the circle) into which the angle $C$ goes during this displacement. From what follows it will follow that this vertex does not depend on the choice of movement. (Try to get this yourself by proving that rotations around the center of our circle are also translations in Lobachevsky geometry.)

**Fig. 3.**

**Theorem of cosines**

Let us introduce the following notation: if $l$ is a neoctor segment, then $|l|$ is its total (Euclidean) length, $\{l\}$ is its length in the new sense, and $[l] = \frac{q \{l\} - 1}{q \{l\} + 1}$ ($q > 1$ is the base of the logarithm in the definition of the distance $\rho$). Note that the vertex $[l]$ is always less than one (but greater than zero).

Consider a triangle $ABC$ whose vertex $C$ coincides with the center of the circle. Let $|BC| = a, |AC| = b, |AB| = c$ and $r$ be the radius of the circle. We continue the side $AB$ to the intersection with the circle at points $M$ and $N$, connect the points $M$ and $N$ with the vertex $C$ (Fig. 3) and denote the formed angles by $\alpha$, $\gamma$ and $\beta$ ($\gamma$ is the angle at the vertex $C$ of triangle $ABC$).

Then $M\hat{N}C = N\hat{M}C = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\alpha + \beta + \gamma}{2}$, and by the general theorem of sines we find:

$$\frac{|a|}{r} = \frac{\cos \frac{\alpha + \beta + \gamma}{2}}{\cos \frac{\alpha - \beta + \gamma}{2}},$$

$$\frac{|b|}{r} = \frac{\cos \frac{\alpha + \beta + \gamma}{2}}{\cos \frac{-\alpha + \beta + \gamma}{2}}.$$
