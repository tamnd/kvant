The nodes must be located at the ends of the segment $L$, and on the segment itself they must contain an integer number of half-waves (Fig. 2), that is, they must fulfill the condition

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{2L}{n} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots).$$

It follows that the speed of the electron inside the box is quantized:

$$v_n = \frac{h}{m\lambda_n} = \frac{nh}{2mL} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots).$$

The correspondence is quantized and so is its kinetic energy. Inside the box, the potential energy of the electron is zero and, therefore, the total energy coincides with the kinetic energy:

$$E = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

Substituting here the possibility of speed value (3), we find

$$E_n = \frac{n^2h^2}{8mL^2} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots).$$

At first glance, this result seems unexpected. It means that the energy of a heavy body moving in a box with infinitely high walls must be quantized. However, even for a very small particle, quantization becomes noticeable only in the case when the “box” has dimensions on the order of atomic ones. Indeed, for an electron whose mass is $m = 9,8 \cdot 10^{-28} e$, placed in a box of length $L = 1$ cm, the possible energy values ​​are

$$E_n = n^2 \frac{(6,62 \cdot 10^{-34})^2}{8 \cdot 9,8 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 10^{-4}} \approx n^2 \cdot 5,6 \cdot 10^{-34} \partial x = n^2 \cdot 3,5 \cdot 10^{-15} \partial v$$

and the “distance” between adjacent levels

$$\Delta E_n = E_{n+1} - E_n = (2n+1) \cdot 3,5 \cdot 10^{-15} \partial v.$$

These are very large “distances”, and the energy spectrum is real.

The energy quantization condition (4), which we obtained by analogy with string oscillations, turns out to be completely strict - it exactly coincides with the one given by the solution of the Schrödinger equation for this problem. We will return to formula (4) and show what interesting results follow from it. For now, let's turn to another problem.

Harmonic oscillator
