P-symmetry relative to p-primes. For rays, this is general axial symmetry, and to obtain inversion *). (For example, n-symmetry with respect to n-prime $L(-1,1)$ is an inversion with respect to a circle with center $O=(0;0)$ of radius 1; it takes a point $A$, distinct from the center $O$, to a point $A'$ lying on the ray $OA$ such that $|OA| \cdot |OA'|=1$). We know that during inversion, circles and straight lines turn into circles or straight lines, and the values ​​of the angles are preserved. In Poincaria language, this means that, for example, with n-symmetry with respect to n-prima $L(-1,1)$, n-prima $L(\alpha,\beta)$ goes into n-prima $L\left(\frac{1}{\alpha},\frac{1}{\beta}\right)$. In particular, n-primes $L(\alpha,0)$, which are obtained at $\alpha \neq \infty$, are transformed into n-primes $L\left(\frac{1}{\alpha},\infty\right)$, which are rays. So, n-symmetries take Poincaria into themselves, and n-primes go into n-primes. It is checked separately (we omit this check) that n-symmetries do not change n-distances $\rho$. (However, in Poincaria, any transformation that takes n-primes into n-primes preserves $\rho$ (there is no ready-made one here); this is the most important difference between Lobachevsky’s geometry and Euclid’s geometry.)

*) See the article by A. Savin on “Inversion and the Circle of Apollonius”, «Kvant», 1971, No. 8, or solution problem M305, «Kvant», 1975, No. 9.

This means that these n-movements can combine any n-segments of equal n-length, and we get that such segments are n-congruent. It can be shown that all n-movement properties are as described.

With n-translations, the angle transforms into an angle equal to it in the Euclidean sense (since this is true for parallel translations, ready-made, axial symmetry and inversion). Therefore, the concept of congruence of angles in Poincaria is not different from Euclid's.
