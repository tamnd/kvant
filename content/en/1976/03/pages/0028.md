Quantization and wave structure

M. Volkinitain

Quantum mechanics is the science of the laws of motion of microparticles (atoms, electrons, neutrons, etc.). This is the physics of the microworld. It explains many important phenomena - radioactive decay, the electrical conductivity of metals, and the nature of chemical bonds. But first of all, quantum mechanics explains the line spectra of atoms - it answered the question of why atoms are capable of emitting or absorbing light only from one hundred certain frequencies of light wave oscillations. Quantum mechanics has shown that electrons in atoms can only have fixed, variable energy values. Physicists usually say that electrons are located at discrete energy levels $E_1$, $E_2$, ... - the energy of an electron in an atom is quantized. The absorption or emission of light by an atom occurs when an electron passes from one energy level to another. In the first case, the electron moves from a level with lower energy to a level with higher energy, in the second case - vice versa. The frequency of the emitted (or absorbed) light wave is determined by the conditions of the Bohr frequencies.

$$v_{nn'} = \frac{E_n - E_{n'}}{h},$$

(1)

where $E_n$, $E_{n'}$ are energy levels, $n$, $n'$ are the values ​​of the quantum number characterizing these levels.

One of the main tasks of quantum mechanics is to find quantized energy values ​​for a given system of particles, in particular for atoms. According to equation (1), solving such a problem means simultaneously calculating the spectrum of the system, that is, determining the set of frequencies of light emitted by this system.
