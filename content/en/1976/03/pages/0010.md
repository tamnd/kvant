They are, as it were, at “points at infinity” parallel to the straight lines $a$. Well, figure 13 shows an “infinite triangle”, the sides of which are parallel in pairs. In short, this is Lobachevsky’s model of nominal geometry. Of course, I have not described this model completely: it is necessary to indicate how the defined lengths change, which figures are considered equal (congruent), etc. But in general terms, the model is built this way.

— And the other scientists you named built such a model?

- Oh no! Now many different models of Lobachevsky geometry are known. For example, in the works of Klein and Cayley, a model was constructed in which only points lying inside a certain circle $K$ are also considered, but they consider the chords of this circle (without ends) to be “straight lines”. On this model it is even easier to see that through the straight line point $a$ there are infinitely many “lines” that do not intersect with $a$ (Fig. 14). However, angles, for example, change more complexly in this model than in the Poincaré model.
