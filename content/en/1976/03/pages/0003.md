The cover depicts the "modular figure", which plays an important role in function theory and number theory. They first appeared in Gauss. From the point of view of the Poincaré model, the Lobachevsky planes (see the article “The Magic World of Henri Poincaré”) are a covering of two Lobachevsky congestion planes with “isosceles” triangles, in which the sides are parallel and the angles at the base are equal to 60°. On the Euclidean plane, of course, there are no such triangles. Triangles, constantly decreasing in their “Euclidean” dimensions (but not from the point of view of Lobachevsky’s geometry), are “pressed” to the line separating the planes.
