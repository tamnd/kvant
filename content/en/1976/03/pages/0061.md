gases equation of state (Clapeyron-Mendeleev equation) has the form

$$pV = \frac{m}{\mu}RT$$

(R is the universal gas constant).

Equilibrium states and equilibrium processes can be represented graphically. To do this, the values ​​of any two parameters are plotted on two mutually perpendicular axes, for example, pressure p and volume V, volume V and temperature T. In this way, so-called state diagrams of the system are obtained. In any of the diagrams (p, V), (V, T) or (p, T), each equilibrium state is denoted by a dot, and the equilibrium process is linear.

The school course examines in detail three particular processes:

isothermal (T = const), isobaric (p = const) and isochoric (V = const) processes occur with a gas of constant chemical state (μ = const) and constant mass (m = const). Corresponding graphics are shown at figure 2.

But we must not forget that in the general case, all parameters without exception, including m and μ, can change. Let, for example, a gas transition from state 1 (with parameters p₁, V₁, T₁, m₁, μ₁) to state 2 (with parameters p₂, V₂, T₂, m₂, μ₂). Generally speaking, this transition can be accomplished in an infinite number of ways, depending on how the individual parameters change. For example, figure 3 shows graphs of the same process 1→2 on different diagrams. Think about how the mass and molar mass of the gas changes.
