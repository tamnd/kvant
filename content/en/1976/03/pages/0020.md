Cayley-Klein model Lobachevsky geometry

This article discusses the model of the Lobachevsky plane, which is different from the model of Henri Poincaré, which is described in the article by S. Gindikin “The Magic World of Henri Poincaré”.

Definition of "plane"

In Euclidean geometry we are dealing with points and lines of an obliqu plane, and with the help of well-known movements (parallel translations, axial and central symmetries and rotations) any point on the plane can be translated into any other, any two points lying at a given distance can be translated into other two points lying at the same distance, and any two straight lines can be translated into two straight lines forming the same angle. Now we will consider geometry, the lines of which will be the interior points of the non-circle), and the lines will be all possible chords of this circle (without ends); Let us describe all the movements of such a “plane”, and then introduce the distances between the “points” and the distances between the “straight lines” so that they are preserved under the specified movements.

It is possible to check that the geometry we will construct is “no worse” than Euclidean: all axioms will be fulfilled in it, except for the “parallel axiom”: it is easy to see that now through a “point” outside the “line” it is possible to check more than one that “does not intersect” it. On figure 1, through the point $A$, which does not lie on the chord $L$, two chords are drawn - $L_1$ and $L_2$, having $L$ at a common end; these chords $L_1$ and $L_2$ are called parallel $L$, since we consider them as straight chords without ends, and from our new point of view the line $L$ does not intersect either $L_1$ or $L_2$. (Note also that the lines “not intersecting” with $L$ are located in the area bounded by the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$, shaded in blue, and the lines intersecting with $L$ are in the unshaded area.)
