Dallee:

$$\{a\} = \log_q \frac{r \cdot (r + |a|)}{r \cdot (r - |a|)} = \log_q \frac{r + |a|}{r - |a|},$$

and therefore

$$[a] = \frac{|a|}{r}. \tag{4}$$

Likewise,

$$[b] = \frac{|b|}{r}. \tag{5}$$

By virtue of formula (1)

$$\{c\} = \log_q \frac{\sin(\alpha + \gamma) \cdot \sin(\beta + \gamma)}{\sin(\alpha) \cdot \sin(\beta)},$$

Means,

$$[c] = \frac{\sin(\alpha + \gamma) \cdot \sin(\beta + \gamma) - \sin(\alpha) \cdot \sin(\beta)}{\sin(\alpha + \gamma) + \sin(\alpha) \cdot \sin(\beta)}. \tag{6}$$

From the correspondence (4) - (6), taking into account (2) and (3), with simple but rather cumbersome calculations, we obtain that

$$\frac{(1 - [a]^2)(1 - [b]^2)}{1 - [c]^2} = (1 - [a] \cdot [b] \cdot \cos \gamma)^2. \tag{7}$$

Relation (7) is an analogue of the cosine theorem of general geometry. Since the quantities $\{a\}, \{b\}$ and $\{c\}$, and therefore $[a], [b], [c]$, do not change when moving, then the definition of the angle value given in the previous paragraph follows these relationships.

Let us now prove two remarkable statements of Lobachevsky geometry.

Two theorems

Theorem 1. The sum of the angles of any triangle is less than π.

Proof. Let us first consider the rectangle ABC, with a right angle at vertex C: $\hat{C} = \frac{\pi}{2}$ (Fig. 4). From the cosine theorem it follows that

$$(1 - [a]^2)(1 - [b]^2) = 1 - [c]^2, \tag{8}$$

or

$$[c]^2 = [a]^2 + [b]^2 - [a]^2 \cdot [b]^2.$$

Dallee, from the same theorem (see Fig. 4)

$$\frac{(1 - [a]^2)(1 - [c]^2)}{1 - [b]^2} = (1 - [a] \cdot [c] \cos \beta)^2;$$

Substituting expression (8) for $1 - [c]^2$ here, we obtain

$$\frac{(1 - [a]^2)^2(1 - [b]^2)}{1 - [b]^2} = (1 - [a] \cdot [c] \cos \beta)^2,$$

where

$$1 - [a]^2 = 1 - [a] \cdot [c] \cdot \cos \beta,$$

And

$$\cos \beta = \frac{[a]}{[c]},$$ that is $\beta = \arccos \frac{[a]}{[c]}$.
