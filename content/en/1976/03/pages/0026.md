We do not have the opportunity to talk in detail about the theory of areas in Lobachevsky geometry. Therefore, we will limit ourselves to only brief remarks. Let us assume that each polygon $T$, not necessarily convex (but without self-intersection), is assigned a certain positive number $S(T)$, and:

1) if the polygon $T$ is divided into several polygons $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_k$, then $S(T) = S(T_1) + S(T_2) + \dots + S(T_k)$;
2) if the polygons $T_1$ and $T_2$ are congruent, then $S(T_1) = S(T_2)$. Then it will be said that $S(T)$ is the area of ​​the polygon $T$.

We present the reader to prove or verify with simple examples that in the Lobachevsky plane the value $S(T) = \lambda [(n - 2)\pi - (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \dots + \alpha_n)]$ can be taken as the area of ​​the $n$-gon $T$, where $\lambda$ is an arbitrary fixed positive, $n$ is the number of sides, and $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$ are the interior angles of the polygon.

Note that the magnitude $(n - 2)\pi$ is the sum of the interior angles of the $n$-gon $T$ in geometric Euclidean, that is, that the magnitude $S(T)$ is proportional to the body and its sums of the angles of the polygon in Eclidean and Lobachevsky geometry; try to prove the stated statement for triangles. “The Magic World of Henri Poincaré” will help you become.
