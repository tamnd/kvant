4. The sum of the squares of the lengths of the diagonals of a prapeze is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of its lateral sides, and the double product of the lengths of the bases.

Proof. Let AC and DB be the bases of the trapezoid ACBD (Fig. 4). Since the vectors $\vec{AC}$ and $\vec{DB}$ are co-corrected, $\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{DB} = |AC| \cdot |DB|$, and equality (3) takes the form $|AB|^2 + |CD|^2 - |BC|^2 - |AD|^2 = 2|AC| \cdot |BD|$, i.e.

$$|AB|^2 + |CD|^2 = |BC|^2 + |AD|^2 + 2|AC| \cdot |DB|$$

Q.E.D.

5. (A well-known theorem.) The sum of the squares of the length of the diagonals of a parallelogram is equal to the sum of the squares of the length of all its sides.

Proof. Vectors AC and DB are co-corrected (Fig. 5), hence $\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{DB} = |AC| \cdot |DB| = |AC|^2$ since $|AC| = |DB|$.

Substituting the found value $\vec{AC} \cdot \vec{DB}$ into (3), we obtain

$$|AB|^2 + |CD|^2 = |BC|^2 + |AD|^2 + 2|AC|^2 = |BC|^2 + |AD|^2 + |AC|^2 + |BD|^2$$

and the theorem is proven.
