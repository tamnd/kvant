The new geometry program (for ninth grade) includes the concept of the scalar product of vectors. The scalar product of vectors is widely used in solving various geometric problems and proving theorems. Below we place two notes - by E. Gotman and Y. Ponarin, and which demonstrate the use of the vector method with several examples. The reader is required to be familiar with the simplest operations - addition of vectors and multiplication of a vector by a number, as well as the concept of a scalar product of vectors. Let us recall the definition and some properties of scalar multiplication of vectors.

The scalar product of two non-zero vectors is a number equal to the product of the numerical values ​​of the lengths of these vectors and the cosine of the angle between the vectors.

The scalar product of vectors a and b is denoted by a·b; according to the definition a·b = |a|·|b|·cos a, b). The properties that scalar vector multiplication has are similar to the law of operations on numbers:

1) a·b = b·a;
2) (λa) b = λ(a b), where λ is a certain number;
3) a·(b+c) = a·b+a·c

(for their proof, see the textbook on geometry for the 9th grade, p. 59).
