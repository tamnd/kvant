To the article “Restless Arc”
(see «Kvant» No. 6)

1. No, it is not. An experiment with other sizes indicated in the article will work just as well if a glass one is used together with an aluminum plate. A.S. Popov, who carried out the experiment on a larger scale, found that a heated arc on a glass plate swings for less time than on a metal one.

2. All other things being equal, a wide arc is preferable because it has a longer contact length with the mica. It is not difficult to verify this experimentally. It is quite difficult to answer the second part of the question experimentally, because to carry out the experiment, the preference is now to use identical arcs of different thicknesses.

3. No. A mica sheet lying on an aluminum plate has the same temperature as the plate itself. If the arc is heated at the same temperature, then when it comes into contact with mica there will be no heat transfer.

4. Obviously, the longer the arc remains hot, the longer the arc swings. To extend this time, you can suggest the following “grid”. Bend the ends of the arc upward so that they are in the flame of a dry fuel tablet placed on the inner surface of the arc (the tablet should be covered in some way). In this case, the arc will swing throughout the entire burning time of the tablet.

5. Instead of an arc in the experiment, you can use a thin-walled metal tube. If a mica sheet is placed on a slightly curved plate, the heated tube will swing back and forth.
