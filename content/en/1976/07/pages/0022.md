If light scattered by a part at point $O$ is observed in the direction $YO$, then it is clear that only waves propagate in this direction in which the oscillations of the vector $E$ are directed along $OZ$. This means that light scattered at right angles to the incident light is completely polarized.

Observations show that the polarization of light in the atmosphere is not complete. In addition, the degree of polarization depends on the time of day.

You can make sure that the sky light is polarized using any polarizer. But, as we have already said, some people can observe the polarization of light with the naked eye. Since the light reaching the object being observed is not fully polarized, the picture it sees is not very clear. This picture was described by L.N. Tolstoy. It will be much more noticeable if you increase the degree of polarization of light using polarizing light filters.

What should the field of view look like ideally? Heidinger established that the observer, looking for a few seconds at a uniform field illuminated by linearly polarized light, or at a screen illuminated by natural light, but observed through a polarizing filter, should see a faintly pronounced pale yellow light.
