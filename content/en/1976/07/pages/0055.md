This book consists of two chapters. The first chapter is devoted to problems about circles and balls, it formulates the “problem of 13 balls”, with which the names of such outstanding scientists as D. Gregory and I. Newton are associated, and talks about the history of its solution, and the second chapter deals with the problem of polygons, polyhedra and arbitrary figures. The problem, closely related to the “13-ball problem,” opened a new large branch of geometry called “discrete or combinatorial geometry.” This geometry studies extremal geometric problems associated with finding “good enough” locations of a finite number of points or figures.

The main role in Yaglom’s book is played by 16 problems, related thematically, but mathematically almost independent; so he can safely skip tasks that seem very difficult or difficult for the reader. The book also formulates hitherto unsolved problems; Some of them may well serve as a topic for independent work in the field of combinatorial geometry.

The book is accessible to high school students and can be useful in mathematical circles.
