Graphical solution of cubic equations
A. Krasnodemskaya

The famous Cardan formula
$$x = \sqrt[3]{\frac{k}{54} + \sqrt{\frac{-D}{108}}} + \sqrt[3]{\frac{-k}{54} - \sqrt{\frac{-D}{108}}} - \frac{b}{3a},$$
where $k = (2b^3 - 9abc + 27a^2d)/a^3$, $D = (b^2c^2 - 4ac^3 - 4b^3d + 18abcd - 27a^2d^2)/a^4$, allows you to accurately find the roots of the cubic equation
$$ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d = 0.$$ (*)

True, calculations using this formula take a lot of time, not to mention the fact that in many cases you have to operate with complex numbers. Is it not possible to find the roots of the equation (*) that are empty by approximation, but quickly, say, graphically - using a compass and ruler? It turns out that you can’t get by with just a compass and a ruler - as was shown in the article by V. Nilme («Kvant», 1975, No. 6), with a compass and a ruler you can only construct quantities that express through the initial data using the action of addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root extraction (for example, the roots of a quadratic equation are expressed through coefficients, so quadratic equations can be solved graphically using a compass and rulers - this is described in the article by A. Presman, «Kvant», 1972, No. 4).
