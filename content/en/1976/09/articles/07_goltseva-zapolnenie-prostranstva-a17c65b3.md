## Filling space

Fig. 1

Among the regular and semiregular polyhedra (Platonic and Archimedean solids) and equihedral semiregular polyhedra, there are polyhedra that can fill space tightly, “without holes.” figure 1 shows filler polyhedra, and Figures 2–4 show several spatial layers obtained from them. One of the fillings of space is obvious - filling with cubes. The space can be filled with other polyhedra of the same type: truncated octahedra or rhombic dodecahedrons, as well as by combining polyhedra of different types, for example, tetrahedrons and octahedra (Fig. 2); truncated octahedra, cubes and rhombicuboctahedra (Fig.3); rhombicuboctahedra, truncated cubes and truncated tetrahedra (Fig. 4, *a*, *b*).

Consistently repeating such layers, you will get a “packing” of space. figure 5 explains how this wonderful polyhedron can be constructed.
