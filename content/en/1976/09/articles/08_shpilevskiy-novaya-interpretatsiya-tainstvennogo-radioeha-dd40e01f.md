A new interpretation of the mysterious radio echo

In January 1965, the first issue of the new popular science magazine of the USSR Academy of Sciences, “Earth and the Universe,” was published.

Over the years of its existence, the magazine has published many interesting materials devoted to the study of Lunya and the planets of the solar system, quasars, pulsarms, cosmic microwave background radiation and other mysterious objects discovered by radio astronomers. He spoke about the study of our Galaxy and the processes occurring in other galaxies, about “black holes,” the causes of earthquakes, tsunamis, the movement of terrestrial materials, etc. Materials published in the journal Earth and Universe have varying levels of difficulty. Some of them are designed for well-prepared readers; the other does not require any special training and is accessible to high school students.

We are publishing, with a slight abbreviation, two articles devoted to the problem of searching for space civilizations. They were published in the second issue of the magazine in 1976 in the section “Hypotheses, discussions, proposals”, which regularly appears on the pages of the magazine. Having become acquainted with them, we can try to give our own interpretation of the “message of the space probe.”

The Earth and Universe magazine is published six times a year.

In 1960, radio astrologer R. Bracewell expressed the idea that space zones are the most economical and effective way to establish first contacts between neighboring developments of civilization. How will such a zone accessible to near-Earth or lunar orbit operate? First, to make itself known, the probe must use radio signals from the Earth and send them back, like a radio echo, with a large but reasonable task. Only after establishing two-way communication will he move on to other forms of information transmission, for example, transmitting a television image of the part of the sky from which he came, etc. However, neither Bracewell nor anyone else for more than a decade could find a concrete example of the use of their general messages. This was done by a young English astronomer from the University of Glasgow, D. Luenen. He suggested that

Luenen's interpretation
On October 11, 1928, Hulse and Stoermer, sending radio signals at a wavelength of 31.4 m every 20 seconds into the inosphere, responded to them with radio signals with the following delay times: 8, 11, 15, 8, 13, 3, 8, 8, 12, 15, 13, 8 and 8 seconds. Luenen tried to explain the result of this experiment. The essence of his interpretation of the past can be summarized by listing the assumptions he uses.

1. A series of variable delay times of radiozha in the experiment of Hulse and Sturm is the latest space probe, more precisely, its “calling card”, which can be understood if you build a graph “number of the sent signal - radiozha delay time” and compare it with a suitable part of the starry sky.

2. The six points of this graph lying to the right of the vertical eight-second barcher are identified as the incomplete constellation Bootes.

3. Located on the graph to the left of the barcher, we consider the three-second radio point to be a star in Bootes, isolated from the constellation in order to indicate to us that the probe was sent precisely from this star.

4. The difference in distances between Arcturus and Bootes on the star map and on the graph can be attributed to the large proper motion of Arcturus, if we assume that the probe transmitted to us an image of its constellation, “photographed” by it many years ago, immediately after landing on Earth.

Luenen's chart. If you arrange the radio signals in accordance with their delay time, then seven points will stretch out in a vertical barer, to the right of which six points form a configuration that is very reminiscent of the pattern of the constellation Bootes on modern star maps. There is only one point to the left of the barerea. If this point is mirrored to the right of the barerea, then its new location almost coincides with the star in Bootes.

Lunen's interpretation of the radio echo may not satisfy everyone, primarily because it is ambiguous. On the Lunen graph, the point of the three-second radio echo can well be considered not a star in Bolopas, but Arcturus. You can try to identify the Lunen graph with other constellations, or even construct a graph in a different way altogether.

The star in Bolopasa is double. Its components: a red giant and a spectroscopic double star are objects that are unsuitable for the development of civilization on the planet, if such exists near them.

It is surprising that there are no stars from and p close to ε Bolopas on the Lunen graph, comparable in brightness to the star ζ Bolopas present on the graph. At the same time, against the background of the marked non-canvas of the constellation Bolopas, the ellipsis of the vertical receptacle barBER is striking. It's hard to believe that it was built just to highlight the star ε Bolopas. It is impossible to associate barBER with stars located to the left of the main stars of the Bolopas constellation.

All these shortcomings prompted me to look for a new interpretation of the radio echo.

Probe from T China

When constructing a graph “the number of the sent signal - the delay time of the radio echo,” we are not limited in any way in the choice of directions of the coordinate axes. Therefore, another graph can be constructed for the mentioned radio echo series. Having looked closely at the six points lying below the recessive barBER (horizontal body), everyone who saw the constellation Cetus at the culmination will certainly note the similarity of their configuration with this constellation. Next, following Lunen's idea, we first make assumptions.

Cetus constellation.

1. A series of radio echoes in the experiment of Hulse and Stermar is a message from a space probe, to decipher which we use graphics and compare it with a suitable part of the starry sky.

2. The six points of this graph lying below the eight-second barer are identified as the incomplete constellation Cetus.

Let us note that we can talk about the hollowness and non-hollowness of a constellation only from our earthly point of view. As you know, in the past, different peoples united stars into constellations in different ways. The creators of the probe in their space message, apparently, must indicate only the brightest objects surrounding the star from which the probe was sent. It seems more likely that the image of their constellation (along with the program for its transmission) was stored in the probe's memory before sending. This means that the view of their constellation from Earth should be calculated on the basis of astroνομological observations. Errors in these observations and calculations are certainly reflected in our graph. In addition, one cannot forget about the errors in the experiments of Hulse and Stermar. The moments of departure of the radio signal and the return of the radio echo were hardly recorded in 1928 with an accuracy better than ±1 second. All this, including the stars’ own motion during the probe’s flight to Earth, and distortions in star maps associated with the construction of flat pictures of the spherical sky, should explain the discrepancy in the relative positions of the stars on the graph and the stars on the map.

3. The point of the three-second radio echo lying above the barer is the star m Ceti, isolated from the constellation in order to indicate to us that it was from it that the space probe was sent.

Indeed, moving this point below the barer gives the wave a satisfactory hit on its inner figure of six stars. Let us recall that the similarity of m Ceti with our Sun, the solitary nature of the star and its proximity to us (11.8 light years) have long forced astronomers to pay special attention to it. And in 1960, while implementing the Ozma project, several local radio astronomers reliably waited for radio signals from T. Keith at a wavelength of 21 cm.

4. The vertical horizontal barer is a segment of either the celestial equator or the ecliptic for an earthly observer.

The thought of this arises when comparing our graph with the cartosid palate. Not only does the configuration of six points on the graph help the figure constellation Cetus, but also the six corresponding to the latter are located relative to the equator and ecliptic in approximately the same way as the six points of the graph are relative to Barer. However, it is unlikely that from a distance of 11.8 light years it would be possible to measure the angle of inclination of the earth’s axis to the plane of the earth’s orbit (and therefore find out how the celestial equator of earthlings passes among the stars). Most likely, the barer is a segment of the ecliptic, which can be determined with a mountain of greater distances than 11.8 light years. By the way, the discrepancies in the inclination of the barer and the ecliptic to the line connecting the stars β and ν Ceti can, for example, be explained by the fact that from m Ceti most likely only the average orbital plane for all the planets of the Solar System can be determined (and is it possible to find out the number of these planets). It is possible that when the compilers of the cosmic message decided to include a segment of the ecliptic in it to facilitate the identification of their constellation, it “came into their heads” to use the resulting barer to highlight m Cetus.

5. The seven points of the barer and the point of intersection of the perpendicular dropped from the three-second point to the barer are a message about the existence of eight planets around M Ceti.

This interpretation of the points that make up the barer arises because in the barer the two right points, corresponding to the thirteenth and fourteenth radio echo, seem to be missing. Consequently, their addition to this series of radio echoes (and they cannot be the beginning of another, repeated series) was intended to increase the already existing number of points. For what purpose? Here it should be noted that the cosmic message, generally speaking, must be as simple as possible for broadness and detail and maximum information. Therefore, nothing should be overlooked in our chart. Next, the intersection point of the redistribution used to isolate m Cetus from the constellation should be counted among the seven barer points. In addition, if we already know from the message that the probe landed from the star T Ceti, then we ask, what information could we extract from the message? It is logical to think that this will be a message about how many planets are near M Ceti and which of them the probe was sent from. The eight points of the barer seem to indicate just this: the number of planets in T. Ceti is eight. It is remarkable that our Sun (a star slightly larger than Ceti) has nine planets. Perhaps this is how it should be: similar planetary systems are formed in physical burning stars under similar conditions.

6. The probe was sent from the third in order of distance from the planet T. Ceti.

Judging by the graph, the point corresponding to the planet from which the probe was sent lies at the intersection of the redistribution drawn from the three-second point of the barer. This is the third point on the barer, counting in the order of the radio echo. It is important to note that our Earth is the third planet farthest from the Sun. Without a doubt, this coincidence is not accidental. As is known, the “habitable zone” in the vicinity of a star must necessarily be on a planet that is not too close to its star and not too far from it. We must also not forget about the similarities between Whale and the Sun. True, today's luminosity of M. Cetus is almost half that of the sun. But this consistency may indicate that Ceti is much older than the Sun. This means that the planetary system of T. Ceti is also older and, finally, the civilization that sent the probe is older and more developed compared to the earthly one.

So, the same method of reasoning and the same initial ideas that Lunen replenished lead to a different interpretation of the tantalizing radio echo. It is difficult to say which of them is more likely. Obviously, only new experiments and astronomical observations can lead to a scientific solution to the problems of the tantalizing radio echo.
