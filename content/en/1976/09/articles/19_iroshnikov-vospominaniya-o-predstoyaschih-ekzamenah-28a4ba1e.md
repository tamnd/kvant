Memories of upcoming exams

The summer holidays are over, the new school year begins. It also begins in our “Applicant Workshop”. For many of you, this is the last academic year; in the spring you will have to answer: what did you learn at school, are you able to study at college? And therefore, this year you will not only have to complete the school course in mathematics and physics, but also repeat everything you have learned earlier in order to be “fully armed” by spring.
The article published below will not teach you how to solve this or that specific problem. But it will help you develop the right approach to any task, get out of a dead end if you find yourself in one, and pay attention to the most essential aspects of the task. That is why we are opening our new academic year.

It started a long time ago, back in 7th grade. When I came across difficult problems in planometry, and then in stereometry, I sometimes turned to my father for help, but at the same time, “real help” he offered:
- Let's decide "at speed"!
He sat at his table, and I sat at mine. Each one “pulled away” alone!
- Ready! - the father will cheerfully say, look at his watch and... hide his piece of paper in the table, and at this time I am still “digging” somewhere in the middle of the decision.
When I finish, we compare the answers and discuss the best solution method. It happened, however, to the husband: Mishka Tumanov is already on the hockey rink, but nothing works out for me, and the answer is thrown up from time to time -

gives me “leading questions.” One day I started a conversation:
- Dad! Why do you decide faster? Is my intuition working poorly?
- This is possible, but the main thing is that you decide haphazardly!
— ?!
- You waste a lot of time when you start thinking about a solution without having yet built a good, clear drawing with the data from the problem conditions marked on it. After all, without this, there is still no incentive for your billions of brain cells to begin joint, complex work, which is usually called “logical thinking.” Your brain, with all its convolutions, is the same computer, but a computer will never be able to work without ordered source information.
- Is that all? Is this the only thing I need to fix?
- No, of course not! There are many techniques to make your head work faster and more purposefully. All of them at once, please, and you won’t remember!
- What should we do?
- Let's do this! Here's a sheet of cardboard for you. Graph it into cells, measure them, and every time we compare and analyze our solutions, write briefly in these cells what helped us find a solution to the problem! In a year or two you will have good material, and then intuition will appear!...
Three years have passed! The cardboard hanging on the wall is almost completely covered in writing, it has turned yellow and warped, but all this time it has helped me a lot. Father often laughs:
- And according to what “commandment” will you perform your task today?
Now my father very rarely solves many geometric (and physical!) problems. If I have difficulties, and Mishka Tumanov is already whistling under the window, then the only answer is

APPLICANT'S COMMANDMENTS

1. Immediately start drawing according to the given conditions - you will think about it later!
2. A good drawing is a good assistant; with it the idea of ​​a solution will “come on its own.” A bad drawing will not only complicate the solution, but will also lead you into a dead end when trying to “prove” something that does not exist in reality. Make a clear drawing in the middle of the sheet - a ruler, triangle, compass, protractor will also help you in “construction tasks.” If conditions allow, draw devils (at least roughly) to scale! (See also «Kvant», 1976, No. 6, p. 49).
3. Avoid drawing partial cases (rectangular, equilateral or equilateral triangles, equal circles, etc.) if they are not provided for by the conditions of the problem - looking at such a drawing, you will soon “convince” that this will always be the case, and your thought will be directed in the same direction!
4. In Vsterometry, make a large full-page drawing with dotted invisible lines! This way you won’t get bogged down in layers of lines and symbols, and you’ll have a place to “spread your brains” - you can write formulas and very important dependencies on the drawing itself (next to the segments!) without unnecessary letter symbols!
5. Put all the data on the drawing! If you forget something, you won’t be able to solve the problem!
6. “Assigned” - take off the drawing! “Find” - in red! With this you ensure
Concentrate your thoughts on the main thing!
7. Label segments and angles in small Latin and Greek letters! Large - only for agreement with the conditions! It won’t bother your eyes, you won’t get confused, and there will be less writing!
8. In the conditions of a problem in the physics of rotation - within reasonable, of course, limits (see also «Kvant», 1975, No. 5, p. 60).
9. Remember and write down next to figure all geometric definitions, axioms, theorems (laws), properties and consequences on this issue - this is also necessary information for your brain cells by the time they learn the logical construction of a solution to a problem!
10. The preparation is complete - switch your “computer” to full power!
11. Spend 2-3 minutes on a thorough general analysis of the specifics of the problem conditions - it will pay off handsomely! If during these minutes you use all the power of your geometric imagination, then even under complex conditions of the problem you will be able to discover a rational (short and elegant!) solution. By immediately adopting a crazy template solution, you increased the amount of computational work and the chances of errors.
12. If the task is complex, find the “logic” for solving the problem, write a solution plan. In a complicated and particularly “unmanageable” problem, a solution plan is required.
13. Don't worry! (This is especially for applicants.)
14. Give full rein to your intuition! - someone said that intuition is a gap in logic, but a fruitful gap; that this is an opportunity to take a step in an unpredictable direction; what a measure of talent! Why suppress it? Intuition will help you outline the shortest path to solving a problem.

15. A thought can unnoticed “go to the side” - watch it (or, more precisely, watch yourself)!

16. A successful auxiliary construction sometimes immediately reveals the “secrets” of the problem conditions. If the drawn auxiliary line still turns out to be unnecessary, then immediately sort it out - everything unnecessary interferes with the thought process. (See also «Kvant», 1975, No. 10, p. 48).

17. If you can’t find a geometric expression for the length of the “desired” segment, then try to do this for its individual parts and sum them up!

18. Similar triangles can be quickly constructed by transferring parallel lines using a ruler and a triangle.

19. The distorted shape in a three-dimensional drawing is built side by side in its natural form - a right angle will become a real right angle, the similarity of triangles will become obvious, etc. (see also «Kvant», 1974, No. 10, p. 32).

20. Elements of different planes and sections are highlighted with colored pencils.

21. Vsterometric fits the model well, even a hastily folded one made of paper.

22. A geometric problem is usually solved in several ways. If it turns out that you have chosen a very cumbersome path, return to figure and try a different concept, in time you will only win!

23. Don't you like “proof problems” when you have to prove, for example, that a = b? So you simply look for an expression for the length of segment a through the length of the “given” segment b.

24. If your figure is “silent”, then turn it around and look again - with a new angle, new thoughts may appear, and then the right decision!

25. In some planetary problems, solutions are achieved by “exiting into space” (see «Kvant», 1975, No. 5, p. 45).

26. Nothing works? Don't be discouraged! A general analysis of the geometric situation that developed at figure was carried out - even Suvorov recognized the need to retreat in time! And the mathematical calculations begin again, on a blank sheet of paper - psychologically it is very difficult to notice the inaccuracy in the old record!

27. Don't forget that trigonometry is used to make solving geometric (and physics) problems easier. However, do not get carried away and do not go beyond the boundaries of its reasonable combination with planimetry. In the 10th grade, you have already forgotten planimetry, but replacing it with trigonometry is not always wise - this can lead to very cumbersome solutions. For oblique triangles, the cosine theorem is of great help.

28. In “text” algebraic problems and in physics, make figure graphics and analyze the situation (see also «Kvant», 1971, No. 11, p. 48).

29. The rational choice of unknowns when solving a problem is a delicate matter! Mobilizing all your experience and intuition!

30. When designing a control system, it is necessary that all correspondences arising from the conditions of the geometric problem be used.

31. Don’t be afraid to use control systems in geometry with the crown and more unknowns - algebra is a good help! Write an equation with a “blue” parameter and after 5-6 lines of an equation with a “red” parameter (see paragraph 6), and fill the construction gap with a whole of additional equations, no more introducing new and new “unknown” segments into them - when solving the system they will be excluded (the “direct counting” method, see also «Kvant», 1975, No. 11, p. 45).

32. The separated “red” and “blue” segments can sometimes be “brought together” by a number of geometric transformations.

33. Sometimes: composition of a “herd” of equations and count their number and the number of unknown segments and angles, but... being afraid of identical equations!
Trigonometry will give you insufficient equations.

34. The solution of a large group of geometric problems is facilitated by the introduction of additional elements (length, area, volume, angle), not directly specified in the conditions of the problem (see «Kvant», 1974, No. 2, p. 46).

35. If the conditions of the problem talk about several segments, then first write the formulas in general form (in letter notation) and study them! This is preferable, however, in any case, or you may not be warned about several segments of the problem condition, but you are still required to find them all.

36. Because of possible simplifications, do not rush to replace the letter designations of numbers from the condition, but sometimes these numbers are so “selected” that it is they that determine the shortest path to the solution, and non-tortic similar problems in general do not have a unique solution (see also «Kvant», 1973, No. 3, p. 44, and No. 11, p. 52).

37. In many institutes, the main criterion is initiative and flexibility of thought! And so, during application exams, we are given psychological “traps” - the correct solution turns out to be not where it seems at first glance, but in the depths of almost imperceptible nuances of the problem conditions! Exercise your “flexibility of mind” and analyze “unusual” options!

38. Maximum care! Don’t do several complex algebraic transformations “in your head” at the same time - do them sequentially in an “extra” line! The progress of a great solution can be “negated” because of just one forgotten minus sign...

39. If you need to find an error, then don’t look for it in the old record of complex algebraic calculations - it’s better to do everything again and compare the results!

40. Do not part with a slide rule - it will not only save time on approximate calculations, but will also guide you when extracting the exact root of large integers, calculate snus and tangent, logarithms, and help you when checking the finished answer.

41. If everything is “stuck” for you, then you don’t need to hum some favorite tune under your breath, you don’t need to whistle quietly, you don’t need to thoughtfully draw an intricate vignette in the margins - you just think that this helps resolve the difficulties, but time goes by. Be active in overcoming difficulties!

42. Don't rely on your neighbor! Independence is a mandatory element of studying at the institute!

43. A beautiful and efficient solution is certainly better than cumbersome algebraic calculations, but it takes a lot of time to figure it out, so consider that the best thing is to get the correct answer... by any means.

44. Respect the examiner: write the number carefully, write all the formulas in a column, don’t skimp on paper! Know how to cross it out carefully, then you won’t have to spend a lot of time preparing the final copy! At the end, after the word “answer”, the solution to the problem will be clearly written. All this is the best guarantee that the examiner will understand your notes and will not give you a “3” or even a “2” for not finding a solution to the problem!

45. When you have a problem to solve and there is still time left, look through everything from the very beginning - not so much to find possible errors, but to find a more elegant solution! After all, mathematics is the most subtle and romantic subject that you take at school, and you will receive pleasure when, having found a new, more beautiful solution, you will thereby discover for yourself some new riddle of this greatest science of all the Hayk of Humanity...
