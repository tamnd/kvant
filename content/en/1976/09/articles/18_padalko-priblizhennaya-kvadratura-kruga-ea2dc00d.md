Approximate squaring of a circle

The unsolvability of the problem of finding a square equal in size to a given circle using a compass and ruler was well proven in the 19th century. But the approximation problem is easy to solve. First, let’s find the ratio of the diagonal of the square to the diameter of the circle: $S_{кр} = \pi R^2$, side of an equal square $a = \sqrt{\pi R^2} = R\sqrt{\pi}$, diagonal of the square (according to the Pythagorean theorem) $l = R\sqrt{2\pi}$, from which

$$\frac{l}{2R} = \frac{R\sqrt{2\pi}}{2R} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2}.$$

By choosing the sought approximations for this number, we obtain an approximation of the solution to the problem of squaring the circle. For example, let's put

$$\frac{l}{2R} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2} \approx \frac{\sqrt{2\cdot 3,1416}}{2} \approx \frac{5,013}{4} \approx \frac{5}{4},$$

i.e. $l = \frac{5R}{2}$ (see Fig.), then the relative error of the square area is equal to

$$\delta = \frac{\left|\pi R^2 - \frac{1}{2} l^2\right|}{\pi R^2} = \frac{\left|\pi - 2\left(\frac{l}{2R}\right)^2\right|}{\pi} \approx 0,0053.$$
