6. Would complex numbers help?

Everything that is written below is intended for a reader familiar with complex numbers.

However, “acquaintance” with complex numbers, in essence, is nothing more than the habit of using certain words. Complex numbers appeared in mathematics in much the same way as negative numbers or fractions a little earlier. Since not every real number can have a square root, it is convenient to supplement the supply of numbers with a new number $i$, the square of which is equal to -1 (as well, if you want to make a universal subtraction operation, introduce negative numbers). Together with $i$, numbers of the form $a + bi$ are introduced, where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers, and it is agreed that operations on these numbers are performed according to the following rules:

$$(a + bi) + (c + di) = a + bi + c + di = (a + c) + (b + d)i,$$
$$(a + bi)(c + di) = ac + adi + bci + bdi^2 = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i$$

(in the last equality we taught that $i^2 = -1$). The numbers constructed are called complex numbers; the numbers $a$ and $b$ are called the corresponding real and minimal numbers $a + bi$; two complex numbers are equal if and only if their real parts are equal and their minimum parts are equal. Let us also give Moivre's formula (we will need it below):

$$(\cos \alpha + i \sin \alpha)(\cos \beta + i \sin \beta) = \cos (\alpha + \beta) + i \sin (\alpha + \beta),$$

which is an immediate consequence of the formula for the variation of complex numbers and the well-known trionometric formulas.

Let's return to formula (3). In the case where $\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27} < 0$, you can try to find a solution to equation (2) using it, extracting cube roots from complex numbers.

Taking the cube root of the complex number $a + bi$ means solving the equation

$$(x + iy)^3 = a + bi,$$

that is, the equation $x^3 - 3xy^2 + i(3x^2y - y^3) = a + bi$. The last equality means that

$$\begin{cases} x^3 - 3xy^2 = a, \\ 3x^2y - y^3 = b. \end{cases}$$

This system is again reduced in various ways to a cubic equation. For example, like this:

$$y^2 = \frac{x^3 - a}{3x} (\text{из первого уравнения}),$$

$$y \left( 3x^2 - \frac{x^3 - a}{3x} \right) = b,$$

$$y = \frac{3bx}{8x^3 + a},$$

$$x^3 - 3x \cdot \frac{9b^2x^2}{(8x^3 + a)^2} = a,$$

$$(x^3 - a)(8x^3 + a)^2 - 27b^2x^3 = 0,$$

or, assuming $x^3 = z,$

$$(z - a)(8z + a)^2 - 27b^2z = 0. \quad (4)$$

Of course, we can again turn to formula (3). But it turns out that applying this formula to equation (4) always results in a negative square root. However, this is not surprising, since, as is easy to prove, the cube root of a complex number has a meaning.

Thus, formula (3) cannot be used in this way. But still, with its help you can find approximate values ​​of the roots of equation (2). To do this, you need to have, in addition to the table of square and cube roots, also trionometric tables and apply the Moivre formula, from which it follows that

$$\cos \alpha + i \sin \alpha = \left( \cos \frac{\alpha}{3} + i \sin \frac{\alpha}{3} \right)^3.$$
