⟦folio 62⟧

33. Sometimes: composition of a “herd” of equations and count their number and the number of unknown segments and angles, but... being afraid of identical equations!
Trigonometry will give you insufficient equations.

34. The solution of a large group of geometric problems is facilitated by the introduction of additional elements (length, area, volume, angle), not directly specified in the conditions of the problem (see «Kvant», 1974, No. 2, p. 46).

35. If the conditions of the problem talk about several segments, then first write the formulas in general form (in letter notation) and study them! This is preferable, however, in any case, or you may not be warned about several segments of the problem condition, but you are still required to find them all.

36. Because of possible simplifications, do not rush to replace the letter designations of numbers from the condition, but sometimes these numbers are so “selected” that it is they that determine the shortest path to the solution, and non-tortic similar problems in general do not have a unique solution (see also «Kvant», 1973, No. 3, p. 44, and No. 11, p. 52).

37. In many institutes, the main criterion is initiative and flexibility of thought! And so, during application exams, we are given psychological “traps” - the correct solution turns out to be not where it seems at first glance, but in the depths of almost imperceptible nuances of the problem conditions! Exercise your “flexibility of mind” and analyze “unusual” options!

38. Maximum care! Don’t do several complex algebraic transformations “in your head” at the same time - do them sequentially in an “extra” line! The progress of a great solution can be “negated” because of just one forgotten minus sign...

39. If you need to find an error, then don’t look for it in the old record of complex algebraic calculations - it’s better to do everything again and compare the results!

40. Do not part with a slide rule - it will not only save time on approximate calculations, but will also guide you when extracting the exact root of large integers, calculate snus and tangent, logarithms, and help you when checking the finished answer.

41. If everything is “stuck” for you, then you don’t need to hum some favorite tune under your breath, you don’t need to whistle quietly, you don’t need to thoughtfully draw an intricate vignette in the margins - you just think that this helps resolve the difficulties, but time goes by. Be active in overcoming difficulties!

42. Don't rely on your neighbor! Independence is a mandatory element of studying at the institute!

43. A beautiful and efficient solution is certainly better than cumbersome algebraic calculations, but it takes a lot of time to figure it out, so consider that the best thing is to get the correct answer... by any means.

44. Respect the examiner: write the number carefully, write all the formulas in a column, don’t skimp on paper! Know how to cross it out carefully, then you won’t have to spend a lot of time preparing the final copy! At the end, after the word “answer”, the solution to the problem will be clearly written. All this is the best guarantee that the examiner will understand your notes and will not give you a “3” or even a “2” for not finding a solution to the problem!

45. When you have a problem to solve and there is still time left, look through everything from the very beginning - not so much to find possible errors, but to find a more elegant solution! After all, mathematics is the most subtle and romantic subject that you take at school, and you will receive pleasure when, having found a new, more beautiful solution, you will thereby discover for yourself some new riddle of this greatest science of all the Hayk of Humanity...
