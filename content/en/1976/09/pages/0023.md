It can be considered proven that in a perfect way there is a living, continuous movement of the smallest particles, since only such a movement is capable of communicating those points that move the observed grain in one direction or the other.

It's easy to see why microsoptic grains were needed to detect thermal motion. Further, in a relatively rarefied substance, in a gas, under normal conditions there will be 2.7 1019 molecules in each cubic centimeter; all these molecules move at enormous speeds in all possible directions. Therefore, the number of shocks experienced by a body of the same size is so great that we not only do not have time to notice an individual shock, but also do not notice the random predominance of shocks in one direction over shocks in the opposite direction. The smaller the body, the fewer shocks it experiences, and for microsoptic dust grains the number of shocks it experiences is no longer so great that the predominance of shocks from one or the other random direction is constant, and the dust grain is thrown from side to side. Such a speck of dust will contain millions more molecules, so its random movement that we see is not molecular movement itself; we do not notice only a single molecule, but only the predominance of shocks in some direction; but this speck of dust is a sure indicator of the existence of molecular motion, just as the sight of a ship rocking on the sea indicates the existence of waves.
