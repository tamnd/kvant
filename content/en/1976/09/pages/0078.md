6) When symmetric relative to the straight line $BK$, the bisector of angle $BA'L$ goes into the bisector of angle $BCM$, since $BK$ is the bisector of angle $A'BC$ (see 2°a)). In this case, the point $O$ of intersection of these bisects appears on the axis of symmetry $BK$. Consequently, point $O$ is removed at the same distance from the following pairs of sides: $A'L$ and $A'B$, CM and BC, $A'B$ and BC, from which it follows that point $O$ is equilateral from side $BM$ and $CM$, $A'L$ and $BL$, i.e. that $O$ belongs to the bisectors of angles $BMC$ and $A'LB$.

6°. Let us first analyze the case when $45^\circ \leqslant \widehat{ABC} < 90^\circ$.
Let's draw a ray $BD$, a peredicular ray $AB$, and construct a ray $BD'$, a symmetrical ray $BD$ relative to a straight line $BC$.
1) Let us assume that the messenger is at point $P$, located inside the angle $ABD'$ (or on its side $BD'$). Let $Q$ be some point of the river $BC: Q \in BC$. It is clear that if the messenger points the horse from point $Q$, then to the road $AB$ he will travel along the straight line $QR$, peredicular to $AB$ (see Fig. 2).
Let's construct a point $P'$, symmetrical to the point $P$ relative to the straight line $BC$. The length of the path $PQR$ is equal to the length of the broken line $P'QR$, since $|PQ| = |P'Q|$, i.e., is equal to $|P'Q| + |QR|$. But $|P'Q| + |QR| > |P'R| > |P'B| = |PB|$, since the base of the oblique $P'R$ is further removed from the base of the peredicular $P'S$ than the base of the oblique $P'B$. Therefore, if $45^\circ \leqslant \widehat{ABC} < 90^\circ$, the messenger needs to find the knight at the vertex $B$ of angle $ABC$.
2) Let now the messenger be at point $P$, located inside the angle $CBD'$ (Fig. 3).
Again, take an arbitrary point $Q \in BC$, draw ($QR) \perp (AB)$) and construct a point $P'$, symmetrically to the point $P$ relative to the side $BC$. Draw ($P'S) \perp (AB)$ ($S \in BA$)); then $|PQ| = |P'Q|$ and $|PQ| + |QR| = |P'Q| + |QR|$. But $|P'S| < |P'Q| + |QR|$; therefore, the messenger must find the horse at point $X = (P'S) \cap [BC]$.
