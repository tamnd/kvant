Filling space

Among regular and semiregular polyhedra (Platonic and Archimedean solids) and isohedra semiregular polyhedra*) there are polyhedra that can fill space tightly, “without holes.” On Fig.1 there are polyhedra - fillers, and on Fig.2-4 there are several spatial layers obtained from them. One of the ways to fill space is to fill it with cubes. The space can be filled with other polyhedra of the same type: truncated octahedra or rhombic dodecahedra, and also by combining polyhedra of different types, for example, tetrahedra and octahedra (Fig. 2); truncated octahedra, cubes and rhombicuboctahedra (Fig.3); rhombicuboctahedra, truncated cubes and truncated tetrahedra (Fig. 4 a, 6).

The first page of the cover depicts a spatial “layer” of rhombic dodecahedrons. After adding and repeating such layers, you will get a “packing” of space. At Fig.1 it is explained how this remarkable polyhedron can be constructed. The cubes have a common center and are cooked by a factor of two. The vertices of the octahedron are the centers of the faces of a large cube. Prove that the quadrilateral highlighted in color is a rhombus.

R. Goltseva

*) See «Kvant», 1976, No. 1, p. 2 and p. 8.
