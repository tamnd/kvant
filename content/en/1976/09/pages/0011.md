⟦folio 9⟧

February 10, 1547 Tartaglia, together with Cardano, responds to Ferrari. He objects to Tartaglia’s reproaches, points out shortcomings in his book, and in one case reproaches him for appropriating someone else’s results and for having a bad memory (it seems that at that time this was a difficult accusation). In conclusion, Tartaglia is called to a public debate “on geometry, arithmetic or related disciplines, such as Astrology, Music, Cosmography, Perspective, Architecture, etc.”

On February 19, Tartaglia, in his response, tries to get into a squabble with Cardano himself: “I wrote there in such a heated and insulting tone in order to force his lordship (and not you) to write something of his own, or I have old scores with him.”

The discussion of the terms of the dispute is dragging on. Tartaglia begins to understand that Cardano will remain in the

rone. Then he begins to emphasize Ferrari’s lack of independence, namely his “creature of Cardano,” as he called himself in his challenge. His “Questions”, which he traditionally sent in response to a challenge, he addresses to both of them: “You, Messer Gerolamo, and you, Messer Luigi...”

Eventually Tartaglia agreed to a DISPUT with Ferrari. DISPUT took place on August 10, 1548 in Milan, in the presence of many famous persons, but in the absence of Cardano. ODISPUTE only short records of Tartaglia have been preserved, from which it is almost impossible to reconstruct the true picture. It looks like Tartaglia has lost order.

However, DISPUT had nothing to do with the problem that caused the dispute. And in general, they had as little to do with clarifying the true DISPUTES as duels.
