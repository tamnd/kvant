Let's try to figure out what our formula does and doesn't give. Let's start with the simplest.

3. Theorem

If the expression $\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27}$ is non-negative, then the right side of formula (3) is a solution to equation (2).

Proof. Let's put
$$\alpha = \sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27}}},$$
$$\beta = \sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} - \sqrt{\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27}}}.$$

Obviously, the right side of formula (3) is exactly equal to the sum $\alpha + \beta$. And the product $\alpha \beta$ is equal to $-\frac{p}{3}$:

$$\sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27}}} \times \sqrt[3]{-\frac{q}{2} - \sqrt{\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27}}} = \sqrt[3]{(-\frac{q}{2})^2 - (\frac{q^2}{4} + \frac{p^3}{27})} = \sqrt[3]{-\frac{p^3}{27}} = -\frac{p}{3}.$$
