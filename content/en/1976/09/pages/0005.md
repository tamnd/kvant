Niccolo was left early in the care of his mother. Due to poverty, he studied at school for only two weeks. During this time in the class writing up to the letter “K”. Tartaglia left school without ever learning to write his last name. However, he continues to study on his own and becomes a “master of the abacus” (which is like an arithmetic teacher in a private commercial school). Since 1534, Tartaglia has lived in Venice.

Tartaglia's scientific pursuits were stimulated by promises of engineers and artillery from the famous Venetian arsenal. In 1537 he published the book “New Science”, devoted to issues of mechanics. This book played an important role in the development of ballistics. In 1546 he published the book “Problems and Various Inventions.” If in the first of these two books Tartaglia, always following Aristotle, also asserts that a body thrown at an angle first flies along an inclined path, then along a circular arc and finally falls down vertically, then in the second book he already writes that the trajectory does not have a single part that is completely straight. Tartaglia translated Archimedes and Euclid into Italian (which, as opposed to Latin, he calls “folk”).

Having received Fiore's challenge, Tartaglia hoped to achieve an easy victory. He was not afraid even when he discovered that all 30 Fiore problems contained equations (1) for different a and b. Tartaglia believed that Fiore himself did not have the ability to solve the problems he proposed: “I thought that none of them could be solved, because brother Luca (i.e. Pacioli - S.G.) assures in his work that this kind of equation cannot be solved with a general formula.”
