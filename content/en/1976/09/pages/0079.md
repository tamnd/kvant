1. Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ be the areas of triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$. Let's put $|AO| = x$, $|OC| = y$. Then $\frac{4}{x} = \frac{S_1}{x + y}$ and $\frac{9}{y} = \frac{S_2}{x + y}$, from which for the area $S$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ we obtain the expression: $S = S_1 + S_2 = 4 \frac{y}{x} + 9 \frac{x}{y} + 13$. It is easy to check that this expression at $\frac{y}{x} = \frac{3}{2}$ is sufficient to have a minimum of 25 cm$^2$.

2. First of all, note that at the moment when the true time is 2n o'clock, both hands are at the $n$ o'clock position, i.e. at $0 < n < 12$ they show the wrong time, after which they move for an hour at normal speed, continuing to show the wrong time.

Exactly one hour later, i.e. at $(2n + 1)$ o'clock, the minute hand is at $n$ o'clock, and the hour hand is at $(n + 1)$ o'clock, after which the hands change speed. During the next hour, after $t$ minutes, the hour hand will be at the $(5n + 5 + t)$ minute mark; and the true time, equal to $2n + 1$ hours and $t$ minutes, corresponds on a minute scale to the position of the hour hand at the $(10n + 5 + \frac{t}{12})$ minute division. If at this moment the clock shows the correct time, then it should be $10n + 5 + \frac{t}{12} = 5n + 5 + t$, hence $t = \frac{60n}{11}$.

Similar reasoning for the minute hand leads to the equation $t = 5n + \frac{t}{12}$. Where is the location for $t$.
