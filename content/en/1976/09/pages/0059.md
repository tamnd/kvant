B. Shortest path

Let's solve the problem from paragraph 18 of the book “Geometry 6”.

Given a line $l$ and two points $A$ and $B$ lying on one side of it. Find the shortest path from $A$ to $B$ with an entry into straight line $l$.

Fig. 3.

Let us denote by $A'$ the point symmetrical to the point $A$ relative to the straight line $l$ (see Fig. 4). Let $X$ be some point on the line $l$. Then $|AX| = |A'X|$ and $|AX| + |BX| = |A'X| + |XB|$.

Let from $A$ to $B$ with entry into the straight line $l$ be equal to the length of the broken line $A'XB$; it will be the shortest if the length of this broken line is the smallest. Then the broken line $A'XB$ should turn into the segment $A'B$, and the point $X$ should turn into the point $C = [A'B] \cap (l)$.

Let $ACB$ be the desired one.

From the solution of this problem it follows that for the shortest path $ACB$ the angles $1$ and $2$ are equal to each other (see Fig. 4). So, the shortest path can be characterized by the fact that for it the “angle of incidence” is equal to the “angle of reflection”.

This fact follows, in particular, from Fermat’s famous at and pas: light (through reflection, refraction, etc.) propagates between two points along a path that takes the least amount of time to reach *).

Basic symmetry is often used to find the shortest path or to implement the “angle of incidence equals angle of reflection” situation.

*) More precisely, not the smallest, but the extreme. For more details on this, see the articles by S. Verov, «Kvant», 1975, No. 12, p. 29, A. Zemlyakova, «Kvant», 1976, No. 5, p. 17 and L. Turiyansky, «Kvant», 1976, No. 8, p. 17.
