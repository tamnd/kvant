This mysterious audible world

E.SOKOLOV

IT’S GOOD ON A QUIET AUGUST EVENING to sit on the high, steep bank of the Oka and watch the world around you. The heat of the day has already subsided, the smell of mown grass smells, midges swarm in the slanting rays of the sun, thoughts are carried far, far away... And then one day, when I was admiring the sunset in such a blissful mood, I heard the noise of an airplane engine above my head. For some time I looked at the cloud from behind which a sound was heard, but the plane did not appear. And suddenly, out of the corner of my eye, I noticed that a plane flashed through the gap between the clouds, but not at all in the place where I expected (Fig.1).

⟦column⟧

If the train had passed, would you still be wandering through the forest? But the railway was only 50 meters away. Only you couldn’t see anything, but you could hear the train well. “And in the ocean, even in clear water, you can’t see anything further than 20 meters.” If it were not for sound sonars, submarines would enter foreign ports as if they were home. What about dolphins? What about sperm whales? What about bats? “So, let’s sum it up,” I intervened in the argument. – Does everyone agree that the audible world can be as important and useful as the visible world? And if so, then let's find out how this mysterious audible world works. For this we need very little: one formula and logical thinking.

First formula and logical thinking

“Yes, I screwed up! – I thought. – Of course, one could easily realize that the point at which we see the plane and the point at which we hear the plane are completely different points. After all, by the time the sound reaches us from point A, the plane will have already moved to point B.” Over dinner, I told about this incident to my students - participants in a summer school camp - as an example of the fact that in any matter, even simple ones, one should use logical thinking, and not rely only on habit. Let's start, as usual, with reasonable simplifications. Suppose that on some planet Yalmez, flat as a table, there are no mountains, no forests, no clouds from which sound could be reflected, but there is only an observer located at point H, and an airplane flying in a straight line with a constant speed v at an altitude h (Fig.2). We will take the moment $ког-_{Рис.} 2$ as the zero time reference and the plane will be directly above the observer. With this choice, the law of motion of the aircraft will have the form

$x_{c} ( t ) = vt$.

A flying airplane will now be for us simply a source of sound, which is generated by engine parts vibrating in operation and turbulent jet streams and propagates in all directions at the speed of sound c. An observer, who, following the example of submariners, would better be called a listener, listens carefully to the roar of the aircraft coming to him, and we begin to find out what he hears. We will obtain our main working formula if we translate the words already spoken above into mathematical language: “by the time the sound reaches us from point A, the plane will have already shifted to point B.” Let's do it this way. Let the plane at time $\tau$ be at point A with coordinate $x_{c} = v\tau$. The sound emitted at this moment begins to spread in all directions.
