"Magic" dice (dice)

Imagine that you have three cubes. Regular six-sided cubes with some numbers written on each side. You invite your partner to take any cube that he likes best, and you choose one of the two remaining cubes. Next, you start playing: everyone rolls their own dice, and the one whose dice shows the highest number wins. So, the dice can be such that with a large number of throws, on average you will win, that is, you will win against your partner more often. Even if your partner changes the cube, for example, takes the cube you played with, he will still lose if you use the right to choose a cube from the two remaining ones. Could this be possible? It turns out it can. The whole secret is in the numbers that are placed on the faces of the cubes. The task is to find these numbers and place them on the faces of three cubes so that the cubes have the “magic” property described above. Interestingly, the solution to this problem can be a special case of cyclicity in team competitions. Indeed, let’s take three teams of six players, cyclically beating their opponents in type II competitions. This is easy to do, for example, by “doubling” the players in the first example of paradox 2: A: 5 5 2 2 2 2 B: 4 4 4 4 1 1 C: 3 3 3 3 3 3 Next, take three dice used in gambling, and instead of numbers 1 – 6, we plot the ratings of the three teams on their faces. Now the dice have become “magic” - they have the property described above, which helps their owner win against their partner. Whichever dice your partner chooses, among the remaining two there is always one that gives you a better chance of winning than your partner. To do this, you need to choose a cube whose “team” is stronger than your partner’s “team”. Indeed, throwing two dice at the same time is a choice of one of the 36 games of the Type II competition. If “your team” beats “your partner’s team” with a score of m: n, where m > n, m + n = 36, then out of 36 possible outcomes of the toss, you win in m cases, and your partner wins in n cases. The probability of your winning is P = m/36, P > > 1/2. The "team games" results for our dice are as follows: A: B = 20: 16, B: C = C: A = 24: 12.
