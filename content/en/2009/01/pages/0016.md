Solid state physicists need to obtain background information about the strength and elastic characteristics of building materials under different conditions. Electrical engineering - electrical characteristics of the conductors and insulators used. When studying condensed matter, physicists (theorists and experimentalists) use the results of atomic physics and cannot do without them. It is not enough to know that the body is made of atoms or molecules. To understand the nature of the properties of macroscopic bodies and the phenomena occurring in them, it is necessary to know the structure of the “bricks” from which they are built. But about particles - the building material of molecules and atoms, about electrons, protons and neutrons - it is enough to know what can be gleaned from any reference book. Moreover, I think that even to explain the phenomenon of life, this knowledge is quite sufficient. Clearly, this does not mean that we can already answer all the questions posed by molecular biology. Many years of intensive research lie ahead. And, most importantly, you need to wait for new ideas to appear. In recent decades, molecular biology, which has absorbed much of solid state physics, has made brilliant achievements. Physical knowledge about the structure of the molecules from which all living organisms are built is still completely sufficient. I think, although not everyone has this opinion, that any special deepening into the physics of elementary particles will never be required for the development of molecular biology. As before, molecular biologists, like solid-state physicists, will only need to know about electrons, protons and neutrons what is well known today.
