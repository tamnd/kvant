Now consider the arrangement of regular decagons, as shown in figure 9. Note that the icosahedron, dodecahedron, as well as the truncated icosahedron (the shape of the fullerene molecule $C_{60} )$) have a regular decagonal cross-section. This results in a stack of self-wedging icosahedrons (Fig.10), a new stack of dodecahedrons (Fig. 11) and masonry from truncated icosahedrons (Fig.12). We seemingly slightly changed the description of the structures several times. However, each time such a change led to new results. The language of arrows, which allows us to describe the slopes of the faces, led to the appearance of a structure made of cubes; the description of self-locked masonry from cubes through a hexagonal section allowed Fig. 12 to generalize the design, build systems from octahedrons and dodecahedrons, and then from other bodies. Reasoning with the movement of the sides of the section makes it possible to construct self-locked structures in a regular way. The presence of language is very important in mathematics, and precise words, despite the seeming “insignificance” of reformulations, lead to new results. The arched effect is quite well known, when in ancient times an arch was built from stones (Fig.13). only the classic convex arch, but also a concave arch!
