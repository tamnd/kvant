Let us now consider a separate rectangle divided into 4 cells. Let the lengths of its sides be x (horizontally) and y (vertically). It is clear that one of the white cells has a horizontal side length at least x/2. For definiteness, let this cell be the upper left one. The horizontal line dividing the rectangle can then be moved down until the ratio of the area of ​​the upper left cell (white) to the lower left (black) becomes equal to 2. In this case, the condition of the problem (that the area of ​​any white cell is not greater than twice the area of ​​any black cell) will still be satisfied, and the total area of ​​the white cells will only increase (or not change). The straight line cannot be moved any further (the conditions of the problem will be violated). Similarly, after this you can move the vertical line to the right (until the area of ​​the upper left cell becomes 2 times larger than the area of ​​the upper right). The sides of the top left square will then be 2x/3 and 2y/3, the area of ​​the white cells will be 4xy/9 + xy/9 = 5xy/9, and the area of ​​the black cells will be xy – 5xy/9 = 4xy/9. We found that the ratio of the area of ​​white cells to the area of ​​black cells is 5/4 and cannot be greater. Let us prove that the second can combine the two largest available piles with each of his next moves. First, the first will definitely make a pile of two nuts, after which the second will make a pile of three nuts, and the situation after the second’s move will be like this: the largest pile has an odd number of nuts, and each of the other piles has one nut. In such a situation, the former has two options. Or he will make a pile of two nuts - and then the second one will add it to the largest pile. Or he will increase the largest pile by one nut - then there will be an even number of nuts in it, and since N is odd, then there will be at least one more pile of one nut left, and the second one will be able to add one nut to the largest pile. As a result, the described situation occurs again. We see that the second one always has a move. The piles will eventually run out and the first one will lose. Let N > 2 be even.
