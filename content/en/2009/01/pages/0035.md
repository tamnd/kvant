It is curious that... ...J. J. Thomson's famous experiments with cathode rays were preceded by almost forty years of work by physicists from different countries to study electric discharge in rarefied gases. It was believed that these rays were either a stream of molecules charged negatively upon collision with the cathode, or a new type of electromagnetic waves, deflected, unlike ordinary light, by a magnetic field. Only Thomson, in a long series of experiments, managed to obtain decisive evidence by 1897 that cathode rays consist of electrons. This is how the first elementary particle was discovered. ...the task of determining the charge and mass of $\alpha -$ and $\beta -$ particles, which arose immediately after the discovery of radioactivity, led over time to the creation of mass spectrographs - instruments that use electric and magnetic fields to measure the masses of the smallest ions. The first of them was built in 1919 by Francis Aston, a student of JJ Thomson, who discovered the existence of isotopes seven years earlier. ...the rotation of charged particles in a magnetic field was discovered back in 1895 by the English physicist Joseph Larmore. Subsequently, the idea of ​​confining particles using this field was very well developed during the creation of accelerators - in them, particles make millions of revolutions in a circular orbit and can live in it for many hours. But the problem of magnetic confinement - isolation - of high-temperature plasma in thermonuclear reactors has not yet been completely solved. ...the use of magnetic fields with certain complex configurations in experiments at accelerators makes it possible to focus particle beams in the same way that optical lenses focus light rays. ...cosmic rays, discovered in 1910, began to be intensively studied, including using a cloud chamber placed in a magnetic field. It was she who made it possible to observe the anomalous traces that led to the discovery in 1932 of the positron - “an electron with a positive charge.” ...unlike the variable fluxes of solar cosmic rays, galactic and metagalactic rays are fluxes of atomic nuclei that vary little over time. Their most important feature is the particle energy, reaching $10^{21}$ eV, which is several orders of magnitude higher than the energies obtained in man-made installations. Thus, the Universe turns out to be like a giant accelerator of atomic nuclei. ...in 1985, a geophysical rocket with a small particle accelerator launched from Kerselen Island in the Indian Ocean. At a certain height, it released a stream of electrons, which subsequently moved along the line of the earth's magnetic field and caused an artificial aurora over the Arkhangelsk region. These experiments were intended to study the mechanism of the occurrence of northern lights, the structure of the Earth's magnetic field, processes in its ionosphere and their influence on the weather. ...the scale of modern research in particle physics can be judged by the parameters of the Large Hadron Collider, which is preparing for launch in Geneva. The perimeter of its circular tunnel, located at a depth of approximately 100 meters underground, is 27 kilometers; superconducting magnets will accelerate protons to an energy of 7 TeV and then collide them with each other at a frequency of 800 million times per second; More than two dozen countries, including Russia, took part in planning and financing the construction of the collider.
