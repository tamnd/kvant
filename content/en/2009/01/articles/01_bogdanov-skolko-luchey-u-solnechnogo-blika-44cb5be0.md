Thus, we come to the conclusion that when the gate voltage is U = Ne C, the states of the quantum dot with N and N + 1 electrons are separated by an energy gap $e^{2} ( 2 C )$, and, therefore, the law of conservation of energy seems to block the transfer of electrons between the source and drain - the quantum dot turns out to be “locked”. On the contrary, at a voltage U = ( N + 1 2 ) e C electrons can be transferred to the quantum dot “for free”, since the states with N and N + 1 electrons correspond to the same energy value - the quantum dot turns out to be “open”. So, we see that a metal drop about ten angstroms in size can work as an effective transistor - either passing current or turning off. At low temperatures, $kT_{=} e^{2} ( 2 C )$, the current flowing through such a “single-electron transistor” is extremely small at all gate voltages, except for special values ​​U ( N ) = ( N + 1 2 ) e C. In English it is called nanoscience (nano-science), in Russian we talk about nanophysics, nanochemistry and other nanodisciplines. All these tasks are still far from being completed. Therefore, many scientific works are devoted to the study of each of these systems, forming the cutting edge of modern science and technology.

How many rays does a sun flare have?

(See page 4 of the cover)

Optical instruments (cinema cameras, telescopes, microscopes, etc.) always distort the objects they depict. For example, in photographs of bright lamps, electric welding flashes, or sun glare, these bright objects are often surrounded by symmetrically diverging rays. If there are several such objects in a photograph, then the number of diverging rays and their orientation are the same. The reason rays appear in photos around bright objects is light diffraction - the ability of electromagnetic waves to bend around the boundaries of obstacles and penetrate into the geometric shadow area. In optical instruments, diffraction usually occurs at the edges of the aperture, which is used to change the illumination and depth of field of images.
