Problem conditions

1. Boat on the river A boat is sailing along the river, It is sailing from afar. Suddenly, a gust of wind blew the cap off the sailor.

A quarter of an hour flew by until they figured it out and with the same speed they chased after the cap.

Where did you manage to catch up with her? Here is a question for an expert, If a river flows four miles in an hour.

2. Match in your hands, break the match in half, break part again. Tell me, why is it more difficult to break it a second time?

The pencil sank three-quarters of the way in. Now tell me, what was his density?

7. Tame hippopotamus It was in Africa, someone told me. Maybe where is Tunisia now, or maybe... where is Lesotho? The greedy leader collected taxes from the people with the help of a sacred tame hippopotamus.

He sailed on a boat with scales and a soldier. The people balanced the hippopotamus with gold. I fed the animal and the scales broke. People laughed at the ruler's greed.

3. Sisyphus' work Sisyphus drags his stone up the mountain from time immemorial, as usual. For him there will never be unemployment.

Then the leader became enraged. He obliged the soldier, so that the warrior could measure the tribute before sunset! Simplicity was enough for a complex task. What would you do in this case?

He slipped a lever under the stone: Part four of it. Won three times in strength. It's like nothing!

8. Treasure in a jug The fisherman cried in the boat that there was still no luck, He cast his confused glance at the water. Suddenly I saw an object on the waves, almost completely submerged in the water.

With what gain will it be, will he defeat the “enemy”, if he now takes hold of the second edge of the lever?

4. Clown on a tightrope A clown walks on a tightrope. He holds half a log in his hands. For an artist, what is more important – the weight of the log or the length?

He threw himself into the river, all wet and chilled. Soon the boat lifted from the abyss with a wax seal on the stopper a three-liter jug, ancient, made of clay.

5. Ball on a hill We rolled the ball up a hill. We still don’t understand to this day: Where did he move longer - Up the mountain or down the mountain again?

There is a handle on the side, a lid with a hole, a spout with a sieve inside. Look at all these “tricks” according to science!

Problem solutions

1. In the reference frame associated with water, the speed of the boat is constant in magnitude, so it will sail to the cap in a quarter of an hour. This means that the cap’s time in the water is half an hour. Let us now move to the reference frame associated with the shore. Since time flows the same in both systems, the capless cape will float two miles in half an hour. 2. When breaking repeatedly, the lever becomes shorter, therefore more force is required. 3. In the first case, the gain in strength was not four, but only three times. This means that the lever itself is significant. Having written down the equilibrium condition of the lever, we find that the mass of the lever is six times less than the mass of the stone. Then in the second case the gain in strength will be 1.2 times. 4. A tightrope walker needs a pole to maintain balance. The fact that the pole should be heavy is obvious, and no one argues with that. If you lay the pole not across the rope, but along it, then the balancing property of the pole disappears. This means that its geometric dimensions are also important. It turns out that to maintain stability, the length of the pole plays a greater role than its weight. 5. When a ball goes up a hill, its speed is reduced by two forces: gravity and friction. When the ball goes down the hill, its movement is impeded only by friction. This means that the ball will take longer to roll down the hill. 6. It is clear that the density of a pencil is three quarters of the density of water, i.e. $750 кг/м^{3}. 7.$ You need to put the hippopotamus in the boat and note the depth of its immersion. Then you need to remove the hippopotamus and load the boat with gold until the depth of the dive becomes the same. In this case, the mass of the load will be equal to the mass of the hippopotamus. 8. In the first case, the buoyant force balances the gravity of the jug and the gravity of the gold. In the second case, the buoyant force, reduced by one third, balances only the gravity of the jug. This means that the mass of gold is 1 kg. 9. Water, wetting the plywood, will flow under the disk, a buoyant force will appear, and the plywood disk will float up. Mercury does not wet glass, so it will not leak under the glass disk, Archimedean force will not appear, and the glass disk will remain at the bottom. 10. If the temperature outside is higher than the temperature of the human body, then it makes sense to wear clothes that do not allow heat to pass through. Then under clothes the temperature will not be 45 degrees, but only 36.6. 11. When an earthquake occurs in the ocean at great depth, a large mass of water begins to move at low speed - like in a wide pipe. Near the shore there is shallow water, the “pipe” narrows, but the volume of water flowing through the cross section must remain unchanged, so the speed of water movement increases, with all the ensuing consequences. 12. Let's consider all the questions in order. In order for the tea to brew, the teapot must remain at a high temperature for a sufficiently long time - that is why the teapot is made of a substance with poor thermal conductivity (porcelain). The smaller the surface area of ​​a body (all other things being equal), the slower it cools, and the smallest surface area is that of a ball. The white color of the inner surface of the kettle prevents heat loss due to radiation. The handle is needed for easy carrying of the hot kettle. If the lid of the kettle fits tightly to the body, then when tea is poured out, a volume of air with reduced pressure is formed inside the kettle and the tea leaves stop flowing out - to avoid this, a hole is made in the lid. And finally, a sieve in the spout prevents tea leaves from getting into the cup.
