PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent no later than May 1, 2009 to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 1–2009” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M2116” or “F2123”. In the “From” column, please write your first and last name legibly. In the letter, enclose an envelope with your address written on it and the required set of stamps (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking the decisions). Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics can also be sent to the email addresses math@kvant.info and phys@kvant.info, respectively. Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you study. Problems M2118 and M2121 were proposed at the IV All-Russian Olympiad in Geometry named after I.F. Sharygin, problem M2119, M2120 and M2123 – at the XXX Tournament of Cities.

Problem M2116–M2123, F2123–F2129

M2116. A complete set of dominoes is laid out on the table in a closed chain, and for all pairs of neighboring dominoes the modulus of the difference in points on the squares with which they touch is calculated. Let us denote by S the sum of all such modules of differences. It is clear that the smallest value of S is 0 (this occurs when the closed chain is laid out according to domino rules). What is the greatest value that the sum S can take? A. Gribalko

M2117. Is there an arithmetic progression of 2008 different natural numbers whose product is equal to the exact 2009th power of the natural number? G. Galperin

M2118. Prove that in triangle ABC with angle A equal to $120^\circ,$, the distance from the center of the circumcircle to the point of intersection of the altitudes is AB + AC. V.Protasov

M2119. In the infinite sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3},^{K}$, the number $a_{1}$ is equal to 1, and each subsequent number $a_{n}$ is constructed from the previous $a_{n} -_{1}$ according to the rule: if the largest odd divisor of the number n has a remainder of 1 when divided by 4, then $a_{n} = a_{n} -_{1} + 1$, if the remainder is 3, then $a_{n} = a_{n} -_{1} - 1$. Prove that all numbers in this sequence are positive, and each natural number appears in it an infinite number of times. A. Zaslavsky

M2120. A polynomial P(x) with real coefficients is such that the equation P(m) + P(n) = 0 has

⟦column⟧

there are infinitely many solutions in integers m and n. Prove that the graph of the function y = P(x) has a center of symmetry. A. Shapovalov

M2121. The opposite sides of a convex hexagon ABCDEF are parallel. Let's call it “heights” - vectors with ends on straight lines containing opposite sides, perpendicular to them and directed from AB to DE, from EF to BC and from CD to AF. Prove that a circle can be drawn around this hexagon if and only if the sum of its “heights” is equal to the zero vector. A. Zaslavsky

M2122. a) Prove that any natural number n greater than 17 can be decomposed into the sum of three natural pairwise coprime terms, each of which is greater than 1. b*) Find out whether there is a finite or infinite set of natural numbers that cannot be represented as the sum of three coprime natural terms in a set, any two of which are not coprime. V. Letsko

M2123. The test consists of 30 questions, each with 2 possible answers (one is correct, the other is incorrect). In one attempt, Vitya answers all the questions, after which he is told how many questions he answered correctly. Will Vitya be able to act in such a way as to be guaranteed to find out all the correct answers no later than after the 24th attempt (and answer all questions correctly on the 25th attempt)? V. Kleptsyn
