- And Yu.I. Manin? – Manin now and for the last 20 years has been primarily engaged in theoretical physics. This fact alone suggests that he ceased to be a “pure” mathematician. But the fact that the best part of his work, both in “pure” mathematics and in theoretical physics, will probably remain, and even some will have very specific applications, is quite expected. As well as the fact that something else, on the contrary, will die away, as temporary and unnecessary.

– Do you like physics? – All my life I really loved physics, but I was unlucky with my teachers. I never had good physics teachers. Never: neither at school, nor at New York University, nor when I was studying mechanics and mathematics. The lectures that the professors from the physics department gave me were simply very bad. And I studied theoretical physics from mathematicians. In addition, the theory of knots, which I have been studying for many years, turned out to be in demand precisely by physicists.

– V.I. Arnold says that mathematics is a part of physics. Do you agree with this? – Arnold says not only that mathematics is a part of physics, he adds that this is the part of physics where experiments are very cheap. This is a very important addition. Arnold himself, especially in recent years, is very fond of demonstrating that mathematics is an experimental science, and a mathematician is a person who experiments. He says to himself: I experiment with my hands, I count, I conduct experiments, and then I draw conclusions from these experiments. But you can conduct experiments on a computer. The work of a mathematician is precisely related to conducting such inexpensive experiments. However, Arnold gives his words a deeper, I would say, philosophical meaning. And in this regard, he positions himself as a student of Andrei Nikolaevich Kolmogorov. Arnold believes in the reality of what mathematicians study. He believes that mathematics is, to some extent, a science about the world around us. In 1990, I interviewed Vladimir Igorevich (the interview was published in «Kvant» No. 7, 1990), and asked him the following question: “When you prove a theorem, do you create it or do you discover it?” He responded with the following quote from A.K. Tolstoy:

In vain, artist, do you imagine that you are the creator of your creations! They forever hovered over the earth, invisible to the eye... There are many invisible forms and inaudible sounds in space, There are many wonderful combinations of words and light in it.

– Can you explain to schoolchildren what practical applications modern mathematics has – not the mathematics of Newton and Euler, but that which was created in recent decades? Are there any such examples? – There are many such examples, it is not difficult to list some of them, but explaining to schoolchildren the essence of the matter clearly is not at all easy. I will name, without going into detail, three examples, in the hope that «Kvant» will find authors who will be able to clearly explain the basics of the corresponding mathematical

theories and clearly describe how these theories work in practice. The first example is algebraic geometry over a finite field and cryptography. The mathematical theory can be described as the geometry of elliptic curves in a multidimensional space, but considered not over the field of real or complex numbers, but over finite fields. This theory makes it possible to build what in English is called “one-way functions” - the basis of the so-called “open source encryption”. This encryption method is used in ordinary bank cards. A swindler, even if he knows how to encrypt your card, will not be able to decipher the information necessary to withdraw money. The second example is operator methods (and the associated theory of Lagrangian manifolds and the Maslov index) and tsunami and tornado predictions. Here we are talking about the works of Viktor Pavlovich Maslov and his school, originally related to the theoretical study of partial differential equations. These works made it possible to construct fairly accurate models of the propagation of phenomena such as cyclones, tornadoes and tsunamis. Maslov's student Sergei Dobrokhotov managed to find, using the theory developed in the last decade, a very effective and realistic model of a tornado. It is interesting that the laboratory of the Institute of Problems of Mechanics, where Dobrokhotov works, is called the Laboratory of Mechanics of Natural Disasters. I would not undertake to explain to schoolchildren, even very advanced ones, either the theory or the mathematical description of the tornado model. However, we can tell you what the Maslov index is, since Arnold came up with an equivalent, fairly clear geometric definition for this invariant. The third example is Chomsky's context-free grammars and programming languages. American mathematical linguist Nahum Chomsky came up with a class of formal languages ​​that he called context-free. These languages ​​are formal mathematical objects based on the formal grammars now named after Chomsky. It was their use that brought about a revolution in programming - the transition from programming in codes to high-level languages. In connection with this example, it is worth noting that even earlier, when the first digital electronic machines were just being created, the starting point for their architecture was the idea of ​​a universal Turing machine, invented by the English mathematician Alan Turing (during the Second World War, he worked, it seems, in the spy agency MI-5).

– What is the profession of mathematician? – Everyone knows that there are theoretical physicists. Everyone knows that there are biologists. But, oddly enough, most people, even many school mathematics teachers, do not know that the profession of “research mathematician” exists. Many people think that mathematics is a large body of knowledge that developed especially rapidly in the seventeenth, eighteenth, and nineteenth centuries, and now represents an established body of knowledge, very useful for practice. However, this is a completely wrong point of view, because mathematics, like theoretical physics, continues to develop intensively. And it is developed by people who are called research mathematicians. How is their life? Some research mathematicians work at universities, and then they teach. They are paid money specifically for teaching. But they view this as by no means the main part of their work, and they consider their main work to be proving new theorems, inventing new theories, solving old but still unsolved problems. In addition, there are now several institutes in the world where mathematicians are paid money simply to develop mathematics.
