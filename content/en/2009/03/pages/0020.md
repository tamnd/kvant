Limitations on heat dissipation limits

In living organisms living on Earth, the level of intensity of circulating heat flows is set by the Sun. A heat flux of about 1 kW $м^{2}$ reaches the Earth's surface from the Sun, of which about 300 W $м^{2}$ is absorbed. In the case of tall trees, this heat flow triggers the main mechanism of water rising up the tree trunk (with the evaporation of water through the stomata). It turns out that, although the area occupied by the stomata is only about 1% of the leaf area, evaporation through them occurs at approximately the same rate as from the open surface. (The nature of this paradox is described in the article by A. Mineev “Leaves are smiling” in «Kvant» No. 4, 2006 - Ed.) The magnitude of the specific heat flux removed through the skin of animals during evaporation is generally of the same order. So, for a person in a calm state it is approximately 50 W $м^{2}$. With maximum physical exertion, for example, a record-breaking sprinter, the ratio of the thermal power released during running to the surface area of ​​the runner's body can reach 2 kW $м^{2}$. OUR INTERVIEWS

Interview with A.B. Sosinsky

Alexey Bronislavovich Sosinsky is a famous mathematician, author of scientific and popular science books and articles, including the monograph (joint with V.V. Prasolov) “Knots, links, braids and three-dimensional manifolds”, the popular science book (translated into 6 languages) “Knots. Chronology of a mathematical theory." For many years, Alexey Bronislavovich was the editor of the mathematics department «Kvant». He is one of the regular contributors to our magazine, an outstanding lecturer and teacher. On the website mathnet.ru you can watch his film-lecture “Gödel’s Theorem”. We also advise you to get acquainted with the report “The Mathematics of Juggling”, read by Jean-Christophe Novelli and Florent Iver, in which A.B. Sosinsky acted as a translator (see website mccme.ru). This is no coincidence: Alexey Bronislavovich has spoken French and English since childhood. Many Russian mathematicians learned to write scientific articles in English from A.B. Sosinsky’s book “How to Write a Mathematical Article in English.” Alexey Bronislavovich is a man of interesting destiny, and we are pleased to present to our readers his story about himself, about mathematicians and mathematics.

– Maybe you can tell us first about your family, who your parents are...

– Just briefly about my pedigree. My father was one of the landless nobles (his father, my grandfather, was a very famous railway engineer), fought against the Bolsheviks, emigrated after the end of the civil war and settled in Paris for many years. There he met my mother, Ariadna Chernova, the daughter of Viktor Mikhailovich Chernov, the leader and chief theoretician of the Socialist Revolutionary Party, chairman of the Constituent Assembly before it was dispersed by the Bolsheviks. I think there is no point in telling readers «Kvant» about the life of my parents and my childhood. Let me start right away from the moment when I ended up at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University. I was admitted to the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics at the age of nineteen straight into the second year. Before that, I lived and studied abroad. I was born in France, and received my first university degree in the States in 1957 - a bachelor's degree from New York University. At the same time, I was offered to enter graduate school at the Courant Institute of Mathematics, but I refused, realizing that at that moment, i.e. in the mid-50s, Moscow (and in particular Moscow State University) was one of the leading - if not the strongest - mathematical centers in the world.
