a red cockroach (Prussian) was used. Cockroach legs are a universal biological object of study for creating a walking machine. The running of a cockroach can be monitored using a movie camera. To do this, the cockroach is placed in a glass test tube and, having secured it, the running cockroach is filmed with a movie camera. The main type of gait of a cockroach is a 3–3 gait (three-strikes). He walks in such a way that he always relies on three legs, forming a support triangle, inside which the center of gravity of his body is located. This significantly alleviates the problem of stability, since the apparatus's three-legged support turns out to be stable. Each robot limb has three degrees of freedom and is driven by three geared motors. Position sensors are installed on the limbs, measuring the angles of rotation of the leg links relative to each other. The walking vehicle motor control system is built on a hierarchical principle. After the appearance of the “six-legged” machine at the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University, which received the name “MaSha” (WALKING MACHINE), a kind of competition began between the USA and the USSR, which was normal for that time. In response to MaSha, the Americans proposed their own version of a six-legged robot. Later, other six-legged robots began to appear, for example those built in Germany. "MaSha" contained many pioneering scientific solutions that were ahead of their time, and gave rise to the creation of smart robots with a hierarchical control system. She can act independently, choosing her own path over rough terrain. It turned out that without information about the forces and moments of force arising between the legs and the underlying surface, it is almost impossible to organize “smooth” walking control. For this purpose, a system of so-called tactile sensors was developed, which capture information about the forces that arise and send it to the robot’s control system. Only thanks to this it is possible to organize a smooth, and not jerky, movement of the robot. The Americans have officially announced that by 2050 there will be no people in their active forces. In 2005, the four-legged robot “BigDog” (Fig.3) was created in the USA. This robot can walk, run and jump, overcome rough terrain; it moves perfectly through snow, stones, and mud. Due to a well-organized control system, it is very stable: if you push it, then, like a person or an animal, it will move its legs and will not fall.
