– Many people are leaving now, but in the 50s, many people in Russia dreamed of leaving for the prosperous West. And you are almost the only person I know who came. Do you regret it? Everyone was moving in one direction (at least they dreamed), and you were moving in the other? “I understood perfectly well that I was going in the wrong direction.” But I don’t regret coming at all, although subsequent events did not unfold the way I naively thought then. I then assumed that Khrushchev would soon be removed and that in his place there would be a liberal, smart ruler, that “socialism with a human face” would appear in Russia, but everything turned out exactly the opposite. But despite this, I never regretted my arrival: at that time, the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics was an absolutely unique oasis, where there were people of the highest professional level, as well as the highest level of culture.

– Do you remember your school? Did your teachers influence your choice of profession? – I studied in a French high school, my first mathematics teachers were rather weak, but as soon as we had “algebra with letters” instead of arithmetic and geometry appeared, I became terribly carried away. I was 13 years old then, and I believed that I understood much more than my teachers. Therefore, it was the mathematics itself that influenced, not the teacher. However, already in the final classes of the French Lyceum, I had an excellent mathematics teacher, and before that, in another Lyceum, for six months there was a unique, original and strong teacher of this subject. Of course, they greatly influenced my final choice. Because the passion that I had at the age of 13 could have disappeared if not for these wonderful teachers.

– So, at the age of 12 you were still, what is called, an ordinary child, not a child prodigy? They didn’t tell you when you were six years old that you had outstanding mathematical abilities? – In fact, I was indeed somewhat of a child prodigy, but my real interest in mathematics arose at the age of 13. I was a year younger than my classmates, having skipped one grade in high school.

– How old were you when you started school? - Apparently, from six; but during the war years I was in occupied French territory, where schools sometimes functioned and sometimes did not. At times I went to elementary preparatory classes.

– So there was no home schooling? – No, there was home schooling, of course. Moreover, it is very serious. But my parents did not teach me the exact sciences or arithmetic, and my education was humanitarian. Somewhere from the age of four I already knew how to write in Russian, and I learned to write in French a little later, already at school. My parents, very educated people, managed to interest me in Russian literature and poetry.

- How did they achieve this? How to make a child smart? – Well, I think that genetics influences this more than training. When I was very little, we lived as a very big friendly family. It was a kind of matriarchy - my grandmother, her three daughters, their husbands, if they were present, because during the war not all of them were with us, and many

Sometimes my mother read especially for me, and it was always interesting to me. And interest in literature, culture, and painting was absorbed on its own, through the air.

– Did your family speak two languages? – No, the family only spoke Russian. And I mastered the French language as soon as I was able to go out into the yard; I don’t remember myself not knowing French. At about two or three years old I already spoke two languages.

– And English only later? – English later. I ended up in the States at the age of ten and then learned English very quickly. My parents immediately sent me to an American school, I studied there for two years, but did not gain any new knowledge. It is known that the American school - both then and now - is at a catastrophically low level. But I learned the language and adapted to American life. After that, my parents transferred me to the French Lyceum in New York.

– Were there math Olympiads when you were studying? – The Olympics still don’t exist in France. In America, when I was a student, the Putnam contest was already held, and I participated in it. As for the French tradition, there is the so-called Concours General (literally - general competition), which was established by Napoleon. This competition consists of solving difficult problems in advanced sections of the school curriculum, and not of “olympiad” intelligence problems; however, I did not participate in it.

– When did you realize that you would be a scientist? – Around 14 years old.

- Already? And so confident? - Yes, confidently. I can tell you the following story. When I was 14 years old, I was thinking about geometry and came to the conclusion that Euclidean geometry is inconsistent. And that the correct geometry is designed in such a way that if the lines are continued, they will eventually close.

– So you came up with projective geometry? – More like spherical. I did not very clearly understand that this geometry is non-orientable. And I wrote my first mathematical work in calligraphic handwriting, where I cited a certain sophism that “proved” the inconsistency of Euclidean geometry. And there he explained a little about how “real” geometry works. Well, for example, I understood that the sum of the angles is greater than 180 degrees. Overall it was a very stupid piece of writing.
