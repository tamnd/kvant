PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent no later than September 1, 2009 to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 3–2009” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M2131” or “F2138”. In the “From” column, please write your first and last name legibly. In the letter, enclose an envelope with your address written on it and the required set of stamps (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking the decisions). Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics can also be sent to the email addresses math@kvant.info and phys@kvant.info, respectively. Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you study. Problem M2131–M2134, M2136 and M2138 were offered in the spring round of the XXX Tournament of Cities. Problem F2138–F2141 were offered at the final stage of the XLIII All-Russian Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics.

Problem M2131–M2138, F2138–F2144

M2131. Let a b denote the number $a^{b}$. In the expression $7^{Щ} 7^{Щ} 7^{Щ} 7^{Щ} 7^{Щ} 7^{Щ} 7$ you need to place brackets to determine the order of actions (there will be 5 pairs of brackets in total). Is it possible to arrange these brackets in two different ways to get the same number? A.Tolpygo

M2132. Several points are given on a plane, no three of which lie on the same straight line. Some points are connected by segments. It is known that any straight line that does not pass through these points intersects an even number of segments. Prove that each point has an even number of segments. I. Bogdanov, G. Galperin

M2133. The castle is surrounded by a circular wall with nine towers manned by knights. At the end of each hour, they all move to neighboring towers, with each knight moving all the time either clockwise or counterclockwise. During the night, each knight manages to be on duty on each tower. It is known that there was an hour when at least two knights were on duty on each tower, and there was an hour when exactly one knight was on duty on exactly five towers. Prove that there was an hour when there were no knights on one of the towers at all. M. Murashkin

M2135. A natural number is called good if it can be converted into a perfect square by adding to its decimal notation on the left a number ending in exactly 2009 zeros. For what n is there an n-digit good number that is not a perfect square? N. Agakhanov

M2136. Let $C_{n}^{k}$ denote the number of ways to select k items from n different items (ways that differ only in the order of selection of items are considered the same). Prove that if the natural numbers k and l are less than n, then the numbers $C_{n}^{k}$ and $C_{n}^{l}$ have a common divisor greater than 1. Folklore

M2137. Given an acute-angled isosceles triangle ABC, in which point H is the point of intersection of the altitudes, points I and O are the centers of the inscribed and circumscribed circles. It is known that points A, O, I, H lie on the same circle $\omega$. Prove that the circle $\omega$ passes through one of the vertices B and C. P. Kozhevnikov
