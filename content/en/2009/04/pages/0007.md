RULER

Potato virus can cause a lot of trouble for tobacco, tomatoes and other plants. Plants, like us, are susceptible to viral diseases. Fortunately for us, plant viruses do not pose any danger to humans. At least, it can be stated that no one has ever demonstrated the opposite. Therefore, for example, we can safely eat potatoes infected with the X potato virus. The letter X here indicates the type of virus. There is also potato virus A and potato virus B. Potato virus X, like the tobacco mosaic virus, has a thread-like shape, only it is narrower and longer: 14 nm in diameter with a length of 1 micron. By the way, a proposal was made to use the tobacco mosaic virus as a standard of 18 nanometers, and the X potato virus as a standard of 14 nanometers.

Modern navigation systems

Modern GPS and GLONASS navigation systems operate with precision in measuring frequency and, accordingly, time interval with a relative error of $1 \cdot 10^{-} 13$. GPS is an abbreviation of the English words Global Positioning System, i.e. global positioning system. The full name of this satellite navigation system is NAVSTAR GPS (NAVigation Satellites providing Time And Range - navigation satellites that provide time and distance measurements; global positioning system). This system was created by the US Department of Defense to accurately determine the coordinates and speed of objects. The Russian satellite navigation system is the Global Navigation Satellite System, or GLONASS for short. The rule of the inverse piezoelectric effect is very simple - the change in the size of the piezoelectric plate $\Delta D$ is directly proportional to the applied voltage $\Delta U : \Delta D = d_{33} \Delta U$, where the proportionality coefficient $d_{33}$ is the piezoelectric module. The use of two numbers in the module index shows that in the general case we can apply stress in one of three directions (along the coordinate axes X, Y or Z), and the change in dimensions can be observed in any other direction. In our case, the direction of the electric field coincides with the direction of movement, so two identical numbers 3 are used. It is easy to calculate that the piezoelectric module has 9 components in total: $d_{11}, d_{12}, d_{13}$, ... $, d_{33}$. (Such physical quantities are called second-order tensors.) Let's take ceramics with a piezoelectric module $d_{33} = = 200 \cdot 10^{-} 12$ Cl N $= 200 \cdot 10^{-} 12$ m V. If we apply a voltage of 5 V to the electrodes, then the change in the dimensions of the plate will be 1 nm. Note that the change in the size of the piezo plate does not depend on the thickness of the plate itself. And this is very good, because when creating a length standard, we do not have to think about the length of the standard itself. Thus, for our nanometer we can already formulate the main thesis: a nanometer is the distance by which the size of a plate made of piezoceramics with a piezoelectric module $d_{33} = 200 \cdot 10^{-} 12$ Cl N $= 200 \cdot 10^{-} 12$ m V changes when a voltage of 5 V is applied to its electrodes. In our standard, we used just such a piezoelectric plate and battery. If the battery has a voltage greater than 5 V, then we will need two electrical resistances, with the help of which we will make a voltage divider with an output of 5 V. And if we find piezoceramics with a different value of the piezoelectric module, then by selecting the required voltage on the divider, we will be able to fulfill the condition $\Delta D = 1$ nm for this case as well.

There are little tricks in every business. The manufacture of our nanometer has its own “tricks”. Here's the first one. If we use a piezoelectric plate made of piezoceramics with a piezoelectric module $d_{33} = 200 \cdot 10^{-} 12$ Cl N, then after each application of a voltage of 5 V, the size of the plate will change by 1 nm. Between voltage applications, it is very important not to forget to short-circuit the plate plates with a conductor. Piezoceramics is a very good insulator: once applied to the plates, the voltage will remain on them for a long time. This can last for hours or days, it all depends on the humidity of the air and the cleanliness of the surface of the record. By shorting the electrodes of the plate with a conductor, we allow the negative charge to flow down the conductor to the opposite plate so that the voltage on the plate becomes zero and the plate returns to its original dimensions. And one more important “trifle”. Electrical voltage can be applied so that the piezo plate becomes thicker, or so that the plate becomes thinner. In other words, the battery can be connected with a plus to the upper electrode, or it can be connected with a plus to the bottom electrode. In both cases, the plate will change its size by 1 nm. Is there a difference in how the voltage is applied? To answer this question, we need to tell you how piezoceramics are made.
