Mechanics and Arctic Research

Our country has developed (which does not exist anywhere else in the world) deep-sea, diving depths up to 6 kilometers, self-propelled or externally controlled self-propelled vehicles - the inhabited “Mir” and the uninhabited “Harpsichord”. They are fundamentally different from the so-called bathyscaphes available in a number of countries, which are simply lowered on a rope, at the bottom they dump ballast and float up. But 6 kilometers of depth means a pressure of 600 atmospheres, which the rather complex structure of the apparatus must withstand. And here we need the mechanics of materials, the mechanics of their behavior in such difficult conditions. The news, which was covered very widely in the newspapers, is the recent expedition to the North Pole on the nuclear icebreakers Rossiya and Akademik Fedorov and the planting of the Russian flag on the bottom of the Arctic Ocean. Nuclear icebreakers have nuclear reactors, which are almost purely mechanical devices. The ability to create durable ships is also mechanics. But even earlier, submarines visited the North Pole and surfaced exactly at the polar point. Who brought them there? Radio? It doesn't work underwater. Echolocation? Although it works, it does so at very short distances. And the boats were brought in by inertial gyroscopic navigation systems of amazing accuracy - another miracle, an outstanding achievement of mechanics both in the past and very recently. Navigation is the science and art of being on a moving vehicle (car, sea or airborne).

Then the radio came to the rescue. A new era began with the advent of inertial navigation systems, which do not use any external signals or observations. The very first such systems, which still remain traditional, are gyroscopic. They are based on a purely mechanical device – a gyroscope. (The simplest idea of ​​a gyroscope is given by a rapidly rotating top, which, despite the force of gravity, does not fall, and at the same time its axis of rotation precesses, i.e., slowly describes a cone.) Gyroscopic navigation systems have undergone tremendous evolution over the past decades and especially in our days. Our country has created gyroscopic navigation systems of amazing accuracy. There is a huge demand for these devices all over the world; international conferences dedicated to them are held every two years. Although these devices are electronic, the principle of their operation and the principle of use are purely mechanical. By the way, acoustic location and communication are also a matter for mechanics. After all, acoustics is a science that spun off from mechanics, just like dynamic meteorology (and a significant part of oceanology) that was separated long ago. Together, these two sciences study movements in the Earth’s air envelope – the atmosphere and movements in the world’s oceans, as well as their interaction. Among a number of the latest achievements of these sciences, one can note the theory of “rings” of the Gulf Stream and the theory of ice hummocking in the Arctic. The latter is akin to the theory of collision of continental plates, but, of course, refers to phenomena on a much smaller scale in space and time and to a simpler environment.

Electricity production at nuclear power plants

A good example of the use of mechanics in technology is nuclear energy, one of the technologies that has rapidly developed in recent decades. Nowadays, nuclear reactors are used not only in energy, but also for transport. The schematic diagram of a nuclear power plant - nuclear power plant - is shown in figure 5. In addition to the nuclear reactor, it includes a double-circuit coolant system (the first circuit is radioactive, it is located inside the reactor vessel, and the second, with low radioactivity, is located outside the reactor vessel), steam turbines that rotate electric generators, the electric generators themselves, electricity transmission systems, a cooling system, and pumps. The heart of a nuclear power plant is the nuclear reactor - the joint brainchild of physicists and mechanics. Fuel (uranium) is loaded into the reactor in the form of fuel elements, which are tubes filled with this fuel. The atomic reaction is reduced or enhanced by moving these fuel elements up or down, i.e. pushing them out of the reactor or pushing them into it.
