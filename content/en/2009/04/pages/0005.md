RULER

Serious efforts were made to ensure that kilograms, grams, meters, centimeters, millimeters and inches were the same everywhere. From 1892 until the end of his life, D.I. Mendeleev headed the first scientific metrological institution in Russia - the Main Chamber of Weights and Measures. Its task was to ensure the unity of measures and the creation of reliable measurement methods and their standards, as well as ensuring the uniformity and accuracy of the measures and measuring instruments used. Now it is the All-Russian Research Institute of Metrology, named after D.I. Mendeleev. The chamber, and now the institute, are located in St. Petersburg. Thanks to the efforts of D.I. Mendeleev, in 1900 in Moscow, at the Moscow District Assay Office, the Verification Tent for Trade Measures and Weights No. 3 was created. Now it is the All-Russian Research Institute of Metrological Service (a federal enterprise). Its emblem is the letter M (the first letter in the word METROLOGY), encircling the globe and at the same time leaning on it. Nice symbolism to show how important metrology is to all of us.

Chamber of Weights and Measures - Research Institute of Metrology named after. D.I. Mendeleev's meter is too large a unit of measurement of length. A suitable quantity here might be a nanometer. From the point of view of mathematics, everything is obvious: 1 nm $= 10^{-} 9$ m, and, it would seem, nothing is simpler. However, in practice, complex issues arise. And the first of them is how to make a ruler with distances between marks one nanometer long. Indeed, the meter is a universal primary unit for measuring lengths and has its own standard. The nanometer appears as a unit of length derived from the meter. How to make a secondary standard - for a nanometer? This issue belongs to the field of nanometrology and is of fundamental importance for nanotechnology. “If you want to create, learn to measure,” these are the words of the famous Russian scientist Dmitry Ivanovich Mendeleev, who made a huge contribution to the creation of the metrological service in Russia. His words are fully true for nanotechnology. So, the next story will be about how to make a ruler for measuring lengths in the nanorange.

How to divide a meter by a billion nanometers?

Nanotechnology is the art of creating new products whose dimensions are in the nanometer range. If you are seriously engaged in the production of such products, then you need to become very good at measuring these same nanometers. To do this, you need not only a convenient, but also an unambiguous standard of length. It seems that everything is simple: to get a nanometer, you need to cut one standard meter into one billion equal parts. If then a billion people receive one nanometer each, disperse to different parts of the world, and then, after a long time, gather again in one place, apply all their nanometers to each other and make sure that their nanometers are the same length, that would be great. And this “great” will mean many things. Firstly, that at first the meter was cut into exactly equal parts. Secondly, that during the travels the nanometer did not wear out, did not fray or stretch. Third, that such nanometers can be used as a measure of length and that two people measuring the same object with “different” nanometers will get the same results.
