Why are carbon nanotubes stronger than steel?

It is obvious that if one single-walled nanotube is placed into another of a slightly larger diameter, then the strength of such a double-walled nanotube will be almost twice as strong as that of a single-walled one. Therefore, the strength of multi-walled nanotubes is several times higher. Nanotube structures have a bright future. They are already used in practice, for example, to strengthen tennis rackets. The plan is to make cables for space elevators from nanotubes to supply space stations in geostationary orbits with everything they need.

(For the beginning, see p. 7)

An ideal nanotube has no defects and is a single molecule in which the carbon atoms are linked together by fairly strong covalent bonds. Steel is a polycrystalline containing many internal defects, such as boundaries and microcracks. When a steel sample is stretched, one of these defects turns into a crack, and the sample fails. The presence of many defects in a metal sample explains the plasticity of the metal. At the same time, the nanotube does not have plasticity.
