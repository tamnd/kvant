Fuel elements are far from the most complex part of the station, but even their manufacturing technology is developed by mechanical scientists together with engineers from the manufacturing plant. The entire assembly loaded into the reactor is transported from the plant on several heavy-duty vehicles. This is where the role of physicists ends. The reactor vessel is calculated using mechanical methods, and, by the way, not by the methods of the 19th century, but by continuously improving methods of recent years. All other thousand-ton complex equipment of the nuclear power plant is also calculated, created and installed by engineering and technical personnel based on the latest achievements of mechanics. True, this requires electrical and thermal calculations (including calculation of the movement of liquid and steam in coolant and cooling systems), which belong to the field of physics. But we can say that heat engineering is a small part of mechanics. Mechanics, having absorbed thermal processes, has, generally speaking, long been dealing with thermotechnical problems more complex than those solved by thermal engineering itself. Using this example, I would like to substantiate the following thesis. In order to make the achievements of a number of other sciences, including physics, useful to humans, one cannot do without mechanics. Mechanics are the basis. Thus, physicists discovered and studied the fission reaction of uranium with the release of energy. Based on this discovery, mechanics created structures that provide people with thermal and electrical energy. Other discoveries in physics (lasers or other sources of radiation), in chemistry, in materials science, and even in biology require the use of the latest advances in mechanics in order to be useful to people. For example, everything that was said above about nuclear power plants remains true for all new chemical technologies. Here the heart is a chemical reactor - again a joint brainchild of chemists and mechanics, and taking into account that the desired acceleration of chemical processes requires in many cases the creation of ever faster-flowing chemical reactors.

Conclusion. Sir Isaac Newton and the ever new mechanics

The current stage of development of science and the progress of scientific knowledge in general are largely determined by the penetration into various fields of science of mathematical modeling methods, which are based on the construction and use of certain mathematical models. We must not forget that the method of mathematical modeling originated in Newtonian mechanics (Newton’s famous laws) and was successfully used by Newton himself in solving a number of serious problems. Subsequently, the art of constructing complex mathematical models was created precisely by mechanics and then applied successively by physicists, geophysicists, chemists, biologists, linguists, and, more recently, by sociologists and economists. The mechanical models are extremely clear. It is no coincidence that a number of famous physicists of the past argued that not a single physical phenomenon can be considered fully understood until it is formulated in mechanical terms. A remarkable mechanic and mathematician, academician Alexei Nikolaevich Krylov wrote in the preface to his Russian translation of Newton’s “Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy”: “Newton’s principles ..." constitute the unshakable foundation of mechanics, theoretical astronomy and physics." Lagrange called this work “the greatest of the works of the human mind.” Newtonian mechanics is truly an unsurpassed achievement of physics (natural philosophy), the entire history of human civilization. She is eternal.
