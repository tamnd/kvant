Study of volcanic eruptions using mechanical methods

The study of volcanoes and their activity has always been the work of specialists - volcanologists, geologists and representatives of other sciences. Mechanics did not interfere with this issue for a long time. There have been separate attempts to consider geysers and other similar formations. But mechanical scientists began to study volcanoes themselves only recently, and a breakthrough immediately occurred in this area. Traditional mechanical methods of mathematical modeling were used to study and predict the activity of volcanoes. At the current stage of research, interesting and encouraging results have already been obtained. Models of various types of eruptions have been constructed, and a number of past eruptions have been described using mechanical methods (in good agreement with the available data). Nowadays, thanks to the work of scientists from the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University, it has become possible to retroactively calculate eruptions that took place in their time, about which much was known, but, as it turned out, much is becoming known only now. A volcanic eruption is one of the most destructive types of natural disasters. Suffice it to say that over the past three centuries, more than 220 thousand people have died as a result of volcanic eruptions. To reliably predict the consequences of volcanic eruptions, it is necessary to learn how to correctly interpret various signals received from a volcano - microearthquakes, deformation of buildings, temperature increases in thermal springs and the composition of gases released. Only mathematical models based on the fundamental laws of mechanics can connect these indirect scattered observations. Let us consider, for example, in more detail what happened in 79 AD during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius, when the cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum were destroyed. As is known from multinomial sources, after a short burst, the eruption quickly declined. The residents of Pompeii, although fairly frightened, decided that the volcano had calmed down and were not going to leave their homes. Suddenly the eruption intensified again, and streams of hot stones fell on the city. Almost instantly the city was buried under a multi-meter layer of them. No one could explain this, but the mechanics did.

Currently, mechanical methods are actively used to analyze and predict volcanic eruptions. The Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University has created models of the flow of magma (molten lava) in a volcano channel for various types of eruptions. These models make it possible to relate quantities observed on the surface, such as magma flow and temperature, to processes occurring in the earth's crust. For a number of volcanoes, their history is known, described by mechanical methods, and thus a forecast becomes possible. For example, taking the eruption parameters for Vesuvius approximately the same as they were at the beginning of our era, scientists calculated the lava flows that could occur today. They found, in particular, that huge streams of hot magma could fall on the city of Naples within 15 minutes. The population and local authorities have become thoughtful, and plans are now being developed to evacuate the population in this case.
