Mechanical model of the formation of supercontinents Another, truly stunning, example of the application of mechanics in a huge period of time from the beginning of the emergence of the Earth to the present day is the formation of continents. Continents and their constituent plates are known to move. And so, according to geologists, over the past three and a half billion years, these plates have assembled several times into

On the existing continents there are areas where the rocks are very old - three billion years or more, and in other places so-called juvenile rocks have been discovered, i.e. rocks that arose much (a billion, two billion years) later. But why all this happens is unclear. To explain, a convection theory of crustal dynamics was proposed. The internal structure of the Earth was chosen to be very simple - in the center there is a hot molten core, which is a source of heat, then there is a mantle, which is divided into two layers - upper and lower (this is seismological data). The layers are thick, the top layer is approximately 650 kilometers thick. These layers differ from each other in their internal structure. The boundary between the layers is the surface of the phase transition of the mantle substance with heat absorption, i.e. When passing through this surface, some chemical bonds are transformed into others and this is accompanied by the absorption of heat. So, the following simplified model was built: there is a spherically symmetrical Earth, then it is given a slight disturbance, a deviation from the spherically symmetrical shape,

and track what happens during further evolution. To solve this problem, a mathematical modeling method is used. It turns out that developing (increasing) disturbances lead to global movements of the Earth's mantle. So-called thermal convective flows arise, and an interesting phenomenon is observed in the two-layer mantle. In some periods, quite short compared to the total time of evolution, the upper, colder layer of the mantle almost entirely merges in one giant jet deep into the Earth, enveloping its core. Instead, part of the substance of the lower, hotter layer rises to the surface. Ascent occurs in three to five less powerful jets than the jet going down. The floating material forms new, juvenile rocks. Note that the most unexpected discovery was the so-called “global self-organization” of the flow, which consists in a collective, i.e. one powerful jet, immersing the substance. The consequence of this is the assembly (above the plunging site) of a supercontinent from individual continents, as well as an increased rate of subsidence of matter. The picture described by the theory is not only qualitatively consistent with the geological history of our planet, but also allows us to draw some conclusions (the authors of the work are Moscow mechanical scientists). Since the theory has some freedom (free parameters that can be set), the time scale is adjusted to the scale of actual new formations, which geologists determine from the decay of radioactive elements.
