With some delay he asks: what is a meter? And here the third character, the experienced professor, shows great pedagogical tact. He doesn’t get annoyed or indignant, but simply comes up to Nono and shows him that a meter is approximately the distance from the floor to Nono’s chin. The boy is happy that he himself is more than a meter tall. He understood everything and is ready to run off to play football... Thus begins the Taiwanese cartoon “The Fantastic Journey of Nana and Nono,” which is dedicated to the popular story about nanotechnology. And this beginning of the cartoon is not accidental. Nanotechnology relies on measurements, in which the nanometer plays a key role. It is the nanometer that will be discussed in the article.

Introduction

Everyone knows that the meter is the basic unit of length. It was introduced in France in the 18th century and was defined as one forty-millionth of the length of the Paris median. In 1799, the first meter standard was made from an alloy of platinum and iridium. When metrology did not have the prefix “nano”

Since ancient times, most measures of length were built based on the convenience of their use. For example, the old Russian sazhen, or straight sazhen, was equal to the distance from the end of the fingers of one horizontally extended hand to the end of the fingers of the other outstretched hand. By decree of Nicholas I, in 1835 Russian and English measures of length were brought into conformity. He decided that one fathom should be equal to seven English feet (from the English foot - foot). When converted to meters, it turns out that 1 straight fathom is equal to 2.1336 meters. Oblique fathom is another old Russian unit of length, equal to 2.48 meters. Initially, an oblique fathom was defined as the distance from the toes of the left foot to the tips of the fingers of the diagonally extended right hand. These were easy-to-use measures. However, using them, we obtained... different lengths. The need and importance of accurate and accurate measurements was well understood by Dmitry Ivanovich Mendeleev, who was a pioneer not only in the creation of the periodic table of elements. It was obvious to him that in a society with growing industrial production, the issue of measurements and metrology takes on key importance.
