Problem solutions

Solution to Problem 1. It follows from the condition that when rotated through an angle $120^\circ$ relative to the point M, vertex A goes to B, and C to D. This means that with this rotation, the directed segment AC goes into the directed segment BD, therefore their lengths are equal, and the angle between them is $120 ^\circ$. This means that the angle between the directed segments AC and DB is equal to $60 ^\circ$. Let us consider the proper motion that transfers the directed segment AC to DB. According to Chall's theorem, it is a rotation, and the magnitude of the rotation angle must be equal to the angle between these directed segments, i.e. $60 ^\circ$. The center of this rotation will be the desired point N. Solution of Problem 2. Consider the movement that transforms the triangle $A_{1} B_{1} C_{1}$ into $A_{2} B_{2} C_{2}$. Since these triangles are differently oriented, the motion in question is improper, and therefore is a sliding symmetry. It is obvious that the midpoint of the segment connecting any point with its image under sliding symmetry lies on the axis of this symmetry. Solution to Problem 3. The motion described in the problem is a composition of three axial symmetries, and therefore improper motion of the plane. According to Chall's theorem, this is sliding symmetry. Obviously, with sliding symmetry, the points that move the minimum distance are exactly the points of the axis. Indeed, all of them are shifted by the same vector, parallel to the axis of symmetry, while other points are also shifted in a direction perpendicular to the axis. It remains to find the axis of this symmetry. To do this, just find two points on it. Let us use a fact that has already helped us in solving the previous problem: the middle of the segment connecting a point with its image under sliding symmetry lies on the axis of this symmetry. Therefore, to find the axis, it is enough to find the midpoints of two segments connecting the points with their images. The search can be greatly simplified if you successfully select two points, the image of which is easy to find for the composition under consideration. Let's choose vertex A of the triangle as the first point. With the first two symmetries it will remain in place, and with the third it will be reflected relative to side BC. The required midpoint will be the base of the height dropped from vertex A. As the second point, take a point symmetrical to point C relative to side AB. With the first symmetry it will turn into C, and with the next two it will remain in place. This means that the second required point is the base of the height dropped from point C. Answer: the required set of points will be a straight line passing through the bases of the heights dropped from vertices A and C of triangle ABC. The case when these bases of the altitudes coincide (and this happens if triangle ABC is right-angled with right angle B) should be analyzed separately. We leave this as an exercise for the readers.

Archaeologists found an ancient scroll in which it was written: “Stand near the birch tree, and walk from it, without turning, to the well, and at the well, turn at a right angle to the left and walk the same distance. Now stand again by the birch tree, and walk from it, without turning, to the oak tree, turn at a right angle to the right and walk the same distance. Drive the second peg into the ground. There is a treasure buried in the middle between the pegs.” It turned out that the well and the oak were still there, but the birch was no longer there. Will archaeologists be able to find the treasure? 15. On the sides of an arbitrary quadrilateral, squares are built to the outside. Prove that a quadrilateral with vertices at the centers of these squares has perpendicular and equal diagonals.
