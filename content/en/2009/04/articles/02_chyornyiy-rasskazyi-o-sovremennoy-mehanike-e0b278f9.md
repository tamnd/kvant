Study of volcanic eruptions using mechanical methods

The study of volcanoes and their activity has always been the work of specialists - volcanologists, geologists and representatives of other sciences. Mechanics did not interfere with this issue for a long time. There have been separate attempts to consider geysers and other similar formations. But mechanical scientists began to study volcanoes themselves only recently, and a breakthrough immediately occurred in this area. Traditional mechanical methods of mathematical modeling were used to study and predict the activity of volcanoes. At the current stage of research, interesting and encouraging results have already been obtained. Models of various types of eruptions have been constructed, and a number of past eruptions have been described using mechanical methods (in good agreement with the available data). Nowadays, thanks to the work of scientists from the Institute of Mechanics of Moscow State University, it has become possible to retroactively calculate eruptions that took place in their time, about which much was known, but, as it turned out, much is becoming known only now. A volcanic eruption is one of the most destructive types of natural disasters. Suffice it to say that over the past three centuries, more than 220 thousand people have died as a result of volcanic eruptions. To reliably predict the consequences of volcanic eruptions, it is necessary to learn how to correctly interpret various signals received from a volcano - microearthquakes, deformation of buildings, temperature increases in thermal springs and the composition of gases released. Only mathematical models based on the fundamental laws of mechanics can connect these indirect scattered observations. Let us consider, for example, in more detail what happened in 79 AD during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius, when the cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum were destroyed. As is known from multinomial sources, after a short burst, the eruption quickly declined. The residents of Pompeii, although fairly frightened, decided that the volcano had calmed down and were not going to leave their homes. Suddenly the eruption intensified again, and streams of hot stones fell on the city. Almost instantly the city was buried under a multi-meter layer of them. No one could explain this, but the mechanics did.

On the existing continents there are areas where the rocks are very old - three billion years or more, and in other places so-called juvenile rocks have been discovered, i.e. rocks that arose much (a billion, two billion years) later. But why all this happens is unclear. To explain, a convection theory of crustal dynamics was proposed. The internal structure of the Earth was chosen to be very simple - in the center there is a hot molten core, which is a source of heat, then there is a mantle, which is divided into two layers - upper and lower (this is seismological data). The boundary between the layers is the surface of the phase transition of the mantle substance with heat absorption, i.e. When passing through this surface, some chemical bonds are transformed into others and this is accompanied by the absorption of heat. Mechanics and Arctic Research

Our country has developed (which does not exist anywhere else in the world) deep-sea, diving depths up to 6 kilometers, self-propelled or externally controlled self-propelled vehicles - the inhabited “Mir” and the uninhabited “Harpsichord”. They are fundamentally different from the so-called bathyscaphes available in a number of countries, which are simply lowered on a rope, at the bottom they dump ballast and float up. But 6 kilometers of depth means a pressure of 600 atmospheres, which the rather complex structure of the apparatus must withstand. And here we need the mechanics of materials, the mechanics of their behavior in such difficult conditions. The news, which was covered very widely in the newspapers, is the recent expedition to the North Pole on the nuclear icebreakers Rossiya and Akademik Fedorov and the planting of the Russian flag on the bottom of the Arctic Ocean. Nuclear icebreakers have nuclear reactors, which are almost purely mechanical devices. The ability to create durable ships is also mechanics. But even earlier, submarines visited the North Pole and surfaced exactly at the polar point. Who brought them there? Radio? It doesn't work underwater. Echolocation? Although it works, it does so at very short distances. And the boats were brought in by inertial gyroscopic navigation systems of amazing accuracy - another miracle, an outstanding achievement of mechanics both in the past and very recently. Navigation is the science and art of being on a moving vehicle (car, sea or airborne).

Electricity production at nuclear power plants

A good example of the use of mechanics in technology is nuclear energy, one of the technologies that has rapidly developed in recent decades. Nowadays, nuclear reactors are used not only in energy, but also for transport. The schematic diagram of a nuclear power plant - nuclear power plant - is shown in figure 5. In addition to the nuclear reactor, it includes a double-circuit coolant system (the first circuit is radioactive, it is located inside the reactor vessel, and the second, with low radioactivity, is located outside the reactor vessel), steam turbines that rotate electric generators, the electric generators themselves, electricity transmission systems, a cooling system, and pumps. The heart of a nuclear power plant is the nuclear reactor - the joint brainchild of physicists and mechanics. Fuel (uranium) is loaded into the reactor in the form of fuel elements, which are tubes filled with this fuel. The atomic reaction is reduced or enhanced by moving these fuel elements up or down, i.e. pushing them out of the reactor or pushing them into it.

In order to make the achievements of a number of other sciences, including physics, useful to humans, one cannot do without mechanics. Mechanics are the basis. Based on this discovery, mechanics created structures that provide people with thermal and electrical energy. Other discoveries in physics (lasers or other sources of radiation), in chemistry, in materials science, and even in biology require the use of the latest advances in mechanics in order to be useful to people. For example, everything that was said above about nuclear power plants remains true for all new chemical technologies. Conclusion. Sir Isaac Newton and the ever new mechanics

The current stage of development of science and the progress of scientific knowledge in general are largely determined by the penetration into various fields of science of mathematical modeling methods, which are based on the construction and use of certain mathematical models. We must not forget that the method of mathematical modeling originated in Newtonian mechanics (Newton’s famous laws) and was successfully used by Newton himself in solving a number of serious problems. Subsequently, the art of constructing complex mathematical models was created precisely by mechanics and then applied successively by physicists, geophysicists, chemists, biologists, linguists, and, more recently, by sociologists and economists. The mechanical models are extremely clear. It is no coincidence that a number of famous physicists of the past argued that not a single physical phenomenon can be considered fully understood until it is formulated in mechanical terms. A remarkable mechanic and mathematician, academician Alexei Nikolaevich Krylov wrote in the preface to his Russian translation of Newton’s “Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy”: “Newton’s principles ..." constitute the unshakable foundation of mechanics, theoretical astronomy and physics." Lagrange called this work “the greatest of the works of the human mind.” Newtonian mechanics is truly an unsurpassed achievement of physics (natural philosophy), the entire history of human civilization. She is eternal.
