Does a tailwind interfere with birds?

N. KONSTANTINOV

WHEN I HAD ALREADY LEARNED TO READ, I CAUGHT A BOOK about travelers - I forgot which book exactly, but I remembered only one episode from it. The travelers set up camp in the forest next to a lake, on which a flock of wild geese landed to rest during the migration. Evening came and it was no longer possible to hunt. “Tomorrow there will be a glorious hunt,” said one. – Don’t you think that the birds will fly away at dawn? - said another. “They won’t fly away, because the north wind is favorable for the geese.” Birds do not fly with a tailwind, as it blows under the flight feathers of their wings.

This conversation seemed strange to me. If I were the birds, I would rejoice at the tailwind - you can fly fast on it, faster than the wind. But I was little, and everything that was written in the books was true for me. So all I remembered was that I didn't understand something. Twenty-five years have passed. I studied in graduate school, my supervisor was Alexey Andreevich Lyapunov, a famous mathematician who at that time was deeply interested in cybernetics and mathematical approaches to biology. He was connected by scientific interests and personal friendship with our major biophysicist, one of the founders of the science of biophysics - Nikolai Vladimirovich Timofeev-Resovsky. By
