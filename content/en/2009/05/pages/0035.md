It's interesting that...

...the most ingenious invention of Archimedes was the water-lifting screw. According to Galileo, this device is “not only magnificent, but simply wonderful, since we see that the water rises in the screw, continuously descending.” The Archimedes screw was used, for example, in Egypt both to raise water to high places (to a height of up to 4 meters), which the Nile flood usually did not reach, and to drain low-lying areas. ...since the times of the followers of Archimedes - Heron and Pappus - mechanics began to be considered the science of simple machines: a gate, a lever, a block, a wedge and a screw. The last two are based on the properties of an inclined plane, the law of action of which, however, Archimedes himself was not aware of. ...by studying the equilibrium of a closed chain thrown on two inclined planes, the Dutch scientist Simon Stevin in his work “Principles of Statics” proved that the force rolling a load is as many times less than its weight as the height of the plane is less than its length. ...in a treatise compiled during the period of his public lectures in 1597-1598, Galileo, not yet knowing the law of distribution of forces, first considered a lever, then reduced a wedge to a lever, an inclined plane to a wedge, and a screw to an inclined plane. ...Galileo established the laws of free fall of bodies using an inclined plane - a trench lined with polished parchment on the inside, using it to “reduce” the acceleration of free fall by a given number of times... ...by analyzing the movement on a plane that “neither goes down nor goes up,” Galileo revealed the property of bodies to maintain their speed, although he had not yet found a complete and accurate formulation of the law of inertia, which Newton had to do. ... while studying the rolling of a ball “from hill to hill,” Galileo suggested that, in modern terms, the speed acquired during descent does not depend on the shape of the path along which the body moves. Galileo, naturally, did not know that this situation follows from the law of conservation of energy, but he anticipated this law and applied it in the simplest cases of falling of a body or movement on an inclined plane and in experiments with a pendulum. ...Galileo, using a geometric method, constructed a theory of motion along an inclined plane and motion along chords of a circle, showing that along an arc of a circle, which is less than or equal to a quarter of the circle, a body slides faster than along a contracting chord. By this, he anticipated the formulation of the problem of finding the line of fastest descent - the brachistochrone, a problem solved in 1697 by Johann Bernoulli. The equation of this curve, which turned out to be a cycloid, is used, by the way, in the design of toboggan runs and roller coasters. ... when screwing a bolt with a thread pitch of 1 millimeter using a wrench 30 centimeters long, we gain two thousand times in strength, which either allows us to securely fasten parts or move large weights with light effort. ...to prevent cars from flying out when turning, the roads in these places are made inclined. The correct lean angle corresponds to the turning radius and the specific driving speed at which the limit must be set. But bicycle tracks have a variable slope at turns - taking into account the different speeds achieved by athletes.
