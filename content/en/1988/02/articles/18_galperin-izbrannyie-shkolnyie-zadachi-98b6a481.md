The square forms a lobe - part of a sphere bounded by two large semicircles. The area of ​​such a lobule is obviously proportional to the angle between the planes of these semicircles: the area of ​​a lobule with an angle of $\theta$ is equal to $2\theta$ ($\theta=2\pi$ corresponds to a complete sphere). Therefore $S+a=2\alpha, S+b=2\beta, S+c=2\gamma$. Now note that the 8 triangles on the sphere are pairwise symmetric to each other, so $a+b+c+S=2\pi$. Adding the first three equalities and subtracting from the sum of four, we get $2S=2\alpha+2\beta+2\gamma-2\pi$, whence $S=\alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi$. This is the required formula for a spherical triangle.

Solid angle. In physics (less often, in geometry), the vertex of the “solid angle” formed by a polyhedral (and in general, conical) surface with vertex $O$ is useful: the solid angle changes by the area carved out by this surface on the unit sphere with center $O$. (The unit of this vertex is called a “steradian.”) Our theorem expresses the vertex of a solid polyhedral angle in terms of the vertices of its dihedral angles.

For a map on a sphere formed by two large circles, Euler's theorem can also be proven by calculating the sum of the angles. In this case, you need to use a formula expressing the sum of the angles of a polygon through its area and side number, $S=(сумма углов)-\pi(n-2)$, and take into account that the sum of all angles together is $2\pi B$, where $B$ is the number of vertices of the map: $4\pi=2\pi B-\pi(2P-2\Gamma)$.

Selected School Problems

Eighth grade
1. Can the sum of money $A$ rubles $B$ kopecks be an integer $k$ times greater than the sum of money $B$ rubles $A$ kopecks ($0\leq A<100, 0\leq B<100$)? If yes, how many times?
2. Find all pairs of natural numbers whose sum of squares is 16,000.
3. Find positive solutions to the equation system
$$\begin{cases} xy(x+y)=6, \\ yz(y+z)=12, \\ zx(z+x)=30. \end{cases}$$
4. Using a double-sided ruler $a$) draw a bisector of a given angle, 6) double a given angle.
5. In an acute triangle from different vertices, draw the median, bisectorius and altitude. Can their intersection points lie at the vertices of an equilateral triangle?
Ninth grade
6. Solve the equation $(16x^{200}+1)(y^{200}+1)=16(xy)^{100}$.
7. There are two non-intersecting circles on a plane. Find the set of points $A$ on the plane such that any line passing through $A$ intersects at least one of the circles.
8. In a triangle, $ABC$ has the average side from each of the other sides by 1. Find these sides if the bisector of angle $C$ intersects the median of angle $B$ at a right angle.
9. Prove that $\cos(\alpha)>\sin(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha$.
10. The natural numbers $a, b, c, d$ satisfy the equality $a+b=c+d=1000$. When does the sum $\frac{a}{c}+\frac{b}{d}$ take its greatest value?
Tenth grade
11. Find approximate values ​​of the roots of the equation
$$0,001x^3+x^2-1=0$$
with an accuracy of 0.1.
12. Find the volume of a spatial figure formed by the intersection of two circular cylindrical surfaces of radius $R$, the axes of which intersect $a$) at a right angle, 6) at an angle $a$.
13. Is it possible to mark out 120 squares of size $1\times 1$ in a rectangle of size $20\times 25$ so that any circle of diameter 1 inside the rectangle intersects at least one of these squares?
14. Prove that if positive numbers $x$ and $y$ are related by $x^y+y^x=x^x+y^y$, then $x=y$.
15. The tangents at point $x=0$ to the graphs of functions $f(x), g(x)$ and $f(x)/g(x)$ intersect the x-axis at the same angle. Find the largest value of $f(0)$.
