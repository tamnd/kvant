Theoretical problems with balls

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
M. R. LIBERZON

This article discusses problems about combinations of bodies in space that include one or more balls. The set of bodies specified in the task can be difficult to depict on a drawing, but this is not necessary: ​​to solve the problem, construct flat drawings that obtain planes in the section of a given spatial figure or when projecting it on a plane. The main thing is to choose the right plane. There is no universal way to make such a choice; you must, of course, try to ensure that the cutting planes pass through as many nodal points as possible - the centers of the balls, points of contact, etc. - and during the design, the given lengths and angles are preserved (or changed in a controlled way).

Let us recall a few simple geometric facts that are often used when selecting planes.

Statement 1. Raduzi drawn to the point of contact of two balls lie on the same straight line. The distance between the centers of the tangent of the balls is equal to the sum of their difference of their radii.

Statement 2. If a plane passes through the centers of the tangent of the balls, then it contains a point of tangency.

Statement 3. Let the ball touch the lateral surface of a right circular cylinder. If the plane passes through the cylinder and the center of the ball, then it contains a point of tangency.

Statement 4. Let the ball touch the lateral surface of a right circular cone. If the plane passes the height of the cone and the center of the ball, then it contains a point of tangency.

As examples, we will analyze several problems that were proposed at different times in the introductory copies at Moscow State University.

Task 1. In the space enclosed between a spherical surface and a plane passing through its center, three identical balls of radius r are placed so that each ball touches the other two, the spherical surface and the indicated plane. Set the radius of the spherical surface.

Solution. The projections of the centers of these balls onto a given plane are the vertices of a regular triangle on the side 2r; the center of the spherical surface lies at the center of this triangle; the distance from this center to any vertex of the triangle is 2r/√3 (Fig. 1).

Let us draw a plane through the center O of the spherical surface and the center A of one of these balls perpedicular to the given plane. The section will produce the figure depicted on figure 2. On this figure point M is the projection of point A onto the given plane, P is the point of tangency of the given ball with the center A and the given spherical surface. In the right triangle AOM the lengths of the legs are known: AM=r, MO=2r/√3. Trace-

Problem 2. In triangle $ABC$ side $AB$ is equal to 2, side $BC$ is equal to 4 and $\angle B = 60^\circ$. When the balls touch the plane of the triangle $ABC$, respectively, at points $A, B, C$ and touch each other in pairs. Determine the radius of these balls.

Solution. Using the cosine theorem, we find the length of side $AC$: it is equal to $\sqrt{12}$. Let the balls touching the plane of the triangle at points $A, B, C$ have their centers at points $O_1, O_2, O_3$, and their radii equal to $x, y, z$. Problem 3. A ball of radius $R$ lies on a plane. The same plane is intersected by a right circular cylinder of radius $r$, and the generators of the cylinder are perpendicular to the plane. The center of the ball is removed from the cylinder axis at a distance $\rho > R + r$. Find the minimum possible radius of a ball that would touch the cylinder, the plane, and the given ball at the same time.

Solution. Let us draw an auxiliary plane through the axes of the cylinder and the center $Q$ of the given ball. The center $O$ of the ball, whose radius we are looking for, lies, very indo, in this plane. In the section (Fig. 4) there is a right triangle $OKQ$, in which the lengths of all sides are expressed in terms of the problem data $R, r, \rho$ and the required radius $x$. The Pythagorean theorem gives the equation for calculating $x$; solving this equation we get $x = (\sqrt{R + \rho} - r) - \sqrt{R})^2$.

Problem 4. Given a cube $ABCDA_1B_1C_1D_1$ ($AA_1 \parallel BB_1 \parallel CC_1 \parallel DD_1$). In the corner $A$ of the cube there is a ball with radius $R = 0,5$. Find the radius of a ball hanging in the corner $C$ of a cube and touching the given ball, provided that the edge of the cube is $a = 1,5$.

Solution. Let $Q$ be the center of the ball hanging at corner $A$. There are three balls hanging at corner $C$ and touching the given ball. However, of these concerns the base of the cube $ABCD$

In this plane lie the centers of all the balls, their points of contact with each other and with the plane $ABCD$. Consider marking the balls $O_2$ and $Q$ (Fig. 5) with this plane. In triangle $O_2KQ$, only the length of leg $KO_2$ is unknown. But $KO_2=MN$, and we find the distance between points $M$ and $N$ from the projection onto the base plane $ABCD$ (Fig. 6);

$$(R+x)^2=(R-x)^2+2(a-R-x)^2,$$
