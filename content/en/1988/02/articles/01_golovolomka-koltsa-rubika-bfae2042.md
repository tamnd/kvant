Recently, a new Erno Rubik's puzzle ("Zacheni kolba") appeared in Hungary, even more tannic than the famous Rubik's cube (which, however, has already been successfully eaten a lot). By peppering and smashing the original 2x4 rectangle, which features three loose flasks, the new Rubik's puzzle inexplicably takes on a variety of shapes (some of which are shown as black-and-white diagrams at figure). The secret of the design, however, is very simple: the puzzle consists of 8 squares, depicted by a network of diagonal folds and fastened with inconspicuous closed transparent nylon threads; the threads are hidden in the grooves, but when the puzzles are selected, they jump from one groove to another, as shown in figure on the left (the jumping on the puzzle itself is not visible, or will occur between the squares pressed against each other). But it is much more difficult to solve the puzzle: turn a 2x4 rectangle into a “3x3 square without a corner” (shown in the middle figure), on which the flask... is fixed.
