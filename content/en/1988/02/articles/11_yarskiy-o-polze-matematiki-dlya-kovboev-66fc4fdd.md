ABOUT THE BENEFITS OF MATHEMATICS
FOR COWBOYS

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. S. YARSKY

Bill came to see Mr. Cohn just after lunch. At this time, the Texas sun turns all Texas cheese into processed cheese, all Texas water into boiled water, and makes all Texas residents dream of some quicker and less painful death.

By the time Bill's temperature dropped below the boiling point of iron, and all the ice in the house had completely dried up, Bill finally gained the ability to pronounce the words:

- You see, Mr. Cohn, it's all about this Stenili! He and I are, one might say, friends. And until recently they argued only once.

- How was it? - Mr. Cohn tried his best to stay awake.

“You see, Mr. Cohn, Stanily is going somewhere to get rich, in Natchez - this is in the Red River riding. Well, I've been there and I tell him:

“It’s supposed to be a seven-day journey there on horseback.”

“Yeah,” says Stenili, “what about Baton Rouge?”

“A day,” I say.

- How about Lafayette?

— About four days. And the road is not easy.

- And until... - and then he names some other town - how long?

“I haven’t been there,” I say, “but I thought about ten or twelve days.”

“You know, Bill,” says Stanily, “all these four cities lie as if by order - at the tops of the rectangular line.”

- So what? - I say.

- Otherwise! - Stenili answers. “I know how far it is to...” here he again named the same town.

“What,” I tell him, “were you there?”

“No,” he says, “I wasn’t.”

- So how can you know?

“I know,” he says, “it takes about eight days to get there!”

Well, words for words, Mr. Cohn, I took out my amble and... exactly eight days later I found myself in this town, if I had never seen him again. And eight days later, Stenili explained to me that there was some kind of “theorem”...*). It turns out that this is what I could have gone to the Red River for.

“Your Stenily is a smart boy!” the heat seemed to have subsided a little and Mr. Cohn had perked up a little.

“Even though he’s not stupid,” Bill chuckled displeasedly.

And so, Mr. Cohn, a few days ago this Stanili came up to me, started talking about the weather, about cattle prices, and then he said:

“Listen, Bill, do you think there’s a horse in our area that you can’t handle, but it’ll go my way?”

Without hesitation I tell him:

“Well, Stenili, on such a horse, and so be it, I will help you get to the nearest cemetery.”

And he again for his own:

*) If ABCD is a rectangle and S is an arbitrary point, then SA² + SC² = SB² + SD².

“It seems to me, Bill, that there is such a horse.”

“Okay,” I say, “I’ll bet ten to one that there is no such horse.” Lay out your dollar and don’t fool me.

“Take your time, Bill,” he says, “and have ten dollars ready.” The answer is at the end. Look!

Then he takes the notebook in which I keep track, opens it on a blank page and writes:

$$\frac{a}{b} - \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a-c}{b-d}.$$

I, Mr. Cohn, ask him:

- Listen, Stanley, why did you start counting from ten - here on the right, in parentheses?

“This is your debt to me,” he says, “where should it be recorded if not in the ledger?”

“You’re in a hurry,” I tell him. “It’s true that I haven’t gone to school for a long time, but that horse of yours, number ten, is lame on all four legs.” And you will receive my ten tenth no sooner than this animal takes at least one step.

“It will take this step,” says Stanley and writes:

$$\frac{8}{2} - \frac{9}{3} = \frac{8-9}{2-3}.$$

“Check,” he says, “the bill presented and fork out...

Tell me, Mr. Cohn, have you ever seen a man who made a fool and immediately admitted it? Have you seen the unnecessary ten dollars?...

“Listen, Stanley,” I say, “I’ll bet you another ten that your gelding won’t take another step!”

“Put it out,” he says, “and here’s your receipt:

$$\frac{5}{3} - \frac{9}{9} = \frac{5-9}{3-9} = \frac{-4}{-6} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

Losing two races in a row has never happened to me before, Mr. Cohn. But then I remembered my agreement with Stanley and decided that all was not lost.

“Listen, Stanley,” I tell him, “this horse can only walk, I don’t argue.” But you said that I wouldn’t be able to make it in time. I'll bet you twenty against your twenty, that this mustang will follow suit for me too.

“Accepted,” Stanley replies.

So, Mr. Cohn, I lost this race too... You, Mr. Cohn, in your school and in a week cannot earn as much as that brat Stanley earned in a couple of hours... And most importantly, I just can’t understand what kind of devil a lame horse is, that is, I wanted to say - the wrong formula gives the right answers? This is exactly what I came to you with!...

— Science requires sacrifices! - said Mr. Cohn. - And you, Billy, are one of these victims!.. First of all, who told you that the wrong formula cannot give the right results? Nonsense! Further, the biggest liar in the world can serve to tell the truth!.. Secondly, while you were here blowing one race after another, I found a subsection of your shame. Take a look at the formula

$$\frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{d} \left( 2 - \frac{b}{d} \right).$$

It doesn't matter which way I got there. It is important that this is the same formula (10). Somewhat transformed, of course. You, Billy, can take any b and d, calculate a and c from them - and that’s it! Simple and hassle-free
