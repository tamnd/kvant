F1081. To clear the air of dust, which under normal conditions settles very slowly, you can use the fact that the dust is contaminated.
In the first experiment, a glass cylinder (Fig. 1) with dusty air is placed in an electric field of $E_1=1\times 10^4$ V/m, directed along the cylinder axis. After a time of $t_1=2$ minutes, all the dust contained in the cylinder settled to the bottom. In the second experiment, a thin wire is pulled along the axis of the cylinder and connected to a high voltage source. It is known that in this case the field voltage is $E \sim 1/r$, where $r$ is the distance to the axis. The source voltage is selected so that the electric field voltage at the cylinder walls is, as in the first experiment, $1 \cdot 10^4$ V/m. Assuming that the dust particles are identical, and the charges of the dust particles are equal, determine the time for all the dust to settle on the walls of the cylinder in the second experiment. There is little dust in the air, so the charge volume can be neglected.
