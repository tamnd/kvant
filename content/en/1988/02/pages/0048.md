Observation and photography of fast processes occurring

S. M. BELOUSOV, R. N. GERASIMOV

The book by J. Darius “Inaccessible to the Eye” pushed us to this work, in which we read about high-speed photography and saw interesting photographs.

We decided to assemble a setup that would allow us to take photographs of rapid processes. And what

This article, written by Leningrad schoolchildren, talks about the extensive and interesting work carried out by the authors - students of the 9th grade of Physics School No. 45 at Leningrad State University - on the basis of the Research Institute of Physics of Leningrad State University.

If these processes could also be observed directly with the eye, we installed a stroboscopic one. The block diagram of our installation is shown in figure 1.

The process of falling drops on the surface of water was taken as the object of study.

The next drop falling into the water interrupts the laser beam, darkening the photodiode. An electrical pulse from the photodiode reaches a timer (adjustable delay), which is needed so that different phases of the process under study can be observed. The delayed pulse enters the forming amplifier, opens, amplified many times, and goes to ignite the flash lamp.
