On the contrary, what is needed for a string subordinate to (4,2)-meter to obey iambic? It is necessary that in words that do not contain strong words of (4,2)-measure, but contain (then the only) even syllable, this syllable should be remote. (For example, if the 3rd and 4th syllables of such a line make up a word, then in this word the stress should fall on the 4th, and not on the 3rd syllable.) And this seems quite feasible.

Four-syllable sizes. Now we can turn to their consideration. Each four-syllable meter we will assign a two-syllable meter in accordance with it: for (4,1) and (4,3) meter, this is, by definition, considered to be trochee, for (4,2) and (4,4) meter - iambic. Further, if a line obeys a four-syllable meter, we will speak of its meter as a non-independent four-syllable if it also obeys a conjugate two-syllable meter, and as an independent one otherwise.

Let's start with the independent four-syllable meter. I must admit that I do not know of any convincing examples of Russian poetry subordinated to an independent four-syllable meter. Probably, there are none - one can come to such conclusions by reading the article by poet L. E. Lyapina “Russian Songs” in the already cited collection “Russian Versification”. (Peonies are the scientific name for four-syllable meters; and five-syllable meters are scientifically called pentons.) Why is this so? In search of an answer, let's try to construct a line subject to an independent four-syllable meter:

I wrote forty articles in the magazine «Kvant». This line obeys (4,3)-meter, but does not obey trochee. Reading it causes obvious difficulties: the stress in the words forty and the log of each station is not in place. What's the matter? The fact is that, as has already been said, approximately a third of the words in a poem should be stressed; Therefore, strong words have not been achieved, and it comes to adding at least weak additional stress to them. For political reasons, these stresses want to be placed in the middle of the interval between strong syllables, and this means that the four-syllable size tends to be dependent.

Non-independent four-syllable meter is a very common phenomenon. If you doubt its existence (as do neokoto poets - polemics with them constitute the main content of the above-read article by L. E. Lyapina), ask any student of a music school - and he will tell you that the four-syllable meter is found very often in music, in particular in songs:

Was I in the field and not a grass...
Oh how I love my cows...
(all this is a non-independent (4.3)-meter; it, like the three-beat one, is very characteristic of Russian song). If this doesn’t convince you, look through Pushkin. Here is a sample of (4,2)-meter from his youthful poems:

In the ovens of Helikon
I was once undressed;
In the name of Apollo
Named Tibullus...

The famous “Winter Road” by Pushkin (On the winter, boring road...) also seems to me to be written in four-syllable meter; however, the composer Sviridov suggested reading these verses as two-syllable.

Moreover, often (one might say, as a rule) only part of the poem is subordinated to the four-syllable meter, and transitions from a four-syllable rhythm to a two-syllable one and back can be unbearable or figurative. This can be easily seen, for example, in Nekrasov’s pose “Who Lives Well in Rus'” (non-independent (4.2) size).

Five-syllable sizes. Since the five-syllable meter can only be independent. Where to look for it? It would seem that the verses are even more drawn-out and melodious than the Russian songs and lyrics of Nekrasov. maybe folk tales would be suitable? And exactly, many lines of this subordination to the five-syllable meter:

Oh, dobi esi, good molde...
or
That is not a husband with a wife, that is not a brother with a sister - A kind molde with a red maiden.
A more consistent five-syllable meter (although also not without a digression) can be found in counterfeits of the tale style, stichizations written by professional sweats, for example, in Lermontov’s “Merchant Kalashnikov”:

How we got together and got ready
Daring Moscow fighters
