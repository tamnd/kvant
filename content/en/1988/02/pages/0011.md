ABOUT THE CAN,
SPRING
AND ROLLING MILL

Doctor of Technical Sciences
V. A. PRUDKOVSKY

Let's assume that you have had to open cans more than once. And from time to time you wondered: couldn’t the metal sheet from which the jar is made be made thinner? Then, for example, from the materials for one tin can, two or even three could be made. What's the matter? Why don't metallurgists save metal? Let's try to figure it out.

Tin cans are made from thin steel sheet - tin. Tinplate is produced by cold rolling. It must be said that more than 500 years have passed since the earliest reliable mention of the use of rolling for deforming metal. In 1486, the great artist, architect, scientist and engineer Leonardo da Vinci depicted a rolling mill for producing thin strips of gold and silver for jewelry. Such a mill had two smooth rolls with a diameter of about 40 mm, which were turned by hand. The essence of the rolling process has not fundamentally changed since then (figure 1, a). A metal strip is pulled into the gap between the rollers rotating in different directions, the thickness of which ($h_0$) is greater than the size of this gap ($S_0$). Under the influence of the rolls, the strip undergoes plastic deformation and changes in shape and should emerge from the rolls with a thickness seemingly equal to the size of the gap between them (while increasing in length and remaining the same width $B$). However, in fact, only the metal of the rolling strip ($h_1$) is always larger than the contamination between the rolls. Why?

The deformation of the strip will occur as a result of the forces applied to it by the rolls. Each roll acts on the strip, and according to Newton’s third law, the strip acts on the roll with the same force; it is called the resistance force of the deformed or simply the resistance of the deformed. Under the influence of this force, elastic deformation of the rolls and other parts of the rolling mill occurs, the initially established gap between the rolls increases, and the thickness of the rolling strip increases.
