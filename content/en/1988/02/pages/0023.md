Let's start with $n=1$. Obviously, any text is subject to (1,1)-meter: all syllables are strong. Reading such poems means chanting (shai-bu-shai-bu!). Poems of this kind exist, for example: No-eats-nebet-men-shoi-sy-nok (Nekrasov), Ko-siko-ca-no-ka-ro-ca (Tvardovsky). But they are rare.

The sizes with $n>3$ are more interesting, especially with $n=4$ and 5.

Combination of sizes. Before turning to them, let's ask ourselves: can the same string be subordinated to two different sizes? Our definition does not exclude this possibility. For example, a line is composed of only monosyllabic words, subject to any size.

Rule 2. a) Prove that the line, subordinate to both iambic and trochee, consists of monosyllabic words. 6) Prove that a string of 9 words subordinated to two different three-syllable meters contains at least 6 words.

In practical terms, this means that different two-syllable meters, as well as different three-syllable meters, cannot be combined. It would seem that it is easier to combine a two-syllable meter with a three-syllable one, although in this case gray restrictions on the length of words arise.

Rule 3. Prove that if a line is subordinate to both a trochee and a dactyl, then the 4th syllable is part of either a monosyllabic word or a word consisting of at least 4 words.

Nabokov gives an example of a line subordinate to iambic and amphibrach: Mysterious and unearthly. But a line is not yet a poem. Moreover, the strong syllables of two-syllable and three-syllable meters combined in one poem would be completely different, and this poem (if it existed) could be read in two completely different ways. One can say that you will not find such a poem in any positive collection.

Another thing is the combination of bisyllabic and tetrasyllabic meters, and the reason is that 4 is divisible by 2. For example, what does it take for a line subject to iambic to also obey, say, (4,2)-meter? It is necessary that in a word containing more than one even syllable, the stress falls on a syllable whose number is not divisible by 4.
