F1080. The thin-walled vessel is continuously pumped out with a pump, however, due to the presence of a microcrack in the wall of the vessel, a constant pressure of $p_y = 0.001$ mmHg is established in it. Art. Outside, normal atmospheric pressure is $p_0 = 760$ mmHg. Art., relative air humidity $\varphi = 80\%$. Find the vapor pressure of water in the container. The saturated vapor pressure of water at a given temperature is $p_n = 17.5$ mmHg. Art.

Let's consider the Stationary mode, when the pressures of water vapor and dry air in the vessel do not change over time. Let us denote the steady-state pressure of water vapor in the vessel by $p_{y\pi}$, and by $p_{yB}$ the steady-state pressure of dry air. The steady pressure of moist air in the vessel $p_y$ is equal to the sum of these partial pressures:

$$p_y = p_{y\pi} + p_{yB}.$$

(1)

Since the air temperature remains unchanged, the condition for maintaining pressure for each of the two components requires that the number of molecules of each gas (vapor or dry air) entering the vessel per unit time is equal to the number of gas molecules leaving the vessel during the same time. The number of gas molecules passing through a microcrack is proportional to the product of their concentration and the average speed of thermal movement of the molecules of this gas. In turn, this is the product of the proportional pressure disturbance divided by $\sqrt{\mu}$, where $\mu$ is the molar mass of the gas. The number of gas molecules taken into a vessel per unit time is proportional to the pressure of this gas in the vessel. Thus, neglecting the molecules that leave the vessel through the microcrack ($p_y \ll p_H \frac{\varphi}{100}$), for the molecules of water vapor and dry air we can write:

$$\frac{p_{0\pi}}{\sqrt{\mu_{\pi}}} \sim p_{y\pi}, \frac{p_{0B}}{\sqrt{\mu_{\beta}}} \sim p_{yB}.$$

(2)

Substituting $p_{yB}$ from (2) into (1), we find the steady-state pressure of water vapor in the vessel:

$$p_{y\pi} = \frac{p_y}{1 + \frac{p_{0B}}{p_{0\pi}} \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\pi}}{\mu_{\beta}}}} = \frac{p_y}{1 + \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\pi}}{p_{0}} - \frac{p_H \varphi}{100}}} \approx \frac{\varphi p_H p_y}{100 p_0} \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\beta}}{\mu_{\pi}}} \approx 2.34 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ мм рт. ст.}$$
