External angle theorema

You can put the angles of a triangle together in a different way than at figure 1, by extending one side of it beyond the vertex and drawing a ray from this vertex parallel to the opposite side (Fig. 2). Thus, it is proved that the external angle of a triangle (adjacent to the internal one) is equal to the sum of two internal angles that are not funny with it. Essentially this is a version of the theorem on the sum of angles of a triangle. The theorem on the external angle of a triangle is used to prove multi-statements. Let us recall only one thing that is used especially often: the magnitude of the central angle is equal to half the magnitude of the arc on which this angle rests, i.e., the position of the magnitude of the central angle resting on the same arc (in the textbook by A.V. About the size of the angle

In offended life, at the angle of the concept is a sharp geometric figure (corner of a table, corner of a house). When talking about the experimental verification of the theorem on the sum of the angles of a triangle, we mean the angle as a part of the plane contained between two rays (a piece of paper). Adding angles—applying one angle to another—has led us to a non-educational angle whose sides lie on the same straight line: a 180° rotated angle.

Applying several angles can lead to some kind of two-layer figure. A natural question arises: what is an angle, for example, of 380°? Such angles are considered in the trionometry course (see “Algebra - 8”, p. 131); They are convenient to use when measuring turns.

Consider, as an example, the rotation of the radio knob used to tune it. Rotate this hand a few degrees, we will get to the desired wave. At the same time, turning 20° from some mark is not the same as turning 380° for tuning the radio, although the corresponding positions of the knob are the same. Moreover, you can rotate your hand in one direction or the other - therefore, you need to enter, in addition to positive angles (corresponding to, say, counterclockwise turns), also negative angles. The sum of two angles will be the rotation that is obtained if you turn your hand first to one angle and then to the second.

Sum of angles of a polygon

Let's imagine a ruler sliding along the sides of a triangle (Fig. 3, a). Near each vertex, the ruler rotates to a vertex equal to the outer corner of the triangle, and after a full circle around the triangle - say, counterclockwise - the ruler returns to its original place, making one full turn - 360°. So, the sum of the external angles of a triangle is 360° (Fig. 3, b).
