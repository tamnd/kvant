To the Moscow River, to the fist howl...
And the king arrived with his retinue,
With boyars and guardsmen...
All this, by the way, is (5.3)-size.

Everything seemed clear. But let me give you a completely different example. In the last year of his life, Gumilyov wrote the “Dragon” pose, consisting of 12 parts of 5 quatrains each. I will give the first part in full, describing the sunrise on the ocean shore, and you will determine the size.

Because of the fresh waves of the ocean
The red bull raised the family.
And the deer of the fog whitened
Under the folding banks.

Beneath the folding banks,
In the noisy damp shadow
Silvery yams
They settled on the moss.

The red bull changes faces
Here I spread my wings wide,
And a huge bird soars,
Devouring space.

And to the enemies of the blue idol
According to the open three skies
He comes out, a shooter and a lyre player,
Holding the key to secrets and miracles.

Blow the winds to make the waves sing
So that in the forests they eat trunks,
Blow the winds in the chimneys of the gorges,
Singing his praises.

Answer: (5,3)-size.

Indeed, suppose that the given poem obeys (n, k)-meter. In all 20 lines, the 3rd and 8th syllables are stressed (in their own words). There are 7 lines in which the word containing the 3rd syllable has more than 3 syllables. This means that either the 3rd syllable is strong, or n≥5. The same is true for the 8th syllable. Further, the 3rd and 8th syllables are simultaneously strong only in the five-syllable size. It is easy to check that the poem really obeys the (5,3)-meter and does not obey other five-syllable meters. We exclude sizes with n>5 from consideration (see below).

To be convincing, I’ll add that out of our 20 lines, 5 obey the (3,3)-size, 3 obey the (4,1)-size, 1 obeys the (4,4)-size, all 20 obey the (5,3)-size, 10 obey the (5,5)-size (this is the cost of our definition, see p. 18) and none obeys any other (n, k)-size with n, not greater than 5. A machine would give us such information if we resorted to its help.

But where is the drawn-out melodiousness of “Merchant Kalashnikov” in “Dragon”? The main reason for its absence is that in almost every line of "Dragon", in addition to the main stresses on the 3rd and 8th syllables, there is a fairly distinct additional deletion either on the 6th syllable. Additional stresses can be traced in other poems by Gumilyov, written in five-syllable size, as well as in Akhmatova and Tsvetaeva. In a certain sense, here too one could talk about the lack of independence of the (five-syllable) meter, but it is impossible to clarify this meaning while remaining within the framework of force-botonics.

Tsvetaeva’s poems, for example, are difficult to approach with the definition of meter that was given at the beginning of the article and which we use all the time. Many of her poems are written in individual meter with fixed but not arithmetic progression numbers of strong syllables, say 3, 6, 8, as in the poem.

Ony meat goes to polchi:
The hour of monks and watchful birds,
The conspirator hour - and the young men,
The hour of love and murder.*)
