Electric charge

Even in ancient times it was known that if you lose a piece of amber, it is capable of attracting small objects. This funny fact was lost among the diverse natural phenomena that interested ancient thinkers... Later, the term “electricity” arose, and in the 18th century it was established that there are two types of electrified bodies. Bodies of the same type repel each other, two bodies of different types attract.

The American scientist B. Frankin (1706-1790), engaged in the study of electricity, drew attention to the phenomenon that appeared to him in other countries. A charged ball placed inside a charged metal cube did not experience any effect from the cube's charge. Frankin shared his bewilderment about this with J. Priestley (1733-1804), and he agreed that perhaps the fact is that the electric force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between charges - in complete analogy with Newton’s law of universal gravitation, widely known at that time. From this law, in particular, it followed that the limit of the force acting from the side of a homogeneous sphere on the body that is located inside it is equal to zero.
