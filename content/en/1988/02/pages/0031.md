M1067. Prove that for non-negative numbers x, y, z satisfying the condition $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$, we have the inequality

$$\frac{x}{1-x^2} + \frac{y}{1-y^2} + \frac{z}{1-z^2} \geq \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Let's prove that

$$\frac{t}{1-t^2} \geq \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} t^2$$

with all $t, 0 \leq t < 1$. On this interval, inequality (*) is equivalent to inequality

$$f(t) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} t(1-t^2) \leq 1,$$

which is proved using a standard study of the function $f(t)$. (The derivative $f'(t) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}(1-3t^2)$ has a unique root $t = 1/\sqrt{3}$ on [0; 1], and $f(0) = f(1) = 0 < f(1/\sqrt{3}) = 1$. Therefore $1/\sqrt{3}$ is the maximum point of $f(t)$ on [0; 1].).

By virtue of (*), the left-hand side proves the inequality inequality, than

$$\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2};$$

equality is achieved only at $x = y = z = 1/\sqrt{3}$.
