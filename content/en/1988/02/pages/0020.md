Determination of size. We postulate that the text (verses) in question is divided into lines, the lines are divided into words consisting of words (we ignore words like “in” or “to”). The syllables in each line are numbered consecutively, starting from the first. One of the words of each word is stressed (this is the syllable on which the stress falls when reading the word in person).

As is always the case with axioms, this statement only approximately reflects reality. Firstly, there are words with two (or more?) stresses: self-zoning, doubly periodic, etc. However, these are long, awkward words that are rarely found in poetry. Secondly, in some words, such as or, through a period, etc., the stress may move to another syllable. This often happens in poetry, an example is the mention of the 26th line from “Eugene Onegin” - Or they shone, my reader; Remember again: Through the forests, Through the seas, the sorcerer carries the boytyrs. Such a transfer of stress is also possible in colloquial speech - say: “A river flows, there is a lot of space through it,” and you will hear that the stress in the words falls on the second syllable. Thirdly, and finally, some groups of words, for example, “homework” in the phrase “Homework assignment,” form so-called rhythmic groups that should be equated with words. For details, see the article by A. N. Kolmogorov and A. V. Prokorov “Model of the rhythmic line of Russian speech, adaptations to the study of the metrics of classical Russian stixa” in the collection “Russian verse composition” (M.: Nauka, 1985).
