Problem 2. In triangle $ABC$ side $AB$ is equal to 2, side $BC$ is equal to 4 and $\angle B = 60^\circ$. When the balls touch the plane of the triangle $ABC$, respectively, at points $A, B, C$ and touch each other in pairs. Determine the radius of these balls.

Solution. Using the cosine theorem, we find the length of side $AC$: it is equal to $\sqrt{12}$. Let the balls touching the plane of the triangle at points $A, B, C$ have their centers at points $O_1, O_2, O_3$, and their radii equal to $x, y, z$. Let us draw three auxiliary secant planes redistributing to the plane of the triangle $ABC$: one through the points $O_1$ and $O_2$ (the section of our figure by this plane is shown in figure 3), another through the points $O_2$ and $O_3$ and the third through the points $O_1$ and $O_3$ (the sections of these planes of the device are similar). In each section there is a right-angled triangle, the length of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the radii of the two balls, one leg is equal to the difference of these radii, and the other leg is equal to the length of the corresponding side of the triangle.

Problem 3. A ball of radius $R$ lies on a plane. The same plane is intersected by a right circular cylinder of radius $r$, and the generators of the cylinder are perpendicular to the plane. The center of the ball is removed from the cylinder axis at a distance $\rho > R + r$. Find the minimum possible radius of a ball that would touch the cylinder, the plane, and the given ball at the same time.

Solution. Let us draw an auxiliary plane through the axes of the cylinder and the center $Q$ of the given ball. The center $O$ of the ball, whose radius we are looking for, lies, very indo, in this plane. In the section (Fig. 4) there is a right triangle $OKQ$, in which the lengths of all sides are expressed in terms of the problem data $R, r, \rho$ and the required radius $x$. The Pythagorean theorem gives the equation for calculating $x$; solving this equation we get $x = (\sqrt{R + \rho} - r) - \sqrt{R})^2$.
