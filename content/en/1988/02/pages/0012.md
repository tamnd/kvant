Well, okay, you say, then you need to make the gap between the rolls less than the required thickness of the rigid and get the required thickness. Logical! Although this is only one way.

But is it really that simple? The thickness of a rigid “normal” tin can is 0.2-0.25 mm. What gap should be installed between the rolls to obtain this sheet thickness? What gap needs to be set to get a sheet with a thickness of, say, 0.1 mm?

Let's first see how the rolling mill (figure 2) is built. The rollers (1), the deformation strip, cannot appear in the air. They are mounted on bearings (2) together with support rolls (3) in a frame called the rolling mill frame (4). The required gap between the rolls during rolling is set using screws, which are called push vintimi vintimi (5).

The deformation resistance force acting on the work rolls from the side of the rolled strip causes elastic deformation not only of these rolls, but also of the support rolls, as well as pressure screws, mill screws and other elements of the rolling mill. In this case, the rolling mill behaves like a circle, although not of a very ordinary design.
