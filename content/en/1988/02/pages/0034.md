When a streamlined body moves at low speed in a liquid, a drag force will arise that is directly proportional to the speed of this body. If the masses of the boat and the load are equal to the corresponding M and m, the speed of the boat at a given time is v, and its acceleration is a, then (according to Newton’s second law) the equation of motion of the boat has the form:

(M+m)a=mg-Av, (*)

where A is a certain constant characterizing the resistance force.

Equation (*) describes motion with variable acceleration. To find the dependence of speed on time, it is necessary, generally speaking, to interpret (*). However, we need to find the speed of the boat after only a short time after it started moving. In this case, the contribution of the second term on the right side of equation (*) can be neglected and we can assume that the boat is moving with some initial acceleration a0. The equation of motion itself is an idea of ​​how to use these problems to solve it. The dependence of acceleration a on speed v is the equation of a straight line in coordinates a, v. Therefore, extrapolating (*) to the values ​​v→0, we obtain the initial acceleration a0.

To construct the dependence a(v), the velocity increment at each time interval of 1 s can be considered equal to the acceleration. The speed of movement at each of the specified time intervals can be taken as its average value. The dependence a(v) obtained in this way is

a (cm/s²) 0.79 0.52 0.35 0.23 0.16 0.1 0.06 0.05
v (cm/s) 6.04 6.71 7.14 7.42 7.62 7.75 7.83 7.88

- shown on figure 2 (solid line). The initial acceleration a0, obtained from the graph at v→0, is ~3.5 cm/s².

Thus, 0.1 s after the start of movement, the speed of the boat is approximately 0.35 cm/s.
