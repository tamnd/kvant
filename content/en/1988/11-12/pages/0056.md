The appearance of a loop shows that the equation of state of a real gas ceases to describe the actual situation. Thus, it turns out that a gas can turn into a liquid only at a temperature below a certain certain temperature, called the critical temperature, at which the loop first appears. It turns out that the critical temperature $T_k$ can be expressed through the parameters $a$ and $b$, which characterize the gas:

$$T_k = \frac{1}{R} \frac{8a}{27b}.$$

This principle was formulated by Christian Huygen in his Treatise on Light, published in 1690. At that time, there were no longer any great difficulties in describing the motion of particles. In free space, particles move in a straight line and uniformly; under the influence of external influences they slow down, accelerate, change the direction of movement (are refracted or reflected) - and all this can be calculated. At the same time, the laws of wave propagation—reflection, refraction, and loss of properties (diffraction)—could not be explained. And Huygens proposed a principle on the basis of which this could be done.
