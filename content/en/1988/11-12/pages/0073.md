You can often hear that FMS students have a feeling of elitism. In principle, such a danger exists because many of our students in their home schools were leaders, “stars,” and some were almost considered “geniuses.” But moving to a school where the requirements are significantly higher, where everyone around is a “star,” in itself contributes to a revaluation of one’s own principles. And here the most important task of the teaching staff is to create an atmosphere at school in which respect for the successes of their friends, civic responsibility, communist rule, and an active life position would be fostered. A comprehensive educational program includes many aspects. School self-government, the Lenin test system are constantly being improved, work is regularly organized in the experimental farm of the Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR and many other rubles. Traditions play a big role in school. This is especially important because the student population is renewed by two-thirds every year. The work of the Komsomol department at the Leningrad School, which includes school graduates who entered the first year of the university, has become a tradition.
