Quoll, which I have to figure out myself. The lecture was to be given in the first class, so I had half a day. Blackull wrote fantastic scat. I went home, sat up all night, gave a lecture, and then came home to sleep.

Quite quickly, A.N. switched me to a new topic. I had to study books on radio engineering, which described the design of receivers and lamps, and understand the principles of radar. The customers came and taught the essence of the task. Its mathematical formulation, proposed by A.N., turned out to be surprisingly successful (A.N. was able to formulate problems so successfully that completely new directions were then expressed from them). While working on this problem, I wrote my doctoral dissertation and a book on statistical sequential analysis.

A.N. did not explain anything like that. I had to figure it out on my own. He gave us an almost engineering formulation of the problem: to minimize the average delay time for a given probability of false alarms. He guessed the answer, of course. A.N. only said that in a very simple situation the answer should be such and such, and it could not be obtained from any theory existing at that time: we simply had to invent it.

And now about general culture. Since we were bound by secretarial duties and lecture activities, we often had to be in Komarovka. We went hiking and listened to music. And in music it was impossible to be familiar with A.N.

A typical example. After some evening, when there was a lot of music, A.N. asked: “Well, what else should you play now? Charpentier or Couperin? I said: “Andrey Nikolaevich, maybe something easier?” A.N. immediately: “Now I’ll find you “Lonely Harmon”.” I had no idea that A.N. might know anything about this “Lonely Harmony.” And one day, while listening to music, I sat and tapped my fingers on the table. A.N. quickly placed a napkin. I understood everything.

My activities (first non-silent transformations, then sequential analysis) are an example of how A.N., with the help of very simple formulations, pushed people into an independent orbit, after which he believed that he had employees who were doing this and knew it better than him (although it was possible to know better than A.N. only the details, but not the general ideas).

About non-mathematical communication with A.N. I can only tell you individual episodes, individual touches. I remember such a characteristic episode. We were together in Hungary at a congress on information theory. We received 1300 forints. Since we were given food and drink there, each of us had a large sum of money. And the question arose: what to buy? A.N. immediately asked about the bookstore. We came there. He immediately saw the book figure by Michelangelo, which cost 1200 forints, bought it, spending everything he had, and said: “And you’ll already buy me a ticket for the tramway.”

I first became acquainted with old Russian cities through A.N. Sometimes we visited provincial Russian cities, in the outback. A.N. knew a lot about each city, and he always had a definite goal: he knew that here you need to see such and such a wall or tower. Apparently, in memory of these poses, A.N. gave me the book “Ancient Russian Cities” for my tollstudio fortieth birthday.

I have never seen A.N. desired. He never, in any form, sought sympathy for himself. And to the question: “How do you feel about all honor and respect?” - A.N. answered simply: “Calm down.”
