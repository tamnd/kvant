8. Variable table rows from top to bottom, columns from left to right. Let us denote by $o_{i,k}^j$ the operation of adding the $i$th row to the $j$th column and subtracting this row from the $k$th column.
Applying the operation $o_{i,1}^1, o_{i,2}^2, \dots, o_{n,n-1}^1$ successively to this table, we get a table in which the number in the $i$th row and $j$th column is equal to zero, and the remaining numbers not in the last row or in the last column have not changed.
Applying also the sequence of operations for all $i=1, 2, \dots, n-1$ and $j=1, 2, \dots, n-1$, we get a table in which non-zero numbers can only appear in the last row or last column. But since the sum of numbers in any row and in any column is equal to zero (this is a conservation property when applying operations), then in the last row and in the last column there is also zero.
