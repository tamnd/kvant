If the fundamental scheme of the formation of cyclics and anticyclics is quite simple, then a detailed description of their movements is poorly developed, primarily because too many factors determine this movement. Until now, we have considered an idealized atmosphere, and now we will consider the possibility of the influence of materials on the general cyclulation of the atmosphere. Continents are higher than the oceans, and high mountain ranges especially change the movement of the atmosphere. In addition, the average temperatures of materials and oceans differ greatly, since the oceans are more inert, they warm up more slowly, but also cool more slowly. figure 7 presents the average annual and average surface temperature at latitude 55° in the northern hemisphere for different periods of the year. The difference in temperatures between materials and the ocean in winter is only slightly less than the difference between the temperatures of polar and tropical regions. This thermal deficiency and the influence of the relief lead to the fact that large vortices stop moving with the wave and the vortex may not change its position for several weeks. This state of the atmosphere is called a “blocking situation” and leads to numerous catastrophic consequences, primarily droughts.

Greenhouse effect and nuclear winter

The external factors that determine the movement of the atmosphere practically do not change over time, but changes occurring in the atmosphere, primarily caused by human influence, can fundamentally change the total effect of these factors. Let's look at this using the example of the so-called greenhouse effect and the phenomenon of “nuclear winter”.
