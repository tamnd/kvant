Determine the average power $P$ released on the resistor at low frequency of the bulb

Currently, in connection with the discovery of the phenomenon of high-temperature superconductivity, the question of creating direct current transfer lines without energy losses due to Joule heat is being studied. It is proposed to use a coaxial cable for direct current transmission, consisting of an inner cylindrical core and an outer cylindrical shell made of a superconductor. The electric and magnetic fields in such a system are depicted at figure 8. It is known that the inductive magnetic field at the surface of a superconductor cannot exceed a certain value $B_{\max}$, otherwise superconductivity is destroyed, and the electric field strength should not exceed $E_{\max}$, otherwise an electrical breakdown of the insulating layer of the cable will occur. Determine how many times the maximum direct current power transmitted through such a cable changes if the diameters of the inner and outer sheaths are doubled. What is the maximum power that can be transmitted through a cable with sheath diameters $D=8$ cm and $d=3$ cm, if $E_{\max}=20$ kV/cm and $B_{\max}=5 \cdot 10^{-2}$ T? Note: the inductive magnetic field in the space between the cylindrical conductors coincides with the field of a straight conductor with current $I$ and is equal to $B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi r)$, where $\mu_0=4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ H/A}^2$ is the magnetic constant.
