25 years old
Novosibirsk FMS

In March 1962, in many corners of Siberia and other regions of the country, plaques appeared with tasks for the correspondence round of the first All-Siberian Olympiad. A certain period of time was given to solve the problems, by the end of which letters with solutions had to be sent to the address of the Olympiad Committee in Novosibirsk, in Akademgorodok. In July, several hundred schoolchildren received invitations to the center area for the second round of the All-Siberian Olympiad, which was conducted by young researchers from Akademgorodok. And quite unexpectedly for many schoolchildren who performed well at the Olympiad, immediately after that they were collected and taken to Academgorodok, where the first Summer School of Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry (LS) began operating on July 10th. The first Summer School was the longest - more than 45 days, and perhaps this is one of the reasons why its 250 students consider their Summer School a bag full of impressions, their own various meetings. In the last few days, all LS students began to worry about the idea of ​​​​forming a physics and mathematics boarding school. The third and final round of the All-Siberian Olympiad was held, with all the LS schoolchildren having their own - and about a hundred schoolchildren, among whom

Many of the authors of the article received invitations to the non-existent Physics and Mathematics Institute.

The idea of ​​​​creating a physiomathematical school (FMS) was put forward by leading scientists of the country, and in Siberia - academicians M. A. Lavrentiev, S. L. Sobolev, S. A. Khristanovich, corresponding members of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Lyapunov, D. V. Shirkov and others. When creating the FMS, several goals were pursued:

firstly, to give schoolchildren the opportunity to develop their abilities, to use a large scientific center; This is especially important for schoolchildren from rural areas, isolated towns and small towns;

secondly, already from school a ground-level understanding of the specialty of research is given and thereby increasing the efficiency of training scientific personnel;

thirdly, to conduct a pedagogical experiment to reflect new teaching methods that better meet the requirements of our time.

In 1963, by decree of the Council of Ministers of the USSR, it was considered expedient to organize, as an experiment at some universities, specialized schoolchildren to interpret the physical and mathematical process. The first such school opened in January 1963 in Novosibirsk, and in the fall three more - in Moscow, Leningrad and Kyiv.

Despite the fact that the Novosibirsk Physics and Music School has much in common with other specialist schools, it also has significant differences. First of all, this is a huge territory in which the selection of students takes place: Siberia, the Far East, Central Asia and Kazakhstan. Representatives of over twenty nationalities study at the school every day - you can meet students from a small Yakut village, and from an Uzbek village, and from a Chukotka reindeer herding state farm. There are no competitive exams at the school; enrollment is conducted through the system of All-Union Olympids in mathematics, physics, and chemistry, and a special Committee has been organized to conduct enrollment at the Presidium of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences. He forms teams of researchers and graduate students who travel to the regions, regional and resource olympids of the Siberian zone, where they provide methodological assistance to local organizational education in conducting the olympids, and also conduct their own academics. The protocols are brought to Akademgorodok, and scientists, members of the Olympid Committee, select six from a small thousand schoolchildren and invite them to the Summer School. It is not necessarily the winners or accolades who invite, but those who have demonstrated the ability to judge, think, and draw conclusions. All other factors being equal, pre-preparatory education is reserved for students from villages and small towns, and children from families of workers and collective farmers.

During the first three weeks of August, Summer School participants listen to lectures by leading Siberian scientists, study with a teacher - young researchers, visit research institutes, and study. At the end of the LSH, the admissions committee enrolls 350-380 students at the boarding school in two different streams: two-year (in the ninth grade) and one-year (in the tenth grade).

In the physics and mathematics school, the organization of the educational process in advanced subjects (mathematics, physics, chemistry) is close to that of Kuzov. Each course consists of lectures, seminars, and laboratory work. The academic year is divided into two semesters, at the end of each of which written and oral exams are taken in mathematics and physics, and at the end of the tenth grade final exams for the high school course are held. A class in Physics and Mathematics ICAL consists of no more than thirty people, and in seminar classes in mathematics, physics, chemistry and in lessons in biology, foreign language and physical education, the class is divided into two groups. During lectures, two or three parallel classes are combined into streams. On Thursday, no more than two compulsory lessons are held, and the remaining time is provided for work in institutes, laboratories, special training camps and for independent work by scientists. In addition, many special courses are held in the afternoon and on other days.

The methodological management of the school is carried out by the Academic Council, which, according to a long tradition, is headed by the rector of NSU, currently corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Yu. L. Ershov. The council approves the composition of lecturers, assistants and educators, programs in specialized subjects, considers changes in the curriculum, and discusses issues of leading work at the school.

Programs for vocational disciplines are in-depth in comparison with general education school programs and are aimed at developing a scientific worldview, independent thinking, and research skills in teaching the exact sciences. This in no way entails a weakening of attention or, worse, neglect of humanities subjects. The opinions of schoolchildren and a comparison of grades suggest that the teacher’s “humanitarian” requirements are higher than in most schools from which they are close.

From the day of its foundation, the Novosibirsk Physics School was conceived as the first student in the system of training scientific personnel. A close connection with the university and scientific departments of the Academic Town is an integral and most important feature of the school. You can often hear that FMS students have a feeling of elitism. In principle, such a danger exists because many of our students in their home schools were leaders, “stars,” and some were almost considered “geniuses.” But moving to a school where the requirements are significantly higher, where everyone around is a “star,” in itself contributes to a revaluation of one’s own principles. And here the most important task of the teaching staff is to create an atmosphere at school in which respect for the successes of their friends, civic responsibility, communist rule, and an active life position would be fostered. A comprehensive educational program includes many aspects. School self-government, the Lenin test system are constantly being improved, work is regularly organized in the experimental farm of the Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR and many other rubles. Traditions play a big role in school. This is especially important because the student population is renewed by two-thirds every year. The work of the Komsomol department at the Leningrad School, which includes school graduates who entered the first year of the university, has become a tradition.
