International Olympiads

This summer, as usual, international school Olympiads in mathematics and physics were held.

These Olympiads will be discussed in detail in one of the next issues of the magazine, but for now we congratulate our Olympiads with Professor R. Potts. 58 countries took part in the Olympiad (of which 8 were represented by observers).

The Soviet team included
Serdey Berlov (Leningrad),
Dmitry Ivanov (Dolgoprudny, Moscow region),
Serdey Ivanov (Leningrad),
Dmitry Tulyakov (Zhdanov, Donetsk region),
Nikolay Filonov (Leningrad),
Yuri Khokhlov (Leningrad).

All team members are loaded with gold or silver medals. In the unofficial team standings, our team took first place (217 points out of 252 possible). The next three places were distributed as follows: China (201), Romania (201), Germany (174).

The problems proposed at the Olympiad were more difficult than those of the previous XXVIII IMO. Five of them (except the first) are traditionally included in the “Problem Book «Kvant» (M1131—M1135), and we present the conditions for the first problem here:

Two circles with a common center are given on the plane, the radii of which are respectively equal to r and R, r<R. Let P be a fixed point on the smaller circle and B a variable point on the larger circle. The line BP intersects the larger circle a second time at point C. The perpendicular ULAR l to the straight line BC, passing through point P, intersects the smaller circle a second time at point A (if l is a tangent to the circle, we assume that A=P).

a) Find the set of values ​​of the expression BC²+CA²+AB².

b) Find the set of midpoints of segments AB.

The XIX International Physics Olympiad was held from June 23 to July 2 in Austria, in the city of Bad Ischl. Schoolchildren from 27 countries came here.

Austria is known to the world for the names of both brilliant musicians, artists and posers, and no less brilliant physicists. Among them are X. Doppler, I. Loschmidt, J. Boltzmann, B. Hess, E. Schrödinger, E. Mach and others. It is probably no coincidence that the Olympiad emblem depicts the famous physicist “Mach cone”.
The USSR team included
Yuri Kravchenko (Moscow),
Alexander Mazurenko (Minsk),
Antoon Malkin (Leningrad),
Vadim Moroz (Leningrad),
Konstantin Penanen (Odeska).

The competition took place, as always, in two rounds. In the theoretical type, the student proposed three tasks, and in the experimental type, two. It must be said that all five tasks, in their content, significantly go beyond the scope of the basic physics course program at our secondary school, and in a number of tasks, beyond the scope of the elective physics course program.

How did Soviet schoolchildren cope with the proposed tasks? For the first problem they scored 41 points out of 50 (each problem was worth 10 points), for the second - 43 points, for the third - 25.5 points, for the fourth - 14 points and for the fifth - 32.13 points. According to the total points (155.63), the USSR team, in the unofficial standings, was second - after the Romanian team (166.75). The next three places were distributed as follows:
Hungary (153.25), Germany (152.26), China (148.25). According to personal results, all five schoolchildren received medals - medium or bronze.
