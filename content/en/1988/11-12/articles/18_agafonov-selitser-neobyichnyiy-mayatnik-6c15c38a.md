Unusual pendulum

Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
A. I. AGAFONOV,
Candidate of Pedagogical Sciences
S. I. SELICER

Among the various types of oscillations, self-oscillations occupy a special place. In mechanics, a typical example of an auto-oscillatory system is a pendulum in a clock. In electricity, a tube generator of continuous oscillations is widely known.

In many books on physics you can find a description of a simple, but very visual electrical device, one of the variants of such a system (Fig. 1). It is easy to make at home from scrap materials.

Spiral spring 1 is made of aluminum wire with a diameter of 1.8 mm, the coil diameter is 35-40 mm, and the entire length of the spring is approximately 140-180 mm. It can be wound using the glass cylinder of a regular fluorescent lamp. At the lower end of the spring there is an electrical contact 2 made of brass; You can also use a ready-made contact from a relay. Bottom contact 3 is made of brass (or copper) foil about 0.15 mm thick. The length of the curved (L-shaped) part of this contact can be adjusted using bolt 4, which clamps the brass strip (its dimensions in millimeters are indicated at figure 2).
