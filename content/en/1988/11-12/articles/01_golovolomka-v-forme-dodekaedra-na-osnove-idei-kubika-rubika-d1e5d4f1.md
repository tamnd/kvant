⟦folio 8⟧

Based on the hinge filled into the design of the banner Rubik's cube, puzzles were created in the form of a 4x4x4 and 5x5x5 cube, as well as Rubik's dominoes 3x3x2 and a number of others. The most beautiful of them is called the dodecahedron (Fig. 1), designed by the south-class Boris Miro. To “assemble” colors on grains, the same algorithms (in accordance with the terms) are suitable here as for the Rubik’s cube.
But a puzzle in the form of a 2x2x2 cube required a different hinge. After the hinge was found, this puzzle began to be produced not only in the form of a cube, but also in the form of a tetrahedron (Fig. 2), which required 4 corners from the cube. If you cut off all 8 corners, you get an octahedron. figure 3 shows an octahedron obtained by this operation from a “regular” Rubik’s cube, i.e. from a 3×3×3 cube. Here more hinges have been added at the vertices of the octahedron.
