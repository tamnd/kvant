Typically, atomic nuclei are drawn in the form of balls. But not all nuclei are balls, some of them are slightly elongated, others are solid, but in almost all known cases the deviation of their surface shapes from a sphere is small (no more than 10%).
The shape of the nucleus can be represented as follows. Let's cut out an ellipse from paper. An ellipse has two axes - major and minor. If you rotate an ellipse around the major axis, you get an elongated ellipse; if you rotate it around the minor axis, you get a solid ellipsid. A prolate ellipsoid has two identical minor axes and one major axis. A solid one has two identical major axes and one minor one.
What has been said about the shape of the nucleus can be sorted as follows: the nucleus has the shape of a sphere or ellipsoid of rotation, with A and B or A and C differing little (~10%) from each other.

In relatively recently discovered superunits, the A axis is significantly larger than the B (and C) axes.
