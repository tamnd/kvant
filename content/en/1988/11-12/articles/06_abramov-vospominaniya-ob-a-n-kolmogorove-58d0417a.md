Dakh A.N. made fantastic movements. A 30-40 km hike for us was an unattainable thing, a torment, and even at 60 years old he covered such distances easily.

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Professor of Moscow State University Vladimir Mikhailovich TIKHOMIROV recalls:

I met Andrei Nikolaevich in my 3rd year, in 1955. A.N. was then the dean of the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics, and I was the secretary of the Komsomol organization of my course. However, an incident occurred: one of my comrades, an ardent and proud man, could not bear the insult that an adult would inflict on me, and responded very rudely. I began to look into the question of the presence of my goods at the university. Then I came to A.N. and outlined the essence of the matter. A.N. said rather sharply that this, of course, is an ugly act that requires the most severe implementation. I tried to protect my friend and said that, of course, he was guilty, but all his comrades knew him as a good and capable person, and therefore he should not be excommunicated from the university. Andrei Nikolaevich repeated his words of implementation, but when it already seemed to me that the matter was completely lost, he suddenly said: “Well, we, of course, do not exclude him.” Andrei Nikolaevich always believed in a good beginning in a person. He supported many, and in almost all cases those whom he supported took their rightful place in science.

A.N. Never set me personal tasks. I participated in A.N.’s seminar. At it, A.N. proposed tasks to everyone. I found one of them very interesting. After I solved it, Andrei Nikolaevich began to discuss with me the prospects for further work. I proposed part of the problem, and A.N. approved of me, expressed his doubts about the other part, and proposed many of his own problems in the development of the entire topic. It was as if he opened the session and showed a separate perspective, where specific goals were seen and a path that had no end opened up.

One day A.N. told me: “You should not have an idea of ​​me as a person who knows only mathematics; I belong to those people who have their own opinion on more or less every issue.” I always knew that A.N. was a mathematician of exceptional breadth, but I could not even suspect to what extent his horizons were limitless in philosophy, economics, politics, geography, and in matters related to art and literature. At the same time, he was very independent, almost always unpredictable. In particular, in their passions. Somehow we were talking about the greatest writers of the 20th century. I thought about it and began to go through the most “simple” targets (in the mid-fifties), namely Gorky, Sholokhov, Faulkner, Rolland, Heminghusey, Remarque... Andrei Nikolaevich without hesitation named the works of A. France and T. Mann as the peaks of world literature of our century, and this was completely unexpected for me. And when it came to pozziia, A.N., also unpredictable for me, singled out the then 24-year-old Yevtushenko.

A.N. was a passionate and tireless skier, he was very fond of cross-country skiing. Once he asked to invite one of the strong skiers from our course to him to make a particularly long transition. In our course there was a student with the first youth rank, A.N.’s request was conveyed to him, he agreed with satisfaction. At first, our fellow student was frolicking, running away, waiting for A.N., then weil was nearby, the shore of strength, and in the end, A.N. simply carried his skis: on long ski trips (especially without much training), sometimes there comes a moment when a person can still somehow hobble on foot, but is no longer able to walk on skis.

I eat Nikolaevich when I was in a rural class. I read his brochure “On the Profession of Mathematics.”
It is written both clearly and clearly, so I remember my impressions and even individual tasks well.
And the first meeting took place in 1963, when A.N. presented certificates to the winners of the All-Russian Olympiad.

In the same year, the first summer mathematics school was organized in Krasnovidovo, and then the Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University. We, students of the very first graduating class, considered ourselves incredibly lucky: both at the summer school and at the Physics and Mechanics School, A.N. was our teacher. And he himself, apparently, had special feelings for the first 19 graduates of the Physics and Mechanics School - many years later he had mercy on everyone.

During my student years, while studying as a mexmate at Moscow State University, I worked at a boarding school as a mathematics teacher and quite often met with A.N. But it was still a complete surprise when, before placement, he invited me to take a professional interest in school problems and become his graduate student. Naturally, I immediately agreed. The first visit to Komarovka was also possible.

I completed my first scientific work at the university under the guidance of V. M. Tikhomirov. So, to a certain extent, I was familiar with the character of A.N.’s school, and this had a great influence.

Although I had to work on a dissertation in education, the topic required mainly thinking in the field of mathematics. A. N. briefly posed the problem: he gave a list of the axioms of the included planometry, which he laid as the basis for the school geometry course, and said that on their basis it was necessary to give an accurate statement of geometry, to study the axiomat. There were no instructions (for example, how to build a sequence of theorems) or tips. A.N.
