It darkened even more. If you approach such a flat, you will notice that the snow on the flat is saturated with water.

The above can be formulated in the language of physics, using certain albeby and optical constants of water and snow.

Albebo of a matte surface is the total, or, as they say, integral, coefficient of reflection or scattering of light, i.e., the ratio of the reflected (or scattered) light flux to the incident one. Pure snow almost completely scatters the light falling on it. The albebo of snow is usually about 0.9, and sometimes (for the purest snow) it even reaches a value of 0.98.

Snow covered or washed away with water is no longer a matte surface, but smooth in the optical sense. Such a surface reflects light according to the law of geometric optics known to us: a small part of the incident light flux is reflected from the smooth surface of water (Fig. 2). With normal padding, the coefficient of light reflection from the water surface is close to 2%.

Qualitatively, what happens with a dark glass is similar to a simple experiment when a little clean water is poured into a glass of granulated sugar: with suitable lighting, the contents of the glass immediately darken.

Do not think that the formation of relief on a snow or ice surface is associated only with the presence of dirt. Let's imagine that for some reason the surface of pure snow has become a little uneven, with protrusions or depressions, even small ones. Then different parts of the surface are inclined differently in relation to the incident solar rays. Therefore, the surface of the snow in different places will not be equally illuminated by the sun. And if such or the sublimation of snow is determined mainly by the level of solar radiation, and not by other factors (say, a warm wind), then the sharper snow protrusions directed towards the sun will “bleed off” under the sun more slowly than the flat holes near the protrusions, i.e. the relief of the snow surface will become more pronounced than it was originally. Snow cones or sharp tongues form on clean snow. This usually happens when the sun is shining brightly, but there is no warm wind. Such conditions often occur high in the mountains, and in the spring, under the bright sun, amazingly beautiful ice teeth, called calgospores, “grow” on the glaciers, reaching heights of about a meter.

This type of surface relief formation is familiar to specialists in radiation physics of polymers. In polymer films irradiated with hard ultraviolet radiation, the effect of photo-etching of the surface is observed, and photo-etching of the first surface of smooth surfaces is accompanied by the growth of free “calgospores” - uneven heights, sometimes of a finite shape (a micrograph of such a surface is placed on the first page of the cover). Of course, for polymers the scale is smaller: the size of irregularities is on the order of micrometers. In this case, as for snow, there is radiation instability of the solid phase boundary.
