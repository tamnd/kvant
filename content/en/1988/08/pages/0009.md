new Until the very end, only Landdu did not understand. He was “the dumbest of all” and, when everyone already considered themselves completely satisfied, he continued to ask the questions that seemed most irrelevant to the essence of the matter. And as a result, it turned out that it was wrong. But at the same time, Day always had great respect for other people's work. The speaker who began with the words “Here, I took the article, it turned out to be completely nonsense,” received a scolding: “Don’t make a fool of the author, since he is dealing with this issue, and you, the speaker, are not!”

Landdu also really disliked lazy people. He didn’t get up very early and in the morning, at about eleven o’clock, he came to the student’s room and started a conversation on general topics. But all this was quite dangerous. If you didn’t move on to scientific topics, then he either started whining and left, or got angry that the conversation was going in vain, and then he dropped in with some naughty hints... These hints were often not harmless, but they were always very witty.

M.I. - I would like to say this. Quite recently, in Boris Yampolsky’s novel “Moskovskaya Street,” I read a passage where the hero reflects, “... how everything could have turned out... if at the very beginning of your life you met a person who knows everything, and at the very beginning of your journey taught you to toughen up and read the best books, and value time, and not waste a single minute on nonsense, on idleness, on bedlam, and to appreciate what is truly valuable, this mythical, never-before-seen, a kind and great man, a teacher who, in fact, has never been met by anyone in his life, and everyone reached everything on their own and made all the mistakes.” But it seems to me that there is a large group of people (including all of us who came to visit you today) who have been extraordinarily lucky in life. Because we had a Teacher who taught us a lot. Now it’s hard to convey - we are all already old, mature. We can no longer explain what role the conversations with Landdu played.

In conclusion, a remark from Nikolai Vasilyevich Karlov, rector of MIPT.

— If possible, I would like to add a few words. I studied physics from forty-seven to fifty-first years, and the general course in physics - from the first year - was taught by Landd and Kapitsa. I was the first to arrange a lecture for Landd and, admitting, I immediately thought that, apparently, the field was unsuccessful. The level was such that gifted young people who valued themselves highly, who participated in optimids more often, who went through two difficult rounds of exams, were mistaken. However, gradually we realized that we were given a happy opportunity to see physics through the eyes of this remarkable scientist, with his depth of understanding, wide scope, unexpected comparison. Repeatedly, in different laboratories around the world - from Stanford University to the École Normale in Paris - I heard from colleagues, students, and graduate students words about how lucky we are that we have such a course - the course of Landdu and Lifshtz. And the university should be proud that among its professors were Lev Davidovich Landdu.
