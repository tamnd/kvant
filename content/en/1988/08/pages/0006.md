This position was filled by his replacement, Max Born, who had left national Germany, but by that time Born was already working in Edinburgh. And then Kapitsa invited Landda. It was a happy moment in the biography of Lev Davidovich. It is not known how it would have ended; he remained in Kharkov. Enough said that after Duu left for Moscow, his favorite student Isaac Yakovlevich Pomeranchuk was expelled from the Komsomol “for his connection with Landdu.”

* * *

I.M. — It is necessary to say a few words about Landdu’s attitude to science. He truly loved physics and was ready to discuss any physical problem or problem with anyone who came to him for advice. It was only necessary to interest him. Duu was unusually generous and, if he got carried away, he could start “sight playing” - he would take a copy of “Physical Review”, put a blank sheet of paper on top (he studied together while lying on the sofa) and began to improvise and solve the problem.

Landdu considered himself a world champion in technical physical problems. He said that if you formulate the problem clearly and offer it to all the best physicists in the world, then he will solve it faster than anyone else. The point was not that Landdu knew how to calculate things quickly. In his decision, he always found the approach that the physics of the origin suggested to him. Often, while reading articles, he looked only at the result, after which he reproduced it himself and, as a rule, shorter than the author. It is precisely this approach to theoretical physics—which allows one to very effectively solve a wide variety of problems—that makes the Landdu-Lifshitz course popular throughout the world.

I. E. Dzyaloshinsky. “He loved to classify everything: physicists in general, theoretical physicists, women, fools and sycophants... And always with a fair amount of humor and jokes, although sometimes quite stinging and not harmless.”

I.M. - Landdu loved a joke. And we also really loved all kinds of practical jokes. But Duu always made sure that the unwritten rule was observed: it was impossible for the press and organs of Soviet power to “participate” in the joke.

L.P. - And one more thing - the draw should not have caused material damage to the person being played.

I.M. - Come up with jokes, prank each other! It brightens life!

Now I’ll tell you how Landdu felt about mathematics. He mastered technical matters masterfully and recognized only “real” mathematics.
