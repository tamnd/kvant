Assignments for the correspondence collective competition TYuF-XI
Curdl is hunted by researchers S. Lem

1. “Come up with it yourself.” Design and manufacture an installation to visually demonstrate the wave properties and laws of sound propagation in air.

2. "Noon". Can the moment in the middle of the time interval from sunrise to sunset be called noon? Using a calendar, you can easily verify that this moment during the year “floats” relative to a certain point in time. Explain the reason for this effect.

3. "Tide" Estimate the height of the tide in the Black Sea on December 12, 1988.

4. "Puddle and wind." Measure the parameters of waves generated by wind in a shallow body of water. Explore the dependence of wavelength on wind speed.

5. "Chladni Figures". Explore the Chladni figures for the disk and square.

6. "BUBBLE". What determines the “lifetime” of a mouse bladder? Why does it burst and how does it happen?

7. "Leskov." Explain how the forger Maroy shortened the English bolts (Leskov N.S. “The Sealed Angel”).

8. "Metro". Suggest methods and measure the speed of an electric metro train in the middle of the stretch between the Universitet and Proskekt Vernadskogo stations (this is for Muscovites). The same goes for the bus you are traveling on, if there are no reliable distance indicators along the route.

9. "Astronaut". What is the maximum travel length an astronaut can expect to travel over 50 years of flight?

10. "Atmosphere of the Moon". Imagine that you managed to create an Earth-like atmosphere on the Moon. Describe its parameters and properties. How quickly will she “lose weight” and how to maintain such an atmosphere?

11. "Champignon". Surprisingly, sometimes mushrooms and even grass disappear through the thickness of the asphalt. Explain these phenomena.

12. "Weather forecast". “The barometer is falling - bad weather” - why is this statement most often true?

13. "Photograph" Light up your teeth with a fact sheet and observe their glow in the dark (to do this, you need to peel off the fact sheet, sew up your eyes well and open them immediately after the flash). Explain and investigate the phenomenon of afterglow.

14. "Tribolominescence." How much sugar will you need to read the last (in a few words) from your predecessor in a dark dungeon? You've run out of matches, and you've already thrown away your flashlights.

15. "Electron". An electron having a speed of $v = 3 \cdot 10^5$ m/s travels with the applied parameter $d$ mismo of a metal ball with a radius of 1 cm. The charge of the ball changes with time according to the law $q(t) = q_0 \cos \omega t$, where $q_0 = 10^{-8}$ C, $\omega = 10^8$ rad/s. Plot the dependence of the electron scattering angle $\varphi$ on the applied parameter $d$. Could this effect be used to explain Compton scattering?

16. "Information". How many bits of information did you get from reading the correspondence competition? How many bits of information do you get from looking at a geographic map that is one page of text long?

17. "Carlson." How much jam should Carlson eat in order not to lose weight during the flight? (Traditionally, task No. 17 has a comic connotation).

The assignments were prepared by members of the Physics Faculty of Moscow State University: V.V. Bradinsky, P.V. Elyutin, A.N. Korotkov, A.Yu. Kuseenko, E.N. Yunosov.

For participants and organizers of Tours

The tournament for young physicists is a collective state of high school students in the ability to solve complex physical problems, make sure to design and defend their solutions, and participate in scientific discussions.

TYuF - improvement of schoolchildren, in which the school is presented with theoretical and experimental research. The conditions of the problem are formulated as briefly as possible, without indicating what exists in a given situation and what can be neglected. Therefore, TUF participants themselves must choose a model for a given problem, make the necessary assumptions, examine the resulting solution, in a word, read through all the necessary stages of service scientific work. Each task has a name that enhances its emotional perception. The named, as a rule, includes a keyword that defines the range of objects under consideration or their properties. Sometimes the naming contains a hint to solve the problem.
