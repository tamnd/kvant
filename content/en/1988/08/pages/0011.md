ABOUT DISPERSION
or How to change the fat content of milk

A. A. KREMER

At all times, milk has been one of the main human foods. All components contained in milk - fats, proteins, sugars, vitamins - are perfectly absorbed by the body. However, it is recognized that the main nutritional value of milk is determined by fat. That’s why a carton of 6% milk costs more than a carton that says “3.5% fat.” But how did these numbers appear on the package? How is the percentage of fat in milk determined?

Chemical methods have been developed for this purpose. They involve the use of concentrated acids and other aggressive reagents, and with one analysis - no more or less - you get. And the total need for such analyzes is more than half a billion (!) per year. At such a scale, it is no longer possible to do without physical methods with their simple and high productivity.

The word "milky" has always been synonymous with the word "cloudy": milk of fog, milky glass, etc. A turbid medium is a medium that is opaque to light, not due to absorption (such as a sheet of metal), but due to scattering. The cloudiness of milk is mainly due to the presence of fat, which is contained in the form of a huge number of balls, the diameter of which varies from 0.5 to 10 microns. The fattier the milk, the more fat globules it contains, the less transparent it is. This means that by changing the proportion of milk, you can determine its fat content. This is just an idea for now. We must not forget that we are going to compete with chemical methods that allow us to change the fat content of milk with an accuracy of 0.1%.
