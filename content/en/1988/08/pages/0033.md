F1129. A coil shaped like a narrow flask (Fig. 3) is placed on a cylindrical permanent magnet near its pole. If the coil is moved so that it performs harmonic oscillations along the $OO'$ axis with an amplitude of $A=1$ mm (which is much smaller than the dimensions of the magnet and coil) and a frequency of $f=1000$ Hz, then an inductive emf is induced in it. The proper part of the oscillation contour is equal to $\omega_0$. What will be the natural frequency if a free point of mass $m$ and charge $q$ is placed between the plates of a capacitor? There is no force of gravity. Edge effects and the power of the "electrostatic image" are transformed.

F1131. Determining the wind speed in a tornado using conventional meteorological instruments is difficult (since the tornado is small in size and moves) and unsafe. It is proposed to change it from a distance using a portable radiator (since there is a lot of dust and small objects inside the tornado, it reflects radio waves). The radar emits radio waves at a frequency of $f_0=10^{10}$ Hz. The spectrum of the signal reflected from the tornado is shown in figure 4 ($\Delta f=f-f_0$). Find the maximum wind speed in Smere.

D. A. Kuptsov

F1132. It has recently been established that the extinction of animal species on Earth is particularly intense during periodic repeating periods of time of long $T=6,2$ million years. This pattern is explained by a hypothesis suggesting the existence of Nemesis stars, which is a satellite of the Sun. This faint and therefore invisible star moves in an orbit centered within the so-called “Oort Belt,” which contains a supply of comets (Fig. 5). By disturbing the movement of comets, Nemesis causes “comet rain” on Earth, the duration of which practically coincides with the time Nemesis remains inside the Oort belt.

Determine the semimajor axis $a$ of Nemesis's orbit and its period of revolution around the Sun, if the perihelion of the orbit is assumed to be at a distance of 0.15a from the Sun. The area of ​​the ellipse is $S=\pi ab$, where $a$ and $b$ are the resulting ellipse.

V. E. Belonuchkin
