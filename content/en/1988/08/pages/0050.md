Simple experiments with boiling water

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
I. I. MAZIN

An empty glass, a pan of water, a thermos, a kettle, a stove and... desire - that's all you may need to conduct experiments.

Why is water drawn into the glass?

Take an ordinary saucepan and pour some water into it (2-3 centimeters in height). Place an empty glass in the pan, bottom up. Place the pan on the stove, bring the water to a boil and let it simmer for five minutes. Now it will turn off the tile. Soon you will see water begin to be sucked into the glass, rising higher and higher until it fills most of it.

Let's try to oblige what we saw.

What causes water from a pan to rise into a glass? It is clear that only atmospheric pressure (surrounding air pressure) can do this. This means that the air pressure inside the glass is less than atmospheric pressure. How long? It is not difficult to estimate the pressure difference ($\Delta p$) outside and inside the glass - it is exactly equal to the hydrostatic pressure of water in the glass after completion of the experiment. Let us assume that the height of the water table is $h \approx 10$ cm, the density of water is $\rho = 10^3$ kg/m$^3$, the acceleration of free fall is $g = 10$ m/s$^2$, then

$$\Delta p = \rho gh \approx 10^3 \text{ Pa} \approx 0.01 \text{ atm.}$$
