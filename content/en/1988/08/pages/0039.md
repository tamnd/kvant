Ruzhnosti. It is important here that diametrically opposite points have different colors - below we will take advantage of the fact that even under this condition the game is losing for a beginner.

Let $n$ be odd (then there are two adjacent black points on the circle, numbered 1 and $n$). With $n=4k+1$, the 1st player will ensure a win if on the first move he connects, for example, the black dots with numbers 1 and 3 (Fig. 2), thereby reducing to a similar game with $n=4(k-1)+2$, where the beginner loses. The 1st player has a similar, but even more cunning move with $n=4k+3$. Here he can cut off three points so that they maintain symmetry around the axis - to do this, it is enough to connect the black points with numbers $2k+1$ and $2k+3$ (Fig. 3).

If we make sure to arrange the remaining $4k$ points so that they are located at equal distances around the circle, then the diametrically opposite points will be of different colors (although the white and black points do not alternate), and therefore the beginner here (i.e., the 2nd player) loses.

More subtle messages are required in the version of the game in which the player who makes the last move loses. We leave the analysis of this option to the readers.

V. G. Chvanov

M1100*. On the bank of a straight river there are logs, one of which makes an angle less than $45^\circ$ with the bank lines (logs are segments that do not intersect each other; their number is finite). Prove that at least one of the logs can be rolled into the river without affecting the others. (The log can only be rolled in a direction perpendicular to it, without turning.)

Let the coast be the upper half-plane and the coast line be the horizontal straight line $b$. Consider some breve $l$ (which makes an angle less than $45^\circ$ with the line $b$). Let us draw rays from its ends that make angles along $45^\circ$ with straight line $b$: from the right end - in the direction $p$ "right - up", from the left end - in the direction $q$ "left - up" (Fig. 1), and consider the set of points lying in the face of the broken line, consisting of the log $l$ and these two rays. It is clear that when rolling out any breve that satisfies the conditions of the problem and does not have common points with this set, we cannot define the breve $l$.

It is convenient to consider the somewhat larger sets associated with the log $l$ - let’s put it on the ground by the “shadow $l$” and denote it $U_l$ (Fig. 2): its boundary is a broken line that goes from the right end of the log $l$ in the direction $p$; then, if the broken line rests on the inner point of some log - along this log to the right, from its right end - again in the direction $p$, etc., and also similarly - at the left end of the log $l$ (here the segments of the broken line go, through, along the logs in the direction $q$). Obviously, for different beams $l_1$ and $l_2$ their topics do not coincide. Moreover, if $l_1 \subset U_{l_2}$ (l$_1$ lies in the shadow of $l_2$), then $U_{l_1} \subset U_{l_2}$. Now it is clear that in a chain of logs $l_1$, $l_2$, $l_3$, ..., in which each log prevents the previous one from rolling out, no log will appear twice: for it $U_{l_1} \subset U_{l_2} \subset U_{l_3}$...
