Now let's give a more rigorous solution. Let us find the kinetic energy of an empty bottle of mass $m$, which rolls along a horizontal surface without slipping at a speed of $v$. For simplicity, we replace the bottle with an empty cylinder and assume that all its mass is concentrated in thin walls.

All points of the cylinder participate in a complex motion: this is translational motion with the speed of the center of mass $v$ and rotation with a linear speed $v$ in the absence system associated with the center of mass.

Let's consider a small section of the “rim” with mass $\Delta m_i$. Its speed is the sum of the speed $\vec{v}_i$ in the center of mass system and the speed $\vec{v}$ of the center of mass itself (see figure). The kinetic energy of this section is equal to

$$\Delta E_{ki} = \frac{\Delta m_i(|\vec{v}_i + \vec{v}|)^2}{2} = \frac{\Delta m_i(v_i^2 + v^2 + 2v_i v \cos \alpha_i)}{2},$$

where the angle $\alpha_i$ for different sections is taken from 0 to 2l. The total kinetic energy of the cylinder consists of the kinetic energies of all its sections:

$$E_k = \sum_i \Delta E_{ki} = \sum_i \frac{\Delta m_i(v_i^2 + v^2)}{2} = \sum_i \Delta m_i v^2 = mv^2$$

(here we taught that for all sections $v_i = v$, and cos $\alpha_i$ takes all possible values ​​- both positive and negative, so $\sum_i \cos \alpha_i = 0.$)

If a cylinder rolls down an inclined plane, then, having descended from a height $h$, it will acquire a speed $v$, which can be found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$mgh = mv^2,$$

And

$$v = \sqrt{gh}.$$

From this it can be seen that this speed is $\sqrt{2}$ times less than the speed for the case of rolling without rotation. This is due to the fact that the potential energy of the cylinder is converted into kinetic energy of both translational and rotational motion.

When there is water inside the cylinder (our case of a bottle with water), which, due to its slight rotation, is practically not involved in rotation, the total kinetic energy of a uniformly rolling cylinder with mass $m$ filled with water with mass $M$ will be equal to

$$E_k = mv^2 + \frac{Mv^2}{2} = \left( m + \frac{M}{2} \right)v^2.$$
