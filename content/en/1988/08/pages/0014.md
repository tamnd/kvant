On September 26, 1951, residents of Western Europe were shocked by a fantastic sight: the Sun had a deep ultramarine color, and in the evening the Moon was the same color.

Even 200 years ago this phenomenon would have been perceived as a terrible omen. You and I, armed with scattering theories, immediately understand that we observe similar scattering of sunlight. But on what? The culprit was soon found. The wind brought a huge cloud of tree tar droplets from Canada, rising into the air due to severe forest fires in Alberta. The droplet sizes and parameter $K$ turned out to be such that the droplets scattered similarly.

Let the milk be poured into a bowl and let the light fall on the wall of the bowl perpendicularly. $$\Delta \Phi_2 = QSE_1 = Q \pi r^2 E_1.$$
