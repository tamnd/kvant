⟦folio 3⟧

I. M. Khalatnikov. — Lev Davidovich Landdu was born in 1908 in Baku. He showed interest and ability in exact sciences very early. Now everyone knows about this, and Dai himself said this: “I learned to differentiate at the age of thirteen, but I don’t remember a time when I couldn’t differentiate.” Just at the age of thirteen, he graduated from school and entered the Economic Technical Institute. He studied there for a year, and in 1922 he became a student at Baku University, in two faculties at once - physics, mathematics and chemistry. Soon the rector recommended a replacement student at Leningrad University. In 1927, at the age of nineteen, Dai graduated from Leningrad State University. He began working at the Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute, which was then headed by Abram Fedorovich Yuffe, “Papa Yuffe,” the father of Soviet physics. In 1929, among the best graduate students of the institute, Landdu was sent abroad and visited the largest European centers of theoretical physics. He also visited Cambridge, where Pyotr Leonidovich Kapitsa was then working. Nine years later, in a difficult year for Landdu, Kapitsa will come to his aid, at the risk, perhaps even of his life.

We did not see Dai abroad, but we heard stories from those who were close to him at that time. They said that he wore a red jacket...

L. P. Pitaevsky. — He was, as he himself said, of extreme left-wing convictions...

I.M. - Yes, he liked to imitate bourgeois confusion, and in this he was somewhat like Mayakovsky. He said that this family should be divorced, and that one, on the contrary, should be formed. But everyone there loved him.

At this time, it was decided to create new physical centers in the country. This is how physical and technical institutes arose in Kharkov, Tomsk and Dnepropetrovsk. In 1932, Landdu left for Kharkov. The power of intellect, the commitment of this personality, made a huge impression on the students. He was a substitute lecturer - and naturally everyone wanted to become theoretical and work with Landdu. So he decided to build a small “fence” around himself and came up with a theoretical minimum. Only those who managed to pass all the exams of this minimum received the right to work with Dai. He treated the upbringing of young people with unusual sincerity and believed that if someone spent so much work and was able to pass all the minimum exams, then it was his duty (duty!) to take care of such a person. But his care had nothing to do with petty care or comprehensive patronage. Landdu helped the student grow into a master, “spread his wings.”

Duu was a natural teacher. Teaching was a convenience for him. Therefore, he begins to think about writing a course in theoretical physics that the future could study theoretically. The Znamentiy “Course of Theoretical Physics” by Landdu and Lifshtz originated, thus, in Kharkov.

It must be said that Landdu had a lot of character and a sharp tongue. Therefore, he not only made friends, but also made enemies in the most thorough manner. Times were bad. An anonymous domos could deprive a pure person of his name, his freedom, and his life. This kind of food did not happen with Landdu either. In 1937, he headed the department of theoretical physics at Kharkov University and headed the theoretical department of the Ukrainian Physico-Technical Institute (UPTI). Suddenly - without any responsibilities - he was fired from the university. The clouds over Duu's head were thickening. And then his entire department submitted their resignations. This was an extremely brave and dangerous step. It was treated server-side. People's Commissar of Education of Ukraine V.P. Zatonsky invited everyone to Kyiv and promised that Landdu would be returned. This is how the friendship and love of his employees for Duu manifested itself.
