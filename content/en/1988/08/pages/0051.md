Let the volume of the glass be $V=200$ cm$^3$, the initial temperature (before heating) $T_1=300$ K, the final temperature $T_2=373$ K. Let the atmospheric pressure be $p=1$att≈$10^5$ Pa. From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation we find how much air remains in the glass:

$$pV = \frac{m_1}{M}RT_1,$$

$$pV = \frac{m_2}{M}RT_2,$$

where

$$\frac{m_2}{m_1} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \approx 0.8.$$

Thus, after cooling the hot air to its original temperature, it will occupy approximately 80%, and the rising water will occupy the remaining 20% ​​of the volume of the glass. But we saw that water filled large halves of the glass! This means that we have explained, at best, only a third of the effect. If we also take into account that the rise of water occurred in literally a few seconds, and during this time the air could not have time to cool down to room temperature, then we are forced to completely abandon the proposed explanation.

Where is the error in our reasoning? We were probably mistaken in believing that the glass contained only air, forgetting about water vapor. But during the five minutes of rapid boiling water, water vapor continuously entered the glass, which mixed with the air and tried to displace it. We can say that at the moment when we turned off the stove, there was mainly not air, but water vapor inside the glass, and in a saturated state. As it is left, the saturated vapor pressure decreases, and very sharply (see figure). In order for the pressure to drop by 0.01 atm, it is enough to cool the steam by only 0.3 degrees. It is clear that such cooling can occur almost instantly.

Moreover, as experience shows, if water is boiled long enough, then after cooling it will fill almost the entire glass. There are essentially no restrictions on the height of a column of water - after all, a pressure of 1 atm is created by a water column 10 m high.

The question arises: will the required amount of water have time to evaporate in 5 minutes? Let's try to answer. Of course, the rate of evaporation depends on the power of the stove, the size of the pan, etc., so we will use specific numbers obtained empirically. In our case, only a 1 cm layer of water evaporates from the pan in about 30 minutes. Then, in 5 minutes, $m \approx 3$ g of water will evaporate from a glass area of ​​≈20 cm$^2$. At a temperature of $T=373$ K and a pressure of $p=1$ atm, the mass of saturated steam would also occupy the volume

$$V = \frac{m}{M}RT_p \approx 5 \text{ л!}$$

So, if we assume that water vapor is uniformly mixed from the air, then it turns out that at the end of the experiment only $(0,2 \text{ л}/5 \text{ л}) \cdot 100 \% = 4 \%$ of the volume of the glass will be filled with gas, and the remaining 96% will be filled with water. And such a result can really be observed.
