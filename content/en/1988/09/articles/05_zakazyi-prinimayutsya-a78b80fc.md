3) Breguet's formula, apparently, is not known to every schoolchild. Therefore, we present its conclusion.

With uniform horizontal flight, as already mentioned, $F_x v = N \eta_{\text{дв}}$. Let's express $F_x$ through lift $F_y = mg$ and aerodynamic quality $K$:

$$\frac{mgv}{K} = N \eta_{\text{дв}}.$$

The value $N \eta_{\text{дв}}$ is power, or the second work of the engine against resistance forces. Let us assume that per unit time the engine consumes the same mass of fuel $\mu$ ($[\mu] = \text{kr/c}$). Then in time $dt$ the fuel mass will change by $dm = -\mu dt$ kg. Divide both sides of the equality (*) by $\mu = -dm/dt$:

$$-\frac{mgv \cdot dt}{K \cdot dm} = \frac{N \eta_{\text{дв}}}{\mu}.$$

No matter $v \cdot dt = dx$ — movement in the horizontal direction. This means that the equation describing the change in mass along the path, passable in horizontal flight, has the form

$$dx = -\frac{KN \eta_{\text{дв}}}{\mu g} \frac{dm}{m}.$$

Intercalating this equation from the starting point ($x = 0, m = m_0$) to the end of the flight ($x = L, m = m_0 - m_{\text{rop}}$), we obtain:

$$L = -\frac{K_3 N}{\mu g} \ln \left(1 - \frac{m_{\text{rop}}}{m_0}\right) = -\frac{K_3 N}{\mu g} \frac{\lg(1 - \bar{m}_{\text{rop}})}{\lg e}.$$

Now $\mu g/N = C_y$ remains - and we get Breguet’s formula.

4) Let us explain this paragraph as follows. The change in kinetic energy of the mass of air thrown out per unit time is equal to

$$N = m_s \left(\frac{v_2^2}{2} - 0\right) = m_s \frac{v_2^2}{2}.$$

(We neglect the change in the potential energy of this mass.) On the other hand, we can consider the lifting body (for example, a helicopter rotor) as a fan that has overcome the pressure difference created by the lifting force. This pressure difference passes through the “sucked” air from above at a speed of $v_1$, which requires a power of $N = Gv_1$.

Considering that $G = m_s v_2$, from the equality of two expressions for power we obtain:

$$m_s \frac{v_2^2}{2} = m_s v_2 v_1,$$

or $v_2 = 2v_1$.

Looking ahead, let us explain how the power required to maintain a unit of weight $-N/G$ is found. From the equality $G = 2\varrho s v_1^2$ we express $v_1$: $v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{G}{2\varrho s}}$. Substituting this $v_1$ into the equality $N = Gv_1$, we find the ratio $N/G$:

$$\frac{N}{G} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\varrho}} \sqrt{\frac{G}{s}}.$$
