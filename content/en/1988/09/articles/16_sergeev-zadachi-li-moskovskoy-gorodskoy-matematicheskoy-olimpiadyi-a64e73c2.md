Task LI of the Moscow City Mathematical Olympiad

On February 28, 1988, the final round of the LI Moscow City Mathematical Olympiad took place at Moscow State University. 654 schoolchildren in grades 7–10 took part in it. Below is a task of this type (4 hours were allotted to solve them).

7th grade
1. Prove that for prime $p > 7$ the number $p^4 - 1$ is divisible by 240.
2. At the midpoints of edges $AB$ and $B'C'$ of cube $ABCDA'B'C'D'$ points $M$ and $P$ are taken. Draw on face $BCC'B'$ all points whose shortest distances along the surface of the cube to points $M$ and $P$ are equal.
3. Using calipers and a ruler, draw a straight line parallel to this point through this point. A caliper is a tool similar to a compass, but has two needles at the ends. It allows you to transfer equal distances, but does not allow you to draw (scratch) circles, arcs of circles and make serifs.
4. 20 telephones are connected by wires so that each wire connects two telephones, each pair of telephones is connected by no more than one wire, and no more than two wires come from each telephone. You need to paint the wires (each wire entirely single red) so that no more than two wires come from each telephone. What is the smallest number of colors sufficient for such shading?
8th grade
1. On a string of four numbers 1, 9, 8, 8, we perform the following operation: between each two adjacent numbers in the total number, which will be obtained by subtracting the left number from the right. We will perform the same operation on a new line, etc. Find the sum of the numbers in the line that will be obtained after one hundred such operations.
2. There is a ruler without divisions and a special tool that allows you to measure the distance between two arbitrary points and plot this distance on any already drawn line from an arbitrary point on this line. How can you use these tools and a pencil to divide this segment in half?
3. Prove that no quadruple of natural numbers $x, y, z$, $t$ satisfies the equality $3x^4 + 5y^4 + 7z^4 = 11t^4$.
4. You are given four coins, some of which may be counterfeit. It is known that a real coin weighs 10 g, and a fake one - 9 g. All with one cup show the total weight of the coins placed on this cup. Find the smallest amount of weighing that needs to be done to be sure of which coins are counterfeit and which are real.

9th grade
1. A convex quadrilateral is divided by diagonals into even triangles, the areas of which are expressed in integers. Prove that the product of these numbers cannot appear and make serifs.
2. Prove that for primes $p \geq 5$, $i = 1, 2, \dots, 24$ the number $p_1^2 + p_2^2 + \dots + p_{24}^2$ is divisible by 24.
3. There are two perpendicular straight lines on the bottom of the plane. Using calipers, indicate three points on the plane that are the vertices of an equilateral triangle. A caliper is a tool similar to a compass, but has two needles at the ends. It allows you to transfer equal distances, but does not allow you to draw (scratch) circles, arcs of circles and make serifs.
4. Let $x$ and $y$ be natural numbers. Function distance
$$f(x, y) = \frac{(x+y-1)(x+y-2)}{2} + y.$$
Prove that the set of values ​​$f(x, y)$ is all natural numbers, and for any natural number $i = f(x, y)$ the numbers $x$ and $y$ are uniquely determined.
5. 20 telephones are connected by wires so that each wire connects two telephones, each pair of telephones is connected by no more than one wire, and no more than three wires extend from each telephone. You need to paint the wires (each wire entirely single red) so that no more than three wires come from each telephone. What is the smallest number of colors sufficient for such shading?
10th grade
1. The calculator performs the operation path: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root. Find a formula that can be used to determine the smallest of two arbitrary numbers $a$ and $b$ using this calculator.
2. Is there a line on the coordinate plane about which the graph of the function $y = 2^x$ is symmetrical?
3. Can any parallelepiped be cut by a plane so that the section produces a straight graph of the function?
4. There is a ruler without divisions and a standard length that allows you to plot a certain fixed distance on any already drawn line from an arbitrary point on this line. How can you draw a perpendicular to a given line using these tools and a pencil?
