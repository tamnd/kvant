5. Let's take a pair of natural numbers and divide the larger ones by the smaller ones with the remainder (if the numbers are equal, then we will also divide one of them by the other). From the resulting quotient and remainder we form a new pair of numbers and do the same with it. As soon as one of the numbers turns out to be equal to zero, we repeat the calculations. Prove that if you start with numbers not exceeding 1988, then you will not be able to complete more than six divisions.

Selected problems of the Moscow City Olympiad in Physics

8th grade

1. Force F (Fig. 1) is applied perpendicular to the free end of the lever with the bracing axis at point O at a distance l₁. The other end rests against a smooth wall at a distance l₂ from the axis. Angle α=135°. Find the magnitude of the force acting on the axis.

2. Four identical small balls move along a thin tube without friction to the right with equal speeds so that the distances between them are equal to l₁, l₂, l₃ (Fig. 2). The tube is closed with a stopper. How will the balls be located and how will they move after all collisions have stopped? The impacts of the balls against each other and against the cork are absolutely elastic.

3. 1 kg of ice and 1 kg of a low-melting substance that does not mix with water, at t = -40 °C, are placed in a heat-conducting vessel with an internal heater. The heater produces constant power. The dependence of the temperature in the vessel on time is shown on figure 3. The specific heat of ice is cₙ=2·10³ J/(kg·K), of solid matter c=10³ J/(kg·K). Find the specific heat of the substance and its specific heat in the molten state.

4. The silver-plated (mirror) glass sphere has a round hole with an opening angle of 2α, into which a single parallel beam of rays falls, perpendicular to the plane of the hole (Fig. 4). Some of the rays, having undergone one reflection, exit the sphere back through the hole. What proportion, in terms of power, does the extracted beam make up of the external one? α - arbitrary.

9th grade

1. Three identical massive balls are located at the same height above the horizontal plane (Fig. 5). The first ball is mounted on a weightless knitting needle, the end of which is closed in a hinge. The second ball is mounted on the same knitting needle, but its end can slide along the plane without friction. The third ball does not interact with the plane in any way. The balls simultaneously begin to fall without any initial speed. In what order will they fall onto the plane?

2. Someone designed a teaching helicopter with a very low mass and a witt diameter of d=8 m. Will a pilot weighing M=80 kg be able to take off on such a machine? (Compare the power required with the horse's power.)

3. A horizontal cylinder with a piston is filled with water containing water vapor. In the initial state, its volume V₀=1 l, pressure p₀=10⁵ Pa, temperature t₀=30 °C. If you close the piston and cool the cylinder at a constant volume V₀, then at t₁=10.5 °C dew will appear in it. You can do it differently: leave the piston free and cool the air from its initial state at a constant pressure p₀. At what temperature will dew appear in this case? The dependence of the density of saturated water vapor on temperature is shown in figure 6.

4. The space station, which is a metal ball with a radius of R=1 m, is located in deep space. At some moments, an “electron gun” is turned on on board, emitting into space a beam of electrons with energies W = 9·10⁴ eV. The current strength in the beam is I=1 µA. Find the direction of the electric field at the surface of the station body τ=1 min after turning on the “gun”. (At the initial time E=0.)
