V. E. MATIZEN, V. N. DUVROVSKY

In this article we will mainly talk about the signs that a tetrahedron belongs to one or another class. Examples of such classes are regular triangular rectangles and regular tetrahedra, which can be considered analogues of isosceles and equilateral triangles, respectively. But unlike planimetry, where one can only name right-angled triangles, the interesting classes of tetrahedra in vsterometry are not limited to this. I already talk about two such classes in «Kvant» (see «Kvant», 1983, No. 7, p. 34): these are the classes of equal-gravity and frame tetrahedra. The class of equi-equal tetrahedra includes tetrahedra, all of whose faces are equal to each other. The class of frame tetrahedra includes tetrahedra all of whose edges touch a certain sphere. An article published in 1983 discussed numerous features of "equifacetism" and "framework". Getting acquainted with it to understand further necessity (albeit useful): we will study the properties of three other classes of tetrahedra.

Orthocentric tetrahedron

Unlike a triangle, whose altitudes always intersect at one point - the orthocenter, not every tetrahedron has a similar property (Fig. 1). A tetrahedron whose altitudes intersect at one point is called orthocentric. We will begin our study of orthocentric tetrahedra with the necessary and sufficient conditions for orthocentricity; each of them can be taken as a specific orthocentric tetrahedron.

So, we will prove the equivalence of the following properties of the tetrahedron:
O1. The altitudes of the tetrahedron intersect at one point.
O2. The bases of the altitudes of the tetrahedron are the orthogonals of the faces.
O3. Each two opposite edges of the tetrahedron are perpendicular.
O4. The sums of the squares of opposite edges of a tetrahedron are equal.
O5. The segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges of the tetrahedron are equal (these segments are called bimeduans).
O6. The products of the cosines of opposite two catch boundaries are equal.

Now we will prove that $O3^\circ\Leftrightarrow O4^\circ\Leftrightarrow O5^\circ$. Note that every two bimedannas are diagonals of a parallelogram (Fig. 3), the sides of which, being the midlines of the faces, are parallel to the two opposite edges of the tetrahedron and are equal in length to half of these edges. From these equalities it immediately follows that $O4^\circ\Leftrightarrow O5^\circ$.

Incentric tetrahedrons

The segments connecting the centers of gravity of the tetrahedron faces with opposite vertices (“medians of the tetrahedron”) always intersect at one point (this point is the center of gravity of the tetrahedron). If in this condition we replace the centers of gravity of the faces with the orthocenters of the faces, then it turns into a new definition of an orthocentric tetrahedron (check it!). If we replace them with the centers of circles inscribed in the faces, called ingrediates, we obtain the definition of a new class of so-called ingredic tetrahedrons.

The characteristics of the class of ingredient tetrahedrons are also quite interesting:
1. The segments connecting the vertices of the tetrahedron with the centers of circles inscribed in opposite faces intersect at one point.
2. The bisectors of the angles of two faces drawn to a common edge of these faces have a common base.
3. The product of the lengths of opposite edges is equal.
4. The triangle formed by the second intersection points of three edges emerging from one vertex with any sphere passing through the three ends of these edges is equilateral (Fig. 7).

Checking the equivalence of these conditions is not difficult, and we leave it to the reader, limiting ourselves to only one indication: for any tetrahedron, the sides of the triangle referred to in conditions I4 are proportional to the product of the opposite edges of the tetrahedron.

Prove two more properties of ingredient tetrahedra and find out whether they can be taken as definitions of this class:

9. the cosine of an angle of one pair of opposite edges of a tetrahedron is equal to the sum of the cosines of two angles of the cosine of pairs of its opposite edges;
10. straight lines along which the planes of the three faces of the tetrahedron intersect with the plane tangent to its circumscribed sphere at the common vertex of these faces and form angles with each other of no 60°.
