Moscow chess lover A. Sutin has been collecting chess computers from famous companies for several years. And he not only collects, but also conducts an unlimited match session with them with normal time control.

Here are two examples from Soutine's no-limit match. In both batches the machine was set to the highest level.

It must be said that A. Soutine not only plays with computers. His Playmatic carefully studied all the matches of the last match between G. Kasparov and A. Kaspov (Seville, 1987) and made a number of valuable comments.

A. Karpov — G. Kasparov
In this position (23rd game) Kasparov made a tactical miscalculation - 50... R7f3??, and after 51. gf R:f3 52. Rc7+ Kph8 53. Ch6!! We found out that sacrificing a rook is incorrect. After 53... R:d3 54. C:f8 R:h3+ 55. Kpg2

Rg3+ 56. Kph2 R:g1 57. B:c5 d3 Black resigned (White plays 58. Ce3, gave up the bishop for a pawn, and the passage of “c” and “d” is unstoppable).

In 8 minutes, Playmatic found the move 53. Ch6 for White, and for Black together they mistakenly played 50... R7f3 and chose 50... Cb4!, which preserved the chances of a draw.

G. Kasparov — A. Karpov
And this position is from the 24th, final battle (after White’s time mistake 33. Qb1-d1), when A. Karpov had the opportunity to regain his chess crown with the move 33... Nc5! (34. Qd8+ Kph7 35. In response to 33... g6 the machine sacrificed a piece to g6 and then proved that in all variations White has the upper hand.
