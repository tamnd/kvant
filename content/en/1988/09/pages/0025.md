$$x^3 + ax + b = 0$$
may have 3, 2 or 1 solution. To find out exactly how much, you need to draw a “discriminant curve” $4a^3 + 27b^2 = 0$ (Fig. 25) on the plane with coordinates $a, b$. If the point $(a, b)$ lies inside the violet zone, the equation has 3 solutions, if it lies on its border, except for the tip, then 2 solutions, in other cases the equation has 1 solution.

A similar problem for a 4th degree equation
$$x^4 + ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$
leads to a dovetail: in the space with coordinates $a, b, c$ there is a dovetail similar to the one shown in figure 20. Inside the trihedral pencil case there are points $(a, b, c)$, for which our equation has 4 solutions. On the boundary of the pencil case, in addition to the return and self-intersection lines, the equation has 3 solutions. On these rulers, eliminating solutions 2; The equation has the same number of solutions for all $(a, b, c)$ lying above the surface (from the side of the pencil case). On the entire surface, excluding the boundaries of the pencil case, but including the top, there is only one solution; below the surface there are no solutions at all.
