So, we will prove the equivalence of the following properties of the tetrahedron:
O1. The altitudes of the tetrahedron intersect at one point.
O2. The bases of the altitudes of the tetrahedron are the orthogonals of the faces.
O3. Each two opposite edges of the tetrahedron are perpendicular.
O4. The sums of the squares of opposite edges of a tetrahedron are equal.
O5. The segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges of the tetrahedron are equal (these segments are called bimeduans).
O6. The products of the cosines of opposite two catch boundaries are equal.
O7. The sum of the squares of the areas of the faces is less than the sum of the squares of the opposite edges.
Let us denote by $a, b, c, a_1, b_1, c_1$ the edges of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ ($a=BC, a_1=DA, b=CA, \dots$; Fig. 2), by $m_a, m_b, m_c$ the bimedannas ($m_a$ connects the midpoints of the edges $a$ and $a_1$, etc.); the same letters indicate the lengths of the corresponding segments - from the context it will be clear what is meant - a segment or its length. We denote the values ​​of the two angles of the tetrahedron at the edges $a, b, \dots$ as follows: $\hat{a}, \hat{b}, \dots$. First, let us consider a tetrahedron that is orthogenric as “half”, namely, we will prove that the following conditions are equal:
$O1^\circ$. heights $AA_1$ and $DD_1$ intersect.
$O2^\circ$. point $D_1$ (projection of vertex $D$ onto face $ABC$) lies at the height of face $ABC$ drawn from vertex $A$ (or on its extension).

$O3^\circ$. Edges $BC$ and $AD$ ($a$ and $a_1$) are perpendicular.
$O4^\circ$. Vimedannas $m_b$ and $m_c$ are equal.
$O5^\circ$. $b^2+b_1^2=c^2+c_1^2$.
$O6^\circ$. $\cos b \cdot \cos b_1=\cos \hat{c} \cdot \cos \hat{c}$.

It is quite easy to establish that $O1^\circ\Leftrightarrow O2^\circ\Leftrightarrow O3^\circ$: the projection of the lines $AA_1$ and $AD$ onto the face $ABC$, we obtain from the theorem on three perpendiculars that each of these three statements is equivalent to the perpendicularity of the lines $AD_1$ and $BC$ (or, possibly, the coincidence of the points $D_1$ and $A$).

Now we will prove that $O3^\circ\Leftrightarrow O4^\circ\Leftrightarrow O5^\circ$. Note that every two bimedannas are diagonals of a parallelogram (Fig. 3), the sides of which, being the midlines of the faces, are parallel to the two opposite edges of the tetrahedron and are equal in length to half of these edges. The conditions $O3^\circ$ and $O4^\circ$, i.e. $a \perp a_1$ and $m_b=m_c$, are equivalent to the fact that the corresponding parallelogram is a right angle. Let us write down the well-known parallelogram equality (the sum of the squares of the diagonal) for two other analogous parallelograms: $b^2+b_1^2=2(m_c^2+m_a^2), c^2+c_1^2=2(m_a^2+m_b^2)$. From these equalities it immediately follows that $O4^\circ\Leftrightarrow O5^\circ$.

To prove that $O6^\circ\Leftrightarrow O1^\circ$, consider the points $P$ and $P'$ of intersection of the planes $ADD_1$ and $ADA_1$ with the line $BC$. The ratio $k(P)=BP:PC$ is equal to the ratio $S_{AD_1B}:S_{AD_1C}$ of the areas of triangles $AD_1B$ and $AD_1C$ (Fig. 4).
