M1102. Prove that there are puzzle natural numbers whose sum of cubes is equal to the cube of a natural number if a) n=3, b) n=4, c) n is any natural number greater than 2.

Here are examples of sets of three and four cubes of various natural numbers, the sum of which is a cube:
a) $3^3+4^3+5^3=6^3$,
b) $1^3+5^3+7^3+12^3=13^3$.

We can build sets of a large number of cubes by “building up”: by multiplying all the cubes in equality a) by $2^3$ (or in equality b) by $6^3$), we obtain an equality of cubes, the smallest of which is $6^3$; it can be replaced with $3^3+4^3+5^3$. Thus, from a) we obtain the equalities
$$3^3+4^3+5^3+8^3+10^3=12^3 \quad (n=5),$$
$$3^3+4^3+5^3+8^3+10^3+16^3+20^3=24^3 \quad (n=7)$$
etc. for all odd n, and from b) -
$$3^3+4^3+5^3+30^3+42^3+72^3=78^3 \quad (n=6),$$
$$3^3+4^3+5^3+8^3+10^3+60^3+84^3+144^3=156^3 \quad (n=8)$$
etc. for all even n.

M1103. a) On an infinite plane divided into square cells, a certain - perhaps infinite - number of rectangles of size $1\times 2$ are painted black so that no two black rectangles have common points (hereinafter vertices). Prove that the rest of the plane can be tiled with these rectangles. b)* Let several rectangles be shaded on the checkered plane

Let us cover the original (blue) checkered grid with a larger (black) grid, dividing the plane into squares $2\times 2$ cells. Each black domino $1\times 2$ either fits entirely into one square $2\times 2$ of the new grid, or into two adjacent ones forming a rectangle $2\times 4$, and other black dominoes do not enter this section of the black grid. In both cases, the rest of the area is easy to cover with dominoes (at figure 1 these dominoes are circled with pink lines); let cells $2\times 2$ split into two dominoes. This implies the statement of problem a).
