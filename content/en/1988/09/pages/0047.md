M1105. After several straight cuts, the surfaces of the convex manifold were turned onto a plane. The result is a polygon for which it is known which points of its boundary “glue together,” that is, correspond to the same point on the surface of the manifold. What was the original polygon like if, when cut, the result is a) a rectangle with side 1 and $\sqrt{3}$, b) an equilateral triangle with an angle of 120°, and in both cases the points of each side are glued together, symmetrical about its middle.

if $A_1, B_1$ and $C_1$ are images of points $A, B, C$ under some inversion with center $D$ (i.e. points $A_1, B_1, C_1$ lie, respectively, on rays $DA, DB, DC$ and $DA_1 \cdot DA = DB_1 \cdot DB = DC_1 \cdot DC$), then the sides of triangle $A_1B_1C_1$ are proportional to the products of opposite edges tetrahedra $ABCD$, and this triangle is a right triangle. (According to the main property of inversion, the circle described around the triangle $ABC$ transforms during inversion into the circle described around the triangle $A_1B_1C_1$; it is easy to see that in our problem the side $B_1C_1$ will be the diameter of the second circle.)
