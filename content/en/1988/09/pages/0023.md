The Thoughtful Reader is right, as always; but I have one more argument in stock that will probably convince him. The line he is talking about is the edge of the image of the surface, or, as they say, its visible outline. At the points of this line, the tangent plane to the surface is perpendicular to the drawing (see Fig. 14, which shows the visible contour of the sphere). But for a developing surface, the tangent plane at all points of the generatrix is ​​the same (remember - the ruler is attached!), and at points that do not belong to the supposed return edge, it is clearly not perpendicular to the plane of the drawing. This means that it is not perpendicular to it and at the points of this line, but is located as shown in figure 15, which irrefutably shows that our line is a return edge.

You can look at all this from the other side: a developing surface (the generatrices of which are not parallel and do not all pass through one point) consists of straight lines tangent to one line - the return edge. This means that a developable surface can be obtained as follows: take a spatial curve (no piece of which is flat) and draw all tangents to it (Fig. 16).
