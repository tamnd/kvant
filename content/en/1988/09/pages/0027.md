How was the Moon formed?

Among the chaos collided, there may have been catastrophic collisions leading to unexpected consequences. Recently, a group of American astrophysicists led by A. Cameron put forward a new hypothesis for the formation of the Moon. According to their idea, the Moon arose from the fusion of fragments of the collision of the proto-Earth with another planet, the mass of which is about 0.12 of the Earth's mass (imagine the collision of the Earth with Mars!).

The variants of collisions were calculated on a high-speed computer and a movie was made from successive time stages of the calculation. Computer cinema allows you to see with your own eyes how this happens. First, two planets merge, while their bodies lose their spherical shape, stretching towards each other by gravitational attraction. Then comes the blow. It was not central - otherwise the Earth would not have acquired the required rotation speed after the collision. A shock wave runs through both planets, heating the material above its melting point. Both bodies merge and the Earth is formed; at the same time, fragments fly apart. Some of these fragments fall to the Earth; and those whose speed is high go beyond the boundaries of the earth
