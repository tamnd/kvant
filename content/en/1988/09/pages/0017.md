The apparatus that carries out such movement is called an orthopter (“straight wing”), and its various variants appear in hundreds of applications.

It is easy to see that with such a movement, the cross-sectional area of ​​the impact column, which carries the weight of the object, is many times smaller than the area covered by the wing during its horizontal movement. This, as we already know, entails a sharp increase in the required power. As a result of this, and also due to some other specific losses, such a device is 4-5 times inferior in economic efficiency to a helicopter.

In nature, the described type of flight is used only by insects with a very small load on the wing area, for example, daytime butterflies.
