When the angular velocity of rotation reaches the value $\omega = v/R$, the point of the ball B (Fig. 2), the maximum distance from the axis of rotation OO′, will have a linear speed equal to the speed of the belt: $\omega R = vR/R = v$. The slipping of the ball relative to the belt at this point will stop, and the friction force (and therefore its moment) will disappear. However, at all other points of other ABC (at which the ball is in contact with the tape) the linear speed at this moment is less than v (these points are close to the axis of rotation), so the ball continues to accelerate.

As the angular velocity of the ball increases, the point of no piercing moves closer to the OO′ axis, and the number of sections with a linear velocity less than v decreases. At the same time, the number of sections where the linear speed is greater than v is growing; the friction force is directed in the opposite direction and creates a moment of force, braking. At some point, the accelerating and decelerating moments of force become equal, and the angular velocity changes.

Let us now carry out the corresponding calculations. First of all, let’s find at what point there will be no piercing under steady-state conditions.

We will assume that the tape is pressed against the ball in all places with the same force, then any two identical areas of contact will be subject to the same (according to the model) friction forces. Let the desired point of no piercing be point D (see Fig. 2). Let's look at a small area of ​​others near this point. Its length is $\Delta l = R\Delta a$; force acting on it $\Delta F = k\Delta l = kR\Delta a$ (k is some constant); her moment $\Delta M = kR\Delta a \cdot R \cos a$. Total moment of friction forces

$$M = \sum_i \Delta M = kR \sum_i R\Delta a \cos a_i,$$

acting on the arc BC, in steady state must be equal to zero. This means that the sum of the projections of arcs BD and DC (“positive” and “negative” corresponding) onto the axis of rotation OO′ must be equal to zero:

$$\sum_i R\Delta a \cos a_i = 0 \Rightarrow O'D' = D'C = R/2,$$

where does the desired steady angular velocity come from?

$$\omega = \frac{v}{D'D} = \frac{2v}{\sqrt{3}R} \approx 1.15 \frac{v}{R}.$$
