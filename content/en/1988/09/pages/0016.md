This is not surprising. The source of large losses in animals is the failure to sweep a circle along the wing span. In birds, this is supplemented by the absence of an elastic element in the body-wing system (insects have it in the form of an elastic exoskeleton), which causes losses to overcome the inertia of the wing with each change in the direction of the flapping. On the other hand, insects have very poor aerodynamics due to their miniature size and low speeds.

Thus, the main source of the “ability” of insects and small birds that generates our feelings to take off vertically and perform a fluttering flight is the simple construction of a small load on the area swept by the wing.

We see that promises of an energy-dependent artificial flapping flight apparatus that does everything that a bird “can do” must be treated with great caution.
