**O8. The edges of the described parallelomed are equal,**

that is, all its faces are rhombuses. (To prove this, you only need to note that the edges of the tetrahedron are the diagonals of the faces of the parallelepiped, and the bimedians of the tetrahedron are equal and parallel to the edges of the parallelepiped - Fig. 6.) The condition of equality of all faces of the described parallelepiped (and thereby its edges) characterizes the regular tetrahedrons of the pyramid. Another type of tetrahedrons—equilateral—can be defined as follows: their described parallelepipeds are rectangular. By the way, now we can give a negative answer to the question posed at the end of the first section: a tetrahedron whose libraries intersect at one point is not necessarily orthocentric. An equally spaced tetrahedron also has this property, since the libraries of an equispaced tetrahedron coincide with the bimedines and intersect at the center of its circumscribed parallelepiped. Another example can be obtained from a right parallelepiped, the base of which is a rhombus - the corresponding tetrahedron has two opposite edges perpendicular, and the rest are equal.

We are not aware of other examples of tetrahedra whose libraries intersect at one point. It is also not known whether there are any general conditions under which this property holds. Perhaps readers will find them.
