Incentric tetrahedrons

The segments connecting the centers of gravity of the tetrahedron faces with opposite vertices (“medians of the tetrahedron”) always intersect at one point (this point is the center of gravity of the tetrahedron). If in this condition we replace the centers of gravity of the faces with the orthocenters of the faces, then it turns into a new definition of an orthocentric tetrahedron (check it!). If we replace them with the centers of circles inscribed in the faces, called ingrediates, we obtain the definition of a new class of so-called ingredic tetrahedrons.

The characteristics of the class of ingredient tetrahedrons are also quite interesting:
1. The segments connecting the vertices of the tetrahedron with the centers of circles inscribed in opposite faces intersect at one point.
2. The bisectors of the angles of two faces drawn to a common edge of these faces have a common base.
3. The product of the lengths of opposite edges is equal.
4. The triangle formed by the second intersection points of three edges emerging from one vertex with any sphere passing through the three ends of these edges is equilateral (Fig. 7).

Checking the equivalence of these conditions is not difficult, and we leave it to the reader, limiting ourselves to only one indication: for any tetrahedron, the sides of the triangle referred to in conditions I4 are proportional to the product of the opposite edges of the tetrahedron.

Prove two more properties of ingredient tetrahedra and find out whether they can be taken as definitions of this class:

9. the cosine of an angle of one pair of opposite edges of a tetrahedron is equal to the sum of the cosines of two angles of the cosine of pairs of its opposite edges;
10. straight lines along which the planes of the three faces of the tetrahedron intersect with the plane tangent to its circumscribed sphere at the common vertex of these faces and form angles with each other of no 60°.

Conformant tetrahedrons

The last class of tetrahedrons with which we want to introduce readers is the class of consonant tetrahedrons. We call tetrahedrons congruent if they have
C1. The heights are equal.
This definition can be replaced by any of the following:
C2. The projection of a tetrahedron onto a plane perpredicular to any bimedian is a rhombus.
C3. The faces of the described parallelenide are equal in size.
