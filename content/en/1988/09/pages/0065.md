Liquid nitrogen and copper nut
V.V. UTESHEV

Once in the laboratory we observed how liquid nitrogen boiled into a round copper nut floating in it. The boiling process proceeded very interestingly and not at all as one might expect. Liquid nitrogen began to boil immediately after the nut was immersed in it, but a few seconds later, just before completion, boiling was suddenly heard sharply.

It is clear that the reason for boiling is the large difference in the initial temperatures of the nut and nitrogen. But the question arises: why does the nut cool down not monotonously, not smoothly, as one might assume, but has two modes of intensity, and the change of modes occurs very quickly (in a jump)?

Any boiling process is always associated with heat transfer. In our case - from the nut to the nitrogen. Therefore, the answer to the question about the boiling characteristic can be found by analyzing the heat transfer mechanism.

As is known, heat from one body to another can be transferred in different ways, for example, through thermal conductivity or convection. In convection, heat is transferred by moving and moving large masses of matter heated to different temperatures.

In contrast to convection, with thermal conduction the transfer of heat is not accompanied by the movement of matter.

In nature, convective phenomena occur literally at every step. Sometimes they want to actively use them, sometimes, on the contrary, they want to get rid of them.
