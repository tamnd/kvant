3. Any graph with $n$ vertices either has two vertices connected by an edge, or no two vertices are connected by an edge. In the first case we have a complete subgraph with two vertices, in the second we have an empty subgraph with $n$ vertices (coinciding with the entire graph).

4. Let $r(m-1, n)$ and $r(m, n-1)$ be even numbers. Let's take an arbitrary vertex $X$ in a graph with $r(m-1, n)+r(m, n-1)-1$ vertices. If the number of vertices connected by edges to $X$ is not less than $r(m-1, n)$, then we proceed as in the first case in proof (1); if the number of vertices connected by edges to $X$ is no more than $r(m-1, n)-2$, then we proceed as in the second case in the proof of equality (1). It remains to consider the case when the vertex $X$ is connected edgewise with $r(m-1, n)-1$ of other vertices. But such a condition cannot be satisfied for all vertices of the graph. Indeed, if each vertex of the graph is edge-connected with $r(m-1, n)-1$ vertices, then the number of edges is $\frac{1}{2}(r(m-1, n)+r(m, n-1)-1)(r(m-1, n)-1)$ - a fractional number (compare with the solution to Exercise 2).
