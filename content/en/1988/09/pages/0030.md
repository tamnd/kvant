The gravitational differential not only separates substances according to their density, it leads to the fact that some chemical reactions, energetically impracticable under normal conditions, become possible for large masses in a gravitational field.

Imagine that as a result of a certain chemical reaction, two substances are formed from one substance (say, iron sulfide) - one lighter (sulfur), and the other heavier (iron). The energy required to maintain such separation can be drawn from the gravitational energy released as a result of the sinking of the heavy material and the explosion of the light. Gas may also turn out to be a light substance under gravitational differentiation.

Here is an unexpected example of a geophysical phenomenon caused by gravitational differential. On August 21, 1986, a tragic message came from Cameroon: clouds of gas erupted from the small Lake Nyos during the night, and about two thousand people from nearby villages suffocated and died.

This lake is located in a volcanic crater, therefore, in the first reports of the flood karst, it was believed that the volcano emitted some asphyxiating gases.

After studying the lake, the physics of what happened became clear. The culprits really turned out to be an extinct volcano and a lake in its crater. But here’s what’s amazing: the behavior of the volcano was, at first glance, quite innocent - there was no eruption or earthquake. The gas that caused the sudden death of thousands of people turned out to be ordinary carbon monoxide CO₂, harmless in small concentrations. The essence of the state phenomenon is as follows.

Nyos is a small lake, but its average depth is more than 100 m. The volcano on which it is located is extinct, but as a result of residual geological processes in its depths, carbon monoxide is slowly released. This gas dissolves well in water, and its solubility increases rapidly with increasing pressure. Remember how violently carbon dioxide escapes when you open a bottle of mineral water.
