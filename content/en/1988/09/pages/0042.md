Wonderful numbers.
Friendly numbers and twin primes

Each of you had to factor numbers many times. Consider a number and the sum of all its divisors other than the number itself. If this sum is greater than the original number, the number is called redundant; if in case of equality, the number is called perfect.

With n=2 and n=4 the numbers found by Pythagoras and ichn al-Wanna are obtained. For n=7, we obtain the numbers 9,363,584 and 9,437,056, found in 1638 by P. Descartes (1596-1650), without depending on Curra’s theorem and bn. These three cases exhaust all the values ​​of n≤20,000 for which Kurra's ichn method gives other numbers.

Leonhard Euler (1707-1783) was the first after Descartes to obtain new other numbers. In total, he discovered 59 pairs of numbers, the average of numbers: 3². 7. 13. 5. 7 and 3⁴. 5. 11. 29. 89, as well as 3². 7. 13. 107 and 3⁴. 5. 11. 2 699. He proposed five methods for finding other real numbers.

The mathematicians of Ancient Greece were also very interested in prime numbers - numbers that have equal two divisors. Average numbers, other than numbers, differ by 2: for example, 3 and 5, 5 and 7, 11 and 13, 17 and 19.

Pythagoras said: “My friend is the one who is my second self, like the numbers 220 and 284.” These two numbers are replaced by the fact that the sum of the divisors of each of them is equal to the second number. They were called ore-bearing.
