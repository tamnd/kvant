⟦folio 8⟧

We will not reproduce here the formulas and reasoning that Friedman gave to his conclusions. Let us point out, however, that in 1934 it turned out to be possible to draw a number of important conclusions not in the complex language of the general theory of relativity, which Aleksandr Aleksadrovich used in his works, but in the simpler and more familiar Newtonian language. The remarkable Soviet theoretical physicist Ya. B. Zeldovich spoke about this very clearly on the pages of «Kvant» (No. 3, 1984), who did an extraordinary amount both for the deep development of Friedman’s ideas and for their propaganda.

In the first half of the 20s, Friedman continued to work simultaneously in the field of atmospheric physics and in the field of relativity theory. In July 1925, being the director of the Main Geophysical General Laboratory, he, together with the professional aeronaut pilot P.F. Fedosenko, made a geronic flight into the atmosphere in a hot air balloon; A national altitude record was set (7400 m). Friedman wrote down his impressions of the flight. Fedosenko did the same. Wonderful articles! From them - especially, of course, from Fedosenko's article - we learned that the courageous observer pilot of the First World War, Alexander Friedman, remained the same brave man, becoming a world famous scientist.

In the summer of 1925, for the first time in many years, he went to Crimea for three weeks. Returning home - he arrived in Leningrad on August 17 - Aleksadr Aleksadrovich fell ill with typhoid fever and from this absurd accident died at the age of 37 on September 16, in the prime of his creative powers and talent. Newspapers responded to this sad event with a number of publications. In particular, “Evening Red Gazeta” - the predecessor of “Evening Leningrad” - on September 18 published an interview with the doctor who treated Fridman.
