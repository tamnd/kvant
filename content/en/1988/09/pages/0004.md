Alexander Alexandrovich
FRIEDMAN
(on the 100th anniversary of his birth)

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. Y. FRANKEL

It is difficult to determine to which field of science the activities of the remarkable Soviet scientist Alexander Alexandrovich Friedman can be attributed. If we turn to the biographical directory of Physicists, we will find in it a separate article about Alexander Alexandrovits. But an equally informative article is available in a similar reference book “Mathematics and Mechanics”. Finally, in the third similar collection of biographies, “Astronomers,” we will also find the name of Friedman, and at the same time we will learn that one of the criteria for the reliability of the Moon is named in his honor. Who was this compatriot of ours, whose life is rightly called a feat, and whose discovery in the field of cosmology is one of the most revolutionary in science, close in scale to what was made by Copernicus? He was “one in three persons,” or rather, even in four: a physicist, a mechanic, a mathematician, and an astronomer (astrophysicist). The model of the time-varying, expanding Universe that he created back in 1922 is now in the center of attention of astrophysicists; it received experimental confirmation in a number of discovery works, the first of which was carried out by the American astronomer E. Hubble four years after Friedman’s death, and one of the most significant - the discovery of electromagnetic radiation arbitrary in the Universe (referred to as relict radiation) - about a quarter of a century ago. Perhaps, it was from the moment of this discovery made by the American physicists R. Wilson and A. Penzinas that the paradoxical conclusions from Friedman’s theory, which at one time seemed to him to be partly a curiosity, acquired the force of practical unrefuted facts, and the science of the Universe began to develop rapidly.

A. A. Fridman was born in St. Petersburg on June 16, 1888. His father, also Alexander Alexandrovich, was a ballet dancer, conductor, composer, and studied at the St. Petersburg Conservatory with N. A. Rimsky-Korsakov. Mother, Lyudmila Ignatevna Voyachek, daughter of the famous St. Petersburg composer, organizer and conductor I. K. Voyachek, was a pianist. The musical “gene” did not manifest itself in Alexander Fridman: he simply loved music, nothing more. In 1897, he entered the preparatory class of the 2nd St. Petersburg Gymnasium, located not far from house No. 35 on the Moika River embankment, where his family lived.

The hypnasia had a strong teaching staff. Suffice it to say that two of the teachers were authors of gymnasium textbooks. Information on the history of the gymnasium - the oldest in St. Petersburg - can be found in its materials, which form a special fund of the State Historical Archives of Leningrad. In this background, of course, there is information about students and teachers, and materials from meetings of the Pedagogical Council of the gymnasium, and even conduit pages (remember “Conduit and Schwambrania” by Lev Kassil!), in which the work of class guards about the misdeeds and pranks of gymnasium students was punctually entered.
