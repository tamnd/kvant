Demician B.B. Golitsyan (and with the approval of V.A. Steklov) became interested in issues of geo- and hydrodynamics and meteorology. In 1913, he began work (along with teaching mathematics at the Mining Institute and the Institute of Railway Engineers) at the Main Physical Observatory. In Pavlovsk, near St. Petersburg, there was a branch of this observatory, and Friedman studied the behavior of air flows there, organized kite lifts of instruments that measured the strength and direction of winds at various altitudes, and for the same purpose he himself flew on an airship. The fifth - air - ocean became Alexander Aleksadrovich’s true passion for life!

When the World War began, Friedman already volunteered for the active army in August 1914. He was entrusted with establishing an air service

A house on Plexanova Street (formerly Kazanskaya), in which the 2nd Petersburg Zemnaya is located. Now this building is school No. 232.

The family of A. A. Friedman lived in this house on the Moika Embankment. The house has been preserved and is under state protection as an architectural monument of the 19th century.
