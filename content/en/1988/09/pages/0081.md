15. $\frac{3}{4}\pi - \arcsin(\sqrt{2}\sin \frac{\pi}{18})$. Solution.
Let's make a replacement $\cos x + \sin x = 2\sin y$, i.e. $\sin(x + \frac{\pi}{4}) = \sqrt{2}\sin y$. Our equation will become $\sin 3y = \frac{1}{2}$ (check!); its solution is $y \in \left\{ \frac{\pi}{18} + \frac{2}{3}k\pi, k \in Z \right\} \cup \left\{ \frac{5}{18}\pi + \frac{2}{3}m\pi, m \in Z \right)$ (Fig. 8). But $0 \leq x \leq \pi$ means $\frac{\pi}{4} \leq x + \frac{\pi}{4} \leq \frac{5}{4}\pi$, $-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \leq \sin(x + \frac{\pi}{4}) \leq 1$, $-\frac{1}{2} \leq \sin y \leq \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
As can be seen from figure 5, sin $y$ can take three values, but only one of them, namely $\sin \frac{\pi}{18}$, falls into the desired range. Thus, $\sin(x + \frac{\pi}{4}) = \sqrt{2}\sin \frac{\pi}{18}$, and of all the solutions to this equation, only $x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} - \arcsin(\sqrt{2}\sin \frac{\pi}{18})$ is in the interval [0; $\pi$].
