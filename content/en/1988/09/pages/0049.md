F1116. In two identical chemical loops, the body is drawn up to the same level: in one - cold, in the other - hot. The petlets are supported and the drops are counted. Which loop will produce more drops?

THE FORCE OF ELASTICITY IS GOOD - THE MOVEMENT IS SLOW.

Let us write down the law of conservation of mechanical energy for positions $O$ and $1$ (see figure):

$$mgH = mgh + \frac{1}{2} k(H - l - h)^2 + \frac{1}{2} mv^2$$

and for positions $O$ and $2$:

$$mgH = \frac{1}{2} k(H - l)^2.$$

Let us also take into account the equilibrium conditions at height $h$:

$$mg = k(H - l - h).$$

By eliminating the unknown parameters $m$, $k$ and $l$ from equation (1) - (3), we find $v$. The easiest way to do it is this way. Divide equation (2) by equation (3) and find $l = \sqrt{H^2 - 2Hh}$; we rewrite the term $k(H - l - h)^2/2$ in equations (1), taking into account equality (3), in the form $mg(H - l - h)/2$; after which we get

$$v^2 = g(H - h + \sqrt{H^2 - 2Hh})$$

And

$$v = \sqrt{g(H - h + \sqrt{H^2 - 2Hh})} \approx 28 \text{ м/c.}$$

D. V. Pavlov

In the initial state, the equilibrium of the piston is ensured by the fact that in the lower part of the vessel the gas is compressed more strongly than in the upper part ($p_2 > p_1$). If we denote the area of ​​the piston section by $S$, then the condition of its equilibrium will be written as:

$$(p_2 - p_1)S = Mg.$$

Immediately after knocking out the stand, three forces act on the vessel in the upper direction: the force of gas pressure on the upper and lower walls of the vessel $p_1S$ and $p_2S$ and the force of the gravity of the vessel $mg$. According to Newton's second law,

$$ma_0 = mg - p_1S + p_2S.$$

From equations (1) and (2) the acceleration is found:

$$a_0 = g \frac{M + m}{m}.$$

The drop comes off the end of the pipette at the moment when the force of surface tension can no longer balance the gravity of the drop.

As the temperature of water increases, the coefficient of its surface tension, and therefore the force of surface tension, decreases. Moreover, it decreases quite noticeably - by about 20% when the temperature increases from 20 to 100 °C. Thus, the force of gravity of a drop is less than that of cold drops, and the number of hot drops, on the contrary, is greater.
