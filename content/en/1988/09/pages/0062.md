1) The coordinate system is shown at figure, and it is reasonable to associate the beginning of the time counting with the moment of the shot.

2) It is obvious that the movement of the kite can be considered rectilinear and uniformly accelerated with the acceleration of free fall $\vec{g}$. The movement of the arrow is more complex - its trajectories are parabolic, and the speed changes both in magnitude and direction. However, the situation will be simplified if we consider the movement of the arrow in projections on the coordinate axes $X$ and $Y$. It is easy to see that the coordinate $x$ of the arrow changes over time in the same way as during rectilinear uniformly accelerated motion, and the coordinate $y$ as during rectilinear uniformly accelerated motion, and with the same acceleration $\vec{g}$ as for the kite.

3) Let us write down the equations of motion of both bodies in projections on the coordinate axes $X$ and $Y$:

$$x_k = L,$$
$$y_k = H - \frac{gt^2}{2},$$
$$x_c = v_0 \cos \alpha \cdot t,$$
$$y_c = v_0 \sin \alpha \cdot t - \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

4) At the moment of meeting ($t = t_B$), the coordinates of the kite and the arrow are equal:

$$L = v_0 \cos \alpha \cdot t_B,$$
$$H - \frac{gt^2}{2} = v_0 \sin \alpha \cdot t_B - \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

We have obtained two equations with two unknowns ($t_B$ and $\alpha$). Solving them, we find

$$tg \alpha = \frac{H}{L}.$$

(In principle, knowing the angle $\alpha$, you can find the time of meeting $t_B$, and then the coordinates of the meeting place.)

Of course, the movement of a kite may actually be different (after all, it is alive!). But fundamentally the solution to the problem will look exactly the same. Only the final equations will be different.

(Note that the same method is used when aiming moving missiles at a target. The locator continuously determines the coordinates of the target, the computer - solving the corresponding equation, similar to ours - finds the meeting point of the missile with the target and makes adjustments to the movement of the missile.)

As for Prince Guidon, in fact it is completely unclear how the whole story would have ended if he, instead of acting, began to consider the real. But speaking seriously, it is still an absolute challenge for a person to be able to instantly estimate a large number of parameters ($H, L, v_0, \alpha$) and act with such excellent accuracy. We have to admit that the question of what accuracy is, with which we started the conversation, and the question of solving the main kinematic problem are still completely different questions.
