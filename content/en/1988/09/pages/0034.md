Problem 5. Prove that in the case of an absolutely elastic impact, the relative velocities of the bodies before and after the impact are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction:

$$v_1 - v_2 = -(v_1' - v_2').$$

Problem 6. Prove the equalities:

$$v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2} v_2,$$

$$v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2} v_1 + \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} v_2.$$

In the special case when $m_1 = m_2$, these formulas take on the simplest form:

$$v_1' = v_2, v_2' = v_1.$$

Consequently, at the moment of an absolutely elastic impact, an exchange of speed occurs. As already mentioned, this effect was first described by Marzi.

It is convenient to depict an absolutely elastic impact of bodies of equal mass on graphs of the motion of bodies, i.e., on graphs of coordinates versus time (Fig. 2). This graphical interpretation is used in the following problem.
