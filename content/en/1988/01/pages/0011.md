MUCH OR LITTLE?
(Reasonings of a theoretical physicist about numbers)

Doctor of Physics and Mathematics
M. I. KAGANOV

One day I wondered how many books a person can read in a lifetime. I estimated this number as follows. Let's say a person has been reading for 60 years. There are 52 weeks in a year. Let a person read two books a week. So, about 100 per year. In total, about 6,000 books in a lifetime. Without discussing how it came about, I call this number different lodyam. “So little!” - said one. “Is it really necessary so much?!” - others were surprised. And I thought: we do not have a requested sensory assessment of numbers. Only by comparing one number with others, only by giving the number a certain meaning, do we evaluate its magnitude. The number of books read, of course, has a completely definite meaning (it’s not just an empty 6000), but it can only be assessed (evaluated, said - is it a lot or a little) by someone who can make a comparison, for example, thinking: “How many books per week (month, year,...) do I read?”...

*) The dimension of the current is [I]=[Q]/s, and the dimension of the charge Q can be found using Coulomb’s law: [Q]²/[R]²=[F], where F is force. Since [F]=r·cm/c², then [Q]=r¹/²·cm³/²/c. Hence [I]=r¹/²·cm³/²/c. So the dimension of all electrical quantities is in grams, centimeters and seconds.
