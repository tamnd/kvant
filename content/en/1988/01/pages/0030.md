To solve the problem, we need, firstly, to determine the position of the center of mass of the cap. For this purpose, let us mentally divide the cap into a stack of narrow wheels of the same width $dH$ (Fig. 1). In this case, the mass of the wheels increases linearly downwards from the top to the base of the cap. The center of mass of each 柱a is on its axis. Let us mentally “flatten” the cap so that each prick turns into an equilateral trapezoid, and the weight cone turns into an equilateral triangle. The center of mass of each component of the cap remains in place (on the axis), therefore the center of mass of the entire system remains in place. But, as is known, the center of mass of a triangular plate is at the intersection point of the medians. Consequently, the center of mass of the cap is located on its axis at a distance $2/3$ $H$ from the top.

The equilibrium position of the system will be stable if, with its slight mixing from the equilibrium position, the center of mass rises (the potential energy of the system increases); then the system, left to the smaba, returns to its original equilibrium position.

In our case, in order for the cap to occupy a stable equilibrium position on Buratino’s head, its center of mass (point $M$ on figure 2) must be below the center of Buratino’s head (point $O$). Therefore, condition $AM > AO$ must be fulfilled, i.e.

$$\frac{2}{3}H > \frac{R}{\sin \alpha/2} \Leftrightarrow \frac{2}{3}H > \frac{R}{1/2},$$

or $H > 3R = 22,5$ cm. And according to the conditions $H=20$ cm. This means that the cap will not stay on Pinocchio’s head.
