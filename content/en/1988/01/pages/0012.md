⟦folio 10⟧

It would be nice to write in centimeters, seconds and grams. The answer to the question why all physical quantities are expressed in terms of grams, centimeters and seconds may seem unexpected. The point is that it has to do with the definition of physics. Physics deals with those phenomena that can be described using centimeters, seconds and grams. The centimeter and second are needed to describe movement in space (kinematika), and the gram is needed to connect this movement with the force causing the movement (dynamics). Remember that according to Newton's law, force equals acceleration times mass. Of course, there are many phenomena that we cannot describe using physical quantities. For example, everything that relates to the development of human society - history, economics, etc. The study of these phenomena, which sometimes requires complex mathematical methods, does not belong to physics. And of course, physics does not exhaust measurements. A fundamental role in it is played by ideas, provisions, laws, sometimes expressed in the form of mathematical equations, and sometimes in a limiting Slivian formulation. Here is a striking example: all bodies are made up of molecules, molecules are made up of atoms, atoms are made up of nuclei and electrons, and nuclei are made up of protons and neutrons. This phrase contains enormous information, the concentration of centuries-old study of nature, study based on the measurement of quantities, which (all!) can be expressed in centimeters, seconds and grams.

The fundamental laws of nature, undoubtedly, include the laws of conservation. In addition to the well-known ones - the law of conservation of energy and momentum - there are also more exotic ones. For example, the law of conservation of baryon charge, which states that the number of a particle such as a proton or neutron does not change during the interaction between particles.*)

*) Antiparticleism is attributed to a baryon charge of the opposite sign (for a proton and neutron the baryon charge is equal to 1, for an antiproton and antiptron - 1).

Or another example: the state of atoms and subatomic particles is characterized by a certain quantity, which is called the wave function. It is most often denoted in Greek by the letter $\psi$. The wave function $\psi$ is a function of the coordinate $\vec{r}$. Since all points of empty space are equivalent, any point can be chosen as the origin of coordinates. So, the function $\psi(\vec{r})$ according to the equation of quantum mechanics can be either even or odd -

$$\begin{align*}
\mathrm{lino} & \psi(-\vec{r}) = \psi(\vec{r}), \\
\mathrm{lino} & \psi(-\vec{r}) = -\psi(\vec{r}),
\end{align*}$$

Moreover, no matter what happened to the particle before, this is a property of the wave conservation function. They say that there is a law of conservation of parity. If a particle has an even wave function, then such a particle can be assigned a parity of +1, if the wave function is odd, then -1. In addition, the wave function of several particles that do not interact with each other is the product of the wave functions of individual particles, and its parity is the product of the parities of individual particles. This makes it possible to generalize the parity conservation law to very complex cases. For example, there are two standard particles - even and odd, and as a result of the birth of new particles; if there are two of them, then one of them must be even and the other odd, and if there are three, then they can all be odd. It is important for us: the dimensionless number +1 or -1 will be preserved.

But to verify this and similar statements, measurements are needed. For this, something must be accounted for by the action on this “something” as their forces. And we again come to physical quantities, the placement of which can be reduced to a centimeter, second, gram. I would hate to be seen as the “enemy” of dimensionless numbers.
