4. Potential distribution in a conductor. Let us show that the potential difference of any two points of a conductor, including conduction points, is equal to zero. Let there be arbitrary points $M$ and $K$ inside the conductor. Let us mentally cross from point $M$ to point $K$ the test charge $q$ along some trajectory $MBK$ lying inside the conductor (Fig. 2). The field forces will not do work on the moving charge $q$, since there is no field inside the conductor. Therefore, the potential difference is $\varphi_M - \varphi_K = 0$. If the points $M$ and $K$, one or both, lie on the surface of a conductor, then the proof that the potential difference between them is zero is similar.

Since the potential difference of any two points of a conductor is zero, then the potential of all points of the conductor, including conduction points, is the same. Therefore, they talk about the potential of a conductor without indicating its specific point. Since all points on the surface of a conductor have the same potential, the surface of the conductor will be an equivalent surface.

5. Cavity inside the conductor. Let's remove part of the substance from the inner region of the conductor. Since the substance being removed is neutral, it should be expected that the electric field at all points outside the conductor, inside the conductor and in the resulting cavity will not change. And this will indeed be the case, and there will be no charges on the inner surface of the conductor (on the surface of the cavity). All the charge of the conductor will be concentrated on the outer surface of the conductor, and the presence of a cavity inside the conductor will not affect the distribution of charge over the outer surface. There will be no field in the cavity or in the conductor. The potential of all points of the conductor and the cavity turns out to be the same.

Interference and interferometers

A light wave passing through a body (or reflected from its surface) carries enormous information about this body: its size, density, shape, etc. But the problem is that this information is protected in the characteristics of the wave, which change with enormous speed both in space and in time. After all, an electromagnetic wave, of which light is a special case, represents the propagation of oscillations of electric and magnetic fields. The propagation speed is $c = 3 \cdot 10^8$ m/s, and the frequency of oscillations in the direction of the electric field ($E$) or inductive field ($B$) in the light wave is of the order of $10^{14}$ Hz.
