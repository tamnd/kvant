To understand what exactly happens during interference, we need to remember something. First of all: a wave with propagation speed $c$ and frequency $\omega$ (or oscillation period $T=2\pi/\omega$) is characterized by wavelength $\lambda=cT=2\pi c$.

Although the magnitude of the direction of the electric field $E$ at each point of the wave is continuously changing, the direction values ​​at two different points are uniquely related to each other. So, for example, if the distance between two points is equal to or a multiple of the wavelength $\lambda$ (sometimes said: a multiple of an even number of half-waves), then the direction values ​​at these points are always the same.
