The watermelon moves in a circle of radius $R$ with a speed $v$ equal to the speed of the airplane, and has a centripetal acceleration $a = v^2/R$ directed towards the center of the circle (Fig. 5). The watermelon is acted upon by a force from gravity $mg$ and an elastic force from a spring scale $F = 15mg/16$. The equation of Newton's second law, written in projections onto the radial direction, has the form $mg - F = ma$. Substituting the expressions for $F$ and $a$ into the last equality, we obtain after simple transformations

$$v = \sqrt{gR}/4 \approx 2 \text{ km/c.}$$

2. To find the sections of the cycle where the temperature increases, we draw a series of isotherms (Fig. 6). Now you can clearly see that the temperature is rising in section 2-3 and in section 1-4 - parts of passage 1-2. Moreover, temperature $T_4$ is the maximum for process 1-2.

Let $T_3$ be the gas temperature at point 3, and $p_1$ and $p_2$ be the pressures at points 1 and 2. Let us express $T_3$ and $p_2$ in terms of $T_1$ and $p_1$. For points 3 and 1 $V_3/T_3 = V_1/T_1$, open $T_3 = T_1V_3/V_1$. By condition $V_3/V_1 = 5/2$, therefore $T_3 = 5T_1/2$. For points 2 and 1 $p_2V_3 = p_1V_1$, thus $p_2 = p_1V_1/V_3 = 2p_1/5$.

Using the first law of thermodynamics for section 2-3, we find the number of heat processes 1-2:

$$Q_{23} = \frac{3}{2} R(T_3 - T_1) = \frac{9}{4} RT_1.$$

To determine the temperature $T_4$, pressure $p_4$ and volume $V_4$, we write the straight line equation for process 1-2 in the form $p = AV + B$, where $A = (p_1 - p_2)/(V_1 - V_3) = -2p_1/(5V_1)$, $B = (p_2V_1 - p_1V_3)/(V_1 - V_3) = 7p_1/5$. So, the required equation of the line

$$p = -\frac{2}{5} p_1 \frac{V}{V_1} + \frac{7}{5} p_1.$$

Excluding pressure $p$ from this equation using the Mendleev-Clapeyron equation $pV = RT$, written for one mole of gas, we obtain the dependence of temperature on volume in process 1-2:

$$T = -\frac{2}{5} \frac{p_1}{V_1} V^2 + \frac{7}{5} \frac{p_1}{R} V.$$

A study of the extremum of this dependence shows that the temperature is maximum at $V = V_4 = 7V_1/4$ and is equal to $T = T_4 = 49T_1/40$. At the same time $p_4 = RT_4/V_4 = 7p_1/10$.

According to the first law of thermodynamics, the amount of heat and energy of the gas supplied to the gas in section 1-4, and $A$ is the work of the gas. Since

$$\Delta U = \frac{3}{2} R(T_4 - T_1) = \frac{27}{80} RT_1,$$

$$A = \frac{p_1 + p_4}{2} (V_4 - V_1) = \frac{51}{80} p_1V_1 = \frac{51}{80} RT_1,$$

we have

$$Q_{14} = \frac{27}{80} RT_1 + \frac{51}{80} RT_1 = \frac{39}{40} RT_1.$$

The final total amount of heat added to the gas in areas where its temperature rises is equal to

$$Q = Q_{23} + Q_{14} = \frac{129}{40} RT_1 \approx 8 \text{ кДж.}$$
