⟦folio 36⟧

Physics 8, 9, 10
The article published below, “On One Amazing Tenacious Error,” is intended for the eighth-classic class, “Conductors in an Electrostatic Field” for the ninth-classic class, and “Interference and Interferometers” for the tenth-classic class.

About one amazing tenacious mistake

Let's imagine such a scene in a physics lesson.

Teacher. A stone is thrown at an angle to the horizon and moves along a parabolic trajectory (we neglect air resistance). What forces act on the stone at the moment when it finds itself at a certain point of the parabola?

Teacher. First of all, the stone experiences the force of gravity. (No more pauses.) In addition, the throwing force acts on the stone, directed tangentially to the trajectory.

Teacher. As you know, force is a measure of the interaction of a body. The force is also due to the attraction of the stone to the Earth. Everything is clear here. What body acts on the stone with the throwing force?

Teacher. Suppose the stone was thrown by hand. This means that the hand determines the throwing force.

Teacher. But the interaction between the stone and the hand stopped as soon as the stone was in flight. You can accumulate speed, energy; it is impossible to accumulate strength. The interaction stopped - and at the same moment the corresponding force disappeared.

Teacher (a little confused). It turns out that only gravity acts on a stone in flight?

Teacher. That's right. Unless, of course, you take into account the interaction of stone with air.
