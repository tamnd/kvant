Problem solutions

M1061—M1064, F1073—F1077

We are going to establish directions on the roads gradually. Let this already be done for roads connecting cities from a certain set $M$ so that the requirement of the problem is satisfied: from any city in this set one can drive to any other of them, following the chosen directions. (To begin with, we can assume that $M$ consists of one city, and the directions to ignore are not established.) Let us prove that if not all cities are included in $M$, then we can select directions on several more roads and increase $M$ while meeting this requirement.

It is clear that there is a road connecting city $A$ from $M$ with city $B$, not included in $M$. Let's construct a path $c=BC_1C_2...C_kA$ from $B$ to $A$ that does not pass along the road $AB$. To do this, take any city $D$, different from $A$ and $B$, and consider the path $a$ from $A$ to $D$, not passing through $B$, and the path $b$ from $D$ to $B$, not passing through $A$ (Fig. 1). As $c$ we can take the path going first along $a$ - to the first city common to $a$ and $b$ - and then along $b$. Let $C_m$ be the first of the cities $C_1$, ..., $C_k$ included in $M$ (if there are no such cities, put $m=k+1$, $C_m=A$). Let's select directions on roads $AB$, $BC_1$, $C_1C_2$, ..., $C_{m-1}C_m$ in order: from $A$ to $B$, from $B$ to $C_1$, etc., and add cities to $M$ $B$, $C_1$, ..., $C_{m-1}$. Our condition will not be violated, since from any annexed city one can get to the “old” set $M$ (up to $C_m$) and any of them can be reached from $M$ (via $A$).

By repeating this process, we will include all cities in $M$. On the “unregulated” roads that remain after this (if there are still any), we will set traffic directions arbitrarily.

You can reason a little differently, first proving that in the conditions of the problem any two cities are connected by two paths going along different roads (this statement is the content of one of the problems of the World Olympiad).

Our problem gives only a sufficient condition. It turns out that the following theorem is true: in order for arrows to be placed on the roads so that traffic in the directions prescribed by them can be reached from any city to any other, it is necessary and sufficient that the original road network be connected and have no isthmuses (i.e., after closing any road, one could get from each city to any other, Fig. 2). This not very complicated theorem was proved in 1939 by G. Robbins*), known, in particular, as R. Courant’s advisor on the wonderful book “What is Mathematics.”

F1073. A small elastic ball is released from a height of $H=1$ m above the floor, and a plate is closed on its way, from which it bounces. What speed will the ball have when it hits the floor? How should the plate be positioned so that the ball hits the floor as far as possible from the starting point? What is this maximum distance?

The speed of the ball at the moment it hits the floor does not depend on where and how the plate is installed (the impact on the plate is elastic), and is determined from the law of conservation of energy:

$$mgH=m \frac{v^2}{2} \Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2gH}.$$

Let's determine where and how the plate needs to be installed so that the distance from the starting point of the ball's trajectory to the point where it hits the floor is maximum.

It is clear that the plate must be installed so that immediately after impact the ball’s velocity vector is directed at an angle $\varphi=45^\circ$ to the horizon. This means that the angle between the ball’s velocity vectors before the impact and immediately after the impact is $45^\circ$, the normal to the plate should make an angle $45^\circ+22,5^\circ=67,5^\circ$ with the horizon, i.e. the plate should be installed at an angle $\alpha=90^\circ+67,5^\circ=157,5^\circ$ to the horizon.

Obviously, the distance the ball will travel will be greater, the greater the speed of the ball at the moment of impact. And this speed is greater, the further the distance from the starting point of the ball’s trajectory. Therefore, the plate must be installed on the floor (see Fig.ok). In this case, the speed of the ball immediately after the impact will be $v=\sqrt{2gH}$, further the ball will lie down
