Today copper, so familiar, is difficult to imagine as a metal of “bubbling secrets”. Although she has a weak history behind her. Copper was the first metal to be used by humans. But because of its softness, the instruments of our distant objects quickly became dull. Copper was replaced by harder and more durable bronzes - alloys of copper with tin, arsenic and other elements. And pure copper retreated into the shadows for a long time.

A new wave of interest in the ancient metal was associated with its high electrical conductivity. In this indicator, copper is slightly inferior only to silver, but do not forget about its price. Pozny, many current-conducting parts are made from copper. But often high electrical conductivity alone is not enough...

What metal should electrical contacts be made of? It is clear that they must conduct current well, but, in addition, it is desirable to ensure their resistance to erosion. The word “erosion” may not be familiar to everyone, but its meaning is simple: this is the name for the destruction of the surface of a part. If the material is not strong enough, then as a result of endless closing and opening, the contacts will lose their shape and fail.

The same combination of high strength properties (over a wide range of operating temperatures) and conductivity is required for welding electrodes, high-temperature conductors, etc.

Is it possible to treat copper in such a way that its resistivity is almost not increased, but the strength of the air is significant?

What is strength?

Under the influence of external forces, materials can change their shape - deform. But this can happen in different ways. If you stretch the elastic band and let it go, it will shrink again. This is an example of elastic deformation. But plasticine, on the contrary, is completely “unable to remember” and obediently accepts any outline into its hands. This deformation is called plastic.

Metals behave under load more like plasticine than rubber. Although it is, of course, much more difficult to deform them. An alloy of copper and aluminum is easy to produce. To do this, you need to take both components in the required proportion, melt them together, mix the liquid and let it cool and crystallize.

The result will be approximately what is shown in figure 3, b. How to add oxygen now?

Let's place a copper-aluminum alloy in a gas atmosphere containing oxygen. At least just into the air. As they diffuse, gas molecules will fill the space between metal atoms.

Well, good. We can prepare an alloy of copper with aluminum and oxygen. What should I do? Now it's time to reveal the idea behind our plan. Aluminum has, as chemists say, a greater affinity for oxygen than copper. In other words, it oxidizes more easily. This means that we can try to create conditions when aluminum was oxidizing, but copper was not. Then aluminum oxide particles would form inside the copper and our goal would be achieved. By the way, it is absolutely not necessary to use aluminum for these purposes. You can choose any other metal with a greater affinity for oxygen than copper. Titanium, hafnium, zirconium, nickel, tin, etc. are suitable.

You've most likely seen how an incompetent tourist's fire goes out even in dry weather. If firewood is piled haphazardly, oxygen access to the burning substance is difficult and the reaction may stop. This does not mean that there is no oxygen at all inside a poorly built fire. Of course, it penetrates there through numerous cracks. But it may not be enough.

We are coming to a very important conclusion. The chemical reaction is a “threshold” phenomenon. For it to occur, it is necessary to create a certain minimum (above the “threshold”) concentration of the reacting substances. Otherwise, the reaction will not work.

We encounter manifestations of this pricit every day. Careful chemical analysis shows that almost all the elements of the periodic table are present around us and in ourselves. Fortunately, their concentrations, as a rule, are below the “threshold”, and they do not react with each other. Otherwise, our life would be difficult to imagine...

Let's return to metals. The oxidation reaction of aluminum is easier than the oxidation reaction of copper because the “threshold” of oxygen concentration is lower for it. This means that it is possible to select such a concentration of oxygen in the gas atmosphere that aluminum will still oxidize, but copper will not.
