As a rule, it is a percentage. This sample is placed in a chamber where it is held at a specially selected concentration of oxygen, at which only aluminum can oxidize. Oxygen atoms begin to diffuse into the sample. At its edge an alloy of three elements is formed - copper, aluminum and oxygen. The beginning of the process is schematically represented at figure 4.

In the ternary alloy zone, the oxidation reaction of aluminum occurs. In this case, aluminum and oxygen atoms leave the copper crystal lattice and “organize” their own independent phase - Al₂O₃ oxide. The interstices of the copper grid are freed, and fresh portions of oxygen enter them from the outside. But the supply of aluminum at the edge of the sample will soon be depleted. The entire outer zone turns out to be simplified by oxide, and the oxidation processes will move further - into the depths of the sample, where oxygen will be continuously “pumped up” from the surface. When the reaction zone passes through the entire sample, the process ends: all aluminum is completely converted into oxide*).

The oxide inside copper is formed in the form of very small particles of the order of hundreds of angstroms in size (1Å=10⁻⁸ cm). They are clearly visible in an electron microscope.

Instead of a conclusion

Let us now arm ourselves with numbers: the experiment shows that the electrical conductivity of the intra-non-oxide material is 80-90% of the electrical conductivity of pure copper, this increases the service life of the product tens of times. And in the “good old” copper there are still many “boiling secrets”!

The alloy that we have described has come a long way from research into the physical foundations of the process and the creation of its mathematical models to industrial production. A similar material based on platinum is now being created. But it will be significantly stronger than pure platinum. Its introduction allows saving a lot of precious metal.

On the outside, intra-non-oxide copper is no different from pure copper, and intra-non-oxide platinum is no different from pure platinum.
