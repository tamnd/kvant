If the brake pads did not act on the wheels of the automatic, then in the absence of punctures between the tires and the highway, the automatic would go down the slope with acceleration. In this case, along the path S, it would “gain” kinetic energy ΔK, and according to the law of conservation of energy

$$\Delta K = mgS \sin \alpha,$$

where mgS sin α is the change in potential energy.

According to the conditions of the problem, the automat's density does not change. This means that the work A of the friction forces (between the brake drums and pads) along the path S is exactly equal to mgS sin α. Let's find a job A.

If the automatic vehicle has passed the path S, then each block has passed along the surface of the drum the path S₁ = Sr/R, and the friction force between each block and the drum has done work

$$A_1 = kNS_1 = kNS \frac{r}{R}.$$

The total work of friction forces is

$$A = 8A_1 = 8kNS \frac{r}{R}$$

(each of the four wheels has two pads).

Thus, according to the law of conservation of energy

$$8kNS \frac{r}{R} = mgS \sin \alpha.$$

From here comes N:

$$N = \frac{mgR \sin \alpha}{8kr} = 630 \text{ Н.}$$
