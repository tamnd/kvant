⟦folio 27⟧

An alloy of copper and aluminum is easy to produce. To do this, you need to take both components in the required proportion, melt them together, mix the liquid and let it cool and crystallize.

The result will be approximately what is shown in figure 3, b. How to add oxygen now?

Let's place a copper-aluminum alloy in a gas atmosphere containing oxygen. At least just into the air. As they diffuse, gas molecules will fill the space between metal atoms.

Well, good. We can prepare an alloy of copper with aluminum and oxygen. What should I do? Now it's time to reveal the idea behind our plan. Aluminum has, as chemists say, a greater affinity for oxygen than copper. In other words, it oxidizes more easily. This means that we can try to create conditions when aluminum was oxidizing, but copper was not. Then aluminum oxide particles would form inside the copper and our goal would be achieved. By the way, it is absolutely not necessary to use aluminum for these purposes. You can choose any other metal with a greater affinity for oxygen than copper. Titanium, hafnium, zirconium, nickel, tin, etc. are suitable.

You've most likely seen how an incompetent tourist's fire goes out even in dry weather. If firewood is piled haphazardly, oxygen access to the burning substance is difficult and the reaction may stop. This does not mean that there is no oxygen at all inside a poorly built fire. Of course, it penetrates there through numerous cracks. But it may not be enough.

We are coming to a very important conclusion. The chemical reaction is a “threshold” phenomenon. For it to occur, it is necessary to create a certain minimum (above the “threshold”) concentration of the reacting substances. Otherwise, the reaction will not work.

We encounter manifestations of this pricit every day. Careful chemical analysis shows that almost all the elements of the periodic table are present around us and in ourselves. Fortunately, their concentrations, as a rule, are below the “threshold”, and they do not react with each other. Otherwise, our life would be difficult to imagine...

Let's return to metals. The oxidation reaction of aluminum is easier than the oxidation reaction of copper because the “threshold” of oxygen concentration is lower for it. This means that it is possible to select such a concentration of oxygen in the gas atmosphere that aluminum will still oxidize, but copper will not.

Now, finally, let's consider the process of internal oxidation as a whole. The initial sample is an alloy of aluminum mixed with copper, in which the share of aluminum does not exceed 30%. This is what copper-based alloy samples look like after internal oxidation. Strengthening particles are SiO₂, particle diameter d≈1000 A (photo a, magnification 100,000) and Al₂O₃, d≈150 A (photo b, magnification 200,000) and d≈50 A (photo c, magnification 200,000). The smaller the particles, the different the material.
