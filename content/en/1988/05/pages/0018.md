It is clear that the density of the atmosphere should decrease with height. The dependence of the density of the Earth's atmosphere on altitude above sea level can be approximately expressed by the so-called Bolytsman barometric formula

$$\rho \approx \rho_0 e^{-\frac{h}{h^*}}$$

suitable up to $h \approx 80 \div 100$ km. In this formula, $\rho_0 = 1,3$ kg/m$^3$ is the density value at sea level, and the quantity $h^* = 7,16$ km in the denominator of the exponent is called the density scale factor. It is clear that at an altitude of $h^*$ the density of the atmosphere is $e = 2,72$ times less than at sea level.

Aerodynamic force

A force acts on a body moving in the air. To verify this, it is enough, for example, to place your palm horizontally at the window of a moving bus or train carriage (carefully, of course!). When turning the palm around the horizontal axis, the force from the air will act on the hand either upward, when the wind hits the palm, or downward, when it hits the back side. In the first case, the angle of attack $\alpha$ (between the plane of the palm and the velocity vector $\vec{v}$ of the oncoming flow) is considered positive, in the second - negative (figure 1).
