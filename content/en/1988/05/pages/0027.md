Today copper, so familiar, is difficult to imagine as a metal of “bubbling secrets”. Although she has a weak history behind her. Copper was the first metal to be used by humans. But because of its softness, the instruments of our distant objects quickly became dull. Copper was replaced by harder and more durable bronzes - alloys of copper with tin, arsenic and other elements. And pure copper retreated into the shadows for a long time.

A new wave of interest in the ancient metal was associated with its high electrical conductivity. In this indicator, copper is slightly inferior only to silver, but do not forget about its price. Pozny, many current-conducting parts are made from copper. But often high electrical conductivity alone is not enough...

What metal should electrical contacts be made of? It is clear that they must conduct current well, but, in addition, it is desirable to ensure their resistance to erosion. The word “erosion” may not be familiar to everyone, but its meaning is simple: this is the name for the destruction of the surface of a part. If the material is not strong enough, then as a result of endless closing and opening, the contacts will lose their shape and fail.

The same combination of high strength properties (over a wide range of operating temperatures) and conductivity is required for welding electrodes, high-temperature conductors, etc.

Is it possible to treat copper in such a way that its resistivity is almost not increased, but the strength of the air is significant?

What is strength?

Under the influence of external forces, materials can change their shape - deform. But this can happen in different ways. If you stretch the elastic band and let it go, it will shrink again. This is an example of elastic deformation. But plasticine, on the contrary, is completely “unable to remember” and obediently accepts any outline into its hands. This deformation is called plastic.

Metals behave under load more like plasticine than rubber. Although it is, of course, much more difficult to deform them.
