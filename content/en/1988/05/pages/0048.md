conservation laws are a consequence of some initial properties of the world around us, namely its symmetry. In particular, the law of conservation of energy is connected with an almost very important property of time (no matter how unexpected this connection may seem) - with its homogeneity. It is quite simple to explain the property itself; as for the derivation of the law of conservation of energy from it, then you will have to take our word for it.

It seems quite common that any experiment that studies some physical phenomenon, whether it was carried out yesterday, today or in a thousand years, should give the same result (if only it is carried out in the same way). Or, to put it another way, physical laws do not change over time. Physicists formulate it this way: time has the property of homogeneity. It is from this almost next property (“almost”, because upon closer reflection many questions may arise, and in general we have a rather vague idea of ​​what time is) that follows the fact that in a closed system the total energy remains constant. And this is the law of conservation of energy.

Before such a period of time, reports appear that somewhere someone observed deviations from the law of conservation of energy. Of course, no physical law is guaranteed against the fact that in some experiment we can leave the scope of application of this law. But when we talk about the law of conservation of energy, we must clearly understand that a statement about the violation of this law is equivalent to a statement about the violation of the homogeneity of time. (This is clearly a missing piece of fantasy writing about the so-called time machine. After all, the creation of such a machine simultaneously means the abolition of the law of conservation of energy. And even then, any miracles arose, starting with the perpetual motion machine and ending with immortality.)

Apparently, it is precisely because of the deep connection of energy with time that energy plays a completely unique, one might say, magical role. Remember Archimedes: “Give me a point of support...”? So, energy is the fulcrum with the help of which, at least in principle, practically all physical problems are solved. However, of all the laws of conservation of energy, this is perhaps the most complex. This is mainly due to the fact that energy exists in various forms: mechanical (kinetic and potential), internal (sometimes called tell energy), electrical, etc. And since the time of Einstein, who formulated the principle of equivalence of energy and mass, it has become clear that when converting energy, the possibility of changing mass must also be taken into account. That is why it is often quite difficult to trace the transition of energy from one form to another and it is completely impossible to write down the law of conservation of energy in a general form. However, for the mechanical energy of a macrocellular system this is relatively easy to do. Only not in the form of a conservation law, but in the form of a law of change in mechanical energy.

So, let's consider some system (it can be one body or several interacting bodies). In particular, a given system can be closed, but this is not necessary, i.e., in principle, external forces can act on it. In this case, some internal processes may occur in the wave system, leading to transitions of mechanical energy into some other types or vice versa. But we will still make one significant assumption - we will assume that we will extract the tello in the system.

Let us denote the mechanical energy of the system by $W$, and all other types of energy, except mechanical, will be denoted by $Q$, by analogy with tell energy, and are designated as the amount of tellotes.
