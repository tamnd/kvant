Before answering these questions, then, the above formulation of Lenz's rule states that the induction current is always directed so as to try to attract the prince of its appearance, and does not say that this attraction (reaction) will necessarily attract. The counteraction of the prince, causing the induced emf, appears and can be detected only when there is an opportunity for the flow of induced current. When such a magnet moves, closed induction currents will appear in the copper plate (red lines on figure 2), the direction of which will change. According to Lenz's rule, the interaction of these currents with an external magnetic field should attract the prince, causing the currents, i.e., the movement of the magnet. Thus, the vibration of the magnet will fade, like the vibration in a very important life.

Closed induction currents that arise in solid conducting bodies moving in magnetic fields or placed in changing magnetic fields are called output currents, or Foucault currents - after the name of the French scientist who discovered them. Depending on the conditions, eddy currents can be either harmful or beneficial.

Foucault currents in transformer cores and rotating parts of electrical generators and motors cause unnecessary heating and waste energy. To weaken the output currents, such parts are made of thin sheets of steel, separated by thin layers of dielectric so that the insulating layers intersect the possibility of the output current flow line.
