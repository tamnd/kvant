CONVERSATION WITH AN ACADEMICIAN
V. P. MASLOV

Victor Pavlovich, when and how did you become interested in physics and mathematics? What will have the greatest impact - school, clubs, family?

I didn’t participate in krupki; at school I wasn’t particularly interested in mathematics or physics. On the contrary, I was quite weak in these subjects, especially in physics. And this was a time when many of my friends were interested in physics, then there was a fashion for physics. Maybe it was my backwardness in physics that played some role: I decided that I did not have enough education, that I needed to go to the physics department in order to take advantage of it.

You probably didn’t take part in the Olympics?

Once I took part in a math olympiad. I went more or less by accident, my friends just went there. It was the second round, I solved only one problem, after which I left. I received a certificate for this.

After graduating from school, you went to study at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. Did you have other options for choosing your life path?

Yes, I was generally more interested in the humanities. But my stepfather, a famous historian, Professor Boris Fedorovich Porshnev, strongly dissuaded me from the humanitarian path. As a matter of fact, he persuaded me to go to the physics department.

Who do you consider to be your teachers? Which of your colleagues influenced you the most?

My teacher is Sergei Vasilievich Fomin. He worked in the fifties at the Department of Mathematics of the Physics Faculty of Moscow State University. In my second year I became interested in certain frequency mathematical problems, in particular algebraic ones. I then turned to the famous algebraic Igor Rostislavovich Shafarvich. I studied with him for some time and even decided to switch to mechanical engineering. But when I wanted to do this, Professor Anatoly Alekseevich Blasov, whose department I was in, did not let me down. To test whether I was really a mathematician, he gave me a mathematical problem. I solved it pretty quickly. But Blasov did not want to understand this evidence, since it was not direct, as he apparently wanted, but indirect.
