M1081—1085, F1093—1097

We will assume that the “penultimate digit” of a single-digit number is 0.

If the number $N$ ends with the digits $ab$, then, obviously, the last two digits of the number $3N$ coincide with the last two digits of the number $3(10a+b)=30a+3b$. Therefore, if $a$ is even, the penultimate digits of the numbers $3N$ and $3b$ will be of the same parity. Let's write down several degrees of faith 3:

$$3^1=3, 3^2=9, 3^3=27, 3^4=81.$$

IX last numbers - 3, 9, 7, 1; then they will repeat periodically. The penultimate digits of the numbers $3 \cdot 3=9, 3 \cdot 9=27, 3 \cdot 7=21, 3 \cdot 1=3$ are even, therefore, if the statement of the problem is true for $3^n$, then it is also true for $3^{n+1}$. And since it is true for $3^3=27$, we conclude by induction that it is also true for all natural $n>2$.

V. I. Plachko
