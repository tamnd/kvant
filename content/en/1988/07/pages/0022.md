It is easy to imagine how the astronomers were tasked. After all, according to the theory of relative science, no body can move at a speed exceeding the speed of light in a vacuum. This is one of the most important laws of physics. After the first surprise and even confusion, a solution to this paradox was - at least in principle - found. Superluminal speeds turned out to be a cosmic optical illusion.

Here is the simplest example showing how the illusion of superluminal movements arises.

Let's imagine that we have a “flashlight” that moves towards us, but not exactly along the line of sight, but at a slight angle to it (Fig. 4). First, our flashlight is turned off, and then twice - at points $S_1$ and $S_2$ - it turns on and sends two light signals in our direction. During the time between its two inclusions, the light source traveled a path $S_1S_2$ on our figure. The displacement we see is smaller: it represents a projection $S_1S'$ of this path onto what is called the picture plane (having fixed the source at point $S_1$, observed from point $O$, the further movement of the source will be projected onto a plane perpedicular to the line of sight $OS_1$ - this is the picture plane).
