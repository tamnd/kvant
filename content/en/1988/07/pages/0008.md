The theorem from Problem 4 was known before Clifford. He, however, came up with a remarkable design (Clifford's goal) in which this task is included. A complete n-supporter of N is defined as n “general position” lines; N contains n complete (n-1)-siders $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$, obtained by drawing lines one at a time. The Clifford point of a “complete 2-side” (a, b) is the point of intersection of lines a and b, and the Clifford circle of a complete 3-side (a, b, c) is a circle containing three Clifford points of 2-sides (a, b), (a, c) and (b, c) (i.e., a circle circumscribed around the triangle from side a, b, c). Further, if n is even, then the n Clifford circles of (n-1)-supporters $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$ intersect at one point—the Clifford point of the complete n-supporter N (for n=4, this is the theorem of Problem 4). If n is odd, then the n Clifford points of (n-1)-supporters $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$ belong to the same circle—the Clifford circle of the complete n-supporter N.

Kreple was not a prominent scientist, although he is a member of the Berlin (Prussian) and corresponding member of the St. Petersburg (Russian) Academies of Sciences. But a large entrepreneur is obliged to be a good breaker in people - and Kreple proved that he possesses these abilities to the fullest.
