Introduction

We all literally come across the processes of evaporation and condensation at every step. And although a boiling kettle will immediately be imagined as large, of course, the most important example is the natural whirlpool of water, without which life on Earth would be at least different.

So, the solar body evaporates moisture at the earth's surface, convective points and diffusion allow parameters to reach high atmospheric layers. As you move upward, the air temperature drops, vapors condense and form clouds. The merging and enlargement of droplets inside the clouds is completed before jem - the cycle closes.

We will talk about one of the most important links in this chain - the formation of droplets when cooling water vapor, but first let us recall one important concept.

As you know, saturated vapors are those that are in equilibrium with the liquid phase. Each temperature $T$ corresponds to its specific value $p^0(T)$ of saturated vapor pressure (Fig. 1). Line $p^0(T)$ divides the plane $p, T$ into areas corresponding to the liquid and gaseous states.

Equilibrium between a liquid and a gas is an example of so-called dynamic equilibrium. Through the interface there is a constant exchange of molecules between the phases: there are, as it were, counter processes of evaporation and condensation. If the vapors are saturated, then the flows of particles in both directions are the same and the amount of substance in both phases is unchanged.

The concentration of saturated vapor is determined by the rate of evaporation per unit surface area of ​​the liquid, i.e., the density of saturated vapor is a measure of the volatility of a substance.
