Constructions using one ruler

Propositions 1-3 of Euclidean “Principles” assert the possibility of unlimited continuation of a given line segment, drawing a line through two known points and drawing a circle with a given center (point) and radius (segment). Together with the assumptions implied by Euclid, but not explicitly formulated, about the possibility of indicating the point of intersection of two given (for example, each with two properties points) lines, two known circles (defined by properties by centers and radii) and given straight lines and circles, these statements describe the entire array of “Euclidean-solvable” construction problems: these problems must coincide with the listed construction problems, i.e., be solved using a ruler and a compass.

In 1797, Lorenzo Mascheroni’s (1750-1800) book “Geometry of the Compass” was published in Italy, in which it was established that all construction problems solvable by a compass and a straightedge are also solvable using a compass alone (with the natural limitation that a straight segment, of course, cannot be constructed with a compass - however, knowing two of its points, you can use a compass to indicate an unlimited number of others points of the segment). Much later, a book by Georg Mohr (1640-1697), published in 1672 (125 years before Mascheroni!) in Dutch and Danish, was found, in which the same theorem was proven.
