⟦folio 31⟧

Tasks

M1111—M1115, F1123—F1127

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than October 1, 1988 to the address: 103006 Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant».
Send solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 7 - 88” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1111” or “F1123”.
In the column “...sender’s address” please write your first and last name legibly. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope).

Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”).
At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.

M1111. A circle is circumscribed about the corresponding triangle ABC. The tangents to the circle drawn at points A and C intersect the tangent drawn at point B at points M and N, respectively. In triangle ABC, the altitude BP is drawn (point P lies on side AC). Prove that the line BP is a bisector of angle MPN.

V. I. Chinik

M1112. There are two numbers written on the board: 1 and 2. It is allowed to add new numbers as follows: if there are numbers a and b on the board, then you can write the number ab+a+b. Is it possible to get a) the number 13121 using this method? 6) number 12131?

Prove that there are an infinite number of natural numbers that cannot be represented in the form

c) x+y+xy, d) x+y+2xy with natural x and y.

A. A. Berzins, V. G. Ilchtsev

M1113*. There are 21 cities in the country. Aviation communication between them is carried out by several airline companies, each of which serves 10 free airline companies, connecting in pairs with certain cities (in this case, planes of several companies can fly between two cities). Every two cities are connected by at least one free airline. Is this possible with each of the airline's local consultants?

D. V. Fomin

M1114. Prove that for any tetrahedron the inequality holds

$$r < \frac{ab}{2(a+b)},$$

where a, b are the lengths of two crossing edges, and r is the radius of the inscribed ball.

I. F. Sharyzin

M1115*. a) The first line contains 19 natural numbers not exceeding 88, and the second line contains 88 natural numbers not exceeding 19. Let us use a segment of one or more numbers of numbers in one line. Prove that you can select a segment from the given rows so that the sums of the numbers in them are equal.

6) Let n, m, k be natural numbers. Prove that if

$$1+2+\ldots+n=mk,$$

then the numbers 1, 2,..., n can be divided into k groups so that the sums of the numbers in each group are m.

A. V. Anzhans
