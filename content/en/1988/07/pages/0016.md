You can read more about rings made of tetradra in the fascinating book by W. Ball and G. Coxeter “Mathematical All Things and Entertainment” (M., Mir, 1986).

Flexes by Robert Connelly and Klaus Steffen

It must be said that a ring of tetradra as a bendable polyhedron raises a number of objections. First of all, there is a hole in it. Secondly, there are edges, each of which has four faces. So it is not clear whether this ring should be called a polyhedron.

To avoid any doubts, when searching for flexes, one could limit oneself to only convex polyhedra, that is, polyhedra lying on one side of each of their faces. But there is the famous Cauchy theorem that any convex polyhedron is inflexible (see, for example, the article by N.P. Dolbilin “Rigidity of convex polyhedra”, «Kvant», 1988, No. 5). It was proven in 1813. Although this theorem does not exclude the existence of non-convex flexes, many mathematicians believed that such flexes also do not exist. Therefore, the flexor, discovered by Robert Connelly in 1977, is a sensation. At one time, the construction proposed by Connelly was discussed in the article by V. Zalgaler “Continuously flexible polyhedron” («Kvant», 1978, No. 9) and seemed to me then so complicated that I didn’t even begin to understand it. However, Klaus Steffen soon managed to come up with a flexor so simple that it can be glued together in literally a matter of minutes. How to do this is shown on page 4 of the cover «Kvant» of No. 5 for this year.

Flexmas

Flexms are creatures that inhabit the world of flexagons and flexes. You've probably already noticed the image of flexan (p. 13). To use with flexmans, cut out a square of thick paper with a side of 15-20 cm. It needs to be diagonally with the mushroom up and along the dashed line (figure 16, a) with the mushroom down, and then folded to form a triangle. Now you will need to perform four identical operations. The result of the first of them - sibb along the dashed line figure 16, b - is depicted on figure 16, c, the final result - on figure 16, d. There remain four more identical operations - bending small triangles, and before us is a flexman.

The most useful property of flexans is their ability to walk on inclined planes. It is necessary to place the Flexan on a fairly gentle inclined plane, and it immediately begins to descend along it in small steps. Each of the flexans has a unique characteristic or, in any case, a unique gait. I think that getting to know him will make you feel comfortable.
