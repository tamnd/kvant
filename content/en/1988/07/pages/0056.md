Alma said my plan was great. In any case, she needed to remove the map of the island, and the method proposed by many allows her to complete this task faster than others.

“Paint at my expense,” she generously offered.

Here we come to the very place where I began my story. We took spray paint from a contractor who accepts orders for construction work. I bought twenty thousand gallons of the cheapest paint. Returning to the island, we easily recruited a team of Hiiki boys and trained them to use the sprayer.

Aguz was appointed foreman. We marked the territory of each tribe with paint of the same color as on my “map”. It would have been too expensive to paint the entire area, so we decided to spray the paint in spots about ten feet in diameter at one hundred foot intervals. From an airplane, each tribe's territory would appear to be painted with polka dots, and the boundaries would be easily distinguishable.

Each time I accompanied the team to supervise the work. Everything was done as it should. There was no doubt that the four territories had a common border: each of them had a territory of a different color adjacent to some part of the border.

Five colors would have to be decisive!

We started spraying yellow paint on the twelfth day of work. The Yellow Territory was already bordered by paint, green and purple. We were approaching the blue territory. My nerves were on edge.

The painting crew moved slowly through the underbrush. The setting sun cast long shadows. A parrot with beautiful bright plumage, having received its portion of paint from the spray, took off noisily and disappeared, emitting productive screams. A small brown snake, splashed with yellow paint, slithered away to a secluded place. Suddenly I grabbed Alma by the shoulder.

- I swear by the shadow of Mobius! “I exclaimed hoarsely, unable to stop my white-beating heart. “I see a blue spot from here!”

Alma's beautiful gray eyes sparkled with triumph.

- So who was right?

I sat down on a large tree stump and wiped the sweat that was rolling down my face. My head was pounding from unbearable pain. There was a knock in the views. I was completely confused. According to the dialect, five regionalists could not have a common border. I knew that the four-color problem had been proven for the case where the country number does not exceed 35. But what if some error had crept into these proofs? If the island really disproves the four-color problem, my discovery will be one of the greatest turning points in topology!

A few days later, when the next flight arrived from Monrovia, we decided to take an aerial photograph of the island. Unfortunately, the plane was small, with two open cockpits, so only a photographer with a camera could fly. Once the pictures are taken, the pilot will drop off the photographer and take me to get an aerial view of the painted island.
