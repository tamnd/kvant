In the second half of the 18th and early 19th centuries, the remarkable humanist teacher Juhann Heinrich Pestaloschi (1746-1827) lived in Switzerland. Nowadays, Pestaloshi is considered throughout the world to be the founder of the theory of primary education of children, the idea of ​​​​connecting education with productive labor. We could talk about him for a long time - but Pestaloshi is not our hero here. Pestalosi tested and implemented his ideas in the excellent primary boarding school he founded for poor children. Pestaloshi's staff traveled throughout Switzerland, selecting prospective students and persuading (often the hardest) their parents to send their children to school.

In 1814, one of Pestaloshi's last employees met a young shepherd, Jacob Steiner, the son of a poor peasant, in the mountains of Switzerland. Jacob at that time barely knew how to read and write; as a student he acquired some knowledge of mathematics and astronomy, which was especially fascinating to him at that time. This knowledge and interests of the young shepherd amazed Pestaloshi’s employee, who began to convince Steiner the father to refuse the services of valuable help and send his son to school. The author of this article, Isaac Monseevich Yaglom, died on April 17 of this year. Readers «Kvant» know this name well: I. M. Yaglom owns such a large number of popular books on mathematics that probably everyone interested in mathematics has read something written by him. Over the years, more than ten of his articles were published on the pages of the magazine. For more than forty years of his life he faithfully served the popularization of mathematics.

Isaac Monseevich Yaglom was born in 1921. Fortunately, when he was in high school, wonderful mathematicians enthusiastically organized mathematical clubs and competitions at Moscow University. In 1938, Yaglom received first place at the Olympiad, and the next year, having become a student, I. M. Yaglom turned out to be one of the most active leaders of the circle. Even before the war, D. O. Shklyarsky, who died at the front, decided to create a collection of circle problems and began collecting materials. Shklyarsky's plan was completed after the war, with most of the work done by Isaac Monseevich, who became the main custodian of the “circle wisdom.” The three-volume book “Problems and Theorems of Elementary Mathematics” by D. O. Shklyarsky, N. N. Chentsov and I. M. Yaglom has for many years become the main guide for children interested in mathematics. This book opened the “Mathematics Circle Library” series. Several more books by I.M. Yaglom were published in this series.

For Yaglom, it is important that already in school years children can not only solve isolated red problems, but also, by working on good planning of a series of problems, they can continuously move on to professional mathematical activity. Such “Library” books as “Convex Figures” (written together with V. G. Boltyansky), “Non-elementary problems in elementary presentation” (together with A. M. Yaglom), and the two-volume book “Geometric Transformations” are oriented towards this.

In the last year of his life, Isaac Monseevich was acutely concerned with the humanitarian aspects of mathematics: he wrote a lot about its history, connections with the natural and human sciences, and its place in the life of society. This activity fully demonstrated high culture and broad erudition, which gave birth to a person who had the good fortune to be personally acquainted with Isaac Monseevich.

We invite the reader's attention to become dedicated to Jakob Steiner, the largest representative of the “golden age” of German geometry. It is very characteristic that the attention of I.M. Yaglom was attracted by this colorful, controversial figure. In Steiner, a fantastic geometric intuition and uneducated conservatism coexisted, expressed in a categorical opposition to coordinates and complex numbers in geometry, which, as he was more confident, would lead to the destruction of the true two-dimensional geometry.
