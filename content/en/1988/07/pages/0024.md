Then $\sin \vartheta_0 = \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}$, and the maximum value of the apparent speed is
$$\tilde{v}_{\text{max}} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}}.$$
The speed $\tilde{v}_{\text{max}}$ tends to infinity when the speed of real movement $v$ tends to the speed of light $c$.

To complete our consideration, it remains to compare two quantities: the time interval between signal emissions - $(\Delta t)_0 = t_1 = \frac{s}{v}$ and the time interval between their reception - $\Delta t = \frac{s}{v} (1 - \frac{v}{c} \cos \vartheta)$.

Perhaps the reader will have a question: why did our ratio $(\Delta t)_0 / \Delta t$ not coincide with the ratio $T_0 / T$ from formula (1)? Where did the set of $\sqrt{1 - (\frac{v}{c})^2}$ go? The fact is that this factor, found in almost all formulas of the theory of relativity (it is called the relativistic root), is associated with a more subtle effect, which eludes our simple calculation: after all, we did not use the theory of relativity in its entirety, but only the fact of the finiteness of the speed of light. We can say that we have made a calculation in an approximation in which all powers of $\frac{v}{c} < 1$ higher than the first are neglected. And the formula for the Doppler effect is an exact result of the theory of relativity.

We hope that the reader understands how the illusion of movements at superluminal speeds can arise. But the flashlight example we looked at is not the only conceivable possibility. Here's another situation that we suggest you figure out on your own. Let there be a certain strip perpendicular to the line of sight, which at some moment all flashed simultaneously. What will be seen observed?

You can see not a stripe, but a flaring sphere. This could be the shell of a quasar, illuminated by its central source. In this case, the picture of the observed phenomenon - an increase in the size of the light of the brushed spot - will correspond to superluminal speeds. It is easy to come up with other models of this kind.

It is much more difficult to find out what the actual nature of the powerful explosions, flashes, and emissions that create the illusion of superluminal movements is. This is a task for further research.
