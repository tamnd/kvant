If a ray passes from a less dense medium to a more dense one (Fig. 1, a), it is pressed against the normal (α₂ < α₁), and if from a more dense medium to a less dense one (Fig. 1, b), it moves away from the normal (α₂ > α₁). On the contrary, when passing from a more dense medium to a less dense medium, rays incident at angles greater than α₀ will be completely reflected. This is why this angle is called the angle of total reflection. Mathematically, this manifests itself in the fact that equation (*) has no solutions for α₁ for α₂ > α₀ and n₂ > n₁.

These phenomena can be clearly imagined as follows. It is often called the cone of total reflection. Outside this cone it will be dark.
