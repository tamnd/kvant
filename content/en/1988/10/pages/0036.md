$$0 = \frac{\omega_1^2}{2g}(kl)^2 + C \Rightarrow C = -\frac{\omega_1^2}{2g}k^2l^2.$$

The final equation (1) takes the form:

$$y = \frac{\omega_1^2}{2g}(x^2 - k^2l^2).$$

This is a parabolic equation; here $\omega_1$ denotes the angular velocity at which the $k$ part of the day area is exposed.

To determine $\omega_1$ we use the property of incompressibility of a liquid. The initial volume of liquid $V = 2lh_0a$ during rotation will be distributed equally on both sides of the rotation axis: $V/2 = Sa$, where $S$ is the area under the surface of the liquid, i.e. part of the plane, under the graph of the function $y=f(x)$ on the segment $kl \leq x \leq l$. This area of ​​the curved trapezoid corresponds to a certain interval taken in the range from $kl$ to $l$:

$$S = \int_{kl}^{l} ydx = \frac{\omega_1^2}{2g}\left(\int_{kl}^{l} x^2dx - k^2l^2 \int_{kl}^{l} dx\right) =$$

$$= \frac{\omega_1^2l^3}{6g}(1 - 3k^2 + 2k^3).$$

Now from equality

$$lh_0a = a \frac{\omega_1^2l^3}{6g}(1 - 3k^2 + 2k^3)$$

we get

$$\omega_1 = \frac{1}{l} \sqrt{\frac{6gh_0}{1 - 3k^2 + 3k^3}}.$$

The pressure on the bottom does not change during rotation. It is still equal to the force of the heavy liquid poured into the cove. The force of pressure on the side wall returns. Let's show it.

Let us mentally select a thin wall column of liquid with a height of $h$. From the condition of its vertical equilibrium it follows that the fluid pressure on this column at its lower base is equal to $p_h = \rho gh$ (see Fig. 2). But the pressure at a given point in the liquid is isotropic (Pascal's law). Consequently, the fluid pressure on the wall when the cove rotates linearly depends on the depth. If the liquid near the side wall during rotation at a speed $\omega_1$ rises to a height $H$, then the force of pressure on the wall can be expressed through the average pressure $p_{cp} = \rho gH/2$:

$$F_1 = p_{cp}Ha = \frac{\rho ga}{2}H^2.$$

In the resting cove there was

$$F_0 = \frac{\rho ga}{2}h_0^2.$$

Thus, the increase in pressure force will be $n = (H/h_0)^2$ times.
