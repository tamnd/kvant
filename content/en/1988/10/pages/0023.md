⟦folio 21⟧

The proportion of molten material increases with crater size. When the average size reaches the characteristic temperature of size or chemical transformation of rocks - approximately 300 degrees, this proportion becomes overwhelming. Mass melting occurs when craters with radii exceeding 30 km form on Earth (on the Moon - 10 km). Correspondingly, the mass of the meteorite for the formation of such a crater in order of magnitude should exceed 3 × 10^14 kg. Such craters are evidence of rare events. Their size traces have been preserved throughout almost the entire geological history of the Earth, however, only a few craters with a radius of more than 30 km have been discovered on the entire planet.

Starting from about this size, the formula $R \sim E^{1/4}$ becomes inapplicable, since taking into account the heat of fusion makes the energy balance of the metorite more complex. Craters with massive rock sizes and appearance look different. As the size increases, a new feature becomes more and more noticeable - frozen concentric waves. Already craters with a radius of more than 1 km have a distinct central rise, and the imprints of gradational catastrophic collisions - craters with radii of more than 30 km have 3-4 ridges and rotations. What is clearly visible is not the size of the erosions nor the multi-ring structures of the giant craters on the Moon hidden by sediments. Look at the photo of the lunar crater Orientale - the concentric rings in its relief look like waves running through the water from a fallen stone.

Further, when small craters form, part of the rock and the meteorite itself scatters in the form of a melt. Such stone drops scattered in flight are called texts. When large craters form, texts scatter over hundreds and thousands of kilometers, forming text fields around the craters. The boundaries of text fields are especially clearly sequenced at the bottom of the oceans, where the sedimentary layer grows quite slowly. For example, from the Bosumtwi crater (radius 5 km), formed a little over a million years ago on the shore of the Gulf of Guinea, there is an oval-shaped tectin field $2000 \times 1000$ km into the ocean. There is a tectin field on Earth that occupies almost the entire Indian Ocean! However, traces of its (underwater?) crater have not yet been discovered.
