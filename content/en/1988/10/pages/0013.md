Equation $x^3+px+q=0$

Teacher, the question may have arisen: “Is it worth fencing the garden? After all, it is already clear that the number of roots of a quadratic equation depends on the sign of the discriminant!” The fact is that our method allows us to study a cubic equation, which is much more difficult to solve. Moreover, it is suitable for determining the number of roots and equations that cannot be solved in radicals at all. (In passing, note that the problem of the number of roots of an equation, in contrast to the problem of calculating them, always has a solution: there is an algorithm that determines the number of its roots from the coefficients of the equation.)
