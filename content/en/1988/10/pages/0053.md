Task 1. A ray of light falls on a plane-parallel plate with a thickness of $H=1$ cm from a stack with a refractive index of $n=1,73$ (Fig. 4). Due to multiple reflections from the edges of the plate, a number of light spots are formed on the screen E. Find the distance between the spots if the angle of focus is $\alpha=60^\circ$, and the beam above is perpendicular to the refraction of the screen. The plane of the beam coincides with the plane figure.

After a single refraction on both faces of the plate, the beam exits the plate parallel to the parachute beam. Since during further reflections and refractions the angles of emphasis on the face are the same, all rays hitting the screen are parallel to the parachute one. We find the distance $d$ between these rays, equal to the distance between the spots on the screen, from figure:

$$d = AC \cos \alpha, \ AC = 2H \ tg \beta,$$

$$tg \beta = \frac{\sin \beta}{\sqrt{1-\sin^2 \beta}}, \ sin \beta = \frac{\sin \alpha}{n},$$

where

$$d = \frac{H \sin 2\alpha}{\sqrt{n^2-\sin^2 \alpha}} \approx 0,58 \text{ см.}$$

Task 2. Estimate the error when fixing the angular position of a star visible from the Earth at an angle $\beta=45^\circ$ above the horizon. The refractive index of air at the Earth's surface is $n=1,0003$.

The position of the star, visible from Earth, differs from the true one due to the transformation of atmospheric rays. The thickness of the atmosphere, i.e. the height at which there is practically no air and therefore the refractive index is equal to unity, is several tens of kilometers. This is much less than the radius of the Earth, so in this case the atmosphere can be considered flat. Its refractive index subsequently changes from unity at the upper layers to a value of $n>1$ at the Earth's surface. Parallel rays come from the star, indicating the upper layers of the atmosphere at an angle $\pi/2-\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the true angular position of the star above the horizon. And we see the star at an angle $\beta>\alpha$ (Fig. 5). According to the law of refraction in an elephant medium

$$\sin \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \alpha \right) = n \sin \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \beta \right),$$

or

$$\cos \left( \beta - (\beta - \alpha) \right) = (1 + (n-1)) \cos \beta.$$

Given that $n-1 \ll 1$ and therefore $\beta-\alpha \ll \beta$, the approximation has:

$$\cos \beta + (\beta-\alpha) \sin \beta = \cos \beta + (n-1) \cos \beta.$$

Thus,

$$\beta-\alpha = (n-1) \text{ ctg } \beta = 3 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ rad} \approx 1'.$$
