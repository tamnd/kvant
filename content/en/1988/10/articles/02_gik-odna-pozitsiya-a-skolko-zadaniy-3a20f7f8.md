ONE POSITION -
AND HOW MANY TASKS!

Among the various genres of chess problems, there is this one: you can come up with one position, and there are several tasks for it. Let's look at an example that was once published in Kvanit.

A. Kuznetsov, N. Plaskin. White: Krs3, La5, h6; Black: Kb1, point a6, h7. Checkmate in 2 moves on a regular board and on a vertical cylindrical board.

On the general board everything is simple: 1. R:a6 Krs1 2. Ra1×. On a vertical cylinder (lines “a” and “h” are glued to each other) on 1. R:a6 follows 1...ha, and on any retreat of the rook - 1...a5. Solves 1. Ra5—a5! The rook goes around the steep path and remains in its place. 1...Krs1 2. Ra1×.

This task attracted the attention of V. Popov, a regular correspondent for the Chess Page from Donetsk. He managed to reduce the number of actions and give the game more dynamism.

Checkmate in 2 moves on a regular board and on a vertical cylindrical board.

1. Kr:g4! a2 2. Фc1×; on the cylinder, move 1. Kp:g4 is not suitable due to the answer 1...Kph1! Solves 1. Qc2—c2! Kh1 2. Qb1× (1...a2 2. Qc1×).

V. Popov did not limit himself to this idea, but continued in PONSIAN style. Having moved the king from g3 to g2, and the pawn from a3 to a4, he formulated a new task (for each of the boards): checkmate in 3 moves.

1. Kg3! Now the answer 1...a3 leads to consideration of a two-move, however, the h1 square on the black king's cylinder is freed, but - 1...Kph1 2. Qh2×.

Another condition: The queen is moved to b3, the black pawn from a4 to a5 and the task is checkmate in 4 moves (on the general board and on the vertical cylindrical board).

After 1. Kg3! a4 2. Qc2 a3 we arrive at the original two-move. Extending the solution in itself is not so interesting, but false traces add additional elegance to the problem: 1. Qc2? g3! It turns out that there is an order of moves: if now 2. Kph3 is on the cylinder, then 2...a4+! 3. Kra3 Kh1! 4. Qh2+gh!

There are known positions in which, in addition to our two tasks, two more are added: checkmate is placed on a horizontal cylindrical board and on a torus. And the next position can be turned into nine different tasks at once! This was established by participants in a competition held by the magazine “64 - Chess Review”.

1. Winning.
As the simplest study of the position (retroanalysis) shows, after assessing Black’s move, it is they who have the upper hand - 1...ab, etc.

2. Cooperative checkmate in 2 moves.
In the cooperative task, black starts and helps white achieve the goal. 1. a6 b7+ 2. Kra7 b8Ф×.

3. Serial cooperative checkmate in 8 moves.
