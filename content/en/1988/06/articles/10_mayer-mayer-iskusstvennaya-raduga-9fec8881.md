LOSSING THEIR SIZE, THEY FALL. All this is reflected in the rainbow - in the brightness of its color, the width of the corresponding light stripes, and its lifespan. When there are few drops, the rainbow shines and disappears.

artificial rainbow

V. V. MAYER, R. V. MAYER

The theory of the rainbow or random observations of this natural phenomenon is one thing, and its experimental study is quite another. But to do this, you must first obtain the rainbow artificially.

Artificial rainbows have been known for a long time. In one of the experiments, a round flask filled with water is illuminated through a hole in the screen with a parallel beam of light and a color camera is observed appearing on the screen. The main drawback of this experiment is that it does not entirely reflect the real state of things: a real rainbow is obtained not from one drop, but from a huge number of drops. You can do it another way: using a spray bottle or a small fountain, create a cloud of drops falling in the air and observe the rainbow on them. The conditions of such an experiment correspond to natural ones, but obtaining the required cloud is not at all easy, and experimenting with it is not very pleasant.

We offer you one more experience and hope that you will definitely complete it.

Prepare a new sheet of dioral or tin approximately 200x200 mm in size, a candle, water or glycerin, a microelectric motor, a 4.5 V battery, a pencil lead or a piece of nichrome conductor with a diameter of 0.2-0.3 mm and a length of 200-400 mm, an eraser, steel wiring with a diameter of about 0.3 mm and a length of 100 mm (such wiring can be obtained by stretching a suitable spring), a tin or polystyrene lid for the jar. It turns out that using the listed items, you can get a stable set of examples of identical drops, ready for observing and studying the rainbow.

The main idea is the following: if a liquid is sprayed onto a non-wettable surface, then under the influence of surface tension forces the droplets turn into liquid balls. And this is exactly what is needed to create a rainbow. And now let's get down to business.

You can wash the metal plate with soap and dry it out. Stir it over a candle flame to create a layer of soot evenly on the surface of the plate. Water and glycerin do not mix the soot, but if the air humidity is high, the soot can peel off from the plate and “get wet,” as it were. To eliminate this undesirable phenomenon, the plate must first be coated with a thin layer of Moment glue, nitro varnish or nitro paint, and after this layer has dried, soot will be applied.

Glycerin is preferable to water in experiments because it evaporates much more slowly. Further, in hot weather, you will be able to observe the “glycerin radut” for several days, and after an hour there will be no trace of the “water” radut.

To apply droplets of liquid to smoked surfaces, you need to spray. Unfortunately, a regular spray bottle is not suitable for this. The fact is that it produces drops whose sizes fluctuate

within a fairly wide range, and on such drops the rainbow turns out to be smeared, without contrast. Good rainbows produce approximately equal drops with a diameter of 0.3 to 1 mm.

One of the possible designs of a droplet generator is shown in figure 1. The microelectrodriver 1 is fixed to the base 3 with a rigid crimp 2 and two bolts. A piece of eraser 4 is mounted on the motor shaft, through which a steel wire 5 is passed. The ends of the wire, when the shaft rotates, are alternately immersed in a liquid linted into a shallow jar (lid) 6, and, leaving it, splashes drops. It is clear that the diameter of the droplets is determined by the diameter of the wire, the depth of immersion in the liquid and the speed of its rotation. To be able to change this speed, connect the microelectrodriver to the battery through an improvised rheostat consisting of a stylus or a piece of low-voltage conductor. Drops falling from the ends of the rotating wire fly not only in the direction shown in figure, but also in other directions, therefore, the drop generator must be limited from the sides by a U-shaped protective screen made of cardboard. Placing a sheet of black paper in front of the open part of the screen, adjust the rotation speed of the splashing wire (it should be no more than a few revolutions per second) and determine the place where the drops fall. Insert a smoked metal plate there and, moving it, add as evenly as possible

To observe a rainbow, it is enough to place a plate with drops under straight rays and select the required angle φ between these rays and the direction of observation (Fig. 2; the rainbow is shown schematically by a curved line). As follows from the rainbow theory, this angle for water should be equal to φ_b=42°, and for glycerin - φ_r=27°.

If everything is done well enough, you will see a beautiful bright rainbow and, perhaps, next to it a second, much fainter one. The phenomenon of the present is so impressive that it is really worth the effort to see it.

When viewed from above, you will notice a dimly lit circle with a multi-colored border, which is a rainbow (Fig. 3; a rainbow is schematically shown as a circle). Questions for independent work

1. Is the rainbow you created similar to the one sometimes seen in the dew? Does the sequence of colors in an artificial radtura correspond to that which occurs in a natural one?

2. Obtaining the observation scheme presented in figure 3, measure the distances OA, OS, OB, calculate the angles α and β and determine the angle φ at which the rainbow is observed on water and glycerin drops. Do your measurements support the theoretical calculations?

3. Explain why in your experiments an illuminated white area is obtained inside the rainbow (see Fig. 4), but in nature the area is not observed either.

4. When you place a Polaroid film in front of your eye, on a full rainbow you will notice two diametrically opposite dark stripes that rotate as the Polaroid rotates. What is the reason for this phenomenon?

5. Show experimentally that the smaller the diameter of the drops, the weaker the red color of the rainbow, the darker the philological color, and in general the rainbow becomes more whitish.
