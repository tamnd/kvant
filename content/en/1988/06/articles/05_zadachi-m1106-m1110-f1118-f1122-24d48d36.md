Tasks

M1106 — M1110, F1118 — F1122

M1106. Each of the three primroses connecting the midpoints of opposite sides of a convex hexagon divides its area in half. Prove that these three primaries intersect at one point.

V.V. Arbitrary

M1107. Prove that if $a, b$ and $c$ are the side lengths of the triangle, then

$$2 \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a} \right) \geq \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 3.$$

L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1108. In a convex $n$-gon ($n > 4$), no three diagonals pass through the same point inside the polygon. What is the maximum number of diagonals in it that can be checked so that all the parts into which they split the $n$-gon turn out to be triangles?

M. Khovanov, 10th grade student, Moscow

M1109. In one of the second geometry assignments, the following task was placed: calculate the length of the side of a right triangle placed in parabolic $y = x^2$. The instructions for the problem said that one of the vertices of the triangle coincides with the vertex of the parabolic. Is this indication correct? can the side length of a right triangle inserted into this parabola be equal to a) 3; b) 1988?

V. S. Shevelev

M1110. For a given natural number $n > 1$, we write down the most common divisors of all possible pairs of distinct numbers from 1 to $n$. Prove that a) the arithmetic mean of all $n(n-1)/2$ numbers written out increases without limit with $n$, but does not exceed $\ln n + 1$; b) their geometric mean does not exceed 10 for any $n$.

V. F. Lev

F1118. A fisherman who lived at the mouth of a river that flowed into the ocean moved to a new place of residence several kilometers upstream. To his surprise, he discovered that the time between the beginning of the high tide and the beginning of the low tide increased, and the time between the beginning of the low tide and the beginning of the high tide decreased. How to oblige this circumstance?

A. S. Vugov

F1119. A certain amount of applied asymmetry $O—O$ was poured into a narrow cage with parallel vertical walls. At a certain rotation speed, the $k$th part of the bottom area will be revealed. How did the pressure force on the bottom and on the narrow side walls change (compared to the case of a stationary cove)? When rotating, the liquid does not splash out. Neglect surface tension.

G. S. Lanuds

⟦folio 26⟧

F1120. To compare the thermal conductivity of different materials, it was proposed to use the following methods. Two identical cylinders made of the materials under study are placed on a hot plate (Fig. 2). A piece of water is placed on the cylinders. Where the wax begins to melt most quickly is where the cylinder has the best thermal conductivity. Is this method correct?

A. I. Vuzdin

F1121. A metal ball of radius $r$, removed from other objects, then through resistance $R$. A beam of electrons, whose speed far from the ball was $v$, hits the ball. $n$ electrons hit the ball per second. What is the amount of thermal conductivity on the ball per second? What is the riddle?

I. F. Ginzburg

F1122. There are known cases of observing the world of the sea in the empty. At what distance from the observer does such a mirage appear? Assume that the speed of light in the surface layer in empty spaces changes according to the law $c(z)=c_0(1-az)$, where $c_0$ is the speed of light at the surface of the earth, $z$ is the height above the surface.

V. I. Klyachin

Problems

M1106 — M1110, P1118 — P1122

M1106. Each of the three lines joining the midpoints of the opposite sides of a convex hexagon divide its area in half. Prove that these lines intersect at one common point.

V. V. Proizvolov

M1107. Prove the inequality

$$2 \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a} \right) \geq \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 3,$$

where $a, b, c$ are the sides of a triangle.

L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1108. No three diagonals of a convex $n$ vertex polygon have a single common point inside it ($n \geq 4$). What largest number of diagonals may be drawn so that all the parts into which they split the polygon are triangles?

M. Khovanov, 10th form student, Moscow

M1109. An old geometry problem book contains the following exercise: compute the length of the sides of an equilateral triangle inscribed in the parabola $y=x^2$. As a hint for finding the solution, the authors claim that one of the vertices must coincide with the parabola's vertex. Is this correct? Can the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle equal a) 3; b) 1988?

V. S. Shevelev

M1110. For a given integer $n>1$ select all the greatest common divisors of pairs of distinct integers from 1 to $n$.
