⟦folio 33⟧

F1101. A light horizontal rod 2a long can be freely manipulated around a vertical axis passing through its middle. Identical massive balls are mounted on a rod and can move along it without friction and elastically reflect from the stop at its end (Fig. 1). First, the balls are fixed at distances a/2 from the axis. The rod is pressed up to angular velocity $\omega_0$, after which the balls are simultaneously released. What trajectories will the balls move along? How long will it take the rod to make a full revolution? Construct a graph of dependence $\omega(t)$. The dimensions of the balls are much smaller than the length of the rod.

Due to the symmetry of the initial location and initial velocities, the balls will always be at symmetrical points and have symmetrical velocities. Then it is easy to show that the balls will move freely, except for moments of reflection from the stops.

Indeed, suppose that the ball $A$ is acted upon by a force $F$, perpendicular to the rod (there are no longitudinal forces, since there is no friction). Then, for reasons of symmetry, the ball $B$ should be acted upon by a force $-F$ (Fig. 2). But this means that from the side of the balls a pair of forces will act on the rod, spinning it. Neglecting the mass of the rod in comparison with the masses of the balls, we conclude that these forces should be negligible (in the limit, no forces are required at all to rotate a weightless rod). Thus, we come to the conclusion that between collisions with stops the balls will move uniformly and rectilinearly.

Let us now analyze the collisions of balls with stops. Firstly, during a collision, the $X$-component of the momentum (Fig. 3) is preserved, since otherwise the balls at the moment of impact will be acted upon by forces perpendicular to the rod, which is impossible due to the previous reasoning. Secondly, due to elasticity, energy is conserved. Therefore, when the balls collide with the stops, the law of equality of angular incidence and reflection is satisfied.

From the above reasoning and the geometry of the problem, it is clear that the trajectories of each ball will be an equilateral triangle on the side $l=2a\cos 30^\circ=a\sqrt{3}$ (see Fig. 3).

The rod will make a full revolution in the time $T$ that it takes for the ball to make such a triangle with a speed equal to the initial speed $v_0=\omega_0 a/2$. Thus,

$$T=3l/v_0=6\sqrt{3}/\omega_0.$$

Let's find the dependence $\omega(t)$. It is clear that $\omega=v_t/r$, where $v_t$ is the component of the ball’s velocity perpendicular to the radius, i.e. $v_t=v\cos \alpha=va/2r$. Hence,

$$\omega=va/2r^2=\omega_0/(1+(2x/a)^2)$$

(see Fig. 3). If we start from the moment when $\alpha=0$, then $x=v_0t$,

$$\omega=\omega_0/(1+(\omega_0 t)^2)$$ at $-\sqrt{3}/\omega_0 \leq t \leq \sqrt{3}/\omega_0$.

At moment $t=\sqrt{3}/\omega_0$ there will be a strike against the stop, and the cycle will repeat (see Fig. 4).
