⟦folio 44⟧

In the back room I found other members of the Moebius Society and the administration of the Purple Hats Club engaged in a noisy and intellectual argument. Slapenarsky, a tablecloth wrapper like a toga, sat in a chair and pressed a handkerchief with ice cubes to his lower jaw.

“Simpson is back,” I said. “He fainted, but I thought he was okay.”

“Thank God,” muttered Slapenarsky.

The administrator and owner of the Purple Hats still had no idea what had happened that chaotic evening, and our attempts to explain only made the situation worse. The police beatings further quelled the confusion and panic.

Finally, we managed to dress the injured colleagues, put them on their feet, and we left the battlefield, promising to return back with our lawyers. The manager apparently believed that his club had fallen victim to a conspiracy by some foreigners, and threatened to exact compensation from us for the damage caused, in his words, to the “impeccable reputation of the club.” It turned out that the secret trial, which spread throughout the city, served as excellent advertising for the club, and the Purple Hats dropped the lawsuit.

Simpson got off lightly, but Slapenarsky suffered a broken jaw. I took him to Willings Hospital, which is near the university.

He stayed in the hospital for several weeks, forbidding visitors from visiting him, and I saw him only on the day of his discharge, when I accompanied him to the station. Slapenarsky left for New York, and I haven’t seen him since then. A few months later he died of a heart attack. Professor Simpson entered into correspondence with the widow of Professor Slapenarsky in the hope of finding at least drafts of his late colleague’s work on the theory of zero-sided surfaces.

Whether topologists will be able to understand Slapenarsky's drafts (of course, if they succeed), the future will show. We've used a ton of paper, but so far we've only been able to construct general double-sided and single-sided surfaces. Although I helped Slapenarsky “fold” into a zero-sided surface, excessive excitement erased all the details from my memory.

But I will never forget the remark that the great topologist made on that memorable evening before I left.

“It’s fortunate,” he said, “that Simpson and I managed to free our right hands before returning.”

- What could serve? - I asked perplexed.

Slapenarsky lived well.

“We would have turned ourselves inside out,” he said.
