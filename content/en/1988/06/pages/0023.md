⟦folio 21⟧

As a result of his experiments, he changed the light pressure; the value he obtained coincided with the value predicted by Maxwell's theory with an accuracy of 20%. Lebedev showed that the light flux has not only energy, but also momentum.

For a long time it was not possible to use light pressure due to its low power. Only the advent of lasers - devices capable of concentrating light energy into a very narrow and powerful beam - made it possible to apply Lebedev's discoveries.

For example, you can hold small particles in the air, balancing the force of gravity with the force of light pressure, and mix them. Under the influence of light pressure, particles with different masses will accelerate differently. Therefore, it is possible to sort very small particles using special traps.

Light pressure can be used to separate a mixture of two gases. Such separation can be realized if the frequency of laser radiation (with which the mixture is irradiated) coincides with the frequency of the transition of atoms of one of the gases from an unexcited state in air.

By absorbing a photon, an atom of such gas receives an impulse in the direction of the laser beam. During the reverse transition of an atom from an excited state to a non-excited state, the momentum vector of the emitted photon has an arbitrary direction. During subsequent absorptions and emissions, the “emission” pulses cancel each other out, and the “absorption” pulses add up, and ultimately the resonant atom receives a common impulse directed along the laser bow. Thus, the laser beam, passing sequentially through the chambers, in which there is a mixture of gases, carries with it atoms of one of them into the second chamber.
