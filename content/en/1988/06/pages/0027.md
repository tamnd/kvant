M1106. Each of the three primroses connecting the midpoints of opposite sides of a convex hexagon divides its area in half. Prove that these three primaries intersect at one point.

V.V. Arbitrary

M1107. Prove that if $a, b$ and $c$ are the side lengths of the triangle, then

$$2 \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{a} \right) \geq \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{b} + \frac{b}{a} + 3.$$

L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1108. In a convex $n$-gon ($n > 4$), no three diagonals pass through the same point inside the polygon. What is the maximum number of diagonals in it that can be checked so that all the parts into which they split the $n$-gon turn out to be triangles?

M. Khovanov, 10th grade student, Moscow

M1109. In one of the second geometry assignments, the following task was placed: calculate the length of the side of a right triangle placed in parabolic $y = x^2$. The instructions for the problem said that one of the vertices of the triangle coincides with the vertex of the parabolic. Is this indication correct? can the side length of a right triangle inserted into this parabola be equal to a) 3; b) 1988?

V. S. Shevelev

M1110. For a given natural number $n > 1$, we write down the most common divisors of all possible pairs of distinct numbers from 1 to $n$. Prove that a) the arithmetic mean of all $n(n-1)/2$ numbers written out increases without limit with $n$, but does not exceed $\ln n + 1$; b) their geometric mean does not exceed 10 for any $n$.

V. F. Lev

F1118. A fisherman who lived at the mouth of a river that flowed into the ocean moved to a new place of residence several kilometers upstream. To his surprise, he discovered that the time between the beginning of the high tide and the beginning of the low tide increased, and the time between the beginning of the low tide and the beginning of the high tide decreased. How to oblige this circumstance?

A. S. Vugov

F1119. A certain amount of applied asymmetry $O—O$ was poured into a narrow cage with parallel vertical walls. At a certain rotation speed, the $k$th part of the bottom area will be revealed. How did the pressure force on the bottom and on the narrow side walls change (compared to the case of a stationary cove)? When rotating, the liquid does not splash out. Neglect surface tension.

G. S. Lanuds
