NULL-SIDED
PROFESSOR

M. GARDNER

Dolores, the slender black-haired star of the Chicago night club "Purple Shlyary", froze in the very center of the dance floor and, accompanied by the audible accompaniment of an orchestra playing some kind of oriental melody, began belly dancing, her famous number "Cleopatra". It was completely dark in the hall, and only the emerald beam of the projector fell on her from above, glistening on the airy “Egyptian costume” of the dancer.

The first thing to fall was the pronunciation of the veil, falling from the head and covering Dolores in captivity. Another moment, and Dolores would have thrown off her veil with an elegant gesture, when suddenly a loud sound, like a gunshot, came from somewhere above, and a burned man fell from the ceiling head-first.

There was an incredible commotion.

Metrodel Jake Bowers ordered the lights on and tried to calm the onlookers. The club manager, standing by the orchestra and watching the performance, threw a tablecloth over the prone figure and rolled it onto his back.

The stranger was breathing heavily and was unconscious. He was well over fifty. A short, shakily trimmed red beard and mustache were striking. The stranger was completely athletic and had the build of a professional wrestler.

With great difficulty, the waiters managed to move him to the management office. The audience was excited, the ladies were on the verge of history. Staring in amazement, first at the ceiling, then at each other, the spectators heatedly discussed how the stranger could have fallen. The only hypothesis, not alien to common sense, was that the body was thrown high in the air from somewhere on the side of the dance floor. However, none of those who attacked in the hall saw how this happened.

Meanwhile, in the control room, the stranger came to his senses. According to him, he was Dr. Stanislav Slapenarski, professor of mathematics at the University of Vienna, who had been invited to lecture at the University of Chicago.

Before continuing this amazing story, I consider it my duty to warn the reader that I was not very fond of the described episode and rely entirely on interviews with the metrodel and waiters. Nevertheless, I was lucky enough to take part in a chain of extraordinary events that led to the professor’s sudden emergence, which caused so much noise.

These gatherings began a few hours before members of the Mobius Society gathered for their annual banquet in one of the secluded dining rooms on the second floor of the Purple Shlyary club. The Moebius Society is a small, little-known Chicago organization of mathematicians working in the field of topology, one of the branches of modern mathematics.
