The task in the “damn flower” puzzle is to remove the rope loop from the “flower”. If the wire from which the “flower” is made was sufficiently soft, we would simply pull out the highest “stem” from the wheels enclosing it (diagram below), then in the same way sequentially decipher the remaining “stems”, and then nothing would interfere with calmly removing the rope. But such a “solution” is prohibited by the game’s privileges. It will be easier to understand how the loop is actually imprinted if you understand the more famous, one might say, classic version of this puzzle - meled (Fig. A). Here, instead of a rope, there is a rigid wire loop-shuttle, and together with the “flower” there is a chain of movable wheels on rods that can move vertically.

The inside of the ring is the same as on figure A. Note that the $k$-th pole can be removed from the shuttle only when the $(k-1)$-th pole has also been threaded into it, and the poles with lower numbers can be removed (Fig. B). We will consider the operation of threading a ring into the shuttle or removing the wheels from the shuttle in one move. Let $L_k$ and $M_k$ be the smallest number of moves required for a shuttle with $k$ rings to be hooked to one pole (Fig. B) and, accordingly, to all poles (Fig. A).

1) Prove that $L_k=2L_{k-1}+1$, $M_k=M_{k-2}+L_{k-1}+1$.
2) Express $L_k$ and $M_k$ in terms of $k$, write the corresponding sequence of moves.
3) How to remove the string from the “damn flower”?

The above remark will help you solve these problems, and the answers and instructions are placed on p. 79.
