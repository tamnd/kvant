As for theoretical explanations, there are more questions than answers. For example, according to the Bardeen-Cooper-Schriefer theory, the correspondence $2\Delta(0)/T_{\kappa p}=3,5$ is obtained. For new superconductors, different changes give this value values ​​from 3 to 12. Further, these substances have a number of special features, but it is not very clear which of them are of fundamental importance. For example, what role does the complexity of crystals play, do the existence of lanthanum or indium atoms play a role in the mechanism of superconductivity, or do they play a role in the process of the mechanical truss that holds the crystal lattice together? What is the role of oxygen? It is known that in the intrium compound there is an elephant, consisting of a chain Cu - O - Cu - O, and there are planes in which two oxygen atoms come to each copper atom. When oxygen is removed, it first leaves the chain, and the substance loses superconductivity. But in the lanthanum compound there are no such chains.

So, it’s not very clear what to cling to. It has been experimentally established that in new superconductors, electrons are combined into Cooper pairs. But what is the mechanism of attraction? The mechanism of transfer of phonans - quanta of the flask lattice - is followed by an isotopic coefficient, i.e., a change in $T_{\kappa p}$ with the transition to another isotope. Changes were made to the samples by replacing the isotope $O^{16}$ with $O^{18}$. For the lanthanum compound, the coefficient is observed, although it is less than predicted by the VKS theory. But in a 90-degree sodium superconductor, as well as in the same substance with european sodium, this coefficient is practically absent. Hence the conclusion is drawn that, in addition to the long-known phonon mechanism, there is another mechanism for transmitting interaction between electrons. In principle, quasiparticles of other types can exist in a substance—for example, those associated with the excitation of electrons remote from conducting words. They are called plasmens. There are others: the substance may be close to the transition to a magnetically ordered state. This state has its own quasiparticles - magnons. But even if there is no real ordering, it can arise in the form of fluctuations and the interaction of electrons is created. Theories have emerged that take advantage of this circumstance.

I cannot list all the theories - there are a lot of them. I will just note the very interesting concept of two-electron centers. It is known that oxygen leaves very easily from new superconductors, while in ordinary oxides it is bound very poorly. There is a concept according to which two electrons can immediately leave the oxygen atoms for the copper; this makes the oxygen neutral and thus makes it easier to escape from the lattice. And this circumstance that the electrons are either in a collectivized metallic state or find themselves localized in pairs on oxygen leads to their attraction.

So, now is the time for research and search both for the mechanism of high-temperature superconductivity and for methods of practical application of new superconducting materials. Research is being carried out by large groups of people, and it is possible that they are increasing their success. However, the discovery of high-temperature superconductors has already accomplished one important thing: it has destroyed the long-standing prejudice that superconductivity is explained by three temperatures. This encourages people to take further positions, and even if it is not possible to “tame” the discovered superconducting ceramics, other classes of superconductors with higher $T_{\kappa p}$ and more suitable for practical use will certainly be found.
