ON THE THRESHOLD OF BIG SCIENCE

In 1977, on the pages «Kvant», Academician N.K. Kioin spoke about the main stages in the formation of Soviet physics. In this story, a lot of space was devoted to the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology (LPTI), which at the end of the twenties became one of the main centers of physical science in the USSR.

As a second-year student at the Leningrad Polytechnic Institute, Isaac Konstantinovich began working at LFTN. We offer our readers from the article by N.K. Kioin “How Soviet Physics was Created” («Kvant», 1977, No. 10-12), in which he shares his memories of student life, closely connected with LFTN.

...Opposite our institute, across the street called “The Road to Sosnovka,” there was the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology (then it was called the Physics and Technology RENTENOVSKY INSTITUT). Its director was Academician A.F. Ioffe…. The bulk of the employees were young people who started working as students of the Faculty of Physics and Mechanics.

...Students were selected to work at the institute in this way. Faculty teachers - employees of the institute, including senior students who supervised the students' laboratory work, had instructions from A.F. Ioffe to keep an eye on students who showed interest and ability in experimental physics, and to invite them to work at the physics-technical institute. In 1927, as a second-year student, I was invited to work at the Institute of Physics and Technology. There, young people immediately approached modern science. We are not very friendly and did not take into account the fact that we had not yet “got through something” at our faculty. At scientific seminars, which took place regularly on Fridays, from 5 to 7 pm, scientific works just completed by the institute’s staff or published in foreign journals were discussed. We took part in the seminar, we had to listen and understand what was being said, and if we didn’t understand, then we could cope. At first we didn’t even dare to ask because we didn’t understand anything. But quite quickly the concept of a real person, apparently because we were led to a style of presentation and read a lot ourselves.

...When I started working at the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology, under the influence of lectures and seminars by Ya. I. Frenkel, who told us about modern ideas in metal physics, I decided to study the issues of this particular area of ​​physics and, above all, the Hall effect. I decided to check whether the Hall effect would arise in liquid metals. Earlier, at the beginning of the century, this issue was dealt with by such luminaries of science as Nernst and Drude.
