Let us now move on to the most important thing - the study of the physical properties of the center of mass.

Let the points move to $s_1$, ..., $s_n$ in a short time $\Delta t$. Then, as can be seen from (1), the displacement of the center of mass will be equal to

$$\vec{s}_{\mu M} = \frac{m_1 s_1 + m_2 s_2 + \ldots + m_n s_n}{m_1 + m_2 + \ldots + m_n}$$

(recall that the projections of the displacement vector on the axis are equal to the change in the corresponding coordinates). Dividing the displacement by $\Delta t$, we find the speed of the center of mass:

$$\vec{v}_{\mu M} = \frac{m_1 \vec{v}_1 + m_2 \vec{v}_2 + \ldots + m_n \vec{v}_n}{m_1 + m_2 + \ldots + m_n}$$

So, the first property: if the entire mass of the system is mentally concentrated at the center of mass, then the momentum of this imaginary point will be equal to the total momentum of the system. What follows from this? We know, for example, that the momentum of a closed system is conserved. This means that if the system is closed, the speed of its center of mass $\vec{v}_{\mu M}$ remains constant.

Let's look at an example. A uniform thin stick of length $l$ stands vertically on a smooth floor (Fig. 2). She is released, after which she falls flat. How to find out how much the lower end of the stick will shrink by the time it falls? A stick in a gravitational field is not a closed system; but, since only vertical forces act on it, the horizontal projection of its momentum does not change, in our case it remains equal to zero. This means that the center of mass will not mix in the horizontal direction, that is, the center of the stick will fall to the place where it stood, and its lower end will shift by $l/2$.

Let's continue our discussion of the properties of the center of mass. Let us consider a system of two point materials with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$. Let this system not be closed, that is, each body is acted upon by both internal and external forces.
