⟦folio 22⟧

F1103. A mathematical mat of length $l$ is accumulated at point $A$ on a cone with a distance angle of 2β (Fig. 2). How far will the matnik go before it breaks away from the surface of the cone, if the matnik is lightly removed from its equilibrium position? The angle of inclination of the cone to the surface of the earth is $\alpha$, the surface of the cone is considered smooth.

L. G. Markovich

F1104. The ends of two homogeneous rods are tied to each other with weightless and inextensible threads. One of the rods was tied by the middle to the shitatvu. Prove that the quadrilateral formed in this way from threads and rods is a trapezoid.

V. T. Karpetyan

F1105. A metal disk with a radius of $r=10$ cm rotates in a horizontal plane at a speed of $v=60$ rpm. A flat block, the mass of which is much less than the disk, falls onto it from a height of $H=10$ cm. The lower edge of the block is always parallel to the plane of the disk, the friction coefficient between metal and plastic is $\mu=0,1$. At what distance from the axis must the block fall so that when it falls again it falls outside the disk? Assume that after the rebound the block rises to its previous height; neglect the dimensions of the block.

L. A. Zakrevsky

F1106. A few words about superconductors. Superconductors have the property of pushing out a magnetic field (the so-called Meissner effect), thanks to which they can float above a magnet. This feature of superconductors is supposed to be used to create ultra-high-speed magnetic levitation trains, prototypes of which have already been tested. And now the task itself. A load of exactly the same mass is placed on a superconducting sample of mass $m$, hovering above a permanent magnet. How many times must the magnetic induction of the field created by the magnet be increased in order for the superconductor with the load to float at the same distance from the magnet?

A. I. Buzdin

F1107. Freshly fallen fluffy snow sparkling in the sun. Estimate the characteristic distance between individual “sparks”.

E. N. Yunosov, I. V. Yaminsky

M1091. A natural number is called successful, if its digits can be split up into two groups with equal sums.
a) Find the least $a$ such that both $a$ and $a+1$ are successful.
b) Is there an $a$ such that $a, a+1, a+2$ are all successful?

N. I. Zilberberg

M1092. A convex paper polygon is folded 10 times (along straight lines) and then cut along a straight line. Find the maximum number of pieces.

S. V. Kazakow
