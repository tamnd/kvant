M1072. Factor the number 989·1001·1007+320 into prime factors.

Answer: 1009·997·991. You can guess this expansion using the following message: if $f(x)=(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)$, then $f(x)+f(-x)=-2(a+b+c)x^2+2f(0)$, i.e. for $a+b+c=0$ the sum $f(x)+f(-x)$ is constant. In our case, we can take $f(x)=(x-10)\times(x+2)(x+8)$, then the given number is $f(999)-2f(0)=-f(-999)=1009\cdot997\cdot991$. It is easy to check that all three factors here are prime.

S. V. Fomin

M1073. In the hexagon $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5A_6$ I found a point O, from which all sides are visible at an angle of 60°. Prove that if $OA_1>OA_3>OA_5$ and $OA_2>OA_4>OA_6$ then $A_1A_2+A_3A_4+A_5A_6<A_2A_3+A_4A_5+A_6A_1$.

Let us plot on the sides of the angle of magnitude $60^\circ$ from its vertex $P$ the segments $PB_i=OA_i$, $i=1, 2, ..., 6$, as shown in figure. Since the segments $B_1B_2, B_2B_3, ..., B_6B_1$ are respectively equal to the segments $A_1A_2, A_2A_3, ..., A_6A_1$, it is sufficient to prove that

$$B_1B_2+B_3B_4+B_5B_6<B_2B_3+B_4B_5+B_6B_1.$$

This inequality is obtained by adding the 4 triangle inequalities (see Fig.ok):

$$B_1B_2<B_1C_1+C_1B_2,$$
$$B_3C_2<B_3C_1+C_1C_2,$$
$$C_2B_4<C_2C_3+C_3B_4,$$
$$B_5B_6<B_5C_3+C_3B_6,$$

where $C_1, C_2, C_3$ are the points of intersection of the segment $B_1B_6$ with the segments $B_2B_3, B_3B_4$ and $B_4B_5$. (The indicated segments intersect due to the inequalities given in the conditions. For example, from the inequalities $PB_3<PB_1$ and $PB_6<PB_2$ it follows that the points $B_3$ and $P$ lie on the same side of $B_1B_6$, and $B_2$ and $P$ are on different sides, therefore $B_3$ and $B_2$ lie on opposite sides of $B_1B_6$, i.e. line $B_1B_6$ intersects segment $B_2B_3$; similarly, it is proved that line $B_2B_3$ intersects segment $B_1B_6$, and, consequently, segments $B_2B_3$ and $B_1B_6$ intersect.)

A. S. Merkuryev
