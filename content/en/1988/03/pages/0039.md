Why do you think colored circles appear before your eyes if you hit your head on a door frame in complete darkness? what if it’s about a lantern?

Our eyes are poorly suited for strictly analyzing the color state of light. For this purpose, it is better to use physical devices (the simplest of them is a prism). But by mixing just three colors in a certain proportion, for example, red, yellow and blue, you can get almost any color (at least a mixture of colors). This, by the way, is widely used in color television. All colors on a color TV screen result from the appropriate superposition of three primary colors. Similarly, only three inks are used for color printing. So, by mixing yellow and blue paints, we get a green color; by directing yellow and blue rays onto the screen, we will see a green spot. It is absolutely clear that the green color obtained in this way is not at all a “pure” green color, which is part of the rainbow and corresponds to an electromagnetic wave of a certain length. Quite simply, in these two cases the same color noise is created. Despite what has been said, the richness of colors perceived by the human eye is very great - artists are good at distinguishing up to three hundred shades.

And depending on this, there is another “color” story, from the field of elementary particle physics. Invented by the greatest theoretical physicist of our time, Gell-Mann, exotic grains of matter - quarks - not only received the right to an unusual existence (they cannot exist in a free state), but also acquired a “color”. They came in three "colors" - red, green and blue. According to this theory, an ordinary proton consisting of three “multi-colored” quarks will be “white,” i.e., “colorless.” By mixing these three colors in different combinations, you can get any other color in the visible spectrum, which is what is used in color television.

Thus, modern physical science is able to speak increasingly about the color of even such objects, the immediate possibility of observing which it answers with no less confidence. Honestly, human imagination has no limits...
