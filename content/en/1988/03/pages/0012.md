And if it is necessary to change strong points, then the devices are shunted - a resistance (shunt) is connected parallel to the current-carrying part of the changing device, a certain number of times less than the resistance of the current-carrying part of the device. If, for example, a current of 100 A flows in the circuit, and the device is designed for current changes of no more than 1 A, then the shunt resistance should be 99 times less than the resistance of the coil in the device. Then a current of 1 A will flow through the device, and through the shunt - 99 A. The greater the variable current, the less the shunt resistance should be. This is supplemented by an increase in the cross-sectional area of ​​the shunt conductor.

As long as we are talking about changing the descats and even a few soten amperes, problems with shuntimi do not arise. However, when it is necessary to change points of thousands of amperes, the shunt becomes a weighty and burdensome part of the device. Thus, the shunt to the device for changing the current of 10,000 A had a mass of 35 kg. In practice, as the variable current increases, the shunt mass increases in proportion to the square of the current. This means that if you need to change the current of 50 kA, the mass of the shunt should be 875 kg, and if it is also 100 kA - 3.5 tons!

Let's add to this that changing devices with shuntimi for electrolysis plants were not produced in our country. We bought them in Germany. But even abroad it was impossible to buy the 75 kA kilometer meters necessary for the Ural plant. The situation became especially aggravated with the beginning of the war. Two aluminum plants in the European part of the USSR found themselves in occupied territory. There was only one aluminum plant left in the Urals, which could not be started up without changing the equipment.

This prompted I. Kikon to take up this important problem. Its decision involved not only the Ural, but also other electrolysis plants, for example, the Solina Kama Potash Plant. The author of these lines has correspondence with this plant, from which it is clear how important the rapid development and production of new devices was for him.

What devices were proposed to replace shunt devices?

Ampere through which no current passes

The idea of ​​​​changes in very large direct currents, proposed by I.K. Kikonan, was extremely simple. Around any constant electric current there is a constant magnetic field at a point located at a given distance from the conductor with current, depending only on the strength of the current. This dependence is well known for the magnetic field around a cylindrical conductor. In this case, the field induction is inversely proportional to the distance from the conductor and directly proportional to the current strength.

At electrolysis plants, current-carrying wires are copper bars of straight cross-section, and even folded together. And in this case, the magnetic field induction is determined by the current in the tires, but its dependence on the current strength is somewhat more complicated - it deviates slightly from linear. This, however, does not prevent one from estimating the current strength from the magnetic field induction. It was proposed to evaluate the induction itself in the simplest way - by the deflection of the magnetic needle.

As is known, a magnetic needle (magnetized rod) in a uniform magnetic field is acted upon by a pair of forces, the rotational moment of which differs from the strength of the needle and is ultimately established along the lines of induction of the magnetic field. But you can make sure that the needle is not installed along the induction lines, but is rotated at an angle proportional to the current. To do this, a force must act on the arrow, the moment of which “unfolds” the arrow.
