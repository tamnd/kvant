In low light, vision is mainly due to rods, but unfortunately they are not capable of providing color vision. Cones are responsible for color vision, and they begin to “turn on” only when the lighting is available. This, by the way, is the origin of the expression “all cats are gray at night” - in very low light, all objects seem gray.

The faint light from the stars reaching the Earth allows us to determine the colors of only the brightest of them; Meanwhile, the colored images of the starry sky made with the help of a telescope amaze with the richness and beauty of colors - unfortunately, they are inaccessible to the eye. However, the concept of our “colorless” vision is surprising: the eye can, in principle, register even single photons. A photon is a quantum of electromagnetic vibration, like the smallest energy portion of radiation. (Here we are faced with the fact that, according to quantum theory, light, on the one hand, is an electromagnetic wave, and on the other hand, a flow of particles - quanta or photons.)

As we have already said, the formation of color sensation is a complex process. The light-sensitive cells in the eye are connected not only to the brain, but also to each other. The eye itself carries out the primary processing of light information. It is no coincidence that during the development of the embryo, the eyes are formed by the growth of fibers directly from the brain, and in fact it is part of the brain.

By the way, that place on the retina, when the optic nerve opens and branches out, is devoid of light color - there are no rods and cones there; it is called “solid”. If the image of an object on the retina falls on the solid one, then the object is not visible. The famous physicist Mariotte discovered the blind spot; he reported on this phenomenon at a meeting of the Paris Academy in 1666. He was the “physiologist” (from the επιgraph to the article) who taught the French king a humane way to “cut off the heads” of his courtiers.

The important role of the brain in the perception of external images is illustrated by this example. If a person wears glasses that make the image upside down, then at first the person will see everything “upside down”. However, after some time, the perception of external images will become instilled. But after removing the glasses, objects will seem upside down, and it will take some time for everything to fall into place.
