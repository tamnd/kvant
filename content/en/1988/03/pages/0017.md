What can transform the isotopes of the obtained straight pairs?

Line engagement coefficient

Each pair of straight lines obtained has a characteristic, which can take values ​​+1 and -1. This characteristic is called the gearing coefficient. It preserves under isotopies, and therefore if two obtained pairs of straight lines have different coupling coefficients, then they are not isotopic. This is how the coefficient of engagement is determined. The most economical way to combine one straight line with another that intersects it is to move the line along a common perpendicular and rotate it through the smallest angle necessary to match the directions. In this case, the straight line will move either like the handle of the right gimlet or like the handle of the left one (Fig. 5); then, that the type of gimlet does not depend on which of the two straight lines we moved. In the first case, the gearing coefficient is -1, in the second - +1.

It is clear that reorientation of one of the straight lines of the pair entails a change in the gearing coefficient.

When the orientation of the pair is replaced by the opposite one, the coefficient of engagement does not change; therefore, it is a characteristic of the obtained pair and depends only on the obtained orientation. When reflected in a mirror, the coefficient of engagement of a pair of oriented straight lines changes (Fig. 6).

Let us now recall the accident that we encountered in our search for an isotopy between two pairs of skew lines that preserves the distances and angles between the lines (Fig. 7). Then we could not answer the question about the isotopicity of the sets shown in figure 8. But now we know that such pairs (with their canonical resulting matrices) are obtained from each other by reflection in the mirror and therefore have different coupling coefficients. This means that such pairs cannot be connected by isotopy, during which the distances and angles between the pairs do not change. And if two pairs have the same distances, angles and coefficients of engagement, then these pairs can be connected by such an isotope.
