⟦folio 21⟧

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After formulating the problem, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue should be sent no later than June 1, 1988 to the address: 103006 Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant».
Solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 3—88” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M1091” or “F1103”.
In the column “... sender’s address”, please write your first and last name separately. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision verification in this envelope).

The condition of each original problem proposed for publication is assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you study.

Tasks

M1091—M1095, F1103—F1107

M1091. Let's call a natural number lucky if the digits in its decimal notation can be divided into two groups so that the sums of the digits in these groups are equal a) Find the smallest number a such that the numbers a and a+1 are lucky.
6) Is there such a such that the numbers a, a+1 and a+2 are lucky?

N. I. Zilberer

M1092. A polygon cut out of convex paper is folded 10 times (bending along some straight lines) and then cut in a straight line. What is the largest number of pieces he can get?

S. V. Kazakov

M1093. On the circle, the numbers 0, 1, 2 are placed at n points. Then, simultaneously at all points, perform the following transformation: each number 2 is replaced by 0, and then 1 is added to the number following it clockwise. First, let the number of twos be equal to k ≥ 1.
a) After how many transformations are completed, not a single two is left?
b) Let, in addition, at the n−k remaining points there were initially ones. Prove that in the end there are k ones and n−k zeros (Fig. 1).
H. Alexandru (Romania)

(1)
inequality follows
$$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 \leq 2(ab + bc + ca).$$
(2)
6) Is the reverse true: inequality (2) implies inequality (1)?

B. A. Senderov

M1095*. On the plane, given a circle with center O and two points A, B (different from O) such that straight line AB passes through point O. Construct the chord MN of this circle, which is visible from point A at a given angle α and
a) parallel to line AB;
b) passes through point B. (If B lies outside the circle, then the continuation of the chord MN must pass through B.)
