What is the center of mass

If you throw a stone at an angle to the horizon, it is known to travel in a parabola. What if you throw a long stick and twist it properly? Of course, different points of the stick move in different ways, describing rather complex trajectories, but the movement of the stick as a whole is somewhat similar to the movement of a stone: rise, top position, descent. Moreover, if we neglect air resistance, then the movement of one specific point of the stick will be no different from the movement of a stone flying freely along a parabola. This point is the center of mass of the stick.

The center of mass exists for any body, moreover, for any system of bodies. It has remarkable properties, some of which this article will introduce you to.

Let's start by determining the position of the center of mass. Let us consider a system of material points with masses $m_1, ..., m_n$. How, knowing their coordinates, find the coordinates of this “most important point”? The answers look like this:

$$x_{\mu m} = \frac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + ... + m_n x_n}{m_1 + m_2 + ... + m_n},$$

similar for coordinates $y_{\mu m}$ and $z_{\mu m}$. Why the center of mass is determined in this way will become clear later, when we consider its diametric properties. For now, let’s get familiar with expression (1) by discussing several related issues.
