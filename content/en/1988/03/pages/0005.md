TECHEKE «Kvant» and led them until the last days of their lives.

I.K. Kikoin loved our magazine and, despite his enormous busyness, always found time to develop topics, search for authors and discuss articles. He loved young people - you had to see how his eyes lit up when meeting talented schoolchildren. He loved physics infinitely; his studies were the main meaning of his entire life. Not long before his death, speaking to schoolchildren, he said: “... in my long life I didn’t have time to enjoy my beloved physics, I didn’t have enough time, I see clearly now that I didn’t have enough. But there was not a single day in my life, not a day off, not a holiday, not a vacation, when I didn’t do it. I often have dreams about physics.” This is how we remember this wonderful man and scientist.

In this issue we publish a number of materials dedicated to the memory of I.K. Kikoin.

AT THE BEGINNING OF THE JOURNEY

Isak Konstantinovich Kikoin graduated from secondary school No. 1 named after L. M. Pozemsky in Pskov in 1923. He always recalled his years as a scientist with gratitude. The school helped him find his calling and instilled in him a love for physics, a science that he devoted his whole life to.

In February 1983, sixth graders from Pskov School No. 1 wrote Isak Konstantinovich a letter asking him to tell about the things he had learned at school, about himself, about his work. Isak Konstantinovich readily responded to the request. At different times, he wrote four letters to schoolchildren.

We present excerpts from these poems to your attention.

...I was born on March 28, 1908 in the city of Žagre (this is in the Lithuanian SSR).

...The family adhered to the creative rule - to teach children to do as much work as possible around the house, to take care of all family members; a love of work and responsibility for the assigned work were instilled.

Throughout my life I have remained grateful to my parents for this. In my life I often encountered difficulties and remembered the behests of my father.

In our family, children were taught to write and read early. At three years old I already knew how to read and write. Every day I received a task from my father, for which I had to report to him. My father also asked me texts that I had to answer him by heart. Now I understand that these oral tasks contributed to the formation of a good memory in me, which I retained for the rest of my life.

...In 1916 we moved to Opochka, Pskov province (now region), where I went to school.

Considering it was not difficult for me, so much so that, having “skipped” the grade twice, in 1921 I found myself in the last grade. But in the same year, my father was transferred as a mathematics teacher at your school, which was then called the school’s 1st and 2nd students.

Naturally, I went to study at the same school. Thus, I spent the last two academic years in our school... I have the brightest and most pleasant memories of these powerful years.

Quite early, while still in elementary school, I diligently studied mathematics and physics; Probably, the influence of the father affected.

The school where you are now studying created exceptionally favorable conditions for in-depth study of many of these subjects.

We were taught mathematics by a first-class teacher, Dmitry Mikhailovich Lyapunov, to whom I am very grateful for the knowledge I received from him.

I was very lucky with physics too.

I was entrusted with managing the school's physics room and, together with my school friend, managing the school library (both were my social work).

...The physics room was well equipped with instruments, and I had the opportunity to carry out many experiments. True, many devices needed repair, and I gained experience in manufacturing the parts necessary for this.

The school library was extremely rich, containing several parts of thousands of volumes. There were a lot of books on physics and mathematics.

... Almost every day I happily spent time studying in the library.
