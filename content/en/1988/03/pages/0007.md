One small detail: at the time when I passed the exams (July), the certificates of those graduating from the Land Surveying School were still being printed in the printing house, so before the exams, for presentation to the admissions committee, they gave me a duplicate certificate, sent on a fancy typewriter, with exactly the same text and with original signatures and an official seal.

...At the beginning of the school year I come to Leningrad. I came to my faculty and read the list of pleasant students. My last name is not there!

...The next morning (it was August 28) I went to the office to find out the reason for this obvious misunderstanding. When I told my last name to the head of the offices, he ordered... to bring my personal file. He began to leaf through it and said: “You are not accepted because you did not appear for the exams.” In response to my explanation that I took the exams in Pskov to the provincial selection committee, the answer was: “You graduated from school in 1923, and not this year, 1925, and according to the existing rules you must take the exam here - at the institute.” In response to this, I said that this year I graduated from the Pskov Land Surveying School. This was followed by an answer that surprised me: “We know nothing about this.” I began to argue that my personal file should include a certificate of completion of the Land Surveying School in Pskov. Looking through the personal file again and finding this certificate, he categorically stated: “Since your certificate is not in the printing house, it cannot serve as an official document for us.” I explained to him the reason... and showed the printing house the certificate issued to me, emphasizing that the texts, seals and signatures in both documents exactly match.
