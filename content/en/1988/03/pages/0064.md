$\neq \frac{\pi}{4}; R = \frac{d}{\sqrt{2}}, \text{ если } \alpha = \frac{\pi}{4}.$

Solution. Let's consider our own section of the prima (Fig. 2 and 3). According to the conditions of the problem, the point $P$ lies at the height of the point $S$, or on its extension, but does not coincide with the point $S$, since $d > 0$. Let's denote it by $AD = a, SO = h$. Then $AO = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}, h = \frac{a \text{ ctg } \alpha}{\sqrt{2}}$. Point $P$ can lie either above the base of the prima (maybe even above point $S$), or below this base. Therefore, either $AE \perp SC$ or $AE \perp SA$. From $\triangle AOP$ we get $OP = \frac{a \text{ tg } \alpha}{\sqrt{2}}, AP = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2} \cos \alpha}$.

Depending on the position of the point $P$ we have: either $d = |AE - AP| = |a \sqrt{2} \cos \alpha - \frac{a}{\sqrt{2} \cos \alpha}| = \frac{a |\cos 2\alpha|}{\sqrt{2} \cos \alpha}$ or $d = AP = \frac{d}{\sqrt{2} \cos \alpha}$, i.e. either $a = \frac{d \sqrt{2} \cos \alpha}{|\cos 2\alpha|}$ or $a = d \sqrt{2} \cos \alpha$. But in all cases $2R = h + x = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} (\text{ctg } \alpha - \text{tg } \alpha) = \frac{a \sqrt{2}}{\sin 2\alpha}$, and therefore either $R = \frac{d}{2 \sin \alpha}|\cos 2\alpha|$ or $R = \frac{d}{2 \sin \alpha}$.
