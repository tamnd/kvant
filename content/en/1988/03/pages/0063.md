1. The friction force (μmg cos α) is directed against the equal force of gravity and the external force ($\sqrt{F^2_{\min}} + (mg \sin \alpha)^2$). Equating the forces, we get
$$F_{\min} = mg \sqrt{\mu^2 \cos^2 \alpha - \sin^2 \alpha} \text{ при } \mu \geqslant tg \alpha,$$
$$F_{\min} = 0 \text{ при } \mu < tg \alpha.$$
2. From the law of conservation of charge $2CU = Q_1 + Q_2$, from the equality of potential differences on the plates of capacitors $Q_1/(2C) = Q_2/C$ and from the law of conservation of energy $2\frac{Mv^2}{2} = 2\frac{CU^2}{2} - \left(\frac{Q_1^2}{2(2C)} + \frac{Q_2^2}{2C}\right)$ we have
$$v = \sqrt{\frac{CU^2}{3M}}.$$
3. From the equilibrium condition for the left piston, the pressure $p_1$ to the left and to the right of it must be the same. Then the volumes in the left and right compartments before the start of steam condensation should be the same. Thus, while $p_1 \leqslant 2p_0$, the length of each compartment is $(2L-a)/2 = L-a/2$ and the displacement of the left piston is $x = a/2$ at $a \leqslant L$.
With further insertion of the right piston, water vapor condenses, and the pressure, identical in both compartments, will be constant and equal to $2p_0$. This means that the distance between the pistons $l$ will be constant and equal to $L/2$ (according to the Boyle-Mariotte law $p_0L = 2p_0l$, hence $l = L/2$). From here (the volume of condensed water is equivalent to)
$$x = 2L - a - L/2 = 3L/2 - a \text{ при } L \leqslant a \leqslant 3L/2.$$
Finally,
$$\text{при } a > 3L/2 \text{ x} = L.$$
4. The work against the rolling forces of the left ax $F$ on the path $s$ (the size of the butt) is equal to $Fs \sim mv^2/2$, where $m$ is the mass of the ax, $v$ is its speed. Let's assume that the ax, like the hands, moves at approximately the same speed as the nori when running. Thus,
$$F \sim mv^2/(2s) \sim 5 \cdot 10^3 \text{ H}$$
at $m \sim 1$ kg, $s \sim 10^{-2}$ m and $v \sim 10$ m/s.

Option 3
1. From the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation for one mole of gas - $pV = RT$ - it follows that the angle of inclination $\alpha$ of the isochor to the temperature axis is such that $tg \alpha = p/T = R/V$. Thus, the maximum slope corresponds to the minimum volume and vice versa:
$$V_{\min} = RT_1/p_2, V_{\max} = RT_2/p_1.$$
2. Let there be a charge $q$ on the plate of the capacitor, then a charge $Q - q$ will remain on the plate, a charge $Q - q$ will appear on the other plate, and a charge $+q$ will appear on the plate with a radius $R$ (from the law of conservation of charge). Equating the potential differences between the capacitor plates and between the balls, we obtain
$$4\pi \varepsilon_0 q/C = (Q - q)/r - q/R,$$
where
$$q = \frac{Q}{1 + r(1/R + 4\pi \varepsilon_0/C)}.$$
3. Since the speed $v$ of the bead is constant, the friction force $\mu N$ is balanced by the “rolling” force $mg \sin \alpha$. From Newton's second law we get
$$\left(\frac{mv^2 \cos^2 \alpha}{R}\right)^2 + (mg \cos \alpha)^2 = N^2 = \left(\frac{mg \sin \alpha}{\mu}\right)^2.$$
From here
$$v = \sqrt{Rg} \cos \alpha \left(\left(\frac{\sin \alpha}{\mu}\right)^2 - \cos^2 \alpha\right)^{1/4} \text{ при } tg \alpha > \mu.$$
4. A rough estimate in order of magnitude from the law of conservation of energy gives: $mv^2_{\pi,T}/2 + mgl/2 \sim Fh$, where $F \sim (M_{\psi,\pi}/2)g$ is the pressure force of one leg of a standing person. Hence $v_{\pi,T} \sim \sqrt{(Mh/m-l)g}, v_x/v_{\pi,T} = 2$ (since $v = \omega r$), i.e.
$$v_x \sim 2 \sqrt{(Mh/m-l)g} \sim 15 \text{ м/c}$$
at $M/m \sim 70$, $h \sim 0,1$ m, $l \approx 1,5$ m, $g = 10 \text{ м/c}^2$.
5. First, thin metal vessels quickly heat up, and their volume increases, so the liquid level falls. Then the liquid itself warms up, its volume increases, and the level in the tube rises.
