Oral exam objectives
1. After the force holding the cork ball at a depth of H=1 m ceased, the ball emerged from the water and rose to a height of h=0.5 m above the surface. Determine the average force of resistance of water to the movement of the ball. Air resistance is not taken into account. Mass of the ball m=100 g, density of the plug q=200 kg/m³, density of water q_B=1000 kg/m³.

2. Two satellites move in the same direction in circular orbits lying in the same plane, with speeds v₁=7.8 km/s and v₂=7.6 km/s. Determine the minimum distance between the satellites and the period of time after which they will again be at the same distance. The radius of the Earth is taken equal to R=6400 km.

3. The mathematical mat is oscillating. In the position of the most deviation, the acceleration of the weight of the mat is 20 times greater than at the moment of passing the equilibrium position. Find the angle of maximum deflection of the mat.

4. A narrow cylindrical tube with a length of l = 1.25 m, open on both sides, is immersed halfway into the river. The top opening of the tube is then closed and taken out of the routine. In this case, so much mercury remains in the tube with a length of h = 27 cm. What is the atmospheric pressure? The density of mercury is q=13,600 kg/m³.

5. Two loaded balls, masses m₁=0.2 g and m₂=0.8 g and charges q₁=3·10⁻⁷ C and q₂=2·10⁻⁷ C, are connected by a light non-conducting thread with a length of l=20 cm and move along the line of force of a uniform electric field of intense E=10⁴ N/Kl. The vector of the intense electric field is directed vertically downwards. Determine the acceleration of the balls and the tension of the thread.

6. A mathematical mat is a ball with a mass m=1 g and a charge q=10⁻⁸ C, suspended on a light non-conducting thread. Determine how many times the oscillation period of this mat will change if it is placed in a uniform electric field of intense E=5·10⁵ N/Kl, directed a) vertically downwards; b) horizontally.

7. Determine the charge of the capacitor in the electrical circuit shown in figure 1, where R₁=20 ohms, R₂=30 ohms, R₃=10 ohms, R₄=40 ohms, E=10 V, r=0, C=2 mK.

8. The elevator motor is powered by a direct current source with EMF E=240 V and internal resistance r=0.2 Ohm. Determine the lifting speed of the elevator if its mass is m = 500 kg, the motor winding resistance is R = 0.6 Ohm, the current in the circuit is I = 50 A. Also determine the electromagnetic induction emf that occurs in the motor windings when it rotates. Mechanical loss claims.

9. A proton flies into a uniform magnetic field with induction B=0.4 T at an angle α=30° to the field direction and moves along a helical line with radius R=0.5 cm. Find the kinetic energy of the proton (proton mass m=1.67·10⁻²⁷ kg, charge q=1.6·10⁻¹⁹ C).
