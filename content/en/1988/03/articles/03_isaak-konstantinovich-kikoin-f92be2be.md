Isaac Konstantinovich Kikonn
(on the occasion of his 80th birthday)

March 28, 1988 marks the eightieth anniversary of the birth of the founder of the Quantum magazine, Academician Isaac Konstantinovich Kikon (died December 28, 1984). He was an outstanding Soviet physicist, twice Hero of Socialist Labor, laureate of the Lenin and State Prizes. He made a major contribution to solving a number of important government problems and, above all, to eliminating the US monopoly on atomic weapons. I. K. Kikonn was the closest landowner of the leader of these works, Academician I. V. Kurchatov, one of the founders of the Institute of Atomic Energy and the permanent deputy director of this institute.

I. K. Kikonn belonged to the remarkable school of physics created by academician A. F. Ioffe. The greatest Soviet physicists came out of it - academicians A. P. Aleksadrov, A. I. Alikhanov, L. A. Artsimovich, P. L. Kapitsa, I. V. Kurchatov, G. V. Kurdyumov, N. N. Semenov, Yu. V. Khariton and other scientists. From their student years, Ioffe's students were actively involved in independent scientific work. This was the case with Isaac Konstantinovich - he began scientific work in his second year, and soon after graduating from the institute he completed a series of works in the field of solid state physics that received international recognition. We will not try to talk about his scientific works here - there are many of them and they are varied. Let us dwell only on his contribution to accountability education.

Two very important problems occupied his mind for many years. The scientific and technological revolution continuously increases the requirements for workers. Automation of production, the birth of new technologies, the invasion of computers in various spheres of work - such is the greatness of the time. Only a person with good physical and mathematical training can reliably wield a complex technical pole. And the level of teaching of these sciences in secondary schools must remain lacking for some time. In an effort to improve it, I. K. Kikonn led the work of improving the program and textbooks in physics - a difficult task that major scientists are extremely reluctant to undertake these days.

Another problem that concerned I. K. Kikonna was the search for young talents, on whom the future of our science directly depends. He was convinced that our people are talented, and we have many nuggets like M.V. Lomonosov. But how can we smooth out in time and not miss these new Lomonosovs, Mendleyevs and Lobachevskys? It was necessary to create some kind of unified all-union mechanism that would help attract young talents. School Olympiads became such a mechanism, and Isaac Konstantinovich was the chairman of the Central Organizing Committee of the All-Union Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry Olympiads for schoolchildren.

I. K. Kikonn, together with academician A. N. Kolmogorov, organized one of the first physics and mathematics boarding schools in the country at Moscow State University. They also laid the foundation for the world’s first popular science physics and mathematics magazine for high school students, “Quant” and “Bibliography.”

I.K. Kikoin loved our magazine and, despite his enormous busyness, always found time to develop topics, search for authors and discuss articles. He loved young people - you had to see how his eyes lit up when meeting talented schoolchildren. He loved physics infinitely; his studies were the main meaning of his entire life. Not long before his death, speaking to schoolchildren, he said: “... in my long life I didn’t have time to enjoy my beloved physics, I didn’t have enough time, I see clearly now that I didn’t have enough. But there was not a single day in my life, not a day off, not a holiday, not a vacation, when I didn’t do it. I often have dreams about physics.” This is how we remember this wonderful man and scientist.
