Capillary phenomena

Let us now consider how the surface of the liquid will behave near the walls of the vessel in which it is poured.
Obviously, taking into account the walls of the vessel, two more terms will be added to the general expression for the energy of the liquid - vapor - vessel wall system. This is the energy of interaction of liquid molecules with molecules of the walls (at a given temperature, the greater the number of surfaces in contact, the greater the

flowing liquid and vessel) and the energy of interaction between vapor molecules and walls. The equilibrium position of the surface of the liquid and its vapor near the walls of the vessel will be established such that the total potential energy of the system takes on a minimum value.
In most cases, this leads to the fact that the surface of the liquid near the walls is curved.

Thus, if the interaction between liquid molecules and the walls of the vessel turns out to be the broadest in comparison with other molecular interactions—the liquid wets the vessel—the interface between the liquid and vapor near the walls “creeps” upward, and a certain angle $\theta$ with the vertical (Fig. 5) is formed. The angle $\theta$ is called the contact angle. At a fixed temperature it will always be the same for a given liquid and wall materials. The stronger the interaction of liquid molecules with molecules of the walls of the vessel, compared with the interaction of liquid molecules, the smaller the angle $\theta$. In the limit, when $\theta \rightarrow 0$, they say that the liquid completely wets the materials of the walls of the vessel. On the contrary, when the attraction of liquid molecules to each other transforms over the attraction of liquid molecules and the walls of the vessel, we have the case of a non-displacing liquid. In this case, the angle $\theta$ is obtuse. In the limit, when $\theta \rightarrow \pi$, we speak of complete non-displacement, and the liquid boundary near the walls of the vessel behaves as if the liquid were repelling from the vessel.
