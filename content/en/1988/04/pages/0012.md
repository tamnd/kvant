But how do you assemble circuits from such small “bricks”? Already today, technical methods have been developed that make it possible to “assemble” devices measuring ten and hundreds of nanometers. This is, for example, the method of molecular epitasy*, when molecules are laid layer by layer into thin films. We want to talk about another method that has attracted the attention of researchers in recent years. This is the so-called Langmuir method, which makes it possible to obtain thin films of molecular organization containing exactly 1, 2, 3, ..., 25, etc. molecular layers in the sample.

The history of these films began in the 18th century with the experiments of the American scientist W. Franklin. Franklin poured a small amount of olive oil onto the surface of the pond, which was covered with small fish, and soon the fish died down and the surface became smooth. From this Franklin concluded that nothing new touching the oil would cover the entire surface. The custom was described in 1790 in the journal Philosophical Notes, published by the Royal Society of London (Academy of Sciences of England). After this years, the English physicist W. Rayleigh put forward the proposal that in such experiments a film with a thickness of only one layer of molecules is formed on the surface of water. Knowing the initial volume of oil, you can divide it by the area of ​​the film to calculate its thickness (and therefore the size of the oil molecule). It turned out to be extremely small - only about 10 nm. Subsequently, experiments were carried out in laboratories,

A similar experience can be done at home in the bath. To do this, drop some oil into the filled bath (preferably olive oil, as it spreads over the surface of the water better than sunflower oil). After a few minutes, the film will spread throughout the entire bath. Note that it is quite difficult to determine the oil boundary (it is better to see it by shining a flashlight on the surface). In addition, the film will be uneven in thickness; it will contain windows, as it were, where the layer of oil is very thin, and thicker partitions. But, nevertheless, we will be able to estimate the thickness of the film. However, in our experience there is one major disadvantage - we do not know how many molecules in thickness form the film, and we cannot achieve uniformity of the film in thickness.
