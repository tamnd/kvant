Now it is easy to write the law of motion of the galaxy on the boundary of the ball, and therefore the law of movement of the boundary of the ball itself. To do this, let us write down the total energy $E$ of the galaxy. It adds up kinetic energy $E_\kappa = \frac{mv^2}{2}$ and gravitational potential energy $E_\pi = -\frac{GMm}{R}$ (note that gravitational energy is negative):

$$E = E_\kappa + E_\pi = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{GMm}{R}.$$

We emphasize that, due to the law of conservation of energy, the total energy $E$ does not change - $E = \text{const}$.

From formula (1) it is clear that if $E > 0$, then the radius of the ball can increase unlimitedly - $v$, although it decreases, it does not vanish. At $R \to \infty$ the energy $E$ is entirely determined by the kinetic energy of motion. If $E < 0$, then gravity stops the expansion of the ball, $v$ becomes zero at a certain maximum value of the radius $R_{\text{max}}$ -

$$R_{\text{max}} = \frac{GMm}{|E|},$$

and then the ball begins to skim.

Since our ball was chosen arbitrarily, and the distribution of matter in the Universe is uniform, the movement of the boundary of the ball essentially describes the movement of any large masses in the Universe. Let us rearrange equation (1), dividing it by $m$, in the form

$$\frac{v^2}{2} - \frac{GM}{R} = \text{const}',$$

where $\text{const}' = E/m$. This equation describes over time the distances $R$ between any distant galaxies or particles of matter in the Universe (in the past, when there were no galaxies). Thus, equation (3) is the dynamic equation of the Universe.

Let's add the following to what has been said. According to Einstein's theory, gravity changes the geometric property of space, “curves” it. If const' < 0 ($E < 0$), then the geometry turns out to be similar to the geometry on the sphere, parallel lines intersect, and the space itself turns out to be closed, having a finite volume. The curvature of space is given by the “rainbow of curvature of the world” $l$, and the total volume of the closed Universe in order of magnitude is $V \sim l^3$. The change in $l$ over time is described, like the change in other distances in the Universe, by law (3).

Tracing the change of $R$ or $l$ in the past, we come to the conclusion that the expansion began with the formation of the exact dimensions and infinite density of matter $\varrho$ - as they say, from a singular state. We used the word “formally” to support that this conclusion is only a mathematical idealization, or at very high densities (as estimates show, at $\varrho \approx 10^{94}$ g/cm$^3$) new physical patterns should appear. In particular, these conditions should appear and which determined the emergence of repulsive forces that caused the initial explosion of the Universe. The question naturally arises: what happened before that? However, there is no answer yet, and specialists are considering various possibilities here.

All of the above allows us to imagine the evolution of the Universe in the past as follows. Before the singular state, the Universe rolled up, the density of matter increased, and ultimately a superdense singular state. In this state, we still know the laws of nature very poorly, or rather, we only agree on some of them. In this state, it is believed that enormous gravitational repulsion forces will arise, which stop the rolling of the Universe and cause it to begin to expand. It is in this expanding Universe that we live today.

Is such a “scenario” for the evolution of the Universe possible?
