Surface tension and capillary phenomena

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. I. BUZDIN,
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. S. KROTOV

Last year, university entrance exams in physics were conducted according to a new program, which included a number of questions that were not previously available. Thus, a new topic has appeared: “Properties of the surface of liquids. Surface tension. Wetting. Capillary phenomena" (we have given the verbatim wording), which makes it possible for the examination committee to propose tasks on this range of issues.

Since this type is practically absent from textbooks for university preparation in recent years, and the topic contains a large number of subtleties, when writing this article we sought to forestall the possibility of unexpected exam situations. This is partly why we will pay more attention to the discussion of individual theoretical issues, and not just deal with solving standard problems.

Surface tension

To begin with, it is worth saying a few words about the specification of surface tension forces acting at the boundary of a liquid and its vapor.
