Tasks

M1096 — M1100, F1108 — F1112

M1096. The diameter $d$ of the circle is divided into $k$ equal parts, and through each division point a chord is drawn, redistributing the diameter. Prove that the sum of the lengths of all chords is at least 0.5 $kd$ and less than 0.8 $kd$.

P. Kharitonov, A. Chazirov (10th grade students)

M1097. The coordinates of the vertices of an equilateral triangle are integers. Prove that the square of the base is an even number.

B. V. Prozovolov

M1098. On the circle there are $n$ points, the numbers 1, 2, ..., $n$ measured in a row. The two play the next game. Everyone takes turns drawing a chord connecting two points with the numbers of one quarter. Any chord should not have common points (further ends) with those drawn earlier. The one who makes the last move wins. For each $n=4, 5, 6, ...$, find out which player has a winning strategy: the starter or his partner.

V. G. Chevaov

M1099*. There are 6,783 cosmonauts in the squad preparing for a flight to Mars, and it is known that among any four of them, three can be selected to form a complex mission for the landing module. Prove that it is possible to select 5 astronauts, any three of which form a complex experiment.

N. N. Silkin, M. V. Volkov

M1100*. On the bank of a straight river there are logs (segments that do not intersect each other; Fig. 1). Each breve makes an angle of less than 45° with the linear breve. Prove that for any arrangement of breves there is sure to be a breve that can be rolled into the river without specifying the others. (It is not allowed to turn the log while rolling.)

V. G. Iltsche

F1108. To clearly demonstrate the effect of laser radiation on a substance, a target was made in the form of a small silver ball fixed at the top of a light pistol cone (Fig. 2). One side of the ball is polished and the other is blackened. The target is placed with the base of the cone on a rough horizontal plane, and radiation from a powerful pulsed laser is focused on the surface of the target ball.

Estimate the minimum energy of the light pulse at which the target will topple over. Which side of the ball (reflecting or absorbing) should the radiation be directed to?

Assume that the mass of the ball is $m=3$ g and much greater than the mass of the final stand; cone height $h=6$ cm, base diameter $d=3$ mm; reflection coefficient of the polished surface $r=0,99$, absorption coefficient of the blackened surface $a=0,99$; laser pulse duration $\tau=10$ ns.

E. N. Yunosov, I. V. Yaminsky
