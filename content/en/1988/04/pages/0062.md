Should I connect a tube (with a single end)?

Since the coefficient of the application surface depends on temperature (decreases with its increase), when one end of the tube, for example the left one, is heated, the forces of the application surface on the right will begin to dominate, and the drop will creep - to the right if the liquid wets, and to the left if it does not wet the tube material.

Let us now consider a standard exam task from previous years.

Problem 4. What is the height of the liquid rising between two vertical parallel glass plates, the distance between them d (Fig. 6)? Surface coefficient σ, liquid density ρ, complete wetting.

Let the width of the plate be L, then along the perimeter of the upper boundary of the liquid along the glass plates there is a slur hoot pressure $F_H=2\sigma L$ (coefficient “2” appears due to the fact that there are two plates), directed vertically upward, since the wetting is complete. This slurhoot is the liquid between the plates, which means it is equal to the slurry of this liquid:

$$F_H=\rho g L d$$

(Ldh is the volume of liquid rising between the plates). Using the previously obtained expression for $F_H$, we finally find the height of the liquid rise:

$$\Delta p=\rho g h,$$

or, substituting the found expression for h,

$$\Delta p=\frac{2\sigma}{d}.$$

Now it is not difficult to solve the last problem.
