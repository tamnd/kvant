The area of ​​triangle ABC is $(AB \cdot AC \cdot \sin \alpha)/2$, where $\alpha = \angle BAC$, and the area of ​​quadrilateral AKNM is $AN \cdot KM/2$ (since, obviously, its diagonals AN and KM are perpendicular). Therefore, it is enough to prove that $AB \cdot AC \cdot \sin \alpha = AN \cdot KM$.

Triangles ACL and ANB are similar ($\angle CAL = \angle NAB$, since AL is a bisector, and $\angle ACL = \angle ANB$, since these angles subtend the same arc AB of circle ABC). Therefore, $AB/AN = AL/AC$, i.e. $AB \cdot AC = AL \cdot AN$. It remains to note that $KM = AL \cdot \sin \alpha$ (KM is the chord of a circle with diameter AL on which the inscribed angle KAM of magnitude $\alpha$ rests).
