— Do you think that computational science, information science, will absorb mathematics?

- This is a rather difficult question. Most of the most talented scientists in the United States have used computers from the very beginning of their scientific careers. If they are interested in graphs or numbers, then a wide variety of experiments can be carried out on the computer. When playing computer games, schoolchildren in the United States begin to use computers at the age of 6-7 years. Then the range of mathematical studies can be expanded using the same computers.

The situation on the labor market is such that the demand for specialists in computational methods is enormous. Therefore, many talented young people consider computers in the context of their future profession.

Both young and established mathematicians come to the conclusion that computational experiment is a source of important and interesting problems. For example, at the Berkeley International Competition, the results of three of the sixteen plenary papers were based on the use of computers.
