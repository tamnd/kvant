Let y be the smallest non-negative integer such that f(y) is composite. Let p denote the smallest prime divisor of f(y), then f(y) ≥ p². Below we will prove that if y ≤ q - 2, then p ≥ 2y + 1, therefore

$$y^2 + y + q \geq (2y + 1)^2 = 4y^2 + 4y + 1,$$

and therefore y² < q/3, i.e. y < √q/3.

So, it remains to prove that p ≥ 2y + 1 for y ≤ q - 2.

Let us assume the opposite: p ≤ 2y and consider the difference

$$f(y) - f(x) = (y - x)(y + x + 1).$$

When x changes from 0 to y - 1, the expression y - x takes the values ​​y, y - 1, ..., 1, and y + x + 1 takes the values ​​y + 1, y + 2, ..., 2y. Therefore, for some x, 0 ≤ x ≤ y - 1, the number f(y) - f(x) is divisible by p. But due to the choice of y and p, f(y) is divisible by p and f(x) is prime, so f(x) must be equal to p. At the same time, y - x < y + x + 1 ≤ q + x - 1 < q + x + x² = f(x),

Note that the result of the problem applies to the value of the Euler trinomial f(x) = x² + x + 41: it is easy to check that f(x) is prime for x = 0, 1, 2, 3, and from this (since √41/3 < 4) it follows that f(x) is prime for all 40 integer x from 0 to 39.

F1088. The weightless leaf is fixed in a weightless suspension at a speed of f = 10 rpm so that it can freely swing relative to the upper step (Fig. 1). A monkey with mass m = 30 kg begins to climb down the leaf. At what distance l from the suspension will it be when the vertical position of the leaf ceases to be stable?

Let the leaf deviate from the vertical by a small angle a. At this moment, the monkey’s movement will be considered as a movement in a circle with radius ~lα (Fig. 2). Centripetal acceleration of a monkey -

$$a = (2\pi f)^2 l\alpha.$$

This acceleration can be imparted to her by the reaction force T from the side of the leaf - this is the only force that has a projection F on the horizontal plane. If F > ma, then the monkey (with the leaf) will return to the vertical - the vertical position of the leaf will be stable.

From the considered 丝 (Fig. 2) it is clear that F = mga. From the condition

$$mg\alpha > m(2\pi f)^2 l\alpha$$

we find:

$$l < g/(2\pi f)^2 = 10 \text{ m}.$$

When l is greater than 10 m, the equilibrium is unstable.
