M1078. The function $f$ defined on the set $N_0$ of all non-negative integers and takes a value in this set. Prove that the equality $f(f(n))=n+1987$ cannot hold for all $n \in N_0$.

Let us assume that the statement of the problem is false and the function $f$ satisfying $f(f(n))=n+1987$ for $n \in N_0$ exists. Then
$$f(n+1987)=f(f(f(n)))=f(n)+1987,$$
and therefore
$$f(n+1987k)=f(n)+1987k$$
(*)
with all $n, k \in N_0$. Now consider an arbitrary integer $r$, $0 \leq r \leq 1986$, and divide the number $f(r)$ by 1987 with a remainder:
$$f(r)=1987p+q, 0 \leq q \leq 1986.$$
By conditions $f(f(r))=r+1987$, and by (*) $f(f(r))=f(q)+1987p$. Since $r \leq 1986$, only two cases are possible:
1) $p=0$, i.e. $f(r)=q$, and $f(q)=r+1987$;
2) $p=1$, i.e. $f(r)=q+1987$, and $f(q)=f(f(r))-1987=r$.
In both cases, obviously, $f(r) \neq f(q)$, and therefore $r \neq q$. Thus, the set $0, 1, ..., 1986$ can be divided into pairs $(a, b)$ so that in each pair there are $f(a)=b, f(b)=a+1987$. But this set has an odd number of elements. The resulting contradiction shows that a function with the required properties does not exist.

Answer: you can; the sought points are, for example, points $P_i(i; i^2)$, $i=1, 2, ..., n$, i.e. points on the parabola $y=x^2$ with natural abscissas.

In fact,
$$P_iP_j=\sqrt{(j-i)^2+(j^2-i^2)^2}=|j-i|\sqrt{1+(j+i)^2}.$$
No number of the form $1+m^2$ (we have $m=j+i$) cannot be the square of the rational number $r$, because in this case $r$ would be natural and the equality $r^2=1+m^2$ would result in a contradictory inequality:
$$1=r^2-m^2=(r-m)(r+m) \geq r+m>1.$$
Thus, all distances $P_iP_j$ are irrational.

At the same time, the area of ​​the triangle $P_iP_jP_k$ at $i<j<k$, as can be seen from figure, is equal to
$$\frac{1}{2}((k-i)(k^2-i^2)-(k-j)(k^2-i^2)-(k-i)(j^2-i^2)),
$$
i.e. rational.

In general, the area of ​​any triangle whose vertices have integer coordinates is rational. This shows that the conditions of the integer lattice problem, caring only that the distance between them are irrational.
