RAMSEAN THEORY
COUNTS

M. GARDNER

Graph theory studies a set of points, or vertices, connected by linear lines. Although several papers on Gramsean graph theory were published in the 1930s by the Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdsch and others, serious work on what are now called Ramsey numbers did not begin until the late 1950s. One of the research stimuli was given on p. 14 innocent looking puzzle.

This puzzle can easily be converted into a graph problem. Six peaks correspond to six leads. Let's connect a pair of vertices corresponding to two familiar leads with a red line, and a pair of vertices corresponding to two unfamiliar leads with a blue line. Now the task is to prove that no matter how we choose the colors of the leads, we will inevitably end up with either a red triangle (corresponding to the three pairwise familiar) or a blue triangle (corresponding to the three pairwise unfamiliar).

Ramsey theory, which deals with such problems, is named after Frank Plumpton Ramsey. Ramsay was only 26 years old when he died in 1930 after major surgery.

His father was chairman of the Magdalene collection at Cambridge, and his brother was the archipedian of Canterbury. Economists know Ramsey for his remarkable contributions to economic theory. Logicians know Ramsey for his simplifications of Bertrand Russell's extensive type theory and for his invention of the division of logical paradoxes into logical and semantic.

In 1928, Ramsay gave a talk to the London Mathematical Society on the now classic paper “On a Problem in Formal Logic.” In this work, Ramsey obtained a profound result about sets, which is now known as Ramsey's theorem. It turns out that this theorem, like many set theorems, has a wide variety of unexpected applications to combinatorial problems. This theorem in its full generality is too complex to explain here. For our purposes, it is sufficient to look at how it applies to graph coloring theory.

If each of the $n$ vertices is connected by a line to every other vertex, then the graph is called a complete graph with $n$ vertices and is denoted by the symbol $K_n$.
