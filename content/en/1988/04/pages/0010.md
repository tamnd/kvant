Of course, the law of conservation of total energy is independent. But we did not include the energy of gravity in our reasoning. And this energy is unique - it is negative (see formula (1)). How does this affect the processes occurring in the Universe? Let's try to understand this using the following example: consider a gas ball, between the particles of which gravitational forces act. Let us assume that at first the substance of the ball was cold and was scattered in space, the mutual gravitation between particles was extremely small, and the gravitational energy of the substance was practically equal to zero. Let the matter gradually, under the influence of albeit weak gravity, gather into a ball, and this ball is removed further and further by gravity. It is clear that the substance of the ball is gaining more and more speed and, consequently, more and more kinetic energy of motion. This positive energy will increase due to gravity. But due to the law of conservation of energy, the total energy of the system must be conserved. This means that the increase in positive energy of motion is accompanied by a decrease in the potential energy of gravity, i.e., an increase in its absolute value (after all, $E_\pi < 0$). Now it is clear that as the system scans, the positive part of its energy can increase due to gravity. This is the circumstance in the past, when gravity was not paid due attention and was not taken into account. Since the positive part of the energy of an isolated system can increase, then the increase in energy (which necessarily occurs) does not necessarily lead to the fading of processes.

Thus, the conclusion about “heat death” as it was made in the middle of the last century, when the fact of the non-existential nature of the Universe was not taken into account, is incorrect.

Let us now see how exactly gravity “works” in the model of a pulsating Universe, determining the choice to freeze all macroscopic movements in the world.

In a pulsating Universe, in each cycle there is an increase in energy, an accumulation of heat (according to the second law of thermodynamics, which, of course, is not violated). The accumulation of thermal energy occurs on a large scale, for example, during the glow of stars processing nuclear energy reserves for study. We will assume that in the signal state the energy cannot decrease sharply.

Entropy increases from cycle to cycle. At first glance, this should lead to the fading of the pulsations, to a decrease in their amplitude. It would seem that the picture should be similar to the damping of oscillations of a pendulum, when the tension in its suspension gradually transfers the energy of oscillations into the body. In fact, the picture of pulsation in such a model of the Universe looks completely different - the amplitude of the pulsation of the Universe increases!
