— One of our readers sent us the following problem related to the Erdsch conjecture: $n$ of infinite arithmetic progressions cover the first $2^n$ natural numbers; prove that they cover the entire natural series. What can you say about this task?

— This statement, or its variant with the replacement of $2^{2n}$ by $2n!$, is relatively easy to prove by induction. But the fact is that the Erdsch conjecture itself (in which $2^n$ appears instead of $2^{2n}$ - and this estimate is already exact!) was proven by Crittenden and Van den Eijden (1969). The proof is difficult; it cannot be discussed briefly.

When solving such problems, the first stage is very important - to make sure whether the hypothesis is valid: you either try to build countermeasures, or prove some special cases, weaker statements or consequences of the hypothesis; This will happen until you finally gain confidence in the validity of what you are proving.
