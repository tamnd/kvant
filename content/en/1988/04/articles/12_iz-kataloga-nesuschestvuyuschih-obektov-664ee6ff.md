⟦folio 61⟧

The pressure in the liquid between the plates is $\Delta p=2\sigma/d$ less than atmospheric pressure. This means that the plates are pressed against each other layer $F=\Delta pS$, where $S=a^2$ is the area of ​​the plate. In order to tear one plate from another, it is this force that must be compensated. As a result, substituting numerical data, we find the lifting force:

$$F=\frac{2\sigma}{d}a^2=4,8\text{ H.}$$

However, as it turns out, it costs nothing to separate the plates by lowering them into water. You can also make the task easier by moving them relative to each other.

For self-control, we suggest you solve the following problems.

Urazhennya

1. What relative error is made when measuring atmospheric pressure along the height of the soil column, if the internal diameter of the barometric tube is $d=5\text{ mm}$, and the surface tension coefficient of mercury is $\sigma=0,48\text{ H/m}$? Does such a barometer underestimate or overestimate readings?

2. The capillary tube is immersed in water in such a way that the length of the unimmersed part is $l=0,2\text{ m}$. The water rose in the tube to a height of $l/2=0,1\text{ m}$. In this position, the upper end of the tube is occupied with a finger and the tube is immersed in water until the water level in the tube is equal to the water level in the vessel. Find the length of the part of the tube protruding from the water in this position. External atmospheric pressure $p_0=10^5\text{ Pa}$.

3. Two soap puddings of different sizes are connected by a tube (Fig. 7). Which one will deflate?

4. A rimmed tent protects well from jedr, but if a hand touches the ceiling of the tent during a djedr, does the ceiling begin to “leak”? Why?

5. Where will a drop of wetting liquid move in a horizontally located conical capillary?

6. On a soap bubble with radius $R$ there is a bubble with radius $r$. What is the radius of curvature of the film separating them?
