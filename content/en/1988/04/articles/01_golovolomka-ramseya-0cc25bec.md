⟦folio 9⟧

Here we show a three-coloring of the complete graph $K_{16}$, that is, a graph with 16 vertices, all pairs of vertices of which are connected by edges. This coloring is replaced by the fact that it does not contain a single single-valued triangle, while any (triangular) coloring of a complete graph with a large number of vertices (for example, $K_{23}$) necessarily contains such a triangle. A complete graph with the smallest number of vertices, in which, when coloring, a forbidden single-valued configuration (in this case, triangles) is necessarily encountered, is called a “Ramsey graph” (for a given set of forbidden configurations). Thus, the graph $K_{17}$ is the corresponding Ramsey graph. Of course, for other numbers of colors and other sets of configurations, their own Ramsey graphs arise; you can read about them in the article by M. Gardner in this issue. Each Ramsey graph (with n vertices) has a puzzle and a cgram associated with it. The puzzle is to color the complete graph $K_{n-1}$ without creating any forbidden configurations. (So ​​the figure given here is the solution to this puzzle for $n=17$). Izra is carried out on the Ramsey graph (say, on $K_{17}$) by two lines, which alternately cut out the edges of the graph (here - in three colors), known forbidden configurations (here - single-valued triangles); The loser is the one who is forced to create such a configuration. There is no prostration (why?).
