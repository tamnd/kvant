"Physics for Fighting"
V. F. YAKOVLEV

Studying at the institute was difficult for me. Maybe that’s why I was especially indignant at the frequent occurrences in physics and mathematics courses of remarks like “as is known...”, “from here we easily obtain...”, “easy to see...” Where is it known? Why is it easy? On the contrary, it is very difficult, and sometimes simply impossible! Such repairs not only inform students and students, but contribute to the emergence of a set of inferiority in them.

Or maybe they are covering up the incompetence of textbook authors? Indeed, a capable student will be able to understand even a text riddled with errors. I am sure that if I were offered to write, for example, a course on the analysis of infinitesimals, a response from me, lecture constructs and textbooks, I would do it in such a way that Euclid and Archimedes would understand everything perfectly. It follows that textbooks for smart people are easy to write, a task that can be accomplished even in fights. But writing a textbook for people with moderate abilities is not easy. But what a noble task this is!

Imagine: “Quantum mechanics for the mentally retarded”, “Higher mathematics for round fights” - these books would not be worth the price! And one must also make note of the text of these books: “from here it is difficult to obtain...”, “it is not easy to see that...”, etc. A teacher who has overcome the corresponding undead wisdom would simply grow wings.

After what has been said, it is easy to imagine what my joy was when, reading the book of the famous physicist G. A. Gamow “Matter, Earth, Sky”

A few words about the personality of the author of the book. My attempts to establish it for a long time did not lead to success: I did not find such a name either in the physical journals of those years, or in the address books “All Petersburg” or “All Moscow”, or in the multilingual dictionary of pessimists.

But one day, when I once again read the given gift inscription, I looked at the signature “S. M." with fresh eyes. Why "M"? And then it dawned on me. How do you most often choose your song? They keep their name, and turn their absence into a surname. If this is so, then the author was filled in with Sergey Olimpevich, and his last name, they believe, began with the letter “M”. This was quite a good clue, and now it was possible to try to start a search - for example, in the regularly published lists of members of the Russian Physico-Imic Society, the minutes of the meeting of which were published in the journal of this society.

Having taken several volumes of the magazine, which were temporally not far from the “mark” I knew - 1908, I quickly asked about the problem and found Sergei Olimpevich Maksimovich. After that, we again turned to the above-mentioned reference books, the yearbooks “All Leningrad” and the book “Scientists of Leningrad” published in 1934 by the USSR Academy of Sciences, I easily discovered that Sergei Olimpevich was born on August 5, 1876 in St. Petersburg, in the 30s of his life in Leningrad (on Ogorodnikov Avenue) and worked at the State Institute of Geodesy and potatoes, dealing with applied issues of aerial photography and the physics of physical image phenomena, as well as changing various characteristics of photographic materials (there is also a field of physics: sensitometry, or physical metrology). S. O. Maksimovich taught at the Photocinema Technical School, collaborated at the State Optical Institute and at the Leningrad

Fig. 1. Tellog extension.
All bodies elongate from the tellote, which is why, for example, rails are always made shorter than they should be. Anyone can repeat this at home.

Place the relative, presented and independently, on a cold stove so that he rests against the wall himself, and with his head against a stack of books on the edge of the stove. Plant a garden, and you will see how, as it grows, the relative will lengthen and call out the books until they fall. Despite the obviousness of the phenomenon, we will continue it. As the temperature rises, the phenomenon will change: the relative will become deformed and finally jump up and run away. In this way, everything will be smoothly convinced of the law, which is called the transformation of tellot into movement.

If you put a relative directly on a hot stove, he may go into a spheradal state, and the experiment will fail.

Fig. 2. Simple electric machine.
Working with large electric machines is not cheap and not safe. Meanwhile, everyone can forgive a stronger gold watch? The never-fading shine of the metal, the movement of the hands, as if indicating time - everything tells us about constancy, about eternity. Actually this is not true. Take a massive gold watch with an anchor movement and immediately lower it in the evening into a mixture of nitric and hydrochloric acids, poured into a large porcelain jar. The next day, the watch will seem to disappear: only the glass and the dial will remain. They must be taken out and rinsed, dried and stored in a hygroscopic container. Don't worry about the clock: nothing is lost in nature! Pour the green container into a bottle with a ground stopper and store it in the dark.

Fig. 4. Refraction of light.

"What are you doing! — the mistress of the house screams in horror, seeing how you quickly headed towards the mirror with a raised stick. “You will break the mirror!”

I haven't been to anything. From the laws of optics you know that the angle of incidence is related to the angle of refraction under certain conditions, and therefore it is enough to grab the mirror with the end of a stick so that this condition is met, and the stick will break, but the lady’s mirror will not crack - to the considerable surprise of those approaching.

The declared glass, not a mirror, under such conditions, of course, would have shattered into pieces.

Fig. 5. Sound propagation.

As you know, sound travels along the surface of metal bodies. This is the basis of the interesting experiment depicted here. A thin-walled, rigid cauldron is placed on the head of the person who wishes to be shot, and they shoot at him point-blank with a pistol, machine gun or mortar. To the person under the cauldron, a service shot will seem no louder than a click.

Fig. 6. Man on action. (On the physiology of birds).

This experience will create a great sensation, especially among the mistress of the house. “Which of you can hatch chickens within a quarter of an hour?”

Having cast a triumphant glance over the silent gathering, you leave and, having found a hen in the yard, select from under her two ten good hatched eggs. Return them to the hall, lay them out on a stool, but first place them under it, unnoticed by the public, light a kerosene lamp and hide it under the skirts of the frock coat. The hidden telltale of kerosene will quickly develop chickens, which will fill the room with cheerful circling. You just have to be careful not to crush people.

Fig. 7. Interface and diffraction.

One might expect that there will be more light, but it turns out that the sheet of paper will darken. This is the interface, which the great Newton called the golden key of Providence.
