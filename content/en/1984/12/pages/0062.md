Problem 2
To determine the laser wavelength, we use an object micrometer as a diffraction grating:
$$d_0 \sin \alpha_0 = n_0 \lambda_0.$$
Here $d_0=10^{-5}$ m is the constant of the object micrometer, $\alpha_0$ is the angle at which a maximum of $n_0$ order is observed, $\lambda_0$ is the laser wavelength. From here
$$\lambda_0 = \frac{d_0 \sin \alpha_0}{n_0}.$$
Let's do the same for the diffraction grating and find its constant:
$$\lambda_0 = \frac{d_1 \sin \alpha_1}{n_1}, \quad d_1 = d_0 \frac{n_1 \sin \alpha_0}{n_0 \sin \alpha_1}.$$
Now let's calculate the wavelength $\lambda_x$ of the yellow neon line. To do this, we will fix the lamp tightly, cover it with a ruler, leaving only a thin strip, and look at it through a diffraction grating. We will see several of her pictures. For any of them we can write
$$\lambda_x = \frac{d_1 \sin \alpha_2}{n_2}$$

After carrying out appropriate measurements and calculations, the value was obtained for the desired wavelength
$$\lambda_x = (5,9 \pm 0,2) \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ м.}$$
