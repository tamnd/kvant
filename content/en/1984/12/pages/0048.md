Chisle and S. Strukov (28 points), and third prizes were awarded to participants who scored at least 18 points. In total, 98 out of 192 participants were awarded, that is, half of all schoolchildren.

Despite the unprecedented success of the USSR team, the biggest sensation was the excellent performance of the Mongolia team, which had previously been among the outsiders, and this year took 10th place, ahead of teams from countries such as France, Australia, and Sweden. Mongolian schoolchildren won 3 second prizes.

The jury awarded one special prize. It was awarded to Fedya Nazarov for the original solution to the 5th problem.

During the inspection and evaluation of the work, the participants were given improvements that were fun and entertaining in nature, according to the given Czechoslovakia competitions for sixth and seventh graders. One of these problems is depicted at figure on p. 45. Several problems of these competitions are also given in the Problem Book of section «Kvant» for junior schoolchildren” of this issue.

The Olympada was perfectly organized. I will remember for a long time the participation in the opening and closing ceremonies of the Olympics in the assembly hall of Charles University in Prague, excursions and walks around “golden Prague”, a visit to the castle-residence of the Czech queens in Karlštejn and a very interesting and extensive system of caves near Prague, friendly conversations with peers from many countries.

The next, XXVI International Mathematical Olympiad will take place in 1985 in Finland.

The XV International Physics Olympics will take place in Sweden from June 24 to July 1. The Humanities School in the small resort town of Sigtuna, forty-seven kilometers from Stockholm, was chosen as the venue for the Olympic Games.

Sigtuna Humanities School is the largest and most famous integrative school in Sweden. Currently, the school has 540 high school students, including 335 full-time students. (Teams of guide-translators were formed from among this year’s graduates and worked with teams from all countries.) Along with the standard courses for Sweden, this school has a special, “international” course, where training is conducted in English. Among the school's students are children from different European countries. Successful passing of exams at this school gives its graduates the right to enter universities in five hundred countries around the world. The school was founded in 1925. Among the school's graduates are the current King of Sweden, Carl XVI Gustaf, and the current Prime Minister Olof Palme.

Teams from 18 countries gathered to participate in the Olympics: Australia, Bulgaria, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Holland, Iceland, Cuba, Norway, Poland, Romania, the Soviet Union, Finland, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden, Yugoslavia. Forward-
