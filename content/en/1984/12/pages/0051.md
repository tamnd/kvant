⟦folio 49⟧

Experimental tour

Describe your method for solving the experimental task. It should be clear to the reader from your description why and how you solve various aspects of the task, as well as the sequence of execution of these moments. A formal mathematical calculation of errors is not necessary; simply estimating the error is sufficient.

Problem 1
Materials: sinusoidal oscillation generator with a frequency of 0.20 kHz; oscilloscope with two channels (dual beam); graph paper; diode; capacitor with a capacity of 0.10 µF (black, square); resistor with unknown resistance (red); commutation board; connecting wires.

Construct the circuit shown at figure 4; determine experimentally the average value of the power released in the resistor R when connecting points A and B to an alternating current generator with a frequency of 0.20 kHz and a voltage amplitude of 2.0 V (that is, between the minimums and maximums of 4.0 V).

Problem 2
In the spectrum of a neon lamp, several spectral lines are visible in the yellow-orange-red region. One of the yellow lines (at the short end of this group of spectral lines) is the brightest. Determine the wavelength of this line. Evaluate the accuracy in your wavelength calculations.

Accessories: neon lamp connected to an alternating voltage source of 220 V; laser (its wavelength is unknown); diffraction grating with unknown constant; micrometer object (a glass plate in the center of which has a cabinet 1 mm long, containing 100 divisions); wooden meter ruler; tripod; clamps.

Note. Next, you know the wavelength of the laser, you are not allowed to use this data.

Carefully!
1. When performing the task, do not allow the laser beam to hit your eye!
2. Do not touch the surfaces of the diffraction grating and the micrometer object with your fingers!
