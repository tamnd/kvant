5. The left side of the inequality can be rewritten as:

$$\frac{p}{n} < \frac{d}{n(n-3)/2}.$$

This statement can be read as follows: the arithmetic mean of the side lengths of a convex polygon is less than the arithmetic mean of the lengths of its diagonals, which repeats the formulation of problem M846, the solution of which is placed in «Kvant» No. 7 for 1984 on p. 53.

Let us prove the second part of the inequality. First consider the case of an odd number of sides: $n = 2k + 1$. Note that the length of the diagonal that connects two vertices located one after the other is less than the sum of the length of the side with ends at these three vertices. Hence $d_2$ is the sum of the lengths of such diagonals, less than $2p$, $d_3$ is the sum of the lengths of diagonals straddling the vertices lying on the perimeter of the polygon through two, less than $3p$, ..., $d_k < kp$. But the sum is $d_2 + d_3 + ... + d_k = d$, and the sum is $2p + 3p + ... + kp = (k + 2)(k - 1)p/2$. Considering that $n = 2k + 1$, we get $(k + 2)(k - 1)p/2 = \left( \frac{n}{2} \right) \left( \frac{n+1}{2} \right) - 2)p/2$. This proves the inequality.

In the case of $n = 2k$, “diametrical” diagonals will arise, on one and the other side of which there are $k-1$ vertices. It should be noted that the length of each of them is less than $p/2$ and therefore $d_k < kp/2$. At the same time $d_2 + d_3 + ... + d_k < p(2+3 + ... + (k-1)) = (k-2) \times (k+1)p/2$.

Finally we get

$$d < \frac{kp}{2} + \frac{(k-2)(k+1)p}{2} = \frac{p}{2} \left( \frac{n}{2} \right) \left( \frac{n+1}{2} \right) - 2).$$

F. Nazarov's solution, for which he received a special jury prize, is based on an easily established inequality for the projection of the side of diagonals onto a line of a given direction and the subsequent interpretation of these inequalities in all directions. It is known that the corresponding interval for one segment is equal to twice the length of this segment. Thus, the inequality for projections is transferred to the inequality for the segments themselves.
