finding the polar (or complementary) triangular angle.

Polar angle*). Let the triangular angle $SABC$ be given. Let us draw a redistributor through its vertex $S$ to the plane $SBC$ and select a ray $SA_1$ on it (from two possible ones) so that the angle $ASA_1$ is obtuse. In other words, points $A$ and $A_1$ must lie on opposite sides of the plane $SBC$. (Explain for yourself why the angle $ASA_1$ cannot be straight.) The rays $SB_1$ and $SC_1$ are selected similarly: $(SB_1) \perp (SAC), (SC_1) \perp (SAB)$, the angles $BSB_1$ and $CSC_1$ are obtuse. The treghedral angle is $SA_1B_1C_1$ and is said to be polar to the angle $SABC$.

Many uses of polar angles are based on the following two interesting properties:

1) uelol, polar to polar, coincides with the original tregon angle;
2) for mutually polar triangular angles $SABC$ and $SA_1B_1C_1$

$$\alpha_1 = \pi - \hat{A}, \beta_1 = \pi - \hat{B}, \gamma_1 = \pi - \hat{C}, (4)$$

$$\hat{A}_1 = \pi - \alpha, \hat{B}_1 = \pi - \beta, \hat{C}_1 = \pi - \gamma. (5)$$

Prove these properties yourself (see Exercise 5).

Proof of the second cosine theorem. Let us write down the first cosine theorem for the angle $SA_1B_1C_1$ polar to the given angle $SABC$: $$\cos \alpha_1 = \cos \beta_1 \cos \gamma_1 + \sin \beta_1 \sin \gamma_1 \cos \hat{A}_1,$$ and use correspondences (4) and (5). We get

$$\cos (\pi - \hat{A}) = \cos (\pi - \hat{B}) \cos (\pi - \hat{C}) + \sin (\pi - \hat{B}) \sin (\pi - \hat{C}) \cos (\pi - \alpha),$$

from which (3) immediately follows.
