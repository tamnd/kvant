Second radiga

Obviously above the main bright radio one more, less bright one is visible. It is concentric with the first, but the order of colors in it is reversed. Its lower edge is red, the upper edge is violet. It arises as a result of the same processes of refraction and reflection of light in drops of water. But if the main radigue arises as a result of double refraction and single reflection of light, then the secondary one is formed as a result of double refraction and double reflection of light.

As can be seen from figure 4, the light in the drop is refracted at point A, reflected twice at points B and C and leaves the drop after the second refraction at point D. This radig is visible at an angle not of 42°, but of 52°. “Deprived” reflection leads to some loss of light energy (refraction of light also occurs at point C!), so it is less bright. The second radigue comes from completely different drops, located at a large angle to the axis of the cone.

It is clear that the light from certain drops (for both radigs) that gets into your eye cannot get into the eye of your neighbor or anyone else in general. Each observed nature shows his “individual” radiation that arose in “his” millions of drops.

Conclusion

The radigi theory presented here, proposed by Newton, does not describe all the processes occurring in drops of djedr. The radiga picture is complicated by the additional processes of interference and diffraction of light, which plays an important role when the droplets are very small in size. The picture described above applies to droplets with a diameter of 0.5–2 mm.

Additional phenomena somewhat change the appearance of the radio. All this leads to the fact that the color in radigue is less pure than the color that can be seen when sunlight is refracted in the form.
