Task 2. Solve the equation: $\sqrt{2} - 2\cos x + \sqrt{10} - 6\cos x = \sqrt{10} - 6\cos 2x$, where $x \in [0; \pi/2]$.

Solution. Let $|CA|=1, |CB|=3, |CD|=1, ACD=DCB=x$ (Fig. 2). Then $|AD|=\sqrt{2} - 2\cos x, |DB|=\sqrt{10} - 6\cos x, |AB|=\sqrt{10} - 6\cos 2x$.

This equation means that $|AD|+|DB|=|AB|$, hence $D \in [AB]$.

Therefore, $S_{ACD} + S_{CDB} = S_{ACB}$, that is, $x+3\sin x=3\sin 2x, 4\sin x=6\sin x \cdot \cos x$. It follows that $\cos x=2/3$, since $\sin x \neq 0$ (from the condition: $x \in [0; 90^\circ]$).

Answer: $x=\arccos 2/3$.

Other angles are spatial analogues of angles on a plane: many of their properties are similar to those of plane angles. For example, the bissertor of a convex angle (the so-called half-plane dividing the angle into two congruent angles) can be defined as the set of points of the angle equidistant from its faces. In the same way as planes, vertical and mixed angles are determined, and their values ​​are equal to the correspondence between $\varphi$ and $\pi-\varphi$, where $\varphi$ is the value of the original mixed angle.

Now we will analyze the solution to two problems, in some senses imaginary of each other.

Task 1. A dihedral angle of magnitude $\varphi$, $0<\varphi<\pi$ is intersected by a plane. Within what limits can the value $\varphi$ of the yield obtained in the section lie?

Task 2. Within what limits can the value $\varphi$ of the angle, which is a cut-off projection of the angle of the value $\varphi$, $0<\varphi<\pi$, lie?

Answer to task 1: $0<\varphi<\pi$. Since the magnitude of the other angle is less than $\pi$, it lies on one side of the plane of any of its faces. Therefore, the angle in the section lies along the angle of magnitude $\varphi$. To do this, the easiest way is to indicate two rays with a common vertex on the edge of the other angle, lying in different faces, such that the angle between them is equal to $\alpha$ (and then a cutting plane is drawn through these two rays).

Triangular angles. Let a triangle ABC be given and a point S not lying in the plane of this triangle. The trihedral angle SABC (convex) is the union of all rays with vertex S intersecting triangle ABC. (Sometimes they will also define a non-convex triangular angle SABC - the union of all rays with vertex S that do not intersect the interior of triangle ABC - we will also not consider triangular angles.)

Trihedral angles are one of the possible spatial analogues of triangles (another analogue is terrahedra). The pose of polygeometric triangles (in this case, the plane angle corresponds to the sides of the triangles, and the dihedral angle corresponds to the angles of the triangles). For example, the well-known theorem “each plane angle is less than the sum of the other two” is an analogue of the triangle inequality. Applying this theorem to the triangular angle SABC1 (Fig. 4), we obtain another positive property of triangular angles: “the sum of plane angles is less than 360°.”
