XXV
International Mathematical Olympiad

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. P. SAVIN

The anniversary XXV International Mathematical Olympiad was held from June 29 to July 10 in the capital of the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic, Prague. She collected recommendations for greater integration of exhibition materials into classroom work from many countries. The USSR stand was a great success, where the magazine «Kvant» was presented in font, as well as the library «Kvant».

At the end of the symplosum, the jury, consisting of the scientific leaders of the team, was present to the use of direct obligations - selecting a task for improvement. This work is very difficult because it is necessary to take into account the different levels of preparation between schoolchildren from developing countries and from young countries in Asia, Africa and Latin America. At the same time, it is necessary to process very complex tasks so that the winners can be identified.

The jury, headed by Professor F. Zitek, did an excellent job. This can be judged both by the results of the Olympiad and by the results of a survey conducted by a correspondent for the magazine «Kvant»: each jury member was asked to arrange the displayed tasks in order of increasing difficulty. Although individual jury members varied widely, the successful order of the task according to their difficulty matched the order obtained from the analysis of the participants' work.

Each problem was solved very quickly and according to the established tradition of holding the competition over two days, offering 3 problems on each day for 4.55 hours of work. Thus, the maximum amount of points that a participant could score was 42 points, as it has been for the last 5 years.

The tasks for which the competition was held were as follows.

First day
1 (Germany). Let x, y, z be non-negative real numbers such that x+y+z=1. Prove that
$$0 \leq xy+yz+xz-2xyz \leq \frac{7}{27}.$$

2 (Netherlands). Specify any pair of natural numbers (a, b) such that:
1) the number ab (a+b) is not divisible by 7,
2) $(a+b)^7-a^7-b^7$ is divisible by $7^7$.
Justify the answer.

3 (SRR). Two different points O and A are given on the plane. For each point X of this plane, different from point O, we denote by a(X) the magnitude of the angle AOX, expressed in radians (0≤a(x)<2π), where the angle a(x) is measured counterclockwise from the point OA, and by C(X) the circle with center O and radius OX+a(X)/OX.

Let a finite set of colors be given, and each point on the plane be surrounded by one of them. Prove that there is a point Y such that a(Y)>0 and on the circle C(Y) there is at least one point of the same color as the point Y.

Second day

4 (SRR). Let the line CD in a convex quadrilateral ABCD touch a circle with diameter AB. Prove that line AB touches a circle with diameter CD if and only if lines BC and AD are parallel.

5 (MPR). Let d be the sum of the lengths of the diagonals, and p the perimeter of a flat convex n-gon (n>3). Prove that

$$n-3 < \frac{2d}{p} < \left[ \frac{n}{2} \right] \left[ \frac{n+1}{2} \right] - 2$$

([x] is the integer part of x, that is, the largest integer that does not exceed x).

6 (Poland). Let a, b, c, d be odd integers satisfying the following conditions:

1) 0<a<b<c<d;
2) ad=bc;
3) a+d=2^k, b+c=2^m for some integers k and m. Prove that a=1.

(The country that proposed this task is indicated in parentheses after the task number.)
