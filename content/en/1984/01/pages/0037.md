Franklin gave such a rather strange interpretation to the electrical phenomena known in his time. Strange because it could not explain the most important fact known at that time - the attachment of light objects to assigned topics.

Electricity and...stockings. Another explanation was proposed in 1759 by the Englishman Robert Simmer. The reason for this was the contented observation he carried out.

Simmer decided to wear two pairs of stockings: black woolen ones for warmth and white silk ones for beauty. Removing both stockings from the nori at once and pulling one out of the other, Simmer saw how both stockings swelled, reproduced the shape of the leg and were pulled towards each other. However, stockings of the same color, both black and white, repel each other. If you hold two white stockings in one hand and two black stockings in the other, then when you bring your hands together, the mutual repulsion of stockings of the same color and the pulling of multi-colored ones leads to a funny fuss between them; stockings of opposite colors seem to pile up on each other and intertwine into one bizarre ball.

These observations led Simmer to the conclusion that every body has not one, but two electrical fluids - positive and negative, contained in the body in equal quantities. When two bodies are rubbed, one of them can pass from one body to another, then in one body there is an excess of one of the liquids, and in the other - its deficiency. Both bodies will become charged with electricity (charges) that are opposite in value.

Electrical conflict. This is how two ideas about electricity emerged. For a long time, almost a century and a half, none of them received universal recognition.

When at the end of the 18th - beginning of the 19th centuries. As the opportunity arose to obtain and study direct electric current, a debate arose about what exactly “flows” in a circuit containing a current source and conductors. There were also doubts about whether the electricity that is obtained by rubbing bodies and that which flows in an electrical circuit are the same. The latter even received a special name - galvanic electricity. But still, many believed that in the conductors of an electrical circuit there were two Simmer electricity at the same time, and they called electric current... an electrical conflict, since these electrical texts are in opposite directions. So, for example, when in 1820 H. Oersted published a brochure in which he described the action of current on a magnetic needle, which he had discovered, he called it: “Experiments concerning the action of an electric conflict on a magnetic needle.” Then the readers understood this strange name for us, and the brochure was a success due to the fundamental importance of the discovery made.

Simmer was right. The old dispute between the two theories—Franklin and Simmer—received a final resolution only at the end of the 19th and beginning of the 20th centuries.

Now we know that Simmer must be recognized as the winner in the dispute. “Simmer's electric liquids” are negatively charged electrins and positively charged protons, which are contained in equal numbers in every neutral body, in every atom of any substance.

But something turned out to be true in Franklin’s theory: when rubbing bodies, only one “fluid” can pass from one body to another - negatively charged electrins. However, the body they transfer to becomes negatively charged, while Franklin considered it to be positively charged.
