Let a rectangle $T$ with vertices $A, B, C, D$ (Fig. 7) be given on the plane $Oxy$. Let us identify the side $AB$ with the side $CD$ so that $A$ is identified with $D$, and $B$ with $C$; further we will identify the side $BC$ with the side $AD$ so that $B$ coincides with $A$, and $C$ with $D$ (with this identification it turns out that all the vertices of the rectangle $T$ merge into one point). Let us define the “distance” between two points of a rectangle as the length of the shortest path between these points, taking into account the above according to the identification.

For example, let's calculate the “distance” between points $M\left(-\frac{3}{4}a; \frac{1}{4}b\right)$ and $N\left(\frac{3}{4}a; b\right)$ (see Fig. 7). First of all, we replace that the segment $MN'$, where $N'=\left(\frac{3}{4}a; -b\right)$, is shorter than the segment $MN$; therefore, the shortest path from $M$ to $N$ must be sought among the paths connecting $M$ with $N'$ (since $N'$ and $N$ are the same point according to the identification condition). The right identity of the side $AD$ is at the same time the right identity of the side $BC$; Therefore, by “attaching” the side $a \leq x < 2a$, $-b \leq y \leq b$ to the side $BC$ (see Fig. 7), we see that every other path from $M$ to $N'$, including the segment $MN'$, is longer than the segment $M'N'$. This means that the shortest path from $M$ to $N$ is the union of the segments $MM_1$ and $M'_1N'$ and the “distance” between $M$ and $N$ is $\sqrt{(a/2)^2+(3b/4)^2}$.
