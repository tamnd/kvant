The basis of geometric optics, which operates with the concept of “light ray”, is made up of three laws - the laws of rectilinear propagation, reflection and refraction of light. In ancient times, when these laws were formulated, the question of the nature of light had not yet arisen, and nothing about the physical realization of light was hidden behind the concept of “ray”. This is how it was done in the textbook “Physics 10” (§§ 37 and 65). However, at the end of the 19th - beginning of the 20th centuries. It became clear that light has not only wave properties, but also corpuscular properties. From the point of view of corpuscular (quantum) nature, light is a stream of elements of light particles - photons. In a homogeneous medium, the beam can be considered the trajectories of photons.

But it is interesting that long before this an amazing principle was formulated, from which all the basic laws of propagation follow.

Fig. 1.

light. This principle, found by the French mathematician Pierre Fermat (1601-1665) around 1660, says: of all possible paths between two points, light travels along the one along which the travel time is called the shortest.

From Fermat’s principle (as it is generally called) it follows that in a homogeneous medium (in such a medium the speed of light is the same everywhere) light should propagate straight: a straight line is the shortest distance between two points, therefore, the propagation time is the shortest.

Let us now show that the law of light reflection is also a direct consequence of Fermat’s principle.

Law of light reflection. Let MM be a plane mirror.
