Flat Klein bottle

Let us turn again to the rectangle on figure 7. Let us introduce the following identification rules: side AD is identified with BC while maintaining the order of the points, that is, the point (-a, y) ∈ AD is identified with the point (a; y) ∈ BC, and side AB is identified with the side CD with a change in the order of the points, that is, the point (x: -b) ∈ AB is identified with the point (—x: b) ∈ CD; in part, all vertices will be counted as one point. We will introduce “straight lines” and rules for measuring “distances” as above. You can check that again in the neighborhood of each point the geometry is Euclidean (carry out this check for the neighborhood of the vertex of a rectangle). This model of local Euclidean geometry is called a flat one-sided gorom or flat Klein bottle.

Conclusion

So, the principle of equal demands on all axioms has certainly justified itself: we discovered that the Euclidean geometry of the plane is not based on a single axiom about parallels, but that other axioms significantly determine its appearance. Moreover, it turned out that even Euclideanity in the neighborhood of each point and the satisfiability of the fifth space in space do not lead to Euclideanity as a whole (example: a cylinder and a flat torus). This means that even if in all separately studied parts of space the geometry turns out to be Euclidean, then in general the structure of such a world is not necessarily as simple as a two-dimensional plane or three-dimensional space.

In higher geometry courses it is proven that besides the five examples listed - the plane, the cylinder, the flat torus, the infinite Möbius strip and the flat Klein bottle - there are no other complete local “worlds”. (Completeness, roughly speaking, means that one can unlimitedly extend a “line” with each point, even if only along itself.)

As for the local Euclidean spaces of three dimensions, they differ significantly from them, there are 18 types. Of these, we will mention only one example: this is a layer of space between two parallel planes, which are considered identified by points symmetrical with respect to the middle plane in the layer.
