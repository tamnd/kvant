Euclidean What does the geometry look like on a cylinder (that is, on a strip P with the edges in relation) as a whole? How is the “distance” arranged on a cylinder? Which ones are “straight” on it? What axims are fulfilled on it?

Before answering these questions, let's look at one somewhat unusual example. Let there be two parallel roads in a certain area, along which road stations are quite often located; From each station there is one telephone line - to a symmetrical station on another road. In interior areas there is no telephone service, and messages are transmitted, say, by runners. Let us take as the distance between two points the time required for the transmission of any message. Then it is clear that in order to quickly transmit a message from point A to point B, sometimes it is better for the messenger to run not directly from A to B, but in the opposite direction, to a nearby telephone exchange.

The same kind of unusual distance occurs on our strip with glued edges. Indeed, consider points A, B, C in strip P. For points A and B (the coordinates $y_A$ and $y_B$ differ by less than half the width of the strip - π) the distance is conditional, that is, the length of the segment $AB$. But for A and C (for which $|y_A - y_C| > \pi$) the distance is $|AK'| + |KC|$, where $A'$ is obtained from A with two segments $CA'$ with the edge of the strip and $KK' = -AA'$ (Fig. 5). In the language of the “telephone” example, it is more profitable to send a messenger from C to point K, transmit the message instantly by telephone to K' and deliver it to A with a second messenger along the segment $K'A$.

Problem 1. Prove that the distance from point $A(x_A; y_A)$ to point $B(x_B; y_B)$ on a strip П with glued edges is given by the formula

$$|AB| = \begin{cases} \sqrt{(x_A - x_B)^2 + (y_A - y_B)^2} \\ \frac{\varepsilon \left|y_A - y_B\right|}{\sqrt{(x_A - x_B)^2 + (2\pi - |y_A - y_B|)^2}}, \\ \varepsilon \left|y_A - y_B\right| > \pi. \end{cases}$$

“Straight lines”, like distances, are unusual in our geometry - they come in three types (Fig. 6, a). Firstly, a straight line will be any ordinary straight line parallel to the edges of the strip. Secondly, “straight” will be any segment (with glued ends) connecting the edges and perpendicular to them. And thirdly, the “straight line” will be a set of periodically repeating parallel oblique segments - such as in figure 6, a. On a cylinder in space, these three straight lines represent, respectively, the generators of the cylinder, circles parallel to the guide, and helical lines (Fig. 6, b).

As for the axioms, we note first of all that the parallel axiom is satisfied “in general” (on the entire surface)!
