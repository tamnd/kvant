The rectangle $T$ with the identified parameters of the opposite sides and indicated above by the rule for calculating “distances” is called a flat torus.

“Straight lines” on a flat torus are defined as in the strip $Π$—the cylinder model. Problem 6. Prove that a “line” on a flat torus will be closed if and only if the number $(a \ tg a)/b$ is rational, where $tg a$ is the slope (relative to the axis $Ox$) of the segments in $T$ that make up this line.

We claim that the geometry on a flat torus constructed in this way is locally Wuclidean. Indeed, each interior point of the rectangle $T$ has a small neighborhood, where all the objects and modification rules introduced for the flat torus are the same as in Euclidian geometry. For the point $M_1$ on the boundary, the proof is seen from figure 7. For the vertex, this can be seen from the same figure: all the neighborhoods of the identification, and the geometry around the vertex also turns out to be the geometry of a Euclidian circle!

So, the flat torus is another example of a local Wuclidean world. On it, as on a cylinder, the fifth postulate is valid “as a whole,” however, the structure of the torus “as a whole” differs from the general plane and from the cylinder.

Problem 7. Prove that there are no long distances on a flat torus, although there are straight lines of how long. Explore the shape of a “circle” on a flat torus as its radius increases.

There is another important difference between a flat torus and a cylinder: a flat torus cannot, while preserving the length of the curves, be represented in three-dimensional space in the form of a surface on which pairs of identified sides - $AB$ and $CD$, $BC$ and $AD$ - would actually be glued together, as happens with the edges of the strip $Π$ when folding it into a cylinder. But this difference is already called external, since it manifests itself only when trying to connect the geometry of the strip $Π$ and the rectangle $T$ with the geometry of some surfaces in a space external to them. If the rectangle $T$ is considered to be made of rubber and is allowed to stretch, then a torus can be glued from it; For more details on this, see the article by A. Infinite Möbius strip

Let us again consider the infinite strip $Π : a \leqslant y \leqslant b, -\infty < x < +\infty$, but this time we will identify the edges of the strip $Π$ according to the following law: the point $(x; a)$ is identified with the point $(-x; b)$ (that is, the straight line $y = b$ is first reflected in the mirror relative to the axis $Oy$ and only then is identified with the straight line $y = a$). The definitions of "distance" and "straight" are similar to the case of a cylinder and a flat torus. The resulting local Wuclidean plane is called an “infinite Möbius strip”.
