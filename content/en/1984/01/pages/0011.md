At first, it was quite difficult for the young researcher, since he had the most damaged ideas about radioactivity. And everyone around was doing just that, and Marie Curie’s daughter Irene, who defended her doctoral dissertation at that time, was especially successful.

However, Frederick surprisingly quickly mastered both the theories and the experiment. “The boy is a real firework,” Marie Curie said about him. The process of acquiring radioactivity led to the fact that a year later the marriage of Irene Curie and Federac Jolu was registered in the district of Paris.

What connected these people during thirty years of their happiness and difficult life?

“Irene... seemed the direct opposite of Frederick; She was truly calm and unperturbed, just as he was hot-tempered and expansive... During arguments, she did not make the slightest concession. With ruthless straightforwardness, Irene put forward her positions, directly throwing them at the owner, even if he occupied a high social position. On the contrary, Federac Jolu, never conceding on the merits, carefully used his abilities to psychologically prepare the owner to accept his point of view” (P. Bicard)*.

Obviously, for them, Niels Bohr’s favorite phrase could not have been better suited: “Opposites are not contradictions, but complements!”

In 1930, Federak defended his doctoral dissertation, and the Jolus-Curies continued their work on the study of radioactivity. They wanted to make a powerful radiation source in order to bombard various substances with alpha particles. Making such a source was not easy and far from safe. Years will pass, and the radioactive full will remind itself in the most terrible way.

*) P. Bicard - professor of physics. Friend of Jolus-Curie.
