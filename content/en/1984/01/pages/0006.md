⟦folio 4⟧

Its purpose is not an attempt to replicate Dumas, but a desire to follow the development of physics and astronomy over the decade (1971-1981) using the example of a number of key problems *).

But, as the English say, “the proof of the pudding is in the eating” - in order to evaluate the quality of the pudding, you need to eat it - and it’s time, finally, to give the list in question.

Macrophysics

1. Controlled thermonuclear synthesis.
2. High temperature superconductivity.
3. New substances (problems of creating metallic hydrogen and other “unusual” substances).
4. Metallic extonic (electron-hole) liquid in semiconductors. Metal-dielectric transitions. Spin glasses.
5. Phase transitions of the second order and close to them (critical phenomena).
6. Surface physics.
7. Behavior of matter in superstrong magnetic fields.
8. Study of very large molecules. Liquid crystals.
9. Razers, grazers and lasers of new types.
10. Solitons. Strange attractors. Nonlinear phenomena.
11. Superheavy elements (distant transruans). "Exotic" kernels.

Microphysics

12. Quarks and gluons. Quantum chromodynamics.
13. Unified theory of weak and electron magnetic interaction. W-, Z-bosons. Leptons.

In this article, as well as in the book I mentioned, you can find quite numerous references to literature on all the issues raised.

14. Great lunch. Proton decay. Neutrino mass. Super lunch.
15. Fundamental line. Part interaction at high and ultra-high energies.
16. Violation of CP invariance. Nonlinear phenomena in vacuum, in superstrong magnetic and electronic fields. Phase transitions in vacuum.

Astrophysics

17. Experimental verification and limits of applicability of the general theory of relative energy.
18. Gravitational waves.
19. Cosmological problem. Connection of cosmology with high energy physics.
20. Neutron stars and pulsars. Physics of black holes.
21. Quasars and galactic nuclei. Formation of galaxies.
22. Origin of cosmic rays and cosmic gamma and RENT radiation.
23. Neutrino astronomy.
The division of all these problems into macrophysics, microphysics and astronomy is quite arbitrary. Selecting 23 topics rather than, say, 20 or 30 is also arbitrary. The almost complete absence of questions related to biology is not due to underestimation of the importance of the connections between physics and biology (on the contrary, I treat biophysics with special respect and interest), but to the desire to somehow limit the topic and, moreover, to my completely insufficient competence in biology. Finally, the main thing: there cannot and should not be a concern to occupy ourselves only with “key” problems. The latter are somehow highlighted and attract special attention due to the features noted above. However, no matter how rationally one defines the concept of “key problem,” will it be harmful even for two physicists who compile exactly the same lists of such problems?
