⟦folio 13⟧

It was like fireworks: at the beginning of 1934 there were three artificial radionzotopes, by the end there were already about fifty. They were obtained by irradiating elements with α-particles, protons and deuterons in newly created accelerators. In Italy, Enrico Fermi's group used uncharged projectiles - neutrons, which easily penetrated the nucleus. The use of artificial radionzotopes in chemistry and medicine has already begun. Over time, biology, energy, metallurgy, food industry, criminology, agriculture, etc., etc. were added to this list. More than a thousand artificial radioactive isotopes are known (and stable isotopes - about 300), and the number of their “specialties” has exceeded a hundred.

How is it an accident that it was Irene and Federic Jollu-Curie who discovered artificial radioactivity? The quality of the experimental physicists allowed them to make this discovery?

About the two main qualitative directions of B. Pontecorvo*): powerful scientific imagination and the ability to recognize even the most seemingly incredible and strange fact. We can add to this a third one—scientific foresight.

*) B. Pontecorvo - Soviet physicist, academician. He worked for several years in the Jollus-Curie laboratory.

Disaster at the equator

It's winter in Moscow now, summer in Australia. What time of year is it in the stylized equator? The residents of this city answer this question with a joke: “If it’s sunny, then it’s summer, if it’s raining, it’s winter.” What if you answer seriously?

The height of the Sun's culmination changes every day. For us, in the semichariki service, the day of the lowest culmination of the Sun occurs on December 22 (“winter solstice”). In the southern half, as is known, this day is June 22 (“summer solstice”). Now imagine that we are moving towards the equator; the data of the lowest culmination remains unchanged. But as soon as you step over the equator, this value changes abruptly, at 6 months. Let's take a step back - a 6 month jump! How to explain this paradox?

In fact, there is nothing surprising here. However, not all physical things change continuously - sometimes there are gaps and jumps. The mathematical theory of “non-smooth” changes is called the theory of singularities or (with the light hand of the French mathematician P. Thomas) the theory of catastrophe.

In the described case, the simplest “catastrophe” occurs at the equator: any small change in one value (geographic latitude) leads to a significant abrupt change in another (the date of the lowest culmination).
