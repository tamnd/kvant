Rotation of the Earth and acceleration of gravity

The textbook “Physics 8” on page 104 says: “The rotation of the Earth leads to the fact that the acceleration of gravity, changed relative to any body on the surface of the Earth, is different at different points.” Let us find out the reasons for this difference.

Regarding movement, as is known, the movement of the same body, but relative to different reporting systems moving relative to each other, looks different. The trajectories of movement, speed, and acceleration are different.

If one of the reporting systems of motion is relative to each other with a constant speed (without acceleration), then the speed $\vec{v}$ of the body relative to one of the reporting systems, conventionally accepted as stationary, is equal to $\vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_2$, where $\vec{v}_1$ is the speed of the body relative to the moving system and $\vec{v}_2$ is the speed of the moving system relative to the stationary one. The acceleration of the body relative to both systems in this case is the same (it is, as they say, absolute).

It’s a different matter if one of the systems reports movement relative to each other with acceleration. Then not only the speed, but also the acceleration relative to the acceleration $\vec{a}$ of the body relative to the stationary reporting system is equal to the sum of the accelerations $\vec{a}_1$ and $\vec{a}_2$, where $\vec{a}_1$ is the acceleration of the body relative to the moving system and $\vec{a}_2$ is the acceleration of the moving system relative to the stationary one, that is

$$\vec{a} = \vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2.$$

One example of just such a case is the free fall of a body to the Earth. As the Earth rotates on its axis, the reporting system associated with any point on its surface moves with acceleration. Therefore, the acceleration of a falling body relative to a stationary system must be equal to the sum of the acceleration of the body relative to the moving system and the acceleration of the moving system relative to the stationary one.

A fixed reporting system can be associated with some unchanging body, for example, with the Sun. But you can do it easier, without leaving the Earth. After all, there are points on the surface of the Earth that do not participate in its rotation. These are the surfaces of the Earth through which the axis of rotation passes. Therefore, in order to find the value of the acceleration of a falling body relative to a stationary frame of reference, it is necessary to change the acceleration of gravity at the earth's pole.
