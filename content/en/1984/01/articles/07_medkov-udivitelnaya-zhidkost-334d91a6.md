Amazing liquid

N. A. MEDKOV

This article will talk about some of the properties of an unusual substance - ferromagnetic fluid - and simple experiments with it.

If mankind has been familiar with solid magnets since ancient times, then ferromagnetic liquids, that is, colloidal solutions of solid ferromagnetic materials, were obtained in the 60s of our century. Now these new substances have already been thoroughly studied and even find some practical applications.

Let us also “get to know” ferromagnetic fluid and its properties.

How to prepare ferromagnetic liquid

First of all, you will need castor oil (you can buy it at the pharmacy) and iron oxide hydrate Fe₂O₃ • nH₂O (you can find it in the school chemistry room).

The author of this article is Ilya Medkov, a 10th grade student at Moscow School 204.

Pour 10-15 g of oil into a porcelain mortar and mix it with the modified ferromagnet until a liquid forms, similar to thick sour cream. Grind the prepared mixture in a mortar for 10-15 minutes.

What you have obtained is not a real collective solution, since the particles are heterogeneous and their horizontal dimensions are larger than 10⁻⁹ m. (To obtain truly collective solutions, you need complex apparatus, for example, a vibrating mill to change the particles and a centrifuge for their subsequent separation by size.) However, for studying the characteristic properties of ferromagnetic liquids, this mixture is completely suitable.

In our experiments, we used a school demonstration electromagnet with a winding of 2400 turns as a source of magnetic field. It was powered from an alternating current network with a voltage of 220 V through a diode bridge (any diodes with a maximum current are suitable, more

Having prepared the ferromagnetic liquid and assembled the electromagnet, you can begin experiments.

Experiments and observations

1. Place 30-40 g of liquid in a shallow cup (necessarily made of non-magnetic material!), place the cup on the end of the magnetic circuit and turn on the field. You will observe a rise in liquid in the center (in our experiments it is about 5 mm). If the mixture is not mixed well enough or the content of ferromagnetic powder is too high, you will get a “hedgehog”, such as in figure 1.

2. Mix the liquid thoroughly after the previous experiment. If you get a “hedgehog”, prepare a new portion of the mixture with a higher oil content. Place the cup on the end of the magnetic circuit again, take sticks of non-magnetic material and, with the magnetic field turned on, draw some pattern on the surface of the liquid (Fig. 2,a). Wait a few seconds and turn off the field. The surface of the liquid will be smoothed out, and the outline of the drawing you have applied will still be visible on it (Fig. 2, b). Then turn the field back on - figure will appear again (Fig. 2,c). The routine can be continued several times by turning the magnetic field on and off.

3. For this experiment you will need a very strong magnetic field. You can use the same electromagnet, but the theoretical approach is a cone. Near the tip of the cone, the magnetic field will be strongest. If you drop a drop of liquid onto the tip and then turn on the field, the liquid will instantly change color, becoming orange or bright red (depending on its composition).

The experiments are finished. Let us think about why and how these amazing phenomena will occur.

In the first experiment, we saw that the liquid rises in the center of the cross section of the magnetic circuit. This effect is known as magnetostatic and consists of an increase in fluid pressure in the region of a strong field.

The ability to “remember” is characteristic of many materials. Likewise, a magnetic fluid, having once assumed a form in which its energy is minimal, again “remembers” its state when the field is turned on.

Other experiences and observations will be binding themselves.

Possible practical applications

Many sharpened structures using electromagnetic fluids have been invented. Here are some of them.

In high-quality loudspeakers, magnetic fluid can be poured into the gap between the electromagnetic coil and its core. This allows a significant increase to improve the characteristics of the diamiks.

Based on this amazing liquid, you can create a special pump. It is a tube made of flexible material placed together with an electromagnet in a chamber with liquid. When the electromagnet is turned on, the liquid pulsates, deformation of the elastic tube occurs, and the substance in it is pumped in the desired direction. This is somewhat reminiscent of the functioning of the human esophagus, hence the name: peristaltic pump. Such a pump can be used, for example, to supply fuel on spacecraft.

Ferromagnetic fluids can also be used in magnetic fluids for sealing bearings of turbines, as a heat bed in heat engines, etc.

Conclusion

Experiments with ferromagnetic liquids are very interesting. If you liked our experiences, you can continue them or come up with new ones. Thus, in popular chemistry books you can find a recipe for preparing pyrophoric iron. It is such a fine powder that it spontaneously ignites in air (be careful with it!). Ferromagnetic liquids containing pyrophoric iron have very interesting properties.
