Cubic parabola (6) will always continue through the origin. The tangent of its inclination angle at the origin of coordinates is determined by the formula

$$f'(0) = -p.$$ (16)

Thus, the cubic itself has the equation

$$y = k(x) = -px.$$ (17)

This cubic divides the entire plane into two parts: the upper one, lying above it, and the lower one, lying under it. An arbitrary point $(x^*, y^*)$ of the plane lies above the straight line (17), if

$$y^* > -px^*.$$ (18)

Point $(x^*, y^*)$ lies under straight line (17), if

$$y^* < -px^*.$$ (19)

Let us find out which of the considered two half-spaces contains the point $(x, y)$ of the cubic parabola, that is, the point that satisfies equation (6). To clarify this issue, we must compare the values

$$x^3 - px$$ (20)

from the size

$$-px.$$ (21)

It is clear that at $x < 0$ value (20) is less than value (21), and at $x > 0$ value (20) is greater than value (21). For $x < 0$, the point $(x, y)$ of the cubic parabola satisfies condition (19), that is, it lies under the tangent, and for $x > 0$ this point satisfies condition (18), that is, it lies above the tangent. Therefore, at the origin, a cubic parabola goes from one side to its other side.

The phenomenon of a line transitioning from one side cubically to the other near the point of tangency is of general interest. Will deal with this phenomenon.
