From here we get:

Let us now find the field strength created by an infinite charged straight line with charge density $\sigma$. Let us select a small section of length $dx$ on the straight line. The charge of this section is $\sigma \cdot dx$.
The field strength created by this section at point $M$, located at a distance $r$ from the straight line, is equal to (Fig. 2)
$$\Delta E = |\vec{\Delta E}| = \frac{\sigma \cdot dx}{4\varepsilon_0(x^2 + r^2)}.$$
From the symmetry messages it is clear that the vector $\vec{E}(r)$ must be redefined by a straight line at any point. Consequently, the contribution to the field at point $M$ plot $dx$ is equal to
$$dE = \Delta E \cdot \cos \varphi = \frac{\varrho \pi r^2 \cdot dx}{4\varepsilon_0(x^2 + r^2)} \frac{r}{\sqrt{x^2 + r^2}} = \frac{\varrho r^3}{4\varepsilon_0(x^2 + r^2)^{3/2}} dx.$$
The total field strength at point $M$ is the sum of such contributions made by all sections of the straight line, that is
$$E(r) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} dE = 2 \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{\varrho r^3 dx}{4\varepsilon_0(x^2 + r^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\varrho r}{2\varepsilon_0}.$$
This is the field strength at a distance $r$ from the beam axis at those points that are sufficiently far from the ends of the beam. Near the ends the field is “distorted”. Taking these distortions (edge ​​effects) into account is quite complicated. We will simplify the problem: we will assume that points $A$ and $B$ lie inside an infinite long beam of ions at a distance $l$ (along the beam axis) from each other.
Let us consider the motion of an electron inside an infinitely long beam of ions. The force acting on the electron when it is at a distance $r$ from the beam axis is equal to
$$F = \frac{e\varrho}{2\varepsilon_0} r = -\frac{|e| \varrho}{2\varepsilon_0} r$$
and is directed over the beam axis. The acceleration of the electron in this direction is
$$a = \frac{F}{m}, \text{ или } r'' = -\frac{|e| \varrho}{2\varepsilon_0} r.$$
From this equation it follows that the motion of an electron in projection onto a plane overriding the beam axis is a harmonic vibration with a period
$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_0 m}{|e| \varrho}}.$$
In the direction of the beam axis, the electron speed remains constant and equal to the speed it had at point $A$.
figure 3 shows several possible trajectories of an electron leaving the beam at point $B$. It is obvious that the time of motion of the electron in the beam is determined by the conditions
$$t_1 = \frac{T}{4}, t_2 = \frac{T}{4} + \frac{T}{2} = \frac{3T}{4}, t_3 = \frac{3T}{4} + \frac{T}{2} = \frac{5T}{4} \text{ и т. д.},$$
or, in general terms,
$$t_n = (2n - 1) \frac{T}{4} \quad (n \in \mathbf{N}).$$
On the other hand, $t_n = l/v_n$. So the possibility of the value of the electron speed is determined by the conditions
$$v_n = \frac{l}{t_n} = \frac{4l}{(2n - 1)T} = \frac{4l \sqrt{|e| \varrho}}{(2n - 1)2\pi \sqrt{2\varepsilon_0 m}} =$$
$$= \frac{2l}{\pi(2n - 1)} \sqrt{\frac{|e| \varrho}{2\varepsilon_0 m}} \quad (n \in \mathbf{N}).$$
