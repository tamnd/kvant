The number of relict photons is counted using radio telecommunications. Technically, this is quite a difficult task, since there are many photons of local, terrestrial and solar origin on Earth. Having overcome all these difficulties, radio astronomers measured the total flux of photons in the cosmic microwave background radiation. It turned out to be equal to 1.3 · 10^12 (cm² · s · ster)⁻¹. From here the concentration of relict photons in the Universe indicated above was calculated.

There is reason to believe that in addition to photons, the Universe is also permeated by other particles, in particular, neutrinos and antineutrinos of different types. If the rest mass of neutrinos is zero, then their contribution to the total density is approximately equal to the contribution to the density from radiation. However, even a very small rest mass of one type of neutrino (25,000 times less than the rest mass of an electron) would radically change our understanding of the average density of matter in the Universe.

Modern physics is already quite willing to attribute a small rest mass to the neutrino, although it does not define the number. In turn, astronomers argue that the mass of galaxies and clusters of galaxies is greater than the mass of the total matter they contain. 4. The universe does not remain in the same state all the time - it evolves. This can be judged by measuring the speed of movement of galaxies of galaxies. Observations show that even galaxies are moving away from us with a speed $u$ proportional to the distance $r$ to them. Formula $\vec{u} = Hr$ expresses the significant Hubble law. The proportionality coefficient $H$ is called the Hubble constant and is approximately equal to 50 (km/s)/Mpc. Let us note here that the name “constant” in this case only indicates the independence of $H$ from the magnitude and direction of the vector $\vec{r}$. At the same time, $H$ depends on time.

In order to establish this law, it was necessary to learn how to determine the speed of movement of galaxies and the distances at which they are located.

The principle for determining speed is as follows. The emission of galaxies contains a spectrum

ral lines belonging to certain chemical elements (remember, for example, the “yellow line” of sodium that you see when table salt NaCl enters the flame of a gas burner). The laws of physics are the same. The wavelength of the determined line at the moment of emission is $\lambda_{изл}$ the same on Earth and in any of the most distant galaxies. If the source moves away, then according to the Doppler coefficient, the observed one at rest will receive radiation mixed towards the red end of the spectrum, in other words, the wavelength for the observed $\lambda_{набл}$ will increase. The relative magnitude of the displacement in cosmology is denoted by the letter $z$ and is given by the formula

$$z = \frac{\lambda_{набл} - \lambda_{изл}}{\lambda_{изл}}.$$

For the case when the speed $u$ is much less than the speed of light $c$, $z \approx u/c$. In the case when the speed $u$ approaches $c$, the formula connecting $u$ and $z$ becomes more complex, but we do not need it.

It is therefore understood that by changing the spectral lines in the spectra of celestial bodies and galaxies, it is possible to calculate their speed of removal (this is the name for the speed component directed along the “ray of sight”))⁺).

The Doppler effect makes it possible not only to change the speed of a galaxy moving away from us, but it also “works” on automatic roads. Traffic inspectors are armed with a special device—a “pistol”—that measures the speed of approaching or receding vehicles. Unlike space objects, they do not automatically emit, but only reflect the electromagnetic pulse emitted by the “gun”. Think about how this affects the Doppler coefficient values?
