“Having achieved satisfactory constancy of sensitivity when using a tube with platinum leaves and iron powder *), I set myself another task: to achieve such a combination that the connection between the sawdust, caused by electric vibration, is immediately destroyed automatically” [emphasis added by me - S.R.], - Popov wrote in his article “A device for detecting electric vibrations” in the January issue of the “Journal of the Russian Physico-Chemical Society” in 1896 And he coped with this task quickly. We can say that in his receiver Popov electromechanically implemented not only amplification, but also what we now call feedback.

Popov called his receiver a “lightning detector,” but in the experiments described in the article, the receiver recorded not only lightning strikes, but also discharges of an electrophore machine, and discharges in a large Hertzian vibrator, and even the rapid movements of a charged ebonite stick. Diagram and description of the device

(from the article by A. S. Popov “Device for detecting electrical vibrations”)

“The attached diagram shows the location of the parts of the device. The tube with sawdust is suspended horizontally between clamps M and N on a lightweight clock spring, which is bent in a zigzag on the side of one clamp for greater elasticity. A bell is located above the tube, so that during its operation it can give light blows with a hammer on the middle of the tube, protected from breaking by a rubber flask. It is most convenient to mount the handset and bell on a common vertical plate. The relay can be placed as desired.

The device operates as follows. The battery current of 4-5 volts constantly circulates from terminal P to the platinum plate A, then through the powder contained in the tube to another plate B and through the winding of the electromagnetary back to the battery. The strength of this current is not sufficient to power the armature, but if the tube AB is subjected to electrical oscillation, the resistance will instantly decrease and the current will increase so much that the armature will close. At this moment, the target, searching from the battery to the bell, is transferred to point C. It closes, and the bell begins to operate, but immediately the cooperation of the handset again reduces its conductivity and the relay opens the target of the bell.
