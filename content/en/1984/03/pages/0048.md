F850. figure shows the experimentally obtained dependence of the force of the spring on its length. Find the period of small oscillations of a load of mass $m = 60$ g, suspended vertically on this spring in a field of gravity.

![Graph of gravitational force vs time](image)

$$F_{ynp} H$$

When mixing from the equilibrium position at $\Delta x$ (see figure), a restoring force acts on the load

$$F(\Delta x) = F_{ynp}(x_0 + \Delta x) - mg = F_{ynp}(x_0 + \Delta x) - F_{ynp}(x_0).$$

For small $\Delta x$ with good accuracy

$$F_{ynp}(x_0 + \Delta x) - F_{ynp}(x_0) = F'_{ynp}(x_0) \cdot \Delta x = k(x_0) \cdot \Delta x,$$

where $k(x_0)$ is the spring stiffness when its length is $x_0$. Geometrically, this approximation corresponds to the replacement of a portion of the curve $F_{ynp}(x)$ by a straight line, that is, a tangent drawn at point $F_{ynp}(x_0)$. The tangent of the angle of inclination of this tangent to the $x$ axis is $k(x_0)$. Therefore, small vibrations of the load are equal to

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{k(x_0)}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{m}{tg a}}.$$

From the graph we find $tg a$:

$$tg a \approx \frac{0,2}{0,01} = 20.$$

Thus,

$$T = 2 \cdot 3,4 \sqrt{\frac{60 \cdot 10^{-3}}{20}} \approx 3,4 c.$$

F851. Find the force of interaction between two non-conducting halves of a ball of radius $R$, each of which is uniformly charged throughout the volume with the corresponding density $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$. The dielectric permeability of the ball material is considered equal to unity.

First, we prove the fact that the force of interaction between the charged halves of the ball is proportional to the product of the charge densities, that is, $F \sim \rho_1 \rho_2$.

Let us mentally fix the charge of the first half, and change the charge of the second, for example by 2 times. The second half is affected by the field of the first, as well as the own field of the second half, but the total effect of this second field is zero. Since $\rho_2$ has changed twice, then the fusion acting on the second half from the field of the first has changed twice - after all, at each point $f = \rho E \cdot dv$ ($E$ is the field voltage of the first half at a given point, $\rho \cdot dv$ is the charge of the elementary volume near the point), that is, the force is proportional to $\rho_1$.
