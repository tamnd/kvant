⟦folio 33⟧

At the beginning of the geometry elective, Pyotr Ivanovich drew three circles (Fig. 1) on the board and proposed solving the following problem:

Three circles of the same radius R intersect at a common point S and at points M, N, P. Prove that points M, N, P lie on a circle of the same radius R.

We thought a little. Nothing works: you can’t see how to be present to the task. Then Pyotr Ivanovich put a small matryoshka doll on the table and wrote on the board:

Problem 1. Inside triangle ABC there is a point H such that HAC=HBC, HBA=HCA, HCB=HAB. Then H is the orthometer of triangle ABC, that is, the point of intersection of its heights.

We proved this almost immediately. In the notation that appeared in the drawing on the board (Fig. 2), very indo, 2(α+β+γ)=180°, whence α+β+γ=90°. But then the external angle AA1B of triangle ACA1 is equal to (α+β)+γ=90°, which means AA1 is the height. Similar to BB1 and CC1, and therefore H is an orthometer.

“That’s right,” said Pyotr Ivanovich, placed a second, slightly larger nesting doll next to the first one, and wrote on the board:

Problem 2. A point symmetrical to the orthometer relative to the side of the triangle lies on its circumcircle.

From the drawing drawn on the board (Fig. 3), in which D denotes the point of intersection of the height AA1 with the circumscribed circle, we quickly established the equality HBC=CAD (since each of these angles is 90° together with the angle B1CB) and CAD=CBD (the angles are based on a common arc CD). Therefore HBC=DBC and point D is symmetrine H relative to CB.

Then a third nesting doll appeared on Petra Ivanovich’s table, and on the board there was this entry:

Problem 3. The circumradius of a triangle is equal to the radius of the circle passing through two vertices and the orthometer.

In the previous drawing (see Fig. 3) Petr Ivanovich drew a circle passing through points C, H, B and segments CD and DB. From the spot he began to be told that the triangles CHB and CDB are constructible according to the previous problem, therefore their circumscribed circles have the same radius. Pyotr Ivanovich hid the second nesting doll in the third.

—Are there still nesting dolls? - we asked.

“Yes,” answered Pyotr Ivanovich, taking out the smallest nesting doll, “but the task will be oral,” and he wrote:

Problem 4. Other circles of equal radii connecting their two points of intersection are constructive.

“The others, of course, are constructive, because they are symmetrical with respect to the straight line passing through the points of intersection,” we answered (Fig. 4).

“Well, now any of you must first solve the problem about three circles,” said Pyotr Ivanovich and... called me to the board.

I started to say something incomprehensible, but then there was a random thank you call. Then Pyotr Ivanovich took out the fifth, largest nesting doll, meaningfully placed the dolls into each other and said:

- So that by next time everyone will solve this problem. At home I solved it pretty quickly.*) What about you?
