The polynomials $g(x)$ and $f(\xi)$ are related by the relations
$$f(\xi) = g(x) - \beta.$$
Thus, the number of roots of the polynomial $g(x)$ is equal to the number of roots of the polynomial $f(\xi) + \beta$ or, which is the same, the number of roots of the equation $f(\xi) = -\beta$. But we already know this number (see (14)).
Consequently, for $p < 0$ (see (38)) the polynomial $g(x)$ has one real root, for $p = 0$ the polynomial $g(x)$ also has one real root, except for the case when the value $\beta$ (see (37)) is equal to zero.
Consider the case $p > 0$. If the quantity $\beta$ (see (37)) satisfies the condition
$$|\beta| \leq \frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}} p^{3/2} \tag{40}$$
(see (31)), then the polynomial $g(x)$ has three real roots, and at the ends of the segment (40) two roots merge into one. Substituting into (40) the values ​​$\beta$ and $p$ from correspondences (37) and (39) and squaring the resulting correspondence, we obtain the conditions
$$\left( \frac{2a_1^3}{27} - \frac{a_1a_2}{3} + a_3 \right)^2 \leq \frac{4}{27} \left( \frac{a_1^2}{3} - a_2 \right)^3. \tag{41}$$

The resulting condition is a complete criterion starting from three real roots of the polynomial $g(x)$, since when it is satisfied the case $p < 0$ is excluded. After all, the first inequality (41) for $p < 0$ is negative, while the left side, being a square, cannot be negative.

Let us now indicate the way to study the graph of a fourth-degree polynomial
$$y = h(x) = x^4 + b_1x^3 + b_2x^2 + b_3x + b_4.$$
To clarify its behavior, you should study the behavior of the polynomial
$$h'(x) = 4x^3 + 3b_1x^2 + 2b_2x + b_3.$$
This polynomial already has degree 3 and the question of how many real roots it has has already been resolved.
Since the polynomial $h(x)$ increases without limit, remaining positive when $x \rightarrow +\infty$ and $x \rightarrow -\infty^*$), then if the polynomial $h'(x)$ has only one real root, the polynomial $h(x)$ has only one minimum. If the polynomial $h'(x)$ has three different real roots, then the polynomial $h(x)$ has two minimums and one maximum. In conclusion, I will give the Cardan formula (1501-1576) for solving the cubic equation (14), that is, the equation
$$x^3 - px = c. \tag{42}$$

Once the solution is obtained, we can replace $p$ and $c$ according to formulas (37) and (39). Thus, we can obtain a formula for finding all the roots of the polynomial (5).

To solve equation (42), arbitrarily replace the variable with the sum of two new unknowns $u$ and $v$, that is
$$x = u + v. \tag{43}$$

We will connect the new unknowns $u$ and $v$ with some additional correspondence. Substituting $x = u + v$ into equation (42), we obtain
$$u^3 + v^3 + (3uv - p)(u + v) = c. \tag{44}$$
