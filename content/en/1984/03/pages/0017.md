From the prehistory of radio
(to the 125th anniversary of the birth of A. S. Popov)

Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences
S. M. RYTOV

How the invention arose, why it arose precisely at the end of the century, how the structure built by the inventors grew into a columnar and magnificent building - all this becomes truly understandable only if we consider that the radio pioneers could build on a wide and solid foundation, the construction of which was completed several years before the invention itself.

Academician L. I. Mandelstam

Radio is one of the main elements of modern civilization, determining its appearance. Not only within the Earth, but also on an international scale, in cosmic and even cosmological problems, radio waves are now both a means of promise and a means of knowledge.

If we compare the modern significance of radio with its humble beginnings, it may seem that the entire path of development of radio - both ideological and practical - ends with our century. But this is a misconception. As the outstanding Soviet radiophysicist and radio engineer Academician L.I. Mandelstam wrote, “the invention of radio, as well as its development, became possible only thanks to the revolution in ideas about electronic phenomena that is associated with the names of Faraday, Maxwell and Hertz. The history of the development of these ideas is, in essence, the prehistory of radio.”

In 1831, Faraday discovered the phenomenon of electromagnetic induction - the appearance of an electric field with any change in the magnetic field. In 1833 he discovered the laws of electrolysis; in 1837 - detects the influence of the environment on the capacitance of the capacitor; Since 1845, a number of further brilliant discoveries have followed, including the discovery of rotation of the plane of polarization of light in a magnetic field, the discovery of diamagnetism and parametgnetism. In all his titanic experimental work, Faraday relied on the idea of ​​strong lines, on the idea of ​​the role of the medium in electromagnetic phenomena, on the concept of short-range action from point to point, as opposed to Newtonian ideas of instantaneous long-range action. Despite the vagueness of his ideas about the nature of electromagnetism, despite the lack of understanding and description of its concepts, Faraday followed his own path, and this path led him from one discovery to another. But among the natural data side were such brilliant physicists as Ampère, Weber, Kirchhoff, and W. Thomson.
