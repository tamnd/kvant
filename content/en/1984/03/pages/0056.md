$\Delta F_m = \frac{1}{2} B I \Delta l$.

These forces, directed along the radii of the coil, will cause stretching of the wiring of the coil. To determine the forces generated in response to tension, it is convenient to consider the equilibrium of any half of the peg.

At figure 4, 6 the forces acting on the polyturn are shown. These are magnetic forces and forces $\vec{F}_\pi$ that arise during deformation. At equilibrium, the sum of the projections of all forces on the vertical axis must be equal to zero. Let's find the projection of the total magnetic force. The non-technical ones among you have probably encountered similar problems and know that this projection is equal to the force acting on a unit turn multiplied by the magnitude of the projection of the semicircle, that is, by the diameter of the turn. Let's show this using strict calculations.

Let us choose as an infinite small element of the ring length the section $dl$, enclosed between two radii, wired at angles $\varphi$ and $\varphi + d\varphi$ to the axis $X$ (see Fig. 4, 6). Here $d\varphi$ is the infinite small increment of the angle $\varphi$. The length of this element will be equal to $dl = Rd\varphi$, the force acting on it is equal to

$$dF_m = \frac{1}{2} B I R d\varphi,$$

and the projection of this force onto the $Y$ axis is

$$dF_{my} = \frac{1}{2} B I R \sin \varphi \cdot d\varphi.$$

Then the projection of the total magnetic force acting on the entire polyturn will be equal to

$$F_{my} = \int_0^\pi \frac{1}{2} B I R \sin \varphi \cdot d\varphi = -\frac{1}{2} B I R (\cos \pi - \cos 0) = B I R.$$

A rupture of the solenoid winding, very rarely, will occur provided that the projection of the total magnetic force is greater than or equal to twice the maximum tensile load on the wiring:

$$B I R \geq 2 F_{np},$$

or $\frac{\mu_0 I^2 R N}{L} \geq 2 F_{np}$.

From here we find the desired value of point $I$:

$$I \geq \left( \frac{2 F_{np} L}{\mu_0 R N} \right)^{1/2}.$$

Exercise

1. A metal rod of length $l$ and mass $m$ is suspended on two identical springs (stiffness of each spring $k$) in a uniform magnetic field with induction $\vec{B}$ (Fig. 5). Vector $\vec{B}$ is perepidicular to the drawing plane. By what maximum amount $\Delta x_{\max}$ will the bar move from the equilibrium position if a short impulse of point $I$ is passed through it during a period of time $t$? Run by mixing the bar in time $t$.

2. Having passed the accelerating potential difference $U = 3,52 \cdot 10^3$ V, the electron in a uniform magnetic field with induction $B = 0,01$ T is peredicular to the induction line and moves along a circle of radius $R = 0,02$ m. Calculate the ratio $e/m$ of the electron charge to its mass.

3. A long and thin uncharged plate of non-magnetic metal moves at a constant speed $v = 11,3$ m/s in a uniform magnetic field with induction $B = 1$ T. Vectors $\vec{B}$ and $\vec{v}$ are mutually perepidicular and parallel to the plane of the plate (Fig. 6). Determine the surface density σ of electric charges generated on the lateral planes of the plate due to its movement.
