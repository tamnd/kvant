F848. A ball rolls along a main horizontal circular iron of length l. The speed of the ball is known with an accuracy of $\varepsilon = \pm 5\%$. For what values ​​of v can we say with certainty that after time t after the ball is at point A (see figure), it is not at point B?

Since the speed of the ball is known with an accuracy of $\varepsilon$, then for any moment of time it is possible to indicate not a point, but a certain area on the circle within which the ball is located. The front boundary of this area moves at a speed of $v + \varepsilon v = v(1 + \varepsilon)$, and the rear boundary moves at a speed of $v(1 - \varepsilon)$. After time t after the ball was at point A, the length of the segment will be 2vet. The fact that the ball is not currently at point B means that point B does not fall inside this region, that is (see figure).

$$2v\varepsilon - \frac{3l}{4} < v(1 + \varepsilon)\tau - nl < \frac{l}{4},$$

where $n$ is the number of complete revolutions of the ball in a circle during time t. From here we find $l$. From here there is a ball, “spreading” at a speed of 2ev. Therefore, after time $t = l/2ev$ (when the area will always be a circle), the position of the ball cannot be determined. During this time, the ball will make $n = vt/l = 1/2\varepsilon$ full revolutions. So the problem makes sense for $n < \frac{1}{2\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{2(1/20)} = 10$.

Answer:

$$\frac{l\left(n - \frac{3}{4}\right)}{(1 - \varepsilon)\tau} < v < \frac{l\left(n + \frac{1}{4}\right)}{(1 + \varepsilon)\tau},$$ where $n = 1, 2, \dots, 9$.

Let's denote the partial vapor pressure in the room $p_n$; then we exclude relative humidity -

$$\varphi = \frac{p_n}{p_1} 100\%.$$

To find $p_n$ we use the law of conservation of energy. Let us mentally select a volume $V_1$ in the room, containing a given mass of dry air $M$ and a certain amount of steam $m_1$, and move it to the wet thermometer. There, the volume containing the same amount of dry air is denoted by $V_2$, and the amount of steam in this volume is denoted by $m_2$. The air density in the room is practically the same, so such movement does not require work to be done by any forces other than air pressure forces, and we take their work into account.

Work of pressure forces -

$$A = p(V_1 - V_2).$$

The air is cooled from temperature $T_1$ to temperature $T_2$, and its internal energy decreases by

$$\Delta U = cv \cdot \Delta T = c \frac{M}{\mu_B} \cdot \Delta T.$$

The decrease in the internal energy of steam during cooling can clearly be compared to this value, since at this temperature the steam in the air is small ($p_n \ll p$).

When “insufficient” water evaporates, heat is absorbed

$$\Delta Q = L \frac{m_2 - m_1}{\mu_n}.$$

$$p(V_1 - V_2) + c \frac{M}{\mu_B} \cdot \Delta T = L \frac{m_2 - m_1}{\mu_n}.$$ (*)
