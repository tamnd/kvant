M838. All points lying on the sides of a regular triangle ABC are divided into two sets E1 and E2. Is it true that for any such partition in one of the sets E1 and E2 there is a trio of vertices of a right triangle?

ANSWER: true. We will carry out the proof by contradiction. Let the points of the set E1 be surrounded with this color, and the points of the set E2 - with red. Assume that there is no right triangle with single-pointed vertices, and consider a regular hexagon inscribed in triangle ABC (see figure). Each two of its opposite vertices should be colored differently - if, for example, the opposite vertices P and Q are blue, then any of the other four vertices should be red, since together with P and Q they form a right triangle; but then any three of these red points form a forbidden one-part right triangle.

It is clear that in this case it will find two cose vertices of different colors of the hexagon. Either these two vertices, or those opposite them (also of different colors!) lie on one of the sides of the triangle. For the sake of definition, let the merging vertex K and the red L lie on the side AB, then the vertices K1 and L1 opposite them will be red and blue (see figure). But then, no matter what color vertex A is painted, one of the opposite triangles AKL1 and ALK1 will be single-weighted. Contradiction.

This reasoning shows that even a set of eight points—the vertices of a hexagon and any two vertices of a triangle—cannot be subsets without right triangles.
