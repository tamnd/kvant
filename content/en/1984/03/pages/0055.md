Problem 4*). When an electric current $I$ passes through a solenoid with a length $L$, a radius $R$ and a large number of turns $N$, a uniform magnetic field with induction $B = \mu_0 NI/L$ ($\mu_0$ is the magnetic constant) is created inside the solenode. The lineius of magnetic induction is parallel to the axis of the solenode. Determine the current value at which the solenoid winding will break if the maximum design load on the wire is $F_{np}$.

First of all, we note that it is no coincidence that the task conditions speak of a large number of turns of the solenoid, that is, it is implied that the winding length is much larger than the cross-sectional diameter of the solenode. Only in this case can we assume that the magnetic field inside the solenoid is uniform, and induction

The fields of the entire solenoid can be neglected. This means that inside the solenode near the surface of the ring coil with current, the magnetic induction vector is perpendicular to the plane of the coil and is equal to $\mu_0 NI/L$, and on the outside the induction is zero.

Now let's turn directly to the problem. Obviously, a rupture of the solenoid winding can occur as a result of the action of a magnetic field. The question arises: what value of magnetic induction should be taken in order to correctly calculate the forces acting on the elements of the current-carrying coil?

Most of the applicants who solved this problem mistakenly took the entire amount of induction. In fact, the field both on the inside and on the outside of the solenoid coil is a superposition of the fields created by the coil itself and all other coils. Let the induction model created by the turn be equal to $B_1$, and for all other turns equal to $B_{N-1}$. Using the superposition principle, we can write:

$$B_1 - B_{N-1} = 0 \text{ — снаружи стороны},$$

$$B_1 + B_{N-1} = \mu_0 NI/L \text{ — внутри соленода.}$$

From these equalities we obtain that $B_{N-1} = \mu_0 NI/(2L)$. Consequently, the field induction in which each turn is located is equal to half the field induction in the solenode. It is this value that we must have to calculate the forces acting on the elements of the coil.
