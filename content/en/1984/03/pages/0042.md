**M837***. Let $a, b, c$ be positive integers, each two of which are coprime. Prove that the largest integer that cannot be represented as

$$xbc + yca + zab$$

(food $x, y, z$ are non-negative integers), equals

$$2abc - ab - bc - ca.$$

It is convenient to consider a more general problem at once:

let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be natural polar coprime numbers. Let us call the natural number $u$ “red” if it can be represented in the form

$$u = a_1a_2\dots a_n \left( \frac{x_1}{a_1} + \dots + \frac{x_n}{a_n} \right),$$

(1)

food $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ — non-negative integers, $u$ “blue” — otherwise. What is the largest number?

(In our problem $n = 3$.)

It is one of the shortest solutions to this problem based on the following two statements:

1) any integer $u$ admits a unique representation (1), where $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ are integers, and $0 \leq x_2 \leq a_2 - 1$, ..., $0 \leq x_n \leq a_n - 1$, $x_1$ can be any (we will call such a representation standard);

2) the integer $u$ is red if and only if its standard representation is $x_1 \geq 0$.

$$m = a_1a_2\dots a_n \left( n - 1 - \frac{1}{a_1} - \dots - \frac{1}{a_n} \right).$$
