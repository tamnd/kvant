Ambient light colors

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. A. BOROVOY

The blue of the sky, the white clouds that constantly change their shape, the colors of sunrise and sunset have always evoked a feeling of admiration in people. They fed the imagination of poses and artists, and naturally attracted the attention of scientists.

“Aerial blue is born from the solid density of the illuminated air, located between the upper surface and the earth,” Leonardo da Vinci wrote. About a hundred years ago, in 1869, the famous English physicist and popularizer of science J. Tyndall first conducted experiments in which he observed the blue tint of scattered light. However, it took a little more effort and work of scientists such as Rayleigh, Smoluchovsky, Mandelstam, Einstein to finally explain the colors of the daytime and evening sky with diffused light.

We will begin our acquaintance with this phenomenon with simple model experiments, then we will try to qualitatively explain the results obtained and from them “build a bridge” to the color of the sky.

I. For experiments, you need a straight-walled vessel with strong walls, for example an aquarium. Its length should be at least 30 cm. In addition to the vessel, you will need a light source that produces a weakly diverging beam of rays. You can use a slide projector, placing in place of the slide a piece of black paper with a round deviation of 2-3 mm in diameter in the center, or you can use a fairly strong electric flashlight for this purpose.

The rituals must be carried out in a darkened room.
