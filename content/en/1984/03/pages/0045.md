F847. An electron flies into a volumetric beam of positive electrons, which has a circular cross-section, at point A, the speed of which is parallel to the axis of symmetry of the beam (Fig. 1). The volume density of charges in the beam is equal to q, the length of the beam is l (the length of the beam is much greater than its radius). At what speed will the electron fly out of the beam at point B? The collision between the electron and the electrons is not taken into account.

Fig. 1.

An electron flying in a beam is affected by the electric field created by the naim. Let us find how the voltage of this field depends on the distance to the beam axis.

The voltage $\vec{E}(r)$ at points located inside the beam sufficiently far from its ends at a distance $r$ from the OB axis can be represented as the sum

$$\vec{E}(r) = \vec{E}_1(r) + \vec{E}_2(r),$$

where $\vec{E}_1(r)$ is the field voltage created by those charges that are inside the cylinder of radius $r$, and $\vec{E}_2(r)$ is the field voltage created by those located on the lying charges, and ending with negative ones, we come to the conclusion that $\vec{E}_2(r) = 0$. Moreover, charges located in a cylinder of radius $r$ do not create a field at all points of the region inside the cylinder.

Let us now determine the voltage $\vec{E}(r) = \vec{E}_1(r)$. It is known that from the point of view of the picture of word lines, the field voltage is defined as the number of word lines, an arbitrary area of ​​unit area, redistributing the lines. Consequently, if we align the charge of a cylinder of radius $r$ uniformly to the axis of the cylinder, then the fields created by the previous distribution of charges and the new “compressed” distribution in the cylinder will coincide. Therefore, we define the quantities $\vec{E}_1(r)$ as the field voltage of an infinite charged straight line at a distance $r$ from it.
