Physics

1. Let's consider two limiting cases: 1) when the load no longer slides down the prism; 2) when the load is not yet moving upward relative to the prism. The forces acting on the load in both cases are shown in figure 7. a and b. These are the gravity force $m\vec{g}$, the prism reaction force $\vec{N}$ and the friction force $\vec{F}_{\text{тр}}$ ($F_{\text{тр}} = \mu N$). Let us denote the acceleration of the prism, and therefore the load, in the first and second cases by $\vec{a}_1$ and $\vec{a}_2$, respectively. Let's project the forces and accelerations on the horizontal and vertical coordinate axes and write the corresponding equation of motion for the load. For case 1) we get:
$$N \sin \alpha - \mu N \cos \alpha = ma_1,$$
$$N \cos \alpha + \mu N \sin \alpha - mg = 0,$$
where
$$a_1 = g \frac{\text{tg} \alpha - \mu}{1 + \mu \text{tg} \alpha} = 4 \text{ m/c}^2.$$
Similarly for case 2)
$$N \sin \alpha + \mu N \cos \alpha = ma_2,$$
$$N \cos \alpha - \mu N \sin \alpha - mg = 0,$$
And
$$a_2 = g \frac{\text{tg} \alpha + \mu}{1 - \mu \text{tg} \alpha} = 11.5 \text{ m/c}^2.$$
Thus, the prism must be moved with acceleration $\vec{a}$ such that
$$4 \text{ m/c}^2 = a_1 \leqslant a \leqslant a_2 = 11.5 \text{ m/c}^2.$$
2. From the law of conservation of energy
$$\frac{m\omega^2(l_1^2 + l_2^2)}{2} = mg(l_1 - l_2)$$
we find the angular velocity $\omega$ of the system:
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2g(l_1 - l_2)}{l_1^2 + l_2^2}} \approx 2.8 \text{ c}^{-1}.$$
3. The speed of the ball $v$ at the moment of impact with the wall can be found from kinematic correspondences
$$v^2 = (v_0 \cos \alpha)^2 + (v_0 \sin \alpha - gt)^2,$$
$$l = v_0 \cos \alpha \cdot t.$$
Then from the law of conservation of energy
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = cm\Delta T$$
let's find the increment $\Delta T$ of the ball temperature:
$$\Delta T = \frac{(v_0 \cos \alpha)^2 + (v_0 \sin \alpha - gt)/(v_0 \cos \alpha))^2}{2c} \approx 8 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ K}.$$
4. Let us write down Newton’s second law for the mercury compound:
$$m\omega^2 = p_0 S - p S.$$
Here $m = qlS$ is the mass of mercury, $\omega$ is the angular velocity of rotation of the tube, $b$ is the radius of the circle along which a drop of mercury moves ($l$ can be neglected in comparison with $b$), $S$ is the cross-sectional area of the tube and $p$ is the air pressure in the tube, which we find from Boyle’s law - Mariotta $p_0 dS = pbS$. From here we get
$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{p_0}{qlb} \left(1 - \frac{d}{b}\right)} \approx 25 \text{ c}^{-1}.$$

6. The efficiency of an electric motor is equal to the ratio of its mechanical power to the power consumed by the motor from the generator. According to the law of conservation of energy, the total power $\mathcal{E}I$ consumed by the generator is the sum of the mechanical power $P_{\text{м}}$ of the electric motor and the thermal power $I^2(R + r)$ released in the motor and generator:
$$\mathcal{E}I = P_{\text{м}} + I^2(R + r).$$
From here
$$P_{\text{м}} = \mathcal{E}I - I^2(R + r).$$
The power consumed by the electric motor is equal to
$$P_{\text{п}} = UI = (\mathcal{E}I - Ir) I.$$
Positional motor efficiency
$$\eta = \frac{P_{\text{м}}}{P_{\text{п}}} = 1 - \frac{IR}{\mathcal{E}I - Ir} = 0.9 = 90\%.$$
7. In an electric field, an electron acquires kinetic energy
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = eU.$$
In a magnetic field, the Lorentz force imparts acceleration to the electron
$$a = \frac{eBv \sin \alpha}{m} = B \sin \alpha \cdot \sqrt{2U(e/m)^3} \approx 1.5 \cdot 10^{18} \text{ m/c}^2.$$
8. Induction EMF arising in the circuit
$$\mathcal{E}_i = \left| \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \right| = \frac{Br^2\omega}{2}$$
causes a current in the circuit
$$I = \frac{\mathcal{E}_i}{R} = \frac{Br^2\omega}{2R}.$$
For one revolution of the circuit, an amount of heat is released in it
$$Q = I^2RT = I^2R \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{\pi B^2 r^4 \omega}{2R} \approx 5 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ Дж}.$$

$$f = \frac{dF}{d-F}.$$
