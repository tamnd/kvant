This equation has three roots

The last two roots at $p < 0$ are minima and therefore have no general geometric meaning. For $p > 0$, all three roots of (9) are different, and, therefore, there are three points of intersection of the line $L$ with the x-axis. At $p = 0$, three roots merge into one triple root $x = 0$.
The derivative function (6) is given by the formula
$$f'(x) = 3x^2 - p.$$ (10)
By studying the sign of this function for different values ​​of $x$, we can divide the line $L$ into sections of increasing and decreasing function $f(x)$ and find the maximum and minimum points. For both integers we should find the roots of the equation
$$f'(x) = 3x^2 - p = 0.$$ (11)
For negative $p$, the function $f'(x)$ (see (10)) is positive for any value of $x$ and, therefore, the function $f(x)$ increases throughout the change $x$: $-\infty < x < \infty$.
For $p = 0$, the function $f'(x)$ (see (10)) is positive for all values ​​of $x \neq 0$. Thus, it increases at $-\infty < x < 0, 0 < x < \infty$. And since in the first of these sections the function $f(x)$ is negative, in the second it is positive, then it will increase throughout the change from $-\infty$ to $\infty$.
At the point $x = 0$, where $f'(x) = 0$, the function $f(x)$ also increases. Thus, for $x = 0$, where $f'(x) = 0$, the function $x^3$ has neither a maximum nor a minimum.
In the case of positive $p$, equation (11) has two roots
$$x_1 = -\sqrt{p/3}, x_2 = \sqrt{p/3}.$$ (12)
You should check whether the function $f(x)$ at points $x_1$ and $x_2$ has maxima or minima. The same points $x_1$ and $x_2$ divide the entire extent of change $x$ into sections:

**Point of inflection.** Let $L$ be the graph of some two half-spaces and lie at the point $(x, y)$ of a cubic parabola, that is, a point satisfying equation (6). To clarify this issue, we must compare the values

$$x^3 - px$$ (20)

It is clear that at $x < 0$ value (20) is less than value (21), and at $x > 0$ value (20) is greater than value (21). For $x < 0$, the point $(x, y)$ of the cubic parabola satisfies condition (19), that is, it lies under the tangent, and for $x > 0$ this point satisfies condition (18), that is, it lies above the tangent. Consequently, at the origin, the cubic parabola passes from one side to the other near the point of tangency. It turns out that the condition for the inflection point is satisfied (see (3))

$$f''(x_0) = 0.$$ (22)

Let's prove this statement. The equation cubatively for another near the point of tangency can be written in the form

$$y = K(x) = f'(x_0) \cdot x + y_0.$$ (23)

Here $f'(x_0)$ is the tangent of the angle of inclination of the cubic absissa, and $y_0$ is some constant, which is determined by the equation

$$f(x_0) - K(x_0) = 0.$$ (24)

This condition expresses the fact that cubatively at the point $a$ to the line $L$ passes through the point $a$. Let's consider the function

$$h(x) = f(x) - K(x).$$ (25)

This function satisfies the following two conditions

$$h(x_0) = 0, h'(x_0) = 0.$$ (26)

Thus, the graph of the function

$$y = h(x)$$ (27)

touches the x-axis at point $x = x_0$.

If $a$ is an inflection point, then the graph of the function $h(x)$ at point $x_0$ passes from one side of the absis axis to the other. First of all, let us prove that $h''(x_0) = 0$. Since $h'(x_0) = 0$, the function $h(x)$ has a minimum at the point $x_0$ and therefore for all $x$ sufficiently close to $x_0$, we have

$$h(x) \geq h(x_0) = 0.$$

Posibly, the graph of the function $h(x)$ does not go at point $x_0$ from one side of the absis axis to the other. Exactly the same

Let us now deal with the general cubic parabola (5). Let us first find out how many real roots the polynomial (5) has.

Consider equation (14)
$$x^3 - px = c.$$

As already mentioned, for $p < 0$ this equation has only one real root. For $p = 0$ this equation also has only one real root, with the exception of the case $c = 0$, when it has a triple root $x = 0$. If $c = 0$, equation (14) has three roots at
$$f(x_2) \leq c \leq f(x_1)$$
(see (15)), where
$$f(x_1) = \frac{2}{3}p\sqrt{\frac{p}{3}}, \quad f(x_2) = -\frac{2}{3}p\sqrt{\frac{p}{3}},$$
(see (12)), so inequality (15) can be written in the form
$$|c| \leq \frac{2}{3}p\sqrt{\frac{p}{3}},$$
(31)

and for extreme values ​​$c$ from the interval (31) has one simple and one double root. Outside the segment (31), equation (14) has only one real root.

Polynomial (5) can be reduced to the form
$$\eta = f(\xi) = \xi^3 - p\xi$$
(32)

substitution of variables
$$x = \xi + \alpha, \quad y = \eta + \beta.$$ (33)

Such a replacement means a parallel shift of the coordinate system.

Making substitution (33), we get
$$\eta = \xi^3 + (3\alpha + a_1)\xi^2 + (3\alpha^2 + 2a_1\alpha + a_2)\xi + (\alpha^3 + a_1\alpha^2 + a_2\alpha + a_3) - \beta.$$ (34)

In order for the polynomial (5) to be reduced to the form (32), it is sufficient that the conditions be met
$$3\alpha + a_1 = 0, \quad \alpha^3 + a_1\alpha^2 + a_2\alpha + a_3 - \beta = 0.$$ (35)

From the first equation of system (35) we have
$$\alpha = -\frac{a_1}{3}.$$ (36)

Substituting this equation for $a$ into the second equation of system (35), we obtain
$$\beta = \frac{2a_1^3}{27} - \frac{a_1a_2}{3} + a_3.$$ (37)

Assuming that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are given by formulas (36) and (37), we can rewrite (34) as
$$\eta = f(\xi) = \xi^3 - p\xi,$$
(38)

Where
$$p = \frac{a_1^2}{3} - a_2.$$ (39)

The geometric shape of the cubic parabola (5) coincides with the geometric formula of the parabola (38). The latter depends on the sign of the quantity $p$ (39).

Let us now clarify the question of how many roots the polynomial $g(x)$ has (see (5)).

The polynomials $g(x)$ and $f(\xi)$ are related by the relations
$$f(\xi) = g(x) - \beta.$$
Thus, the number of roots of the polynomial $g(x)$ is equal to the number of roots of the polynomial $f(\xi) + \beta$ or, which is the same, the number of roots of the equation $f(\xi) = -\beta$. But we already know this number (see (14)).
Consequently, for $p < 0$ (see (38)) the polynomial $g(x)$ has one real root, for $p = 0$ the polynomial $g(x)$ also has one real root, except for the case when the value $\beta$ (see (37)) is equal to zero.
Consider the case $p > 0$. If the quantity $\beta$ (see (37)) satisfies the condition
$$|\beta| \leq \frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}} p^{3/2} \tag{40}$$
(see (31)), then the polynomial $g(x)$ has three real roots, and at the ends of the segment (40) two roots merge into one. Substituting into (40) the values ​​$\beta$ and $p$ from correspondences (37) and (39) and squaring the resulting correspondence, we obtain the conditions
$$\left( \frac{2a_1^3}{27} - \frac{a_1a_2}{3} + a_3 \right)^2 \leq \frac{4}{27} \left( \frac{a_1^2}{3} - a_2 \right)^3. \tag{41}$$

The resulting condition is a complete criterion starting from three real roots of the polynomial $g(x)$, since when it is satisfied the case $p < 0$ is excluded. After all, the first inequality (41) for $p < 0$ is negative, while the left side, being a square, cannot be negative.

In conclusion, I will give the Cardan formula (1501-1576) for solving the cubic equation (14), that is, the equation
$$x^3 - px = c. \tag{42}$$

Once the solution is obtained, we can replace $p$ and $c$ according to formulas (37) and (39). Thus, we can obtain a formula for finding all the roots of the polynomial (5).

To solve equation (42), arbitrarily replace the variable with the sum of two new unknowns $u$ and $v$, that is
$$x = u + v. \tag{43}$$

We will connect the new unknowns $u$ and $v$ with some additional correspondence. Substituting $x = u + v$ into equation (42), we obtain
$$u^3 + v^3 + (3uv - p)(u + v) = c. \tag{44}$$
