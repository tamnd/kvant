Work, energy and Archimedean force

The work of force with which one body acts on another is equal, as is known, to the change in the potential energy of interaction of these bodies, taken with the opposite sign (“Physics 8”, §57):

$$A = -\Delta E_p.$$

If, for example, a body of mass $M$ falls in a vacuum from a height $h_1$ to a height $h_2$ ($h_1$ and $h_2$ are absent from an arbitrary chosen zero level, as shown in figure), then the gravitational force does work equal to the product of the projection $Mg$ of this force on the direction of movement on the model of movement $h_1 - h_2$:

$$A = Mg(h_1 - h_2).$$

(1)

This equality can also be written as follows:

$$A = Mgh_1 - Mgh_2 = -(Mgh_2 - Mgh_1) = -\Delta E_p,$$

that is, work is equal to the change in potential energy taken with the opposite sign.

Now let us assume that the fall of the same body occurs in some liquid (see figure). The presence of liquid, of course, will not change the mass of the body, nor the acceleration of gravity, nor, therefore, the gravitational force acting on the body. But when the body takes a new position at height $h_2$, it will leak out the liquid that was there, which moves at the top, to height $h_1$, and then the space vacated by the body. The body and liquid in the volume of the body seem to be placed in places. At the same time,

the potential energy of the body will decrease by values ​​$\Delta E_p = (Mgh_2 - Mgh_1)$, and the potential energy of the liquid will increase by values ​​$\Delta E_p = (mgh_1 - mgh_2)$, where $m$ is the mass of the liquid in a volume equal to the volume of the body. The total change in potential energy $\Delta E_p$ will be equal to the sum of $\Delta E_p$ and $\Delta E_p$, so we get

$$A = -\Delta E_p = -((Mgh_2 - Mgh_1) + (mgh_1 - mgh_2)) = (Mg - mg)(h_1 - h_2).$$

(2)

Let us compare equalities (2) and (1), we see that in the second case, in addition to the force $Mg$, directed downwards, the body is also acted upon by a force $-m\vec{g}$, directed upwards. But $mg$ is a model of the gravitational force acting on a fluid flowed out by a body (and equal to its weight). Therefore, the force $-m\vec{g}$ is the Archimedean force. The statement that such a force changes significantly in physics as Archimedes' law: a body immersed in a liquid is acted upon by a pushing force from the surrounding liquid, equal to the weight of the liquid in the volume flowed out by the body.

Formula (2) can be rewritten in another form. Let us denote the volume of the body by $V$, the density of the body by $\rho_T$ and the density of the liquid by $\rho_{ж}$. Then the mass of the body $M = \rho_{ж}V$, the mass of the liquid in the volume $V$ is equal to $m = \rho_{ж}V$ and

$$A = (\rho_{ж}Vg - \rho_{ж}Vg)(h_1 - h_2) = (\rho_{ж} - \rho_{ж})Vg(h_1 - h_2).$$

(3)

The value $\rho_{ж}Vg$ is a model of the Archimedean force.

From formula (3) it is clear that if the density $\rho_{ж}$ of the body is greater than the density $\rho_{ж}$ of the liquid, then the resulting force acting on the body is directed downward, and the body sinks. If, on the contrary, the density of the body is less than the density of the liquid, the resulting force is directed upward.
