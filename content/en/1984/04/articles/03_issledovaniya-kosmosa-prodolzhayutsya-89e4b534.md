Space exploration continues

Every year brings new successes in space exploration for Soviet science. A lot was done in this area last year.

Cosmonauts Vladimir Lyakhov and Aleksadr Aleksadrov worked for 150 days on board the Salyut-7 orbital station. In particular, they carried out an extensive program of research into the natural resources of the Earth, according to which, together with the cosmonauts, they carried out examinations on special ships, aircraft, and ground stations. For example, in September 1983, a complex experiment “Intercosmos - Black Sea” was carried out, during which “multi-story” observations of the state of the sea and the atmosphere above it at various depths and altitudes were simultaneously carried out.

An outstanding achievement is the installation of additional solar panels at the Salyut-7 station, which significantly increased the power supply to the station. This is a big step towards future installation work on assembling complex technical structures in space. In the photo above you see cosmonaut A. Aleksadrov during his re-entry into space.

The study of various technical processes in conditions of zero gravity and deep vacuum, carried out at the Salyut-6 and Salyut-7 stations during long-term space flights, makes it possible to begin the production of valuable semiconductor materials, crystals with unusual properties, and new medicinal substances.

Research on the planet Venus continued successfully. In the past year, information that was very valuable for planetary science was obtained about the composition and structure of the Venusian atmosphere, and then the surface of this planet in equatorial order (the photograph shows a panorama of a section of the surface of Venus obtained from the descent module of the Venera-14 station). It turned out that the surface of the planet was heated to 460 °C. The atmosphere of Venus is in a basic state of carbon dioxide and nitrogen and exerts a pressure of 90 atm on the surface of the planet.

In 1983, two Soviet spacecraft entered the orbits of the satellites of Venus.

In 1983, the Soviet space observatories Astron and Prognoz-9 were launched, intended to explore the distant depths of the Universe. Stars have been discovered in whose atmosphere there is hundreds of times more uranium, lead and tungsten than in the atmosphere of the Sun. Several galaxies with unknown high levels of ultraviolet radiation have been recorded.

The X-ray telescope installed on Astron also operates successfully. It uses supernovae to study supernovae, as well as flaring X-ray stars - bursters*. This telescope allows you to register the effect of “turning on” the source of X-ray rays in the constellation Hercules.

In gaseous stars (such as our Sun), the magnetic field is generated in an outer, partially ionized shell about 1/10th the radius of the star.

So, cosmic magnetic fields are created by currents flowing in an electrically conductive medium. In a similar way, fields are created in the electromagnet coil (figure 1, 6). But to maintain current in the electromagnet, an external energy source is needed. Meanwhile, in space conditions, “battery” EMF, acting due to chemical or thermal effects, is usually weak or completely absent. The main source of magnetic fields in space conditions is the movement of the conducting medium itself.

Cosmic magnetic fields are not constant. For example, the large-scale field of the Sun almost periodically changes its polarity every 11 years. Associated with these changes is the cyclicity of solar activity, which manifests itself in the form of dark spots, the solar corona, flares and other phenomena. In recent years, cyclicity has been discovered in many stars. The Earth's magnetic field close to us also turns out to be unstable. After large intervals of time - approximately in the course of years - it changes its direction by 180°: the north magnetic pole becomes south and vice versa.

Why does the magnetic field change in a conducting medium?

The electric field created by a charge disappears if this charge is neutralized by a charge of the opposite sign. In a plasma consisting of an equal number of moving positive and negative charges, any excess charge of the same sign that accidentally appears in any area is quickly neutralized. It is clear that the higher the electrical conductivity of the plasma, the shorter the electric field neutralization time. The magnetic field in plasma is created by moving charges, that is, electric currents. Obviously, as the current changes, the field will change.

Let us first discuss how currents change not in plasma, but in ordinary metal wires that are found in every room.

Consider a turn of radius $r$ of wire with cross section $\pi l^2$ with resistivity $Q$ (Ohm m). Let the coil first be connected to a battery and a current $I$ (A) flow through it, which creates a magnetic field $B$ (T) in the space around the coil. The maximum field is achieved at the center of the coil. It is perpendicular to its plane and equal in magnitude to $B_{\max} = I/2r$. The magnetic energy of this system can be estimated as

$$W \approx \mu_0 \frac{B^2_{\max}}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \quad (\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \Gamma_{\mathrm{H}} \cdot \mathrm{m}).$$

How then can we explain that, say, the solar magnetic field changes over a shorter period of time - 11 years?

The reason is that the environment is moving. The movement of a medium in which there are free positive and negative charges is equivalent to the movement of a conductor. The textbook “Physics 9” shows that the movement of a conductor in a magnetic field is accompanied by the appearance of electric movements of force, and if the ends of such a conductor are closed, a current will arise in the circuit. This secondary current itself creates a magnetic field, which, when added to the original field, changes it. It is remarkable that as a result of such a change in a perfectly conducting plasma, the resistance of which is zero, the total magnetic flux $\Phi = BS$ (S is the area of ​​the circuit) should remain unchanged. This means that the magnetic field created by a moving conductor seems to move with it. Such a field is usually called a magnetic field frozen into the plasma. One can clearly imagine a magnetic field in a moving conducting medium, where on the surface of a light thread thrown into a flow: the thread is not only transferred, but also stretches if the particles moving in the flow move away from each other.

When frozen, the movement of the plasma as a whole carries magnetic lines. The movement of some parts of the plasma relative to other deformations of the magnetic lines: they stretch and bend. The magnetic line, which initially passes through two close parts of the plasma, will continue to pass through these same particles, that is, follow their movements (figure 2). The number of magnetic lines that form any area, covering a closed loop of moving liquid particles, remains unchanged (at $\varrho = 0$). Magnetic induction $B$ of the field can occur during deformations, when areas with the same field direction come together, and weakens when areas with the opposite direction of the field come closer. A simple way to enhance the magnetic field is to scavenge the plasma layer (that is, to decrease the value of $S$). This idea is used to provide the simplest explanation for the giant magnetic fields of pulsars. A pulsar is a compact neutron star with a radius $r$ of the order of 10 km. If we assume that a neutron star was formed by the descent of an ordinary star with a radius of $r_0$ about 1 million km, then, during the process of its descent, its radius decreased by a factor of $10^5$. Magnetic flux conservation is introduced.

$$\pi r_0^2 B_0 = \pi r^2 B,$$

where $B_0$ is the field of the original star, $B$ is the field of the pulsar. Consequently, during the downward slope the field is enhanced by $(r_0/r)^2 = 10^{10}$ times. Therefore, the modest magnetic field of an ordinary star with a magnetic induction of, say, 0.01 T turns into a field with a magnetic induction of $10^8$ T.

On the other hand, plasma movements lead to bending and entanglement of magnetic lines. And for small-scale fields, as we have seen, Joule losses become significant.

It turns out that there may be such motions of the plasma that the effect of a successful large-scale field predominates or at least is not inferior to the weakening. In this case, the moving plasma works as a generator.

It is natural to call such a machine “dynamics” (the generator was once called a dynamo machine). The action of a dynamo is usually demonstrated by the example of a current-carrying frame rotating in the field of a magnet.

Hydromagnetic dynamo

The dynamics in the conducting medium that we are talking about should, unlike a technical generator, operate without wires, windings, and most importantly, without an external magnetic field, which must be present in a conventional generator *). Since the source of energy in this dynamo is only hydro-
dynamic motion of plasma, it was called “hydromagnetic”. To trigger a hydromagnetic dianon, only a weak initial magnetic field is zeroed. Generally weak fields exist due to various effects of interaction between charged parts of the plasma.

The idea of ​​explaining the magnetic fields of the Earth and the Sun using hydromagnetic movements was put forward by the famous English physicist J. Larmore in 1919. But is it possible for a self-excited hydromagnetic dianono to “work” without external sources of EMF? A positive answer to this question is best used by an example first demonstrated in 1971 by Academician Ya. B. Zeldovich to participants in a symposium in Krakow.

Let's take a ring tourniquet consisting of individual rubber rings (figure 3, a). Let the rubber band imitate magnetic lines that have the same direction (it is convenient to place it with arrows). Let's fold the strand into a figure eight (figure 3, b), place one ring of the figure 8 on top of the other and stretch the doubled strand to the previous size (figure 3, c). Pay attention to the direction of the arrows; they are the same. Thus, the number of magnetic lines passing through any cross-section of the bundle has doubled. By repeating the process of folding and stretching, you can each time double the number of magnetic lines in the bundle until the elasticity of the rubber accommodates this. Note that if you started from some point in the original tourniquet, you could return to the same point by going around one circle. After doubling the return to the same point, two rounds of the circle are required. Thus, the doubled bundle is not entirely equivalent to the original one. To get rid of this, you can each time cut the strand at the shiba point of the figure eight and stitch the freed circles, then folding them into one double strand, equivalent to the original one.

Non-uniform rotation

The simplest movement, characteristic of all planets, stars and galaxies, is rotation. However, if all parts of the body rotate with the same angular velocity, that is, the rotation is uniform, then the magnetic lines will rotate with the body, remaining practically unchanged. For bending, stretching and revolutions of the linear relations of the motion of neighboring parts of the plasma, that is, the rotation must be non-uniform. It is clear that solid bodies rotate uniformly, otherwise they collapse. A liquid or gaseous body is capable of performing non-uniform rotation. Observations of the Sun have shown that the equatorial parts of our star complete a revolution several days faster than the circumpolar regions. Indirect evidence indicates that the inner, not directly observable, regions of the Sun rotate even faster. Inhomogeneous rotation of the gaseous disk of the Galaxy: at the same time, its internal parts make more revolutions than the external ones.

To visualize non-uniform rotation, imagine that in front of you are several concentric circles of carcels (figure 4, a). Those that are close to the center circle, say, fast carcels located further from the center.

Initially, the line was located along the diameter, but now it has become close to the circle. Thus, the inhomogeneous rotation turned the radial magnetic field into a field directed almost along the azimuth.

It should not be overlooked that the hydromagnetic dynamo operates under conditions of strong turbulent entanglement and destruction of magnetic lines. Thus, the growth or maintenance of the field occurs against the background of the continuous birth of new and dying of old magnetic fields.

Hydromagnetic dynamo is widely used to describe the magnetic fields of stars, planets and galaxies. In part, the hydromagnetic dynamo appears to be the main “engine” of repeating solar activity.
