F856. A constant current is passed through a heating coil with constant resistance. By what percentage will the average amount of the body released in the spiral per second change if a simultaneous alternating (sinusoidal) current is passed through it, the amplitude value of which is 10% of the direct current?

When only direct current $I_0$ flows through the spiral, the power released in it is equal to $P_1 = I_0^2 R$.

The instantaneous value of the current in the spiral, when both direct and alternating current flows through it, is equal to $I_0 + I \sin \omega t$. The instantaneous power released in the spiral in this case is
$$P_2 = (I_0 + I \sin \omega t)^2 R.$$

So the instantaneous power when connecting an additional alternating current source to the spiral changes to
$$\left( \frac{P_2 - P_1}{P_1} \right) 100\% = \frac{R (I_0 + I \sin \omega t)^2 - R I_0^2}{R I_0^2} 100\% = \left( 2 \frac{I}{I_0} \sin \omega t + \left( \frac{I}{I_0} \right)^2 \sin^2 \omega t \right) 100\%.$$
Since $\sin \omega t = 0$, $\sin^2 \omega t = \frac{1}{2}$ (averages of $\sin \omega t$, $\sin^2 \omega t$), the average power changes by
$$\frac{P_2 - P_1}{P_1} 100\% = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{I}{I_0} \right)^2 100\% = 0,5\%.$$
V. G. Dudnikov

F857. The shiny can has a flat dent from which a beam of light is reflected onto the screen. Cooled liquid is poured into the jar. The reflected scene is noticeably weakened. Explain the phenomenon.

When cooled liquid was poured into the jar, the temperature of the jar became below the dew point, and small drops of condensed moisture from the surrounding air appeared on the surface of the jar. Now the light is scattered by these droplets, and therefore the reflected signal is noticeably attenuated.

V. G. Dudnikov
