16. In a quadrilateral $ABCD$, line segment $KL$ connects two opposite sides, line segment $MN$ connects two other opposite sides, and line segment $PQ$ connects two diagonals. Prove that the segments $KL, MN$ and $PQ$ intersect at one point $Z$ and each of them is bisected at this point.

17. In the quadrilateral $ABCD$, the intersection points of the medians of triangles $BCD, ACD, ABD, ABC$ are denoted by $A', B', C', D'$. Prove that each of the segments $AA', BB', CC', DD'$ passes through the point $Z$, a distance within the limits of the problem, and is divided by this point in the ratio 3:1, counting from the vertices of the quadrilateral.

18. There are three segments in a quadrilateral, each of which connects one of the vertices with the intersection point of the medians of opposite edges. Prove that these three segments intersect at one point $Z$, and each of them is bisected at this point.

19. In the quadrilateral of the quadrilateral $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5A_6$ their midpoints $B_1, B_2, B_3, B_4, B_5, B_6$ are successively offset. Will the point of intersection of the medians of triangle $B_1B_3B_5$ coincide with the point of intersection of the medians of triangle $B_2B_4B_6$?

20. On the sides of the hexagon $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5A_6$ their midpoints $B_1, B_2, B_3, B_4, B_5, B_6$ are successively offset. Will the point of intersection of the medians of triangle $B_1B_3B_5$ coincide with the point of intersection of the medians of triangle $B_2B_4B_6$?

21. Given six points, no three of which belong to the same line. The point of intersection of the medians of the triangle with the vertices at each or three of these points is connected by a segment to the point of intersection of the medians of the triangle with the vertices at the other three points. Prove that all 10 segments possible in this way have a common midpoint.

22. The opposite sides $AB$ and $DC$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ are separated by the points $M$ and $N$ in the same ratio $k$ (i.e. $\overrightarrow{AM} = k\overrightarrow{MB}$, $\overrightarrow{DN} = k\overrightarrow{NC}$); $P$ and $Q$ are the midpoints of sides $AD$ and $BC$. In each ratio, is each of the segments $MN$ and $PQ$ divided by their point of intersection?

23. Opposite links $AB$ and $DC$ of the spatial closed broken line $ABCD$ are separated by points $M, N, P, Q$, respectively, in the ratios $\alpha : \beta$, $\beta : \gamma, \gamma : \delta, \delta : \alpha$, where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ are positive numbers. Prove that the points $M, N, P, Q$ lie in the same plane. In each ratio the segments $MP$ and $NQ$ are divided by their point of intersection?

25. Each link of the closed spatial broken line $A_1A_2 \dots A_{2n}A_1$ intersects the plane $\alpha$ at one point, and the link $A_iA_{i+1}$ is divided by the intersection point $c$ by the plane $\alpha$ in relation $k_i (i=1, 2, \dots, 2n-1)$. In each relation, the link $A_{2n}A_1$ is divided by the intersection point $c$ by the plane $\alpha$?

26. On the sides of the triangle $ABC$ the points $M$ and $N$ are also taken, which is $\overrightarrow{CN} = \alpha\overrightarrow{CA}, \overrightarrow{CM} = \beta\overrightarrow{CB}$. Let $S$ be the intersection point of lines $AM$ and $BN$. Compute the ratios $|AS|:|AM|, |BS|:|BN|$.

27. On sides $KL$ and $LM$ of triangle $KLM$ the points $A$ and $B$ are also taken, which is $|LA| = 3|AK|, |BJ| = 4|BM|$. Calculate the ratio of the areas of triangles $KLM$ and $KLC$, where $C$ is the intersection point of lines $AM$ and $KB$.

28. On the sides $AB, BC, CA$ of the triangle $ABC$ the points $C_1, A_1, B_1$ are also taken, which are $|AC_1| = \frac{1}{3}|AB|, |BA_1| = \frac{1}{3}|BC|, |CB_1| = \frac{1}{3}|CA|$. When the segments $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ intersect, a triangle $A_2B_2C_2$ is formed. Find the ratio of the areas of triangles $A_2B_2C_2$ and $ABC$.

Note. On each relation the segments $BB_1$ and $CC_1$ (and, similarly, for the point $B_2$ and $C_2$).

29. The area of ​​parallelogram $ABCD$ is 1. Point $M$ divides side $BC$ in the ratio 3:5. Calculate the area of ​​the quadrilateral $CMPD$, where $P$ is the intersection point of lines $AM$ and $BD$.

30. On the side $AB$ of the parallelogram $ABCD$, a point $M$ is taken, cutting off the $\frac{1}{n}$ part of this side, that is, $|AM| = \frac{1}{n}|AB|$. What part of the diagonal $AC$ is cut off by the straight line $DM$?

31. A circle is inscribed in triangle $ABC$ and touches sides $BC, CA, AB$, respectively, at points $A', B', C'$. Prove that the segments $AA', BB'$ and $CC'$ intersect at one point. Note. Let us introduce the notation: $|AC'| = |AB'| = p, |BC'| = |BA'| = q, |CA'| = |CB'| = r$ (Fig. 6). Let's load the vertices $A, B, C$ with the masses $1/p, 1/q, 1/r$. Make sure that $C'$ is the barycenter of the two materials of the point $\frac{1}{p} A$ and $\frac{1}{q} B$, and therefore (by the mass grouping theorem) the barycenter $Z$ of the material system of the point $\frac{1}{p} A, \frac{1}{q} B, \frac{1}{r} C$ lies on the segment $CC'$. Similarly check that $Z \in [AA']$ and $Z \in [BB']$.

32. A circle is inscribed in angle $PAQ$ and touches the sides of the angle at points $P$ and $Q$. Line $BC$ ($B \in [AP], Q \in [AQ]$) touches the circle at point $T$. Lines $BQ$ and $CP$ intersect at point $M$. Do the points $M, T, A$ lie on the same line?

33. On the sides of the triangle $ABC$ or on their chosen longitudinal ones there are also points $A_1, B_1, C_1$, which are $\overrightarrow{BA_1} = \alpha\overrightarrow{A_1C}, \overrightarrow{CB_1} = \beta\overrightarrow{A_1A}, \overrightarrow{AC_1} = \gamma\overrightarrow{C_1B}$. Prove that if $\alpha\beta\gamma = -1$, then the points $A_1, B_1, C_1$ lie on the same line (Menelaus’ theorem).

Note. Distance of the four material system point $1A, \gamma B, (-1)A, \alpha\gamma C$.

34. Prove that if the notation of the previous problem satisfies the equality $\alpha\beta\gamma = 1$, then the lines $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ intersect at one point or are parallel (Ceva’s theorem).

Note. Load the vertices $A, B, C$ with the masses $1, \gamma, \alpha\gamma$ and prove that for $1 + \gamma + \alpha\gamma \neq 0$ the center of mass of these three point materials belongs to each of the lines $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$.
