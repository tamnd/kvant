M872. There are three circles $C_1, C_2, C_3$ of radii $r_1, r_2, r_3$ on the plane - each outside the other two, $r_1 > r_2$ and $r_1 > r_3$. Prove that the last two pairs of tangents form a quadrilateral in which a circle can be inscribed, and find its radius.

Answer: $r = r_1r_2r_3 / (r_1r_3 + r_1r_2 - r_2r_3)$.

Let us denote the centers of these circles by $O_1, O_2, O_3$, the points of intersection of the tangents by $A$ and $B$ (Fig. 1).

The solution to the problem is based on the following obvious, but often used message: the point of intersection of external tangents to two circles serves as the centromometry of these circles (the same is true for the point of intersection of internal tangents). It immediately follows from it that the quadruple circle must be prepared ית $C_2$ with center $A$ and $C_3$ with center $B$, therefore its center $O$ must lie at the intersection of the segments $AO_2$ and $BO_3$. F883. When a stationary earthly projectile ruptures, it scatters into a very large number of fragments, flying uniformly in all directions at a speed of $v_0$. When the jumper moves, the magnetic flux changes, producing a circuit “closed” by the jumper. As a result, an induced emf will arise in the circuit.

During a short period of time $\Delta t$, when the instantaneous speed $v$ of the jumper can be considered unchanged, the instantaneous value of the induced emf is equal to

$$\delta_i = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = vbB \cos \alpha.$$

So, the current through the jumper at this time is equal to $i = \Delta q/\Delta t$, where $\Delta q$ is the charge accumulated on the capacitor during the time $\Delta t$, that is

$$\Delta q = C \cdot \Delta \delta_i = CbB(\cos \alpha) \cdot \Delta v$$

(since there is no resistance of the lead wires, the instantaneous value of the voltage across the capacitor is $\delta_i$). So,

$$i = CbB \cos \alpha \cdot \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = CbBa \cos \alpha,$$

where $\alpha$ is the acceleration with which the jumper moves.
