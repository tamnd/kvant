Moscow City Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren

In 1984, the Moscow Mathematical Olympiad was held for the 47th time. Problems of its final type are published below; The names of the authors of the problems are indicated in brackets.

8th grade

1. Solve the equation $\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} + x^2 - 4 = 0$.
(A. N. Dranitsinkov)

2. Each two of the six computers are connected by their own wire. Show how to color each of these wires in one of five colors so that five wires of different colors come out of each computer.

(B. B. Alekseev)

3. Prove that the sum of the distances from the center of a regular heptagon to all its vertices is less than the sum of the distances to them from any other point.

(S. B. Gashkov, A. V. Ryabinin)

4. The sum of the heel of non-negative numbers is equal to one. Prove that they can be arranged in a circle so that the sum of all five pairwise products of connecting numbers is no more than $\frac{1}{5}$.

(S. B. Gashkov)

5. Cut the square into 8 acute triangles.

(M. Gardner)

6. Is an even number all 64-digit natural numbers that do not contain zeros and are divisible by 101?

(S. B. Gashkov)

9th grade

1. The side edges of a triangular straight line have the same length, and the side faces have the same area. Prove that the base of this straight line is an isosceles triangle.

(A. N. Dranitsinkov)

2. Each two of the 13 computers are connected by their own wire. Is it possible to color each of these wires in one of 12 colors so that 12 wires of different colors come out of each computer?

(B. B. Alekseev)

3. Some triangles can be cut from a paper strip of one width, but cannot be cut from any strip of smaller width. What is the smallest area this triangle can have?

(S. B. Gashkov)

4. There are at least four non-negative numbers placed around the circle, the sum of which is equal to one. Prove that the sum of all pairwise products of connecting numbers is not greater than $\frac{1}{4}$.

(S. B. Gashkov, A. N. Dranitsinkov)

5. Are there three non-zero digits that can be used to form an infinite number of decimal notations for the squares of different numbers?

(S. B. Gashkov)

6. In a rectangle of size $3 \times 4$ 4 points are randomly located. Prove that between any two of them the distance is no greater than $\frac{25}{8}$.

(S. B. Gashkov)

10th grade

1. Without using a calculator, tables, etc., prove the inequality $\sin 1 < \log_3 \sqrt{7}$.

(N. N. Sergeeev)

2. The jury of the Olympiad decided, based on its results, to connect each section in such a way that using this number it would be possible to unambiguously reconstruct the points received by the section for each task, and so that from each schoolchildren, more numbers would be connected to the one who scored the highest amount of points. Help the jury solve this problem!

(N. N. Sergeeev)

3. Solve the equation in integers $19x^3 - 84y^2 = 1984$.

(B. B. Alekseev)

4. In some kingdoms, in some geographical quantities, $n_1, n_2, n_3, \dots$ can reach the end, where $n_1 < n_2 < n_3 < \dots$ is an infinite sequence consisting of natural numbers. Prove that this sequence can be processed, i.e., there is a number $N$ such that any amount that can be dropped without changing the issued coins can actually be dropped only with coins of value $n_1, n_2, \dots, n_N$ end.

(S. B. Gashkov, I. N. Sergeeev)

5. The square is cut into acute triangles. Prove that there are at least 8 of them.

(M. N. Grinichuk)

6. A triangular section of a cube touches a sphere inscribed in the cube. Prove that the area of ​​this section is less than half the area of ​​the cube.

(S. B. Gashkov)

4. Answer. 16 h 36 $\frac{12}{13}$ min. Solution. The clock at the moment of stopping shows five hours without something, that is, 4 hours $t$ minutes. At the same time, the minute hand traveled half a circle, that is, 180° and another α°, but the hour hand did not reach α° to the middle of the circle. In 1 minute, the minute hand rotates by 6°, and the hour hand by 0.5°. In t minutes, the minute hand will rotate by $t \cdot 6^\circ = 180^\circ + \alpha$, and the hour hand by 60°—α°=t·0.5°. Hence $t = 36 \frac{12}{13}$ min.
