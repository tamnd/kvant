Would lie on the surface of a spherical segment with a height of $h_t$ (see Fig.1) compacted to the ground. Let's find the mass $m(t)$ of these fragments.

Let $m_0$ be the mass of all fragments (mass of the projectile); Then

$$m(t) = m_0 \frac{S(h_t)}{S(R_t)} = m_0 \frac{2\pi R_t h_t}{4\pi R_t^2} = m_0 \frac{h_t}{R_t},$$

where $S(h_t)$ is the area of ​​the spherical segment with height $h_t$, $S(R_t)$ is the area of ​​the entire sphere at time $t$. Substituting $R_t = v_0 t$ and $h_t = ut + gt^2/2 + v_0 t - H$ (see Fig.1), we get

$$m(t) = m_0 \left( \frac{u + v_0}{2v_0} + \frac{gt}{4v_0} - \frac{H}{2v_0 t} \right).$$

The resulting formula is correct for the time interval $t_1 \leq t \leq t_2$, where $t_1$ is the time elapsed after the explosion until the fragments began to fall to the ground, $t_2$ is the time elapsed before the fragment fell to the ground after it. Let's find the values ​​of $t_1$ and $t_2$.

The first fragment to fall to the ground will be the one whose speed at point $A$ is equal to $|\vec{u} + \vec{v}_0| = u + v_0$; hence,

$$(u + v_0) t_1 + \frac{gt_1^2}{2} = H,$$

where

$$t_1 = \frac{\sqrt{2gH + (u + v_0)^2} - (u + v_0)}{g}.$$

Time $t_2$ is found from the condition

$$(u - v_0) t_2 + \frac{gt_2^2}{2} = H,$$

where

$$t_2 = \frac{\sqrt{2gH + (u - v_0)^2} - (u - v_0)}{g}.$$

Let's return to formula (1). The concept that the frequency of fragments falling to the ground at a given time is the value of an arbitrary $m'(t)$:

$$m'(t) = \frac{m_0 g}{4v_0} + \frac{m_0 H}{2v_0 t^2}.$$

Obviously, the smaller $t$ the greater the value of $m'(t)$. Consequently, with the most frequency, fragments will fall to the ground after a time $t_1$ (see (2)) after the shell explodes.

I. I. Solodovnikov

F884. Problem of N. E. Zhukovsky. The beetle flies along a local (non-threading) straw resting on a smooth floor and a smooth vertical wall (Fig. 1). The straw is homogeneous, its length is l, its mass is m; beetle mass M (M>m). The straw forms an angle a with the horizon. The initial speed of the beetle at point B was equal to v_0. How should a beetle move for the straw to remain

Let at some instant of time the acceleration with which the beetle flies down along the straw be equal to a. The beetle is acted upon by gravitational forces $Mg$ and reaction force $\vec{f}$ from the straw. Let's find the direction of the force $\vec{f}$.

Let's write down the equation of motion of the beetle:

$$Mg + \vec{f} = M\vec{a},$$

or, in projections on the X and Y axes (see Fig.1):

$$Ma = Mg \sin \alpha + f_x, \quad f_y - Mg \cos \alpha = 0.$$
