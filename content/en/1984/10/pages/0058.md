X All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren

According to established tradition, the final, zonal stage of the All-Russian Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry Olympiad for schoolchildren in grades 8-10 takes place during the spring school holidays. This year the All-Russian Olympics celebrated its tenth anniversary. It has become one of the most widespread modern schoolchildren in the Russian Federation, and its winners worthily represent the resource field at the All-Union and International Olympids.

As usual, the zonal stage was preceded by district, city and regional (territorial) Olympids, as a result of which each region (edge) was represented by the winner of the regional (territorial) stage for each class.

This year, the final stage of the Olympiad was held for the North-Western zone in Vologda, for the Central zone in Sarapsk, for the South-Western zone in Nalchik and for the Siberian and Far East zone in Yakutsk. In addition, at the same time, the final stage of the Olympiad was held at the Physics and Mathematics School of Interpgmata at Moscow, Leningrad and Novosibirsk Universities.

The final stage assignment in mathematics consisted of five problems, the solution of which took four hours. As the test showed, problems in geometry and number theory caused the most difficulties for students. Compinator-type tasks also caused significant difficulties. Members of the school discussed the competition tasks in detail with the schoolchildren and analyzed the mistakes made.

The final stage of the Olympiad in Physics was carried out in two types - theoretical and experimental, and all schoolchildren took part in the experimental type, regardless of the results of the theoretical type. The theoretical type task included four tasks, and the experimental type - two. Four hours were allotted to solve each problem. The test showed that all the tasks were feasible for the Olympiad students.

Schoolchildren who took 1st to 4th places at the zonal stage of the All-Russian Olympiad were awarded diplomas, certificates and memorable gifts. From among the prizes, the RSFSR team was formed to participate in the All-Union Olympiad.

The teachers who prepared the prizes for the Olympids were also awarded special certificates.

Below are the problems in mathematics and physics of the final stage and the names of the prizes of the X All-Russian Olympid for schoolchildren.

Mathematics

8th grade

1. Each cube was divided into four identical squares and each resulting square was painted with one of three different colors. It turned out that any two squares that have a common side are colored different colors. Prove that there are eight squares of each color in total. Give an example of such a coloring.

2. Construct a flat closed eight-link broken line that intersects each link once, and at the interior point of the link.

3. Circles with centers $O_1$ and $O_2$ of the same radius touch each other $A$. A circle with center $O_3$ twice the radius contains within itself a circle with center $O_1$, touching it at point $B$, and a circle with center $O_2$ intersects at points $P$ and $Q$. Prove that the line $AB$ passes through either the point $P$ or the point $Q$.

4. See task 3, point a) for 10th grade.

5. The natural numbers $a, b, c, d$ satisfy the equality $ab=cd$. Prove that the number $a^{1984}+b^{1984}+c^{1984}+d^{1984}$ is composite.

9th grade

1. Find all natural numbers $n$ such that the sum of the decimal digits of the number $2^n$ is 5.

2. In the cells of the square table $3\times 3$ there are signs “+” and “-”, as shown in figure 1. It is allowed to change all the signs to the opposite ones in any column or in any line. Is it possible, by repeating this procedure several times, from this table to obtain the table shown in figure 2?

3. What is the maximum value of the difference between a three-digit number and the sum of the cubes of its digits? Is this maximum reached for every three-digit number? What is the minimum value of this difference?

4. Through the point $C$, which lies in a circle with center $O_1$, two tangents are drawn to this circle, touching it at points $A$ and $B$. Through points $C$ and $B$ a circle is drawn with center $O_2$, tangent to line $AB$ at point $B$ and intersecting the circle with center $O_1$ at point $M$. Prove that the prime $AM$ divides the segment $BC$ in half.

5. Are there points $A, B, C$ on the plane such that for any point $P$ of this plane the length of at least one of the segments $PA, PB, PC$ is an irrational number?
