If we now force the cannons to move evenly and rectilinearly and shoot on the move without changing the load of the ordium, then, according to the principle of the relative projectile, they must again return to the muzzle of the cannon. This is already much more interesting.

Under some conditions, you can independently make the appropriate installation, schematically depicted in figure, and demonstrate its operation in a physics lesson. The most difficult thing is the cannon. The gun spring is compressed using handles 1 and fixed in this position by trigger 2. The projectile (metal ball) is placed on a cup attached to the spring. When firing on the move, the trigger device aligns when it hits a wedge 3 mounted on a board 4 on which the gun is launched. The ordium adjustment (aiming) is carried out using a plumb line 5 and three screws 6 (for greater accuracy of the mounting, it is better to make the platform from two plates 7 and 8). With precise aiming, the answer should align with mark 9.

The procedure for preparing and conducting the experiment is as follows. Using two screws 6, the road-board 4 is installed on the table so that the cannon rolls along it at approximately constant speed (so that the moving system can be considered inertial). By placing, for example, a ruler under the wheels of the cannon, the cannon is mounted on the cannon using three screws 6. Then the cannon is loaded and the shot is fired from the spot (for this, the comparison device 2 is forced with another ruler). The ball flies out and returns again to the muzzle of the cannon. Then the ruler-stopper is removed, the cannon picks up speed, the trigger device hits wedge 3, the ball flies up, simultaneously moving along the road at the same speed as the cannon, and, to the applause of the spectators, the ordium again hits the muzzle. The actual experience is quite effective.

In conclusion, here are some practical tips. It is better to make the guns massive so that when hitting a wedge and when firing, the guns do not get knocked off. The sufficient diameter of the muzzle is about 3 cm. To achieve the imagination of the spectators, the ball must fly out approximately 1.5-2 m, during which time the platform with the cannon must travel approximately 0.5 m. The board-road must be made rigid so that it does not get driven away under the weight of the ordium, and the two screws 6 fixed on the board should be sharpened, then the board-road itself will be stable. In order for the experiment to be repeated several times, at the end of the road the guns must either be stopped smoothly by hand or a soft barrier must be made at the end of the road.

*) Here it is assumed that the Earth is an inertial reporting system, although in fact any real reporting system can be considered inertial only with one approximation or another.
