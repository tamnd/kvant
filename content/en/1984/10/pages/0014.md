From everything that has been said about the physical quantity called force, it follows that the only result of the action of a force on a body (material point) is the acceleration acquired by the body under the influence of this force.

5. Pulse

We already know that when two bodies interact, both bodies receive accelerations that depend on their masses. But it is possible to find a value that will be the same for both interacting bodies and will not depend on the masses of these bodies.

We will find this quantity by rewriting Newton's second law - $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ - in a slightly modified form. Recall that the acceleration of a body can be written as $\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{v} - \vec{v}_0}{t}$. Then $\vec{F} = m\frac{\vec{v} - \vec{v}_0}{t}$, from where

$$\vec{Ft} = m\vec{v} - m\vec{v}_0.$$

The quantity $m\vec{v}$ is called the body impulse, and the quantity $\vec{Ft}$ is called the force impulse.

Now we can say that during the interaction of two bodies, the impulse with each force changes, with which the bodies act on each other, are equal in magnitude and directed in opposite directions. Therefore, the changes in the impulses of both bodies are also equal in magnitude and mutually opposite in direction. This means that the geometric sum of the impulses of interacting bodies remains constant for any interaction of these bodies. This statement is called the law of conservation of momentum. It can be shown that the law of conservation of momentum is valid not only for two, but also for any number of bodies interacting with each other. This is one of the most important laws of nature.

6. Work and Energy

We defined the impulse of a force as the product of forces $\vec{F}$ and the time of its action $t$. By analogy, we can derive quantities equal to the product of the forces acting on the body and the displacement of the body. This quantity is called the work of force. In the case when the direction of the force vectors $\vec{F}$ and the displacements $\vec{s}$ coincide, the work of the force is equal to $Fs$.

In the general case, when the direction of the force and the direction of movement of the body make an angle $\alpha$ with each other, the expression for the work has the form

$$A = Fs \cos \alpha.$$

Unlike the momentum of a body, which is a vector quantity, work is a scalar quantity.

It can be proven that the work done by a force acting on a body is always equal to

$$A = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{mv_0^2}{2}.$$
