M872. There are three circles $C_1, C_2, C_3$ of radii $r_1, r_2, r_3$ on the plane - each outside the other two, $r_1 > r_2$ and $r_1 > r_3$. From the point of intersection of external tangents to the circle $C_1$ and $C_2$, tangents to the circle $C_3$ are drawn, and from the point of intersection of external tangents to $C_1$ and $C_3$, tangents to $C_2$. Prove that the last two pairs of tangents form a quadrilateral in which a circle can be inscribed, and find its radius.

Answer: $r = r_1r_2r_3 / (r_1r_3 + r_1r_2 - r_2r_3)$.

Let us denote the centers of these circles by $O_1, O_2, O_3$, the points of intersection of the tangents by $A$ and $B$ (Fig. 1).

The solution to the problem is based on the following obvious, but often used message: the point of intersection of external tangents to two circles serves as the centromometry of these circles (the same is true for the point of intersection of internal tangents). It immediately follows from it that the quadruple circle must be prepared ית $C_2$ with center $A$ and $C_3$ with center $B$, therefore its center $O$ must lie at the intersection of the segments $AO_2$ and $BO_3$.
