How physical quantities are entered
Academician I. K. Kikoin

Physics differs from other natural sciences in that the object laws established in the study of physical natural phenomena are expressed quantitatively (mathematically). To do this, physicists introduce quantities that characterize the phenomenon or process being studied. Mathematical correspondences between quantities are established experimentally in the form of corresponding equations or formulas, which are called the physical laws of nature.

How are physical quantities introduced? For each physical quantity, the method of measurement must be indicated. More precisely, it is impossible to introduce physical quantities without indicating at least the fundamental method of measurement. Let us explain this with some examples. Let's start with the quantities used in mechanics.

1. Speed

The concept of speed has been introduced since time immemorial. And you can imagine how this was done. Perhaps some person who had a device for measuring time - this could be, for example, a sundial - observed the movement of caravans in the desert. He determined the distance the caravan travels in a certain period of time. perhaps he did this by counting the number of steps the camel took. A camel's stride is a measure of length. Corresponding, expressing a period of time and the distance traveled, he established a curious correspondence between them. It turns out that the ratio of the paths traveled by the caravan for any time intervals is equal to the ratio of these time intervals. In modern language, this means the following: if we denote the path traveled by a caravan during a period of time $t_1$ by $s_1$, and the path traveled by a caravan during a period of time $t_2$ by $s_2$, then $\frac{s_1}{s_2} = \frac{t_1}{t_2}$.
