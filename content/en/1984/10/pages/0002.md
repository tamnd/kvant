Physics studies only those properties of a body that can be expressed numerically, and to study these properties in physics, physical quantities are derived that characterize certain properties (how this is done is described in the article by Academician I. K. Kikon in this issue of the journal). For each physical quantity, the method of its measurement must be indicated. To measure a physical quantity means to compare it with a homogeneous quantity taken as a unit. For such a comparison, some device or body is needed that would reproduce a single given quantity. Such devices are called measures. Each physical quantity has its own units of measurement and, accordingly, its own measures. For example, a millimeter ruler, a centimeter, which tailors use, are measures of length; zgi and weights - measures of mass, etc. Each measure, with varying accuracy, will reproduce the stage that is taken as a single quantity. An etaon of mass is, as is known, a specially made cylinder made of an alloy of platinum and iridium. The mass of this cylinder is called kilogramm and is taken as the international unit of mass.

The kilogram as a standard of mass received “international recognition” relatively recently - at the beginning of the 20th century. And measurements of mass have been made since time immemorial, and in time immemorial there were measures and instruments for measuring mass. In our time, we imagine they were used and inconvenient. With the development of culture, science and technology, they improved and their number grew. Over time, it has become very convenient to use the creation of etons that “store” units of measurement.
