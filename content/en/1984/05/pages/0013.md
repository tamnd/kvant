Generating function method

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
S. M. VORONIN,
A. G. KULAGIN

The method, called stats in the title, is a beautiful and unexpected mathematical technique that allows the properties of a task from number theory, probability theory and combinatorics to be applied to a task from analysis. It often turns out that for the analytical reformulation of a problem obtained by this method, it is possible to quickly find a solution, while other approaches to the original problem give nothing. It should be noted right away, however, that the method of generating functions is not a “magic wand” with which you can solve all problems (see Fermat’s problem below, for solving which it turns out to be useless).

First, without going into the intricacies of the theory, we will show how the method of generating functions works in four classical problems. Then we will briefly discuss the rationale for the method, and at the end we will propose a number of problems that can be solved with its help.

Weighing problem

In the 50s of the 18th century, L. Euler solved the following problem: what other numbers can be added to 1, 2, 2², 2³, ..., 2ⁿ,... grams, and in how many ways?

We do not know how quickly and how Euler came to its solution, but the final train of thought of the great St. Petersburg mathematician, who must precede us, is striking in its unexpectedness. Judge for yourself.
