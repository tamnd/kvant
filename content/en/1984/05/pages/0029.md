§ 2. Properties of inversion

Let us see how the lines and circles of the plane $a$ are transformed during inversion. It follows directly from the definition that a straight line passing through the center of inversion goes into itself. In this case, the center $O$ and $\infty$ change places. The primaya, as it were, “turns along the plane”: the part of it that is outside the circle, together with the “infinity,” turns into the diameter of the circle, and the diameter turns into two rays, and the point $O$ turns into $\infty$, as if “connecting” these rays.

What happens to the line that does not pass through the center $O$?
