§ 4. Construction problems

Inversion can be used with great success when solving construction problems.

The solution to all problems is based on the method of constructing a point $A'$ symmetrical to a given point $A$ relative to the circle $C$ (see Fig. 14). This method is as follows (for the point $A$ lying outside the circle $C$). Draw a tangent $AM$ and from point $M$ lower the perpendicular to $OA$. The base of the perpendicular is the required point $A'$ (see problem 3 from § 3).

Exercise 12. Think of a construction for the point $A'$ if $A$ lies inside the circle $C$.

Knowing how to construct images of a point, we can easily construct images of straight lines and circles. For example, if you want to construct an image of a line $l$ that does not pass through the point $O$, it is enough to lower the perpendicular $OD$ from point $O$ to the line $l$, construct the point $D'$ and describe the circle at $OD'$ as a diameter.

Exercise 13. How to construct an image of a circle that does not pass through the point $O$? Remember what was written about this in § 2.

Let us now give an example of a construction problem solved using inversion.

Problem 7. Construct a circle tangent to the given line $l$ and passing through the given points $A$ and $B$. How many solutions does this problem have?

Solution. Let us assume that the desired circle $T$ has been constructed. Let's make an arbitrary inversion with the center at point $A$. The straight line $l$ will go into a certain circle $l'$, the point $B$ into the point $B'$, and the circle $T$ into the straight line $T'$ tangent to the circle $l'$ (see Fig. 15).

From here the construction method immediately follows: we perform an inversion with the center at the point $A$, from the point $B'$ we draw tangents to the circle $l'$, we perform another inversion with the center at the point $A$, we obtain the required. Generally speaking, there will be two solutions. (Can there be one solution?)

§ 5. Inversion and stereographic projection

Consider the sphere $Q$ tangent to the plane $a$ at point $O$. Let point $N$ be diametrically opposite to point $S$. The stereographic projection of the sphere $Q$ onto the plane $a$ is called a mapping, which to each point $B$ of the sphere, with the exception of the point $N$, associates the point $A$ of the plane $a$, obtained as a result of the intersection of the line $NB$ with the plane $a$ (Fig.16).
