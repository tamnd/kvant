⟦folio 8⟧

The situation is different with a particle that obeys quantum mechanics. Since we, in accordance with the correspondence of uncertainties, cannot accurately indicate its coordinates and speed at a given moment in time, then, obviously, we cannot predict what they will become in the future. It is not difficult to guess that all this is a consequence of the fact that particles (electrons, atoms, neutrons, etc.) have wave properties.

In fact, let us consider the following roughly schematized experiment, which, nevertheless, is very close to the actual experiments carried out. Let's imagine that electrons emitted by some source, for example a hot wire, fly through two branches in the screen. Behind the screen we can move the electron counter parallel to it. The counter registers each electron that enters it. This means that the number of electrons can be realized indefinitely.

It is natural to assume that each electron entering the counter has gone through one of two reactions. Therefore, if we count the number of electrons that entered the counter through the second branch closed, then do the same with the electrons that entered the counter through the second branch while the first one was closed, we can expect that the number of electrons that entered the counter through both branches will be equal to the sum shown by the counter. We are sure that this would be the case if we fired from a machine gun through an armored shield with two arms, behind which in some place there was a box of pitch, where the leaked bullets got stuck. There is no doubt that the number of bullets that fall into a box with pitch when opening both slots in the screen is equal to the sum of the numbers of bullets that fall into the same box through each of the slots separately (of course, over the same period of time, say, an hour).

But when we “shoot” electrons, this doesn’t work! Moreover, it may turn out that a counter installed in the proper place, which registers the same number of electrons as they pass through each branch separately (when one of the branches is closed), is not registered, and does not register a single electron when both branches are open.

The question naturally arises: how can this be?

It is not difficult to see here the same phenomenon that is observed when the screen is illuminated by two identical light sources, that is, the phenomenon of interference.

Let us now try to answer the question why quantum mechanics is incomprehensible. This will help us find the roots of the idealistic conclusions that some philosophers and physicists have come to when analyzing the current situation in physics.

The main difficulty in understanding distances above physical phenomena lies in the fact that when analyzing them we try to use the same concepts to which we are accustomed in higher life. The centuries-old experience of mankind has led to the fact that a person considers to himself understandable what he can imagine in the form of a geometric or mechanical image. This experience, the practice of studying the world around us, led to the creation of a number of concepts with the help of which the real world is reflected in the human brain. But until the 20th century, mankind was concerned only with macro-specialized topics moving at relatively low speeds. Macro-special bodies are those that can be seen, determined their shape and size (create a geometric image) and study their movement (create a mechanical image). Corresponding concepts were also adapted to this experience.

Newtonian mechanics, developed in relation to the movement of macroscopic bodies, established that the mechanical state of the body is uniquely determined by the coordinates and implus (or speed, if the mass of the body is considered unchanged).

But now we have moved to the world of objects obeying quantum law, alien to Newtonian mechanics. Human experience has not yet had time to develop images of objects and concepts corresponding to this world.

If experience shows that these objects have both the properties of particles and the properties of waves, then in reality they are not waves or particles, but must be something else, “one”
