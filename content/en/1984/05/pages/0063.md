The problem can be reformulated as follows: prove that the points of the plane $a+b+c=1$ are no less than $1/\sqrt{3}$ away from the origin. Using the formula for the distance from a point to a plane (see § 62 of the textbook “Geometry 9-10”), we find that the distance from the origin of coordinates to the plane in question is $1/\sqrt{3}$. 6) Additional (compared to a)) conditions mean that the coordinates of points $(a; b; c)$ satisfy the inequalities $a<b+c, b<a+c$ and $c<a+b$, which define half-spaces. The intersection of these half-spaces cuts out a triangle MNP from the midlines (Fig. 12 6) on the plane $a+b+c=1$. Since the vertices of this triangle, points $M, N$ and $P$, are maximally distant from the origin, compared to the remaining points of this triangle, and $|OM|=|ON|=|OP|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, the inequality being proved holds.
