Inversion
Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
V. M. UROEV

In this article we talked about one remarkable geometric transformation of the plane. This transformation - inversion - allows you to solve problems that are very difficult to even approach at first glance.

§ 1. What is inversion

Suppose a circle C of radius R with center O is given on the plane α. The inverse of the plane α with respect to this circle is called a mapping in which each point A of the plane α, with the exception of point O, is associated with a point A' lying on the ray (OA) such that

$$|OA'| = \frac{R^2}{|OA|}.$$  (1)

From this definition it is clear that during inversion, points A and A' change places, mapping into each other. Center O of circle C, called the center of inversion, has no image, and no point during inversion falls into it (Fig. 1). The interior of a circle bounded by circle C is transferred by inversion to the exterior region, since from the inequality |OA| < R it follows that |OA'| > R, and vice versa, the outer area is mapped to the inner one.

The points of circle C remain in place during inversion, and from the fact that the inside of the circle goes into the outside, it follows that there are no other fixed points.
