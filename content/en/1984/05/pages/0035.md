Bernoulli's law

The textbook “Physics 8” talks about the dependence of the pressure $p$ of a fluid on the speed $v$ of its movement. Let us derive a formula connecting these quantities quantitatively.

We will assume that the pipe through which the liquid flows has a wide and a narrow section. The pipe is located horizontally, so the potential energy of the liquid in the gravity field does not change during the flow. Let us select the volume of fluid limited by sections $S_1$ and $S_2$, where the fluid velocity is $v_1$ and $v_2$, respectively (see figure). This volume will move to the right as the liquid flows. After a period of time $\Delta t$ it will take a position between sections $S'_1$ and $S'_2$, shown in figure by dashed lines.

Since the fluid is incompressible, the decrease in fluid volume in a wide section of the pipe is equal to its increase in a narrow section:

$$S_1v_1\Delta t = S_2v_2\Delta t,$$ or $S_1v_1 = S_2v_2$.

But what does it mean that a liquid is incompressible? When they say this, they only mean that the changes in the volume of the liquid are relatively small. Liquids always have slight deformations, and it is they that lead to the emergence of pressure forces - elastic forces of liquid rolling.

The selected volume of liquid is subject to pressure forces from two sides - from the liquid following it, and from the liquid in front. When moving this volume, pressure forces do work. According to the kinetic energy theorem, the total work done by pressure forces is equal to the change in kinetic energy of the fluid:

$$A = E_{k2} - E_{k1}.$$

Let's calculate the work $A$ of pressure forces. In section $S_1$, the fluid pressure is $p_1$, and the pressure force is $F_1 = p_1S_1$. During the time $\Delta t$ this section moves to the right at a distance $v_1\Delta t$, and, therefore, the force $F_1$, directed along the displacement, does work

$$A_1 = F_1v_1\Delta t = p_1S_1v_1\Delta t.$$

In section $S_2$ there is a pressure force $\vec{F}_2(F_2 = p_2S_2)$, directed opposite to the displacement. The work done by this force is negative:

$$A_2 = -F_2v_2\Delta t = -p_2S_2v_2\Delta t.$$

Total work of pressure forces

$$A = A_1 + A_2 = (p_1v_1S_1 - p_2v_2S_2)\Delta t.$$

This work is equal to the equation for the kinetic energy of the liquid.

Now let’s find the difference between the kinetic energies of the selected volume of liquid before and after movement. It is obvious that the energy of the fluid between sections $S'_1$ and $S_2$ does not change. Therefore, the total change in energy will be the same as if part of the fluid contained between sections $S_1$ and $S'_1$ took the place of the fluid contained between sections $S_2$ and $S'_2$.

The kinetic energy of the liquid mass $m_1$ flowing in time $\Delta t$ through section $S_1$ (that is, the liquid in the volume between sections $S_1$ and $S'_1$) is equal to

$$E_{k1} = \frac{m_1v_1^2}{2} = \varrho v_1\Delta t \cdot S_1 \frac{v_1^2}{2},$$

where $\varrho$ is the density of the liquid. For section $S_2$ the kinetic energy of the corresponding one is equal to

$$E_{k2} = \varrho v_2\Delta t \cdot S_2 \frac{v_2^2}{2}.$$

Consequently, during the time $\Delta t$ the kinetic energy of the allocated volume of liquid changes by the following values:

$$E_{k2} - E_{k1} = (\varrho v_2S_2 \frac{v_2^2}{2} - \varrho v_1S_1 \frac{v_1^2}{2})\Delta t.$$
