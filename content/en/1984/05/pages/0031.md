Task 1. If, during inversion with respect to a certain circle $C$, the points $A$ and $B$ of the circle $T$ transform into each other, then the circle $T$ transforms into itself (see Fig. 5).

Solution. The center of inversion $O$ must lie on the straight line $AB$, which transforms into itself, and $OA \cdot OB = R^2$, where $R$ is the radius of the circle $C$. Let us draw a tangent $OD$ to the circle $T$. By the proven theorem $|OA| \cdot |OB| = |OD|^2$. So, $|OD| = R$. Let us now draw an arbitrary secant $OK$ to the circle $T$. By the theorem about the square of the tangent $|OK| \cdot |OL| = |OD|^2 = R^2$, and, therefore, the points $K$ and $L$ transform into each other, and therefore the circle $T$ transforms into itself.

Task 2. Let $A'$ and $B'$ be points that are symmetrical points $A$ and $B$ with respect to a circle of radius $R$ with center $O$. Prove that $|A'B'| = \frac{R^2 \cdot |AB|}{|OA| \cdot |OB|}$.

Note. Use the similar triangles $OAB$ and $OA'B'$.

Task 3. Lines $MA$ and $MB$ touch circle $C$ at points $A$ and $B$, point $D$ is the midpoint of chord $AB$. Prove that the points $D$ and $M$ are symmetrical with respect to the circle $C$ (Fig. 6).

Proof. $\triangle OAD \sim \triangle OAM$. Therefore $\frac{|OA|}{|OM|} = \frac{|OD|}{|OA|}$. It immediately follows that $|OA|^2 = R^2 = |OM| \cdot |OD|$.

Task 4 (Polemeus' theorem). Prove that in any cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$ the product of the lengths of the diagonals is equal to the sum of the products of the lengths of opposite sides (Fig. 7):

$$|AC| \cdot |BD| = |AB| \cdot |CD| + |BC| \cdot |AD|.$$

Proof. Let's perform an inversion centered at vertex $A$. Then the circumscribed circle $T$ will go to the line $T'$, the vertices $B, C, D$ - respectively, to the points $B', C', D'$ on the line $T'$. Let us denote $R$ as the radius of the circumscribed circle. Turning to Problem 2, we get

$$|C'B'| = \frac{R^2 \cdot |BC|}{|AB| \cdot |AC|},$$

$$|B'D'| = \frac{|BD| \cdot R^2}{|AD| \cdot |AB|},$$

$$|C'D'| = \frac{R^2 \cdot |CD|}{|AC| \cdot |AD|}$$

From this it is clear that the statement of Polemaeus’ theorem expresses the following fact: $|B'D'| = |B'C'| + |C'D'|$. Indeed, substituting the found values ​​of $|C'B'|, |B'D'|, |C'D'|$ into this equality and bringing them to a common denominator, we arrive at the desired correspondence.
