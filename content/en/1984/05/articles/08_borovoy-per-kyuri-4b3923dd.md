Pierre Curie

(on the 125th anniversary of his birth)

The famous French physicist, one of the founders of the doctrine of radioactivity, Pierre Curie was born on May 15, 1859.

Not many scientists were honored to have a new effect, law, or unit of measurement named after them. The name Curie appeared on the pages of physics textbooks more than once: Curie's principles, Curie's law, Curie point and simply the Curie - a unit of measurement of radioactivity.

These names correspond to the scientist's major discoveries. So, many people probably know that one of the ways to demagnetize a permanent magnet is to heat it up very much. The temperature at which demagnetization occurs is called the Curie point. The laws of behavior of magnetic bodies at different temperatures were discovered by the scientist and described in his doctoral dissertation.

There is another discovery that was made by Pierre Curie together with his older brother Jacques. This discovery is piezoelectric. When some crystals (for example, quartz) are deformed, electric charges of opposite sign appear on their facets. The Curie brothers were the first to experimentally discover this effect, as well as its opposite - with the help of a piezoelectric field it is deformed in an electronic field. Now this phenomenon is used in a variety of fields of science and technology, from radio engineering to precision watches.

In 1895, Pierre Curie married Maria Skłodowska. From this moment begins the same period of happy, difficult and unusually fruitful life of the Curie spouses, which brought them world fame and was tragically cut short by the death of P. Curie as a result of an accident on April 19, 1906.

Since 1897, Curie's scientific interests have focused on studying the phenomenon of radioactivity.

While studying the radioactivity of uranium salts, Marie Curie discovered that their study was much more powerful than the study of pure uranium. Then she suggested that the salts contained an as yet unknown radioactive substance. Thus began work that led to the discovery of two new elements - polonium and radium.

It took four years to separate tenths of a gram of uranium ore waste from water. The main work was carried out by scientists using their own meager savings in an old wooden barn, without any equipment. Household activities that emit harmful gases have to be done in the yard.

The Curies could have become rich people if they had patented the method of obtaining sake, but they published the results of their research in full, without seeking to derive any material benefit from them. Any interested person received similar information.

A major physicist, a world-renowned scientist, a Nobel Prize laureate (together with M. Curie), P. Curie did not achieve what he dreamed of—a good laboratory for his research. When in 1903 they wanted to award him the Order of the Legion of Honor, he wrote in a letter: “I ask you to tell Mr. Minister that I have no need for an order, but I really need a laboratory.”

His wife said it best about Pierre Curie: “Believing only in the peaceful power of science and reason, he lived to seek the truth.”

A. A. Borovoy
