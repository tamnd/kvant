"BERMUDA TRIANGLE"
IN THE CENTER OF THE BOARD

Last year we talked about the success of the Soviet grandmasters G. Kasparov and V. Smyslov in the quarter-final matches of the contenders. The recipients also performed confidently.

V. Korchoi - G. Kasparov
This situation arose in the sixth game of the match. White made the natural move 63.d5 - after all, in the endgame the pawn must move forward. If now 63...g2?, then 64. Rd4+ Kf3 65. Jld1 g1Q 66. Rg1 Rg1 67. d6 with a draw. However, an intermediate check 63...Rg6+! destroyed the whites' hope. Only after 64. Kreg 7 (64. Kf7 g2 65. Rd1 Dg6 with the inevitable threat of 66... ​​Rd5) the pawn moved - 64... g2 65. Rd1 Kreg 66. d6 Re6+ 67. Rd7 Dg6+ 68. Rd6 g1Q. The queen easily copes with the rooks, and White is soon done.

It turns out that in the position on the diagram White could save himself with 63. Rd1! (This was first discovered by the chess master and candidate of chemical sciences S. Rosenberg.) The twist of the rook’s maneuver is that the black king does not yet have access to the e5 square - the “d” pawn retained control over it. To get to this square, the king will have to move along the triangle f4-e4-e5. In pawn endings, the king moving around the triangle often ensures victory. In this case, on the oborles flank. Now he applies the same idea on the queenside, but with much less effectiveness. 7. a4 b6 8. Kfd2! This move by White, typical of closed positions, looks unconventional here. White is not bothered by the fact that many of his pieces have not yet entered the battle, and they make a second move with the same knight. 8... e5. After 8... Bb7 9. C:b7 Rg7 the rook on b7 looks unsightly, but in the end it will still work out on this square. 9. K:c4 ed10. Q:d4 Cc5 11. Q:d3 0—0 12. Kc3. The second horse joined the game. Black no longer wants to tolerate the white bishop on the large diagonal and places it. 12...Bb7 13. Cxb7 Rg7 14. Qf3 Q8. So, the rook has moved along the triangle a8-b8-b7, and it is not natural for the black pieces to be seen with the naked eye. Kasparov coolly and gracefully turns his weight into the window. 15. Bf4 a6 16. e4 Rya7 17. Kd5 b5 18. Ka5 ba 19. Rfc1 Cd4 20. R:a4 C:b2 21. Ke7+Kph8. 22.Jlc2 Qe8 23. R:b2 Q:e7 24.Kc6 Qc5 25.K:a7 Q:a7 26.e5 Kg8 27.Ce3 Qa8 28.Q:a8 R:a8 29.f4 Ke7 30.Jld2. The blacks dealt.

Let us now present the seventh game of another match. In it, things got along without geometry.

V. Smyslov - 3. Ribble
Queen's Tambit
1. d4 Kf6 2.Kf3 e6 3.c4 d5 4.Kc3 c5 5.cd K:d5 6.e3 Kc6 7.Cd3 Ce7 8.0—0 0—0 9.a3 cd 10.ed Cf6 11.Ce4 Kce7 12.Ke5 g6 13. Ch6 Cg7 14.C:g7 Kp:g7 15.Jlc1 b6 16.K:d5. In a position with an isolated tail, White's exchanges, as a rule, are included in Black's lans. Thus, White seems to be meeting his opponent's wishes. But don't rush to conclusions. 16...K:d5 17.C:d5 18.Jlc7 Cb7 19.Fg4. The white rook has penetrated the seventh horizon, and this decides the outcome of the duel. 19...Jad8 20.Jld1 a5 21.h4 Jlc8 22.Jld7 Fe4 23.Fg5 Cc6 24.f3 Qf5 25.Jla7 Ca4 26.Jle1 Rlc2 27.b4 Cb3 28.ba ba 29.Jle4 h6 30.Fe3 Jlb2 31.Jlg4 g5 32.hg h5.

The actions of the whites of the present are clear and understandable, so no comments are required. In a strong ceyton, Rieble came up with a way to aggravate the situation, but the logic of Smyslov’s game achieves complete triumph. 33.Jlg3 h4 34.Jlg4 h3 35.g6 h2+ 36.Kp:h2 Jlh8+ 37.Kpg3 Jl:g2+ 38.Kp:g2 Qc2+ 39.Ff2 Jlh2+ 40.Kp:h2 Q:f2+ 41.Kph3 Qf1+ 42.Jlg2. Here the game was postponed, and Black still had hope of a perpetual check. However, Rieble resigned without further play, having convinced himself during the analysis that the maximum he could count on was two or three checks... Black has no defense against the cold-hearted g6:f7, Ra8 and f8Q.

After this victory for Smyslov, the fate of the match was a practical decision - all remaining meetings ended in a draw and Smyslov won the match with a score of 6.5:4.5.
