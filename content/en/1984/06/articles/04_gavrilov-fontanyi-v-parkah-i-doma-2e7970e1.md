Fountains in parks and houses

S. L. GAVRILOV

The ancestor of the modern fountain appeared a long time ago - when early man lined the spring with stones. The water poured out like shavings, and it became convenient to fill his earthenware and wooden dishes. Over the millennia, stone cladding has acquired a natural meaning. Such works of art appeared as the fountains of Ancient Greece, Rome, and then Versailles and Petrodvorets (Petrodrofos). But until the end of the century, the utilitarian purpose of the fountains was preserved - the population took water from them. This is understandable: the word “fountain” itself comes from the Latin uns - source.

Russian architecture paid great attention to the appearance of fountains. Since the 18th century, fountains in Russia have been an integral part of palaces and park architecture. The most striking example of this is the fountains of Petrodvorets, the country residence of the Russian tsars, founded by Peter I in 1709 (see photo above). Such famous architects as Rastrelli, Quarenghi and others took part in its stropelva. The hydraulic engineering work was supervised by engineer Tuvolkov.

Here is what the Russian artist Alexander Benois wrote about Petrodvorets: “This is the residence of the king of the seas. Fountains in Peterhof are not an appendage, but the main thing. They are a symbolic expression of the watery kingdom, a cloud of spray from the sea that splashes off the shores of Peterhof.”

It is noteworthy that due to the favorable location of the palace grounds, no water-lifting structures had to be built to feed the fountains. Water was collected in the surrounding rivers and flowed through an extensive pipeline to Petrodvorets. And the pressure necessary for the fountains to operate was created due to the natural difference in levels.

Western European architects also showed continued interest in fountains. Since the era of the Renaissance, many beautiful fountains have been built in Europe, which still make a lasting impression. But the most outstanding monument of landscape architecture using fountains is Versailles, the residence of French queens, built in the second half of the 17th century according to the design of the architect Andre Le Nôtre.
