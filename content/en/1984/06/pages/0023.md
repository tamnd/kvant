Let now $M$ be a point of rank $r > 1$ (with respect to the center $O$). From the definition of rank given in the condition, it follows that, moving along a certain path from the point $M$ to $O$, we intersect exactly $r - 1$ drawn straight lines - the bisector perpendiculars of some segments $OP_1, OP_2, \dots, OP_{r-1}$. (This path can be constructed like this: first we go along a straight line segment from point $M$ to any border point of the region $D_r(O)$ and $D_{r-1}(O)$, then along another segment to the border point of the region $D_{r-1}(O)$ and $D_{r-2}(O)$, etc. Having reached the region $D_1(O) = D(O)$, we follow a straight line segment we go to point $O$. This means that there are $r - 1$ nodes $P_1, \dots, P_{r-1}$, to which $M$ is closer than to $O$. The lemma is proven.

Now we apply the lemma to analyze our observations.

$1^\circ$. Let the lattice be such that when transferred to the vector $\overrightarrow{OQ}$; where $O$ and $Q$ are any of its nodes, the entire lattice is combined with itself. (This condition is satisfied by a square lattice, a “triangular” lattice on figure 1, and in general any lattice of the ends of vectors $m \cdot \overrightarrow{OA} + n \cdot \overrightarrow{OB}$, where $OAB$ is a fixed triangle, and $m$ and $n$ are arbitrary integers.) Below we will call only parallel translations to vectors “translations”. $\overrightarrow{OQ}$. Let us prove that for any internal point $M$ of the “Dirichlet domain” $D(O)$ there is a transfer $T$ such that $T(M) \in D_r(O)$, and if $T(M)$ lies inside the domain $D_r(O)$, then there is exactly one such transfer $T$. In other words, the region $D(O)$ is divided into pieces, the transfers $T$ of which form the region $D_r(O)$. Let $M$ be a point such that $r - m$ is a node $Q$ in distance from it. By the key lemma $M \in D(O) \cap D_rQ$, and therefore when we transfer $T$ to the vector $\overrightarrow{OQ}$ we get that $T(M) \in D_r(O)$. The transfer $T$ is uniquely defined if there is no lattice node $X$ for which $|MQ| = |MX|$.
