Let us now explain why it follows that $D_r(Q)$ are equal in size for different $r$. Let us set the following limit for a given lattice by “density”: $\alpha = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{K(N)}{N^2}$, where $K(n)$ is the number of nodes lying in a square of size $N \times N$ with center $O$. Let us prove that $\alpha = \frac{1}{S_r}(S_r - \text{плошадь области } D_r(Q))$ for each

dogo $r$. Let $L_r$ be the closest distance of point $D_r(O)$ from $O$, $N > L_r$. Then for all $K(N)$ nodes $Q$ of square $N \times N$ the union of area $D_r(Q)$ covers square $(N - L_r) \times (N - L_r)$ and is contained in square $(N + L_r) \times (N + L_r)$, so

$$\left(1 - \frac{L_r}{N}\right)^2 = \frac{(N - L_r)^2}{N^2} \leqslant \frac{S_r \cdot K(N)}{N^2} \leqslant \frac{(N + L_r)^2}{N^2} = \left(1 + \frac{L_r}{N}\right)^2.$$

At $N \to \infty$ the right and left sides are as close to 1 as possible, so there is a limit $\lim_{N \to \infty} = \frac{S_r \cdot K(N)}{N^2} = 1$, so $\alpha = \frac{1}{S_r}$; the value $\frac{1}{\alpha}$ is the “average area per node”, in particular, the area $S_1$ of the region $D(O)$ is also equal to $\frac{1}{\alpha}$.
