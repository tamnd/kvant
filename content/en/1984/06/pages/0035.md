Tasks

M866—M870; F878—F882

M866. a) In all cells of a 20x20 square there is one soldier. For each maximum d, can the soldiers be rearranged into other cells so that each moves a distance of at least d? (The distance varies along a straight line between the centers of the old and new cells; the side of the cell is 1.)
Solve the same problem
6) for a square 21×21;
c) for direct shedding of m×n cells.

S. S. Krotov

M867. At a dance lesson, 17 boys and 17 girls lined up in two parallel rows to form 17 pairs. Moreover, in each pair, the height of the boy differed from the height of the girls by no more than ten centimeters. Prove that if in each row the boys and girls are overgrown in height, then in each pair the boy and girl will still differ in height by no more than ten centimeters.

A. G. Pechkovsky

M868. Heights are drawn from the vertices of the base of the tetrahedron to the lateral faces. Prove that three straight lines connecting the bases of the heights in each face are parallel to the same plane. (Plane angles at a vertex are not right angles.)

I. F. Sharyzin

M869*. Pairs of consecutive natural numbers (8, 9); (288, 289) have the property that each of these numbers contains any prime factor of at least the second power.
a) Find another such pair of consecutive numbers.
6) Prove that there are an infinite number of such pairs.

A. V. Anzhans

M870. On one side of the endless corridor there is an infinite number of rooms, numbered in order by integers, and on each side there is a piano. A small number of panists live in these rooms. (Several panists can live in one room.) Every day, some two panists living in neighboring rooms - the kth and (k+1)th - come to the conclusion that they are interfering with each other, and move to the (k−1)th and (k+2)th rooms, respectively. Prove that after a finite number of days these migrations will stop.

V. G. Iltsev
