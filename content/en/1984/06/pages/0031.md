Let us illustrate the application of Lemma a) -6) by answering the following question. Is the number 97531 a square or a cube of an integer? No. It is not a square, if only because it is equal to 3 modulum 4, and is not a cube, since it is equal to 7 modulum 9 (since the sum of its digits is 25). Example 3. Prove that the sum of the squares of five consecutive integers is not the square of an integer.

Solution. Let's try to reason by modulum 3. If the first of the numbers is divisible by 3, then their squares form, by modulum 3, the sequence 0,1,1,1,0,1 and therefore their sum is equal to 0 by modulum 3. We did not get a contradiction: the square of an integer can be equal to 0 by modulum 3. Well, this means that we chose the modul poorly. Let's try to reason according to modulum 4. It is easy to see that in this case, depending on whether the first number is even or odd, we end up with either the sequence 0,1,0,1,0 with a sum of 2, or the sequence 1,0,1,0,1 with a sum of 3. But the result of Lemma 6) says that the square of an integer cannot equal either 2 or 3 modulum 4.

Example 4. Prove that the number 1^1+2^2+3^3+...+1983^{1983} cannot be represented as m^k, where m and k are natural numbers, k ≥ 2.

Solution. Let's start with reasoning according to modulum 3. Since any integer is equal to 0.1 or -1 modulum 3, then a^n is equal to a^2 modulum 3 for even n and a^n is equal to a modulum 3 for odd n. From this we can deduce that the numbers 1^1, 2^2, 3^3, ... form, modulum 3, a periodic sequence with a period of length 6, consisting of the numbers 1, 1, 0, 1, -1, 0. Using this periodicity, it is easy to show that this sum is equal to 2 modulum 3 and, therefore, cannot be a square. But how can we show that this sum cannot be a cube, the fifth power, etc.?

The necessary help is provided by modulum 4. According to this modulum, the numbers 1^1, 2^2, 3^3, ... also form a periodic sequence with a period of 1.0, -1, 0 of length 4, which means that the sum 1^1+2^2+3^3+...+1983^{1983} is divisible by 4. Therefore, if this sum had the form m^k, where k ≥ 3, then it would be divisible by 8. Let us examine it modulum 8. If n is even and n ≠ 2, then n^k is divisible by 8, and if n is odd, then n^n is equal to n modulum 8 (see Lemma e)), so this sum is equal modulum 8 to the sum 2^2+1+3+...+1983. But this amount is not divisible by 8 (it is equal to 984068).

Example 5. Two sequences of integers. (x_n) and (y_n) are constructed so that x_1=x_2=10, y_1=y_2=-10, x_n+2=(x_n+1)+1)x_n+1, y_n+2=(y_n+1)+1)y_n+1 for all natural n. Is the equality x_k=y_l possible for some k and l?

Solution. Let's try to choose modulum m in such a way that the sequences are constant according to this modulum, that is, equal to 10 and -10, respectively. If x_n, x_n+1 and x_n+2 are equal to 10 modulum m, then the numbers 10 and (10+1)10+1=111 are equal modulum m. Therefore, as m we need to take the number 101. It is easy to see that by this modulum the sequence (y_n) also turns out to be constant and equal to -10. But 10 and -10 are not equal modulum 101. Therefore, the equality x_k=y_l is impossible.
