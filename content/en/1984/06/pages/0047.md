Post scientific history

The Great Laplace was present at a meeting of the Paris Academy of Sciences on September 18, 1820, when Amler first demonstrated the electrodynamic interaction of currents - when current is passed through two conductors, they are mutually displaced. They said that after the end of the lecture, when the audience, full of impressed, was already leaving, Laplace stopped at the door, waiting for Daniel Colladon, who was assisting Ampère. When he approached, Laplace, distinguished by his small character and suspiciousness, patted Colladon on the shoulder and, looking intently into his eyes, said: “Now, young man, admit it! After all, it was you who pushed the wire.”
(Based on materials from Liushchia M. History of Physics. M.: Mir, 1970.)

The story told by I. K. Kikon

This happened at the end of the 20s in Leningrad. The film studio, together with a group of physicists, was tasked with making a popular science film about physics. In the group of physicists was Isaac Konstantinovich Kikoin, then still a young employee of the RENT Physics and Technology Institute.

According to the scenario, it was necessary to take away the experience with the repulsion of like charges. At the rehearsal, I took a celluloid ball, brought an electrified ebonite stick to it, and the ball bounced off properly. After that we started filming. Motor, camera... bring the stick to the ball - no effect.

Then the script was changed a little. The laboratory assistant held the ball on a string, brought a stick to it, and I.K. Kikoin stood nearby and imperceptibly pushed the ball. The shoot went brilliantly.

Why didn’t such a simple “real” experience happen? The fact is that in those years, powerful lamps were used for lighting during photography, emitting ultraviolet rays, which, by ionizing the effect, make it conductive. It is clear that as soon as the lamps were turned on, the charges from the ball and stick quickly drained through the conductive effect.
