Quite a long time ago it was noticed that geomagnetic opportunities and bright auroras were more frequent and stronger during the maximum of the solar cycle. Most of the increases can be associated with the passage of spots across the solar disk or with powerful flares. But at the same time, exactly the same spots pass across the disk without causing geomagnetic possibilities; There are strong outbreaks that have no consequences on Earth. Therefore, one has to conclude that sunspots and flares themselves are not directly related to geomagnetic capabilities. But there is one constant effect that indicates the presence of streams of charged particles moving from the Sun. This is a trend of magnetic storms repeating with a period of about 27 days. This number is close to the rotation period of the Sun. It follows that the solar “agent” that causes them spreads in the form of a limited path following the solar rotation. Interestingly, the 27-day repeatability is more pronounced during the minimum solar activity, when the “spotlight” is small. These thin-walled regions of the Sun, causing geomagnetic possibilities, are called M-regions, and for a long time they could not be deposited with any specific formations on the Sun. The situation has become somewhat clearer recently, when so-called coronal holes were discovered. This name was given to regions of the solar corona with exceptionally low density and temperature. These areas are characterized by low levels of radiation in the extreme ultraviolet, RENTEN microwave ranges.

It is assumed that the presence is related to the structure of the solar magnetic field - in the vicinity of the hole the field is directed in such a way that it does not have a noticeable effect on the movement of charged particles of the coronal gas, and it is from this region that the most powerful streams of charged particles rush into outer space.
