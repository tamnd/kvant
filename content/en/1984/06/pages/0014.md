These lines are taken from the wonderful poem by Semyon Kirsanov, “The Sun Before Calm.”
And in this passage, most of the effects studied by solar-terrestrial physics are mentioned.

Let's follow the posture and find out what “the Sun did.” But first, let's talk about the Sun itself.

I

The Sun is a star located about 33,000 light years from the center of the Galaxy, in one of its spiral arms - the Orion Arm. Solar mass $M_{\odot} = 1,99 \cdot 10^{30}$ kg; radius $R_{\odot} = 6,96 \cdot 10^{5}$ km, and the average density of the Sun's matter $q_{\odot} = 1,41 \cdot 10^{3}$ kg/m$^3$, that is, 4 times less than the average density of the Earth's matter. And the force of gravity on the Sun is 28 times greater than on Earth.

These properties - low average density and enormous gravitational force - are most important for understanding what happens inside the Sun. A study of the radiation spectrum of the Sun showed that the substance of the Sun consists of the same chemical elements that are found on Earth.*) The temperature inside the Sun is so high that substances cannot exist there in solid and liquid states, that is, the Sun is a hot ball of gas. The temperature in the interior of the Sun, according to estimates, should be higher than $10^7$ K. At such temperatures, the gas is completely ionized and turns into plasma - a substance consisting of isons and electrons. So the Sun is a hot plasma ball. Such a high temperature is maintained by searching for thermonuclear reactions from the depths of the Sun.

The thermal energy released in reactions is radiated in all directions, and part of it falls on the Earth.

The outer part of the Sun is called the solar atmosphere. Based on physical features - mainly, different values ​​of density and temperature - three layers are distinguished in the solar atmosphere: the photosphere, the chromosphere and the corona.

*) Let us recall that the gel element was first discovered when studying the spectrum of sunlight, hence its name.
