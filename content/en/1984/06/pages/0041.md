M855. Is it possible to pierce a rigid regular tetrahedron with edge 1 through a hoop of diameter a) 1; b) 0.95; c) 0.9; e) 0.85?

a) Answer: yes. To do this, we position the tetrahedron so that the straight line passing through the midpoints of its two intersecting edges is perpendicular to the plane of the circle and passes through its center (Fig. 1).

6) Answer: you can. Let us show that a tetrahedron can be passed through a circle of diameter $3/\sqrt{11} \approx 0,905 < 0,95$; this is the diameter of the circle passing through the vertex of the tetrahedron and the midpoints of the two edges of the base.

Let us place such a circle on the vertex $A$ of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ and advance it so that it passes through the vertex $B$ and the midpoints $M$ and $N$ of the edges $AC$ and $AD$ (Fig. 2). After this, we will rotate the circle around the axis $MN$ by the vertex $B$. The plane of the circle will intersect the tetrahedron along the trapezoid $MNL_1K_1$. The diameter of the circle circumscribed about this trapezoid is $|NK_1|/\sin NMK_1$; it is easy to calculate that during rotation it decreases ($|NK_1|$ decreases, and $\sin NMK_1$ increases). Therefore, the circle can be rotated freely until it falls into the plane $MNLK$, where $K$ and $L$ are the midpoints of the edges $BC$ and $BD$. Now it can be removed from the tetrahedron through edge $CD$ in the same way as we put it on through $AB$ (the axis of rotation is similar to $MN$, in this case the straight line $MK$ or $NL$ will be).
