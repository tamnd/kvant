F862. A lens with focal length $F$ and diameter $d$ is inserted into a hole of the same diameter in a screen made of light strip material $(d/F<2)$. The screen is positioned so that the optical axis of the line coincides with the bisector plane and is perpendicular to the edge of the dihedral right angle (Fig. 1). The edges are in the middle. The consideration from the edge of the el to the focus of the line is $a(a>F)$. The screen is illuminated by a wide beam parallel to the line axis. What fraction of the light beam reflected from the mirror will come out and pass through the line? At what distance from the line do these beams produce an image?

A beam of rays passing through the lens is collected at its focus (Fig. 2). Rays, indices of focus, fall on the mirrors at obtuse angles (since $d/F<2$). Therefore, after the first reflection from one of the mirrors, the rays fall on the second mirror. After reflection from two mirrors, all rays (all light flux) fall on a screen with a lens.

For a lens, the source of these rays is the minimum image $S$ of the lens focus, created by the mirror faces of the angle at a distance $2a+F$ from the lens. Only rays lying inside the solid angle $\Omega$ pass out through the lenses (at figure 2 the rays limiting the outgoing beam are shown).

The fraction $k$ of the luminous flux reflected from the mirrors and re-passing through the lens is equal to the relative luminous flux propagating the internal solid angle $\Omega$ to the luminous flux reflected from the mirrors. But the luminous flux propagating the internal solid angle $\Omega$ is equal to the luminous flux entering the lens of the internal beam of diameter $\delta$ (Fig. 2), and the luminous flux reflected from the mirrors is equal to the luminous flux entering the lens, that is, the luminous flux of the internal beam.
