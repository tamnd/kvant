GAMES AND PUZZLES

The blue 24-facet, shown at figure on the right, was discovered by the astronomer and mathematician Judan Kepler in 1619. He called it Stella octangula (octagonal star) due to the fact that its 24 faces are located on 8 planes. Looking at it carefully, you can see that Kepler's Stella octangula is a union of two intersecting regular tetrahedra, the vertices of which form a cube.

The Kepler star polyhedron can be cut into four equal parts (left at figure) and made into an interesting puzzle. Each part is cut out of thin cardboard or thick paper according to the pattern shown in figure below, and glued together. The puzzle is to assemble a Kepler polyhedron from four such pieces. Igor Glitskov from Obkinsk sent it to us.
