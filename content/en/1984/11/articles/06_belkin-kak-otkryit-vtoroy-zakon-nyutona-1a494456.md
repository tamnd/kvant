How to “discover” Newton’s second law?

Newton, in his famous work “Mathematical Principles of Natural Physiophy”, defines force in the following words:

“An applied force is an action performed on a body to change its state of rest or uniform linear motion.”
In other words, force is the cause of acceleration. Newton formulates the second law of motion as follows:

“The change in motion is proportional to the force applied to the movement and occurs in the direction of the straight line along which this force acts.”
The words “change in movement” undoubtedly mean a change in the speed of movement, since it is speed that is the main quantitative characteristic of movement, and the direction of the speed vector is the direction of movement. Thus, Newton's second law applies to the statement that the acceleration (which is not related to the change in speed) of a body is proportional to the force applied to it and is directed in the same way as the force.

But the word "proportional" suggests that force can be expressed numerically. Meanwhile, the above verbal definition of force does not allow us to compare force as a coefficient number. Но ясно, что второй закон Ньютона — это такой закон, который может быть получен только из опыта. What experience? Unfortunately, Newton did not consider it necessary to report this in his book. But it is not difficult to imagine what such an experience must be like.

First of all, for the experiment you need to use a force that does not depend on the body to which it is applied. This condition is satisfied, for example, by the force of urgo erasure (compressed) arrivals. It is limited only by the extension of the applied force, and with a given extension, the applied force acts on any body attached to it with the same force.

If we use the force of urgostyration for the experiment, then the relationship between force and acceleration can be established in two ways.

Method one. The urgostyling force applied in a stretched (compressed) manner is assumed to be equal to one (the standard of force). Some body is attached to the attachment and the acceleration of the body changes when it moves under the influence of a force (of course, when the body moves with an extension, the applied body should not change!). Then, to the same body, that is, to a body of the same mass, 2, 3, 4, etc. are attached. The same and similarly stretched (compressed) units are applied and the acceleration imparted to the body by forces now equal to two, trem, four, etc. units changes again.

With all these changes, the mass $m$ of the body remains the same. Therefore, there must be some quantity associated with force and acceleration, the same in all experiments. It should be equated with massela. Such an experiment would show that the ratio $F/a$ would be the same for any action of forces, so we can write

$$m = \frac{F}{a},$$ or $F = ma.$

This method requires a preliminary selection of the strength level. Then the change in acceleration makes it possible to determine the mass of the body and express it in a certain number.
