M891. A circle touches two sides of a triangle and its two medians. Prove that this triangle is equilateral.

A. A. Muratov

M892. a) Prove that among the numbers $2^m+2^k$, as well as among the numbers $3^m+3^k$, there are infinitely many squares, and among the numbers $4^m+4^k$, $5^m+5^k$ and $6^m+6^k$ there is not a single square of an integer (here $m$ and $k$ are natural numbers, $m \neq k$).

6)* Are there squares among the numbers $7^m+7^k$?

A. I. Zaichik

M893. Each two of the $n$ computer blocks are connected by a wire. Is it possible to cover each of these wires in one of $n-1$ colors so that $n-1$ wires of different colors come from each block, if $n=6$; 6) $n=13$?

V. B. Alekseev

M894. a) The sum of the heel of non-negative numbers is 1. Prove that they can be arranged in a circle so that the sum of the heel of pairwise products of connected numbers does not exceed 1/5.

6)* There are $n \geqslant 4$ non-negative numbers placed around a circle, the sum of which is equal to 1. Prove that the sum of all $n$ pairwise products of connected numbers does not exceed 1/4.

S. B. Gashkov, A. N. Dranitsnikov

M895*. Prove that the cross-sectional area of ​​a cube by a plane tangent to the sphere inscribed in it does not exceed half the area of ​​the cube. Unwind the case when this section is a) a triangle, 6) a quadrilateral. C) Prove that in case a) the total surface area of ​​the tetrahedron cut off from the cube is less than the area of ​​the cube.

S. B. Gashkov, I. F. Sharytsin

F903. On the upper generatrix of the main cylinder of radius $R$, the axis of which is inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizon, a flexible weightless rope of length $l$ (Fig. 1) is fixed. A small weight is attached to the other end of the rope. Define:
1) the length of the hanging part of the rope in the load equilibrium position;
2) the period of small vibrations of the load in a vertical plane parallel to the cylinder axis.

S. S. Krotov

F904. A sphere of radius $R$, composed of two equal-density fitted thin-walled hemispheres of mass $m$ each, is filled with a liquid with density $\rho$.
1) What vertical force must be applied to the lower hemisphere so that the liquid does not spill out, if the upper sphere is fixed (Fig. 2)?
2) With what force should the left and right hemispheres (Fig. 3) be compressed so that the liquid does not spill out?

L. G. Markovich

F905. A thin-walled gas-filled cylinder of mass $M$ with a height $H$ and a base area $S$ floats in water (Fig. 4). As a result of the loss of thermal in the lower part of the cylinder, its immersion depth increased by $\Delta H$. What was the initial gas pressure in the cylinder? Atmospheric pressure $p$, temperature does not change.

O. Yu. Nikshina

F906. A conductor whose resistance depends on temperature is connected to a constant voltage source $U$. The resistance of the conductor changes depending on the temperature according to the law $R = R_0(1 - \mu t)$, where $R_0$ is the resistance at $t = 0^\circ C$, $\mu > 0$. Determine the steady-state temperature of the conductor if the ambient temperature is $0^\circ C$, and the thermal power released by the conductor into the environment is $W = b \cdot \Delta t$, where $\Delta t$ is the temperature difference between the conductor and the environment. Neglect the change in conductor dimensions due to thermal expansion.

R. Yu. Vinokur

F907. Two metal balls of mass $m$ each with radii $r$ and $2r$ are placed in an electric field, the direction of which is directed from the large ball to the smaller one and is equal to $E$. The distance between the centers of the balls is $R_0 = 4r$. The large ball carries a charge $q\left(k \frac{q}{r^2} \ll E\right)$, the small ball is not charged. The balls are released. The time between the first and second colliding balls is $t$. Find the time between the $n$th and $(n+1)$th collisions and the distances the balls will travel between these collisions. What is the average acceleration of the balls over a sufficiently long period of time? The collisions are considered to be absolutely elastic.

A. A. LanUDEC
