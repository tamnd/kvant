In 1684, in the journal “Acta Eruditorum” (“Works of Scientists” or, as they say now, “Scientific Notes”) published in Leipzig, a seven-page article by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646-1716) appeared “A new method of maxima and minima, as well as tangents, for which neither fractional nor irrational quantities, and a special kind for this calculus." This was the first publication on differential calculus, although it appeared twenty years earlier, and the first steps were taken fifty years earlier - at the very beginning of the 17th century. However, Leibniz's article marked an important stage in the history of infinitesimal analysis and the 300th anniversary of its appearance is a natural occasion to recall the formation of this great mathematical theory.

Golden Age of Analysis

Infinitesimal analysis was created during the 17th century as a result of the collective efforts of a remarkable group of mathematicians. The main reference points were the tangent problem and the problem of calculating the areas of curved figures (the quadrature problem), which subsequently led to a correspondence to differential and integral calculus. Drawing tangents, in turn, is associated with problems about extrema (maxima and minima), inflection points, and convexity of curves. To quadratures the properties of calculating various mechanical quantities.

The development of infinitesimal analysis was closely intertwined with the creation of new mechanics. At the beginning of the century, Galileo, while studying the free fall and motion of a body thrown at an angle to the horizon, simultaneously solved the first analytical problems.
