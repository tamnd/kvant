So, Leibniz looked at the bridge only from a minor sovereign, and even then, at first, he was only the ducal librarian. Not the most enviable place for a 30-year-old scientist and politician who has not yet given up his frequent plans. But Leibniz is full of enthusiasm and dreams of the best library in the world, until the actual funds available cooled him down. He is also involved in legal activities, but they prefer to keep him busy with everyday affairs, for which he has no taste, rather than with global legal problems. Leibniz's access to diplomatic activities was very limited. Thus, he is instructed to prepare a text motivating the Duke’s right to participate in the Franco-German peace negotiations. Johann Friedrich was a catalog monarch in a Protestant state, and Leibniz wanted to take advantage of this circumstance to realize another of his cherished ideas - to separate the catalog and Protestant religion.

In 1679, a new duke, Ernst August, came to the throne, under whom things got worse. But Leibniz is full of projects, the range of which is unusually wide: improvement by class, the art of nails, hammers, improvement of carriage wheels, fishing rods, rudders, ships, literary art, firefighting, reorganization of archives, compilation of the “Code of Laws of Ernst-August”, etc. Almost not a single project found support.

Each of Leibniz's projects had ingenious insight, but he often lacked implementation. Success came when a talented practitioner took care of the finishing touches and the finishing touches. This was the case with the steam engine: “... Leibniz, scattering around himself, as always, brilliant ideas without caring whether the credit for discovering these ideas would go to him or others, Leibniz, as we now know from Papen’s correspondence (published by Hermann), suggested to him the basic idea: the use of a cylinder and a piston” (Engels) *).

The constant struggle for influence at court distracted Leibniz from mathematics for a long time. Leibniz's new turn to mathematics was stimulated by two circumstances.

Since 1682, with his support, “Scientific Notes” began to be published in Leipitz and Leibniz proposed publishing his results there. In 1683-1684, the journal published articles by Zirnhaus on quadratures, in which Leibniz reveals traces of his recent conversations with the author, without the need for references. Once Leibniz unsuccessfully tried to convince Zirnhaus of the effectiveness of calculus, now Zirnhaus himself published some results in this direction. It is very likely that Zirnhaus did not forgive that the original source of his statements were the statements of Leibniz. It happens that incomprehensible thoughts are buried deep, and after a while they arise as if they were their own.

In May 1684, Leibniz published an article criticizing Zirnhaus (without priority claims and without indicating the full name of his colleague), and in October he was published to replace him with an article, the anniversary of which is the reason for our discussion.
