Volkov (Gorky), who received the highest mark for solving this problem, defined the ratio $V_2/V_1$ as follows: using a pear, he should pour water into the smaller (first) vessel to the brim (from level $V_0$), and then pour this finished volume of water $V_1$ into the second vessel as many times as necessary until it was completely filled (from level $V_0$). The absolute winner of the 8th grade Olympiad, M. Sverdlov (Minsk), solved the problem in another way. Lowering first a large vessel into a bucket of water, and then two vessels at once (one inside the other) and holding them in a vertical position, using adhesive tape he removed the volumes of the immersed part of the large vessel (Fig. 2). From the equilibrium equation $m_2=\varrho_0V_2$ and $m_1+m_2=\varrho_0(V_1+V_2)$ we obtain $m_2/m_1=V_2/V_1$. Then, by pouring water into the larger vessel to the upper mark and pouring the volume of water $V_1$ into the empty smaller vessel, the water level in it was adjusted. Next, water from volume $V_2$ was poured in portions of $V_1$ using a smaller vessel with a mark, and these portions were counted.

In all cases, the desired ratio of vessel masses was approximately equal to the third.

Task 2. Determine the resistances of three resistors.

Equipment: battery, two resistors with a given resistance $R_1=1$ kOhm and $R_2=1,3$ kOhm, three resistors with unknown resistances $R_3$, $R_4$ and $R_5$, milliampere with unknown measurement limits, connecting wires.

The solution to the problem was essentially reduced to calibrating the milliammeter scale in ohms, connecting it to the circuit shown in figure 3. Using two reference resistances $R_1$ and $R_2$, it was possible to obtain two points on the milliammeter scale. Since the points turned out to be close to each other, the section of the scale adjacent to these points could be considered approximately linear. Using this, it is easy to find the resistance $R_3$ of an unknown resistor by connecting it in series with the first resistor and determining their total resistance $R_1+R_3$:

$$R_1+R_3=1200\text{ Ом}, \Rightarrow R_3=200\text{ Ом}.$$

Resistance $R_4$ is determined in the same way, only this resistor had to be connected in parallel with a resistor with resistance $R_2$ and resistance $R_2R_4/(R_2+R_4)$ was determined:

$$\frac{R_2R_4}{R_2+R_4}=1200\text{ Ом}, \Rightarrow R_4\approx 20\text{ Ом}.$$

Resistance $R_5$ is determined directly using the linearity of the scale around the selected area:

$$R_5=1,5\text{ КОм}.$$

Most of those who solved the problems were successful. Having measured the current strength $I_1$ and $I_2$, corresponding to resistors $R_1$ and $R_2$ (in terms of units), and taking into account that the resistance of the battery and ammeter is much less than the resistance of the eton resistors, I calculate the voltage of the battery (in terms of units), and then, using Ohm’s law, the resistances $R_1+R_3$, $R_2R_4/(R_2+R_4)$ and $R_5$, from which the resistances $R_3$ and $R_4$ were calculated.

The best results in completing this task were shown by N. Adezaryan (Yerevan), N. Matulyonis (Vilnius), G. Nikolashvili (Tbilis), A. Sannikov (Leningrad), M. Sverdlov (Minsk).
