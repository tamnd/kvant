F889. The sun is at angular height f above the horizon. At what angle α to the surface of the Earth must a body be thrown in a vertical plane passing through the Sun in order for the body to travel the longest way across the Earth?

Let us consider separately the case corresponding to different correspondences between α and φ.

1) If you throw a body at an angle α≤φ, then the path traversed by the shadow during the flight of the body will be equal to the flight distance, that is, $S_1 = \frac{1}{g} v_0^2 \sin 2\alpha$ (see Fig. 1).

For $\varphi < 45^\circ$ ($\alpha < 45^\circ$), the value of $S_1$ is greater, the larger α; therefore, it is necessary to throw the body at an angle α=φ, and then

$$S_{1\max} = \frac{1}{g} v_0^2 \sin 2\varphi. \tag{1}$$

If $\varphi \geq 45^\circ$, then α should be equal to 45°, since $S_1$ is maximum at $\alpha=45^\circ$ (in this case $S_{1\max} = \frac{1}{g} v_0^2$).

2) If you throw a body at an angle α>φ, then the shadow will travel a distance equal to (see Fig. 2)

$$S_2 = 2|OA| + S_x = 2|OA| + \frac{1}{g} v_0^2 \sin 2\alpha. \tag{2}$$

Let's find $|OA|$. To do this, we need to obtain the equation of the tangent to the parabola at point M and determine the abscissa $x_A$ of the point of intersection of the tangent with the X axis ($|x_A| = |OA|$).

To obtain the equation of the tangent AM, we define the coordinates $x_M$ and $y_M$ of the point M. The angle coefficient of the tangent is tanφ and is equal to an arbitrary function $y(x)$ describing the parabolic at the point M. It is easy to show that

$$y(x) = (tg\alpha)x - \frac{g}{2v_0^2\cos^2\alpha}x^2,$$

and therefore,

$$tg\varphi = \left((tg\alpha)x_M - \frac{g}{2v_0^2\cos^2\alpha}x_M^2\right)' = tg\alpha - \frac{g}{2v_0^2\cos^2\alpha}x_M,$$

where

$$x_M = \frac{1}{g} \left(tg\alpha - tg\varphi\right)v_0^2 \cos^2\alpha,$$

$$y_M = \frac{1}{g} tg\alpha \left(tg\alpha - tg\varphi\right)v_0^2 \cos^2\alpha -$$

$$-\frac{1}{2g} \left(tg\alpha - tg\varphi\right)^2 v_0^2 \cos^2\alpha = \frac{1}{2g} \left(tg^2\alpha - tg^2\varphi\right)v_0^2 \cos^2\alpha.$$
