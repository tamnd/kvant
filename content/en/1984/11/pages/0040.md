P906. A conductor whose resistance depends on temperature is connected to a source of constant tension $U$. The resistance of the conductor depends on temperature according to the rule $R=R_0(1-\mu t)$, where $R_0$ is the resistance at $t=0$ °C, $\mu>0$. Determine the established temperature of the conductor if the surrounding temperature is $0$ °C, while the power of heat loss to the surroundings is $W=b \cdot \Delta t$, where $\Delta t$ is the temperature difference between the conductor and the surroundings. Changes in the conductor’s size may be neglected.

P. Yu. Vinokur

P907. Two metallic balls of mass $m$ each, whose radii are $r$ and $2r$ are placed in an electric field whose tension is directed from the larger ball to the smaller one and equals $E$. The distance between the centres of the balls is $R_0=4r$. The larger ball carries the charge $q(kq/r^2 \ll E)$, the small one is uncharged. The ball are released. The time between the first and second collisions of the ball is $\tau$. Find the time between the $n$-th and $(n+1)$-st collisions and the distance covered by the balls during that time. What is their average acceleration during a sufficiently long period of time? The collisions are assumed absolutely elastic.

A. A. Lapides

Problem solutions

M876 — M878, M880*); F887 — F889

We will assume that the circle is located outside the triangle $PCD$ (see figure; the second case, when it hangs in this triangle, is considered similarly). Let $C'$ and $D'$ be the points of intersection of the sides of the angle with the tangent to the circle at the point $A$, so that (in the case under consideration) this circle hangs into the triangle $PC'D'$. Obviously, this triangle is prepared for triangle $PCD$ with center $P$, therefore, $E$ is the point of tangency of the hanging circle of triangle $PCD$ from side $CD$. Let us denote by $M, M', N$ and $N'$ the points of tangency of these two circles with lines $PC$ and $PD$. By the theorem on the equality of tangents drawn to a circle from one point,

$$2|BC| + |BE| = |BC| + |CE| = |M'C| + |CM| = |M'M|,$$

similarly,

$$2|DE| + |BE| = |N'N|,$$

by the same theorem, $|M'M| = |PM'| - |PM| = |PN'| - |PN| = |N'N|$, therefore $|BC| = |DE|$, as required.

A. S. Merkuryev
