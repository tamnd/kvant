The last years of Leibniz's life were overshadowed by a controversy with Newtom about nature. Gradually the dispute escalated into accusations against Leibniz of plagiarism. It has been hinted that he may have become acquainted with Newton's manuscripts in London. Today the independence of Leibniz's discovery seems to be proven. In London there was not enough detailed text, on his first visit Leibniz was not ready to accept Newton’s theory, there was no one who understood the calculus of the present to convey it to Leibniz. By his second visit to London, Leibniz already mastered his calculus. At first, the debate took place without the participation of Newton and Leibnitz. It is surprising that Newton, who always avoided natural disputes, this time energetically became involved in the controversy. Leibnitz probably offended him greatly, never once recognizing him as the creator of the new calculus (there have already been publications). In England they organized a real persecution of Leibniz.

Everything was further complicated by the fact that in 1714 the Duke became King George I of England. Leibnitz plans to move to London and become a royal histographer, but he is insultingly denied even a seat for the coronation (forcing him to complete the History). The fact that the king did not want to have the defeated enemy of the banner Newton in his retinue also played a role. Leibniz died in 1716. The inscription on the gravestone is very lakonin: “Leibnitz’s ashes.”

Perhaps Leibniz's scientific career would have turned out differently if he had been able to find himself an enlightened and strong patron. In his declining years, in 1711, Leibnitz met Peter I at the wedding of Tsarevch Alexei. Peter accepted Leibnitz into Russian service as a Privy Councilor of Justice, discussing with him the organization of the Academy of Sciences in St. Petersburg. But Leibniz’s plans related to Peter I, like many of his other projects, were not destined to come true. Leibnitz wrote to Peter I:

“Although I often had to act on political and legal matters, and the knowledge of princes sometimes used my advice in these matters, I still preferred science and art, since they constantly contain the glory of God and the well-being of the entire human race... science and science constitute the real treasure of the human race, or through their art, it overcomes nature and civilized peoples in differences from barbarians. Therefore, from an early age I loved science, knowledge and happy names... to make various and very important discoveries, praised in the press by impartial and famous people. I just couldn’t find a powerful sovereign who was sufficiently interested in this.”

Apparently, over the years, Leibniz’s assessments shifted: for a long time he gave priority to politics over science, but life cruelly taught him how ungrateful the position of a scientist in the palaces is. And subsequent generations will remember not so much Leibniz’s failures in the political field as his scientific achievements, which played a decisive role in the further development of science. Among such works, undoubtedly, is the work of 1684 on differential calculus.

Concluding the story about the magnetic memory of computers, we note that the ideal memory has not yet been created; the dream of science fiction writers about a magnetic crystal that remembers everything and knows everything remains a dream, but quite close to realization. From the point of view of physics, it comes down to a suitable material; from the point of view of engineers, convenient technology and an affordable price are required.

The authors understand that it is impossible to cover everything in a short article. Therefore, they want to once again remind readers that the phenomenon of magnetism, known since antiquity, still serves today as the most advanced scientific thought and perspective. His theoretical developments are far from complete, and the search for new magnetic materials is only just beginning. Magnetophysics and materials are waiting for new enthusiasts.
