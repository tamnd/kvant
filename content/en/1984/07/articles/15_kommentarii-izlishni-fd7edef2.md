⟦folio 49⟧

QUANTUM SMILES

No comments necessary

“Even in the past, Professor Gregory of the University of Edinburgh reported on the experiments of the French researcher Benoit. The latter, based on the proposal that there is some kind of “distance connection” between animals, experimented with uligs. He took 50 smiles, divided them into pairs and placed each pair separately. After some time, having marked the shells of each pair with the same letter, the researcher sent one smile from each pair to America. Then a certain smile, marked, say, with the letter A, was subjected to electric current in Paris. At the same moment, her “half”, Smile, who was in America, reacted to the current in exactly the same way.”
Mezentsev Vl. In the labyrinths of wildlife. M.: Moscow worker, 1983, p. 195.
The supernatural sensitivity of private smiles needs no comment. All that remains is to smile in confusion: how is it that engineers and scientists have not yet become interested in such a unique way of transmitting signals over long distances? After all, the discovery, judging by the note, was made more than 100 years ago. Instead of, as they say, mastering such extraordinary results from the “labyrinths of nature,” people laid expensive cables across the ocean, invented radio, launched communications satellites, and used lasers to transmit information over long distances. People were working.
Such mystical messages can simply be collected. There (p. 203) we read:
"IN. L. Durov (sign trainer - editor's note) mentally ordered the dog: “Come to the piano and hit the white key with your paw.” The dog did everything exactly. V.M. Bekhterev, who was present, tried to make a suggestion to the animal: the dog should approach one of the researchers and snatch the scarf from his hands. Bekhteev did not say a word to anyone about his plan. The suggestion was successful: the dog completed this task.”
Comments are really unnecessary.
