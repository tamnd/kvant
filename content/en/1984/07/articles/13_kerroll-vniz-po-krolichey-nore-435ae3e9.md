Harmonic series

(For the beginning, see p. 32)

Well, of course. Each of the groups, limited by numbers, for example 1/5+1/6+1/7+1/8 or 1/9+1/10+...+1/16, has a sum greater than 1/2. And there are an infinite number of these groups! The partial sums of the harmonic series become greater than 1/2+1/2+...+1/2. Taking the sum of 2n groups, we get a number greater than n. The row diverges.

* * *

This amazing fact manifests itself in the most unexpected way. For example, when solving the following problem: how, if you have n stacks, put them on top of each other so that the top one is exposed to the side at the maximum distance?

If there are only two kippichs, then they obviously need to be arranged so that the center of mass of the upper one is above the edge of the lower one: then the upper kippich will protrude to the side by 1/2 (we will take the long kippich equal to 1). The center of mass of such a pair is 1/2 away from the edge of the lower kippich. Let's place this pair on the third kippich so that its center of mass is above its edge (Fig. 1). In this case, the upper kippich will protrude to the side at a distance of 1+1/2.

Let us use the coordinate system associated with figure, the origin of which is located exactly under the edge of the lower kippich, and we will understand by the first body of the lower kippich, and by the second, a stack of (n-1) kippich placed on it. Then $x_1=1$, $x_2=0$, $m_2=(\frac{n-1}{2})m_1$, $x=\frac{1}{n}$. The statement has been proven. And along with it, the following was proven: n kippiches can be composed in such a way that the top one will extend to the side beyond the edge of the bottom one at a distance of $S_{n-1}$, where $S_n$ is the nth partial sum of the harmonic series.

The value $S_n$, as we know, grows without limit with n. So, a fairly high stack of kippiches can be bent to the side as far as is appropriate! Incredible but true! True, the values ​​of $S_n$ grow very slowly. And if we want, for example, the lateral deviation to be at least twice the length of the kippich, we need to place no less than thirty-two kippichs on top of each other.

* * *

If you calculate the partial sums of a harmonic series, bringing them to a common denominator, an interesting detail will immediately appear: firstly, the denominator of the total fraction will always be even, and secondly, all the terms in the numerator, with the exception of one single one, are also even. The first is not surprising: it is due to the presence of the 1/2 term in the harmonic series. Secondly, if it were possible to prove it for an arbitrary partial sum of a series, they would immediately call to assert that among the numbers $S_n$ there is not a single integer. Let's try to prove this.

Once again, the degree of half can serve us well, which was encountered every now and then in the harmonic series. Taking an arbitrary partial sum of a series, obtiem in it the greater half: 1/2p The denominator of all other terms of this sum contain two to powers less than p. Therefore, when bringing the entire sum to a common denominator, the numerator of the fraction 1/2p will be multiplied by an odd number, and the numerator of all other fractions will be multiplied by an even number. This justifies the statement made earlier: in the numerator of the total fraction, all terms, with the exception of one, are even; when added to the numerator, the result is an odd number, and since the denominator of any fraction is even, the fraction cannot equal a whole number.

* * *
