SCIENTISTS - GRANDMASTER OF COMPOSITION

On the “chess page” in No. 12, 1983, you became acquainted with the etyds of the international grandmaster of chess composition V. Korolkov. Now we will tell you about two more grandmasters - the ethidists. Interestingly, both of them are famous not only in the world of chess, but also in the world of science.

Vladimir Bron is a prominent Soviet scientist, Doctor of Technical Sciences, and at the same time the winner of a number of composition competitions. The next two etidas, depicted for us by the author himself, are distinguished by their novelty of meaning and are full of intense struggle.

V. Bron, 1946 WIN.

1. c4 B:c4 2. d7 Rd3 3. Bf3+! Kph 6. Cd5! Novotny's old theme - the white piece appeared at the intersection of roads along which the black rook and bishop move, and any movement leads to disaster (4.:C:d5 5. d8Q, 4.:J:d5 5. g8Q Rd2+ 6. Qg2). But another witty ethid maneuver awaits us ahead. 4.:J:d5 6. g8K+! Kpg5 7. Cc1+ Kpf5. The king will soon eat the white pawn, but the promoted knight will take three black pieces one after another - “checkers theme”. 8. Ke7+ Kpe6 9. K:d5 Kp:d7 10. K:b6+ Kpc6 11. K:c4, and White wins. A classic ethide, illustrating a sequential synthesis of it (Novotny’s theme + checkers theme).

V. Bron, 1962 WIN.

1. b6+ Kpa8 2. Ce6 Rxg7! The continuation 2...gh+ 3. Kph1 is also interesting! Rd8 4. Bb3! Kpb7 5. g8Ф L:g8 6. Cd5+! Kpc8 7. C:g8 Kpd7 8. Cd5! g4! 9. c6+ Kpe7 10. c7 C:c7 11. bc Kpd7 12. Kp:h2 Kp:c7 13. Cc4. Sketch in etide! 3. Cd5+ Rb7 4. c6 Rc7! 5. Kph1! (5. hg Ca7!!) 5... gh 6. Cg2 g4 7. Cd5 g3 8. Cg2 g5 9. Cd5. Now it all depends on which of the g pawns moves forward: 9...g2+ 10. C:g2 g4 11. Cd5 g3 12. Cg2 Ra7! 13. c7+ Rb7 14. Ce4! C:c7 15. bc Kpa7! 16. c8L! with a win; 9...g4 10. Cg2 Rf7 11. c7+ Rf3! 12. c8L! with the same result.

Grand master of composition Gna Nadarenshvili is known in Georgia not only as a wonderful ethidist, but also as a prominent doctor, the chief neurologist of Tbilisi, and the author of many scientific works. The following ethides are also processed by the composite itself.

G. Nadarenshvili, 1958. Draw.

White's pieces are scattered all over the board, but in the end they manage to gather them into a ball. 1. Kb4+ Kpa5 2. Kc6+ Kpa4. The black king ran away from one horse, and another started in pursuit. 3. Kb6+ Kpa3 4. Kc4+ Kpa2. It's the elephant's turn to act. 5. Cf3!! Q:f3 (5...Qf8+ 6. Kph7 Q:f7+ 7. Kph8 Q:c4 8. Cd5! Q:d5 9. Kb4+)

6. Kb4+ Kpa1 7. Kc2+ Kpa2 8. Kb4+. Eternal check with only knights. This opened the topic, which the author called “mkhedruli” - the Georgian horsemen’s dance.

G. Nadarenshvili, 1970. Draw.

1. Cc4 Rc5 2. Cg8!! Kpb1 3. Rb8! Rc1+ 4. Kpd2 Rc2+ 5. Kpd1 a1Q 6. R:b4+ Rb2 7. Rc4! Rc2 8. Rb4+ Rb2 9. Rc4. Positional draw.

G. Nadarenshvili, 1970. Draw.

When creating this ethid, the author was primarily interested in the possibility of the most defenseless chess character - the pawn (transformation, taken for passage, etc.).

1. e4 d4 (1...h4 leads the draw - 2. ed hg 3. d6+ Kp:d6 4. Kpb7 g2 5. a8Q g1Q 6. Qf8+ Kpe6 7. Qe7+ Kpf5 8. Q:f7) 2. e5 h4 3. e6 h3 4. e7 h2 5. e8R!! with victory. But 5. e8Q h1Q+ 6. Qe4 Qf3!; 5. e8K+ Kpd7 6. Kpb8 h1Q 7. a8Q Q:a8+ 8. Kp:a8 Kp:e8, and the h-pawn moves forward.
