Another step towards absolute zero

Candidate
physical and mathematical sciences
A. I. BUZDIN

Recently, Japanese scientists from the University of Tokyo set a new record in obtaining ultra-low temperatures. They managed to reach a temperature exceeding absolute zero by only 27 millionths of a kelvin (27 μK).

Obtaining columnar temperatures is, of course, not an end in itself. Advancement towards absolute zero allows us to discover new physical phenomena, study the behavior of unusual phases of matter and, ultimately, better understand the nature of various physical interactions.

When liquid helium was obtained at the beginning of the century and it became possible to work with temperatures on the order of several kelvins, new fundamental phenomena such as superconductivity and, somewhat later, superfluidity were discovered.*)

For a long time it was believed that the supercurrent region was only an isotope of helium with mass number four (helium-4). In the last decade, installations have been created in which it is possible to obtain temperatures of about

one thousand kelvin (1 mK), which makes it possible to observe the superfluidity of another isotope of helium - with mass number three (helium-3). It turns out that the transition of helium-3 to the supercurrent state (at a temperature close to 1 mK) has a fundamentally different character than the appearance (at $T \approx 2,2$ K) of superfluidity in helium-4. This discovery marks a new direction on the surface, which allows us to expand our understanding of low-temperature physics.

Now it’s the turn of the microkelvin temperature range. What can you expect from entering this new area? In principle, related social appeals are possible. For example, scientists are pre-paying to discover the superconductivity of a number of normal metals (gold, platinum); Moreover, it arose that this superconductivity would have a different character than that observed so far.

It should be noted that each new step towards absolute zero is associated with enormous technical and technical difficulties, since the creation of new cooling techniques is required. Let's talk a little about one of them.
