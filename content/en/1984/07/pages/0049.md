Let's consider an example of constructing the central projection of the point $A$. Point $A$, defined by its horizontal projection $a_1$, is located at a height of $h=100$ mm (Fig. 4) from the object plane.

To determine the central projection of point $A$, the following constructions are performed: through point $L_1$, a projection of the projected ray $LA$ is carried out through point $a_1$, continuing it upward beyond point $a_1$; the zero mark of the left perspective scale $O_L$ is connected to the point $a_1$ by a straight line segment $O_L a_1$; from the mark 100$L$ (left perspective scale) corresponding to the given height of the depicted point $A$ above the object plane, draw a straight line parallel to the segment $O_L a_1$ until it intersects with the projection of the projected ray $LA$.

The resulting intersection of point $A_L$ is the central projection of point $A$ for the left projection center. Similar constructions for this point $A$ are performed for the right projection center, as a result of which the position of point $A_R$ is determined - the central projection of point $A$ for the right projection center.

Further construction of stereoscopic drawings leads to finding all the characteristic points of the depicted object and connecting them to each other with lines.

A separate half-picture is constructed for each projection center. In the simplest cases, the image of both half-pictures can be drawn on one removable sheet.

In practice, a stereobasis $b$ less than normal ($b<65$ mm) is usually used. In this case, it is necessary to make a new template. On the manufactured template, place two points $L'_1$ and $R'_1$ on the segment $L_1 R_1$ symmetrically relative to its center, so that $|L'_1 R'_1|=b$. Further, all constructions are performed in exactly the same way, only with the replacement of $L_1$ by $L'_1$ and $R_1$ by $R'_1$.
