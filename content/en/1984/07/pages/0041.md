⟦folio 39⟧

Tasks

1. From point A, a raft and a boat simultaneously sailed down the river, and at the same moment a second similar boat set off to meet them from point B. When the first boat reaches point B, will the raft be closer to point A or to the second boat?

2. Non-intersecting contours are drawn on paper: 3 red and 3 blue. Figure was drawn with another sheet of paper, so that exactly one of the contours was completely covered, and all the others were partially visible. Determine the color of the covered outline.

3. How many natural numbers are there, divisible by 11, less than 100,000, whose digits add up to 11?

4. On the store window, apples and pears are laid out in the form of a triangle. In the first row there is one apple, in the second there are two pears, in the third there are three apples, etc. How many apples and how many pears were there if there were n rows?

5. Have you noticed that if you step off a well-trodden path, you can fall quite deeply into the loose snow? And at the beginning of spring, when the snow settles as it melts, the paths sometimes turn out to be even higher than the surrounding virgin snow. How can this be explained?

These tasks were suggested to us by: G. A. Galperin, V. V. Proizvolov, V. D. Vyon, Elkhan Adaev (7th grade student), A. A. Byalko

Down the rabbit hole

L. CARROLL

Many readers of “Alice in Wonderland” and “Alice Through the Looking Glass” know that Lewis Carroll is the pseudonym of the famous English mathematician Charles Dodgeon (1832-1898), the author of scientific articles and monographs on algebra. Undoubtedly, the logical and geometric fantasy of the mathematician Dodgeon will greatly influence the work of the writer Lewis Carroll. This is probably why a mathematician or physicist reading Alice is promised to begin to fantasize; he always has interesting questions.

And so «Kvant» for younger schoolchildren》 decided on the following experiment: in this and the next issue we place the first two chapters of L. Carroll’s book “Alice in Wonderland” in the wonderful translation by N. M. Demurova (M., “Science”, 1979), as well as those questions and tasks that came to our minds when listing this wonderful book. As usual in KMS, the answers are given in the following issues.

We are waiting for your feedback (they can be sent to the address: 103006, Moscow, Gorky St., 32/1, editorial office of the magazine «Kvant», ALICE). Those readers who have the indicated book by L. Carroll can add their tasks and questions to the next chapters of the book to the reviews. If you need this material and we receive sufficient reader response, the publication of Alice in Wonderland will continue.
