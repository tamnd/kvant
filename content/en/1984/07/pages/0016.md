In the case of a pawn endgame, the task of analyzing it can be reduced by checking the decomposition not by the composition of the pieces, but by the different positions of the pawns. This can be done because pawns do not move backward and do not move from one file to another without being captured. Decomposition turns out to be a decisive technique for placing the KLP-KL endgame in the external memory of a computer.

Lack of space does not allow us to talk in more detail about other techniques - the use of symmetry, the method of “group processing”, etc. These techniques are designed to satisfy limitations both in the amount of available computer memory and in computation time, which allows us to bring the analysis of small-figure endgames to the end.

At the same time, the middlegame chess programs that we have already talked about play poorly even in multi-piece endgames (10-12 pieces).

It is not yet known how to fill this gap. Perhaps it would be quite simple to formulate rules of behavior in the endgame, recommending empty and not optimal, but close to optimal moves. Such rules can be developed by studying endgames, the full analysis of which has already been completed on a computer. These rules would also be very valuable for chess players, since they are useful more conveniently than huge reference arrays stored in the machine’s memory.
