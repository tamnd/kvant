Substituting $n = \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \beta}$, $\cos \beta = \sqrt{1 - \frac{\sin^2 \alpha}{n^2}}$, we get
$$d = \frac{d'}{2} \frac{(n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha)^{1/2}}{\cos \alpha}.$$

In this expression, $d'$, sin $\alpha$, cos $\alpha$ are the quantities that we can change and calculate, use our tools. To avoid the unknown refractive index $n$, let's do this.
Let's make changes $d'$ for two values ​​of the angle of incidence $\alpha$ (the values ​​of sin $\alpha$ and cos $\alpha$ can be easily found by measuring the distances from the eye to the glass and to the point of intersection of the ray of vision with the glass). Then
$$\frac{d'}{2} \frac{(n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha_1)^{1/2}}{\cos \alpha_1} = \frac{d'_2}{2} \frac{(n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha_2)}{\cos \alpha_2}.$$

From here, after several transformations, we get
$$d = \frac{d'_1 d'_2}{2} \left( \frac{\sin^2 \alpha_2 - \sin^2 \alpha_1}{(d'_2)^2 \cos^2 \alpha_1 - (d'_1)^2 \cos^2 \alpha_2} \right)^{1/2}.$$

◆ Consider the motion of the rings and the rope in a reference frame moving together with the ring $O'$. In this system, the ring $O'$ is motionless. At the moment when $\widehat{AOO'} = \alpha$, the thread is “picked up” at speed $v$. Since the thread is inextensible, the ring $O$ at this moment must have such a speed $u$ (in the system associated with the ring $O'$) that its projection onto the direction $OO'$ is equal to $v$, that is
$$u = \frac{v}{\cos \alpha}.$$

In a stationary reference frame, the speed of the ring at this moment is equal to
$$v_o = u - v = v \left( \frac{1}{\cos \alpha} - 1 \right).$$
