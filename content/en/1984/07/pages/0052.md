Tasks

M871—M875; F883—F887

M871. The numbers 1 or -1 are written in cells of a 3x3 table. Then the number in each cell is written down by the product of the numbers in all neighboring cells (cells that have a common side are considered adjacent). Prove that after several repetitions of this operation there will be units in all cells.

I. K. Zhuk, I. V. Voronovich

M872. There are three circles $C_1, C_2, C_3$ of radii $r_1, r_2, r_3$ on the plane, each outside the other two, $r_1>r_2$ and $r_1>r_3$. From the point of intersection of external tangents to the circle $C_1$ and $C_2$, tangents to the circle $C_3$ are drawn, and from the point of intersection of external tangents to $C_1$ and $C_3$, tangents to $C_2$. Prove that the last two pairs of tangents form a quadrilateral in which a circle can be inscribed and its radius found.

L. P. Kuptsov

M873. The teacher wrote the square trinomial $x^2+10x+20$ on the board. Then each student in turn increased or decreased by one one of their choice of one of the lower coefficients (the coefficient of $x$ or the free term), but not both at once. The result is the trinomial $x^2+20x+10$. Is it true that at some points a quadratic trinomial with whole roots was written on the board?

A. A. Berzins

M874*. For which integers $m$ and $n$ does the equality a) $(5+3\sqrt{2})^m=(3+5\sqrt{2})^n$ hold? 6) $(a+b\sqrt{d})^m=(b+a\sqrt{d})^n$, where $a$ and $b$ ($a\neq b$) are coprime natural numbers, and $d>1$ is a natural number whose divisors do not have prime squares?

Yu. V. Mikheev

M875. $n\geqslant3$ natural numbers are written around the circle, and the ratio of the sum of two neighbors of any of these numbers to itself is a natural number. Prove that the sum of all such ratios a) is not less than 2n, 6)* is less than 3n.

O. R. Musin
