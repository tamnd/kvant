We find the speed $u_0$ from the condition
$$\frac{mu_0^2}{2} - \frac{mv_0^2}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \mu g \frac{mX_1^2}{l} \Rightarrow u_0 = v_0 \sqrt{2}.$$
Thus,
$$\tilde{X}_1 = \frac{v_0}{\omega_0} \sqrt{2}.$$
Let the distance the sled travels after the push to stop is equal to
$$X_2 = \tilde{X}_1 - X_1 = \frac{v_0}{\omega_0} (\sqrt{2} - 1),$$
and the ratio of the braking distance in the first and second cases -
$$X_1 : X_2 = 1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1).$$
With an initial speed $u_0 = v_0 \sqrt{2}$ the sleigh would cover the path $X_1$ in time $\tilde{t}_1$, determined from the condition $v_0 = v_0 \sqrt{2}$ сوس $\omega_0 \tilde{t}_1$, that is, in time
$$\tilde{t}_1 = \frac{\pi}{4} \sqrt{\frac{l}{\mu g}}.$$
Before stopping completely, they would move for a time $t_1 = \frac{\pi}{2} \sqrt{\frac{l}{\mu g}}$ (the period of oscillation does not depend on the initial speed). Therefore, the time during which the sled will move after the push until the second stop is equal to
$$t_2 = t_1 - \tilde{t}_1 = \frac{\pi}{4} \sqrt{\frac{l}{\mu g}},$$
and the ratio of braking time in the first and second cases -
$$t_1 : t_2 = 2 : 1.$$
