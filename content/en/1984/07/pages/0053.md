⟦folio 51⟧

F883. When a stationary anti-aircraft shell ruptures, it disintegrates into a very large number of fragments flying uniformly in all directions at a speed of $v_0$. Such a projectile, flying down at a speed of $u$, explodes at a height of $H$ above the ground. When will fragments fall to the ground most often?

I. S. Solodovnikov

F884. Zadchaa N. E. Zhukovsky. The beetle crawls along a hard (impermeable) straw resting on a smooth floor and a smooth vertical wall (Fig. 1). The straw is homogeneous, its length is $l$, its weight is $m$; beetle mass $M$ ($M \gg m$). The straw makes an angle $\alpha$ with the horizon. The initial speed of the beetle at point $B$ was equal to $v_0$. How should the beetle move so that the straw remains motionless? How does the beetle's acceleration depend on the distance it travels along the straw? How long does it take the beetle to reach the bottom point? Will the beetle be able to climb the straw from point $A$ to point $B$?

F885. The ends of a uniform rod of constant cross-section are maintained at temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ ($T_2 > T_1$). The temperature coefficient of linear expansion of the rod materials is $\alpha$. What is the length of the rod if at $t = 0^\circ C$ it was $l_0$?

L. G. Markovich

F886. Along two parallel metal guides, inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizon and located at a distance $b$ from each other, the mass bridge $m$ can slide without metal friction. The guides are closed to an uncharged capacitor emkosti $C$, and the entire structure is in a magnetic field, the induction $\vec{B}$ of which is directed vertically (Fig. 2). At the initial moment, the jumper is held at a distance $l$ from the base of the “slide”. How long does it take for the jumper to reach the base of the “slide” after it is released? What speed will it have at the base? The resistance of the conductors is a problem.

A. N. Buzdin

F887. A beam of parallel light rays, having a circular cross-section with a diameter of $d$ (Fig. 3), falls on a glass body, which consists of two cones connected by their bases. Body measurements are indicated at figure 3; stack refractive index $n$. What picture will be observed on a screen installed at a distance of $l$ from the body?

A. V. Helvas

M871. The numbers +1 and —1 are written in the nine little squares of a 3 by 3 square. Then each number in the little squares is replaced by the product of its neighbours (two little squares are neighbours if they have a common side). Prove that after a finite number of such operations the nine little squares will be filled with +1's.

I. K. Zhuk, I. V. Voronovich
