⟦folio 57⟧

M860*. a) Let O and R be the center and radius of the circumcircle of triangle ABC, Z and r be the center and radius of its circumcircle, K be the point of intersection of the medians of the triangle with the vertices at the points of tangency of the incircle with the sides of triangle ABC. Prove that point Z lies on the segment OK, and $|OZ|:|ZK|=3R/r$.
b) Let a, b, c be the lengths of the sides of triangle ABC, $\vec{n}_a, \vec{n}_b, \vec{n}_c$ be unit vectors perpendicular to the corresponding sides of the triangle and directed outward. Prove that $a^3\vec{n}_a + b^3\vec{n}_b + c^3\vec{n}_c = 12S \cdot \vec{MO}$, where S is the area of ​​triangle ABC, M is the point of intersection of its medians, O is the circumcenter.

The solution to problem a), as well as b), will be reduced to proving a vector equality - in task a) this equality

$$\vec{OZ} = \frac{3R}{r} \vec{ZK}.$$

(1)

In solving both problems, we will use the following basic message: to prove the equality of two vectors, it is enough to prove the equality of their projections on at least two (non-parallel) axes. These axes will be the sides of the triangle.

We will also use known facts about the “centres of gravity” of two and three points: the sum of two vectors drawn from point P to the ends of the segment is equal to $2\vec{PN}$, where N is the midpoint of this segment; the sum of three vectors drawn from point P to the vertices of the triangle is equal to $3\vec{PK}$, where K is the point of intersection of the medians of the triangle.

Let us denote by a, b, c the lengths of the sides of triangle ABC, by $T_a, T_b, T_c$ the points of tangency of the inscribed circle with these sides, by α, β, γ the values ​​of angles A, B, C; let N be the midpoint of side BC (Fig. 1).

a) Equality (1) is equivalent to the following:

$$\vec{OZ} = \frac{R}{r} \left( \vec{ZT}_a + \vec{ZT}_b + \vec{ZT}_c \right).$$

(2)

Let's check that the left and right parts give the same projections onto the side BC (we take the projections taking into account the sign, considering the direction of the vector $\vec{BC}$ to be positive, that is, the projection of the vector $\vec{v}$ onto the axis BC is equal to $|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between the axis and the vector, or $(\vec{v} \cdot \vec{BC}) / |BC|$; very indo, the projection of the sum of vectors is equal to their sum projections). For the other two parties (in fact, one of them is enough for us) the check is similar - after all, under the conditions the parties have equal rights.

Since the projections of points O and Z onto side BC are points N and T_a, the projection of the vector $\vec{OZ} = \vec{BZ} - \vec{BO}$ onto the axis BC is equal to

$$|BT_a| - |BN| = \frac{a+c-b}{2} - \frac{a}{2} = \frac{c-b}{2}$$

($|BT_a| = (a+c-b)/2$ because the tangent segments drawn from each vertex of the triangle to the inscribed circle are equal).

The projection of the vector $ZT_a$ onto BC is equal to zero, and the projection $\vec{ZT}_b + \vec{ZT}_c$—the sum of vectors of length r forming angles $90^\circ - \gamma$ and $90^\circ + \beta$ with the axis BC—is equal

$$r \cos(90^\circ - \gamma) + r \cos(90^\circ + \beta) = r \sin \gamma - r \sin \beta = \frac{r}{2R} (c - b)$$
