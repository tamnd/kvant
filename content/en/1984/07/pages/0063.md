F871. Initially, an uncharged capacitor capacitor C is connected to a series-connected battery with voltage U and coils with inductance L. The current through the coils is first detected and then decreased. At the moment when the current becomes zero, the capacitor is disconnected from the circuit and connected again, swapping its terminals. What is the maximum current that will then flow through the coils? How will this current change if the switching process is repeated 1984 times? Neglect circuit resistance.

At the moment when the current through the coils becomes zero, the energy of the capacitor will be equal to the work done by the battery. Consequently, the desiccant at this moment is determined by the condition

$$\frac{q_0^2}{2C} = U_0 q_0,$$

where

$$q_0 = 2CU_0.$$

Let's find the maximum current through the coils after connecting the capacitor. The maximum current condition is $I' = 0$, that is $\delta_{инд} = -LI' = 0$; This means that at the moment when the current is maximum, the direction to the capacitor is $U_0$ and “compensates” for the direction of the battery (Fig. 1). From the law of conservation of energy -

$$U_0 CU_0 = \frac{CU_0^2}{2} + \frac{LI_0^2}{2}$$

— find the maximum current value:

$$I_0 = U_0 \sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}.$$

Let's consider the circuit after switching (Fig. 2). The current through the coils is zero (only at the moment when the energy of the coil is zero can it be disconnected from the circuit “with impunity”). At the moment when the current reaches the desired maximum value $I_1$, the direction to the capacitor will be equal to $U_0$; this means that by this moment the charge $3CU_0$ will flow through the chain and, according to the laws of conservation of energy,

$$U_0 3CU_0 + C \frac{(2U_0)^2}{2} = \frac{CU_0^2}{2} + \frac{LI_1^2}{2}.$$

From here

$$I_1 = 3U_0 \sqrt{\frac{C}{L}}.$$

Before the next, second, switching, when the current through the coils is zero, there will be a charge $q_1$ on the capacitor, which, as in the first case, we will find from the condition

$$U_0 (2CU_0 + q_1) + C \frac{(2U_0)^2}{2} = \frac{q_1^2}{2C}.$$

From here

$$q_1^2 - 2CU_0 q_1 - 8C^2 U_0^2 = 0 \Rightarrow q_1 = CU_0 \pm 3CU_0.$$

The root - $2CU_0$ is not suitable (think about what case it corresponds to). So, $q_1 = 4CU_0$.
