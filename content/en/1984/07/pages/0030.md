Chenini pressure pipe, designed even for 10 atm, can rupture. If the closing time is taken to be 10 s, then the pressure increase is only ~2 atm, which no longer threatens the pipe rupture. Such a blow is called indirect.

Thus, the simplest way to combat water hammer is to select a safe time to close the valve. Having received these results, N. E. Zhukovsky proposed replacing the plug valves (similar to samovars) used in main pipelines with slow-closing valves, which are still used today. After this was done, massive pipe ruptures occurred. As already mentioned, all the described theoretical results on the propagation of shock waves were tested experimentally by Zhukovsky, and even with the very primitive instruments available at that time they were brilliantly confirmed.

You have probably encountered the phenomenon of water hammer more than once in everyday life. Sometimes it happens like this: you open the water tap, ... and there is a strong clanking sound, accompanied by vibration of the supply pipe. This phenomenon is directly related to water hammer, which is caused by a malfunction in the crane. Try to explain how water hammer “works” in this case. *)

However, one should not think that water hammer only causes harm. At the suggestion of N. E. Zhukovsky, he uses it, for example, for a very useful task - finding places where pipes buried in the ground burst. For this purpose, including the routes that are presumably damaged, in front of the device installation valve that records the pressure. Then, closing the valve quickly enough (but not dangerous to the strength of the pipe) creates a weak indirect blow. The resulting excess pressure, one to the point of leakage, will drop sharply, and a wave of reduced pressure will appear back to the valve. By
