Search for $\vec{OM} = (\vec{OA} + \vec{OB} + \vec{OC})/3$, and the projections of vectors $\vec{OB}$ and $\vec{OC}$ are opposite and equal in magnitude, the projection $\vec{MO}$ is equal to one third of the projection of the vector $\vec{AO}$; further, by searching for $\vec{PN} = (\vec{PB} + \vec{PC})/2$, the projection of $\vec{AO}$ is equal to $(|PC| - |PB|)/2 = (b^2 - c^2)/2a$. F867. Imagine that you are standing in front of a large sheet of thick pronunciation stack (for example, in front of a glass display case). How can you determine the thickness of the stack if there is no access to its edges (say, the edges of the stack are walled up in the walls), you cannot enter the room behind the glass, and you cannot break the glass? You have at your disposal a ruler, a wall paper, a pencil and a pocket calculator to check your calculations.

◆ If we place a ruler at right angles to the glass, then we see two of its reflections: in the near (I) and far (II) surfaces of the stack (see Fig.). The first image gives us, as it were, a “ruler behind the mirror”, along which we can change the position of the end of the second image at different viewing angles. This allows you to determine the thickness of the stack.

Looking through the glass, we see the end of the second reflection of the ruler not at point $a'$, where it actually is, but at point $a''$. We can fix the position of this point using the “ruler behind the mirror”.

Let the stack thickness be $d$ and the refractive index be $n$. As can be seen from Fig. 4,

$$d' \text{tg} \alpha = 2d \text{tg} \beta \Rightarrow d = \frac{d'}{2} \frac{\sin \alpha \cos \alpha}{\sin \beta \cos \beta}.$$
