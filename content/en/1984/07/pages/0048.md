THE TERM SHOULD BE LIGHTED WITH AN EVEN, SOFT, DIFTED LIGHT.

When viewing a stereo pair through two-color glasses, additional pale lines or half-picture contours sometimes appear along with the main image. In this case, it is necessary to weaken the illumination of the stereo drawing or thicken the color density of the filters. It is often enough to increase the density of one blue filter. If the image, when viewed, turns out to be insufficiently clear and precise, the lighting should be increased.

The drawing shown here was obtained by projecting onto a horizontal plane. Therefore, it must be viewed in a horizontal position.

* * *

To make stereo drawings, we will use a special device called a stencil template. This is a reusable device that needs to be made in advance. To do this, in turn, you need to build a so-called perspective scale. We will teach from him.

Building a perspective scale

Perspective scale establishes correspondence between the natural and perspective linear dimensions of the image to be prepared in advance. To do this, in turn, you need to build a so-called perspective scale. We will teach from him.

The construction of a perspective scale is shown in figure 2, where the following are indicated:

point $S$ is the projection center, located at a height of $h=300$ mm from its horizontal projection $S_1$;

$a$ - distance (160 mm) between the horizontal projection $S_1$ to the base of the natural scale $O_1$:

$O_1N$ - natural height scale (150 mm), divided into equal scale segments;

$O_1N_S$ is a perspective scale in which the scale segments have different lengths (as they move away from the point $O_1$ they become longer).

$D_k$ is the length of an arbitrary perspective scale segment. All linear image elements of the image are scaled in natural scale units.

The perspective scale is determined by construction or calculated using the formula $x_k=aK/(h-K)$. In the latter case, the lengths of individual segments of the perspective scale are counted from the zero mark $O_1$.
