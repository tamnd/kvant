F877. At what positions of the exact source relative to a thin line of its own with a focal length of $F=1$ m can both the image and the source be seen from at least one point?

The image and the source can be seen from one point if region II (see Fig. 1) does not lie entirely inside region I. The extreme case is the position of point $A$ such that the rays $AB$ and $DC$ are parallel. It's easy to see that it will be in that (and only that)

Our cover

The photograph reproduced on the front cover was taken at the Laboratory of High Directions of the Moscow Order of Lenin and Order of the October Revolution Energy Institute.

In the foreground is the so-called air gap - horn-shaped electrodes separated by air. The gap is connected to the test transformer (visible in the background).

When the direction is higher than a certain one, the air gap in the very bottom part, where the distance between the electrodes is smallest, breaks through and an arc discharge occurs. The arc discharge channel rises, stretches and breaks. At the moment the channel is closed, an electrical breakdown occurs again below. This is exactly the moment captured in the photograph.

Air gaps are used to protect electron beams from excessive increases in direction (for example, as a result of any transient processes in the electrical network or after a lightning strike).

More details about the occurrence of various electron breakdowns are described in the issue in the article “What is electron breakdown.”
