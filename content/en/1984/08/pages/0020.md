$$P(A_3) + P(A_4) + \dots = \frac{10p^4q^2}{1 - 2pq} \approx 0,3988$$.

By adding the initial numbers $P(A_1) + P(A_2)$ to it, we find the probability of the author winning the game $P(\text{гейм автор}) = P(A_1) + P(A_2) + \dots = p^4 + 4p^4q + \frac{10p^4q^2}{1 - 2pq} \approx 0,7357$.

Thus, the author will win on average about 74 games out of 100 from the reader, and the reader will win only 26 out of 100.

Circuits like the one shown in figure 2 are called Markov chains, named after the outstanding Russian mathematician A. A. Markov (1856-1922). A Markov chain consists of a finite or infinite number of states (y us state is the score in the game), connected by transitions (arrows), which indicate transition probabilities (y us - numbers p and q). Such chains describe random processes provided that the transition probabilities do not depend on the previous process.
