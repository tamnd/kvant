6) Answer: the required set is the interior of a regular octahedron with vertices in the middle of the edges of this tetrahedron (Fig. 2).

Repeating the process of solving problem a), we divide the given tetrahedron $T$ into four tetrahedra, the bases of which are the faces of the given one, and the vertices are located at the point $M$. Let $d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4$ be their heights, that is, the distance from the point $M$ to the faces of the tetrahedron $T$, then the sum of their volumes is equal to $(d_1S+d_2S+d_3S+d_4S)/3$ (where $S$ is the area of ​​the face of this tetrahedron), therefore, $d=d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4$ is the height of the tetrahedron $T$. In order for a quadrilateral to be formed from segments of length $d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4$, each of these lengths must be less than $d/2$ (say, the inequality $d_1 < d/2$ is equivalent to $d_1 < d_2 + d_3 + d_4$). Obviously, each of the inequalities “cuts off” from the tetrahedron a tetrahedron half the size (tetrahedrons with red edges at figure 2). After four “cuts”, a regular octahedron will remain.

Conversely, for any point inside this octahedron the inequalities $d_i < d/2$ will be satisfied, therefore a quadrilateral can be formed from segments of length $d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4$. (Indeed, let $d_1 > d_2 > d_3 > d_4$; the intervals $|d_1 - d_2|, d_1 + d_2|$ and $|d_3 - d_4|, d_3 + d_4|$ contain a common point $l$, since $d_1 - d_2 < d_3 + d_4 < d_1 + d_2$; now the required quadrilateral can be obtained by placing triangles on the sides $d_1, d_2, l$ and $d_3, d_4, l$ to each other.
