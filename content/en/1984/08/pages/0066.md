4. Let's find the number of apples. In the first row we have 1 apple, in the first and second rows 1+3=4, then 1+3+5+7=16. Note that the resulting numbers are the squares of the number of apples added. The explanation for this can be seen in figure 3: the square is made up of positions containing successive odd numbers of cells. Each even row contains one more fruit than the odd row above it, so the number of pears is equal to the square of the number of apples in which they lie next to the same number as apples. Naturally, the answer to the problem depends on whether the number of apples is even or odd. If $n$ is an even number $n=2k$, then there are $k$ apples and $k^2+k$ pears in the display case. If $n$ is an odd number $n=2k+1$, then the number of apples will be $(k+1)^2$, the number of pears will remain equal to $k^2+k$.

Two problems about Fibonacci numbers
(see «Kvant» No. 7, p. 14)

1. Solution. Let us take the quotient of any two distinct, non-unity Fibonacci numbers $f_k/f_n$. Obviously, only $n \geq 3$, $k>n$ can be considered. Let $m=k-n$, then $f_k/f_n = f_{n+m}/f_n$. Let us show that for any such $n$ and $m$ the condition $f_{m+1} < (f_{n+m}/f_n) < f_{m+2}$ is satisfied.
Indeed (we use Problem 6 from the article by I.M. Yaglom*)
$$\frac{f_{n+m}}{f_n} - f_{m+1} = \frac{f_{n+m}-f_n f_{m+1}}{f_n} = \frac{f_{n-1} f_m}{f_n} > 0,$$
$$\frac{f_{n+m}}{f_n} - f_{m+2} = \frac{f_{n+m}-f_n f_{m+2}}{f_n} = \frac{f_{n-1} f_m+f_n f_{m+1}-f_n(f_{m-1}+f_m)}{f_n} = \frac{(f_{n-1}-f_n) f_m}{f_n} < 0.$$
Thus, the quotient of any two distinct, non-unity Fibonacci numbers is between two adjacent Fibonacci numbers, and between them there are no and cannot be other Fibonacci numbers.
