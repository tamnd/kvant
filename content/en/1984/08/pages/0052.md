It is convenient to solve, as it were, the inverse problem: an additional triangle $ABC$ to a large similar triangle with other similar and pairwise different triangles. Obviously, this problem is equivalent to the original one. Let for definition $|BC|:|AC| = k > 1$. Moreover, to triangle $\Delta_0 = ABC$ there are similar triangles $\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \Delta_3, \Delta_4$ and $\Delta_5$ in accordance with figure 3. It is easy to see that in this case the similarity coefficient of triangles $\Delta_{n+1}$ and $\Delta_n$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$ is equal to $k$, therefore the similarity coefficient of triangles $\Delta_0$ and $\Delta_n$ with $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$ is equal to $k^n$, and $\Delta_5$ and $\Delta_0$ are $|CM|:|AB| = (|CK| + |CM|):|AB| = k + k^3$. From here it is clear that the only pair of congruent triangles here can only be $\Delta_5$ and $\Delta_4$ - if $k + k^3 = k^4$.
