So from the Earth we see all the luminaries located higher above the horizon than they actually are. For the same reason, we see the sunset of the stars and the Sun a few minutes later, and the sunrise a few minutes earlier than it would be in the absence of the atmosphere; As a result of refraction, the figures of the Sun and Moon are distorted near the horizon. Rays of different colors bend differently: blue (for which the A value is larger) is stronger than green, and green is stronger than red. This is especially pronounced near the horizon, giving rise to many red phenomena.

We also see earthly objects not exactly in the direction where they are. And this is also due to the optical impurity of the air. Air density, as is known, depends on temperature: φ=ρμ/RT. The pressure p depends on the altitude, but at a given altitude it is almost the same. But the air temperature at the same altitude can be different - above a meadow, above a forest, above the surface of a reservoir, near a mountain slope, etc. it is not the same. As a result, light rays passing through the table and non-uniform air begin to “curl”, and we see objects shifted up, down, or to the side. In the foothills, as a rule, all distant objects seem to be raised and slightly moved away from the mountains, and sometimes objects generally hidden by the mountain range become visible. Moreover, if you look at the same object from different sides, from different distances or at different times, then it will seem to be in different places.

Atmospheric mirror

At times, especially in calm sunny weather, a stable layer of warm, that is, less dense, air forms near the surface of the earth or water. Rays falling obliquely from above onto this layer will propagate in a medium with decreasing optical density. If the layer is thick enough, total internal reflection can occur within the layer; The rays can, having traveled a certain distance, emerge from the layer again, but now they will be directed upward, as if after reflection from a mirror. Therefore, we see a mirror image of all objects, or rather their mirage, somewhere down, underground or under water (see figure 4a on p. 24). We can see the objects themselves, or other rays can enter our eye directly, minutes heated layer.

Something similar happened with the top girls. Only the layer of heated air is not under their feet, but to the side, at the edge of the forest, which protected this night layer from cooling. And the mirror, as we see, is special - only very inclined rays are reflected in it, gradually bending inside a thick layer - this is what distinguishes a mirage from general reflection.

If a sufficiently thick layer of warm air is located not under your feet, but at some height above your head (figure 46), the mirage will be in the air in the air, as in the case of the Flying Dutchman. The object itself can be located far beyond the horizon and be invisible. Such mirages form with very flat rays, inclined at small angles to the Earth's surface.

The direction in which sound travels also changes when moving from cold to warm air. Therefore, there are also sound mirages in the atmosphere, which differ from ordinary echoes in that they are generated not by reflection, but by refraction of sound.

During the 1914 war, it was noticed that artillery cannonade, which was generally excessive up to a distance of 50-60 km, again became excessive if separated at a distance of 100-150 km. It turned out that it was a mirage generated by the refraction of sound in a warm layer of air existing at an altitude of 50-60 km.

What happens if a layer of cold air forms in the warm air? Light rays propagating in this layer may appear as if in a trap - reflecting alternately from the upper and lower left, they will not be able to escape beyond their limits (figure 4 in). In this case, you can see a curious combination of repeatedly repeated upright and inverted images of the same objects, as in a kaleidoscope or in a room with many mirrors. This respect is fantastically intertwined with many mirages and is actually assigned the scientific purpose of Fata Morgana.
