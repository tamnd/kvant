⟦folio 21⟧

Curved straight

A savage, sharpening a bow arrow with a sharp shell, checked the quality of his product by bringing it to his eye. Without knowing it, he used the property of the straightness of light rays: if the light ray, without deviating, slid along the surface of the arrow, then the arrow was straight. The builders of the Egyptian pyramids did the same, controlling the straightness of their edges, and even talked about this in one of the inscriptions engraved on the stone. Modern workshops follow suit. When a sniper or surveyor points his vision finder at an individual object, they rely on the straightness of the light rays. And how could it be otherwise? After all, the very concept of a straight line arose from the idea of ​​a light ray - nothing can be straighter than it.

But the light beam can also bend. This occurs when it penetrates a heterogeneous medium. You've probably noticed how much the outlines of objects underwater are distorted when you look at them through the surface of the water. The reason for these distortions is that, passing through the boundary of water and air, the light beam turns and is refracted.

As is known, the behavior of a light beam when passing from one medium to another is determined by the law of rectilinearity. Let us recall this law: the incident ray, the rectilinear ray and the perpendicular to the boundary of two media, restoring at the point of incidence of the ray, lie in the same plane; the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant value for two given media. If $\Theta_1$ is the angle of incidence, $\Theta_2$ is the angle of refraction, then

$$\frac{\sin \Theta_1}{\sin \Theta_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1},$$

where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the refractive indices of the (absolute) media.

What will happen if, along the path of its propagation, a light beam passes through several different media, that is, through several media with different refractive indices?

Let's turn to figure 1 a. According to the law of refraction, when moving from medium I to medium II

$$\frac{\sin \Theta_1}{\sin \Theta_2} = \frac{n_2}{n_1},$$ or $n_1 \sin \Theta_1 = n_2 \sin \Theta_2$;

at the next transition, from environment II to environment III,

$$\frac{\sin \Theta_2}{\sin \Theta_3} = \frac{n_3}{n_2},$$ or $n_2 \sin \Theta_2 = n_3 \sin \Theta_3$;

during the transition from environment III to environment IV

$$\frac{\sin \Theta_3}{\sin \Theta_4} = \frac{n_4}{n_3},$$ or $n_3 \sin \Theta_3 = n_4 \sin \Theta_4$;

etc. From all those recorded, it corresponds that when a light ray passes from one medium to another, the product of the refractive index of the medium and the sine remains constant
