F875. Along the horizontal direction of the rails, the distance between which is equal to $d$, a jumper whose mass is equal to $m$ can slide without friction (see figure). The rails are connected by a resistor with resistance $R$ and placed in a vertical magnetic field, the inductance of which is $B$. The jumper is given the speed $v_0$. Find the distance passed by the jumper to the stop. How does the answer depend on the direction of the vector $\vec{B}$?

◆ When the jumper moves, the flux of magnetic induction through the circuit changes; it is formed by the jumper, rails and resistor. An induced emf will arise in the circuit and a current will appear. As a result of the magnetic field acting on the current flowing through the jumper, the jumper will be inhibited.

Let's find the braking force $F$. Let the speed of the jumper be $v$. In a short period of time $\Delta t$ the jumper will move along the rails to a short distance $\Delta x = v \cdot \Delta t$. The change in the area of ​​the circuit will be equal to $v d \cdot \Delta t$, the flux of magnetic induction during this time will change to $\Delta \Phi = B \cdot v d \cdot \Delta t$, an EMF will appear in the circuit

$$\mathcal{E} = - \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = - B v d,$$

and according to Ohm’s law, current $i = \mathcal{E} / R$ will flow through the jumper. The force acting on the jumper from the magnetic field will be equal to

$$f = i B d = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} v;$$

the force $f$ is directed, according to Lenz’s rule, against the speed $v$.

Let us now write down the equation of motion of the jumper (for small displacement $\Delta x$):

$$ma = f = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} v,$$

or, given that $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t},$

$$m \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \Rightarrow m \cdot \Delta v = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} \cdot \Delta x.$$

We see that the change in the speed of the jumper is proportional to the change in its coordinate $x$ (at the initial moment $x_0=0$). This means that the complete change in speed $v_{кОН} - v_0 = 0 - v_0 = -v_0$ is associated with a change in coordinate $x$
