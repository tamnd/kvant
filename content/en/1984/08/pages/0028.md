How quantum theory was created

(See beginning on p. 2)

You see that the momentum of a quantum is related to the wavelength in exactly the same way as that of a particle: $p=h/\lambda$. This means that for a traveling wave, light quanta can be considered as de Broglie particles - photons.

So what is a photon? This is a portion of the excitation of a quantum oscillator - a traveling electromagnetic wave with a given wave vector $k$.

What are “zero” oscillations of the electromagnetic field? These are zero-point oscillations of quantum oscillators - electromagnetic waves with all possible wave vectors.

Other fields that exist in nature, for example, gravitational fields, can also be represented as a set of oscillators. Therefore, in empty space there should be zero oscillations of the gravitational field. But, according to the relative theory, the field is also associated with changes in the geometric properties of space. And these geometric properties fluctuate, geometry flickers! The ratio of circumference to radius fluctuates around 2π...

The same applies to fields describing particles, for example, electrons and positrons or pi-mesons. There are zero oscillations in the void of the electron-positron, pion and, in general, fields of all possible particles. Zero oscillations in the vacuum of the fields describing particles with spin 1/2 manifest themselves in the temporary birth and destruction of pairs electron - positron, nucleon - antinucleon... The vacuum is filled with such not fully born particles, they are called “virtual”. But as soon as two nucleons or an electron and a positron are collided in a vacuum, virtual particles can turn into real ones - this is how new particles are released during the collision. The real part of the large energy is surrounded by a cloud of virtual particles, and the greater the energy of the particle, the larger the clouds. It is enough to specify another realizable part with its edge, and all the virtual particles of the cloud will become realizable!

After it became clear what a quantum is, the painful philosophical question, the problem of waves and particles, disappeared. Light is waves whose energy changes in portions, and if we consider all kinds of traveling waves, then the totality of them fills the gas of particles: each has a certain energy and momentum.

To understand means to be able to answer any reasonable question, and in physics those and only those questions are considered reasonable, the answers to which can be verified by a mental or real experiment.
