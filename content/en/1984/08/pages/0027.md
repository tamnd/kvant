The fairy is swaggering again

The position of the astronomer, surveyor, radio operator and radar operator is complicated by another inevitable and very annoying problem. Under the influence of wind in the air, local changes in density, temperature, concentration of electrons and other characteristics of the state of the air will occur every now and then. These cases, which quickly replace disturbances, are unstable and mobile. Passing through such trembling, heterogeneous air, the beam is forced to slightly change its direction all the time. His path turns out to be both very winding and very changeable - neighboring rays either converge, then diverge, or intertwine. Therefore, images of distant objects and lights (for example, stars) never stand still, but always seem to dance and flicker, now flashing, now pale. This restlessness of the optical, sound or radio picture, generated by Morgana’s pranks, limits the real possibilities of astronomical and geodetic changes, location technology, technical communications, etc. During strong gusts of wind, when the density of the air suddenly changes sharply, the beam is sometimes simply “blown away” for a moment by the wind to the side or scattered by it into sleep-independently swaying independent beams. Let's trace the path of a radar pulse (optical or radio) sent by the operator into the atmosphere. According to Morgana's map, he is placed in the air not as if on an asphalt highway, but as if along a bumpy road - he will be tossed around by irregularities in the air, and his path will not be at all straight. When it overtakes the desired object and, having been reflected from it, returns to the operator, then it will return, generally speaking, along a different road and will appear from a place other than where it was sent.
