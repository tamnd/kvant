Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be the given numbers, $b_1=\{a_1\}, b_2=\{a_1+a_2\}, \dots, b_n=\{a_1+a_2+\dots+a_n\}$ ($\{x\}$ is the fractional part of $x$). All numbers $b_1, \dots, b_n$ lie in the interval $[0; 1]$. Let's divide it into $n+1$ equal intervals $\Delta_0=[0; 1/(n+1)]$, $\Delta_1=[1/(n+1); 2/(n+1)]$, ..., $\Delta_n=[n/(n+1); 1]$. If at least one of the numbers $b_i$ falls into one of the extreme intervals $\Delta_0$ or $\Delta_n$, then the statement of the problem will obviously be satisfied for the sum $a_1+\dots+a_i$. Otherwise, $n-1$ intervals $\Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{n-1}$ will contain $n$ points $b_1, \dots, b_n$, so at least one of them will contain two points, $b_k$ and $b_l$. Let $k>l$, then $a_{l+1}+a_{l+2}+\dots+a_k=(a_1+\dots+a_k)-(a_1+\dots+a_l)=\epsilon$ is the number ($|b_k-b_l|<1/(n+1))$.

It is easy to see that the number $1/(n+1)$ cannot be reduced under conditions of reduction - it is enough to take all the numbers $1/(n+1)$.

**M865. Let $[a, b]$ denote the least common multiple of the integers $a$ and $b$. Prove that for any $n+1$ numbers $a_0 < a_1 < ... < a_n$ the following inequality holds:

$$\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} + \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} + ... + \frac{1}{[a_{n-1}, a_n]} \leq 1 - \frac{1}{2^n},$$
if $a) n=2; b) n=3; \theta)^{*} n$ is any natural number.

**For small $n$ the problem can be solved using brute force.**

a) Since $[a, b] \geq 2a$ for $a < b$, and $a_0 \geq 1, a_1 \geq 2$, the inequalities $\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} \leq \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} \leq \frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} \leq \frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} + \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} \leq \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}$ are valid.

b) If $a_2 \geq 4$, then, as in point a), $\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} \leq \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} \leq \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{[a_2, a_3]} \leq \frac{1}{8}$, if $a_2=3$, then $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$, $[a_0, a_1] = 2$, $[a_1, a_2] = 6$, $[a_2, a_3] \geq 6$, and $\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} + \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} + \frac{1}{[a_2, a_3]} \leq \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} < \frac{7}{8}$.

c) We will carry out the proof using the method of mathematical induction. At $n=1$ our inequality is very interesting. Let us now assume that it is true for $n=k$ and prove it for $n=k+1$. Let's consider two cases

1) Let $a_{k+1} \geq 2^{k+1}$, then $[a_k, a_{k+1}] \geq a_{k+1} \geq 2^{k+1}$ and by induction

$$\left( \frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} + ... + \frac{1}{[a_{k-1}, a_k]} \right) + \frac{1}{[a_k, a_{k+1}]} \leq \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2^k} \right) + \frac{1}{2^{k+1}} = 1 - \frac{1}{2^{k+1}}.$$

2) In the case where $a_{k+1} < 2^{k+1}$, we use the identity $[a, b] = ab/(a, b)$, where $(a, b)$ is the most common divisor of the numbers $a$ and $b$. Since $b-a$ is divisible by $(a, b)$, and therefore $(a, b) \leq b-a$ (at $b>a$), this identity implies that $1/[a, b] \leq (b-a)/ab = 1/a-1/b$. That's why

$$\frac{1}{[a_0, a_1]} + \frac{1}{[a_1, a_2]} + ... + \frac{1}{[a_k, a_{k+1}]} \leq \left( \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{1}{a_1} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{a_1} - \frac{1}{a_2} \right) + ... + \left( \frac{1}{a_k} - \frac{1}{a_{k+1}} \right) = \frac{1}{a_0} - \frac{1}{a_{k+1}} < 1 - \frac{1}{2^{k+1}}$$

Q.E.D.

Note that this estimate is exact: for any $n$, equality is achieved for the sequence $a_0=1, a_1=2, ..., a_n=2^n$.

**F873. The bow on the ship's main body forms an angle $60^\circ$ with the ship's heading at its speed of 20 km/h. Without changing course, the ship's speed doubled and the angle became 30°. Find the wind speed from these data (assuming it constant). At what speed of the ship will the angle become 90°?**

**The pennant on the ship’s masthead shows the direction of vector $\vec{u} + \vec{v}$, where $\vec{v}$ is the ship’s speed vector (it determines the ship’s course), $\vec{u}$ is the wind speed vector.**

Let's project the vector $\vec{u}$ on the direction of the ship's course and on the direction perpendicular to the course. Let $u_1$ and $u_2$ be the absolute values ​​of the corresponding projects. Since the direction of the wind is

unknown, in the general case we can write the following relations:

at ship speed $|\vec{v}| = 20$ km/h —
$$\frac{\pm u_2}{v \pm u_1} = \text{tg} 60^\circ = \sqrt{3},$$

(1)

at ship speed $2|\vec{v}| = 40$ km/h —
$$\frac{\pm u_2}{2v \pm u_1} = \text{tg} 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

(2)

Having solved equations (1) and (2) together, we find
$$u_1 = \frac{1}{2} v, \quad u_2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} v.$$

From here you can find a model of the wind speed vector:
$$|\vec{u}| = \sqrt{u_1^2 + u_2^2} = |\vec{v}| = 20 \text{ km/ч}.$$

The direction of the vector $\vec{u}$ cannot be found from these data, but we can say that the angle $\alpha$, which the wind speed vector forms with the ship’s heading, is determined, up to sign, by the condition
$$\text{tg} \alpha = \pm \frac{u_2}{u_1} = \pm \sqrt{3}.$$

In the case when the pennant forms an angle $90^\circ$ with the heading of the ship, the speed $v_x$ of the ship and the speed $u$ of the wind are related by the relation
$$|\vec{u}|^2 - |\vec{v}_x|^2 = u_2^2,$$ or $u_1^2 + u_2^2 - |\vec{v}_x|^2 = u_2^2,$
where
$$|\vec{v}_x| = u_1 = \frac{1}{2} v = 10 \text{ km/ч}.$$

So, the pennant will make an angle $90^\circ$ with the ship’s heading at a ship speed of $|\vec{v}_x| = 10 \text{ km/ч}.$

F875. Along the horizontal direction of the rails, the distance between which is equal to $d$, a jumper whose mass is equal to $m$ can slide without friction (see figure). The rails are connected by a resistor with resistance $R$ and placed in a vertical magnetic field, the inductance of which is $B$. The jumper is given the speed $v_0$. Find the distance passed by the jumper to the stop. How does the answer depend on the direction of the vector $\vec{B}$?

◆ When the jumper moves, the flux of magnetic induction through the circuit changes; it is formed by the jumper, rails and resistor. An induced emf will arise in the circuit and a current will appear. As a result of the magnetic field acting on the current flowing through the jumper, the jumper will be inhibited.

Let's find the braking force $F$. Let the speed of the jumper be $v$. In a short period of time $\Delta t$ the jumper will move along the rails to a short distance $\Delta x = v \cdot \Delta t$. The change in the area of ​​the circuit will be equal to $v d \cdot \Delta t$, the flux of magnetic induction during this time will change to $\Delta \Phi = B \cdot v d \cdot \Delta t$, an EMF will appear in the circuit

$$\mathcal{E} = - \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = - B v d,$$

and according to Ohm’s law, current $i = \mathcal{E} / R$ will flow through the jumper. The force acting on the jumper from the magnetic field will be equal to

$$f = i B d = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} v;$$

the force $f$ is directed, according to Lenz’s rule, against the speed $v$.

Let us now write down the equation of motion of the jumper (for small displacement $\Delta x$):

$$ma = f = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} v,$$

or, given that $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t},$

$$m \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \Rightarrow m \cdot \Delta v = - \frac{B^2 d^2}{R} \cdot \Delta x.$$

We see that the change in the speed of the jumper is proportional to the change in its coordinate $x$ (at the initial moment $x_0=0$). This means that the complete change in speed $v_{кОН} - v_0 = 0 - v_0 = -v_0$ is associated with a change in coordinate $x$

⟦folio 54⟧

dinata (with full movement $X$) respectively

$$m(-v_0) = -\frac{B^2d^2}{R} X.$$

From here we find the path $X$, traversed by the jumper until it comes to a complete stop:

$$X = \frac{mRv_0}{B^2d^2}.$$

In the case when the vector $\vec{B}$ makes an angle $\alpha$ with the normal to the plane of the rails,

$$X = \frac{mRv_0}{B^2d^2 \cos^2 \alpha}.$$

Indeed, the induced emf and, consequently, the current $i$ flowing along the jumper are determined by the flux of magnetic induction through the circuit, and the flux in this case is determined by the projection of the vector $\vec{B}$ onto the normal to the plane of the circuit.

F876. Two satellites move in the same orbit at a small (compared to the radius $R_0$ of the earth) distance from each other. This distance during periodic movement changes from $l_1$ to $l_2$. Determine the minimum and maximum distance of the satellites from the center of the earth if the satellites' orbital period is $T$.

According to Kepler's second law, the areas noticeable by the radius vector of the satellite in equal periods of time are equal. During the same time, one satellite moved from point $a$ to point $a'$, and the other satellite moved from point $b$ to point $b'$ (see figure). This means that the areas of sectors $Oaba'$ and $Oba'b'$ are equal. If we subtract the area of ​​sector $Oba'$ from these areas, we obtain the areas of sectors $Oab$ and $Oa'b'$, which are thus the same.

Since, according to the conditions, the distance between the satellites is small, instead of sectors, the corresponding equidistant triangles can be considered. Therefore, the areas of triangles $Oab$ and $Oa'b'$ are the same, that is

$$\frac{1}{2} l_1 R_1 = \frac{1}{2} l_2 R_2. \tag{1}$$

According to Kepler's third law, the ratio of the squares of the satellite's orbital periods in different orbits is equal to the ratio of the cubes of large half-seine orbits. If $T_0$ is the period of revolution of the satellite in a circular orbit near the earth’s surface, then

$$\frac{T^2}{T_0^2} = \frac{\left(\frac{1}{2} (R_1 + R_2)\right)^3}{R_0^3}, \tag{2}$$

where $R_0$ is the radius of the earth. Period $T_0$ will be found from the equation of motion of the satellite in a circular orbit of radius $R_0$:

$$m\omega^2 R_0 = mg,$$

where

$$T_0^2 = \frac{4\pi^2 R_0}{g}.$$

Thus, from (2) we find:

$$R_1 + R_2 = 2R_0 \sqrt{\frac{T^2 g}{4\pi^2 R_0}}. \tag{3}$$
