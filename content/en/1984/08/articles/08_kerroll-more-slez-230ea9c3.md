Then she heard some splashing nearby and swam there to find out who was splashing there. At first she decided that it was a walrus or a hippopotamus, but then she remembered how tiny she was now, and looking closely, she saw only a mouse, which, apparently, had also fallen into the water.
“Should I talk to her or not? - thought Alice. “Everything is so amazing these days that perhaps she can speak.” In any case, it’s worth a try.”
And she began:
- Oh Mouse! Don't you know how to get out of this puddle? I trust so much that perhaps she can speak too. In any case, he will try to stand in the area and all the mice... Ax, more mine! - Alice said sadly. “I think I offended her again!”
The mouse swam away from her with all its might, even waves appeared in the water.
- Mouse, dear! - Alice closed everything to her affectionately. - Please, come back. If cats and dogs are not your thing, I won’t say another word about them!
Hearing this, the Mouse turned and slowly swam back. She turned terribly pale (“From anger!” thought Alice.)
“Let’s climb out to the shore,” said the Mouse with a quiet, trembling head, “and I’ll tell you my story.” Then you will understand why I don’t respect cats and dogs.
And in fact it was necessary to get out. The beam became more and more crowded from all the birds and animals flying into it. There were Robin Goose, Dodo Bird, Little Lori, Ed the Eaglet and all sorts of other kind creatures. Alice swam forward, and everyone followed her to the shore.
