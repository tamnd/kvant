What is electrical breakdown

L. A. ASHKINAZI

We all know why high voltages are used in power transmission lines. Indeed, the higher the voltage in the lines, the lower the current in it (at constant transmitted power) and the lower the heat losses. It would seem that the higher the voltage, the better. However, power lines with an operating voltage of, say, several million volts do not exist, and the development of each subsequent power line with a voltage greater than the previous ones requires more us or. Apparently, there is some effect that limits the operation of the power line voltage.

It turns out that this is the same effect that limits the “voltage work” between a cloud and the ground (or between two clouds).

Both light and sound

A short thunder - a deep collapse, Christmas light and ozone, Distant lightning a carnival.

N. Mayorov

These three lines contained practically all the information about lightning, although their author was a poet and not a physicist.

So, lightning is a short-term (“short”) process in the atmosphere with the generation of sound (“thunder”) and light (“the birth of light”) and with the occurrence of chemical reactions (“and ozone”). During this process, energy is released, at least in the form of light and sound - we see light and hear sound.

The first question that will immediately arise is where does this energy come from? The answer is very clear: energy accumulates in a capacitor, the plates of which are the cloud and the earth, and the dielectric is the air between them. Let's try to estimate the energy stored in this system.

Let the cloud have dimensions of 3×3 km, and the distance between the cloud and the ground is 1 km. Then the capacitance of the capacitor will be about 0.1 µF (how was this value obtained?). Despite the internal dimensions of the capacitor, its capacitance is modest - in radio applications there are capacitors with a capacitance 1000 times larger. True, the energy of a charged capacitor also depends on the voltage, and between the cloud and the ground it reaches 10^9 V. Thus, the energy is about 5·10^10 J (how was this value obtained?), which, for example, is 10^9 times more than the energy of a charged physical capacitor (capacitance 800 μF, voltage 300 V).

There are three main mechanisms of breakdown of solids and liquids.

If a substance consists of isons (atoms linked by ionic bonds), then an electric field can break these bonds. With such a mechanism, the higher the energy of the bonds, the higher the electrical process. Indeed, such a pattern is observed, for example, for ionic crystals (Fig. 3). Then the following happens. Firstly, it moves through the substance, destroying chemical bonds and generating new free ions, which immediately begin to carry out their “dirty work”. Secondly, all ethions take energy from the field and give it to the substance (braking during collisions). the substance heats up, melts and evaporates, resulting in a breakdown in vapors. It is difficult to make out what is earlier and what is later - all these processes occur simultaneously. And they end - depending on the power of the electrical source and the breakdown voltage - with a quiet click.

The distance between the electrodes along the volume of the dielectric, of course, will not change, but the distance along the surface of the dielectric, that is, along the boundary between the insulators and the air, will increase. Apparently, there is some special type of breakdown associated with the surface of a solid body. In practice, this type of breakdown is observed in an experiment with a ceramic cube, to the opposite edges of which a potential difference is applied. In this case, the paths between the electrodes inside the dielectric coincide in terms of their influence, and the corresponding breakdown voltages have a ratio of approximately 10:3:1. There is something mysterious about this. It would seem that surface is a purely geometric concept. Why is the breakdown voltage across the surface different than others? The answer is that in fact, surface is not only a geometric concept.

After a breakdown surface on the dielectric, as a rule, there is no damage. This means that the breakdown still occurs in the gas surrounding the sample. But why then does the breakdown channel “spread” in a pattern? Could it be that the gas in close proximity to the sample is somehow different from the gas further away? But this can only result from the release of gas from the sample. Indeed, on the surface of a solid there is always a layer of sorbed gas and moisture. To check whether “surface breakdown” occurs in a layer of denser gas near the surface of the sample, you can check this experiment. Place the sample in a vacuum and heat it. When heated, gas and moisture sorbed on the surface are removed, but new ones are not sorbed, since the sample is in a vacuum. Under such conditions, the surface stress is significantly higher. Therefore, surface breakdown is essentially a breakdown in a gas (small lightning). However, this process has been studied very well, as evidenced by the epigraph to the section.
