So, we have found out under what conditions bodies float in liquid, and under what conditions they sink. Now let's continue the little experiment. Take an apple, cut it in half and put the half in a pan of water - the half floats. And place the other half on the bottom of an empty pan, medium down, and carefully fill it with water, so that water does not penetrate between the apple and the bottom of the pan (to do this, you can grease the edges of the pan with oil in advance). This half will lie on the bottom as if glued. (The fact that everything will happen exactly this way is evidenced by the photograph on the cover of this issue of the magazine.) What explains the columnar behavior of apple halves? The fact is that in the second case - the half is at the bottom - there is no buoyancy force, since there is no water under the apple.
