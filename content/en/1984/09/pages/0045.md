With a rapid increase in load from $F_2$ to $F_1$, the work of the elastic forces in a time greater than the damping time of the wheel is equal to the area of ​​the trapezoid shaded at figure 3 vertically. The load force all this time can be considered equal to the approximation $F_1$; the work that is done against the load forces is $F_1(x_1-x_2)$ (the area of ​​the horizontal shaded rectangle is figure 3). Energy equal to the difference of these works passes into the body, that is

Thus, as a result of the processes of reducing the load from $F_1$ to $F_2$ and then increasing it to the previous value $F_1$, energy passes into the body

$$U = U_1 + U_2 = \frac{(F_1 - F_2)^2}{k} = \frac{(Mg(1-\cos \alpha))^2}{k}.$$

Substituting this value $U$ into equation (*), we find $v_x$:

$$v_x = \sqrt{v_0^2 + gH - 2Mg^2(1-\cos \alpha)^2}/k.$$

This formula is applicable as long as $k$ is large enough to ensure that the period and decay time are small compared to the descent time.
