Main Problems Competition

Teams were given two hours to solve these problems and submit written reports.

1. "Helmholtz resonator". Calculate and measure the resonant frequency of sound vibrations in a spherical flask with a narrow pot. The volume of the flask is 0.5 l, the cross-sectional area of ​​the pot is 4 cm², the height of the pot is 2 cm, the speed of sound is 330 m/s.

2. "Spark". When a DC circuit containing high inductance is opened, a spark discharge occurs between the contacts of the opening switch. If you connect a capacitor parallel to the switch, the spark discharge will be significantly weakened. Investigate and explain the phenomenon of spark extinction when connecting a capacitor.

3. "Doppler Effect". The source and applicator of sound oscillations (repeater and microphone) are mounted on the ends of the plane, which is suspended from the motherboard and can oscillate (a figure in the form of an inverted letter T is formed). The signal arriving at the loudspeaker from the sound generator is fed to the $X$-plates of the oscilloscope. An amplified signal from the microphone is supplied to the $Y$ plates of the oscilloscope. Thus, on the oscilloscope screen, using the ellipse method, you can observe the phase difference between the sound generator signal and the amplified microphone signal. Previously, with the pendulum at rest, by slightly changing the frequency of the generator, an image of an inclined straight line is obtained on the oscilloscope screen. Then, with the pendulum swinging freely, the periodic transformation of the straight line into an ellipse is observed on the oscilloscope screen (if the pendulum is in the plane of swing).
