**M869***. Pairs of consecutive natural numbers (8, 9); (288, 289) have the property that each of these numbers contains any prime factor of at least the second power.

a) Find another such pair of consecutive numbers.
b) Prove that there are an infinite number of such pairs.

◆

If the pair of numbers $m, m+1$ has the need property (each of the numbers does not contain prime factors to the first power), then the pair of numbers $4m(m+1), 4m(m+1)+1$ also has it: the first number is equal to $2^2m(m+1)$, by assumption, and the second is equal to $4m^2+4m+1=(2m+1)^2$, because it is a square. Applying this operation to the pair of numbers (8, 9), we get exactly the pair $4 \cdot 8 \cdot 9=288; 289=17^2$ specified in the conditions. From this pair, in turn, you can get another pair $4 \cdot 288 \cdot 289=331828; 331829=577^2$. (However, readers indicated in the answer to problem a) smaller numbers: $675=3^3 \cdot 5^2, 676=2^2 \cdot 13^2; 12167=23^3, 12168=2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 13^2$, etc.)

Continuing in the same way, using the operation

$$(m; m+1) \rightarrow (4m(m+1); 4m(m+1)+1),$$

in which both numbers of the pair, very/no, are returned, we can construct how many angularly necessary pairs of numbers.

A. V. Anzhans
