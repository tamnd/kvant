Archimedes' Law

Doctor of Technical Sciences
A. L. STASENKO

... he made a furvin (bag) like a larger ball, inflated it with dirty and stinking smoke, made a loop from it, sat in it, and a nether force lifted it above the helpless...
(story about the flight of the foreman Kryakutny to Ryazan in 1731)

Take a cube made of wood or plastic, which may have remained from the ancient times of kindergarten. Hold it in the air somewhere between the floor and the ceiling and release it. The cube will fall to the floor. Why? It is clear: he is attracted by the Earth with a force called gravity.

Now, roll up your sleeves, place this cube in a bathtub filled with water, somewhere between the bottom and the surface of the water, and release. The cube will float up. Why? Doesn't the force of gravity of the Earth act on him through the water? It can’t be: after all, the duck or dumbbell sinks to the bottom!

This means that there is still some kind of upward force acting - a buoyant force. In the case of a cube, it “overpowers” ​​the force of gravity; in the case of a dumbbell, it is not enough to force the dumbbell to move upward.

How can we find out what this buoyant force is equal to? Let's turn to figure 1 and see what effect water has on a cube dropped into it. Water pressure forces act on all faces of the cube. But, as you know, the pressure in a liquid is different at different depths - the greater the depth of immersion, the greater it is. This means that the pressure forces acting on the upper and lower faces of the cube are different. It is not difficult to guess that the force acting on the lower boundary is greater. Let's see how much more.

The upper face is acted upon by a downward pressure force equal to

$$F \downarrow = \varrho_* g y^2.$$

The force acting from the water on the lower boundary is directed upward and is equal to

$$F = F \uparrow - F \downarrow = \varrho_* g (y + a) a^2 - \varrho_* g y^2 = \varrho_* g a^3.$$

This is the pushing force. But $\varrho_* g a^3$ is the weight of water that would be poured into our cube more if it were hollow, that is, this is the weight of water in the volume of the cube.
