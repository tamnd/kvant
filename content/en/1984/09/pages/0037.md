F894. In one of the projects, it was planned to use a solar sail with an area of ​​$S=1$ km$^2$ for spacecraft flights in the Solar System. The sail unfolds as the vehicle moves around the Sun in Earth's orbit, the radius of which is $R_3=1,5\cdot 10^8$ km. With further movement, the sail is constantly oriented perpendicular to the sun's rays, the pressure of which in the earth's orbit is $p=10^{-5}$ Pa.

1) At what mass of the spacecraft can it fly away from the Solar System?
2) At what maximum mass can the vehicle reach the orbit of Mars, whose radius is $R_M=2,3\cdot 10^8$ km?

The gravitational influence of the Earth and other planets is not taken into account. The product of the solar mass and the gravitational constant is $M_CG=1,3\cdot 10^{11}$ km$^3/c^2$.

V. A. Danilin

F895. One of the hypotheses about the origin of astronomers goes back to the ancient Greek legend about the son of the boron of the sun Helus - Phaethon, struck by Zeus (Jupiter). According to this hypothesis, the rock blocks from which the planet Phaethon was supposed to form came too close to Jupiter. Under the influence of Jupter's gravitational field, the swarm broke up into separate blocks - astronds. The swarm radius was estimated to be approximately $10^4$ km; the mass of the swarm (the total mass of the astronds) is $10^6$ times less than the mass of Jupter.

How far from the center of Jupter would the swarm have to travel before it began to fall apart?

V. E. Belonukkin

F896. In a closed sauce there is water vapor at a temperature of $100^\circ\text{C}$ and remaining water. The mass of steam is $M=100$ g, the mass of water is $m=1$ g. The vessel is heated until all the water has evaporated. To what temperature should the vessel be heated? How much heat will this require? The pressure of saturated water vapor increases by 3.7 kPa with an increase in temperature by $1^\circ\text{C}$. The specific heat of evaporation of water is $r=2,25\cdot 10^6$ J/kg, the specific heat capacity of water vapor is $c_V=1,38\cdot 10^3$ J/(kg K).

A. I. Buzdin

F897. The charged part, located at infinity in a state of rest, is attached to a uniformly charged semicircular water AB (Fig. 5). The ratio of the particle velocities at points B and A is $v_A/v_B=n$. Find the ratio of particle accelerations at these points.

V. T. Karpetyan
