About the aggregate states of matter

The molecular-kinetic theory of the structure of matter - one of the greatest achievements of physical science - is based on three statements: 1) every substance consists of particles - molecules or atoms; 2) particles (molecules, atoms) undergo random thermal motion; 3) particles (molecules, atoms) interact with each other. This theory allows us to understand a wide variety of phenomena, including the fact of the existence of matter in three, as they say, states of aggregation - solid, liquid, gaseous.

Why can collections of perfectly identical particles, for example water molecules (H₂O), form such columnar bodies as ice, water, water vapor? The “blame” for this is the third of the above statements that underlie the molecular-kinetic theory - the statement that forces act between molecules (“Physics 9”, §§ 4 and 5). However, it is more convenient to explain the existence of various states of aggregation using not “force” but “energy” language.

Thermal motion of molecules is characterized by their average kinetic energy. It is known to determine body temperature. For ideal gases, for example, temperature T and the average kinetic energy of translational motion of molecules mv²/2 are related to the following relations:

$$\frac{m v^2}{2} = \frac{3}{2} k T,$$

where k is Boltzmann's constant ($k=1,38 \cdot 10^{-23}$ J/K). Kinetic energy, together with the concentration n of molecules (the number of molecules per unit volume), also determines the second main macrosoppersia parameter of the gas - its pressure p:

$$p = \frac{2}{3} n \frac{m v^2}{2}.$$

If the molecules had only kinetic energy, they would separate in all directions (the movement of molecules is chaotic!) and the substance would occupy the entire volume available to it. The only possible state of matter would be a gaseous state.

The interaction of molecules (like any interaction) is characterized by potential energy. It depends on the distance between the molecules. If molecules had only potential energy, they would occupy such positions relative to each other that the sum of the forces acting on each molecule from its neighbors would be equal to zero. In this case, the potential energy of interaction between molecules would be minimal. The only possible state of matter would be a solid state.
