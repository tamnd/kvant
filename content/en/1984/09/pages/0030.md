Traditional autumn holiday for young mathematicians
(For the beginning, see p. 8)

But even this weather could not disperse the shadowy groups of children who crowded in the mornings on conference days in front of the classes where the conference was taking place. The reports were read simultaneously in three sections - this was due to both their abundance and their diversity in level and topic.

Please, listeners paid the most attention to the guys' reports on their own solutions to interesting difficult problems. For example, Denis Kosygin (Physical School at Moscow State University) proved that the sum of the digits of the numbers of the sequence $2^n$ (and $5^n$) tends to infinity, Elena Shashkova (vocational school no. 13, Leningrad) solved a geometric problem of estimating the width of a door and a corridor, allowing one to drag a table of given dimensions sideways, and Nikolai Grigoriev (s. sh. no. 57, Moscow) spoke about tessellation of figures with segments. Some conference participants presented their achievements in the field of mathematics, which significantly went beyond the scope of the school course - of course, they had to limit themselves to stating the problem and explaining the result. Among these

the most interesting scientific reports were devoted to algebra (for example, Oleg Matveev from Sverdlovsk spoke about estimating the number of roots of polynomials from a matrix, Alexey Rodin from the Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University spoke about a formula for calculating the determinant); The jury also liked the report of Boris Muzikantsky (s. sh. No. 57, Moscow) on some problems of mathematical logic.

An increasing number of schoolchildren are interested in programming; a number of reports in Batumi were related to computational experiments (the work of Sverdlovsk residents), with algorithms in the development of which the speakers took part (several interesting reports on this were presented by members of the club of young programmers "UTA" from Tallinn, in part, they studied models of ring and counter acquaintance or, for example, book exchange).

On the eve of the ceremonial closing of the holiday, friends make up a traditional evening - perhaps the most symbolic point of the program. The guys got away, enriched not only with pleasant, replenishing and new knowledge, but also with new friends from different cities. And the strongest friendships were probably established by those “specialized in dating and exchange” from Tallinn; At the winter kankikhs they have guests from the Faculty of Medical School at Moscow State University.
