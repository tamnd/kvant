Thus, the answer in general problems a) and b):

To substantiate the answer, we must, firstly, prove that for any permutation there is a soldier who will be mixed up at a distance no more than d, and, secondly, give an example of a permutation in which any soldier will be mixed up at this (or greater) distance.

The first part is almost estimable: for any permutation, the central soldier will be mixed along the hypotenuse of a right triangle, whose rolls (parallel sides of the rectangle) do not exceed k and l, that is, at a distance not greater than d=$\sqrt{k^2+l^2}$.

The second part - an example of permutation - is shown in Figures 1-3. In the simplest case, a 2k×2l rectangle is cut into four k×l rectangles, and pairs of rectangles located diagonally (more precisely, the soldiers standing in them) are rearranged with each other using parallel translations. Similarly, the rectangle (2k+1)×l is cut into pairs of permutable rectangles (k+1)×l and k×l. For rectangles (2k+1)×(2l+1), the soldier from the central cell 1 is sent to corner 2, the soldier from 2 to the opposite corner 3, and the soldier from 3 to the center to place 1 (Fig. 3); the rest - standing in two rectangles (k+1)×(l+1) with removed corners and two diagonal rectangles k×l - are swapped, as in the previous options.

Note that if the “distance” is measured not directly, but by the number of moves of the rook (as in the conditions of problem M883 of this issue), then the solution to the problem will not change in any way, and the answer will be (in the same notation) the number d=k+l=$\left[\frac{m}{2}\right] + \left[\frac{n}{2}\right]$.

**M867. At a dance lesson, 17 boys and 17 girls lined up in two parallel rows to form 17 pairs. At the same time, the height of the boy and girl in each pala differs by no more than ten centimeters. Prove that if in each clan the boys and girls are overgrown in height, then in each clan the boys and girls will still differ in height by no more than ten centimeters.**

Let the boys and girls be built in pairs in descending order of height. Suppose that in one of the pairs, say the k-th, the boy’s height differs from the girl’s height by more than 10 cm, for example, the boy (let’s call him B) is taller than the girl (G). Then B, and therefore all the first k boys, is higher than the girls following G by more than 10 cm. Thus, during the first formation in pairs with this k boys, only the girls preceding G could become boys, and there are only k—1 of them. Contradiction.
