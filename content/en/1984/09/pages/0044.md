Let's find $\Delta h$:

$$h_1 = \frac{g' t_1^2}{2}, \quad h_2 = \frac{g' t_2^2}{2}, \quad \Delta h = h_1 - h_2 = \frac{g'}{2} (t_1^2 - t_2^2).$$

Thus,

$$t_{\min} = \sqrt{\frac{t_1^2 - t_2^2}{2}}.$$

P. M. Shikhaliev

F879. A Baeron of mass $M$, mounted on springs with a stiffness $k$, rolls along a horizontal section of the track at a speed $v_0$. After passing a straight inclined section of the path with a height difference $H$, making an angle a with the horizon (Fig. 1), the input acquires a speed $v_x$. Determine the value of $v_x$, neglecting the friction in the wheels. Consider that the period of oscillations in the fire on the springs is much less than the time it takes to pass the inclined section of the track and much longer than the time it takes to pass the twists that accompany the inclined and straight sections of the track; the springs “spring” only in the direction perpendicular to the plane of the rails.

When the carriage passes rounded, vibrations occur. In this case, part of the kinetic energy of the car turns into oscillation energy, which, when the oscillations die out, turns into the body. Thus,

$$\frac{M v_x^2}{2} + U = \frac{M v_0^2}{2} + MgH,$$

(*)

where $U$ is the vibration energy. Let's define $U$.

When passing the first rounding, the load on the springs decreases, since the normal pressure force becomes equal to $F_2 = Mg \cos \alpha$, and not $F_1 = Mg$, as it was on the straight section. When passing the second rounding, the load increases to its previous value. During the descent along the inclined section, the oscillations cause them to die out, so both processes should be considered independent.

Let us consider a spring rolled by force $F = F_1$ and the acceleration being in equilibrium. The length of the outside is less than the length of the unloaded spring by $x_1 = F_1/k$. When the load is weakened to a value of $F_2 < F_1$, oscillations occur around a new equilibrium position corresponding to the rolling of the spring to $x_2 = F_2/k$. After the oscillations have damped, the spring is established in this equilibrium position. The total work of the elastic forces of the spring, equal to the difference in elastic energies in the first and second positions, is

$$A_{\text{упр}} = \frac{1}{2} (F_1 x_1 - F_2 x_2) = \frac{k}{2} (x_1^2 - x_2^2) = \frac{1}{2k} (F_1^2 - F_2^2)$$

(at figure 2 of this work corresponds to a trapezoid platform protected by a vertical one). If the weakening of the load occurs in a time shorter than the period of oscillation, then we can assume that during the change in coordinate the normal pressure force is always equal to the approximation $F_2$, so the work against the load forces is equal to

$$A_{\text{нарр}} = F_2 (x_1 - x_2)$$

(at figure 2 of this value corresponds to the site of a horizontal protected rectangle). The difference between the two calculated work of oscillation energy transferred to the body during damping of oscillations, that is

$$U_1 = \frac{(F_1 + F_2) (F_1 - F_2)}{2k} - \frac{F_2 (F_1 - F_2)}{k} = \frac{(F_1 - F_2)^2}{2k}$$
