A solid state is a state in which the potential energy $W_p$ of the interaction of molecules is overwhelming compared to the kinetic energy $W_k$:

$$W_p \gg W_k.$$

How can this condition be changed? For example, heating a solid. It is clear that when heated, the kinetic energy of a body part increases, but their potential energy practically does not change. In this way, it is possible to ensure that the kinetic energy becomes approximately equal to the potential energy. Then a transition from the solid to the liquid state will occur, which is characterized by the approximate equality of $W_p$ and $W_k$:

$$W_p \approx W_k.$$

In this state, the potential energy is still high enough so that the molecules cannot fly apart from each other, and the kinetic energy has already reached high enough for the molecules to acquire a certain mobility, that is, the ability to move within the volume. The liquid retains its volume, but becomes flowing and therefore does not retain its shape.

We will continue to supply energy, heating the liquid now. The kinetic energy of the molecules will grow and at some point exceed the potential energy of their current interaction, so that it (the potential energy) can be exceeded:

$$W_p \ll W_k.$$

Six will go into a state that corresponds to an ideal gas (“Physics 9”, § 6).

What if we continue heating, now with gas? Will a further increase in the kinetic energy of molecules lead to another aggregate transition? It turns out yes. But only because molecules (atoms) are not just very small balls. They are complex systems of charged parts, even smaller than atoms. When the gas reaches a high temperature, the collisions of its parts lead to the fact that the molecules disintegrate into atoms, and the atoms into their constituent charged particles.

The result is a special kind of substance, consisting of an equal number of positively and negatively charged particles (in general, electron neutral). This is the fourth state of matter - plasma.

Unlike other states of aggregation, plasma consists of electron interactions between two parts. This is why the properties of plasma differ significantly from the properties of matter in other states.

Oscillatory movement is a periodic movement, that is, one in which the coordinate of the body is repeated at certain intervals of time (the minimum interval is called the oscillation period). A typical example of oscillations is the movement of a body attached to a circle (“Physics 10”, §§ 2 and 4). This movement of the layer is caused by elasticity $F_x = -kx$, changing from point to point, so the acceleration of the body also changes. When moving, a oscillating body also passes through a position in which the force acting on it becomes zero. This is the equilibrium position; it serves as the center of oscillation. The maximum deviation of the body from the equilibrium position is called the vibration amplitude.
