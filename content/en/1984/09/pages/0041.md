V. L. Gutenacher, N. B. Vasiliev

M868. From the vertices, the bases of the tetrahedron in the lateral faces are drawn higher. Prove that three straight lines connecting the bases of the heights in each face are parallel to the same plane. (Plane angles at a vertex are not right angles.)

Let us present three solutions to the problem. Two of them rely on the following useful fact:

if in triangle ADB (in which ol D is not a straight line) we connect the bases of the altitudes AK and BL, then a triangle KDL is formed, similar to Danin’s, with DKL=DAB, DLK=DBA (Fig. 1).

(Indeed, let ADB = φ be acute. Then for the triangles under consideration it is common, and the sides containing it are proportional:

$$\frac{|DK|}{|DL|} = \frac{|DA| \cdot \cos \varphi}{|DB| \cdot \cos \varphi} = \frac{|DA|}{|DB|}.$$

In the case of an obtuse angle ADB, the reasoning is similar.)

First decision. Let ABCD be the given tetrahedron and ABC its base. Let us prove that the three lines from the statement are parallel to the plane that touches the circumscribed sphere of the tetrahedron at vertex D. Obviously, each of the lateral faces of the tetrahedron intersects this plane tangent to the circumscribed circle of this face, so it is enough to prove that these three lines are parallel to the corresponding tangents.

Consider, for example, the face ABD; let AK and BL be the altitudes of this face, DE be the tangent to its circumcircle (Fig. 2). Then

$$\widehat{ABK} = \frac{1}{2} \widehat{AD} = \widehat{ADE}$$

(the first equality follows from the theorem on the measurement of an angle inscribed in a circle, the second - from the theorem on the angle between a tangent and a chord). So, angles DBA, DLK and ADE are equal in magnitude, therefore lines KL and DE are parallel.

Their parallelism also follows from the following useful theorem: in any triangle ADB, the radius DO of the circumcircle and the height DH form identical angles with the lateral sides: ADO=HDB (Fig. 3).

Second solution. Let a, b, c be the lengths of the edges DA, DB, DC of the tetrahedron. Let us plot on the rays DA, DB, DC the segments DA1, DB1, DC1, equal in length to the product bc, ca, ab, respectively.

Let us prove that the lines described in the conditions are parallel to the lines A1B1, B1C1 and C1A1 (thereby parallel to the plane A1B1C1).

Let us consider, as before, the ABD facet. Triangles KLD and B1A1D are similar, since according to (*)

$$\frac{|DK|}{|DL|} = \frac{|DA|}{|DB|} = \frac{a}{b} = \frac{ca}{bc} = \frac{|DB_1|}{|DA_1|}.$$

therefore, lines KL and A1B1 are parallel.
