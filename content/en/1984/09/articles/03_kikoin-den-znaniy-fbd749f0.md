Day of Knowledge

Dear friends, readers «Kvant»!
I congratulate you and, in your person, all schoolchildren of the Soviet Union on the holiday - the beginning of the school year, which by decision of the Soviet government was declared the Day of Knowledge.

Knowledge! Think about the deep meaning contained in this word. Without knowledge, nothing can be done. An old Russian proverb says: “Don’t forget and weave bast shoes.” Many of you may not even know what bast shoes are, but several decades ago it was one of the cheapest and most accessible types of education in the Russian village.

In our country, human knowledge is given special importance. A Soviet citizen must have a large stock of knowledge in a variety of areas of human activity.

A person has a driving institute of curiosity. The need for knowledge is essential for a person of any age. In the Soviet country, all conditions have been created to satisfy this need. Our main source of knowledge is school. Our state has passed a law on free education in primary, secondary and higher schools.

It is a great happiness to learn something new every day in various fields of human knowledge, be it mathematics, physics, literature, chemistry, etc. The solution to a difficult problem that is found gives great satisfaction to one’s self. Successfully completing a community assignment on a classroom or even school scale brings just as much joy.

When I remember my school years, they represent to me the brightest and most joyful period of my childhood and youth. My friends and I spent most of the day at school, and during the holidays we dreamed of the day when we would again find ourselves in our native classroom smelling of fresh red and immerse ourselves in the vaccine-rich school “everyday life.” I would like the school to become a second home for you, and your studies would bring you the joy of learning about the world around you.

The Communist Party and the Soviet government have always paid greater attention to the issue of schooling. Recently, the highest body of state power - the Supreme Soviet of the USSR - adopted a resolution on the reform of school education.

The law provides for a number of measures that should bring the level of school education in accordance with the new requirements put forward by various people in our society. Modern science and technology are developing new means of industrial and agricultural production, which are recognized to dramatically increase labor productivity. This will lead to an increase in the output of the national economy and, as a result, to a further improvement in the well-being of the Soviet people.

You, the future graduate of the school, will be represented by participation in the implementation of scientific and technological progress. This is a very exciting task. Perhaps you will be lucky and you will discover new patterns in natural phenomena, develop new methods for solving physical and mathematical problems or new technical techniques. It all depends on you, on your attitude towards school work.

Editor-in-Chief of the magazine «Kvant» Academician I. K. Kikoin
