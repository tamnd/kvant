F878. The two main gutters, forming equal angles $a$ with the horizon, are located in the same vertical plane (Fig. 1). From point $A$ of one zone and from point $B$ of another zone, simultaneously slide two bodies together without initial velocities. The time of movement to the horizontal plane of the first body (from point $A$) is $t_1$, the second is $t_2$. How long after the body began to move was the distance between them the smallest?

When bodies move, the difference in heights at which they will be at the same moment in time will always be the same and equal (see Fig. 1) $\Delta h = h_1 - h_2$. Consequently, the distance between the bodies will be minimal when they are on the same vertical, that is, the horizontal distance between the bodies will be zero. Since the initial velocities of both bodies are equal to zero and the angles of inclination of their trajectories to the horizon are the same, it is clear that the bodies appear on the same vertical at the moment when, having traveled the same paths, the bodies shift along each vertical by $\Delta h/2$. So the time $t_{\min}$, through which the distance between the bodies will be minimal, is the time during which the body from the initial point will shift in height by $\Delta h/2$, that is

$$\frac{g' t_{\min}^2}{2} = \frac{\Delta h}{2} \Rightarrow t_{\min} = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta h}{g'}}$$

where $g'$ is the vertical projection of the acceleration of bodies, which will be found, use Newton’s II law: in projections onto the vertical axis (see Fig. 2)

$$mg' = mg - mg \cos^2 \alpha \Rightarrow g' = g \sin^2 \alpha.$$

$$h_1 = \frac{g' t_1^2}{2}, \quad h_2 = \frac{g' t_2^2}{2}, \quad \Delta h = h_1 - h_2 = \frac{g'}{2} (t_1^2 - t_2^2).$$

F881. Two thermal processes are performed over the gas, heating it from a homogeneous state to the same final temperature. On a p-V diagram, processes are depicted by straight lines (see figure). In which process is more heat imparted to the gas?

According to the first law of thermodynamics, when a gas passes from the state $A$ ($p_0, V_0$) to the state $B$ ($p_1, V_1$)

$$\Delta Q_1 = \Delta U_1 + A_1,$$

and

$$A_1 = \frac{p_0 + p_1}{2} \left( V_1 - V_0 \right).$$

When a gas passes from state $A$ to state $C$ ($p_2, V_2$)

$$\Delta Q_2 = \Delta U_2 + A_2, \quad A_2 = \frac{p_0 + p_2}{2} \left( V_2 - V_0 \right).$$

Since the final temperature of the gas in state $B$ and in state $C$ is the same, $\Delta U_1 = \Delta U_2$. To find out in which process a greater amount of heat is imparted to the gas, it is necessary to compare the values ​​of $A_1$ and $A_2$. Let's follow this:

$$A_1 - A_2 = \frac{1}{2} \left( \left( p_0 V_1 - p_0 V_2 \right) + \left( p_2 V_0 - p_1 V_0 \right) \right) < 0,$$

since $p_0 V_1 < p_0 V_2$, $p_2 V_0 < p_1 V_0$. Consequently, $A_2 > A_1$ and $\Delta Q_2 > \Delta Q_1$, that is, in the process $A \rightarrow C$ a greater amount of heat is imparted to the gas.
