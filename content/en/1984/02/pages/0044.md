F858. After the football player hit, the ball flew towards the goal, located at a distance of $L=32$ m, at a speed of $v=25$ m/s at an angle of $\alpha$ to the horizon ($\cos \alpha=0,8$). Due to the side wind blowing along the goal perpendicular to the speed $\vec{v}$, the horizontal displacement of the ball in the plane of the goal turned out to be equal to $s=2$ m. Find the time of flight of the ball to the plane of the goal. The ball does not spin; wind speed $u=10$ m/s.

V. I. Chivilev

F859. A ball of radius $R$ and mass $m$ moves along a horizontal surface under the action of a constant tension force $\vec{F}$ of a light non-tensile thread. The point of attachment of the thread is at a height $h$ above the surface (Fig. 2), the thread is horizontal all the time, the ball moves forward. At what maximum friction coefficient is this possible? What is the uniform acceleration of the ball?

L. G. Markovich

F860. In the Konchek non-conducting hole with an angle $2\alpha$ at the apex at a depth $h$ there are two small equally charged bodies connected by a thread (Fig. 3); the mass of each body is $m$. After burning out, the threads of the body begin to slide up the surface of the hole and fly out of the hole at a height of $H$ above its base with a speed of $v$. Body friction coefficient at the top of the hole $\mu=a \text{tg} \alpha$ ($a<1$). Determine the charges of bodies.

O. Ya. Savchenko

F861. In an oscillatory RLC circuit, the resistance is low, so the oscillations are weakly damped in it. At what relative change in the length of the coil $\Delta l/l$ will the oscillations in the circuit be undamped? Consider the inductance of the coil to be inversely proportional to its length.

A. I. Kirkinsky
