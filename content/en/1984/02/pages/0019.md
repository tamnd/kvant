However, let's leave such harsh operations and see what happens to ametnus in natural conditions. First of all, thanks to the ability to break and grow together, ametnus can take the form of a tape twisted by any odd number of half-turns (Fig. 6). (Note that a strip twisted by an integer number of revolutions does not correspond to any film, since it has two closed curves as its boundary.) In fact, having been torn across, it can unwind by any integer number of revolutions, and then grow together in the old place (trace this on a strip of paper). In the same way, ametnus can tie in any knot and tie again (Fig. 6).
