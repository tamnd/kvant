So familiar and so amazing water!

Doctor of Pedagogical Sciences
N. A. RODINA

If all parts of the surface of the globe are sequentially formed, then 70% of the snares will look like the photograph on page 35 - the surface of the Earth is more than 2/3 covered with water.

The oceans account for the bulk of water; it is contained in lakes, rivers, underground and in the air. The supply of water in the atmosphere - in the form of steam - is twice the resources of all rivers.

The average depth of the oceans is 3800 m, the deepest is the so-called Marinsky Trench in the Pacific Ocean - about 11,000 m. If you level off the land and reach the oceans so that the Earth becomes exactly a ball, then its entire surface will be covered with a layer of water about 2500 m thick.

Knowing this, and also taking into account that the average radius of the Earth is 6400 km, we can calculate the approximate volume of water on Earth and the radius of the “drop” that water would form if it were all collected in water.

Of course, it can be shown, and you can do it yourself, that the volume of water is still only a small part of the total volume of the Earth. But of all the planets in the solar system (and in it, in addition to the nine large planets, there are also thousands of small planets - asteroides), not one can compare with the Earth in terms of total water. For example, Venus and Mars have dry surfaces that are not found anywhere else on Earth. Even the Sahara desert would seem like just a swamp compared to them.

Without water, life on our planet would be impossible. It is found in animal organisms and plants. A person annually consumes about two tons of water. We get it all the time; we need it just like air.
