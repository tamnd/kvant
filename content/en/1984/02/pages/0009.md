To understand the essence of Grifficis's theory, you need to remember how the deformation of a solid body occurs. During deformation, as is known, an elastic force $F$ arises. The stress σ is equal to the ratio of the magnitude of this force to the area $S$ of the cross section of the body: $\sigma = F/S$. On the other hand, Stress is determined by the magnitude of the relative deformation of the body $\varepsilon = \Delta l/l$ (Δl is the change in body length, $l$ is the initial length). For each material there is a limiting strain value $\varepsilon_{\text{max}}$ that it can withstand and a corresponding Stress value $\sigma_{\text{np}}$ (readability limit) (see the textbook “Physics 9”).

Grifficis establishes that the strength of thin glass filaments can be hundreds of times greater than the strength of object (say, window) glass. He suggested that massive glass products have very small, invisible cracks, which sharply reduce their strength.

It is well known that any response in a deformed body causes a concentration of stress. The picture of the stressed state can be visualized using “force” lines, the density of which is proportional to the stress in the materials. If there is a response in the body, then the lines of force bypass it (after all, the void cannot bear the load) and are damped at its edges (figure 1), where the material is overloaded. The average stress σ in the section does not change, since otherwise the equilibrium conditions of the body would be violated.

The magnitude of the “concentrated” stress depends on the shape of the response, its size and the radius of curvature of its edges. For example, at the edges of a branch having an elliptical cross-section, the stress increases to the value

$$\sigma_{\text{конц}} = \alpha \sigma,$$

where $\sigma$ is the voltage away from the response, and $\alpha$ is the so-called stress concentration factor. The value of $\alpha$ is determined by the length of the branch and the radius of curvature at the edges. As calculations show, for an elliptic response

$$\alpha = 1 + 2\sqrt{\frac{c}{q}},$$

where $c$ is half the length of the branch, $q$ is the radius of curvature at the edges.
