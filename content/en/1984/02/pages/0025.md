Yakov Ilyich Frenkel

(on the occasion of his 90th birthday)

The name of the outstanding Soviet physicist Ya. I. Frenkel is firmly associated with the goal of a number of physical effects. Before we briefly talk about them, here are some data from his biography.

Yakov Ilyich was born on February 10, 1894 in Rostov-on-Don. Since 1909, his life has always been associated with the city on the Neva. In 1913, he graduated from a well-known gymnasium in St. Petersburg with a gold medal. By this time, the young man was completing his first independent work in physics, which was highly appreciated by Professor A.F. Ioffe. After graduating from the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Petrograd University, Ya. I. Frenkel remained with him to prepare for professorship. From the beginning of 1918, he taught physics at Tavrichi (Simferopol) University. In 1921, Frenkel moved to Petrograd and began working at the Physico-Technical Institute (PTI) and at the Faculty of Physics and Mechanics of the Polytechnic Institute. Soon he becomes the head of the theoretical department of the Physics Institute and the head of the department of theoretical physics in physics. The entire thirty-year scientific and pedagogical activity of Yakov Ilcha is connected with both of these institutions, geographically located two steps away from each other.

Ya. I. Frenkel devoted his efforts to developing a wide range of problems. Let us name just some of the results he obtained.

He was the first to introduce the idea of ​​the quantitative-translational nature of the motion of molecules in liquids and built the foundations of the kinetic theory of liquids (works 1926-44).

Introduced the concept of crystal lattice defects (1926). “Frenkel defects” (or “Frenkel pairs”) are a combination of an empty node in the lattice - a hole - and an atoma that has passed from this node into the space between the node. The processes of diffusion, heat and electrical conductivity in solids are inextricably linked with it.

The Frenkel exton, the quantum of emergence, is a bound state of an electron and a positive hole capable of moving through a crystal (proposed in 1931). Exton physics is now one of the most rapidly developing areas of solid state physics.

To describe the processes occurring in their address, Frenkel proposed using the concepts of statistical physics and thermodynamics, considering the nucleus as a system of a large number of particles (nucleons). He (independently of N. Bohr) created a droplet model of the nucleus (Bohr-Frenkel model, 1936). The concept of core temperature was introduced, the emission of particles from the core was interpreted in terms of evaporation, and absorption - condensation. These communication processes turned out to be unusually fruitful and gave especially valuable results after the discovery of uranium fission (1939). Ya. I. Frenkel (simultaneously with N. Bohr and J. Wheeler) developed the theory of fission of heavy nuclei.

A romantic scientist, like Ya. I. Frenkel, by the very nature of his mind can find common features in various and seemingly far from each other phenomena, connecting the distant with the close, establishing the features of the new in the old. This artistic vision of the world was greatly facilitated by the fact that Yakov Ilch owned a brush: his portraits and landscapes were more than once exhibited on ledges and reproduced in various books and magazines. He was a good violinist, had a keen sense of music and performed it with exceptional soulfulness.

The author of our country's first complete course of theoretical physics, Yakov Ilch Frenkel, performed fundamental work not only in the field of liquid and solid physics and nuclear physics, but also in classical and quantum mechanics, astrophysics, geophysics and biophysics.

V. Ya. Ilyin
