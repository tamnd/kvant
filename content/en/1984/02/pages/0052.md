How deep will it be if it has the shape of a cube? ball?

thickness $\Delta h$ such that within each fluid density can be considered constant (see figure). Then the weight of the liquid in the volume of the cube is equal to

$$P = gS \cdot \Delta h \left( \varrho_1 + \varrho_2 + \dots + \varrho_n \right)$$

($S$ is the area of ​​the cube face). Since the density of the mixture varies linearly with depth, the sum in parentheses is $(\varrho_1 + \varrho_n)n/2$. If $\varrho_1$ is the density at the top face of the cube, and $\varrho_n$ is at the bottom, then

$$\varrho_1 = \varrho_0 + a \left( H - \frac{a}{2} \right), \quad \varrho_n = \varrho_0 + a \left( H + \frac{a}{2} \right),$$

where $a$ is the length of the cube edge. Thus,

$$P = gS \cdot \Delta h \cdot n \frac{\varrho_1 + \varrho_n}{2} = gSa(\varrho_0 + aH) = gV(\varrho_0 + aH).$$

Now from the equilibrium condition of the cube -

$$mg = gV(\varrho_0 + aH)$$

— let’s find the depth of its immersion:

$$H = \frac{m - \varrho_0 V}{aV}.$$

In a similar way, you can find the immersion depth of the ball. Therefore, you will ensure that the ball is immersed in the mixture to the same depth as the cube.

From the above solution it is clear that if a body of mass $m$ and volume $V$ has such a shape that the areas of its horizontal sections taken at the same vertical distance from some point $O$ of the body are equal, then when the body is completely immersed in the mixture, point $O$ will be at a depth $H = \frac{m - \varrho_0 V}{aV}$.

F845. A small bump got inside the chamber of the automatic wheel of radius $R$. At what minimum speed will the automatic kameik rotate with the wheel if the coefficient of friction between the kameik and the camera is $\mu$?

Three forces act on the inner chamber kameik (see figure): gravity $mg$, normal reaction force $\vec{N}$ and friction force $\vec{F}$. In order for the kameik to rotate together with the wheel, these forces must impart to it a centripetal acceleration $v^2/R$, where $v$ is the linear speed of rotation of the wheel, equal to the speed of automatic movement (the wheels rotate without slipping).

Let us write down the equation of motion of the kameika in projections on the axes $X$ and $Y$ (see figure):

$$F = mg \sin (\pi - a) = 0,$$

$$N + mg \cos (\pi - a) = mv^2/R.$$

From equation (1) we find:

$$F = mg \sin a.$$

The absolute value of the friction force cannot be greater than $\mu N$, and therefore $N \geqslant F/\mu$. The minimum value $v$ corresponds, as can be seen from equation (2), to $N = \frac{F}{\mu} = \frac{m}{\mu} g \sin a$. Taking this into account we get:

$$v_{\text{min}}^2 = Rg \left( \frac{\sin a}{\mu} - \cos a \right).$$

The expression in parentheses is minimal when the arbitrary function $f(a) = \frac{\sin a}{\mu} - \cos a$ is equal to zero, that is

$$\frac{\cos a}{\mu} + \sin a = 0 \Rightarrow -\mu = \text{ctg} a.$$

Then

$$\sin a = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \text{ctg}^2 a}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}.$$
