**M835***. Pshakhmatovs from country $A$ and pshakhmatovs from country $B$ came to the one-round chess tournament. It turned out that no matter how you divide the chess players into pairs (cheats are different from different countries and chess players from different countries), there will be at least one pair of chess players who have already met before others with another. Prove that it is possible to choose a chess from country $A$ and pchess from country $B$ such that each of the chosen a chess meets each of the chosen chess $a + b > n$.

We carry out the proof by induction on $n$.

For $n = 1$ the statement of the problem is obvious ($a = b = n = 1$). Now we need to prove it for $n = k$, assuming that it is true for all $n < k$.

In the case where each of the $k$ chess players from country $A$ has previously met each player from $B$, there is nothing to prove ($a = b = k$). Therefore, we will assume that somehow two chess games - $a$ from $A$ and $B$ - have not met each other. Then the remaining chess ones satisfy the conditions of the problem for $n = k - 1$. (If you make pairs from them and add ($a, \beta$) to them, then by condition one of these pairs, but obviously not in ($a, \beta$), will get the encountered chess ones.) By the assumption of indyktion, you can reverse the group $A$ from $a_1$ chess of the country $A$ and the group $B$ from $b_1$ of chess of the country $B$ so that every chess player of group $A$ met each of $B$ and $a_1 + b_1 > k - 1$. If it turns out that $a_1 + b_1 > k$, everything is in order ($a = a_1, b = b_1$). It remains to consider case $a_1 + b_1 = k$; reasoning is illustrated by figure in the margin.

Let $A$ be a group of $k - a_1 = b_1$ chess from $A$ not included in the group $A$, $B$ be a group of $B$ chess not included in $B$. We will organize two new tournaments - for groups $A$ and $B$ ($n = a_1$) and $A$ and $B$ ($n = b_1$). One of these tourers (for definition, taking into account groups $A$ and $B$) satisfies the conditions of the problem. (Otherwise, it is possible to make pairs from chess groups $A$ and $B$, and independently from groups $A$ and $B$, such that none of these pairs will contain the encountered chess ones, and this contradicts the condition for the “common” tour.) By the induction assumption, in the group $A$ one can find $a_2$ chess, each of which met each of these $a_1 + b_2 > a_1$. Additionally, by selecting groups $A$ and $B$ one can find these $a = a_2$ chess met with each of the $b_1$ chess groups $B$. So we can select $a = a_2$ chess from the country $A$ and $b = b_1 + b_2$
