To find the time when a calla is placed, let us imagine that at the same time a calla is hidden from each point of the wheel, and consider the movement of these callas in a reporting system falling with acceleration $g$. In this system, the calli moves by inertia with a speed of $v = \omega R$. After time $t$ they will be on a circle of radius

$$r = \sqrt{R^2 + v^2 t^2}$$

(Fig. 2). To find the position of the calli in a fixed reporting system connected to the ground, this circle must be lowered by a distance

$$s = gt^2/2$$.

It is clear that at the moment when $r + s = H$, the calla must reach the point $B$ on the ground. From the condition

$$\sqrt{R^2 + v^2 t^2} + \frac{gt^2}{2} = H$$

we find:

$$t_{\text{пад}}^2 = \frac{2}{g^2} \left((v^2 + gH) \pm \sqrt{v^4 + 2gHv^2 + g^2R^2}\right).$$

In this formula, only the minus sign determines us, since the second root corresponds to the arrival at the point $B$ of the calla that is located at the point $N$ of the circle (see Fig. 2), and this calla is delayed by the wheel. Substituting $v = \omega R$, we find:

$$t_{\text{пад}} = \sqrt{2(\omega^2 R^2 + gH - R \sqrt{\omega^4 R^2 + 2gH\omega^2 + g^2R^2})}$$

(*)

Knowing the time it takes for the calla to reach point $B$, it is easy to find the point of its separation from the wheel. Indeed, in a falling reporting system, this calla, after time $t = t_{\text{пад}}$ after separation, should end up at point $M$ of the circle (see Fig. 2). Consequently, it comes off at point $A$ such that

$$tg a = \frac{vt_{\text{пад}}}{R} = \omega t_{\text{пад}},$$

where $t_{\text{пад}}$ is defined by formula (*).
