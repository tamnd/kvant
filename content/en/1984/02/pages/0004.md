⟦folio 2⟧

Mathematical creativity and social progress

Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B.V. GNEDENKO

Social progress is primarily associated not with the number of products produced, but with the general new ideas that change the productive forces and social consciousness, and open up new paths in science and production. Of course, for social progress, new ideas alone are not enough; their implementation, their material implementation into action, is also required.

The creation of a new thing is always, to some extent, the destruction of the old, already established. That is why it takes so long and difficult for fundamentally new concepts in science and unusual approaches to solving practical problems to emerge and develop their way.

This is excellently described in the recent article by A. D. Aleksadrov “Dupidity and genius” (see «Kvant», No. 11, pp. 12-17 and No. 12, pp. 7-12 and 15 for 1982), dedicated to the history of the discovery of Lobachevsky’s non-Euclidean geometry. When reading this article, there is only one wish that readers refer to the words “Dupidity” not to talented people who discover and discover new things in the boundless ocean of the unknown, but to those who are ready to categorically judge everything, even what they do not understand, and close the way to everything new and promising.

In order for humanity to move forward along the path of progress, it needs a constant flow of courageous minds, capable of seeing in the inherent and established the possibility of improvement, who are not afraid of making decisions in the name of the best. That is why the atmosphere of creativity, the search for new solutions and the formulation of fundamentally new problems is so important for young people.

The years of school and university education have a unique feature, since it is then that a person finds true passions, enriches his mind with a huge amount of knowledge, sets life goals for himself and begins to test his creative powers. It is at this time that the question will arise for young people: what can I add to what the genini has passed for humanity? Do I have the ability and talent for this? The answers to these questions can be determined later in a person's life. Society is extremely interested in its members getting accustomed to independent thinking, to discovering new things, to creativity, to improving what is already known.
