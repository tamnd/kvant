It follows that any root of the trinomial $ax^2+x+3$ is the root of the polynomial on the left side; therefore this polynomial is divisible by $ax^2-x+3$. Sweeping. Equality (1) shows that if we put $f(x)=ax^2+3$, then the polynomial on the left side of the inequality will have the form $f(f(x))-x$. It is easy to prove that in this case the equation $f(f(x))=x$ is equivalent to the equation $f(x)=x$. Having solved the last quadratic equation, we can further apply the method of integrals to this inequality.
