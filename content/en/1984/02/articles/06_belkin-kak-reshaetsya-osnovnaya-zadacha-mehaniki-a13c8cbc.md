How is the main problem of mechanics solved?

The main task of mechanics, as repeatedly stated in the textbook “Physics 8,” is to determine the position (coordinates) of a moving body at any moment in time. Mechanics must “be able” to do this, otherwise it cannot be the science of motion. The laws of motion discovered by Newton, and the former Newton’s second law (the fundamental law of motion) $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$, allow us to solve this problem.

Newton's second law relates a force applied to a body and causes an acceleration force. But acceleration is a rapid change in speed, and speed, in turn, is a rapid change in the displacement of a body. Therefore, having solved the equation expressed by the basic law of the diagram, we learned about the rapid change in the coordinates of the body, and therefore about the coordinates itself at any moment in time. To do this, in addition to the force, you also need to know the initial conditions - the initial coordinates and the initial speed of the body.

At first glance, it seems that the equation of Newton’s second law is very simple and that its solution is also simple. However, we must remember that the equation $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ is a vector one. This means that it “hides” three scalar equations for the projection of forces and acceleration on the coordinate axes:

$$F_x = ma_x,$$
$$F_y = ma_y,$$
$$F_z = ma_z.$$

These equations are indeed easy to solve, but only in the case when the force projections $F_x, F_y$ and $F_z$ are constructed, that is, when their values ​​are the same for any values ​​of the coordinates of the point, and therefore at any time. Then the values ​​of the acceleration projections $a_x, a_y$ and $a_z$ are constant, the motion of the body is therefore equal to the acceleration, and the coordinates $x, y$ and $z$ are determined by the equalities

$$x = x_0 + v_{0x} t + \frac{a_x t^2}{2},$$
$$y = y_0 + v_{0y} t + \frac{a_y t^2}{2},$$
$$z = z_0 + v_{0z} t + \frac{a_z t^2}{2}.$$

Here $x_0, y_0$ and $z_0$ are the initial coordinates, and $v_{0x}, v_{0y}$ and $v_{0z}$ are the corresponding projections of the initial velocity on the coordinate axes.

In a school physics course, only such problems are considered when the forces are constructed. A significant solution to the main problem of mechanics, if the force and its projection depends on the coordinates. In reality there are usually both above. How is the problem solved in this case?

Of course, now we need to start the initial conditions and strength. But now “to know the force” means to know how it changes when the coordinates of the body change.

Let, for example, a force $F$ act on a body, which is determined (and in a known way) depends on the coordinate $x$. To determine the coordinate $x$ of a body at any moment of time, the previous formulas are inappropriate, because if the force $F_x$ changes from point to point, the acceleration $a_x$ of the body will also change. We will solve the problem gradually, so to speak, step by step.
