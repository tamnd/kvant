Model of a panel of a typical residential building, studied by the photoelastic method. A clear interface picture is visible on the screen, from which one can determine the distribution of deformations and directions that arose during the load (the load is applied at four points).

The photoelasticity method, or polarization-optical method, allows you to study the process of occurrence of deformations and directions caused by immi in a structure.

The idea of ​​the photoelasticity method was developed back in the last century. In 1815, Scottish physicist David Brewster discovered that when certain deformed substances were illuminated with polarized light, a bright interface pattern was observed on a screen placed behind the illuminated sample. Brewster proposed that the location and configuration of the interface stripes are determined by the deformations that occur in the sample as a result of the action of the load.

Later, in the work of Maxwell, Fresnel and other scientists, an exact mathematical relationship was established between the optical phenomena observed in deformed materials and the mechanical deformations and directions existing in them. Thus, it became possible to “decipher” the pattern of stripes and translate it into a picture of the distribution of deformations and directions in the object under study. And such an object can be a model of a place, the wall of a building, a section of a dam, a ship’s bulkhead, etc.
