Mathematical creativity and social progress

Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR B.V. GNEDENKO

Social progress is primarily associated not with the number of products produced, but with the general new ideas that change the productive forces and social consciousness, and open up new paths in science and production. Of course, for social progress, new ideas alone are not enough; their implementation, their material implementation into action, is also required.

The creation of a new thing is always, to some extent, the destruction of the old, already established. That is why it takes so long and difficult for fundamentally new concepts in science and unusual approaches to solving practical problems to emerge and develop their way.

This is excellently described in the recent article by A. D. Aleksadrov “Dupidity and genius” (see «Kvant», No. 11, pp. 12-17 and No. 12, pp. 7-12 and 15 for 1982), dedicated to the history of the discovery of Lobachevsky’s non-Euclidean geometry. When reading this article, there is only one wish that readers refer to the words “Dupidity” not to talented people who discover and discover new things in the boundless ocean of the unknown, but to those who are ready to categorically judge everything, even what they do not understand, and close the way to everything new and promising.

In order for humanity to move forward along the path of progress, it needs a constant flow of courageous minds, capable of seeing in the inherent and established the possibility of improvement, who are not afraid of making decisions in the name of the best. That is why the atmosphere of creativity, the search for new solutions and the formulation of fundamentally new problems is so important for young people.

The years of school and university education have a unique feature, since it is then that a person finds true passions, enriches his mind with a huge amount of knowledge, sets life goals for himself and begins to test his creative powers. It is at this time that the question will arise for young people: what can I add to what the genini has passed for humanity? Do I have the ability and talent for this? The answers to these questions can be determined later in a person's life. Society is extremely interested in its members getting accustomed to independent thinking, to discovering new things, to creativity, to improving what is already known.

At school and at university there are students who cope with the most difficult questions with play, while for others they are insurmountable challenges. But are these the only teachers called upon to say a new word in science, or will the most diligent ones do it? In fact, everything is not so simple here.

I have observed many times that many mathematics students who passed their exams excellently did not become creative mathematicians. And this is an accident not because they did not make an effort, did not strive to create something new. They just didn’t succeed; they seemed to be deprived of creativity. At the same time, often students who did not shine during exams, who were considered far from unfamiliar with one or another area of ​​mathematics, became the authors of excellent works, created either new directions of mathematical research, or proved excellent theorems. As it turned out, a creative fire burned in them, they had a house of comparison, the ability to look at things from a different perspective, as a result of which they replaced the new and interesting where many specialists passed by, not seeing anything personal for science.

In one of the articles by academician A. N. Kolmogorov, “Nuka requires combustion” (Izvestia, 1962, February 21), it is written: “Talent, giftedness, say, in the field of mathematics, physical experiment, design of new devices, are not given by nature to everyone. No amount of hard work can replace this natural talent. It produces truly valuable fruits in science only in conjunction with talent, just as, of course, talent will turn out to be fruitless without persistent and concentrated work.”

One should not think that mathematical talent is extremely rare. My experience shows that there are quite a lot of mathematically gifted people, but often they themselves do not believe in their giftedness or do not find the strength to develop it through persistent work directed in a certain direction. Sometimes natural talent does not receive development, because people who have it do not have interesting problems for independent reflection or have not completed school creativity. After all, creativity needs to be taken into account in the same way as any business. It is not without reason that young people have always strived to get into those universities where outstanding leaders and leaders worked, where significant scientific problems were put forward and solved. A young person who finds himself in such a team will learn more about the number of pressing issues that require resolution, learn possible approaches to solving them, and observe how others deal with similar tasks and move towards their cherished goal. All this brings him closer to penetration in the creative process and has a huge stimulating and perceptive effect on his personality and character.
