The reserves of the iron element as action (heavy hydrogen $^3\text{H}$), which can be used for thermonuclear reactions, are enormous - action is part of heavy water, which makes up 1/6000 of all the water available on the globe. And when two nuclei merge, the energy is about 13 MeV, which is much higher than the initial energy of the fusion nuclei. So this class energy is practically unexplored in this direction.

But in order to use this energy for peaceful purposes, it is necessary to prematurely re-initialize the energy of the nuclei. So this class energy is practically unexplored in this direction.

First of all, it was necessary to learn how to heat hydrogen to a high temperature at which the nuclei are capable of entering into a thermonuclear reaction. The simplest thing is to heat the gas with electric current. To do this, a discharge is created in the gas, and at high current values, the gas is heated due to Joule heat. In this case, the gas is completely ionized and turns into plasma. At very high temperatures, hydrogen plasma is a “mixture” of electrons and nuclei that can enter into a thermonuclear reaction. However, high-temperature plasma is very unstable - with huge currents flowing through the discharge, the one-dimensionally charged layer of plasma pushes apart, hitting the walls of the gas-discharge tube and quickly cooling.

The problem of plasma “containment” is one of the most difficult problems that must be solved on the way to controlling a thermonuclear reaction. And Soviet physicists made an invaluable contribution to solving this problem. Under the leadership of academicians L.A. Artsimovich and M.A. Leontovich, an installation in which a specially selected and oriented magnetic field makes it possible to retain high-temperature plasma was constructed in Istichit atomic energy. This is the so-called TOKAMAK - “toroidal chamber in a magnetic field.” Nowadays, all over the world, experiments on plasma confinement are carried out mainly in installations of this type. And they are called TOKAMAK all over the world.

Currently, research on the problem of controlling a thermonuclear reaction is at such a level that engineers and designers are already beginning to consider purely engineering, technical issues related to the implementation of a prototype of a future thermonuclear station. One can hope that by the end of the century or so a prototype will be built and tested, and then it will be possible to resolve the issue of the industrial use of thermonuclear energy.

Of course, I cannot give a comprehensive picture of the development of Soviet physics. I talk mainly only about those jobs that I, one way or another, had to deal with. Therefore, I did not talk about many outstanding achievements of Soviet physicists - such as the birth of quantum electrons in the work of Nobel Prize winners Academicians N. G. Basov and A. M. Prokhorov, the discovery of electronic parametric resonance by Academician E. K. Zavoisky, the discovery of the principle of aWTO charged particles by Academician V. I. Veksler and much more. However, I hope that my story helped readers «Kvant» imagine what a huge contribution Soviet physicists made to the development of science and technology over the 60 years of Soviet power.
