⟦folio 12⟧

S. Gindikin

Pierre-Simon Laplace

The Chancellor of the Imperial Senate, who received more than 100 thousand livres of annual annuity, with no less zeal than a simple academician, Laplace sought to respect all the irregularities and disturbances in the movement of the luminaries with an eye to the universal order, to extend the method of mathematical analysis to the phenomena of earthly physics and to subordinate to his formulas the phenomena of social life in which the average person sees a mystery or a blind clue.

Arago

On March 5, 1827, at 9 o'clock in the morning, the Marquis of Laplace, peer of France, one of the first holders of the Legion of Honor, awarded the highest distinction of the order - Grand

Cross. “What we know is insignificant compared to what we don’t know,” were his last words. Laplace was called the “French Newton”; He died exactly one hundred years after the death of Newton, who was his idol.

Posthumous tributes to Laplace were given with some confusion. Fourier's speech said: “Perhaps I should mention the successes of Laplace in a portion of political activity, but all this does not exist: we are pure Great Mathematicians. We must separate the immortal creator of Celestial Mechanics from the minister and semantor.” Those around him were embarrassed that Laplace managed to be a republican and a monarchist, an Athensian and a Catholic, and received honors under the Empire and after the Restoration. (However, the former Jacobin Fourier also later became a baron.)
