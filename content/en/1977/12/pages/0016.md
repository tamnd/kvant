Revolution, Empire, Restoration

Revolutionary events captured a significant part of French scientists. Laplace's friend, the astronomer Bailly, was the first zerr of Paris, Condorcet was a member of the municipality, and the outstanding mathematician Monge was minister of the sea. In 1791, a number of academicians nominated their candidacies for the Legislative Assembly (Condorcet, Lavuzier). In connection with this, Marat made a composite pamphlet “Modern Charlatans”. Laplace also got it in the backside: “Among the best academic mathematicians are Laplace, Monge and Kuzen: a family of authors accustomed to following well-known formulas and climbing up to their orders, like a mill horse, the Comorian is accustomed to doing a certain number of turns before being ousted.”

Laplace, along with Lagrange, Monge, Lavuzier, was recruited to work on the Metric Commission, whose goal was to create a unified system of measures. During the period of the Jacobin dictatorship, Laplace was recalled from the commission due to “the insufficiency of resurublikan virtues and too weak hatred of tyrants.” In 1799, he returned to the commission and, under his supervision, etons of the meter and kilogram were made.

In the summer of 1793, as recognized by the Committee of Public Safety, a large group of scientists began scientific research to organize defense against the expected aggression. Laplace was not among them. He retired to quiet Melun, where he began work on the multi-volume “Celestial Mechanics” - the main work of his life.

In 1793 the Convention governed the existing academies. In 1793-1794, some academicians ended their days on gluten. Together with the Girondists, he was sentenced to death, but Condorcet escaped. According to the “Law on Suspects”, Lavuzier was executed as a tax farmer. Bailly, whom Laplace tried to hide in his house in Melen, also died on the scaffold.

Laplace returned to Paris after the Thermidran coup in the fall of 1794. Along with Lagrange and other major scientists. he took the place of professor at the Normal School. This new type of educational completion was conceived during the Convention; it was recognized to train teachers and scientists for all of France. The involvement of prominent scientists as teachers was new. Later, the Polytechnic School was created to train engineers at the same high level. Laplace gave lectures here too. He becomes chairman of the Chamber of Weights and Measures and actively collaborates in the Bureau of Longitudes, created to streamline astronomical changes and the time service.

In 1795, the Directory established the National Institute of Sciences and Research
