In 1814, before the fall of Paris, the Senate made an unexpected move: on the initiative of Talleyrand, it called on the Bourbons. Laplace was one of the first to sign this decision. During the Hundred Days he did not leave the province.

After the Restoration, the ranks of the Institute of Words received the name academy. The Academy of Sciences meekly removed from its ranks those objectionable to the monarchy, Monge and Carnot. Honors were showered on Laplaceage. In the first year of the reign of Louis XVIII, he was installed as Marquis and Prair of France and received the Grand Cross of the Legion of Honor. In 1816, he is the president of the Bureau of Longitudes and chairman of the commission for the reorganization of the Polytechnic School, he is elected to the “Academy of Immortals” - a rare distinction for a representative of the exact sciences. Laplace's speeches in the chamber of razrov were rare, colorless and uncompromisingly monarchical. When part of the Institute protested against the introduction of censorship by Charles X, Laplace opened up about this protest in print. Saint-Simon was displeased: “Gentlemen, the study of unorganized matter, infinitely small, allebra and arithmetic! Who gave you the right to occupy meter advanced positions?... You brought out one lesson from science, namely, that those who flatter the greats of the world take advantage of their favor and zest.”

Many stories have been preserved about Laplace's behavior at the Academy of Sciences. Here are two of them.

Arago and Poisson competed for one place at the Academy. Laplace stated that preference should be given to the older Poisson. There was a sharp exchange of views.

Lagrange. But you yourself, Mr. de Laplace, were elected to membership in the Academy when you had not yet done anything outstanding, showed only hope, and all your great discoveries were made later.

Laplace s. But I still believe that the title of academician should be pointed out to young people as a future reward in order to encourage their efforts.

Gall e. You are like a coachman who ties a piece of hay to the end of the pole of his cart to lure the horses.

Such a trick ends with people being exhausted and petrified.

Laplace had to give in.

Another time, in 1822, Fourier and Bugo ran for the position of permanent secretary. Laplace took two biological one. His neighbor saw that he had written the name Fourier on his back. After this, Laplace put the bicollet in his hat, asked his neighbor to choose one of them, the other tore it and loudly declared that he did not know which of the candidates he had given his vote to.

After Lagrange's death in 1813, Laplace's influence in the Academy of Sciences became especially strong. In 1826, a year before Laplace's death, young Abel appeared in Paris. He writes: “Imak, Celestial Mechanics is finished. The author of such a work can with satisfaction follow the path that he has trodden in science.” Elsewhere: “It is obvious that any Laplace theory of the city is superior to anything that any lesser mathematician can create. It seems to me that if the desired number is ever achieved in mathematics, one needs to study mathematicians, not sub-mathematicians.”

Celestial mechanics

The beginning of Laplace's scientific activity came at a difficult time. A major stage in the construction of infinitesimal analysis has been completed. There were no problems around which the efforts of the greatest mathematicians could be concentrated. It seemed to many that the days of pure mathematics were numbered. Further, the versatile Lagrange, whose algebraic works were ahead of his time, at some point stopped studying mathematics. “Doesn’t it seem to you that high geometry is nearing decline? It is supported by milk from you and Euler,” he wrote to d’Alembert in 1772.

Under these conditions, the center of interest shifted towards applied mathematics, where the indisputable problem was to construct a theory of the motion of celestial bodies based on the law of universal gravitation.
