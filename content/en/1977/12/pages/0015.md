Beaumont - Paris, 1749-1789

The future Marques was born on March 23, 1749 into a peasant family in little Beaumont (Normandy). Later, he was reluctant to talk about his childhood and, after 21 years, never saw his parents. Thanks to an unknown patron, Laplace graduates from the College of the Benedictine Order. At the age of 17, he was already teaching mathematics at a military school.

Laplace begins to intensively study mathematics and mechanics. In 1770, having prepared himself with a letter to recommend himself to the great d'Alembert, he went to Paris. For a long time he fails to put into practice recommendations until a happy idea comes to his mind - to present his thoughts on mechanics in writing. The originality of the young man’s thoughts made a strong impression on D’Alembert: “You have proven yourself and I am completely satisfied with this. My help is at your service.”

With the help of D'Alembert, Laplace becomes a teacher at the military school, and then takes the position of examiner in the royal artillery corps, vacated after Bezu's death. In 1784, the young Bonaparte brilliantly passed his exam, which Laplace had the opportunity to recall in 1804: “I want to add to the greetings of the people my greeting to the Emperor of France, Zero, whom twenty years ago I had the good fortune of attracting an open career, carried out by him with a weak poppy and with a maxim for France.”

Almost all of Laplace's scientific activity was devoted to celestial mechanics (see below). But his interests are much broader. Thus, in 1779-1784, he collaborated with Lavouzier on a variety of issues (certain telecapacities, problems of philologiston, atmospheric electricity): “I really don’t know how I allowed myself to be drawn into work on physics, and you will find, perhaps, that you would have done better if you had refrained from doing so; but I cannot resist my other Lavuzier, who includes in this joint work as much pleasantness and intelligence as I could wish. Moreover, since he is very rich, he does not want anything that would impart to experience the clarity necessary for such meager research.” Laplace also takes part in public life: he is a member of the Academy of Sciences, he maintains the capital for the poor, and the sanitary condition of city slaughterhouses.

Laplace's authority is growing. In 1784 he became an academician (in mechanics).
