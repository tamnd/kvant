Let (Fig. 5) $(BCD) \perp [AO]$, $[AE] \perp [BD]$, $\hat{AEC} = \alpha$, $S_{\Delta BDC} = Q$, $S_{\Delta ABD} = S$. Then, projecting the face ABD onto the face BCD, we obtain the equality $Q = 3S\cos \alpha$, and projecting the faces BCD, ABC and ACD onto the face ABD, we obtain the equality $S = Q\cos \alpha + 2S\cos \beta$. From these two equalities we find the exclusive dependence:
$$3\cos^2 \alpha + 2\cos \beta - 1 = 0.$$

Similarly, the general formula for a regular n-gonal paramid is easily proven:

$$\sin \frac{\pi}{n} \sin \alpha = \cos \frac{\beta}{2}.$$

Sometimes applying the formula allows orthogonal projection is not only useful, but also leads to an effective solution.
