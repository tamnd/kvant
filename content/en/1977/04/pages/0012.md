Linear accelerator. While the trajectory of the particles passes inside the dees, they are protected from the action of the electric field, and when the field between the dees acquires the desired direction and magnitude, the particles fly out into the gap, accelerate in it and spring back inside the dees. This is how acceleration occurs.

What does the period $T$ of revolution of a particle in a cyclotren depend on? A particle moving with speed $v$ passes a circle of radius $R$ in time $2\pi R/v$. Since $v = \frac{eBR}{m}$ (see (7)), then

$$T = 2\pi \frac{m}{eB}.$$ (11)

The period of revolution is determined by the mass of the particle and the magnitude of the magnetic field induction. At low energies, the rest mass $m_0$ can be substituted together with the relativistic mass $m$. If the magnetic field is constant throughout the entire gap between the dees (flat magnetic stripes), the right side of equality (11) turns into a constant and the period of revolution will be constant. This result is of utmost importance. The frequency of the high-cast voltage supplying the cyclotron is unchanged, and the period of revolution of the particles is also unchanged. If the frequency is chosen correctly, then during the entire acceleration cycle the particles return, fall into the accelerating gap between the dees and are placed inside the dees in time.

Particles are continuously introduced into the cyclotron chamber near its center. Having acquired energy, they unwind along the turns of the spiral and leave the cyclotron. While some particles are finishing the acceleration cycle, others are in the middle of the journey, and still others are just beginning it. The “jet” of accelerated particles escaping from the cyclotron consists of clumps of particles following one after another, disappearing their accelerating intervals at moments of time suitable for acceleration.

Let's return to formula (11). Not for the first time, it does not seem difficult to ensure the constancy of the circulation period in this case. The mass of the particle $m$ gradually increases. At the same time, the radius of the described spiral turn increases. It would seem that it is not difficult to ensure the constancy of the relation $m/B$, and hence the constancy of $T$. To do this, it is enough to create a field between the stripes in the cyclotron, the induction of which increases from the center to the periphery. However, this path is closed.
