Ke 2. Using this figure it is not difficult to obtain the thin line formula:

$$\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F},$$

as well as expressions for its linear (transverse) increase:

$$\Gamma = \frac{H}{h} = \frac{f}{d} = \frac{f - F}{F} = \frac{F}{d - F}$$

and for angular magnification:

$$\gamma = \frac{\mathrm{tg} u'}{\mathrm{tg} u} = \frac{h/f}{h/d} = \frac{d}{f} = \frac{1}{\Gamma}.$$

Let us now consider several specific problems.

Problem 1. On the surface of water ($n_B = 1,3$) lies a biconvex glass line ($n_{cT} = 1,5$) with a radius of curvature $R_1 = R_2 = 10$ cm. Determine the front and rear focal lengths of the line. What is the focal length of this line in air?

This is a relatively simple task. Direct application of formulas (1) and (2), where $n_1 = 1$, $n_2 = n_B = 1,3$ and $n = n_{cT} = 1,5$, gives $F_1 \approx 14$ cm and $F_2 \approx 18,5$ cm.

For the focal length of a line in air, formula (3) leads to the result $F = 10$ cm.

Problem 2. At figure 3 the path of the ray ABC through a positive line is given. Construct the path of an arbitrary ray DE after refraction in the face.

Let us draw a ray $A'O$ parallel to the ray $AB$ and passing through the optical center of the line. It won't break. The point $G$ of intersection of this ray with ray $BC$ lies in the focal plane $H$. Ray $D'O$, parallel to $DE$, will intersect the focal plane at point $P$. The ray $DE$ will also pass through the same point and be refracted.

Task 3. What words should they squeak for a nearsighted person who is reading text located no further than 20 cm?

Glasses do not in any way correct defects in the eye. Their role is reduced to displaying objects in the surrounding world at a distance from which the eye can distinguish objects. In our case, in order for a myopic person to see distant objects, for example, a star, the glasses must create an image of the star no further than 20 cm from the eye, and the eye will look at this image. Let's assume that the lens of the glasses is close to the eye (a small gap between the lens and the eye will not significantly distort the calculations below), and write the formula for the lens:

$$\frac{1}{d} - \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{F}.$$

Here $d$ is the distance to the star, and $f$ is the maximum distance from the star image to the eye. The term $\frac{1}{f}$ is taken with a minus sign, since the image is minimal. Since $d$ is very large, we can safely put $\frac{1}{d} = 0$. For the conditions of problem $f = 20$, see

Thus, a nearsighted person should be prescribed glasses with diverging lenses of optical power - 5 dnmr.

Problem 4. Using a line with focal length $F$ on the screen, a reduced and enlarged image of an object located at a distance $L$ from the screen is obtained. Name of image sizes.
