Lenses and Lens systems

The phenomenon of the assumption of light on the spherical interface between two optical media allows one to obtain images of luminous objects. This possibility is realized using a lens - an arbitrary body bounded by two spherical surfaces. The lens is the main optical element in devices such as a camera, projection lamp, microscope, telescope, etc.

figure 1 shows a cross-section of the assumption of a spherical surface separating two optical media with different assumption indices. Obviously, a high-quality image of any object is possible only in the case when a beam of rays emanating from any point of the object (for example, from point $P$), after the assumption, gathers again into a point. In general, the spherical separation of two media does not provide this condition. Thus, the ray $NB$, after the assumption, will intersect the axis $PQ$, strictly speaking, at a different point than the ray $MA$. However, under certain conditions, a beam of rays emitted by a point can be collected practically into a point. This will be the case when the height $h$ at which the ray of this ray intersects the surface is small compared to the radius of curvature $OC$ of the surface. In other words, when the angle $\alpha$ is small. Rays that satisfy this condition are called this condition, called above.
