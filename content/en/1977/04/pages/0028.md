"Above-the-cloud" glow

When on a sunny day there was a cloud in the sky, in front of you you can see the sun, covered by a cloud, with rays of rays diverging in different directions. As they move away from the cloud, the beams gradually expand. If you mentally continue them behind the cloud until they intersect each other, then they will “converge” in the place where the sun is located.

At the same time, it is known that the rays from the sun go to the ground almost parallel (the angular diameter of the sun is about 30′).

Why do the rays from the sun, covered by a cloud, diverge so widely? And why is there such a columnar expansion of individual light rays from the “transcendental” radiance?
