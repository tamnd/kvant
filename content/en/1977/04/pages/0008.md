Let us calculate, as an example, what the length of the drift tube should be, in which a proton with an energy of 10 MeV hits. Let us write formula (2) in the form

$$K_n = \frac{m_0 c^2}{2} \left( \frac{v_n}{c} \right)^2.$$

From this formula we find

$$v_n = c \sqrt{\frac{2K_n}{m_0 c^2}}.$$

Substituting $K_n = 10$ MeV and $m_0 c^2 = 938$ MeV, we find $v_n = 0,146$ s=4.4×10⁷ m/sec. We see that at an energy of 10 MeV the proton speed is only 7 times less than the speed of light. At high energies it is even higher.

How long should the drift tubes be? Using formula (4), we find that at a frequency of voltage oscillations $f = 1/T = 100$ сс the length of the drift tube, which is a proton with an energy of 10 MeV, should be $2,2 \cdot 10^5$ m=220 km, at a frequency of 1 MHz - 22 m, at a frequency of 10 MHz - 2.2 m. All these values ​​are too large. To get this, it is enough to remember that we “designed” an accelerator with a very low energy of 30 MeV and the Zaki furnace had about 60 drift tubes! To get a linear accelerator of reasonable size, you need significantly higher frequencies.

In proton accelerators, the total frequency is chosen from 150 to 300 MHz, and in electronic accelerators it is even higher. For comparison, we point out that long-wave radio stations operate at frequencies of about 200 kHz, the medium wave range lies at frequencies of ~1 MHz, and short-wave radio stations operate at frequencies of ~10 MHz.
