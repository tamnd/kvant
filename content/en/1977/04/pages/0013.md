Even with small deviations from the average horizontal plane, the accelerated particles quickly move up or down and hit the dant covers. Therefore, conventional cyclotrons, in which no measures are taken to compensate for the effects associated with the relativistic increase in mass, can accelerate protons only to low energies - approximately up to an energy of 20 MeV.

The second way is to come to terms with the fact that the period of revolution of particles gradually increases, and accordingly change the period of the accelerating voltage. This is exactly what is done most often. In this case, however, the cyclotron can no longer operate continuously. At the moment when the period of high-frequency voltage has increased in accordance with the energy of the accelerated particles, it makes no sense to let in new particles - their period of revolution is not equal to the period of change of the high-frequency field. When the accelerating cycle is completed, the frequency can be raised again and new particles can be injected. The Cyklotron will thus produce separate portions or, as is often said, “spits” of accelerated particles. Accelerators of this type are built up to an energy of the order of 1 GeV (ring machines are used when accelerating to higher energies). Such accelerators are called phasomrons.

As we found out, with a circular (or, more precisely, spiral) trajectory shape, the period of revolution of particles increases with radius. To avoid this, we artificially increase the time of particle motion at low energies. This can be done by removing the path, for example, bringing the circle together into a polygon shape with rounded corners. Trajectories of this type are depicted in figure 5. As the energy of the particle increases, the corners of the polygon become more and more rounded, and the trajectory of the particle approaches a circular one.

To make a particle move along such a trajectory, it is necessary to create a magnetic field of a complex configuration. To do this, the electromagnet of an isochronous cyclotron is made of several individual magnets, located in areas where the trajectory should be rounded. The electromagnet strips have a complex shape (see figure 6).

This path, despite its attractiveness, is very difficult. But you can walk along it if you need high intensities, which means not individual “sprays”, but a high-quality “stream” of accelerated particles.
