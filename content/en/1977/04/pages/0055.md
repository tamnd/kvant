Problem 5. Using the positive line, we obtain an image of two exact sources $A$ and $B$. One of them is located on the optical axis at double the focal length from the line, the other is offset from the mak axis, which is the straight line connecting the sources and forms an angle $\varphi = 30^\circ$ (Fig. 4) with the optical axis. At what angle $\psi$ to the axis should we place a flat screen in order to simultaneously obtain some images of sources on it?

Obviously, the screen needs to be positioned along the ray $AB$ (drawn from the source $A$ through the point $B$) after its prefilling in the line. We use the formula for angular increase:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\Gamma} = \frac{d}{f} = \frac{F}{f-F}.$$
Here $f$ is the distance from the source image $A$ to the line, and $F$ is the focal length of the line. Because $A$ is at twice the focal length of the line, $f=2F$. Hence,
$$\gamma = \frac{F}{2F-F} = 1, \text{ и } \psi = \varphi = 30^\circ.$$

Task 6. A complex lens consisting of two mono lines: a positive one with a focal length of $F_1=20$ cm and a negative one with a focal length of $F_2=-10$ cm. The lenses are located at a distance of $l=15$ cm from each other. Using a lens, an image of the Sun is captured on the screen. What focal length $F$ should the line be so that the image of the Sun obtained with its help has the maximum size?

Here we are already dealing with a line system.

Let us find the size of the image of the Sun created by a complex lens by examining the course of the rays sequentially in both lines. The image created by the first lens is very much in its focal plane. The size of this image is $H_1=F_1tg\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angular diameter of the Sun as seen from Earth (Fig. 5). The magnification given by the second lens is $\frac{H_2}{H_1} = \frac{f_2}{d_2}$. According to the lens formula we have
$$-\frac{1}{d_2} + \frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{F_2},$$
where $d_2=F_1-l$ (the image of the Sun in the first lens is the minimum source for the second). From here
$$f_2 = \frac{F_2(F_1-l)}{F_1+F_2-l}.$$
