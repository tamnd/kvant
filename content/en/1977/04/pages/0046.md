E. Palitnik
Is there any food left in the refrigerator?

More precisely, the question would have to be formulated as follows: “How much does food usually dry out faster in the refrigerator than in the open air?” Let's try to figure this out.

Let's start with two facts that are certainly known to you:

1) Cold air is a soft menu (please remember why?).
2) The shallower the air, the more water in the form of steam can be present in it. How can this be explained?

The air, as a rule, comes into contact with some open bodies of water. In water at any temperature it will find molecules that can fly out from the surface of the water and form water vapor. Simultaneously with the evaporation process, the reverse transition of molecules from vapor to liquid (condensation) also occurs. Obviously, the higher the vapor density, the more active the condensation process is. If the evaporation of water occurs in a limited volume (for example, in a closed vessel), then there will certainly come a moment when the number of molecules leaving the liquid is equal to the number of molecules returning back. In this case, they say that equilibrium occurs between the liquid and its vapor (the amounts of liquid and vapor no longer change). Steam in this state is called saturated, thereby emphasizing that under constant conditions, further evaporation is no longer possible.

It is clear that the higher the temperature, the more intense the water evaporates and the greater, therefore, the density of water vapor must be for equilibrium to occur. In other words, the higher the temperature, the more water vapor can be contained in a given volume of air. For example, at 20°C in a room with an area of ​​12 m² and a height of 3 m, about 600 g of water can be in the form of steam, and at 100°C - about 20 kg!

The degree of air humidity (the content of a certain amount of water vapor in the air) is usually characterized using a special physical quantity - relative humidity. Relative air humidity is the ratio of the mass of water vapor contained in 1 m³ of air to the maximum mass of water vapor that is in this volume at a given temperature.
