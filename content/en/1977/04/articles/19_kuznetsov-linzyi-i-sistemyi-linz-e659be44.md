Lenses and Lens systems

The phenomenon of the assumption of light on the spherical interface between two optical media allows one to obtain images of luminous objects. This possibility is realized using a lens - an arbitrary body bounded by two spherical surfaces. The lens is the main optical element in devices such as a camera, projection lamp, microscope, telescope, etc.

figure 1 shows a cross-section of the assumption of a spherical surface separating two optical media with different assumption indices. Obviously, a high-quality image of any object is possible only in the case when a beam of rays emanating from any point of the object (for example, from point $P$), after the assumption, gathers again into a point. In general, the spherical separation of two media does not provide this condition. Thus, the ray $NB$, after the assumption, will intersect the axis $PQ$, strictly speaking, at a different point than the ray $MA$. However, under certain conditions, a beam of rays emitted by a point can be collected practically into a point. This will be the case when the height $h$ at which the ray of this ray intersects the surface is small compared to the radius of curvature $OC$ of the surface. In other words, when the angle $\alpha$ is small. Rays that satisfy this condition are called this condition, called above.

If a thin lens is made of materials with a refractive index n, next to the lens there is a medium with a refractive index n₁, and to the right - with a refractive index n₂, then the following relations hold:

$$\frac{n_2}{F_2} = \frac{n - n_1}{R_1} + \frac{n - n_2}{R_2},$$

$$\frac{n_1}{F_1} = \frac{n - n_1}{R_1} + \frac{n - n_2}{R_2}.$$

Here F₁ and F₂ are the front and rear focal lengths of the lens, R₁ and R₂ are the radii of curvature, respectively, of the front and rear surfaces of the lens. These relationships can be obtained (do it yourself!) by considering the course of rays, indix from infinite remote calculus.

Problem 5. Using the positive line, we obtain an image of two exact sources $A$ and $B$. One of them is located on the optical axis at double the focal length from the line, the other is offset from the mak axis, which is the straight line connecting the sources and forms an angle $\varphi = 30^\circ$ (Fig. 4) with the optical axis. At what angle $\psi$ to the axis should we place a flat screen in order to simultaneously obtain some images of sources on it?

Obviously, the screen needs to be positioned along the ray $AB$ (drawn from the source $A$ through the point $B$) after its prefilling in the line. We use the formula for angular increase:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\Gamma} = \frac{d}{f} = \frac{F}{f-F}.$$
Here $f$ is the distance from the source image $A$ to the line, and $F$ is the focal length of the line. Because $A$ is at twice the focal length of the line, $f=2F$. Hence,
$$\gamma = \frac{F}{2F-F} = 1, \text{ и } \psi = \varphi = 30^\circ.$$
