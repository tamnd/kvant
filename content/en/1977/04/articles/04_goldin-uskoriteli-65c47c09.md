ACCELERATORS

L. Goldin

The editorial board of the magazine «Kvant» has long asked me to write an article about accelerators. I delayed this for several years. Does the reader of the magazine need to know about the almost military tricks that physicists and engineers are constantly using in order to make these colossal, incomparable structures work: isn’t it all too special? Then I finally made up my mind, realizing that the material could be presented in such a way that the reader would not only understand the structure of the zoological tree of the accelerator family, but would also gain information and ideas that would always be useful to him. To what extent I succeeded, my readers will say.

For several decades now, nuclear and particle physics has been determined by what accelerators scientists have at their disposal. And today, each new major accelerator will be preceded by new discoveries and changes in basic theoretical principles. To study the depths of the Universe, hypothetical instruments are built. Multimeter mirrors of optical telecopes and antennas of radio telecopes stretching for kilometers did not surprise anyone. In proton accelerators, the total frequency is chosen from 150 to 300 MHz, and in electronic accelerators it is even higher. Not any vibrations can be excited in a pipe, but only those to which the pipe is tuned. The tuning of a pipe is determined by the correspondence between its length and the wavelength. In a pipe closed at both ends, it is possible to excite only such waves (standing) for which half the wavelength is placed an integer number of times in the pipe. To perform musical works on the organ, you need to excite a variety of sounds in the pipes. The organs therefore contain thousands of pipes.

In a similar way, electromagnetic oscillations are established in closed metal pipes. The length of the pipe must be well matched to the frequency of electromagnetic oscillations at which it must operate. This carefully made, polished, studied tube is the linear accelerator's resonator and is placed along its drift tube. To excite vibrations, a radiator is inserted into the pipe - an antenna or coil.

The electric field voltage created in the resonant is maximum near its axis and drops almost to zero near the walls. If this were not so, then the electric field would create large points in the walls, which would lead to heating of the walls - a waste of power. A field of this configuration can be created in a pipe only if its diameter is matched to the wavelength. We see that both the length and transverse dimensions of the resonant must be selected very carefully.

Drift tubes placed in the resonator somewhat distort the picture of electromagnetic oscillations. A space free from vibrations appears inside the tubes, since the field cannot penetrate through their conducting walls. The field will be squeezed out of the drift tubes into accelerating gaps.

By accelerating particles to very high energies, there is no need to “hide” particles in drift tubes. Through the accelerator, ordinary electromagnetic waves can be transmitted. If the speed of the particle is close to the speed of light, then, once it hits a favorable section of the wave, where the directional vector of the electric field has reached the magnitude of the direction, the particle will accelerate all the time.

The 《very large》 energies that we talked about can differ from each other for different particles. For protons and other heavy particles, these are energies of the order of many thousands of MeV. And for electrons, the energy of the order of several MeV is already very large.

The accelerators we have just talked about are called traveling wave accelerators. A large linear accelerator with a traveling wave for accelerating electrons to an energy of 2000 MeV was built at the Physico-Technical Institute of the Academy of Sciences of the Ukrainian SSR in Kharkov.

Cyclic accelerators

High-energy linear accelerators turn out to be very expensive. As an example, we point out that the linear electron accelerator in Stepford (USA) with an energy of 20,000 MeV has a length of more than 3.5 km. Throughout this continuous stretch there are uniquely powerful and very expensive high-frequency stations.

It turns out to be more economical to roll the trajectory of accelerated particles into a spiral or a ring, continuously returning them to the same accelerating stations. Such accelerators are called cyclic. To eliminate the trajectory, magnetic fields are used. No matter how expensive magnets are, no matter how high the cost of electricity required to power them, these costs are more than offset by savings on high-frequency devices.

Let's write some simple formulas. The curvature of the trajectory is caused by the Lorentz force acting on the particle from the magnetic field. This force is known to be equal to

$$F = evB,$$

where $e$ is the charge of the particle, $v$ is its speed, and $B$ is the magnetic field induction (it is assumed that the direction $v$ is perpendicular to $\vec{B}$). The Lorentz force is perpendicular to both the direction of the magnetic field and the direction of the particle velocity. It imparts to the particle a construction acceleration of $a = \frac{v^2}{R}$, so that the motion passes in a circle. The magnitude of the force $F$ and the radius of the circle are related, as is known, by the formula

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{R}.$$

Equating the rules of parts of formulas (5) and (6), we find

$$evB = \frac{mv^2}{R},$$

or

$$p = mv = eBR,$$

where $p = mv$ is the momentum of the particle. If we understand by mass the relativistic mass of the particle, determined by the formula

$$m = m_0 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}},$$

then correspondence (7) turns out to be valid at relativistic energies.

Formula (7) shows that the induction of the magnetic field and the size of the accelerator are related not to the particle energy that is familiar to us, but to its momentum. Momentum and kinetic energy in relativistic mechanics are related by a rather complex formula

$$p = \frac{1}{c} \sqrt{K(K + 2m_0c^2)}.$$

Under the root of this expression there is a quantity that has the dimension of the square of energy. If we multiply this expression by $c$, we get

$$pc = \sqrt{K(K + 2m_0c^2)}.$$

This formula shows that together with the momentum $p$ it is convenient to consider the quantity $pc$, proportional to it, which has the dimension of energy ($pc$ is measured in electron volts). At $K \ll m_0c^2$ (non-relativistic case) this expression transforms into the familiar formula $p = \sqrt{2m_0K}$, and at $K \gg m_0c^2$ (high energies) we simply have $pc = K$. In these units, formula (7) takes on a form familiar to all physicists.

$$pc (\theta) = 3 \cdot 10^8 BR (m \cdot m).$$

In the vacuum chamber of an accelerator, the field induction usually does not exceed 1.6 $m \lambda$. Let us present some estimates for protons. To accelerate protons to an energy of 20 MeV (pc=200 MeV) it is necessary to accelerate with a radius of $R \approx 60$ cm. At an energy of 600 MeV (pc=1200 MeV) $R \approx 3,6$ m. When accelerating to 10 GeV * (pc=11 GeV) $R=30$ m (accelerator of the Institute of Theoretical and Experimental Physics in Moscow, accelerator of the High Energy Laboratory in Dubna). At an energy of 70 GeV (Serpukhov accelerator) $R \approx 200$ m, and at an energy of 500 GeV (Fermilab accelerator, USA) $R \approx 1,5$ km. These estimates determine the scale of the facilities and place severe economic barriers to increasing the ultimate energy of accelerators. The dimensions, and with them the cost of cyclic accelerators, are ultimately determined simply by the magnetic properties of iron.

Is any progress possible here? The maximum inductions achievable when using various ferromagnets are very close to each other, so hopes for the invention of an alloy with a significantly higher saturation induction do not come. The only feasible way is to abandon conventional magnets and switch to superconductor magnets. In this case, the induction can be raised 2-3 times. No matter how expensive superconductors are and no matter how difficult it is to work with low temperatures, accelerator technology must begin to move in this direction. The construction of the first superconducting accelerator is already underway at Fermilab.

Let's return to formula (9). As particles accelerate, their momentum $p$ increases, which means that either the trajectory radius $R$ or the field induction $B$ must increase. The easiest way, of course, is to work in a constant magnetic field. The trajectory of accelerated particles in this case has the form of an unwinding sprale. Formula (10) determines the radius of its outer turn. Magnetic fields are electronified and take the form of disks that cover the entire spiral. This is how cyclotrons are designed. It is still possible to make a continuous magnetic strip with a radius of 60 cm or 3.6 m. However, to accelerate protons to an energy of 10 GeV, strips with a radius of about 30 m are needed. It is also impossible to make continuous strips. It is this circumstance that limits the maximum energies available with the help of cyclotrons.

In high-energy accelerators, a magnetic field is created only on a narrow ring road. The radius $R$ along which the particles must move is thus given, and the trajectory of the particles at the end and at the beginning of the acceleration is the same. An increase in particle momentum should be accompanied by the appearance of a magnetic field. Accelerators with a ring field that increases in time are called synchrotrons (the magnetic field increases synchronously with the pulse).

Cyclotrons, phasotrons and microtrons

Cyclotrons are used to accelerate protons and heavy ions. They were invented by J. Lawrence. A diagram of the cyclotron device is shown in figure 4. The magnetic field, “wrapping” the particles, is created in the gap between the strips of the electronic agent. The battery chamber is placed in this gap, filling the shape of a giant tin can. The vacuum chamber contains electrons, between which an accelerating electric field is created. They also look like a tin can. To get electrons, this jar must be cut along the diameter and the halves slightly expanded so that a gap forms between them. Each of the halves forms an electron. A high-frequency accelerating voltage is applied to them. As can be seen from figure 4, b, the electrons are directed in the shape of the Latin letter $D$. They are called duannas. The shel between the two acts as an accelerating gap.

While the trajectory of the particles passes inside the dees, they are protected from the action of the electric field, and when the field between the dees acquires the desired direction and magnitude, the particles fly out into the gap, accelerate in it and spring back inside the dees. This is how acceleration occurs.

What does the period $T$ of revolution of a particle in a cyclotren depend on? A particle moving with speed $v$ passes a circle of radius $R$ in time $2\pi R/v$. Since $v = \frac{eBR}{m}$ (see (7)), then

$$T = 2\pi \frac{m}{eB}.$$ (11)

The period of revolution is determined by the mass of the particle and the magnitude of the magnetic field induction. At low energies, the rest mass $m_0$ can be substituted together with the relativistic mass $m$. If the magnetic field is constant throughout the entire gap between the dees (flat magnetic stripes), the right side of equality (11) turns into a constant and the period of revolution will be constant. This result is of utmost importance. The frequency of the high-cast voltage supplying the cyclotron is unchanged, and the period of revolution of the particles is also unchanged. If the frequency is chosen correctly, then during the entire acceleration cycle the particles return, fall into the accelerating gap between the dees and are placed inside the dees in time.

Particles are continuously introduced into the cyclotron chamber near its center. Having acquired energy, they unwind along the turns of the spiral and leave the cyclotron. While some particles are finishing the acceleration cycle, others are in the middle of the journey, and still others are just beginning it. The “jet” of accelerated particles escaping from the cyclotron consists of clumps of particles following one after another, disappearing their accelerating intervals at moments of time suitable for acceleration.

Let's return to formula (11). Not for the first time, it does not seem difficult to ensure the constancy of the circulation period in this case. The mass of the particle $m$ gradually increases. At the same time, the radius of the described spiral turn increases. It would seem that it is not difficult to ensure the constancy of the relation $m/B$, and hence the constancy of $T$. To do this, it is enough to create a field between the stripes in the cyclotron, the induction of which increases from the center to the periphery. However, this path is closed. Therefore, conventional cyclotrons, in which no measures are taken to compensate for the effects associated with the relativistic increase in mass, can accelerate protons only to low energies - approximately up to an energy of 20 MeV.

The second way is to come to terms with the fact that the period of revolution of particles gradually increases, and accordingly change the period of the accelerating voltage. This is exactly what is done most often. In this case, however, the cyclotron can no longer operate continuously. At the moment when the period of high-frequency voltage has increased in accordance with the energy of the accelerated particles, it makes no sense to let in new particles - their period of revolution is not equal to the period of change of the high-frequency field. When the accelerating cycle is completed, the frequency can be raised again and new particles can be injected. The Cyklotron will thus produce separate portions or, as is often said, “spits” of accelerated particles. Accelerators of this type are built up to an energy of the order of 1 GeV (ring machines are used when accelerating to higher energies). Such accelerators are called phasomrons.
