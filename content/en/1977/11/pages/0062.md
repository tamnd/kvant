8th grade

5. 7 dwarfs are sitting at a round table. Before each groan there is a mug. Some of these mugs are filled with milk. One of the dwarfs pours all his milk into the mugs of the others equally. Then his neighbor to the right does the same. Then the next neighbor on the right does the same, and so on. After the last, seventh, dwarf poured out his milk to all the others, each mug contained the same amount of milk as was in it at the beginning. All mugs together contain 3 liters of milk. How much milk was initially in each mug? (M454)

6. We will call a 2n-digit number special if it is itself a perfect square and the numbers formed by its first n digits and its last n digits are also perfect squares; in this case, the second number begins with the number 0, but should not be equal to zero.

a) Find all two-digit and four-digit special numbers.

6) Are six-digit special numbers possible? (Prove that they do not exist, or give an example of such a number.)

7. Given a set of positive numbers {a₁; a₂; ...; aₙ}. For each of its subsets, we write down the sum of the numbers included in it (we consider sums of one, two, ..., n terms). Prove that all the written numbers can be divided into n groups so that in each group the ratio of the largest number to the noisiest number does not exceed 2. (M453)

8. There are a thousand tickets with numbers 000, 001, ..., 999 and one hundred boxes with numbers 00, 01, ..., 99. A ticket is allowed to be placed in a box if the box number can be obtained from the number of this ticket by crossing out one of the numbers. Prove that a) it is possible to arrange all the tickets into 50 boxes;

6) it is impossible to distribute all tickets into less than 40 boxes;

b) it is impossible to distribute all tickets into less than 50 boxes.

9th grade

5. Given a square sheet of checkered paper 100×100 cells. Several non-self-intersecting broken lines were drawn, straddling the sides of the cells and not having common points. These broken lines go strictly inside the square, and their ends go to its border. Prove that, in addition to the vertices of the square, there is a node (inside or on the boundary) that does not belong to any broken line.

6. Given natural numbers x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ, y₁, y₂, ..., yₙ. The sums x₁+x₂+...+xₙ and y₁+y₂+...+yₙ are equal to each other and less than mn. Prove that in the equality x₁+x₂+...+xₙ=y₁+y₂+...+yₙ you can cross out some of the terms so that you get a true equality again.

7. There are 1000 squares on the plane with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. Let M be the set of centers of these squares. Prove that it is possible to mark a part of the squares so that each point of the set M falls into at least one and at most four-term squares.
