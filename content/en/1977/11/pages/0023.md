This was reflected in the comic names of a number of disciplines: for example, the theory of probability was called the theory of inconsistency, equations in arbitrary partials were called equations with unfortunate arbitrary ones, finite differences were called arbitrary limbs, etc. However, this underestimation of other areas of mathematics did not last long, since within Luisitia itself a process of expanding interests began. Thus, P. S. Aleksandrov and P. S. Urson became interested in the problems of set-theoretic topology and soon laid the foundation for the existence of the Moscow Topological School, from which numerous first-class scientists emerged and which gave the world a large number of original ideas, understood and directed by research. A. Ya. Khinchin in the twenties began to be interested in the problems of metric theory of numbers, from there he approached questions of probability theory, and by the end of the twenties he completely directed his energy to these two areas of research. Probability theory soon became one of the essential parts of the interests of the Moscow mathematical school. The creation of the Moscow School of Probability Theory is the merit of two outstanding scientists - A. Ya. Khinchin and A. N. Kolmogorov. Next, the interests of Moscow mathematicians turned to classical analysis. At the same time, we found out that the ideas of function theory provide an opportunity for server advancement. Qualitative theory of differential equations, differential equations in arbitrary partials become the object of research by the Moskvychs. Among the representatives of this direction should be named V.V. Stepanovi and I.G. Petrovsky.

One should not think that only Moscow was the center of mathematical thought of that time. First-class research at this time continued in Ukraine - in Kiev and Kharkov. In Kiva, it should be noted, first of all, the research on aspect algebra cultivated by D. A. Grave and his students. At the beginning of the revolution, one of his students O. Yu. Shmidt, a versatile scientist and excellent lecturer, began to popularize new algebraic ideas in Moscow. O. Yu. Shmidt was the founder of the Moscow algebraic school. In Kharkov, S. N. Bershtein developed the ideas of P. L. Chebyshev in the field of improved approximation of functions by polynomials and successfully worked in the field of probability theory.

Research in the field of mathematical physics and number theory successfully developed in Leningrad. Both of these areas of research were traditional for Leningraders and originate from L. Euler, M.V. Ostrogradsky and P.L. Chebyshev.
