Rotations and intersections of polyhedra

The school curriculum places great emphasis on geometric transformations and, in particular, rotation of figures. At a meeting of a mathematical circle, we looked at “non-standard” problems, when we rotate not a flat figure, but a spatial one. In such problems, you need to have a good idea of ​​how the figures will be located in space after rotation, so without geometric imagination it is difficult to outline a solution path. And you can develop this imagination with practice. We think that the proposed tasks will help readers with this.

Problem 1. A cube with edge a was rotated 90° around a straight line connecting two parallel edges that do not belong to the same face of the cube. Find the volume of the common part of the original and rotated cubes.

Let's outline a solution. Let $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of two parallel edges that do not belong to the same face.

Fig. 1.

Ber cube (Fig. 1). Let us cut the cube with a diagonal plane perpendicular to the segment $MN$. We will receive two confessions. After rotation, the common part of each prism and its image will consist of a rectangular parallelepid, the base of which is a square with sides $a$, and a regular quadrangular paramid with the same base and vertex at the point $M$ or $N$ (Fig. 2), therefore the required volume $V$ of the common part of the cubes is equal to twice the sum of the common parallelepids and prisms. Find the length of the height $AA_1$ of the parallelepid

$$|AA_1| = |EA_1| = a \sqrt{2}/2 - a/2 = a(\sqrt{2} - 1)/2$$

and prism height

$$|MN|/2 - |AA_1| = a/2,$$

we get:

$$V = 2 \left( \frac{a^3 (\sqrt{2} - 1)}{2} + a^2 \cdot \frac{a}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \right) = a^3 \left( \sqrt{2} - \frac{2}{3} \right).$$
