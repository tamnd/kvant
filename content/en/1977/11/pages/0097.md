3. Let $(O; R)$ and $(O; r)$ be given concentric circles; $[AB]$ — the required chord; $\{C, D\} = [AB] \cap (O; r)$. (Point $A$ can be chosen arbitrarily on the larger circle.)

According to condition $[AC] \cong [CD] \cong [DB] \Rightarrow C = H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}(B)$. But

$$B \in (O; R) \Rightarrow C \in H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}((O; R)),$$
$$C \in (O; r) \quad \left\{ \begin{array}{l} C \in (O; r) \cap H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}((O; R)) \\ C \in H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}((O; R)) \end{array} \right\} \Rightarrow C \in (O; r) \cap H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}((O; R)).$$

So, the problem has a solution if the smaller circle intersects the image of the larger circle in the homothety $H_A^{\frac{1}{3}}$. The latter will happen when $R > r \geq \frac{R}{3}$ (see Fig. 4).

4. Let $AB$ and $CD$ be the common external tangents to two given circles $(O; r)$ and $(O'; r')$, externally touching $(A, B, C$ and $D$ the points of tangency).

If $r = r'$, then $ABCD$ is a square, and a circle can be inscribed in it.

If $r \neq r'$, then $(AB) \cap (CD) = S$. Let us draw tangents $MN$ and $KL$ to the circle $(O; r)$ at the points of intersection of it with the line $SO$ (Fig. 5).

Let $H$ be a homothety taking circle $(O; r)$ to circle $(O'; r')$:

$$H_S([MN]) = [KL] \quad \left\{ \begin{array}{l} H_S([AC]) = [BD] \\ \Rightarrow \frac{|KL|}{|MN|} = \frac{|BD|}{|AC|} \Rightarrow \frac{|AC|}{|MN|} = \frac{|BD|}{|KL|} = k; \end{array} \right.$$

and the homothety $H_S^k$ takes the quadrilateral $MKLN$ to the quadrilateral $MKLN$ inscribed in the circle $(O; r)$; therefore, a circle can be inscribed into the homothetic quadrilateral $ABCD$.

The homothety $H_M^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ takes the circle $\Gamma$, circumscribed about the triangle $ABC$, into the circle $\gamma$ with center $O_1$ passing through the midpoints of sides $A_1, B_1$ and $C_1$.
