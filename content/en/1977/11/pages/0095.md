At different times, first Pierre and then Marie Curie refused the Legion of Honor, one of the highest orders of the French Republic. In a letter addressed to the dean of the Institute of Physics, Chemistry and Natural Sciences, Pierre Curie spoke briefly but very expressively on this matter: “I ask you, be kind enough to convey my gratitude to Mr. Minister and notify him that I have no need for an order, but I have a significant need in the laboratory.” (He never received the desired LABoratory until the last days of his life.)

I want to fall through the earth in order to have peace...” (from a letter from Marie Curie to her brother Jozef Skłodowski). “You yourself have provided evidence of the introduction of the radium craze. This rewarded us with all the delights of popularity: we were visited by journalists and photographers from all over the world; they went so far as to convey the conversation between my daughter and the nanny and describe our tiger cat. In addition, all sorts of eccentric personalities and unknown inventors wrote letters to us and personally visited us... Finally, collectors of autographs, again, secular people... And yet not a single minute of peace in the laboratory,... I feel like I’m becoming dumber from this way of life...” (from a letter from Pierre Curie to Georges Gouy).

But not only the attitude towards the word is free of their personalities. They also showed an attitude of selflessness and refused to patent the technological process for obtaining radium. But such a patent brought them enormous wealth. “By agreement with me,” writes Marie Curie in her memoirs, “Pierre refused to extract a material choice from our discovery; we did not take out any patent and, without hiding anything, discovered the results of our research, as well as a method for extracting pure radium. Moreover, all interested parties were given the required explanations. This went towards the good will of radium, which could freely develop, first in France, then abroad, supplying scientists and doctors with the products they needed...

It is overwhelmingly interesting that my friends assert, and not without reason, that if Pierre Curie and I had legalized our rights, we would have acquired the means necessary to build the Radium Institute ourselves, and not have to overcome the endless obstacles and difficulties that lay a heavy burden on us and now lie on me. And I think that we were right, ... mankind also needs a dreamer, for whom selfless service to some cause is truly exciting, that it does not even occur to them to care about personal material wealth.”

Their selflessness was inextricably combined with selflessness, a readiness to sacrifice in the name of interesting science and human interests. To his delight, the area of ​​skin was damaged! In a note for the Academy of Sciences, he calmly describes the observed symbols: “The skin has turned red on a surface of six square centimeters; has the appearance of an eye, but does not hurt or is only slightly painful. After a while the beauty, without spreading, begins to become more intense; on the twentieth day, the formation of a structure, then a wound, which was treated with dressings; on the forty-second day, the epidermis began to tear from the edges to the center, and on the fifth and second day there still remains a wound about a square centimeter in color, which has a grayish color, which indicates deeper penetration of the tissue.

Let us add that Madame Curie, by transferring several centigrams of a very active substance in a sealed glass tube, received occlusions of the same nature, although the small test tube is in a thin metal case.

In addition to such sharp effects, during our work with very active substances, we experienced various types of their effects. Hands generally have a tendency to crack; the ends of the fingers holding test tubes or capsules with strong radioactive substances become hard and sometimes very painful; In one of us, inflammation of the tips of the fingers lasted two weeks and ended with the skin peeling off, but painful sensitivity disappeared only through desramation.

These experiments continued the path to treating cancer tumors with the help of radioactive radiation. The whole point was the dose of radiation received. And yet she was unusually happy. Shortly before her death, she wrote: “I am one of those people who think that science is a great beauty. A scientist in his laboratory is not just technical; This is a child face to face with natural phenomena acting on him like a fairy tale...”
