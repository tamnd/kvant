6) Let P be a polynomial of degree 2, k a natural number. Prove that there is at most one polynomial of degree k that commutes with P.

b) Find all polynomials of degree 4 and 8 that commute with a given polynomial P of degree 2.

d) Polynomials Q and R commute with the same polynomial P of degree 2. Prove that they commute with each other.

e) Prove that there is an infinite sequence of polynomials P₂, P₃, P₄, ..., Pₙ, ..., where Pₙ is a polynomial of degree k in which any two polynomials commute and the polynomial P₂ has the form P₂(x)=x²−2.

7. Let A be a 2n-digit number (the first digit is not zero). a) Find all two-digit and four-digit special numbers.

8. The Martian alphabet consists of 10 letters, and any three-letter word forms a Martian surname (that is, there are 1000 different surnames on Mars). According to Martian law, the name Martian must consist of two letters and is obtained from his surname by crossing out one letter.

a) Prove that it is possible to make a list of 50 names so that a Martian with any surname can choose one of these names.

6) Prove that it is impossible to compile such a list with less than 40 names.

b) Prove that it is impossible to compile such a list with less than 50 names.

d) In the past, Martians had four-letter surnames, and a two-letter name was obtained from the surname by crossing out any two letters. Prove that in the past it was possible to bypass a list of 34 names.
