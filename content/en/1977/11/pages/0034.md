“Which of us would not want to lift the veil behind which our future is hidden, in order to penetrate at least with one glance into the upcoming conditions of our knowledge and the secrets of its development to the next degree? What new methods and new facts will be discovered in the new state on the wide and rich field of mathematical thought? - with these words D. Gilbert began his report. Then he continued. — History teaches that the development of science proceeds continuously. We know that every age has its own problems, which the subsequent era either solves or is pushed aside as free in order to replace them with new ones. In order to imagine the possible nature of the development of mathematics in the near future, we must work through in our imagination the questions that still remain open, survey the problems that modern science poses, and the solutions of which we expect from the future. Such an overview of the problem seems to me today, at the turn of the new century, to be especially timely.” And Hilbert offers the attention of listeners twenty-three problems from various areas of mathematics, “the study of which can significantly stimulate the further development of science.”

The first six problems of Hilbert's report relate to the foundation of various mathematical disciplines, the next nine - to more special questions of algebra, algebraic geometry and number theory, the remaining eight - to the theory of functions, differential equations and expressions and expressed calculus.

It should be noted that some of these problems were posed before Hilbert. Thus, the first on the list - the problem of contirum - was posed by G. Kant in 1878; questions related to the third problem were discussed by K. Gauss in his correspondence with Hermann. As for the questions that make up the content of the eight problems, one of them - the only one about the zeros of the zema function - was posed by B. Riemann in 1859, the other, called the only one about the Holbach zeros, back in 1742 in the latter’s letter to L. Euler, and finally, the 21st problem - the problem put forward by B. Riemann in 1857. The remaining problems, the author of which was Hilbert himself, constitute only part of the problems posed by him by that time. These circumstances support the special nature of the choice of problems contained in the document - here are only those most important, in Hilbert’s opinion, tasks that were then facing mathematics, reflection on which could help “to imagine the possibility of the development of mathematical knowledge in the near future.”

The further course of events showed that the choice of problem made by Hilbert was in the main: the development of ideas related to their content constituted a significant part of the mathematics of the 20th century. Many talented mathematicians from different countries of the world took part in solving these problems, including Hilbert himself and his many students. A remarkable place among them belongs to accounting mathematicians. At that time, Russia was not yet a powerful mathematical power like Germany or France, although it already had recognized mathematical schools and a number of outstanding mathematicians for the world, among them, among them, the leading mathematical genius - N. I. Lobachevsky, P. L. Chebyshev. However, the golden age of accounting mathematics was still to come. At the congress in Paris, the Russian delegation was relatively small - 9 people (compare: France - 90, Germany - 25) and made only one report “On the disappearance (we would say about zeros - S.D.) of the function H of several variables,” which was made by Kharkov professor M.A. Tikhomandrikiy.
