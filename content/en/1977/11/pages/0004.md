Constitution of the USSR

The science of a society building communism

60 years will separate us from that significant day when the workers and peasants of Russia took power into their own hands, creating the world's first pro-Pagarian state. The Great October Social Revolution opened the path to the social for humanity. Since then, ten states have consciously embarked on this path. Every year socialism becomes an increasingly powerful political, economic and social system of the modern world.

The October Revolution radically changed the role and place of science in the state. Even Karl Marx, developing the ideas of scientific socialism, came to the conclusion that the social will have a scientific attitude towards the process of its development and improvement. V.I. Lenin deeply and meaningfully developed the question of the role of science in the construction of socialism and communism. In his significant speech at the Third All-Russian Komsomol Congress, delivered in 1920, he said: “We know that a communist society cannot be built unless industry and agriculture are revived, and they must not be revived in the old way. We need to revive them on a modern basis, based on the latest science.”

Despite extremely unfavorable conditions - civil war, devastation, monstrous backwardness and poverty - Lenin paid great attention to the scientific development of young Soviet science. It still relies on the principles envisaged by Ilch. For the first time in world history, the organization and planning of science began to be carried out as one of the most important government tasks. At the same time, much attention was paid to the development of physical and mathematical sciences, which have always been the basis of technology and production.

Before the revolution, there was only one physics institute in Russia (in Moscow), and even that was built without any support from the tsarist government. It arose on the initiative of the Russian physicist P. N. Lebedev, partly donated. But already in 1918, on the instructions of V.I. Lenin, the Physics-Technical and Optical Institutes were created in Petrograd, and a special radio laboratory was created in Nizhny Novgorod (Gorky). Soon they were joined by the Physics and Mathematics Institute of the Academy of Sciences and the Radium Institute in Petrograd, and the Central Aerodynamics Institute in Moscow. Teler in the country operated a network of research institutes specializing in the field of physical and mathematical sciences. Many of them, such as the Physico-Technical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences named after A. F. Ioffe, the Physical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences named after P. N. Lebedev, the Institute of Physical Problems of the USSR Academy of Sciences named after S. I. Vavilov, the Institute of Atomic Energy named after I. V. Kurchatov, the Mathematical Institute of the USSR Academy of Sciences named after V. A. Steklov, became the largest centers of world science. Research physics and mathematics institutes are available in all resource areas of our country. He successfully developed Soviet science at institutes that belonged to other academies, including the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences, as well as higher educational institutions.
