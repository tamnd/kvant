Olympida
in physics

The final stage of the XI All-Russian Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren was held from April 14 to 19 of this year in the city of Frunze. The participants in this stage, as always, were the winners of the Resursivan Olympids, zonal Olympids held in the RSFSR, the Olympids of the cities of Moscow and Leningrad, and the winners of the X All-Union Olympids, who received Diplomas of I and II degrees. In total, 136 people came to Frunze: 50 tenth graders, 47 ninth graders, 39 eighth graders.

The opening of the final stage of the Olympics took place on April 14. The wonderful Minister of Public Education of the Kyrgyz SSR Z. D. Dzhantakova, the representative of the jurisprudence of the Olympiad in Physics, Professor of the Kyrgyz State University L. V. Tuzov, and others addressed the participants with welcoming words and wishes for success.

On April 15, the first - theoretical - round began. As always, he walked through the classes. The 8th grader was given 4 problems, the 9th grader - 5 problems, and the 10th grader - 5 problems. In the preparation and preparation of these tasks, the host teachers of various physical conditions of the country and school teachers: L. G. Aslamazov, L. P. Bakanina, V. E. Belonuchkin, Yu. M. Brook, E. I. Butikov, N. I. Goldfarb, M. V. Grabilenkov, A. R. Zilberman, O. F. Kabardin, S. M. Kozel, A. S. Kondratiev,

S. S. Krotov, Z. V. Oganesova, V. A. Orlov, E. L. Ramishevsky, N. A. Rodina, O. Ya. Savchenko, V. E. Skorovarov, I. Sh. Slobodetsky, A. L. Stasenko, E. L. Surkov, V. G. Kharitonov and others.

Below we present the full texts of problems of the theoretical type of the final stage of the XI All-Olympid (all these problems were included in the “Problem Book «Kvant»” (see «Kvant», 1977, No. 7, 8, 9)).

8th grade

1. The current model of the crane is capable of lifting 10 liters of concrete without the volume of cable. How many litas will a real crane, made of the same materials, lift, if the linear dimensions of the crane, cable and litas are 12 times larger than in the model?

2. Two ice floes are moving forward with the same absolute speed: one to the north, the other to the west. It turned out that at any moment of time on both ice floes it is possible to arrange the clocks in such a way that the speeds of the ends of the second hands relative to the Earth will be equal, and for each moment of time such an arrangement is unique. Determine how far the ice floes move per day if the length of each second hand is 1 cm. The clock dials are located horizontally.
