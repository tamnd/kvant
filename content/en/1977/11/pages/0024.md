Georgian mathematicians (from left to right): A. M. Razmadze, A. K. Kharadze (stont), G. N. Nikoladze, N. I. Muskhelshvili

In analytic number theory, remarkable results were obtained by I. M. Vinogradov M. V. In 1924, he showed that many problems of analytic number theory can be reduced to estimates of “trigonometric sums,” that is, sums of the form

$$\sum e^{2\pi i f(x)},$$

where $f$ is some function, and $x$ runs through one or another sequence of integers. In 1934, I.M. Vinogradov succeeded in this way in almost completely solving Goldbach’s value problem: every odd natural number can be represented as a sum of no more than three prime numbers.

New universities, formed in a number of cities in the country in the first years of the revolution, quickly turned into significant mathematical centers. First of all, we must name here Tbilisi and Tashkent, which were famous for their research in mathematical physics (Tbilisi), nonlinear differential equations and mathematical statistics (Tashkent). The organizers of the Georgian Mathematical School were N. I. Muskhelshvili, G. N. Nikoladze, A. M. Razmadze and A. K. Kharadze*). The founder of the Tashkent mathematical school was V.I. Romanovsky.

By the beginning of the Great Patriotic War, Soviet mathematics had gained enormous scientific prestige throughout the world. Her research covered practically all areas of mathematical thought. Many mathematicians took part in solving problems of natural science, technology and organization of production. The results of mathematicians were especially significant in connection with the problems raised by the development of authorization. The problem of flying in a skimming fluid prompted the construction of a theory of quasiconformal mappings by M. A. Lavrentiev. A major success on the eve of the Great Patriotic War was achieved in the construction of a mathematical theory of “shimmy” and “corkscrew”, which were the cause of the death of many aircraft. As a rule, having got into a “spin” or “shimmy”, the plane died along with the occupant.

*) See «Kvant», 1975, No. 9, p. 12
