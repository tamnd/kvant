The Great Patriotic War did not pass many Soviet mathematicians: thousands of them went to the front, many switched to solving problems necessary for victory, the rest did not stop working at their posts, believing in victory and creating new scientific values ​​for the future. Such prominent scientists as Yu. V. Linnik, A. A. Lyapunov, M. V. Bebutov fought at the fronts. Unfortunately, not everyone returned from the war fields, and Soviet mathematics lost many talented scientists. Some young mathematicians, having served in the Army throughout the war years, forever connected their lives with it. Many major military specialists who made significant contributions to Soviet military science began their career as mathematicians before the war.

Already the first days of the war showed that Soviet science could be an effective weapon in the fight. Tables were created for bombing from low-speed aircraft (“maize bombers”). A. N. Kolmogorov created the theory of artificial dispersion to increase the likelihood of hitting cells. This theory found application in mine attacks on naval vessels, as well as in anti-aircraft artillery fire.

M.A. Lavrentyev created a theory of the action of a shaped charge, the idea of ​​which was known to the hot workers and was used in the blasting of rocks. During the war it was used both by us and by other warring states. After the war, this theory was widely used in peaceful practice in the construction of dams, canals, and various types of embankments.

Problems of enormous importance in mathematics arose in connection with ensuring the quality of mass industrial products. Individual quality control of each manufactured product required a huge number of inspectors. It was impossible to do this, since there was already a catastrophic lack of force work. In addition, in some cases, checking each product is unacceptable, since it leads to irreparable damage. For example, to test a fuse, you need to detonate it. It was at this time that the idea of ​​constructing a theory of statistical product quality control arose. A. N. Kolmogorov took an active part in its creation. Currently, this theory is vigorously developing along two paths: 1) checking the quality of already manufactured batches (acceptance control) and 2) managing the quality of the product during the manufacturing process (technical or operational control).
