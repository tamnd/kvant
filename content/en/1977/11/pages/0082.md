Problem 3. Under the conditions of the previous problem, determine what angle the thread forms with the vertical at the moment when the projection of the speed of the load onto the vertical direction is called?

At the initial moment, when the load is in position 1, its speed is zero. Of course, the projection of the velocity onto the vertical axis $Y$ (Fig. 6) is also zero. In position 2, the vertical projection of the velocity is again zero, since the velocity is directed horizontally. This means that when the load moves from position 1 to position 2, this projection first increases, then decreases, and somewhere it is maximum. Let us find the condition for the maximum vertical projection of the speed of the load.

When the load moves, its acceleration also continuously changes:

in position 1 it is equal to $g$ and directed vertically downwards, and in position 2 the acceleration in abscylite value is equal to $2|\vec{g}|$ and directed vertically upwards. Therefore, as $h = l \cos \alpha$.

You can get the answer in a shorter way. As is known, at the extremum points of a function, its arbitrary function turns into a thread. The vertical projection of speed in an arbitrary position of the load is determined by the corresponding

$$v_y = v \sin \alpha = \sqrt{2|\vec{g}|l \cos \alpha} \sin \alpha.$$

Let’s find an arbitrary one with respect to $\alpha$ and equate it to zero:

$$\sqrt{\frac{|\vec{g}|l}{2\cos \alpha}} (3\cos^2 \alpha - 1) = 0.$$

The condition $3\cos^2 \alpha - 1 = 0$ coincides with the maximum condition (*) found above.
