By what I have just said, I would not like to give readers the false impression that in our day science can be fully developed only within the walls of academic institutions. In fact, major scientific discoveries can be made in factory laboratories, college departments, and research institutes of applied processes. It's all about the person, his talent, passion, and the atmosphere around him. If he is interested in science, it is in the foreground, and the problems of his personal career do not bother him; if he is a generator of new ideas and is able to find approaches to solving them; if he is able to think critically about his results and listen to the suggestions of others; If institutions have sound scientific content and mutual assistance, then success is assured.

In connection with the mathematization of science and strengthened scientific and technological progress, fundamental scientific problems arise today in institutions of the applied process.

It is important not to neglect practice problems, since they give rise to formulations of mathematical problems of great importance. In order for individual practical problems to be balanced against general problems, it is necessary to build mathematical models of real phenomena in the essence of these problems and their basis. It is also important to see behind general mathematical constructions the possibility of reflecting real phenomena of the world around us - biological, physical, economic, engineering, organizational. In our time, when mathematical methods have produced literally all sciences, when many thousands of mathematicians work in scientific institutions of the applied process, it is especially important that the emergence of mathematics be visible to a sufficiently wide audience both by mathematicians themselves and by representatives of other fields of knowledge and activity.

We have already said that the Great October Revolution, being a powerful impetus for the progress of mathematics in our country, created exceptionally favorable conditions for the development of its abstract directions and the application of mathematical methods in natural science, engineering, and production organization. This success caused a huge influx of capable young people into universities, their desire to test their strength in scientific research and make their contribution to the progress of mathematics. Therefore, during the years of Soviet power, many hundreds of major mathematical researchers were trained, who obtained significant results in various fields of mathematical science, continued their own promising paths in our science, and created new areas of scientific research.

Further, a brief description of what Soviet mathematicians have done over the past sixty years would require a multi-volume essay and the work of a large group of specialists. That is why here we are forced to limit ourselves to only a cursory overview of a small proportion of important areas of scientific research. In doing so, we will be forced to mention only a small number of names, although many of those who will not be mentioned here deserve the highest praise for their scientific discoveries.
