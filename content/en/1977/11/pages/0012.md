Borisovich Khariton and Viktor Nikolaevich Kondratev. Yu. B. Khariton continued a lot of work related to explosive chemical reactions. The largest theoretician in this area is another student of Semenov, Academician Yakov Borisovich Zeldovich. He developed both the theory of chain reactions and the theory of combustion. These theories are of great importance for technology, in particular for explosive technology.

In 1928, at one of the regular seminars, Abram Fedorovich Ioffe said: “I must read you a short note, a copy of which was sent to me by Professor Raman from India.” This was a reprint of a note that was published in the famous English magazine “Nature” (in Russian - “Nature”). It has a section called “Letters to the Editor,” where scientists typically briefly report their new work. This magazine is daily, and therefore you can quickly publish the necessary information.

Raman's note discussed the fact that when light is scattered in a liquid, the spectrum of the scattered light differs from the spectrum of the incident light. If a liquid is illuminated by light of a certain wavelength, for example, yellow, then the scattered light contains the same wavelength (the same color), but, in addition, other colors, new spectral lines appear. This is the phenomenon that Raman wrote about - the appearance of additional lines in the spectrum of the scattered liquid.

When Ioffe read this note, Professor Rozhansky, who was sitting next to me, who usually listened attentively at seminars but rarely spoke, suddenly showed the necessary excitement. He stood up and said: “I don’t understand anything. I know well that the work described here was done by Mandelstam in Moscow about two years ago. I know for sure that this phenomenon was discovered by Mandelstam at critical

L. I. Mandelstam

and for two years he studied it carefully.” This is how we, young physicists, learned that in Moscow there was a large scientific school of physicists, headed by Leonid Isaakovich Mandelstam.

Raman's note indicated that he had just made his discovery. And Leonid Isaakovich Mandelstam, as we learned, not only discovered this phenomenon, but together with Grigory Samuilovich Landsberg carried out research, found the main patterns, gave a complete theory of this phenomenon and has already sent a detailed article to print. But it was received by the editors of the Journal, in which it was sent to Mandelstam, two weeks later than Raman’s note in Nature. This note was broadcast over the radio as Raman was in a hurry. Unfortunately, the effect of the appearance of additional spectral lines in scattered light is now often called the Raman effect. True, objective lost scientists, soon after this phenomenon was discovered, in accordance with the designation and books, called it the Mandelstam-Landsberg-Raman effect.
