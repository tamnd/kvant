Now let’s find $\overrightarrow{MN} = \overrightarrow{MA} + \overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{DN}$ and write down the conditions for the perpendicularity of the vectors $\overrightarrow{MN}, \overrightarrow{AD_1}$ and $\overrightarrow{MN}, \overrightarrow{DC_1}$; this gives us two equations from which we find $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and then the length of the vector $\overrightarrow{MN}$.

Let's carry out the necessary calculations:

$$\overrightarrow{MN} = \overrightarrow{MA} + \overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{DN} = -\overrightarrow{AM} + \overrightarrow{AD} + \overrightarrow{DN} = -\alpha \cdot \overrightarrow{AD_1} + \overrightarrow{AD} + \beta \cdot \overrightarrow{DC_1} = (\beta \alpha; a(1-\alpha); a(\beta-\alpha)).$$

Writing the scalar products $\overrightarrow{MN} \cdot \overrightarrow{AD_1}$ and $\overrightarrow{MN} \cdot \overrightarrow{DC_1}$ in coordinates and equating them to zero (the condition of perpendicularity of two vectors), we obtain the system

$$\begin{cases} a^2(1-\alpha) + a^2(\beta-\alpha) = 0, \\ a^2\beta + a^2(\beta-\alpha) = 0, \end{cases}$$

where

$$\begin{cases} 1 + \beta - 2\alpha = 0, \\ 2\beta - \alpha = 0, \\ \alpha = \frac{2}{3}, \beta = \frac{1}{3}. \end{cases}$$

Now

$$\overrightarrow{MN} = \left( \frac{a}{3}; \frac{a}{3}; -\frac{a}{3} \right),$$

$$|\overrightarrow{MN}| = \frac{a^2}{9} + \frac{a^2}{9} + \frac{a^2}{9} = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$
