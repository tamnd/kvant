Let's write Newton's second law in projections on the horizontal and vertical axes (see Fig. 7):

$$|\vec{F}| \cos \alpha - |\vec{F}|_{\text{tp}} = 0,$$
$$|\vec{N}| + |\vec{F}| \sin \alpha - m |\vec{g}| = 0.$$
Besides,
$$|\vec{F}_{\text{tp}}| = \mu |\vec{N}|.$$
From these three equations we find
$$|\vec{F}| = \frac{\mu m |\vec{g}|}{\cos \alpha + \mu \sin \alpha}.$$

We see that the absolute magnitude of the force $\vec{F}$ is a function of the angle $\alpha$. To find the extremum of this function, we calculate its derivative and equate it to zero:

$$-\frac{\mu m |\vec{g}| (-\sin \alpha + \mu \cos \alpha)}{(\cos \alpha + \mu \sin \alpha)^2} = 0.$$

From here
$$tg \alpha = \mu, \text{ и } \alpha = \arctg \mu.$$

The problem can be solved in other ways. Then the actions of the forces $\vec{N}$ and $\vec{F}_{\text{tp}}$ are the action of one force - the support reaction force $\vec{R}$ (see Fig. 7). Since the body moves uniformly, the vector sum of all forces is zero, that is, the forces $mg$, $\vec{R}$ and $\vec{F}$ form a closed triangle. However, from its side is the force of gravity $mg$,

directed vertically downwards. The other side of the triangle is the support reaction $\vec{R}$. The magnitude of this force depends on the direction of the force $\vec{F}$, but its direction is unchanged and is determined only by the value of the friction coefficient (see Fig. 7):

$$tg \beta = \frac{|\vec{F}_{\text{tp}}|}{|\vec{N}|} = \mu.$$

The force $\vec{F}$, generally speaking, can be directed at any angle $\alpha$ to the horizon. But the body moves uniformly, and the triangle of forces must be closed. This is achieved by the fact that simultaneously with a change in the direction of the force $\vec{F}$, its magnitude and the magnitude of the force $\vec{R}$ change. figure 8 shows two triangles defined by the corresponding

$$mg + \vec{R}_1 + \vec{F}_1 = 0$$
$$mg + \vec{R}_2 + \vec{F}_2 = 0.$$
