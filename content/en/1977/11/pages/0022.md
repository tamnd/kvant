Immediately before the October Revolution, N. N. Luzin completed his doctoral dissertation “Integral and trigonometric series.” Numerous results, beautiful constructions, formulation of a large number of new problems for further research, lively presentation - all this attracted the attention of mathematical youth to Luzin's ideas. In addition, Luzin knew how to interest his specialists, convince his followers of the importance of the issues that arose for science, and instill faith in the presence of creative abilities. These qualities were still in 1916-1917. attracted the first group of students to him - D. E. Menshov, P. S. Aleksandrov and A. Ya. Khinchina. Their first independent works were in the circle of ideas of the teacher - the theory of integrals, the convergence of trigonometric series, the structure of the so-called Borel sets. These works largely determined the further interests of his research. D. E. Menshov devoted his main works to the theory of trigonometric series and series of orthogonal functions; A. Ya. Khinchin - methodological theory of functions and its application in number theory, probability theory and statistical physics; P. S. Aleksandrov - set theory, and later - topology.

Already the first admissions of students to Moscow University after 1917 gave rise to the second generation of active students of Luzin. Luzin organized a seminar at which each participant was asked to design a definition for a monograph; then establish a discussion of the abstract with the formulation of questions, identifying opportunities for managing and summarizing the evidence. Such discussions perfectly educated scientific mathematicians and taught them to think independently.

In the twenties, Luzin’s students called their group “Lusitans.” This ascent was emphasized by his teacher, his scientific ideas, and lectures. Luzin's students were proud of their involvement in the group developing his ideas, and strived to give the maximum of what they were capable of in science. As P. S. Aleksandrov recalls, “the ideological basis for this scientific enthusiasm was the great patriotic ideas of Soviet scientific culture, which aroused in students and scientists that genuine scientific passion, which had never manifested itself with such force in the degree of trust in the mathematics department of Moscow University.”
