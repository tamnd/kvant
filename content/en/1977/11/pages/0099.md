The curve that is shown here is called "kappa" due to the complexity of the same letter and the Greek alphabet. This curve was first constructed in 1662 by the Belgian mathematician René François de Luz (1622–1685), when he corresponded with Christan Huygens.

The method given here for constructing the soul of a kapysha belongs to I. Newton. Let point O be offset on line l. Take the square AMB (M is the vertex of a right angle). The upper right branch of the kapysha is described by vertex M when the square moves so that vertex B slides along the right ray of the line l, M is above the line l and the leg AM passes through O. The remaining branches of the kapysha are constructed similarly.

By lengthening the brass knuckles AM of the AMB square, we can get arbitrarily distant points of the hood.
