The oxygen pressure in them was changed with a pressure gauge, and to prevent phosphorus vapor from damaging it, a trap cooled with liquid air was placed between the pressure gauge and the vessel in which the reaction took place. Phosphorus vapor condensed in the trap, resulting in a flow of phosphorus vapor directed towards the trap. Oxygen, on its way into the vessel, also passed through the trap, but in the direction opposite to the direction of the flow of phosphorus vapor. Bodenstein believed that at low oxygen pressures it was simply “blown away” by the phosphorus parametrios and did not enter the vessel at all, so the reaction did not occur because there was no oxygen in the vessel.

After reading this note, Semenov decided to repeat his experiments. At the same time, he became convinced that Bodenstein’s criticism was partially correct - the oxygen pressure did not change quite accurately. But even after eliminating the errors that arose, it turned out that the reaction between phosphorus and oxygen in the gaseous state will still begin only at certain minimum pressures. At the same time, another completely incomprehensible phenomenon was discovered. The critical pressure itself depends on the size of the vessel in which the reaction took place. If the vessel is spherical, then the critical pressure increases in proportion to the diameter of the vessel. The larger the vessel, the greater the pressure at which the reaction begins. As N.N. Semenov later wrote, having discovered this phenomenon, he completely ceased to understand anything.

Semenov spoke about his work at a seminar at the Physico-Technical Institute. Everyone already knew Bodenstein’s criticisms and began to fall back on experience again. No matter how much Semenov convinced those who began that there were no more mistakes in his experiments, they reacted to this with distrust, especially since Nikolai Nikolaevich could not explain the results obtained at that time.

N. N. Semenov

Being firmly convinced that he was right, N. N. Semenov published several articles about the results obtained. After this, a letter came from Bodenstein, in which he completely agreed with these results, recognized the greater significance of the discovered phenomena and offered Semenov further work to be published in the journal, which he himself edited. Soon after this, N. N. Semenov created a qualitative theory of the phenomena he discovered.

This was a major discovery that received worldwide recognition. It followed entirely a new direction in science - the study of branched chemical chain reactions, which was successfully developing not only here, but also abroad. For these works, N. N. Semenov, together with the English chemist Hinshelwood, who worked on the same issues, was awarded the Nobel Prize in 1956.
