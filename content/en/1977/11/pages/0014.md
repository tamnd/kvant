⟦folio 12⟧

G. V. Kurdomov

Russian physicists led by Pyotr Savvich Tartakovsky, and in Dnepropetrovsk - led by Georgy Vyacheslavovich Kurdomov, now an academician.

Unfortunately, in Tomsk the incization of Leningrad physicists did not meet with much support from local physicists, and this institute practically collapsed. But the institute, organized in Dnepropetrovsk, quickly grew stronger and became famous for its work. This is primarily explained by the fact that it was located in the center of Ukrainian metallurgy, and the director of the institute, G.V. Kurdomov, was a leading specialist in this field. The work that he did while still at the Leningrad Institute of Physics and Technology related to the RENT genographic study of one of the most important processes in metallurgy - marketing transformations in steels. Kurdomov was the first to show the nature of the marketing transformations that occur during steel hardening. This classic work earned him worldwide fame. It is of great technical importance.

In 1932, the Ural Physico-Technical Institute (Ural Physico-Technical Institute) was separated from the Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute, which later settled in Sverdlovsk, the largest industrial center of the Urals. I was included there too. Although we were called the Ural Physico-Technical Institute, before the construction of the building in Sverdlovsk we worked in Leningrad and moved to Sverdlovsk only in 1936. Nowadays this institute is called the Institute of Metal Physics; it became one of the largest physical institutes in our country. Now it is headed by the famous theoretical physicist academician Sergei Vasilyevich Vonsovsky, who at the time of the institute’s creation was a young employee who had just graduated from the university.

During the first five years, physics in our country developed very rapidly, and the number of scientific workers grew rapidly. I remember that during my time in 1927-1930, the Leningrad Physico-Technical Institute grew by three. In the end, several independent institutes emerged from it: the Institute of Chemical Physics under the leadership of N. N. Semenov, which subsequently moved to Moscow; Institute of Communications, headed by Professor D. A. Rozhansky; Highly cultural institute under the leadership of A. A. Chernyshev. The Agrophysical Institute was also organized, headed by A.F. Ioffe himself. He believed that physics should be used not only in industry, but also in agriculture.

Thus, starting in 1928, guided by the decisions of the party and government and his own incision, A.F. Ioffe was able to establish physical institutes in a number of industrial centers of the Soviet Union, which greatly contributed to the development of science and industry in our country.
