XI All-Union Olympiad for SCHOOLCHILDREN

I. Klumova, M. Smolyansky

Olympics
in mathematics

This year the XI All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren was dedicated to the 60th anniversary of the Great October Revolution.

The final round of the XI All-Union Olympiad in mathematics took place in the capital of Estonia, the city of Tallinn.

There are 153 schoolchildren in Tallinnik - winners of the Resource Olympiads and children who took 1st and 2nd places at the previous Olympiad. For the first time, a team (3 people) of students from vocational schools, winners of the Leningrad vocational school Olympiad, was represented at the Olympiad. The hosts of the final round of the Olympiad, the Talin residents, also competed with their team of 3 people.

The modern students included 42 eighth-graders, 56 ninth-graders and 55 tenth-graders.

The opening of the final stage of the XI All-Union Olympiad took place on April 14 in the assembly hall of the Tallinn Political Institute. The Chairman of the Organizing Committee, Academician of the Academy of Sciences of the Estonian USSR A.K. Khumal congratulated the participants of the Olympiad on the start of the compilation, wished them success and expressed greetings and congratulations from the Minister of Education of the USSR M.A. Prokofiev.

For almost six months, the jury meticulously selected problems for the Olympiad from a large list of “candidate” problems submitted by mathematicians from different cities. To help readers notice these tasks, we present their terms in full. Most of them have already been published in the Problem Book «Kvant» (see Nos. 7-9)*.

As usual, the final stage of the Olympiad was held in two rounds.

The schoolchildren began solving the problems of the first round on April 15. Each class took 5 hours to solve the problems.

Below we provide a list of tasks for the first round.

8th grade

1. A non-self-intersecting closed broken line is given on the plane, no three vertices of which lie on the same straight line. It is known that if a straight line passes through two or more cut off points, then the sum of all numbers written near these points is equal to zero. Prove that the number of special pairs is even. (M457)

2. Several points are marked on the plane that do not lie on the same straight line, and a number is written next to each one. It is known that if a straight line passes through two or more cut off points, then the sum of all numbers written near these points is equal to zero. Prove that all numbers are equal to zero. (M451)

3. The segment connecting the midpoints of two circumcircles AB and AC of triangle ABC intersects sides AB and AC at points K and L. Prove that points A, K, L and the circumcenter O of triangle ABC are the vertices of a rhombus.
