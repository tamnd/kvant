Coordinate method

Vector algebra and the coordinate method, contained in the new school mathematics curriculum, are a powerful tool for solving many geometric problems, primarily because they do not require consideration of complex geometric configurations. These methods reduce a geometric problem to an algebraic one, which is usually easier to solve than the original geometric one. Every engineer must master the coordinate method, and therefore special attention is paid to the importance of the coordinate method in entrance exams to technical disciplines.

The program of entrance examinations for universities in mathematics (see “Quant”, 1977, No. 2) stipulates that “the examinee must be able... to use the methods of algebra and basic analysis when solving geometric problems” (clause 8 of the “Basic skills” section).

These questions are the subject of this article.

3. Section problems

Typically, in such problems, you need to find the cross section of a polyhedron by a plane (that is, its shape and area). The section produces a polygon, the vertices of which are the points of intersection of the cutting plane with the edges of the polyhedron, and the sides are the straight segments along which the cutting plane intersects the faces of the polyhedron.

It is worth recalling that a cutting plane intersects parallel planes along parallel lines - this often helps in solving problems, for example, on the section of a cube. When calculating the cross-sectional area, a formula is useful that relates the area of ​​a figure to the area of ​​its projection $S_{\pi p} = S_{\phi \pi r} \cdot \cos \varphi$ (see “Geometry 10”, § 50). Often this formula allows you not to find the section itself at all, but immediately gives its area.

Problem 5. Name the shape and cross-sectional area of ​​a cube $ABCD_1B_1C_1D_1$ with edge 1 plane passing through the vertex $A$, the middle of the edge $BC$ and the center of the face $CDD_1C_1$.

Let us construct a rectangular coordinate system, as shown in figure 5. The equation of the cutting plane as a general equation of the plane has the form

$$ax + by + cz + d = 0.$$

The relationship between the coefficients $a, b, c, d$ can be found from the condition that the points $A(0; 0; 0), E(1; 1/2; 0)$ (the middle of the edge $BC$) and $M(1/2; 1; 1/2)$ (the center of the edge $CDD_1C_1$) lie in this plane, that is, the coordinates of these points must satisfy the equation of the plane. From here we get the system

$$\begin{cases} a \cdot 0 + b \cdot 0 + c \cdot 0 + d = 0, \\ a \cdot 1 + b \cdot \frac{1}{2} + c \cdot 0 + d = 0, \\ a \cdot \frac{1}{2} + b \cdot 1 + c \cdot \frac{1}{2} + d = 0. \end{cases}$$

Two two-fer angles at the edges of the base are right angles, and the other two are equal to $\varphi$. Find the radius of the sphere inscribed in the parameter.

Let's construct a rectangular coordinate system, as shown in figure 8. The center $O_1$ of the sphere must be removed from all faces of the paramid, in particular, from the coordinate planes, at a distance $r$—the radius of the sphere, therefore its coordinates have the form $O_1(r; r; r)$. The plane $SBC$ contains the points $B(a; a; 0)$, $C(0; a; 0)$ and $S(0; 0; a \mathrm{tg} \varphi)$, and the equation is found:

$$\mathrm{tg} \varphi \cdot y + z - a \mathrm{tg} \varphi = 0.$$

The distance from the point $O_1$ to the plane $SBC$, on the one hand, should be equal to $r$; on the other hand, it can be expressed through the coordinates of the point $S$, $B$, $C$: the vector $\vec{O_1Q}$, perpendicular to the plane $SBC$ ($Q \in (SBC)$), is collinear to the vector $\vec{n} = (0; \mathrm{tg} \varphi; 1)$ (the coordinates of the vector $n$ are found from the equation of the plane $SBC$), therefore

$$\vec{O_1Q} = h \cdot \vec{n} = (0; h \mathrm{tg} \varphi; h).$$

Now, knowing the coordinates of the point $O_1$, the plane $SBC$ is found (that is, the coordinates of the point $Q$ satisfy the equation of the plane $SBC$):

$$\mathrm{tg} \varphi (h \mathrm{tg} \varphi + r) + h + r - a \mathrm{tg} \varphi = 0,$$

where

$$h = (a \mathrm{tg} \varphi - r - r \mathrm{tg} \varphi) \cos^2 \varphi,$$

$$|O_1Q| = |h| \cdot |\vec{n}| = (a \mathrm{tg} \varphi - r - r \mathrm{tg} \varphi) \cos^2 \varphi \cdot \frac{1}{\cos \varphi} = (a \mathrm{tg} \varphi - r - r \mathrm{tg} \varphi) \cos \varphi$$
