V. Mayer

"Sticky" jet

It is quite difficult to explain theoretically the result of the experiment that we suggest you do. But the observed effect is very beautiful, and the experience of the present itself is simple that you can perform it without much difficulty.

Connect a glass tube with a diameter of about 6 mm with a pipette-like end, the end of which has a diameter of about 1 mm, with a rubber hose to a water tap. Let the water flow, get a stream and direct it to the upper end of a standing vertical clean glass tube with a diameter of 4-6 mm and a length of about half a meter. In this case, depending on the direction and angle between the tubes, you will observe remarkable phenomena: the jet will “stick” to the tube, change the direction of its movement, go around the tube and, finally, circle it several times!

Interestingly, the effective application of jets of liquid or gas to a solid surface attracted the attention of the young Romanian avnator Henri Koanda. In 1910, while testing a jet aircraft, he discovered that the engine flames were “sucked” to the protective planes of the fuselage. The Koanda effect has now found practical application.
