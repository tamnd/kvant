Experimental problems of the Physics Olympiad

On April 17, an experimental round of the final stage of the XI All-Union Schoolchildren Olympiad in Physics took place in the laboratories of the Faculty of Physics of the Kyrgyz State University. Each participant in the Olympiad was asked to solve two experimental problems. Let's talk about these problems and their solutions.

8th grade

Task 1. Determine the density of the metal found in one of two pieces of plastamine, if it is known that the masses of plastamine in the common pieces are the same. Evaluate the power of obtaining results. It is not allowed to extract metal from plasticine.

Equipment: 1) weight with weights, 2) glass of water, 3) stamps.

Using scales with weights, you can determine the mass of a piece of plasticine with metal ($m_{\pi\pi}+m_{\mu}$), the mass of pure plasticine ($m_{\pi\pi}$) and, by their difference, the mass of metal ($m_{\mu}$) found in one of the pieces of plasticine. By lifting these pieces of plasticine first in air and then in water, you can find the protruding force and, knowing the density of water, you can calculate the volumes of the pieces ($V_{\pi\pi}+V_{\mu}$ and $V_{\pi\pi}$). The volume of metal ($V_{\mu}$) can be determined by the difference between these volumes. Then the density of the metal

$$\rho_{\mu}=\frac{m_{\mu}}{V_{\mu}}.$$

There were two options: in some pieces of plastine, finding a piece of pure plastine with a mass equal to the mass of plastine in the composite piece, projectilin was given (in an indefinite amount). In this case, to solve the problem, it would be necessary to take a piece of pure plasticine, the mass of which is equal to the mass ($m$) of the composite piece (volume $V$), and determine its volume ($V_0$). The density of the metal can be found by re-

Task 2. Using two diameter meters and two measuring rulers, determine the possibility of a mechanical circuit and the parameters of the component elements in the box (Fig. 1) without opening it.

Notes. It is not allowed to protrude the wire end and their distance with force, exceeding the maximum readings of the diameter gauge.

This problem with a “black box” was solved by many Olympiad participants. Inside the box was a mechanical circuit consisting of a freely moving lever, a spring and four wire leads with shouts at the ends (Fig. 2). It was possible to unravel this scheme by comparing alternately the mixing of the wire ends with one of them stationary.

Let the end $A$ remain motionless at first, and the end $D$ shift, for example, to the right by $\Delta x_D = 2$ cm. In this case, the end $B$ also shifts to the right, but by an amount of $\Delta x_B = 3$ cm, and the end $C$ moves motionless. If you leave the end $B$ motionless, and the end $D$ is moved by $\Delta x_D = 1$ cm, then the ends $A$ and $C$ will move in the same direction, but by $\Delta x_A = \Delta x_C = 3$ cm. If you leave two ends motionless at once: $A$ and $B$ (for which between the corresponding calls it was possible hold down the ruler), then the ends $D$ and $C$ will not move in any way. But if at the same time the end $C$ is attached to the lever directly, and the end $C$ is attached through a spring. Specific changes make it possible to determine the compliance of the lever area, the stiffness of the spring and its maximum permissible stretch.

9th grade

Task 1. Determine the volume of air removed by the Komovsky pump in one cycle and the atmospheric pressure.

Equipment: 1) Komovsky pump, 2) vacuum gauze with a cap, 3) pressure gauge, 4) ruler, 5) mathematical tables.

Using a ruler, you can change the internal diameter and average height of the hood and determine the volume $V_0$ of air under the hood.

Let us denote atmospheric pressure by $p_0$, the pressure under the hood after the first revolution of the pump handle by $p_1$, and the volume of air removed per revolution by $\Delta V$. Then, when the handle is slowly rotated (when the air temperature does not change), the following correspondence is performed:

$$p_0 V_0 = p_1 (V_0 + \Delta V).$$

After the second revolution, we can write a similar equality:

$$p_1 V_0 = p_2 (V_0 + \Delta V).$$

In principle, from these two controls one can find the atmospheric pressure $p_0$ and the volume $\Delta V$ of air removed per revolution. However, it is practically inconvenient to change the air pressure after each revolution ($p_1$ differs very little from $p_2$). The best pressure changes after changes to $n$ revolutions and create an appropriate control system:

$$\begin{align*}
p_0 V_0^k &= p_k (V_0 + \Delta V)^k \\
p_0 V_0^n &= p_n (V_0 + \Delta V)^n.
\end{align*}$$

From here

$$V_0^{n-k} = \frac{p_n}{p_k} (V_0 + \Delta V)^{n-k}.$$

Progolaarithmic this control, one can find $\Delta V$ and then $p_0$.
