In this case, of course, you need to be careful not to lose the roots or introduce extraneous solutions, which requires special care due to the presence of a parameter in the equation.

Example 3: Solve the equation
$$x^2 - \sqrt{a-x} = a.$$

Having rewritten this equation in the form $x^2 - a = \sqrt{a-x}$, we immediately conclude *) that we must solve the equation
$$(x^2 - a)^2 = a - x$$
(2)
and reverse those roots that satisfy the equation
$$x^2 - a \geq 0.$$ (3)

Equation (2) can be viewed as the square relative $a$:
$$a^2 - a(2x^2 + 1) + (x^4 + x) = 0,$$
whence $a = x^2 + x$ or $a = x^2 - x + 1$.

Equation
$$x^2 + x - a = 0$$
(4)
has roots at $a \in [-1/4; \infty]$:
$$x_1 = \frac{-1 - \sqrt{1 + 4a}}{2},$$
$$x_2 = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 4a}}{2},$$

with $x_1 = x_2$ at $a = -1/4$. Since for the roots $x_{1,2}$, by virtue of equation (4), we have
$$x^2 - a = -x,$$
then the verification of inequality (3) is evidence of the verification of the inequality $-x \geq 0$, which for $x_1$ is valid for all $a \in [-1/4; \infty]$, and for $x_2$ is satisfied only for $a \in [-1/4; 0]$.

Equation
$$x^2 - x + 1 - a = 0$$
(5)
has roots at $a \in [3/4; \infty]$:
$$x_3 = \frac{1 - \sqrt{4a - 3}}{2},$$
$$x_4 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{4a - 3}}{2},$$

*) See, for example, G.V. Dorofeev, M.K. Potapov, N.Kh. Rozov, “Mathematics manual for university applicants” (M., “Nauka”, 1976, pp. 176–177).

with $x_3 = x_4$ at $a = 3/4$. Since for the roots $x_{3,4}$, by virtue of equation (5), we have
$$x^2 - a = x - 1,$$
then the verification of inequality (3) is a witness to the verification of the inequality $x - 1 \geq 0$, which for $x_3$ is false for any $a$, and for $x_4$ it is satisfied for $a \in [1; \infty]$.

To summarize, let's write down the answer:
at $a \in ] -\infty; -1/4[$ there are no roots;
for $a = -1/4$ one root: $x = -1/2$;
for $a \in ] -1/4; 0]$ two roots: $x = x_1, x = x_2$;
for $a \in ] 0; 1[$ one root: $x = x_1$;
at $a \in ] 1, \infty[$ there are two roots: $x = x_1, x = x_4$.

In the next problem, the same method can be successfully applied to solve a system of equations.

Example 4. Solve a system of equations
$$\begin{cases} ax^2 + 2by = a, \\ by^2 + 2ax = b. \end{cases}$$

It is clear that if $a = b = 0$, then any pair of numbers $(x, y)$ satisfies the system. If $b = 0$ but $a \neq 0$ or if $a = 0$ but $b \neq 0$, then the system has no solution. Let, finally, $a \neq 0$ and $b \neq 0$. Then, expressing $y$ from the first equation of the system and substituting it into the second, we obtain an equation of the fourth degree relative to $x$ with two parameters $a$ and $b$, which can be considered as a quadratic relative $b$:
$$4b^2 - 2b \cdot 4ax - a^2(1 - x^2)^2 = 0.$$
From here we find two equations:
$$b = \frac{a(1 + x)^2}{2}, \quad b = -\frac{a(1 - x)^2}{2}.$$

Our readers A. M. Avoyan and V. M. Anumyan (Yerevan) sent a worthy task in their letter. It turns out that the approach under consideration can sometimes be solved also by equations in which there is no parameter.
