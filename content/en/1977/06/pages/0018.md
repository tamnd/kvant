⟦folio 16⟧

Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)

Kepler: the squares of the average times of formation of the planets are related to the cubes of their average distances from the Sun. (Newton calls this law semi-cubic relation.) Even when Newton compared the fall of an apple and Lunyi, he knew that if a body moves in a circle, a force acts on this body, pulling it towards the center of the circle; he also knew that centripetal acceleration is proportional to the square of the speed and inversely proportional to the distance.

True, the honor of discovering the laws of circular motion is attributed to Huygens, but Huygens published his discovery only in 1673 (although he knew about it back in 1659). Newton therefore had to figure it out on his own.

Newton reasoned something like this. Kepler's third law states that for planets of the same system

$$\frac{R_1^3}{T_1^2} = \frac{R_2^3}{T_2^2} = \dots = \text{const.}$$

Let the planets move in a circle. Let us introduce the angular velocity of rotation of the planet in orbit $\omega = 2\pi/T$. Then Kepler's third law will take the form:

$$\omega_1^2 R_1^3 = \omega_2^2 R_2^3 = \dots = \text{const.}$$

Let us now assume that the force is backward

$$F = \gamma \frac{M_\odot m}{R^2}.$$
