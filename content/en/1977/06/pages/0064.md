⟦folio 62⟧

I came home and immediately got down to business. I was overcome with such determination that I even surprised myself. First, I decided to do the most difficult lessons, as Olga Nikolaevna taught me, and then take on the easier ones. Just on this day, an arithmetic task was assigned. Without thinking twice, I opened the puzzle and began to read the problem:

“There were 8 saws in the stores, and three times as many tops. One team of carpenters was sold half of the tops and three saws for 84 rubles. The remaining axes and saws were sold to another team of carpenters for 100 rubles. How much does one ax and one saw cost?

At first I didn’t understand anything at all and began to read the problem a second time, then a third time... Gradually I realized that the one who composes the problems generally confuses them so that the students cannot solve them right away. It is written: “There were 8 saws in the stores, and three times as many tops.” Well, they would simply write that there were 24 tops. After all, if there were 8 dranks, and there were three times as many tops, then it is clear to everyone that there were 24 tops. And one more thing: “One team of carpenters was sold half of the tops and 3 saws for 84 rubles.” They would simply say: “We sold 12 tops.” As if it wasn’t clear, since there were 24 tops, then half will be 12. And so they sold all this, which means for 84 rubles. Then again it says that the remaining saws and axes were sold to another team of carpenters for 100 rubles. What are the remaining ones? As if it’s impossible to say in human terms? If there were 24 axes in total, and 12 were sold, then there were 12 left. And there were only 8 saws; They sold 3 to one brigade, which means they sold 5 to another brigade. They should have written it that way, otherwise they will confuse, confuse, and then they will probably say that the guys are stupid - they didn’t know how to solve problems!

I rewrote the problem in my own way to make it look simpler, and this is what I came up with:

“The stores had 8 saws and 24 axes. One team of carpenters sold 12 tops and 3 saws for 84 rubles. Another team of carpenters sold 12 tops and 5 saws for 100 rubles. How much does one saw and one ax cost?”

Having rewritten the problem, I read it again and saw that it had become a little shorter, but still I could not figure out how to do it, because the numbers were confused in my head and prevented me from thinking. I decided to somehow shorten the problem so that it had fewer numbers. After all, it doesn’t matter at all how many of these same saws and tops were in the stores, if in the end they all sold. I shortened the problem, and it turned out like this:

“One team was sold 12 tops and 3 saws for 84 rubles. Another team was sold 12 tops and 5 saws for 100 rubles. How much does one saw and one ax cost?”
