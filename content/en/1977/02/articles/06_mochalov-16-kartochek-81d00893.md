The German priest Bartholomew Pitiscus (1561-1613) is known in the history of mathematics as the author of several books on trigonometry and extensive trigonometric tables. It was he who first coined the term “trigonometry”.

The name of the great scientist - astronomer, physicist and mathematician - Johannes Kepler (1571-1630) is now known to any schoolchild. When studying the motion of planets, he had to carry out enormous computational work and he used decimal fractions, which he learned about from Bürgi.

The Scottish baron John Napier (1550-1617) is famous as the inventor of logarithms and “Napear's sticks” - a simple device for multiplying multi-digit numbers *). In his book Slavology, published independently in 1617 before his death, he used the decimal point. In his other book, “Constructing the Amazing Table of Logarithms,” which was published after his death, but was written at great length, he recommends using the decimal point.

Henry Professor of Mathematics and Astronomy in London and Oxford

*) «Kvant», 1975, № 2, c. 42.

Brings (1561-1631) together with Napier developed a system of decimal logarithms and published the first tables of them.

The English mathematician William Oughtred (1575-1660), the inventor of the first slide rules, was a parish connection for 50 years and taught mathematics to those interested. It was he who first suggested denoting multiplication with an “oblique cross” ×.

Although “sign fantasy” did not sparkle even in the 18th century, the main struggle was already between the decimal point and the decimal point. As can be seen from our table, these signs appeared almost simultaneously. Napier, hesitating between them, used both signs. Since the appearance of G. Böckler's “New Arthmetic” (1661), the decimal point has taken root in mathematical books published in Germany. Gradually, it became firmly established in continental Europe, while in English countries they prefer the decimal point, and in England it is now placed in the middle of the line: 14.382, and in the USA - at the bottom: 14.382. (The multiplication sign in English countries is usually the “slash cross” ×.)

In the last 10-15 years, under the influence of algorithmic programming languages, the decimal point gradually became a decimal point. Since they use a decimal point and a slash for multiplication, programming people—and more and more—prefer this notation.
