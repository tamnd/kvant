Mathematics entrance exam program
for applicants to universities in 1977

GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS

At the mathematics exam, applicants to a higher education institution must show:
a) clear knowledge of mathematical definitions and theorems provided by the program, the ability to prove these theorems;
b) able to accurately and concisely express mathematical ideas in oral and written presentation, using appropriate symbols;
b) confident knowledge of mathematical knowledge and skills provided by the program, and the ability to apply them when solving problems.

The mathematics program for those entering higher education in 1977 consists of two options: option “A” and option “B”. Option “A” is intended for applicants who graduated from school in 1977 (who studied for 10 years under the new program). Option “B” - for all other persons who have completed secondary education*. Option "B" is not shown here.

Each version of the program consists of three sections. The first of them is a list of basic mathematical concepts and facts that applicants must know (be able to use them correctly when solving problems, refer to them when proving theorems). The second section contains theorems that you need to be able to prove and formulas that you need to be able to derive. The content of the theoretical part of the exams should be drawn from this section. The third section describes the basic mathematical skills that the examinee must possess.

OPTION "A"
I. BASIC MATHEMATICAL CONCEPTS AND FACTS

Arithmetic, algebra, beginnings of analysis

1. Set, element of a set; subset, union and intersection of a set.
2. Sets of natural, integer, rational and real numbers. Relationships between them.
3. Natural numbers. Prime and composite numbers. Divisor, multiple. Common divisors. Least common multiple.
4. Divisibility signs by 2, 3, 5 and 10$^n$.
5. Real numbers, their representation as decimal fractions. Comparison of real numbers.
6. Numerical intervals. Modulus (absolute value) of a real number and its geometric meaning.
7. Numeric expressions. Expressions with variables. Identically equal expressions. Abbreviated multiplication formulas.
8. Degree with a natural indicator. Monomial and polynomial. Standard form of a polynomial.
9. Polynomial with one variable. Korey polynomial.
10. Function. Method of specifying functions. Areas of definition, a lot of knowledge of functions. The inverse function of this one.
11. Numerical functions. Graph of a numerical function. Increasing and decreasing functions; periodicity, even, odd.
12. Extremum of a numerical function. The largest and smallest value of a numerical function on an interval. Necessary condition for the extremum of a function (Fermat's theorem).
13. Basic numerical functions: linear, square ($y=ax^2+bx+c$), power ($y=ax^n$, $n\in\mathbb{Z}$), exponential ($y=ax^a$, $a>0$), logarithmic; trionometric function ($y=\sin x$, $y=\cos x$, $y=\tg x$); arithmetic root

$$n y = \sqrt{x} (n \in \mathbb{N}).$$
14. Number sequences. Limit of number sequence.
15. Arithmetic and geometric progressions.
16. Equation. Set of solutions to the equation. Graph of an equation with two variables. Equivalent equations.
17. Inequalities. Set of solutions to inequalities. Equivalent inequalities.
18. Systems of equations and inequalities. System solution. Set of solutions of the system. Equivalent systems.
19. Function limit. Continuity of function.
