Decimal dobies, which make it possible to carry out calculations with dobies in the same way as with integers, occupy one of the first places among the “whales” on which our technical arithmetic calculation is based. Modern ideas about decimal additions developed gradually. The invention of decimals cannot be attributed to any one person: their history is complex and confusing. During the 15th and 16th centuries, many European scientists used their individual properties. Mathematicians in the countries of the Middle East, Central Asia and North Africa actually touched decimals back in the 11th-13th centuries.

The history of the “decimal sign”, which served to separate the whole and fractional parts of a number in decimals, is also very complicated.

We would like to introduce «Kvant» readers to the history of the development of the “decimal sign”. Using the example of the number 14 $\frac{382}{1000}$, we will show how decimal dobies were depicted in the 16th-17th centuries - see. table on p. 13.

François Viet de la Birottier (1540–1603) was a lawyer and advisor to the French queens Henry III and Henry IV. He studied mathematics “in his free time from work.” Vnet made significant contributions to all areas of contemporary mathematics, but his achievements were especially great in the development of algebraic symbolism. (However, his symbolism was not widely used. Modern algebraic symbolism mainly originates from R. Descartes’ Discourse on Method (1637).) In one of his first books, “Mathematical Tables,” published in 1579 in Paris, the author talks about the use of sixth decimals in calculations and himself widely uses them.

The outstanding Flemish scientist Simon Stevin (1548-1620) is one of the “universal geniuses” of the Renaissance. Stevin's works are related to a variety of issues in contemporary mathematics, mechanics and physics. But the small book “Tithe”, published in 1585 in Leiden, brought him the most fame.
