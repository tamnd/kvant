⟦folio 9⟧

denoted “only” with one typo: the last substitution in (14) was printed as $abac \leftrightarrow abacc$. Reader G.N. Spiridovich from Leningrad, not taking the word of the author of the brochure, found an exclusion algorithm for the “distorted Tseytin calculus”. Could you?

Tasks
1. Construct an algorithm that finds word equivalence for the associative calculus in the alphabet $\{a\}$ with a single substitution
$$a a \leftrightarrow \Lambda.$$
2. Same problem for calculus in alphabet $\{a, b\}$ with substitutions
$$\begin{cases} aaaa \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ bb \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ abab \leftrightarrow \Lambda. \end{cases}$$
3. Same problem for calculus in alphabet $\{a, b\}$ with substitutions
$$\begin{cases} aaaaa \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ bb \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ abab \leftrightarrow \Lambda. \end{cases}$$
4. Same problem for calculus in alphabet $\{a, b, c\}$ with substitutions
$$\begin{cases} aa \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ bb \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ cc \leftrightarrow \Lambda, \\ ab \leftrightarrow c, \\ ac \leftrightarrow b, \\ ba \leftrightarrow c, \\ ca \leftrightarrow b, \\ cb \leftrightarrow a. \end{cases}$$
5. Construct an associative calculus with an undecidable equivalence problem with 7 substitutions in a two-letter alphabet. e are also indicated. It is allowed to take advantage of the fact that for the Tseytin calculus (p. 8) the equivalence problem is undecidable.
6. There is a number $n$, which does not contain a zero. If there are two identical numbers or two identical two-digit numbers next to each other, then they can be crossed out. On the contrary, you are allowed to put two identical numbers or two identical two-digit numbers in any place. Prove that by combining these operations one can obtain a number less than 10$^9$. (This problem, invented by G. Galperny, was proposed at the XXXIX Moscow Mathematical Olympid.)

Tasks
our readers

1. Among 15 identical-looking balls, there is one “defective” one, which differs from all the others in weight, and one marked “standard”.
How to find a “defective” ball by no more than weighing on a cheek scale (without weights)?
M. Karajyan
(r. Stepanavan)

2. The lines passing through the bases of altitudes $BB_0$ and $CC_0$ of triangle $ABC$ are parallel to side $BC$ and intersect sides $AB$ and $CA$ (or their extensions) of the corresponding one at points $C_1$ and $B_1$. Prove that
a) $|B_0C_1| = |B_1C_0|^2$;
b) $|B_1C_1|^2 = |B_0B_1|^2 + |C_0C_1|^2 + |B_0C_0|^2$.
Y. Alla
(r. Võru)

3. Prove inequalities
$$1,71 < 1 + \frac{1}{2!} + \frac{1}{3!} + \dots + \frac{1}{n!} < 1,72.$$
S. Berkolayuk
(v. Kotovo, Belgorod region)

4. Solve equations
a) $x^5 = \overline{yyyx}$;
b) $(x + y)^2 = \overline{xy}$;
b) $(xx)^2 = yzt \frac{x}{2}$.
I. Mikhalkovich
(Minsk region)

5. In the terraedron $ABCD$, the points $A', B', C', D'$ are the centers of circles described by the faces $BCD$, $CDA$, $DAB$, $ABC$ corresponding on the plane $B'C'D'$, $C'D'A'$, $D'A'B'$, $A'B'C'$, intersect in one point.
Nechyen Kong Kwee
(r. Hanoi)
