⟦folio 49⟧

1964). Three congestible cones with a common vertex lie on a plane. Each of them touches two adjacent ones. Namely, at the top of the free section of one of these cones.

It is not difficult to say that if you describe a pyramid to the frame around each of these cones, you will get a regular triangular pyramid ZABC, in which three given cones are “inscribed”; their common vertex is in the center of the base of the pyramid, and the bases of the pyramid are in the side faces of the pyramid (Fig. 4; to solve the problem it is enough to consider only one of these cones).

Since the ZABC pyramid is regular, then $D\hat{CO} = \pi/6$. As shown above,

$$tg D\hat{OO}_1 = \cos D\hat{CO} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Thus, the desired angle is $2\arctg \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Example 4 (Kyiv Polytechnic Institute, 1970). Six congruous cones have a common vertex, and each of the cones has one common tangent with four others. The name of the sum of objects of cones to the volume of a ball, touching the plane of the bases of all cones.

To solve this problem, we choose a cube as a frame, in the center of which there is a common vertex of the cones, the bases of the cones are inscribed in the faces of the cube (Fig. 5; only one of the cones is shown). In this case, each of the six cones will have one common tangent with the four friends, and the ball touching the base of the cones will be inscribed in the cube. Let the edge of the cube be equal to $a$. Then the volume of one cone

$$V = \frac{1}{3} \pi \frac{a^2}{4} \cdot \frac{a}{2} = \frac{\pi a^3}{24},$$

and six cones $\pi a^3/4$. The volume of the sphere is $\pi a^3/6$, from where the ratio is found; it is equal to 3:2.

Exercises

1 (KSU, mekhanite, 1973). Two straight circular cones, each with its own section forming an equilateral triangle with sides $a$, lie on a horizontal plane, touching each other, having a common vertex. At what height above the plane is the point of contact of the bases of these cones?

2 (KSU, mekhanite, 1972). Four straight cones have a common vertex, and each cone has one common generatrix with its three friends. The name of the sum of the volumes of a cone to the volume of a sphere touching the plane of the bases of the cones.

3 (MSU, physicist, 1967). Given are three straight circular cones with angle $\alpha$ in the axial section and base radius $r$. The bases of these cones are located in the same plane and touch each other externally in pairs. This is the radius of the sphere touching all three cones and the plane passing through their vertices.

4 (LSU, mekhanite, 1965). On a plane there are angular $n$ congruous lines of a cone that have a common vertex at a point lying on this plane. Each cone touches two other cones. The name is the angle at the apex in the axial section of the cone.
