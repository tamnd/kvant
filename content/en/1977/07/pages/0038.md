Substituting in accordance with (3) together $a, b$ and $c$ their expressions through $x, S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4$ and $S_5$, we obtain:
$$S_1S_4 + (x - S_1 - S_4)S_5 - (x - S_2 - S_4)(x - S_1 - S_3) = 0,$$
and therefore
$$x^2 - x(S_1 + S_2 + S_3 + S_4 + S_5) + (S_1S_2 + S_2S_3 + S_3S_4 + S_4S_5 + S_5S_1) = 0.$$

Substituting $S_1 = S_2 = S_3 = S_4 = S, S_5 = 3S/2$ into this equation, we obtain the answer to tasks a): $x = 4S$ (see Fig. 10).

Our solution to problem 6) was proposed in 1824 by the namesake of the famous Gauss - “drive advisor” Gauss (Herr Hofrath Gauss). It was published in the journal “Astronomische Berichte”, 1824, vol. 2, p. 343. This solution makes significant use of the convexity of the original pentagon. However, the proven result is valid for an arbitrary pentagon - and not only a non-convex one, but also one whose sides intersect themselves (for example, as on figure 11). This is described in detail in the article by A. Lopshtz “The Mobinus Problem and Its Continuation”, «Kvant», 1977, No. 3.
