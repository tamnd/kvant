⟦folio 32⟧

Tasks

M451—M455; F463—F467

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with asterisks.

Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than October 1, 1977 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant». After the address on the envelope, write the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example, “M451, M454” or “F467”. Solutions to problems in each subject (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be sent in separate envelopes. Please also send problems from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solution). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», new problems in physics” or “...new problems in mathematics”). After generating tasks, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. In this and subsequent issues, “Problem «Kvant»” is composed mainly of problems proposed at the last World Olympiad.

M451. Several points that do not lie on the same line are marked on the plane, and a number is written next to each one. It is known that if a straight line passes through or more offset points, then the sum of all numbers written near these points is equal to zero. Prove that all numbers are equal to zero.

F. Vainitein

M452. Triangles $T_1$ and $T_2$ are inscribed in a circle, and the vertices of triangle $T_2$ are the midpoints of the arcs into which the circle is divided by the vertices of triangle $T_1$. Prove that in the hexagon $T_1 \cap T_2$ the diagonals connecting opposite vertices are parallel to the sides of the triangle $T_1$ and intersect at one point.

N. Netsvetaev

M453. Given a set of positive numbers $\{a_1; a_2; ...; a_n\}$. For each of its subsets, we write down the sum of the numbers included in it (sums of one, two, ..., $n$ terms are considered). Prove that all the written numbers can be divided into $n$ groups such that in each group the ratio of the largest number to the smallest does not exceed 2.

M454. 7 dwarfs are sitting at a round table. There is a mug in front of each one. Some of these mugs are filled with milk. One of the dwarfs pours all his milk into the mugs of the others equally. Then his neighbor to the right does the same. Then the next neighbor to the right does the same, and so on. After the last, next gnome poured out the rest of their milk, each mug contained the same amount of milk as was in it at the beginning. All mugs together contain 3 liters of milk. How much milk was initially in each mug?

B. Gutenacher
