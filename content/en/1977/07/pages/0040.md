There are five forces acting on the ball (see Fig. 18): gravitational force $\vec{F}_T = M \vec{g}$, buoyant force $\vec{F}$, air resistance force $\vec{F}_c$, Earth reaction force $\vec{N}$ and frictional force from the Earth $\vec{F}_{Tp}$.

Let us denote by $\vec{v}'$ the speed of the ball relative to the Earth.

Then
$$\vec{F}_c = -\alpha (\vec{v}' - \vec{u}).$$

From the condition that the balloon moves uniformly in the horizontal direction, it follows

$$|\vec{F}_c| - |\vec{F}_{Tp}| = 0$$

And
$$|\vec{F}| + |\vec{N}| - M |\vec{g}| = 0.$$

Besides,
$$|\vec{F}_{Tp}| = \mu |\vec{N}|.$$

Taking into account the fact that $\vec{F}_c = -\alpha (|\vec{v}'| - |\vec{u}|)$, from the last three equations we obtain
$$|\vec{v}'| = |\vec{u}| - \frac{\mu}{\alpha} (M |\vec{g}| - |\vec{F}|).$$

First of all, let's estimate in which of the two cases the efficiency is higher. In both cases, the voltage on the light bulb is the same, therefore, the voltages on section AB are the same (see Fig. 19). The current flowing through the lamp is also the same in circuits a) and b), which means that the current in section AB in circuit a) is greater than in circuit b). Therefore, the power loss in section AB in the first case is greater than in the second. In addition, circuit a) ensures that power is consumed in the resistat section BC.

So, the power losses in circuit a) are greater, and the efficiency is, accordingly, less.

Now let's find the specific efficiency values ​​in schemes a) and b). In the first case, the power released in the light bulb is equal to $\frac{U^2}{R_1} + \frac{R_2 R_\pi}{R_2 + R_\pi}$ (here $R_1$ is the resistance of the AB resistor section, and $R_2$ is the BC section). Then
$$\eta_1 = \frac{U^2}{U^2} \frac{R_1 + \frac{R_2 R_\pi}{R_2 + R_\pi}}{R_1}.$$

In order to calculate $\eta_1$, it is necessary to determine $R_1$ and $R_2$.

With a series connection, the voltages in individual sections of the circuit are proportional to the resistances of these sections:
$$\frac{U - U_\pi}{U_\pi} = \frac{R_1}{R_2 R_\pi}.$$

Besides,
$$R_1 + R_2 = R_p = 2R_\pi.$$
