Accuracy of change in a. e.m. is very high. Thus, the relative mass of hydrogen according to modern changes is equal to

$$\mu_H = 1,007825036 \text{ (11) a. e. m.}$$

The number in brackets (11) means that the indicated value is known with such accuracy that an error of approximately 11 units is possible in the last two digits. In other words, the last two digits could actually be 47, 25, or any other number in between.

Thus, the mass in a. e.m. is determined with greater accuracy than mass in kilograms. But here’s the problem: physicists don’t know how to change the mass of a macroscopic body, for example, a metal part is not represented in a. e.m. In kilograms they can, and in a. e.m. - no. The part cannot be “launched” into the mass spectrometer, and a carbon atom cannot be placed on the scale. This means that we need to find some way to change the mass of carbon atoms in kilograms, and then we can easily convert the mass changed into a. e.m., to kilograms and vice versa - from kilogram to a. e.m. We will tell you how the mass of atoms in kilograms was changed, and with greater accuracy.

But first, let’s explain how Avogadro’s number is related to everything said above. The fact is that Avogadro's number and 1/12 of the mass of atoms $^{12}$C are related. Indeed, Avogadro's number - $N_A$ - is, by definition, the number of parts in a mole of a substance. 1 mole, i.e. 12 e of carbon $^{12}$С, contains $N_A$atoms:

$$N_A = \frac{12e/mol}{m_{12}C^2}.$$

But $m_{12}C$($e$) = 12 a. e.m., so the exceptional value of the quantity $N_A$ is equal to

$$N_A = \frac{1}{12 m_{12}C}.$$

Thus, Avogadro's number is simply the reciprocal of the atomic unit of mass, expressed in grams. It would be possible not to write $N_A$ at all, but in many formulas, out of habit, they write $N_A$, and therefore Avogadro’s number remained in physics as a constant, equal to the number of parts in a mole of a substance.

Measuring $N_A$ is the same as changing the mass of some atoms in grams - “weighing” the atom. Knowing the mass of atoms, it is not difficult to determine the value of atomic mass units in grams (i.e., the reciprocal of Avogadro’s number).

Some time ago at the American Bureau of Standards - an institute that specializes in ultra-precise changes - a group of physicists conducted a very interesting Avogadro number. The experiments were carried out according to this plan. First, the volume $V_0$ ($cm^3$) that comes to one atom in the crystal was changed. This is done very precisely using RENTenostructural analysis of the sample. Then the crystal density $\rho$ ($e/cm^3$) was changed. Then, knowing the molar mass $\mu$ of the crystal substance and the mass of atoms $m = \rho V_0$, we find Avogadro’s number.

$$N_A = \frac{\mu}{\rho V_0}.$$

Using a mass spectrometer, the relative atomic mass of Si was determined with a very high degree of accuracy. Thus, the molar mass of Si was found with the same accuracy. This turned out to be necessary due to the fact that it varied slightly among different samples of natural silicon.

Next, it was necessary to change the silicon density as accurately as possible. To do this, it was necessary to very accurately determine the mass; they did something like what Archimedes once did with the crown of King Hiero.
