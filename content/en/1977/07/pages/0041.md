F426. In a two-layer batch of opaque parts of radius $r_1=5$ µm with a mass content of substance $m_1=0,04$ e per cubic meter of air pressure $l_1=50$ m. How much substance in a cubic meter of air is sprayed by another source of aspiration, which creates a partial radius of $r_2=10$ µm, if the pressure is reduced to $l_2=20$ m?

Since the capacitor is not charged, its plates do not participate in creating the electric field. Therefore, the field inside the capacitor is created only by the external charged plate.

We assume that the linear dimensions of the plate are much larger than its thickness and the thicknesses of the gaps $l_1$ and $l_2$. This means that the electric field is uniform. Its voltage is directed away from the plate if $Q > 0$ (Fig. 20), and towards it if $Q < 0$. According to the absolute value of the voltages $\vec{E}_1$ and $\vec{E}_2$ in the gaps $l_1$ and $l_2$ are the same:

$$|\vec{E}_1| = |\vec{E}_2| = \frac{|Q|}{2\varepsilon_0 S}.$$

The potential difference between the plates of the capacitor is equal to the work done by the electric field to move a unit positive charge from one plate to another:

$$U_{12} = -|\vec{E}_1|l_1 + |\vec{E}_2|l_2 = \frac{|Q|}{2\varepsilon_0 S}(l_2 - l_1).$$
