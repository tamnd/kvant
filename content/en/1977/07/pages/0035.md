M455. We will consider polynomials $P$, $Q$, $R$, ... in one variable with leading coefficient 1. We will say that two such polynomials $P$ and $Q$ commute if the polynomials $P(Q(x))$ and $Q(P(x))$ are also effectively equal (that is, after opening the parentheses and reducing them to the standard form all coefficients of these polynomials coincide).

a) For each number $a$, find all polynomials of degree at most 3 that commute with the polynomial $P(x) = x^2 - a$.

b) Let $P$ be a polynomial of degree 2, $k$ a natural number. Prove that there is at most one polynomial of degree $k$ that commutes with $P$.

b) Find all polynomials of degree 4 and 8 that commute with the given polynomial $P$ of degree 2.

d) Polynomials $Q$ and $R$ commute with the same polynomial $P$ of degree 2. Prove that they commute with each other.

d) Prove that there is an infinite sequence of polynomials $P_2$, $P_3$, $P_4$, ..., $P_h$, ..., where $P_h$ is a polynomial of degree $k$ in which any two polynomials commute, and the polynomial $P_2$ has the form $P_2(x) = x^2 - 2$.

I. Bernishtein, E. Turkevitz

F463. Two ice floes are moving forward with equal speeds in absolute value, one to the north, the other to the west. It turns out that at any moment of time on both ice floes it is possible to arrange the clocks in such a way that the speeds of the ends of the second hands relative to the Earth will be equal, and for each moment of time such an arrangement is unique. Determine how far the ice floes move per day if the length of each second hand is 1 cm. The clock dials are located horizontally. (8 grades)

F464. figure 1 shows two closed loops: ABBA and ABGA. Both cycles were carried out with an ideal monatomic gas.

1) Indicate in which sections of the cycles the gas receives and in which sections it releases heat.

2) Which cycle has a higher efficiency? How many times? (9 grades)

Estimate the maximum values ​​of voltage and current that can be obtained from such a generator if the belt speed is $v = 20$ m/sec and its width is $l = 1$ m. A breakdown occurs when the power plant field voltage is $E_0 = \frac{30}{kb/cm}$. (9 grades)
