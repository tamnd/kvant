F427. Two pebbles with numbers of turns $n_1=125$ and $n_2=1000$ are wound on a moral ferromagnetic core with a diameter of $d=5$ cm and a cross-sectional area of ​​$S=1$ cm$^2$ (Fig. 22). According to the first pebble, local mok $I_1=1$a, the second pebble is connected to the galvanometer. When the circuit of the first pebble is opened, a charge of $q=10^{-3}$ k passes through the galvanometer. The total resistance of the circuit of the second pebble is $R=100$ ohm. Determine the magnetic permeability of the material from which the core is made.

When electric current flows through the first coil, a magnetic field arises in the core. The induction of a magnetic field created by a current-carrying conductor is always proportional to the current in the conductor. It also depends on the configuration of the conductor and the magnetic permeability of the medium.

From the reports of symmetrina it is very clear that in the toron the magnetic field induction is the same in absolute value at all points of the circle, the center of which coincides with the center of the toronad (see Fig. 22). In addition, the cross section of the toronad is small. Thus, the field inside the toronad can be considered homogeneous. A rigorous calculation carried out for this case gives

$$|\vec{B}| = \mu_0 \frac{n_1 I_1}{\pi d},$$

where $\mu_0 = 4 \cdot 10^{-7} \text{ eH/m}$ is the magnetic constant, $\mu$ is the magnetic permeability of the core material.

The flux of magnetic induction through the cross section torover the area $S$ is equal to

$$\Phi = \left| \vec{B} \right| S = \mu_0 \mu \frac{n_1 I_1}{\pi d} S.$$

When the first coil circuit is opened, the magnetic flux will decrease. Let the flow decrease by $\Delta \Phi$ over a short period of time $\Delta t$. In the second coil an electromotive force of induction will arise

$$|\mathcal{E}_2| = n_2 \frac{|\Delta \Phi|}{\Delta t}$$

and he will get it like this

$$I_2 = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_2|}{R} = \frac{n_2}{R} \frac{|\Delta \Phi|}{\Delta t}.$$

During time $\Delta t$ sells charge through the galvanometer

$$\Delta q = I_2 \Delta t = \frac{n_2}{R} |\Delta \Phi|.$$

The entire charge passed through the circuit of the second coil is determined by the complete change in the magnetic induction flux:

$$q = \Sigma \Delta q = \frac{n_2}{R} \Sigma |\Delta \Phi| = \frac{n_2}{R} \Phi =$$

$$= \frac{n_1 n_2 \mu_0 \mu I_1 S}{\pi d R}.$$

From here

$$\mu = \frac{q \pi d R}{n_1 n_2 \mu_0 I_1 S} = 1000.$$
