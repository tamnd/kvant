To derive a stability criterion, let us turn again to a rocking rock and consider the case when the stone and the support in the area of ​​contact are spherical. We will assume that the stone and the block on which it stands have been worn down or weathered and become smooth, without chips or protrusions, so that the area of ​​contact between the stone and the support is small and can be taken as a point. figure 1 shows the contour of the stone and the support by a vertical plane passing through the point of their contact (point C). $$\frac{OQ}{\sin \alpha} = \frac{r}{\sin[\pi - (\alpha + \beta)]} = \frac{r}{\sin(\alpha + \alpha \frac{R}{r})}.$$

The one of interest shows small deviations of the stone from its equilibrium position. By "small deflection" we mean that the distance "traversed" by the point of contact on the surface of the support, i.e. arc C'A (and therefore arc C'A = C'A), is small compared to the radii r and R of the surface of the stone and support. And this means that the angles α and β are small, i.e. α << 1 and β = α \frac{R}{r} << 1.
