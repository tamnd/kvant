Exercise 10. Prove that all vectors from $B$ “look in the same direction,” that is, they form acute angles with some axis.

Let's call the *pseudoprojection* of the vector $p$ onto the axis $l$ the usual projection if the angle between $p$ and $l$ is acute, and the number $0$ otherwise. We fix some axis $l_0$. Let $l_\alpha$ be the axis forming an angle $\alpha$ with the $l_0$ axis. Let's put

$$g(x) = \begin{cases} \cos x, & \text{ если } \cos x > 0, \\ 0, & \text{ если } \cos x \leq 0. \end{cases}$$

Exercise 11. Check that $\int_0^{2\pi} g(\varphi - \alpha) d\alpha = 2$ for any $\varphi$.

If $\varphi$ is the angle between the vector $p$ and the axis $l_0$, then the pseudoprojection of this vector onto the axis $l_\alpha$ is equal to $|p|g(\varphi - \alpha)$.

Let us denote the sum of pseudo-projections of vectors $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ onto the axis $l_\alpha$ by $f(\alpha)$.

Exercise 12. Prove that the average value of the function $f$ on the interval $[0; 2\pi]$ is equal to $\frac{1}{\pi}$.

Exercise 13. Prove that the $l_\alpha$ axis can be chosen so that the sum of the pseudo-projections of these vectors onto this axis is not less than $\frac{1}{\pi}$.

Exercise 14. Prove the statement of Problem 4.

The constant $\frac{1}{\pi}$ in Problem 4 cannot be replaced by any larger constant. This can be verified by taking a sufficiently large $n$ and vectors going along the sides of a regular $n$-gon.

If in Problem 4 we replace $\frac{1}{\pi}$ with $\frac{1}{4}$, the corresponding statement will also be true for vectors in space.
