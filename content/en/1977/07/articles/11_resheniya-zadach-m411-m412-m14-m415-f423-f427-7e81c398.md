Mathematics of variables

In the 16th-17th centuries, technical science and navigation posed problems that were insufficient for the mathematics of antiquity, which dealt with stationary objects and built-in quantities.

At the same time, the belief began to spread that the world was governed by laws of nature that could be known. To formulate

These laws and solutions to the problems at hand required new mathematical methods.

Galileo Galileo, the founder of Dynamics, was one of the first to think about creating new methods for understanding the world. He thought about how the speed of a falling body changes, according to what law a magnet oscillates, how a point located on the rim of a rolling wheel moves.

To describe the physical laws of mathematical motion, it was necessary to introduce the concept of a variable quantity. This was done by the French physiologist and mathematician Rene Descartes, who lived at the end of the 16th and first half of the 17th centuries.

“The turning point in mathematics was the Cartesian variable of magnitude. Thanks to this, movement and dialectics entered mathematics.” (F. Engels).

To record the relationship between quantities, Descartes used letters, and the relationships between unknown and known quantities were expressed in the form of an equation.

By choosing certain units of measurement, you can express all studied quantities in numbers. Consequently, the dependence between quantities turns into dependence between numbers. For example, if the unit for measuring distances and time projections is the meter and the second, then the dependence of the path traveled by a body in free fall on time will be expressed by the formula $s=1/2 gt^2$, where $g=9.81$. When the number $t$ changes, the number $s$ changes, and therefore this formula connects the numerical variables $t$ and $s$ with each other.

However, for a long time people avoided talking about numerical variables and instead talked about magnitude variables. To distinguish between variable quantities and length measurements, the numbers are not 1, 2, 3, but 1 m, 2 m, 3 cm, etc.; Moreover, although 3 cm and 0.03 m are expressed by different numbers 3 and 0.03, still 3 cm = 0.03 m.

Descartes's discovery of mathematics gave a general method for the study of curves. Now the place of geometric considerations, the table popular among Greek mathematicians, was taken by algebra.

Birth of the term

In science, it often happens that long time is used to or another concept, but it appears only implicitly, without having a specific purpose - which scientist calls it in his own way. Because of this, the same calculations are repeated over and over again. The introduction of a new term leads to a clarification of the corresponding concept, its liberation from everything random and unimportant, and to the identification of common features in calculations carried out independently of each other in various fields of science.

Since 1673, the famous German philosopher and mathematician Gottfried Wilhelm Leibn sought to use the word “function” in his manuscripts. However, he used this term in a very narrow sense, he was talking only about segments of tangents to curves, about their projections on the coordinate axes and about “other kinds of lines that perform a certain function for a given figure” (from the Latin “functus” - to perform).

Johann Bernul, one of the first students of Leibniz, gave a definite function, free from geometric terms: “a function of a variable is called a wheel, formed in any way from this quantity and constants.” This definition delighted Leibniz: he guessed that the departure from geometric terminology marks a new era of mathematics - the study of functions as independent objects, moreover, based on numbers, and not on geometry.

In Bernoulli’s time, “in what angular way” was understood as argumentative operations, root extraction operations, trionometric and inverse trionometric, exponential and logarithmic “operations,” as well as their various combinations. Such functions are now called “elementary” (they are now taught in school).

In the 17th and especially in the 18th centuries, mathematicians began to consider functions obtained by summing an infinite number of elements of functions. This led to an expansion of the class of functions being studied. One of the most remarkable mathematicians of the 18th century, a member of the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences, Leonard Euler, defined a function as follows: “when some quantities depend on others in such a way that when the latter change, they themselves undergo a change, then the former are called functions of the latter.” In one of his works, he even talks about the graph of a function as a curve drawn by “free movement of the hand.”

Dispute over the concept of function

The question of what a function is and how the concepts of a function and its analytical expression are interconnected attracted the attention of mathematicians in connection with a dispute in which the most prominent scientists of the 18th century took part - Euler, d'Alembert, D. Bernoulli and many others.

Solving the problem of structure oscillations, Euler and d'Alembert received an answer that included a certain function. This function was related to the original oscillating structure formula. Euler believed that the initial deviation of the structure from the equilibrium position can be specified in different parts of the structure by different formulas, for example:

$$y = \begin{cases} ax, \text{ если } 0 \leq x < \frac{l}{2}, \\ a(l-x), \text{ если } \frac{l}{2} \leq x < l \end{cases}$$

(l is the length of the structure). D'Alembert believed that such functions were not allowed.
