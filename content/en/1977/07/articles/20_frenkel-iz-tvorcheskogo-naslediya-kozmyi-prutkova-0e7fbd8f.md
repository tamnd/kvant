From the creative heritage of Kozyma Prutkov

Careful familiarization with the works of Kozyma Prutkov allows the author of this publication to put forward a bold hypothesis that the famous catirik was also a talented scientist, in many ways anticipating the development of physics and mathematics. For a number of reasons, he informed the world about the ideas and trends he had foreseen, not in an explicit, but in a disguised form. We will limit ourselves to citing materials related to physics and confirming our hypothesis. The publication of these materials is timed to coincide with the upcoming 175th anniversary of the birth of K. Prutkov (1803-1863). The epigraph to the publication is inspired by the poems of Thaddeus Kuzmchts Prutkov, the talented son of a brilliant father.

It is the 175th anniversary of the birth of Prutkov. This, one might say, is a division of science and culture. However, even many scientists do not know how great his contribution to the development of science is.

Comment. The formulation of the question is almost more important than its solution, and this formism confirms this observation. Science has answered Prutkov’s rhetorical question: the depths of the oceans should be changed in meters, in accordance with the currently accepted International System of Units (SI).

The most distant point on the globe may be close, but the closest point is far from something.

Comment. This form clearly expresses the idea of ​​relative space. Without dwelling on the obvious development of this idea within the framework of relativistic theory, he points out that this formism apparently gave rise to the following conclusion, put by the famous French writer Anatole France into the mouth of Riquet, the dog of Mr. Bergeret - the hero of “Modern History”: “People, animals and stones grow as they approach, and become huge when they are near me. I don't change. Wherever I am, I am always equally great.”

It is known that cause-and-effect relationships are closely related to space-time relationships. The latter were briefly touched upon by Prutkov in the aphorism:
Shelks the mare in the nose - she hits the tail.
Problems of cause-effect relationships are vigorously discussed by physicists in connection with the hypothesis of the existence of taxins - particles moving in the void at superluminal speed. It turns out that the possibility of such movements is not rejected by the special relative theory (and its creation). The difficulties here lie precisely in the problem of cause and effect.
One might think that it was the Taxins who stood in the mind's eye of Kozma Prutkov when he formulated a deep aphorism, which we recently found in his notebook and expressed to him in sonorous brass:
«Cogitatu, ergo sunt»,

Astronomy

Human! He raised his gaze from earth to heaven - what an amazing order we see there!

Comment. For the sake of objectivity, it should be noted that the fruits of K. Prutkov’s thoughts did not always lead to the correct conclusion, and sometimes give rise to false consequences. Thus, the above observation about the arrangement of the stars in a “surprisingly worthy order” (quite fair and subtle) apparently served as the reason for the following statement (belonging to an unknown theologian): “The divine origin of all things follows from the fact that any three stars that are not located on the same straight line occupy the vertices of a triangle.”

Quantum mechanics

When throwing stones into the water, look at the circles they form; otherwise such throwing will be empty fun.

Comment. It is well known that in 1924, the French physicist Louis de Broglie proposed that the movement of a body of mass m with speed v can be associated with a wave process characterized by wavelength λ. The connection between these complementary characteristics of a physical object (corpuscular and wave) is expressed by the famous de Broglie formula: λ = h/mv (h is Planck’s constant). It seems undoubted that it was these messages that occupied K. Prutkov in his experimental research, which was reflected in the commented phorism. An unbiased mind, armed with knowledge of modern quantum mechanics, cannot fail to see in this phorism a direct anticipation of the independently expressed views of de Broglie. The forism of Kozma Prutkov also contains an acknowledgment of further reflections on the stone-circular (read - corpuscular-wave) parallel, perhaps, to the conditions of the French scientist.
