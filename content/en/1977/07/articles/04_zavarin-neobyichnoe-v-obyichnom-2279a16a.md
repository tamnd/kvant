Example 1. For a homogeneous ball, the center of gravity coincides with the geometric center. Therefore, the ball is unstable on a convex surface. However, if the ball has a medium “top,” then it can stand stable on the top of a convex surface (see exercise 3).

Example 2. A funny children's toy “roy-insert” is filled with example 1. A piece of lead or steel hidden at the spherical base of the roly-poly insert gives the toy amazing stability.

Does everyone know that Vanya-insert had (and maybe still has) relatives here and there? Listen.

《Once upon a time there were twenty-five oat soldiers in the world. All of them were the sons of one mother - an old oatmeal spoon - and, therefore, were each other’s siblings. They were very beautiful: a gun on the shoulder, a wheel on the chest, a red and blue uniform. It’s a miracle, what kind of soldiers...》 This is the standing oval soldiers from Andersen’s fairy tale. Why were they called boners? Probably because, no matter how they are applied, they always return to a vertical position. When they opened the box in which these soldiers were placed, they all jumped up, as if on command. Each soldier was attached to the main medium of a lead hemisphere and stood surprisingly stable.

Example 3. There is a legend that the Royal Council, checking the location and cunning of Columbus, suggested that he place the egg with the sharp end on the table. Columbus solved the problem in no time; he knocked it over and set it on the table. At the same time, he not only changed the shape of the surface at the point of contact, making it flat, but also lowered the egg’s center of gravity.

Unusual in the ordinary

A thoughtful approach to loved ones reveals their unexpected and red sides. Here's one example. Need to square the number 487? Please write:

$$487 - 250 = 237, \\
(500 - 487)^2 = 169.$$

Now we write down the resulting numbers one by one:

$$486 - 250 = 236, \\
(500 - 486)^2 = 196, \\
486^2 = 236196.$$

True again. The next number is 488, for him

$$488 - 250 = 238, \\
(500 - 488)^2 = 144, \\
488^2 = 238144.$$

Very interesting squares, aren't they? It doesn't cost much. And then you, probably, when entering 46×46, will not write $(40+6)^2$ as the square of the sum, but will immediately write the answer:

$$2100 + 16 = 2116.$$

Isn't it?
