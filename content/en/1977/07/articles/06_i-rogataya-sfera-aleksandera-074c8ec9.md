The liquid lowered a steel ball, the volume of which was changed with very high precision (a person cannot be made with a silicon crystal). By measuring the mass of the displaced liquid $m'_{ж}$ and dividing it by the volume of the ball $V_{ш}$, we found $\rho_{ж}$. So, the volume of the sample is $V = \frac{m_{ж}}{m_{ш}} V_{ш}$. By dividing the mass of the sample by its volume, we found the silicon density $\rho$.

Based on the results of all measurements, Avogadro's number was found. It turned out to be equal

$$N_A = 6,0220941 \cdot 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1}.$$

The relative error of the calculated value is less than $10^{-6}$%. The user of this value $N_A$ can obtain with the same accuracy the value of the proton mass in kilograms: the figurative value $N_A$ is a.u. m in kilograms.

Alexander's Horned Sphere

Each of you knows perfectly well what a sphere is. Imagine that the sphere is made of a durable film that can be stretched and compressed, but not bent or torn. Then from the sphere one can obtain surfaces whose neck is very different from it, for example, a tetrahedron, a cube or a cylinder with dynamism. From the point of view of one of the sections of “higher geometry” - “topology”, all these surfaces are the same.

On the cover you see one of these “sphere-identical” surfaces (topological image of a sphere) - Alexander’s pink sphere. From an ordinary sphere one can obtain the interesting process depicted in figure 1. At the first step, the “clasped fingers” design is obtained. Then this construction should be repeated on each finger of the “disks” blacked out on figure, the “fingers” (of the next order) should be interlocked again, etc.

Alexander's Horned Sphere has one type of property. If the distance is between a sphere and any circle entirely lying in this sphere or inside it, then this circle can be contracted to a point without intersecting the surface of the sphere. In other words, the area in which the sphere divides space is simply connected. For a long time, mathematicians thought that any topological image of a sphere divides space into two simply connected regions (a generalization of the famous Jordan lemma that an arbitrary closed non-self-intersecting line divides the sphere into two simply connected regions). However, in 1924, the American mathematician J. W. Alexander managed to construct a “horned sphere”, which, like an ordinary sphere, divides space into two regions A and B (“interior”) and “the outer region B is not simply connected.

If you want to get to know topological science more closely, we recommend that you read the article by V. G. Boltyansky and V. A. Efremovich in the collection “Mathematical Education” - No. 2, 3, 4 and 6 (Moscow, 1957-1960).
