Nationwide work
about the rural school

Rural secondary schools are important in solving the tasks set by the 25th Congress of the CPSU in the field of socio-economic and cultural construction. After all, about half of all students in the country attend rural schools.

Specific ways for the development of school education in rural areas are outlined by the Resolution of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR “On measures to further improve the working conditions of rural secondary schools.” It contains a general program for the construction of new and expansion of existing school buildings and boarding schools in rural areas, strengthening the educational and material base of rural schools, providing them with qualified teaching staff, improving the forms and methods of teaching and educating rural school youth.

In the summer of 1976, on the initiative of the Ministry of Education of the USSR, the Central Committee of the Komsomol and the Central Committee of the professional workers of education, higher education and scientific institutions, the All-Union Socialist Improvement for better working conditions of rural secondary schools began. The results of this improvement, in which institutions of the system of the USSR Ministry of Education of Rural Areas participate, will be summed up annually in September. The magazine «Kvant» will lead the development of this improvement and will do everything possible to help its participants in improving the teaching of mathematics and physics to rural schoolchildren.

Scientific and technological progress places particularly high demands on the training of young people, both in the city and in the countryside. Today's high school students will tomorrow come to factories and fields, construction sites and transport enterprises, scientific laboratories and cultural institutions, and with their work they will turn the ambitious plans of the Ten Five-Year Plan into reality. In order for this work to be effective and creative, it is necessary now, at school, to persistently and persistently master knowledge and work skills.

The concerns of rural schools have become a truly vital concern for many physicists and mathematicians. It is gratifying to note that the list of scientific institutions and educational institutions closely associated with schoolchildren from cela is continuously growing, and the forms of such communication are varied. Scientists, university teachers, researchers, and students strive to provide rural schoolchildren with broad exposure to in-depth study of mathematics and physics.

Hundreds of popular books and pamphlets on mathematics and physics have been written by famous scientists. These books have formed a golden fund of popular science and educational literature for youth and are well known to schoolchildren in all corners of our country. More and more rural schoolchildren are being involved in youth scientific societies, participating in

traditional physical and mathematical descriptions. Summer school camps have become widespread, where young men and women combine active study with productive busy subjects under the guidance of students and young scientists. For talented young people from rural areas, special physics and mathematics boarding schools have been opened in a number of cities across the country. Tens of thousands of schoolchildren living in rural areas, workers' settlements, and small towns, far from scientific and pedagogical centers, systematically receive qualified guidance for non-classroom students in mathematics and physics in correspondence subject schools created at universities, technical and pedagogical institutes. Effective assistance to rural youth is provided by correspondence preparatory courses and the organization of many universities and technical schools for applicants.

The editorial collection and editorial staff of the magazine «Kvant» also see one of their main tasks as ensuring in every possible way the increased quality of knowledge of rural schoolchildren in physics and mathematics, creating favorable conditions for living and developing their interest in these sciences.

The general articles published in the magazine are aimed at broadening the horizons of the young reader, introducing him in an accessible form to additional chapters of mathematics and physics, the latest achievements of science, telling about the greatest scientists, revealing the role and place of physics and mathematics in the general system of human knowledge and in the practical activities of man. The materials in the section “Through the Pages of School Textbooks” are recognized to help schoolchildren, including rural ones, better understand the key topics of the new school curricula and obtain additional information on them. The “Ask - We Answer” section is intended to answer questions that schoolchildren often ask in their letters to the editor. The stats placed in the sections “Mathematical Circle” and “Laboratory «Kvant»” are aimed at instilling in the reader a taste for independent exploration, research, and experimentation. Fans of service developments will always teach you interesting and varied tasks in the “Problem «Kvant»” section - from relatively simple to quite complex. Of great interest to schoolchildren is the section “Applicant Practice”, which allows them to specifically become acquainted with the requirements of university entrance exams and technical requirements. The youngest readers will find engaging yet meaningful materials in section «Kvant» for younger students.”

Numerous letters from students and teachers of rural schools received by the editor indicate that the magazine’s materials have a great influence on classroom and non-classroom work and contribute to the in-depth study of physics and mathematics by rural schoolchildren. “The number of readers of the magazine «Kvant» is increasing every year,” writes Turdali Muldabekov, a teacher from the “Socialism” state farm in the Voroshilovsky district of the Syr-Darba region of the Uzbek SSR, in his letter. — The reason for this is obvious: every person interested in mathematics and physics will find interesting materials on its pages. It is known that «Kvant» is a magazine for high school students. But teachers working in rural areas also read the magazine with interest and solve problems and examples published in it.”

The editors turn to rural schoolchildren with a request and invitation, most often to write letters to the magazine, to express their opinions on published materials, write specific questions, and express wishes. Such letters are especially important for us, or only a living figurative connection with rural readers allows the magazine to maximally satisfy their needs and requests.
