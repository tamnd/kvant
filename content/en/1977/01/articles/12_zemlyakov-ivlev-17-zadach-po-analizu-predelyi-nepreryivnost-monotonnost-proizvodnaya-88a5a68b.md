⟦folio 36⟧

17 problems
by analysis
(limits, continuity,
monotony, derived)

The problems given below are addressed to ninth graders. To solve them, you do not need to do any calculations: it is enough to understand the corresponding definitions from the textbook “Algebra and Basic Analysis 9” and their interpretation on function graphs.

§ 1. Limits of functions (clause 38)

1. Figures 1, $a - \theta$ show graphs of functions $y = f(x)$ defined at $x \neq 0$. Please indicate:
a) in what cases does the limit $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ exist (and what is it equal to);
6) in what cases does the limit $f(x)$ at $x \to 0$ not exist (and explain why).

The graph on figure 1, $\theta$ is an infinite broken line, the break points of which have abscissas $x = \pm 1/n$, where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ (even $n$ corresponds to minimum points, odd - maximum points).

2. The functions $y = f(x)$, the graphs of which are shown in Figures 2, $a - \theta$, are defined at $x < 0$. Additional distribute these functions at $x > 0$ (complete the graphs) so that to obtain the functions:
a) there is a limit $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ (can this always be done? if not, then explain why);
6) there is no limit $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ (can this always be done?).

Give not one, but, say, two or three different ways of constructing a graph (in cases where the task requirement can be satisfied).

§ 2. Continuity of functions (item 38)

3. Draw a graph of a certain function $y = f(x)$, which was continuous at all points $x$, except: a) $x = 100$; 6) $x = \pm 1$; b) $x = 1, 2, 3, 10$; d) $x = n$, where $n$ is an arbitrary integer.

4. Among the functions whose graphs are shown in Figure 3, $a - \theta$, indicate those that are not interrupted (discontinuous) at point $x = x_0$ or $x = x_1$.

5. Draw (preferably in several ways) the graphs in Figure 4, $a - \theta$ so that you get graphs of both defined and continuous functions. Is this possible in all cases?

§ 3. Monotonicity of functions

6. The function $f$ is definite and kills on the entire numerical axis. Could the function $f$ be:
a) is always positive (that is, such that $f(x) > 0$ for any $x$)?
b) negative water? in even? d) odd? d) periodic?

7. Indicate the intervals of increasing and decreasing functions, the graphs of which are shown in Figure 5, $a - \theta$.

8. It is known that the function $f$ kills on the intervals: a) $[0,1]$ and $[1,2]$; b) $[0,1]$ and $[1,2]$. Is it possible to say that the function $f$ kills:

1) at the union of these intervals - on the semi-perveral $[0,2]$?
2) on the “subinterval” $[0,1/2]$?

9. Draw a graph of the function $y = f(x)$ so that it:
a) killed on intervals $[-\infty, -2]$ and $[1,4]$ and increased on intervals $[-2,1]$ and $[4, +\infty]$;
b) increased on each interval of the form $[n, n + 1]$, where $n$ is an integer, but did not increase on each interval of length large units;
b) increased by the set of all integer values ​​$x$ and killed by the set of all other numbers.

Is it possible to “make” the function $f$ negative?
