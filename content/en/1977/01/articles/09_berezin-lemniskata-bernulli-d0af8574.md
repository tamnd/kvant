Bernoulli's Lemniscate

The first and fourth pages of the cover show the position of the curves. On the fourth - lemniscathons, or Wamma curves (the article by V. Vavilov in this issue talks about them - p. 20). The first one is Bernoulli's lemniscate*). When determining the correspondence of the parameters defining the lemniscate (l = d = R√2 in the notation of Vavilov’s article), it breaks down into the Bernoulli lemniscate and the circle.

Bernoulli's lemniscate is a substitution curve that has attracted the attention of many mathematicians. It can be defined in different ways.

For example, Bernoulli's lemniscate is a set of points on the plane, the product of the distances of each of them from two fixed points F₁, F₂ is equal to (|F₁F₂|/2)².

*) We are talking about Jacob Bernoulli (1654-1705), an outstanding mathematician, author of work related mainly to mathematical analysis, geometry and probability theory. The Bernoulli family gave the world many outstanding scientists, including mathematicians. In the history of science, scientists from this family with the same names are called like kings - Daniel I, Johann 11, ... “Lemniscate” is a Greek word; In ancient Greece, a lemniscate was a ribbon used to attach a wreath to the winner’s head.

On the first page of the cover there is also a familiar hyperbola, more precisely, an equilateral hyperbola whose asymptotes are perpendicular). If we carry out an inversion transformation (see «Kvant», 1971, No. 8, p. 23) with the center at the center of symmetry of the hyperbola with respect to the circle tangent to it, then the hyperbola will turn into a lemniscate.

You can get a lemniscate using the same hyperbola in another way. Let's construct all the circles, the product through the center of symmetry of the hyperbola, with centers on this hyperbola. The curve tangent to all these circles is the lemniscate (this is the name of this construction and is shown on the cover).

Here is another way to construct a lemniscate. Two squares (Fig. 1) fit around the circumference. All possible secants are drawn through the center O and all possible chords of circles LM' are formed. We transfer these chords along the rays to which they belong so that each point L falls into the point O. Then the points M can form a lemniscate.

You can also construct a lemniscate using drawing tools. To do this, let's carry out some additional constructions on figure 1. Let's draw NF parallel to OM, as well as AC (where C is the center of the circle).
