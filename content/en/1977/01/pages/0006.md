⟦folio 4⟧

V. Efremovich

SPACE AND INTERNAL GEOMETRY OF SURFACES

1. Our space - what is it like?

The geometry we study at school is Eclidian geometry. But is it true that the space in which we live is structured this way? Is it Euclidean? Are Euclid's axioms true in our real space?

How could it be otherwise? After all, the axioms are so very strong: “Only one straight line passes through two points”; “Every straight line is infinite in both directions”; “A perpendicular and an oblique line to the same line are not intersecting,” etc.

It seems that all this is so obvious that it cannot be otherwise! This is what the famous German philosopher I. Kant said: “It is impossible to think otherwise!”, That is, there are truths that we know without any experience - “a priori.” And in support of the existence of such “a priori” truths, Kant cited precisely geometric axioms, as self-evident facts, possessing undoubted merit, independent of any experience. But our great compatriot N.I. Lobachevsky showed that it is perfectly possible to think differently!
