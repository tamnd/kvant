1935 Olympics (No. 116)

A point is called the center of symmetry of a urinary set H if a lobe that is symmetric with respect to O of any urinary set H also belongs to the urinary set H. Prove that a finite urinary set cannot have two different centers of symmetry.

Two solutions are given for this problem; in the second solution it is shown that the exact set with two centers of symmetry is infinite and has infinitely many centers of symmetry: - all points located on a straight line passing through these two centers of symmetry, and spaced from the ends of this segment at distances equal to integer multiples of its length.

1937 Olympics (No. 122)

Three circles in space touch each other in pairs, and all three points of contact are different. Prove that these circles lie on the same sphere or in the same plane.

1947 Olympics (No. 144)

Circles of radius r are shown by circles of radius r/2. What is the smallest number of circles required for emod?

Answer: seven.

1966 Olympics (No. 196)

Is there a spatial rectangle, all sides of which are equal, and the angles between any two estimates are constructed if, for example, one refuses the condition of equality of all its sides, and only requires that the faces of the four sides be equal, and that the angles between any estimates are constructed.

1971 Olympics (No. 211)

The line intersects side AB of triangle ABC at point C₁, side AC at point B₁ and the continuation of sides BC at point A₁. Let C₂ be a point located symmetrically to point C₁ relative to the middle of side AC, and A₂ be the intersection point of lines B₂C₂ and BC.

Prove that

$$\sin B_1A_1C : \sin C_2A_2B = |B_2C_2| : |B_1C_1|.$$

1972 Olympics (No. 215)

There are equal numbers of boys and girls in the class (the class has at least 4 people in total). They are put out in different orders in one row and slotted, is it possible to divide the row into two parts so that there are equal numbers of girls and boys in each part. Let a be the number of lines when such a division of the series is impossible, and b be the number of lines when it is possible to split the series into two parts with the same number of girls and boys in each of them, but only in one way. Prove that b = 2a.

1973 Olympics (No. 219)

In space, n planes (n ≤ 5) are given, so that any of them have one common point and in the space of non-points, more than three planes would pass through the comors. Prove that among the parts in Comoria these n planes divide space there are at least $\frac{2n-3}{4}$ metrahedrons.

An analogue of the statement given on the city plane is more simple: if n straight lines are given on the plane, located on the 同一 side since on each part the girls and boys do not pass through one point, then among the parts on the Komorye these n planes divide the space, there are at least (2n-2)/3 metruhedrons.

1974 Olympics (No. 221)

Given an infinite sequence of squares with lengths 1, $1/2$, $1/3$, ..., $1/n$. Prove that there are squares, sequences such that they do not overlap another square, and determine what the side of the smallest square is, along with all the sequences.

All squares forming a given infinite sequence can be placed in the square on side $1/2$.
