Inkjet sound autorenator

To carry out the experiment described below, you will first need a glass tube with a small hole. Of course, you can use a regular glass pipette, but it is not difficult to make the necessary tube yourself.

On a flame of dry fuel or a gas burner, stretch a low-melting glass tube with a diameter of 4-6 mm so that the resulting overload has an internal diameter of about 1 mm. Using the edge of a file or needle file, carefully file the tube through the middle sections and then break it. You will end up with two tubes with the ends pulled out like a pipette. You can do it differently. Insert the end of the tube into the flame and rotate the tube evenly so that its end begins to melt and the hole becomes covered with glass. When the internal diameter of the melted end of the tube is approximately 1 mm, remove the tube from the flame and cool.

Place the manufactured glass tube into the rubber plug hole, and insert the plug into a rubber hose of suitable diameter (Fig. 1). Connect the hose to the water tap and run water through it. In this case, from the opening of the glass tube there will be a thin stream of water in which two areas can be distinguished.
