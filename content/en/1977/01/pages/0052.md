Problems and Olympiads

In 1976, the publishing house "Mir" published another wonderful book: I. Kürsák, D. Neukomm, D. Hajos, J. Szurani "Hungarian Mathematical Olympiads" (series "Problems and Olympiads").

Hungary is the first country in which (by decision of the Hungarian Physics and Mathematics Society) mathematics Olympiads for high school graduates began to be held. The first Olympics took place in 1894. Since then, the Olympiads have been held in Hungary every year (with the exception of two world wars).

The book “Hungarian Mathematical Olympiads” contains problems from all Olympiads up to 1974 (at each Olympiad the participant was offered three problems).

The first 93 problems (until 1928) were collected and published with solutions by one of the organizers of the Olympiads, Kurshak. The problems of the 1928-1963 Olympiads were collected by the Hungarian mathematicians Hajos, Neukomm and Szurani. And finally, the problems of the Olympiads of the most recent years (including 1974) were collected by the translator of the book, Yu. A. Danilov.

The book consists of three sections. The first section is a simple summary of 222 Olympiad problems (so that you can evaluate these problems, we present some of them below). The second section - this is the first of three sections - is a simple catch-up of 222 Olympiad problems (so that you can evaluate these problems, we present some of them below). In the third section - this is the first of three sections - this is a simple catching up of 222 Olympiad problems (so that you can evaluate these problems, we present some of them below).

1894 Olympics (No. 1)

Prove that the expressions 2x+3y and 9x+5y are divisible by 17 for the same integers x and y.

1901 Olympics (No. 22)

Prove that the n-x sum of powers 1^n+2^n+3^n+4^n is divisible by 5 if and only if the exponent n is not divisible by 4.

The solution to this problem is based on the fact that 1^4=2^4=3^4=4^4=1 (mod 5). This comparison integer is nothing more than a special case of Fermat’s Little Theorem:

If a is an integer and is not divisible by a prime number p, then a^{p-1}≡1(mod p).

1924 Olympics (No. 82)

Given three positive integers a, b and c. Prove that if for all positive integers n it is possible to construct a triangle with stringomes of length a^n, b^n, c^n, then all constructed triangles will be equilibrium.

1926 Olympics (No. 90)

The circle of the katistics without slipping around the circle of the large-sized input, remaining inside it. What line is described by this piercing?

Familiarity with S. Verov's articles on rolls will help you solve this problem (see «Kvant», 1975, No. 5, 8 and 12). The answer here is this: the diameter of the larger circle passing through the point at which the smaller circle touches it at the beginning of the moment passing twice - in the forward and reverse directions.

1933 Olympics (No. 110)

16 of the 64 squares were placed on the chessboard, and on each of the 8 horizontal and each of the 8 vertical there were 2 placed squares. Prove that 8 black and 8 white figures can be considered on the placed cells (no one figure in each placed cell), that on each horizontal and each vertical there is 1 white and 1 black figure.
