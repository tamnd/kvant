On figure 1, which refers to the game “queen to the corner!”, there are 7 dots under the queen, and 5 dots to the left of the queen, just the numbers from the game “Izyanshiztsy”. Let's try to establish a connection between these two games. Only we will place the stones not as points on figure 1, but as shown on figure 4, outside the board.

Correspondence between mi positions. Each position of the queen on the board is comparable to two piles of stones: in the first - as many stones as there are horizontal ones under the queen, and in the second - as many vertical ones as there are to the left of the queen. Then, conversely, each set of stones in two piles (but not more than 7 in each) determines the position of the queen on the board.

We have established a correspondence between positions in two games: each distribution of stones into piles corresponds to a chessboard field and, conversely, each board field corresponds to a pair of piles of stones. Now it is easy to establish correspondence between possible moves. If several stones are taken from the first pile, then the queen is moved the same number of squares down, if from the second, the queen is moved the same number of fields to the left, and if stones are taken from both piles at once, then the queen is moved to the left and down diagonally. For example, if the first move is to take 2 stones from each pile, then the queen moves from f8 to d6.
