(C is a constant depending on the “type” of atoms). It is impossible to verify this formula directly in experiments on the scattering of α-particles—the impact distance d is not measurable. But using our mechanical model, we can obtain the dependence of the scattering angle on the impact distance. And if the experimental data are well described by a formula similar to (*), this will once again confirm the validity of our mechanical analysis of Rutherford’s experiment.

One of the technical studies and available options for creating a peak is illustrated in figure 3. Here, to create a potential peak, a thin rubber sheet (2-3 mm thick) is used, attached to plywood, in which a round hole with a diameter of 20-25 cm is cut (the larger the model, the more convenient it is to work with). It should be remembered that the dependence of the scattering angle on the impact distance should shift at a fixed value of the ball speed.

So you should think about how to “launch” the balls (an inclined plane, a rifle pistol, etc.).

Using a simple device that fixes the impact distance (for example, a guide groove in the horizontal plane), you can conduct an experiment on this model to obtain the dependence of the scattering angle on the particle velocity.
