Instead of the areas of these rectangles, you can also consider the given areas of the rectangles: BCDE, CDEA, DEAB, EABC, ABCD, and look for an expression in terms of their areas of the rectangle ABCDE.

Möbius’s appeal to readers immediately evoked responses: in the second volume of the journal “Astronomical News” (1825; the editor of the magazine was the famous mathematician and astronomer Schumacher), two solutions to this problem were published. However, both solutions were “classical”, obtained by methods of elementary geometry - means that Möbius considered alien to the content of his problem; and only in his book did Mobius give its complete solution by means of “barient calculus”; we would now say - by means of vector algebra.

The reader of this article will be particularly interested in solving the Möbius problem if we tell him the final result:

The area x of a rectangle ABCDE, whose areas of the neighbors EAB, ABC, BCD, CDE, DEA are equal to the correspondence a, b, c, d, e, is the root of the quadratic equation

$$x^2 - x(a + b + c + d + e) + (ab + bc + cd + de + ea) = 0.$$
