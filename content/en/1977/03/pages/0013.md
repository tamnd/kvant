new possibilities of technical application of photocells.

Stoletov is the recognized first physicist of Russia. In 1889, at the II International Electrical Competition in Paris, scientists recognized him as one of the most outstanding physicists of our time.

At the beginning of 1893, academicians Chebyshev, Bredinin and Beketov proposed electing Stoletov as members of the Russian Academy of Sciences. But the President of the Academy, Grand Duke Konstantin, does not allow candidate Stoletov to run for office.

These days, Alexander Grigorievich receives many letters in which advanced Russian scientists express their sympathy to him in connection with the injustice shown to him. “I am very, very outraged by the Academy’s action,” writes Professor of St. Petersburg University I. N. Borgman. “...However, this is not the first time our Academy has done this.” Now it’s better to be spoiled than to become one of its members.”

“What you tell me in your last letter,” writes Stoletov from Odessa, Professor F.N. Shvedov, “didn’t strike me at all... After all, some scientists - Mendeleev - were spoiled... I would console myself with the fact that the better modern Russian scientists - Mendeleev, Mechnikov - are not in a rich house. There is no shame in being in their company.”

Many letters come from abroad. Stoletov pshut Kelvin, Helmholtz, Bolshan.

Despite the sympathy of his friends, Stoletov has a hard time taking the insult inflicted on him. And the university elementary school is beginning to show him its disfavor more and more. All this greatly affects the health of Alexander Grigorievich.

A.G. Stoletov died on the night of May 14-15, 1896.

Stoletov’s significance as a scientist for Russian and world science is enormous. He created the first educational research physics laboratory in Russia and founded a school of Russian physicists.

The Stoletov's work on the magnetization of iron transformed electrical engineering from an empirical science to a theoretical one. His works devoted to the development of a unity system for electrical changes also made a great contribution to electrical engineering.

Based on the photoeffect phenomena studied by Stoletov, photocells were created that serve in plants and factories, sorting and counting products, managing rolling mills and metal smelting, reading drawings and making parts based on them. Photocells turned some into sound, made phototelegraph possible, and work in various automatic devices.

Stoletov's vacuum installation for studying electrical phenomena in allowed gases was a prototype of an electron tube, which made a true revolution in electrical engineering. Radio receivers and radio transmitters, RENTen devices and gas-discharge tubes, radars and electronic microcops, televisions and electronic computers - this is not a complete list of what became possible thanks to the work of Alexander Grigorievich Stoletov.
