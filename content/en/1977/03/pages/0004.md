The Möbius problem and its continuation

A century and a half ago (in 1823), a young thirty-year-old astronomer at the Leippite Observatory, August Möbius, drew the attention of mathematicians to the fact that although Euler had already considered not only simple geometric transformations (movements and subdivisions), but also more general ones, which he called affine*). they are all insufficiently used by geometers.

Möbius points out that multi-geometric problems, the content of which does not use either the concept of length or the measurement of angles, were solved after Euler by means foreign to their content, and expressed his conviction**) that the problems should also be presented as fully as possible, systematically arrange them and strike an independent geometric direction that would not use either trigonometrics or Magister Matheseos (as the Pythagorean theorem was called in the 17th century).
