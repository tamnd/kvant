3. What are orments?

Although ornaments of all 17 types were used in jewelry by artists of Ancient Egypt, their full classification was given only in 1891 by the largest Russian scientist E. S. Fedorov (in part, he proved that the number of differently arranged groups of symmetries of flat ornaments is exactly 17). The task of enumerating orments arose for Fedorov in connection with the needs of crystallography - the science of the geometric structure of crystals. As already mentioned, atoms and molecules of substances that are in a crystalline state form a so-called crystalline structure - a spatial analogue of orments. Sections of similar structures by different planes represent exactly flat ornaments, and it is nice to call the symmetry groups of flat ornaments flat crystallographic groups. Fedorov also obtained a complete classification of spatial crystallographic groups - it turns out that there are exactly 230 of them!

More details about this are written in the books listed at the end of the article. For now, we will limit ourselves to the formulation of one remarkable problem, the solution of which is the main step in the proof of Fedorov’s theorem on the classification of orments. But first, let’s define what a flat ornament is.

Definition. An infinite plane figure Φ is called a plane skeleton if the following conditions are met:

(1) among the displacements that map Φ onto themselves, there are non-quantitative parallel transitions;
(2) among all vectors (parallel to re-axis) mapping Φ onto itself, there exists a vector of the smallest length.

If a flat ornament Φ is mapped onto itself upon rotation around a point A through angles only multiples of 360°/n, where n is a natural number greater than 1, then the point A is called the center of symmetry of order n of this ornament Φ.

Task 1. Prove that an arbitrary planar ornament can have symmetry cepts of orders n=2, 3, 4 and 6.

Let us give an indication of two approaches to solving this problem.

Let 1. Consider two centers of symmetry of an orment of the same order n at the smallest possible distance from each other (the existence of the smallest distance between the centers of the desired document!).

Assuming that n is different from 2, 3, 4, 6, show that there are two centers of a point of order n at an even smaller distance from each other!

Let 2. First prove that for an arbitrary ornament, centers of a fixed order n ≥ 3 form a lattice of paralleloerms - such lattices are described in detail in the article by A. A. Egorov, “Lattices and regular polygons”, «Kvant», 1974, No. 12. Then, using the properties of the symmetry group of the ornament, construct a regular n-gon with vertices at the nodes grids of centers. Egorov’s article proves that this can only be done for n = 3, 4 and 6.
