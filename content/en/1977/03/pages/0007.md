THE OUTSTANDING RUSSIAN PHYSICIST A. G. STOLETOV WAS BORN ON JOL 29 (August 10), 1839, into the family of a poor Vladimir merchant. His father, Grigory Mikhailovich, owned a small grocery store and a leather workshop. The mother, Alexandra Vasilievna, was also of merchant rank, but the STOLETOV family was not at all like typical merchant families, the gloomy world of which was so masterfully portrayed in his plays by A. N. Ostrovsky. Alexandra Vasilievna was an educated woman and she herself taught her children (before they entered the gymnasium) Russian language and arithmetic.

The house had a good library, and Sasha, having learned to read at the age of four, began to use it early. At the age of five he was already reading completely freely.

In 1849, Alexander STOLETOV entered the Vladimir Gymnasium, from which he graduated in 1856. The graduation certificate states that he “... was recognized as having completed the Gymnasium course with the right to enter the University without a secondary exam and was awarded for excellent success in the sciences... with a gold medal.”

In recent years, the students at the gymnasium have clearly identified Alexander's inclinations. His favorite subjects are mathematics and physics. He indulges in it in class, and at home he makes homemade physical instruments and performs various experiments. It's decided. He will be a physicist!

In the autumn of the same 1856, A.G. STOLETOV was assigned to the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics of Moscow University as a “state-paid” student (that is, receiving a state stipendio). At that time there were many mediocre professors at the university, but Alexander was lucky with his teachers. He listened to applied mathematics from Nikolai Dmitrievich Brashman, who educated the great P. L. Chebyshev, astronomy was taught to him by Fedor Aleksadrovich Bredikhin, and lectures on analytical geometry, differential and integral calculus, higher algebra and varnation calculus
