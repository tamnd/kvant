Building a theory
Let's start with the second game, and from the end. If the queen is on square a1, then the one whose turn it is to move has already lost. Therefore, we mark this field with a minus sign (Fig. 2).

If the queen is on a square from which you can get to a1 in one move, then the beginner will go to a1 and win. Therefore, we mark the fields from which you can get to a1 in one move with a “flat” sign.
Let the queen now stand on the c2 square. Will the beginner lose or win? Of course, he will lose: with any of his moves he ends up on the “flat”, after which the opponent places his queen on a1. If we lose c2 we will mark “minus” (Fig. 2).
What to do if the queen is on the same file as c2, but higher? Of course, go to c2, to the “minus”! Then, as we have already found out, the enemy is bound to lose. We mark the fields c3, c4, ..., c8 as flat, because the player who starts with one of these fields is obliged to win when running the game. For the same reason, let's put planes on the fields d2, e2, ..., h2 and d3, e4, ..., h7.
Is it possible to more simply describe the rules by which we arrange pros and cons, without repeating the reasoning each time about which player will win? Of course you can! Here they are.

"Golden Rules"
(1) If there is nowhere to go from the field, we put a minus.
(2) If from the field in question you can get to the field marked with a minus, set the plane.
(3) If all moves lead to fields marked with flats, we put a minus.
Arrangement of pluses and minuses in accordance with the “golden rules” for the game “queen to corner!” shown on figure 3.
We formulated the rules, according to which we began to arrange the pluses and minuses, and then, according to these rules, we finished the arrangement. Let us now prove that we were not mistaken: if the queen is on a square marked with a square, then the beginner will win, but otherwise we will lose.

It’s very easy to prove this, you just need to play by the rules: “Bet on minus!” According to the “golden rule” (2), the first move can be made with a minus. Then, according to the “golden rule” (3), the enemy will be forced to go to the flat. And the beginner is again on the minus, the opponent is again on the flat... This will continue until the queen gets to a square from which there is no way to go - to a1. He will get there just after the start of the beginner, who will thereby win.
If the initial field is marked with a minus, then you will have to go to the flat. Then the second player will apply the rules “bet on minus!” and will win.
