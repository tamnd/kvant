So, the difference between the games “queen to the corner!” and “izanyshizy” - purely external: the positions and moves of one game correspond to the positions and moves of another. Such games are called “isomorphic”.

The word “isomorphism” comes from the Greek words “isos” (constant, unchanging) and “morphe” (form). In mathematics, the word “isomorphism” occurs very often - it is used when it is necessary to note that the difference between objects is purely external, as between our two games.

One-dimensional theory

We have learned to place pluses and minutes on the chessboard, but we do it sequentially: we start from a1, then we move away the cells from which we can get to a1, then those from which we can only get to the already displaced ones, and so on. Is it possible to specify a rule by which you can immediately find out whether a field is winning or losing, without assigning pluses and minutes to all previous fields? Sometimes you can. We will analyze one one-dimensional game, that is, a game with one pile of stones (or, which is the same thing, a game on a strip of checkered paper).

There is a strip of checkered paper of length n (terminals). We stand a chip on one of the cells. There are two hierates, we take turns. A move consists of moving a piece 1, 2 or 3 cells to the left. We give a shout out to those who have nowhere to go. How to scratch to scratch? At what positions of the chip do we defeat the leader, and at what positions does his partner win?

Arrange the “pros” and “cons” on the board according to the “golden rule” and thereby find out when the leader wins and when his partner wins.
