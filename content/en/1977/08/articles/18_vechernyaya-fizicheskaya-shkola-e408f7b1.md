Evening physical school

For two years now, the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University has been running an Evening Physics School (VPS) for schoolchildren in grades 8-10 in Moscow and the Moscow region. Schooling is very... The HFS has adopted a “lecture-seminar” system. The lectures focus on analyzing the most complex issues of the school curriculum. These lectures are given by teachers from the Faculty of Physics. The seminars are taught by graduate and senior students who have extensive experience in leading school physics circles. For HFS students, excursions are organized to the laboratory of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University, to the Research Institute of Nuclear Physics and the State Astronomical Institute named after. Sternbergga. Our school has established friendly relations with the physics and mathematics school at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna. This year, a group of the best VFS students took part in a scientific conference for schoolchildren in Dubna.

In September of this year, competitive admission to the Higher School of Physics is taking place for the third time. Schoolchildren in grades 8-10 can take part in the competition (additional enrollment is carried out for grades 9 and 10). To participate in the competition, you must submit an application to the director of the Higher School of Physics by September 20, fill out a form at the Komsomol Committee of the Faculty of Physics, and also submit 2 photographs measuring 3x4 cm.

Applications will be accepted from September 2 to September 20 from 16.00 to 18.00 daily (except Sundays) at the Komsomol Committee of the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University (assignment of the Faculty of Physics, room 2-47).

Admission to the school is based on the results of an interview. The interview requirements do not go beyond the school curriculum.

Our address: Moscow, Leninskie Gory, Moscow State University, Faculty of Physics. Phone for inquiries: 139-26-56.

Directions: metro to the “University” station, then by buses 103, 119, 130, trolleybuses 4, 34, 49 to the stop “ul. Lebedev".

To the article “Swinging Rock”
(see «Kvant» No. 7)

1. The deflection of the stone on the support to the position shown at figure 2 in the article can be represented as two successive movements: 1 - transfer of the stone in space parallel to itself so that the stone begins to touch the support at point $A$ (in this case, point $O$ will be located on the continuation of the radius of the support $OA$); 2 - rotate the stone clockwise around the center $O$ at an angle $\alpha + \beta$ ($\beta = \frac{R}{r} \alpha$).

During the first movement, all points of the stone, including points $O$ and $P$, will move down to $\Delta h_1 = (R + r) - (R + r) \cos \alpha$.

Let us use without derivation the approximate formula $\cos \alpha = 1 - \frac{\alpha^2}{2}$, valid for small angles $\alpha$. Then

$$\Delta h_1 = (R + r) \frac{\alpha^2}{2}.$$

During the second movement, the center of gravity of the stone rises by

$$\Delta h_2 = (r - CP) \frac{(\alpha + \beta)^2}{2}.$$

Equilibrium is stable when the potential energy of the stone is minimal. This means that when the stone is deflected, its center of gravity rises, i.e. $\Delta h_2 > \Delta h_1$. Substituting the expressions for $\Delta h_1$ and $\Delta h_2$ into this inequality, we obtain the condition for the inequality of a stone on a support

$$CP < \frac{Rr}{R + r}.$$

2. When the rod-insert on the ball is deflected by an angle $\alpha$ without slippage, $\Delta h_1$ and $\Delta h_2$ are equal to the correspondence (see problem 1): $\Delta h_1 = 2R (1 - \cos \alpha)$, $\Delta h_2 = OP (1 - \cos 2\alpha)$. For the maximum deviation angle $\alpha_0$ the equality is valid

$$2R (1 - \cos \alpha_0) = OP (1 - \cos 2\alpha_0)$$

(the meaning of the quantity $OP$ is clear from figure insert). Using the correspondence $\cos 2\alpha_0 = 2\cos^2 \alpha_0 - 1$, from the last equality we obtain that the point $P$ is located on the axis at a distance $\frac{R}{1 + \cos \alpha_0}$ from the center $O$.

3. From the equilibrium condition it follows that the center of gravity of the half-charine is at a distance $5/8r$ from its top. Those who are familiar with the elements of higher mathematics can verify that this is where the center of gravity of a homogeneous semi-spherical happens to be.

To the note “Quadratic equation” (see «Kvant» No. 7, p. 9)

If one root of a quadratic equation is equal to 1, then the sum of the coefficients of the equation is equal to zero, and the second root, according to Vieta’s theorem, is equal to the ratio of the free term to the coefficient at $x^2$.
