Whirlwinds that “make the weather”

This formation is amazing - a whirlwind! It differs from other flows of homogeneous liquid or gas only in the nature of its movement, which includes rotation around its internal axis. But, in comparison with other flows, the vortex has remarkable integrity, stability and durability (see «Kvant» No. 4, 1971).

Vortex smoke rings released by the smoker fly high to the ceiling, while the smoke from his cigarette, barely rising, breaks into shavings, mixes with the air and melts. Classic experiments with large vortex rings, which were knocked away against the wall of the laboratory, are described in an article by the American experimental physicist P. Wood (see «Kvant» No. 12, 1971).

In nature, vortices arise in abundance. They appear in that part of the flow where the speed changes rapidly in a direction perpendicular to the flow. Everyone has seen eddies in a fast river as it transitions from rapids to slow currents near the shore. A whole chain of vortices can stretch behind a moving object, say, a machine gun. They are especially convenient to observe on the highway on blizzard days, when the car is blown by a strong headwind, and snow flakes exhibit the movement of transparent air. Such vortices appear when flowing around obstacles.

The vortex nature of strong winds was replaced in 1821 by W. Redfield, the owner of a small store in the state of Connecticut (USA), who, while traveling around the state after a storm, noticed trees fallen by the wind. In one place the trees lay with their tops to the northwest, while in some there was a system of winds. By talking with sailors and analyzing ship's logs, he established the direction of rotation of large vortices and found the trajectories of their centers. In 1831 it was published

In society, vortex movements are characteristics of the Earth’s atmosphere. However, not all solutions “make the difference”. The weather on the globe largely depends on the presence of giant atmospheric vortices-cycles and anticycles that set the wind regime in a given Earth.

In the output system, called the cyclome (Fig. 1), atmospheric pressure is understood from the periphery to the center. Therefore, testing the Earth's licking influence of the current direction towards the center of the cyclone. All cyclones have a rotational component of wind speed. In the Northern Hemisphere it is directed counterclockwise, in the Southern Hemisphere it is directed clockwise. In developing cyclones (i.e. those in which the pressure in the center continues to fall) upward flows are observed. At the same time, heavy clouds form and precipitation falls.

The last two properties of a cyclone are very similar to each other. In fact, let’s mentally isolate a certain volume of air and see what happens when it rises. Falling into a more rarefied atmosphere, this volume expands, the temperature of the air inside it drops, and the water vapor contained in it condenses.

The direction of rotation of cyclomes in different hemispheres can be explained by the twisting effect of the Corilis force (for more details, see «Kvant» No. 2, 1975), associated with the daily rotation of the Earth. Let us recall the well-known fact that the deflecting effect of this force causes rivers to wash away their right banks. The air flow is not held by the shore, and therefore, as it moves towards the center, it will deviate to the right when looking towards the center, i.e. counterclockwise when viewed from above.

Note that near the equator itself in the latitude band ≤5° on both sides, powerful outlets do not form. This fact fits well with the above obligation, since at the equator the horizontal component of the Corilis force is zero.

Depending on the place of origin, cyclones are divided into tropical and extratropical.

Figure 2 and 3 represent an overhead view of the extratropical and tropical cyclones, respectively. An extratropical cyclone (sometimes simply called a cyclone) is the largest atmospheric vortex, reaching several thousand kilometers in cross-section. Its height varies between 2-4 and 15-20 km. Most of the time, the wind speed there does not exceed 40-70 km/h.

The transverse size of a tropical cyclone (also called a hurricane, tropical hurricane, typhoon, etc.) is much smaller - only a few hundred kilometers, its height is up to 12-15 km. The pressure in hurricanes drops much lower than in an extratropical cyclone. At the same time, the wind speed reaches 400-600 km/h.

The highest wind speeds in a hurricane are observed around the so-called “eye of the storm” - a calm zone in the central part of the hurricane. The black spot of the correct shape on figure 3 is the eye of the storm. Let's try to schematically describe the “meeting” of two vortices. For each single vortex, a region of intense rotation is separated from the stationary zone by the so-called peripheral region, in which the speed gradually decreases to zero. Let's imagine that two hurricanes were at such a distance that the center of each of them fell into the peripheral region of the other. Let, for the purpose of definition, hurricanes rotate counterclockwise. Each of them will set in motion the center of its “brother” in such a way that it rotates counterclockwise relative to it. As is easy to see, this will lead to rotation of the centers of both hurricanes also counterclockwise relative to some locations on the straight line connecting the centers of the hurricanes, close to the more powerful vortex. In the lower layers of the atmosphere the influence flows towards the center of the hurricane, while at the top it flows away from the center. Depending on the intensity of these processes for a given pair of hurricanes, they may merge if “suction” predominates, or retreat from each other if outflow flows in the upper layers become stronger.

At the same time, hurricanes continue to influence each other all the time. According to the American physicist K. Orr, their movement resembles the behavior of boxers in the ring, waiting for the moment to deliver the decisive blow.

A similar scheme allows us to consider the meeting of anticycles and a cyclone-anticyclone pair. In addition, it can be extended to the case when the vortex falls into a powerful influence with it.

The development of a vortex is determined by many factors, and it is practically impossible to make any assumptions in advance about the result of the “meeting” of two vortices.

The path of an already developed hurricane sometimes turns out to be very long, and while passing through it, the vortex experiences various transformations. When leaving the tropics, a hurricane takes the form of a strongly inland cyclone.

Storm cyclones in Western Europe often turn out to be former tropical hurricanes that passed along the coast of North America and crossed the Atlantic. Some of them, having passed through Europe, then go to Asia.

When a hurricane reaches land, due to the “roughness” of the earth’s surface, its lower parts begin to collapse. In addition, when passing over reality, a hurricane is weaker due to a lack of “nutrition” - moisture. But if the hurricane is adjacent to the upper part, it can “unwind the mechanism” with the same force.

Hitting densely populated areas of the world, a hurricane claims thousands of human lives and causes enormous material damage. Its energy is enormous: in one day, a larger hurricane “consumes” energy equal to the energy of the explosion of 13,000 megaton nuclear bombs; The kinetic energy of an average hurricane is equal to the energy reserve of 1000 atomic bombs.

The activity of hurricanes changes the topography of the earth's surface: coral islands disappear, ocean shores “move,” new profiles appear, etc.

Forecasts of the movements of cyclones and anticyclones have become much more reliable over the past two decades thanks to the use of computers and information delivered by satellites, radars and aircraft. New advanced devices and instruments come to the aid of the official. This service is ongoing.
