Here you see three curves. One of them is a circle. The other is “red” - Dinostrata’s quadratrica. Its invention is attributed to the famous sophistio Hippnio, who lived in the fifth century BC. e., - one of the first mathematicians, having whom history has conveyed to us. Hippnias used this curve to solve the problem of trisection of an angle (also called trisectis). Dinostratus (fourth century BC) used it to solve the problem of squaring the circle (hence the quadratrix). Both of them looked only at the part of the curve that was visible as a thick line.

The third curve depicted here - the cochleona - is obtained from the Dinostratus quadratrix as a result of the inversion of the relative drawn circle. Its name comes from the Greek hoxλος (snail). It resembles the lines that are visible on the shells of some bifacial molluscs.
