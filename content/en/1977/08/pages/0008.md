This huge book (more than 500 pages in large format) contains the main results of Gauss: the square law of reciprocity, the problem of dividing a circle, the question of the representability of integers in the form $am^2 + bmn + cn^2$ (in particular, in the form of a sum of squares). The book was published at the expense of the Duke and dedicated to him. In its published form, the book consisted of seven parts. There were not enough delegates for the eighth part. In this part, we were supposed to talk about the generalization of the quadratic law of reciprocity to degrees higher than the second, in particular, about the biquadratic law of reciprocity. Gauss found a complete proof of the square law only on October 23, 1813, and in his diary he noted that this coincided with the separation of his son.
