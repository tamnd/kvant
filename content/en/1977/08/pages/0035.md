But in any case, choosing the best strategy is a very difficult task, allowing for inconclusive decisions.

The best strategy does not always exist. For example, it is not in the game “Sportloto”: you can assign numbers arbitrarily, and at the same time the probability of guessing a certain number of numbers will not change. True, it should be noted that it is possible to influence the size of the winnings: to do this, you need to use a strategy that the majority of those participating in the game are for some reason against - after all, the winnings are divided between those who guess correctly. There are obvious biases (for example, some are sure that they will never win more than six numbers in a row), and there are also implicit ones (numbers that are multiples of heels are chosen more often than others).

In the currently developed game theory, many problems have already been considered. In some of them, the choice has to be made not from a finite, but from an infinite number of strategies. Not all the tasks have been solved - for example, the problem “Pripiss and the Monster”, formed by the American scientist R. Isaacs, has not been solved.

Pripissa, a comora who can move at any speed and in any direction, and the Beast, whose speed is limited, are imprisoned in a completely dark room. The monster captures Pripissa if the consideration between them becomes less than the specified one. What should be the Priscribe's strategy so that she can delay the position as much as possible (probably until the Priprince arrives)?

It is clear that if Pripissa begins to rush around, making full use of her speed, she herself will stumble upon the Beast, and if she stands, then the Beast’s systematic walk around the entire room will bring him success (on average, in a time equal to half the time of the walk). Isaacs concludes that Pripissa’s optimal strategy should be mixed, but no one knows what it is. Apparently, Pripissa should change speed and direction randomly.

It is especially difficult to develop the best strategy when the game is not against an intelligent opponent whose strategy is to inflict the most damage, but against nature. Nature’s strategy cannot be foreseen based on the principle of greatest damage—nature is benign. To penetrate its strategy, you need to know the physical laws, and this is not done by game theory, but by completely different theories...
