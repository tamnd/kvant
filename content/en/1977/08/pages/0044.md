⟦folio 42⟧

Tasks

M456—M460; F468—F472

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. Solutions to the problem from this issue can be submitted in writing no later than November 1, 1977 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant». After the address on the envelope, write the numbers of the problems for which you are sending solutions, for example, “M456, M457” or “F468”. Solutions to problems in each of the subjects (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be written in writing in separate envelopes. Also write assignments from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope). Write the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in duplicate along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope put: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”). After forming the task, we will summarize and indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. In this and the next issue, “Problem «Kvant»” is composed mainly of problems proposed at the last All-Union Olympiad.

M456. At each vertex of a convex polyhedron, 3 edges converge. It is known that each of its faces is a polygon around which a circle can be described. Prove that a sphere can be described around a polyhedron.

V. Proizvolov

M457. There is a non-self-intersecting closed polygon on the plane, no three vertices of which lie on the same straight line. Let us call a pair of non-adjacent links special if the extension of one of them intersects the other (Fig. 1). Prove that the number of special pairs is even.

S. Fomin

M458. The polynomial $x^{10} + x^9 + x^8 + \dots + x^2 + x + 1$ is written. Two people play this game. First, the first one replaces any of the stars with a certain number, then the second one replaces any of the remaining stars with a number, then again replaces one of the stars with a number, and so on (9 moves in total). If the resulting polynomial does not have real roots, then the first player wins, and if there is at least one root, the second player wins. Is it possible for the second player to win with any game of the first?

D. and I. Bernistein

M459. In some countries, you can travel from each city to any other in minutes to the rest of the cities. The cost of each such trip is known. Two routes have been compiled for the cities of the country. Each of these routes includes each city exactly once.
