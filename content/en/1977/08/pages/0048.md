Before solving this problem, let us prove the following auxiliary statement. Let figure $S$ of area $s$ contain figures $S_1, \dots, S_l$ of area $s_1, \dots, s_l$ such that each point of figure $S$ is covered by at most $k$ figures $S_i$. Then $\sum s_i = s_1 + s_2 + \dots + s_l \leqslant ks$. Indeed, consider the figures $T_j$ resulting from the intersection of the figure $S_i$ (Fig. 4); On each of the figures $T_j$ we will write a number showing how many $S_i$ it belongs to. Obviously, $\sum t_j \leqslant s$ ($t_j$ is the area of ​​the figure $T_j$). On the other hand, it is no less obvious that $\sum s_i \leqslant k \sum t_j$ ($k$ is the maximum number of intersections $S_i$). From here we get that $\sum s_i \leqslant ks$.

Let's return to our task.

Let us describe a ring around this point with an internal radius of 2 and an external radius of 3; only 650 rings. Let us prove that there are necessarily at least ten intersecting averages.

Let's take the combination of these 650 rings; they are somehow located in a circle of radius 16+3=19. Let us assume that each point of this circle is covered by no more than nine rings, and we will show that this leads to a contradiction. To do this, let's calculate the areas.

The area of ​​a circle of radius 19 is $S=361\pi$. The area of ​​one ring is $s=5\pi$, so the area of ​​all 650 rings is $650 \cdot 5\pi=3250\pi$. If the rings intersect in at most 9, then according to the statement proved above,
$$\sum s=3250\pi \leqslant 9S=9 \cdot 361\pi=3249\pi$$
- a contradiction.

So, we have proven that there are at least ten intersecting rings.

This implies the statement of the problem. Indeed, if there are two intersecting rings of inner radius 2 and outer 3 with centers $O_1$ and $O_2$. then any point $M$ belonging to their intersection is removed from the point $O_1$ and $O_2$ by a distance $r: 2 \leqslant r \leqslant 3$; so points $O_1$ and $O_2$ fall into the ring we need with center $M$ (Fig. 5). In a ring (of radii 2 and 3) with a center at any point from the non-empty (!) intersection of at least ten rings there will be at least ten points - the centers of these intersecting rings.
