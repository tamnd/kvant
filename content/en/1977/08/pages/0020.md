According to the stories of the supernaturalists, the cavity of a tornado is similar to the inside of a black empty cylinder, illuminated from the inside by the brilliance of lightning flashing between the walls. In some cases, the observed lightning was not seen.

One day, the lower edge of a tornado passed over the observer’s head at a height of 6 m. The width of the internal cavity of this tornado was about 130 m, while the thickness of the wall was only 3 m. In the middle of the cavity there is a bright, transparent cloud glowing with blue light. A little later, when the tornado had already passed over the observer, its end descended to the ground, touched a neighboring house and carried it away in an instant. The house disintegrated in the air.

From its side, a tornado resembles a pillar (Fig. 6), funnel (Fig. 7) or trunk (Fig. 4) hanging from the base of a powerful thundercloud. A group of tornadoes can form at once (Fig. 8).
