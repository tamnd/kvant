⟦folio 14⟧

Gauss's life: his second wife dies, he begins to suffer from severe insomnia. In the same year, 27-year-old physicist Wilhelm Weber, invited by Gauss, came to Göttingen. Gauss met him in 1828 at Humboldt's house. Gauss was 54 years old; his reticence was legendary, and yet in Weber he found a companionship in science that he had never had before.

“The internal difference of these people was also clearly expressed in their external oblique. Gauss - recognized; strongly built, a true representative of Lower Saxony, short on words and withdrawn into himself. A kind of opposite to him is the small, graceful, agile Weber, whose extreme courtesy and conversationalism immediately revealed the radical essence; he was indeed from Wittenberg, that country of “Saxons squared.” On the Göttingen monument to Gauss and Weber, this contrast between their artistic collections is softened and even in age they seem closer than they really were.” (Klein)

The interests of Gauss and Weber lay in the field of electrodynamics and terrestrial magnetism. Their activities had not only theoretical, but also practical results. In 1833 they invent the electromagnetic telephone (this event is depicted in their common monument). The first telephone called the observatory and the physical institute. Due to financial principles, it was not possible to bring the telephone into life.

In the process of studying magnetism, they relied both on observations at the magnetic observatory created in Göttingen, and on materials that were collected in different countries by the “Union for the Observation of Terrestrial Magnetism,” created by Humboldt after returning from South America. At the same time, Gauss created one of the most important chapters of mathematical physics - potential theory.

The joint studies of Gauss and Weber were transformed in 1843, when Weber, along with six other prominent professors, was expelled from Göttingen for signing a letter to the king, which pointed out the latter's violations of the structure (Gauss did not sign the letter). Weber returned to Gottingen only in 1849, when Gauss was already 72 years old.
