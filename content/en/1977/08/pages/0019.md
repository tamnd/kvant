⟦folio 17⟧

Depending on the place of origin, cyclones are divided into tropical and extratropical.

Figure 2 and 3 represent an overhead view of the extratropical and tropical cyclones, respectively. An extratropical cyclone (sometimes simply called a cyclone) is the largest atmospheric vortex, reaching several thousand kilometers in cross-section. Its height varies between 2-4 and 15-20 km. Most of the time, the wind speed there does not exceed 40-70 km/h.

The transverse size of a tropical cyclone (also called a hurricane, tropical hurricane, typhoon, etc.) is much smaller - only a few hundred kilometers, its height is up to 12-15 km. The pressure in hurricanes drops much lower than in an extratropical cyclone. At the same time, the wind speed reaches 400-600 km/h.

The highest wind speeds in a hurricane are observed around the so-called “eye of the storm” - a calm zone in the central part of the hurricane. The black spot of the correct shape on figure 3 is the eye of the storm. An expressive description of the eye of a hurricane is given by a very eater who sang through the typhoon on a meteorological service plane - French journalist P. A. Molin, author of the book “Typhoon Hunters.”

“We are flying at an altitude of 3 km in a well with a diameter of 22 km, in which several cirrus clouds float, peaceful as toys. The steps of this well represent the immovable storm held by the tinsten cause. It is directed towards the top of the wall, the exit from this well 15 km above us. And before our eyes, these boiling walls unfold before our eyes, this gigantic abyss, this large response, which made us call the whole phenomenon the “eye of the typhoon.”

Note that extratropical cyclones do not have an “eye”.

There is also a quiet zone (cavity) for small-scale vortices - tornadoes (tornadoes, blood clots) (Fig. 4). Their dimensions are very small: width - from several meters to 2-3 km, on average 200-400 m, height from several tens to 1500-2000 m, on average several hundred meters. The wind speed in a tornado sometimes exceeds sound speed (1200 km/h!).
