We find the parts of the cylinder from the gas state equation:

$$T_1 = \frac{p_1 V_1 \mu}{R m_2} = \frac{p_1 V_1}{p_2 V_0} T_0 = T_0 \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma} - 1}.$$

Now that the pressure has become the same as on the left, the valve in the partition opens and the gases move (the piston does not move at this time). The temperature of the “mixture” $T_2$ can be determined from the heat balance equation:

$$c_V m_1 (T_2 - T_0) = c_V m_2 (T_1 - T_2),$$

And

$$T_2 = \frac{m_1 T_0 + m_2 T_1}{m_1 + m_2} = T_0 \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}} \right].$$

After displacement, the entire gas of mass $m = m_1 + m_2$ is compressed adnabatally from volume $V = V_1 + V_0$ to volume $V_0$, and its temperature changes from $T_2$ to $T$. At the same time

$$TV_0^{\gamma-1} = T_2 V^{\gamma-1},$$

where

$$T = T_2 \left( \frac{V_1 + V_0}{V_0} \right)^{\gamma-1} = T_0 \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}} \right]^{\gamma}.$$

Substituting this expression for $T$ into formula (*), we finally obtain

$$A_1 = c_V (m_1 + m_2) T_0 \left\{ \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} \left[ 1 + \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}} \right]^{\gamma} - 1 \right\} - p_0 Sl \approx 3674 \text{ дж.}$$

F430. Fig. 10 shows the simplest circuit of the receiver. The diode is considered ideal: its resistance in the forward direction is zero, in the reverse direction it is infinitely high. How many times does the power released at the resistance $R$ change when a capacitor $C$ is connected in parallel to it with such a capacitance that during the period of voltage fluctuation of seven ($U=220$ V, $f=50$ Hz) the charge of the capacitor practically does not change?

Fig. 10.

If resistance $R$ is connected directly to the AC network, then power equal to $\frac{U^2}{R}$ is released in it, where $U$ is the effective voltage value.

If the resistance is connected to the network through a diode (see Fig. 10), the power released in it is 2 times less (the half-period current through the resistance is odd):

$$P_1 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{U^2}{R}.$$

Now let’s find the power $P_2$ released in the resistance when a capacitor is connected in parallel to it. The conditions of the problem say that during the period of voltage fluctuations in the network, the charge of the capacitor practically does not change. This means that the voltage across the capacitor can be considered constant and equal to the amplitude value $U_0$.

Then $P_2 = \frac{U_0^2}{R} = \frac{(V^2 U)^2}{R} = \frac{2U^2}{R}$, since the voltage value is $\sqrt{2}$ times greater than the effective value.

Thus, when a capacitor is connected in parallel with a resistance, the power released in it is 4 times greater.
