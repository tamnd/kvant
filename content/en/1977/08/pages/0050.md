Piston area $S=10^{-2}\,m^2$.

Since the system is thermally posed, the total work $A$ performed on the gas layer acting on the piston and the force of atmospheric pressure is equal to the change in gas energy $\Delta U$:

$$A = \Delta U.$$

Let us denote by $T$ the final temperature of the gas. Then $\Delta U = c_V(m_1+m_2)(T-T_0)$.

To find the work $A_1$ of the force applied to the piston, it is necessary to subtract the work of the atmospheric pressure force $A_2=p_0Sl$ from the total work $A$. As a result we get

$$A_1 = c_V(m_1+m_2)(T-T_0)-p_0Sl.$$

Of all the quantities included in this expression, we do not know only the temperature $T$ at the end of the process. Let's find her. To do this, we will have to consider all these processes sequentially.

It is clear that at the very beginning the pressure on the left side of the cylinder is greater than on the right:

$$p_1 = \frac{m_1}{\mu} \frac{R T_0}{l S} > p_2 = \frac{m_2}{\mu} \frac{R T_0}{l S}.$$

$$p_1 V_1^\gamma = p_2 V_0^\gamma,$$

where $\gamma = \frac{cp}{c_V}$ is the adnabata indicator, and $V_0=lS$ *). From here

$$V_1 = V_0 \left( \frac{p_2}{p_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}} = V_0 \left( \frac{m_2}{m_1} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.$$
