Therefore, the trey coefficient can be found by determining the angle $\beta$.

In addition to the force $\vec{F}_1$, the weight force $mg$ also acts on the body. Since the body moves in a circle of radius $r$, the acting $\vec{F}$ forces $\vec{F}_1$ and $mg$ are directed horizontally and equal in abscult value $m\omega^2 r$:

$$\vec{F} = \vec{F}_1 + mg, \quad |\vec{F}| = m\omega^2 r.$$

Let us denote by $\alpha$ the angle between the radius $OA$ and the vertical, and by $\gamma$ the angle between the direction of the force $\vec{F}_1$ and the vertical (see Fig. 7). Then

$$\beta = \alpha - \gamma,$$

And

$$\text{tg} \beta = \text{tg}(\alpha - \gamma) = \frac{\text{tg} \alpha - \text{tg} \gamma}{1 + \text{tg} \alpha \text{tg} \gamma}. \tag{1}$$

But $\text{tg} \gamma = \frac{m\omega^2 r}{m|\vec{g}|}$, where $r = R \sin \alpha$, and $\sin \alpha = \frac{\sqrt{R^2 - R^2/4}}{R} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, that is, $\alpha = 60^\circ$. Then from expression (1)

$$\text{tg} \alpha - \frac{\omega^2 R \sin \alpha}{|\vec{g}|} = \frac{\sqrt{R^2 - R^2/4}}{R} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},$$

And

$$\mu \geqslant \text{tg} \beta = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{23} \approx 0,23.$$

At $\omega = 8$ rad/sec we get

$$\text{tg} \beta = -\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{29}.$$

The minus sign means that $\gamma > \alpha$ and therefore the force $\vec{F}_1$ passes to the left of the radius $OA$, and the trey force is directed downwards along the tangent (Fig. 8). At the same time

$$\mu \geqslant |\text{tg} \beta| = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{29} \approx 0,18.$$
