- And Verda? - Mots choked with indignation. “We’ll lose five dockers there!”

“Keep it down, generals,” their Dor invariably said. - Let's try to figure it out. Our task is to lose as few dockers as possible,” Dor looked at the table with options again, “but it all depends on what plan the enemy chooses.” What does intelligence say?

Nahit reluctantly answered:

— We don’t have accurate data yet. But, one must think, the enemy is also hesitating. In other words, we can assume that the probability of the enemy accepting each plan is equal to $1/2$. Don’t worry, gentlemen, I, like you, am not familiar with the theory of probability. From all this sophisticated science, I only know that probability $1/2$ means one chance out of two, and also a rule for calculating expected losses. For example, if the probability of a settlement on Kalimna is $1/2$, and our losses as a result of such a settlement will be equal to six dockers, then the expected losses in Kalimna will be $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$. And if, say, the probability of recovery was $1/3$, then this would mean the expected losses $6 \times \frac{1}{3} = 2$.

This is enough to calculate the expected losses in each of our two possible plans:

| STRATEGY | EXPECTED LOSSES OF AROKERS |
| :--- | :--- |
| in Kalimna | in Verde | Total |
| PROTECTION OF VERDA | $6 \times \frac{1}{2} = 3$ | $3 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1.5$ | $3 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1.5$ |
| PROTECTION OF KALYMNA | $1 \times \frac{1}{2} = 1.5$ | $5 \times \frac{1}{2} = 2.5$ | $5 \times \frac{1}{2} = 2.5$ |

You see that when defending Kalimna, we will lose a whole one and a half less dockers.
