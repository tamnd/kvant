Ask - we answer

Editorial office «Kvant» received a letter from reader Elena Glikina from the city of Severodonets, Voroshilograd region. Here's what she writes:

“I happen to encounter the most interesting paradox. I take the equation
$$x^{x^x} = 2;$$
(1)
it can be rewritten as $x^2 = 2$, hence $x = \sqrt{2}$, and therefore
$$\sqrt{2} \sqrt{2} \sqrt{2} = 2.$$
(2)
I take another equation:
$$y^{y^y} = 4;$$
(3)
it can be rewritten as $y^4 = 4$, hence $y = \sqrt{2}$, and therefore
$$\sqrt{2} \sqrt{2} \sqrt{2} = 4.$$
(4)
It follows that $2 = 4$. I ask you to explain the reason for such a paradoxical equation..."

We think that it will be useful for all our readers to think about this letter. That is why we publish on the pages of the magazine both the letter itself and the answer to the question posed in it. Do not rush, however, to read the answer - first try to understand the principles of the appearance of “such a paradoxical equation” on your own.

So, a whole series of arguments led us to the equality $2 = 4$. The falsity of the resulting equality is beyond doubt, and therefore we need to look for an error. We will conduct a detailed logical analysis of each step taken, paying special attention to the legality of each logical transition. One of these transitions is based on the general laws of logic or mathematics, others are associated with specific features of the problem under consideration.

For example, at the last step of our reasoning, we deduce from equalities (2) and (4) that $2 = 4$. In this case, we rely, very indo, on the following property: if $a = b$ and $a = c$ are true equalities, then $u = b = c$ is a true equality. Naturally, we will not question this property.

But the corollary $2 = 4$ is still false, and therefore at least one of the equalities (2), (4) is false.

Let's take a closer look at equality (2). It was obtained as a result of substituting into equation (1) the value $x = \sqrt{2}$ found when solving this equation. However, if $\sqrt{2}$ is the root of equation (1), then equality (2), of course, is true - by virtue of the very definition of the root. Thus, we need to check whether the number $\sqrt{2}$ is really the root of equation (1).
