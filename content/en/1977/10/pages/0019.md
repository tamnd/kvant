In the first years of N. N. Luzin’s pedagogical activity at Moscow University (and these were the years immediately preceding the revolution), there was only one mathematical seminar at the university. It had D.F. Egorov as its leader and was called that: Mathematical Seminar or, more precisely, Mathematical Seminary. The current word “seminar” became widespread at Moscow University only in the mid-twenties and was introduced in Moscow by O. Yu. Shmidt from Kyiv, where, apparently, it was widespread in the school of D. A. Grave. So, the Mathematical Seminary of D. F. Egorov issued every year in the spring; its theme changed from year to year. I first took part in this seminar in 1914, while still a student in the second semester of my first year; Considering that it was intended for senior students, I did not immediately and only after this recommendation from V.V. Stepanov decided to sign up for this seminar. (At that time, the so-called subject system still prevailed at the University, and each student could enroll in any course or seminar of his choice, which was, in particular, due to tuition fees and the lack of state scholarships for students.) As I already said, every year D. F. Egorov’s seminar was devoted to one particular topic. In 1914 the seminar was "Infinite Sequences".
