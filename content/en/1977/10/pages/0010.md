At Ya. I. Frenkel's seminar, he himself told us elements of Sommerfeld's theory and about his research in this direction.

There was another problem in metal physics that worried scientists. There is a phenomenon called the Hall effect. It consists in the following. Let us imagine a rectangular metal plate along which an electric current $I$ (figure 1) flows. It is obvious that between points $A$ and $A'$ of the plate the direction is zero. But if this plate is placed in a magnetic field, the indication $\vec{B}$ of which is redefined by the plane of the plate, then between the points $A$ and $A'$ a certain direction $U$ appears, which is called emf. With. Holla. E.d. With. The Hall effect can be changed using a galvanometer connected by conductors to points $A$ and $A'$.

The appearance in a conductor with a volume under the influence of a magnetic field of a potential difference in a direction transverse to the direction of the current is called the Hall effect. (This phenomenon was theoretically presented in the sixties last century by Maxwell.) How to explain the occurrence of e.m. With. Holla?

It is known that the current represents the movement of electrons, and an electron moving in a magnetic field with a speed of $\vec{v}$, redistributed to the field plane $\vec{B}$, is acted upon by the Lorentz force $\vec{F}_\pi$, equal in absolute value to $e|\vec{v}||\vec{B}|$. the direction of the Lorentz force is determined by the right of the left hand (if the left hand is positioned so that the vector $\vec{B}$ “enters” the palm, the four folded fingers are extended along the direction opposite to the direction of the velocity $\vec{v}$ of the electron, then the bent thumb indicates the direction of the Lorentz force). Let the plate be located in a magnetic field as shown in figure 1. A current flows along the plate along the $Y$ axis (the speed of the electrons is directed in the opposite direction!). Then, under the influence of the Lorentz force, the electrons will deviate towards the $a'$ face of the plate, and a large number of electrons will accumulate on this face. The accumulated electrodes create an electrostatic field inside the plates, the direction of which is proportional to their number. In this field, the electric waves are acted upon by a force $|\vec{F}_\alpha|=e|\vec{E}|$, directed opposite to the Lorentz force, that is, braking them. Obviously, when this force becomes the Lorentz force acting on the electron “flowing” through the plate, the transverse movement of the electrons will stop. From the condition of equality of these forces, one can find the direction $|\vec{E}|$ of the established field of the internal plate:

$$e|\vec{E}|=e|\vec{B}||\vec{v}|,$$

where

$$|\vec{E}|=|\vec{B}||\vec{v}|.$$

In this case, a potential difference is established between points $A$ and $A'$ of the plate:

$$U=|\vec{E}|b=|\vec{B}||\vec{v}|b,$$

where $b$ is the width of the plate. The speed of electrons is easy to determine, knowing the current $I$, the concentration $n$ of electrons in the area (their number per unit volume) and the area $S$ of the cross-section of the plate, redefined direction of the current. Really,

$$I=ne|\vec{v}|S,$$

where

$$|\vec{v}|=I/neS=I/nebd$$

(d—plate thickness).
