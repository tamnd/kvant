Luzinsk Mathematical School

P. Aleksaidrov

The author of this article is one of the largest Soviet mathematicians, Hero of Socialist Labor, academician Pavel Sergeevich Aleksaidrov. The article talks about the formation of one of the most fruitful mathematical schools - the school of academician N. N. Luzin. On the left (university column) there are terms that are little familiar to schoolchildren and were encountered in the article.

DIFFERENTIAL GEOMETRY - a part of geometry that studies the main curves of lines and surfaces using the methods of differential calculus. It examines the properties of tangent lines to plane and spatial curves, tangent planes to curved surfaces, and various types of lines.

The modern Moscow mathematical school of set-theoretic mathematics arose in the first years of the revolution within the walls of Moscow University. Its founders were Dmitry Fedorovich Egorov and Nikolai Nikolaevich Luzin.

In the decades preceding its emergence, starting from the seventies of the nineteenth century, the interests of Moscow mathematicians concentrated mainly around problems of differential geometry and mechanics.

These problems, closely related to the theory of differential equations with partial arbitrary and the theory of conformal mappings, were culturally developed, on the one hand, by the outstanding Latvian geometer Karl Mikhailovich Peterson, who lived in Moscow and held a modest position as a gymnasium teacher, and, on the other hand, were associated with the remarkable works of the great Moscow mechanics: Nikolai Egorovich Zhukovsky and his student of Sergei Alekseevich Chaplygin. The activities of these scientists were closely connected with the Moscow Mathematical Society, which was founded in the mid-1860s, and served throughout the last decades.
