⟦folio 47⟧

F441. Between the plates of a closed flat capacitor there is a powerful charge q. The area of ​​the plates is large, the distance between them is equal to d. The initial charge is located at a distance $\frac{1}{3} d$ from the left plate.

What charge will pass through the conductor connecting the capacitor plates when moving charge q to a new position at which it will be at a distance $\frac{1}{3} d$ from the right plate?

Fig. 11.

Since the capacitor plates are large, the charges induced on them will not change when the charge q moves parallel to the planes of the plates (we neglect edge effects). This means that the charges of the plates will not change even if the charge q is evenly “smeared” along a plane parallel to the plates of the capacitor and initially located at a distance $\frac{1}{3} d$ from the left plate.

Let us denote by $q_1$ the charge of the left plate of the capacitor. Then the charge of the right plate (according to the law of conservation of charge) is equal to $-q_1$. Charges $q_1$ and $-q_1$ of the capacitor plates create an electrostatic field between the plates with intensity $|\vec{E}_1| = \frac{q_1}{\varepsilon_0 S}$ ($S$ is the area of ​​each plate), and the plate with charge $q$ located inside the capacitor creates a field with intensity $|\vec{E}| = \frac{q}{2\varepsilon_0 S}$ (Fig. 11). On one side of the plate the directions of the directed fields coincide, and on the other side they are opposite to each other. Therefore, the potential difference $\Delta\varphi$ between the plates of the capacitor is equal to

$$\Delta\varphi = \left( |\vec{E}_1| - |\vec{E}| \right) \frac{1}{3} d + \left( |\vec{E}_1| + |\vec{E}| \right) \frac{2}{3} d.$$

On the other hand, since the capacitor plates are closed, the direction between them is zero: $\Delta\varphi = 0$. Hence,

$$\frac{1}{3} d \left( \frac{q_1}{\varepsilon_0 S} - \frac{q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} \right) + \frac{2}{3} d \left( \frac{q_1}{\varepsilon_0 S} + \frac{q}{2\varepsilon_0 S} \right) = 0.$$

From this equation we find the charge $q_1$:

$$q_1 = -\frac{1}{6} q.$$

Similarly, you can find the charge $q'_1$ of the left plate of the capacitor when the middle plate (charge q) is located at a distance $\frac{2}{3} d$ from it:

$$q'_1 = \frac{1}{6} q.$$

Thus, when the internal capacitor moves a charge q (or charged plate) along the conductor connecting the capacitor plates, a charge passes

$$\Delta q = q'_1 - q_1 = \frac{1}{3} q.$$

I. Slobodetsky
