Having solved the resulting heel system of the equation, we find

$$tg \alpha_{\text{max}} = \frac{\mu}{\sin^2 \varphi - \mu^2 \cos^2 \varphi} = \frac{4\mu}{3 - \mu^2},$$

$$\alpha_{\text{max}} = \arctg \frac{4\mu}{3 - \mu^2}.$$

The problem can be solved in the same way using the so-called “three force theorem”: if a body is in equilibrium under the action of three non-parallel forces applied to it, then the lines of action of these forces intersect at one point.

In our case, the three forces applied to the stick are the gravitational force $\vec{Mg}$ and the resulting reaction forces $\vec{R}_1 = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{N}_1$ and $\vec{R}_2 = \vec{F}_2 + \vec{N}_2$ (Fig. 8). Since the force tremya at the largest angle $\alpha_{\text{max}}$ of the head of the stick is maximum, the force $\vec{R}_1$ and $\vec{R}_2$ with the radii of the sphere drawn at the points of contact make up the same angle $\beta = \arctg \mu$ (angle tremya).

Let us consider the triangle $ACB$, in which the stick is the base, and the point $C$ of intersection of the lines of action of the forces is the opposite vertex. The linear action of gravity divides the mentioned triangle into two, having a common side of length $l$ (median of the triangle $ACB$) and concreting bases of length $R/2$. Applying the sine theorem to each triangle, we get

$$\frac{\sin(\pi/2 + \alpha - \varphi - \beta)}{R/2} = -\frac{\sin(\varphi + \beta)}{l}$$

And

$$\frac{\sin(\pi/2 - \alpha - \varphi + \beta)}{R/2} = \frac{\sin(\varphi - \beta)}{l},$$

where $tg \beta = \mu$ and $\varphi = \pi/3$.

Dividing these two equations by one another, we get

$$\frac{\cos(\alpha - \varphi - \beta)}{\cos(\alpha + \varphi - \beta)} = \frac{\sin(\varphi + \beta)}{\sin(\varphi - \beta)},$$

from which we will find

$$tg \alpha = \frac{(1 + tg^2 \varphi) tg \beta}{tg^2 \varphi - tg^2 \beta} = \frac{4\mu}{3 - \mu^2}.$$
