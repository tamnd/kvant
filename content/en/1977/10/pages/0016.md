An important section of differential geometry is the theory of geodesic lines on a surface, that is, the shortest lines connecting two points on a surface (on a plane it is a straight line segment, on a sphere it is an arc of a great circle). DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS - equations containing the product of the desired functions. If these functions depend on one argument, the differential equations are called ordinary, and if they depend on several - equations in parts of derivatives, these latter are often called equations of mathematical physics.

CONFORMAL MAPPINGS are surface-to-surface mappings that preserve angles between lines. An example is the formation of subdivisions that you studied in geometry. Another example is the stereoraphic projection of a sphere onto a plane (a projection in which the sphere is projected from the top shelf onto a plane touching it at the south pole). Conformal mappings play an important role in potato mechanics, fluid mechanics, aeromechanics and other fields of science.

The two most outstanding mathematics professors who taught at Moscow University in the first third of the current century: D. F. Egorov and N. N. Luzin, as lecturers and university professors in general, were considered two opposite types. D. F. Egorov accepted, I would say, the classical type, and N. N. Luzin - the romantic one. In the lectures of D.F. Egorov, they were always impeccably carefully prepared, all the evidence was fully productive and presented in an absolute and strict form. There was no element of improvisation in them, and they corresponded to the training and level of understanding of the cases for which they were intended. Outwardly, D.F. Egorov was always extremely serious; he did not use any oratorical techniques or decorations, and to someone who was not accustomed to his style of presentation, his lectures could even come across as somewhat dry. In fact, there was a real uplift in his presentation, but this uplift, like all the empiric side of his personality as a scientist and professor, was subordinated to a structured and even somewhat harsh-looking form.
