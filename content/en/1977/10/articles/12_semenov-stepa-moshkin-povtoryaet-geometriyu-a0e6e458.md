Styopa Moshkin repeats geometry

1. What's wrong? And does she exist?

It’s been a long time since Styopa Moshkin came home so upset.
“Look, dad, how hard I tried,” he wished to his father. “Here is the axis of symmetry - drawn in black (see Fig. 1). This triangle is the Sinenese triangle! But the yellow triangle is an image of this triangle in axial symmetry. Everything is in color, done carefully, as it should! But Pyotr Ivanovich didn’t even look properly, didn’t even pick it up. He took one look and said: “Wrong! You repeat geometry poorly. We’ll have to redistribute!” Why redo it if everything is done so carefully!

The father slowly, waiting for his son to calm down a little, examined him figure. Then he said:
- Yes, unfortunately, Pyotr Ivanovich is right: you repeated this material rather poorly. There are actually mistakes here.

- What kind?! - Styopa exclaimed.
- First of all, tell me what axial symmetry with the l axis is?
- Well, this is a mixing in which the points of the spicy l remain in place, a...
- Don't continue, that's enough. Is this condition met in your drawing?

Styopa froze over the drawing in the position of an error.

Take a look at figure. Do you see the small yellow triangle formed when the lines l, AC and A₁C₁ intersect? And another one on the other side? This is Styopa's mistake. The segment A₁C₁ must intersect the line l at the same point as the segment AC, because in symmetry with the l axis, the points of the spicy l remain in place.

Such points - left in place in some mapping - are called fixed. They can often be used to build an image of a figure.

2. Another formation

Tanya solved the same problem as Styopa, differently, and in the next lesson Peter Ivanovich invited Styopa, with her eyes on Tanin figure (Fig. 2), to tell how she performed the constructions. Styopa immediately noticed that Tanya used a compass and a ruler, and the points A₁, B₁ and C₁ turned out to be the intersections of each circle.

- What does the circle have to do with it? —

Styopa began to think out loud. — A circle is a set of points located at the same distance from a given point. And distances... distances are preserved by axial symmetry! Yeah, Tanya probably used the points M and K of the intersection of lines AC and BC with line l. Point M remains in place, so there must be

$$|MC| = |MC_1|,$$
and similarly
$$|KC| = |KC_1|.$$

This means that the point $C_1$ will be obtained as the intersection of a circle with a center at a point M of radius $|MC|$ with a circle with a center K of radius $|KC|$. True, these circles will intersect at two points. What is the second one?... So this is point C. Everything is clear. Tanya also constructed points $A_1$ and $B_1$. It turns out that a ruler is not needed at all to construct images of a point in axial symmetry. Two points on the l axis and a compass are enough. Hello!

3. Fixed points help Stepa

In the evening, Styopa told Papa about Tanya’s method of constructing images of a point in axial symmetry, and then do his homework. Opening the notebook, he read. “For home - solve problem 33” (see “Geometry 6”, p. 104). This is the task.

Given an axis of symmetry (MK), symmetrical points A and A' and another point B, suggesting that the straight line AB is not

cut the axis of symmetry. Using one line, construct a point B', symmetrical to B relative (MK).

Styopa depicted the indicated elements in black (see Fig. 3) and thought about it.

- If only I could use a square! But you can't. And the compass was also among the “forbidden” instructions. You can only use one line. What to do?.. Tanya had one compass. She drew circles for them and found images of a point as the point of intersection of the circles. I have one line. Alone too! And I can only conduct straight lines. So, maybe look for point B' as the intersection of two lines? What kind of straight lines will this be? Apparently, images of lines passing through point B. How to look for images of lines? We need images of two points. Well, there is an image of one point - after all, the data are points A and A'.

Styopa drew a straight line AB (Fig. 4) in blue and began to reason further.

— We need an image of another point on the line AB. Yeah, if the lines AB and MK intersect at point E, then this point E is fixed in symmetry with the MK axis. This means that the image of the line AB will be the line A'E.

Styopa drew a dotted line A'E (Fig. 4) and thought again.

- Where on line A'E will be point B'? We also need a straight line. How to carry it out? Maybe there is an image of another point?

Styopa re-read the conditions of the problem again. No, there are no images of other points. However, ... it is said “symmetrical points A and A'”. What does it mean? Point A' is the image of point A. But then A is the image of point A'.

Styopa quickly grabbed a beautiful pencil and drew straight A'B. Hooray! It intersected the straight line MK at point F (Fig. 5), the straight line AF intersected with the straight line A'E, and Styopa, triumphant, placed near the point of their intersection: B'.

- What if lines AF and A'E are parallel? - In a hurry, he scribbled figure. - How to solve the problem then? And straight line AB can intersect straight line MK outside the sheet of paper, and then there will be no fixed point!

But, after thinking a little, Styopa calmed down. He found a way to solve the problem in this case too.
