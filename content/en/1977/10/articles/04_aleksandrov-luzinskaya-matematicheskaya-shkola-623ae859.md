Luzinsk Mathematical School

P. Aleksaidrov

The author of this article is one of the largest Soviet mathematicians, Hero of Socialist Labor, academician Pavel Sergeevich Aleksaidrov. The article talks about the formation of one of the most fruitful mathematical schools - the school of academician N. N. Luzin. On the left (university column) there are terms that are little familiar to schoolchildren and were encountered in the article.

DIFFERENTIAL GEOMETRY - a part of geometry that studies the main curves of lines and surfaces using the methods of differential calculus. It examines the properties of tangent lines to plane and spatial curves, tangent planes to curved surfaces, and various types of lines.

The modern Moscow mathematical school of set-theoretic mathematics arose in the first years of the revolution within the walls of Moscow University. Its founders were Dmitry Fedorovich Egorov and Nikolai Nikolaevich Luzin.

In the decades preceding its emergence, starting from the seventies of the nineteenth century, the interests of Moscow mathematicians concentrated mainly around problems of differential geometry and mechanics.

These problems, closely related to the theory of differential equations with partial arbitrary and the theory of conformal mappings, were culturally developed, on the one hand, by the outstanding Latvian geometer Karl Mikhailovich Peterson, who lived in Moscow and held a modest position as a gymnasium teacher, and, on the other hand, were associated with the remarkable works of the great Moscow mechanics: Nikolai Egorovich Zhukovsky and his student of Sergei Alekseevich Chaplygin. The activities of these scientists were closely connected with the Moscow Mathematical Society, which was founded in the mid-1860s, and served throughout the last decades.

CONFORMAL MAPPINGS are surface-to-surface mappings that preserve angles between lines. An example is the formation of subdivisions that you studied in geometry. Conformal mappings play an important role in potato mechanics, fluid mechanics, aeromechanics and other fields of science.

FUNCTIONS OF A COMPLEX VARIABLE.
Functions that, in the neighborhood of any point $x_0$, where they are defined, are sums of series of the form
$$a_0 + a_1(x-x_0) + a_2(x-x_0)^2 + \dots + a_n(x-x_0)^n + \dots$$
(when the fixants of such a series are expressed through arbitrary original functions at the point $x_0$) are called analytic functions.
These include rational, exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions studied in school, as well as all functions found in applications of mathematics.
Discontinuous functions and functions whose graphs have breaks (for example, $y = |x|$ or $y = [x]$) are not analytic.
It is remarkable that two analytic functions that coincide on an extremely small interval are equal to each other by definition. The theory of analytic functions is revealed in its full beauty if it is considered not only for action values, but also for complete values ​​of the argument.
Therefore, this theory is also called the theory of complex variables. It is closely related to the theory of conformal mappings.
In a complex domain, it is possible to express the values ​​of an analytical function inside a certain domain in terms of its values ​​at the boundary of the domain. However, these boundary values ​​may turn out to be not only non-analytical, but even different.

The Luzin school, Luzinnaya, was one of a kind, a unique group of young, mostly gifted and joyful mathematicians.

In August 1924, this team suffered a heavy blow: one of the most talented members of Luzintia and, in general, one of the most talented Soviet mathematicians, Pavel Samulyuvich Urson, died.

The members of Luzintia perceived this blow of fate as personal to each of them, and Luzinnaya as a whole did not survive it for long. By this time, in the bowels of the Moscow, then still unified, school of mathematics, the process of forming separate schools in various areas of mathematics began: in the theory of functions of a complex variable, in topology, in the theory of probability, in differential equations, etc. I repeat, these schools arose within the framework of Lusincia itself, but once they arose, they immediately, like newborn whales, began to live an independent life and quickly and successfully grew and developed.

Luzinnaya as such ceased to exist, turning into a permanent replenishment of each of its participants, merging with the replenishment of his own youth.
