Is it possible to lift yourself by your hair?

The solution to the most complex technical problem, as a rule, begins with consideration of an ideal option, often a great deal from the implementation of the problem. The author of these lines allows himself to act in a similar way and teaches from afar.

Option one
Exercise. On the bow of the boat there is a tsitma in which a boy throws stones from the stern of the boat. Considering the impact of a stone on a surface to be absolutely inelastic and neglecting the force of the impact of the boat on the water and the stone on the air, find the displacement of the center of mass of the system.

Solution. Let's consider the system boat (with shield) - boy - stone. All forces acting on bodies in this system are forces of interaction between the bodies of the system. No external forces act on the system (after all, we neglect the forces of the stone against the air and the boat against the water). But this means that the momentum (momentum of motion) of the system remains unchanged. And if at the initial moment of time (before throwing the stone) the center of mass of the system was motionless (the boat is in still water) or moving with a certain speed $v$ (the boat floats with the current), then at any other moment of time it will be motionless or will move with the same speed $v$. Both throwing a stone and hitting a stone on a bush will not affect the position of the center of mass. In other words, the interaction forces between the bodies of an isolated (closed) system cannot change the position of the center of mass of the system.

Option two, in which the task is somewhat conditioned
Exercise. On the bow of the boat there is a tsitma in which a boy throws stones from the stern of the boat. Considering the impact of the stone on the surface to be absolutely inelastic, and the absolute value of the force of the impact of the boat on the water is equal to $F$, and neglecting the force of the impact of the stone on the air, find the displacement of the center of mass of the system*).

The friction force will reduce the speed of the boat, and by the time the stone hits the shield (in time $t$ after the throw), the speed of the boat will become $\vec{v}_{\pi t} < \vec{v}_{\pi0}$, so by this moment the absolute value of the boat’s momentum will decrease:

1) the speed of the boat after the stone hits the shield will be directed in the direction of throwing;

Thus, by throwing stones from the stern to the bow of the boat, you can move without using oars. After all the stones from the stern have been thrown to the bow, you need to very slowly drag them back. Then everything can be repeated all over again.
If there are no stones in the boat and the oars are lost, then the situation is still not hopeless. The role of a stone can play from the stern to the bow and slowly returns back.
If the traveler is an inventor, then he will entrust the mechanism with making wild buckles from the stern to the boat. It is enough to install an eccentric on the boat, which moves in different directions at different speeds.
The method of movement described above is not original. For many years now, articles about such self-propelled carts have been appearing in magazines. And in recent years, large models of such carriages have been created.
