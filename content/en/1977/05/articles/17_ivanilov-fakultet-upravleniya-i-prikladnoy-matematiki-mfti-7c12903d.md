If you analyze literary works, you will notice a chronological outline of the heroes: in the novels of the 17th century it is a knight, the 18th century is a merchant (remember Robinson Crusoe), the 19th century is an industrialist and financier (for example, the novels of Galsworthy, Mamina-Sibirka, etc.), in the novels of the 20th century “management”, an organizer, appears. In a broad sense, “management” is an applied science that studies management activities. Complex modern facilities, be it an airplane or a factory, require proper management. Therefore, the number of people involved (primarily or indirectly) in management is constantly increasing. For example, planning alone in any developed country involves several tens of thousands of people.

In a narrower sense, “management” is an applied science that studies management activities. This includes the automation of production and the creation of robots, for example, “lunar rovers” and those that are easier to replace a person in particularly difficult conditions, the creation of computer-controlled machines, etc. But the introduction of computer technology and automation is carried out only in production.

When creating a giant particle accelerator in Serpukhov, I’m so confused about the following problem. Preparing an accelerator for wholesale will take up space. Then, near the accelerator location, it is in a “working state”. A single experiment takes a fraction of a second, and processing its results takes weeks. Since the conditions of the next experiment are determined by the results of the previous one, only a few experiments are possible during this testing period. Thus, the main part of the accelerator’s time is idle, “waiting” for the results to be processed

The problem was solved by a computer, which took over the experiments and carried them out within one to two hours. Physicists working on accelerators want to ensure that the computer provides the conditions for conducting the next series of experiments at the direct accelerator.

Another example. Designing a modern aircraft takes many years, and in the end it may turn out that the aircraft, the creation of which took millions of rubles, is already “morally obsolete” - new requirements have appeared, better materials, etc. To speed up design, it is necessary to create a design automation system, when the designer works in contact with a computer, which “prompts” him with solutions, carries out the necessary calculations, and provides the required numerical or graphical information.

Similar examples can be cited not only in technical fields, but also in economics, and even in international relations and in solving environmental problems. All these examples reflect one of the most characteristic processes of the external scientific and technological revolution - the process of replacing the simplest forms of mental labor with the work of “smart” machines. This replacement gave rise to new applied scientific directions.

Finally, there is another, narrowest meaning of the word “management”.

The Industrial Revolution, a production revolution in the 19th century, led to the replacement of physical labor with the labor of machines and mechanisms, and it was in those years that physics, which was the scientific basis of this revolution, and then the leading science of the 19th century, began to spread. True, the fame that always comes with a completed one came to physics only at the beginning of the 20th century.

The scientific and technological revolution, currently in production, leads to the replacement of the mental services of working machines. The applied sciences that arise at the same time need their theoretical foundation, similar to physics for the engineering sciences of centuries past. This foundation is management, understood as an exact science like mathematics and physics.

We can say that mathematics studies the relations “a is greater than b”, “c belongs to D”, physics studies the properties (changes) of relations under the influence of certain forces and fields. Management studies targeted changes in relationships that precede the achievement of certain goals. Therefore, physical models are part of the random control model. You know that, for example, the fall of a body can be explained and described by “attributing” the nature of the goals - to minimize the potential energy of the body.

Management is a physical science in the sense that, like physics, it studies real objects; at the same time it is an exact mathematical science.
