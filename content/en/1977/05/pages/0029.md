Currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ flowing through resistances $R_1$ and $R_2$ are inversely proportional to the values ​​of these resistances:

$$\frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{R_2}{R_1} = \frac{4}{1}.$$

At the same time

$$I_1 + I_2 = I = 5a.$$

From here

$$I_1 = 4a.$$

Similarly, we can write the corresponding expressions for the currents $I_3$ and $I_4$:

$$\frac{I_3}{I_4} = \frac{R_4}{R_3}$$
$$I_3 + I_4 = I.$$

If $R_3 = 2$ ohm and $R_4 = 3$ ohm, then

$$\frac{I_3}{I_4} = \frac{3}{2}$$
$$I_3 + I_4 = I = 5a,$$

where

$$I_3 = 3a.$$

Then from equality (*)

$$I_5 = I_1 - I_3 = 1a.$$

If $R_3 = 3$ ohm and $R_4 = 2$ ohm, then

$$I_3 = 2a,$$
$$I_5 = 2a.$$

Thus, there are two possible values ​​for the current flowing through the amplifier $A_2$.

F410 *). In an electron vacuum device, pure tungsten camode is placed in a large flask containing residual oxygen at a pressure of $p = 10^{-7}$ atom and a temperature of $T = 300^\circ\text{K}$. Assuming that every molecule that gets on the chamber touches it, estimate the time of formation of the monolecular layer. The molecules can be considered balls with a diameter of $d \approx 3 \cdot 10^{-8}$ cm.

Let us find the average number of molecules $z$ that settle on a unit area of ​​the cathode surface in a time of $t = 1$ sec. To evaluate the use of the proposed gas model, according to which all molecules in the state move in those mutually perpendicular directions (in both directions in each direction) with a root mean square speed $\bar{v}$. Let the average number of molecules per unit volume (concentration of molecules) be $n$. Of the total number of molecules, $1/6$ part moves towards the cathode. A unit area of ​​the cathode in time $t$ makes all the molecules appear in a column of unit section and height $\bar{v}$. From here

$$z = \frac{n}{6} \bar{v}.$$

A more accurate expression, taking into account the movement of molecules in all possible directions and with different absolute velocities, has the form $z = \frac{n}{4} \bar{v}$. This difference in the numerical coefficient is insignificant, since according to the conditions of the problem we must give only an approximate estimate of the result in order of magnitude.

Mean square speed of molecules

$$\bar{v} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$$

where $k = 1,38 \cdot 10^{-23}$ J/deg is Boltzmann’s constant, $T = 300^\circ\text{K}$ is the absolute temperature of the gas, $m = \frac{\mu}{N_A} = 5,3 \cdot 10^{-26}$ ke is the mass of oxygen molecules. The concentration of molecules can be expressed in terms of gas pressure and temperature:

$$n = \frac{p}{kT}.$$

Substituting the numerical values ​​into the expression for $\bar{v}$ and $n$ gives $\bar{v} \approx 5 \cdot 10^2$ m/sec, $n \approx 2,5 \cdot 10^{18}$ m$^{-3}$.
