Let's make a table of all the possible outcomes when rolling the red and blue dice. Since any number of points from 1 to 6 on the red die can be combined with any number of points on the blue die, we get table 4.

Any of the 36 combinations of points on the red and blue dice are equally likely to be any other combination, but different amounts of points can be obtained in a different number of ways. Thus, the sum of points 3 (for D'Artagne) is obtained in two ways: 1 + 2 and 2 + 1, and the sum of 2 (for the Englishman) is obtained in only one way (out of 36!): 1 + 1. And there was something to be surprised about!

Probability
You see that rolling 2 and 6 on one dice are equally likely, but rolling 2 and 7 on two dice are not equally likely. In such cases, the sum 7 is said to be favored by six outcomes out of a total of 36 equally likely outcomes, while the sum 2 is favored by only one outcome.

And in general, counting the number of favorable outcomes out of their total number is the most important action for determining the probability of an event. In this case, notations are also used.
