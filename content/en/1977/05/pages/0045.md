Pi
and probability theory

Do you want to experimentally find the number $n$? Try this, all you need is a box of matches and a sheet of paper.

On a sheet of paper, draw a grid of parallel lines at a distance from each other equal to the length of the match. Now throw matches randomly onto a piece of paper (as on the first page of the cover of this issue of the magazine) and count the number of matches crossing the lines of the grid (you can throw one match many times). By dividing twice the total number of matches by the resulting number, you will find the number $n$ (the more matches you throw, the more accurately you will find the number $n$).

This can be proven using probability theory. If a match of length 1 fell at an angle $\phi$ to the grid lines, then its “effective length” is $\sin \phi$. Let some match cross the grid line, then its lower end is at a distance of no more than $\sin \phi$ from the grid line passing above it (see figure). Take this distance to be in the range from 0 to 1. According to the rules of probability theory, this match crosses the grid line with probability $\sin \phi$.

Now, to find the probability that a randomly fallen match will cross the line of the grid, it is necessary to reduce the resulting probability $\sin \phi$ over all $\phi$ ($0 \leq \phi \leq \pi/2$), since all values ​​of the angles $\phi$ are equally probable, that is, find the value of the expression

$$\frac{\pi}{2} \sin \phi d\phi$$

and divide it by $\pi/2$.

Tenth graders will quickly find that the integral is equal to 1, that is, just the $\frac{2}{\pi}$ part of the matches must intersect the lines of the grid.

For the first time such an experiment was carried out with the game (throwing it many times) by the French naturalist J. L. L. Buffon (1707-1788), among whom this experiment is called “Buffon’s needle.” At the end of the 19th century, many mathematicians threw the needle 3-5 thousand times and obtained a value of the order of 3.15 for $\pi$. I wonder what value $\pi$ gets you from one box of matches?
