An external force begins to act on it - the force $\vec{F}$ of the boat rubbing against the water (the “—” sign before $\vec{F}$ means that the direction of this force is opposite to the direction $\vec{v}_{\pi0}$). The friction force will reduce the speed of the boat, and by the time the stone hits the shield (in time $t$ after the throw), the speed of the boat will become $\vec{v}_{\pi t} < \vec{v}_{\pi0}$, so by this moment the absolute value of the boat’s momentum will decrease:
$$M |\vec{v}_{\pi t}| < M |\vec{v}_{\pi0}| = m |\vec{v}_{\kappa}|.$$
This means that the total impulse of the entire system immediately after the stone hits the shield will be different from zero and directed in the direction of the movement of the stone. The absolute value $\vec{u}$ of all systems (its center of mass) after the impact can be determined from the correspondence (see Fig. 1)
$$(M + m) |\vec{u}| = m |\vec{v}_{\kappa}| - M |\vec{v}_{\pi t}|,$$
or (since $M |\vec{v}_{\pi t}| = M |\vec{v}_{\pi0}| - |\vec{F}| t = m |\vec{v}_{\kappa}| - |\vec{F}| t)$
$$(M + m) |\vec{u}| = |\vec{F}| t.$$
And knowing the values ​​$\vec{u}$ and $\vec{F}$, it is not difficult to find the displacement of the center of mass of the system at any moment in time.

So, the presence of the friction force of the boat on the water leads to the following features:

**Fig. 1.**

1) the speed of the boat after the stone hits the shield will be directed in the direction of throwing;
2) the greater the force, the faster the “recoil” speed of the boat kills, the less $\vec{v}_{\pi t}$, the greater, therefore, the final speed of the entire system.
Thus, by throwing stones from the stern to the bow of the boat, you can move without using oars. After all the stones from the stern have been thrown to the bow, you need to very slowly drag them back. Then everything can be repeated all over again.
If there are no stones in the boat and the oars are lost, then the situation is still not hopeless. The role of a stone can play from the stern to the bow and slowly returns back.
If the traveler is an inventor, then he will entrust the mechanism with making wild buckles from the stern to the boat. It is enough to install an eccentric on the boat, which moves in different directions at different speeds.
The method of movement described above is not original. For many years now, articles about such self-propelled carts have been appearing in magazines. And in recent years, large models of such carriages have been created.

**Option three, in which everything is taken into account**

Let us now take into account both the force $\vec{F}$ of the friction of the boat on the water, and the force $\vec{F}$ of the friction of the stone on the water.

After we have dealt with the second option, we can immediately write down what the total impulse of the system will be equal to after the stone hits the shield:
$$(M + m) \vec{u} = \vec{F} t + \vec{F} t.$$
