2. According to the first law of thermodynamics, the amount of heat $Q$ that must be imparted to the gas during the manufacture of the expansion is equal to the work $A$ of the gas. Since the relative increase in volume is small, we can assume that the pressure during expansion decreases according to a linear law. Then

$$A = p_{\text{cp}} \Delta V = \frac{1}{2}(p_1 + p_2) \Delta V.$$

From the Boyle-Mariotte law

$$p_2 = \frac{p_1 V_1}{V_1 + \Delta V} = p_1 \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\Delta V}{V_1}} \approx p_1 \left(1 - \frac{\Delta V}{V_1}\right).$$

Hence,

$$Q = A \approx p_1 \Delta V \left(1 - \frac{\Delta V}{2V_1}\right) = \frac{m}{\mu} RT \frac{\Delta V}{V_1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta V}{V_1}\right) \approx 4,88 \text{ дж.}$$

3. In steady state, $\vec{F}_M$, acting on a free charge in the magnetic field, is compensated by the power plant by force $\vec{F}_3$:

$$|\vec{F}_M| = |\vec{F}_3|, \text{ или } q|\vec{v}||\vec{B}| = q \frac{U}{d}.$$

From here

$$|\vec{v}| = \frac{U}{|\vec{B}| d} = 2 \text{ m/сек.}$$

4. The part of the text located under the area AD of the prism base (Fig. 2) will not be visible through face BC. From triangle ACD by the sine theorem $\frac{l}{\sin(90^\circ + \beta)} = \frac{a}{\sin \gamma}$, where $a = (1 - \alpha) \sqrt{2}l$, $\sin \beta = \frac{1}{n}$, $\gamma = 45^\circ - \beta$. From here we find the refractive index $n$:

$$n = \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{(2\alpha - 1)^2}} \approx 1,6.$$

Ticket 2

1. In the reference frame associated with the center of mass of the colliding atoms, the total momentum is zero:

$$m_\alpha \left( |\vec{v}_\alpha| - |\vec{v}| \right) + m_\gamma \left( |\vec{v}_\gamma| - |\vec{v}| \right) = 0,$$

where $m_\alpha = 3m_\gamma$ and $|\vec{v}_\alpha| = 2|\vec{v}_\gamma|$. Hence the speed of the center of mass

$$|\vec{v}| = \frac{m_\alpha |\vec{v}_\alpha| + m_\gamma |\vec{v}_\gamma|}{m_\alpha + m_\gamma} = \frac{5}{8} |\vec{v}_\alpha| = 625 \text{ m/сек}.$$

2. After each collision with a moving piston, the speed of the gas molecules decreases by $2|\vec{v}|$. How many collisions are possible? Let us denote by $t$ the time between the first and second collisions, then

$$t = \frac{2H - x}{|\vec{v}|} = \frac{2H - x}{|\vec{v}| - 2|\vec{v}|},$$

where $x = |\vec{u}| t$ is the corresponding piston change. From here

$$x = \frac{2|\vec{u}|}{|\vec{v}| - 3|\vec{u}|} H = \frac{2}{5} H < \frac{3}{5} H,$$

therefore, some molecules will undergo two collisions, and their velocities after the second collision are $\|\vec{v}_2\| \geq |\vec{v}| - 4|\vec{u}| = 100 \text{ m/сек}$. Similarly, it can be shown that a third collision is impossible. Therefore, finally

$$100 \text{ m/сек} \leq |\vec{v}_2| \leq 200 \text{ m/сек}.$$

3. The capacitor plates interact with the electric field created by the charged plate. The voltage of this field is $|\vec{E}| = \frac{Q}{2\varepsilon_0 S}$. The charge of the capacitor $q$ creates its own electric field $|\vec{E}_1| = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0 S} = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{d - d_1}$, from where $q = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{\varepsilon_0 S} / d - d_1$.
