M396, M397, M400—M402*); F407—F412

M396. A triangle whose sides are all larger than 1 cm is called “large.” Given a regular triangle ABC with a side of 5 cm. Prove that: a) 1000 “large” triangles can be cut from triangle ABC; b) triangle ABC can be cut into 1000 “large” triangles; c) triangle ABC can be cut so that any two triangles either do not have common lobes, or have a common vertex, or have common sides; e) do points b) and c) for a regular triangle with a side of 3 cm.

We will solve points b) and c) of the problem for a triangle with a side of 3 cm (Fig. 1). Let K and L be the points dividing sides AC into three equal parts, P the midpoint of side BC. Let us restore from points K and L the perediculars KN and LM to side AC; let $N_1 = [AP] \cap [KN)$, $M_1 = [CN_1] \cap [LM)$, $N_2 = [AM_1] \cap [KN)$, $M_2 = [CN_2] \cap [LM)$, etc. We obtain a partition of the triangle into triangles ABP, CPN$_1$, AN$_1$M$_1$, CM$_1$N$_2$, AN$_2$M$_2$, etc. All these triangles are the “large” sides of each of them are more than 1 cm (|BP| = |CP| = 1.5 cm, and the remaining sides are more than 1 cm, since their projections on AC are not less than 1 cm). It is clear from the solution that together with the number 1000 one could write any other number, however large.
