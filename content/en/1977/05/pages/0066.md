Let us assume that at $\vec{F}_1 = \vec{F}_2$ the feed layer is deviated from the horizontal position (Fig. 5). Then
$$p_1 = l_1 \cos \alpha + l_2 \sin (\alpha + \Delta \alpha),$$
$$p_2 = l_1 \cos \alpha + l_2 \sin (\alpha - \Delta \alpha).$$
Obviously $0 \leq \alpha - \Delta \alpha < \alpha + \Delta \alpha < \pi/2$, so $\sin (\alpha - \Delta \alpha) < \sin (\alpha + \Delta \alpha)$. Consequently, $p_1 > p_2$ and the resulting moment tends to rotate the stern into a horizontal position.

"Machine graphics"
(see «Kvant» No. 4, p. 57)
On figure 1 (in the task) there are concentric circles drawn in perspective from a “point of view” very close to them.
As you know, a circle in perspective is depicted as an ellipse. Figure serves as a filler that the center of the circle does not coincide with the center of the depicted ellipse. “In nature” such a figure can be seen on a stump - “annual rings” form like concentric circles.

Fig. 7.

On figure 2 there are also several concentric circles, around the outer one of which a square is described. They are located in the “frontal” (in front of the viewer) plane, but drawn in a “cylindrical perspective”, and with a very close “point of view”. The well-known multi-frame camera “Horizon” shoots in this projection. With this camera, if you come almost close to, for example, a long railway frame, you can photograph it entirely, but at the same time it will turn out to be “curved,” just as the extreme lines in our drawing are curved, in reality the sides of a square.
