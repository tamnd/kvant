If the point $X$ lies on the boundary of $F$ and does not belong to $F_2$, then $X$ contributes 1 to $B(F_1)$ and to $B(F)$. If point $X$ lies on the boundary of $M$, then it contributes 1 to $B(F_1)$, $B(F_2)$, $B(F)$ and $B(M)$. If the point $X$ lies inside $M$, then it contributes 2 to $B(M)$ and contributes 1 to $B(F_1)$ and $B(F_2)$. If $X$ does not belong to either $M$ or the boundary $F$, then it does not contribute to any of the terms of formula (*). Thus, any point $X$ contributes equally to both parts (*), i.e. these parts are equal.

Problem 6. Plan of proof of the spatial main theorem.

Throughout the following we omit the word “integer” and call the triangular primuda a terahe- dron.

Taking sequentially admissible transformations, we can translate:

1) any point at $X_1$;
2) any segment $I$, for which $N(I) = 2$, in $X_2$;
3) any triangle $F$, for which $N(F) = 3$, in $X_3$;
4) any triangle $F$, for which $N(F) = 4$ and $V(F) = 1/6$, in $X_4$;
5) any triangle $F$, for which $N(F) = 4$, into a triangle $T_n$ (see «Kvant», no. 4, Fig. 1 on p. 13), where $n = 6V(F)$.

From 1)–4) and the definition of a convenient function it follows that $h$ vanishes on all functions of zero volume (since $h(X_1) = h(X_2) = h(X_3) = 0$ - see the proof of the main theorem for plane figures) and on any terrahedron $F$ for which $N(F) = 4$ and $V(F) = 1/6$ (since $h(X_4) = 0$).

Using property A) we obtain from 4) that $h$ vanishes on any unit cube, and by induction on any straight polygonal paralleliped $\Pi_n = \{(x; y; z) : 0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1, 0 \leqslant y \leqslant 1, 0 \leqslant z \leqslant n\}$.

The parallelepiped $\Pi_n$ can be divided into a terahedra $T_n$ and a certain number of terahedrons $F_i$, for which $N(F_i) = 4$ and $V(F_i) = 1/6$. Since $h(\Pi_n) = 0$, $h(F_i) = 0$, and $h$ vanish at all possible intersections of $F_i$ with each other and with $\Pi_n$ (these intersections are either a point, a line segment, or a triangle), we obtain from A) that $h$ vanishes at $T_n$ as well. From 5) and the definition of a convenient function it now follows that $h$ vanishes on any terahedra $F$ that has $N(F) = 4$. Any terahedra for which $N(F) > 4$ can be divided into two, at or four terahedrons with the number of integer points less than $N(F)$. By induction we find that $h(F) = 0$ for any terahedra $F$. Dividing any polyboundary into terahedrons, we obtain from A) the statement of the theorem.
