MIPT Olympids

Various mathematical competitions in the history of mathematics have been known for a long time. History has brought down to us information about mathematical show jumping conducted back in the 13th century.

The history of school physics and mathematics Olympids starting in 1894, when the first mathematical Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in Hungary, was held in Leningrad in the spring of 1934. The initiators of this Olympiad were Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences B. N. Delaunay (Chairman of the Olympiad Organizing Committee) and Professor V. A. Tartakovsky.

On the initiative of the Moscow Mathematical Society, in the fall of 1935, the first Moscow Mathematical Olympid for schoolchildren was held. Moscow mathematicians greeted her with great enthusiasm. The organizing committee included leading Moscow mathematicians.

Until the Great Patriotic War, mathematical Olympids were held in Moscow every day and very soon gained universal recognition. During the war in Moscow, mathematics Olympids were held in Ashgabat and Kazan. After the Patriotic War, holding mathematical Olympiads became a tradition in many cities of the Soviet Union.

Following the mathematicians, physicists, chemists, astronomers, geographers, biologists, and then linguistics began to conduct their Olympids.

The first Olympids in physics were held by Moscow University back in 1938. Since then, these Olympids have become a tradition for Moscow schoolchildren.

Subsequently, such Olympids began to be held by the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT) and other universities in Moscow. Moskvychny’s initiative was picked up in other cities of the country.

In February 1962, the first “big” physics and mathematics Olympid for schoolchildren took place, conducted on the initiative of MIPT. Over 6,500 schoolchildren from 58 cities of the Soviet Union took part in it. It was held in one round during the winter holidays.
