By this time, together with the silver industry, a method was invented to coat the stack with aluminum by evaporation in a high vacuum. It turned out that the aluminum layer is stronger than silver and reflects light very well, especially in its short part. Until very recently it was the largest reflector in the world.

In the Soviet Union before the war, the largest reflector had a mirror with a diameter of 1 m; it was made in England and was located at the Simeiz Observatory in Crimea. With him, Academician G. A. Shain and his colleagues carried out a number of interesting spectro-optical studies on the motion of stars and the composition of stellar atmospheres. During the war this instrument was lost. Previously, our optical-mechanical industry did not have experience in the manufacture of large astronomical instruments (especially in polishing and polishing mirrors). However, the Leningrad plant mastered this difficult task and made several reflectors of 1, 1.25 and 1.5 m, and in 1960, at the new Crimean Astrophysical Observatory of the USSR Academy of Sciences, the installation of a 2.6 m reflector was completed, which now remains one of the largest in Europe.

Successful experience allowed us to set an even more difficult task. On the initiative of the largest Soviet optician D. D. Maksutov, a plan was developed to build a reflector with a one-meter mirror (see the introductory note of the article). At the same time, chief designer B.K. Ioannisini did not copy American designs, but took a new, original path. The development of electronic computers made it possible to move away from a conventional equatorial mount and use a simpler one - a horizontal one, in which one axis is directed vertically and the one perpendicular to it is horizontal. This greatly simplified the mechanical part of the structure and the mounting of the mirror with its unloading, since the mirror, depending on the position of the observed object, leans only in one direction and does not experience lateral tilts. This complicated the addition of the phone by closing the celestial sphere: instead of one uniform movement around the polar axis, now you need to strictly rotate the phone at a variable speed around another, the xegete part with the photoplasticity of the arrival is also unevenly rotated. These complex movements can be carried out with the help of electronic machines, which list the coordinates of the observed point in the sky, determine the rate of their changes and give the appropriate commands for the movement of the motoram (the moving part of the telefone has a mass of almost 700 tons).

The main telephoto mirror, 6 m in diameter, was cast and processed at a glass factory near Moscow, for which two special workshops were built there. After being cast from a special grade of low-expansion glass, the red-hot mass was slowly cooled by electronic heating to avoid the development of harmful stresses that would normally cause a glass dish to rupture. This “annealing” process usually lasts for at least a year, only after which it is possible to find out whether the casting has been removed or not.

The huge and complex installation of the telephoto cameras was carried out at the optical-mechanical plant in Leningrad with the participation of a number of other Leningrad heavy engineering plants. The result is an instrument that is comparable in size and weight to the largest metal structures, and in accuracy and subtlety is not inferior to technical microscopes.

The ground mirror has a focal length of 24 m; At this distance in front of the mirror, an image of the observed object is formed.
