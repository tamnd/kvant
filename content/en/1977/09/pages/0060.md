In conclusion, we would like to present a few problems from this book:

**Mathematics**

1. Given an angle on a plane. Find the set of circles circumscribed around the circle of weights of impoverished triangles, whose vertices at the base lie on opposite sides of this angle, and the third vertex lies at a given point A on its bisector.

2. Diagonals are drawn in a convex quadrilateral ABCD. Prove that a quadrilateral with vertices at the intersection points of the medians of triangles ABC, ABD, ACD and BCD is similar to the given one.

3. At the base of a quadrangular straight line there is a rhombus with an angle of 60° at vertex A. The lateral edge emerging from vertex A is equal to the sides of the rhombus. Prove that the remaining lateral edges can be used to form a right triangle.

4. Two pieces are placed on a chessboard of 8x8 cells. Two players take turns making moves for each of their pieces. The first player's piece can only move one square vertically or horizontally for each sede. The second chip is one cell vertically or horizontally, only one cell diagonally. The one who places his fikuku on the parterre fikuku wins. Who wins when played correctly?

5. n players play the following game: there are (2n + 1) checkers, of which 2k are black, the rest are white. The players sit around the table and each of them receives two checkers. By drawing lots, one of the players takes the remaining checker. If he has three checkers of the same color, then he wins, and the game ends; if not, then he keeps the checkers of the same color for himself, and gives the third to his neighbor. If he has three checkers of the same color, then he wins, if not, then he does the same as the first one, etc.

Prove that for k not equal to n, the game will end in a finite number of checker transfers (moves). Find the maximum number of moves in this game.

**Physics**

6. A man walks along the railway shawls. The maximum length of its step is 0.8 m. The sleepers are laid so that there are exactly 200 sleepers on any hundred-meter section. The distance between chawls is not less than 0.3 m and not more than 0.6 m and can vary within these limits from chalet to chalet. With each laying of shawls on 1 km of the path, and with each minimum?

7. The two autonomous units have the same power. The maximum speed of the first is v₁ km/h, the second is v₂ km/h. What maximum speed can the two cars reach if one takes the other in tow?

8. Estimate the minimum permissible daily productivity for a cryta with mass M and radius R.

9. If the internal combustion engine is not properly adjusted, sometimes instead of the relatively slow combustion of the fuel mixture, so-called “detonation” occurs, when the mixture burns quickly with an explosion. Why does the efficiency drop? movements?

10. The spacecraft cabin has a high-rise cave in gravity-free conditions. A well-conducting heat stick ball is heated in a furnace by high-frequency currents and begins to float. Develop a method for experimentally determining the time of complete melting of the ball. For simplicity, assume that the body exchange between the sphere and the environment is proportional to the difference in their temperatures. The cosmonaut-researcher has the opportunity to observe the melting visually, can change the power supplied to the ball and use the instruments and reference books he needs.

11. Estimate what the size of metal dust grains in the Solar System should be so that solar light pressure would impart to them a speed directed away from the Sun? The Sun's luminosity L = 4.10³³ erg/s, its mass M = 2.10³³ g, gravitational constant γ = 6.67.10⁻⁸ dyn.cm²/g².
