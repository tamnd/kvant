For the device of chromatic aberration, the Englishman Gregory in 1663 proposed using as an object not a lens, but a wave mirror, which was free from chromatism, since, as Newton put it, “the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection for all colors.” Moreover, in order to avoid spherical aberration, the reflecting surface of the mirror must have the shape of a parabolide of rotation, which coincides with one point of rays parallel to the axis of the parabolide. Quite soon they learned how to grind a mirror into the desired shape. However, for a long time they did not know how to cover the surface of glass with a light-reflecting layer of silver, and mirrors were made from a special alloy of copper and tin, which is highly polished and has sufficient reflectivity. It was proposed to use your own lens as an eyepiece.

Gregory failed to make his proposed mirror telexan - a reflector, which received its name from the Latin reflecto - bent backwards. It was made by Newton in 1668, but it was not until 1722 that John Hadley in England and then Short in Edinburgh began making parabolic reflectors that were as good as the largest refractors of the time.

Since then, a debate has begun between two fundamentally different telex designs - between refractors and reflectors. Refractors were more difficult to make: firstly, it was necessary to grind four surfaces of two lenses and, secondly, higher demands were placed on the material - optically homogeneous glass.
