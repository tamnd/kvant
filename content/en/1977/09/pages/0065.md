It’s an amazing thing: the less productive Petya should be played in every game, although on average he has already managed to score only one goal, not one and a half! But opponents should also be on alert and place a high-speed defender more often than before: their optimal strategy is to play Alik in one case out of three ($q_1 = 1/3, q_2 = 2/3$; however, any strategy $0 \leq q_1 \leq 1/3, q_2 = 1-q_1$ will give them the same result, for example, $q_1 = 0, q_2 = 1$ - Alik sits on the bench).

3. Russian immediately disappears, since in any case it gives no more points than either of the other two strategies. Here there is a dominance of two strategies over the third. The solution gives: physics - $1/3$, algebra - $2/3$. How to implement such a ridiculous strategy? Petya must throw a needle die, and if a 3 or 6 comes up, he must teach physics, in other cases - algebra. All this, of course, is true if the teacher really grows closer to Petya, in which case they should give a test in algebra with probability $3/4$, and with probability $1/4$ in physics. If the teacher does not reprimand Petya (a much more reasonable proposal), then it would be more correct to apply the Bayesian approach and teach algebra.
