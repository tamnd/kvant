We will prove this fact for one simple case - when the lines $a_1$ and $a_2$ are perpendicular, and the pairs of lines $SA, a_1$ and $TA, a_2$ are parallel. Let's place the origin of coordinates at point $O=A$, direct the $Ox$ axis along $AS$, and direct the $Oy$ axis along $AT$ (Fig. 3). Let us denote the coordinates of points $S$ and $T$, respectively, by $(s; 0), (0; t)$. Let the equation of lines $a_1$ and $a_2$ be of the form $y=r_2$, $x=r_1$. If point $M$ lies on the curve defined by lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$, then points $M(x; y), A_1(x_1; r_2)$ and $S(s; 0)$ must lie on the same line. Points $M(x; y), A_2(r_1; y_2)$ and $T(0; t)$ must lie on another line, and points $A_1(x_1; r_2), A_2(r_1; y_2)$ and $A(0; 0)$ must lie on a third line (Fig. 3).

The points $M, S$ and $A_1$ lie on the same line if the slopes of the lines $MS$ and $A_1S$ are equal (see “Algebra 6”, paragraph 21 and “Algebra and the beginnings of analysis 9”, paragraph 52):

$$\frac{r_2}{x_1-s} = \frac{y}{x-s_r}.$$

Similar equalities are obtained for the point tracks $A, A_1, A_2$ and $M, A_2, T$:

$$\frac{y_2}{r_1} = \frac{r_2}{x_1},$$

$$\frac{y_2-t}{r_1} = \frac{y-t}{x}.$$

Eliminating $x_1$ and $y_2$ from these equalities, we obtain an equation that must satisfy $x, y$:

$$(r_2x+sy-sr_2)(tx+r_1y-r_1t)-r_1r_2xy=0$$

$(s, t, r_1, r_2$ we have CONSTANTS). Opening the brackets and bringing similar terms, we see that this equation is of the second degree with respect to $x, y$:

$$tr_2x^2+tsxy+sr_1y^2-sr_1(t+r_2)y-tr_2(r_1+s)x+tsr_1r_2=0.$$

Thus, all points of the constructed curve lie on the second-order curve.

So, the curve constructed by the Grassmann method using straight lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$ turns out to be a second-order curve. The converse statement is also true: for every curve $m$ of the second order there are lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$ such that Grassmann’s method from them produces precisely the curve $m$.
