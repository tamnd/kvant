Construction of algebraic curves using hinge mechanisms

This note was born from a report made by a Moscow schoolboy, 9th grade student Vadim Knizhnik at a school conference. It tells how, using mechanisms consisting of slotted strips and screws, you can build graphs of various functions.

Mechanisms

We will call a hinge mechanism a system of strips with slots and holes connected by screws. In this case, the slots and holes are located strictly along the axis of the bar. When connected with a screw, strips with holes can only be rotated around the screw, and strips with slots can be rotated and shifted (Fig. 1; connection designations are shown in figure).

These mechanisms work like this. Two perpendicular strips are fixed on the plane, forming a rectangular coordinate system $Oxy$. Mechanisms are attached to these screw bars, which have one “input” - a hole moving along the $Ox$ axis, and one “output” - a hole that is always located on the same vertical as the input, but at a height depending on the position of the input. If you insert a pencil into the “output” and draw the “input” along the $Ox$ axis, then the pencil will draw a curve - a graph of some.

For further purposes, it is useful to maintain another “corner” block, consisting of three planks (with holes) fastened with vintimi. This block, when screwed with the same screws to any two slats with slots, allows them only to move, maintaining a constant angle between themselves (designated at figure - angle).
