Brust's interference experiment

Experimental setup

In 1817, the English physicist, member of the Korpel Society of London D. Brewster (1781-1868) discovered that if two flat-parallel glass plates of the same thickness are placed almost parallel to each other, then, looking through them, one can observe an interference pattern.

The optical scheme of Brust's experiment is depicted in figure 1. Light falling from an accumulation lamp S onto a sheet of white paper B is scattered on it and, passing through plane-parallel plates P₁ and P₂, enters the eye Γ observed. The interference pattern is visible on the black background of the opaque screen E.

To repeat Brust's experiment, you must first select a suitable glass: the plates P₁ and P₂ must be plane-parallel and installed parallel to each other. Glass with a thickness of about 7 mm, which is often used to make mirrors and shop windows, is the most affordable and is often fully suitable for conducting subtle interference experiments. Having taken out a piece of such glass, place it in front of your eye so that some dark object with clear boundaries is reflected in it against a light background (for example, the frame of a window frame is reflected against the background of the daytime sky). Focus (accommodate) your eye on the surface of the glass so that the border of the image of the dark object is visible. If, at the same time, the smooth edges of the object are visible slightly wavy or “blurred” in some places, then it means that the surfaces of the glass are not smooth and such glass is not suitable for experiments. It is usually possible to select glass whose surfaces are so smooth that the boundary between the image of a dark object and a light background appears completely sharp. This is the glass that should be used in experiments.

From the displayed glass, use a craft or diamond glass cutter to cut a piece approximately 80 × 70 mm in size and cut it in half, so that you get two plates P₁ and P₂ measuring 40 × 70 mm. These plates, very accurately, have the same thickness near the cut line with high precision. Also prepare a third glass plate C of the same thickness as the first two, measuring 30 × 40 mm.

To avoid injury from the sharp edges of the plates when working, sand them (wet them with water and work them on an abrasive block or wheel). Be careful not to scratch the surfaces of the records. Wash the treated glass plates with water, wipe dry with a clean soft cloth and remove any remaining dust and lint from their surfaces.
