COORDINATES

I thought each of you had to ask passersby to ask passersby to explain where this or that building or institution is located. The responder can be divided into three categories. The first ones wave their hand in a certain direction and say “there,” sometimes adding “about twenty minutes away.” The second say something like this: “Pass the bakery, there will be a turn to the left, but you don’t turn there, but go straight past the cafe, and when you pass the bookstore, turn left, walk past the school, then there will be a yellow two-story house with columns, and your institution will be in the direction of it.” Still others answer like this: “Go straight to Chernyshevsky Street, turn left along it and the second house from the intersection with Dobrolyubova Street on the right side is the one you are looking for.”

And so, following the advice, you marvel in the indicated direction. If there is any doubt about the correctness of the chosen path, he turns to passers-by and eventually finds the right house. As they say, “language will take you to Kyiv.”

What should seafarers do? Who can help them find their way on the high seas? How to tell others where the island they discovered is located?

And 200 years before ours, the Greek scientist Hipparchus offers well-known geographical coordinates: shiromu and dolama. Using these two numbers, you can accurately determine the position of an island, village, mountain or well in the desert and plot them on a map or globe. Having learned to determine the latitude and longitude of a ship’s location on the open sea, sailors were able to choose the direction they needed without any trouble.

Recall that eastern longitude and northern latitude are indicated by numbers with a plus sign, and western longitude and southern latitude are indicated by numbers with a minus sign. Thus, a pair of numbers with signs will uniquely determine a point on the globe. For example, the pair +70°, +60° defines a point in the center of Vaygach Island, located in the Kara Sea.
