In the 14th century, the French mathematician N. Orsem introduced, by analogy with geographical coordinates, coordinates on a plane. He proposed covering the plane with a rectangular grid and calling what we now call *abscissa* and *ordine* latitude and longitude.
This innovation turned out to be extremely successful. On its basis, the coordinate method would emerge, linking geometry with algebra. The main task in creating this method belongs to the great French mathematician Rene Descartes. Among them, such a coordinate system is called Cartesian. Many ways of indicating a place are based on this system. For example, on a cinema ticket there are two numbers: row and seat - they can be considered as the coordinates of your place in the theater. Similar coordinates are adopted in chess, however, a letter is taken along with one of the numbers: vertical rows of cells are designated by letters of the Latin alphabet, and horizontal rows by numbers.
Thus, each square of the chessboard is assigned a pair of letters and numbers, and chess players are able to record their games.
The same “chess” principle is now being applied to the city plans. The city plan is divided into squares, numbered using letters and numbers, and on the reverse side all the streets depicted are listed in alphabetical order and indicate in which square they are located.
There are other coordinate systems on the plane. Let's remember the passage, which shows the direction with his hand and tells how long it takes to walk. The *polar coordinate system* is based on this principle. To enter it, select a starting point called *pole* (the system is therefore called “polar”); from this point a ray is drawn, called the *polar axis*. To determine the coordinates of a point on a plane, it is connected by a segment to a pole and the length of this segment and the angle between it and the polar axis are calculated.
Sometimes the coordinates appear in veiled form. For example, in the assignment for Moscow University on the Lenin Hills, the rooms are numbered so that by the audience number you can find out the floor on which it is located. To do this, the number must be divided into two parts: the number of hundreds and the rest. The number of hundreds is the floor. For example, room 1503 is on the 15th floor, and room 817 is on the 8th floor.
