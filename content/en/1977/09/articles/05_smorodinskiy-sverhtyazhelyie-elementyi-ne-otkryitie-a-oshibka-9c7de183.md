We will prove this fact for one simple case - when the lines $a_1$ and $a_2$ are perpendicular, and the pairs of lines $SA, a_1$ and $TA, a_2$ are parallel. Let's place the origin of coordinates at point $O=A$, direct the $Ox$ axis along $AS$, and direct the $Oy$ axis along $AT$ (Fig. 3). Let us denote the coordinates of points $S$ and $T$, respectively, by $(s; 0), (0; t)$. Let the equation of lines $a_1$ and $a_2$ be of the form $y=r_2$, $x=r_1$. If point $M$ lies on the curve defined by lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$, then points $M(x; y), A_1(x_1; r_2)$ and $S(s; 0)$ must lie on the same line. Points $M(x; y), A_2(r_1; y_2)$ and $T(0; t)$ must lie on another line, and points $A_1(x_1; r_2), A_2(r_1; y_2)$ and $A(0; 0)$ must lie on a third line (Fig. 3).

The points $M, S$ and $A_1$ lie on the same line if the slopes of the lines $MS$ and $A_1S$ are equal (see “Algebra 6”, paragraph 21 and “Algebra and the beginnings of analysis 9”, paragraph 52):

$$\frac{r_2}{x_1-s} = \frac{y}{x-s_r}.$$

Similar equalities are obtained for the point tracks $A, A_1, A_2$ and $M, A_2, T$:

$$\frac{y_2}{r_1} = \frac{r_2}{x_1},$$

$$\frac{y_2-t}{r_1} = \frac{y-t}{x}.$$

Eliminating $x_1$ and $y_2$ from these equalities, we obtain an equation that must satisfy $x, y$:

$$(r_2x+sy-sr_2)(tx+r_1y-r_1t)-r_1r_2xy=0$$

Thus, all points of the constructed curve lie on the second-order curve.

So, the curve constructed by the Grassmann method using straight lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$ turns out to be a second-order curve. The converse statement is also true: for every curve $m$ of the second order there are lines $a_1, a_2$ and points $A, S, T$ such that Grassmann’s method from them produces precisely the curve $m$.

Here is a method for finding such lines and points along the curve $m$. Let us take the curve $m$ of the second order (Fig. 4).

Let us fix three arbitrary points $S, T, L$ on this curve. Through the point $L$ we draw arbitrary straight lines $a_1, a_2$; let them intersect $m$ at points $P$ and $Q$, and let lines $TP$ and $SQ$ intersect at point $A$. Then the points $A, S, T$ and straight lines $a_1, a_2$ define “according to Grassmann” exactly the curve $m$.

The method is simple, but it is not easy to prove that it is exactly the curve $m$. Here we will only outline the course of the proof.

First of all, it is necessary to show that on the curve defined by the lines $a_1, a_2$ both the points $A, S, T$ and the lines $a_1, a_2$ define “according to Grassmann” precisely the curve $m$.

The same points also lie on the original curve $m$, that is, the “Grassmann curve” we constructed has already five common points with the curve $m$. And then we need to show that five points define at most one second-order curve. The easiest way to do this is with an equation.

The equation of the second order curve has the form

$$Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0.$$

All three coefficients $A, B, C$ cannot simultaneously equal zero, since then this equation would define a first-order curve—a straight line. Let's assume that $C \neq 0$. Then the left side of this equation can be divided by the coefficient $C$, resulting in the equation

$$A_1x^2 + B_1xy + y^2 + D_1x + E_1y + F_1 = 0,$$

where $A_1 = A/C, B_1 = B/C$, etc.

If we substitute the coordinates of the point $S, T, L, P, Q$ into the resulting equation together with $x, y$, we obtain a system of five linear equations for the coefficients $A_1, B_1, D_1, E_1, F_1$. And also the system, provided that no three points from $S, T, L, P, Q$ lie on the same line, has a unique solution.
