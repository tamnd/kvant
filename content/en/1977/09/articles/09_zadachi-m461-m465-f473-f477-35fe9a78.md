Tasks

M461—M465; F473—F477

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. More difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than November 1, 1977 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant».
After the address on the envelope, write the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example, “M456, M457” or “F468”. Solutions to problems in each of the subjects (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, please send in separate envelopes. Please also send assignments from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (mark on the envelope: “Problem «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”). After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. In this issue, “Problem «Kvant» is composed mainly of problems offered at the last All-Union Olympiad.

M461. On the table there are scales and $n$ weights of various masses. The grips are placed on the scales one by one (at each step, any weight is taken from the table and added to one or the other scale).

a) Prove that the grip can be placed in such an order that first the left cup is moved, then the right one, then the left one again, the right one again, and so on.

This sequence of weighing results is comparable to a word from the letters $L$ and $R$: LRLRLR... Here the letter $L$ means to translate the left cup, and the letter $R$ means to translate the right cup.

6)* Prove that for any word of length $n$ from the letters $L$ and $R$ you can put the grip on the scales in such an order that this word corresponds to the sequence of weighing results.

M462. The plane intersects the lateral edges of a regular quadrangular rectangle at points spaced from the vertex at distances $a$, $b$, $c$ and $d$ (Fig. 1). Prove that

$$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{c} = \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{d}.$$

K. Shvartsman

M463.* Given natural numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m, y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n$. The sums $x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_m$ and $y_1 + y_2 + \dots + y_n$ are equal to each other and less than mn. Prove that in the equality $x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_m = y_1 + y_2 + \dots + y_n$ you can cross out some of the terms so that the equality is true again.

K. Sibirkov

M464.* On a given plane there are 1000 squares on the side, parallel to the coordinate axes. Let $M$ be the set of centers of these squares. Prove that it is possible to mark a part of the squares so that each point of the set $M$ falls into at least one and at most four marked squares.

A. Plotkin

M465*. There are a thousand tickets with numbers 000, 001, ..., 999 and one hundred boxes with numbers 00, 01, ..., 99. A ticket is allowed to be placed in a box if the box number can be obtained from the number of this ticket by crossing out one of the numbers. Prove that
a) you can put all the tickets into 50 boxes;
b) it is impossible to arrange all tickets into less than 40 boxes;
b) it is impossible to arrange all tickets into less than 50 boxes.

Let there be $10^k$ tickets with $k$-digit numbers from 00...0 to 99...9. The ticket is allowed to be placed in a box, the number of which can be obtained from the number of this ticket by crossing out some $k$-digit tickets, which can be distributed into 34 boxes.
d) Find the minimum number of boxes in which $k$-digit tickets can be placed. Try solving this problem for $k=4$, 5, 6, ...

S. Fomin

F473*. Figure 2 was made with a strokosoppic photograph of the movement of two steel balls of the same diameters, but different masses. The arrow on figure shows the direction of movement of one of the balls before the collision.
1) Determine the ratio of the masses of the balls.
2) Indicate in which direction the balls move towards the ball. (9 grades)

F474. The target shown in figure 3 is assembled from identical voltmeters. The first voltmeter reads $U_1=10$ V, and the third reads $U_3=8$ V. What is the reading of the second voltmeter? (9 grades)

F475*. figure 4 shows the voltmeter characteristic of a light bulb from a flashlight. The light bulb is included in the circuit shown in figure 5.
1) Find graphically the current in the light bulb.
2) At what position of the potentiometer slide is the direction between points $A$ and $B$ equal to zero?
3) At what position of the potentiometer slide will the direction between points $A$ and $B$ hardly change with small changes in h. d.s. batteries?

Neglect the internal resistance of the battery. (10 grades)
