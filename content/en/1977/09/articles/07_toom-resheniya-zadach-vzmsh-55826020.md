Solutions to VZMSH problems

In this issue we publish solutions to some problems from the introductory work at the VZMSH in 1977 («Kvant», 1977, No. 1, p. 53).

Problem 1. In the equality of two fractions, the numerators and denominators of which are two-digit numbers, the numbers are replaced by letters: identical - identical, different - different:

$$\frac{KY}{PE} = \frac{KA}{KY}.$$

What number does each letter mean (it is enough to give all possible answers)?

Solution. Let us denote by $\frac{y}{x}$ the irreducible fraction equal to $\frac{KY}{PE}$. In other words, let $\frac{KY}{PE} = \frac{y}{x}$, and the natural numbers $x$ and $y$ have no common divisors other than 1. Thus, $PE = ax$ and $KY = ay$, and at the same time $KY = bx$ and $KA = by$. Then $ay = bx$, whence, since $x$ and $y$ do not have common divisors, $a = cx$, $b = cy$. So, $KY = cxy$, $KA = cy^2$, $PE = cx^2$, where $c$, $x$, $y$ are natural numbers.

On the one hand, $|KY - KA| = |Y - A|$ and $Y \neq A$. So, $0 < |KY - KA| < 10$. On the other hand, $KY - KA = cy(x - y)$. Hence,

$$0 < cy|x - y| < 10.$$

(1) Hence $c < 10$. Since $PE = cx^2$ and $P \neq 0$, we get $x > 1$. We similarly conclude that $y > 1$. $y \geq 2$ and (1) imply $c < 5$. So, $1 \leq c \leq 4$. Let's look at the possibilities of $c = 2, 3, 4$ first. Taking into account the absence of common divisors $y$ and $y$, and inequality (1), we obtain nine options for $c, y$ and $x$ (see table). Check shows that none of these options are suitable. Let now $c = 1$. Inequality (1) takes the form $0 < y|x - y| < 10.$

(2) Since $KA = y^2$, $PE = x^2$, we have $4 \leq x \leq 9$ and $4 \leq y \leq 9$. From $y \geq 4$ and (2) it follows $0 < |x - y| \leq 2$. So, $|x - y| = 1$ or $|x - y| = 2$. Let us show that $|x - y| \neq 2$. If $y \geq 5$, then from (2) $|x - y| < 2$. If $y = 4$ and $|x - y| = 2$, then $x = 6$ is a contradiction with the absence of common divisors $y$ and $y$. So, $|x - y| = 1$. Hence $4 \leq x \leq 8$ and $y = x + 1$ or $5 \leq x \leq 9$ and $y = x - 1$. Checking all 10 options gives three possible answers:

1) $KY = 20$, $KA = 25$, $PE = 16$;
2) $KY = 30$, $KA = 36$, $PE = 25$;
3) $KY = 42$, $KA = 49$, $PE = 36$.

Problem 9. A large rectangle is divided into cells $1$ cm $\times$ $1$ cm. A number is written inside each cell. It is known that the sum of the numbers in every horizontal line is equal to 1, and in every vertical line it is equal to 2. Can the area of ​​a rectangle be equal to $1976$ cm$^2$?

Answer: it cannot.

Solution. Let us prove this by contradiction, that is, let us assume that the area of ​​such a rectangle is equal to $1976$ cm$^2$, and we arrive at a contradiction. Let the number of lines be $m$, and the number of stolniks be $n$. Then $mn = 1976$. Let's calculate the sum of all the numbers written in the cells in two ways. On the one hand, the sum of the numbers in each line is 1, and the lines are $m$, which means the sum of all numbers is $m$. On the other hand, the sum of the numbers in the kkedom stolnik is 2, and in the stolnikov $n$, which means the sum of all numbers is $2n$. So, $m = 2n$, whence $2n^2 = 1976$, $n^2 = 988$, which is impossible for the whole $n$.
