COORDINATES

I thought each of you had to ask passersby to ask passersby to explain where this or that building or institution is located. The responder can be divided into three categories. The first ones wave their hand in a certain direction and say “there,” sometimes adding “about twenty minutes away.” The second say something like this: “Pass the bakery, there will be a turn to the left, but you don’t turn there, but go straight past the cafe, and when you pass the bookstore, turn left, walk past the school, then there will be a yellow two-story house with columns, and your institution will be in the direction of it.” Still others answer like this: “Go straight to Chernyshevsky Street, turn left along it and the second house from the intersection with Dobrolyubova Street on the right side is the one you are looking for.”

And so, following the advice, you marvel in the indicated direction. If there is any doubt about the correctness of the chosen path, he turns to passers-by and eventually finds the right house. As they say, “language will take you to Kyiv.”

What should seafarers do? Who can help them find their way on the high seas? How to tell others where the island they discovered is located?

And 200 years before ours, the Greek scientist Hipparchus offers well-known geographical coordinates: shiromu and dolama. Using these two numbers, you can accurately determine the position of an island, village, mountain or well in the desert and plot them on a map or globe. Having learned to determine the latitude and longitude of a ship’s location on the open sea, sailors were able to choose the direction they needed without any trouble.

Recall that eastern longitude and northern latitude are indicated by numbers with a plus sign, and western longitude and southern latitude are indicated by numbers with a minus sign. Thus, a pair of numbers with signs will uniquely determine a point on the globe. For example, the pair +70°, +60° defines a point in the center of Vaygach Island, located in the Kara Sea.

One of the most powerful writers, Jules Verne, has some novels set in situations related to geographic coordinates. In the novel “The Amazing Adventures of Uncle Antifer,” one of the characters knows the latitude of the island on which the treasure is hidden, and the other knows the longitude of this island. Let us remember the text of the note from the novel “The Children of Captain Grant”:

“On June 7, 1862, the three-masted ship Britannia Glasgow was wrecked ... drive ... south ... berg ... two sailors ... Captain Gr ... reach ... continent ... pl. ... cruel ... ind ... thrown this document ... longitude

In the 14th century, the French mathematician N. Orsem introduced, by analogy with geographical coordinates, coordinates on a plane. He proposed covering the plane with a rectangular grid and calling what we now call *abscissa* and *ordine* latitude and longitude.
This innovation turned out to be extremely successful. On its basis, the coordinate method would emerge, linking geometry with algebra. The main task in creating this method belongs to the great French mathematician Rene Descartes. Among them, such a coordinate system is called Cartesian. Many ways of indicating a place are based on this system. For example, on a cinema ticket there are two numbers: row and seat - they can be considered as the coordinates of your place in the theater. Similar coordinates are adopted in chess, however, a letter is taken along with one of the numbers: vertical rows of cells are designated by letters of the Latin alphabet, and horizontal rows by numbers.
Thus, each square of the chessboard is assigned a pair of letters and numbers, and chess players are able to record their games.
The same “chess” principle is now being applied to the city plans. The city plan is divided into squares, numbered using letters and numbers, and on the reverse side all the streets depicted are listed in alphabetical order and indicate in which square they are located.
There are other coordinate systems on the plane. Let's remember the passage, which shows the direction with his hand and tells how long it takes to walk. The *polar coordinate system* is based on this principle. To enter it, select a starting point called *pole* (the system is therefore called “polar”); from this point a ray is drawn, called the *polar axis*. To determine the coordinates of a point on a plane, it is connected by a segment to a pole and the length of this segment and the angle between it and the polar axis are calculated.
Sometimes the coordinates appear in veiled form. For example, in the assignment for Moscow University on the Lenin Hills, the rooms are numbered so that by the audience number you can find out the floor on which it is located. To do this, the number must be divided into two parts: the number of hundreds and the rest. The number of hundreds is the floor. For example, room 1503 is on the 15th floor, and room 817 is on the 8th floor.

So far we have talked about coordinates on a sphere or plane, defined by two numbers. There are also coordinates specified by a single number. These are the coordinates on a straight line. I think each of you knows that it is enough to set one number - the distance from the point to the beginning of the cutoff - to indicate the position of this point on a straight line. In life, we very often come across such coordinates. An example is a railway with kilometer columns along it. Another example is the numbers of houses on the street. What about time? Although we denote each moment in time with a whole bunch of numbers: year, month, day, hour, minute and second, it is clear that we can get around it with a single number, expressed, for example, in seconds. One number specifies the position of a point on a circle. When we say “Vanya was born on September 14,” we are essentially dealing with coordinates on a circle (along what?).

Well, how many coordinates will determine the position of a point in space? Naturally, three. These three numbers can be obtained, for example, like this. Let us connect the center of the Earth and our point with a beam and consider the latitude and longitude of the intersection of the beam with the Earth and the distance from our point to the center of the Earth. Such a coordinate system is called spherical. You can do it differently for your friend. Let's choose a certain plane and introduce a Cartesian coordinate system on it, and compare to our point the coordinates of its projection onto this plane and the distance from it to the plane, taken with the plane sign for one half of the space and with the minus sign for the other; this way we get a Cartesian coordinate system in space.

The spherical coordinate system is usually used on airfields. A radar is installed next to the lake. This device has certain distances to the aircraft, the angle at which the aircraft is visible above the horizon, and the angle between the direction towards the aircraft and the direction to the north.

In conclusion, let's discuss one business proposal. Each point in space can be specified by numbers. Moreover, you can set the position of my or your apartment using numbers. So why write long addresses on envelopes? It would be enough to agree on a coordinate system (for example, long, hiss and floor number) and the letters would perfectly reach the addressee.

Now it’s time to remember Captain Grant’s letter. After all, if you make a mistake in one of the numbers in such a short address or this number cannot be read (you never know what will happen in a long distance), the letter will not reach the addressee. The primary address is more stable. If you make a minor mistake, the letter will still get there. The address contains, as they say, redundant information that Captain Grant also included in his letter. He wrote his letter in three languages ​​and included not only the coordinates of the crash site, but also the continent on which it occurred. But this was not enough. So please don’t switch to a simplified address.
