P. L. Chebyshev's research in number theory was of outstanding importance for science. For the first time since Euclid, with surprisingly witty and surprisingly elementary reasoning, he obtained the most important results in the problem of the distribution of prime numbers in the works “On Determining the Number of Prime Numbers Not Exceeding a Given Value” and “On Prime Numbers.” P. L. Chebyshev proved that the function $x(x) - x$ of simply numbers not exceeding them $x$ satisfies the inequality

$$0,92 < \frac{\pi(x)\ln x}{x} < 1,06^*$$

for sufficiently large $x$. This inequality was then somewhat studied by the English mathematician Sylvester, and the French mathematician Hadamard strictly proved that

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \left[ \pi(x): \frac{x}{\ln x} \right] = 1$$

or asymptotically $\pi(x) \sim x/\ln x$.

Here Chebyshev proved the statement (the so-called Bertrand postulate), which is that between $x$ and $2x(x \geq 2)$ there is always at least one prime number **).

His works, exceptionally rich in new ideas and methods, gave a powerful impetus to the development of diverse branches of mathematics and mechanics; in addition, he personally posed important tasks and problems for young scientists.
