4. $\log_{x-1} (x^2 - 5x + 7) = 1$.

Solution. Let's find the ODZ:

$$x^2 - 5x + 7 > 0, \quad x - 1 > 0,$$

whence $x > 1$. According to the definition of a logarithmic function, we have $x^2 - 5x + 7 = x - 1; \quad x_1 = 2, \quad x_2 = 4$.

Since both values ​​are included in the ODZ, they are solutions to the original equation.

5. $\sqrt[3]{2x - 1} + \sqrt[3]{x - 1} = 1$.

Solution. Let's cube both sides of the equation:

$$3x - 2 + 3\sqrt[3]{(2x - 1)(x - 1)} \times (\sqrt[3]{2x - 1} + \sqrt[3]{x - 1}) = 1.$$

The expression in parentheses represents the left side of the original equation - it can be replaced with the right side. After this, the equation is reduced to the form

$$(2x - 1) (x - 1) = (1 - x)^3.$$

Answer: $x_1 = 0, \quad x_2 = 1$.

6. Can the equation

$$\sqrt{x + a\sqrt{x + b}} + \sqrt{x} = c$$

have infinitely many solutions?

Solution. Move $\sqrt{x}$ to the right and square both sides:

$$x + a\sqrt{x + b} = c^2 - 2c\sqrt{x} + x.$$

After bringing similar terms we get: $(a + 2c)\sqrt{x} = c^2 - b$. For $a + 2c = 0$ and $c^2 - b = 0$ (and only in this case), the equation becomes an identity that is valid for all non-negative $x$.

Answer: The equation has infinitely many solutions at $a = -2c$ and $b = c^2$. The solutions in this case are $x \ge 0$.
