Task 2

When the body moves to the right of the equilibrium position (point $B$ on figure 13), the tangent of the tangent angle to the potential energy graph becomes negative, i.e. the force is positive and takes the body out of the equilibrium position. The point $B$ is called the inflection point; Thus, we have proven that the equilibrium at the inflection point is unstable. Equilibrium positions are also points $A$, $C$ and sections $(C, \infty)$ and $(-\infty, A)$. Point $C$ and the entire section $(C, \infty)$ correspond to an indifferent equilibrium. If you move a body from any point on the potential energy line $(C, \infty)$, then it remains in equilibrium in the new position. A similar situation occurs at section $(-\infty, A)$, with the exception of point $A$ itself. The equilibrium at point $A$ is unstable; to make sure of this, it is enough to slightly shift the body to the right; the tangent of the angle of inclination becomes negative, i.e. the force is positive, and therefore directed from the equilibrium position (the sled rolled down from $A$). Task 3

A change in potential energy by a constant value $C$ graphically corresponds to a displacement of the abscissa axis up or down (depending on the sign $C$) relative to the ordinate axis. In this case, very/no, the angle of inclination of the tangent to the “new” axis $x$ coincides with the angle of inclination to the “old” axis $x$. Since the force is equal in magnitude to the tangent of this angle, it remains unchanged during this transformation of potential energy.

Task 4

A cube floating in a liquid is subject to two forces: the gravitational force and the buoyant force. As long as the immersion depth of the cube $h$ satisfies condition $0 \leq h \leq a$, the total force is equal to $F = \rho_K a^3 g - \rho_K a^2 h g$.

Let us put $h = h_0 + x$, where $h_0 = a \frac{\rho_K}{\rho_K}$.

Then $F = -\rho_K a^2 g x$ if $-h_0 \leq x \leq a - h$. After the cube is completely immersed in the liquid ($h > a$), regardless of the depth, a constant force $F = -(\rho_K - \rho_K) a^3 g$ acts on it; similarly, a constant buoyant force $F = \rho_K a^3 g$ acts on a cube not immersed in liquid. As a result, the dependence of dreams $F$ on the coordinate $x$ has the form shown in figure 3. As one would expect, $x = 0$ is the equilibrium position. It is easy to verify by directly calculating the area that

$$U(x) = \frac{\rho_K a^2 g}{2} x^2, \text{ если } -h_0 \leq x \leq a - h_0;$$

$$U(x) = \frac{\rho_K a^2 g}{2} (a - h_0)^2 + (\rho_K - \rho_K) a^3 g x$$

at $x > a - h_0$, and

$$U(x) = \frac{\rho_K a^2 g}{2} h_0^2 - \rho_K a^3 g x,$$

when $x < -h_0$.

The potential energy graph is shown at figure 4. We chose the constant $C$ so that $U(0) = 0$. It can be seen that $x = 0$ is the position of the missing equilibrium.
