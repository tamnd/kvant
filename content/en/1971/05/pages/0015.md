⟦folio 13⟧

The energy of the body is equal to the area taken with the opposite sign *) under the graph of the dependence of the action of force on the body on the coordinates **). Then its graph is a straight line parallel to the x-axis. The area under it is equal to $F \cdot (x - x_0)$, from where we obtain for the difference in potential energies

$$U(x) - U(x_0) = -F \cdot (x - x_0)$$

(Fig. 7a, b).

Using correspondence (8) we obtain

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{mv_0^2}{2} = F \cdot (x - x_0).$$

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - Fx = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} - Fx_0,$$

that is, potential energy $U = -Fx$. However, this is not the only possibility; with the same reason we could write such an equality

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} - Fx + C = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} - Fx_0 + C,$$

where $C$ is a constant number. But then the potential energy is $U = -Fx + C$. Thus, we see that the potential energy is determined exactly up to an arbitrary constant. This can be explained by the very law of conservation of mechanical energy. After all, if we say that the quantities $E = \frac{mv^2}{2} + U$ are conserved, then $E' = \frac{mv^2}{2} + U + C$ is also conserved, where $C$ is some constant. But in this case $U' = U + C$. This means that potential energy can only be determined to an accuracy of an arbitrary constant, which is always chosen from messages of convenience *). Of course, such uncertainty of potential energy does not in any way affect the conclusions we have drawn about the characteristics of motion in a given field, the presence of equilibrium positions in it, and the dependence of the body’s speed on the coordinate. You can easily verify this; you only need to simultaneously change the potential energy by values ​​$C$ and change the total energy of the body by the same amount. The fact is that to fully describe the movement of a body, it is necessary to specify its position and speed at some moment

*) It should be noted that if the force is negative, then the corresponding participation should be assigned a minus sign.

*) A field in which the force acting on the body is constant everywhere is called homogeneous. Homogeneous, for example, is the gravitational field near the Earth's surface $F = mg$ and the electric field inside a flat capacitor.
