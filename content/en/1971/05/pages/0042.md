⟦folio 40⟧

WHAT DO WE LOSE OR FIND HERE?

V. A. Petrov

Almost every written exam paper in mathematics includes some kind of equation. When solving it, the examinee must not only accurately check the calculations necessary to obtain the answer, but also be required to make sure that these calculations are correct. You need to be able to analyze each of the proposed transformations: does it lead to an equivalent equation, does it threaten the loss or acquisition of a solution.

Issues of tactics of “fighting” with built roots and with the loss of roots were considered in the article by A. G. Mordkovich “Someone loses, someone finds” («Kvant» No. 5, 1970) *). Now we invite you to check whether you are confident enough in this tactic. First, solutions to the equations are given. You need to analyze these decisions and answer the following question. Is the answer correct? If not, then what is here - are the roots lost or the structure found? Why? What is the correct answer? Compare your thoughts with ours on page 62.

1. $2\sin 4x = \sqrt{2(1 - \cos 4x)}$.

Solution. Using the formula
$$2\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \cos 2\alpha,$$
we get
$$2\sin 4x = \sqrt{4\sin^2 2x}$$
or
$$\sin 4x = \sin 2x,$$
which is already very easy to solve.

Answer: $x = n\pi$ and $x = \frac{\pi}{6} + n\frac{\pi}{3}$, where $n = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$

*) This “tactics” is covered in more detail in the book by G.V. Dorofeev, M.K. Potanov, N.H. Rozov, very useful for applicants, “Mathematics Manual for Those Building Universities”, M., “Nauka”, 1970.

2. $8\cos x\cos 2x\cos 4x = 1$.

Solution. Multiplying both sides of the equation by sin $x$ and using the double angle sine formula three times, we get the simple equation
$$\sin 8x = \sin x.$$
Answer: $x = (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{9}$ and $x = 2n\frac{\pi}{7}$, where $n$ is any integer.

3. $\sin \left(\frac{1}{5}\arccos x\right) = 1$.

Solution. From this equation we find
$$\arccos x = \frac{5\pi}{2} + 10n\pi$$
($n = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$).
