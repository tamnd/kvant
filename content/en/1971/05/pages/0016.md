$E = \frac{mv_0^2}{2} + U(x_0)$ By adding some constant value to $U$, the energy of $E$ will increase by exactly the same amount.

Let us now consider the motion of the cube outside in the absence of friction (Fig. 8) and calculate what the potential energy is in this case. According to Hooke’s law, the force acting on the cube is equal to $F = -kx$, where $k$ is the stiffness of the spring $x$ is its deformation. In this case, the graph of the force is straight, the tangent of the angle of inclination of which to the axis $x$ equals $k$ (Fig. 9a)

The potential energy difference $U(x_1) - U(x_0)$ is equal to the area of ​​the trapezoid $ABCD$ protected on Fig. 9a, the height of which is $BC = x_1 - x_0$. and we get the main $\frac{AB + CD}{2} = \frac{kx_0 + kx_1}{2}$. Using the formula for the area of ​​a trapezoid, we find that $U(x_1) - U(x_0) = \frac{(x_1 - x_0)(kx_0 + kx_1)}{2} = \frac{k(x_1^2 - x_0^2)}{2} = \frac{kx_1^2}{2} - \frac{kx_0^2}{2}$.

As a result, for $U(x)$ we obtain the following expression: $U(x) = \frac{kx^2}{2}$
