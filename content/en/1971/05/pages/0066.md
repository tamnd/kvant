You ask “What does indecisiveness lead to?”
(see «Kvant» No. 4, p. 7)

We only need one fact from analytical geometry: if the ends of a segment have coordinates $(a_1, b_1)$ and $(a_2, b_2)$, then the middle of this segment has coordinates
$$\left(\frac{a_1 + a_2}{2}, \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}\right).$$

Let Petya turn to the stage at point $(a, b)$, then at point $(c, d)$ - to Lucy’s house, at point $(e, f)$ - to the store and at point $(a_1, b_1)$ - again to the stage. Let's find the coordinates of all these points, which are the midpoints of the segments connecting the last turning point with the corresponding vertex of the square
$$c = \frac{a}{2}, d = \frac{b + 1}{2}, e = \frac{a}{4},$$
$$f = \frac{b + 1}{4};$$
$$a_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{a}{4} + 1\right) = \frac{1}{8}(a + 4)$$
$$q_1 = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{b + 1}{4} + 1\right) = \frac{1}{8}(b + 5)$$
Check that then
$$a_1 - \frac{4}{7} = \frac{1}{8}(a - \frac{4}{7}),$$
$$b_1 - \frac{5}{7} = \frac{1}{8}(b - \frac{5}{7}).$$

Thus, the distances from the turning points to the stage to the point (4/7, 5/7) form a geometric progression and decrease by 8 times after each “round” in the direction of the stage - Lucy’s house - the store. This means that Petya is approaching a triangle with the coordinates of the vertices
$$\left(\frac{4}{7}, \frac{5}{7}\right), \left(\frac{2}{7}, \frac{6}{7}\right), \left(\frac{1}{7}, \frac{3}{7}\right)$$
