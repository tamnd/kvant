Stamps and match labels dedicated to Russian mathematicians

In 1946, on the occasion of the 125th anniversary of the birth of the great Russian mathematician and mechanic Pafnutya Lvovich Chebyshev, figure artist E. Bulanova issued a series of postage stamps consisting of two miniatures. We present one of them at figure.

Above the portrait of the scientist is the inscription “Great Russian Mathematician,” which, however, does not fully reflect the role of P. L. Chebyshev in the development of Russian science. In addition to his brilliant mathematical achievements, P. L. Chebyshev left behind a number of outstanding research and inventions in mechanics, primarily related to the theory and practice of hinged-lever mechanisms that convert circular motion into rectilinear motion.

P. L. Chebyshev was the founder and inspirer of the St. Petersburg mathematical scale. Its outstanding representatives were academicians A. M. Lyapunov, A. A. Markov, V. A. Steklov. In 1967, a series of matchstick labels appeared with portraits of significant Russian physicists and mathematicians, among whom we see the St. Petersburg Mathematical Scale and his most prominent students. A. M. Lyapunov was not only a brilliant mathematician, but also a great mechanic. In 1967, for the 100th anniversary of his birth, also by figure E. Bulanova, a stamp was issued with a portrait of the scientist. Below it is a formula that gives the surface equation of the equilibrium figure of a rotating fluid. By the way, A. M. Lyapunov’s research on the equilibrium figures of a rotating fluid, the particles of which are engraved according to the law of universal gravitation, was undertaken on the initiative of his teacher P. L. Chebysheva.
