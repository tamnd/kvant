⟦folio 2⟧

The scientific interests of P. L. Chebyshev are distinguished by their great diversity and breadth. He left behind extensive research in the field of mathematical analysis, especially in the theory of approximation of functions by polynomials, in intercalculus, number theory, probability theory, geometry, ballistics, theory of mechanisms and other fields of knowledge.

In each of these areas of science, Pafnuty Lvovich obtained functional results, developed new ideas and methods that determined the development of these branches of mathematics and mechanics for many years and retained their significance to this day.

At the same time, it gives rise to Chebyshev’s ability to obtain excellent scientific results using simple, elementary means.

Another important feature of P. L. Chebyshev’s scientific activity is his constant interest in practical issues, the desire to connect theoretical problems of mathematics with the needs of natural and technical, practical activities of people. In the light of modern trends in the development of science, the programmatic setting of scientific activity by P. L. Chebyshev seems extremely practical: “The practical activity of man is extremely diverse, and in order to satisfy all its requirements, there are not enough sciences and many different methods. But of these, especially important are those that are necessary to solve various modifications of the same problem, common to all practical human activity: how to allocate one’s means to achieve the greatest possible benefit” (P. L. Chebyshev, Socinnia, vol. II, St. Petersburg, 1907, p. 239).

It should be noted that for Pafnutya Lvovich himself, his interest in practice turned out to be extremely fruitful, since many of his mathematical discoveries were made while solving applied problems. For example, the study of the hinge mechanism, known as the “Watt parallelogram,” led him to the creation of the basic theory of the best approximation of functions by polynomials, which has now turned into a widely developed mathematical field of great applied importance. In terms of the richness of mathematical ideas, the significance of the results obtained and the study of their acquisition, the work of P. L. Chebyshev “The Theory of Mechanisms Known as a Parallelogram” (1854) is outstanding. In this method, Chebyshev introduces and solves the famous problem: “Given a continuous function f(x); among all polynomials P(x) of degree n, find one such that in a given interval the maximum value of the difference |f(x) - P(x)| it was possible to do less." If the function itself is a polynomial of degree n+1, then the problem posed is equivalent to finding a polynomial of the same degree, with the same coefficient for the leading term, which deviates less from zero on the interval under consideration. This is how the famous “Chebyshev complete nomes” arose - polynomials that “deviate least away from zero” in the interval [−1, +1]: T_n(x) = \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} \cos(n \arccos x). (These expressions are polynomials in x of degree n. The coefficients of the highest degree of these polynomials are equal to one.) Chebyshev also studied the approximation of functions after a variety of functions and trionometric polynomials. (A rational function is the ratio of two polynomials, and a trionometric polynomial is a polynomial of sin x and cos'x.)

In probability theory, Chebyshev managed to obtain a number of important results using extremely simple means. Many results and conclusions were only named, not completed, but all of Chebyshev’s work in this area was the basis on which the Russian school of probability theory developed.
