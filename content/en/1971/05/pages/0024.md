Physics
helps
geometry

B. Yu. Kogan

When studying physics, of course, mathematics is used. But it turns out that the other way around is also true: physics benefits mathematics. Here are some examples of how the concept of center of gravity can be used in geometry.

1. Theorem on medians of a triangle. Let us place material points of equal mass at the vertices of the triangle (Fig. 1). The center of gravity of the system of points A and B is located at point K - the middle of the segment AB. This means that the center of gravity of the system of points A, B and C lies on the median of the SC. Reasoning in a similar way, we will come to the conclusion that this center of gravity lies on the median AL and on the median BM. But the system of points A, B and C has only one center of gravity. Therefore, the medians of a triangle intersect at one point.

Further, since point C has mass m, and point K “represents” a system of points A and B, having mass 2m, then KS is half the size of SC. Therefore, the point of intersection of the medians divides each of them in a ratio of 2:1.
