3. Generalization of the previous theorem. Consider an arbitrary quadrilateral ABCD (Fig. 3). Let us place material points at its vertices: at vertex A - masses m, at vertex B - masses αm, at vertex D - masses βm and at vertex C - masses αβm, where α and β are arbitrary positive numbers. Then the center of gravity of the system of points A, B, C and D will lie at the intersection of straight lines KM and LN, where K is the center of gravity of the system of points A and B, M is the center of gravity of the system of points C and D, L is the center of gravity of the system of points B and C, and N is the center of gravity of the system of points A and D. Further, since lengthwise arbitrary correspond to weights, then

$$\frac{AK}{KB} = \frac{\alpha m}{m} = \alpha, \quad \frac{DM}{MC} = \frac{\alpha \beta m}{\beta m} = \alpha,$$

$$\frac{NS}{SL} = \frac{\alpha m + \alpha \beta m}{m + \beta m} = \alpha, \quad \frac{BL}{LC} = \frac{\alpha \beta m}{\alpha m} = \beta,$$

$$\frac{AN}{ND} = \frac{\beta m}{m} = \beta,$$

$$\frac{KS}{SM} = \frac{\alpha \beta m + \beta m}{\alpha m + m} = \beta.$$
