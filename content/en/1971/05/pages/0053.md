The third law states that all forces in mechanics are forces of interaction between bodies, equal in magnitude, opposite in direction and applied to different bodies (Fig. 4). The latter is sometimes forgotten by asserting, for example, that a body standing on the ground is balanced according to Newton’s third law by the forces acting on it: the force of gravity $\vec{P}$ and the reaction force of the support $\vec{N}$ (Fig. 5). In fact, the equality of these forces has nothing to do with Newton’s third law and, as we will soon see, may not be satisfied at all.

Which of these forces, however, in accordance with Newton’s third law, has its own “pair”, equal in magnitude, opposite in direction and applied to another body, in our case - to the earth: support reaction force $\vec{N}$ - force of pressure of the body on the ground $\vec{N}'$ force of attraction of the body to the earth $\vec{P}$ - force $\vec{P}'$, pulling the earth towards the body (Fig.6).
