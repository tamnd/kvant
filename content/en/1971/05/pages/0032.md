⟦folio 30⟧

Tasks

M81. An arbitrary point $P$ is taken inside the square $A_1 A_2 A_3 A_4$. From vertex $A_1$ a perpendicular is dropped to line $A_2 P$, from vertex $A_2$ - to $A_3 P$, from $A_3$ - to $A_4 P$, from $A_4$ - to $A_1 P$. Prove that all four perpendiculars (or their extensions) intersect at one point.

A. N. Vilenkin

M82. There are several identical automatic vehicles on the ring road. It is known that if all the gasoline available in these automatic vehicles were poured into one, then this car would be able to drive along the entire ring road and return to its original place. Prove that at least one of the cars standing on the road can compress all the stakes, taking gasoline from the rest of the cars along the way.

M83. Prove that the numbers 1, 2, ..., $n$ for any $n$ cannot be divided into two groups so that the product of numbers in one group is equal to the product of numbers in the other.

M84. Let $A$ be the base of the perpendicular dropped from the center of the given circle onto the given straight line $l$.

On this line two more points $B$ and $C$ are taken so that

$$AB = AC.$$

Two arbitrary secants are drawn through the points $B$ and $C$, one of which intersects the circle at points $P$ and $Q$, the second - at points $M$ and $N$. Let lines $PM$ and $QN$ intersect line $l$ at points $R$ and $S$. Prove that

$$AR = AS.$$

This problem (or some of its variants) is sometimes called the “butterfly problem”; the origin of this name is clear from figure 1.

M85. Prove that if $a_1, a_2, ..., a_m$ are pairwise distinct natural numbers, none of which is divisible by the square of an integer greater than one, and $b_1, b_2, ..., b_m$ are integers other than zero, then

$$b_1 \sqrt{a_1} + b_2 \sqrt{a_2} + \ldots + b_m \sqrt{a_m} \neq 0.$$

L. N. Vaserstein
