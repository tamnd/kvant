Fig. 2. The body, moving to the left, approaches the point $x_1$, where its speed is zero. One cannot accumulate at this point, since a force acts on it (see the sled rule on page 12). Therefore, it will continue to move to the right, increasing speed.

Knowing the potential energy, it is easy to determine the speed of the particle at each point. Since $\frac{mv^2}{2} = E - U$, then $v = \pm \sqrt{\frac{2}{m} [E - U]}$.

Two signs of speed correspond to two possible directions of movement along the $x$ axis. figure 2 graphically shows the dependence of the particle velocity on its position for the case when the energy and potential energy correspond to figure 1a. The direction of movement is indicated on the graph by an arrow.

So, we see that with the help of potential energy graphs we can simply and clearly find out where the possibility of movement is, what type of trajectories it has and how the speed of a body depends on its position. When clarifying these questions, we needed to know the behavior of potential energy as a function of coordinates “in general,” that is, how it behaves along the entire trajectory.

Let's now see what is associated with the rate of change of potential energy, that is, the quantity

$$\frac{\Delta U}{\Delta x} = \frac{U(x_2) - U(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1},$$

where $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$ is very small

According to the law of conservation of mechanical energy

$$\Delta U = U(x_2) - U(x_1) = \frac{mv_1^2}{2} - \frac{mv_2^2}{2} = \frac{m}{2} (v_1 + v_2) (v_1 - v_2)$$

where $v_1$ is the speed of the body at point $x_1$, and $v_2$ is its speed at point $x_2$. In a small area $\Delta x$ the force acting on the body can be considered constant. Movement under the influence of a constant force, which means $F$, equal to acceleration, and acceleration $a = F/m$. In this case, $v_2 - v_1 = a \Delta t = \frac{F}{m} \Delta t$ ($\Delta t$ is the time of movement), and $\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}$ is equal to the average speed $\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2} = v_{cp} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$. Let's substitute these expressions into formula (4):

$$\Delta U = m \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \left( -\frac{F}{m} \Delta t \right) = -F \Delta x.$$

From here

$$\frac{\Delta U}{\Delta x} = -F.$$

Formula (5) can also be obtained otherwise, taking into account that with uniform motion only the potential energy of the body changes and its change is equal to the work of the force acting on the body.

Let us find out the geometric meaning of formula (5). Let us be given a potential energy graph. It is clear that the smaller the value of $x_2 - x_1$, the more accurately the rate of change of potential energy coincides with the tangent of the tangent to the potential energy curve (Fig. 3). Action
