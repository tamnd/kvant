When studying physics, of course, mathematics is used. But it turns out that the other way around is also true: physics benefits mathematics. Here are some examples of how the concept of center of gravity can be used in geometry.

1. Theorem on medians of a triangle. Let us place material points of equal mass at the vertices of the triangle (Fig. 1). The center of gravity of the system of points A and B is located at point K - the middle of the segment AB. This means that the center of gravity of the system of points A, B and C lies on the median of the SC. Reasoning in a similar way, we will come to the conclusion that this center of gravity lies on the median AL and on the median BM. But the system of points A, B and C has only one center of gravity. Therefore, the medians of a triangle intersect at one point.

Further, since point C has mass m, and point K “represents” a system of points A and B, having mass 2m, then KS is half the size of SC. Therefore, the point of intersection of the medians divides each of them in a ratio of 2:1.

3. Generalization of the previous theorem. Consider an arbitrary quadrilateral ABCD (Fig. 3). Let us place material points at its vertices: at vertex A - masses m, at vertex B - masses αm, at vertex D - masses βm and at vertex C - masses αβm, where α and β are arbitrary positive numbers. Then the center of gravity of the system of points A, B, C and D will lie at the intersection of straight lines KM and LN, where K is the center of gravity of the system of points A and B, M is the center of gravity of the system of points C and D, L is the center of gravity of the system of points B and C, and N is the center of gravity of the system of points A and D. Further, since lengthwise arbitrary correspond to weights, then

$$\frac{AK}{KB} = \frac{\alpha m}{m} = \alpha, \quad \frac{DM}{MC} = \frac{\alpha \beta m}{\beta m} = \alpha,$$

$$\frac{NS}{SL} = \frac{\alpha m + \alpha \beta m}{m + \beta m} = \alpha, \quad \frac{BL}{LC} = \frac{\alpha \beta m}{\alpha m} = \beta,$$

$$\frac{AN}{ND} = \frac{\beta m}{m} = \beta,$$

$$\frac{KS}{SM} = \frac{\alpha \beta m + \beta m}{\alpha m + m} = \beta.$$

Therefore, if the sides of this quadrilateral are divided so that

$$\frac{AK}{KB} = \frac{DM}{MC} = \alpha, \quad \frac{BL}{LC} = \frac{AN}{ND} = \beta,$$

4. Finding matches. Using the concept of the center of gravity, problems of the following type can be easily solved. On the sides of the triangle $ABC$ there are points $K$ and $L$ (Fig. 5). Given that $\frac{AK}{KB} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{BL}{LC} = \frac{3}{5}$, find the ratios of $\frac{AS}{SL}$ and $\frac{CS}{SK}$.

Solution. Let us place material points at the vertices $A, B, C$ so that the correspondence holds
$$\frac{m_B}{m_A} = \frac{AB}{KB} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{m_C}{m_B} = \frac{BL}{LC} = \frac{3}{5},
$$
for example, let's put
$$m_A = 10m, \quad m_B = 5m, \quad m_C = 3m.$$
Then the center of gravity of the system of points $A$ and $B$ will lie at point $K$, and the center of gravity of the system of points $B$ and $C$ will lie at point $L$. Consequently, the center of gravity of the system of points $A, B$ and $C$ lies on the straight line $CK$ and at the same time on the straight line $AL$, that is, it is located at the intersection point $S$. From here it is easy to find that
$$\frac{AS}{SL} = \frac{m_B + m_C}{m_A} = \frac{5m + 3m}{10m} = \frac{4}{5},
$$
$$\frac{CS}{SK} = \frac{m_A + m_B}{m_C} = \frac{10m + 5m}{3m} = \frac{5}{1}.$$
