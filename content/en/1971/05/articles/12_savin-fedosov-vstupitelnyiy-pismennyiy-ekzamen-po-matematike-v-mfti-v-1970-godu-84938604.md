ENTRANCE WRITTEN EXAM IN MATHEMATICS AT MIPT IN 1970

A. P. Savin, B. V. Fedosov

The problems offered at the entrance exam in mathematics at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology have always attracted the attention of teachers and school graduates, since the level of these problems is somewhat higher than in many technical universities.

In 1970, the composition of the tasks on the written exam at MIPT changed somewhat. Instead of the four problems usually offered, in 1970 the LET contained five problems (one easy problem was added). This set of tasks apparently continued in the future.

You can get acquainted with the problems of the MIPT entrance exams of previous years in the collection “Problems in Elementary Mathematics”*), which has already been published in several editions.

I would like to note that the difficulty of the entrance exam in mathematics at MIPT is often exaggerated. The fact that after all the exams in mathematics and physics (oral and written) there is still a competition of several people per place speaks for itself.

So that we can get a real idea of ​​the written exam in mathematics in 1970, we will analyze one of the options in detail.

Option 0

1. A subway passenger goes down a moving escalator in 24 seconds. If a passenger walks at the same speed, but on a stationary escalator, then he descends in 42 seconds. How many seconds will it take him to descend while standing on the step of a moving escalator?
2. Solve the equation
   tg 5x—2 tg 3x=tg 5x·tg² 3x.

3. In triangle ABC the altitudes AD and CE are drawn. Find AC if BC=a, AB=b and DE/AC=k.

4. In a regular tetradir ABCD, two sections are drawn, each of which is parallel to the edge AB and CD. Area of ​​part of face ABC enclosed between secants

planes, s cm² greater than the area of ​​the part of the ACD face enclosed between the same planes. How much is the area of ​​one section greater than the area of ​​the other?

5. Find real solutions to the system of equations:

$$\begin{cases}
4xy + y^2 + 2z^2 = -3, \\
4xz + x^2 + 2z^2 = 1, \\
8yz + y^2 + 2z^2 = 1.
\end{cases}$$

Analysis of problems

1. Let us denote by $v_1$ the speed of the escalator, $L$ its length and $v_2$ the speed of the passenger. Then the conditions of the problem will be written in the form of equations:

$$\frac{L}{v_1 + v_2} = 24,$$

$$\frac{L}{v_2} = 42.$$

Let's transform these equations:

$$\frac{v_1}{L} + \frac{v_2}{L} = \frac{1}{24}, \quad \frac{v_2}{L} = \frac{1}{42}.$$

From the last two equations we immediately find that

$$\frac{v_1}{L} = \frac{1}{56},$$

and the required time is $\frac{L}{v_1}$, that is, 56 seconds.

The “highlight” of the problem is that from system D to equations (1) and (2) with three unknowns, you only need to find the ratio of two unknowns $L$ and $v_1$. Those applicants who tried to find $L$, and $v_1$, and $v_2$, naturally, could not do this.

2. Let's transform this equation:

$$tg 5x - tg 3x = tg 3x (1 + tg 3x tg 5x),$$

(1)

$$\frac{tg 5x - tg 3x}{1 + tg 3x \cdot tg 5x} = tg 3x.$$

In the last transformation we divided both sides of equation (1) by $1 + tg 3x \cdot tg 5x$. In this case, we can lose the roots of equation (1), which are the roots of equation $1 + tg 3x tg 5x = 0$. Transforming the original equation further, we get

$$tg 2x = tg 3x,$$

where

$$3x = 2x + k\pi \quad (k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots)$$

or

$$x = k\pi \quad (k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots).$$

Let's check whether we lost any roots when dividing equation (1) by $1 + tg 3x \cdot tg 5x$. Let $x_1$ be the root of the equation

$$1 + tg 3x \cdot tg 5x = 0.$$

Then, substituting $tg 5x_1 = -\frac{1}{tg 3x_1}$ into the original equation, we obtain

$$-\frac{1}{tg 3x_1} - tg 3x_1 = 0$$

or

$$tg^2 3x_1 = -1$$, which is impossible. Therefore, no roots were lost.

Answer:

$$x = k\pi \quad (k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots).$$

3. Let us denote $\triangle ABC$ by $\alpha$, then from the right triangle $ABD$ (Fig. 1)

$$BD = AB \cos \alpha = b \cos \alpha,$$

and from right triangle $BEC$

$$BE = BC \cos \alpha = a \cos \alpha.$$

From the triangle $BDE$ using the cosine theorem we find the length of the segment $ED$:

$$ED^2 = a^2 \cos^2 \alpha + b^2 \cos^2 \alpha - 2ab \cos^3 \alpha.$$ (1)

From the triangle $ABC$ we also use the cosine theorem to find the length of the segment $AC$:

$$AC^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos \alpha.$$ (2)

Now, using the correspondence $\frac{DE}{AC} = k$ given in the problem statement, from equations (1) and (2) we obtain

$$\frac{(a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos \alpha) \cos^2 \alpha}{a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos \alpha} = k^2.$$

Hence $\cos^2 \alpha = k^2$. Solution $\cos \alpha = k$ corresponds to the case of acute angle $\alpha$, and $\cos \alpha = -k$ corresponds to the case of angle $\alpha$. It remains to substitute the found values ​​into equation (2),

4. Let us consider the polygons obtained in the section (Fig. 2) of the tetrahedron indicated by the planes. It is easy to show that both the quadrilateral EFGH and the quadrilateral KLMN are rectogenous. Let us turn two angles by planes ABC and ACD onto the plane (Fig. 3) and denote the length of the segment EF by $x$, and the length of the segment LM by $y$. Since both triangles ABC and ACD are regular, and the sides of the rectangular triangles EFGH and KLMN are respectively parallel to the edges AB and CD, the segment KL becomes a continuation of the segment LM, and the segment FG becomes a continuation of the segment EF. If we denote the edge of the tetrahedron by $a$, then it is obvious that $FG = a - x$, and $KL = a - y$. The trapezoid EFLK and FGML, as you can easily see, have equal heights. Let's find them. The height $h$ of the trapezoid EFLK is equal to the difference in the heights of regular triangles EFC and KLC, therefore

$$h = x \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - (a - y) \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} (x + y - a).$$

Let us find the area of ​​the indicated trapezoids. The area of ​​the trapezoid EFLK is

$$\frac{EF + LK}{2} h = \frac{x + a - y}{2} h,$$

and the area of ​​the trapezoid FGML is equal to

$$\frac{FG + ML}{2} h = \frac{a - x + y}{2} h.$$

Using the conditions that the difference between the indicated areas is equal to $s$, we obtain

$$\frac{x + a - y}{2} h - \frac{a - x + y}{2} h = s$$

or

$$s = h (x - y);$$

Substituting instead of $h$ the expression found for it, we get

$$s = (x - y) (x + y - a) \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

$$\sigma = y(a-y) - x(a-x) = (x-y)(x+y-a).$$

Comparing the expressions for $s$ and $\sigma$, that $\sigma = 2s/\sqrt{3}$

5. Let's add the first and third equations with the notified second. We get $2x^2 + 2y^2 + 8z^2 + 4xy + 8xz + 8yz = 0$

Reducing by 2, we see that on the left side of the equation there is a complete square of the expression $x+y+2z$, hence $z = -\frac{1}{2}(x+y)$. (1)

Subtract the third equation from the second equation of this system and obtain $x^2 + 4xz - y^2 - 8yz = 0$. Let us substitute the found value $z$ (1) $x^2 - 2x(x+y) - y^2 + 4y(x+y) = 0$ into it.

After opening the brackets and bringing similar terms, the equation is reduced to the form $x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 = 0$. Let us express $x$ from it through $y$:

$$x = y \pm 2y.$$

So, either $x = -y$ or $x = 3y$.

Let's consider the first case: $x = -y$. From equation (1) we obtain $z = 0$. And then from the third equation of the original system it follows that $y^2 = 1$.

From here we find two values ​​for $y$: $y = 1$ or $y = -1$.

Thus, in the first case we get two solutions:

$$x_1 = -1, y_1 = 1, z_1 = 0;$$

$$x_2 = 1, y_2 = -1, z_2 = 0.$$

Let's consider the second case: $x = 3y$. From the same equation (1) it follows that $z = -2y$. Substituting this value for $z$ into the third equation of the original system, we obtain that $-7y^2 = 1$. So, in the second case there are no valid solutions.

It remains to check that the solutions found in the first case satisfy the equations of the initial system. This turns out to be true. Thus, two valid solutions of this system have been found.

The main difficulty of this problem is transforming the equation of a given system to a simpler system. Those applicants who try to solve this problem “at ease” by eliminating the unknowns have encountered insurmountable difficulties. Note that this system can be reduced to other systems that differ from those we have analyzed, but they also allow a simple solution.
