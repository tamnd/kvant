§ 4. Proof of the Hurwitz-Borel theorem.

We must show that the infinite set of suitable fractions of the number $\alpha = [0; m_1, m_2, \dots]$ has quality coefficients less than $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$, that is, that for the infinite many $k$ the inequalities $\mu_k < \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$ or $\frac{1}{\mu_k} > \sqrt{5}$ hold. Let's use Theorem 7.

There are three possible cases.

1°. Among the infinite numbers $m_1, m_2, \dots$ many different from 1 and 2. This case is the simplest. If $m_k > 2$, then $m_k \geq 3$ and $\frac{1}{\mu_k} = m_k + \frac{1}{m_{k+1} + \dots} + \frac{1}{m_{k-1} + \dots} > m_k \geq 3 > \sqrt{5}$.

2°. Among the numbers $m_1, m_2, \dots$ there are only a finite number different from 1 and 2, but two are an infinite number. In this case, for infinite many $k$ we have:

$$\frac{1}{\mu_k} = m_k + [0; m_{k+1}, \dots] + [0; m_{k-1}, \dots] > 2 + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{8}{3} > \sqrt{5}$$

3°. Starting at some point, all partial partials are equal to 1. Let, say, $m_{k_0} > 1$ and $m_k = 1$ for $k > k_0$. If the difference $k - k_0$ is even, then

$$\frac{1}{\mu_k} = 1 + [0; 1, 1, \dots, 1, m_{k_0}, \dots] > 1 + 2 [0; 1, 1, \dots] = 1 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{5} + 1} = \sqrt{5}$$

(We used here the fact that if $k$ is even and $m'_{k+1} > m'_{k+1}$, then

$$[m_0; m_1, \dots, m_k, m'_{k+1}, \dots] > [m_0, m_1, \dots, m_k, m'_{k+1}, \dots]$$

and equality $[1; 1, 1, \dots] = \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{2}$).
