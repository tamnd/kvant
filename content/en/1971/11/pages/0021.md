Exercises

1. A remarkable property of mathematical methods is that the same “mathematical model” can describe facts from seemingly completely different areas of physics. Refer to the fact that Coulomb's law; describing the interaction of two precise electric charges, written mathematically in the same way as Newton’s gravitational law, formulate the “electrostatic analogues” of assumptions 1 and 2.

2. Prove that the force of attraction of an exact mass by a homogeneous unbounded plate is inversely proportional to the distance of this mass from the plate.

3. Find the force of attraction exerted by a homogeneous material hemisphere on an exact mass located on a straight line connecting the center of the sphere with its pole.

4. In Hinton's imaginary flat world (see «Kvant» No. 4, 1970), the force of interaction between exact masses is inversely proportional to the distance between them. Keeping in mind that the “spheres” of a flat world are circles, formulate and prove propositions for the Hinton world similar to propositions 1 and 2. Show that the force of attraction experienced by an imaginary inhabitant of one of the planets of the Hinton system is proportional to the distance of the inhabitant from the center of the planet. If there are collebose planets in the system, then there is no gravitational field in the interior of the collebose (it is assumed that the planets are made of homogeneous matter).
