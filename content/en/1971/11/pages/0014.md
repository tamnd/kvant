⟦folio 12⟧

Theorem 3. Point $A_k$ ($n \geq 2$) we answer the ($k-2$)th suitable fraction $\alpha_{k-2}$ of number $\alpha$.

Proof.
For $A_2$ and $A_3$ this is clear: $A_2$ corresponds to $p=0$, $q=1$, that is, an approximation equal to $0=\alpha_0$ (recall that $m_0=0$); $A_3$ corresponds to $p=1$, $q=m_1$ (Fig. 8), that is, an approximation of $1/m_1=\alpha_1$.

Let us already know that for $i \leq k-1$, where $k \geq 4$, the point $A_i$ corresponds to the approximation $\alpha_{i-2}$. Let us show that $A_k$ corresponds to $\alpha_{k-2}$.

We must prove that $A_kC=p_{k-2}$, $A_kH=q_{k-2}$ (Fig. 9). Triangle $A_{k-2}A_kE$ is similar to triangle $OA_{k-1}M$; triangle $A_{k-2}A_kB$ is similar to triangle $OA_{k-1}D$; The similarity coefficient in both cases is equal to $m_{k-2}$. We have:
$$A_kC = BC + A_kB = A_{k-2}F + m_{k-2}A_{k-1}D = p_{k-4} + m_{k-2}p_{k-3} = p_{k-2},$$
$$A_kH = EH + A_kE = A_{k-2}G + m_{k-2}A_{k-1}M = q_{k-4} + m_{k-2}q_{k-3} = q_{k-2}$$
(see article by N. M. Beskin).

The theorem has been proven.

Theorem 3, together with the corollary of Theorem 2, leads us to the following conclusion:
If for a given irrational number $\alpha$ there are an infinite number of approximations with a quality coefficient less than the given one, then there are an infinite number of them among the suitable fractions of the number $\alpha$.

The last question remains: how to find the quality coefficients of suitable fractions?

Fortunately, this question can be completely resolved, and the answer is written down in an elegant and convenient formula.

§ 3. Quality coefficients of suitable fractions.

Theorem 4. Let $\alpha=[0; m_1, m_2, \dots]$, $\mu_k$ be the quality coefficient of the $k-1$th suitable fraction. Then:

$$\frac{1}{\mu_k} = m_k + [0; m_{k+1}, m_{k+2}, \dots] + [0; m_{k-1}, m_{k-2}, \dots, m_1]$$
