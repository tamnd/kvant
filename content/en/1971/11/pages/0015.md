Proof.
Consider the points $A_{k+2}$ and $A_{k+1}$, corresponding to the approximations $\alpha_k$ and $\alpha_{k-1}$ (Fig. 10). Through $q_k$, $r_k$; $q_{k-1}$, $r_{k-1}$ we denote their distances to the axes $OX$, $OY$. We know that $\mu_k = r_{k-1}q_{k-1}$ (see § 3). Let's prove the formula:

$$q_k r_{k-1} + q_{k-1} r_k = 1$$ (3)
$$r_k / r_{k-1} = [0; m_{k+1}, m_{k+2}, \dots]$$ (4)
$$\frac{q_k}{q_{k-1}} = [m_k; m_{k-1}, m_{k-2}, \dots, m_1]$$ (5)

Formula (3) proves this. Divide the parallelogram $OA_{k+1}BA_{k+2}$ with straight lines parallel to the coordinate axes into triangles: $A_{k+1}QO$, $OPA_{k+2}$, $A_{k+2}MB$, $BRA_{k+1}$ and a straight line $MPQR$ (see Fig. 11). The area of ​​the parallelogram $OA_{k+1}BA_{k+2}$ is 1, since this is the main parallelogram of our lattice. Finding the areas of the parts:

$$1 = \frac{1}{2} r_{k-1} q_{k-1} + \frac{1}{2} r_k q_k + \frac{1}{2} r_{k-1} q_{k-1} + \frac{1}{2} r_k q_k + (r_{k-1} - r_k) (q_k - q_{k-1}) = q_k r_{k-1} + q_{k-1} r_k.$$

Formula (4), in essence, is already known to us: if we discard the part of our nose-pulling algorithm that precedes obtaining the point $A_{k+2}$, we obtain the same algorithm for the vectors $OA_{k+1}$, $OA_{k-2}$. Therefore, formula (4) follows directly from formula (2).
