IN TASKS
ON UNIFORM
PROCESSES

B. A. Kordemsky

Mathematics entrance exams offer a wide variety of equation problems. Many of them fall into the category of tasks that describe uniform processes: moving at a constant speed, supplying water through a pipe with a constant throughput, performing work at constant labor productivity, paying for a process at a constant rate, and others.

Often, such problems are easiest to solve geometrically, by constructing a graph of the relationship between the quantities discussed in the conditions of the problem.

On the graph, a uniform process is depicted by a straight line (Fig. 1); in this case, the dependent and independent variables $y$ and $x$ are interconnected by correspondence

$$y = kx + b^*$$

Each point $M$ of the graph depicts a certain “state” of the uniform process under consideration: abscissa $x_0$ and ordinata $y_0$ points $M$ tell about the magnitude values ​​characterizing the process.

For example, when a body moves uniformly along a straight line, taken as the coordinate axis, the graph of its coordinate versus time is represented as a straight line **).

At figure 2 the abscissa and ordinate of the point $M_0$ show that someone, having traveled 3.5 km (OA km) by taxi, paid 45 kopecks (OB kopeck) for the journey.

At figure 3 straight line $AD$ is a graph of the length of the arc of a uniformly burning candle. Line segment $OA$ (taken on the scale of axis $Oy$)

*) In general, a straight line can also be specified by a correspondence $x = c$ (y is arbitrary), but such equations usually do not arise in problems, since in this case $y$ does not depend on $x$.

**) See article by Yu. V. Zaichkov “Motion Graphs”, «Kvant» No. 6, 1970, pp. 39-45.
