V Weighing mathematical optimization.
V. L. Gutenkhær

From April 15 to April 20, the V Weighing Mathematical Optimization took place in Prgue. But the jury was optimized (chairman Jan Bardzin, deputy chairman Nikolai Vasiliev) began its work on April 13. From these tasks that the jury members brought with them, it was necessary to display the most interesting and original tasks. This time, unlike previous years, the jury decided to give schoolchildren problems that would be close to serious mathematical science, and in the second round they even conducted a small experiment: to offer one more difficult problem, breaking it down into a series of gradually conditioned questions, in the solution of which it would be proposed to advance as far as possible.

The first and second rounds of optimization took place on April 16 and 17 separately for the 8th, 9th and 10th grades.

We place below a list of 23 tasks that were offered to schoolchildren. The average ones are essentially 14 different problems in total. We publish 10 problems in the “Kvant Problem Book” (see «Kvant» No. 10, 11).

TASKS

8th grade (first day)

1. Prove that there is a value of a number divisible by 2$^{100}$, in whose decimal notation only the digits 1 and 2 are involved.

2. Given a triangle $A_1A_2A_3$. On its side $A_1A_2$ the points $B_1$ and $D_2$ are taken, on the side $A_2A_3$ - points $B_2$ and $D_3$, on the side $A_3A_1$ - points $B_3$ and $D_1$ so that if we construct parallelgrams $A_1B_1C_1D_1$, $A_2B_2C_2D_2$ and $A_3B_3C_3D_3$, then the lines $A_1C_1$, $A_2C_2$ and $A_3C_3$ will intersect at one point. Prove that if $A_1B_1=A_2D_2$ and $A_2B_2=A_3D_3$, then $A_3B_3=A_1D_1$.

3. Two people play this game. The first ones write two rows of 10 numbers one below the other so that the following rule is satisfied: if the number $b$ is written under the number $a$, and the number $d$ is written under the number $c$, then $a+d=b+c$. The second player, knowing this rule, wants to determine all the written numbers.

He is allowed to ask the first player a question like “What number is in third place in the first line?” or “What number is in ninth place on the second line?” etc. In what is the smallest number of such questions can the second player find out all the numbers?
