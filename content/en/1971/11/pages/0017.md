Strictly speaking, the case when all incomplete partials are equal to 1 has not been considered. In this case we have

$$\frac{1}{\mu_k} = 1 + [0; 1, 1, \dots] + [0; 1, 1, \dots, 1]$$

and for even $k$ we get

$$\frac{1}{\mu_k} < 1 + 2 [0; 1, 1, \dots] = \sqrt{5}$$

due to the properties of continued fractions.

In all cases, the existence of an infinite multi-approximation with a quality coefficient less than $1/\sqrt{5}$ is proven.

It is clear that at the same time in the case $3^\circ$ and only in this case the number $\sqrt{5}$ cannot be notified. Strictly speaking, in this case

$$\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{1}{\mu_k} = \sqrt{5},$$

that is, for very large $k$, the number $1/\mu_k$ is very close to $\sqrt{5}$.

Thus, we have proven all statements of the Hurwitz-Borel theorem.

In fact, we have proven even more. It is clear that Theorem 7 allows us to solve the question of the best approximations of the individual for each $\alpha$. For example, if the partial partials of a given number $\alpha$ are not all 1, starting from some number, that is, if the case $3^\circ$ does not occur, then, as can be seen from our reasoning, $\sqrt{5}$ can be replaced by $2^2/3$ (and in fact even by $2\sqrt{2}$; prove). From Theorem 7 it follows that

$$m_k < \frac{1}{\mu_k} < m_k + 2.$$

From this it is clear that if incomplete partial numbers $\alpha$ grow unlimitedly, then the number $\frac{1}{\mu_k}$ can be arbitrarily large, that is, the number $\mu_k$ can be arbitrarily small, that is, for a given $\alpha$ there are approximations with an arbitrarily small quality factor.

If the numbers $m_k$ are all smaller than a certain number $N$, then the quality coefficient of approximations of this number cannot be less than $\frac{1}{N+2}$. For example, the quality coefficient of rational approximations of the number $\sqrt{2} = [1; 2, 2, \dots]$ cannot be less than $\frac{1}{4}$ (in fact, the smallest quality coefficient among the approximations of the number $\sqrt{2}$ has the fraction $\frac{3}{2}$; this coefficient is equal to $6 - 4\sqrt{2} \approx 0,34$).

So, surprisingly, it turns out that the “best” approximate numbers, like $\sqrt{2}$, are the worst approximations by rational numbers.
