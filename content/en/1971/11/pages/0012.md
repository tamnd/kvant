We get two broken lines: $A_1 A_3 A_5 \dots$ and $A_2 A_4 A_6 \dots$, volumetric axis $l$ (Fig. 3). It may happen that one of the points $A_k$ lands directly on $l$ - this is where the process stops. It may turn out that this will not happen - then the process will be endless.

All together is called the nose-pulling algorithm (ABH).

Let us now project all the constructions onto a straight line perpendicular to the $l$ axis. The axis $l$ itself will go into one point, the vectors $a_k$ into segments, which we denote by $b_k (k=1, 2, 3, \dots)$. Let us obtain the Euclidean algorithm already known to us for the segments $b_1$ and $b_2$. In particular,

$$\frac{b_1}{b_2} = \left[ m_1; m_2, m_3, m_4, \dots \right]$$

and, therefore,

$$\frac{b_2}{b_1} = \left[ 0; m_1, m_2, m_3, \dots \right].$$

Now we will show how to use ABH to solve our geometric problem (see § 1). Let us consider the lattice mentioned in § 1, the points of which correspond to the approximation of the number $\alpha$ (it is convenient for us to assume that $0 < \alpha < 1$; obviously, when proving the Hurwitz-Borel theorem we can limit ourselves to such numbers).

Let us take ABH to the vectors $a_1 = \vec{OA}_1$ and $a_2 = \vec{OA}_2$, on which our lattice is built, and to the axis $l = OY$. It turns out two broken lines $A_1 A_3 A_5 \dots$, $A_2 A_4 A_6 \dots$, solid axis $OY$ (Fig. 4).

The numbers $m_1, m_2, \dots$ appearing in ABH are nothing more than partial partial numbers $\alpha$. More precisely, the expansion of $\alpha$ into a continued fraction looks like this:

$$\alpha = \left[ 0; m_1, m_2, \dots \right]$$

(recall that $0 < \alpha < 1$).

Let us now prove several properties of our broken lines.

Theorem 1. The points $A_1, A_2, A_3, \dots$ are lattice points.

The validity of this assertion is clear from figure 4. Its complete proof is based on Theorem 2 of Part I. We leave the details to the reader.

Theorem 2. Between the broken lines $A_1, A_3 A_5 \dots$ and $A_2 A_4 A_6 \dots$ (maybe in the protected area in the drawing) the power of the lattice is mute.

Proof. ABH consists of sequential deposition of vectors: $A_1 B, BA_3; A_2 C, CA_4; A_3 D, DE; \dots$. For each such vector, we construct a parallelogram, the sides of which are this vector and the vector to which it is parallel. We obtain a sequence of parallelograms

$$OA_2 BA_1, OA_2 A_3 B, OA_2 CA_3, OCA_4 A_3, OA_4 DA_3, \dots$$
