⟦folio 59⟧

MATHEMATICS

1. Three points $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are given on the plane that do not lie on the same line. The fourth point $A$, different from them, is symmetrically reflected with respect to $P_1$, the resulting point is symmetrically reflected with respect to $P_2$, the new point is reflected with respect to $P_3$, then with respect to $P_1, P_2, P_3, P_1$, and so on. Does the resulting point necessarily coincide with the original point $A$ at some step? (10)

2. A quadrilateral is inscribed in the circle, after which a circle is inscribed in each of the four formed segments, touching the corresponding side of the quadrilateral in its middle (Fig. 1). For each quadrilateral, the sum of the lengths of the resulting four circles will be the smallest? (10)

3. It is easy to prove that the square $10 \times 10, 20 \times 20, 30 \times 30$, etc., are covered by figures of the form

![Graph](image)

from the heel of the cells, but the square $5 \times 5$ is not (the side of the cell is taken as one).

Is it possible to cover squares $15 \times 15, 25 \times 25, 35 \times 35$, etc. with such figures? (9-10)

4. Five nodes are selected on an infinite sheet of checkered paper (a node is the point where linear lines intersect). Prove that from these five nodes it is certainly possible to choose two such that the segment connecting them passes through another grid node. (9-10)

5. Draw a chord that is divided by two given radii of given

Fig. 1.

of the circle in this relation $k_1:k_2:k_3$. (9-10)

6. Inscribe in this triangle $ABC$ a parallelogram $BMNK$ so that the points $K, M, N$ lie on the sides $AB, BC$ and $AC$, respectively, and so that the diagonal $KM$ has the given length. (9)

7. Prove that the number 11...11, written in $2^n$ units, has at least $2^n$ different divisors (including 1 and the number itself). (8-9)

8. Prove that in a right-angled triangle the ratio of the square of the sunny circle to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the medians drawn from the acute angles does not exceed 1/5. (8-9)

9. Ten backpacks contain 50 cans of canned food, and different backpacks contain different numbers of cans. It is allowed to “equalize” the number of cans in two backpacks, that is, transfer several cans from a backpack with a large number of cans to a backpack with a smaller number so that the difference in the number of cans in them becomes minimal. Prove that after a certain number of such “equalizations” it is possible to achieve that there are equal to 5 cans in each backpack. (8)
