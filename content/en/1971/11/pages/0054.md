at a distance $1/3$ of the length of the highway, azonchik $C$ - in front of the end $B$ to the maximum distance. Gontsik $A$ donated for the first time. B at the moment when $B$ finished his first line, and after another 10 minutes $A$ donated the end of $C$ for the first time. Gonzik $B$ will arrive on the edge 2.5 minutes less than $C$. How long will it take for the tip $A$ to arrive?

It is not surprising to get confused in this network of intertwining movements, if therefore, in the “habitual” scheme of composing a control system*. But this entire network becomes transparent and leads to only one equation if we use graphs.

Solution i.e. Let $Ox$ be the time axis, $Oy$ the distance axis (Fig. 7), $OM_1=1$ the length of the ring highway, $M_1M_2$ the second “turn” of the same highway. The points $B$, $C$ and $D$ are such that

$$OB=BC=CM_1=M_1D=1/3.$$

Straight line $OA$ is the expected movement schedule of the messenger $A$. Let's construct a straight line $DD_1 \parallel Ox$ and mark the point $E$ of its intersection with $OA$; according to the condition, $BE$ is the movement schedule of the messenger $B$. Let $x$ be the abscissa of the point $E$; Let's mark a point $F$ on the axis $Ox$ such that $OF=x+10$, and draw a straight line $FG \perp Ox$ to the intersection with $OA$ at point $G$. Then, by condition, $CG$ is the movement schedule of the messenger $C$.

From figure we find

$$v_A=\frac{OD}{x}=\frac{4}{3x}, \quad v_B=\frac{BD}{x}=\frac{1}{x},$$

in addition, according to the condition,

$$v_C=\frac{1}{x+2.5}.$$

Let's build $CC_1 \parallel Ox$; it is clear that the difference between the paths $FG-C_1G$ of the messengers $A$ and $C$ in time $x+10$ (min.) is $FC_1=OC=\frac{2}{3}$. We get the equation

$$\frac{4}{3x}(x+10)-\frac{1}{x+2.5}(x+10)=\frac{2}{3}.$$

Solving it, we find $x=20$ (min.). During this time, the messenger $A$ makes a circle $4/3$
