Problem 5. What is the minimum horizontal force that can be used to tip a cube lying on a horizontal plane over an edge?

Typically, when solving such a problem, they proceed from the fact that when overturning, the moment of the applied force $F$ relative to the rotation axis $O$ (Fig. 7) is balanced by the moment of gravity. By choosing the point $O$ for the moment equation, we exclude the floor reaction force from the distance and simplify the solution of the problem. It is clear that the force $F$ will be minimal when it is applied to the top face of the cube and is equal to $\frac{mg}{2}$.

Is such an overturn possible at any coefficient of friction between the cup and the floor? Obviously not. For tipping, it is necessary that at $F = \frac{mg}{2}$ the cube has not yet begun to slide along the plane. Therefore, $\frac{mg}{2} \leq F_{трмax} = kmg$, or $k \geq \frac{1}{2}$.

It is interesting that the cube can be overturned even with a less friction coefficient by a force less than $\frac{mg}{2}$. Think about how to direct the force.

Exercises

1. What should the coefficient of friction be so that a wedge hammered into a log does not slip out of it? The angle at the top of the wedge is $30^\circ$.

2. On an inclined plane with an angle of inclination $k$ to the horizon $\alpha$ there is a cylinder of radius $r$. What is the highest height of the cylinder at which it still does not tip over? The cylinder is made of a homogeneous material.

3. A wheel of radius $r$ and mass $m$ stands in front of a step of height $h$. What is the smallest horizontal force $F$ that must be applied to the axis of the wheel so that it can rise up the step?

4. Two identical cylindrical logs lie close together on the ground. The same log is placed on top of them. At what minimum coefficient of friction between the logs will they not roll out?
