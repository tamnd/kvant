Energy of interaction of molecules in gases, liquids and solids

Let us now establish the number of criteria for various gases, liquids and solids from the point of view of molecular-kinetic theory.

Gases

You can get a deeper understanding of the state of matter called a real gas if you picture the dependence of the potential energy of one of the molecules on the distance to its close neighbors (Fig. 10). When a molecule moves, its potential energy is almost exactly zero over most of its path, since the distance between molecules in a gas is, on average, much greater than their size. The average potential energy of a molecule is negative and very small. Its magnitude is equal to the area of ​​the figure limited by the potential curve between points 1 and 2 and the $r$ axis, divided by the length of the segment 1-2 (the average value of the segment 1-2). The total average energy is necessarily greater than zero (straight line at Fig. 10), since at $E < 0$ we would have a bound state of molecules. This is possible only if the average kinetic energy of a molecule in a gas is greater than the average value of its potential energy:

$$\bar{E}_{\kappa} > |\bar{U}|.$$

After all

$$\bar{E} = \bar{E}_{\kappa} + \bar{U},$$

and $\bar{U}$ is negative.
