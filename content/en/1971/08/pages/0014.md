Radioactive state of the atmosphere

I have briefly discussed some of the most important properties of radioactive bodies, which have proven very useful in solving the problem of the abundance of radioactive substances in the earth and atmosphere. The pioneers in this area were Elster and Geitel, teachers of the gymnasium in Wolfenbitt, Germany.

Geitel in 1900 noticed that the opening of air has the property of an arbitrary weak discharge of the electroscope. He showed that this phenomenon is caused by the formation of negative and positive ions in the air. In search of a possible cause for this phenomenon, Elster and Geitel proved that it could be associated with the presence of radioactive substances in the atmosphere. Then they tried to carry out an extremely daring experiment. I already point out that the emanation from radioactive bodies converts active residue, which can concentrate on negatively charged wiring. If there is emanation in the atmosphere, then the active deposit must collect on a negatively charged conductor placed in the atmosphere.

Elster and Geitel strung an insulated wire 20 or 30 m long (Fig. 1) outside the laboratory window. Using an electrical machine, the wire was charged to a negative potential of several thousand volts. After a few hours, the wire was quickly cleaned, welded around a frame attached to the electroscope, as shown in figure2, and the discharge rate of the electroscope was changed.
