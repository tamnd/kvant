FOUR EXPERIENCES WITH VISION

J. Greg

In the 10th and 11th issues «Kvant» over the past year, we have already talked about some experiments with black-and-white and color vision (see articles by G.I. Kosourov “Don’t believe your eyes” and “Orange sky”). Now we will tell you about several more worthy experiments with vision. With them, given at the beginning, the features of our vision are used - the ability to see in the dark and retain the memory of the images seen for some time. The last experiment demonstrates another nice visual illusion. All experiments are taken from the book by James Gregg, which we will talk about on page 60 in the same issue of our magazine. The publication was prepared by V. A. Leshkovtsev.

About test 1. Adaptation to darkness

On a bright sunny day, when you enter an unlit room from the street, it seems to you that it is deep night in the house. But soon you are already perfectly oriented, despite the relatively low light: your eyes have adapted - adaptation to the dark has occurred. As a result of adaptation, the color of the eye to light increases a million times. Under ideal conditions, an eye that is fully adapted to darkness can notice the light from a single candle 20 km away.

Observe the adaptation process very simply: in the evening, go from a well-lit room to a dark one. Notice how long it takes you to read the numbers on a watch with a luminous face or to read a newspaper headline if there is little light coming from a window or a slightly open door.

Very weak light is better seen if you look at a little bit of it. So, a dim star in the sky will seem brighter if you look a little away from it. Check it out when you get a chance.
