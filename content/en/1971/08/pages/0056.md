In order for a heat engine to work (that is, the condition $\eta > 0$ is fulfilled), a temperature difference between the heater and the “refrigerator” is necessary. It would be nice to bring $T_2$ closer to zero or $T_1$ to infinity; then the efficiency would be close to unity, and this is an ancient dream technically. But $T_1$ cannot be too large, it is limited at least by the melting of materials. And it is especially impossible to reduce $T_2$, because for intensive cooling you need to increase $T_2$, on the contrary. The compromise solution gives $\eta \sim 10\%$.

Let, for example, $T_1 = 2000^\circ$ K. (Metals such as tungsten do not float.) Then from formula (2) we obtain that $T_2 = 1800^\circ$ K. Next, from (1) we find the maximum radiation flux $q = \sigma T_2^4 = 6 \cdot 10^5$ W/m$^2$.

Then, to emit the useless energy of the reactor $(1 - \eta)$ $W$ an area of ​​no less than

$$S = \frac{(1 - \eta) W}{q} = \frac{0,9 \cdot 10^{10}}{6 \cdot 10^5} = 1,5 \cdot 10^4 \text{ m}^2.$$
