Pythagoras (VI century BC). Portrait from a fresco by Raphael. The personality of Pythagoras already became popular in ancient times. He founded a mathematical and philosophical school, and many remarkable scientists of Greece called themselves followers of Pythagoras.

Many learned societies were supposed to have members, often simply due to prejudice, the reasons for which were forgotten long ago.

Ancient mathematicians were surprised by a special property of these two numbers: each of them is equal to the sum of all its own divisors:

$$6 = 1 + 2 + 3,$$
$$28 = 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14.$$

Before Euclid, only these two perfect numbers were known, and no one knew whether there were other perfect numbers or how many such numbers there could be. The great founder of geometry deals a lot with the study of the properties of numbers; Of course, he could not help but be interested in perfect numbers. Euclid proved that any number that can be represented as the product of the factors $2^{p-1}$ and $2^p-1$, where $2^p-1$ is a prime number, is a perfect number.
