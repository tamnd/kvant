⟦folio 32⟧

Place the end of the ruler in the same position where you placed the sheet of paper. Moving the ruler slightly, find such a distance from the projector that any part of the image is clearly focused on the white end of the ruler. Now get started: quickly move the ruler up and down, up and down.

Observe that those standing behind the projector see the full image of the color figure in the air at the place where you move the ruler. This happens because individual pieces of the image, directly visible only at those moments when they are projected onto the ruler, are retained by the eye for some time, enough for you to have time to move the ruler to show the next piece of the frame.

In the same way, a television picture is obtained. Only there is not a ruler, but an electron beam showing horizontal lines of the image one after another. And the whole picture is obtained thanks to the inertia of vision.

About torture 4. Upside-down cube

Using 12 wires or round sticks, make a cube frame as shown in figure 6. The effect will be better if all the material is shown

Fig. 6.

in matte black color. First make two quadrangular sides of the frame, and then fasten them with four sticks. Attach a longer stick - a holder - to one of the corners. Everything can be done with glue and then reinforced with black thread.

Hold the cube in front of you so that its far corner (at figure 6 is a solid arrow) is visible approximately in the middle of one of the sides of the cube (the line of sight at figure 6 is shown by a dotted arrow). Close your other eye.

Look, without looking away, at the far corner of the cube. Suddenly you will see how the cube “turns inside out” so that the far corner appears to be facing forward, the closest one. When the stick is rotated slowly, the cube “flips” in the direction opposite to the direction of rotation. Move the stick back and forth, and you will see how strange the upside-down cube behaves.

Get lost first to learn to see everything properly. It is better to conduct the experiment against the backdrop of a peephole wall. If this doesn't work, try changing the background to rotate the cube, tilt it, or slightly deform it.

With a little fiddling, you will observe the effect of a complete absence of ivo. Remember that it only works when you look with one eye.

This effect is called the von Horibostl cube irony. When both eyes work together, they see depth so well that they cannot be fooled by this cube, but one eye will definitely make a mistake in determining which part of the cube is closer. This iriznyaya is one of the most interesting.

Some people have the effect of receiving it when observing it with their eyes. The main thing is to fix your gaze with both eyes on one (far) corner of the cube.
