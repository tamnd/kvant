Rutherford visiting Capizza. First on the right is E. Rutherford, second is F. Aston, third is P. L. Capizza.

...Rutherford's most remarkable quality as a teacher was his ability to direct work, support the scientist's endeavors and correctly evaluate the results obtained.

The greatest thing he valued in his students was independence of thinking, initiative, and individuality. It must be said that Rutherford used every opportunity to bring out his individuality in a person.

I remember, early in my work at Cambridge, I once said to Rutherford: “We have X working on a hopeless idea and wasting time, equipment and process.” “I know,” Rutherford replied, “that he is working on a hopeless problem, but this is his own problem, and if the work does not work out for him, then it will teach him how to mouse on his own and will lead to another problem, which will already have an experimental solution.” That's how it turned out later.

He was ready to sacrifice a lot just to cultivate independence and originality of thinking in a person, and if they manifested themselves, he surrounded him with care and especially encouraged his work...

...Rutherford believed that a novice scientist should not be given technically difficult work. For a novice worker, even if he is talented, he needs success, otherwise the necessary disappointment in his abilities may occur. If a student has success, then he must be fairly assessed and celebrated.
