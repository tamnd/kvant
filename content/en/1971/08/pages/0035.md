⟦folio 33⟧

TASKS

Solutions to problems from the “Kvant Problem Book” can be sent no later than one and a half spaces after the corresponding issue of the journal goes out of print. The solution to each problem must be written on a separate sheet(s); at the end of each decision you must indicate your last name, first name and patronymic; six-digit postal code, home address; the class and school you attend.

M96. It is known about five positive numbers that if from the sum of any three of them we subtract the sum of the remaining two, then the difference will be positive. Prove that the product of all ten such differences does not exceed the square of the product of the given heel of numbers.
S. T. Berkolaiko

M97. In the trapezoid $ABCD$ with bases $AB=a$ and $CD=b$ there is a segment $A_1B_1$ connecting the midpoints of the diagonals. In the resulting trapezoid $A_1B_1CD$, the segment $A_2B_2$ is again drawn, connecting the midpoints of the diagonals, and so on (Fig. 1). Is it possible for a number to appear twice in the sequence of segment lengths $AB, A_1B_1, A_2B_2, \dots$? Will this sequence be monotonic (increasing or decreasing)? Is she aiming for some kind of limit?
A. L. Rosenmal

M98. Prove that in the table

1
1 1 1
1 2 3 2 1
1 3 6 7 6 3 1

where each number is equal to the sum of the three above it, each line (starting from the third) has an even number. Does each line (except the first two) contain a number divisible by three?

M99. In a triangle, $ABC$ side $AC$ is the largest. Prove that for any point $M$ of the plane $AM+CM$ is at least $BM$. In what cases is equality possible?

N. B. Vasiliev
