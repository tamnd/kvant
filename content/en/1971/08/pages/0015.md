We must now consider the question of how to determine what radioactive substance was concentrated on the wires. The presence of part of solid bodies such as radium, uranium or thorium in the atmosphere is impossible. However, emanations from radium, thorium and active gases are gaseous, and if these substances are present in the earth, then it is quite reasonable to expect that some of the emanation released by them may enter the atmosphere. The question of what kind of emanation this is can be answered in full definition. If there is only an emanation of radium in the air, then the active residue collected on the wires should decay at a rate characteristic of radium, that is, a few hours later the activity decreases exponentially, halving within 28 minutes. It turned out that active sediment from the air will disintegrate almost exactly at this rate. Therefore, we can conclude that there is an emanation of radium in the atmosphere.

Preliminary experiments have shown that the radioactivity of the atmosphere is due to the presence of radium emanations. Bumstead in New York and Blanc in Italy, however, have convincingly shown that in these areas there is some amount of thorium emanation in the air. This can be verified by testing

in the best possible way. The wire is installed outdoors, charged negatively and left for several days. After removing the wires, part of the activity, due to the active deposit of radium, quickly decreases. After 5 or 6 hours there is still activity, but the decline is much slower, decreasing by half every 11 hours. This decay rate is characteristic of the active sediment of thorium and indicates that there is also an emanation of thorium in the atmosphere. Blank has recently shown that in some parts of Italy 70 per cent of the activity must be attributed to thorium, but in most other places it appears that the effect is due to radium.

From the above, we can conclude that the atmosphere above the surface of the Earth contains large quantities of emanations of radium and thorium, as well as active substances that arise during their transformations right there in the atmosphere. The magnitude of their arbitrary onization is small, but can be precisely changed by a device specially designed for this purpose by Ebert.
