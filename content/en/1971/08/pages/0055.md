I wonder how long it will take for its average temperature to drop, for example, by $\Delta T = 2^\circ$? The heat capacity of a human water cylinder is $mc = 50 \text{ ке} \times 1 \text{ ккал/кг} \cdot \text{рад} \approx 200 \text{ кдж/рад}$. Assuming constant heat loss by radiation, we obtain

$$\tau = \frac{mc \Delta T}{Q - Q'} = \frac{200 \cdot 10^3 \frac{\partial JC}{\partial P} \cdot 2^\circ}{375 \frac{\partial JC}{\partial C}} > 15 \text{ мин.}$$

And this is only when cooling from 37 to $35^\circ$! Thus, instant (“faster than thought”) transformation into “super-frozen ice” is still very far away, even in this “coldest” case. (Try to calculate the steady-state temperature at $\tau \rightarrow \infty$.) Now let’s assume that we are in space in the Earth’s orbit around the Sun. Let's find the equilibrium temperature $T_p$ of our body, when it should radiate not only that released by internal heat, but also that heat $q_C \cdot \frac{S}{2}$, which is absorbed in one second by placing our body facing the Sun (the amount of solar radiation flux in the Earth's orbit, the so-called solar constant, is approximately $1400 \text{ вт}/\text{m}^2$). We have the equation

$$q_C \frac{S}{2} + Q' = \sigma T_p^4 S,$$

from which

$$T_p = \sqrt{\frac{(1400 \cdot 0,5 + 150)}{5,7 \cdot 10^{-8}}} = 350^\circ \text{ K} = 77^\circ \text{ C}.$$

This is much more than the doctors can allow us. Of course, you can make the half of the body that is irradiated by the Sun mirrored (but then it will not emit anything), and the other half, from which the internal heat of the organization $Q'$ should be removed, can be made completely black (Fig. 2) *), but this motley harlequin bone will indicate more likely the fear of frying than of freezing in the recognized kingdom of terrible eternal cold.
