In order to get around this difficulty, Sturt suggested that the radioactive substance is not distributed evenly inside the Earth, but only in its thin surface layer. If we assume that radium is evenly distributed in this layer, then it can be easily shown that the thickness of this layer is about 70 km.

Assuming that radium is contained in a surface layer 70 km deep, it is easy to calculate the temperature of the Earth. The calculation results are shown in figure 4.

At a depth of 70 km, the temperature should increase to approximately 1500 °C. The temperature inside the Earth mass below 70 km should be approximately constant and equal to 1500 ° C.

I hope you can now understand how the study of radioactivity has profoundly changed our view of the Earth's interior. The new conclusions are by no means final, but what has been done casts doubt on the validity of the old theories about the origin of the Earth and the changes in its internal body.
