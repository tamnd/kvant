Solution to Apollonius' problem

So, the path on the plane is given by three circles... Stop! Take a look at figure 3. How many more ways can three circles be arranged, even if we cut them to either touch or have no common points at all? What if we consider the possibility of intersections? Should we really consider each case separately? Of course not! Let us first consider at once all cases in which at least two of the given circles touch (it makes no difference whether it is external or internal). Let us assume that it is interesting for an auxiliary circle of arbitrary radius with a center at the point of contact of these circles. Then, by property 7, the tangent circle will turn into parallel lines, the third circle into a circle or straight line. The required circle should be obtained by inverse of the circle tangent to the specified parallel lines and the circle (or straight line). And the problem “to construct a circle tangent to two given parallel lines and a given circle (or straight line)” can be solved by each of you within a few minutes. The construction, of course, must be done using a compass and a ruler. Now let us note that with the help of only a compass and a ruler we can easily construct the circle or straight line into which any given circle or straight line goes when inverted with respect to each line of the circle. To do this, you need to select some three points on it, construct the points at which they pass (this is what you are dealing with), and then draw a circle (or straight line) through the resulting three points.

From all that has been said, the following construction proceeds:

a) construct parallel lines and a circle (or straight line) into which the given circles go when inverted with respect to the auxiliary circle with the center at the point of contact;

b) construct circles (there may be several of them) tangent to the obtained lines and the circle (or straight line);

b) on the three initial given circles, mark those points that go into the found points of contact;

d) for each triple of points, restore the original circles.
