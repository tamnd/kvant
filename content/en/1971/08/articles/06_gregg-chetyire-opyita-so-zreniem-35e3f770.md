FOUR EXPERIENCES WITH VISION

J. Greg

In the 10th and 11th issues «Kvant» over the past year, we have already talked about some experiments with black-and-white and color vision (see articles by G.I. Kosourov “Don’t believe your eyes” and “Orange sky”). Now we will tell you about several more worthy experiments with vision. With them, given at the beginning, the features of our vision are used - the ability to see in the dark and retain the memory of the images seen for some time. The last experiment demonstrates another nice visual illusion. All experiments are taken from the book by James Gregg, which we will talk about on page 60 in the same issue of our magazine. About test 1. Adaptation to darkness

On a bright sunny day, when you enter an unlit room from the street, it seems to you that it is deep night in the house. But soon you are already perfectly oriented, despite the relatively low light: your eyes have adapted - adaptation to the dark has occurred. As a result of adaptation, the color of the eye to light increases a million times. Under ideal conditions, an eye that is fully adapted to darkness can notice the light from a single candle 20 km away.

Observe the adaptation process very simply: in the evening, go from a well-lit room to a dark one. Notice how long it takes you to read the numbers on a watch with a luminous face or to read a newspaper headline if there is little light coming from a window or a slightly open door.

Very weak light is better seen if you look at a little bit of it. So, a dim star in the sky will seem brighter if you look a little away from it. Check it out when you get a chance.

Let us present a simple experiment using the adaptation of the eye to darkness.

Take a piece of black cardboard measuring 20x25 (cm). On both sides of the center at a distance of 5 cm, glue a white circle and square to the cardboard. Hold the cardboard like a book while reading (Fig. 1).

O torture 2. Followers

The number of successors you can observe is endless. The term "after-time" means that the image remains after the discharger that caused it ceases to act. A sequence is called positive when it contains the same distribution of bright and dark areas as the discharger itself, and imprinted when the distribution of bright and dark areas is the opposite.

Nanomore interesting are the induced afterproducts. The induction effect is caused by something additional to the main discharger, such as the background against which the view is viewed successively; this background can be either a pattern, a gray field, or a colored field. First, it always shows the main discharger - figure, color. Then watch looking at some background. It is easiest to detect the induced sequence on a neutral gray background. figure2 shows figures with which you can get a good follow-up. Such figures should be glued onto gray cardboard. Leave only one figure in your field of vision and look at it for 30-60 seconds. Then look at the gray background. There should not be too much light in the room (dark lighting). You may see a bird flying on the ceiling, on the wall, on your palm, or your eye projects simultaneously onto any surface that you are looking at.

Cut out any images from colored paper. Experiment with shapes of different colors. Look at such a figure, move your gaze to the gray background - and you will see an additional color to the original one. Thus, you will become acquainted with one of the basic principles of forming induced sequences.

Take a background of the same color as the first discharger. Look at the bright red square for a minute, and then move your gaze to a piece of red paper. What does one after the other look like?

Leave the same main discharger, but take the background green-blue, that is, the color complementary to red. How does the current sequence differ from the previous one and from the one that turned out on a gray background? Try different colors and different combinations for the main discharger and for the background.

Experience 3. Inertia of vision

The horse running on the movie screen, of course, is in no way dead. She is not dead on the television screen either. And in general, the “moving” objects in the frame are motionless - the frames themselves move, but at such a speed that the human eye does not replace their change. You can see movement on the screen because human vision has a certain inertia. In other words, any visual sensation persists for some time after the image that caused it disappears. Therefore, if the images change often enough, the visible image of the previous image smoothly passes into the subsequent image, and under certain conditions they can be seen simultaneously.

in matte black color. First make two quadrangular sides of the frame, and then fasten them with four sticks. Attach a longer stick - a holder - to one of the corners. Everything can be done with glue and then reinforced with black thread.

Hold the cube in front of you so that its far corner (at figure 6 is a solid arrow) is visible approximately in the middle of one of the sides of the cube (the line of sight at figure 6 is shown by a dotted arrow). Close your other eye.

Look, without looking away, at the far corner of the cube. Suddenly you will see how the cube “turns inside out” so that the far corner appears to be facing forward, the closest one. When the stick is rotated slowly, the cube “flips” in the direction opposite to the direction of rotation. Move the stick back and forth, and you will see how strange the upside-down cube behaves.

Get lost first to learn to see everything properly. It is better to conduct the experiment against the backdrop of a peephole wall. If this doesn't work, try changing the background to rotate the cube, tilt it, or slightly deform it.

With a little fiddling, you will observe the effect of a complete absence of ivo. Remember that it only works when you look with one eye.

This effect is called the von Horibostl cube irony. When both eyes work together, they see depth so well that they cannot be fooled by this cube, but one eye will definitely make a mistake in determining which part of the cube is closer. This iriznyaya is one of the most interesting.

Some people have the effect of receiving it when observing it with their eyes. The main thing is to fix your gaze with both eyes on one (far) corner of the cube.
