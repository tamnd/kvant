§ 2. Lattices

Let us assume that two intersecting lines are given on the plane: $l_0$ and $m_0$ and, in addition, two scale segments are fixed: $a$ and $b$. On both sides of line $l_0$ we draw parallel lines $l_{\pm 1}, l_{\pm 2}$... at a distance $a, 2a$, ... from it.

Similarly, on both sides of $m_0$ at a distance $b, 2b$, ... draw lines $m_{\pm 1}, m_{\pm 2}$, ... Mark all points of intersection of lines $l_i$ with lines $m_j$. The set of these points is called a lattice (Fig. 2).

We emphasize that we call a lattice the set of points of intersection of lines, without referring to the most straight lines. This is important because the same lattice can be obtained from completely different families of lines. Thus, the lattice shown in figure 3 is obtained both by the intersection of blue lines and by the intersection of red lines.

There is no need to look for examples of daleko grilles. The intersection points of the lines in the “checkered” terraces make up a lattice. And also, the points in the terrades in which the first classicists write do not form a grid - the distances between adjacent horizontal lines are not the same (Fig. 4).
