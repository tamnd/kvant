Let's use the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{mv_2^2}{2} + \frac{mv_1^2}{4}.$$

Eliminating $v_2$ from these equations, we find

$$v_1 = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} v.$$

The vector nature of momentum can also be taken into account. Let us introduce mutually perpendicular coordinate axes $x$ and $y$.

For projections of momentum onto such axes, scalar relations are satisfied

$$mv_{2y} + \frac{m}{2} v_{1y} = 0,$$

$$mv_{2x} + \frac{m}{2} v_{1x} = mv.$$

The law of conservation of energy will take the form

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = m \frac{v_{2y}^2 + v_{2x}^2}{2} + \frac{m}{2} \frac{v_{1x}^2 + v_{1y}^2}{2}.$$

Having solved this system and taking into account the next relation $v_1 = \sqrt{v_{1x}^2 + v_{1y}^2}$, we get the same answer.
