6. Since the square triangular $ax^2+bx+c$ has no real roots, then $b^2-4ac<0$, hence $ac>\frac{b^2}{4}$. Let us prove that $c(a+b+c)>0$.

Really,

$$c(a+b+c)=ac+bc+c^2>\frac{b^2}{4}+bc+c^2=\left(\frac{b}{2}+c\right)^2\geq 0.$$

Since in a positive product one factor $a+b+c$ is negative, then the other factor $c$ is negative. So, $c<0$.

Solution option. It is known that if a square triangular $ax^2+bx+c$ is not equal to zero for any $x$, then its sign is the same for all $x$. Note that the number $a+b+c$ is the value of our triangle at $x=1$, and the number $c$ is its value at $x=0$. This means that these two numbers have the same sign. Since $a+b+c<0$, so is $c<0$.
