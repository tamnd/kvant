5. Let $CAC_1 = 2\alpha$ (Fig. 3). According to conditions $tg2\alpha = \frac{4}{3}$. Since $AN$ is the bisector of angle $CAC_1$, using known trigonometric formulas it is easy to find that $(\Rightarrow NAC = \alpha)$

$$\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{V_5}, \cos \alpha = \frac{2}{V_5}.$$

Let us denote $\Rightarrow ACB$ in triangle $CAB$ by $\beta$. Now it is easy to write the volume of this prism as follows:

$$V = \frac{1}{2}AC \cdot CB \sin \beta \cdot AA_1 = 24CB \sin \beta.$$

The plane passing through the points $A, M$ and $N$ cuts off the paramid $ABCNM$ from the prism, whose lateral face $ACB$ is perpendicular to the base, since the prism $ABCA_1B_1C_1$ is straight (by condition). The base $BCNM$ is a trapezoid, the plane sides $CN$ and $BM$ are parallel. From equalities

$$BM = \frac{BB_1}{2} = 4, CN = AC \cdot tg\alpha = 3$$

we find: $S_{BCNM} = \frac{7}{2}BC$ (BC is perpendicular to $CN$ and $BM$ and is the height of the trapezoid). The volume of the paramid is $ABCNM$, therefore equal to

$$V = \frac{1}{3}S_{BCNM} \cdot AC \sin \beta = 7CB \sin \beta.$$

The volume of the other part cut off by the plane from the prism is the difference between the volumes of the prism and paramid: $V_2 = V - V_1$. From here

$$V_1 : V_2 = V_1 : (V - V_1) = 7 : 17.$$

In this problem, it was not necessary to find the numerical values ​​of the volumes of the parts of the prism that interest us; the plane only needed their ratio. To get the answer, it was enough to express them through the same, although unknown to us, quantities $BC$ and $\beta$. However, this very circumstance was not understood by some of the applicants who are trying hard.
