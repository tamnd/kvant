Indeed, on each of the lines $y=1, y=2, \dots, y=kN$ the lattice points lie with intervals equal to 1; therefore, exactly one lattice point lies on the segment of each of these straight lines enclosed in the rectangle $\tilde{P}$ (the lattice points do not fall on the vertical sides of the rectangle $\tilde{P}$, since their abscissas are irrational). Divide the rectangle $\tilde{P}$ into $N$ equal rectangles $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_N$ with the base $\frac{1}{N}$.

It is clear that either each of them contains exactly $k$ lattice points, or one of them (say, $p_m$) contains at least $k+1$ lattice points (say, $A_0, A_1, \dots, A_k$).

In the first case, the theorem to be proved is obtained immediately: the rectangle $P$ obviously contains one of the rectangles $p_i$, which means it contains $k$ lattice points.

In the second case, we perform the following construction (Fig. 9). To determine the type, assume that $A_0$ is the lowest point of $A_0, \dots, A_k$. Let's connect point $A_0$ with point $O$ and draw segments equal and parallel to $A_0O$ from points $A_1, \dots, A_k$.

Let these be segments $A_1B_1, \dots, A_kB_k$.

The points $B_1, \dots, B_k$ belong to the lattice $R$; in fact, the figure $A_0OB_iA_i$ with $1 \leq i \leq k$ is a parallelogram (since $A_0O \parallel A_iB_i, A_0O=A_iB_i$) and the points $A_0, O, A_i$ belong to $R$; This means that by the parallelogram property (Theorem 2) $B_i$ belongs to $R$.

The points $B_1, \dots, B_k$ lie in the rectangle $P$. Indeed, for $1 \leq i \leq k$ the segments $A_0A_i$ and $OB_i$ are equal and parallel, and, therefore, there are projections onto the $Ox$ axis. But the segment $A_0A_i$ lies in the rectangle $p_m$ and its projection onto the axis $Ox$ is less than $\frac{1}{N}$. This means that the abscissa of the point $B_i$ according to the model is less than $\frac{1}{N}$, that is, $B_i$ lies in $P$. So, $B_1, \dots, B_k$ are $k$ lattice points lying in the rectangle $P$. The theorem is proven.

§ 5. Conclusion

From Theorem 3 other interesting consequences can be obtained.

For example, this:

Theorem 4. There is an infinite set of fractions $\frac{p}{q}$ maximum

$$\left| \alpha - \frac{p}{q} \right| < \frac{1}{q^2}.$$

Proof. In Theorem 3 we take $k=1$. Then, according to this theorem, for each $N$ there is an approximation $\frac{p}{q}$ of the number $\alpha$ such that $q \leq N$ and

$$q \left| \alpha - \frac{p}{q} \right| < \frac{1}{N}.$$
