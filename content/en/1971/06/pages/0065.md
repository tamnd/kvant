3. When the key is switched, a redistribution of charges in the capacitor system will occur. The performance of a battery is determined by the product of the charge flowing through it and the em.c. This work, generally speaking, can be spent partly on increasing the energy of the capacitors, partly on the release of heat in the circuit. In our circuit, the capacitance does not change (in both cases, two identical capacitors connected in parallel are connected in series with one capacitor of the same capacitance). For example, on a system of capacitors it is also constant and equal to $E$. Consequently, the energy of the system does not change, and all the work done turns into heat.
To calculate this work, it is necessary to determine the charge flowing through the battery. The easiest way to do this is to monitor the charge on the left capacitor. Before switching, this capacitor contains half the system charge, that is, $\frac{1}{3} CE'$ (the system capacity is $\frac{2}{3} C$).
After switching, the charge was satisfied.
This means that charge $\frac{1}{3} CE^2$ will flow through the battery. This work will turn into heat, which will be released in the circuit (in the connected wires, contacts and at the internal resistance of the battery).
