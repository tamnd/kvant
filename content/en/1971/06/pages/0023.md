Fig. 4. A four-row step, formed due to the presence of four dislocations of the same type located very close to each other. A series of a large number of dislocations produces multi-edge steps.

The height of the crystal step can be changed. It turned out that some crystals have steps many rows of atoms high (Fig. 5). The theory explains the appearance of such multi-row steps as starting from a group of closely spaced dislocations, which, moreover, are either all right-handed or all left-handed. From each dislocation the step receives one row of atoms. figure 6 shows a crystal of podmium kadmium with a step many rows high. A short straight line at the center of the spiral is a collection of screw dislocations.

The theory helped to understand other features of the crystal structure that had long mystified crystallographers. We are talking about the packing of crystal planes of a crystal.
