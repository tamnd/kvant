By introducing impurities of higher valency into the semiconductor, we can always obtain a sufficient number of negative current carriers. In this case, the impurity atoms are called donors, and the semiconductors themselves containing donor-type impurities are called $n$-type semiconductors.

It is possible to introduce trivalent atoms, for example, aluminum, into a thermanium crystal. An aluminum atom, in order to sit firmly in a thermanium crystal, must have two outer electrons, so it captures the missing electron from neighboring thermanium atoms (Fig. 3). A hole is formed in the crystal. Impurity atoms of this type are called $^*$ acceptors, and semiconductors are called $p$-type semiconductors.
