Written exam
in physics at MIPT in 1970

V. E. Belonuchkin, S. M. Kozel

The written entrance exam in physics is not held in all universities. However, the tasks offered in the written work are also interesting for those who are only going to take the oral exam. Here we will analyze the tasks of one of the options for written work in physics for the entrance exam at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology.

Vilet No. 6

1. A particle of mass $m$, moving with a speed $v$, collides with a resting part of the mass $\frac{m}{2}$ and, after an urgent impact, rebounds at an angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$ to the direction of its initial motion. At what speed does the second part begin to move?

2. A standard cylinder contains 0.2 $e$ hydrogen and 3.2 $e$ oxygen at a temperature of 27°C. Hydrogen is contained with oxygen, and after the reaction is completed, the pressure inside the cylinder increases threefold. What will be the temperature inside the cylinder?

3. Attaching some additional resistance to the voltmeter increases the maximum voltage change by $n$ times. Another added resistance increases the maximum change by $m$ times. How many times will the maximum change in voltage by a voltmeter increase if these two resistances connected in parallel are connected in series with the voltmeter?

4. An astronomical camera for photographing the Sun has a lens with a focal length of $F = 10$ m. In front of the lens is a round circle with a diameter of $d = 5$ cm. The photographic film used has a sensitivity of $W = 10$ mox·sec. What should the exposure time t be under these conditions? It is known that direct solar rays create $E = 4 \cdot 10^4$ mox on a plane of illumination perpendicular to them. The angular diameter of the Sun $\alpha$ is taken equal to 0.01 rad.

Let's consider solving these problems.

1. During an impact, the momentum and kinetic energy of the colliding bodies are conserved. However, momentum cannot be conserved in scalar form:

$$mv = \frac{m}{2} v_1 + mv_2.$$

This entry is correct if both particles, after impact, move along the same straight line along which the impacting part moved before the impact, that is, with a central impact. In our case, as can be seen from the conditions of the problem, the impact was non-central. Therefore, it must be taken into account that the amount of motion is a vector quantity and is added along the right vectors (Fig. 1).

Using the cosine theorem, we can write the law preserving momentum in the form

$$\left( \frac{m}{2} v_1 \right)^2 = (mv)^2 + (mv_2)^2 - 2(mv_2)(mv) \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},$$

where $v_1$ denotes the speed of a particle with mass $\frac{m}{2}$, and $v_2$ denotes the speed of a particle with mass $m$. Besides,

Let's use the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{mv_2^2}{2} + \frac{mv_1^2}{4}.$$

Eliminating $v_2$ from these equations, we find

$$v_1 = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} v.$$

The vector nature of momentum can also be taken into account. Let us introduce mutually perpendicular coordinate axes $x$ and $y$.

For projections of momentum onto such axes, scalar relations are satisfied

$$mv_{2y} + \frac{m}{2} v_{1y} = 0,$$

$$mv_{2x} + \frac{m}{2} v_{1x} = mv.$$

The law of conservation of energy will take the form

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = m \frac{v_{2y}^2 + v_{2x}^2}{2} + \frac{m}{2} \frac{v_{1x}^2 + v_{1y}^2}{2}.$$

Having solved this system and taking into account the next relation $v_1 = \sqrt{v_{1x}^2 + v_{1y}^2}$, we get the same answer.
