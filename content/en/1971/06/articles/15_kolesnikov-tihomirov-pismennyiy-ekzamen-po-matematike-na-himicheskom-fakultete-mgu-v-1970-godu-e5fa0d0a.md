Written exam
in mathematics at the Faculty of Chemistry of Moscow State University in 1970

N. N. Kolesnikov, V. M. Tikhomirov

In 1970, at the Faculty of Chemistry of Moscow State University, only a written exam was held in mathematics.

Each version of the written work contained five problems covering all the main sections of the school mathematics course - algebra, geometry and trigonometry.

Having analyzed the four problems of one of the options, we will see that solving them does not require any knowledge beyond the scope of the mathematics program for building a university.

In this regard, I would like to emphasize once again that applicants often have weak skills of sound logical reasoning and that they are given clearly insufficient attention to geometric problems and theorems.

Along with these two fundamental shortcomings, which I would like to draw the teachers’ attention to, great harm is caused to applicants by negligence and lack of composure when completing and preparing written work, and the inability to complete the solution of problems and examples. A problem in which an idea for a solution is outlined, but not brought to an answer with the necessary intermediate reasoning, cannot be considered completely solved. Carelessness also leads to various errors and clerical errors that distort the course of the decision and the results; sometimes the conditions of the task are misunderstood, but the task with changed conditions is not considered by the examination committee, even if it turns out to be more difficult.

1. In this problem we indicate only the main points of the solution.

Let $S$ be the distance between points $A$ and $B$, $v_a$ the speed of the car, $v_b$ the speed of the bicycle, $t$ the time (in hours) from the start of the movement to the first meeting. For the four entered quantities ($S, v_a, v_b, t$), three equations can be created:

$$\left( v_a + v_b \right) t = S,$$
$$\frac{2}{5} S = v_b (t + 2),$$
$$\frac{7}{5} S = v_a (t + 2).$$

It is impossible to find the values ​​of all unknowns, but this is not required; you need to find the values ​​$\frac{S}{v_b} - t$, which are a combination of unknowns. For the third equation to the second, we get that $v_a = \frac{7}{2} v_b$.

Substituting this correspondence into the first equation and eliminating $\frac{S}{v_b}$ from it and the second equation, we find $t = \frac{5}{2}$ hours. From the second equation it turns out that $\frac{S}{v_b} = \frac{45}{4}$ hours, and the exclusive time $\frac{S}{v_b} - t$ is equal to $\frac{35}{4}$ hours, that is, 8 hours 45 minutes.

Note that this problem (like all the first problems in the versions given at the Faculty of Chemistry) is essentially arithmetic and allows solutions based on the simplest reasoning.

At the same time (before the second meeting), the value is simple $2/5$, and the automatic value $7/5$ is always a path. This means that the ratio of their speeds is 2:7. Let us denote $\Rightarrow ACB$ in triangle $CAB$ by $\beta$. Now it is easy to write the volume of this prism as follows:

$$V = \frac{1}{2}AC \cdot CB \sin \beta \cdot AA_1 = 24CB \sin \beta.$$

The plane passing through the points $A, M$ and $N$ cuts off the paramid $ABCNM$ from the prism, whose lateral face $ACB$ is perpendicular to the base, since the prism $ABCA_1B_1C_1$ is straight (by condition). The base $BCNM$ is a trapezoid, the plane sides $CN$ and $BM$ are parallel. From equalities

$$BM = \frac{BB_1}{2} = 4, CN = AC \cdot tg\alpha = 3$$

we find: $S_{BCNM} = \frac{7}{2}BC$ (BC is perpendicular to $CN$ and $BM$ and is the height of the trapezoid). The volume of the paramid is $ABCNM$, therefore equal to

$$V = \frac{1}{3}S_{BCNM} \cdot AC \sin \beta = 7CB \sin \beta.$$

The volume of the other part cut off by the plane from the prism is the difference between the volumes of the prism and paramid: $V_2 = V - V_1$. From here

$$V_1 : V_2 = V_1 : (V - V_1) = 7 : 17.$$

In this problem, it was not necessary to find the numerical values ​​of the volumes of the parts of the prism that interest us; the plane only needed their ratio. To get the answer, it was enough to express them through the same, although unknown to us, quantities $BC$ and $\beta$.
