distance $L$ from the place of throwing the stone will fall to the ground?
In the coordinate system shown at figure 4, the position of the stone at time $t$ is determined by the coordinates

$$x = v_0 t, \quad y = \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

At the moment $t_0$ falls to the ground $x = L \cos \alpha$, and $y = L \sin \alpha$, that is

$$L \cos \alpha = v_0 t_0,$$
$$L \sin \alpha = \frac{gt^2}{2}.$$

From here

$$L = \frac{2v_0^2 \sin \alpha}{g \cos^2 \alpha}.$$

It would be possible to choose another coordinate system, for example, shown in figure 5. In Nord, this was convenient. In this coordinate system

$$v_{OX} = v_0 \cos \alpha, \quad v_{OY} = v_0 \sin \alpha,$$
$$a_x = g \sin \alpha \quad a_y = -g \cos \alpha.$$

Pozny

$$x = v_0 \cos \alpha t + \frac{g \sin \alpha \cdot t^2}{2},$$
$$y = v_0 \sin \alpha t - \frac{g \cos \alpha \cdot t^2}{2}.$$

Substituting the values ​​of $x$ and $y$ at $t = t_0$ ($x = L, y = 0$) into these equations and solving them together, we find the answer.
