Tunnels in the Earth

Let us show that a harmonic force also acts on a point located inside the Earth (a force $F = \gamma \frac{Mm}{R^2}$ acts on a point outside the Earth).

Let the material point of mass $m$ be located at a distance $r$ from the center of the Earth. The action that the Earth has on a point can be divided into two parts (Fig. 2): the action of the inner sphere (red) and the action of the outer spherical layer (yellow).

As is known, a spherical layer does not create a gravitational field inside itself (if the density $\rho$ in it is constant) *). Therefore, only the “red part”, whose mass will act on the point

$$M = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho.$$ (7)

The force with which the “red part” acts on the point is equal to

$$F = -\gamma \frac{Mm}{r^2} = -\gamma \frac{4\pi\rho m}{3} \cdot r.$$ (8)

Consequently, inside the Earth (in the tunnel) a harmonic force acts on a point, proportional to the distance from the center of the Earth. The movement in the tunnel turns out to be comparable to the movement of the body suspended in the circle - the elastic force of the spring is also proportional to its stretching.
