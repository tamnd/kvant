HOW THEY SEE THE INVISIBLE

People incredibly quickly get used to the most amazing achievements of science and technology. Is it possible to introduce cognition today with a story about a television, a jet plane, or an artificial Earth satellite? Even the lunar rover - this amazing creation of modern science and technology - has taken a common place in our imagination next to an electronic computer, a nuclear power plant and the mechanism for transmitting hereditary characteristics from generation to generation. We are spoiled by the abundance of new wonderful opportunities that the modern scientific and technological revolution continuously puts at the disposal of man and scientist. Almost nothing can surprise you anymore. But this, in general, is bad. For without surprise there is no interest, and without interest there is no desire to truly learn something, to study it, to explore it yourself.

But let's cut ourselves off from this “all-importance” for a minute and think about this question. Atoms consist of electrons and atomic nuclei. Atomic nuclei are formed from protons and neutrons. What are the sizes of these particles? The radius of a proton or neutron is of the order of $10^{-13}$ cm. Consequently, any of these particles, roughly speaking, is contained in a volume of the order of $10^{-38}-10^{-39}$ cm$^3$. The dot that comes at the end of the previous phrase has a radius approximately equal to a quarter of a millimeter. A sphere with such a radius will have a volume of the order of $10^{-4}$ cm$^3$. Therefore, it would be possible to measure $10^{35}$ elementary particles in it! How small these particles must be to decrease in such a gigantic quantity inside our point. Neither the eye nor the microscope, even with the highest magnification, can notice them. But you have all seen clear photographs of traces of such particles in textbooks and popular books. They were obtained using cloud chamber signs. Look at them carefully, and a new world will open before you. Here, for example, are photographs of the traces left by electrons. One trace of length, they pass through the chamber, slightly bending in the magnetic field penetrating it. These electrons have high energy. Other traces were short, highly curved, and their remaining electrons had a small amount of energy. Some traces, as it were, will disperse at the end - a collision has occurred with an atom, ending with the removal of one of the electrons from its shell.

Remember how they discovered the invisible man, the hero of Herbert Wallace's one-shot novel? The pursuer saw traces of bare feet, right before his eyes, appearing one after another in the snow. The man was invisible, but traces of him remained. Also here - the particles are invisible, but the trace of their destructive work is present.

Looking at a photograph of a person unknown to us, we can learn very little about him in essence. Unless we can establish the gender and roughly estimate the age. A photograph will not tell us whether he is pure or not, whether he likes music, what his profession is, whether he has children and, if so, how he feels about them.

Another thing is photographs of traces of elementary particles in a cloud chamber. By studying them, we can accurately determine the nature of the particles, establish their energies, patterns, the nature of interaction with each other, the interaction of other energies, protons or electrons, one cannot help but admire the perfection with which all the details of their short, but full of dramatic life events are recorded.
