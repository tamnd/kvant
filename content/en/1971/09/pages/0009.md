⟦folio 7⟧

M. Ya. Azbel

on the uncertainty principle

In the second issue of our magazine for 1971, an article by M. Ya. Azbel “Dialogue on Temperature” was published. Stamy attracted many open readers. All the letters said that the article was integran. However, there were many typos in the article, for which the editors and the author apologize. The letters from readers raised very integrated questions. Therefore, we decided not to simply give a list of typos, but to ask the author to continue the conversation with the reader.

Author. I never expected that the editors would help me turn an imaginary dialogue with the reader into a real one, and not even a dialogue, but a conversation. This is what reader Volodya Margulis from Saransk writes.

V. Margulis. It seems to me that your article “Dialogue on Temperature” contains many typos. The Bolshman and Planck constants are overestimated by two orders of magnitude, the electron charge by an order of magnitude, and so on.

Author. You are absolutely right. Let me tell you a professional secret: like many theoretical physicists, I remember only one system of units: CGSE. All estimates were carried out in it and the constants in my article were given: Planck’s constant $h \approx 6.5 \times 10^{-27}$ ere·sec, Bolshman’s constant $k \approx 10^{-16}$ ere/erad, electron charge $e \approx 5 \cdot 10^{-10}$ units. CGSE. (Remember that we are only interested in the order of magnitude!)

However, in an article published in the journal, all calculations turned out to be in the SI system and, moreover, in a “deformed form.” This, of course, is unpleasant, but there is no big problem here. Every student reader who benefits from knowing systems of units will benefit from continuing the calculations and returning them to the correct form. Helpful and respectful. The fact is that typos and typos in scientific articles are not uncommon, and one must learn to treat them without worry, correcting them as necessary.

V. Margulis. I did just that, but the correct calculation for electrons is that the potential energy is two orders of magnitude less than the kinetic energy. And for the gel, the potential energy turned out to be much greater than the kinetic energy! *)

*) For those who have not read the article “Dialogue about temperature”, I will explain what we are talking about. From the so-called uncertainty principle it follows that the smallest possible value of the momentum $p_{\min}$ of a particle concentrated in a region whose dimensions are of the order of $a$, $p_{\min} \sim \frac{h}{a}$. Hence the lowest possible kinetic energy is $E_{\min} \sim \frac{p_{\min}^2}{2m} \sim \frac{h^2}{2ma^2}$, where $m$ is the mass of the particle.
