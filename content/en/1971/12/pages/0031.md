Although the large rings produced by the setup described are best suited for lecture demonstration, I find that much more beautiful and symmetrical rings can be obtained by blowing smoke from a paper or glass tube with a diameter of 2.5 cm*). If you look from the side at the blown rings in still air near a lamp or in sunlight, you can see very beautiful spiral streamlines. I managed to form one of these rings as follows. A momentary shutter was mounted on the door of a dark room, and an arc lamp was focused onto its slit using a large concave mirror. The photographic plastic was installed in a dark room so that it was illuminated by a diverging beam of rays coming from the reflection of the arc in the mirror (when the shutter was open). A red lamp was placed in front of the plate, and then the rings were blown out of the tube. As soon as the ring, symmetrical in shape and not moving too quickly, appeared in front of the plate, we held the cord leading to the shutter, and the plate was illuminated by a blinding flash. A clear darkness fell from the ring due to the small size and separation of the light source. Figure 2 taken from the resulting photograph. It can be seen that the ring consists of a layer of smoke and a layer of air, forming a spiral of several terminal turns.
