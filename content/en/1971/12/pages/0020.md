Molecular models

A. I. KITAIGORODSKY

It is useful to remember that words are made by man.
Words that are used in life are often blurry in nature. Not all concepts are the same - the words cilia and beauty, energy and tension. And “good” weather is different for different people.
In science, this state of affairs does not exist, or at least it should not exist. Particularly intolerant is the imprecise use of words in physics.
The simplest physical concepts of nature were used to describe the properties and behavior of objects and bodies among which our life goes, in short, for “large” bodies or, as we also say, for macro-nutritional bodies.
What concepts from the macrocosm can be applied to molecules? All or some? The truth lies in the middle.
What about geometric and mechanical concepts?
Is it possible to talk about the shape of the molecule, its elasticity, the model of bending and torsion, and finally, the laminarity of molecules? Whether and what sense the concept of intramolecular and mechanical concepts make is not only possible, but also advisable.

This phrase means the following.
In some cases, a ULE molecule can be spoken of as a large body.
A body that can be compared to a given molecule is called a mechanical model of the molecule.
Our task is to explain how this model is constructed and how to use it to solve various physical problems.
The mechanical model of the molecule has recently become widespread due to interest in the huge (compared to an atom) molecules from which synthetic polymers are built - nylon, nylon, polystylene (these names are now known as home), as well as substances that are most important for the life of animals and plants - proteins, nucleic acids, and so on.
Any “image” of a molecule must be composed of a description of the relative position of atomic nuclei and the characteristics of electrons moving around these nuclei.
Chemical experience allows you to establish the atomic structure of a molecule (build its atomic model), that is, indicate which atoms the molecule is made of and how they are connected to each other. Some electrons are required to be associated with certain atoms, while others are “generalized.” Chemists say about these electrons that “they carry out a chemical bond.”
