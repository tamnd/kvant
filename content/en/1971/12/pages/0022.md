We can now turn to the problem of mechanical forces.

That forces operate between molecules is evident from the most elementary messages. The vapor of any substance under suitable conditions follows into a drop. If so, then the molecules will undoubtedly be accepted. The substance resists the rampant. This means that being at short distances, molecules repel each other. If there is acceptance at large distances, and repulsion at small distances, then there is an equilibrium distance when these forces are balanced.

Instead of interaction forces, it is much more convenient to talk about interaction energy *). The energy of interaction is easier to measure, and the concept is simpler and clearer than force**). The energy of interaction between molecules (or atoms, or any other particles or bodies) is called the work that must be done in order to move the particles away from each other—so that the interaction stops. The mathematician will say - give it to an infinitely large distance. The closer the particles are, the more work is required to pull them apart. This work reaches its maximum value when the particles are at an equilibrium distance from each other. This work is called binding energy. If the particles collide and repel, that is, if they are at a distance less than the equilibrium one, then the work of discontinuity becomes, of course, less.
