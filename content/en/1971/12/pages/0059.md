Shen take a bath with mercury. All this is located in a sealed chamber filled with air with a temperature of $T_0 = 273^\circ$ K and a pressure of $p_0 = 1,334 \cdot 10^5$ n/m². The mercury in the tube rose to a height of $h_0 = 700$ mm above the level of the mercury in the bath.

By moving one of the walls of the chamber, the isothermal air pressure is reduced to $p_1 = 8 \cdot 10^4$ n/m², while the height of the mercury column is reduced to $h_1 = 400$ mm. Then, by heating at a constant volume of the chamber, the temperature rises to $T_2$ and the height of the mercury column becomes $h_2 = 500$ mm. After this, an isobaric expansion of the air in the chamber occurs, and the mercury column is set to a height of $h = 450$ mm.

Provided that this system is always in a state of thermodynamic equilibrium, calculate: the mass of hydrogen $m$, the temperature $T_2$, the pressure of hydrogen in the final state $p$. The density of mercury at $T_0$ $p_0 = 1,36 \cdot 10^4$ kD/m³, the coefficient of volumetric expansion of mercury is $\beta = 1,84 \cdot 10^{-4}$ erad⁻¹, the gas constant is $R = 8,317 \cdot 10^3$ j/kmol·erad.

Thermal expansion of glass and changes in the level of mercury in the bath should not be taken into account.
