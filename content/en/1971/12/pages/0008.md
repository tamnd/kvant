find the angle at which an α-particle will deviate when flying near an exact positive charge.
By measuring the angle of deflection on the test, it would be possible to find out how much the scattering charge is disconnected from the exact one. In practice, the situation is somewhat more complicated.

In reality, of course, it is impossible to determine the trajectory of an individual particle, but one can study the scattering of a beam of particles.

Let us consider a beam of particles in which 1 particle per second passes through each $1 \, cm^2$ of its section. When such a beam falls on a nucleus, particles passing at different distances from the nucleus will be deflected at different angles. From figure 6 it is clear that the number of particles flying past the core (it is located in the center) at distances between $\rho$ and $\rho + \Delta \rho$ is $2\pi \Delta \rho$. If a particle has an impact parameter $\rho$, then it scatters through an angle $\theta$, determined by the formula

$$\text{ctg} \frac{0}{2} = \rho \cdot \frac{mv_0^2}{z_1 z_2 e^2}.$$

$\theta$ is the angle between the asymptotes of the hyperbola.

This formula is derived like this.

Since $\theta$ is the total change in the angle during scattering (the angle between the velocities before scattering and after scattering), it is related to the angle $\alpha$, which is made up of asymptotes with the axis $x$ (Fig. 7):

$$\theta = \pi - 2\alpha.$$

Then, using formulas (8) and (9), we find

$$\text{ctg} \frac{\theta}{2} = \text{tg} \frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{b}{a} = \frac{L}{a \sqrt{2mE}}.$$

(Since the asymptote is the tangent to the infinitely further hyperbola, then $\rho$ is simply equal to $p_1$ (or $p_2$) from formula (9) for the infinitely further and always $p_1 p_2 = \rho^2$.).

If the speed of a particle at infinity is $v_0$, then $L = mv_0 \rho$, and

$$E = \frac{mv_0^2}{2}.$$

Then

$$\text{ctg} \frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{mv_0 \rho}{\sqrt{2mE}} \cdot \frac{2E}{z_1 z_2 e^2} = \rho \frac{mv_0^2}{z_1 z_2 e^2}.$$

Particles for which $\rho$ lies in the shaded interval will scatter in a certain range of angles from $\theta$ to $\theta + \Delta \theta$. The value $\Delta \theta$ is not difficult to calculate *). Let's write the formula for the “outer” edge of the ring:

$$\text{ctg} \frac{\theta + \Delta \theta}{2} = (\rho + \Delta \rho) \cdot \frac{mv_0^2}{z_1 z_2 e^2}.$$

*) Those who knew how to differentiate can almost say that next we will simply calculate the derivative $d\rho/d\theta$.
