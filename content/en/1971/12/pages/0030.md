Vortex rings

The questions prepared for the lecture on vortex rings revealed interesting changes.

A typical box for demonstrating good vortices is well known and does not require a detailed description*). Our device is significantly larger than those commonly used. This is a Cubic wooden box about a meter long on the side; one of the walls is made of thin oilcloth, freely suspended, with two diagonals of rubber tubes, firmly attached at the corners. Rubber tubes are needed to ensure that the oilcloth returns to its original position.

Such a box throws out air vortices of great force, and the impact of the ring on the wall of the lecture hall is distinctly loud and similar to the sound of a light blow with a towel. The audience can get an idea of ​​the “hardness” of the rotating air whirlwind by successively releasing invisible pegs into the hall. The blow of a peg in a person's face is mistaken like a soft push from a down pillow.

The side view is interesting: it shows the tail (similar to the tail of a comet), which is formed due to the friction of the outer sections of the ring with the atmosphere as it moves forward.

The power of air rings can be shown in this way. For example, place them on a flat cardboard box, standing at some distance from the installation. In this case, the box immediately turns over or even falls to the floor. By hitting the vortex ring you can extinguish the flame of a gas burner.
