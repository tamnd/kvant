The first task is quite simple. Our team received 34 okans for it. The case n=3 can be proved algebraically. For n=5, we note that for $a_1 > a_2 > ... > a_5$ (for definition), the first, third and fifth terms of the sum are positive, the second and fourth are negative, and in absolute value the first is greater than the second, and the fifth is greater than the fourth. For other n, contradictory examples are constructed.

The second and third tasks turned out to be the most difficult for our commission. The second problem has a non-trivial idea: all polytopes $P_1, P_2, ..., P_9$ are contained in the polytopes, we obtain from $P_1$ is prepared for the coefficient 2 of the relative point $A_1$. For this task the team received a total of 21 okans. In the third problem we prove that from the first k terms of such a sequence we can construct the $k+1$th term. Let's take all prime divisors of these k terms of the sequence: $p_1, p_2, ..., p_m$; number

$$2^{(p_1-1) (p_2-1) ... (p_m-1)} - 1$$

is divisible by $p_1, p_2, ..., p_m$. But since all these primes are odd, the number $2^{(p_1-1) (p_2-1) ... (p_m-1)} - 3$ is not divisible by any of them, therefore, it is coprime with all terms of the sequence. For this task we also received little, only 27 okans. Please, the easiest of all the problems was the fifth one. Further, I can’t believe that 7 okans were given for her solution, the same as for the second one. It is solved by the method of mathematical induction. It is only necessary to prove that for the system $S$, which satisfies the conditions of the problem for $k$ points, there is a parallel transfer that forms (together with $S$) the system $S_1$, for which the conditions of the problem for $k+1$ points are satisfied. For this task our team received 51 okans.

In Problem 6, the easiest way is to assume the opposite and write down all possible inequalities for the row with the minimum sum of the numbers in it and, similarly, for the column. Then you need to add up all the inequalities and take into account that the sum in the selected row (column) does not exceed $\frac{n}{2}$. For the sixth task, our team received 38 okans.
