Where can I get information about these curves? Unfortunately, there are no theoretical ways to derive them. Energy curves have to be determined from experience. Since the selection of the necessary curves is a weight operation typical for many areas of physics, we will describe this interesting work in more detail.

When researching, starting from scratch, one must first of all estimate, at least roughly, the parameters of the curves. From experience we know the close distances at which atoms of neighboring molecules approach each other. Looking carefully at these data, we find out that two hydrogen atoms are not closer to each other than at a distance of 2-3 angstroms, two carbon atoms of different molecules are not closer to each other than 3-4 angstroms, etc. These data are taken from studies of solids. molecules in a solid have several slopes of slimy interactions. Therefore, we think that the equilibrium distances on the corresponding atom-atom interaction curves should be somewhat larger than the above figures. Based on a number of indirect reports, it is clear that this excess should be ten percent. Therefore, to begin with, you can select the abscissa of the minima on the curves, which we are discussing as an example, at points 2.6 and 3.8 angstroms.

Information about the binding energy, that is, about the depth of the well of the energy curve, must be “extracted” from the data on the heats of evaporation. For example, that at low pressures a solid substance evaporates. The heat of this phase transformation can be measured quite accurately. The heat expended on the evaporation of the crystal was used to disperse its molecules over a long distance. With some corrections, which we will not dwell on, the measurement of the heat of evaporation leads us to the value of the interaction energy of all molecules.

Of course, the state of affairs is not very simple - the total value changes, but it is necessary to judge the weaker ones. Hydrocarbon substances, consisting of molecules containing 10-20 atoms, have a heat of evaporation of 10-20 kcal/mol. If we assume that the interaction of connecting molecules plays a major role, then this figure adds up to thousands of atom-atom interactions. This means that the depth of the well of the curve should be of the order of 0.01 kcal/mol.

Thus, one arrives at approximate estimates of the parameters of the energy curves of atom-atom interaction and, with their help, constructs first-approximation curves; Then they calculate (this is where computers are needed) the properties of a large number of substances and compare the calculation results with experience. It becomes very necessary to make certain amendments. Actions are sequential, trial and error (physicists say so - work by trial and error), in the end we come to the optimal atom-atom interaction curves for many pairs of atoms.

In general, the calculation is cumbersome and the aditivity hypothesis has only recently been studied. The result turned out to be positive. It turned out that the assumption is fulfilled with good accuracy.

In advance it is not very clear that the work will be successful. If the hypothesis were unfair, if propipeditivity did not work, if atoms of the same chemical type contained in different molecules did not behave the same way, then the computer would tell us that it cannot select the curves so that they give numbers, at least in ways that coincide with experience. For example, successful work on fitting curves shows that the hypotheses underlying the calculation are valid.

Having proved the validity of the additive representation of the interaction energy of molecules, we provide a basis for the concept of molecular forms.
