If you place a small marble ball at the far end of the desert, then when the position of the eye is determined, the steep contour will turn into an ellipse, and if you lower the eye, the image will become a segment and eventually disappear. The increase in size in this case is caused by the fact that the direct and reflected images are observed simultaneously and are shifted relative to each other. I observed a similar situation on a lake: when the water is warm and the air is cold, patches of snow on the opposite shore (too small to be distinguished by the eye from a height of a few meters above the lake level) become clearly visible if you go down to the edge of the water.

The atmospheric conditions necessary for mirages to occur can also cause the dust wings often seen in the desert, and on a large scale, death. These deaths, like mirages, can be reproduced on a small scale.

I sprinkled one of the metal plates with quartz sand and heated it with small burners. After a few minutes, very beautiful little deaths began to move across the surface, twisting the thin sand into watery wings; sometimes it lasted 10-15 seconds.

However, vortices created in this way cannot be filled in a large audience. So I looked for ways to create them on a larger scale. To do this, after removing the sand, he heated the plate well and sprinkled it with adhesive: a dense cloud of white steam immediately rose from the hot surface and soon a real death minator about 2 cm high appeared in the center. By placing the plate in the beam of a flashlight in a dark room, you can demonstrate the wings in a larger audience. I find it best to first sprinkle ammonia on the plate and then heat it: then the wings appear almost continuously and often persist for quite a long time.

figure 7 shows a snapshot of one of these deaths, taken in bright sunlight.

This method of creating atmospheric wings is preferable to the old method of creating them using a quick-release drum with transverse partitions *), since here wings arise for the same reasons and under the same conditions as in nature.

*) See the article by N. E. Zhukovsky “Fundamentals of the theory of winga” («Kvant» No. 4, 1971).
