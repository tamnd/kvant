At the stern of the ship, a cable goes into the water, at the end of which electrodes are fixed about a meter from the surface of the water. Every 10 seconds, a high voltage pulse is sent to the electrodes from a high-voltage capacitor bank installed on the ship, and a small lightning flashes between the electrodes. A sensitive blow is felt on the hull of the ship, and the sound wave hits the bottom through the water column.

Reflected waves on the sea surface are captured by a receiving device towed behind the ship, which geophysicists call a “scythe”. A scythe is a flexible plastic pipe filled with oil, inside of which there are elements that convert sound.
