Systems of algebraic equations

M. I. Shabunin

At university entrance exams, systems of algebraic equations are first proposed; in particular, such systems arise in problems of composing equations. Exam experience shows that many students who come in solve a system of equations cope unsatisfactorily.

The fact is that usually when studying, the question of finding solutions to a system is understood simply as the formal implementation of a series of “usual” algebraic transformations. At the same time, it should be kept in mind that some of the transformation examples may lead to the appearance of extraneous solutions or to the loss of the solution of the original system.

Without appropriate justification for the solution of the system, the logical equations are incomplete. In particular, we may not replace the losses of some solutions. Unfortunately, well-founded, well-presented decisions of the system in the work of applicants are extremely rare.

A detailed presentation of all the important issues relating to the system of equations would require too much space *). Therefore, we will limit ourselves to presenting the basic theoretical principles concerning the solution of three algebraic equations with unknowns, and then consider several examples.

First, let's recall some definitions.

A system of three equations with unknowns can be written in the form

$$\begin{cases}
f_1(x, y, z) = 0, \\
f_2(x, y, z) = 0, \\
f_3(x, y, z) = 0
\end{cases}$$

or, omitting the unknowns for brevity, in the form

$$\begin{cases}
f_1 = 0, \\
f_2 = 0, \\
f_3 = 0.
\end{cases}$$
