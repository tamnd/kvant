It remains to solve the following problem: find a point M on line l for which angle PMQ is the simplest: a circle passing through points P, Q and M touches line l. To construct a point M, you need (if the segment PQ is not parallel to the line l) from the point C of the intersection of PQ and l on the line l, plot the segments CM₁ = CM₂ = √(CP·CQ), and then from the resulting points M₁ and M₂ select the one for which the angle MᵢCP is acute (or straight). The proof that it is for this point that the angle PMQ is maximum is almost obvious and is left to the reader *) (Fig. 2).

Oddly enough, many readers who sent us letters thought about one of the possibilities 1) and 2). The greatest solution was sent by O. Kim, G. Levin, A. Pukhalsky, V. Luzzin, Yu. Obolonkov, A. Kostyurin.

M65

a) Let E, F, G be points on sides AB, BC and CA of triangle ABC for which $\frac{AE}{EB} = \frac{BF}{FC} = \frac{CG}{GA} = k$, where 0 < k < 1.

Find the ratio of the area of ​​triangle KLM formed by lines AF, BG and CE to the area of ​​triangle ABC (Fig. 3a).

6) Cut the triangle with the sixth straight line into pieces from which seven equal triangles can be folded.

a) Let us denote the area of ​​the triangle with vertices X, Y, Z by s(XYZ). Using the fact that the ratio of triangles with the same heights (or with the same bases) is equal to the ratio of the bases (corresponding to the ratio of the heights), it is not difficult to prove that

$$s(ACK) = \frac{1}{k} s(ABK) = \frac{k+1}{k^2} s(AEK) = \frac{k+1}{k^2+k+1} s(AEC) = \frac{k}{k^2+k+1} s(ABC).$$

In the same way it can be proven that

$$s(BLA) = s(CMB) = \frac{k}{k^2+k+1} s(ABC).$$

Pozny

$$\frac{s(KLM)}{s(ABC)} = 1 - \frac{3k}{k^2+k+1} = \frac{(1-k)^2}{k^2+k+1} = \frac{(1-k)^3}{1-k^3}.$$

6) Note that for k = $\frac{1}{2}$ this ratio is equal to $\frac{1}{7}$ and each of the three segments AF, BG, CE is divided by two others in the ratio 3:3:1, so that if points M, K, L are drawn, three more lines corresponding to parallel to these segments, then each of the sides of the triangle ABC will be divided in the ratio 2:1:1:2.
