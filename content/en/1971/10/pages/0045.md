If necessary, the block will slide. If we lift the thread along the block, then when it is at a certain height $h_0$ from the surface, the block will no longer slide, but will begin to turn over. In this case, the force $F$ with which we pull the block is equal to the friction force:

$$F = kmg,$$

and the moment of force $F$ relative to the axis passing through the point $A$ is equal to the moment of gravitational force:

$$Fh_0 = mg \frac{a}{2},$$

where $a$ is the side of the square at the base of the block.

Solving these two equations together, we find

$$k = \frac{a}{2h_0}.$$

By measuring $a$ and $h_0$ using a ruler, we find the coefficient of friction of the block on the surface.

This solution was added by I. Sidorov (Moscow), A. Petrov (Asbest of the Sverdlovsk Region), M. Rozov (Minsk), S. Yudin (Volgograd), D. Shapiro (Chelyabinsk), A. Alexandrov (Glazov Udm. Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic), G. Simonenko (Kaunas).

An interesting solution by A. Kalik from Baku and S. Sobolev from Moscow. Here it is.

Having tilted the block and holding it at a certain angle to the vertical, we apply a certain force $F$ to the block, directed along the block (Fig. 10). In this case, the reaction force $N = mg + F \cos \alpha$ and the friction force $F_{trp}$ will also act on the block. If the friction force does not reach the maximum value equal to $kN = k (mg + F \cos \alpha)$, then the end of the block will not slide. In this case, the friction force is equal to $F \sin \alpha$. Thus, the end of the bar does not slip when

$$F \sin \alpha < kmg + kF \cos \alpha,$$

that is, when

$$F < \frac{kmg}{\sin \alpha - k \cos \alpha}.$$

It is easy to see that at $k > tg \alpha$ the block cannot be made to move, no matter how large the force $F$. Let us find the angle $\alpha_0$ at which the block begins. Using a ruler, measure the height from the horizontal plane to the edge of the tilted block and the width of the block and calculate $tg \alpha_0$, we find the friction coefficient $k$.
