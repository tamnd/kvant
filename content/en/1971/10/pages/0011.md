the energy of reflected waves in electrical impulses (piezoelectric elements).

Through the cable connecting the scythe to the corps, electrical impulses go to the amplifier, and after the amplifier the recordings are recorded. The recorder contains a wide strip of electrosensitive paper. There is a “feather” at the top edge of the tape.
When the sound wave from the electric discharge begins its run to the bottom, the pen begins to move across the paper tape to its lower edge, as if repeating the movement of the wave on a smaller scale. And when the reflected waves arrive at the receiving spit and from it through the amplifier to the pen, the pen leaves points on the sensitive paper corresponding to the layers of rock encountered on its way by the sound wave.

Before the next discharge, the pen returns to the top, and the chutchuta paper is pulled out, and the recorder is again ready to record reflected waves.

A few minutes after the start of work, dotted lines on paper form a visual and easy to read figure structure of the geological section under study (Fig. 3).

The geoacoustic location method can be used not only for searching for minerals. With its help, you can solve problems of engineering geology - choose places for hydroelectric dams, for the foundations of port facilities, jetties, tunnels, pipelines, and even search for ancient cities flooded with water. The cost of exploration by this method is 5-10 times cheaper than seismic exploration and exploration by drilling wells.
