COLLECTOR'S CORNER

USSR - THE HOMELAND OF COSMONAUtics

Advances in space exploration became possible thanks to the work of Russian scientists K. E. Tsiolkovsky, N. I. Kiablicch, F. A. Tsander, S. P. Korolev and others.

K. E. Tsiolkovsky (1857-1935) is considered an outstanding scientist - the father of cosmonautics. Since 1886, he systematically studied the theory of motion of jet vehicles. Tsiolkovsky created a scheme of multi-stage rocket boosters for launching into orbit a multi-ton spacecraft intended for human interplanetary flights, theoretically substantiated the principles of rocket flight and the creation of a liquid-fuel rocket engine.

The first stamp with a portrait of K. E. Tsiolkovsky was published in 1951 in the series “Great Russian Scientists” (artist V. Zavyalov), and subsequently stamps with his portrait were issued several times both in our Union and abroad.

Thus, we see a portrait of Tsiolkovsky in a series of stamps issued in 1964 (they were issued in two varieties: with teeth and without teeth) and associated with the founder of rocket theory and technology - K. E. Tsiolkovsky, N. I. Kiablicch and F. A. Tsander. A diagram of Tsiolkovsky's space rocket is depicted on Hungarian and Polish People's Resources stamps issued in 1964 and 1963. As can be seen in the photo, on the PPR stamp to the right of Tsiolkovsky’s rocket is the formula for rocket motion, which he derived in 1903.

The Soviets succeeded in realizing man's age-old dream and being the first to create artificial Earth satellites and spaceships for human flight.
