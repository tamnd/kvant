⟦folio 61⟧

Scientists, engineers and workers under the leadership of the outstanding scientist and designer S.P. Korolev (1906-1966). His sculptural portrait against the backdrop of the first artificial Earth satellite and the obelisk commemorating the victories of the Soviet people in the liberation of space is depicted on a stamp issued for Cosmonautics Day in 1969.

On October 4, 1957, humanity learned about the launch of the first artificial Earth satellite in the Soviet Union. This event heralded the beginning of a new space age. Becoming the first space power, the Soviet Union issued the first stamp (November 5, 1957), commemorating the beginning of space exploration, which depicts a satellite in orbit around the globe.

On April 12, 1961, at 9:07 a.m. in the Soviet Union, the world's first suptin spaceship "Vostok" was released into low-Earth orbit with people on board - USSR citizen Yuri Alekseevich Gagarin. At 10:55 a.m., the suptin ship, having spent 108 minutes in space, landed in a given area. The very next day the first stamp dedicated to this event appeared. It depicts a portrait of Yu. A. Gagarin and the inscription “Man of the Soviet Country in Space 12-IV-1961.”

Subsequently, many countries issued stamps commemorating the first manned space flight. After the first flight, the launch of spacecraft with astronauts on board began to follow one after another, and all these launches were interrupted by the issue of stamps in various countries of the world. In the photo you see some stamps associated with the launches of manned spacecraft in the Soviet Union.

On October 12, 1964, the three-seat Vostok spacecraft was launched. On the stamp you see the crew of this ship consisting of commander V.M. Komarov, researcher, candidate of technical sciences K.P. Feoktistov and doctor B.B. Egorov. On March 18, 1965, the Voskhod-2 spacecraft with cosmonauts P.I. Belyaev and A.A. Leonov was launched into orbit around the Earth. On the second orbit, A. A. Leonov was the first in the world to exit a ship into outer space. After spending 20 minutes in space (of which 10 minutes in the ship), he returned safely to the ship. During this time, he covered the distance from the Black Sea coast to the island of Sakhalin at a speed of 28,000 kilometers per hour. On one of the stamps you see an image of A. A. Leonov flying in space next to the Vostkhod-2 ship.

On October 11, 12 and 13, 1969, three spacecraft were released into low-Earth orbit for a joint flight: Soyuz-6, Soyuz-7 and Soyuz-8. Portraits of the crew members of these ships are reproduced in a scene of three stamps. These are G. S. Shonin, and V. N. Kubasov (Soyuz-6 ship), A. V. Filipenko, V. N. Volkov and V. V. Gorbatko (Soyuz-7 ship), V. A. Shatlov and A. S. Eliseev (Soyuz-8 ship). Both crew members of the Soyuz-8 spacecraft ascended into space for the second time.

On June 1, 1970, the Soyuz-9 spacecraft was launched into Earth orbit. You can see the crew of this ship A.G. Nikolaev (the second time in space) and V.I. Sevostnov on the stamp issued in honor of this flight. Having spent 424 hours in space (a record for the duration of a space flight) and completed an extensive research program, the Soyuz-9 spacecraft successfully completed quite a long mission on June 19.

A. V. Almyks

HOW MANY COLORS?

On the cover of the magazine you see four circles, each of which contains a chord. They divide the plane into several parts. These parts are painted with three colors so that the neighboring ones have different colors. (Parts whose boundaries have a common segment or arc are called neighboring. Parts whose boundaries contain one common point are not considered neighboring.) We call such a coloring correctly, and a point that belongs to the boundary of more than two parts is a vertex.

Prove that if the plane is divided into parts:
a) several washed, but for correct coloring there are two colors available;
b) circles, in each of which a chord is drawn, but two colors are available for correct coloring. (In the last problem, the plane is divided by arbitrary lines.)

In these problems, for correct coloring it is enough to correctly color an arbitrary plane into parts, when it is no longer possible to bypass it with three colors. Make sure that in each of your examples of four colors to achieve enough colors, proven.

You can read more about this in the books by G. Rademacher and O. Tellitz “Numbers and Figures”, M., “Science”, 1966 and L. I. Golovina and I. M. Yaglom “Induction and Geometry”, M., Gostekhidat, 1956.
