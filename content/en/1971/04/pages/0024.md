What role fell to Poinsot in explaining the question of the motion of a solid body, the same role belongs to Helmholtz in explaining the question of the motion of a liquid.

Almost all of Helmholtz's works on mechanics are related to fluid mechanics. It can be said that modern hydrodynamics owes its development mainly to Helmholtz. Meanwhile, the most important work of the scientist in this area appeared in 1858*), 43 years after the formulas containing the reasons for the preservation of wines were found by Cauchy. But Cauchy considered the result he obtained only from the analytical side and did not foresee the mass of questions that could be solved with proper geometric illumination of the conclusions.

I will now try to explain to you as simply as possible the concept of wine established by Helmholtz.

with a solid base, filled with liquid (or gas), and suppose that this liquid moves like this: a central cylindrical column of some thickness rotates like a solid body around its axis, and the mass of liquid always spins around this column in circles with speeds inversely proportional to the distance from the axis of the column, and these speeds, increasing as they approach the central column, transform on its surface into the speed of the column.

This movement of liquid is called a vine, and the cylindrical column that characterizes it is called a vine cord. Let's say that the direction of the cord is equal to half the product of the velocity of the liquid on the surface of the cord and the perimeter of the normal section of the cord.

Twice the value of this product is called the circulation velocity.

In general, the circulation of speed along some closed contour within the movement of a fluid is equal to the product of the length of the contour and the average of all the velocities of the points of the contour in the direction tangent to the contour.

Since in the movement of a fluid, let us depict on figure 1, the velocities are inversely proportional to the radii, the velocity circulation along all horizontal circles having a center on the axis of the column and enclosing it are equal to each other and, therefore, equal to the double direction of the cord, and the velocity circulation along contours consisting of segments of two circles between segments of two radii, and lying outside the cord (contour ABCD on figure 2) are equal to zero. In addition, it can be proven that the circulation of speed along any closed contour that encloses the direction is equal to twice the direction of the direction, and the circulation of speed along any closed contour that does not cover the direction is equal to zero.
