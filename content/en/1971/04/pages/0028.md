Helmogoly demonstrated the formation of direct vortices with one excellent experiment, described in his speech on vinre storms. We will repeat this experience here.

A small hole is made in the denne of the cylindrical vessel (Fig. 8), plugged with a stopper. The vessel is filled with water. By means of air jets directed by a tube to one edge of the free surface of the water, we cause the liquid to slowly rotate. The liquid begins to flow out of the hole, approaching from the edges of the vessel to its axis. Since the circulation speed along a circle drawn from a point on the cylinder axis through the same particles of liquid should not change with time, then with a decrease in the radii of these circles the speed of part of the liquid will increase. The rotation of the liquid as it approaches the axis will become faster and faster, and we will notice a sharply formed winry, above which a funnel will appear, deeper and deeper.

I will also demonstrate the formation of winry using a rapidly rotating disk.

At figure 9 the device of Prof. F. N. Shvedova. Through the bottom of a glass cylindrical vessel, a vertical axis is sold in the stuffing box, ending with a small disk. This axis can be brought into rapid rotation through endless repairs. Water and oil are poured into the vessel, which floats on top of the water. Rotating the disk, we will notice that the water gradually begins to rotate and forms a winding cord above the disk, which is replaced along the funnel at the interface between water and oil. This funnel is filled with oil, which descends to the disk in the form of a descending tornado. At the moment when the oil comes into contact with the disk, its entire mass is dispersed through the water.

Even more interesting is the way direct winrays are formed in the air. The air above the surface of the water causes rotation with the help of a special rapidly rotating impeller placed at a certain height above the water (Fig. 10). The air vinry captures water along its axis and lifts it in the form of an ascending tornado all the way to the lionfish.
