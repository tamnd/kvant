In 1820, Ampere discovered that an electric current deflects the magnetic needle of a compass. This allowed us to basically change the “current strength” and understand that there is a difference between current strength and voltage. By this time, two current sources were known: the electric battery “voltaic” (invented in 1799 by A. Volta) and the thermodynamic element invented by Seebeck in 1821. However, he did not foresee that it was possible to establish mathematical laws of battery operation. Physicists were surprised at the change in the action of the battery with different connecting elements, but they could not see any clear patterns here. In addition, galvanic batteries at that time were flat, the direction at their ends changed during the experiment (the batteries “sat down”), which greatly confused the results of the experiments. Ohm abandoned these batteries and conducted his first experiments using a thermodynamic element as a current source, which gave a significantly lower current, but the voltage was stable. The potential difference of such an element is about 0.0045 volts. To change the current obtained from thermal action, Ohm came up with a new method. The current-carrying conductor was positioned along the direction of a freely suspended magnetic needle (i.e., along the meridian), and the angle at which the thread had to be twisted changed so that the needle did not deviate from this direction. The angle of twist of the thread characterized the current strength.
