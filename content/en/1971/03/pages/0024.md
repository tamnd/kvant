In a separate moment, such movement can be considered as rotation of a segment relative to the point of contact. The tangent point about which the rotation is currently being made is called the instantaneous center of rotation (for example, point $C$ on figure 5).

The idea of ​​an instantaneous center of rotation makes it easy to calculate the velocities of various points on the segment $AB$. For example, for the speed of points $A$ and $B$ we have

$$v_A = \omega \cdot CA,$$
$$v_B = \omega \cdot CB,$$

where $\omega$ is the angular velocity of the segment $AB$ in its “instantaneous” rotation around the center $C$. In this case, the velocities of the points under consideration will be perpendicular to the corresponding radius of rotation, that is, the segments $CA$ and $CB$. In this way, you can calculate the speed of any point lying on this segment or rigidly connected with this segment. If, for example, a non-actor figure is rigidly connected to the segment $AB$, then the speed of any point $P$ is equal to the product $\omega \cdot CP$.
