5. Let $B'$ be the base of the perpendicular dropped from the point $B$ to the plane $P$ (Fig. 4). According to the conditions

$$\Rightarrow BCB' = \varphi, OB = OA = r, CA = 2r, \\
\Rightarrow CBO = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

Finding the length of the segment $AB$ can be found, for example, from the right triangle $AB'B$, the sides of which are easy to calculate. Note that $CB = CA = 2r$ are segments of tangents to the ball drawn from one point. It follows that

$$BB' = CB \sin \varphi = 2r \sin \varphi.$$

Side $AB'$ of triangle $AB'B$ is equal to the perpendicular $OO'$ dropped from $O$ to $BB'$ ($OA \parallel BB'$, as perpendiculars to one plane; $BB' \perp AB'$ since $AB'$ lies in the plane $P$, perpendicular to $BB'$; $OO' \perp BB'$ by construction; therefore $OO'B'A$ is straight). $OO'$ is from right triangle $OO'B'$:

$$OO' = \sqrt{r^2 - (BO')^2},$$

$$BO' = |BB' - B'O'| = |BB' - OA| = |2r \sin \varphi - r|,$$

$$OO' = AB' = \sqrt{\sin \varphi - \sin^2 \varphi} \cdot 2r.$$

Finally, $AB$ is found from triangle $AB'B$:

$$AB = \sqrt{(AB')^2 + (B'B)^2} = 2r \sqrt{\sin \varphi}.$$
