Let us now consider the case where the ratio $\frac{p}{q}$ is equal to one of the four ratios, for example $\frac{a_1}{b_1}$ (see Fig. 8).

In this case, line $px+qy=k_0$ coincides with line $l_1$. All points of the set $A$, with the exception of the point of the segment $M_1M_2$, lie inside the half-plane $K$, and therefore the ration corresponding to any point of the segment $M_1M_2$ is the cheapest admissible ration. Thus, among the points corresponding to the solution to the problem of the cheapest ration, there are always the vertices of the polygon $A$.

A little theory

To find a solution, it is enough to compare the values ​​of the function $px+qy$ at all vertices. If there is one of these values ​​that is smaller than all the others, then the corresponding vertex is the only solution to the problem; if the values ​​at two vertices are equal and less than the values ​​at the other vertices, then these vertices are necessarily adjacent, and all points on the side connecting these vertices serve as solutions to the problem.
