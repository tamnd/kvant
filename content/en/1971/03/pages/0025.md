Therefore, these tangents are parallel to each other, and the distance between them remains equal to $AB$ all the time. But this means that the trajectories $A_1A_2$ and $B_1B_2$ can be considered as parts of some curve of constant cyrin.

However, we need to construct not two sections, but just this curve. If we want to do this using the described operation, we must choose the line $SS$ so that, by drawing it, the segment $AB$ rotates to $180^\circ$, that is, so that the point $A$ comes to the position $B$, and the point $B$ to the position $A$. However, of such lines - $PQRP$ - is shown on figure 6. Let us trace in more detail how the segment $AB$ describes it.

First it rolls along area $PQ$ and comes to position $A'B'$. Moreover, its ends describe the arcs $AA'$ and $BB'$. Then it rolls along the section $QR$, and its ends describe the arcs $A'A''$ and $B'B''$. Finally, it marks the part $RP$, and its ends move along the arcs $A''B$ and $B''A$. As a result, point $A$ comes to position $B$, and point $B$ to position $A$, and the curve of the constructed cirina is closed. In this case, arcs $PQ$, $QR$

and $RP$ can have any shape and do not have to be the same. The only thing that is required of them is that they be convex and touch each other as shown in figure. As for the segments $AP$ and $PB$, their length cannot be arbitrary, or then the arc described by the point $A$ will not “join” the arc described by the point $B$. Here you can build as follows. Let us draw a straight line through the point $P$, tangent to the arc $PQ$, and place the point $A$ on it. Let us now force this straight line to line $PQRP$. Then the end of the arc, which will cover the point $A$, determines the position of the point $B$.

Line $PQRP$, shown in figure 6, consists of three arcs. But the number of arcs may be greater. For example, on figure 7 the curve of the constructed tsirina is shown, obtained by drawing the line $PQRSTP$, consisting of the heel of the arcs.

The curves of the constructed cirina have one more property that makes them similar to the circle. They are expressed by the following theorem:

The perimeter of the curve of the constructed cirina is $l=\pi D$, where $D$ is the width of this curve.
