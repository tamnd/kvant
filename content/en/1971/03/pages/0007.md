A prinya is called an operon to a convex set on a plane if this set lies entirely in one of the half-planes into which this line divides the plane, and at the same time at least one point of the set lies on the line. Thus, the equation $px + qy=k_0$ defines the operatic line to the set $A$.

Let us now examine separately the two cases mentioned above. Suppose first that the relation $\frac{p}{q}$ is not equal to any of the relations

$$\frac{a_1}{b_1}, \frac{a_2}{b_2}, \frac{a_3}{b_3}, \frac{a_4}{b_4}.$$

Then point $M=(x_0, y_0)$ is the only solution to the problem of the cheapest admissible ration. Indeed, any other point of the set $A$ lies in the half-plane $K$ given by the inequality $px + qy \geq k_0$, and therefore corresponds to a more expensive diet. The lines of the infinite polygon $A$ are the rays of the coordinate axes and the line segments $l_1, l_2, l_3, l_4$. Therefore, on each of these sides, one of the six inequalities of system (2) turns into equality, and at each vertex of the polygon $A$ two inequalities of system (2) turn into equalities. In the case chosen at figure 6, the point $M$ lies at the intersection of the lines $l_1$ and $l_3$ (see Fig. 3); hence,

$$\begin{cases} a_1x_0 + b_1y_0 = c_1, \\ a_3x_0 + b_3y_0 = c_3, \end{cases}$$

where

$$x_0 = \frac{b_1c_3 - b_3c_1}{a_3b_1 - a_1b_3}; \quad y_0 = \frac{c_1a_3 - a_1c_3}{a_3b_1 - a_1b_3}.$$
