BRANDS DEDICATED
MARIE AND PIERRE CURIE

Maria Skłodowska-Curie (1867–1934) was born in Warsaw. In 1833 she graduated from high school with a gold medal, but could not receive higher education in Poland. With great difficulty, she managed to enter the University of Paris in 1891.

Since 1895, Maria Sklodowska worked in the laboratory of the famous French physicist and chemist Pierre Curie (1859-1906), whose wife she became. In 1897, M. Curie began studying the radiation of uranium salts discovered by the French scientist Henri Becquerel. Interested in his wife’s work, Pierre Curie soon took part in it. Maria and Pierre's joint research led in 1898 to the discovery of polonium (named after Maria's homeland of Poland) and radium.

The Curies were the first to establish that radioactive rays caused changes in the cells of living organisms.

After the death of her husband, in 1906, Marie Curie took the position of professor and head of the department at the University of Paris, where she was the first woman professor, and for the first time began giving lectures on radioactivity.

For her work, which played a huge role in the creation of modern physics, Marie Curie was elected a member of the academies of sciences in many countries. In 1907, she was elected a corresponding member of the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences, and in 1926, an honorary member of the USSR Academy of Sciences. In 1903, Marie Curie was awarded the Nobel Prize jointly with Henri Becquerel and Pierre Curie; in 1911 she was awarded this prize a second time.
