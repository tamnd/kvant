To the article “On problems in physics”

1. Since the image is obtained at an infinite distance from the lens, its luminous intensity must obviously be considered infinite. But any area whose illumination may interest us is infinitely distant from the image of the light source. The illumination of any area parallel to the plane of the lens, if, of course, this area is located in the beam of light emerging from the lens, is equal to the illumination of the lens itself, regardless of the distance between the lens and the area.

2. $$\frac{1}{H^2(n+1)^2},$$ and we neglected the reflection of light at the boundary of water and air.

3. $$\frac{a \frac{b+c}{2}}{a+\frac{b+c}{2}} = \frac{a(b+c)}{2a+b+c}.$$

4. When the distance changed from 5 to 10 km, all the time the entire light from the laser minus the absorbed air fell on the lens (this is easy to verify by continuing the corresponding geometric calculations). Consequently, the total power of the laser beam over 5 km was 2 times greater. In 10 km (from 10 to 20) it settles 4 times. In addition, starting from 10 km, the receiver lens no longer intercepts the entire beam. According to the inverse square law, even in a completely free-air atmosphere, a signal at 20 km should be 4 times weaker than at 10 km. This means that, in general, the signal when changing the distance from 10 to 20 km is adjusted 16 times.
