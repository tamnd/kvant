2. For both sides of the inequality to make sense, it is necessary and sufficient to satisfy the following inequalities:

1) $\frac{x}{3} > 0$; 2) $\frac{x}{2} - 1 > 0$;
3) $\frac{x+1}{x} > 0$, $\frac{x+1}{x} \neq 1$;
4) $\frac{x-1}{x} > 0$, $\frac{x-1}{x} \neq 1$;
5) $\log_{\frac{x+1}{x}} \frac{x}{3} > 0$;
6) $\log_{\frac{x+1}{x}} \left( \frac{x}{2} - 1 \right) > 0$.

From 1) and 2) it follows that $x > 2$. From $x > 2$ it follows that $\frac{x+1}{x} > 1$ and $\frac{x-1}{x} < 1$; This means that 3) and 4) are performed automatically. Since for $x > 2$ $\frac{x+1}{x} > 1$, and 6) is equivalent to the inequality $\frac{x}{2} - 1 > 1$ or $x > 4$. Thus, the ODZ is given by the inequality $x > 4$.

Since $\frac{x-1}{x} < 1$ and $\frac{x+1}{x} > 1$ for $x > 4$, the original inequality is equivalent to the inequality

$$\log_{\frac{x+1}{x}} \frac{x}{3} \geq \log_{\frac{x+1}{x}} \left( \frac{x}{2} - 1 \right),$$

and the last inequality is equivalent to the inequality

$$\frac{x}{3} \geq \frac{x}{2} - 1,$$

solving which, we find $x \leq 6$. Taking into account the ODZ, we get the final answer: $4 < x \leq 6$.
