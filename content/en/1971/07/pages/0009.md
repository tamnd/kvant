ARCHIMEDES
AND
QUADRATURE
PARABOLA

Even in ancient times, scientists were engaged in calculating areas. There was no general method yet. (This method gives us integral calculus.) Therefore, when calculating the area of ​​a figure, its specific properties were used. The article describes how Archimedes, using the property of a parabola, calculated the area of ​​a parabolic segment.

Who doesn't know the name Archimedes? This great thinker left an indelible mark on human history. Researchers of his work speak with admiration of him as a great engineer, an astronomer without equal, a brilliant mathematician, a man who knows all the secrets of nature...

The mathematical works of Archimedes captivate the reader with clarity of thought, elegance, brought to modern technical calculations. The famous Greek historian Plutarch writes:

“In all geometry it is impossible to find more difficult and profound problems that would be solved as simply and clearly as those that Archimedes studied.”

One of the first mathematical works of Archimedes, “Quadrature of the Parabola,” is no exception.

This work is devoted to calculating the area of ​​a parabolic segment, that is, a figure bounded by a parabola and a line, and presents, in essence, a proof of the following theorem:

The area of ​​a parabolic segment is equal to four times the area of ​​a triangle that has a common base and height with the segment.

The work is naturally divided into two parts. In the first part, Archimedes shows how this theorem was discovered by him with the help of mechanics, namely with the help of the “law of the lever” he established, and in the second part he gives a geometric proof.
