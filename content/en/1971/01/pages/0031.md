Example 3. Prove that the square bicsectrix of a triangle is equal to the difference between the product of its enclosing sides and the product

segments of the third side into which it is divided by a bicector.

Solved and e. We describe a circle around the triangle $ABC$ and extend the bicsectrix $CD$ of the triangle until it meets the circle at the point $E$ (Fig. 3). Let $BC = a$, $AC = b$, $AD = m$, $BD = n$, $CD = l$, $DE = x$.

According to the conditions, $\angle ACE = \angle BCE$, in addition, $\angle AEC = \angle ABC$, are like inscribed angles based on the same arc. Consequently, the triangles $ACE$ and $BCD$ are similar and the equality $$\frac{l + x}{b} = \frac{a}{l},$$ is true, hence $l^2 = ab - lx$. Chords $AB$ and $CE$ intersect at point $D$. Therefore the equality $lx = mn$ holds. Therefore, $l^2 = ab - mn$.
