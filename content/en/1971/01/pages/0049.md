2. Let the tractor turn $x$ ($2a$) in $n$ hours, therefore $1$ ($2a$) in $\frac{n}{x}$ hours.

A horse turns in $n$ hours $(x-p)$ ($2a$), and $1$ ($2a$) in $\frac{n}{x-p}$ hours.

According to the conditions of the problem, $\frac{n}{x}$ is less than $\frac{n}{x-p}$ by $t$ hours, that is, $\frac{n}{x} + t = \frac{n}{x-p}$ ($x \neq 0, x \neq p$). After the transformation we obtain the equation $tx^2 - ptx - np = 0$. Solving it, we find $x_{1,2} = \frac{pt \pm \sqrt{(pt)^2 + 4ptn}}{2t}$.

Which of these two roots satisfies the conditions of the problem? By condition, $n > 0, t > 0, p > 0$, so $\sqrt{(pt)^2 + 4ptn}$ has a real value. $(pt)^2 + 4ptn > (pt)^2$ and therefore $\sqrt{(pt)^2 + 4ptn} > pt$.

So the root

$$x_1 = \frac{pt + \sqrt{(pt)^2 + 4ptn}}{2t}$$

gives the answer to the question of the problem, exactly how many hectares does a tractor turn in $n$ hours, and a horse turns in $n$ hours

$$x - p = \frac{-pt + \sqrt{(pt)^2 + 4ptn}}{2t}$$.

The second root of the equation is negative and disappears according to the meaning of the problem.

When compiling the initial equation from the value $\frac{n}{x}, \frac{n}{x-p}, t$, some of the applicants for some reason subtracted more from less and put the equation $\frac{n}{x-p} + t = \frac{n}{x}$.

This is the result of not paying attention.

When solving a problem with specific physical or other content, you should be especially careful and always check your calculations. Formal statements must correspond to the actual state of affairs. In addition, it should be remembered that the whole solution to this type of problem is not the solution of the compiled equation, but obtaining an answer to the question posed in the task.
