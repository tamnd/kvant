Calculating the moment of inertia is usually a difficult task. But for bodies that are homogeneous and have a regular geometric shape, such a calculation with respect to some axes of rotation is possible, although not always in simple ways. For one special simple case we will also present the calculation.

Let us calculate the moment of inertia of the wheel, in which almost all the mass is concentrated in its rim, relative to the axis of rotation, by redistributing the plane of the wheel and passing through its center. An example is a weight distribution wheel with thin spokes.

Let us divide the rim into separate small parts with masses $m_1, m_2, m_3$, etc. Each of them can be considered a material point located at a distance $R$ from the rotation axis, where $R$ is the radius of the wheel. The moment of inertia of each point is equal to $m_1R^2, m_2R^2, m_3R^2$, etc. The total moment of inertia of the entire wheel is equal to the sum of the moments of inertia of its part:

$$I = (m_1 + m_2 + m_3 + \dots)R^2.$$

But the sum $m_1 + m_2 + m_3 + \dots$ is obviously equal to the mass of the entire wheel $m$. So the moment of inertia of the wheel about the axis passing through its center and redistributing its plane is equal to

$$I = mR^2.$$
