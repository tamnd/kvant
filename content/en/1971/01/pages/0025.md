From this definition we can deduce an interesting “optical” property of the ellipse: if a light source is placed at one of the focuses of the mirror ellipse *), then all the rays coming out of it will be collected at another focus of the ellipse**). This means that the angles between the straight lines going to the point of the ellipse from its foci and the tangent to the ellipse are equal to each other (Fig. 4).

Let us prove that the ellipse has one more property, which is even convenient to take as the definition of an ellipse. To do this, we draw a tangent $t$ to the ellipse at point $A$ and lower the redistributions from both foci of the ellipse to this tangent (Fig. 5). We extend one of the redistributions beyond the straight line $t$ to a distance $F'_2 N_2$ equal to $N_2 F_2$. Let us now connect the points $F'_2$ and $F_1$ with the point $A$. Let us prove that $F'_2 AF_1$ is a straight line.

This is immediately clear from the fact that $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2$ (by construction) and $\alpha_1 = \alpha_3$ (according to the optical property of the ellipse). Therefore, $\alpha_2 = \alpha_3$, and therefore $F'_2 AF_1$, is a straight line.

*) An ellipside is the geometric locus of a point, the sum of the distances from which to two fixed points is constructed. Such a surface is obtained by growing an ellipse around its axis.

**) The easiest way to prove this property of an ellipse is to use Fermat’s principle (see «Kvant» No. 11, 1970).

Further, since $AF'_2 = AF_2$, then $F_1 F'_2 = F_1 A + AF'_2 = F_1 A + AF_2$, that is, the segment $F_1 F'_2$ is equal to the sum of the distances from the ellipse point to the foci. Let's denote this sum $2a$. Let us now lower the redistribution $F_1 B$ onto the line $F'_2 F'_2$, calculate the length of the leg $F_1 B$ from two right triangles - brown and yellow - and equate both expressions for $F_1 B$ to each other:

$(2a)^2 - (\rho_1 + \rho_2)^2 = (2c)^2 - (\rho_2 - \rho_1)^2$.

Opening the brackets, we get

$\rho_1 \rho_2 = a^2 - c^2$.

So, the product $\rho_1 \rho_2$ does not depend on the point at which the tangent is drawn. Let's draw it at the end of the semi-minor axis (Fig. 6). In this case $a^2 - b^2 = c^2$. Therefore $\rho_1 \rho_2 = b^2$.

Thus, we got the main theorem:

The product of the distances from two foci to any tangent of the ellipse is a constant value equal to the square of the semi-minor axis.
