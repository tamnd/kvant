Conservation of angular momentum means conservation of not only the numerical value of the quantity $I\omega$. We must not forget that angular momentum is a vector quantity. Therefore, conservation of angular momentum also means conservation of its direction. But the moment of impulse is directed, as we know, along the axis of rotation. Therefore, any rotating body that is not affected by external moments of forces cannot change the position of the rotation axis. This is the “secret” of the stability of a rolling wheel, tops, etc. This is why discus throwers, throwing their projectile, cause it to rotate. This allows the disc to fly without tumbling. The ability of a rotating body to maintain the position of its axis of rotation (in the absence of moments of external forces) is widely used in technology. The use of so-called gyroscopic compasses, various stabilizers, etc. is based on this property. A gyroscopic compass is essentially a rotating body. If, at launch, the entrance was installed so that the weld was oriented to the north, then it will maintain this direction of the best magnetic compass, which is influenced by both the iron hull of the ship and changes in the Earth's magnetic field. Gyroscopic stabilizers are sometimes installed on the hulls, which are huge multi-ton wheels driven by powerful engines. In an effort to maintain the position of the rotation axis, such a stabilizer changes the quality of the ship. Gyroscopic stabilizers find important applications in space flight technology and in automatic devices for various purposes.

Kinetic energy of rotating bodies

A rotating body is a moving body, since all its parts, except the point located on the rotation axis, move. Therefore it has kinetic energy. To express the kinetic energy, let's start again with the rotation of a small ball on a thin rod, that is, with the rotation of a point. In this case, the kinetic energy can be written in the general form

$$K = \frac{mv^2}{2},$$

where $m$ is the mass of the ball, and $v$ is its linear speed. But since $v = \omega r$, the expression for $K$ can be written in the form

$$K = \frac{m\omega^2r^2}{2}.$$
