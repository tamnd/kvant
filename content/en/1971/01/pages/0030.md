AUXILIARY CIRCLE

The solution to many geometric problems begins with drawing auxiliary lines that help establish a connection between data and unknown elements of the figure. Recalling a successful auxiliary formation is often difficult. Therefore, you should approach each solved problem and try to find out why certain auxiliary lines lead to the goal. Is it possible to use the found example to solve some other problems?

At the next meeting of the mathematical circle, we will consider one of the interesting examples of solving geometric problems, which consists of introducing an auxiliary circle into the drawing.

Example 1. The altitudes AP, BQ and CR are drawn in an acute triangle. Prove that $\angle ABQ = \angle APR$).

Solved and e. Let H be the point of intersection of the altitudes of triangle ABC (Fig. 1). Since $\angle APB$ and $\angle CRB$ are straight lines, a circle can be described around the quadrilateral BPHR by taking BH as the diameter. Having constructed it, we replace that $\angle ABQ = \angle APR$ (as inscribed angles based on the same arc).
