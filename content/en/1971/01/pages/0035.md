⟦folio 33⟧

There is no more universal phenomenon in nature than the propagation of waves. Waves on the surface of water, sound, light, radio, transfer of deformation from one part of a solid body to another - all these are wave processes. Quantum mechanics shows that the movement of microparticles is controlled by wave laws. All these waves have different physical natures, different propagation speeds, different frequencies and wavelengths. But regardless of this, the movement of any waves has a lot in common. Having studied the laws of propagation of waves of one nature, it is possible to practically use a large number of them without changing them to waves of another type. It is most convenient to study waves on the surface of the wave.

What is a wave? Throw a stone into the ore. On the calm horizontal surface of labor a running circle will appear. The points on the water surface that the wave should reach begin to oscillate relative to their equilibrium position. This position corresponds to a horizontal surface. The farther a point is from the place where the stone fell, the more late it “learns” about the fall of the stone. Disturbance spreads at a certain speed. The points to which the disturbance must simultaneously be located are in the same stage of oscillatory motion (in the same phase). The speed of movement of this phase is the speed of the wave. It changes in the direction normal to the wave front.

For waves of any nature, it is always possible to indicate physical objects that, under the influence of the wave, will experience a disturbance, that is, a deviation from their equilibrium values. For sound, these are periodic increases and decreases in pressure. For radio waves and light - rapid changes in the direction of the electric and magnetic fields.

The properties of all media without exception are such that a disturbance that arises in a certain area spreads, being transmitted from point to point with a finite speed, which depends on the nature of the disturbance and the properties of the medium.

For a wave to occur, a source of disturbance is required, that is, an external cause that causes a disturbance in propagation in a certain region of the medium. A small source, such as, for example, a stone thrown into water, emits in a homogeneous medium (that is, in a medium in which the speed of the wave does not depend on the direction of its propagation) spherical waves (circular on the surface of the wave), propagating along radii. Such wave sources are called point sources.

One of the basic principles of elementary wave theory is the principle of wave independence or superposition principle. He argues that the disturbance caused by a wave at an observed point does not depend on the fact that other waves simultaneously pass through this point. The principle of superposition gives a simple rule for finding the total action of waves from several sources: the total amount of work is equal to the sum of the oscillations caused by each source separately.

A characteristic feature of wave processes is wave interference. Interference is called a set of phenomena that arise in a medium during the propagation of waves from two or more sources that oscillate in accordance (synchronously). It turns out that at some points the oscillations caused by the simultaneous action of two sources will be more confluent or weaker than the oscillations caused by each source separately. It may happen that coordinated waves will cancel each other out.
