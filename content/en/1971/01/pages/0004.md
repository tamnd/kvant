The task of the mechanics of rotational movement is to be able to achieve a certain angle of rotation of the body at any time, if the initial angle of rotation is known. To do this, you need to know the rapid change in the angle of rotation, that is, the corner speed, which is usually denoted by the letter $\omega$. For a body rotating at a constant angular velocity,

$$\omega = \frac{\varphi - \varphi_0}{t},$$

where $\varphi - \varphi_0$ is the change in the rotation angle over a period of time of $t$ seconds.

If the angular velocity is constant, then the value of the angle of rotation at any time can be easily found:

$$\varphi = \varphi_0 + \omega t;$$

if the initial rotation angle $\varphi_0$ is zero, then

$$\varphi = \omega t.$$

This expression is similar to the formula $S = vt$ for uniform translational motion. Consequently, the angular velocity in rotational motion plays the same role as linear velocity $v$ for translational motion.

Angular velocity, like linear velocity, may not be constant. Then, in order to find the speed at any time, you need to know the angular acceleration, which, like linear acceleration $a$, is defined as a rapid change in angular velocity. In uniform rotation

$$\alpha = \frac{\omega - \omega_0}{t},$$

where $\omega$ and $\omega_0$ are the values ​​of angular velocity at the end and at the beginning of a long time period of $t$ seconds.

It is easy to find the connection between angular velocity and acceleration and their linear “twins” $v$ and $a$. Since $\omega = \frac{\varphi}{t}$ and $\varphi = \frac{S}{r}$, then $\omega = \frac{S}{rt}$.

But $\frac{S}{t} = v$ therefore

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r} \text{ и } v = \omega r.$$

In the same way, since $\alpha = \frac{\omega - \omega_0}{t}$, and $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$ and $\omega_0 = \frac{v_0}{r}$, then $\alpha = \frac{v - v_0}{rt}$. But $\frac{v - v_0}{t}$ is equal to the linear acceleration of $a$, so

$$\alpha = \frac{a}{r} \text{ и } a = \alpha r.$$

Thus, the numerical values ​​of the linear quantities $S$, $v$ and $a$ for each point of the rotating body are equal to the corresponding values ​​of the angular quantities (they relate not to individual points, but to the entire body), multiplied by the radius $r$ of rotation of this point.
