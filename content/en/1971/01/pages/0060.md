To the article “Rotational motion of bodies.”

1. In kilograms multiplied by a square meter ($\kappa \cdot m^2$).
2. The rolling times of the cylinders are the same, since their moments of inertia are equal. (Prove it!)
3. The work done by the friction force $A = M \cdot \varphi$ (where $\varphi = 2\pi \cdot 5 = 10\pi$ radan) must be equal to the energy of the wheel before braking

$$M\varphi = \frac{I\omega_0^2}{2}.$$

Hence $M = \frac{I\omega_0^2}{2\varphi} \approx 0,05 \text{ nm}.$

4. Let us first find what angular velocity the Earth will have after 100 lem. Since the time required for one revolution is related to the angular velocity by the formula $\tau = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$, which is $\Delta\tau = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_2} - \frac{2\pi}{\omega_1}$, where $\omega_1$ is the angular velocity of the Earth now ($\omega_1 = \frac{2\pi}{24\cdot 3600} \text{ рад/c} = 7,3 \cdot 10^{-5} \text{ рад/c}$), $\omega_2$ is its angular velocity after 100 years and $\Delta\tau = 1$ s.
Let us now write down the equation of motion of the Earth. $M_{\text{Tp}} = I \alpha$. I is the moment of inertia of the Earth. It is equal

$$\frac{2}{5} \cdot 5,9 \cdot 10^{24} \kappa \cdot 4 \cdot 10^{13} m^2 = 9,4 \cdot 10^{37} \kappa \cdot m^2.$$

Substituting $\alpha$ into the equation of the Earth's motion together with its expression through $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, we obtain $M_{\text{Tp}} = I \frac{\omega_2 - \omega_1}{t} = 2,6 \cdot 10^{19} \text{ nm}.$

5. The work that needs to be done in the time $t = 10$ hour = 3.6·10⁴ s is equal to the change in the kinetic energy of the stabilizer

$$\Delta K = \frac{I\omega^2}{2}.$$

Pozny

$$N = \frac{I\omega^2}{2t} = \frac{Mr^2\omega^2}{2t} = 4,5 \cdot 10^4 \text{ em}.$$

6. The friction force $F_{\text{Tp}} = kmg$ acting on the ball replaces the translational motion of its center of mass and increases the speed of its rotation. Pozny

$$F_{\text{Tp}} = ma \text{ и } F_{\text{Tp}} \cdot r = I\alpha,$$

where $a$ is the acceleration of the translational motion of the ball, $\alpha$ is the acceleration of the rotating ball, $n$ is the module (n-1) - 1.

Movements and $I = \frac{2}{5}mr^2$ – moment of inertia of the ball.

By the moment $t$ the ball will have a translational speed $v = v_0 - at = v_0 - \frac{F_{\text{Tp}}}{m}t = v_0 - kgt$ and an angular velocity of rotation

$$\omega = \alpha t = \frac{F_{\text{Tp}}r}{I} t = \frac{kmgr}{2\frac{5}{mr^2}} t = \frac{5}{2} \frac{kgt}{r}.$$

If at this moment the slippage stops, then $\omega = \frac{v}{r}$, or

$$\frac{5}{2} \frac{kgt}{r} = \frac{v_0 - kgt}{r}.$$

Hence $t = \frac{2}{7} \frac{v_0}{kg}.$

At moment $t$ the speed of translational motion of the ball is equal to $v_0 - kg \frac{2v_0}{7kg} = \frac{5}{7} v_0.$

Let the distance traveled by the ball in time $t$ be equal to $v_0 t - \frac{at^2}{2} = \frac{12}{49} \frac{v_0^2}{kg}.$

7. Let's write down the law of conservation of angular momentum

$$I\omega = mR^2\omega_1,$$

where $\omega$ is the angular speed of the ship, and $\omega_1$ is the angular speed of the astronaut and $I = \frac{2}{3} MR^2$ is the moment of inertia of the ship.

This is equal to the familiarized moment $(MR^2 + 2mR^2)\omega = [MR^2 + 2m(R + l)^2]\omega.$

Since $\omega_1 = \frac{1}{n} \omega$, then after simple transformations of this equation we find that

$$l = R \left( \sqrt{n + \frac{M}{2m}} (n - 1) - 1 \right).$$
