Example 5. Construct an equidistant triangle so that its vertices lie respectively on three given parallel lines.

Fig. 5.

There are several known solutions to this interesting problem using expressions around a point, similarity and algebraic methods. A very short solution is obtained by constructing an auxiliary circle.

Solution. Let $ABC$ be the original equidistant triangle whose vertices lie respectively on the parallel lines $a, b$ and $c$ (Fig. 5). The circle circumscribed around it intersects the line $c$, in addition to the point $C$, at some point $D$. Then, based on the property of inscribed angles, we have $\angle ADC = \angle ABC = 60^\circ$.

The following construction follows from this. From an arbitrary point $D$ of line $c$ we draw rays that form angles $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ with line $c$. Let one of the rays intersect line $a$ at point $A$ and line $b$ at point $B_1$, and the other ray intersect line $b$ at point $B$. Then the segment $AB$ will be the side of the triangle $ABC$.

Let us plot on the line $c$ the segment $DC$ equal to the segment $AB_1$, so that the points $C$ and $A$ lie on the same side of the line $BD$. Then the point $C$ is a triangle of triangle $ABC$, since triangles $ABB_1$ and $BCD$ are equal, which means that $AB = BC$ and $\angle ABC = 60^\circ$.
