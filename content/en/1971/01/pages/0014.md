⟦folio 12⟧

In conclusion, we again present for comparison some of the mechanics of translational motion, and similar formulas relating to rotational motion.

| Forward movement | Rotational movement |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\vec{F} = ma$ | $\vec{M} = I\alpha$ |
| $\frac{\Delta(mv)}{\Delta t} = \vec{F}$ | $\frac{\Delta(I\omega)}{\Delta t} = \vec{M}$ |
| $\Delta(mv) = 0$ at $\vec{F} = 0$ | $\Delta(I\omega) = 0$ at $\vec{M} = 0$ |
| $A = FS$ | $A = M\varphi$ |
| $K = \frac{mv^2}{2}$ | $K = \frac{I\omega^2}{2}$ |

Assignment and questions

1. In what units is the moment of inertia measured?
2. Two cylinders of identical diameters and masses roll down an inclined plane without piercing. One of them is lead and has a cork stop, the other is cork with a lead shell. Which cylinder will roll off the plane in less time?
3. A wheel whose moment of inertia is 0.01 SI units is expressed with an angular velocity of 4 rps. What must be the friction torque for the wheel to stop rotating after 5 revolutions?
4. Tides in the seas and oceans act on the rotating Earth like brake pads and create a braking friction force. Because of this action of the tides, in 100 years the length of a day on Earth will be 1 second longer than the next day. Calculate the tidal friction force.
5. A ship's gyroscopic stabilizer with a radius of $R = 2$ m and a mass of $M = 120$ m is driven by a motor. What power is required to bring the stabilizer rotation speed to 13 rpm within 10 hours? Assume that the mass of the stabilizer is concentrated on its rim (in this case, the moment of inertia is equal to $MR^2$).
6. A homogeneous ball of radius $R$ and mass $M$ is initially launched along a plane so that it slides along it without rolling with a speed $v_0$. There is friction between the ball and the plane, the coefficient of which is $k$. How far will the ball travel before it stops piercing relative to the plane? What will be the speed of the ball at this moment?
7. Two windows of the spherical satellite are located at diametrically opposed points of the shell. The astronaut walks from the center of the ship along a radial ladder to one of the windows, then walks along the side of the ship to the second window, and finally returns along the radial ladder to his place in the center of the ship. At what angle does the ship rotate relative to the stars, if the mass of the ship is $M$, its radius is $R$, the mass of the astronaut is $m$ and we can assume that the entire mass of the ship is concentrated in its shell.
8. To replace the rotation of satellites, it is proposed to use the following devices. Two weights of mass $m$ each are attached to the side surface of the satellite on threads of length $l$. The loads are released simultaneously. They fly away from the satellite and at the moment when the threads are stretched, they follow from the hooks to which they are attached. At what length of the thread will the rotation speed of the satellite after this operation decrease by $n$ times?
Assume that the satellite is a cylinder of mass $M$ and radius $R$; all the mass of the ship is concentrated in the side shell.
