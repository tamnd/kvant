4. Let's write the following equation:

$$\frac{3 \left( \cos 2x + \frac{\cos 2x}{\sin 2x} \right)}{\cos 2x - \cos 2x} - 2 (\sin 2x + 1) = 0.$$ (1)

Now it’s easy for us to determine DL. In fact, at $\sin 2x = 0$ $\frac{\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}$ loses its meaning, and at $\cos 2x = 0$ the denominator of the fraction is zero and the entire fraction loses its meaning. Consequently, the ODZ is determined by conditions $\sin 2x \neq 0$, $\cos 2x \neq 0$. After reduction by $\cos 2x$ and simplification, equation (1) takes the form

$$3(\sin 2x + 1) - 2(\sin 2x + 1) (1 - \sin 2x) = 0$$ (2)

(the denominator was the set $1 - \sin 2x$, which does not vanish in the ODZ, since $\cos 2x \neq 0$). After taking the common factor out of the bracket, equation (2) splits into two:

1) $1 + 2 \sin 2x = 0$;
2) $1 + \sin 2x = 0$.

Solving the first equation, we find

$$\sin 2x = -\frac{1}{2}; x = (-1)^{n+1} \frac{\pi}{12} + \frac{\pi k}{2}$$

($k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$).

All these values ​​are contained in the ODZ.

Solving the second equation, we obtain $\sin 2x = -1$, whence $\cos 2x = 0$, which eliminates the ODZ.

So, the solutions to the original equation are only the values

$$x = (-1)^{n+1} \frac{\pi}{12} + \frac{\pi k}{2}$$

($k = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$).

When solving this equation, many people did not take into account the ODZ and included the values ​​$\cos 2x = 0$, that is, $x = -\frac{\pi}{4} + \pi k$, in the solution.
