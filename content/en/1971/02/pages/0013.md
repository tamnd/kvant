DIALOGUE ABOUT TEMPERATURE

M. Ya. Azbel

A popular science article is always a kind of game between the Author and the Reader, in which the Author is believed at his words (hence the not so rarely realized possibility of desire), but he has no right to write incomprehensibly and cause boredom. Otherwise, the Author will lose: the article will not be read and, therefore, was written in vain.

A generally popular article looks like a long monologue. But the ancient Greeks already understood that scientific dialogue is much more interesting. Since this does not break the rules of the game, let's follow their example. As a result, the Reader will have the opportunity, showing character, to first try to find answers to the questions posed, each of which is a physical task, and sometimes an entire problem.

So the curtain rises - we begin.

Author
Temperature, please, is the first understandable quantity that a person encounters in his life. A five-year-old child does not yet know how to count, but he already says: “I am healthy, I have 36.6,” without even understanding the meaning of the spoken words (for decimal fractions you need to double the size). This means that we are talking about one of the most important physical concepts. Throughout its history, humanity has been trying to understand things that amaze us even in childhood: space, time... For the more instilled the concept, the simpler the question is formulated, the deeper it is usually and the more difficult it is to answer. However, the more difficult it is to ask it - it seems so prominent. Thus, in one of the detective stories, detectives are knocked off their feet, looking for what is in front of their eyes.

Reader
God be with him, and with detectives, and with philosophical reasoning. Can't you be more specific?

Author
Please. This is a question, the consistent clarification of which ultimately leads us to the basic concepts of the general theory of relativity.

All methods of measuring temperature are based on the expression of temperature, that is, on the fact that a hotter body is cooled and a colder body is heated. Isn't it?

Reader
Certainly.

Author
Why is this happening? Why does heat always move from a more heated body to a less heated one?

Reader
Not always! A refrigerator, for example, cools even though there is no colder body near it. The heat here is transferred to a warmer body - the surrounding air and everything that is in the room.

Author
You were ahead of the course of my reasoning, making a very important point. Indeed, if necessary and expended work, you can heat a hot one with a cold body.
