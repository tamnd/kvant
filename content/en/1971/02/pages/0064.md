To the problems of the XII International Mathematical Olympiad
It should be borne in mind that each problem can be solved in several ways, including those different from those indicated here.
The reader is advised to find his own solution to each problem and not to use instructions.
1. First, for an arbitrary triangle, prove that $\frac{r}{\rho} = \text{tg} \frac{\alpha}{2} \text{tg} \frac{\beta}{2}$, where $r$ and $\rho$ are, respectively, the radii of the inscribed and external circle in $\Delta ACB$ (the latter touches the side $AB$, $\alpha = \Rightarrow CAB$, $\beta = \Rightarrow CBA$). To prove the indicated formula, express $AB$ through $r$ and the tangents of the angles $\frac{\alpha}{2}$ and $\frac{\beta}{2}$, then through $\rho$ and the tangents of the same angles, and then equate the resulting expressions. The statement of the problem follows from the immediate proof.
2. In the primacy $\frac{A_{n-1}}{A_n} < \frac{B_{n-1}}{B_n}$, replace all terms with polynomials with respect to $a$ and $b$ with coefficients $x_n, x_{n-1}, \dots, x_0$. Using identical transformations (subtract both sides of the inequality from 1, then consider the reciprocal fractions that lead to the sum, and compare the individual terms) the indicated inequality is reduced to an estimated one.
3. It follows from the condition that everything is $b_n \geqslant 0$. From the fact that the root of the product of neighboring numbers is less than the largest of them, it follows that the ratio of the sum of the roots of these numbers to the larger number does not exceed two divided by the root of the smaller number, which can be transformed as $(1 - \frac{a_{k-1}}{a_k}) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{a_k}} \leqslant 2\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{k-1}}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{a_k}}\right)$. The latter directly leads to the conclusion that $b_n < 2$.
To prove the second part of the statement, take $M$ such that $c < M < 2$, and qualitative sequences $a_n - \text{geomетрическую прогрессию}$ with $a_n = qn$, where $q > 1$ is determined from the correspondence $\frac{\sqrt{q} + 1}{q} = M$, resulting in the required $b_n$.
4. Of six consecutive numbers, at least one is divisible by 5. Show that in this case $n$ and $n+5$ are divisible by 5. Then show that the product of any two components is greater than the third. It follows that each group has three components. By enumeration we can establish that equality is impossible for any integer $n$.
5. Prove that opposite edges are mutually perpendicular. Based on this, prove that the sums of the squares of opposite edges are equal. Hence it turns out that $6(AD^2 + BD^2 + DC^2) = 3(AC^2 + BC^2 + AB^2)$.
