4. When reflected from a spherical mirror of finite dimensions, a parallel beam of rays does not converge at one point.

Let $A'A$ be the outermost ray in the incident beam ($OA=R=2m$), $AE$ be the reflected ray, and the dimensions of the receiver standing at the focus are such that the beam $AE$ hits it. From the triangle $CDO(CD_OA)$ it follows:

$$OC = \frac{R}{2\cos i}.$$ Since $OF = \frac{R}{2}$, then the segments $FC$ and $CP$ are respectively equal:

$$FR = OC - OF = \frac{R}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\cos i} - 1\right),$$

$$CP = OP - OC = R\left(1 - \frac{1}{\cos i}\right).$$

Now from the similarity of the red triangles $APC$ and $CFE$ it follows that

$$\frac{x}{a} = \frac{CF}{CP} = \frac{1 - \cos i}{2\cos i - 1}.$$

Here $a=AP$ is the transverse size of the mirror, and $x=EF$ is the radius of the receiver. From triangle $APO$ we find that $\sin i = \frac{a}{R}$. That's why

$$\cos i = \sqrt{1 - \frac{a^2}{R^2}} \approx 1 - \frac{a^2}{2R^2} \left(a^2 \ll R^2\right)$$

And

$$x = \frac{a^3}{2R^2 \left(1 - \frac{a^2}{R^2}\right)} \approx \frac{a^3}{2R^2} \approx 1,5\text{ см}.$$

To answer the second question, we transform the last equality into the form $a = \sqrt[3]{2R^2x}$.

It is clear that the area of ​​the working part of the mirror, that is, that part of the mirror from which the radiation flux is “removed” by the receiver, is proportional to $a^2 = \sqrt[3]{4R^4x^2}$, that is, proportional to $x^2/3$. Therefore, when $x$ decreases by 8 times, the flux received by the receiver will decrease by 4 times.
