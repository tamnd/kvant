Solution. In this problem, the role of the rod is played by the ray $SB$. At point $A$ the beam “touches” the limit, and at point $B$ - with the screen, forming the boundaries of the shadow. Let's create two equations connecting the projections of velocities at points $A$ and $B$: $\omega \cdot SA = v \cos(\pi - \alpha)$ is the projection onto $LK$, $\omega \cdot SB = u \cos \alpha$ is the projection onto $MN$. Here $\omega$ is the angular velocity of the beam rotation. Dividing the second equality by the first and taking into account that

$$\frac{SB}{SA} = \frac{l}{SL} = \frac{l}{vt} \quad \text{и} \quad tg\alpha = \frac{h}{SC} = \frac{h}{vt},$$

we get

$$u = \frac{lh}{vt^2}.$$

Let now the rod $AB$ of a given length $l$ (Fig. 5) move arbitrarily. The velocities $\vec{v}_A$ and $\vec{v}_B$ of its ends can be different, but since the length of the rod does not change, the projections of these velocities $v''_A$ and $v''_B$ must be equal: $v''_A = v''_B$. The velocity projections $v'_A$ and $v'_B$ define the circular motion of the rod with an angular velocity $\frac{v'_B - v'_A}{l}$ (check this is the most accurate).

3. The rod $AB$ rests its ends on the sides of the obtuse angle $\beta$ (Fig. 6). The upper end of the rod is pulled at speed $v$ along side $AO$. Find the dependence of the speed $u$ of point $B$ on the angle $\alpha$.

Solved and e. Since the length of the rod $AB$ is unchanged, the projections of the velocities of its ends onto the direction of the rod are the same:

$$u \cos \alpha = v \cos(\pi - \alpha - \beta),$$

or

$$u = -\frac{\cos(\alpha + \beta)}{\cos \alpha} v.$$
