IV INTERNATIONAL PHYSICS OLYMPIAD FOR SCHOOLCHILDREN

From July 5 to July 15, the International Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren was held in Moscow. It is only the fourth time it has been held. Mathematicians have a rich tradition in this sense - their first International Olympiad took place back in 1959, and they are held daily. And yet, the International Physics Olympiads are older than similar Chemistry Olympiads - the International Chemical Olympiad was held for the first time in 1970.

Academician of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR V. A. Fabrikant opens the IV International Physics Olympiad for schoolchildren. Photo by M. V. Alpert (APN).

The current Olympiad was attended by high school students and graduates (who graduated from school in 1970) from the People's Republic of Bulgaria, the Hungarian People's Republic, the German Democratic Republic, the Polish People's Republic, the Socialist Republic of Romania, the USSR, the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic and the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia.

The supreme body of the Olympiad was the International Commission, which included the scientific supervisors of all delegates. It was headed by Academician of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR V. A. Fabrikant.

The Soviet team included guys who successfully performed at the All-Union Physics Olympiad last year in Sverdlovsk: Igor Bulzhenkov (Ufa), Mikhail Voloshin (Moscow), Sergei Gorbachevsky (Lomonosov, Leningrad region), Vladimir Kravtsov (Gorky), Igor Lyuksitov (Kyiv) and Boris Petrov (Pskov).

The scientific leader of the Soviet team was Moscow State University professor V.I. Iveronova.

Objectives of the IV International Physics Olympics

Problem 1

A long block of mass $M=1$ is located on a smooth horizontal plane along which it can move without friction. A carriage with a motor can slide along the upper horizontal edge of the block. The mass of the carriage with the motor is $m=100$ e, the friction coefficient is $k=0,02$. A motor at a constant speed of $V_0=10$ cm/sec winds a thread onto a shaft, the second end of which in one case is tied to a sufficiently distant peg mounted on a fixed support, and in the other - to a peg attached to a block (Fig. 1). Keeping the block motionless, allow the carriage to begin moving at a speed of $v_0$, after which the block is released. By the time the block is released, the front edge of the carriage is at a distance of $L=50$ cm from the front edge of the block. For both cases, determine the nature and speed of movement of the block and carriage after the block begins to move and the time during which the carriage reaches the front edge of the block.

Problem 2

The unit cell of a rock salt (NaCl) crystal is a cube whose edge length is $a=5,6$ A ($A=10^{-8}$ cm) (Fig. 2). The red balls on figure indicate the positions of sodium atoms, and the blue balls indicate the positions of chlorine atoms. The entire rock salt crystal is obtained by repeating such elementary cells. The atomic weight of sodium is 23, the atomic weight of chlorine is 35.5. The density of rock salt is $\rho=2,22$ e/cm³. It is necessary to determine the mass of a hydrogen atom from these data.
