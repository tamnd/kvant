On the continuation of the segment AM we plot a point B' such that MB'=MB (see figure). Angle B'MB as an external angle of triangle AMB is equal to the sum of angles MAB and MBA. At the same time, the inscribed angle KMA is measured by half the arc AK and, therefore, is equal to half the sum of the angles MAB and MBA. Therefore MK is the bissertis of the angle B'MB. This means that MK⊥B'B and KB'=KB. It follows that KA=KB=KB'. Dropping the redistributor KH from point K to AM, we obtain AH=HB'=HM+MB, as required.

This problem can also be solved using trigonometry: putting ∠MAB=α, ∠MBA=β, we obtain AM=2R sin β, MB=2R sin α. Pozotomu Pozotomu

AM+MB=2R(sin β+sin α)=4R sin α+β/2 cos α-β/2.
On the other side,

AK=2R sin α+β/2, ∠KAM=β-α/2,
and therefore

AH=2R sin α+β/2 cos β-α/2=1/2(AM+MB).

Archimedes, the author of this problem, published it in the practice “Measuring a Circle” in the following formulation: “If a straight segment inscribed in an arc of a circle, complex into two unequal parts, accepts a redistributor dropped onto it from the middle of the arc, then this redistributor will divide the entire broken line in half.” This statement allows us to calculate the lengths of the chords of the sum and difference of two given arcs.

F1008. Rod AB is placed inside a cylinder. At point A the rod is hinged, at point C it rests on the top edge of the cylinder, points A and C lie in a vertical plane passing through the axes of the cylinder (Fig. 1); the angle that makes up the rod with the horizon is equal to α. The rod is moved along the upper edge so that it touches it at point C' and the angle C'OC is equal to φ. At what minimum coefficient will the triangle be in equilibrium in this position?

Let the rod be in equilibrium at the new position when it touches the cylinder at point C'. The rod is acted upon by the force of gravity, the reaction force of the hinge at point A, the force N of the normal reaction of the cylinder at point C' and the friction force f. The condition for the equilibrium of the rod is that the geometric sum of these forces is equal to zero and the sum of the moments of forces is equal to zero.

Of all the equations for the equilibrium of a rod, we consider one - the equality to zero of the sum of the moments of forces relative to the vertical axis passing point A (relative to this axis, the moment of the reaction force of the hinge and the moment of the gravitational force are equal to zero). It is easy to understand that this condition boils down to the fact that the total vector of forces N and f lies in the vertical plane passing point A (relative to this axis, the moment of the reaction force of the hinge and the moment of gravity are equal to zero).
