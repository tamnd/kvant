1. To solve the problem, it is enough to prove that for any natural number $d$ at least one of the numbers $2d-1$, $5d-1$ or $13d-1$ is not a square of an integer. Costati, the numbers $2\cdot 5-1=9$, $2\cdot 13-1=25$ and $5\cdot 13-1=64$ are the squares of integers.

We will carry out the proof using the method “by contradiction” - we assume that for some natural number $d$ the equalities $2d-1=n^2$, $5d-1=m^2$, $13d-1=k^2$ hold. From the notation of the number $n^2$ it follows that it is odd, which means that $n$ is odd, that is, $n=2p+1$; substituting this value together with $n$ gives $2d-1=4p^2+4p+1$ or $d=2p(p+1)+1$, but the product of two consecutive integers is always that, so $p(p+1)=2r$ and $d=4r+1$. Replacing $d$ with the resulting expression in the remaining two correspondences, we get $m^2=20r+4$, $k^2=52r+12$. This shows that $m$ and $k$ are even numbers; let's denote $m=2a$, $k=2b$. Replacing $m$ and $k$ with $2a$ and $2b$ in these equalities and canceling by 4, we get $a^2=5r+1$, $b^2=13r+3$. Let us subtract the first from the second equality: $b^2-a^2=8r+2$.

Therefore, $b^2-a^2$ is divisible by 2 but not divisible by 4; but $(b^2-a^2)=(b-a)(b+a)$ and, if $a$ and $b$ are numbers of the same parity, $b^2-a^2$ is divisible by 4, and if of different parity, then it is not divisible by 2.

Contradiction.
