First day
1. Let $d$ be a positive integer distinct from 2, 5 and 13. Prove that in the set {2, 5, 13, $d$} we can find two distinct numbers $a$ and $b$ such that the numbers $ab$ - 1 are not the square of an integer.
2. A triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ and a point $P_0$ are given on the plane. Let us set $A_s=A_{s-3}$ for any integer $s \geqslant 4$. We construct a sequence of points $P_0, P_1, P_2, \dots$, so that point $P_{k+1}$ is the image of point $P_k$ when rotated around point $A_{k+1}$ by angle $120^\circ$ clockwise ($k=0, 1, \dots$). Prove that if $P_{1986}=P_0$, then the triangle $A_1A_2A_3$ is equilateral.
3. Each vertex of the pentagon is assigned a certain integer so that the sum of five numbers is positive. If three consecutive vertices correspond to the numbers $x, y$ and $y<0$, then the next match is cut, the numbers $x, y, z$ are replaced by a match to $x+y, -y, y+z$. Such operations are performed sequentially as long as at least one of the five numbers is negative. Determine whether this process must be completed in a finite number of steps.
Second day
4. Let $A$ and $B$ be adjacent vertices of a regular $n$-gon ($n \geqslant 5$) with center $O$. Triangle $XYZ$, a congested triangle $OAB$, first coincides with it, then moves in the plane of the $n$-gon so that the points

$Y$ and $Z$ remain on the boundary, and the point $X$ remains inside this $n$-gon. What figure does the point $X$ make when the points $Y$ and $Z$ together make a full revolution along the boundary of the $n$-gon?

5. Find all functions $f(x)$ defining on the set of non-negative real numbers, taking values ​​in the same set and satisfying the following conditions:
(1) $f(xf(y)) \cdot f(y)=f(x+y)$ for all non-negative $x$ and $y$,
(2) $f(2)=0$,
(3) $f(x) \neq 0$ for all $0 \leqslant x < 2$.

6. Let $M$ be an arbitrary finite set of integer points on the coordinate plane. Is it always possible to color some points of the set $M$ white and the rest red so that for each straight line $L$ parallel to any of the coordinate axes, the absolute vertex of the difference between the number of white and red points on $L$ does not exceed one? Justify your answer.
