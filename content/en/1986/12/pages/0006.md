During 1829-1831. Evariste completely solved this most difficult problem. His first results on the theory of equations appeared in the spring of 1829. Galya sent them to the Academy of Sciences. One of the greatest French mathematicians, Cauchy, looked at his work, but... lost it somewhere.

At the end of the school year in a special mathematics class, Galya again made an attempt to build at the Polytechnic School and again failed. What happened during the exam is not known. Galya later mentioned him, writing that during the exam he was accompanied by the “crazy laughter of the examiners.” Galya's examiners were Binet and Lefbor de Fourcy. We don't know what ratings they gave Galya; one way or another, he did not get into the Polytechnic School.

While Evariste was preparing for the entrance exams, an irreparable misfortune befell him: on July 2, 1829, his father, hounded by the local priest and students, committed suicide. Evariste spent these difficult days, almost coinciding with his failure, at home, with his mother and younger brother Alfred.

On the advice of Richard, Evariste decided to build a Preparatory School, a bitter remnant of the famous Normal School created during the French Revolution. In 1822, the Bourbons closed the Normal School, and in 1826 it was restored under the name Preparatory School, as a continuation of the College Louis-le-Grand. The three-year Preparatory School trained teachers and government officials. On February 20, 1830, Evariste Galya became her student.

The first year of study at the Preparatory School turned out to be the most successful in Galya’s life. Here he met Auguste Chevalier, and this acquaintance soon grew into a strong friendship. Galya was passionate about mathematics. He wrote three works and submitted them to a competition at the Academy.

Suddenly a new blow falls on him. The management of Galya fell into the hands of the secretary of the Fourier Academy, who soon after... died. The Galya manuscript was not in his papers - it disappeared, like the first one, and with it the hope of receiving the Great Mathematical Prize. True, Evarist kept copies of the latest works, and he published them in the Appel and June issues of the Bulletin of Mathematical Sciences, but this was a weak statement. In his repeating unfortunates, he saw not the will of chance, but the result of a flat social organization, which doomed talent to eternal deprivation to the benefit of consequence. And with all the enthusiasm of his youth, Evariste joins the struggle for the political restructuring of society.

In July 1830, clouds had long been gathering over the Bourbon regime and a revolutionary thunderstorm broke out, depriving King Charles X of power. Sympathy Galya was entirely on the side of the resource workers. He actively participates in the work of revolutionary circles and joins the Society of Friends of the People. But the aspirations of the resource workers were not met: the protege of big capital, the “bourgeois king” Louis-Philippe, was brought to power by SEL. The excitement in Paris continues unabated.

In the autumn of 1830, Évariste appeared in print sharply criticizing the double-dealing behavior of the director of the Ginyu Preparatory School in the July days. As a result, on December 9 he is expelled from the School. My hope for a mathematical career was gone. Evariste joins the artillery of the National Guard, mostly members of the Society of Friends of the People. This was the armed force of the revolution, and Louis Philippe's correct formation soon disbanded the National Guard. Evariste is left without a livelihood; Only private lessons allow him to make ends meet.
