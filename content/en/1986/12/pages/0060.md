2. Let $K$ be the midpoint of AF and L the midpoint of BF (make figure). Quadrilateral EFKL is a parallelogram because EK is parallel to BF and EL is parallel to AF (EK and EL are the midlines in triangle ABF). Let H be the midpoint of EF; then KL passes through H, and KH=LH. In the same way, it is proved that the segment MN connecting the midpoints of the segments DE and CE passes through the point H and is bisected by this point. Therefore, the quadrilateral KLMN is a parallelogram.

3. At point D. Instruction. Build movement graphs for both swimmers and make sure that the swimmers will meet at points C and D every other time.

4. $\frac{\pm 3 \pm \sqrt{17}}{2}$. Note. Put $u = \frac{x+2}{x+1}$, $v = \frac{x-2}{x-1}$. Then $u^2+v^2 - \frac{5}{2}uv = 0$, whence either $u=2v$ or $v=2u$.

5. $30^\circ$. Solution (accessible memory). Let $C'$ be a point symmetrical to point C relative to line AB (make figure). Let us describe a circle around the triangle CAC'. Since $\angle CAC' = 150^\circ$, the arc CAC' contains 60°, and therefore the cord CC' is equal to the radius of the circle, and point B is its center. Hence $\angle B = 30^\circ$.

6. $\frac{5}{2} \leq x \leq 3$. Note. Put $y = \sqrt{2x-5}$. Then $y \geq 0$, and $(y+3) + |y-1| = 4$. Hence $0 \leq y \leq 1$.

7. $2\sqrt{S_1S_2}$. Solution. Let the area of ​​triangle ABC be S. Triangle ABC is similar to triangle AMK and CLM (make figure), so

$$\sqrt{\frac{S_1}{S}} = \frac{AL}{AC}, \quad \sqrt{\frac{S_2}{S}} = \frac{LC}{AC},$$

where

$$\frac{\sqrt{S_1} + \sqrt{S_2}}{\sqrt{S}} = \frac{AL + LC}{AC} = 1,$$

that is, $S = S_1 + S_2 + 2\sqrt{S_1S_2}$.

$$tg \gamma \frac{tg \alpha + tg \beta}{1 - tg \alpha tg \beta} < 1$$

$$tg \gamma tg (\alpha + \beta) < 1, \quad tg \gamma < ctg (\alpha + \beta),$$

9. $x = y = z = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$. Note. It is clear that $x \geq 1$, $y \geq 1$, $z \geq 1$. Let us prove that $x = y = z$. Let not all these numbers be equal. Let's choose the most of them. Let's assume it's $x$, that is, $x \geq y$, $x \geq z$. Since $y = (x-1)^2$, $z = (y-1)^2$ and $x = (z-1)^2$, it influences $(z-1)^2 \geq (x-1)^2$, which implies that $z \geq x$. Therefore $x = z$. Same as $x = y$.

10. Let's build $\overrightarrow{AN} = \overrightarrow{CN}$; let $P$ be the midpoint of the segment MN, $Q$ the midpoint of the segment N'M (Fig. 1). We have $\overrightarrow{N'N} = \overrightarrow{AC}$, therefore $\overrightarrow{QP} = \frac{1}{2} \overrightarrow{AC}$. The triangle AN'M is equal-partitioned, so point Q is the bissertrix of an angle equal to angle B. Point P is obtained from point Q by a constant vector. Thus, the geometric location we are looking for is a segment. Its ends are easy to find.

Answer (we count to determine that $AB > BC$): a segment connecting the midpoint of side AC with the midpoint of segment BB'; where $B'$ is a point on side AB such that $AB' = BC$. (By the way, this segment is parallel to the bissertrix of angle B.)

11. $(1 + \sqrt{3}) \left(1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$. Note. $(1 + x)^4 = 2 + 2x^4$, and $(1 + x)^4 - (1 - x)^4 = 2 + 12x^2 + 2x^4$, whence $(1 - x)^4 = 12x^2$, that is, $(1 - x)^2 = \sqrt{12} |x|$. Case $x \leq 0$ has no solutions; in the case of $x > 0$ we obtain a quadratic equation $(1 - x)^2 = 2\sqrt{3}x$ whose roots are $x_{1,2} = 1 + \sqrt{3} \pm \sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} = (1 + \sqrt{3}) \left(1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.

12. a) $b_c = \frac{2ab}{a+b} \cos \frac{\gamma}{2}$. Note. If $CL$ is the bissertrix of angle C, then $S_{CAL} + S_{CBL} = \frac{1}{2} b \cdot l_c \sin \frac{\gamma}{2} + \frac{1}{2} a l_c \sin \frac{\gamma}{2} = S_{ABC} = \frac{1}{2} \times ab \sin \gamma$.

16. Let $l_c = l_b$. Let us prove that angles C and B are equal. Let's assume this is true. Let $\angle C = \gamma$, $\angle B = \beta$ and to define $\gamma > \beta$. Then $c > b$. Comparing now the expressions $l_c = \frac{2ab}{a+b} \cos \frac{\gamma}{2}$ and $l_b = \frac{2ac}{a+c} \cos \frac{\beta}{2}$, we obtain that $l_c < l_b$ (since $\cos \frac{\gamma}{2} < \cos \frac{\beta}{2}$ and $\frac{b}{a+b} < \frac{c}{a+c}$). Contradiction. (In passing, we proved that in a triangle a smaller angle corresponds to a larger bissertrix.)
