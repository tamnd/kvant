Tasks

M1016—M1020; F1028—F1032

M1016. The polygon is described around a circle with center O. Let P be the center of mass of the polygon, K the center of mass of its contour. Prove that points P, O and K lie on the same line, and PO=2PK. (When determining the center P, we consider the polygon as a homogeneous plane, the center K - as a contour made of a homogeneous wire.).

I. Z. Vaishtein

M1017*. Each vertex of a regular pentagon is assigned an integer so that the sum of all five numbers is positive. The following operation is allowed: if trem consecutive vertices are assigned the numbers x, y, z and y<0, then these numbers are replaced by x+y, -y and z+y, respectively. Such operations are performed as long as at least one of the five numbers is negative. Does this process necessarily end in a finite number of steps?

M1018. Let A and B be adjacent vertices of a regular n-gon with center O. Triangle XYZ, equal to triangle OAB, first coincides with it, and then moves in the plane of the n-gon so that points Y and Z remain on the contour, and X remains inside the n-gon. What figure will point X describe when Y and Z make a full revolution along the boundary of the n-gon?

M1019. A certain finite set M of nodes (points of intersection of grid lines) is marked on a sheet of checkered paper. Prove that it is always possible to color some points of the set M white and the rest red so that on each grid line the difference between the number of white and red nodes modulo does not exceed 1.

M1020*. On a sphere of radius 1, a) a curve is drawn whose length is less than π;
b) a closed curve whose length is less than 2π. Prove that there is a plane passing through the center of the sphere that does not intersect the drawn curve. (We can consider that a curve on a sphere is a “broken line” consisting of two large circles.)

V. V. Prasolov, G. A. Galperin
