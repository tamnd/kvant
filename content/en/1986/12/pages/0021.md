Theorem 2. Successive symmetry with respect to two different centers of symmetry is parallel transfer.

Proof. Let $O_1$ and $O_2$ be the centers of symmetry and $M$ an arbitrary point of the plane. Let, further, point $M_1$ be symmetrical to point $M$ relative to the center $O_1$ and point $M_2$ symmetrical to point $M_1$ relative to the center $O_2$. Then $O_1O_2$ is the middle line of triangle $MM_1M_2$ (Fig. 9), and therefore $\overrightarrow{MM_2} = 2\overrightarrow{O_1O_2}$. We see that our transformation will mix an arbitrary point onto the same vector $2\overrightarrow{O_1O_2}$; that means it is a parallel transfer.

Theorem 3. Consecutive execution of parallel transfer and geometry is pre-matter with the same coefficient.

Proof. Let the vector $\vec{a}$ define the parallel translation, $O$ the center of the geometry, $k$ the geometry coefficient. An arbitrary point $M$, under a composite transformation, goes into a point $M_1$ such that (see Fig. 10)

$$\overrightarrow{MM_1} = \vec{a} + (k - 1)(\overrightarrow{OM} + \vec{a}) = (k - 1)\overrightarrow{OM} + k\vec{a}.$$

Let us denote by $O_1$ such a point that

$$\overrightarrow{O_1O} = \frac{k\vec{a}}{k - 1}$$

(we have $k \neq 1$). Then

$$\overrightarrow{MM_1} = (k - 1)\overrightarrow{OM} + (k - 1)\frac{k\vec{a}}{k - 1} = (k - 1)\overrightarrow{OM} + (k - 1)\overrightarrow{O_1O} = (k - 1)\overrightarrow{O_1M},$$

$$\overrightarrow{O_1M} = \overrightarrow{O_1M} + \overrightarrow{MM_1} = k\overrightarrow{O_1M}.$$

That is, our transformation is a geometry with center $O_1$ and coefficient $k$.

Corollary 1. Consecutive application of parallel translation and central symmetry is central symmetry.

Corollary 2. An odd number of successive central symmetries is a central symmetry.
