The XVII International Olympiad for schoolchildren in physics will be held in the UK from July 13 to 20 at the Harrow School in London. Teams from 21 countries are expected to participate in the Olympiad: Australia, Great Britain, Hungary, East Germany, Iceland, Canada, China, Cuba, the Netherlands, Norway, Belarus, Poland, Socialist Republic, USSR, USA, SFRY, Turkey, Finland, Germany, Czechoslovakia and Sweden. For the first time, teams from China and the USA took part in the Olympics. In addition, observers from Australia, Kuwait and Singapore were represented at the Olympiad.

The USSR team included five schoolchildren:

Oleg Volkov is a graduate of the village. w. No. 23 Gorky,
Andrey Gutsin is a graduate of Physics School No. 165 in Novosibirsk,
Andrey Matsyn - graduate of the village. w. No. 91 Moscow,
Serdey Magchilov - graduate of the village. w. No. 16 Odessa,
Grigory Nikolashvili is a graduate of the Faculty of Music named after. Komarova, Tbilisi.

The team leaders were the authors of this article - research associates of the Research Institute of Content and Teaching Methods of the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences.

The organization and holding of the Olympiad was entrusted to the Ministry of Education and Science, the Physical Society and a team of Harrow school physics teachers. The famous English scientist Sir Alec Marison was appointed chairman of the organizing committee, and Professor S. Eisenberg was appointed general secretary.

Both rounds of the Olympiad - theoretical and experimental - were held on the basis of Harrow schools. 4.5 hours were allotted to solve three problems in the theoretical round, and 5 hours for two problems in the experimental round.

Here are the conditions for these tasks.

Theoretical tour
Problem 1

A plane monochromatic light wave with wavelength λ and frequency v is incident perpendicularly on two identical narrow slits L and M, located at a distance d from each other (Fig. 1). Light waves emanating from each slit in a direction at an angle Θ to the normal, at a distance x at time t, are given by the equation

$$y = a \cos (2\pi (vt - x/\lambda)),$$

where a is the amplitude, the same for both waves, x >> d.

1) Prove that the resulting amplitude A of two waves emerging at an angle Θ to the normal can be obtained by adding two vectors, the model of each of which is equal to a, and the direction is determined by the phase of the light wave. Check the geometric, vector diagram, that

$$A = 2a \cos \beta,$$

where β = (πd sin Θ)/λ.

2) The double slit is replaced by a diffraction grating consisting of N identical slits, the distance between which is the same and equal to d. Using the vector method of adding amplitudes, show that the beginning and end of each slits lie on a circle of radius R given by the equation

$$R = a/(2 \sin \beta),$$

where a is the modulus of each vector. Show that the resulting amplitude is

$$a(\sin N\beta/\sin \beta),$$

and get the difference between the resulting wave and the wave incident on the grating.

3) Draw graphs of sin Nβ and 1/sin β as a function of β on the same figure. On a separate graph, show how the integrity of the resulting wave depends on β.

4) Determine the intensity of the main maxima.

5) Show that the number of main maxima cannot exceed (2d/λ)+1.

6) Show that two waves of length λ and λ+Δλ, where Δλ << λ, form main maxima at angular distances

$$\Delta \theta = n\Delta \lambda/(d \cos \theta),$$

where n=0, ±1, ±2, ...

Calculate this angular distance for a linear matrix whose wavelengths are λ=589.0 nm, λ+Δλ=589.6 nm, n= and d=1.2·10⁻⁶ m.

Task 3

Three particles of mass $m$ are connected by elastic non-expanding springs of each compressibility $k$ and are in a state of equilibrium (Fig. 3). Their movement is limited by those surrounded in the previous paragraph.

1) Each particle is mixed from its equilibrium position by a small displacement $u_1$, $u_2$ and $u_3$. Write down the equations of motion for each particle after the shift. Neglect the masses of the springs.

2) Show that the system can perform harmonious vibration according to the law

$$u=a_n \cos \omega t \ (n=1, 2 \text{ и } 3)$$

with accelerations $-\omega^2 u$, where $a_n$ are constant amplitudes, and that the cyclic frequency $\omega$ can have two possible values

$$\omega_0 \sqrt{3} \text{ и } 0, \text{ где } \omega_0^2=k/m.$$

3) The system of springs and particles increases to the number $N$, each particle contains springs with two neighboring ones. At first, the springs do not grow and are in an equilibrium state. Draw up an equation of motion for the $n$th particle ($n=1, 2, 3, ..., N$), expressed through the mixing of the particle itself, as well as the containing parts after removing them from the equilibrium position. Show that

$$u_n(t)=a_n \sin(2ns\pi/N+\varphi) \cos \omega_s t,$$

(where $s=1, 2, ..., N$, $n=1, 2, ..., N$, $\varphi$ is an arbitrary phase) is a solution to the equation of motion, provided that the cyclic frequency is given by

$$\omega_s=2\omega_0 \sin(s\pi/N),$$

where $a_n(s=1, ..., N)$ are constant amplitudes, independent of $n$. Indicate the range of possible frequencies for a circuit containing an infinite number of particles.

4) Determine the ratio $u_n/u_{n+1}$ for a large value of $N$ in two cases: a) low-particle solutions; b) $\omega=\omega_{max}$, where $\omega_{max}$ is the solution for the maximum frequency. Explain graphically the location of the part in the circuit at time $t$ for a) and b).

5) One of the particles was replaced by a particle with mass $m' \ll m$. Make an assessment of the expected significant changes in the distribution of cyclic frequencies. Qualitatively determine the shape of the frequency spectrum for a circuit with intersecting masses $m$ and $m'$ based on the previous result.
