Selected School Problems

Eighth grade
1. Find $x^2+y^2+z^2$ if
$$x+y+z=1, \frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}+\frac{1}{z}=0.$$

2. Points $E$ and $F$ are the midpoints of sides $AB$ and $CD$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$. Prove that the midpoints of the segments $AF, BF, CE$ and $DE$ are the vertices of the parallelogram.

3. Two athletes are swimming along the path of a swimming pool at constant, unequal speeds. They swim out from different ends of the road towards each other at different times and meet for the first time at point $C$. Having met, they turn around, each reach their own end of the path and again swim towards each other until they meet at point $D$, and so on. Where will their 20th meeting take place?

4. Solve the equation
$$\left(\frac{x+2}{x+1}\right)^2+\left(\frac{x-2}{x-1}\right)^2-\frac{5}{2}\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-1}=0.$$

5. In triangle $ABC$, angle $A$ is equal to $75^\circ$, height $CH$ is equal to the polyline of side $AB$. Find the angle $CBA$.

Ninth grade
6. Solve the equation
$$\sqrt{x+2+3\sqrt{2x-5}}+\sqrt{x-2-\sqrt{2x-5}}=2\sqrt{2}.$$

7. On sides $AB, AC$ and $BC$ of triangle $ABC$ there are given points $M, L$ and $K$ such that quadrilateral $KLMB$ is a parallelogram. Find the area of ​​this parallelogram if it is known that the areas of triangles $AML$ and $KLC$ are respectively $S_1$ and $S_2$.

Our calendar

To the 175th anniversary of Avogadro's law
(See beginning on p. 12)

As they say, the difference between atoms and molecules was truly realized precisely thanks to the work of Avogadro.

Meanwhile, Avogadro himself, in his absence, provided how wide possibilities the law he found opens up for determining the chemical formulas of various gaseous substances, for determining their relative atomic and molecular masses. But what is the physical basis of his law, so to speak, where does it come from, Avogadro did not know. A comprehensive explanation for the kinetic theory of gases, which arose in the late 50s, has passed centuries.

Although Avogadro, of course, understood that the number of molecules in, say, one liter of a gaseous substance (according to the opening principle for all gases) is enormous, he did not have a chance to find out what specific quantities we could be talking about here. This problem was reliably solved

only at the beginning of our century, and only after that, the last doubts about the reality of atoms, ideas about which arose about two and a half thousand years ago, disappeared. Measurements carried out by several completely different physics and chemistry, called Avogadro's number.
