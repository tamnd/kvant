Atomic physics in problems

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences Yu. A. SAMARSKY

Atomic physics is a large and important branch of modern physics. The atom—in popular parlance “indivisible”—turned out to be a huge world living according to its own special laws. Many modern advances in science and its applications were impossible without elucidating the complex structure of the atom, without the discovery of new laws of motion of microparticles - the laws of quantum mechanics.

Of the many issues of atomic physics, in this article we will consider only three - the photoelectric effect, discreteness of emission spectra and stability of atoms. In each case, we first recall the basic principles and patterns that determine this phenomenon, and then we will analyze several specific tasks.

* * *

The theory of the photoelectric effect - the effect of electrons being ejected from a substance under the influence of light - was created in 1905 by A. Einstein on the basis of quantum concepts of light. According to these ideas, light, like electromagnetic radiation in general, has a primary structure and, like particles, spreads in space in the form of separate portions. The smallest “particle” of electromagnetic radiation is called a quantum, and later a photon. Photon energy E is the proportional part of the radiation v:

$$E = hv$$

(where $h = 6,63 \cdot 10^{-34}$ J s is Planck’s constant) or circular frequency $\omega$:

$$E = \hbar\omega$$

(where $\hbar = h/(2\pi)$ is also called Planck's constant).

Einstein's equation, or the law of conservation of energy for the photoelectric effect, has the form

ΔN = N₀ - N₀ · 2⁻τ/T = N₀(1 - 2⁻τ/T),
where N₀ is the initial number of radioactive atoms. According to Avogadro's law, N₀ = Nₐ m/M, where M = A × 10⁻³ kg/mol is the molar mass of cobalt. Now we finally get

$$C\Delta t = N_A \frac{m}{A \cdot 10^{-3}} \left(1 - 2^{-τ/T}\right) w,$$

from where we find the half-life T:

$$T = \frac{\tau}{\log_1/2 \left(1 - \left(C\Delta t A \cdot 10^{-3}\right)/(N_A wm)\right)} \approx 5700 \text{ с} \approx 95 \text{ мин.}$$
