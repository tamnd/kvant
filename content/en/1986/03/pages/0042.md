In this case, the length of the spring is zero.

Since at the initial moment all the points accumulated (and the springs grew at the same time), in the future each point will move grounded towards the center of mass. The time after which point $i$ must reach the center of mass is a quarter of the period of oscillation of a load of mass $m_i$ on a spring of stiffness $\alpha_i$:

$$t_i = \frac{T_i}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} \sqrt{\frac{m_i}{\alpha_i}} = \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{kM}}.$$

As can be seen, $t_i$ does not depend on the parameters of a specific point; therefore, all particles will simultaneously arrive at the center of mass after time $\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{1}{\sqrt{kM}}$.

F965. A long cylindrical vessel is filled with an ideal gas to a pressure of $p_0$. First, the temperature of the cylinder is kept constant and equal to $T_0$. Then the temperature of one of the end walls of the vessel is increased $\Delta T$, and the temperature of the opposite wall remains the same. Begin the steady pressure in the vessel and the position of the center of mass of the gas. Consider that $\Delta T \ll T_0$.

◆ After equilibrium is established in different places of the vessel, the gas pressure will be the same, but the temperature will be different: more penetration of the warmer wall and less penetration of the opposite wall. If $\Delta T \ll T_0$, then we can assume that the temperature changes according to a linear law

$$T(x) = T_0 \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \frac{x}{l} \right)$$

(see figure). Let us mentally divide the vessel into thin layers of thickness $\Delta x$ such that within each layer the gas temperature can be considered constant. For gas in each such layer the following conditions are satisfied:

$$p = n(x) \cdot k \cdot T(x),$$

where $p$ is the pressure in the vessel, $k$ is Boltzmann’s constant, $n(x)$ is the volume concentration of gas in a given layer. It can be seen that the concentration also depends on $x$

$$n(x) = \frac{p}{kT_0 \left( 1 + \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \frac{x}{l} \right)} \approx \frac{p}{kT_0} \left( 1 - \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \frac{x}{l} \right)$$

(we used the approximate equality $\frac{1}{1+\alpha} \approx 1-\alpha$ at $\alpha \ll 1$) and according to a linear law it changes from $n_0 = \frac{p}{kT_0}$ at the “cold” wall to $n_l = \frac{p}{kT_0} \left( 1 - \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right)$ at the “warm” wall. Average concentration

$$n_{cp} = \frac{n_0 + n_l}{2} = \frac{p}{kT_0} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right).$$

Let us express through $n_{cp}$ the total number of molecules in the vessel:

$$N = Sln_{cp} = \frac{pSl}{kT_0} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta T}{T_0} \right)$$

($S$ is the area of ​​the cylinder end). On the other side,

$$N = Sln_0 = \frac{p_0Sl}{kT_0},$$
