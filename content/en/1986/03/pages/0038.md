2) Let none of the faces of the paralleleped lie entirely within the face of the tetrahedron. Then on each face of the tetrahedron there should be two vertices of the paralleleped, that is, one edge. It is easy to understand that among these four edges, two, say KL and MN, necessarily lie on one face of the paralleleped and are parallel, and the other two - K'N' and L'M' - on the opposite face and perpendicular to the first two. Therefore, the common edge AB of the tetrahedron faces containing the segments KL and MN is parallel to them, and the opposite edge of the tetrahedron CD is parallel to K'N' and L'M' (and perpendicular to AB; Fig. 3).

Let EF be the common perpendicular lines AB and CD, P and Q the points of its intersection with the planes KLM and K'L'M'. The projection $c_1$ of the tetrahedron onto KK', very indo, is equal to EF, and its projections $a_1$ and $b_1$ onto the lines AB and CD (which are parallel to KL and KN) coincide with the projections of the triangle ABF onto AB and CDE onto CD. Let's consider the projections of our polyhedra on the ABF (Fig. 4, a) and CDE (Fig. 4, b) planes. Reasoning in the same way as at the end of point a), we are convinced that point E lies between A and B, and F - between C and D. Therefore, $a_1 = AB$, $b_1 = CD$.
