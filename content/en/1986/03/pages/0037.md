Let us now show that on each of the 15 lines under consideration there are no more than two points of triune intersection (different from the data), that is, that their total number does not exceed (15·2): $3=10$ (when calculating, it is taken into account that each of these points lies on three lines).

Note that 6 lines connecting any 4 of these points in pairs intersect two at three points (if there are no parallel lines among these lines), and these three points cannot lie on the same line (Fig. 2). Therefore, on the straight line connecting the two remaining points, at most two tri-intersection points can lie, which is what we needed to prove.
