a) Answer: one of the examples is $-a = 2 + \sqrt{3}$. Indeed, since $2 + \sqrt{3}(2 - \sqrt{3}) = 1$,
$\{a\} + \{1/a\} = (\sqrt{3} - 1) + (2 - \sqrt{3}) = 1$.

However, any number of the form $n \pm \sqrt{n^2 - 1}$ is suitable, where $n$ is an integer and greater than 1.

b) Let's assume that the number $a$ is rational and represent it as an irreducible fraction: $a = m/n$. If $\{a\} + \{1/a\} = 1$, then obviously $a + 1/a$ is an integer; let's denote it by k. So, $m/n + n/m = k$, that is $m^2 + n^2 = kmn$. It follows that $m^2$ is divisible by n, and $n^2$ is divisible by m, and the triangle m and n are mutually prime; this is possible only for $|m| = |n| = 1$, that is, for $a = \pm 1$. But in this case $\{a\} + \{1/a\} = 0$. Consequently, the equation under consideration has no rational solutions.
