When we say that a person has two legs and a dog has four legs, we don’t even think about what number system we are using. When we say, “this turtle is old,” we mean that

$$100 = 1 \cdot 10^2 + 0 \cdot 10^1 + 0 \cdot 10^0.$$

And in general, if $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are the digits of a number, then we consider the number $A = a_1 a_2 \dots a_n$ to be equal to $a_1 \cdot 10^n + a_2 \cdot 10^{n-1} + \dots + a_n \cdot 10^0$. In fact, this will only happen in the decimal number system, whose base is ten. However, any natural number $q > 1$ can be taken as the base of the number system; the number $a_1 a_2 \dots a_n$ in such a $q$-ary number system will be equal to

$$a_1 a_2 \dots a_n = a_1 q^n + a_2 q^{n-1} + \dots + a_n q^0.$$

In what number system can a girl have 10 arms, 10 ears and 10 eyes? Only in one in which 10 will be equal to the number 2, that is, $10_q = 1 \cdot q^1 + 0 \cdot q^0 = 2$. Thus, $q=2$ means that in this poem everything happens in the double number system. In this system

$$100 = 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 0 \cdot 2^0 = 4,$$

$$101 = 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 1 \cdot 2^0 = 5,$$

$$1100 = 1 \cdot 2^3 + 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 0 \cdot 2^0 = 12.$$
