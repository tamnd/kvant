⟦folio 42⟧

$$E = \frac{\varepsilon}{(k-1)d}.$$

This electric field creates a negative charge located on plate 1 and all positive charges located on the already charged plates (Fig. 2). Since the direction of the field of an infinite charged plane does not depend on the distance to it and does not change when it moves, we can mentally assign all positively charged planes to the $k$th one on the right. This will result in a flat charged capacitor with a distance between the plates $(k-1)d$. Since the field in a flat capacitor is equal to $Q/\varepsilon_0S$, where $Q$ is the charge of each plate, then the negative charge of the rightmost plate (1) after charging the $k$th one on the right will be equal to

$$Q = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{(k-1)d}.$$

The total charge on all positive charged plates will be the same in magnitude.

The charge of the $k$th plate itself can be found from the following messages. Before charging, the positive charge of all plates on the left up to the $(k+1)$th plate inclusive was equal to $Q' = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{kd}$, the total positive charge after charging the $k$th plate became equal to $Q = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{(k-1)d}$. Therefore, the charge of the $k$th plate itself is equal to

$$Q = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{d} \left( \frac{1}{k-1} - \frac{1}{k} \right).$$

Now you can answer the question posed in the problem. The negative charge that will eventually accumulate on the rightmost plate, as follows from expression (1) for $k=2$, will be equal to

$$Q_1 = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{d}.$$

Charge of plate 3 (expression (2) $k=3$) -

$$Q_3 = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{d} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \right) = \frac{\varepsilon_0S}{6d}.$$

Thus, the required charge ratio

$$Q_1:Q_3 = 6.$$

E. P. Sokolov
