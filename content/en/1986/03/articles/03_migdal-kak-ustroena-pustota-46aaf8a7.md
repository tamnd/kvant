How does emptiness work?
Academician A. V. Middala

We will talk about how our ideas about the most spacious thing in the Universe and, perhaps, the most important object of physical research - emptiness - have changed.

What is emptiness: an abstract concept, “nothing,” along with other physical bodies?

What will remain if an ideal pump removes all particles from a closed volume?

What is in the international space where there is almost no substance?

The development of physics in recent decades has shown that our physical space - the vacuum - is not just a geometric object, not a space in which there is nothing, but a complex system with intersecting properties that are completely different from the properties of solid media, liquids or gases; its study concerns the most profound concepts, such as causality, the connection of geometry with matter, the symmetry of space and time, the connection of symmetry with conservation laws...

You can't push without touching

We know that bodies act on each other when they touch. Let's throw a stone into the water - a wave will run from it and floating branches will slide in - the impact is transmitted from point to point. The sound will spread because pressure is transferred from one volume of the medium to the adjacent one, etc. If you cover a sounding electric bell with a glass cap and pump out the air, you will see how the hammer still hits the bell, but the sound disappears - the sound will not spread in empty space. At the same time, the period of oscillation of the pendulum placed under the cap does not change when removed

In contrast to the forces that arose during the propagation of sound, electric and magnetic forces, gravity also acts in emptiness, light propagates in it, which is why we see the Sun and stars.

It is natural to assume that in the space of larger magnets, the larger the charged or massive body, the state of emptiness changes. The space surrounding these bodies is in a “stressed” state, which it describes with the words: “it has a field in space.” Charges create an electric field, a magnet creates a magnetic field, and a massive body creates a gravitational field. An electric field acts on a charged body, a magnetic field acts on a magnet, and a gravity field acts on a stone, causing them to move. The change in the speed of these bodies is explained by the action of the field in the region of space where they are located at a given moment in time. Force is transmitted through empty space from point to point using fields, as if through an invisible fluid. This mechanism of transmission of influence is called short-range action and is accepted by perfect physics.

But there were other ideas - “long-range action”: the influence of one body on another is instantly transmitted over a distance. Based on this view, Newton built his theory of gravitation. The assumption of instantaneous transfer of influence did not prevent him from finding patterns of movement of celestial bodies that coincided with observational data with great accuracy. Now we know why: celestial bodies move at relatively low speeds, and gravitational interaction propagates at the speed of light and can be considered instantaneous in this case.

The idea of ​​distance is difficult to reconcile with the property of light: it was known that light travels at a finite speed and passes through all intermediate points along the line of the light beam. This is especially clearly visible when a beam of light passes through fog - it is continuous. Newton suggested that a luminous body emits particles that transmit light - corpuscles. Then the finiteness of the speed does not contradict the idea of ​​long-distance, but leaves without explanation the wave properties of light, proven by experiments on interference and diffraction. Newton's corpuscular theory was able to cope with the explanation of these phenomena.

More than a year after Newton, in 1905, Einstein's remarkable work on the quantum nature of light appeared. Einstein showed that the experimentally discovered patterns of the photoelectric effect (the ejection of electrons from an atom during irradiation) can only be explained by assuming that light is a set of particles - photons that interact with electrons and eject them from atoms. (We will talk about facts in more detail later.) The idea of ​​light as a wave could not explain the main feature of the photoelectric effect - the energy of the emitted electron does not depend on the intensity of the light, but only on its particle.

In some cases, Einstein's vision was familiarized with Newoton's theory of corpuscles. And again questions arose that Newoton could not answer: how to combine both ideas - about the wave and corpuscular nature of light? A paradox arose, which was resolved by quantum theory, which proved that light is both a wave and a particle, just as the electron is also a particle; and a wave! This idea is called “quantum-wave dualism.”

Electromagnetic properties of emptiness

James Maxwell, with his amazing equations, found in 1865, united various branches of physics: optics, electricity, magnetism.

The beginning on this path was laid by his powerful presentation by Michael Faraday, who discovered the law of electromagnetic induction in 1831. If you change the magnetic field, an arbitrary wire ring, then an electric current arises in the conductor - the charges in it begin to move under the influence of the ring electric field formed in space. So, an alternating magnetic field generates an alternating electric field in the void.

Back in 1820, another of Maxwell's predecessors, Hans Oersted, discovered that current flowing through a conductor creates a ring magnetic field around itself! If you periodically change the direction of the electric field in a conductor, an alternating current and an alternating magnetic field arise. Maxwell made a brilliant guess that not only moving charges create a magnetic field, it is also created by the alternating electric field itself.

Of these two remarkable properties of emptiness, the third, no less important, is that electromagnetic waves propagate in emptiness. When an alternating electric field licks, it forms around itself, according to Maxwell, an alternating magnetic field, and the magnetic one - according to Faraday's law - creates an alternating electric one in a neighboring place... Thus, what has arisen is transmitted further and further.

From Maxwell's equation it followed that electromagnetic vibrations should propagate at the speed of light. The existence of electromagnetic waves was experimentally proven by Heinrich Hertz in 1886-1889. It was natural to come to the conclusion that light is also an electromagnetic wave. This assumption was tested and confirmed by experience.

How abstract these ideas looked in Maxwell's time!

And how quickly they became the basis for almost all the benefits of modern civilization: from telephone and radio to modern means of space communication - it’s impossible to list everything that was born from experiments in small laboratories over the centuries, from vague agreements of great minds!

Maxwell's theory was a triumph of short-range action: all electromagnetic influences are transmitted through a medium - the ether. But new contradictions immediately arose.

When a body moves in the ether, does the ether move with it? Experiments have given contradictory results; some experiments have shown partial or complete involvement of the ether in movement, others have shown that the ether is not at all carried away by a moving body. In 1851, French physicist Armand Fizeau measured the speed of light in flowing water and showed that the ether is partially captured by the moving medium. American physicist Albert Michelson measured the speed of light along and across the direction of the Earth's orbital motion in 1881. If the ether were motionless, then the speed of light along the movement of the Earth would be the sum of the speed of light in the ether and the speed of the Earth relative to the ether. It turned out that the speed of light is the same, that is, it does not depend on the speed of the source, and if light actually propagates in the ether, it means that the ether is completely carried away by the Earth...

The theory of relative ether had to resolve the contradictions of the ether.

The ether is dead - long live the ether!

At the beginning of the 20th century, the idea of ​​short-range action was further developed and substantiated in the theory of relativity and Einstein’s theory of gravitation. It turned out that not only electromagnetic, but also gravitational influences propagate in emptiness at the speed of light. The speed of light entered not only electrodynamics, but also mechanics and the theory of gravity.

The contradiction between Fizeau's experiment and Michelson's experiment was a new formula for the addition of velocities that followed from the theory of relativity; the properties of the ether did not play a role here. An amazingly complex and interesting medium - vacuum - could again be called ether, if there were no confusion with the most 19th-century concept.

Quantum mechanics
vacuum fields

Without some minimal information about quantum mechanics, it is impossible to form even a rough physical picture of the phenomena occurring in a vacuum. Therefore, before we talk about the amazing properties of vacuum fields, we will have to digress and talk about what results the application of quantum mechanics leads to a system oscillating around an equilibrium position. Such systems are often found in physics and are called oscillators. No matter how specifically the oscillator is structured - be it a magnet, an external weight, an oscillatory circuit - its energy consists of two terms: potential energy, proportional to the square of the quantity, which can be called the “generalized coordinate”, and kinetic energy, proportional to the square of the rate of change of this “coordinate”. For an external weight, the “generalized coordinate” is the displacement of the weight from the equilibrium position; for an oscillatory circuit, it is the charge on the capacitor plates. The proportionality coefficient in kinetic energy determines the “generalized mass”. We call these quantities generalized because they do not depend on the implementation of the oscillator - the “coordinate,” as you can see, does not necessarily have the dimension of length.

Let's stop the magnet - in the equilibrium position its energy is minimal: both kinetic and potential energy are equal to zero. This is how a classic oscillator behaves. But this is what happened when quantum mechanics was applied to the oscillator.

One of the most important principles of quantum mechanics, the uncertainty principle, formulated by Werner Heisenberg in 1927, states that some physical quantities cannot simultaneously give definite values. These are the quantities that are “generalized coordinate” and “generalized momentum” - the product of “generalized mass” and “generalized speed”.
