Necessary girl (see p. 50)

When we say that a person has two legs and a dog has four legs, we don’t even think about what number system we are using. When we say, “this turtle is old,” we mean that

$$100 = 1 \cdot 10^2 + 0 \cdot 10^1 + 0 \cdot 10^0.$$

And in general, if $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are the digits of a number, then we consider the number $A = a_1 a_2 \dots a_n$ to be equal to $a_1 \cdot 10^n + a_2 \cdot 10^{n-1} + \dots + a_n \cdot 10^0$. In fact, this will only happen in the decimal number system, whose base is ten. However, any natural number $q > 1$ can be taken as the base of the number system; the number $a_1 a_2 \dots a_n$ in such a $q$-ary number system will be equal to

$$a_1 a_2 \dots a_n = a_1 q^n + a_2 q^{n-1} + \dots + a_n q^0.$$

In what number system can a girl have 10 arms, 10 ears and 10 eyes? Only in one in which 10 will be equal to the number 2, that is, $10_q = 1 \cdot q^1 + 0 \cdot q^0 = 2$. Thus, $q=2$ means that in this poem everything happens in the double number system. In this system

$$100 = 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 0 \cdot 2^0 = 4,$$

$$101 = 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 1 \cdot 2^0 = 5,$$

$$1100 = 1 \cdot 2^3 + 1 \cdot 2^2 + 0 \cdot 2^1 + 0 \cdot 2^0 = 12.$$

$$T_1 = \frac{n A}{R(n-1)} = 300 \text{ К.}$$
5. Let's write down Newton's second law for the motion of the ball before and after introducing a charge into the center of the circle:
$$m \omega_1^2 R = mg t g \alpha,$$
$$m \omega_2^2 R = mg t g \alpha - \frac{q^2}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R^2},$$
where $\alpha$ is the angle between the suspension thread and the vertical. From these equations one finds
$$\omega_2 = \sqrt{\omega_1^2 - \frac{q^2}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R^3 m}} = 1,7 \text{ с}^{-1}.$$
6. At the moment when the thread occupies a vertical position, Newton’s second law in projection onto the vertical axis will be written in the form
$$F - mg = \frac{mv^2}{l},$$
where $F$ is the tension force of the thread, $v$ is the speed of the ball at this moment, $l$ is the length of the thread. The speed $v$ can be found using the law of conservation of energy
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = m g l (1 - \cos \alpha) + q E l \sin \alpha.$$
Solving the resulting system of equations, we find
$$F = mg + 2 mg (1 - \cos \alpha) + 2 q E \sin \alpha = mg (3 - 2 \cos \alpha) + 2 q E \sin \alpha = 18,4 \text{ Н.}$$

7. We write the power consumed by the lamp immediately after turning it on in the form
$$P = \frac{U^2}{R},$$
where $U$ is the voltage across the lamp, $R = R_0 \times (1 + a t_k)$ is the resistance of the coil at room temperature $t_k \approx 20^\circ \text{C}$ ($R_0$ is the resistance at $0^\circ \text{C}$). After heating, the resistance of the filament increases to $R' = R_0 (1 + a t)$ and the lamp consumes power
$$P' = \frac{U^2}{R'} = \frac{PR}{R'} = P \frac{1 + a t_k}{1 + a t} \approx 44 \text{ Вт.}$$
8. The force with which the beam acts on the plate can be calculated by the formula
$$F = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t},$$
where $\Delta P$ is the momentum of the proton beam transferred to the plate in time $\Delta t$. Let's transform this formula as follows:
$$F = mv \frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv}{e} \frac{e \Delta N}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv}{e} \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv}{e} I.$$
Here $v$ is the speed of protons, $\Delta N$ is the number of protons hitting the plate in time $\Delta t$, $\Delta q$ is the charge of protons reaching the plate in time $\Delta t$. We find the speed $v$ using the law of conservation of energy:
$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = e U, \quad v = \sqrt{\frac{2 e U}{m}}.$$
Now we finally get
$$F = I \sqrt{\frac{2 m U}{e}} = 1,6 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ Н.}$$
9. In the region of a magnetic field, a proton moves along a circular arc and exits at an angle $a$ (Fig. 2). The time $t$ of proton motion in the magnetic field region is related to the time of complete rotation $T$ by correspondence
$$\tau = \frac{\pi + 2 a}{2 \pi} T.$$
To calculate the time $T = 2 \pi R / v$, it is necessary to find the ratio of the speed of the proton $v$ to the radius of the trajectory $R$. We obtain this relation from the equation of motion of the proton
$$\frac{mv^2}{R} = ev B, \quad \frac{v}{R} = \frac{e B}{m}.$$
Thus,
$$T = \frac{2 \pi m}{e B}.$$
Substituting this result into the formula for time $t$ and solving the resulting equation for the magnetic field induction, we find
$$B = \frac{(\pi + 2 a) m}{e \tau} = 8,4 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ Тл.}$$
