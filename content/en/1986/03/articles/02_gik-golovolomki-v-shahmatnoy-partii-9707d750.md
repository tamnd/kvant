PUZZLES
IN THE CHESS
PARTS

The name of the next “page” seems to be somewhat different from the countries. A practical game is real chess, and the grandmaster is occupied with a mathematical problem - a completely different genre, as if not related to the implementation of the game. But...
One day, Sam Loyd, the famous chess composer and “grandmaster of a mathematical problem,” announced that he had found a way to mate the lone king in the center of the board with two rooks and a knight without the support of his own king. Chess lovers were furious at first, but when Loid showed the solution to his Grossey, they had a good time.

You probably decided that such a checkmate is only possible in grandmaster, but look at what an accident one day (told by master O. Sabitov). In one of the games at the tournament in Dushanbe, such an ending arose.

In the strongest ceyton, White reached out with his hand to the f2 rook, and Black, having managed to smooth out that after 1.Re2+ the king cannot retreat due to the loss of the rook (1...Kpf5 2.Rf8+ Ke6 3.Re8+), grabbed his c3 rook, intending to shield it from the check. And check actually followed, but from the other side - 1.Rf4+. Black, in the confusion, did not pay attention to the change in the situation and responded with lightning speed with the prepared 1...Re3!!? White's flag threatened to collapse, and, having forgotten about the check announced by the previous move, they surrounded the non-spinning king and on the other side - 2.Rd4!!, creating a unique position, the like of which cannot be found in the entire history of chess.

Checkmate in 1 move.

To declare checkmate in 1 move, the white knight... rears up! After such a maneuver of the g8 and h7 squares, the former one is under his protection (after all, he did not leave the f6 square, but only raised it), and the white bishop on c3 checkmates. This idea can evoke a completely realistic association... Analyzing his meeting with A. Gipsis, Grand Master E. Gufeld came to this position.

At first, Gufeld decided that White, whom he was playing, was losing: he had a material advantage on his side, but the bishop was under attack, and it was useless for them to retreat due to the exposure of the first rank (Qs1+). But, remembering the joke with the horse rearing up, he found a way out: 1.Re7!! Q:e7 2.Bc3! Compare this situation with a puzzle, and you will understand that the difference is not great. Once again the knight is ready to fly into the air and there is no benefit from losing the queen - 3.Nd5+ or checkmate - 3.Kg4+ Kg8 4.Kh6×.

Problem. The knight is on a1. Can he go around the entire doc, visiting a certain robust field once, and end his journey on h8?

On each move, the knight changes the color of the square it is on. The underside square a1 is black, on the 63rd move the knight ends up on a white square. But the h8 field is black, and, therefore, the conditions of the problem cannot be fulfilled.

There are many similar puzzles. Are they related to the game of chess? The most direct thing.

Consider this position:

White: Kh8, Nb2, p. h7. Black: Kf7.

How to evaluate it? To win, White needs to free his king. If it is their move, this is possible: 1.Kd3 Kf8 2.Ke5, etc. If Black moves, there is no way out of the corner: 1...Kpf8 2.Kd3 Kf7 3.Ke5+ Kf8. White moves, and he is forced to concede the f7 square to the black king. Nice.

For such positions, fill in the following rules: the weaker side is added as a draw if, during its move, it can place the king on a square of the same color as the square on which the non-spinning knight stands.
