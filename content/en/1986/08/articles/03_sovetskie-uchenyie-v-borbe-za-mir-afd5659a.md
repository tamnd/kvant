Soviet scientists in the struggle for peace

More than five years ago, on May 4, 1981, outstanding Soviet scientists, led by the Chairman of the USSR Academy of Sciences, Academician A.P. Aleksadrov and Vice-Presidents, Academicians E.P. Velikov and A.A. Logunov, called upon scientists around the world to “do everything to respond to the threat of nuclear war from the peoples... and turn their will and knowledge to the cause of creating, to preserving conditions on earth that would ensure a decent life for the present and future generations."

This appeal also says: “We are eyewitnesses of alarming events: the arms race, and especially nuclear arms, is becoming increasingly large. Negotiations on dissolution are marking time or have been completely abandoned. Signed agreements do not come into force. The existing agreements and agreements that encourage military activity are supported by overheads, their implementation is deliberately hampered... We cannot allow humanity to be captured by prejudices that prove that the world can only end on mountains of weapons, that it is not reason, but force that will rule the course of history forever and ever.”

The authors of the appeal sharply criticized the pursuit of neutron bombs that was taking place at that time in capitalist countries. Now the fight against the deployment of various types of weapons of mass destruction in space, against the “Star Wars” strategy, which is persistently called upon humanity by American imperialism, has come to the fore.

The danger hanging over humanity cannot but worry scientists. After all, they understand its scale and consequences better than people in other professions. And each of them faces the question of what position he will take in relation to this danger. The position of Soviet scientists is clearly formulated in the address. It was actively supported by scientists from all countries. It is widely supported and promoted by the World Federation of Scientists, voluntary associations and unions of scientists. Their numerous speeches and protests help mobilize the broad masses of the people to actively oppose the aggressive plans of imperialism.

International cooperation in the struggle for the peaceful coexistence of peoples is growing stronger every year. 1986 was declared the Year of Peace by the United Nations. Responding to this initiative, the Soviet Union put forward a realistic long-term comprehensive program for general arms reduction and disarmament, calculated until the year 2000. It is set out in the Statement of the General Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee M. S. Gorbachev, published on January 15, 1986, which received wide international support.

Soviet scientists made a great contribution to the development of avant-garde military plans widely advertised in imperialist circles - the theory of the first strike, limited nuclear war, space anti-missile shield, etc. They convincingly showed the limitations of any international conflict involving the use of atomic weapons, and refuted the arguments of dissolution opponents about the impossibility of strict control over requests for nuclear tests.

Socialism does not need wars and the entire Soviet people are active opponents of war.
