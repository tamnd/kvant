So with normal (non-combined) shooting $d_1 \approx d_2 \approx d_0$, then from (2) it follows:

$$\left( \frac{r}{R} \right)_{\text{o6}} = \frac{\Delta d}{2d_0^2} F_{\text{o6}},$$

where $d_0$ is the distance to the object, $\Delta d$ is the depth of field, $F_{\text{o6}}$ is the focal length of the object used in this shooting. With combined shooting $d_1 \gg d_2$, and from (2) it follows:

$$\left( \frac{r}{R} \right)_{\text{komб}} = \frac{1}{2d_2} F.$$

In order for the quality of images (their blur) to be the same for these two types of shooting, it is necessary to require that when shooting with the same aperture, the spot radii $r_{\text{o6}}$ and $r_{\text{komб}}$ are the same. Then

$$F = F_{\text{o6}} \frac{\Delta d \cdot d_2}{d_0^2}.$$

Substituting the numerical values ​​$F_{\text{o6}}=50$ cm, $\Delta d=0,5$ m, $d_0=5$ m and $d_2=4$ m, we obtain $F=4$ cm.

This means that to obtain high-quality SIM images with a large depth of field, you should use a short-focus lens.

Inquire - we will answer

Dear editors!
In the article “Puzzling Plutoon”, published on the pages of your magazine («Kvant», 1983, No. 3), the invention of a new method for measuring the diameters of celestial bodies was mentioned. Could you tell me what this method is based on?

Your reader O. Andreev

The editors asked the author of this article, V. A. Bronshten, to respond to this letter.

A new way to measure the diameters of distant celestial bodies is called speckle interferometry. The word "speckle" is English and means "speck". The method uses the capabilities of modern electronics.

Until such time, the obstacle in obtaining clear images of celestial bodies with the surfaces of the Earth was the burning of the optical instrument and the vibration of the air. Because of this, the image of the luminary on the photographic plastic was smeared during the exposure (sometimes very long), and it was impossible to change its diameter or judge the shape of the body.

In the new devices, a photodelument block is installed together with photoplastics. Since the electronic comparator was faster than the vibrations of the atmosphere and the instrument, it seemed to hold the image in place, preventing it from blurring. From the photodelument block, the image is transmitted to a television-type screen, from where it can be formed. The diameter measurement is again performed by an electronic device.
