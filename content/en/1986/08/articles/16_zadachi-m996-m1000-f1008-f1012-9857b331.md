Quantum problem solver

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Posted, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than October 15, 1986 to the address: 103006 Moscow K-6, st. Gorky, 32/1, «Kvant».
In the “To” column, write: “Quantum Problem” No. 8 - 86 and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M996, M997” or “F1008”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. In the letter, include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision verification in this envelope). Place the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope place: “Quantum Problem”, a new problem in physics) or “... a new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and the class in which you are a teacher. Please write your first and last name in block letters.
Problems M996 - M998 and M999 a) of this issue were proposed at the XX All-Union Mathematical Olympid for Schoolchildren. A complete list of Olympida problems with solutions will be published in «Kvant» No. 11 for this year.

M996—M1000; F1008—F1012

M996. Two identical squares at the intersection form an octagon. The sides of one square are blue, the other are red. Prove that the sum of the lengths of the syn sides of an octagon is equal to the sum of the lengths of its red sides.

V. V. Proizvolov

M997. Prove that the sum of all numbers of the form $\frac{1}{mn}$, where $m$ and $n$ are natural numbers, $1 \leq m < n < 1986$, is not an integer.

D. A. Mitkin

M998*. Let us consider all tetrahedra $AXBY$ described near a given sphere. Prove that, given fixed points $A$ and $B$, the sum of the angles of the quadrilateral $AXBY$, that is, the sum

$$\angle AXB + \angle XBY + \angle BYA + \angle YAX,$$

does not depend on the choice of points $X$ and $Y$.

I. F. Sharyzin

M999*. a) Prove that for any positive numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ the following inequality holds:

$$\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{2}{a_1 + a_2} + \dots + \frac{n}{a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n} < 4\left(\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_n}\right).$$

Prove that the constant 4 on the right side of inequality 6) can be replaced by 2;
c cannot be replaced by the number 2.

L. D. Kurlyandchik, A. S. Merkuryev

F1009. A flat disk of radius R, located in a horizontal plane, begins to move between two vertical threads, the distance between which is R; the length of each thread is 2l, stiffness k, the ends of the threads are fixed (Fig. 3). Find the force acting on the disk from the side of one thread after time t, if at the initial moment the threads were unfastened and touched the disk, the disk is moving at a constant speed v. Neglect the thickness of the disk; do not take into account the training.

A. S. Rosenberg, 9th grade student.

F1010. Two loaded dielectric threads of infinite length are located in space like two crossing perpendicular straight lines. Linear density of bobbins on threads σ. Find the force of interaction between the threads. Assume that the threads are very thin and redistribution of the bobbins will not occur.

I. V. Shutovsky, 10th grade student.

F1011. A balloon, the lifting force of which is created by hot action, is designed in such a way that the volume of its heated chamber is practically constant, and the pressure in it is equal to the external pressure, since the chamber in its lower part communicates with the atmosphere. Heating in the chamber is carried out constantly to compensate for the heat supply to the environment. Such a ball, at constant heated power, floats in the atmosphere at a certain height. How much will the floating altitude change if, with increasing heater power, the average air temperature in the chamber increases by Δt=0.1 °C from the initial temperature t=57 °C? Ambient temperature t₀=17 °C.

E. N. Yunosov, I. V. Yaminsky

F1012. The end of a long glass cylinder of radius R is covered with a light-transmitting plate in which there is a vertical slit; The width d of the slot can be changed. A beam of parallel light rays (Fig. 4) falls on the plate at an angle α; illumination of the plate E. At the opposite end of the cylinder there is a photocell. Construct a graph of the dependence of the luminous flux received by the camera on the slot width d. Refractive index of glass n.

A. A. Lapides
