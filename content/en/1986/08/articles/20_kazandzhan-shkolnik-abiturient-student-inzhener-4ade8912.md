Schoolchild – applicant – student – ​​engineer

Candidate of Technical Sciences
E. P. KAZANDZHAN

In this article, a mathematics teacher from one of the leading Moscow technical universities shares his thoughts on what qualities a future engineer should develop in himself. And although not all statements in these mathematical notes are indisputable, we hope that they will be useful to our readers, especially to ten-year students preparing to enter college.

Having taught mathematics at a university for many years, I was drawn to talk with some of the misconceptions in the psychology of students - applicants, students. Sometimes it seems that teachers and students speak different languages.

The first meetings are in July and August, when the admissions and university suffering begins. From year to year there are the same bewilderments: what is this arrow for? Did you just take your exams at school? Is there any attempt at all from exams? I have passed more than a hundred exams in my life, and I don’t remember any of them being useless. On the contrary, I can say that everything good in me is due to exams. What else organizes and mobilizes like this? Of course, this is not the only “doping” in training, but it is very powerful. My axiom: having passed the exam, I should learn something new about the passed subject and about myself, useful for the future; Therefore, passing the exam is a step forward in my development. And what about double exams during one summer... Alas, you have to take not two identical exams, but two different ones (fundamentally different!) according to the same program. The school exam is everyone’s lot and is the same for everyone; admission to a university is only for those who have decided to become an engineer. The school exam is aimed at the past - it is a report on the work done. And at the admissions exam, the examiner thinks about the future - will the applicant be able to become a full-fledged student, will he cope?

Sometimes schoolchildren (and even more often parents) wonder what knowledge gave them. This worries me too, but it’s not the first thing to fuck off. In five years today, an applicant should become an engineer. Knowledge is the conditions of a student. The needs of an engineer are much broader - in addition to knowledge, he needs skills, understanding, reflexes, intuition, the ability to analyze a situation, and make decisions. And knowledge alone in some cases is not enough or is worth almost nothing. Thousands of the biggest and best football players “know” how to score goals. Among the hundreds who came to the concert, other strangers “know” how to play a Beethoven sonata. Knowledge of the rules of the game of chess can be acquired in 15 minutes, and the ability to play to the level of a master can be acquired in 15 years.

We often say “knowledge is power”, not noticing that this thesis is already clearly outdated, it needs to be supplemented somehow, say, “knowledge is power, knowledge without understanding is weakness.” Of course, understanding is something extremely subtle; it deepens throughout one’s life, and it would be strange to demand immediately what one has to do gradually, over the years, with work experience. But the correct psychological orientation (the basis of future understanding) must be developed as early as possible.

Let's start by listing what I would like to see in the “baggage” of a high school student, and even more so of an applicant and student, regardless of the amount of his knowledge.

1. Literacy. When checking notes on the admission exam and correcting grammatical errors, I was always faced with shameless bewilderment - what’s wrong, I was in a hurry, what’s the difference, etc. Believe me, I try not to find fault with errors in the language until they lead to mathematical errors or make the text incomprehensible.

It’s hard to resist reading what Academician A. N. Krylov wrote about how exams were done in a French school at the end of the century:

“The assessment of written work in mathematics is also characteristic: it is carried out, firstly, by a French language teacher, who pays attention to the correctness of the language and design, and, secondly, by a specialist teacher who already evaluates the work on its merits.”

This is a reasonable approach. After all, what awaits a schoolchild or student in the future? You will have to make reports, write reports, articles...

2. Accuracy. From the first days of training, you are forced to learn: everything you write, someone will read - this means that a sloppy, careless, incomprehensible presentation of the results of the work done to others makes it meaningless and in no way necessary; It may be difficult for the performer, this is of no interest to anyone and is irrelevant, but it should be easy for the consumer, so think about him, not about yourself. Sometimes a reference comes to a worker who is a neat engineer. In such cases, I always stand up for my profession. We don’t say: a brave test pilot or a hardworking ballerina. Yes, that would be simply ridiculous - there were no hard-working pilots and lazy ballerinas. And sloppy engineers - alas...

3. Attitude to results, control. For a schoolchild this problem does not exist; the teacher is next to him. For an engineer, control is the most important stage in solving a problem (the simple truth is that no matter how difficult it is to get a result, sometimes it is even more difficult to realize it and manage it wisely. And it starts with... neatness, neatness. All records must have a system that is understandable at least to their author; this system must be organized so that it is difficult to make a mistake and easy to check for yourself (and not vice versa). I strive to lead to control of results, turn it into an organic need, as natural as those that are overburdened, whether it looks beautiful, or whether the cook is forgotten, whether the dinner he cooked is tasty. In my opinion, Stdyuno, having calculated, say, the area using an integral and getting a negative result, the teacher bears it - but many schoolchildren and even students naively think that since they are taught this, in their work they will differentiate, interrate...

I dare to conclude that the hottest will have to add A and B and subtract C. The difference is different. The student gets the result and that’s it, but the engineer gets the result and is responsible for it. Unlike a schoolboy or a machine, an engineer thinks

weighs, analyzes, compares, matches, suffers, suffers, rejoices, thinks again, tries to control himself directly and indirectly before giving out information. However, from my pedagogical “participation” - when solving category problems, I forbid students to look at the answer, by the way, and after solving it too. There are several reasons. First of all, matching the answer doesn't mean anything. This is by no means a criterion for the truth of a decision. In addition, there are typos. Secondly, and most importantly: life is not a puzzle and presents problems without answers, and we must prepare for just such a situation. Having received the result, you need to arrange a test for yourself - first a purely technical one (repeatedly going through all the calculations), then a semantic one: having forgotten about all the intermediate torments and transformations, with the result in hand, plunge again into the conditions of the problem - does it contradict them? For control, one must use any, even the most crude and indirect possibilities - dimension, symmetry, limiting cases and particular variants, results of previously solved problems, and finally, common sense.

4. Understanding the particular and the general, the ability to obtain information from correspondence. One can often hear groans about the abundance and cumbersomeness of formulas and the difficulty of filling them out. It seems to me that there is no need to specifically fill out any formulas, in some ways it is even harmful. They must fill it out themselves; they just need to create suitable conditions for this. I am forced to accept that part of the blame here is ours, the adults. The industry could help schoolchildren and students by printing various formulas on the covers of notebooks, books, and notepads. (By the way, Sofya Kovalevskaya learned the work precisely with the help of textbook pages pasted together on the wall.)

It’s annoying to see how, having written down the formula for the roots of a quadratic equation, students use their memory and struggle for 10-15 minutes, although it’s enough to remember only a very simple idea of ​​\u200b\u200bits derivation (selecting a complete square), and the result will be obtained in a minute. Almost any cumbersome formula evokes the same unnecessary mind-memories, especially in trigonometry - you have to remember, but something

It’s doubtful, the coefficient is 2 or 1/2, the sign “+” or “—”, etc. But the formula should work. And there is no need to think and remember - take and substitute something specific into this formula, see whether it works or not, if there is an error, it will immediately come out. There are no slackers in mathematics - all formulas, theorems, definitions work, and what doesn’t work is scrubbed away.

Let me explain why it’s funny for me to think about, say, the formula sin (α-β) =... This is how I act if the reference book is far away, and memory evokes confusion and the differences are expressed through the sequences and cosines of the terms as sums or differences of pairwise products - this is the simple “moral” of four cumbersome formulas. I eat the first thing that comes to mind, for example: sin (α-β)=sin α·sin β+cos α·cos β. But this is obvious nonsense - with α=β=0 on the left is 0, and on the right is 1. The “+” sign won’t help either. I’ll shuffle: sin α cos β—sin β cos α. When α=β=0 there is no contradiction, but there is no guarantee in the “+” sign: 0+0=0—0=0. Let me substitute something else, say α=β=45°: on the left there is 0, but not on the right, which means the sign is not “+”, but “-”. So: sin (α—β)=sin α·cos β—sin β·cos α. Of course, I fervently remember this formula more often than I forget, but, apparently, that’s why I remember that it wasn’t clogged - I always have an “emergency exit.”

5. Attitude to information. It is known that all information needed for operation can be divided into two categories: a) which is more convenient to store in memory; b) which is more convenient to store in memory (in a notebook or reference book). For an engineer, the separation will also occur by itself, but for students it is a server problem. In any flow of information, it is extremely important to regulate the degree of understanding. Taking this into account using the simplest everyday examples. In some situations, we don’t need understanding at all. Or desirable, but not required. For example, you need to know how to throw a coin in the subway, where is the nearest bakery, be able to use the elevator, etc. - clearly there is nothing practical here. Examples of the second type are knowledge of the rules of good manners and traffic rules, the basics of physical culture and rational nutrition.
