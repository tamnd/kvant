Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
G. I. KOSOUROV

In this article we present several experiments on color vision. All of them are associated with various color illusions caused by unusual working conditions of the eye or its color fatigue.

The mechanism of color perception by the eye is very complex and has not yet been fully studied. It is known that the retina of the eye contains two kinds of light-sensitive cells, which are called rods and cones. The rods contain photothymic floral pigment - visual purple, or rhodopisin. Under the influence of light, rhodopisin provides and acts on the first fibers that transmit excitation to the brain. In bright light it ensures completeness and the sticks go blind. In the dark, the reverse process occurs—the restoration of visual purple. Campanula su-
precision vision has a very high color value, but it is achromatic: the rods do not bloom colors.

At sufficiently high brightness, the bell-shaped mechanism of vision comes into force. Cone vision is color sensitive. There are many convincing experiments leading to the conclusion that the cones contain photothymic floral pigments of three varieties with a maximum of floral pigments in the red, green and bluish spectrum. The varying degrees of fading of all three pigments gives our consciousness a sense of color and allows us to see the world in all its diversity of colors, shades, and transitions.

The three-color nature of color vision is the basis for color reproduction in film, television, color photography, and printing. Methods for quantitative measurement of colors are also based on the three-color principle.

The perception of a painting can be significantly improved by lighting it in a special way. Take a color inclusion from Ogonyok, shape it and print a contrast black-and-white range from film to film using the contact method. Insert the range into a projector with a large luminous flux (a "Light" type projector is suitable) and project the range onto the original so that the contours of the projection and the original exactly match. The result will not make you regret the work expended. The picture will become okive, acquire volume and special expressiveness. Turn off the projector and you will see how dull and inexpressive the picture is under uniform lighting.

The following experiments relate to the so-called sequential color image. This phenomenon is due to the fact that complete restorative color treatment is a relatively slow process. If you look at a single-signal figure for a long time, and then move your gaze to white paper, a wall or a ceiling, then the white color will be perceived as if it lacks color, tiring the eye. On a white surface the same figure will be visible, but painted in a color called complementary.

Cut out red, orange, yellow, green, blue and purple squares measuring 2x2 cm from colored paper. Place one of the colored squares in front of you on a sheet of white paper and look at it without directing your eyes for about thirty seconds. You need to look, as they say, at one point, so that the image of the square does not move across the retina. Turn your gaze to the white field, and after a second or two you see on the paper a clear image of a square in a complementary color. This way you will learn that the complementary color to red is green, and the complementary color to blue is orange.
