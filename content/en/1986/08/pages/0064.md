IX Tournament of Young Physicists

This tournament begins in September 1986. It will be held in three stages.

I round - correspondence collective competition. Solutions to the TYUV-IX problems published below can be sent no later than November 20, 1986 to the address: 119899 Moscow, GSP, Moscow State University, Faculty of Physics, Council for Work with Schoolchildren, TUV-IX Organizing Committee. In the “To” column, write: “Correspondence competition TUF-IX” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending. In the letter, include an envelope with the school address written on it (the results of checking the solutions will be sent in this envelope), as well as a list of team members, the name of the physics teacher, school number, and class. Decisions can be individual and collective. Write each decision separately and first indicate the year, school number, class, surnames and names of the authors of the decision. To solve experimental problems, detailed descriptions of the installations, their diagrams, preferably photographs and experimental data should be attached. The most successful solutions to problems and self-generated problems will be awarded with diplomas from the tourer and submitted for publication in «Kvant».

2nd round - qualifying physio. Teams from schools in Moscow and the Moscow region who have scored more than 40 points (out of a possible 50) in the collective competition can take part in it. Qualifying physics 1/4 and 1/2 physics will be held from December 10, 1986 to January 10, 1987 at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University according to the tasks of the collective competition. (Another city may send materials for organizing the 2nd and 3rd rounds locally).

III round - financial. Will take place on February 22, 1987 at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. Its program includes: physical financial tournament, captains competition, big boys competition; rewarding the winners and active participants of the tour.

Anyone can take part in creating assignments for financial tourists. Send the conditions of the problems no later than January 20, 1987. You can receive additional information about the rules for holding TUF-IX, as well as make your suggestions and comments at the above address.

Assignments for the correspondence collective competition TYuF-IX

Most of the tasks are formed on the basis of specific physical phenomena and are designed to conduct server-side theoretical and experimental research that goes beyond the scope of the “school” approach. The conditions of the tasks are formed as briefly as possible and allow for different interpretations and degrees of simplification.

1. “Inventing it myself.” Create a problem yourself and solve it.

2. "Electron". Describe the properties of the electron known to modern science.

Carrying out this work involves compiling an abstract review, in which only the properties of the electron as an elementary particle should be presented, without considering the behavior of a large number of electrons in complex systems.

3. "Braking distance." The automatic braking distance is approximately 40 m, while that of a train is 1500 m. Why is there such a big difference?

4. "Moon Road"
“I go out alone on the road,
Through the fog the flinty path shines.
The night is quiet..."

M. Yu. Lermontov

What physical phenomenon did he describe therefore? Explain how the “Moon Road” will arise. Calculate the spatial distribution of the intensity of reflected light seen by the observer in these cases.

5. "Maneuver". An airplane flies at speed v over a straight section of highway. After what minimum time can he move away from the highway at a distance S? Assume that the total acceleration of the aircraft does not exceed the value a and the flight altitude is constant.

6. "Thermometer". It is convenient to measure temperature using a thermocouple. Determine the minimum time in which it can be used to measure the temperature of the thermostat with a predetermined accuracy?

7. "Incandescent lamp." Incandescent lamp in the network:
a) direct current;
b) alternating current with a frequency of 50 Hz.
Investigate the dependence of the current in the circuit on the direction drop across the lamp. Estimate the amplitude of temperature fluctuations of an incandescent lamp coil connected to an alternating current network with a frequency of 50 Hz.

8. "Limits of applicability." Describe the scope of application:
a) Newton's III law;
b) Coulomb's law.

9. "Electrics". What voltage and power is used in the electric trains of trams, trolleybuses, electric trains of the railway and metro?

10. "Free." Explain why there are drafts?

11. "String phone". From two matchboxes and a kastinite you can make...
telephone. Instead, investigate the principle of operation and properties of such a phone.

12. "Transformer efficiency". Investigate the dependence of the efficiency of a school step-down transformer on the load.

13. "Neon lamp." Why does a neon light come on? In other words, where do the free electrodes in the inert gas (neon) necessary for its ignition come from?

14. "Focal length". Offer evidence by your experiments to measure small (<1 cm) and large (>10 m) lens fax distances.

15. "Energy consumption". Estimate the total energy consumption of an average city apartment. Justify the physical reasons for economical energy use.
