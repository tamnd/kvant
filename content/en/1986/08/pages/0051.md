F991. In the circuit shown at figure 1, $U_0=2,4$ V, $R=600$ Ohm, $r=200$ Ohm, diodes $D_1$ and $D_2$ have the same voltage-ampere characteristics (red curve at figure 2). This current flows through diode $D_2$.

◆
We will solve the graphic problem.

Let's divide the circuit into two parts - a battery and a resistor (B+P) (Fig. 1a) and a circuit with non-linear elements (p+D₂+D₁) (Fig. 16) - and build their volt-ampere characteristics.

For the partition (B+P) I(U) depends, finding is easy:

$$I = \frac{U_0 - U}{R}$$

(at $U \leqslant U_0$)

— straight line $I_{B+P}$ to figure 2.

In a nonlinear circuit, we first find the dependence I(U) for a series-connected resistor $r$ and diode $D_2$ (p+D₂). Let's draw a straight line $I_r = U/r$ and for each current value we sum up the values ​​of the voltage drops at $r$ and at $D_2$. The result will be curve $I_{p+D_2}$ (see Fig. 2).

Now for each voltage value we will find the total current in the linear circuit - we will sum the current values ​​through $D_1$ and in the section (p+D₂). The resulting curve $I_{p+D_1+D_2}$ is the volt-ampere characteristic of the non-linear circuit isolated at figure 16. Note that we do not need to construct the entire curve $I_{p+D_1+D_2}$ - a piece in the area of ​​intersection of this curve with the volt-ampere characteristic of the section B+P (with straight line $I_{B+P}$) is enough. The intersection point of these lines - $U \approx 0,9$ V and $I_{o6\pi} \approx 2,5$ mA - gives the values ​​of voltage and total current in the circuit (p+D₁+D₂). The current through the diode $D_1$ at a voltage of 0.9 V is $I_1 = 1,5$ mA; this means that the current through resistor $r$ and diode $D_2$ is equal to

$$I_2 = I_{o6\pi} - I_1 = 0,9$$ mA.
