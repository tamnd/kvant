4. Understanding the particular and the general, the ability to obtain information from correspondence. One can often hear groans about the abundance and cumbersomeness of formulas and the difficulty of filling them out. It seems to me that there is no need to specifically fill out any formulas, in some ways it is even harmful. They must fill it out themselves; they just need to create suitable conditions for this. I am forced to accept that part of the blame here is ours, the adults. The industry could help schoolchildren and students by printing various formulas on the covers of notebooks, books, and notepads. (By the way, Sofya Kovalevskaya learned the work precisely with the help of textbook pages pasted together on the wall.)

It’s annoying to see how, having written down the formula for the roots of a quadratic equation, students use their memory and struggle for 10-15 minutes, although it’s enough to remember only a very simple idea of ​​\u200b\u200bits derivation (selecting a complete square), and the result will be obtained in a minute. Almost any cumbersome formula evokes the same unnecessary mind-memories, especially in trigonometry - you have to remember, but something

It’s doubtful, the coefficient is 2 or 1/2, the sign “+” or “—”, etc. But the formula should work. And there is no need to think and remember - take and substitute something specific into this formula, see whether it works or not, if there is an error, it will immediately come out. There are no slackers in mathematics - all formulas, theorems, definitions work, and what doesn’t work is scrubbed away.

Let me explain why it’s funny for me to think about, say, the formula sin (α-β) =... This is how I act if the reference book is far away, and memory evokes confusion and the differences are expressed through the sequences and cosines of the terms as sums or differences of pairwise products - this is the simple “moral” of four cumbersome formulas. I eat the first thing that comes to mind, for example: sin (α-β)=sin α·sin β+cos α·cos β. But this is obvious nonsense - with α=β=0 on the left is 0, and on the right is 1. The “+” sign won’t help either. I’ll shuffle: sin α cos β—sin β cos α. When α=β=0 there is no contradiction, but there is no guarantee in the “+” sign: 0+0=0—0=0. Let me substitute something else, say α=β=45°: on the left there is 0, but not on the right, which means the sign is not “+”, but “-”. So: sin (α—β)=sin α·cos β—sin β·cos α. Of course, I fervently remember this formula more often than I forget, but, apparently, that’s why I remember that it wasn’t clogged - I always have an “emergency exit.”

5. Attitude to information. It is known that all information needed for operation can be divided into two categories: a) which is more convenient to store in memory; b) which is more convenient to store in memory (in a notebook or reference book). For an engineer, the separation will also occur by itself, but for students it is a server problem. In any flow of information, it is extremely important to regulate the degree of understanding. Taking this into account using the simplest everyday examples. In some situations, we don’t need understanding at all. Or desirable, but not required. For example, you need to know how to throw a coin in the subway, where is the nearest bakery, be able to use the elevator, etc. - clearly there is nothing practical here. Examples of the second type are knowledge of the rules of good manners and traffic rules, the basics of physical culture and rational nutrition.
