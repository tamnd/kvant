⟦folio 4⟧

Fig. 2.

build as many correct parquet floors yourself as possible.

Main task

It turns out that all the variety of correct parquet floors can be described. If the side length $h$ of the parquet polygons is given, then there are only a finite number of different (non-overlapping) regular parquets. I don’t want to tell you exactly how much.

Listing them all and thereby answering the question about their number is the main task that you have to solve.

Some guidelines

It is natural to begin solving the problem by examining the structure of the parquet tops. A regular $n$-gon has $n$ exterior angles (Fig. 4), the sum of which is equal to four REM right angles (see this for yourself). Therefore, each angle of a regular $n$-gon is equal to

$$a_n = 2d - \frac{4d}{n} = 2\left(1 - \frac{2}{n}\right)d.$$

The tops of the parquet should contain polygons with a sum of angles equal to 4d. So,

$$a_3 = \frac{2}{3}d, \ a_4 = d, \ a_6 = \frac{4}{3}d, \ a_8 = \frac{3}{2}d,$$

and for the parquets shown in Figures 1 and 2, we have:

$$4a_4 = 4d,$$
$$6a_3 = 4d,$$
$$3a_6 = 4d,$$
$$a_4 + 2a_8 = 4d,$$
$$a_3 + 2a_4 + a_6 = 4d.$$

In the general case, denoting by $m_n$ the number of adjacent $n$-gons, we should obtain

$$\sum m_i a_i = 4d,$$

(1)

where in the sum we include terms with those numbers $i$ for which $m_i > 0$ and $a_i = 2(1 - 2/i)d$.
