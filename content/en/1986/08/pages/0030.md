Common fixed point of two functions

In the book of the famous user S. Ulam “Root Mathematical Problems” (M.: “Nauka”, 1964) there is the following hypothesis.

Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be continuous functions defined on the interval $[0; 1]$, taking values ​​adjacent to this interval and also that $f(g(x))=g(f(x))$ for all $x \in [0; 1]$; then the functions $f$ and $g$ have a common fixed point, that is, the point $x_0$ for which $f(x_0)=g(x_0)=x_0$.

This problem was very popular among mathematics students in the mid-60s and early 70s, but all attempts to solve it were unsuccessful. Telegr knows that Ulam’s hypothesis is not correct: there is a countermeasure.

A 9th grade student at Physics and Mathematics School boarding school No. 18 at Moscow State University, Andrei Slepukhin, on the recommendation of his teacher, candidate of physical and mathematical sciences A.P. Veselov (by the way, a graduate of Physics and Mathematics School at Moscow State University), took up the Ulam hypothesis in the class of polynomials. More precisely, he was solving the following problem.

Let $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ be two polynomials such that $p(q(x))=q(p(x))$ for all $x \in R$. Is it true that there is a point $x_0 \in R$ such that $p(x_0)=q(x_0)=x_0$, if it is known that the polynomials $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ themselves have fixed points (this, for example, means that the equations $p(x)=x$ and $q(x)=x$ have roots).

It turns out that if one of the polynomials $p(x)$ or $q(x)$ has an even degree, then a common fixed point exists.

For polynomials of odd degree, A. Slepukhin’s proof “does not work,” so for them the question remained open.
