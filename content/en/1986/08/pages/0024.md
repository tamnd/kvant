The most interesting thing is that any signal can be represented as a sum of monochromatic waves with different frequencies (composed of such waves). This opportunity is given to us by the principle of interference: when waves propagate, oscillations in each place in space add up. Let's add, depending on the phase shift, oscillations of identical frequencies can strengthen each other (with a zero phase difference, the result is oscillations with a total amplitude - figure 1, or they can weaken (figure 1, b). It turns out that the amplitudes and frequencies of added monochromatic waves can be selected in such a way that the waves cancel each other out in almost the entire space, except for the opisthoses in place, and move at the speed of the wave, in other words, they spread signal. If the speed c of monochromatic waves of all frequencies is the same (as, for example, when electromagnetic waves propagate in vacuum), then the maximum moves with speed c. Its width is equal to $\Delta L = 2\pi c / \Delta \omega$. So the duration of the signal is $\Delta t = 2\pi / \Lambda \omega$.

This results in an amazingly simple fundamental relationship:
$$\Delta \omega \cdot \Delta t = 2\pi.$$
