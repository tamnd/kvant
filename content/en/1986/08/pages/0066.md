Quantum for junior schoolchildren
(see «Kvant» No. 7)

1. Let's express $x$ through $y$: $x = \frac{5y - 3}{y + 3} (y \neq -3)$, or $x = 5 - \frac{18}{y + 3}$. Therefore, $x$ will be an integer if and only if 18 is divisible by $y + 3$. For $y + 3$ we get twelve possible values: $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6, \pm 9, \pm 18$, which gives twelve integer solutions to the equation
$$\{(-13; -2), (23; -4), (-4; -1), (14; -5), (-1; 0), (11; -6), (2; 3), (8; -9), (3; 6), (7; -12), (4; 15), (6; -21)\}.$$

2. Let's take a regular patagon inscribed in our “colored” circle. It is easy to understand that he will always have three vertices of the same color. In any case, these vertices form the desired equilateral triangle.

3. Let $A = \overline{ab}$. Then $(a + b) + (a + b)^2 = 10a + b$, that is, $(a + b)^2 = 9a$, which means that $a$ is a perfect square. Since $1 \leq a \leq 9$, we get three possibilities: $a = 1, a = 4$ and $a = 9$. Therefore the number $A$ is 12, 42 or 90.

4. Let us describe a circle around the triangle $ABC$. From condition $\angle CAB + \angle MCB = 90^\circ$ it follows that the median $CM$ belongs to the diameter of this circle (make a drawing). If side $AB$ is not a diameter, then median $CM$ is perpendicular to $AB$ as the diameter passing through the midpoint of the chord, and triangle ABC is equilateral (since median $CM$ is also an altitude). If side $AB$ is a diameter, then triangle $ABC$ is a straight triangle.

Chess page
(see «Kvant» No. 5)

Task 9 (Y. Hartog, 1957). The ceiling problem is one of the most popular geometric topics in the chess room. After 1. Nb5! with the threat 2. Qd4× the seventh overlap occurs immediately: 1...Cf6 2. Qh7× (the f7 pawn is covered); 1...f6 2. Qe7× (teler pawn blocked the bishop's path to the e7 square); 1...Cf2 2. Rf4×; 1...Ke2 2. Re3×; 1...Kb3 2. K:c3×; 1...Kc6 2. Cd5×; 1...c5 2. N:d6×.

Task 10 (L. Kubbel, 1907). 1. Kc3+ Kc2 2. Nd1! 2. Nd5 loses? because of 2...Kpd2! 2...Kd1 3. Kh8! f1Q (3...Ke1 4. Rg2 with the same result) 4. Rg1! Ф:g1. Pat.
