Parquets made of regular polygons

Academician A. N. KOLMOGOROV

What is parquet

The washing is simple, but the most boring parquet is obtained if the plane is divided into equal squares as shown in figure 1, a. Here two squares either have a common side, a common vertex, or no common points at all.

We will call a parquet a covering of a plane with regular polygons in which two polygons have either a common side, a common vertex, or no common points at all.

You should probably see a parquet made of regular octagons and squares (Fig. 2, a). A beautiful parquet can be composed of regular planes, polygons, squares and equilateral triangles (Fig. 2, b).

Parquet produces a pleasant appearance if it is sufficiently symmetrical. A figure is called symmetrical if it can be superimposed on itself in a “non-trivial” way (that is, not in such a way that all the points remain in place).

What is the right parquet

From the point of view of symmetry, our definition of parquet is not very successful. It allows parquets that do not have any symmetry. Taking a common parquet of hexagons (Fig. 1, b), you can “spoil” it by subdividing some of the hexagons into six triangles. It is easy to understand that the result will be “parquet” again in the sense of our definition. But it can be proven (try it!) that by subdividing, for example, three hexagons, as shown in figure 3, and leaving all the rest undivided, we get a parquet floor that is completely devoid of symmetry. To install ugly, insufficiently symmetrical parquet floors, we use the following definition:

A parquet is called regular if it can be superimposed on itself in such a way that any given vertex of it is superimposed on any other given vertex of it.
