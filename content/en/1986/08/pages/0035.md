The perception of a painting can be significantly improved by lighting it in a special way. Take a color inclusion from Ogonyok, shape it and print a contrast black-and-white range from film to film using the contact method. Insert the range into a projector with a large luminous flux (a "Light" type projector is suitable) and project the range onto the original so that the contours of the projection and the original exactly match. The result will not make you regret the work expended. The picture will become okive, acquire volume and special expressiveness. The following experiments relate to the so-called sequential color image. This phenomenon is due to the fact that complete restorative color treatment is a relatively slow process. If you look at a single-signal figure for a long time, and then move your gaze to white paper, a wall or a ceiling, then the white color will be perceived as if it lacks color, tiring the eye. On a white surface the same figure will be visible, but painted in a color called complementary.

Cut out red, orange, yellow, green, blue and purple squares measuring 2x2 cm from colored paper. Place one of the colored squares in front of you on a sheet of white paper and look at it without directing your eyes for about thirty seconds. You need to look, as they say, at one point, so that the image of the square does not move across the retina. Turn your gaze to the white field, and after a second or two you see on the paper a clear image of a square in a complementary color. This way you will learn that the complementary color to red is green, and the complementary color to blue is orange.
