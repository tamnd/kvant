In this article we present several experiments on color vision. All of them are associated with various color illusions caused by unusual working conditions of the eye or its color fatigue.

The mechanism of color perception by the eye is very complex and has not yet been fully studied. It is known that the retina of the eye contains two kinds of light-sensitive cells, which are called rods and cones. The rods contain photothymic floral pigment - visual purple, or rhodopisin. Under the influence of light, rhodopisin provides and acts on the first fibers that transmit excitation to the brain. In bright light it ensures completeness and the sticks go blind. There are many convincing experiments leading to the conclusion that the cones contain photothymic floral pigments of three varieties with a maximum of floral pigments in the red, green and bluish spectrum. The varying degrees of fading of all three pigments gives our consciousness a sense of color and allows us to see the world in all its diversity of colors, shades, and transitions.

The three-color nature of color vision is the basis for color reproduction in film, television, color photography, and printing. Methods for quantitative measurement of colors are also based on the three-color principle.
