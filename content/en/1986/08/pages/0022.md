How do waves transmit information?

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. G. ASLAMAZOV

We are so accustomed to seeing events happening on the other side of the world on the TV screen that we are not even surprised by this. In the modern world, radio, television, and telephone make it quite easy to receive and transmit the necessary information. But until relatively recently everything was completely different...

In order to convey to St. Petersburg what was known about the coronation of the Empire of Elizabeth, which took place in Moscow, a whole group of soldiers with flags was deployed along the entire route between these cities. At the moment of coronation, the first soldier waved the flag, then the next, etc. Thus, the knowledge of the coronation reached St. Petersburg, where the cannons came out. This is how complex a method was used to quickly transfer information in a time-sensitive manner*).

*) Using this example in the article by L. G. Aslamazov and I. K. Kikon “What is a wave?” (*Kvant*, 1982, No. 6) the concept of a wave was discussed. Here we will continue the conversation we started and discuss the problem of information transfer.
