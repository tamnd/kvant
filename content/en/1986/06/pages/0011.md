⟦folio 9⟧

Charles Coulomb and his discoveries

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences S.R. FILONOVICH

This year marks the 250th anniversary of the birth of Charles Coulomb. This name is known to anyone who has ever studied physics. Coulomb’s main scientific achievement was considered to be the experimental confirmation of the law of “shaped squares” - the law of interaction of stationary precise charges. However, Coulomb owns a number of studies in other areas of physics, joining the golden fund of science. We publish an article about the outstanding French scientist and excerpts from the most important works of Coulomb, giving an idea of ​​his work.

Charles Augustin Coulon was born on June 14, 1736 in the city of Angoulême, in southwestern France. His father, who had once tried to make a military career, was a government official by the time his son was born. Coulon's mother came from a prominent family. Charles spent his childhood in Paris, where he received his primary education, attending the Collection of the Four Nations, founded after the end of Cardin Mazarin's famous political activity. The Mazarin College was famous for its high level of teaching mathematics, and, apparently, they instilled in the young Coulon a love for this subject.

After graduating from Collec, Coulon left Paris for his father’s homeland in Montpellier. There he took part in the work of the local scientific society and was even accepted into it in the mathematics class. However, Coulon did not remain in the province for long. He understood that it was necessary to choose some kind of profession that would provide a livelihood. Coulomb's choice fell on the profession of a military engineer, which gave him a decently high position in society and was most consistent with the inclinations of a young lover of science.

To enter the Military Engineering Corps of the French Army, it was necessary to graduate from a special school located in Mezières. In the middle of the 18th century, the Mezieres Military Engineering School was considered one of the best higher technical educational institutions in Europe. After successfully passing the entrance exams, Coulon was enrolled in the School and for a year and a half he studied mathematics, drawing, basic physics, and also received practical sciences: carpentry, stone processing, and geodetic work. In 1761, Coulon graduated from the School and received the rank of lieutenant.

Soon, Coulon receives an order to be assigned to Martinique, an overseas colony of France. There, a young and still completely inexperienced engineer is in charge of a military fort - the main defensive structure of the island. Coulon spent eight years in Martinique and was recalled to his homeland after the construction of the fort was practically legalized.

After returning to France, Coulomb received successive assignments to Bouchain, Cherbourg, Besançon, Rochefort, Lille... And all this time, continuing to serve as a military engineer, performing many technical tasks, including very troublesome ones, Coulomb found time for research in various fields of technology and physics.
