M970. At the initial stop, 32 passengers boarded the bus, which needed to travel to 32 different stops located at a distance of 1 km from each other. The driver decided to vote: which stops to cancel and which to keep. He calls stops in some rocks. The passenger is in favor of canceling the stop if he is going to go further, against if he is going to get off at this stop, and returns if - earlier (not taking into account that if he continues to stop, his stop may also be cancelled). If there are more votes for cancellation than against, the stop will be canceled, and the one (or those) who wanted to get off at it will decide to go to the closest one that has not yet been canceled (if there are two of them, to the first of them). What is a) the smallest, b) the most number of stops that can be saved depending on the order in which the driver calls them?

a) Answer. 2. The last two stops are preserved in any order of heading: no one heads against the last one, one passenger heads against the penultimate one, and at least one passenger heads for it. If the driver calls stops in the following number order: 31, 32, 29, 30, 27, 28, ..., 1, 2, all other stops will be canceled (see table).

6) Answer. 6. Let $m$ stops remain uncanceled after heading, and at the $i$th of them, counting from the end, $a_i$ passengers got off (for example, $a_1=1$ - at the last, 32nd stop, very indo, 1 passenger got off). Then no more than $a_i$ passengers voted against canceling this stop (since subsequent voting could not change the decision to get off at it), and no less than $a_{i-1}+a_{i-2}+\dots+a_1$ were in favor of canceling it. Since the stop was not cancelled, $a_i \geq a_{i-1}+a_{i-2}+\dots+a_1$. Let there be $m$ stops, then

$$32=a_m+a_{m-1}+a_{m-2}+\dots+a_1 \geq 2(a_{m-1}+a_{m-2}+\dots+a_1) \geq 2^2(a_{m-2}+\dots+a_1) \geq \dots \geq 2^{m-1}a_1=2^{m-1},$$

and therefore $m \leq 6$.

Let us indicate a head order such that $m=6$. Let's divide the stops, starting with the last one, into 6 groups consisting of 1, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 stops. In each group, we mark one stop so that neighboring stops are equally distant from the common boundary of their groups, that is, stops numbered 32, 31, 30, 27, 22, 11 (see figure). Let’s carry out the voting from end to beginning, first along the non-canceled stops (it’s easy to see that they are all cancelled), then along the canceled ones (all passengers will vote for each of them).
