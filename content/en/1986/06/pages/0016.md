⟦folio 14⟧

The range of Coulomb's research in the field of electricity was very wide. He studied the phenomenon of charge leakage, charge distribution over the surfaces of conductors of complex shape, investigated the inductive effect of a charged conductor on a neutral one, etc. In general, the results obtained by Coulomb became the experimental basis of the first consistent quantitative theory of electricity, developed by the French mathematician S. Poisson in 1811.

In 1789, the Great French bourgeois revolution began, which upended the entire structure of French society. Revolutionary events also affected Coulomb. In 1791, he retired with the rank of major. In 1793, the Royal Academy of Sciences was dissolved. Coulon and his family leave Paris and live away from the capital for a year and a half.

However, a great scientist like Coulomb could not remain outside scientific life for long. In 1795, the Institute of France was formed, replacing the old Academy. Among the first members of the Institute were Coulon. After being elected to the new Academy and returning to Paris, the scientist continued his research, although its intensity gradually subsided.

In the last years of his life, Coulomb devoted a lot of energy to his work as inspector general of public education, participating in a radical transformation of the education system in France.

The scientist died in Paris in 1806.
