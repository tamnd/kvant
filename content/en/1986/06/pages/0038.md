M967. Let us denote by σ(n) the sum of all natural divisors of n (including 1 and n) and by φ(n) the number of numbers less than n and coprime to it. Prove that for any natural number n ≥ 2
σ(n) + φ(n) ≥ 2n.

Let 1 = d₁ < ... < dₖ = n - all divisors of the number n.
Among the natural numbers not exceeding n, the number of multiples of dᵢ is equal to n/dᵢ, therefore the total number of numbers not coprime to n, equal to n - φ(n), is not more than the sum
$$\frac{n}{d_2} + \frac{n}{d_3} + ... + \frac{n}{d_k} = d_{k-1} + d_{k-2} + ... + d_1 = \sigma(n) - n$$
(for n/d₁ = dₖ, n/d₂ = dₖ-1, ...). Thus,
n - φ(n) ≤ σ(n) - n,
whence the inequality being proved follows.
Note that for prime p our estimate is exact:
$$\sigma(p) = p + 1, \quad \varphi(p) = p - 1, \quad \sigma(p) + \varphi(p) = 2p.$$
V. F. Lev

M968. Three polygons in space are located so that their planes intersect at one point O.
a) Prove that there is a plane whose projections onto which of these three polygons have equal areas.
b) How many such planes will there be, projected through point O?

Fig. 1.

Let us associate the polygon M with the vector $\vec{m}$, perpendicular to the plane of the polygon and equal in length to the area of ​​the polygon. Then the area of ​​the projection of M onto any plane α is equal to the length of the projection $\vec{m}$ onto a line a perpendicular to α (the area of ​​M during the projection is multiplied by the cosine of the angle between α and the plane M, and the length of the vector $\vec{m}$ by the cosine of the angle between the vector and line a, but these angles are obviously equal; see figure 1).

Let us plot from point O the vectors $\vec{m}_1$, $\vec{m}_2$ and $\vec{m}_3$, corresponding to the data of the polygon; their ends and point O do not lie in the same plane. Due to the above, the condition of problem a) is satisfied by the plane passing through the ends of these vectors; the plane of their projection onto the line perpendicular to it coincides. And since the long projections of two any vectors $\vec{m}$ and $\vec{n}$ onto a straight line are equal if and only if the projection $\vec{m}$ coincides with the projection $\vec{n}$ or - $\vec{n}$, we can similarly construct 3 more such planes, replacing the three vectors $\vec{m}_1$, $\vec{m}_2$, $\vec{m}_3$ vector $\vec{m}_2$, or vector $\vec{m}_3$, or both together in opposite directions (Fig. 2). It is clear that these 4 planes are not parallel in pairs, and any plane that satisfies condition a) must be parallel to one of them. Thus, the answer in problem 6) is 4 planes.
A. Onukyan, N. M. Sedrakin
