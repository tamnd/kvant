F978. Two identical weights B and C are attached to a rigid weightless rod ABC of length 3l as shown in figure 1: $|AB|=l, |BC|=2l$. The rod is suspended from point A to a very long weightless thread AO. At the initial moment, the rod is held in a horizontal position, and the thread is vertical; then the rod is released. What will be the speed of point A at the moment when the rod passes the lowest pole?

By the time the rod passes the lower pole, the OA thread will deviate very slightly from the vertical (since it is very long). Therefore, the tension force of the thread acting on the rod can be considered vertical all the time (its horizontal component can be claimed). Consequently, the horizontal component of the rod impulse remains unchanged, that is, equal to zero, and at a lower position

$$|mv_B|=|mv_C| \Rightarrow |v_B|=|v_C|=v.$$

The value of $v$ will be found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$2\left(\frac{mv^2}{2}\right)=mgl+3mgl=4mgl,$$

where

$$v=2\sqrt{gl}.$$

Thus, at the moment of passing the lowest pole, the rod rotates around the center K of segment BC (Fig. 2).

In this case $|AK|=2|OB|$, and the speed of point A

$$v_A=2v_B=2v=4\sqrt{gl}.$$

F979. An ideal gas is contained in a vessel having the shape of two “joined” cylinders of the same length l and with cross-sectional areas S and αS (α>1). In the middle of each cylinder there are pistons connected by a rigid rod (see figure); in this case, the pressure in compartment I is equal to p, in compartment III the pressure is equal to βp, the pistons are in equilibrium. A body Q was brought to the system so that the temperature increased, remaining the same in all compartments. Determine how the pressure in compartment I has changed. The internal energy of one mole of gas is equal to $cT$ (c is the molar heat of the gas). The heat capacity of the cylinders and pistons is pretentious.

Let $V_i, p_i$ and $v_i$ be the volume of the $i$th compartment, the pressure and the number of moles of gas in the vessel $v=v_1+v_2+v_3$. Since the temperature $T$ is the same in all compartments, the Mendeleev-Cliperon equation gives

$$p_1V_1+p_2V_2+p_3V_3=(v_1+v_2+v_3)RT=vRT.$$ (1)

For the same reason, the pistons will not move, since at constant volumes the pressure ratio in the compartments does not change and at any moment of time the equilibrium condition will be satisfied

$$(p_3-p_2)\alpha S+(p_2-p_1)S=0.$$ (2)

Using the conditions $p_3=\beta p_1$, from (2) we find

$$p_2=p_1\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\alpha-1}.$$ (3)
