Let us still calculate (this will be useful to us later) how long such a field from the Earth to the Sun would last.

The trajectory of the fall on the Sun - a straight line segment - is the limiting case of an elongated elliptical orbit with a major hemisphere equal to half the radius of the Earth's orbit. According to Kepler's third law, the squares of the periods of rotation of bodies in orbits are related to the cubes of large semi-sein orbits. This means that the time $t$ of falling on the Sun (that is, the shower of the period of revolution along an elongated orbit with a major hemisphere $R_\oplus/2$) can be determined from the condition $(2t)^2/T_\oplus^2 = (R_\oplus/2)^3/R_\oplus^3$, where $T_\oplus$, $R_\oplus$ are the period of revolution and the radius of the Earth’s orbit. Since $T_\oplus$ is 1 year, $t = 1/4\sqrt{2}$ years ≈ 0.177 years.

The direct field time turned out to be not long, but its initial speed, alas, has not yet been reached. How can you move the field towards the Sun with our own energy costs and with our same starting speed? The most economical field uses Jupiter's gravitational field to decelerate the station in its orbit around the Sun. But in order to carry out this maneuver, you need to fly up to Jupiter at a certain speed. And for this, in turn, it is necessary that, having overcome the earth’s gravity, the station enters orbit, having an initial speed relative to the Sun $v_0=40,5$ km/s; therefore, relative to the Earth, the speed of the station at this time will be 10.5 km/s. So, the field begins with the acceleration of the rocket in the same direction as the speed of the Earth’s movement in its orbit. What starting speed should be given to the rocket?

In order to assume earth's gravity, at launch it needs to be given kinetic energy corresponding to the second cosmic speed $v_{\text{II}}=\sqrt{2gR_\oplus} \approx 11,2$ km/s.*) But be careful - you don’t need to add the second cosmic speed to 10.5 km/s to get the starting speed! From

*) In Matrovskaya issue «Kvant» for this year in the section “School in «Kvant»” the note “Second cosmic speed” was published. We advise readers to get acquainted with it. (Editor's note)

According to the law of conservation of energy, we find that the starting speed from the Earth’s surface in our case is equal to $v_{\text{ct.}} = \sqrt{(v_0 - v_\oplus)^2 + 2gR_\oplus} \approx 15,5$ km/s.

By the way, do you know where on the earth’s surface the launch site is located (from energy messages)? This is the Klimanjaro volcano in Tanzania, a 5.9 km high mountain almost on the equator. Taking into account the continuity of the Earth, the top of the volcano is the most distant point on the earth’s surface from the center (further than Everest!). There is the lowest free fall acceleration on Earth. The top of the volcano, however, is always covered with snow, and there are no plans to build a spaceport there yet.

Let's continue our field. It passes through a section of an elliptical orbit, crossing the orbit of Mars and approaching the orbit of Jupiter (Fig. 1). The intermediate target of the field is Jupiter, the largest of the planets in the solar system. It must slow down the station, reduce its speed relative to the Sun - then the station begins to fall towards the Sun. In order to be at the point of intersection of orbits at the moment of approaching the Jupiter station, at the moment of launch it must be at a point offset by figure 1 by zero time. Make sure that at the moment of crossing the orbit of Mars, it is far enough from the automatic station and does not distort its trajectory.
