F968. Three meshed gears with radii $r_1, r_2$ and $r_3$ (Fig. 1) are mounted on three parallel axes connected by rigid rods. Axes B and C are machined around a fixed axis A with an angular velocity of $\omega_0$; in this case, the dimensions of triangle ABC remain unchanged, and the gear of radius $r_3$ moves translationally. At what angular speed is the gear of radius $r_1$ being repaired?

Fig. 1.

figure 2 shows the position of the system after time $t$. During this time, turn points $B$ and $C$ by angle $\varphi=\omega_0 t$. The gear of radius $r_3$, according to the motion conditions, moves forward. This means that during the time $t$ this gear has rotated as a whole around the axis $A$ through an angle $\varphi$. And around its own axis $C$ - also at an angle $\varphi$. This means that all other ratios on figure 2 are equal to $\varphi r_3$, and the angular speed $\omega$ of the gear of radius $r_1$ is determined by the conditions of motion.

Fig. 2.

From here

$\omega=\omega_0\left(1-\frac{r_3}{r_1}\right)$.

V. Vantselov (Bulgaria)

F969. Two cans made of the same material have the same geometric shape, and all the linear dimensions of one can are one time smaller than the other (see figure). A hole was made in two of each can. The jars are simultaneously lowered into the water. Which bank will please you most? How many times?

Due to the fact that the jars that are immersed in water are made of the same materials, at first the depth of immersion of each of them is linearly related to the size. That is, if the sizes of the cans differ by $k$ times $l_2=kl_1$, then the distances from the water surface to the surface $h_2=kh_1$ differ by $k$ times.

Since the pressure drop on both sides of the connection depends on the immersion depth $\Delta p=\rho_{\text{воды}}gh$, and the speed of water filling the jar through the connection is related to the pressure difference - $\Delta p=\frac{1}{2}q_{\text{воды}}v^2$, then

$v^2=2gh$.

In turn, the speed determines the time $t$, after which the jar will fill with water and move to the bottom.
