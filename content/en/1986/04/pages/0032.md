Now you can win against any opponent if you go first. Mathematicians say that a solution algorithm is given.

Algorithms are all around us. Let's open the cookbook. Here, each sieve contains a list of the sequence of actions (algorithm) that need to be performed in order to prepare a particular dish. Here, for example, is a potato cake sieve.
1. The potatoes are cooked (not boiled) so that the mashed potatoes are completely dry.
2. Melt the butter.
3. Thoroughly rub mashed potatoes with hot butter.
4. Beat 4 eggs.
5. Add them one at a time to the puree, without ceasing to grind.
6. Separately, grind 4 yolks with added sugar.
7. Combine them with the potato mixture.
8. Beat the remaining whites into hard beer.
9. Combine them with the potato and egg mixture, trying to beat it as smoothly as possible.
10. Add baking powder$^1$.
11. Seal on a heatproof dish in the oven. For 10 large potatoes: 300 g butter, 8 eggs, 3 tablespoons sugar, 0.5 teaspoon baking powder.

It is convenient to depict Figure of this sieve in the form of a diagram (see Fig. 4). Here it is not clear that operations 2, 4, 6 and 8 can be performed independently of the others. You can save time preparing this dish if

$^1$) Baking powder consists of a mixture of baking soda, ammonium carbonate and tartaric salt. It can be replaced with a mixture of baking soda and citric acid in equal parts.

Is it possible to perform these operations while there are no potatoes?

Similar figure sequences of individual operations have become widespread in many areas of life, especially in construction. There they are called network diagrams. The use of network diagrams allows you to reduce construction time, indicate the exact time of each construction operation, and calculate the need for machines, people, and materials.

Calculations are now performed by electronic computers (computers) according to a predetermined program - an algorithm; These actions and here it is convenient to display graphics in the form of circles and arrows. Such figure are called "block diagrams".

Let us consider the sequence of actions when solving the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
1. Let's calculate $A = b^2$.
2. Let's calculate $B = ac$.
3. Let's calculate $C = 4B$.
4. Let's calculate $D = A - C$.
5. Let's calculate $E = \sqrt{D}$.
6. Let's calculate $F = -b + E$.
7. Let's calculate $G = -b - E$.
8. Let's calculate $H = 2a$.
9. Let's calculate $x_1 = F/H$.
10. Let's calculate $x_2 = G/H$.
11. Type $x_1, x_2$.

On most modern computers, operations are carried out sequentially: 1st, 2nd, etc. But there are already computers that can simultaneously perform several operations. And thus speed up the work.
