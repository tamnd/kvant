Problem solutions

M956. Four circles of the same radius are drawn on the plane so that three of them pass through one point A, and three through point B. Prove that the four points of their pairwise intersection, different from A and B, are the vertices of a parallelogram.

M957. Prove that from 1985 different natural numbers, all of whose prime factors are contained among the 9 prime numbers 2,3,...,23, you can choose four numbers whose product is the fourth power of an integer.

M956—M960; F968—F972
Let us present one of many solutions to the problem.
Let us denote the centers of the circles by $O_1, O_2, O_3, O_4$, and the points of their pairwise intersection by $K, L, M, N$ (see figure). Since the circles are equal, the point $P$ of intersection of the common chord $AK$ of circles $O_1$ and $O_2$ with their linear centers $O_1O_2$ divides both segments $AK$ and $O_1O_2$ in half. Similarly, the segments $O_3O_2$ and $AL$ have a common midpoint $Q$. Therefore, the segment $PQ$ is the midline of two triangles - $O_1O_2O_3$ and $AKL$, that is, $\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=2\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{KL}$. It is proved in the same way that $\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=\overrightarrow{NM}$, and from the equality $\overrightarrow{KL}=\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=\overrightarrow{NM}$ it follows that $KLMN$ is a parallelogram.

Prove on your own that $A$ and $B$ are the points of intersection of the altitudes of triangles $BKL$ and $AMN$; this fact can be used for another solution to the problem (cf. the final remark in the solution to problem M951 in the previous issue «Kvant»).

Let us prove that from these numbers we can choose quite a lot of pairs $(a,b)$ such that the product $ab$ is the square of an integer; more precisely: from $N$ numbers (with 9 prime factors) you can choose at least $(N-2^9)/2$ such pairs. In our problem there are $N=1985$, so we can identify at least $(1985-512)/2$, that is, at least 737 pairs $(a,b)$. Then to $N=737$ with the integer $\sqrt{ab}$ one can apply the same reasoning again and obtain such a pair $(\sqrt{ab}, \sqrt{cd})$—even $(737-512)/2+1/2=113$ such pairs—that the product $\sqrt{ab} \cdot \sqrt{cd}$ is the square of the integer, that is, $abcd$ is the fourth power of the integer.

It remains to prove the stated fact. According to the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, every natural number can be uniquely represented as a product of prime factors. Product $ab$ of two integers

$$a=2^{\alpha_1}3^{\alpha_2}...23^{\alpha_9} \text{ и } b=2^{\beta_1}3^{\beta_2}...23^{\beta_9}$$

will be a perfect square if and only if the exponents $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ have the same parity (for each $i$ from 1 to 9); in this case, we will call the sets $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,...,\alpha_9)$ and $(\beta_1,\beta_2,...,\beta_9)$ comparable. From $N$ sets of indicators corresponding to $N$ given number (with the set 2,3,...,23), we will select pairs of comparable sets one after another, as long as this is possible. a set without a pair remains at most $2^9$, since there are only $2^9$ pairwise incomparable sets of indicators (each of the 9 places can have either an even or an odd number - a total of $2^9$ options).
