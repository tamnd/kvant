Selected School Problems

Eighth grade
1. Find the smallest value of the expression
$$2x^2 - 2xy + 5y^2 + 2x + 2y.$$
2. Find the amount
$$1 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot 5 + 5 \cdot 7 + 7 \cdot 9 + \dots + 999 \cdot 1001.$$
3. It is known that $n-1$ is divisible by 15, and 1001 is divisible by $n+1$. Find $n$.
4. In the right triangle $ABC$ ($\angle C=90^\circ$) the height $CD$ is drawn. Find: a) the radius of the circle inscribed in triangle $ABC$, if the radius of the circles inscribed in triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$ are equal to $r_1$ and $r_2$; b) the perimeter of triangle $ABC$, if the perimeters of triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$ are equal to $P_1$ and $P_2$.
5. A point is taken on the circle circumscribed about the right triangle $ABC$. Find the sum of squared distances from this point to the vertices of the triangle if the side of the triangle is $a$.

Not only solar possibilities are taken into account, but also the influence of the non-spherical Earth, the attraction of planets, and a small relativistic correction. And yet, Brown’s theory revealed pages of inconsistency and gave, although very small, noticeable discrepancies with observations. Already in the 40s of our century, it was established that the theory is again not to blame here, but that there is uneven rotation of the Earth, and therefore uneven time, which is determined by the rotation of our planet. With the transition to ephemeris time, which flows uniformly (it is determined using atomic clocks), all discrepancies have disappeared.

The development of space probes, including to the Moon, the installation on the surface of the Moon of corner reflectors for laser ranging will significantly increase the accuracy of the lunar theory. Now we can determine the distance to the Moon with an accuracy of 25 cm. The improvement of computers has opened up new possibilities.

A group of French scientists was able to use a computer to test the theories of Delaunay and Hill-Brown and obtained all the necessary quantities with amazing accuracy: up to $1''$ in angles and with “laser” accuracy in distance. To do this, it was necessary to take many thousands of members into the expansions. However, this was not particularly difficult for the computer. The theory created in this way was then successfully applied to the study of the movement of artificial Earth satellites.

Thus ended the centuries-long history of constructing the theory of the motion of the Moon. Now we know what is hidden in such a seemingly simple phrase: “The Moon moves around the Earth in an ellipse...”
