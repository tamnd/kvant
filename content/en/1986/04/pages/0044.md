⟦folio 42⟧

F972. In a high-speed film camera used for filming fast-cleaning processes, the film moves continuously at speed v. To prevent blurring of the image, a tetrahedral glass prism with refractive index n is installed between the film and the lens, which operates at a constant angular velocity (Fig. 1). Neglecting the defocusing of the image, determine at what angular velocity the image on the film is not blurred. Determine the thickness of the prism provided that the image of one car is exposed within $-\alpha_0, \alpha_0$ of its rotation angle, and the minimum size of the various image details is $\delta$.

Consider the point $A$ on the optical axis. Its image $A'$ is also located on the optical axis at the position of the prism, the image at figure 1. When the prism is rotated by an angle $\alpha$, the image $A'$ will be mixed horizontally by $\xi(\alpha)$ from the plane in which the boiling film moves at a speed of $v$, and by an amount $\Delta(\alpha)$ in the direction of film movement (Fig. 2). Horizontal shifting causes the image to become out of focus. According to the conditions, this effect can be neglected.

Let's consider what the change $\Delta(\alpha)$ leads to. Relative to the film, image $A'$ moves at speed $u = \frac{\Delta(\alpha)}{t} - v$. The speed $\omega$ of rotation of the prism is selected such that at small angles $\alpha$ the speed $u$ is close to zero, and the image $A'$ is not mobile relative to the film. This explains the purpose of the rotating prism - it compensates for film changes. The dependence of $\Delta(\alpha)$ on $\alpha$ has the form ($h$ – prism thickness)

$$\Delta(\alpha) = h \sin \omega t \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)$$

(derive this formula yourself, use the law of refraction).

The instantaneous speed of the image $A'$ at the moment when it is on the optical axis is equal to

$$\frac{d\Delta(\alpha)}{dt} \bigg|_{t=0} = \frac{d\Delta(\alpha)}{d\alpha} \cdot \frac{d\alpha}{dt} \bigg|_{\alpha=0} = h \frac{n-1}{n} \omega$$

and according to the conditions

$$h \frac{n-1}{n} \omega = v.$$

(*)

As $\alpha$ increases, the instantaneous speed decreases, and point $A'$ during the rotation of the prism through an angle $\alpha$ ($t=\alpha/\omega$) exposes a segment of length $\Delta = \left(v - \frac{\Delta(\alpha)}{\alpha} \omega\right) \frac{\alpha}{\omega}$ on the film. The image on the film is not blurred if during the exposure time the length of this segment is no more than $\delta$, that is, at $\alpha=\alpha_0 \Delta=\delta$, or $-\alpha_0 h \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right) + h \frac{n-1}{n} \alpha_0=\delta$.

From here comes $h$:

$$h = \frac{\delta}{\alpha_0} \left(\frac{n-1}{n} - \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)\right)^{-1}$$

Taking this into account (*), the prism image on the film is not blurred:

$$\omega = \frac{v n}{n-1} \cdot \frac{\alpha_0}{\delta} \left(\frac{n-1}{n} - \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)\right).$$
