Mathematics
and non-mathematics

Among all sciences, mathematics occupies a modern, special place that belongs only to it alone. Natural sciences - physics, chemistry, astronomy, biology, geology, mineralogy,... - study the world in which we live; The humanities - literature, literary criticism, legal (law), history, ethics, natural science, sociology,... - study human society in its various manifestations, that is, also something that really exists, amenable to observation and even experiment. In contrast to this, mathematics uses some astronomical constructions, such as the number $\sqrt{2}$ or (also not actually existing in nature!) a line that has no width, but only length; The objects of mathematics can most accurately be defined by specifying, as, say, in geometry, a complete list of axioms that these objects must satisfy. At the same time, the outstanding role of mathematics is associated with its application to both the natural and human sciences, with the possibility of “mathematical modeling” of objects in the real world. In mathematical modeling, such a relationship is made between these objects and certain mathematical concepts in which the relationships between these concepts quite accurately convey the relationships between the real “things” that interest us. This makes it possible to study these “things” using pure mathematical reasoning. We will illustrate this general scheme for the use of mathematics here using the example of studying the phenomena of heredity, or more precisely, population genetics.
