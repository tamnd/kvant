F970. In a thin-walled opaque spherical region there is a ball whose radius is several times smaller than the radius of the shell. Suggest a way to determine the density of the substance from which the ball is made. The mass of the shell is unknown.

Let us measure the weight of the sphere with the ball $P = (M + m)g$, where $M$ and $m$ are the masses of the sphere and the corresponding ball. We apply a force $F$ to the sphere (we measure the force), directed vertically downwards, and at moment $t = 0$ we remove the support from under the sphere. The sphere begins to move downward with acceleration

$$g + a = \frac{Mg + F}{M}.$$

Mass $m$ is not included in the formula, since the ball at moment $t = 0$ breaks away from the sphere and with acceleration $a$ (relative to the sphere) moves upward. Having measured the acceleration $a$, we find the mass of the sphere $M = F/a$. The acceleration measurement $a$ must be completed before the ball hits the sphere at its highest point. Having noted this moment in time (by the sound of the impact or by the jump in the shown diameter meter measuring the force $F$), we calculate the radius $r$ of the ball. From $S = a(\Delta t)^2/2$, where $\Delta t$ is the time of movement of the ball, we find:

$$r = R - \frac{a(\Delta t)^2}{4}.$$

Here $R$ is the radius of the sphere, which can be measured directly, and let $S = 2(R - r)$. Now we know both the radius of the ball and its mass $\left(m = \frac{P}{g} - \frac{F}{a}\right)$, which allows us to solve the problem.

S. A. Khorozov

F971. In a magnetic field, the induction $\vec{B}$ of which is vertical, a metal rod is drawn near the vertical axis as shown in figure. The conditional speed of the doctor is $\omega$, $R$ and $\alpha$ are given. Find the potential difference at the ends of the rod.

To solve, we use the expression for the EMF in the form

$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}.$$

First of all, we note that the emf will be constant at any time, since the rod is always in the same conditions. Therefore, we have the right to consider its full turn:

$$-\Delta\Phi = \pi R^2 B,$$

$$\Delta t = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$$

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{\pi R^2 B}{2\pi} \omega = \frac{1}{2} BR^2 \omega,$$

that is, it depends only on $R$ and not on $\alpha$.

L. G. Markovich
