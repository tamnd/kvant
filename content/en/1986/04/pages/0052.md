2V₀ (writing 2, a). Draw the corresponding process on p-T and V-T diagrams.

Since the process is depicted on a p-V diagram as a straight line segment, pressure and volume are related by the relation p=αV+β, where α and β are constants. To determine them, take into account the relationship between the parameters p and V in states 1 and 2, respectively:

$$p_0 = \alpha V_0 + \beta, \frac{p_0}{2} = 2\alpha V_0 + \beta.$$

From here

$$\alpha = -\frac{p_0}{2V_0}, \beta = \frac{3}{2}p_0.$$

Thus, the equation of the thermal process in variables p, V has the form

$$p = -p_0 \frac{V}{2V_0} + \frac{3}{2}p_0.$$

Taking into account the Mendeleev-Clapeyron equation, written in the form pV=AT (A=mR/M=const), we find the process equation in the variables p, T:

$$\left( \frac{3}{2}p_0 - p \right) p = \frac{Ap_0}{2V_0} T$$

and variables V, T:

$$V \left( 3 - \frac{V}{V_0} \right) = \frac{2A}{p_0} T.$$

Since the product pV for states 1 and 2 is the same, the final and initial temperatures coincide and are equal to T₀=p₀V₀/A. In variables p,

Problem 3. How does the temperature of the gas change during the thermal process 1-2-3-4-1 (Fig. 3)?

In section 1-2, the pressure increases at a constant volume, which means (in accordance with Charles’s law), the temperature also increases. In section 2-3, the temperature also increases, since the volume of gas increases at constant pressure (Geylossk’s law). Similar reasoning allows us to conclude that in areas 3-4 and 4-1 the temperature drops. The answer can also be easily obtained by checking the corresponding images on the p-V diagram.

When solving this problem, it is assumed that the mass of the gas remained constant all the time. Answer this question yourself: how should the mass of the gas change so that, at a constant temperature, the thermal process on the p-V diagram is depicted in the same way as on figure 3?
