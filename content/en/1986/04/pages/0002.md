This page shows in different views a saddle-shaped surface known by mathematicians as a hyperbolic parabolon. It can be specified in the spatial coordinate system Oxyz of the equations z=xy - then it looked as shown in figure at the top left. This figure is noticeable from the wonderful book by D. Hilbert and S. Kohn Vossen “Visual Geometry” (M.: “Nauka”, 1981, p. 23), which we recommend purchasing for all geometry lovers.

Please note, look at the portrait of the surface z=xy, for example, lying on it (example on the coordinate axes 0x and 0y). It can be proven that through each surface there are exactly two straight lines containing the faces in it. Moreover, the entire surface is formed by an infinite family of lines connecting two fixed crossing lines. There are two such families; the straight lines composing them are called rectilinear generators; they can be seen at figure at the top right.

One hyperbolic parabolon is not only entertainment for geometry lovers. It is very often used in architecture to create roofs and ceilings: the presence of rectilinear generatrices makes it possible to create a red, non-volumetric curved surface from common surface (attached) elements. A building with such a roof is shown at figure down. The building of the Olympic swimming pool in Moscow has the same roof.

The hyperbolic parabolon has other, lesser-known applications. For example, in biology. So, figure at the top right from N. M. Aglom’s article on population genetics from this issue «Kvant».
