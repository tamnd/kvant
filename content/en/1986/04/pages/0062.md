Written exam
Option 1
1. Let's introduce the coordinate system as shown in figure 4. Let's introduce the notation: $v_0$ - initial bullet speed; $\alpha$ — the angle of inclination to the horizon at which the bullets fly out; $B$ is the point at which the ball is located when passing through the equilibrium position; $C$ and $D$ are the points of maximum distance of the ball from the equilibrium position.
Let a bullet fly out from point $O$ at time $t_0=0$ and hit point $B$ at time $t$. The bullet moves along a parabola and hits point $B$ if the following condition is met:
$$x(t)=L=v_0\cos \alpha \cdot t,$$
$$y(t)=-l=v_0\sin \alpha \cdot t-gt^2/2.$$
In order for the ball with the stuck bullet to compact downwards, it is necessary that immediately the thread is broken, the horizontal component of the momentum of the ball with the bullet is equal to zero. From the law of conservation of momentum it follows that in this case
$$mv_0\cos \alpha-Mv=0,$$
where $v=\omega A=\sqrt{g/l}A$ is the absolute velocity of the ball at point $B$. The horizontal component of the bullet speed and the ball speed at point $B$ are directed towards each other when the condition is met
$$t=n\frac{T}{4},$$
where $T=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}$, $n=1, 5, 9, ...$, if at moment $t_0=0$ the ball is at point $C$ and $n=3, 7, 11, ...$, if at the initial moment the ball is at point $D$.
From the obtained expressions follows a system of three equations with the third unknowns $v_0$, $\alpha$, $t$:
$$\begin{cases} v_0\cos \alpha \cdot t=L, \\ gt^2-2v_0\sin \alpha \cdot t-2l=0, \\ \frac{m}{M}=\sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} \frac{A}{v_0\cos \alpha} \end{cases}$$
and the condition for “quantizing” time
$$t=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} n.$$
The solution of the system gives:
$$v_0=\sqrt{gl\left(\frac{M}{m}\frac{A}{l}\right)^2+\left(\frac{M}{m}\frac{A}{L}\right)^2+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{m}{M}\frac{L}{A}\right)^2-1},$$
$$\alpha=\arctg\left(\frac{l}{L}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{L}{A}\frac{m}{M}\right)^2-1\right)\right),$$
then the “quantization” condition takes the form
$$\frac{m}{M}\frac{L}{A}=\frac{\pi}{2} n.$$
