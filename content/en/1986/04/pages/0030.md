One day, neighbors asked me to help rearrange the furniture in their apartment. The young housewife began to cheerfully lead the “team of workers”, consisting of her husband, father and me.

“Let’s put a sideboard against this wall, and next to it a TV,” Natasha commanded. We piled on the sideboard together - dishes were sent clanging out of it. After numerous “One, two, take it,” “Come on, come on,” the sideboard was installed in the indicated place. However, the bedside table with the TV was not included in the space intended for it. We moved the sideboard to the side to put a bedside table with a TV, but as a result the sideboard blocked the code in the door.

It took us three hours to move the furniture from room to room. At the same time, situations arose when, for example, in order to put a piano in the office, it was necessary to take out the coffee table and all the chairs, send them to the kitchen and return them back only after installing the piano. A joyful readiness to “move over the mountains” replaced extreme determination. The main character of our jokes was Sisyphus*). At some point the towers suddenly took their original positions, and all the men exclaimed to each other. “That’s the best way!”

While waiting for the car with the cabinet, I wondered if it was possible to rearrange something before the cabinet was nailed down. I drew a diagram (Fig. 1), from which I realized that operations 1 and 3 do not depend on the others. After thinking a little, I realized that the sequence of actions can be changed so that not only operations 1 and 3, but also operations 6, 7, 8 can be completed before the cabinet arrives. Thus, figure helped me find a rational permutation.
