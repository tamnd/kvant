The Phobos project intends to obtain the altitude distribution of electron concentrations in the upper layers of the ionosphere of Mars. The study of the ionosphere is important in itself, since its properties largely determine the “life” of the planet. In addition, knowledge of the “structure” of the planet’s ionosphere will help answer the question: does Mars have its own magnetic field?

Since the discovery of the solar wind - streams of plasma emitted by the Sun into interplanetary space - it has become clear that planets do not exist in the void of space.

The solar wind blowing across the planets is an extremely rarefied medium containing only a few particles or several ten particles per 1 cm$^3$. However, if the planet does not have a screen—its own magnetic field—the two plasmas—solar and planetary (ionosphere)—intensely interact with each other. As a result of this interaction, a sharp upper boundary of the ionosphere is formed, separating two plasmas - ionospheric and solar. The ionosphere plasma under the “pressure” of the solar wind turns out to be pressed from the side of the Sun. This is exactly what is happening on Venus, which does not have its own magnetic field.

A completely different picture is observed near the Earth. The Earth has a protective shield - its own magnetic field, which “stops” the flow of solar wind far from the earth’s surface. The Earth's ionosphere is thus protected from the solar wind and behaves largely independent of it.

What's happening on Mars? Mars has its own magnetic field, but it is very weak, ten thousand times weaker than that of the Earth. But still it exists! (Venus is the only non-magnetic planet studied so far). However, it is unclear whether the Martian magnetic field serves as the same shield for the planet as the Earth's field for the Earth. If so, then the upper ionosphere of Mars should behave in the same way (or in a similar way) as the earth’s. If not, if the magnetic field of Mars is so small that the solar wind does not “overwhelm” its presence, then the ionosphere of Mars should be similar to that of Venus.

The answer to this and many other questions has been studied by experts on Mars and Phobos.
