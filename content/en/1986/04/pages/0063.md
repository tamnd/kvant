2. Let us denote by $T_0$ the initial temperature of the gas, and by $T$ the final temperature of the gas in the lower part of the vessel. Let $M$ be the molar mass of the gas, $m$ the mass of the gas in each part of the vessel.
Let us write the equation of state of an ideal gas:

$$p_B V_B = \frac{m}{M} RT_0,$$

$$p_H V_H = \frac{m}{M} RT_0,$$

$$p'_B V'_B = \frac{m}{M} RT_0,$$

$$p'_H V'_H = \frac{m}{M} RT,$$

where the indices “B” and “H” refer to the upper and lower parts of the corresponding vessel; the “stroke” denotes the values ​​​​corresponding to the final state of the gas; $V_B/V_H = 3$, $V'_B/V'_H = 4$. The piston equilibrium conditions have the form:

$$p_B + p_0 = p_H,$$

$$p'_B + p_0 = p'_H,$$

where $p_0 = F/S$, $F$ is the force of gravity of the piston, $S$ is the area of ​​the piston.

From the obtained equalities we find

$$\frac{T}{T_0} = \frac{47}{60}.$$

$$I_H = \frac{P}{U}.$$

According to Ohm's law

$$I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{r} + \frac{R_H R_{III}}{R_H + R_{III}},$$

where $n \geqslant 1$ is a natural number. It's obvious that

$$I = I_H + I_{III} = \frac{U}{R_H R_{III}} + \frac{R_H R_{III}}{R_H + R_{III}},$$
