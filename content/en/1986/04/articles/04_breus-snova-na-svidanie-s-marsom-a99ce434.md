Again to witness with Mars

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
T. K. BREUS

Venus and Mars are the closest neighbors of the Earth. Together with Mercury, they form the so-called terrestrial group of planets, similar in their properties (mass, size, composition) and, apparently, in origin and evolution.

Please, no other planet in the solar system has connected humans with so many legends, exotic hypotheses and bold fantasies as with the planet Mars. Because of its slightly reddish color, Mars seemed ominous to our subject. Actually, the very name of the planet and its satellites is connected with this: translated from Greek, “Mars” means rich, “Phobos” and “Deimos” mean fear and horror. Isn’t it surprising that the two satellites of Mars predicted by the fabulous Laputian astronomers in J. Swift’s famous “Gulliver’s Travels” were actually discovered a century and a half later by the American astronomer A. Hall! And the “Martian canals” discovered by the Italian astronomer G. Schipaprelli, which gave rise to optimistic hopes for the existence of “brothers in mind” on Mars, turned out to be natural geological structures - the russ of “dried up rivers”!

Now, when the number of space stations sent to Mars is in the tens, it must be admitted that dreamers and optimists are disappointed; we have learned a lot from specialization, but the mysteries and hypotheses have not diminished, although they are no longer as exotic as before. The first gray explorations of Mars by Soviet automatic international Mars stations were carried out in 1971. And now the Soviet Union is preparing a new space project for the exploration of Mars and its two satellites. This project was called "Phobos". The USSR Academy of Sciences invited scientific organizations of socialist countries, as well as Germany, Austria, Finland, Sweden and the European Space Agency, to participate in the project.

Two spacecraft will be launched from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in July 1988 and will explore the Sun and the properties of the interplanetary medium for two hundred days on the way to Mars. Then the devices will become artificial satellites of Mars and will teach them to “live” in the circular orbit in which its natural satellite Phobos revolves around Mars. One of the devices will learn to “hunt” for Phobos, gradually approaching it in order to examine it from “different points of view.” The second device will either follow the first or head towards Deimos.

Both satellites of Mars appear to be asteroid bodies captured by Mars. Asterons, like comets, have not undergone significant geological changes since the formation of the solar system. For this reason, the study of asroids and comets makes it possible to judge the conditions for the formation of solar system bodies and their subsequent evolution. And this is very important for understanding the origin and evolution of the Earth, the problems of natural life.

The first step on this path has already been taken - the Vega spacecraft encountered Halley's Comet. The Phobos project will apparently provide direct acquaintance with the asteroid body—Phobos.

There is a lot to be said about the interesting tasks of the Phobos project. From the orbit of an artificial satellite of Mars, research will be carried out on the planet itself - its topography, surface, atmosphere, etc. There are also tasks to study the properties of Mars as a planet as a whole. One such experiment, called Plasma Conditions, will explore the inosphere of Mars.

Like Earth, the inosphere of Mars consists of plasma containing electrons and ions. In the inospheres of the Earth and other planets, the concentration of pollution particles first increases -

increases with increasing height, reaching a maximum at a certain height, and then begins to decrease.

The Phobos project intends to obtain the altitude distribution of electron concentrations in the upper layers of the ionosphere of Mars. The study of the ionosphere is important in itself, since its properties largely determine the “life” of the planet. In addition, knowledge of the “structure” of the planet’s ionosphere will help answer the question: does Mars have its own magnetic field?

Since the discovery of the solar wind - streams of plasma emitted by the Sun into interplanetary space - it has become clear that planets do not exist in the void of space.

The solar wind blowing across the planets is an extremely rarefied medium containing only a few particles or several ten particles per 1 cm$^3$. However, if the planet does not have a screen—its own magnetic field—the two plasmas—solar and planetary (ionosphere)—intensely interact with each other. As a result of this interaction, a sharp upper boundary of the ionosphere is formed, separating two plasmas - ionospheric and solar. The ionosphere plasma under the “pressure” of the solar wind turns out to be pressed from the side of the Sun. This is exactly what is happening on Venus, which does not have its own magnetic field.

A completely different picture is observed near the Earth. The Earth has a protective shield - its own magnetic field, which “stops” the flow of solar wind far from the earth’s surface. The Earth's ionosphere is thus protected from the solar wind and behaves largely independent of it.

What's happening on Mars? Mars has its own magnetic field, but it is very weak, ten thousand times weaker than that of the Earth. But still it exists! (Venus is the only non-magnetic planet studied so far). However, it is unclear whether the Martian magnetic field serves as the same shield for the planet as the Earth's field for the Earth. If so, then the upper ionosphere of Mars should behave in the same way (or in a similar way) as the earth’s. If not, if the magnetic field of Mars is so small that the solar wind does not “overwhelm” its presence, then the ionosphere of Mars should be similar to that of Venus.

The answer to this and many other questions has been studied by experts on Mars and Phobos.
