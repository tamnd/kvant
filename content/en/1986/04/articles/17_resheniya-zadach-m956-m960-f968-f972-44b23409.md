M956. Four circles of the same radius are drawn on the plane so that three of them pass through one point A, and three through point B. Prove that the four points of their pairwise intersection, different from A and B, are the vertices of a parallelogram.

M957. Prove that from 1985 different natural numbers, all of whose prime factors are contained among the 9 prime numbers 2,3,...,23, you can choose four numbers whose product is the fourth power of an integer.

M956—M960; F968—F972
Let us present one of many solutions to the problem.
Let us denote the centers of the circles by $O_1, O_2, O_3, O_4$, and the points of their pairwise intersection by $K, L, M, N$ (see figure). Since the circles are equal, the point $P$ of intersection of the common chord $AK$ of circles $O_1$ and $O_2$ with their linear centers $O_1O_2$ divides both segments $AK$ and $O_1O_2$ in half. Similarly, the segments $O_3O_2$ and $AL$ have a common midpoint $Q$. Therefore, the segment $PQ$ is the midline of two triangles - $O_1O_2O_3$ and $AKL$, that is, $\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=2\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{KL}$. It is proved in the same way that $\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=\overrightarrow{NM}$, and from the equality $\overrightarrow{KL}=\overrightarrow{O_1O_3}=\overrightarrow{NM}$ it follows that $KLMN$ is a parallelogram.

Prove on your own that $A$ and $B$ are the points of intersection of the altitudes of triangles $BKL$ and $AMN$; this fact can be used for another solution to the problem (cf. the final remark in the solution to problem M951 in the previous issue «Kvant»).

Let us prove that from these numbers we can choose quite a lot of pairs $(a,b)$ such that the product $ab$ is the square of an integer; more precisely: from $N$ numbers (with 9 prime factors) you can choose at least $(N-2^9)/2$ such pairs. In our problem there are $N=1985$, so we can identify at least $(1985-512)/2$, that is, at least 737 pairs $(a,b)$. Then to $N=737$ with the integer $\sqrt{ab}$ one can apply the same reasoning again and obtain such a pair $(\sqrt{ab}, \sqrt{cd})$—even $(737-512)/2+1/2=113$ such pairs—that the product $\sqrt{ab} \cdot \sqrt{cd}$ is the square of the integer, that is, $abcd$ is the fourth power of the integer.

It remains to prove the stated fact. According to the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, every natural number can be uniquely represented as a product of prime factors. Product $ab$ of two integers

$$a=2^{\alpha_1}3^{\alpha_2}...23^{\alpha_9} \text{ и } b=2^{\beta_1}3^{\beta_2}...23^{\beta_9}$$

will be a perfect square if and only if the exponents $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ have the same parity (for each $i$ from 1 to 9); in this case, we will call the sets $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,...,\alpha_9)$ and $(\beta_1,\beta_2,...,\beta_9)$ comparable. From $N$ sets of indicators corresponding to $N$ given number (with the set 2,3,...,23), we will select pairs of comparable sets one after another, as long as this is possible. a set without a pair remains at most $2^9$, since there are only $2^9$ pairwise incomparable sets of indicators (each of the 9 places can have either an even or an odd number - a total of $2^9$ options).

**M959**. In the country, aviation connections have been established between certain cities. Prove that it is possible to close no more than 1/(k - 1)th part of the avatoria in such a way that among any k cities there are two that are not contiguous to each other if

To do this, note that the number $n$ of domestic airlines (for the chosen “minimum” partition) leaving any city $A$ is not greater than the number $n_i$ of airlines leading from this city to any group of cities $G_i$ ($i=1,...,k-1$): if it turned out that $n > n_i$, we would transfer city $A$ to the group $G_i$ and thereby reduce the number of domestic airlines by $n - n_i$. Therefore, the total number of airlines leaving the city $A$ is at least $(k-1)n$. Summing up such inequalities for all cities and taking into account that each airline connects two cities, we obtain that $2N \geq 2(k-1)N_{\text{вн}}$. Let us now close all domestic airlines of our “minimum” partition. Then, out of any $k$ cities, at least two will fall into one group and will not be connected to each other, and no more than $N/(k-1)$ airlines will be closed.

**M960. If the difference between the cubes of two consecutive natural numbers is the square of some natural number $n$, then the number $n$ is represented as the sum of the squares of two consecutive natural numbers.**

a) Prove this statement.
b) Here is one example of such numbers: $8^3 - 7^3 = (2^2 + 3^2)^2$; Give at least one more example.
b) Prove that there are infinitely many of them.

a) Let $(m+1)^3 - m^3 = n^2$. Opening the parentheses and multiplying both sides of the equality by 4, we obtain that $3 \cdot 4^2 + 3 \cdot 4m + 4 = 4n^2$ or $3(2m+1)^2 = (2n+1)(2n-1)$. (1)

Since the numbers $2n+1$ and $2n-1$ are coprime, equality (1) is possible only when one of these numbers is a natural square and the other is a triple square. But the number $n$ is odd ($n^2$ is the difference between numbers of different parities), so $2n+1$ gives remainder 3 when divided by 4, and the square of an integer is either divisible by 4 or leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4; therefore, the perfect square is $2n-1$; that is, $2n-1 = (2l+1)^2$ or $n = 2l^2 + 2l + 1 = l^2 + (l+1)^2$.

F969. Two cans made of the same material have the same geometric shape, and all the linear dimensions of one can are one time smaller than the other (see figure). A hole was made in two of each can. The jars are simultaneously lowered into the water. Which bank will please you most? How many times?

Due to the fact that the jars that are immersed in water are made of the same materials, at first the depth of immersion of each of them is linearly related to the size. That is, if the sizes of the cans differ by $k$ times $l_2=kl_1$, then the distances from the water surface to the surface $h_2=kh_1$ differ by $k$ times.

Since the pressure drop on both sides of the connection depends on the immersion depth $\Delta p=\rho_{\text{воды}}gh$, and the speed of water filling the jar through the connection is related to the pressure difference - $\Delta p=\frac{1}{2}q_{\text{воды}}v^2$, then

$v^2=2gh$.

In turn, the speed determines the time $t$, after which the jar will fill with water and move to the bottom.

F970. In a thin-walled opaque spherical region there is a ball whose radius is several times smaller than the radius of the shell. Suggest a way to determine the density of the substance from which the ball is made. The mass of the shell is unknown.

Let us measure the weight of the sphere with the ball $P = (M + m)g$, where $M$ and $m$ are the masses of the sphere and the corresponding ball. We apply a force $F$ to the sphere (we measure the force), directed vertically downwards, and at moment $t = 0$ we remove the support from under the sphere. The sphere begins to move downward with acceleration

$$g + a = \frac{Mg + F}{M}.$$

Mass $m$ is not included in the formula, since the ball at moment $t = 0$ breaks away from the sphere and with acceleration $a$ (relative to the sphere) moves upward. Having measured the acceleration $a$, we find the mass of the sphere $M = F/a$. The acceleration measurement $a$ must be completed before the ball hits the sphere at its highest point. Having noted this moment in time (by the sound of the impact or by the jump in the shown diameter meter measuring the force $F$), we calculate the radius $r$ of the ball. From $S = a(\Delta t)^2/2$, where $\Delta t$ is the time of movement of the ball, we find:

$$r = R - \frac{a(\Delta t)^2}{4}.$$

Here $R$ is the radius of the sphere, which can be measured directly, and let $S = 2(R - r)$. Now we know both the radius of the ball and its mass $\left(m = \frac{P}{g} - \frac{F}{a}\right)$, which allows us to solve the problem.

F971. In a magnetic field, the induction $\vec{B}$ of which is vertical, a metal rod is drawn near the vertical axis as shown in figure. The conditional speed of the doctor is $\omega$, $R$ and $\alpha$ are given. Find the potential difference at the ends of the rod.

To solve, we use the expression for the EMF in the form

$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}.$$

First of all, we note that the emf will be constant at any time, since the rod is always in the same conditions. Therefore, we have the right to consider its full turn:

$$-\Delta\Phi = \pi R^2 B,$$

$$\Delta t = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$$

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{\pi R^2 B}{2\pi} \omega = \frac{1}{2} BR^2 \omega,$$

that is, it depends only on $R$ and not on $\alpha$.

F972. In a high-speed film camera used for filming fast-cleaning processes, the film moves continuously at speed v. To prevent blurring of the image, a tetrahedral glass prism with refractive index n is installed between the film and the lens, which operates at a constant angular velocity (Fig. 1). Neglecting the defocusing of the image, determine at what angular velocity the image on the film is not blurred. Determine the thickness of the prism provided that the image of one car is exposed within $-\alpha_0, \alpha_0$ of its rotation angle, and the minimum size of the various image details is $\delta$.

Consider the point $A$ on the optical axis. Its image $A'$ is also located on the optical axis at the position of the prism, the image at figure 1. When the prism is rotated by an angle $\alpha$, the image $A'$ will be mixed horizontally by $\xi(\alpha)$ from the plane in which the boiling film moves at a speed of $v$, and by an amount $\Delta(\alpha)$ in the direction of film movement (Fig. 2). Horizontal shifting causes the image to become out of focus. According to the conditions, this effect can be neglected.

Let's consider what the change $\Delta(\alpha)$ leads to. Relative to the film, image $A'$ moves at speed $u = \frac{\Delta(\alpha)}{t} - v$. The speed $\omega$ of rotation of the prism is selected such that at small angles $\alpha$ the speed $u$ is close to zero, and the image $A'$ is not mobile relative to the film. This explains the purpose of the rotating prism - it compensates for film changes. The dependence of $\Delta(\alpha)$ on $\alpha$ has the form ($h$ – prism thickness)

$$\Delta(\alpha) = h \sin \omega t \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)$$

(derive this formula yourself, use the law of refraction).

The instantaneous speed of the image $A'$ at the moment when it is on the optical axis is equal to

$$\frac{d\Delta(\alpha)}{dt} \bigg|_{t=0} = \frac{d\Delta(\alpha)}{d\alpha} \cdot \frac{d\alpha}{dt} \bigg|_{\alpha=0} = h \frac{n-1}{n} \omega$$

and according to the conditions

$$h \frac{n-1}{n} \omega = v.$$

(*)

As $\alpha$ increases, the instantaneous speed decreases, and point $A'$ during the rotation of the prism through an angle $\alpha$ ($t=\alpha/\omega$) exposes a segment of length $\Delta = \left(v - \frac{\Delta(\alpha)}{\alpha} \omega\right) \frac{\alpha}{\omega}$ on the film. The image on the film is not blurred if during the exposure time the length of this segment is no more than $\delta$, that is, at $\alpha=\alpha_0 \Delta=\delta$, or $-\alpha_0 h \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right) + h \frac{n-1}{n} \alpha_0=\delta$.

From here comes $h$:

$$h = \frac{\delta}{\alpha_0} \left(\frac{n-1}{n} - \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)\right)^{-1}$$

Taking this into account (*), the prism image on the film is not blurred:

$$\omega = \frac{v n}{n-1} \cdot \frac{\alpha_0}{\delta} \left(\frac{n-1}{n} - \sin \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha_0}{\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2 \alpha}}\right)\right).$$
