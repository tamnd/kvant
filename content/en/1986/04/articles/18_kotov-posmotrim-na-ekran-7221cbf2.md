Let's watch it on the screen

Y. V. KOTOV

Perhaps the most popular device in modern computers, especially among non-professionals, has become cathode-ray screens, which give the machine some similarity with televisions. Additional with a keyboard and such devices as “light pens”, “visors” and “mice”, they oblige input into a computer, reading and editing information, and managing the computing process. Several such devices will be connected to large computers, which allows a large number of users to work simultaneously. Next, small desktop computers (so-called personal computers) are equipped with screens or connected to ordinary household televisions.

Unfortunately, experts in the Russian language could not offer a term with a Russian root for such screens with a keyboard, and they are called the English word “ispela”, sometimes “terminal”. Displays are alphanumeric and graphic. The first can show on their screens only letters, numbers, signs and what they consist of - texts, programs, numerical data. Graphic displays are universal in the sense that they display both graphic information (diagrams, graphs, drawings) and alphanumeric information. Modern graphic displays are divided into two large groups - vector and raster. If in vector devices the electron beam sequentially extracts programmed image elements - segments, symbols and sometimes arcs - then in raster devices it, like in a TV, “shades” the screen with successive lines, while changing its brightness. On the simplest raster screens, the image is made up of many dots located in a row, and if there are not enough of these rows, for example, 200, 300, the image quality is low. More advanced raster screens also make it possible to obtain color images. There are average ones, and those where the quality of the “picture” is noticeably better than on a household TV, since the image is decomposed into 1000 or more lines, and there is also no inevitable interference coming from the air.

Graphic screens make communication with the machine especially visual and, with appropriate programs, accessible to all ages. To increase demand for “household” personal computers, an entire “computer games” industry is developing in the West. Computer games, of course, are a good thing; they develop the eye, quick reactions, and communication skills; they are useful for getting in-game moments. We're not talking about computer-aided design, scientific research and other server-side activities.

The purpose of this note is to show Kwaンタ readers who do not have access to such equipment how a display screen will be implemented in the educational process.

The photographs show examples of images obtained on the screen of the graphic terminal “EC-7064” connected to an EC series computer at the Moscow Architectural Institute and the Moscow Institute of Radio Engineering, Electronics and Automation.

In the first photo we see - on the left side of the screen - an ornament typed with a light pen from standard elements drawn by programming on the right side of the screen. At the initial moment, only dots are lit on the left side of the screen; nodes of a square grid of 5x5 cells are placed. The student selects a typical figure from the “menu” that he likes with a pen and then “plows” it into the desired grid cell, indicating with the pen two diagonally located nodes. In this case, you can “solder” the figure not only in the original orientation, but also with a rotation at an angle that is a multiple of a straight line. You can “draw” two or three different figures into one cell; using special terminal buttons you can erase incorrectly entered ones

In the third photo we see an image of a simple mechanism (similar to a robotic manipulator), the grip of which is to grasp or release a square object. Using the keyboard buttons, you control the movement of the mechanism and the operation of the gripper. With one press of the corresponding button, you can turn one or the other lever in one direction or another in a “large”, “medium” or “small” step. The goal is to place several objects next to each other or on top of each other in as few steps as possible. To help you monitor your student's work, the capture checkpoint leaves a mark on the screen. It is interesting that despite the very simplicity of the task, even professors who sit down at the screen for the first time do not immediately master the art of controlling the robot. And there are more mechanisms modeled in space!

Now try to imagine how to define the shapes shown in the next two photographs in a special graphic language. The “wheel” figure consists of segments, circles, and others. But it also uses repetition (“cycle”) and coordinate transformations (rotation around a given point). To depict a part, you need numbers and special codes to set the dimensions and desired position of its constituent parts - such as parallelepids, cones are also recognized, and in addition, set and clarify the angle from which the part is visible (in other words, set an axonometric or perspective projection).

In the sixth photograph we see an image of intersecting bodies - in this case, a cone and an ellipson. The construction and study of lines of intersection of a surface is one of the traditional topics of the institute course in descriptive geometry. Many generations of students have been constructing such lines using compasses and rulers. Now, using the keyboard keys, the object can be viewed from different angles and with a change in scale. You can also change the position or size of specified bodies. In the photograph we see that the intersection line consists of two oval parts. Can you tell me how to move the cone so that the intersection line contains only one part?

The seventh photo shows graphs of various sinusoids. Each sine sinusid is given by several numbers, among which are the coefficients included in its equation. By changing one or another coefficient, the student can see how it affects the shape of the curve. Of course, the sinusoid is the simplest example, but even with a slight condition of the analytical representation of the function, a mathematician will not always say how the shape of the graph changes when different quantities change. Here a new, modified computer line can be obtained in a few seconds!

The eighth photograph shows an image of a typical part being designed. Its parameters are given in the form of several numbers in two lines at the bottom of the screen. In the process of automated design of such parts, designers check on the screen what the parts being created and their working drawings will look like. Here we see a gear wheel. If some of its parameters are obtained as a result of calculations of machine kinematics and strength calculations, then others (for example, the width of the boss) are determined structurally. The designer also checks whether the program has placed the dimensions and designations correctly; if necessary, he can make adjustments according to the rules predefined in the program. The image is finally displayed on the plotter.

Finally, in the ninth photograph there is a more complex object drawn by a plotter. By the way, the power of machines, which have recently been considered large, is no longer enough to quickly form and display images of such complexity on the screen. You have to wait in front of a dark screen for a minute or two... and hope for further rapid progress of the electronics. Nevertheless, even now the value of such images is very high. By changing one or more numbers in the description of an object, you can get an option for comparison that differs in composition and proportions. It is not difficult to view an object from different angles and distances. The selected “frames” are transferred to paper with greater accuracy and detail, and the resulting machine drawings can be hand-painted (painted, detailed) by artists and architects.
