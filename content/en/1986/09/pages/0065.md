2. Let us denote the vectors $\vec{AB}, \vec{AC}, \vec{AD}$ by $\vec{b}, \vec{c}, \vec{d}$. Then we prove the inequality takes the form
$$\vec{c}^2 + (\vec{d} - \vec{b})^2 + \vec{d}^2 + (\vec{c} - \vec{b})^2 \geq \vec{b}^2 + (\vec{d} - \vec{d})^2$$
after opening the brackets and rearranging the terms we get
$$(\vec{c} + \vec{d} - \vec{b})^2 \geq 0.$$

3. Obviously, $a = b = c = 0$ is the solution. Let us show that there are no other solutions. The square of any number has a remainder of 0 or 1 division by 4. From our equality it therefore follows that the numbers $a^2, b^2, c^2$ are divisible by 4; This means that the numbers $a, b, c$ are divisible by 2. Let us put $a = 2a_1, b = 2b_1; c = 2c_1$. We have
$$a_1^2 + b_1^2 + c_1^2 = 4a_1^2 b_1^2.$$
Now we can see that the numbers $a_1^2, b_1^2, c_1^2$ are divisible by 4; we put $a_1 = 2a_2, b_1 = 2b_2, c_1 = 2c_2$, we get
$$a_2^2 + b_2^2 + c_2^2 = 16a_2^2 b_2^2.$$
Continuing such reasoning, we find that $a, b$ and $c$ are divisible by $2^n$ for any $n$.

This sum is equal to $\frac{n}{2} \left( \frac{n}{2} - 1 \right)$ for even $n$ and $\left( \frac{n-1}{2} \right)^2$ for odd $n$. This is the answer, since it is precisely the three-lenth arithmetic progressions contained in the sequence 1, 2, 3, ..., $n$.
