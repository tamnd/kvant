Euler and Lagrange are perceived today as the greatest mathematicians of the 18th century, teacher and student, whose talents amazingly complemented each other. Euler, who sought to look as far forward as possible, spoke about things for which there is not yet a suitable language, to leave for posterity problems that would serve as guidelines, and Lagrange, who in everything reached to the depth of structures, sought to create a picture devoid of blind spots, to pass on to subsequent generations the language and methods that would eventually be delivered to solve new problems.

Our cover

The second cover page shows three photographs of folded models made from flat sheets of avatman without tears and glued together. We will call such structures structural spaces with guiding surfaces. They can be made from flat sheet material (avatman, thin cardboard, metal or plastic) by folding it along lines in the proper direction according to patterns, two of which are shown at figure, and then bending the sheet with the folds to give it the overall shape of a cylinder. For the three models shown, the “guide surface” is a cylinder, but similar structural spaces can be constructed with other guide surfaces, for example, finite, hyperbolic and elliptical.

As the basis of a network of lines along which folds are made, using elements of various parquets (see «Kvant», 1979, No. 2, p. 9) and gratings on the plane. At the same time, not only examples or broken lines are tried on here, but also curved lines of folds, which gives the resulting models greater aesthetic expressiveness.

In addition to natural spaces, structural spaces have various interselial mechanical properties (static and dynamic), due to which they are promising for the search for new architecture and structural forms.

A. I. Volkov
