“Today,” Ivan Petrovich began the math club lesson, “our lesson is devoted to one problem.”
- So difficult? - we were surprised.
- No. Especially this problem is that it will help us solve other problems. Here are its terms:

Problem 1. In a trapezoid whose bases are a and b, a straight line parallel to the base is drawn through the point of intersection of the diagonals. Find the length of the segment of this straight line cut off from it by the sides.

Ivan Petrovich called one of us to the board, and a drawing appeared on the board (Fig. 1). We immediately noticed the similarity between triangles AMO and ABC, MBO and ABD and wrote the formulas on the board

$$\frac{x}{b} = \frac{h_2}{h}, \quad \frac{x}{a} = \frac{h_1}{h},$$

in which $h, h_1, h_2$ are the heights of triangles ABD, MBO, AMO, and $x = MO$. Adding these equalities, we got

$$\frac{x}{a} + \frac{x}{b} = \frac{h_1 + h_2}{h} = 1,$$

that is, $x = ab/(a+b)$. Similar to $ON = ab/(a+b)$, the answers open:

$$MN = \frac{2ab}{a+b}.$$

“Ivan Petrovich,” was sent from the class, “I can already see how a new problem can be created from this problem.” The following note appeared on the board:

Problem 2. Prove that in a trapezoid, a straight line segment parallel to the base, which belongs to the point of intersection of the diagonals and whose ends are on the lateral sides of the trapezoid, is divided in half at this point.

“Good problem,” Ivan Petrovich shrugged, “and now I’ll change figure,” and he, continuing the sides of the trapezoid until the intersection at point $E$ (Fig. 2), drew a straight line through points $O$ and $E$. The teacher designated the points of intersection of the line with the bases as $F$ and $H$.

“I would like to draw your attention above,” he continued, “to the following problem:

Problem 3. Prove that in an arbitrary trapezoid, the midpoint of the base, the intersection of the lateral sides and the intersection of the diagonals belong to the same straight line.

The solution was obtained immediately: after all, it follows from the previous problem, since $EH$ is the median in triangle $EBC, EMN, EAD$.

“That’s right,” noted Ivan Petrovich. - How can I solve this problem in another way?
