Wolf, Baron and Newton
(About the film “Well, just wait!”, the novel by Cyrano de Bergerac and laser thermonuclear fusion)

Academician of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR
V. A. FABRIKANT

This article will discuss a physical phenomenon that could be observed in the original episode of the animated film “Well, Just Wait!” Remember how a wolf on a Parsian yacht breaks the agreement of a hare sailing on a steamboat? To speed up the yacht's progress, the wolf blows on the sail. At first glance, it may seem that the situation, in a certain sense, is similar to the one described by Baron Munchausen: having found himself in a swamp, the baron pulled himself out by his hair. However, there is a significant difference in these two cases. The wolf did not violate one of the basic laws of mechanics - Newton's third law, while the baron “violated” it, which, of course, is impossible.

In Newton’s Principia*) the third law is formulated as follows: “In reality there is always an equal and opposite reaction, otherwise the actions of two bodies on each other are equal and directed in opposite directions.” Newton said: “If something presses or pulls on something else, then it itself is pressed or pulled by the latter. If someone presses his finger on a stone, his finger is also pressed on a stone.” From Newton's third law it follows that no interaction of bodies inside a closed system (from which nothing breaks out) can change the movement of the system as a whole. In particular, the interaction of individual parts of Baron Munchausen’s organization (hands and hair) could not cause a change in the speed of his descent into the swamp, much less pull him out of there.

In order to at least slow down the dive, the baron had to begin to strip himself and forcefully throw the objects of his clothes down into the swamp, that is, to break the closed system. Heavy boots would be especially helpful here. Finally, the baron could stand on the saddle and jump beyond the swamp. But at the same time he would push the whizz when and thereby speed up the poor animal's descent into the swamp.

However, let's return to “Well, wait a minute!” Let's assume that the yacht's sail is made of materials that absorb the structure emitted by the Wolf. then the yacht with the Wolf on it would be a closed system, and in the absence of wind it would not be able to move from its place, no matter how hard the Wolf tried. Volka’s attempts to speed up the yacht’s progress with blowing would also be free: the pressure on the pars of the air stream emitted by the Wolf would be balanced by the effect of the Wolf’s thrusts on the yacht’s firing. The fact is that the Wolf strangling forward experiences a recoil directed backwards (action and reaction).
