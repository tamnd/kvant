Vice Ilya built an iron chariot (there is a belief that thunder during a thunderstorm is the rumble of the chariot of vice Elijah as he rode across the heavens) and, sitting in it, began to throw up a magnetized iron ball. The ball pulled the chariot behind it each time, and thus the vice would reach the Moon. The vice did not take into account the recoil that the chariot would experience with each throw of the ball, and turned out to be the predecessor of the “truth-loving” Baron Münchgraysen.

In the second half of the nineteenth century, the French playwright Edmond Rostand wrote the play Cyrano de Bergerac, which is still very successful. The play is called by the author a heroic comedy, which accurately reflects its character - cheerful and at the same time romantically sublime. Maxim Gorky wrote a special article in which he very highly appreciated the noble image of Cyrano, outlined by Rostand.

Rostand attributed to Cyrano the method of flying to the moon, “invented” by the vice Ilya, and did not mention rockets. Here is the relevant passage from the play:

"Lie down on an iron sheet
and strong fish
Toss the magnet
he is a sheet of iron with you
Pulls up. You again.
So keep practicing until the moon.”
Why Rostan turned the chariot into an iron sheet is unclear.
Relatively recently, the name Cyrano was assigned to one of the lunar craters, which, of course, is quite fair.

A few words about Jules Verne
“From the Earth to the Moon by Direct Route in 97 Hours 20 Minutes” is the title of a science fiction novel by Jules Verne (1828–1905), which was written in 1865. Jules Verne describes landing on the moon in a projectile fired by a giant cannon. It must be admitted that this is a much less rational method than shooting with rockets, invented by Cyrano.

But more than two hundred years have passed! True, in another novel by Jules Verne - “Around the Moon” - the thrifty Frenchman Michel Ardant took the bags with him, but only to soften the impact on the Moon due to the recoil phenomenon. But in most of Jules Verne’s novels, a more precise coordinate is given for the point of the path where the attraction of the Earth is balanced by the attraction of the Moon - 47/52 of the distance between the Earth and the Moon. This is natural, since the ratio of the masses of the Moon and the Earth, as well as Newton’s law of gravity, were already known. Michel Ardant's companions - the president of the cannon club Barbicane and Captain Nicole - used algebra for the corresponding calculations, for which Michel Ardant razzed them as "X-eaters". By the way, according to Jules Verne, irrevocability should be divided only at one point of the trajectory - at the very one where the resulting gravitational force is zero. Jules Verne is wrong. For the appearance of irrelevance, it is not at all necessary for the force of gravity to vanish. Not to mention the experience of astronauts, every sportsmite is in a state of impermanence during the jump (if you do not take into account air resistance).
