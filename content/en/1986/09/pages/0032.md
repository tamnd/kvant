⟦folio 30⟧

System energy increased by
$$n = \frac{(m + \Delta m)v_m^2}{2m v_m^2} = 1 + \frac{\Delta m}{m} \text{ раз.}$$

Let’s give our system a “rest” and, when the weight passes (in the opposite direction) the equilibrium position again, we repeat the same procedure with the plasticine ball. However, now, thanks to the previous intervention, the maximum speed of the weight turns out to be equal to $v_m' = \sqrt{(m + \Delta m)/m} v_m$, and, accelerating the ball, before placing it on the weight, we will have to do more work than the first time ($\Delta m(v_m')^2/2 > \Delta m v_m^2/2$).

So, every polarion we will increase the energy of the system, and, it is clear that this increase in energy leads to an increase in the amplitude of oscillations (we will consider the mass of the ball to be so small compared to the mass of the weight that the change in the frequency of oscillations of the system can be neglected).

The emphasis is on the fact that, unlike conventional resonance, in which the frequency of the external force must be equal to the natural frequency of the system’s oscillations, in the case of parametric resonance, the amplification of the oscillations occurs most effectively when the frequency of change of one or another parameter of the system is twice the natural frequency of the oscillations.

Let's consider another example, or one that constructs the general principle of the occurrence of parametric resonance. Let us imagine an oscillatory circuit consisting of a capacitor and a coil in which free electrical oscillations can occur (“Physics 10”, § 13). We will periodically change the capacitance of the capacitor. To do this, at the moment when the charge on the capacitor is maximum, its plates quickly develop, and when the charge on the plates is zero, we just as quickly return them to their previous position. Continuing this process, one can be convinced that the amplitude of oscillations in such a system will increase indefinitely, although there is no external EMF in the circuit. The fact is that when developing charged plates, we each time make a positive equality, and when bending uncharged plates, we do not do any work at all. It is easy to see that in this process the frequency of the external influence is twice as high as the natural frequency of the system’s oscillations.

What happens if we take an oscillatory circuit, in which there are initially no obvious oscillations, and begin to change the capacitance of the capacitor purely mechanically (the distance between the plates or their area) with a frequency twice its own? It turns out that in this case, too, the process of increasing oscillations will occur! The fact is that there is always a small, accidentally formed charge on the capacitor plates. This charge gives the “initial impetus” to the rapid growth of oscillations according to the scheme described above. This prince contains generators and amplifiers of electromagnetic oscillations, called parametric machines. The first parametric machine was designed in 1933 based on the research of Soviet physicists - academicians L. I. Mandelstam and N. D. Papaleksi.

Well, what if the system is not ideal, that is, there are energy losses in it?

With ordinary resonance, energy losses determine the maximum amplitude of oscillations. In the case of parametric resonance, the role of these losses takes over. As we have already discussed, with parametric resonance, for every polarniode, the energy of the system increases by a certain number of times, but at the same time, part of the energy is irretrievably lost (released in the form of heat). If the increase in energy exceeds the losses, then the oscillations, although slower than in the absence of losses, but to excite oscillations in a system with friction, the corresponding parameter of the system should be changed to a greater extent - a ball of greater mass should be changed to a weight, the capacitor plates should be expanded to a greater distance. The person swinging on the swing will have to squat deeper, but if you manage to start swinging, then without changing the depth of the squat, you can swing very strongly.
