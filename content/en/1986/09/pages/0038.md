M1001. There are 1001 stones in the pile. She randomly divides herself into two piles, counts the number of stones in them and writes down the product of these two numbers. Then with one of these heaps (which has more than one stone) the same operation is performed: it is divided into two and writes down the product of the numbers of stones in two new educational heaps. Then the same operation is repeated with one of the three resulting heaps, and so on, until all heaps contain one stone. What is the sum of 1000 recorded works?

A. S. Merkuryev

M1002. a)*The scatterbrain mathematician, having entered the three-digit code of his entrance, presses the buttons (with the numbers 0,1,...,9) one per second. The door opens if three digits of the code are dialed in a row in the required order. The mathematician is confident that even in the event of “extreme bad luck” (if the desired combination is encountered last), he will be able to enter the entrance no later than in 16 minutes. 42 sec (1002 sec). Is he right? How should he act to get home in time?

Answer the last question if 6) only buttons with numbers 1, 2, 3 are working (other numbers are not included in the code);
c) *all buttons are working, but the mathematician remembers that all three digits of the code are different.

b) there are at most one fifth of these lines on which there is no such point M.

O. P. Musin
