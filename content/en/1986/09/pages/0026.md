Problem 4. On a line there are three points A, B, C, of ​​which B is in the middle between A and C. Through an arbitrary point K that does not belong to the segment AC, use one line to draw lines parallel to AC.

“Also a good problem,” according to Ivan Petrovich, “but we’ll talk about it next time, and today we’ll look at a more complex problem:

Problem 5. On each of two parallel lines there are one segments of length a and b. Using one line, construct a segment:

$$x = \frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

We ourselves would not have been able to cope quickly if it were not for the help of Ivan Petrovich:

— Let us continue the sides of the trapezoid ABCD, whose bases are the given segments a and b (Fig. 3). We denote the intersection points of the diagonals AC and BD and the segments AB and DC as O and E. Let us draw a straight line EO, which will intersect the bases of the trapezoid at points F and H. We connect points D and F, A and F, B and H, C and H, and obtain points K and L. The segment KL is the required one.

Indeed, triangles BKF and AKH, CFL and LHD are similar, therefore

$$\frac{BK}{KH} = \frac{BF}{AH}; \quad \frac{FL}{LD} = \frac{FC}{HD}.$$

But BF = FC and AH = HD (problem 2), which means

$$\frac{BK}{KH} = \frac{FL}{LD},$$

Consequently, the segment KL is parallel to the base BC and AD. And since point O belongs to the segment KL and MK = KO, and OL = LN (problem 2), we obtain

$$OL = \frac{1}{2} \frac{ab}{a+b}, \quad KL = \frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

— And so that you can better understand such problems, do the following tasks at home:

6. Segments of length a and b belong to one of the parallel lines, and a segment of length c belongs to another of them. Using one line, construct a segment of length x so that

$$\frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}.$$

7. Parallel segments are given. Divide one of them in half using one line.

8. Using one line, divide the trapezoid into two equal-sized shapes.

9. Given two parallel lines. Draw a third line parallel to the data through this point using one line.

10. Magnify a given segment lying on one of two parallel lines using one line by 2, 3, ..., n times.

11. Find the half, third, ..., nth part of such a segment.

“And all these problems,” let us remind the teacher, “are solved with the help of the first problem that we met today.”

Problem 12.
