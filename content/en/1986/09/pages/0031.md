This dependence of transfer coefficients on the characteristics of molecular motion is very natural. After all, the average speed of molecules v determines the speed of transfer of one or another characteristic in the process of establishing equilibrium. The length of the free collision l appears in the formula because after each collision the parameters of the movement of the molecule are determined by the parameters of the system in the places where these collisions occur, that is, at points spaced from each other by a distance l. So, in our example with body conductivity, the amount of energy transferred by a molecule during the next collision determines the temperature difference at those points in the medium where this and the previous collision occurred.

What is parametric resonance?

From the school physics course (see “Physics 10”, § 9 and 20) you know about resonance in an oscillatory system, which arises as a result of an emerging force that changes with a frequency equal to the frequency of free oscillations of the system (in fact, due to the resonance of the present at a slightly lower frequency). It turns out that the presence of such a force is not the only possibility for the occurrence of resonance phenomena. Here's a good example. When swinging on a swing, you can significantly increase the amplitude of its oscillations only by periodically squatting and straightening, that is, changing the position of your center of gravity.

From this example it is clear that the sharp impact of the amplitude of oscillations can be caused not only by a periodic external force, but also by a periodic change in one of the parameters of the oscillatory system, provided that the frequency of these changes is determined by the connection with the frequency of natural oscillations of the system. This resonance is called parametric.

To clarify the mechanism of this method, successful oscillations are processed to a system that is well known to you - an external weight (“Physics 10”, § 2). However, in order to excite the normal properties of the oscillation of the weight, we will do the following.

At the moment when a weight of mass m passes the equilibrium position with a maximum speed v_m, let us place on it some additional weight of mass Δm, say, a plate ball. To ensure that the speed of the weight does not change at this moment, we first accelerate the ball to the same speed v_m. Note that in the process of accelerating the ball we do work, which ultimately goes to increase the total energy of the system to a value of (m+Δm)v²/m/2. This increase in energy leads to a greater, in comparison with the previous, maximum calculation of the solution of other x'm, the magnitude of which can be found from the law of conservation of energy.

$$\frac{(m+Δm)v²}{2} = \frac{k(x'm)²}{2}.$$
