Below we present several problems of subsequent equations.

1. (1973). The nine marked points are located inside a single square. Prove that among them there are three that either lie on the same line or are the vertices of a triangle with an area not exceeding 1/8.

2. (1975). Let $A, B, C, D$ be four points in space. Prove that

$$AC^2 + BD^2 + AD^2 + BC^2 \geq AB^2 - CD^2.$$

3. (1976). Find solutions to the equation in terms of the number $a, b, c$:

$$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = a^2 b^2.$$

4. (1978). An integer $n$ is called good if it can be represented in the form $n = a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_k$, where $a_1, a_2, ..., a_k$ are natural numbers (not necessarily distinct) satisfying the relations $\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + ... + \frac{1}{a_k} = 1$.

It is known that the natural numbers 33, 34, ..., 73 are good. Prove that all natural numbers at least 33 are good.

5. (1979). Some organizations have $n$ people ($n \geq 5$), as well as $n + 1$ committees consisting of three people, and the composition of accountants is not the same in different committees. Prove that there are two committees with exactly one common member.

6. (1980). Find the maximum number of three-fold argumentative progressions that can be chosen from the sequence of numbers $a_1 < a_2 < ... < a_n$.

7. (1980). A sphere is inscribed in a terrad, which touches the faces of the terrad at their centers of gravity (that is, at the points of intersection of the medians). Prove that terrade is correct.

8. (1980). Find all numbers $a_0$ for which the infinite sequence ($a_n$) defined by the equalities $a_n = 2^{n-1} - 3a_{n-1}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, ...$ is strictly increasing, that is, $a_{n-1} < a_n$ for all $n$.

9. (1981). Given an angle whose magnitude is $\frac{180^\circ}{n}$, where $n$ is a natural number, is not made by 3. Prove that (meaning $n$) it is possible to divide this angle into three equal angles only using a compass and ruler.

10. (1981). Given $n$ points, no four of which lie in the same plane. Let $A$ be the set of all terradras with vertices at these points. Plane $a$ does not contain any of the point data. Prove that among all segetras from the set $A$ by plane $a$ there are at most $n^2(n-2)^2$ even angles.

11. (1982). In a society of 1982 accounts, among any group of four accounts there is at least one who is familiar with the other threes. What is the name of a group of people who know everyone?

12. (1984). Mathematical equation is solved in two ways. First, the solution is found using a linear equation, and second, using a quadratic equation. The second method is more efficient, as it requires fewer steps and less computational effort.

13. (1985). In the space of given four numbers, that of the six pairwise distances between them, no more than two exceed 1. Find the maximum possible value of the sum of all these six distances.
