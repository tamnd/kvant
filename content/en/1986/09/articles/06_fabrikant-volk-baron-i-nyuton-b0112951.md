Wolf, Baron and Newton
(About the film “Well, just wait!”, the novel by Cyrano de Bergerac and laser thermonuclear fusion)

Academician of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR
V. A. FABRIKANT

This article will discuss a physical phenomenon that could be observed in the original episode of the animated film “Well, Just Wait!” Remember how a wolf on a Parsian yacht breaks the agreement of a hare sailing on a steamboat? To speed up the yacht's progress, the wolf blows on the sail. At first glance, it may seem that the situation, in a certain sense, is similar to the one described by Baron Munchausen: having found himself in a swamp, the baron pulled himself out by his hair. However, there is a significant difference in these two cases. The wolf did not violate one of the basic laws of mechanics - Newton's third law, while the baron “violated” it, which, of course, is impossible.

In Newton’s Principia*) the third law is formulated as follows: “In reality there is always an equal and opposite reaction, otherwise the actions of two bodies on each other are equal and directed in opposite directions.” Newton said: “If something presses or pulls on something else, then it itself is pressed or pulled by the latter. If someone presses his finger on a stone, his finger is also pressed on a stone.” From Newton's third law it follows that no interaction of bodies inside a closed system (from which nothing breaks out) can change the movement of the system as a whole. In particular, the interaction of individual parts of Baron Munchausen’s organization (hands and hair) could not cause a change in the speed of his descent into the swamp, much less pull him out of there.

In order to at least slow down the dive, the baron had to begin to strip himself and forcefully throw the objects of his clothes down into the swamp, that is, to break the closed system. Heavy boots would be especially helpful here. Finally, the baron could stand on the saddle and jump beyond the swamp. But at the same time he would push the whizz when and thereby speed up the poor animal's descent into the swamp.

However, let's return to “Well, wait a minute!” Let's assume that the yacht's sail is made of materials that absorb the structure emitted by the Wolf. then the yacht with the Wolf on it would be a closed system, and in the absence of wind it would not be able to move from its place, no matter how hard the Wolf tried. Volka’s attempts to speed up the yacht’s progress with blowing would also be free: the pressure on the pars of the air stream emitted by the Wolf would be balanced by the effect of the Wolf’s thrusts on the yacht’s firing. The fact is that the Wolf strangling forward experiences a recoil directed backwards (action and reaction).

Cyrano de Bergerac

The recoil effect, as is known, underlies the application of the package for space travel. This is where we come to the French package of Cyrano de Bergerac (1619-1655). This is a very unique figure in the history of world literature. Cyrano came from a seedy, dual family and was in great need all his life. In that era, only those who entered the service of the retinue of one of the rich aristocrats passed through. For Cyrano, such a path was closed due to his extremely independent and fiery character. Cyrano wrote poetic leaflets directed against the all-powerful First Minister of France, Cardinal Mazarin (“Mazarins”). He tried to get rich through gambling, but without success. Nature rewarded Cyrano with a huge caricatured nose, which caused constant ridicule, which served as a reason for an endless number of duals. And this gambler and duzlyat was a deep thinker, a follower of the materialist philosopher Gassendi *) and the creator of the wonderful science fiction novel “Another World, or States and Imperials,” published in 1657, after the death of the author. The novel is clearly atheistic in nature, and in it Cyrano wittily sings biblical legends.

What is interesting for us is that in the novel Cyrano describes his flight to the moon... with the help of a package! There is an old engraving depicting Cyrano flying towards the Moon in a basket, to which are attached packages that emit jets of fire towards the Earth, thereby raising the basket. Thus, Cyrano foresaw the use of the package for space travel three hundred years ago. It is also curious that in a novel written more than thirty years before the publication of Newton’s Elements, Cyrano claims that the attraction of the Moon will overpower the attraction of the Earth at a distance less from the Moon than from the Earth, since the mass of the Moon is less than the mass of the Earth, and even calculates the ratio of these distances (he gets, of course, the wrong numerical result - 3 together is about 9).

A few words about Jules Verne
“From the Earth to the Moon by Direct Route in 97 Hours 20 Minutes” is the title of a science fiction novel by Jules Verne (1828–1905), which was written in 1865. Jules Verne describes landing on the moon in a projectile fired by a giant cannon. It must be admitted that this is a much less rational method than shooting with rockets, invented by Cyrano.

But more than two hundred years have passed! True, in another novel by Jules Verne - “Around the Moon” - the thrifty Frenchman Michel Ardant took the bags with him, but only to soften the impact on the Moon due to the recoil phenomenon. But in most of Jules Verne’s novels, a more precise coordinate is given for the point of the path where the attraction of the Earth is balanced by the attraction of the Moon - 47/52 of the distance between the Earth and the Moon. This is natural, since the ratio of the masses of the Moon and the Earth, as well as Newton’s law of gravity, were already known. Michel Ardant's companions - the president of the cannon club Barbicane and Captain Nicole - used algebra for the corresponding calculations, for which Michel Ardant razzed them as "X-eaters". By the way, according to Jules Verne, irrevocability should be divided only at one point of the trajectory - at the very one where the resulting gravitational force is zero. Jules Verne is wrong. For the appearance of irrelevance, it is not at all necessary for the force of gravity to vanish. Not to mention the experience of astronauts, every sportsmite is in a state of impermanence during the jump (if you do not take into account air resistance).
