Lesson
one task

I. A. KUSHNIR

“Today,” Ivan Petrovich began the math club lesson, “our lesson is devoted to one problem.”
- So difficult? - we were surprised.
- No. Especially this problem is that it will help us solve other problems. Here are its terms:

Problem 1. In a trapezoid whose bases are a and b, a straight line parallel to the base is drawn through the point of intersection of the diagonals. Find the length of the segment of this straight line cut off from it by the sides.

Ivan Petrovich called one of us to the board, and a drawing appeared on the board (Fig. 1). We immediately noticed the similarity between triangles AMO and ABC, MBO and ABD and wrote the formulas on the board

$$\frac{x}{b} = \frac{h_2}{h}, \quad \frac{x}{a} = \frac{h_1}{h},$$

in which $h, h_1, h_2$ are the heights of triangles ABD, MBO, AMO, and $x = MO$. Adding these equalities, we got

$$\frac{x}{a} + \frac{x}{b} = \frac{h_1 + h_2}{h} = 1,$$

that is, $x = ab/(a+b)$. Similar to $ON = ab/(a+b)$, the answers open:

$$MN = \frac{2ab}{a+b}.$$

“Ivan Petrovich,” was sent from the class, “I can already see how a new problem can be created from this problem.” The following note appeared on the board:

Problem 2. Prove that in a trapezoid, a straight line segment parallel to the base, which belongs to the point of intersection of the diagonals and whose ends are on the lateral sides of the trapezoid, is divided in half at this point.

“Good problem,” Ivan Petrovich shrugged, “and now I’ll change figure,” and he, continuing the sides of the trapezoid until the intersection at point $E$ (Fig. 2), drew a straight line through points $O$ and $E$. The teacher designated the points of intersection of the line with the bases as $F$ and $H$.

“I would like to draw your attention above,” he continued, “to the following problem:

Problem 3. Prove that in an arbitrary trapezoid, the midpoint of the base, the intersection of the lateral sides and the intersection of the diagonals belong to the same straight line.

The solution was obtained immediately: after all, it follows from the previous problem, since $EH$ is the median in triangle $EBC, EMN, EAD$.

“That’s right,” noted Ivan Petrovich. - How can I solve this problem in another way?

Problem 4. On a line there are three points A, B, C, of ​​which B is in the middle between A and C. Through an arbitrary point K that does not belong to the segment AC, use one line to draw lines parallel to AC.

“Also a good problem,” according to Ivan Petrovich, “but we’ll talk about it next time, and today we’ll look at a more complex problem:

Problem 5. On each of two parallel lines there are one segments of length a and b. Using one line, construct a segment:

$$x = \frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

We ourselves would not have been able to cope quickly if it were not for the help of Ivan Petrovich:

— Let us continue the sides of the trapezoid ABCD, whose bases are the given segments a and b (Fig. 3). We denote the intersection points of the diagonals AC and BD and the segments AB and DC as O and E. Let us draw a straight line EO, which will intersect the bases of the trapezoid at points F and H. We connect points D and F, A and F, B and H, C and H, and obtain points K and L. The segment KL is the required one.

Indeed, triangles BKF and AKH, CFL and LHD are similar, therefore

$$\frac{BK}{KH} = \frac{BF}{AH}; \quad \frac{FL}{LD} = \frac{FC}{HD}.$$

But BF = FC and AH = HD (problem 2), which means

$$\frac{BK}{KH} = \frac{FL}{LD},$$

Consequently, the segment KL is parallel to the base BC and AD. And since point O belongs to the segment KL and MK = KO, and OL = LN (problem 2), we obtain

$$OL = \frac{1}{2} \frac{ab}{a+b}, \quad KL = \frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

— And so that you can better understand such problems, do the following tasks at home:

6. Segments of length a and b belong to one of the parallel lines, and a segment of length c belongs to another of them. Using one line, construct a segment of length x so that

$$\frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}.$$

7. Parallel segments are given. Divide one of them in half using one line.

8. Using one line, divide the trapezoid into two equal-sized shapes.

9. Given two parallel lines. Draw a third line parallel to the data through this point using one line.

10. Magnify a given segment lying on one of two parallel lines using one line by 2, 3, ..., n times.

11. Find the half, third, ..., nth part of such a segment.

“And all these problems,” let us remind the teacher, “are solved with the help of the first problem that we met today.”

- Who came up with this problem?

“I can’t tell you the first author of the problem,” said Ivan Petrovich, “but it is known that it appears in the 12th century treatise of the Indian mathematician Bhaskara “The Crown of Astronomical Teaching” in this form:

Problem 12.
