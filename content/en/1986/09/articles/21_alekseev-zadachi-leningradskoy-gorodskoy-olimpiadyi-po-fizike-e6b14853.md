2. Marcinin gives birth to a full and lifelike knock. It is known that throughout the history of the Martian civilization (now extinct), an odd number of Martians were born. Prove that there were at least 100 days in each of which the number of inhabitants of Mars was odd.

3. In Svambrania, one free avalilia was closed. It is known that after this it is possible to travel from any airport in Schwambran to any other, perhaps with transfers. Before the line was closed, this could be done with no more than $N$ landings. Prove that it is now possible to travel from any airport to any other in no more than $2N$ stops. 5. There is a sheet of checkered paper with size: $a) 30 \times 45, 6) 30 \times 30$ cells. The two play the next game. In one move (they take turns), a cut is made along the line connecting two adjacent grid nodes. The first player starts cutting from the edge of the sheet. Each subsequent cut should continue the line formed by the previous cuts. The player whose move breaks the sheet into two pieces wins. Who wins when played correctly?

6. The set $A$ consists of real numbers. It is known that the sum of any two of its elements is also its element, and any segment $[a, b]$, $a < b$ contains a segment entirely consisting of elements of the set $A$. Prove that $A$ contains all real numbers.

7. Consider the following algorithm:
Step 0. Put $n = m$.
Step 1. If $n$ is even, halve $n$. If $n$ is odd, increase $n$ by 1.
Step 2. If $n > 1$, go to step 1. If $n = 1$, finish executing the algorithm.
How many natural numbers $m$ are there for which this algorithm will execute step 1 exactly 15 times?

8. The king went around the board $9 \times 9$, raising it exactly once on each of its squares. (The king's route is not closed and possibly self-intersecting.) What is the maximum possible length of such a route if the length of the diagonal move is $\sqrt{2}$, and the length of the vertical and horizontal moves is 1?

9. The diameter of a set on a plane is the maximum distance between two of its points. (If such a thing exists.) It is known that the sum of the diameters of the polygons $M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$ is less than the diameter of their union. Prove that there is a line that does not intersect any of these polygons and on each side of which lies at least one of them.

10. Prove that

11. Evaluate Integral
$$\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dx}{1+x^3+\sqrt{1+x^6}}.$$

Objectives of the Leningrad City Olympiad in Physics

8th grade

1. Determine the radius of the orbit of the Earth's satellite, which is always located above the same point on the earth's surface. The radius of the Earth is $R=6400$ km, the acceleration of gravity on its surface is $g=10 \text{ m/c}^2$.

4. On the edge of a cart with mass $M$ and length $l$, standing on a smooth horizontal table, there is a load with mass $m$. You only push the load with a constant force $\vec{F}$, directed horizontally, towards the opposite edge of the cart (Fig. 2). How long will it take for the load to reach the opposite end of the cart? Friction coefficient between load and trolley $\mu$. Assume that the entire mass of the cart is concentrated in the board.

It is known that on a regular bicycle the radius of the wheels is 1.2 times greater, and the radius of the wheel gear (connected to the gears) is 1.5 times greater than that of a folding bicycle. Find the correspondence between the radius of the wheel and the general bicycles, losses on the internal training claim.

9th grade

1. A light spring of length $l$ with stiffness $k$ is attached at one end to a vertical axis around which it can rotate freely, and at the other end to a small load with mass $m$. The entire system is on a horizontal smooth table, the spring is not stretched. The load is hit with a speed $v$, directed perpendicular to the spring. Find the minimum and maximum distance of the load from the axle.

2. See problem 7 for 8th grade.

3. If you collide two glass balls, one of them will break. Why don't both balls break at once?

4. In a vanchine with water (water depth $h$, vanchniki area $S$, $\sqrt{S} \gg h$) dissolved ions are formed (charge she +q, mass $m$, mass of all ions $M$). The solution was mixed. Describe the qualitative and quantitative assessment of the distribution of ions in the bath after equilibrium has been established. How does this distribution depend on the dissolution temperature?

5. There is water in the pan at a temperature of $t_1 = 60^\circ$ C. The pan is closed with a lid with a mass of $m = 5$ kg and an area of $S = 100$ cm² and slowly heated to $t_2 = 70^\circ$ C. How many times will the lid of the pan bounce during this time, if the saturated vapor pressure at $t_1$ is $p_1 = 2,0 \cdot 10^4$ Pa, at $t_2 = 3,1 \cdot 10^4$ Pa, atomspheric pressure $p_0 = 10^5$ Pa.

6. Hot metal balls of the same temperature are thrown into a large point of water. It is known that a ball with a radius of $r_1 = 0,5$ cm heats up water by $\Delta t_1 = 0,1^\circ$ C, and with a radius of $r_2 = 1$ cm - by $\Delta t_2 = 1,2^\circ$ C. Estimate the change in water temperature when throwing a ball with a radius of $r_3 = 1,5$ cm.

1. See problem 1 for 9th grade.

2. Thin light rubber was stretched on a horizontal hoop with radius $r$; In the center of the hoop, a small weight of mass $m$ was attached to the rubber. If you lift the hoop, the weight will drop to a distance of $d (d \ll r)$. Estimate the magnitude of the period of small horizontal oscillations of the weight when the hoop is attached to a horizontal smooth table (there is no deflection).

3. See problem 4 for 9th grade.

In the crystal there is a visible axis $X$, along which the conductivity is equal to $\sigma_1$ (that is, if you cut a straight wire from the crystal with an axis parallel to the axis $X$, then the resistance of such a wire will be equal to $R = l / (S\sigma_1)$, where $l$ and $S$ are the length and cross-sectional area of ​​the wire). The specific conductivity along the $Y$ and $Z$ axes is $\sigma_2$, $\sigma_2 \neq \sigma_1$. What will be the specific conductivity of a straight wire cut from this crystal at an angle of $a$ to the axis $X$?

How will the power released in the frame change if the middles of the opposite sides are closed by wiring made of the same materials and length $a$?

Find the correspondence between the gear ratios (that is, between the radii of the gears connected by the movement to the wheels) at different speeds. For simplicity, we can assume that in an electric car engine the rotor rotates in a constant magnetic field.
