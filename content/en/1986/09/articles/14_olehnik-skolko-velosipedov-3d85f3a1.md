⟦folio 34⟧

How many bicycles?

S. N. OLEKHNIK

Raising a cloud of dust, a group of guys on two-wheel bicycles rushed along the country road, followed by guys on three-wheel bicycles. The little ones pedaled hard, trying to keep up with the big ones.

Seventh-graders Sasha and Natasha, having let the cyclists pass, walked along the road again.

“I counted only 30 wheels,” said Sasha. “And I counted 12 guys,” said Natasha.

After some time, Sasha, turning to Natasha, said: “I know how many two-wheel and three-wheel bicycles there were!”

“It’s probably not difficult to calculate,” Natasha answered. - If you give me a piece of paper and a pencil, I will also tell you how many two-wheel and three-wheel bicycles there were.

Natasha denoted the number of two-wheel bicycles by x, the number of three-wheel bicycles by y, and wrote down the equation system

$$\begin{cases} x+y=12, \\ 2x+3y=30 \end{cases}$$

and, having solved it, I got that x=y=6.

“This is the correct answer,” said Sasha. - But the problem is solved in the mind. Listen! If we put all the guys on two-wheel bicycles, then there would be 12 two-wheel bicycles, and there would be 24 wheels in total.”

“And we counted 30 wheels: that means the difference of 6 wheels was due to three-wheel bicycles,” Natasha interrupted Sasha.

“That’s right: that means there are 6 three-wheel bicycles; and then there will also be 6 double-oscillating ones.”

“Look how simple it is! Even a first-class student can solve this problem. Let's present it at a math class for junior grades and select a few more similar problems. For example, I remembered this:

There is a rabbit and pheasants in the cage. It is known that there are 35 heads and 94 legs in the cage. We need to find out how many pheasants and how many rabbits are in the cage.”

“And I know several old problems:

I bought 112 rams, old and young, dal 49 rubles and 20 altin. For the old one I paid no 15 altin and no 2 money, and for the younger one 10 altin; and there is a record of how many rams he bought, old and young. (1 altin=3 kopecks=6 money; 1 ruble=100 kopecks.)

Someone bought 64 rubles sukon. Of these, 20 rubles were white, 13 rubles were black, 5 were red, 19 were green, 7 were azure, and the price for them was 486 rubles. Their price was not the same: for a black roll he paid four rubles cheaper than white, for red - three rubles cheaper than black, for green - two rubles cheaper than red, and for azure - one ruble cheaper than green. We know how much money he paid for each roll.

Devandach people carry 12 loaves: each man carries 2 loaves, each woman carries half a loaf, and each child carries a quarter of bread. How many were the man, woman and children?
