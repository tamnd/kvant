Let us now recall one of the computer's capabilities in solving chess-mathematical problems. American mathematician S. Kim came up with an interesting generalization of the famous 8 queens problem.

Arrange the greatest number of queens on the board (8x8) so that each of them aligns exactly with p other queens.

The condition p=0 means that the queens do not threaten another friend, that is, we come to the classical problem, which was of interest to the great Gauss. The required number of queens on an 8x8 board is equal to everything. For p=1 the maximum is ten, for p=2 - fourteen. A complete solution to the problem was obtained by the young Kyiv mathematicians S. Bely and E. Rovensky. They proved that for p=3 the maximum is eighteen, and for p=4 - twenty-one (for p>4 the necessary arrangements do not exist).

These results were first published in «Kvant» No. 8 for 1985. And just recently S. Bely and E. Rovensky sent us a copy of their articles published in a service scientific publication, which considered a generalization of the problem to all square boards n×n for n≤8.
