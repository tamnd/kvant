Selected School Problems

Eighth grade
1. a) Let $a > 0, b > 0, c > 0$. Prove the inequality
$$abc \geq (a+b-c)(a+c-b)(b+c-a).$$
6) Let $r$ and $R$ be the radii of the circle circumscribed by the triangle and the circle circumscribed around it, respectively. Prove that $R \geq 2r$. Find out in what case this inequality turns into equality.
2. Find the smallest value of the polynomial
$$x^6+2x^4+2x^3+x^2+2x-1.$$
3. Prove that the number $n^4+64$ is composite.
4. Prove that the equation
$$x^2+y^3+z^5=t^7$$
has an infinite number of solutions in natural numbers.
5. On sides $AB$ and $BC$ of triangle $ABC$ there are given points $M$ and $N$ such that $AM:MB=BN:NC$. Let $Q$ be the intersection point of lines $AN$ and $CM$. Prove that the area of ​​the quadrilateral $MBNQ$ is equal to the area of ​​the triangle $ACQ$.

Ninth grade
6. Solve the system of equations
$$\begin{cases}
x^2-yz=y-x, \\
y^2-xz=z-y, \\
z^2-xy=x-z.
\end{cases}$$
7. The angles $A, B, C$ of triangle $ABC$ are equal to the corresponding $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$; the sides opposite the corresponding angles are equal to $a, b, c$.
a) Prove the inequality
$$a \cos \alpha+b \cos \beta \leq c.$$
6) Put the smallest value of the product
$$\sin \frac{\alpha}{2} \sin \frac{\beta}{2} \sin \frac{\gamma}{2}.$$

For each triangle, is this the most value achieved?
8. Find all integers $k$ such that $k^5+3$ is divisible by $k^2+1$.
9. Triangle $ABC$ is circumscribed by triangle $MNP$ ($M \in AB, N \in BC, P \in AC$) such that side $MN$ is perpendicular to side $BC$ and side $MP$ is perpendicular to side $AC$. Find the position of point $M$ (on side $AB$) such that side $PN$ is smallest.
10. Prove that the equation
$$x^{10}-x^7+x^2-x+1=0$$
has no solutions.

Tenth grade
11. Solve the equation
$$x^2+2x \sin xy+1=0.$$
12. Calculate the amount
$$\frac{1}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}+\frac{1}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5}+\dots+\frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}$$
13. Prove equality ($n \geq 2$)
$$tg x \cdot tg 2x+tg 2x \cdot tg 3x+\dots+tg(n-1)xtg nx=\frac{tg nx}{tg x}-n.$$
14. Solve the equation
$$\sqrt[3]{2-x}+\sqrt{x-1}-1=0.$$
15. On the sides of an acute triangle, as on the diameters, there are semicircles on the outer side of the building. The heights of the triangle were continued until they intersected with the constructed semicircles, after which these intersection points were connected to the nearest vertices of the triangle. Prove that you can glue a tetrahedron from a given hexagon.

In preparing the publication, we used the tasks proposed by S.R. Sefibekov, as well as A. A. Egorov, V. N. Dubrovsky and E. A. Yasinov.

One task lesson
(For the beginning, see p. 23)
transforming the harmonic mean of the numbers $a$ and $b$ (here they are the bases of the trapezoid). The segment $MN$ is less than the segment $L_1L_2$ - the geometric mean of two numbers, and, finally, the segment $MN$ is less than the segment $L_5L_6$ - the quadratic mean of two numbers $a$ and $b$ - a segment dividing the trapezoid into two equal parts and a parallel base, that is
$$\frac{2ab}{a+b} < \sqrt{ab} < \frac{a+b}{2} < \sqrt{\frac{a^2+b^2}{2}}.$$
