⟦folio 4⟧

In the first years of the magazine's existence, under this heading, mainly articles were published containing original solutions to the problem with international experiments. After becoming the editor of the magazine, she began to more actively manage student creativity, and abstracts from school theoretical conferences in mathematics began to appear on the pages of the magazine.

Category “Competition problems”

This section, created from the very birth of the Matematika magazine, aimed to attract the most capable students to independent creative work at the task level of international experiments. To a certain extent, the progressive concepts of Gyorg Pøly were implemented here, namely that in the classroom it is possible to create such an environment that students themselves strive to determine the research work.

In the period from 1962 to 1967, we published problems (and then their solutions) and a list of students who were able to solve these problems. After 1968, original solutions and generalizations of the problems of schoolchildren themselves were published, and since 1969, also problems compiled by schoolchildren. At the same time, greater importance is attached to the ability to pose and solve problems that arise when studying the real world.

Sections for younger students

Since 1980, the magazine “Matematika” has published 10 issues daily. The main goals and objectives of the magazine have not changed, but it is distributed through materials for primary schoolchildren (grades 4-6). The headings “Stats for juniors” and “Tasks for juniors” have been created. In articles for juniors, situations that are close to practice and to intuitive ideas are studied, first of all, and preference is given to the element involved, dialogue. Their main role, however, is to instill in students from an early age a taste for the beauty of mathematical exercises and, to the extent possible, independent work. Problems for younger schoolchildren are especially carefully selected. Students in grades 5, 6 and 7 have the opportunity to participate in a correspondence competition to solve these problems. The twenty best seventh-graders receive the right to continue their education at a mathematical gymnasium without competitive exams.

Information

Since the first issue in 1985, our magazine has increased its volume. At the same time, additional volume is now allocated to a new section devoted to information issues. Its goals, defined in economic language, are as follows:

1. Develop the intellectual abilities of students, introduce them to such mathematical concepts as system, model, information, algorithm, program control, format languages, etc.

2. Expand and deepen the concept of mathematical modeling using the example of real devices and technology, management and service processes, introduce them to their use, optimization and algorithmization.

3. To reveal the possibility of information and mathematics for solving scientific, technical and economic problems and, on their basis, to introduce quantitative methods of solution.

In conclusion, we note that for more than 20 years, the magazine “Matematika” has been a good helper for secondary school students. This is evidenced by at least the magazine's triple figure, which increased from 15,000 (in 1962) to 78,000 (in 1986).

The editorial team strives to satisfy the interests of the reader as fully as possible and help in training personnel with broad mathematical knowledge. That's why we are constantly looking for various forms of contact with readers.
