We will call a graph cyclic if any two vertices of this graph belong to some simple cycle.

Theorem. Let $\Gamma$ be an arbitrary cyclic graph with vertices $P_0, P_1, \dots, P_n$, and $G(\Gamma)$ be a cerra group “travel along $\Gamma$” with tokens 1,..., $n$. Then the following statement is true:

1) if all simple cycles $\Gamma$ are of even length, then $G(\Gamma)$ coincides with the group $A_n$;

2) if the graph $\Gamma$ is different from the graph $\Gamma_6$ depicted in figure 10, and contains at least one simple cycle of odd length, then $G(\Gamma)$ coincides with the group $S_n$.

We omit the proof of this statement. Note that partial cases of this theorem have already been proven earlier. An interesting exception is related to the graph $\Gamma_6$ (Fig. 10). All permutations of the group $G(\Gamma_6)$ were calculated on a computer and it was found that it is a proper subset of the group $S_6$, consisting of 120 elements. If we want the game “travel along the graph $\Gamma_6$” to always have a solution, then we must use at least three identical tiles. This is the only graph with this property.

Game modifications

Let us now consider some modifications of “travel through graphs” associated with turning over chips and restrictions on the “rules of movement”.

Let the tiles for the game HOUSE be square and painted on both sides: one side white and the other black. Let's place the chips on the playing field shown in figure 7, and some of them will be placed with the white side up, and some with the black side up. In addition to the old rules for moving chips across the playing field, we will introduce one new operation $R$: if cell number 0 is free, then you can simultaneously turn over all the chips located in cells 4, 5 and 6. The goal of the game is to use valid transformations and operation $R$ to turn all the chips with the white side up (“turning on the lights in the windows”). The problem is conditioned by writing the corresponding numbers from 1 to 9 on the white side of each chip and requiring that at the end of the game each chip not only has the white side up, but also occupies the cell with the corresponding number. We will call this game GLOWING WINDOWS.
