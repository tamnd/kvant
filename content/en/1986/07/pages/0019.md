The fact is that when a stream of molten substance A comes out of the crater and rushes upward, at some point it will not reach substance A for further growth. A vacuum is created inside the jet, and somewhere on the border of the formed barrel and the crater a fracture appears, into which cold liquid B rushes. The upper part of the jet is still continues its upward movement. So liquid B fills the barrel from the inside, cooling and forming its inner walls, after which they finally harden.

In the lower part of the lamp, meanwhile, the melting process is still going on, and another ball of molten substance A comes out of the crater. It rises up already inside the formed tube. Having risen to its upper end, he removes it using his still heated mass. With each portion of substance A, the tube is removed, forming a corrugated trunk growing upward (Fig. 3). Next to it, having developed the fallen debris of volcanic activity, after some time one or more such trunks may grow. The trunks are intricately intertwined, like the stems of exotic plants, among the stone blocks that dot the bottom, and continue to sink as the liquid in the sap heats up. The picture freezes for a while. This phase can be called the stone forest phase.

If you turn off the lamp at this moment, the “petrified forest” in it will remain unchanged - the lamp itself will not be able to return it to its original state. However, despite the fireworks of events that have taken place, we have not yet reached the operating mode, so we will leave the lamp on and continue observations.

Time passes, liquid B warms up, the shards lying at the bottom begin to melt, and the upward-going trunks gradually settle down. However, you won’t see any flattened drops among the existing shards—they all gradually take on a spherical shape. Under general conditions, the flattening of droplets on a non-wettable surface occurs due to gravity. It counteracts the forces of surface tension, which tend to give the drop the shape of a sphere - a body whose surface is minimal for a given volume. In the lamp, in addition to the force of gravity and surface tension, the drop is acted upon by the Archimedes force, which almost completely compensates for the force of gravity. Therefore, the drop appears to be able to prevent it from taking on a spherical shape.
