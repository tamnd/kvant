Throughout my life I will never forget when I first learned the name of Svante Arreninus. It was in June 1884 that his work came into my hands. It was too difficult to overcome at once, and I spent a feverish night with bad dreams. What was written in the work insisted on being different from what was attractive and known, which at first I was inclined to take the whole thing as nonsense.

V. Ostwald

The words given in the epigraph refer to one of the first works on electropyretic dissociation by the famous Swedish physical chemist S. Arreninus. Somewhat later, Arreninus was awarded the Nobel Prize for creating the theory of this phenomenon.

Electrical dissociation refers to the disintegration of the molecules of the substance being dissolved into positive and negative ones under the influence of the electric field of the solvent molecules. The devices in which such a process occurs and are therefore capable of conducting electric current are called power supplies.

figure 1 shows the simplest device with which you can determine whether a given radiator is a power supply or not. The device consists of a battery, a flashlight bulb and two electrodes, such as graphite rods. If the light comes on when the electrodes are lowered into the solution, it means that the solution being studied is an electrolyte.
