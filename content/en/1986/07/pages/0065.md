15. On the 1st and 2nd (from the beginning). Solution. Let the number $Y$ be obtained from the number $X$ by eliminating the $m$ digit. Examples show that $m$ can equal 1 or 2 (you can take $X=66$, $Y=6$). Let's assume $m \geqslant 3$. Let's write down:

$$X = A \cdot 10^{n+1} + b \cdot 10^n + C,$$

$$Y = A \cdot 10^n + C.$$

Here $C < 10^n$, $b \leqslant 9$, and if $m \geqslant 3$, then $A \geqslant 10$. We have

$$X - 10Y = b \cdot 10^n - 9C,$$

$$-9 \cdot 10^n < -9C \leqslant b \cdot 10^n - 9C \leqslant b \cdot 10^n \leqslant 9 \cdot 10^n,$$

whence $|X - 10Y| < Y$, $9Y < X < 11Y$. Therefore, if $X$ is divisible by $Y$, then $X : Y = 10$, and this contradicts the fact that $X$ does not end in zero.
