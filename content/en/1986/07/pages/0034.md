Introduction: polis, polara and their properties

Two points $P$ and $Q$ are said to be conjugate to circle $(k)$ if the circle with diameter $PQ$ is orthogonal to circle $(k)$ (Fig. 1). The set of all points conjugate to the point $P$ relative to the circle $k(O; r)$ is called

lar point $P$ relative to $(k)$. Next we will fix the circle $k(O; n)$, and therefore we will omit the words “relation $(k)$”.

Property I (construction of a polar). The polar of the point $P$ is straight; it can be constructed as shown in figure 2. If the line $l$ serves as a polar for the point $P$, we also say that $P$ is the pole of the line $l$.

Property II (construction of a pole). Any straight line that does not pass through the center of the circle has a single pole; it can be constructed as shown in figure 3.

Property III (reciprocity). The polar $l_p$ of any point $P$ belonging to the polar $l_Q$ of the point $Q$ passes through $Q$.

Property IV (characteristic property). For the point $M$ polars $l_P$ points $P$ and only for them the correspondence is satisfied

$$OM^2 - PM^2 = 2r^2 - OP^2.$$ (1)

We leave the proofs of these properties to the reader.
