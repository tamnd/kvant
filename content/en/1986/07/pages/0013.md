⟦folio 11⟧

A stable system is formed - a hydrogen molecule. The energy of formation of a molecule—the “well depth” in the $U(r)$ graph—is $E_0 = 4,5$ eV. When atoms “merge” into a molecule, called recombination, this energy - the energy of recombination - must be given to surrounding bodies. In the reverse process, called dissociation, this energy—dissociation energy—must be supplied from an external source. Let us note again that in the $\uparrow \uparrow$ state, hydrogen atoms cannot form a molecule at all.

Potential energy graphs are very clear: the system tends to move as a sled would move on a slippery mountain, the slope of which is shown on the graph. For graph $\uparrow \uparrow$ the sled would move to the right, in the area of ​​large distances. This means that atoms repel and move away from each other. On graph $\uparrow \downarrow$ there is a slope along which the sled would move to the left, towards the origin of coordinates, which corresponds to the attraction of hydrogen atoms.

How do collisions occur?

Generally, the magnetic moments of atoms are randomly oriented. Half of the collision will originate in the $\uparrow \uparrow$ state, and the other half in the $\uparrow \downarrow$ state.

It is interesting that not every collision of two hydrogen atoms in the $\uparrow \downarrow$ state will lead to the formation of a molecule, that is, to recombination. Turning to our illustration with a sled, we can easily imagine that, having rolled onto a completely slippery mountain from somewhere on the right, the sled slides down into a hole, but by inertia it flies out onto the opposite slope and is left at the height from which it began sliding on the right slope. Then the sled will go down again, again reach the pit and go to the right with the same kinetic energy with which it arrived. It is clear that this imaginable movement will occur due to the law of conservation of energy, provided there is a complete absence of friction. To realize mountains, part of the kinetic energy always goes to work against the friction forces, that is, in the tello, and sooner or later the sled is left at the bottom of the pit. When hydrogen atoms collide, the law of conservation of energy is also valid. And there are no more difficulties here. If there are only two atoms colliding, then they have nothing to transfer their energy to. When they collide, the atoms will stick together, leak out into a stable position by inertia, come together until they are completely left, and then scatter again, like perfectly elastic balls.
