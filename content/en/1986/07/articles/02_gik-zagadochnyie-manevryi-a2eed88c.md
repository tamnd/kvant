MYSTERIOUS MANEUVERS

Anatoly Karpov and Garry Karpov played 75 games before the start of their third match for the chess crown, in which 24 meetings were to be held. One more anniversary: ​​100 games. The overall score between the two leaders of modern chess is still a draw - each has won 8 victories.

A. Karpov - G. Karpov.
Here is a position from the ninth game of the first match. Black has just captured the pawn - g5:h4 (Bg6 was better), believing that the exchange will simplify the available draws for them. Indeed, after g3:h4 White has only one point of penetration into the continuous camp - f4, and at the same time two pieces, the king and the knight, cannot get through it. But Karpov discovers a mysterious knight maneuver.

1. Ng2!! Psychologically, such a move doesn’t even come to mind.

1...hg+2.Kp:g3 Ke6 3. Kf4+ Kpf5 4.K:h5 Ke6 5.Kf4+ Kpd6 6.Kpg4 Cc2 7.Kph5 Cd1 8.Kpg6 Kpe7 9.K:d5+ Kpe 10.Kc7+ Kpd7 11.K:a6 C:f3 12.Kp:f6 Kpd6 13.Kpf5 Kpd5 14.Kpf4! It became clear that Black could not resist in this endgame. 14...Ch1 15.Kpe3 Kpc4 16.Kc5 Cc6 17.Kd3 Cg2 18.Ke5+ Kpc3 19.Kg6 Kpc4 20.Ke7 Cb7 21.Kf5 Cg2 22.Kd6+Kpb3 23.K:b5 Kpa4 24.Kd6. The blacks dealt.

G. Karpov - A. Karpov.
In this position, which arose at the end of the ninth game of the second match, everyone expected 1.K:d4 C:e4 2.K:e4 Q:e4 3.Ke6 Qe1+ 4.Ff1 with a difficult position for White.

1.e5! By opening the b1-h7 diagonal, White created the threat of 2.K:h5! gh 3.Фg8+ Kph6 4. Cc2. After thinking for half an hour, Karpov found a forced path to a draw. 1...C:f3 2.gf C:e5 3.f4! The moves e4 - e5 and f3 - f4 are geometric in nature. Thanks to them, White forces the black bishop to leave the large diagonal. Indeed, missing the a1 - h8 diagonal is very dangerous in view of 4.f5!, approaching the black king. 3...C:f4 4.Qg8+ Kph6 5.Cc2 Qg7! As expected in this one, subtle acting is demonstrated on both sides. After 5...Qf6 6.Ke4! White forced it, 5...d3 6.C:d3 Qf6 7.Ke4 Qa1+ 8.Kpg2 Kc6 9.Qf8+ with a win is also not good. 6.Q:d8 C:g3 7.Fg Qe5 8.Ff8+ Kpg5 9.Kpg2. The storm, having swept across the board, subsided, and the opponents agreed to a draw.

G. Karpov - A. Karpov.
Black thoughtlessly moved the rook from c8 to d8. 1.Q:d7!! R:d7 2.Jle8+ Kph7 3.Ce4+. Black has dealt, after 3...g6 4.J:d7 their position is hopeless.

Gracefully winning this game, the 11th of the match, Karpov equalized the score and intercepted the match. The second half proceeded with his superiority, and as a result, the chess crown found a new owner.

Magazine reader L. So-kolov dedicated an interesting serial problem to grandmasters A. Karpov and G. Kasparov.

Serial cooperative checkmate in 7 moves with the black king on a7 and h7.

There are two problems here at once: one is obtained by moving the black king to a7 (A. Karpov is the 12th chess king!), the other is obtained by moving the black king to h7 (G. Karpov is the 13th chess king!).

Here is the solution to the problem with the king on a7: 1—7.b7—b5—b4—b3—b2—b1C! - Ce4 - Cb7. On the b7 square the bishop appeared together with pawns, and now follows Cc1—e3×. With the king on h7: 1 - 7.g7-g5-g4-g3-g2-g1C!-Cd4-Cg7 and Cd3×.
