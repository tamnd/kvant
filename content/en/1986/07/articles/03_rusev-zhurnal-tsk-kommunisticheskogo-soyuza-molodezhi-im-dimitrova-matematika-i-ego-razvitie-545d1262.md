Journal of the Central Committee of the Communist Youth League
them. Dimitrova
"Mathematics"
and its development

The People's Republic of Bulgaria is a country of a mature socialist society. Its citizens are creative individuals, fighters and transformers. He searches and analyzes, opening new paths in science and practice. Therefore, it is not enough for him to simply acquire modern knowledge; he must learn to think independently and effectively, and to approach the problems of life creatively. Hence the need for training personnel who were able to navigate changing conditions, the latest achievements of science and technology, and independently solve problems. We must prepare the generation so that it can organize the achievements of the scientific and technological revolution with the use of the socialist system, create and implement new things in science and science.

Mathematics has always been the basis of technical progress, therefore the Bulgarian Communist Party and the government considered the important issue of increasing the mathematical culture of young people.

In 1962, the Secretariat of the Central Committee of the BCP decided to create the magazine “Mathematics” for secondary school students. The magazine was to be published six times a year and was assigned the following tasks:

— introduce schoolchildren in a popular and attractive form to the major achievements of mathematics, its history and its creators;
- explain in more depth and detail the issues studied in the school mathematics course;
- support the practical applications of mathematics and its educational value;
— help students with extracurricular mathematical work;
— reflect interesting events in international mathematical life;
— introduce students to leading Russian mathematicians;
- interfere with materials on the mathematics being studied.

Our magazine began to solve these problems in its popular science articles and sections: “From the history of mathematics”, “Znamennye mathematicians”, “Competition problems”, “Problems”, “Problems for the matriculation certificate”, “Entertaining mathematics”, etc.

"School" topics

Popular science articles are related to curriculum; on the one hand, they relate to topics that expand the knowledge of schoolchildren on this issue, and on the other hand, to topics that deepen their knowledge. The averages of these topics are called:

1. Basic types of set mappings and numeric mappings.
2. Defined intergal and its applications.
3. Principles of continuity.
4. Vectors and their applications.
5. Properties of triangles and quadrilaterals.
6. Correct examples.
7. Equations and systems of equations.
8. Inequalities and systems of inequality.
9. Algebraic operations and their properties.

The journal covers not only topics from classical sections of mathematics, but also sections such as combinatorial analysis, mathematical logic, solution and graph theory, mathematical literature, many chapters of computational mathematics, that is, those sections that can be combined under a common title.

Discrete mathematics

The goals that precede the article in this direction are the following:

1. Strengthening the applied direction. The connection between discrete mathematics, the use of computers and computational mathematics, on the one hand, and applications to economics and humanities, on the other, makes the study of discrete mathematics necessary for most special secondary education.

2. Introduction to mathematical modeling. Discrete models have their own characteristics that allow them to be specialized (linear programming, mathematical models of languages, etc.).

3. Disclosure of the essential role of discrete mathematics for improving the computing culture of students.

4. Introduction to combinatorial analysis, which is becoming increasingly important in applied mathematics in our time. Many sections of probability theory and other disciplines are also associated with combinator.

5. Improving students' logical literacy.

Exactly what topics in this direction should be covered is a difficult question. But the editorial board of the magazine believes that they should first of all be ones that can be associated with the topics of the school course.

Scientific worldview

In connection with one of the main tasks of secondary school - the education of a scientific worldview - the magazine "Matematika" publishes articles showing the origin of mathematical concepts, explaining the role of mathematical abstractions, the power of mathematical methods that make it possible to simply and under certain conditions use questions of the natural world - physics, biology, astronomy and other sciences. By focusing special attention on the philosophical foundations of mathematics, we contribute to a deeper understanding of the subject and its methods, a broader view of the subject itself and its place in the system of scientific knowledge, and its role as a driving force of progress. Here are examples of articles in this direction: “Matematika and objective reality”, “Mathematical methods of punishment”, “Matematika as a profession”, “Talent and aesthetics in scientific creativity”, “Music of mathematics”, “Mathematical approach to physical considerations”.

History of mathematics

Familiarization with the history of science and scientific discoveries plays a huge role in the development of the scientific worldview of young people. The classics of Marxism-Leninism also point to the need for a scientific approach. By studying the complex path traveled by science, we can, to a certain extent, come closer to understanding its role today. Here are a few examples of articles presented for this purpose: “Ideas of N. I. Lobachevsky”, “From the history of mathematics”, “First steps from the history of counting”, “Matematika of the ancient peoples of Mesopotamia”, “From the history of logarithms”, “From number to figure”.
We also note the special place we devote to stories about the development of Bulgarian mathematics.
In the section “Famous Mathematicians,” readers are introduced to the lives of major scientists in the field of mathematics and, taking into account the level of readers’ training, with reviews of the scientific work of these scientists. We also include biographies of prominent Bulgarian mathematicians and some of their leading works.

The magazine “Matematika” strives to explore the work of students through the heading “Student Creativity” and “Competition Problems”. Back in the very first issue of the magazine, in the “Student Creativity” section, an article by a 9th grade student “Inequality between the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean of some numbers” was published. In the first years of the magazine's existence, under this heading, mainly articles were published containing original solutions to the problem with international experiments. After becoming the editor of the magazine, she began to more actively manage student creativity, and abstracts from school theoretical conferences in mathematics began to appear on the pages of the magazine.

Category “Competition problems”

This section, created from the very birth of the Matematika magazine, aimed to attract the most capable students to independent creative work at the task level of international experiments. To a certain extent, the progressive concepts of Gyorg Pøly were implemented here, namely that in the classroom it is possible to create such an environment that students themselves strive to determine the research work.

In the period from 1962 to 1967, we published problems (and then their solutions) and a list of students who were able to solve these problems. After 1968, original solutions and generalizations of the problems of schoolchildren themselves were published, and since 1969, also problems compiled by schoolchildren. At the same time, greater importance is attached to the ability to pose and solve problems that arise when studying the real world.

Sections for younger students

Since 1980, the magazine “Matematika” has published 10 issues daily. The main goals and objectives of the magazine have not changed, but it is distributed through materials for primary schoolchildren (grades 4-6). The headings “Stats for juniors” and “Tasks for juniors” have been created. In articles for juniors, situations that are close to practice and to intuitive ideas are studied, first of all, and preference is given to the element involved, dialogue. Their main role, however, is to instill in students from an early age a taste for the beauty of mathematical exercises and, to the extent possible, independent work. Problems for younger schoolchildren are especially carefully selected. Students in grades 5, 6 and 7 have the opportunity to participate in a correspondence competition to solve these problems. The twenty best seventh-graders receive the right to continue their education at a mathematical gymnasium without competitive exams.

Information

Since the first issue in 1985, our magazine has increased its volume. At the same time, additional volume is now allocated to a new section devoted to information issues. Its goals, defined in economic language, are as follows:

1. Develop the intellectual abilities of students, introduce them to such mathematical concepts as system, model, information, algorithm, program control, format languages, etc.

2. Expand and deepen the concept of mathematical modeling using the example of real devices and technology, management and service processes, introduce them to their use, optimization and algorithmization.

3. To reveal the possibility of information and mathematics for solving scientific, technical and economic problems and, on their basis, to introduce quantitative methods of solution.

In conclusion, we note that for more than 20 years, the magazine “Matematika” has been a good helper for secondary school students. This is evidenced by at least the magazine's triple figure, which increased from 15,000 (in 1962) to 78,000 (in 1986).

The editorial team strives to satisfy the interests of the reader as fully as possible and help in training personnel with broad mathematical knowledge. That's why we are constantly looking for various forms of contact with readers.
