Test 3. Take a saucer or small plate. Place a blotter soaked in a solution of table salt at the bottom, and sprinkle crystals of potassium permanganate on top of the blotter. We will strengthen two graphite electrodes at opposite ends and connect them to the source (to two batteries connected in series). The crystals will begin to dissolve, and we will see that crimson tongues will stretch towards the positive electrode. By measuring the speed of their movement, which is easy to do with a watch and a ruler, you can estimate the speed of mixing of ions in the solution. It is clear that it will depend on many factors, and above all, on the source voltage. The effect of electric current does not pass through the electrolyte without leaving a trace. It causes irreversible chemical changes in it.

Test 4. Prepare a solution of table salt, add a few drops of phenolphthalene to it and lower the ends of the electrodes connected to the poles of the battery. After some time, the solution at the negative electrode turns crimson. This indicates that alkali has appeared in the solution, that is, a chemical reaction has occurred.

Effectively, when the chain was closed, the positive sodium ions Na+ began to move towards the cathode, and the negative chlorine ions Cl− - towards the andus. Sodium reduction reaction occurs at the cathode

Na+ + e− = Na0,

which immediately reacts with water, resulting in the formation of an alkali:

2Na + 2H2O = 2NaOH + H2↑.

On the Andes, another process occurs at this time - the formation of chlorine gas:

2Cl− − 2e− = Cl2↑.

That is why this experiment must be carried out in a well-conducted room.

So, when electric current passes through electrolytes (they are also called conductors of the second kind, in contrast to conductors of the first kind, for example metals, in which the current carriers are free electrons), a chemical transformation occurs in them. As a result, the substances included in the electrolyte can be released in free form. This phenomenon is called electrolysis.

Electrolysis has many practical applications. First of all, to obtain pure metals. So, for example, you can get magnesium, sodium, aluminum. Currently, all industrial aluminium is produced in special installations - electrolyzers (Fig. 2) - by electrolysis of a melt mixture of aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) and cryolite (Na₃AlF₆). In this case, the following happens at the cathode:

$$\text{Al}^{3+} + 3e^- = \text{Al}^0.$$

It is clear that we will not receive alominium, but we will try to get some copper.

Test 5. Prepare a concentrated solution of copper sulfate (CuSO₄) and lower two graphite electrodes connected to the battery strips into it. After some time, a red coating of copper will appear on the cathode:

$$\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- = \text{Cu}^0.$$

Let's become alchemists for a few minutes and try to “transform” one metal into another. To do this, we will repeat the following experiments, only together with the negative electrode we will lower an alchemist's path (for example, from a school uniform) into the solution. In the process of electrolysis, together with alchemy's, a copper track will be obtained.

Coating objects with a layer of metal using electrolysis is called galvanization. You can metallize not only metal objects, but also objects made of wood and fabric. With sufficient care, you can make products that are not inferior in beauty to a carpet maker. For example, you can cover sheets of plants, lace, and dead insects with a layer of copper. In order to make them hard, you need to support them for several minutes in melted wax. Then coat them evenly with a layer of graphite to make them conductive and place them in a galvanizing bath.

Teler, let's take up another, no less interesting job - making galvanoplastic copies. As an original, you can take some child’s image, for example from plaster, or make it yourself from paraffin. To get a copper copy, you must first thoroughly rub the surfaces of the original with graphite powder, and then wrap it with thin copper wire so that it touches the graphite layer. The wire must be connected to the negative pole of the battery and the prepared form should be immersed in a 20% solution of copper sulphate. Then the process will proceed in the same way as when receiving a copper purchase. When the thickness of the copper reaches 1-2 millimeters, the mold should be removed from the solution and the metal copy should be carefully separated from it.

The method for electrolytically producing metal copies was discovered by the Russian scientist V. S. Jacobi. Galvanoplasty immediately found wide practical application. In particular, in printing, which allows for a significant increase in the quality of text and illustrations. Jacobi himself did a lot to introduce galvanoplasty into printing and coinage, as well as for the production of artistic products. And at present, the scope of application of galvanoplasty in technology is general and varied.
