However, the types of Bravais lattices do not exhaust the variety of symmetry properties of lattices. After all, as you have already seen from the example of hexagonal close packing, Bravais lattices can be left one inside the other! As a result, symmetry elements are combined in various combinations, so-called space groups are formed. There are all 230 different groups. All of them were found in 1890 by the Russian scientist E. S. Fedorov.

So, any crystal can be composed of repeating parts. This property of crystals is called translational symmetry (translation - transfer in space). Otherwise, they also say that crystals have long-range order. This is perhaps the most characteristic property of crystals, which distinguishes them from all other bodies.

There is, however, an equally important class of substances - amorphous bodies in which there is no long-range order. Liquid bodies are in an amorphous state. But a solid body can also be amorphous. The simplest example is ordinary glass. figure 10 shows the structure of glass and quaria, which has the same chemical composition as glass. Quartz is a crystal, and glass is an amorphous body. Although there is no less order in glass than this, it does not mean that there is complete chaos in the arrangement of atoms. A certain structure in the arrangement of close neighbors, as can be seen from figure, is also preserved in glass. They say that amorphous bodies have a close order.

Amorphous materials have recently found important applications in technology. Unique properties of amorphous metal alloys (metallic glasses). They are obtained by cooling liquid metal very quickly - at a speed of the order of several thousand degrees per second. This can be achieved, for example, by sprinkling small drops of metal on the surface of a fast-running cold disk. The drop is “smeared” over the disk in a very thin layer (several micrometers thick), and it is good for the warm-water metal to cool so quickly that its atoms simply do not have time to arrange themselves correctly. It turned out that amorphous alloys have increased hardness, high corrosion resistance, and an optimal combination of electrical and magnetic properties. The scope of such materials is expanding rapidly.
