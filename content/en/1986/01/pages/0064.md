1. It is most convenient to connect the reporting system with an inclined plane and direct the coordinate axes as shown in figure 12. Consider the movement of the ball between two successive impacts on the plane.
Since the projection of the ball's rooting onto the axis $OY$ is constant: $a_y = g\cos \alpha = const$, the projections of velocity onto the same axis immediately after the first impact and immediately before the second impact are equal in magnitude. Due to the fact that the impacts are elastic, the velocity projections before the impacts are the same as after them. Thus, for all impacts $|v_y| = v_0\sin \beta$, where $v_0$ is the speed of the ball at the moment of the first collision with the plane.
Time t between two successive impacts on the movement of the ball along the $OY - y = |v_y|t - g\cos \alpha \cdot t^2 / 2$ axis, assuming $y = 0$ at $t = \tau$:

$$\tau = \frac{2|v_y|}{g\cos \alpha} = \frac{2v_0\sin \beta}{g\cos \alpha}.$$

4. The action of the lens-mirror optical system in this case is as follows. The lens creates a minimum image of an object $A$ at a distance $f_1 = d_1F / (F - d_1)$ ($d_1$ is the distance of the object from the line) with magnification

$$\Gamma_1 = \frac{f_1}{d_1} = \frac{F}{(F - d_1)}.$$

This image is reflected in the mirror (without changing dimensions), and the resulting reflection is a new object for the line, located at a distance $d_2 = 2F + f_1$ from it. The lens creates an image of this object at a distance of $f_2 = Fd_2 / (d_2 - F)$ with magnification

$$\Gamma_2 = \frac{f_2}{d_2} = \frac{F}{d_2 - F} = \frac{F - d_1}{F}.$$

General increase in system

$$\Gamma = \Gamma_1\Gamma_2 = 1$$

- it does not depend on the initial distance between the object and the line.
This can also be verified by plotting the path of rays in a given optical system.
