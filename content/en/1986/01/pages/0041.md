the first 4 observation points with mismo “run” 10 marks. How long will it take for the disk to stop? After what period of time will the marks “run” in the other direction?

After turning off the disk, it begins to brake evenly (with dry friction, the forces during braking do not change in magnitude). In 4 seconds at the nominal speed, 400 marks must pass, and the disk “lags behind” by 10 - which means that during this time the disk speed has dropped by $2 \cdot \frac{10}{400} = \frac{1}{20}$ from the nominal value. Consequently, the disk will stop in $4 \cdot 20 = 80$ seconds, and after 40 seconds it will “run” for the first time in the other direction.

A. R. Zilberman

F954. A decorative square curtain measuring $1,5 \times 1,5$ m hangs on a cornice along a vertical wall. The lower edge of the curtain is lifted in line with the top, so that the curtain is folded in two, and released. Find the difference between the force acting on the cornice and time. The curtain is thin, smooth and weighs 3 kg.

♦
Let us consider the position of the curtain after time $t$ after the start of its fall. Since the curtain is soft, its left part falls freely, and the right part is motionless (see figure). In this case, at the moment of time $t$ the left part of the movement with a speed $gt$. Since the curtain is soft and thin, we neglect the size of the inflection area and its momentum. After a short time interval $\Delta t$, another part of the curtain with the mass

$$\Delta m = \frac{m}{d^2} gt \frac{d \cdot \Delta t}{2}$$

(d - side of the curtain), which had an impulse before braking

$$\Delta p = \Delta m \cdot gt = \frac{m}{d} (gt)^2 \frac{\Delta t}{2}.$$

Let us apply the law of momentum change to this cuckoo curtain to determine the force $T(t)$ acting on it from the hanging part of the curtain (according to Newton’s third law, exactly the same magnitude of force will act downward on the hanging part of the curtain). So,

$$T(t) - \Delta m \cdot g = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{m}{2d}(gt)^2,$$

where

$$T(t) \big|_{\Delta t \to 0} = \frac{m}{2d}(gt)^2.$$

Since the right part of the curtain is motionless, the sum of the forces acting on it at any moment of time is zero. Therefore, at time $t$ we obtain

$$T(t) + \frac{m}{2} g + \frac{m}{2d^2} d \frac{gt^2}{2} g - N(t) = 0,$$

where $N(t)$ is the force acting from the side of the cornice on the curtain at time $t$.

Thus,

$$N(t) = \frac{m}{2d}(gt)^2 + \frac{m}{2} g + \frac{m(gt)^2}{4d} =$$

$$= \frac{mg}{2} \left| 1 + \frac{3}{2} \frac{gt^2}{d} \right| = 15 \left[ 1 + 10t^2 \right] H$$

($t \leq \sqrt{\frac{2d}{g}} \approx \sqrt{0,3} \text{ c.}$).

S. S. Krotov
