⟦folio 13⟧

Traces in the sand and... the structure of matter

Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
L. G. ASLAMAZOV

Of all the two hundred billion men, women and children who have ever walked on wet sand since the modern world until the meeting of the British Association at Aberdeen in 1885, how many are there who, when asked, “Did the sand shrink under your foot?” would answer differently than “yes”!
Lord Kelvin, Baltic Lectures, 1904

Have you ever thought about the answer to this question? At first glance, it seems that by compacting the sand, we always make it denser, darken the sands and press them closer to each other. But in reality the matter may be the circumstance itself. And the proof of this is the traces that remain on some occasions when you step on wet sand near the seashore or river. This is what O. Reynolds, a scientist known for his work on hydrodynamics, said about this in a report at a meeting of the British Association in 1885: “When the Hora presses on sand, dense after a gentle tide, the area around the foot immediately becomes dry... The pressure of the foot loosens the sand, and the stronger it is, the more water leaves... It makes the sand dry until the blue arrives sufficient amount of water."
