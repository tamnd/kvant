$$I = \frac{|\Phi_{\text{BH}}|}{L} = \frac{SnB_0}{L} = \frac{\pi R^2 nB_0}{L}.$$

Let this current be created by the movement of $N$ electrons along the turns of the coil. Then

$$I = \frac{N}{2\pi Rn} ev,$$

where $e$ is the charge of the electron, $v$ is the speed of the ordered movement of the electrons.

Total impulse of all electrons

$$p = Nmv = 2\pi RnI \frac{m}{e}.$$

When the current disappears, this impulse is transmitted to the coil. Let us pay attention to the fact that in this case the problem of transferring a rotational impulse is reduced to the problem of transferring a linear impulse. In the above formula, under $p$, you need to remember the arithmetic sum of the pulses of all the electrons, equal (after the current disappears) to the arithmetic sum of the pulses of all the elements of the coil. The coil acquires kinetic energy after the disappearance of the current

$$E_{\kappa} = p^2/2M.$$

The angle of twist of the thread will be found from the law of conservation of energy:

$$\frac{p^2}{2M} = \frac{G\varphi^2_{\max}}{2},$$

where

$$\varphi_{\max} = \frac{p}{\sqrt{MG}} = \frac{2\pi^2 R^3 n^2 B_0}{L \frac{e}{m}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{MG}} \approx 1,2 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ рад.}$$

$$\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_i} = \frac{\sin a_i}{\sin a_{i+1}},$$

where

$$n_{i+1} \sin a_{i+1} = n_i \sin a_i.$$

Thus, the product of the refractive index and the sine of the angle of incidence does not change when moving from layer to layer and remains constant for all words through which the beam passes.
