⟦folio 51⟧

Find the length of an edge of a cube whose volume is $\sqrt{3}$ times the volume of a given paramid.

Solution. If $a$ is the side of the base, then $r = \frac{a \sqrt{3}}{6}$, that is, $a = 2r\sqrt{3}$. Then the area of ​​the base of the paramid
$$S_{\text{occh}} = \frac{a^2\sqrt{3}}{4} = 3r^2\sqrt{3}.$$

The radius of a circle circumscribed about a regular triangle is 2 times greater than the radius of a circle inscribed in this triangle, so $|OB| = 2r$. From $\triangle SBO$ we get $|SO| = 2r \text{tg} \alpha$ (Fig. 2). Paramid volume
$$V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 3r^2\sqrt{3} \cdot 2r \text{tg} \alpha = 2r^3\sqrt{3} \text{tg} \alpha.$$

Having designated the desired length of the edge of the cube as $x$, we compose the equation
$$x^3 = \sqrt{3} \cdot 2r^3\sqrt{3} \text{tg} \alpha,$$
where
$$x = r^3\sqrt{6 \text{tg} \alpha}.$$
Let us now express $tg \alpha$ through the angle function $\varphi$ using formula (9):
$$\sin \alpha = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \text{ctg} \frac{\varphi}{2},$$
So
$$\text{tg} \alpha = \frac{\cos \frac{\varphi}{2}}{2\sqrt{\sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{6}\right) \sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6}\right)}}.$$

Pozny
$$x = r \sqrt[3]{\frac{\cos \frac{\varphi}{2}}{2\sqrt{\sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{6}\right) \sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6}\right)}}} = r \sqrt[3]{\frac{\cos \frac{\varphi}{2}}{6\sqrt{\sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{6}\right) \sin \left(\frac{\varphi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6}\right)}}}.$$

1 (LSU, 1979). The side of the base of a regular quadrangular paramid is equal to $a$, the angle between adjacent lateral edges is equal to $a$. Find the area of ​​the lateral surface of the paramid.

2 (Kiev Polytechnic Institute, 1980). Find the volume of a regular quadrangular paramid with sides of the base $a$ and a plane angle at the vertex equal to the angle of inclination of the side edge of the paramid to the plane of the base.

3 (KSU, 1980). Find the values ​​of the two geometric lateral edges of a regular hexagonal paramid inscribed in a sphere, knowing that it is equal to the angle at which the lateral edge of the paramid is visible from the center of the sphere.

4 (MAI, 1980). In a regular quadrangular paramid $SABCD$, the angle between the perpendiculars $AL$ and $AM$, dropped from the point $A$ to the lateral faces $SBC$ and $SCD$, is $a$. Find the volume of the paramid if the volume of a cube whose edge is equal to the side of the base of the paramid is equal to $v$.

5 (Kiev Polytechnic Institute, 1980). Find the volume of a regular quadrangular paramid if its side edge is equal to $l$, and the angle of inclination of the side faces to the plane of the base is equal to $a$.

6 (Tashkent State University, 1981). The volume of a cone inscribed in a regular quadrangular paramid is equal to $Q$. The dihedral angle formed by adjacent lateral edges is $a$. Find the length of the side of the base of the paramid.

7 (KSU, 1981). The side of the base of a regular quadrangular paramid is equal to $a$, the dihedral angle between the lateral edges is equal to $a$. Calculate the volume and lateral surface of the paramid.

8 (Kiev Institute of Civil Engineering, Civil Engineering, 1981). The total surface of a regular quadrangular paramid is equal to $S$, and the plane angle at the vertex of the lateral face is equal to $a$. Find the height of the paramid.

9 (KSU, 1983). In a right quadrangular paramid, the sum of the magnitude of the dihedral angles between adjacent and opposite lateral edges is equal to $180^\circ$. Find these angles.

10. For a right $n$-gonal paramid, the side of the base is equal to $a$ and the dihedral angle at the lateral edge is equal to $a$. Find the volume of the paramid.
