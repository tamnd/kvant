Interview with Academician Yu. A. Osipyan

The editor-in-chief of our journal, Academician Yu. A. Osipyan, is a famous physicist, director of the Institute of Solid State Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences, professor of the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. In a conversation conducted with Yu. A. Osipyan by correspondent «Kvant» and presented by our reader, he decided about the formation of a young scientist, about the role of the individual in science, about the rapidly developing new direction - dislocation physics, about the features of the work of a modern researcher.

Yuri Andreevich, what was your path into science?

— I graduated from school in 1949. But even earlier, in grades 8-9, it was clear to me that I would study physics. I was very interested in metal physics. Where to study next?

At that time, faculties were just emerging that combined university-wide physical education with real knowledge in specific areas. And I learned that the science of metals was carried out by the Faculty of Engineering and Physics of the Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys (now called the Faculty of Physics and Chemistry). I decided to go there, I entered and was very pleased, because the institute had a whole bunch of good teachers in both the theoretical and experimental fields of physics. I was able to gain quite a lot of useful knowledge there, which will affect me throughout my entire scientific life.

When the time for my thesis came, life brought me together with Academician G.V. Kurdyumov, a recognized leader of our metal physics. At the Institute of Metal Physics, which he headed, under the guidance of Professor B. Ya. Lyubov, I did my diploma.

At first I started doing experiments. But, analyzing the experimental conditions, I encountered theoretical problems. Got a taste for theory; I thought that I could do something theoretically and how. The knowledge of physics and mathematics that had accumulated by that time made it possible to solve what seems to me an interesting problem - about the mechanism of low-temperature phase transformations in metals.

— Was this actually your first scientific work?

- Yes. We often encounter facts of transformations associated with changes in the state of aggregation - this is melting, crystallization, condensation. However, facic transformations can also occur in already solidified metals. The problem with the condition is how to explain that some of them go very quickly at low temperatures. According to the laws that govern phase transitions, these transformations must take years - the ends meet have not been preserved...

An idea arose: did quantum effects influence these phenomena at such temperatures? It should be noted that the application of quantum mechanical concepts to phase transitions was still a novelty at that time. However, when I did the calculations taking into account this new factor, it turned out that “all the best.” Honestly speaking, I really liked the work, and it quickly became famous among metals.

And so, having defended my thesis, I received the qualification of a theorist. True, by that time I was already dissatisfied -

— It is known that you became the organizer of the new institute - solid state physics - at the age of 30. Did this distract you from your actual scientific work?

- Distracted, and how! But I was calmed and reassured by the thought that as soon as the institute was formed, the opportunity for conducting experiments would immediately increase many times over. There is another side to the matter: during the creation of the institute and during my further work there, I had the opportunity to collaborate with many outstanding scientists who had a huge impact on our activities. Judge for yourself.

Of course, among them was my scientific supervisor, Academician G.V. Kurdyumov, who formed the scientific directions of the new institution and entrusted me with heading it. This is also the then chairman of the USSR Academy of Sciences M.V. Keldysh. He, too, was not afraid of my youth and strongly supported my appointment. Academician N. N. Semenov, director of the Institute of Chemical Physics, actively helped me and provided me with the opportunity to create our institute within his own. And when there was not even a task yet, we could put together a scientific team.

The scientific problem was greatly influenced by the staff of the Institute of Physical Problems, headed by Academician P. L. Kapitsa. Then I had to talk with him many times and use his advice. I am proud that at that time I had the opportunity to communicate with L. A. Artsimovich, Academician-Secretary of the Department of General Physics and Astronomy...

It is gratifying to remember that at that time we received a lot of help from physicists who worked at other institutes—academicians V.K. Vainshtein, A.I. Shalnikov, and Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences Yu.V. Sharvin. After all, for a long time I was the only full-time employee of the new institute, united in design organizations, drawing how everything would be located. There was absolutely nothing - a forest and a swamp in Chernogolovka near Moscow.

“And so the institute was created and has been operating for almost a quarter of a century. Were your hopes met?

— Yes, now I can say with satisfaction that our team is dedicated, efficient, and we have acquired a fairly good reputation as a physics institute in the Academy of Sciences system, and have obtained interesting and significant results. But our successes must definitely be supported by other institutes located in Chernogolovka, with which we closely interact. Of course, this is the Institute of Chemical Physics. Its employees, who have a very good knowledge of methods of influencing solids, synthesize new materials. Without contact with them it would be difficult for us.

We cannot do without daily communication with scientists from the L. D. Landdu Institute of Theoretical Physics. Kostati, this scientific division arose at the expense of part of the theoretical research staff of the Institute of Physical Problems, whom I at one time “recruited” to our institute. But then a broader idea was born - not to limit ourselves to solving problems of solid state theory, but also to deal with theoretical problems of hydrodynamics, explosion, nuclear physics, that is, to create a kind of “think tank” of theoretical physics in Chernogolovka. This was done. And so people from the school of Academician L. D. Landdu began to work next to us - new academicians and corresponding members of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Abrikovov, L. P. Gorkov, I. E. Dzyaloshinsky, I. M. Khalatnikov. Major scientists then came to this institute - academicians A.V. Migdal, S.P. Novikov, corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A.I. Larkin, professors V.L. Pokrovsky, Ya.G. Sinai and others. Of course, this is a unique collection of theoretical and mathematical physics in one place.

I thought that such a “concentration” of theoretical thought, including the theory of solids, did not exist anywhere in the world. You see, when talking about science, one cannot help but talk about individuals.

Why did I dwell in such detail on the theoretical ones? It’s just that many problems cannot be solved without them. In addition, communication with theorists is specific; it is stimulating. If you want to go to a theorist to ask something, you need to grow to the level of concretely posing the question and speak to him in a special language. All this requires preliminary work and reflection. I believe that contacts with theorists improve the skills of experimenters.

— Yuri Andreevich, what exactly did you do at the new institute? How did your personal scientific plans come to fruition?

— While the construction of the institute was progressing, I continued to think about some aspects of the physical nature of the solidity of solids.

It is known that there are practically no crystals with an ideal structure. They always have defects, in particular so-called dislocations - one-dimensional violations of the crystal lattice, volume lines of atomic planes in the crystal. So, the theory of dislocation served to explain the processes of plastic deformation and strength, this was the main purpose of this theory. But I wanted to find out whether dislocations affect not only the mechanical, but also the physical properties of a solid?

On its electronic, optical, magnetic properties? I tried to find out if dislocations interact with electrons? And how is this interaction expressed in the behavior of electrons and dislocations? It turned out that here lies the answer to many questions that were then raised in solid state physics.

For example, the photoplastic effect was discovered, which revolutionized, in general, ideas about the nature of crystal hardness. It was believed that in order to carry out plastic deformation of a crystal, something must be “created” with the crystal lattice, and that changes in the state of electrons cannot affect the plastic properties.

But I, together with my collaboration I.V. Savchenko, managed to observe the following. When the light was turned on, some crystals experienced an increase in their resistance to plastic deformation by 30-40, and sometimes by 100 percent. Turned off the light - read dropped to the original value. This was very strange, because as we knew, the light that we turned on does nothing with the grating, only with electrons, and they should not affect the reading!

Ten years of painstaking—both experimental and theoretical—work made it possible to establish the nature of this now widely known effect. Light created electrons, their state affected dislocations, and dislocations influenced plasticity.

On the other hand, the presence of a dislocation in a crystal affects its electrical resistance and other electrical properties. And then we came across many new phenomena. In general, a fairly broad field has emerged, which we call dislocation physics. This has been the subject of my—and many students’—scientific activity to this day. And it must be said that the work of our institute largely determines the state of this scientific field.

— What is the current state of affairs in solid state physics in general?

— Today is characterized by the fact that the role and volume of research in solid state physics is growing noticeably. Its, so to speak, specific gravity within all of physics is notified. This is expressed in the fact that it has more and more scientific employees, more funds are allocated to it, more appropriate equipment is allocated, and it produces more “products.”

There are many fundamental problems in solid state physics. It can only seem that she is busy with private issues. Nothing like it! For example, recently it was possible to conduct an experiment that makes it possible to determine with great accuracy the value of the world CONSTANTS - the ratio of electron contamination to its mass. This has never happened in solid-state experiments. The indicator of public evaluation of work in solid state physics serves

If the design of applications of this scientific field to practice, then I dare to say that solid body physics gives more than any other field. Well, look, I will name only three directions.

First: Structural materials - everything from which buildings and machines are constructed, all material. Scientists need to “put” physical knowledge under the concept of the origins of processes here.

Second. Regulation and automation. Here, every time, the “heart” of the devices is a sensor of some signal. As a rule, this is a solid body - a piece of semiconductor or metal. And then the system is “busy” with the success or processing of the received signal.

And third. The scientific and technological revolution associated with computing technology and computerization is based on solid body physics. Without its access, in principle, modern computers would not be possible, because only solid-state electronic circuits - at reasonable costs - can process the enormous required amount of information.

So solid body physics is the foundation for solving many applied problems; many issues of intensifying production and technical reconstruction of the national economy are associated with it. These issues receive limited attention today. In implementing the plans for accelerating the scientific and technical process outlined by the party, our science calls for everyone to contribute.

— Schoolchildren often imagine that they can study physics only by being a “cog” in a huge team of scientists. Is this true?

- Yes and no. For example, work on nuclear physics, elemental particle physics requires the simultaneous conditions of a large number of people, especially in an experiment. Huge machines are needed - accelerators, nuclear reactors. And to create them, and to maintain and carry out experiments, we need really large teams.

In our field, in solid body physics, a lot of experiments are carried out, sometimes, as in classical physics, by one or two researchers. For example, the experiences I talked about. And therefore, the reasonable design of the experiment plays no less important role than the unique technical capabilities of its implementation. There is still room for ingenious, I would say, individual experiences.

This manner of work was reflected in the fact that our institute was built as a team of largely independently working employees. We always, however, welcome interaction, but not perarchical, but as people of equals, assorted people.

This is not the first time I am talking today about personality in science. Probably because two people had a great influence on me - academicians G.V. Kurdyumov and P.L. Kapitsa. Both are known as people who themselves have done something concrete in science, although, of course, they are also leaders and organizers of the scientific work of entire teams. I would like to educate my employees and students so that they do not forget this in any way and show their individuality in every possible way. Speaking about solid body physics, I want to emphasize that this is an area of ​​physics where creative people are simply needed.

— Can this be considered as an invitation to the reader of “Kvat”?

- Of course, but I didn’t want to decide in this case only about the physics of a solid body. In general, he studies physics - it’s very interesting. I just sympathize with those people who are not interested in physics, they simply have no idea how exciting it is. The ability to “digest” a large amount of information simultaneously, constructing logical circuits, testing hypotheses, determining the results obtained - physics is rich in all this. And how many other areas in which you can apply your strength, how much romance there is in those engaged in this science! Just, I thought, you don’t have to wait for the knowledge about where to study and what to do to come to you on its own. This knowledge must be sought and achieved. And collaborating with them will certainly help to gain additional interest in our field of activity, namely in science, specifically in physics.
