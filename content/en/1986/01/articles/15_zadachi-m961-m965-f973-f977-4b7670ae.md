**M942.** The numbers 1, 2, ..., 2n−1, 2n are divided into two groups of n numbers each. Let $a_1 < a_2 < ... < a_n$ be the numbers of the first group in ascending order, $b_1 > b_2 > ... > b_n$ be the numbers of the second group in descending order. Prove that $|a_1 - b_1| + |a_2 + b_2| + ... + |a_n - b_n| = n^2$.

Note that of the two numbers $a_k$ and $b_k$, one is always less than $n$, and the other is always less than $n$. Indeed, if, for example, $a_k \leq n$ and $b_k \leq n$, then all numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_k$ and $b_k, b_{k+1}, ..., b_n$ do not exceed $n$. In total we get $k + (n - k + 1) = n + 1$ different natural numbers not exceeding $n$, which is impossible. Similarly, it is proved that both numbers $a_k$ and $b_k$ cannot be greater than $n$.

Thus, each of the terms $|a_k - b_k|$ in our sum is equal to the difference between a number greater than $n$ and a number not exceeding $n$. Expanding the model and rearranging the terms, we get

$$|a_1 - b_1| + |a_2 - b_2| + ... + |a_n - b_n| = (n + 1) + (n + 2) + ... + (n + n) - (1 + 2 + ... + n) = n^2.$$

V.V. Proizvolv

**M943.** The sequence $a_1, a_2, ...$ is given by the rules: $a_{2n} = a_n$ for $n \geqslant 1$ and $a_{4n+3} = 0$ for $n \geqslant 0$. Prove that this sequence has no period.

We will carry out the proof by contradiction. Let the sequence have period $T = 2^p \cdot q$, where $q$ is odd. If $q = 4m + 3$, then at $k \geqslant p + 2$

$$a_{2k} = a_{2k+T} = a_{2p(2^k - p + q)} = a_{2p-1(2^k - p + q)} = ...$$

$$... = a_{2k-p+4m+3} = a_{4(2^k - p - 2 + m)+3} = 0.$$

but at the same time

$$a_{2k} = a_{2k-1} = ... = a_1 = 1.$$

Contradiction.

If $q = 4m + 1$, then in the above reasoning the number $T$ must be replaced by the number $3T = 2^p (4 \cdot 3m + 3)$, which must also be the period of our sequence.

The sequence $(a_n)$ is associated with a replacement infinite broken line $A_0 A_1 A_2$... with equal links, each of which (starting from $A_1 A_2$) makes an angle $90^\circ$ with the previous one, and at the vertex $A_n (n \geqslant 1)$ the broken line turns to the left if $a_n = 1$, and to the right if $a_n = 0$. This broken line is called the “main broken line of the dragon” (see the article by N.V. Vasilyev and V.L. Gutenkhaer “Dragon Curves” in «Kvant» No. 2, 1970).

Yu. V. Nesterenko
