“The next lesson of our circle is related to solving geometric problems,” said the teacher. “These tasks are not entirely ordinary - we will reconstruct figures using some known elements. People faced similar problems back in ancient times, when they had to restore the boundaries of areas that were washed away as a result of the Nile flood.”

The teacher showed the children a fragment of a broken gramophone head and asked them to determine the radius of this head (the center of the head was not preserved, but some part of its edge was preserved).

The guys remembered that the center of the circle described around the triangle is located at the intersection point of the middle of the perpendiculars drawn to the sides of the triangle. So it's a circle, it's easy to find its radius. Therefore, to solve the problem with a plate, you can use triangle ABC, the vertices of which lie on the arc of the plastic circle (Fig. 1).

“The second problem is more difficult,” the teacher said and drew triangle ABC on the board. He drew medians in it and marked their bases D, E, F, then erased the triangle, leaving only the bases of the medians. The teacher suggested that the children restore the triangle.

Petya was the first to raise his hand. He went to the board and connected these points with segments. Segments DE, DF and EF are the midlines in triangle ABC. And the middle line is parallel to the side of the triangle. This gives the keys to solving the problem: through each of these points we need to check a straight line parallel to the non-passage of the middle line through it, and as a result of the intersection of these straight lines we will obtain the desired triangle (Fig. 2).

“Okay!” said the teacher. "Now - the third task." The teacher drew an acute triangle ABC on the board and drew two altitudes AE and BF in it. Then I erased the triangle again, leaving points E, F and part of the base - segment MN. “Restore the erased triangle,” said the teacher. The guys wondered. Seryozha connected points E and F. What next? If we could determine the position of points A and B - the ends of the base of the triangle - the problem would be solved. “What can you say about angles AFB and BEA?”

The teacher addressed the Krugkovites. “These angles are right,” the guys answered. Seryozha agrees that points E and F belong to the locus of points from which segment AB is visible at right angles. Such a locus of points is a circle*), the diameter of which is AB. You need to find the center of this circle. It lies on the line MN and on the perpendicular bisector drawn to the segment EF, that is, it is the intersection point of MN and this perpendicular (Fig. 3). Having identified points A and B, the guys easily found point C. Solution problem!

“The fourth task is the most difficult: you need to reconstruct the triangle from the bases of its three altitudes.”
Petya came to the board again. He drew a triangle ABC, drew altitudes in it, and marked their bases: H₁, H₂, H₃. I erased the triangle, connected the points H₁, H₂, H₃ and thought. The teacher advised him to make a sketch by hand, believing that the problem had already been solved, and suggested that the bicsectrices of the triangle H₁H₂H₃ belong to the altitudes of the triangle ABC. At the end of the lesson, the teacher offered four more problems to solve at home.

1. In triangle ABC, the median AD and height AH were drawn from vertex A, and the median BF was drawn from vertex B. The triangle has been erased, leaving only three points D, F and H. Restore the triangle.

2. Draw a parallelogram. It was then erased, leaving the middles of three sides. Reconstruct the parallelogram.

3. Given points M, N, K are the midpoints of three equal sides of a convex quadrilateral. Rebuild the quadrilateral.

4. Reconstruct the triangle if the midpoints of its two sides and the line on which the bicector lies to one of these sides lies.
