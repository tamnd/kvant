Symmetrical objects are often found in nature. The property of symmetry is possessed by honeycombs, colonies of viruses (photo on the left, taken with an electron microscope), and crystals (at the top are figure from the atlas to the tract of R. J. Gaüy, published at the beginning of the 19th century).

But why do bees build hexagonal honeycombs while viruses prefer pentagonal honeycombs? What repeating parts can be used to make a crystal? These issues are discussed in the article “Traces in the sand and... the structure of matter.”
