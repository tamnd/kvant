It is easy to see that formula (3) is equivalent to Heron’s formula: since $S=pr$, then taking into account (1) and (3) we obtain $S^2=p^2r^2=pxyz=p(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)$.

Second proof. Let $O_1$ and $r_1$ be the center and radius of the circle tangent to the side $BC$ and the extended sides $AC$ and $AB$, $F$ the point of tangency of this circle with the line $AB$ (Fig. 4). Then (see formulas (2)) $BF=p-c=z$. Triangles $ODB$ and $O_1BF$ are similar ($ODB=BFO_1=90^\circ$, $FBO_1=\frac{1}{2}CBF=\frac{1}{2}(180^\circ-CBD)=90^\circ-OBD=DOB$). Therefore, $OD:BF=DB:O_1F$, that is, $r:z=y:r_1$, whence $r_1=yz/r$. Priugangular triangles $AO_1F$ and $AOD$ are also similar, hence $AF:AD=O_1F:OD$, therefore $p:x=r_1:r$. Substituting $r_1=yz/r$ into this equality, we obtain $p:x=yz/r^2$, that is, $pr^2=xyz$, which, as was shown at the end of the first proof, is equivalent to Heron’s formula.

Third proof. Let us first prove that in any triangle

$$\text{ctg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{B}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{C}/2) = \text{ctg} (\hat{A}/2) \text{ctg} (\hat{B}/2) \text{ctg} (\hat{C}/2).$$

Really,

$$\text{ctg} (\hat{C}/2) = \text{ctg} ((180^\circ - \hat{A} - \hat{B})/2) = \frac{\text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)}{1 - \text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)},$$

pose

$$\text{ctg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{B}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{C}/2) = \frac{\text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)}{1 - \text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)}$$

$$= \frac{\text{ctg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{B}/2)}{1 - \text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)}$$

$$= \frac{\text{ctg} (\hat{A}/2) + \text{ctg} (\hat{B}/2)}{1 - \text{tg} (\hat{A}/2) \text{tg} (\hat{B}/2)}$$

Answer: $\sqrt{2} < a < \sqrt{5}$ or $-\sqrt{5} < a < -\sqrt{2}$.

3. In each of the three boundaries of the prism $ABCD_1B_1C_1D_1$, at the base of which $ABCD$ is a rhombus, $AC=8$, $BD=6$, $AA_1=1$, a ball is inscribed, touching a cone with the vertex at the point $O=(A_1C_1)\cap(B_1D_1)$ and the base inscribed in the rhombus $ABCD$.
