Problem 4. Due to the finite resolution of photographic film, objects located from the camera at distances from $d_1 = 15$ m to $d_2 = 30$ m are clearly depicted on it. Without changing the focus, the lens task is to blur the diameter of the open part of the lens). At the same time, the near limit of the depth of field became equal to $d'_1 = 10$ m. Find the far limit of the depth of field.

Let the size of the grain structure of the photographic emulsion be $a$, the diameter of the camera lens $b$, and its focal length $F$. If the camera is aimed at an object located at a distance of $d$ from the camera, the film is located from the lens at a distance of $f$, determined by the lens formula (Fig. 4):
$$f = \frac{dF}{d-F}.$$

An object located at a distance of $d_1 < d$ will appear clearly at a distance of $f_1 = d_1F/(d_1 - F)$. In this case, each point of this object will mark a circle on the film. If the diameter of this circle does not exceed the size of the granular emulsion, we will not notice defocus. The same will happen for objects located at a distance $d_2 > d$. Each point of objects is depicted

point at a distance $f_2 = d_2F/(d_2 - F)$ from the lens, and on the film it will form a circle, the diameter of which should not exceed $a$.

From the similarity of triangles on figure 4 we have
$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{f - f_2}{f_2} = \frac{f_1 - f}{f_1},$$
where do we get it from
$$\frac{2}{f} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2}. \tag{1}$$

If the lens diameter becomes $b'$, then
$$\frac{a}{b'} = \frac{f - f'_2}{f'_2} = \frac{f'_1 - f}{f'_1},$$
or
$$\frac{2}{f} = \frac{1}{f'_1} + \frac{1}{f'_2}, \tag{2}$$

Where
$$f'_2 = \frac{d'_2F}{d'_2 - F} \text{ и } f'_1 = \frac{d'_1F}{d'_1 - F}.$$

From (1) and (2) we find
$$\frac{1}{f'_2} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} - \frac{1}{f'_1},$$
whence, expressing $f$ in terms of $d$, we obtain
$$d'_2 = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{d_1} + \frac{1}{d_2} - \frac{1}{d'_1}} \Rightarrow \infty.$$

So, the far limit of the depth of field lies at infinity, and, therefore, all objects located further than 10 m from the lens are clearly depicted on the film.
