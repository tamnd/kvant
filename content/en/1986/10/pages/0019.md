The material for the pooches, which must withstand enormous skimming stresses, is tungsten carbide cemented with cobalt. This super-hard alloy is much stronger than steel.

The container is made from the clay mineral pyrophyllite or other similar materials. Pyrophyllite is a flat conductor of tell and electricity. This allows you to get high temperatures in the container using electric current for heating.

Graphite and a metal catalyst (iron, nickel or their alloys) are placed in the container and scimitared between the poochsons. When the required pressure ($p \geq 6$ GPa) is available, an electric current is passed through the pooches, which heats the contents of the container to a high temperature. In this way, conditions are created that correspond to the p-T region of diamond formation.

The process is monitored by the strength of the current flowing through the apparatus. Diamond is not electrically conductive, and its formation causes an increase in the electrical resistance of the contents of the container and, accordingly, a decrease in the current through the apparatus. After the process is completed, turn off the heating, allow the container to cool, remove the pressure and remove the container from the apparatus.

The resulting diamonds - crystals ranging in size from fractions of a millimeter to 1-2 millimeters - are washed in acids to remove particles of metal and graphite, sorted and sent for the manufacture of diamond tools.

Scientists have developed other methods for producing diamonds and their products. If the process is in the presence of a catalyst (accelerator) at higher pressures, then a huge number of tiny diamond crystals are formed simultaneously, which grow together into a durable black coating - carbonado. Carbonado is stronger and more resistant to impact than regular diamonds.

Polycrystalline diamonds can be produced into any desired shape and thus avoid the labor-intensive processing of diamond blocks. To do this, a workpiece made of a carbohydrate-containing material is given the required shape, taking into account shrinkage, placed in a tube heater, covered with powdered catalyst, and the assembly is placed in a container. After the formation of polycrystalline diamonds, the finished diamond product of a given shape is removed from the container - for example, in the form of a cutter. All that remains is to sharpen the cutting edge, secure it in the holder and place it on the machine. Diamond cutters are hundreds of times more durable than regular ones. They form hard alloys, metals, and plastics. Diamond cutters operate at enormous speeds and provide high surface cleanliness.

Diamond crystals sintered with a metal bond are used to make dusts and grinding wheels for cutting and processing hard metals, graphite, and stones.

Diamond bits and bits are used when drilling wells in rocks. At the same time, the drilling speed returns more than twice, and the cost of work is reduced by 40-60%.
