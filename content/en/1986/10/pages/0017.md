The field to the left of the curve corresponds to the conditions of graphite stability. This means that if the processes of carbon release (for example, as a result of a chemical reaction or precipitation from solution) occur at a temperature and pressure corresponding to equilibrium, carbon will be released in the form of diamond and graphite simultaneously. A diamond placed under graphite stability conditions (left field) turns into graphite, and graphite placed under diamond stability conditions (right field) turns into diamond. However, these transformations occur very slowly at room temperature. Therefore, under normal conditions, diamonds exist for millions of years. When heated, the rate of transformation increases. At a temperature of 1800 K and atmospheric pressure, diamond turns black and quickly turns into graphite. A temperature of 1800 K is called the graphitization temperature of diamond. The transformation of graphite into diamond at room temperature could not be achieved, even when subjecting graphite to pressures as high as 40 GPa. The reason for the failure is the unusually low rate of transformation at room temperature. To obtain appreciable amounts of diamond under these conditions, the process must take millions of years. Only at a temperature of 3000 K and a pressure of 12 GPa was it possible to obtain original wireless diamond crystals from graphite.

It is difficult to provide conditions for the direct transition of graphite → uncut diamond, so other processes are used to produce diamonds - for example, the release of carbon from a solution in liquid metal (for example, iron), which requires less high pressure and temperatures.
