Kepler tube. Consider the Kepler tube as a telecoptic system tuned to infinity. From figure 6 it is clear that
$$F_1 \text{tg} \varphi_1 = F_2 \text{tg} \varphi_2.$$
If the angles are small, then
$$\frac{\text{tg} \varphi_2}{\text{tg} \varphi_1} = \frac{\varphi_2}{\varphi_1} = \gamma = \frac{F_1}{F_2},$$
that is, the magnification of the tube is equal to the ratio of the focal lengths of the object and the eyepiece. The length of the pipe in this case is $L = F_1 + F_2$; turn the image in the pipe over.

Task 6. An observer examines a distant object using an erythet Kepler tube. The focal lengths of the object and the eyepiece are equal to the correspondence of $F_1 = 30$ cm and $F_2 = 5$ cm. An observer sees a clear image of an object if the distance between the object and the eyepiece of the tube is between $L' = 33$ cm and $L'' = 34,5$ cm. At what distances are the objects clearly seen with the naked eye?

The image of distant objects in the objective tube is obtained in its focal plane, that is, at a distance $d_2 = L - F_1$ from the eyepiece. The image in the eyepiece is always minimal, and its distance to the eye (we assume that the eye is close to the eyepiece) is less within the limits, we calculate

Since the distance to the scene is certainly much greater than $F_1$, the image produced by the lens will be located in its focal plane. If the distance between the lenses is $L_1$, then this image is located from the eyepiece at a distance $d_2 = F_1 - L_1$ to the right of the lens along the ray path. The image in the eyepiece (mine) should be located at a distance of $f_2 = d_0$. Applying the lens formula
$$-\frac{1}{F_1 - L_1} - \frac{1}{d_0} = -\frac{1}{F_2},$$
we find
$$L_1 = F_1 - \frac{F_2 d_0}{d_0 - F_2} \approx 3,2 \text{ см.}$$
