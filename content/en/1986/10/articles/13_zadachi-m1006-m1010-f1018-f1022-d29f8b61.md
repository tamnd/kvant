M1006. Two straight lines are drawn through the two vertices of the triangle, dividing it into three triangles and a quadrilateral.
a) Can the areas of all four parts be equal?
b) Which three of these parts can have equal areas? How many times does the area of ​​a quadrilateral differ from them?

G. A. Galperin, A. P. Savin

M1007. Prove that triangles with side lengths $a, b, c$ and $a_1, b_1, c_1$ are similar if and only if $\sqrt{a a_1} + \sqrt{b b_1} + \sqrt{c c_1} = \sqrt{(a_1 + b_1 + c_1)(a + b + c)}$.

V. P. Chuchin

M1008. The staircase consists of $2n+1$ steps. On the $n$ lower steps there is one stone each. Two people take turns lifting stones. The first one can move any stone up to the first free step, and the second one can move the stone down one step if it is free. The goal of the first is to place the stone on the top step. Can a second one fit him?

S. L. Eliseev

F1018. The boat, tied at the shore of a large lake (the coastline is straight), suddenly became untied, and the wind drove it at a constant speed of $v_0 = 2,5$ km/h at an angle of $\alpha = 15^\circ$ to the shore. Will you be able to catch up with the boat if your speed on the shore is $v_1 = 4$ km/h, in the water - $v_2 = 2$ km/h? At what speed of the boat is this generally possible?

S. S. Krotov

F1019. A small cylindrical vessel with a base area of ​​$S$ is lowered into a large vessel with a liquid whose density is $\rho_1$, into the bottom of which a tube of length $l$ (Fig. 1) is inserted; The walls of the vessels are rigidly fastened to each other. A colored liquid of density $\rho_2(\rho_2 > \rho_1)$ is poured into a small vessel to a height of $H$ so that the liquid levels in the large and small vessels coincide. At some point in time, the tube is opened. The heavy liquid begins to flow into the large vessel, after a while the light liquid flows from the large vessel into the small vessel; then the process is repeated. What mass of heavy liquid will flow out of the small vessel for the first time? What mass of heavy liquid will flow out each time in the future? What mass of light liquid will flow into a small vessel each time? Assume that the liquids do not mix; surface tension claim.

V. E. Skorovarov
