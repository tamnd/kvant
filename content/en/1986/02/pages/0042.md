⟦folio 40⟧

1.25R, where $R$ is the radius of the earth. At some points, Sutini ejects from the station in the direction of its movement and goes into an elliptical orbit with an anoeze removed from the center of the earth at a distance of 10R. At what ratio $m/M$ will the essence meet the station after completing one revolution around the earth?

Let $T_1$ be the period of revolution of the essence in orbit 1 with a distance from the center of the earth to the apogee 10R, $T_2$ be the period of revolution of the essence in orbit 2 with a distance from the center of the earth to perigee $nR$ ($n$ is an unknown number). The convergence of the essence and the station at point $A$ after one revolution of the essence will occur under the condition

$$\frac{T_1}{T_2} = k > 1, \quad \text{где } k \text{ — целое число.}$$

Expressing $T_1, T_2$ through the masses of the station $M$ and the essence $m$, using for this purpose Kepler’s laws, the laws of conservation of momentum and energy, we will find the possibility of correspondence between these masses, which lead to the fulfillment of condition $T_1:T_2 = k$.

According to Kepler's third law

$$\frac{T_1}{T_2} = \left( \frac{a_1}{a_2} \right)^{3/2} = \left( \frac{45}{5+4n} \right)^{3/2} = k,$$

where $a_1, a_2$ are the semimajor axes of the corresponding orbits (see Fig.ok). Let us express $n$ in terms of the masses $m$ and $M$.

When the station and the essence move separately around the earth, their full mechanical energies are preserved. Since the potential energy of a body of mass $m$ in the gravitational field of the earth at a distance $r$ from the center of the earth is equal to

$$U = -\frac{GmM_3}{r} = -\frac{mgR^2}{r}$$

(g is the acceleration of gravity, $M_3$ is the mass of the earth), then from the law of conservation of energy it follows that

$$\frac{mv_1^2}{2} - \frac{mgR^2}{5R/4} = \frac{|m(v_1')^2|}{2} - \frac{mgR^2}{10R},$$

$$\frac{Mv_2^2}{2} - \frac{MgR^2}{5R/4} = \frac{M(v_2')^2}{2} - \frac{MgR^2}{nR},$$

where $v_1'$ is the speed of the station at apogee, $v_2'$ is the speed of the station at perigree, $v_1$ and $v_2$ are the speeds acquired by the station and the station, respectively, at the moment of distance at point $A$ (see Fig.ok).

According to the law of conservation of velocity momentum, $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ are related by correspondence

$$(M + m)v_0 = mv_1 + Mv_2,$$

where $v_0$ is the speed of the station and the essence before the distance. We find the value $v_0$ from the equation of motion of the station and the essence as a whole (before the distance):

$$\frac{(m + M)v_0^2}{5R/4} = G \frac{(m + M)M_3}{(5R/4)^2},$$

where

$$v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{GM_3}{5R/4}} = 2\sqrt{\frac{gR}{5}}.$$

Kepler's second law allows us to establish a connection between the speed $v_1$ and $v_2'$,

$$\frac{5}{4}Rv_1 = 10Rv_1',$$
