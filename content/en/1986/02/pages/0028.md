⟦folio 26⟧

What's the answer?

The reason for writing this note was the following task:
At what p does the equation
$$\lg(x^2+2px)-\lg(8x-6p-3)=0$$
has one root?

This problem, proposed at the entrance exam in mathematics at the Belarusian Technical Institute. S. M. Kirov, was at one time included in the collection of problems, which was published in the second edition in 1983 *). This collection provides an incomplete solution - only one possible case is considered.

To get what's going on here, we'll first look at a simpler example with exactly the same formulation, in which, having painted a picture, you can immediately see everything.

Here is this example:
At what p does the equation
$$\frac{\lg px}{\lg(x+1)}=2$$
has one root?

Solution. This equation is equivalent to the system
$$\begin{cases} px>0, \\ x>-1, \\ px=(x+1)^2, \\ x>-1, \\ x^2+(2-p)x+1=0. \end{cases}$$
First case. The quadratic equation has a single root (Fig. 1).

In this case, the discriminant of the equation is zero, that is, $(2-p)^2=4$, whence p=0 and p=4. Since p=0 is not suitable for us, we are left with p=4. For p=4 there is a single root x=1.

Second case. A quadratic equation has two roots, the larger of which falls into the ODZ, and the smaller of which does not.

Thus, if $x_1$ and $x_2$ ($x_1<x_2$) are the roots of a quadratic equation, then there must be $x_2>-1$ and $x_1\leq-1$ (see figure 2, a, b).
