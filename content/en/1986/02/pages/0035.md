In 1742, in the correspondence of two academicians of the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences, H. Goldbach and L. Euler, the following problem arose (it is called the Goldbach problem): is it true that every even number greater than two can be represented as the sum of two prime numbers, and every odd number greater than seven can be represented as the sum of three prime numbers. A significant contribution to the solution of these problems was made by Soviet mathematicians I. M. Vinogradov and N. G. Chudakov.

The class of problems associated with representing numbers as sums of numbers of a given type is called additive number theory (from the Latin additivus - added, resulting from addition).

As opposed to prime numbers, mathematicians consider numbers that have many, many factors. A natural number is called supercomposite if it has more different divisors than any of the natural numbers less than it. A unit with its only divisor is a supercomposite number (since there is no natural number smaller than it). The next supercomposite number will be the prime number 2, which has two divisors 1 and 2; further - the number 4 with three divisors 1, 2 and 4; then 6 with four divisors. One might assume that every number $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot \dots \cdot n$ would be supercomposite, but this is not the case. Try to find the following ten super composite numbers.

There is a formula for finding the number of different divisors of numbers. It is known that every natural number $n$ can be represented as a product of powers of prime numbers and, moreover, in a unique way:

$$n = p_1^{k_1} \cdot p_2^{k_2} \cdot \dots \cdot p_s^{k_s}$$

(this theorem is called the main theorem)

Useful facts

Divisibility test by 11

In order for a number written in the decimal number system to be divisible by 11, it is necessary and sufficient that the difference between the sum of the digits in odd places and the sum of the digits in even places is divisible by 11.

Wilson's theorem

The number $(n-1)!+1$ is divisible by $n$ if and only if $n$ is a prime number.

Ferl's little theorem

If $p$ is a prime number, then for any natural number $a$ the number $a^p$ is divisible by $p$.
