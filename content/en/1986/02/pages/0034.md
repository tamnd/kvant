Numbers are simple and...

Let's take a look around: how many numbers surround us! Number of our house, apartment; numbers of buses and trams we travel on; air temperature, atmospheric pressure; the level of our knowledge in mathematics and literature... I think you can notify this list many times.
We perceive each number as the degree of manifestation of something or as an indicator of the object’s belonging to one or another class of objects, phenomena (flight on a given route, an airplane of a given brand, for example TU-134, etc.). At the same time, we don’t think about the numbers themselves at all. Of course, when we meet, we note “round” numbers; some check library numbers on the bus for “luck”: whether the sum of the first and last three digits matches. But these properties are inherent not to the numbers themselves, but to their representations in decimal notation.
People began to be interested in the properties of numbers a very long time ago. Even the ancient Greeks divided natural numbers into prime numbers, which have only two different divisors, and composite numbers, which have more than two divisors. One has only one divisor, so it is not classified as a prime or composite number.
The ancient Greeks were able to prove that there are infinitely many prime numbers. In 1837, the German mathematician Lejeune Dirichlet proved that in any arithmetic progression, the sum of the differences and the first term as primes contains infinitely many prime numbers.
The properties of divisibility helped the Greeks in solving equations in integers. The services of Duophant, who is called the last great mathematician of antiquity, are especially great here. The years of his life are not precisely established, but there is every reason to believe that he lived in the 3rd century AD.
Problems in number theory, despite the simplicity of their formulation, are usually very difficult. So, several hundred years are waiting for solutions to problems with large numbers, and their representations in decimal notation.
