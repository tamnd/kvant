As a result, a copper-gold alloy was formed in the extremely narrow contact zone between the ball and the plate.

The melting point of pure gold is 1063 °C, pure copper is 1083 °C, and alloys of gold and copper melt at lower temperatures. For example, an alloy consisting of equal numbers of gold and copper atoms melts already at 910 °C. It is this circumstance that is key to unraveling the secret of the jewelers of Etruria. They increased the temperature to such a limit that only the zone of the forming alloy melted, while the gold and copper themselves remained in a solid state. Upon subsequent cooling, the melt solidified, and the grain, practically without losing its spherical shape, was welded to the copper base. This process simultaneously occurs in all grains, and the entire pattern glued to the papier appears to be “reduced” (by analogy with transfer paintings) onto copper. The papyrus was stripped away at very high temperatures, and the product was ready. The copper did not have time to be described, since the process occurred quite quickly and a significant part of the oxygen was taken over by the papyrus during scraping.

The ancient secrets of jewelry are still not fully understood. It remains unclear, for example, how the Estruci managed to produce tiny gold balls. But what is most surprising is how the masters of the distant past were able to develop such advanced technology. To what amazing combination of chance, experience and utterance did the Estruci owe their discovery? maybe this will someday become known, but for now we can only pay tribute to the disappeared people and repeat the words of the ancient Roman historian Gaius Sallust: “WHAT people manage to achieve by cultivating the land, floating metal and erecting buildings depends on their spiritual strength.”
