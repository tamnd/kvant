F960. In order to get two completely identical coils, they are wound onto a non-magnetic core simultaneously, using wires that are complex together (Fig. 1). However, the coil is connected through keys K to battery B with voltage U₀, the second to resistor R. The key is closed. Calculate the current through the resistor. Draw a graph of the current through the battery versus time.

After time t the key is opened. How much heat is generated in the resistor starting from this moment? The inductance of each coil is L. The battery is considered ideal. Wire resistance claims.

When calculating the induced emf in the circuit of each coil, it is necessary to take into account the total magnetic flux through it, that is, the sum of the fluxes created by the coil currents.

After closing the key, the same EMF $E_1 = E_2 = U_0$ arises in both circuits, and the power released on the resistor is equal to

$$P = \frac{E_2^2}{R} = \frac{U_0^2}{R}.$$

So, the current through the 2nd coil is equal to $I_2 = U_0/R$. Magnetic flux, arbitrary 1st coil, is equal to

$$\Phi_1 = LI_1 + LI_2,$$

where $I_1$ is the current through the 1st coil and through the battery. (The flux through each coil produced by the current $I_2$ is the same - the coils are wound together.) Therefore,

$$E_1 = U_0 = \left| \frac{d\Phi_1}{dt} \right| = \left| L \frac{dI_1}{dt} + L \frac{dI_2}{dt} \right| = \left| L \frac{dI_1}{dt} \right|$$

(since $I_2 = \text{const}$). From here

$$I_1 = \frac{U_0}{R} + \frac{U_0}{L} t.$$

Graph $I_1(t)$ is shown on figure 2. The current jump at the first moment is due to the fact that the total flux through the coils should not change abruptly, and immediately after switching on, an additional flux will appear directed against the flux created by the 1st coil.

By the time the key opens, the total flow through the 2nd coil is equal to

$$\Phi_{2\tau} = -LI_2 + LI_{1\tau} = L \frac{U_0}{L} \tau = U_0 \tau.$$

Immediately after the key is opened, a current will appear in this coil

$$I_p = \frac{U_0 \tau}{L},$$

since the flow through the coil should not change abruptly. The heat that will be released on the resistor from the moment of opening is

$$W = \frac{1}{2} LI_p^2 = \frac{U_0^2 \tau^2}{2L}.$$
