“Move to the formation of broad-profile specialties that combine deep fundamental knowledge and remaining practical training focused on a specific industry. These lines from the CPSU Central Committee Project “Main Directions for the Restructuring of Higher and Secondary Specialized Education in the Country” summarize the experience of leading universities. One of these challenges is the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. Interest in the topic among schoolchildren has not waned for four decades. The history, the middle of the past and the future of physics are sent to the abyss with its rector - Lenin Prize laureate Academician O. M. Belotserkovsky.

Interview with an academician
O. M. Belotserkovsky

— Oleg Mikhailovich, the education of a physicist in 1946 was training in deep and complex processes occurring in science and production. How could you comment on them today, with the position, one might say, of history?

— Each stage in the development of society determines its level of secondary and higher education. It must be said that even before the war, the gap between science, production and education began to be felt more and more. And the idea of ​​physics as a university capable of bridging this gap was born precisely in these pre-war years. In 1938, an article appeared in Pravda signed by the largest scientists of the countries, where the issue of creating a new type of university was discussed and where essentially all the main features of the physics system were named.

Life required the rational and optimal implementation of scientific achievements in production. In turn, science had to quickly respond to the demands of rapidly developing industry, especially its new branches. To implement all this, special specialists were needed: with fundamental general training and good knowledge of specific problems.

The fizek idea was realized only after the war. First, by the creation of the Faculty of Physics and Technology of Moscow State University, and in the early 50s the faculty was transformed into the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. Academicians P. L. Kapitsa and M. A. Lavrentiev stood at the origins of fizek. Academician S. A. Khristanovich was, in essence, the first rector of MIPT. A fundamental role in the development and establishment of our institute was played by I. F. Petrov, who for years was the director of the institute. I would compare it to a powerful launch vehicle that launches a satellite into orbit. I believe that it was he who brought Fizek into the orbit of great science.

I personally am very pleased that I came to physics, which has now become a truly technical university.

— You transferred to the Faculty of Physics and Technology of Moscow State University from your fourth year at the Bauman Moscow Higher Technical University. What determined your choice?

— 16 or 17 of us transferred from Baumasky to Moscow State University. We were admitted to the “two-half” course. It seemed to me then that it was possible to “lose” two years in order to acquire fundamental knowledge and at the same time work in applied science. After all, previously only university graduates worked in the academic field, and technical college graduates went into production.

- Tell me what kind of physical education you would have had during your academic years. After all, these were the first years of its existence.

— Just like now, there was a lot of physics in the program. The teaching staff was very strong. Laboratory work became academics

Lectures on physics were excellently given by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences S. M. Rytov. I treasured my notes from these lectures. And in the physics exam I got a B from him and was terribly upset.

— Were there then such elements of the physical education system as elective questions on exams, basic institutes, bosses, and government?

— The state exam in physics was passed by L. D. Land. And he took it very harshly.

There were no optional questions, but there were written tests. More difficult than now. If you scored 70-80% of the maximum number of points, then you are practically guaranteed an A. You went to the lecturer, and he didn’t so much ask questions as talk to you.

Bases and chiefs have existed from the very beginning. The main emphasis was on physics and engineering training. There were few basic institutes: six - eight.

— What changes took place in the physical education system in subsequent years?

— Over time, the fizek system has evolved, this is natural. New faculties arose. We now have a department of aeromechanics and flight technology. Over the twenty years of its existence, it has proven itself to be excellent.

Another new element was the admission of applicants in Vladivostok, Kyiv, Krasnodar, Chelyabinsk, Khabarovsk. Recruiting the strongest guys locally, training them in Dolgoprudny according to a special program and distributing them after the fourth year back to specialties that are urgently needed by a given resource or region - all this meets modern requirements, meets, if you like, the world level of personnel training.

In general, our institute began to pay great attention to “our” applicants. Correspondence and evening physics and technology schools have been created, on-site physics and mathematics optimizations are being conducted, and advertising issues of our institute newspaper “For Sciences” are being published. To process 700-800 future students, the institute “screens” 10-12 thousand schoolchildren.

— If you compare today’s applicants with the applicants in your cohort...

“Nowadays more in-depth, intensive, even sophisticated training is required. Young people also sometimes turn to this and love themselves to think: could we do it today? And I'm not sure.

Now the guys come better prepared. Admission last year, for example, we had the strongest middle technical schools. Two optimization winners for one place! That's what it says. But, on the other hand, today’s students are younger and less patient. We were more persistent. At that difficult time there were still significant situations. However, it seems to me that it has been preserved to a certain extent even now. The specificity of our profession is such that we mainly need enthusiasts.

— Oleg Mikhailovich, what are the main features of the current appearance of MIPT?

— Nowadays, in physics, there seems to be a rather successful union of “three pillars”, three educational cycles: general institute, faculty and basic. Within the framework of the first of them, the highest departments of general physics, higher mathematics, and theoretical physics work. Faculty and basic cycles correspond to the main areas of personnel training.
