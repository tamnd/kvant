M1011. Prove that for $n$ positive numbers $a_1 \geq a_2 \geq a_3 \geq \dots \geq a_n$ the following inequalities hold:
a) $a_1^2 - a_2^2 + a_3^2 \geq (a_1 - a_2 + a_3)^2$;
b) $a_1^2 - a_2^2 + a_3^2 - a_4^2 \geq (a_1 - a_2 + a_3 - a_4)^2$;
b) $a_1^2 - a_2^2 + \dots - (-1)^n a_n^2 \geq (a_1 - a_2 + \dots - (-1)^n a_n)^2$.

L. D. Kurlyandchik

M1012. Prove that:
a) several non-intersecting circles can be placed on a plane so that each one touches exactly 5 others;
b) the number 5 in point a) cannot be replaced by 6.

D. V. Fomin

M1013. On sides $AB$ and $BC$ of triangle $ABC$ two points $M$ and $N$ are taken. When parallel lines passing through points $M$, $B$ and $N$ intersect the base $AC$ at points $K$, $D$ and $L$. Prove that the area of ​​the trapezoid (or parallelogram) $KMNL$ is not greater than the area of ​​one of the triangles $ABD$ and $DBC$ (Fig. 1).

V. V. Rozhdestvensky

M1014. Let $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ be distinct pairwise coprime natural numbers. Prove that there are infinitely many natural numbers $b$ such that the numbers $b + a_1, b + a_2, \dots, b + a_n$ are also pairwise relatively prime.

V. F. Lev

M1015. Is it possible to factor the polynomial $x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 12 = 0$ into factors with integer coefficients?

S. L. Manukyan

Find the radius of the circle on the floor where the rocks will fall. Assume that the impacts of the shards on the ceiling are absiltonically elastic, and on the floor - inelastic; The salts should not reach the walls.
