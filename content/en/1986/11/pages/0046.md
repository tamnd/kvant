We need to find the maximum value of the function $S(x)$ on the segment $[-1; 1]$.

The function $S(x)$ is even, so it suffices to consider it on the interval $[0; 1]$. On this segment $S'(x) = 2(4 - 9x^2)$, $S'(x) = 0$ at $x = 2/3$.

Consequently, our function reaches a maximum on the segment $[0; 1]$ at one of the points 0, 1, 2/3. The calculation shows that $S(0) = 0$, $S(1) = 2$, $S(2/3) = 32/9$, so the most value of $S(x)$ on the segment $[0; 1]$ is $S(2/3) = 32/9$.

2 (1978). The vertex $A, B, C$ of the parallelogram $ABCD$ has a corresponding coordinate $(-2; -3), (1; 3), (6; 1)$. Find:

1) all values ​​of $a$ for which the coordinates of vertex $D$ are a solution to the system of inequalities

$$\begin{cases} 3x + 2y + 2a \leq 0, \\ 6x - 2y + 7a \geq 0; \end{cases}$$

2) all values ​​of $a$ for which the coordinates of at least one point of the segment $AC$ is a solution to this system.

Solution. In problem 1), you essentially need to know only the coordinates of the vertex $D$, which are instantly found from the condition $\overrightarrow{DC} = \overrightarrow{AB}$, where $\overrightarrow{AB} = (3; 6)$, $\overrightarrow{DC} = (6 - x_D); 1 - y_D)$ - see Fig. 2. Hence $x_D = 3$, $y_D = -5$, and, substituting these values $x$ and $y$ into given system, we get the answer: $a \in [-4; 1/2]$.

Let's move on to a more serious task 2). The equation of the straight line $AC$ is written in the form $y + 3 = k(x + 2)$, where $k$ is the angular coefficient of the straight line $AC$, equal to the tangent of the angle of inclination of the vector $\overrightarrow{AC}$ to the axis $Ox$, that is, $k = \frac{y_C - y_A}{x_C - x_A} = \frac{1}{2}$. Thus, the equation of the line $AC$ has the form $y = \frac{1}{2}x - 2$. Let's rewrite the data into conditions into conditions in the system in the form

$$\begin{cases} y \leq -\frac{3}{2}x - a, \\ y \leq 3x + \frac{7}{2}a, \end{cases}$$

and let $l_1$ and $l_2$ be the lines given by the equations $y = -\frac{3}{2}x - a$ and $y = 3x + \frac{7}{2}a$ respectively (see Fig. 2).

Each of the lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ divides the plane into two parts, and the inequalities of this system are satisfied by the points lying in the lower half-planes. Now the geometric meaning of problem 2): it is required to find all values ​​of the parameter $a$ for which at least one point of the diagonal $AC$ belongs to the area shaded on figure 2. To do this, it is necessary and sufficient to simultaneously fulfill the following three conditions:

a) the point $Q$ of intersection of lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ lies above line $AC$;

b) point $A$ lies below line $l_1$;

b) point $C$ lies below line $l_2$.

It is easy to find the coordinates $(x_Q; y_Q)$ of point $Q$: $x_Q = -a$, $y_Q = a/2$. Condition a) indicates the inequality $y_Q \geq \frac{1}{2}x_Q - 2$, hence $a \geq -2$. From condition 6) we obtain that $a \leq 6$, and from condition b) that $a \geq -\frac{34}{7}$. Thus, the values ​​of the parameter $a$ must be on the segment $[-2; 6]$.

Answer: 1) $-4 \leq a \leq 1/2$; 2) $-2 \leq a \leq 6$.
