Geometric choice of the cosine sum formula

To find an expression for the cosine of the sum of angles a and β, where α+β<180°, we proceed as follows. Consider triangle ABC, where A=α, B=β, C=γ=180°-(α+β). By the cosine theorem
$$c^2+a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\gamma.$$ (1)

According to the theorem of sines
$$c=2R\sin\gamma, a=2R\sin\alpha, b=2R\sin\beta,$$
where R is the radius of the circle circumscribed around the triangle.

Substituting these expressions into (1) and dividing by 4R², we get
$$\sin^2\gamma=\sin^2\alpha+\sin^2\beta-2\sin\alpha\sin\beta\sin\gamma$$
Replacing $\sin^2\gamma$ with 1−$\cos^2\gamma$, we obtain a quadratic equation for cos γ. Solving it, we find
$$\cos\gamma=\sin\alpha\sin\beta$$
Remembering that $\gamma=180^\circ-(α+β)$, that is, cos γ = −cos (α+β), we get
$$\cos (α+β)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta.$$
The second root (+ sign) is constructed (think why), and we get the formula for the cosine of the sum.
