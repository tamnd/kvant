A. S. STEINBERG

You turn on the tape recorder, but instead of music, you hear incomprehensible wheezing. In the workshop you hear the standard diagnosis: wear of the magnetic head. Due to prolonged contact with the tape, it has become worn out and does not provide quality reproduction. Many people are probably familiar with this situation.

Now imagine the advertisement: “Buy tape recorders with metal glass heads!” Most likely, the combination of the words "metal glass" shows you the countries. But you can safely trust the advice of advertising - among the wide range of useful properties of metallic glasses (sometimes they are also called amorphous metals) there is also increased resistance to wear. What is metallic glass?

Glass (plain glass, not metal) has been known to man for a long time. A sand mixture of suitable composition appeared in the fire, melted, then froze and... it turned out to be glass. This happened about six thousand years ago, and since then the scope of glass has been constantly expanding.
