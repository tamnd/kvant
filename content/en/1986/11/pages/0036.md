..., $f_n(\dot{x}) = f(f_{n-1}(x))$; similarly we define $f_{-1}(x) = g(x)$, ..., $f_{-m-1}(x) = f_{-1}(f_{-m}(x))$ for $m \geqslant 1$.
Substituting $f_{n-1}(x)$ instead of $x$ into the conditions for any integer $n$, we obtain the equality
$$f_{n+1}(x) = f_n(x) + f_{n-1}(x).$$
This formula allows us to express $f_n(x)$ for any $n$ in terms of $x = f_0(x)$ and $y = f(x) = f_1(x)$; as shown in the margin,
$$f_n(x) = a_n y + a_{n-1} x,$$
where the sequence $a_n$ is given by the correspondence $a_{n+1} = a_n + a_{n-1}$ and the “initial conditions” $a_0 = 0$, $a_1 = 1$ for all goals $n$ (we again bypass for now the question of the domain of definition $f_n$ for $n < 0$). The sequence $a_n$ is well known: for $n \geqslant 1$ these are significant Fibonacci numbers*); it is enough for us to know that $|a_n|$ increases without limit at $n \rightarrow \pm \infty$ (for $n > 0$ this is obvious; for $n < 0$ it is enough to note that $a_n = (-1)^{n+1} a_{-n}$ and $|a_{-n}| = |a_n|$).

By derivation of Property 3 above, two cases are possible: the sign $f(x)$ of either all $x$ coincides with the sign - $x$, or - with the sign $x$.

In the first case, $f_n(x)$ and $f_{n-1}(x)$ have different signs for any $x \neq 0$, so for $n \geqslant 2$
$$|f_{n+1}(x)| = |f_n(x) + f_{n-1}(x)| \leqslant
\leqslant |f_n(x)| \leqslant \dots \leqslant |f_2(x)| = |f(x) + x| \leqslant |x|.$$
Therefore, $|y + (a_{n-1}/a_n)x| \leqslant |x/a_n|$. Since the right side of this inequality tends to zero as $n \rightarrow \infty$, the sequence $a_{n-1}/a_n$ must have some limit $\mu$ (equal to $y/x$); at the same time $y = -\mu x$.

In the second case, $f_n(x)$ for all $n$ has the same sign (sign $x$). Since $f$ is a continuous function and $|f(f(x))| = |f(x) + x| \geqslant |x|$, the domain of $f$ or the domain of the inverse function $f_{-1}$ are all real numbers and our formulas for $f_n$ are defined and valid for all $x$ and all targets $n$. Now for any $n < 0$ we have
$$|f_n(x)| = |f_{n+1}(x) - f_{n-1}(x)| \leqslant |f_{n+1}(x)| \leqslant \dots
\leqslant |f_{-1}(x)| \leqslant |x|,$$
hence $|y + (a_{n-1}/a_n)x| \leqslant |x/a_n|$. Passing here to the preposition for $n \rightarrow -\infty$, we obtain, as in the first case, that $y = \mu_1 x$, and $\mu_1 = -\lim_{n \rightarrow -\infty} (a_{n-1}/a_n)$.

Note that $\mu_1 = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} (a_{n+1}/a_n) = \mu^{-1}$; as follows from point a),
$$\mu = (\sqrt{5} - 1)/2, \mu^{-1} = (\sqrt{5} + 1)/2.$$

These numbers also participate in Binet's general formula for Fibonacci numbers, given in the margin.
