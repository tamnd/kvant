5. Answer. It is forbidden. Solution. Let us prove that if there are $n$ cities in a country, then the required road network can be built only when one of the numbers $n$ or $n-2$ is the square of an integer. From the condition ($n$ cities and $n-1$ roads) it follows that from any city there is only one path leading to any other city. Let's choose some city $A$ and call it “good”. Let's call the city $B$ “good” if the length of the path from $A$ to $B$ is an even number, and “bad” if the length of this path is odd. It is not difficult to verify that the length of the path between two cities is even if and only if these cities are either “good” or both “bad”.
Let us denote the number of “good” cities by $x$, the number of “bad” cities by $y$. Then $x+y=n$. There are $xy$ pairs of cities in which one city is “good” and the other is “bad”. Therefore, among the numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ there are $xy$ odd numbers. If $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ is an even number, then $xy=\frac{1}{2}\cdot \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ and therefore $xy=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1\right)$ and therefore $n=(x-y)^2$. If $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ is an even number, then $xy=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1\right)$ and therefore $n=(x-y)^2$. Since neither 1986 nor 1984 are squares of integers, then at $n=1986$ the king's requirements cannot be satisfied.
