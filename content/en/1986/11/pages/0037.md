F1004. A thin single-pod rod, which can be freely adjusted in a vertical plane around a horizontal axis passing through the end of the rod, hangs motionless above the water (Fig. 1); length of the rod $l$, density of the material $\rho$ (ρ is less than the density of water $\rho_0$). Slowly lowering the axle, the rod is placed in the water. Find the relationship between the angle of deflection of the rod from the vertical and the distance from the axis to the surface of the water; graph this relationship.

During the process of immersion, three forces act on the rod: gravity, the Archimedes force and the reaction force from the axis. During slow immersion, at any value of the distance $x$ from the axis to the surface of the water, the rod will be in the equilibrium position.

As an equilibrium condition, we use the equality to zero of the total moment of forces relative to the axis of rotation. Since the moment $M_A$ of the Archimedes force before the start of immersion of the axis ($x > 0$) and after it ($x < 0$) is expressed by different formulas, these stages should be considered separately.

I. $x > 0$ (see Fig. 2). The moment of gravity is equal to

$$M_T = f_T \cdot d_T = \rho S l g \cdot \frac{l}{2} \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2} \rho S l^2 g \sin \alpha$$

(S is the cross-sectional area of ​​the rod); Archimedes' moment of force -

$$M_A = f_A \cdot d_A = \rho_0 S \left( l - \frac{x}{\cos \alpha} \right) g \cdot \frac{1}{2} \left( l + \frac{x}{\cos \alpha} \right) \sin \alpha = \frac{1}{2} \rho_0 S \left( l^2 - \frac{x^2}{\cos^2 \alpha} \right) g \sin \alpha.$$

We will consider the moment of gravity to be positive; then Archimedes' moment of force is negative (since he tends to turn the rod in the opposite direction). Then the equilibrium condition will be written in the form

$$M = M_T + M_A = \frac{1}{2} \rho S l^2 g \sin \alpha -$$

$$- \frac{1}{2} \rho_0 S \left( l^2 - \frac{x^2}{\cos^2 \alpha} \right) g \sin \alpha = 0 \Rightarrow \sin \alpha \left( \frac{x^2}{\cos^2 \alpha} - \left( 1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} \right) l^2 \right) = 0.$$
