$$U = I_1 \cdot 1r + I_2 \cdot 3r + I_4 \cdot 2r,$$

where $r$ is a resistance of 1 ohm. From equation (1)–(3) we obtain

$$I_2 = \frac{1}{2} I_1, I_3 = \frac{1}{4} I_1, I_4 = \frac{3}{4} I_1.$$

Substituting into (4) the points $I_2$ and $I_4$, expressed through $I_1$, we obtain

$$U = I_1 \cdot 4r.$$

On the other hand, the resistance $R$ of the entire circuit is

$$R = \frac{U}{I_1 + I_2} = \frac{U}{I_1 + I_1/2} = \frac{2}{3} \frac{U}{I_1}.$$

Hence,

$$\frac{3}{2} I_1 R = I_1 \cdot 4r,$$

from where $R = \frac{8}{3} r = \frac{8}{3}$ Ohm.

F1007. A ray of light falls on a ball composed of two hemispheres, which are made of glasses with refractive index $n_1$ and $n_2$ ($n_1 > n_2$). The ball begins to rotate at a beehive speed $\omega$ around an axis perpendicular to the direction of the beam (Fig. 1). Set the maximum deflection of the light spot on the screen installed directly behind the ball.

Fig. 3. a) $\Omega = \alpha_0$; $\varphi = 2\varphi_0 = \pi - 2\alpha_0$; $\delta) \alpha_0 < \Omega < \frac{\pi}{2}$; $\alpha > \alpha_0 \rightarrow \frac{\pi}{2}$; $\varphi < 2\varphi_0 \rightarrow 0$.

Since the beam passes through the center of the ball, the beam is not refracted at the surface of the ball (at the entrance and exit).

During time $0 \leq t < \frac{\pi}{2\omega}$ the incident beam passes the first “half” of its path in an optically denser medium. Moreover, while the angle of rotation $\Omega$ is less than the angle $\alpha_0$ of total internal reflection from the refraction boundary - $\alpha_0 = \arcsin \frac{n_2}{n_1}$, the beam, refracted at the boundary, passes into a less optically dense medium (Fig. 2) and $\frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \beta} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$. With increasing angle of rotation at $\alpha \rightarrow \alpha_0$, the refracted beam is “pressed” to the boundary of the media, that is, $\beta \rightarrow \frac{\pi}{2}$. From the moment $\Omega$ passes the value $\alpha_0$, the ray is reflected from the interface and emerges from refraction with a refractive index of $n_1$. So at moment $t = \frac{\alpha_0}{\omega}$ there is a jump of the ray on the screen (Fig. 3, a).

Subsequently ($\alpha_0 < t < \frac{\pi}{2\omega}$) $\alpha \rightarrow \frac{\pi}{2}$, that is, the reflected beam is “pressed” against the boundary of the media (see Fig. 3, b), which approaches the vertical.

From the moment the boundary passes the vertical position ($t = \frac{\pi}{2\omega}$), the ray falls into an optically less dense medium, is refracted at the boundary and exits the denser medium. The angle of incidence $\delta$ and the angle of refraction $\gamma$ are related by the relation $\frac{\sin \delta}{\sin \gamma} = \frac{n_1}{n_2}$. When the boundary of the media passes through the vertical ($\delta = \frac{\pi}{2}$) $\gamma_0 = \arcsin \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \alpha_0$, there is a “jump” of the beam on the screen (Fig. 4). Subsequently ($\delta \rightarrow 0$), the beam is “pressed” to the normal to the boundary of the media (Fig. 5), and at $\delta = 0$ it passes through the sphere without refraction.
