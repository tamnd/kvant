square ABCD is located. Line AB of the square lies on the graph of the function $y=x+8$, and vertices C and D lie on the graph of the function $y=x^2$. Determine the length of the side of the square.

Solution. Since the straight line $y=x+8$ intersects the axis at an angle $45^\circ$, the diagonals of the square are parallel to the coordinate axis. To determine this, we assume that the diagonal AC is parallel to the $Ox$ axis, and the diagonal BD is parallel to the $Oy$ axis. Then $x_B=x_D$, $y_A=y_C$. Let's put $x_B=x_D=x$ and express the coordinates of all vertices in terms of $x$.

From the conditions of the problem it follows that $y_B=x+8$, $y_D=x^2$, $y_C=x^2$, $x_A=y_A-8=x^2-8$. The vector DC is inclined to the $Ox$ axis at an angle $45^\circ$, so $y_C-y_D=x_C-x_D$, that is, $x^2-x^2=x_C-x$. Since $x_C-x\neq 0$, we get that $x_C+x=1$. Therefore $x_C=1-x$.

Diagonal AC is bisected by diagonal BD, so $x_D=(x_A+x_C)/2$ and we arrive at an equation for $x$:

$$2x=(1-x)^2-8+(1-x),$$

or

$$x^2-5x-6=0.$$

The roots of this equation are $x_1=6$, $x_2=-1$.

If $x=6$, then $x_B=x_D=6$, $x_C=1-x=-5$, $x_A=x_C^2-8=17$, $y_B=x+8=14$, $y_C=x_C^2=25$, $y_A=y_C=25$, $y_D=x^2=36$.

The square is positioned as shown in figure 3. In this case $DB=y_D-y_B=22$, $AB=\frac{DB}{\sqrt{2}}=11\sqrt{2}$.

If $x=-1$, then $x_B=x_D=-1$, $x_C=2$, $x_A=-4$, $y_B=7$, $y_C=4$, $y_A=4$, $y_D=1$. The square is positioned as shown in figure 4. In this case $DB=y_B-y_D=6$, $AB=\frac{DB}{\sqrt{2}}=3\sqrt{2}$.

Answer: $11\sqrt{2}$ or $3\sqrt{2}$.

Sets of points on the coordinate plane

Let $Ax+By+C=0$ be the equation of the line $l$. Then the set of points whose coordinates satisfy the inequality $Ax+By+C>0$ and $Ax+By+C<0$ are two half-planes on which the straight line $l$ divides the coordinate plane. Two points $(x_1;y_1)$ and $(x_2;y_2)$ lie in the same half-plane if and only if the expression $Ax+By+C$ takes values ​​of the same sign in them, that is, the coordinates $x$:

$$(Ax_1+By_1+C)(Ax_2+By_2+C)>0.$$

Consider two intersecting lines $l_1$ and $l_2$, given by the corresponding equations $A_1x+B_1y+C_1=0$, $A_2x+B_2y+C_2=0$.

The straight lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ divide the coordinate plane into four parts $E_1, E_2, G_1, G_2$ (Fig. 5). Now it is not difficult to prove that the inequality $(A_1x+B_1y+C_1)(A_2x+B_2y+C_2)>0$ (*) is satisfied by points belonging to one of the sets $E_1\cup E_2$ or $G_1\cup G_2$ (for the reading shown in figure 5. - the set $E_1\cup E_2$).
