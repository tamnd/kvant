4 (1982). Find the area of ​​the figure, which is specified on the coordinate plane of the system of inequalities
$$\begin{cases} x^2 + y^2 \geq 10, \\ 3x^2 - 4x - 32 \leq 0, \\ (3x - 2y)(3y - x + 10) \geq 0. \end{cases}$$
Solution. First of all, it is necessary to determine which figure is specified by the conditions of the problem. To do this, consider each inequality of the system.

The first inequality is satisfied by points lying in (and on the boundary of) a circle of radius $\sqrt{10}$ centered at the origin (Fig. 6). Solving the second inequality, we obtain $x \in [-8/3; 4]$. Consequently, the second inequality defines a vertical strip lying between the lines $x = -8/3$ and $x = 4$ (including the points of these lines). The third inequality of the system has the form (*), and in this case it is satisfied by the points inside the two vertical angles formed by the lines $3x - 2y = 0$ and $3y - x + 10 = 0$ (including points lying on the lines). This set is shaded at figure 6; the boundary straight lines will be denoted by $l_1$ and $l_2$, respectively. To find out how the figure we are interested in is structured, we find the coordinates of the point $A, B, C, D$ at which the lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ intersect the lines $x = -8/3$ and $x = 4$: $A(-8/3; -4), B(-8/3; -38/9), C(4; -2), D(4; 6)$. We see that point $B$ lies below point $A$, so that lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ intersect into stripes $-8/3 \leq x \leq 4$. Further, straight line $l_2$ touches circle $x^2 + y^2 = 10$, since the system
$$\begin{cases} x^2 + y^2 = 10, \\ 3y - x + 10 = 0 \end{cases}$$
has a edistic solution $(x=1, y=-3)$. Taking $l_1$ passes through the center of this circle. Therefore, the figure we are interested in is the trapezoid $ABCD$, from which a semicircle of radius $\sqrt{10}$ has been removed (the border of this figure is visible at figure 6 in red), so that the required area is
$$\frac{(AB+CD)h}{2} - 5\pi,$$
where $AB=2/9, CD=8, h=20/3$. Thus we get the answer:
$$\frac{740}{27} - 5\pi.$$

On the coordinate plane, we consider the set $M$ of all points whose coordinates $(a; b)$ satisfy the conditions $-3 < a < 0, 0 < b < 9$ and are such that the equation $(b+2a)x^4 + (b+7a)x^2 + b-a = 0$ has four distinct roots. 1) Does the point $N(-2; 3)$ belong to the set $M$? 2) Find the area of ​​the polygon whose object contains the set $M$.
Solution. The algebraic-geometric formulation of the problem suggests that the solution must also be algebraic-geometric. We begin with an algebraic analysis of the condition, and then use the results of this analysis to determine the geometric nature of the set $M$.
A given biquadratic equation has four distinct roots if and only if the equation
$$(b+2a)z^2 + (b+7a)z + b-a = 0$$
has two different positive roots. To do this, it is necessary and sufficiently simultaneous to fulfill the following conditions:
a) equation (1) is quadratic $(b+2a \neq 0)$ and its discriminant is positive;
$$(b+7a)^2 - 4(b+2a)(b-a) > 0;$$
b) the product and sum of the roots of equation (1) are positive numbers, that is
$$\frac{b-a}{b+2a} > 0, \quad -\frac{b+7a}{b+2a} > 0$$
(we used Vieta's theorem), which is equivalent to the system
$$\begin{cases} (b-a)(b+2a) > 0, \\ -(b+7a)(b+2a) > 0. \end{cases}$$
