Let us mention three more solutions to this problem. The first of them is similar to what we presented and is based on the fact that the set of points X of the trapezoid ABCD with bases AB and CD, for which the triangles AXD and BXC are equal in length, is a segment connecting the bases (compare with the auxiliary statement proved above). M964. Prove that in the sequence $(a_n)$ of different natural numbers satisfying the condition $a_n < 100n$ there is a number whose decimal notation
a) the number 1 appears;
b) 1986 units occur in a row.

M965. Given six numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_6$. To calculate head-on the sum of their pairwise products $a_1a_2+a_1a_3+...+a_5a_6$, you need to spend 15 multiplications and 14 additions. Show how you can immediately find: the sum of these numbers, the sum of their products by twos, threes, fours and heels, spending only 15 additions and 14 multiplications.

a) It is enough to show that if in the decimal notation of various natural numbers $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$, the digit 1 does not appear, then the number $a_n/n$ can be arbitrarily large (in particular, $a_n/n > 100$ for some $n$).

Note that among the first $9^k$ numbers of our sequence there is a $a_n$ whose notation consists of at least $k+1$ digits, that is, $a_n \geq 10^k$. In fact, the number of natural numbers written in no more than $k$ digits other than 1 is $9^k-1$ (each of the $k$ digits of such a number contains one of the 9 digits 0, 2, 3, ..., 9, and the number 0 is excluded; we mean that numbers with fewer than $k$ digits are padded with leading zeros to $k$-digits). Since $n \leq 9^k$, the inequality $a_n/n \geq (10/9)^k$ is valid, and its right-hand side grows without limit with increasing $k$.

6) In exactly the same way as in point a), it is proved that if the numbers in the notation are $a_1, a_2, ..., a_n$, the numbers $S_n^k$ are the sum of their products of $k$ pieces. Then we can successively find

$$S_2^1 = x_1 + x_2, \quad S_2^2 = x_1x_2,$$

then

$$S_3^1 = S_2^1 + x_3, \quad S_3^2 = S_2^2 + S_1^2 \cdot x_3, \quad S_3^3 = S_2^2x_3,$$

then $S_n^k (1 \leq k \leq 4), \quad S_5^k (1 \leq k \leq 5)$ and finally $S_6^k (1 \leq k \leq 5)$, use the formulas

$$S_n^1 = S_n^{1-1} + x_n, \quad S_n^k = S_n^{k-1} + S_n^{k-1}x_n$$

($1 < k < n$), $S_n^n = S_n^{n-1}x_n$.

To calculate all $S_n^k (1 \leq k \leq n)$ it will take $n-1$ additions and $n-1$ multiplications. So to do this down to $n=6$, you need $1+2+3+4+5=15$ additions and the same number of multiplications. The last multiplication $S_6^k = S_5^kx_6$ can be saved by counting the product of all 6 numbers.

This sequence of operations is illustrated by a diagram in the margin (blue arrows are additions, red arrows are multiplications).

Briefly this decision can be explained as follows. We need to find the coefficients of the polynomial $(x+a_1)(x+a_2)...(x+a_6)$ for $x, x^2, ..., x^5$, and we successively find the product of $x+a_1$ by $x+a_2$, then by $x+a_3$, ..., by $x+a_6$.

Similarly, one can prove that all sums of products of $n$ numbers by $k (k=1, 2, ..., n)$ can be found in $1+2+...+(n-1)=n(n-1)/2$ additions and the same number of multiplications. It will be interesting to figure out whether it is possible to do this with fewer operations.

F973. The body begins to move from point A and after time T gets to point B a) with maximum possible speed; b) at the minimum possible speed; distance |AB| equals l. Calculate the speed of the body in these two cases, if the speed at point A is v₀=0 and the acceleration of the body is -a<a<a₀.

a) To arrive at point B with maximum speed, you need to move as follows. Up to some point in time t₀ you need to move with acceleration -a₀, then until the end with acceleration a₀. Point t₀ is found from the condition that the traversed path is equal to l:

$$l = -\frac{a_0 t_0^2}{2} - a_0 t_0 (t - t_0) + \frac{a_0 (T - t_0)^2}{2} \Rightarrow t_0^2 - 2 T t_0 + \frac{T^2}{2} = \frac{l}{a_0}, t_0 = T - \sqrt{\frac{T^2}{2} + \frac{l}{a_0}}$$

(the sign in the solution of the quadratic equation is chosen from the condition t₀<T). Скорость в момент времени t обозначаем через V(t). График функции V(t) показан на рисунке 1 красной линии. Пусть U(t) — скорость при другом режиме движения, удовлетворяющим нашим условиям, таком, что U(T) > V(T). Let us show that then U(t) ≥ V(t) for any t (see Fig. 1). Suppose that U(t₁) < V(t₁) for some t₁ (this possibility is shown in Fig. 1). If t₁ ≥ t₀, then the average acceleration on the segment t₁ ≤ t ≤ T is greater than a₀ (the black line at Fig. 1 is steeper than the red one), and if t₁ ≤ t₀, then the average acceleration on the segment 0 ≤ t ≤ t₁ is less than -a₀ So, if U(T) > V(T), then U(t) ≥ V(t) for any t and a body moving with speed U(t) will cover a segment larger than l in time T. So, V(T) is the maximum speed. We find it:

$$V(T) = -a_0 t_0 + a_0 (T - t_0) = a_0 (T - 2 t_0) = a_0 \left( -T + 2 \sqrt{\frac{T^2}{2} + \frac{l}{a_0}} \right) = \sqrt{2 a_0^2 T^2 + 4 a_0 l} - a_0 T.$$

6) Similarly: in order to arrive at point B with minimum speed, you need to move with acceleration a₀ until a certain moment in time, then until the end with acceleration -a₀. Point t₀ is found from the condition that the traversed path is equal to l:

$$l = \frac{a_0 t_0^2}{2} + a_0 t_0 (T - t_0) - \frac{a_0 (T - t_0)^2}{2} \Rightarrow t_0 = T - \sqrt{\frac{T^2}{2} - \frac{l}{a_0}}$$

(graphic of speed v(t) versus time t is shown in figure 2). The speed at time T is equal to a₀ t₀ - a₀ (T - t₀) = a₀ (2 t₀ - T) = a₀ \left( T - 2 \sqrt{\frac{T^2}{2} - \frac{l}{a_0}} \right) = a_0 T - \sqrt{2 a_0^2 T^2 - 4 a_0 l}.

The proof that this speed is minimal is similar to the previous proof: if u(t) is another mode of motion and u(T) < v(T), then u(t) ≤ v(t) for any t.

Moreover, a) it may turn out that t₀ < 0: this will happen for l > \frac{a_0 T^2}{2}; then there is no solution. Then part 6 (under the root is a negative expression) has no solution.

F974. Determine the period of small oscillations of the system shown in figure: mass of the load m, elasticity of the spring k, ∠AOB=α. The rod and block are smooth, the spring is weightless. Reason for fixation at point A. Will the period of oscillation change if load m is also fixed at point O?

Let us assume that the load has moved a small distance x. Let's see how the thread tension changes.

Since the spring is weightless, at any moment of time (see figure)

$$F_y = T \cos \alpha,$$
that is, $k(\Delta l + \Delta l') = T \cos \alpha'$,
where $\Delta l' = |OO'|, \alpha' = \angle AO'B$. As can be seen from figure, $|OO'| = x/\cos \alpha$ and $\alpha' = \alpha + \Delta \alpha$, where $\Delta \alpha = \frac{x \mathrm{tg} \alpha}{l - x}$.
Since $\cos(\alpha + \Delta \alpha) = \cos \alpha \cos \Delta \alpha - \sin \alpha \sin \Delta \alpha \approx \cos \alpha - \Delta \alpha \cdot \sin \alpha = \cos \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin \alpha \mathrm{tg} \alpha$,
with a small mixture of cargo visa

$$T = \frac{k(\Delta l + \frac{x}{\cos \alpha})}{\cos \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin \alpha \mathrm{tg} \alpha} = \frac{k(\Delta l \cos \alpha + x)}{\cos^2 \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin^2 \alpha} \approx \frac{k(\Delta l \cos \alpha + x)}{\cos^2 \alpha}$$

(we neglected the second term in the denominator, since $\frac{x}{l - x} \to 0$ at $x \to 0$).

Now let's write down the equation of motion of the load:

$$ma = mg - T = mg - \frac{k \Delta l}{\cos \alpha} - \frac{kx}{\cos^2 \alpha} = -\frac{kx}{\cos^2 \alpha}$$

This equation describes a harmonious formula for the period of oscillation of a load on a spring.

From the solution it is clear that if a load is secured at point O, the period of oscillation will increase, since in this case, for the same value of α, the value of T will be less; the acceleration of the load fixed at point A will increase - the period of oscillation will decrease.

The volume of the conducting plate contains a huge number of free electrons. When the plate moves inside the capacitor, three forces act on free electrons: the Lorentz force from the magnetic field $evB$, directed downward; force $eE$ from the charges located on the surface of the plate movement, directed upward; force from the charges on the plates of the capacitor $eE_0$, directed downward. The sum of all these three forces inside the conductor must be equal to zero, that is

$$vB - E + E_0 = 0.$$

In this case, the charge on a capacitor with capacitance C must be equal to the charge on plates with area S:

$$Q = CU = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d} E_0 d = \varepsilon_0 E_0 S.$$

F977. On the wall, the plane of which is deviated from the vertical by $4,87^\circ$ (see figure), a mirror is mounted. From what maximum distance can a person, whose height is 170 cm, enlarge at least part of his image in the mirror?

Let us pay attention to the fact that the thermal process is specified in coordinates $p, T$. It consists of three sections: two isobars (1→2) and (3→4) and an isochore (2→3). The constancy of the volume in the section (2→3) follows from the fact that the pressure $p$ changes in proportion to the temperature $T$. If we denote the molar heat capacities of a gas at constant volume and at constant pressure by $c_V$ and $c_p$, we can write

$$Q = Q_{12} + Q_{23} + Q_{34} = c_p(T_2 - T_1) + c_V(T_3 - T_2) + c_p(T_4 - T_3) = (2c_p - c_V)\Delta t.$$

For a monatomic ideal gas $c_V = \frac{3}{2} R$, $c_p = \frac{5}{2} R$ ($R = 8,31$ J/(mol K). Final:

$$Q = \frac{7}{2} R \Delta t = 2910 \text{ Дж/моль.}$$
