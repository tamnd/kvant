F974. Determine the period of small oscillations of the system shown in figure: mass of the load m, elasticity of the spring k, ∠AOB=α. The rod and block are smooth, the spring is weightless. Reason for fixation at point A. Will the period of oscillation change if load m is also fixed at point O?

Let us assume that the load has moved a small distance x. Let's see how the thread tension changes.

Since the spring is weightless, at any moment of time (see figure)

$$F_y = T \cos \alpha,$$
that is, $k(\Delta l + \Delta l') = T \cos \alpha'$,
where $\Delta l' = |OO'|, \alpha' = \angle AO'B$. As can be seen from figure, $|OO'| = x/\cos \alpha$ and $\alpha' = \alpha + \Delta \alpha$, where $\Delta \alpha = \frac{x \mathrm{tg} \alpha}{l - x}$.
Since $\cos(\alpha + \Delta \alpha) = \cos \alpha \cos \Delta \alpha - \sin \alpha \sin \Delta \alpha \approx \cos \alpha - \Delta \alpha \cdot \sin \alpha = \cos \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin \alpha \mathrm{tg} \alpha$,
with a small mixture of cargo visa

$$T = \frac{k(\Delta l + \frac{x}{\cos \alpha})}{\cos \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin \alpha \mathrm{tg} \alpha} = \frac{k(\Delta l \cos \alpha + x)}{\cos^2 \alpha - \frac{x}{l - x} \sin^2 \alpha} \approx \frac{k(\Delta l \cos \alpha + x)}{\cos^2 \alpha}$$

(we neglected the second term in the denominator, since $\frac{x}{l - x} \to 0$ at $x \to 0$).

Now let's write down the equation of motion of the load:

$$ma = mg - T = mg - \frac{k \Delta l}{\cos \alpha} - \frac{kx}{\cos^2 \alpha} = -\frac{kx}{\cos^2 \alpha}$$

This equation describes a harmonious formula for the period of oscillation of a load on a spring.

From the solution it is clear that if a load is secured at point O, the period of oscillation will increase, since in this case, for the same value of α, the value of T will be less; the acceleration of the load fixed at point A will increase - the period of oscillation will decrease.

The volume of the conducting plate contains a huge number of free electrons. When the plate moves inside the capacitor, three forces act on free electrons: the Lorentz force from the magnetic field $evB$, directed downward; force $eE$ from the charges located on the surface of the plate movement, directed upward; force from the charges on the plates of the capacitor $eE_0$, directed downward. The sum of all these three forces inside the conductor must be equal to zero, that is

$$vB - E + E_0 = 0.$$

In this case, the charge on a capacitor with capacitance C must be equal to the charge on plates with area S:

$$Q = CU = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d} E_0 d = \varepsilon_0 E_0 S.$$
