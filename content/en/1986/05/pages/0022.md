About the fate of some understandable mechanics

Even in ancient times, people tried to find quantitative characteristics of the mechanical movement of the body. However, more than one century passed before “convenient” physical concepts were formed.

Thus, in the 17th century, when classical mechanics were actually being created, the question arose with particular urgency about what quantity was appropriate to use as a measure of motion. R. Descartes proposed “quantity of motion” as such a concept, which, in modern language, means the product of the mass of a body by the model of its speed, and formulated the “law of conservation of momentum.”

The fact that Descartes did not take into account the vector nature of speed led to a number of errors. In particular, when considering the practical problem of controlling the acceleration of a body, which was important for that time. According to Descartes, for example, it turned out that if two bodies collide, moving towards each other at the same speeds, and one body is slightly more massive than the other, then after controlling the initial movement of the more massive body. In reality, when controlling balls with similar masses, they fly apart in different directions.

Descartes' error was later corrected by H. Heugens and I. Newton.
