The work of transferring a single positive charge along a closed contour in the electric field of charges is always zero; Means,

$$E_0 d - Ed/2 + U = 0.$$

(3)

To find the value of $U = \frac{\varepsilon_0 E_0 S}{C}$, we find the value of $E_0$ from the system of equations (1)–(3). Let us assume for generality that the thickness of the plate is not $d/2$, but $d'$; Then

$$E_0 d - Ed' + \frac{\varepsilon_0 E_0 S}{C} = 0,$$

(4)

and from (1) and (4) we find $E_0 = \frac{vBd'}{\varepsilon_0 S/C} + d - d'$.

At $d' = 0$ $E_0 = 0$, and therefore $U = 0$.

At $d' = \frac{d}{2}$ $E_0 = \frac{vB}{1 + \frac{2\varepsilon_0 S}{Cd}}$ and $U = \frac{\varepsilon_0 vBS}{C + \frac{2\varepsilon_0 S}{d}}$.

At $d' = d$ there will be no field $E_0$, $E = vB$, and the voltage on the capacitor $C$ will always be $U = vBd$, regardless of its em-capacity.

E. A. Romishchevsky

F977. On the wall, the plane of which is deviated from the vertical by $4,87^\circ$ (see figure), a mirror is mounted. From what maximum distance can a person, whose height is 170 cm, enlarge at least part of his image in the mirror?

Let us pay attention to the fact that the thermal process is specified in coordinates $p, T$. It consists of three sections: two isobars (1→2) and (3→4) and an isochore (2→3). The constancy of the volume in the section (2→3) follows from the fact that the pressure $p$ changes in proportion to the temperature $T$. If we denote the molar heat capacities of a gas at constant volume and at constant pressure by $c_V$ and $c_p$, we can write

$$Q = Q_{12} + Q_{23} + Q_{34} = c_p(T_2 - T_1) + c_V(T_3 - T_2) + c_p(T_4 - T_3) = (2c_p - c_V)\Delta t.$$

For a monatomic ideal gas $c_V = \frac{3}{2} R$, $c_p = \frac{5}{2} R$ ($R = 8,31$ J/(mol K). Final:

$$Q = \frac{7}{2} R \Delta t = 2910 \text{ Дж/моль.}$$

S. M. Kozel

The image $A'B'$ of person $AB$ in the mirror is located symmetrically to the person himself relative to the flat mirror and is equal in size to the person. Consequently, the limiting case when a person sees at least part of his image in the mirror corresponds to the situation given in figure. As can be seen, $\angle AOB = \alpha$ and

$$OB = AB/tg \angle AOB = AB/tg \alpha \approx AB/\alpha$$

(we taught that the angle $\alpha$ is small and $tg \alpha \approx \alpha$).

Thus,

$$OB = \frac{AB}{\alpha} = \frac{1,7 \text{ м}}{4,87^\circ \pi} = 20 \text{ м.}$$
