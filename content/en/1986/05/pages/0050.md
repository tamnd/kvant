The required angle $M\widehat{AN}$ is the acute angle of the right triangle $APM$; That's why

$$\cos M\widehat{AN} = \frac{|AP|}{|AM|} = \frac{|AB| \cdot \cos \alpha \cdot \cos \alpha}{|AB|} = \cos^2 \alpha,$$

$$M\widehat{AN} = \arccos(\cos^2 \alpha).$$

If the angle is $\alpha = 90^\circ$, then the desired angle is right, which can also be written by the resulting correspondence.

So, the answer in all cases is the same: $\arccos(\cos^2 \alpha)$.

We see that it is convenient to depict triangular angles on a cube, two of whose faces have been redistributed. This is due to the fact that the cube contains two right angles. But the cube also contains a linear dimension—the length of its edge. This makes it possible to depict distances in the drawing.

Problem 3. The base $MN$ and vertex $K$ of the isosceles triangle $MNK$ are located on different faces of a right dihedral angle with edge $l$. Points $M$ and $K$ are distant from $l$ by a distance $a$, and the projection of point $N$ onto edge $l$ is equidistant from the projections of points $M$ and $K$ onto edge $l$. Find the distance from point $N$ to $l$ if $MK$ forms an angle with $l$ equal to $60^\circ$.

Solution. Let the length of the edge of the auxiliary cube $ABCDA_1B_1C_1B_1$ be equal to $a$ and let the two angles from the problem statement be formed by grams $AA_1B_1B$ and $ABCD$ (Fig. 7). Then the point $M$ lies on the edge $A_1B_1$, and the point $K$ lies on the edge $CD$ (or on their extensions). Let's align point $M$ with the vertex of the cube $A_1$. Let point $K$ be on edge $CD$ (it can be on the continuation of edge $CD$ beyond vertex $D$, which does not change our distances).

Fig. 7.

Let us drop the redistribution $KP$ onto the edge $AB$ and mark the midpoint $Q$ of the segment $AP$. In the plane of the face $AMB_1B$ we reconstruct from the point $Q$ the redistribution to the edge $AB$. The vertex $N$ of the triangle $MNK$ lies on this redistribution, since its projection $Q$ is equidistant from the projections of the points $M$ and $K$. Note that point $N$ is located above edge $MB_1$; otherwise triangle $MNK$ will not be equilateral.

Let's draw the segments $MD$ and $KQ$. $|MD| = a\sqrt{2}$ is like the diagonal of a square from side $a$. By condition, the angle between the crossing lines $AB$ and $MK$ is equal to $60^\circ$. But line $CD$ is parallel to line $AB$, hence $MKD = 60^\circ$. From the right triangle $MDK$ there is

$$|MK| = \frac{|MD|}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{2a\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}, |DK| = \frac{a\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

Under conditions $|NK| = |MK|$ and $|AP| = |DK|$. Then $|QP| = \frac{1}{2}|AP| = \frac{1}{2}|DK| = \frac{a}{\sqrt{6}}$. From the right triangle $QPK$ there is $|QK|^2 = |QP|^2 + |KP|^2 = 7a^2/6$. Finally, from the right triangle $NQK$ we get

$$|NQ| = \sqrt{|NK|^2 - |QK|^2} = \sqrt{|MK|^2 - |QK|^2} = \frac{1}{2}a\sqrt{6}.$$
