L. G. Markovich

F996. In a fly-by mass spectrometer, the source emits a stream of charged particles that are initially free-flying (Fig.4) and pass through the first sensor ($\Delta I_1$), located at a distance $L$ from the grid (C). Behind the grid, along the normal to it, the particles are acted upon by an electric force $F$. The particles turn, fly back through the grid and fly through the second sensor ($\Delta I_2$), located at the same distance $L$ from the grid. By changing the operating mode of the source, change the time between comparative sensors and find the shortest flight time t. What is the mass of the particles? (The initial speed depends on the voltage of the source, but its exact value is unknown.) How can you find the masses of particles if the source simultaneously emits several types of particles with different masses?

I. I. Vorobiev

F997. It is known that the luminous trail of a falling meteorite becomes brighter as it approaches the Earth. However, in the upper layers of the atmosphere it persists significantly longer than at Earth. Why?

A. S. Butov
