A little about the thermometer and Ferdinand's thermoscope

Candidate of Physical and Mathematical Sciences
A. I. BUZDIN

Hot, warm, cold, frost...
This is how we characterize in everyday life the degree of accumulation of different bodies. The measure of heating is temperature: the higher the temperature, the hotter the body.

We can easily distinguish hot from cold by touch, but science begins with quantitative measurements. Today, even a schoolchild knows that in order to change the temperature, you need to take a thermometer, that is, a thermometer. It was with the invention of the thermometer that the development of the science of heat began.

Most thermometers take advantage of the property of heat to expand when heated (and shrink when cooled). The first thermometer, which was built by Galileo in the very
at the beginning of the 17th century, he worked on water. It is understood that with its help it was possible to change temperatures no lower than the freezing point of water and no higher than its boiling point. Over time, the most widely used thermometer was the routine thermometer. In severe frosts, however, it stops working - at a temperature of - 38.8 °C the core freezes. An alcohol thermometer comes to the rescue here (the freezing point of alcohol is much lower than that of root, and is equal to - 97 ° C), which is also now widely used.

The credit for the invention of the alcohol thermometer goes to Duke Ferdinan II, who ruled Florence in the mid-17th century. Along with state affairs, the Duke devoted a lot of time to natural sciences. At one time he was a student of Galileo and made a significant contribution to the development of methods for measuring temperature. Before the thermometer came into common use, the thermoscope was common. This temperature control device only allows you to judge whether the temperature is higher or lower than a certain value. But it was impossible to measure the temperature with a thermoscope.
