Apparently, before our mental expansion in the region of space (volume $\Delta V$), protected by red at figure 2, energy was stored

$$\Delta W = w \Delta V = \frac{\varepsilon_0 E^2}{2} \Delta V = \frac{Q^2}{32 \pi^2 \varepsilon_0 R^4} \Delta V.$$

After compression, this area is already inside the sphere, and the direction of the field in it becomes equal to zero. The field practically did not penetrate into the formed gap between the hemispheres, and in the rest of the space it remained almost unchanged. It is easy to verify that the volume of the protected area coincides with the volume of the formed area, which is easily calculated:

$$\Delta V = \pi R^2 \Delta x.$$

Thus, according to the law of conservation of energy, we can write $\Delta A = \Delta W$, or

$$F \Delta x = \frac{Q^2}{32 \pi^2 \varepsilon_0 R^4} \pi R^2 \Delta x.$$

Hence the repulsion force between the two hemispheres turns out to be equal

$$F = \frac{Q^2}{32 \pi \varepsilon_0 R^2}.$$

So, we can assume that the energy of a charged body is the energy of interaction of its charges, and we can attribute it to the electric field created by the body in space. Which of these points of view to choose is a matter of taste within the power plant. However, when studying variable fields, the only possible approach is the second approach, connecting energy with the electric field.

Droplet model of the nucleus

Once the great English physicist E. Rutherford, the founder of nuclear physics, was asked what practical interest his discoveries in the field of radioactivity might have. The scientist replied that absolutely none. At that time, it was truly impossible to even imagine a way to use intranuclear processes and phenomena. Today, everyone understands the importance of nuclear energy in human life.

The most important role in the discovery of the possibility of using atomic energy was played by the ability of some heavy nuclei to fission (“Physics 10”, § 110). In 1938, it was discovered that when uranium nuclei are bombarded with neutrons, fragment nuclei of lighter elements are formed.

Comrade This process is always accompanied by the emission of several new neutrons.

Why can kernels do this? The first theory of nuclear fission was created in 1939 by the Danish theoretical physicists N. Bohr and the American J. Wheeler and, independently of them, the Soviet theoretical physicist Ya. I. Frenkel. This name is an explanation based on the droplet model of the atomic nucleus. According to this model, the nucleus, which is a structure of nucleons, behaves like a drop of electrically charged liquid. Let's look at this in more detail and, first of all, find out what and how the binding energy of a nucleus depends on - the energy that must be spent to separate the nucleus into its constituent nucleons.

Nuclear forces that attract nucleons to each other manifest themselves only at very short distances (they have a limited range of action, on the order of $10^{-15}$ m), therefore each nucleon practically interacts only with its close neighbors, and not with all the nucleons present in the nucleus. The situation is the same in an ordinary drop - since the forces of mechanical attraction act at distances not exceeding the distance between molecules, we have to consider the interaction of close neighbors. The number of neighbors of each nucleon can be considered constant.
