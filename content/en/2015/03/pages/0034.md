Cascades of regular polyhedra

Regular polyhedra (Fig. 1) have attracted the attention of scientists, builders, architects and many others since ancient times. They were amazed by the beauty, perfection, and harmony of these polyhedra.

A tetrahedron can be inscribed into a cube, and the vertices of the tetrahedron will lie at the vertices of the cube (Fig. 6). In turn, the cube can be inscribed in the dodecahedron so that the vertices of the cube lie at the vertices of the dodecahedron (Fig. 7).

⟦figure⟧

Fig. 1

Here we will tell you how regular polyhedra can be fit into each other. By inscribing one polyhedron into another, another into a third, etc., you can get beautiful “cascades” of regular polyhedra. An octahedron (Fig. 2) can be inscribed in a cube. The centers of the faces of the cube form the vertices of the octahedron inscribed in it.

When one regular polyhedron is inscribed into another, the vertices of the first can lie at the midpoints of the edges of the second. Thus, an octahedron (Fig. 8) can be inscribed into a regular tetrahedron.

⟦figure⟧

Fig. 3

In turn, the centers of the faces of the octahedron form the vertices of the cube inscribed in it (Fig. 3). Polyhedra with this property are called mutually dual. Another example of mutually dual regular polyhedra is the dodecahedron and icosahedron (Fig. 4, 5).
