Let us first consider a magnetic line near a circular turn. If the current in a straight wire is zero, then the magnetic field is created only by the circular current. The magnetic lines of this field are simple loops wound on a circular turn and closed in one revolution. In another limiting case, when the current in the circular coil is zero, the source of the magnetic field is the current in the straight wire. The magnetic lines of this field are circles perpendicular to a straight wire. Now it is clear that if both currents are non-zero, then the magnetic lines near the circular coil will be helical and each of the lines will go around the circular coil many times (see Fig.9). It is interesting that only in rare cases does the helix close; most often, it densely fills some toroidal surface (“donut”), having neither beginning nor end. So, if we start drawing a helical magnetic line from a certain point, then it, having made a full revolution around the straight wire, will not end up at the starting point. The same will happen on every next revolution. As a result, we obtain a semi-infinite helical line emanating from a given point. But in a similar way, we can complete this magnetic line, starting from the starting point in the opposite direction. So we get a line that has neither beginning nor end and densely fills the surface of the torus. Such hypersensitivity to initial conditions is characteristic of chaotic processes: small differences in the initial conditions give rise to huge differences in the final state. Theoretical prediction in this case becomes impossible. It is surprising that the chaotic behavior of magnetic lines occurs under strictly defined (deterministic) external conditions, which are determined by the configuration of conductors and currents. Such phenomena belong to the so-called deterministic, or dynamic, chaos. The chaotic nature of magnetic lines is not an exception, but is typical for most current systems, and only in some of the simplest cases magnetic lines are simple closed curves. But even in the case of the simplest systems, for example a circular loop with current, the pattern of magnetic lines changes significantly taking into account the weak, almost uniform magnetic field of the Earth.

Conclusion Concluding the article, let's try to highlight the main thing that we would like to keep in mind about magnetic lines: i Magnetic lines do not have sources and drains, but this does not mean that magnetic lines are always closed curves. i Near wires, magnetic lines are close to circles, but do not necessarily close geometrically precisely. i In the far zone, at a great distance from current-carrying wires, the pattern of magnetic lines has a simple appearance. i In the intermediate region, at moderate distances from current-carrying wires, magnetic lines often have a tangled, chaotic appearance and only rarely close. In this case, a small violation of the original symmetry is enough for the lines to become open and “tangled.”
