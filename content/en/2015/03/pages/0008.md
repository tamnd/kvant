K.Yu. Bogdanov we came across another attempt to explain the effect with small particles. There was a discussion about slowly pouring tea from a vessel with a spout (Fig.7). Two countercurrents formed in the center and along the edges (black arrows). These currents can be seen by the seagulls moving in them. And here is the $там^{1}$ explanation offered: “Leaves of mate tea float quite easily against the current when clean water is poured into it from a small height. The reason for the unusual phenomenon is the desire of the entire liquid (mate + jet + water in the upper cup) to reduce its surface energy. The surface tension of mate is lower than that of pure water. Therefore, the surfactants from the mate rush up the stream, carrying the tea leaves with them. Apparently, the same thing will happen if instead of mate you take a soap solution with tea leaves.” The value of the surface tension coefficient for this tea is not estimated and is not given.

Meanwhile, similar behavior can be observed not only with mate tea, but also with coffee, and with regular tea, and simply with cloudy water. Apparently, the author drew attention to such behavior precisely in these circumstances. In our opinion, the phenomenon can be explained by following the movement of tea leaves. Our arrows, showing the movement of tea leaves (not water!), are highlighted in red on figure 7. The main reason is the same as the phenomenon we described. Indeed, in this flow, two mutually perpendicular directions can be distinguished: along the flow and across it. Across the flow, the meniscus is caused by the wettability of the liquid, and therefore the tea leaves move to the edges of the vessel. In the longitudinal direction, the tea leaves are carried away by the current, but the meniscus at the very edge is convex. The tea leaves, although they resist the flow, tend to move back - away from the bulge. Therefore, when the liquid is carefully drained, a reverse flow is observed. What explanation can you offer, dear readers?

Vladimir Alekseevich Leshkovtsev (08/16/1922–05/25/2015)

On May 25, 2015, the oldest employee of our magazine, Vladimir Alekseevich Leshkovtsev, passed away. The idea of ​​​​creating the magazine «Kvant» is more than half a century old, but it was published only after several years of intensive preparation and careful selection of the editorial board and editorial staff. In order for «Kvant» to be “emitted” regularly, month after month, it took not only the creative efforts of its authors and members of the editorial board, but also the ability of the editorial staff to ensure the uninterrupted publishing process. One of those who, from the first issue of the magazine for twenty years, was responsible for its publication was Vladimir Alekseevich Leshkovtsev, a full-time deputy editor-in-chief. It is difficult to say who Vladimir Alekseevich did not know at the USSR Academy of Sciences and the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences, the founders of the journal. His close, literally personal contacts with a huge number of scientists and teachers greatly helped in solving the complex problems of publishing the journal. However, Vladimir Alekseevich was not only engaged in “routine” - his articles on the history of Soviet physics, reviews and reviews of popular science books, editorials related to memorable dates repeatedly appeared on pages «Kvant». Vladimir Alekseevich’s specialty was the “Philatelist’s Corner” section, which presented a whole collection of stamps on scientific and technical topics. One cannot fail to mention his active work as one of the best lecturers of the All-Union Society “Znanie”; the employees of «Kvant» had the good fortune to constantly be convinced of his oratory skills without leaving their workplace. Those whom fate brought closely together with Vladimir Alekseevich Leshkovtsev will not forget their joint work, confidential communication with him, his lessons of wisdom, the value of which only increases with time.
