Three planets – three physics and – partly – the “Electricity” section in textbooks. A reflection on what the physics of a civilization of electric rays and eels looks like, existing in the depths of the ocean, is provided to the land reader as an easy home exercise. And dolphins and bats hold joint conferences on acoustics (I was invited once). Some laws of physics can be changed to some extent, as long as we do not come into conflict with something fundamental, for example, the law of conservation of energy. Let's say, you can replace Ohm's law - current is proportional to voltage - with the law "current increases with voltage, but very slowly", and nothing fundamentally will change. Moreover, it is possible to invent and create conditions in which this law will be observed in a certain range of values. But it is impossible to do so (without using external energy sources) so that the sign in Ohm’s law changes. When the system of units changes, the coefficients in all physical laws must change. And the absence of a coefficient suggests that it was this law that was once applied to determine the units of the quantity it is talking about. There are laws that only apply under certain conditions, such as Ohm's law. You can come up with such a tricky medium that in Coulomb’s law the charge will not be in the first power, although it would be more common to say that this is a nonlinear medium with a dependence of the dielectric constant on the field strength. But under no circumstances can charges (as well as masses in the law of universal gravitation) have different degrees - this would mean that the result of calculations can depend on which charge we call “first” and which “second”. Any physical law is satisfied under certain conditions and with some accuracy, and these limits are either verified experimentally or follow from some connections with other laws. And in many cases we have the right to ask: what will happen if such and such is discovered in the next sign? You can ask this about the most fundamental laws. For example: what will happen if the law of conservation of energy is violated in some distant sign? What happens if you change the sign of the friction coefficient or

It is impossible to change the constants much - atoms, molecules, and so on will not be formed in the changed world. If such a world is possible and if the laws of physics there are the same, then there cannot be complex systems in it, and therefore no human observer. Hence the conclusion is drawn: perhaps there was more than one successive Universe, but we see exactly this one, because there is no one else to see. Such reasoning lies on the border of the field of today's human science; if you want to go deeper, ask on the Internet “fundamental constants” and “anthropic principle”. The parameters of objects and materials can be changed within fairly wide limits; fundamental contradictions do not arise soon. One can ask how the world will change if some of the parameters of all or some materials increase (or decrease) tenfold. For example, strength, Young's modulus, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, electrical strength, refractive index, melting point and so on. Quite intellectual physical and engineering entertainment. You might want to think about this too. When determining possible variations of quantities, laws and constants, one must ensure consistency. A forecast for the development of a situation that is impossible according to existing views may itself turn out to be impossible, because the laws of science, the apparatus of understanding and forecasting, were created through the study of possible situations. However, this is not the case in all systems - for example, in chess problems it is possible to analyze positions that could not be obtained in a real game.
