Pinocchio and his scientific work

I. BOYARINOV

ONE DAY, PINOCOCIO RETURNED HOME FROM SCHOOL, clearly puzzled. Instead of going for a little walk and rest as usual, he sat down at the table and put a blank sheet of paper in front of him. “The teacher said that soon we will have a scientific and practical conference at our school, at which students should talk about the results of their research work,” Buratino explained, responding to Papa Carlo’s perplexed look. -What kind of work is this? I've never done it! Once I finish school and university, then I find myself a position as a junior researcher in a laboratory - then I’ll start conducting research. What can I do now? And I know so little now! – My dear Pinocchio, you don’t have to wait so long! Mathematics is so big that you can always find new and accessible problems! Have you already completed similar triangles? - We've passed. “So come up with something new about them,” suggested Papa Carlo. – What could be new here? There are three signs of similarity... The sides of one triangle are proportional to the sides of another... - That’s how it is, but if one side of the triangle is increased by 25 times, the other by 15 times, and the third by 9 times, then can the new triangle be similar to the original one? – asked Papa Carlo. Let this be your task 1. “No, there’s no way the new triangle can turn out to be similar to the original one,” Buratino objected confidently.

There is a formula for the sides of similar triangles:

AB BC CA = = = K. A 1 B 1 B 1 C 1 C 1 A 1

The sides must be proportional. And if one side is enlarged by 25 times, the other by 15, then there will be no proportionality between the sides. What similarity could there be? “Nothing,” Buratino concluded confidently. “We can say, perhaps, that you are entangled in three pines, or rather, in three directions!” – Papa Carlo smiled. In this case, let's simplify the situation and consider just two sides of such figures. Here's Problem 2. Two opposite sides of rectangle ABCD are increased by 2 times, and the other two sides are increased by 8 times. Can the new rectangle be similar to the original one? – I’m just warning you, don’t rush to answer so as not to make a mistake. Think about it! Now Pinocchio showed caution, did not rush, concentrated, and after a few minutes drew two rectangles on checkered paper - one 1 ¥ 2, the second 4 ¥ 8. These rectangles clearly showed that the situation described in problem 2 was quite possible. If the first side of length 2 is increased by 2 times, the sides of length 1 are increased by 8 times, then exactly the second rectangle will be obtained. This example made Pinocchio wary. “Okay,” Papa Carlo said approvingly. – Now you are convinced that sometimes reality contradicts our usual ideas. Let's consider a generalization of Problem 2, let this be a training Problem 3 for you. The two opposite sides of the rectangle ABCD are increased by p times, and the other two sides by q times. Can the new rectangle be similar to the original one and if so, under what conditions? Pinocchio coped with this task. You too can solve it by checking your answer with the one given at the end of the magazine. Inspired by this beginning, Pinocchio returned to task 1.
