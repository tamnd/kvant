Definition. We call an electrical circuit a connected flat graph (possibly with multiple edges, but without loops), in which: • each edge is associated with a positive number; • one of the edges is highlighted, its ends are marked with “+” and “–” signs; • when any edge is removed, the graph remains connected. The requirement of connectivity after removing an edge is necessary to ensure that there are no “extra” sections in the circuit (through which current does not flow). The question may arise why we require that the graph be flat - after all, an electrical circuit can be soldered from wires located in any way in space. The explanation is this: for our task, flat electrical circuits are enough for us, and for them some definitions are formulated more simply. Definition. The selected edge is called a battery, the rest are called resistors. The number associated with the battery is called the battery voltage, and the numbers associated with the resistors are called their resistances. The vertices marked with “+” and “–” signs are called battery terminals.
