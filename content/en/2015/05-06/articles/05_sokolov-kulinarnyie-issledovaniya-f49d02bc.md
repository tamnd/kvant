Let's turn to chemistry for help and ask it what the cooking process is and what its speed depends on. The answer is this. The cooking process is a chemical reaction that involves breaking certain chemical bonds in protein molecules. This is the first. And secondly, the rate of chemical reactions is extremely dependent on temperature. For the process of cooking, as well as for many other processes of organic chemistry, this law is as follows: with an increase in temperature by $10 ^\circ C$, the reaction rate increases k times, where k is some constant number for a given reaction. This relationship is called a geometric progression. It is not often mentioned in school physics textbooks. But be prepared for the fact that if we encounter a geometric progression in life, then something will definitely happen. For example, borscht left on the stove will turn sour very quickly, or someone will inappropriately get sick after eating four servings of cold ice cream.
