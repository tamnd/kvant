Imagine that you have almost arrived at the North Pole, only a few kilometers left. For such small distances, we can assume that the earth in this place is flat. And so, for example, from point $A_{1}$ you flew in 1 minute to point $A_{2}$, which is 2 times closer to the north pole than $A_{1}$. “What a good plan,” said the woodpecker, “perhaps this is the route I’ll fly.” – Wait, let’s say that in my (very approximate) picture the angle $A_{1} OA_{2}$ is equal to $40^\circ$ (by the way, it seems to be approximately equal to this). Thus, approaching the north pole 2 times, you will rotate around the earth's axis by approximately $40^\circ$. “Yes, that’s exactly how it will be,” agreed the woodpecker, “but I won’t linger at point $A_{2}$ and will fly further.” “And after a while you will rotate around the earth’s axis by another $40^\circ$, ending up at point $A_{3}$,” said Busenka. – How long do you think it will take you to get to point $A_{3}$? Woodpecker Spiatel thought about it. “Judging by the picture and common sense,” he finally said, “this crooked triangle $A_{1} OA_{2}$ has the same shape as the triangle $A_{2} OA_{3}$.” They differ only in size - the second triangle has side $OA_{2}$ 2 times smaller than the corresponding side $OA_{1}$ of the first triangle. This means that the curved side along which I need to fly is also half as large. So I can treat it in half a minute. - This is what’s terrible! - Busenka exclaimed. “Poor woodpecker,” Ogryza picked up, “you’d better choose some other place to rest.” After all, in another quarter of a minute you will arrive at point $A_{4}$, then after another 1/8 minute to point $A_{5}$ and so on! – Are you saying that I will never reach the North Pole? That I will continue to wander around these points - $A_{1}, A_{2}, A_{3}, A_{4}$ ... Don’t confuse me. I have already heard the story of how the tortoise chased Achilles. If in one minute I flew half the distance to the North Pole, then in another minute I will already be there! - Yes, but from which direction will you arrive? – Busenka asked. - From the southwest. “There is no southwest side at the North Pole,” Ogryza said sternly. – Having departed from point $A_{2}$, in half a minute you will arrive at point $A_{3}$, turning around the earth’s axis at $40^\circ$, in another quarter of a minute you will again turn to $40^\circ$, after 1/8 of a minute - again at $40^\circ$ and so on. This means that to reach the North Pole, you will have to make an infinite number of revolutions! A couple of minutes ago you said you were dizzy? “Oh,” said the woodpecker, “it’s very dizzy.” I think I'm seasick. Or rather, it got screwed up. This is a dangerous activity - flying to the northeast!
