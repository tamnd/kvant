ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YEARS AGO ACADEMICIAN LENZ (LENZ,

Emilius Khristianovich, 1804–1865), who participated in the remarkable circumnavigation of Russian warships, proposed the simplest scheme for the circulation of the waters of the world ocean, considering it a layer of water of constant thickness (depth). Taking into account the high temperature at the surface of tropical waters and their low temperature at depth, where they come from high latitudes, from the condition of conservation of mass flow, he concluded that a continuous circulation of water in the ocean must arise - a fact that had long been known to navigators. (Of course, the real picture of currents is complicated by the presence of continents, differences in depths, the movement of air masses, and the rotation of the Earth.) This first kinematic model of circulation in the world's oceans can be illustrated with a simple example, by placing a large piece of ice at one end of a bathtub filled with water, and at the other end (under the bathtub) organizing heating (Fig.1).

But why does cold water not flow from Greenland exactly south along the meridian, but deviate towards North America, i.e. to the right? And why does the warm water it displaces not flow exactly towards Greenland, but is diverted towards Europe, i.e. again to the right? This remarkable fact is explained by the inertia of the moving mass and the rotation of the Earth. Let us explain this figure 2, b. Let at the initial moment of time a certain particle be at point A, having a speed of $u_{A}$ along the meridian. If the Earth did not rotate, then after some time this particle would end up at point B on the same meridian. Now let’s take into account the rotation of the Earth with angular velocity $\omega$, which leads to the transfer of point A to point D, and point B to C. It is clear that the transfer speed $v_{A}$ of point A is greater than the transfer speed $v_{B}$ of point B - simply because point A is further from the axis of rotation than B. If someone forced a particle to move from A to B strictly along the meridian, then it would pass through the considered meridian a period of time would end up at point C. However, since the particle of water is “free”, it, by inertia, tends to maintain the speed $v_{A}$ and, once on the parallel BC, ends up at point $D '$, lying to the right of C. It looks as if in the system of the rotating Earth this particle in the process of movement was acted upon by a force directed to the right. This is the force of inertia, called the Coriolis force (Coriolis, Gaspard-Gustave, 1792–1843).
