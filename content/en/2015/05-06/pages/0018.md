M2404. A numismatist has 100 coins that are identical in appearance. He knows that among them there are 30 real and 70 counterfeit coins. In addition, he knows that the masses of all real coins are the same, and the masses of all counterfeit coins are different, and any counterfeit coin is heavier than the real one; however, the exact coin weights are unknown. There are two-pan scales without weights, on which you can compare the masses of two groups consisting of the same number of coins in one weighing. In what minimum number of weighings on these scales can a numismatist be guaranteed to find at least one real coin? S. Berlov, I. Bogdanov
