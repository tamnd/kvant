What is the process of cooking? While our schoolchildren are having lunch, let's try to figure out for ourselves what the secret of the pressure cooker is. After all, it’s a fact that everything cooks faster in it. The question is why? figure 2 shows a diagram of the construction of a pressure cooker. As you can see, it differs from a regular saucepan only in that it is equipped with a tight-fitting lid. “Well, then everything is clear,” someone might rush to answer, “under the lid in the pressure cooker, the pressure is greater than atmospheric pressure, and therefore everything cooks faster there.” “Wait,” we will answer. – Of course, the pressure in a pressure cooker is greater than atmospheric pressure. Designers take this fact into account and install a fuse valve in its cover. When the pressure under the cap exceeds atmospheric pressure by a certain specified amount, the weight is lifted, the valve opens, and some of the air and water vapor is released into the atmosphere. But pressure alone does not increase the speed of cooking.
