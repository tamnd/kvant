Competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

We continue our next competition on solving mathematical problems for students in grades 6–8. Send solutions to the problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» or by email: savin.contest@gmail.com (marked “Contest “Mathematics 6–8”). Be sure to include your name, class, and home address. As before, we welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by mathematical clubs. We ask circle leaders to provide an email address or contact phone number. According to tradition, the winning clubs of the correspondence competition are invited to the final in-person tournament.

6. Petya calls a natural number lucky if it can be uniquely divided into the sum of 20 different natural terms (the order of the terms is not important). Find all the lucky numbers. Folklore

7. The rating of each of the 20 chess players is expressed as a natural number from 1 to 20; any two have different ratings. The chess players are divided into two teams of 10 people so that the total ratings in the teams are equal. Teams play a 100-game tournament match, in which each chess player plays one game against each of the 10 chess players on the opposing team. In any game, the chess player with the highest rating wins; for each victory of its player, the team receives 1 point. What could be the result of the match tournament? P. Kozhevnikov, based on the problem of V. Proizvolov

9. Given a square ABCD. Through vertex C we draw a straight line m that has no other points in common with the square (see figure). 10. On a square checkered field of 10 ¥ 10 there is a squadron of 10 ships. Ships are 1 ¥ 2 rectangles without common points with sides along the grid lines. a) Prove that it is possible to fire 32 shots so as to be sure to hit some ship. b) Is it possible to get by with 30 shots? A. Berdnikov, D. Kuznetsov
