"Mathematical component"

The winner of the prestigious “Enlightener” award in 2015 for the best popular science book in Russian in the “Natural and Exact Sciences” category was the book “Mathematical $составляющая»^{1}$. The editors and compilers of the book are N.N. Andreev, S.P. Konovalov, N.M. Panyunin; designer R.A. Koksharov. Two of this team are members of the editorial board of our journal. “Mathematical Component” is a collection of short popular science articles written by famous scientists. The book consists of three parts. The first (“blue”) part contains stories showing the vital necessity of mathematical discoveries and research for humanity. The second (“green”) part is about the manifestations of mathematics in ordinary phenomena and things. The third (“red”) part is also devoted to applied issues, but is addressed to readers with more serious mathematical training. The fascinating style of presentation and colorful illustrations make the book accessible to a wide range of readers, including schoolchildren. Below we present an expanded version of one of the articles from the collection “Mathematical Component”.

Satellite navigation

N. PANYUNIN

THE CONCEPT THAT YOUR GPS OR GLONASS receiver receives your coordinates from satellites in orbit is erroneous. Firstly, the satellites simply don’t know them, and secondly, the satellites send their messages “into space”, to everyone at once, and not just to your receiver. How does the navigator determine your coordinates? In a satellite navigation system, two main segments can be distinguished: space and management. A space segment is a constellation of satellites evenly spaced around the Earth. The control segment, located on Earth, ensures, in particular, synchronization of “system-wide” time on all satellites and adjustment of satellite orbits. Each satellite constantly transmits navigation messages containing, in particular, the coordinates of the satellite at the time the message was sent and the time of sending. The receiver receiving such a message can calculate the distance to the satellite: $d = ( t^{(} пр^{)} - t^{(} отпр^{)} )$ s. In this formula, the signal travel time (from the sending time $t^{(} отпр^{)}$ to the receiving time $t^{(} пр^{)} )$ is multiplied by the speed of propagation of the radio signal, i.e. the speed of light c.
