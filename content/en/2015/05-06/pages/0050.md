Let's draw second-order curves!

In this “Kaleidoscope” we will talk about some devices that allow you to accurately draw second-order curves: parabolas, ellipses, hyperbolas.

Parabolograph Cavalieri

This elegant way of drawing a parabola was invented by the Italian mathematician Bonaventura Cavalieri (Italian Bonaventura Francesco Cavalieri, Latin Cavalerius, 1598 - 1647) back in the 17th century. Description of the design. A right angle slides along a ruler that is motionless relative to the sheet so that one of its sides (horizontal) is constantly in contact with the ruler (Fig.1). At the second right angle, the top with the lead slides along the vertical side of the first, and the sides slide so that they always pass through the pins, one of which is rigidly fixed to the ruler, and the other to the horizontal side of the movable angle.

Rationale. Consider a triangle formed by the sides of the second angle and a ruler. In it, the square of the length of the height lowered onto the hypotenuse is equal to the product of the projections of the legs onto the hypotenuse. Drawing a parabola using thread

Description of the design. A square slides along a ruler that is motionless relative to the sheet so that one of the legs (horizontal) is constantly in contact with the ruler. One end of the thread is fixed at the top of the square, which does not lie on the ruler, the other end of the thread is secured with a button on a sheet of paper. The pencil presses the thread against the leg of the square so that it is always taut (Fig. 2, 3). Rationale. Let's say the length of the thread is equal to the length of the vertical leg (as an exercise, we suggest reducing the general case to this particular case).
