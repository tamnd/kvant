The main light-sensitive molecule in all vertebrates (people, animals, birds...), as well as in the octopus, is the retinal molecule $C_{20} H_{28} O$. Did you recognize her? This is a carotene molecule divided in half, to which an oxygen atom is attached. With the help of an oxygen atom, the retinal molecule combines with protein and forms the light-sensitive substance of our eye - visual purple, or rhodopsin. Cyanidin (from Latin “blue”) determines the color of blackcurrants, blackberries, and cherries. Naturalists define the color of cyanidin as purple. And here they repeat a common mistake! In everyday life, we usually use the name “purple” incorrectly. We call this word the color that is obtained by mixing red and blue paints.
