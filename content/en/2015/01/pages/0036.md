Combinatorial problems

13. In the cave there were 100 bags with masses of 1 kg and 2 kg lying in a row. The robbers kept gold in some bags, and silver in others. Next to each two-kilogram bag there were two bags of silver, and next to each kilogram there were at least one bag of silver. Ali Baba took all the gold from the cave. What is the largest amount of gold he could get? A. Shapovalov

14. Alice and Basilio share the obtained pile of 100 wallets, which contain 1, 2, ..., 100 gold pieces respectively. They take turns, Alice begins. In one move, you can take any wallet from the heap and either take it for yourself or give it to your partner. Once someone has 50 wallets, someone else takes all the rest from the pile. What is the largest amount of gold that Alice can secure for herself, no matter how Basilio acts? A. Shapovalov

15. Petya marked 20 red and 20 blue dots on the circle. Then Vasya draws the chords so that the ends of each chord are the same color and so that these chords do not intersect (even at the vertices). What is the largest number of chords that Vasya is guaranteed to be able to draw, no matter how Petya marks the points? E. Bakaev

16. There are $n^{3}$ unit cubes. Petya paints them in two colors (possibly in different ways), with each edge one color. Vasya will win if he can use all the cubes to form a cube with side n, in which each face is the same color. At what minimum n will Petya not be able to stop him? A. Shapovalov

17. Each of n identical test tubes contains no more than half a test tube of a solution of different concentrations. The chemist knows where everything is, and can pour from one test tube to another any amount of solution he needs (as long as the test tube does not overflow). In what minimum number of transfusions can he be guaranteed to ensure that the solution in each test tube is of the same concentration (with some test tubes possibly becoming empty)? A. Shapovalov

⟦column⟧

18. In the Kingdom of Crooked Libra, pan scales that show equilibrium when the masses of the loads on the pans are equal are prohibited. However, for any a > 1, unequal-armed scales are allowed, showing equilibrium if the weight of the load on the left pan is a times greater than the weight on the right. Having only one scale, Tolya can identify one counterfeit (lighter) coin out of 8 in two weighings. What is the value of a on Tolya’s scales? (All real coins weigh the same.) A. Shapovalov

19. The six teams in the one-round tournament scored 10, 7, 6, 6, 3 and 3 points. How many points (not necessarily a whole number) were awarded for a win, if 1 point was given for a draw and 0 for a loss? A. Shapovalov

20. There are 14 players participating in a chess tournament. In each round, they are divided into pairs randomly, but two chess players who have played each other before cannot play a second time. The tournament ends when such a division cannot be made. Find the minimum possible number of tours. A. Blinkov

21. The checkered strip 1 ¥ n is divided into single cells. Is it possible to paint the sides of the cells in three colors so that when throwing out segments of any of the colors, along the remaining segments it is possible to go from each vertex to any other? A. Shapovalov

22. Andrey painted some cells of the 100 ¥ 100 square. Then he is allowed to color the cells according to the following rule: if three cells are painted in a 2 ¥ 2 square, then he paints the fourth. What is the smallest number of cells that Andrey must paint initially so that later he can paint the entire square? A.Garkavy

23. The 5 ¥ 5 square table is filled with numbers. Petya rearranged its columns so that no column remained in place. In the resulting table, Vasya rearranged the rows so that no row remained in place. The result is the original table. What is the greatest possible number of different numbers in it? B.Frenkin

24. There is an invisible rook on the ¥100 checkered board. In one question you can specify any set of cells and find out how many of them are captured by the rook. How many questions like this are needed to know for sure the position of the rook? (It is considered that the rook hits the square on which it stands.) A. Shapovalov
