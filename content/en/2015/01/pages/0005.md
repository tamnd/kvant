These are not Chinese dragons, but two main molecules of the plant and animal kingdoms - chlorophyll and heme

Have you ever noticed that red always has a little blue in it?

– All the colors that we see around us arise from the mixing of seven pure colors. Thus, the “scarlet-purple-lilac-blue” line, widely represented in flower colors, corresponds to a mixture of red and blue in different proportions. But black is the absence of light at all. So, for example, soot is black precisely because it absorbs everything that falls on it.

So, the light coming to us from the Sun has no color. Color appears only after interaction with a substance. Different molecules absorb different colors of sunlight. Therefore, after light is reflected or passed through a substance, only those colors remain in it that the molecules refused to “eat.” When it comes into contact with a substance, the light becomes colored. If the substance included in the paint absorbs everything except red color, then a ball painted with such paint will appear red to us in the light, and such paint, of course, will be called red.

Colors of Nature

The molecules that Nature uses as colors are called pigments. The most noticeable pigment on our planet is undoubtedly $C_{55} H_{72} O_{5} N_{4} Mg$, i.e. chlorophyll (from the Greek “green” and “leaf”). We call a living organism that has such molecules a plant.

The magnesium atom, which is at the center of a wonderful lace of carbon, nitrogen and hydrogen atoms, absorbs all incident light except green. This, remaining “uneaten”, reflected green light gives the forests and fields their emerald color. And the chlorophyll molecule itself, after absorbing light, acquires a magical ability - it begins to synthesize carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water:

$CO_{2} + H_{2} O \to ( CH_{2} O )_{n} + O_{2}$.

Thanks to this constantly ongoing reaction, life is maintained on Earth. If a magnesium atom in a chlorophyll molecule is replaced by an iron atom, the changed molecule will absorb all colors except red. Chemists call such a molecule “heme” (from the Greek “blood”). It is the main part of the hemoglobin molecule (from Greek “blood” and Latin “ball”), which the body of animals with blood circulation uses to transport oxygen. It is this molecule that gives the red color to animal blood. – So, are the most important molecules of the plant and animal kingdoms twin sisters? - Yes, that's true! And even the grape snail (Helix pomatia), which has aristocratic blue blood, is original in only one thing - instead of magnesium and iron in its hemoglobin molecule - copper.

Summer ends and golden autumn begins. Chlorophyll breaks down, and carotene and lycopene become the main suppliers of Nature's colors. The carotene molecule (from Latin "carrot") $C_{40} H_{56}$ is a long chain of carbon and hydrogen atoms (hydrocarbons) that ends in two benzene rings. This design absorbs all light except yellow very well. Therefore, carrots, persimmons and mangoes, which contain a lot of carotene, have a beautiful yellow-orange color. The “hydrocarbon” skeleton of the carotene molecule is very stable. But its ends, the benzene rings, can change in response to the chemical environment. At the same time, the “tastes” of the molecule also change - it begins to “eat” other colors, and objects containing such modified molecules acquire a new color.
