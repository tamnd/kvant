Problem 5. Consider a finite set of boys, each of whom knows several girls. The question is, under what conditions can young men be married so that each of them marries a girl he knows? Theorem (Philip Hall, 1935). A solution to the wedding problem exists if and only if any K boys from a given set of N boys are collectively familiar with at least K girls $( 1 \le K \le N ).$ The need is obvious. If some K boys know less than K girls, then some of them will not find a match. Let us prove sufficiency by contradiction. Let N be the smallest natural number for which the theorem is false. Obviously, N > 1. It may be that every K boys $( 1 \le K < N )$ know more than K girls, or it may be that there is a subset of K boys who know exactly K girls. In the first case, the first boy can marry any girl he knows, and then the condition of the theorem will still be satisfied for the remaining boys and girls. Indeed, let every K boys know more than K girls. One of these girls may have married the first boy. But without her, the boys know at least K girls. This means that after the marriage of any couple, there remain N - 1 boy and M - 1 girl (where M is the initial number of girls), for which the condition of the theorem is still satisfied. But N was the smallest, for which the theorem is false, which means that all the young men can be married. In the second case, there are K boys who know exactly K girls. Since K < N, we can marry these boys to these girls. Let's show that the rest of the young men can also be married. Consider an arbitrary subset of the remaining N – K young men. Let there be L young men in it. Then, according to the theorem, K married boys plus these L boys know at least K + L girls. Since K married boys are not familiar with unmarried girls, the remaining L boys must be familiar with at least L unmarried girls. This means that for the remaining N – K young men, the condition of the theorem is satisfied, and they will be able to get married. The theorem has been proven. Hall's theorem allows us to solve Problem 4 in another way. Let's call the schoolchildren boys and the plates girls. Let us consider the schoolboy's acquaintances to be the plates on which lies the piece of pie that the schoolboy brought.
