Kaleidoscope within a kaleidoscope

Kaleidoscope «Kvant» this issue is dedicated to... kaleidoscope! The name comes from the Greek words $\kappa \alpha \lambda ος$ - beautiful, $\varepsilon \iota \delta ος$ - view, $\sigma \kappa ο\pi \varepsilon \omega$ - looking, observing. This optical toy device was invented at the beginning of the 19th century and quickly became a favorite pastime in many countries, including Russia.

Inside the cylindrical tube of the kaleidoscope are three long rectangular mirrors, forming a mirror prism. Behind the triangle at the base of the prism there is a “backfill”. When the kaleidoscope rotates, it sprinkles, creating a random picture. The image formed in the triangle is reflected in the mirrors and fills the entire field of view with a beautiful pattern.

Conventional kaleidoscopes use identical mirrors, which means that the triangle at the base of the prism is equilateral.

⟦column⟧

Let's reflect a triangle located on a plane relative to its sides, then reflect new triangles relative to the resulting sides, etc. (each reflection is equivalent to flipping the triangle). Gradually, an image will appear, which is observed in the kaleidoscope.
