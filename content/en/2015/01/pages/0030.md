KVANT 2015/№1

About the top and the gyroscope

S. DVORYANINOV

IN THE PREVIOUS TIMES, WHEN ALARM CLOCKS WERE mechanical, many guys waited impatiently for such a device, so necessary in everyday life... to break. Then it was possible to remove the watch case and see its mechanism - a system of gears connected to one another. They differed in the number of teeth. You turn one gear, not very quickly, but the one next to it can rotate very quickly in response. It all depends on the gear ratio - the ratio of the number of teeth on each gear. And then, if the watch could not be repaired, the next stage began - the mechanism was disassembled into separate parts. And here on the table are a dozen small tops - an axis and a gear on it. You could grab the axle with two fingers and spin it vertically. Afterwards, this top spun on the table for a long time and did not fall. Why is the name such - spinning top? Perhaps little wolf cubs spin around in a similar manner, trying to catch themselves by their own tail and then falling exhausted from fatigue. There is also such a toy - a spinning top. It unwinds by periodically pressing the handle. A regular top falls over time because friction causes its rotation speed to decrease. And the spinning top has a constant energy supply, which compensates for the loss of kinetic energy. This is still an amazing phenomenon: a top stands on one leg and does not fall! If we try to tilt the rotating top, we will not be able to do this. In response, the top will only move a little to the side and continue to rotate as if nothing had happened. It’s better to feel this property with your own hands, and here’s how. When you change a tube or tire on your bike, don't rush to put the wheel back in place. Take the wheel axle in both hands, place it horizontally and ask a friend to spin the wheel. So, the wheel spins, you feel its weight. Now try tilting the wheel to the left or right, tilting the axle, or turning the wheel left and right. You will immediately notice that this is not easy to do. At the slightest attempt to change the position of the wheel axle, the pressure on your

fingers increases. However, you can move the wheel back and forth or up and down without much effort - during such movements the wheel axis retains its original direction. But when turning or tilting, this direction changes. This simple experiment clearly shows the main property of a top - to keep the direction of the rotation axis unchanged in space. Let's continue our experiments with the top. Having untwisted the top on a sheet of cardboard, you can throw it up. After spinning in the air and ending up on the cardboard again, the top will continue its rotation. The cardboard can be tilted slightly - the axis of rotation will remain vertical. True, in this case, the fulcrum of the top will slowly begin to slide down the inclined plane. To prevent this from happening, you can make a small depression in the cardboard - a hole into which the support is placed. Then the cardboard can be tilted in different directions quite significantly, and the axis of the top will remain vertical. And now - the most interesting part. Let's imagine that we, being at the North Pole of the Earth, spun our top around a vertical axis and placed it on a horizontal piece of cardboard. The cardboard shows the directions north and south, west and east, and the cardboard can be placed according to these directions. Let's cover our top, for example, with a paper cap. Let it rotate there, remaining invisible to us. Using the compass, we will now set off from the indicated point along the meridian towards the equator, holding the cardboard with the top in front of us. At each point on the earth's surface, the vertical is directed along the radius of the earth; Accordingly, each point has its own horizontal plane. So, when we move along the meridian from north to south, it will seem to us that our horizontal cardboard remains unchanged in its horizontal position. But when we go halfway to the equator and get to the parallel corresponding to $45 ^\circ$ northern latitude, the cardboard will rotate in space from its initial position through an angle of $45 ^\circ$. But for us this change will be completely unnoticeable. Let's now remove the cap that hid our top. What do you think the direction of the rotation axis will be? Will the axis still be vertical? No, now the axis of the top will not be vertical. It will maintain a vertical direction at the North Pole. And the angle between this old vertical and the new vertical at the point we have reached will be equal to $45 ^\circ$. It is at this angle in the north direction that the axis of the top will now be deflected from the vertical (Fig.1). So, when moving along the meridian to the south, the axis of the top deviates to the north. We will watch this deviation. Let at some point on our path the deviation of the axis from the vertical be, say, $30 ^\circ$.
