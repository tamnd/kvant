Royal Society of London

One of the undoubted advantages of scientific work is participation in scientific conferences. They always try to hold them in the most interesting places, which allows you to combine business with pleasure. If the conference venue is London, then be sure that you will be offered an excursion along the route “Buckingham Palace - Westminster Abbey - Parliament - Trafalgar Square - National Gallery - Tower”. Along the route of the excursion is the building of the Royal Society. The Royal Society is the English Academy of Sciences, the world's first academy, founded in 1662. The Academy has a museum, the Center for the History of Science, which houses the archives of Sir Isaac Newton, the scientific instruments of Sir Henry Cavendish, and even the quadrant of Sir Francis Drake, the first Englishman to circumnavigate the world. But my gaze always stops at a small piece of pink paper. This is a litmus test, something with which chemistry began for all of us at school. I don't know about you

but I have always been extremely interested in the question of how this magical paper is made. Today I know the answer to this question and I want to share it with you. The inventor of litmus is Robert Boyle (1627–1691). Sir Robert Boyle was a completely unusual man. The thirteenth child of Richard Boyle, Earl of Cork, he became a student at Eton University at the age of eight. By the age of thirty, he became a famous encyclopedist in Europe. He is a philosopher, a physicist, a chemist, a theologian, and a poet. His most famous discoveries and inventions are litmus paper, modern ink, the evacuation pump, the production of vacuum, the observation of cold boiling, the creation of analytical chemistry, the analysis of mineral waters, the production of white phosphorus, the discovery of the gas law. He is the initiator of the creation and president of the Royal Society of London, director of the East India Trading Company, philanthropist (Boyle bequeathed all his capital to the development of science in England). Boyle also conducts optical research, in particular, studying the question of the appearance of color in nature. It forms those concepts that today seem to us

Places associated with the name of Robert Boyle: Lismore Castle, Eton, Oxford, London are simple and understandable, but which in his time were extremely revolutionary. Having noticed the dependence of the color of plant juice on the acidity of the environment, he created the first chemical device - an indicator of the acidity of the environment. To do this, he soaks a strip of paper with the blue-red juice of litmus lichen. This is how the first litmus test appeared. This was the first step from ancient alchemy to modern chemical science. And COUNT ROBERT BOYLE took this step.
