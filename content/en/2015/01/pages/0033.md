On one of the days, excursions were organized for the participants, but the main event of this day was the personal Olympiad. In all comparisons, schoolchildren from Moscow became the best. The Grand Prix of the personal Olympiad was awarded to Alesya Ivanova in the 6th grade (5th grade of school 2007), in the 7th grade - Boris Shapoval, in the 8th grade - Timofey Zaitsev (both from school 179). In the 9th grade, no grand prize was awarded, and the first place was shared by Andrey Balakin and Nikita Tsoi (both from school 2007). This year’s tournament was organized by the Foundation for Mathematical Education and Enlightenment, as well as the educational program “Big Change”. The selection of tasks and the preparation of options was carried out by a methodological commission consisting of: A.D. Blinkov, A.V. Shapovalov, I.V. Raskina, E.A. Akopyan, Yu.S. Kotelnikova, P.V. Chulkov, V.V. Trushkov, E.V. Bakaev, O.A. Zaslavsky, A.V. Khachaturyan, A.V. Gribalko, A.G. Bannikova, M.A. Artemyev, V.V. Arutyunov, A.A. Zaslavsky, A.P. Zertsalov, A.A. Garkavy. It is pleasant to note that this list is replenished with new people every year, and those who recently came to the tournament as participants are already full members of the jury. The most interesting tasks of the tournament are given below.

TASKS

Arithmetic and algebra

1. Kolya wrote a natural number, all of whose digits are different. If you swap any two digits of this number that differ by 1, then it will increase, and if you swap any two digits that differ by 2, it will decrease. What is the largest number Kolya could write? N. Chernyatyev

2. A natural number has exactly 2014 digits, with the central four digits being 2, 0, 1, 4 (in that order). Could this number be a perfect square? A. Shapovalov

3. Four mathematician friends are in solitary confinement. Each of them was told that their numbers on the list were different, two-digit, and one of these numbers was equal to the sum of the other three. But even after finding out the numbers of the other three, none of them could figure out their own number. So what were their numbers? A. Shapovalov

4. There are p saucers (p for simple) around the table at meter intervals, each with one cookie on it. Carlson walks around the table k meters, stops and takes a cookie from the saucer. Then the Kid, starting from the same place, walks m meters around the table, stops and takes a cookie from the saucer there. Then Carlson walks k meters from his stopping place and takes cookies from the saucer (if there are any left there), etc. They make all transitions in one direction. Which of them will get more cookies and by how many, if k and m are natural, distinct, and both are less than p? M. Shapovalov

5. Sasha wrote a program that sequentially calculates the sum of natural numbers, starting from 2014, while removing arbitrarily one digit in decimal notation from each addend (it is allowed to remove the first digit, even if there is a zero after it). The program should finish working as soon as the sum received at the next step is evenly divisible by 3. Can it happen that the program will run endlessly? A. Gribalko

6. Petya wrote a natural number on the board, and then erased the last digit and wrote it a little higher, in the exponent. It turned out that the result is divisible by the first number written. What is the maximum number that Petya could initially write on the board? A. Shapovalov

Geometry

7. The diagonals of a convex quadrilateral ABCD intersect at point O. It is known that AB = BC = CD and – AOD $= 120 \infty$. Prove that AO = DO. E. Bakaev

8. Points E and F are marked on sides BC and CD of square ABCD, respectively. Prove that the perpendiculars drawn from point A to EF, from point B to AE, and from point D to AF intersect at one point. A.Garkavy

9. On the sides of a convex quadrilateral, regular triangles are built facing the inside. It turned out that triangles built on one pair of opposite sides have a common vertex. Prove that triangles built on another pair of opposite sides have a common center. E. Bakaev

10. The incircle of triangle ABC touches sides AB, BC, CA at points $C_{1}, A_{1}, B_{1}$ respectively, BH is the altitude of triangle ABC. Prove that the orthocenters of triangles $AC_{1} B_{1}$ and $CA_{1} B_{1}$, as well as points H and $B_{1}$ lie on the same circle. D. Shvetsov
