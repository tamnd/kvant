2. Are there ten pairwise distinct natural numbers such that their arithmetic mean is greater than their greatest common divisor: a) (2) exactly six times; b) (2) exactly five times? I. Akulich

3. (5) Point K is marked on side AB of square ABCD, and point L is marked on side BC so that KB = LC. Segments AL and CK intersect at point P. Prove that segments DP and KL are perpendicular. E. Bakaev

4. (5) See problem M2366 “Problem book «Kvant»

5. Given N right triangles. They chose one leg from each and found the sum of their lengths, then found the sum of the lengths of the remaining legs and, finally, found the sum of the lengths of all hypotenuses. It turned out that the three numbers found are the lengths of the sides of a certain right triangle. Prove that all original triangles have the same ratio of the larger leg to the smaller one if: a) (2) N = 2; b) (3) N – any natural number greater than 1. E. Bakaev

2. (4) The vertices of the triangle are labeled A, B, C in a clockwise direction. The triangle is sequentially rotated clockwise: first around vertex A by an angle equal to $\angle A$, then around vertex B by an angle equal to $\angle B$, and so on in a cycle (each time the rotation is made around the current position of the next vertex). Prove that after six rotations the triangle will take its original position. V. Rastorguev

3. (5) Given 15 integers, none of which are identical. Petya wrote down on the board all possible sums for 7 of these numbers, and Vasya wrote down all possible sums for 8 of these numbers. Could it be that they wrote the same sets of numbers on the board? (If a certain number is repeated several times in Petya’s set, then Vasya’s must repeat it the same number of times.) I. Bogdanov

5. (5) There was a pile of silver coins on the table. Each action either added one gold coin and wrote down the number of silver coins on the first piece of paper, or removed one silver coin and wrote down the number of gold coins on the second piece of paper. As a result, only gold coins remained on the table. Prove that at this moment the sum of all the numbers on the first sheet was equal to the sum of all the numbers on the second. E. Bakaev

1. (4) A square table is given. In each of its cells there is either a plus or a minus, and there are equal numbers of pluses and minuses. Prove that either some two rows or some two columns have the same number of pluses. B.Frenkin

2. (5) See problem M2367 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

4. (7) There are 25 checkpoints at regular intervals on the ring road, each manned by a policeman. The policemen are numbered in some order with numbers from 1 to 25. It is required that they cross the road so that again there is a policeman at each post, but clockwise behind number 1 there is number 2, behind number 2 there is number 3,..., behind number 25 there is number 1. Prove that if you organize the crossing so that the total distance traveled is the smallest, then one of the policemen will remain at his post. E. Bakaev

5. (8) See problem M2368 “Problem book «Kvant»”.

6. (8) Let's call a natural number even if all its digits are the same (for example: 4, 111, 999999). Prove that any n-digit number can be represented as the sum of at most n + 1 even numbers. A. Shapovalov

7. The web has the appearance of a checkered grid of $100 \times 100$ nodes (in other words, it is a grid of $99 \times 99$ cells). In some corner of it there is a spider, and in some 100 nodes there are flies stuck to the web. During a move, the spider can move to any node adjacent to it. Can a spider be guaranteed to eat all the flies, spending no more than: a) (5) 2100 moves; b) (5) 2000 moves? I. Bogdanov
