PROBLEM "QUANTUM"

Problems in mathematics and physics

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue should be sent to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) in different envelopes. On the envelope, in the “To” column, write: “Problem book «Kvant» No. 1–2015” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M2366” or “F2373”. In the “From” column, please write your first and last name legibly. In the letter, enclose an envelope with your address written on it and the required set of stamps (in this envelope you will receive the results of checking the decisions). Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics can also be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively. Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (mark on the envelope: “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “Problem book «Kvant», new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate the school number and class in which you study. Problem M2366–M2373, F2373–F2379

M2366. Since the beginning of the school year, Andrei has been recording his grades in mathematics. When he received another rating (2, 3, 4 or 5), he called it unexpected if up to that moment it had occurred less frequently than each of the other possible ratings. (For example, if from the beginning of the year he had received consecutive grades of 3,4,2,5,5,5,2,3,4,3, then the first five and the second four would be unexpected.) For the entire academic year, Andrei received 40 grades - 10 each of fives, fours, threes and twos (it is not known in what order). Is it possible to say exactly how many ratings were unexpected for him? E. Bakaev

M2367. Prove that in any polygon circumscribed around a circle there are three sides from which a triangle can be formed. T. Kazitsyna, B. Frenkin

M2368. Inside a right triangle, two equal circles were built so that the first touches one of the legs and the hypotenuse, the second touches the other leg and the hypotenuse, and these circles also touch each other. Let M and N be the tangency points of the circles with the hypotenuse. Prove that the midpoint of the segment MN lies on the bisector of the right angle of the triangle. E. Bakaev

M2369. Grisha wrote down 100 numbers on the board. He then increased each number by 1 and noticed that the product of all 100 numbers did not change. He again increased each number by 1, and again the product of all numbers did not change, and so on. In total, Grisha repeated this procedure k times, and all k times the product of numbers did not change. Find the largest possible value of k. I. Bogdanov, G. Galperin

M2370. Are all natural divisors of the number 100 possible? (including 1 and the number itself) divide: a) into two groups; b) into 100 groups so that the groups have the same number of numbers and the product of the numbers in the first group is equal to the product of the numbers in the second group? c) Find all natural n for which it is possible to divide all divisors of the number n into two groups so that the groups have the same number of numbers and the product of the numbers of the first group is equal to the product of the numbers of the second group. M. Malkin

M2371. a) Let us call the center of a three-cell corner the point belonging to all three of its cells. The chessboard $99 \times 99$ was completely covered with three-square corners without overlaps, after which each corner was rotated around its center by $90^\circ$ (in either direction). Prove that in this case some square of the board turned out to be uncovered by a single corner. b) Solve the same problem if each corner can be rotated around its center by $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$. A. Gribalko

$M2372^{*}$. Given a sphere Ω and a point P inside it. Three pairwise perpendicular chords $AA ', BB '$ and $CC '$ are drawn through P. Points $X, X '$ are projections of point P on the plane ABC and $A ' B ' C '$. Prove that all lines $XX '$ pass through one point. A. Zaslavsky
