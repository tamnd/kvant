3. By a lucky chance, excellent student Ruslan and the first beauty Lyudmila had the chance to do a simple laboratory work in physics together, “Determination of the specific heat of fusion of an unknown substance.” Ruslan turned on the furnace, setting a certain heating power, placed a piece of the substance under study in the capsule and began measurements exactly at 10:00 Moscow time. When Ruslan went to the teacher, the bored Lyudmila secretly switched the toggle switch for setting the furnace power to another position (which, naturally, she did not remember) and did not change it again. To Ruslan's great surprise, the result of the work was completely incorrect, and then, under the threat of a bad mark, Lyudmila confessed to what she had done. The teacher took pity on the children and, giving them reference data, asked them to determine: 1) the power of the furnace set by Lyudmila; 2) the exact Moscow time when Lyudmila switched the power setting toggle switch. Using the graph of the temperature t of a substance versus time $\tau$ (Fig.10) obtained by Ruslan with the “help” of Lyudmila, help the schoolchildren cope with the teacher’s task.

3. One mole of an ideal gas participates in the cyclic process 1–2–3–1 of a heat engine operating in heat engine mode. In state 1, the gas has a temperature of $T_{1}$ and a volume of $V_{1}$. It is known that all gas transitions from one state to another are polytropic. The polytropic index of process 2–3 is one more than the polytropic index of process 3–1 and one less than the polytropic index of process 1–2. In process 1–2, the gas volume increases k times. One of the processes in the cycle is isothermal, and in this process the volume of gas changes its value within the widest possible range in this cycle. 1) Determine the volume and temperature of the gas in state 3. 2) Draw on the pV diagram a cycle corresponding to the conditions of the problem, indicating for each of the processes its polytropic index. Reference. A process during which the heat capacity of the gas does not change is called polytropic: C = const. The equation of such a process has the form $pV^{n} =$ const, or $p_{1} V_{1}^{n} = p_{2} V_{2}^{n}$. The value n is called the polytropic index. E. Vishnyakova
