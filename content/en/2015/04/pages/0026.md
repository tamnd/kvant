Competition named after A.P. Savin

"Math 6–8"

We are starting another competition to solve mathematical problems for students in grades 6–8. Send solutions to the problems within a month after receiving this issue of the journal to the address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant» or by email: savin.contest@gmail.com (marked “Contest “Mathematics 6–8”). Be sure to include your name, class, and home address. As before, we welcome participation in the competition not only by individual schoolchildren, but also by mathematical clubs. We ask circle leaders to provide an email address or contact phone number. According to tradition, the winning clubs of the correspondence competition are invited to the final in-person tournament.

1. Once in a mathematics lesson, Tolik Vtulkin wrote from left to right in a line in ascending order all the natural numbers from 1 to 11. He noticed that from the number 2 to the end of the line there are only 10 numbers in it - exactly 5 times more than the number 2 itself. And from the number 3 to the end of the line there are only 9 numbers in it - exactly 3 times more than the number 3 itself. Finally, from the number 4 to the end of the line there are only 8 numbers in it - exactly 2 times greater than the number 4 itself. Tolik called the numbers 5, 3, 2 multipliers and wrote them in the second line under the numbers 2, 3, 4:

1

2 3 4 5 6 5 3 2

7 8 9

10 11

Developing the theory of multipliers, Tolik wrote in ascending order all the natural numbers from 1 to n, and under some of them - their multipliers. In the second line he has the numbers 11, 9, 7, 5, 4, 3, 2. Find the smallest possible value of n. S.Dvoryaninov

2. There is a square checkered board of size $2n \times 2n$ cells (n – natural), in which initially all the cells are white. Let's paint some of them black. Let's call a coloring elegant if exactly n squares are colored in each horizontal and each vertical (for example, the usual coloring of a chessboard is elegant).

⟦column⟧

Let's take two arbitrary elegant colorings. Petya is sure that if you allow any two horizontals or any two verticals to be swapped, then with several such operations you can get the second from the first coloring. Kolya believes that this is not so. For which n is Petya right, and for which n is Kolya right? I. Akulich

3. On the plane there are two equal triangles that have no common points (neither inside nor on the borders). The straight line $П_{1}$ divides the area of ​​each of them in half, and the straight line $П_{2}$ divides their perimeters in half. Can lines $П_{1}$ and $П_{2}$ be mutually perpendicular? I. Akulich

4. The children made 45 double-sided cards, each with two different numbers written on different sides, with each pair of numbers appearing on exactly one card. The teacher places these cards on the table. Will he be able to do this in such a way that the children will be able to determine for at least one card what number is written on its back? I. Bogdanov

5. The height of a trapezoid whose diagonals are mutually perpendicular is equal to h. What is the smallest possible length of the midline of such a trapezoid? S.Dvoryaninov
