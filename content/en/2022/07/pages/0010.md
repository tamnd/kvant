The University of Glasgow is in the process of organizing a musical student society. After graduating from the mathematics department of Trinity College, Cambridge, William entered the College de France (Sorbonne), a physics laboratory, where he acquired the skills necessary for experimental physical work. But he does not abandon theory, where his interests now focus on the then most advanced edge of physics - electricity and magnetism. In his first physical publications, he developed an image method for solving electrostatic problems, which was soon included in all classical physics courses. These (and other) of his works are noticed at the University of Glasgow. Thomson returns to his hometown, defends his dissertation and begins to create a well-equipped physics laboratory. At this time, the professorial position of the head of the department of theoretical physics becomes vacant and Thomson is offered to take part in the competition. In 1846, the 22-year-old scientist became a professor and head of the department. And he worked in this position for 53 years. Also a kind of record! Thomson continues to attend Trinity College, Cambridge, where he meets Joule. Joule's talent, his brilliant ideas and personal charm captivate Thomson so much that he became an adept of the science of heat transformations for a long time. He

invents the name of this new science - thermodynamics. Joule is only 6 years older than Thomson, but he had already come to his great conclusion about the unity of the concept of energy in mechanics, heat and electricity and confirmed the conservation of this quantity with precise measurements. While studying gas processes, Joule formulated a law called the first law (beginning) of thermodynamics. Thomson enthusiastically joined in the joint work and soon experimentally discovered an effect that seemed to violate this law. When a compressed gas expands into a vacuum space, if all vessels are well insulated, the temperature should not change, since the energy does not change. But if the expansion occurs slowly enough (through a porous partition), then the temperature changes, sometimes decreasing, and sometimes even increasing. It turned out that the whole point is that the gases are not ideal. During expansion, the interaction forces between gas molecules, which were usually neglected, change. The phenomenon soon became known as the “Joule–Thomson effect” and has since been widely used to cool gases and bring them to a liquid state. Thus, this effect has found an interesting application in the gas industry. Natural gas coming from a well always contains a lot of water vapor. If this gas is released into the gas pipeline, water vapor will condense and the process of metal rusting will actively begin. Therefore, this condensation must be carried out in the field, immediately after production. Thanks to Joule and Thomson, Trinity College, along with the University of Berlin, becomes a world center for the development of physical science.
