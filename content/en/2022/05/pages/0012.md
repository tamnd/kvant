Calculations of nuclear transformations in the conditions of an expanding space environment required considerable effort, and Gamow attracted Ralph Alpher (astrophysicist) and Robert Herman (astrophysicist) to them. The first publication, prepared by Gamow and Alpher, appeared in print in 1948 under three names: Alpher, Bethe, Gamow. This is the most famous joke in the history of physics: for the sake of a beautiful name, Gamow entered Bethe’s name without being asked, and so a work arose that immediately became famous under the name DEJ theory. The most spectacular result of this theory was the prediction of cosmic background radiation. Electromagnetic radiation must have coexisted with hot matter in the early Universe. It does not disappear with the general expansion of the world and remains, only very cooled, until now. Gamow and his collaborators tried to estimate what the temperature of this radiation should be today. Taking into account the possible uncertainties inherent in the very unreliable astronomical data on the general parameters of the Universe as a whole, and the scant information about nuclear constants, the predicted temperature should have been in the range from 1 to 10 K. In 1950, in a popular science article, Gamow wrote that, most likely, the temperature of cosmic radiation was about 3 K. The result was obtained in an elegant and bold way; and if we can talk about the “style” of scientific work, then it was the style of the last third of the century, and not its middle. 15 years later, Arno Penzias (radio astronomy) and Robert Wilson (radio astronomy) accidentally discovered this radiation. This was the largest discovery in cosmology since Edwin Hubble's (astronomy) discovery in 1929 of the expansion of the universe predicted by Friedman.

Gamow had three years of life left, and he managed to see his success recognized - at the 1967 symposium in New York, Gamow accepted the congratulations of his colleagues and celebrated his success. His Universe did not disappoint, it turned out to be hot! Ten years after Gamow's death, discoverers Penzias and Wilson received the Nobel Prize; it is awarded only during life. Why did the DEJ theory go unnoticed for a long time, and why did the discovery of Penzias and Wilson become accidental? There is a general scientific stream, the mainstream, and there are bold searches sideways or even upward. The concept of a “hot Universe” seemed unlikely at that time: it was competed by the model of a “cold Universe” (Yakov Zeldovich, nuclear bomb and theoretical physics) and the theory of a stationary Universe (Fred Hoyle, astrophysics). But in the end, the work of Gamow and his students was recognized. Steven Weinberg (theoretical physics) wrote: "Gamow, Alpher and Hermann deserve enormous credit for, among other things, wanting to take the early Universe seriously and exploring what the known laws of physics have to say about the first three minutes." With the discovery of cosmic microwave background radiation, cosmology began to flourish. Intensive work, in which almost all leading physicists and astronomers, as well as young active theorists, participated, quickly led to the creation, based on Gamow’s ideas and new observational data, of a very complete and reliable cosmological theory, which is now called the theory of the hot Universe, or the theory of the Big Bang.
