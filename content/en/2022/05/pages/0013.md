As soon as the war ended, Gamow and Tuve resumed the Washington conferences. The first post-war meeting in the fall of 1946 was called “Related Problems in Physics and Biology.” Since then, Gamow has not let events in biology out of his sight. So the spider sits and waits for the fly to appear; and then he jumps out of the ambush. Gamow used this comparison when he was asked what he was waiting for to get involved in biology, since he had been interested in it since 1946. As it turned out, in order to get involved in biology, he had to wait until 1953, come on a short visit to the University of California at Berkeley and accidentally meet Luis Alvarez (atomic physicist) in the corridor there. In his hands (this latest in Gam's friendly meetings and scientific adventures of the Nobel laureate) was the latest Nature magazine with an article by James Watson and Francis Crick on the structure of deoxyribonucleic acid - DNA. A new, biological phase began in Gamow’s life, and in this new life and scientific adventure there was a lot of exciting, dramatic and, of course, funny and cheerful. Gamow proceeded from the following. Proteins are the basis of all living things. They serve as building materials for living tissues, form hormones, enzymes, etc. There are more than a million different proteins in the human body. Proteins are built from 20 amino acids; The individual properties of a protein are determined by which amino acids it is formed from and in what sequence. Protein synthesis is controlled by nucleic acids, which store and transmit a complete set of information about the structure of proteins. Watson and Crick showed that this information is recorded using “words” lined up one after another, with four “letters” for these words.
