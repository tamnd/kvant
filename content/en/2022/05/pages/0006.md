They published an amateur magazine, “Otbrosy physics,” and organized wit and erudition competitions. At first, members of the circle were Gamov, Dmitry Ivanenko (nuclear physics, field theory, gravity), Andrei Anselm (theory of liquids, molecular physics), Vladimir Kravtsov (metrology of atomic masses). Then Viktor Ambartsumyan (astrophysics), Lev Landau (theoretical physics), Matvey Bronstein (theoretic physics) joined. Everyone who writes about Gamow mentions jokes and pranks, humor and mischief. Those who are more insightful say that the center of his world was physics. Our heroes are young, talented, who know better than others how atoms and the universe work, and in addition, they know that they know it better than others. They believe that “sweat should not be visible”, that it should seem to everyone that work, science, physics - all this is so for them, a game, almost entertainment. This was partly true, especially for Gamow, although this did not at all exclude work. Gamow, Ivanenko and Landau published in 1928 in the Journal of the Russian Physical and Chemical Society an article “World Constants and Limit Transition”, in which they gave a hierarchy of physical theories based on a system of fundamental constants, including the speed of light, the gravitational constant and Planck’s constant (the so-called cGh-system). The authors considered this work a joke and never referred to it, but it attracted the attention of researchers with its ideas concerning the fundamental principles of physics and the principles of its development. In short: the authors built a classification of physical theories based on what quantities are considered fundamental and basic in them. Comrade is forever his: in an article published in the journal Nature in 1948, Gamow developed equations for the mass and radius of a galaxy containing about one hundred billion stars like the Sun, and his equations included only fundamental constants (gravitational constant, Planck’s constant, elementary charge, etc.). In 1926, his scientific supervisors recommended him for an internship in Germany - this is natural. However, permission and all the necessary documents were received only in the spring of 1928. Gamow arrives in Göttingen, the then center of development of quantum mechanics. He works in the group of Max Born (one of the creators of quantum mechanics); intending to tackle some unsolved problem, he chooses the theory of the atomic nucleus, in particular, the problem of alpha decay, one of the types of radioactivity. Assumes that alpha particles tunnel through a potential barrier and makes a calculation accordingly. This was the first successful explanation of the behavior of radioactive elements based on quantum theory. Simultaneously with Gamow and independently of him, the idea of ​​the role of the tunnel effect in the process of alpha decay was expressed by Ronald Gurney (quantum mechanics, nuclear physics, photoprocess) and Edward Condon (quantum mechanics, nuclear physics), but only Gamow managed to obtain quantitative results. Based on his theory, Gamow estimated the size of nuclei and explained the Geiger–Nettall law, which relates the energy of an emitted alpha particle to the half-life of nuclei. The theory and its author were recognized by the scientific world. Cheerful, witty and sociable, he becomes a popular figure among both domestic and European physicists. At the end of his four-month internship in Göttingen, Gamow moved to Copenhagen, where the Danish Academy of Sciences awarded him a one-year fellowship to work with Niels Bohr (quantum mechanics) at the Institute of Theoretical Physics.
