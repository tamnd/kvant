Second: the usual task when writing popular science books is how to stretch the thread from “scientific” to “popular”; one is too far removed from the other, and almost no one knows school physics. Some authors and publishers practice multi-layers (usually two layers, more have been proposed) of text: for example, small font, insets, underlays, etc. In some of Gamow’s books, this technique is carried out fundamentally - more serious “lectures” that the character listens to alternate with the character’s experience (in a dream) of some events that illustrate what he heard. Third and most rare: the character who represents science in Gamow is able to say and, moreover, from time to time says “we don’t know”, “science doesn’t know”. Gamow was awarded the Kalinga Prize by UNESCO for his books, the most prestigious award for the popularization of science. George Antonovich (George) Gamow left his mark on this world: the “George Gamow Tower” in Boulder, the “Gamow Square” in his hometown of Odessa, and the “Gamow Crater” on the Moon, which would look down on it all if it were not located on the far side of the Moon.
