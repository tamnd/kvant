7. The spaceship is located in a half-space at a distance a from its boundary. The crew is aware of this, but has no idea in which direction to move to reach the boundary plane. The spaceship can fly in space along any trajectory, measuring the length of the path traveled, and has a sensor that gives a signal when the boundary is reached. Can a starship be guaranteed to reach the border by covering a path of length a) (6) no more than 14a; b) (6) no more than 13a? M. Evdokimov

Oral round for 11th grade

1. A polynomial of the third degree has three different roots strictly between 0 and 1. The teacher told the students two of these roots. He also reported all four coefficients of the polynomial, but did not indicate in what order these coefficients appear. Is it necessary to restore the third root? B.Frenkin

2. Let us call a triangle ABC located in space convenient if for any point P outside its plane it is possible to form a triangle from the segments PA, PB and PC. What angles can a convenient triangle have? D. Brodsky

Let x be the number of words in the crossword puzzle, y be the smallest number of words that can cover the crossword puzzle. Find the maximum of the ratio x y for a given n. B.Frenkin

5. Given an equilateral triangle ABC. Let us choose an arbitrary circle Z that is internally tangent to the circumcircle of triangle ABC at point B and does not intersect line AC. Mark points P and Q on Z so that lines AP and CQ touch Z, and segments AP and CQ intersect inside triangle ABC. Prove that all straight lines PQ obtained in this way pass through one fixed point that does not depend on the choice of circle Z. A. Mardanov

6. The letter A is written on the board. It is allowed, in any order and quantity: a) to assign A to the left; b) attribute B to the right; c) simultaneously assign B to the left and A to the right. For example, BAAB can be obtained this way $(^{A} o$ BAA o BAAB ), but ABBA cannot be obtained. Prove that for any natural number n, half of the words of length n can be obtained, but the other half cannot. Folklore, suggested by A. Shen
