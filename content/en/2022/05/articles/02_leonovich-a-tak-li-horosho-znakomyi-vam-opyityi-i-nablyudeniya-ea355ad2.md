EXPERIMENTS and OBSERVATIONS

Summer is ahead. Of course, the holidays are a great opportunity to relax and have fun at the dacha or in the camp, at home or in nature. But there it is quite possible in practice, “live”, to be convinced of the action and omnipotence of physical laws - after all, they grew out of experiments and observations. And it is not necessary to have complex equipment for this; often all that is needed is a careful look, the simplest available tools, ingenuity and basic calculations. We hope that you will not spend the coming months in idleness, but will give free rein to your curiosity and ingenuity, work with both your hands and your head, find answers to accumulated questions and be puzzled by new ones in anticipation of future school years. And our magazine will help you. We offer a handful of summer exercises to warm up.

Questions and tasks

1. As the Earth moves along an elliptical orbit, its speed changes all the time. Could you find the corresponding

current acceleration, having, for example, a level with $жидкостью?^{2.}$ Lay out a handkerchief on the table and place an empty glass bottle on it, neck down. How to pull a handkerchief out from under a bottle without touching it $ней?^{3.}$ You met two guys in the evening who were riding bicycles with different diameter wheels at the same speed. Which of them has a brighter flashlight, powered by a generator on the rim $колеса?^{4.}$ How to throw a cardboard (flat) match onto the floor from a height, for example, 1 meter so that it falls on $ребро?^{5.}$ Will you be able to place an unbroken egg vertically on a flat surface (i.e. solve the Columbus problem $яйце)?^{6.}$ Turn a full bottle upside down. Why does water pour out? $бульканьем?^{7.}$ Place lit matches on metal, stone and wooden surfaces. Which match will burn longer and which will go out faster?

8. It's hot, but your watermelon is warm. It turns out that if you immerse it in a thread mesh in very hot water (but no more than half a minute!), then when you cut it after 5-10 minutes, you will find an almost frozen core. Like this $объяснить?^{9.}$ Why is dew in the evening warmer than $утром?^{10.}$ Do you think the thermometer you have would work if the liquid in it had the same coefficient of thermal expansion as $стекло?^{11.}$ Note that in summer the full Moon is visible in the sky for less time than in winter. Why $так?^{12.}$ How does the power and brightness of an incandescent lamp change from $временем?^{13.}$ The same current flows through the lamp filament and through the supply wires. Why does the thread get hot, but the wires remain tactile to the touch $холодными?^{14.}$ If you have small nails scattered across the floor, what is the easiest way to collect them together and pour them (that is, pour them!) into the box?

It's interesting that...

... Archimedes' law was based on an experimental fact: when a body is immersed in water, it becomes lighter. Therefore, the scientist’s observations in the bath are sometimes called the first physical experiment in history, and his law is sometimes called the first physical law. ... after conducting experiments with pendulums of equal length, Galileo came to the remarkable conclusion about the equality of the velocities of free fall of bodies, regardless of the type of substance. ... more than three centuries later, a member of the Apollo 15 expedition, astronaut Dave Scott, while on the surface of the Moon, released a hammer and a feather from his hands, and they landed on the Moon at the same time - in full agreement with Galileo’s law. ...Newton's scientific rival, the tireless Robert Hooke, created many instruments for conducting experiments at the Royal Society of London. By constructing a chronometer with a spiral spring and studying the deformations occurring in it, Hooke established the law of elasticity for solids. ... in fact, the first experimental evidence of the Earth's rotation around its axis was obtained in 1851 by the French scientist Leon Foucault. A small heavy ball, suspended on a long wire, slowly rotated relative to the earthly observer during oscillations. Since the plane of oscillation of the pendulum maintains its position in inertial space, this proves that the Earth is rotating. ... the 1927 experiment of American physicists Davisson and Germer in bombarding a nickel crystal with electrons brilliantly confirmed de Broglie’s hypothesis about the connection between waves and particles - wave-particle duality. ... the young German experimenter Rudolf Mössbauer, studying the absorption and scattering of gamma quanta using a gramophone with a crank handle (!), discovered an effect in 1958 that was awarded the Nobel Prize. … future Nobel laureates, Phystech graduates Andrei Geim and Konstantin Novoselov managed to obtain graphene, a two-dimensional material consisting of carbon of monatomic thickness, literally “on the knee,” using the simplest technology with ordinary adhesive tape.
