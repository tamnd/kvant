The piston is connected by a chain to the end of a large rocker arm, which is a double-armed lever. The pump under load is connected by a chain to the opposite end of the rocker arm. During the downward stroke of the piston, the pump lifts a portion of water upward. The cylinder is then filled with steam again and the pump piston is lowered. The constant cooling and reheating of the machine's working cylinder was very wasteful and inefficient, however, these steam engines made it possible to pump water from greater depths than was possible with horses. Heating the machines with coal mined in the same mine that the machine served turned out to be profitable, despite the monstrous gluttony of the installation. Newcomen's machine was not a universal engine and could only work as a pump. Newcomen's attempts to use the reciprocating motion of a piston to rotate a paddle wheel on ships were unsuccessful. However, Newcomen’s merit is that he was one of the first to implement “in hardware” the idea of ​​​​using steam to produce mechanical work.
