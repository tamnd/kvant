Since the Earth seems flat to us, we will assume that the observer is standing on a plane tangent to the sphere of the Earth. Then, at sunset, its rays go to the Moon and to the observer parallel to the plane on which the observer stands. But parallel lines appear to us to be converging on the horizon (perspective). Let's draw a vertical plane through the centers of the Sun and Moon. For clarity, in the same plane we will place a number of telegraph poles with wires that converge on the horizon (Fig. 3). The illusion of the lunar terminator is that parallel rays converging on the horizon appear to be coming from above. Model of the Moon. Let's take a ball, for example, for ping pong or an unnecessary Christmas tree ball and paint it white and black. The white half will symbolize the illuminated part of the Moon, and the black half will symbolize the unlit part. The white side is considered to be facing the Sun, with the Sun's rays perpendicular to the terminator plane. Looking at the ball-Moon from below, we see some part of the illuminated side of the Moon and some part - the unlit.
