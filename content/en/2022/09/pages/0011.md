CONDENSER PROBLEMS

Capacitance is, one might say, the main parameter of a capacitor. It can be called the main one because it almost always must have a certain value (sometimes in filters - “no less than such and such”). After capacitance, the second parameter of a capacitor that is important for practice is the operating voltage. It should usually be “no less than such and such.” This can be seen from the formula for energy, and in cases of “non-energy” applications it simply determines the possibility of using a particular capacitor - at a higher voltage it will fail. The maximum voltage is determined by the insulation breakdown, i.e. its destruction, formally – transformation from an insulator into a conductor. The physical processes that develop in this case are numerous and intricately intertwined. You can get acquainted with them, for example, in the book “Records and Limits, or Introduction to Extreme Materials Science” (Library «Kvant», issue 136) and in the article “What is Electrical Breakdown” (magazine «Kvant», 1984, No. 8). Let us recall some of these processes. For example, impact ionization, when an electron accelerated by an electric field, when colliding with an atom, transfers enough energy to it for ionization. Is it clear what happens next? Where there was an electron, there became two, where there were two, there became four, where there were four, then eight... At the same time, part of the electrical energy turns into heat, and the unfortunate dielectric - by the way, aluminum oxide, which melts at just over two thousand degrees and boils at three thousand - turns into steam with such pressure that it bursts the capacitor from the inside (Fig. 7). Then the question arises: what is the difference between a dielectric and a semiconductor? In school, metals and semiconductors are usually distinguished by whether the resistance increases or decreases with heating, but the question does not arise about dielectrics because their resistance is considered infinite. How can we now distinguish a dielectric from a semiconductor if the resistance of both drops when heated? Generally speaking, the distinction is relative. Thus, it is sometimes written that dielectrics include substances with a resistivity of more than $10^{8}$ Ohm m or a band gap of more than 3 eV.
