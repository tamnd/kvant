SHUKHOVSKYS

March 14. The first meeting of the commission for the acceptance of the tower by the People's Commissariat for Postal Service. March 21. The act of handing over the tower. Articles in the newspapers “Izvestia” and “Working Moscow”. On March 19, 1922, the transmitter of the Shabolovskaya radio station was tested, and in September 1922, the first radio broadcast was heard from Shabolovka. The design of the tower on Shabolovka consists of six sections, each of which is a single-sheet hyperboloid of rotation, formed by straight steel channels located along the generatrices of the hyperboloid. A hyperbola is a curve on a plane, the modulus of the difference in distances from any point of which to two data, called foci, is constant (Fig. 3). Hyperbola

is a conic section, along with an ellipse and a parabola, but differs from them in that it has asymptotes - straight lines, which it approaches, but never reaches. The hyperbola y 1 x studied at school has asymptotes perpendicular to each other and coincide with the axes of the Cartesian coordinate system (Fig. 4). When a hyperbola is rotated around its axis of symmetry, perpendicular to a segment with ends at the foci, a surface is obtained - a single-sheet hyperboloid of revolution. It turns out that through each point of the hyperboloid there pass two straight lines that lie entirely on it. Each of them, when rotating around the axis of the hyperboloid, “sweeps” the entire surface. Such lines are called generators. Generators are divided into two families: one

The family includes those generators that, when rotated around an axis, transform into each other. Accordingly, a single-sheet hyperboloid can be represented in two ways as a kind of “parquet” laid out with straight lines of the same family (Fig. 5). Thus, a curved surface consists of straight lines. It was this property that V.G. Shukhov used: each section of the tower on Shabolovka is “woven” from two forming families. The hyperboloid design is easy to manufacture and highly durable. For comparison, the most famous tower at that time - the Eiffel Tower - consists of more than 10 thousand different types of parts, custom-made with great detail.
