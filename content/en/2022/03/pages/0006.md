Shukhov designed and built more than two hundred hyperboloid structures in Russia. Most often these were water towers, but there were also fire towers, and supports for power lines (NiGRES across the Oka near Nizhny Novgorod), lighthouses (near Kherson). There were attempts to make similar masts for ships (“Andrew the First-Called” and “Emperor Paul I”). Even in the case of the simplest structures - single-section water towers - each project was unique: fulfillment of technical requirements was combined with architectural reference to the area.

The first hyperboloid design was tested on the territory of A.V.’s office. Baria, where Shukhov worked. And publicly, the first such design was presented at the All-Russian Industrial and Art Exhibition, held in 1896 in Nizhny Novgorod (Fig. 6). The privilege (in modern terms - a patent) for an openwork tower in the form of a hyperboloid was declared in the same 1896 (Fig. 7). The most famous of Shukhov's towers is indeed the tower on Shabolovka. The history of its construction begins with a resolution of the Council of Workers' and Peasants' Defense dated July 30, 1919: “In order to ensure reliable and constant communication between the center of the Republic and the western states and outskirts of the Republic, the People's Commissariat of Posts and Telegraphs is entrusted with extremely urgently establishing a radio station in Moscow, equipped with the most advanced instruments and machines and with power sufficient to carry out the instructions.
