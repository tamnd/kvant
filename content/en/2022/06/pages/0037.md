YOUNGER

Cracked ice on the river; the thickness of the ice at the water's edge is thin. In the middle of the river, the current maintains a higher water temperature and prevents ice from freezing. Unlike rivers, lakes take a long time to defrost because the lower layers of ice do not have time to warm up due to their high heat capacity. And besides, there is more water and more ice in the lake, therefore it will take longer to defrost. A small amount of water will freeze faster and melt faster. Also, rivers are freed from ice much faster with the current, and the larger it is, the more intense the melting process occurs. Why does the snow on the river, with warming and further freezing, become dense ice, while the snowdrifts on the banks do not freeze? The thing is that water has a greater heat capacity and thermal conductivity than snow and ice, and water absorbs more heat and heats the adjacent layers more intensely. Heat is transferred quickly from layer to layer, but more energy needs to be spent to heat each layer. Thus, a large mass of snow melts on the river, and when it gets colder, it completely freezes and turns into ice. During subsequent cooling, the surface of the reservoir freezes evenly if a lot of water has melted. You can even skate on such a surface. But sooner or later, spring takes its toll, and water bodies are gradually freed from ice. Melt water is especially cold. Fans of winter swimming know this well - after all, it is even colder to swim in it than in an ice hole in the bracing frost. The thing is, I'm freezing...

The ice on the surface of a reservoir is formed from purified water of the near-surface layers, from which all the sediment has sunk to the bottom. Very clean water can be cooled to a temperature of $^{40}$ q C. In this regard, you can conduct the following experiment: in a slight frost, shake settled spring water in a bottle, and it will freeze in less than a minute - impurities will increase its freezing point. Salt water, due to its physicochemical properties, freezes at $3 q^{C}$. Ice freezing occurs unevenly, especially on a sunny day, due to the fact that at the edges of the ice hole the ice cools the water and freezing ice floes and does not allow the ice that the sun melts to melt (unlike in the middle of the ice hole).
