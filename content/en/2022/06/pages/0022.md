The last digit of the product is equal to 0, and the two is transferred to the adjacent table. It should use the number 4 (2 + 2): due to the transfer of two, a shift occurs two positions down. The number 4 will be the second digit of the result from the right. In this case, the triple will also be transferred to the adjacent most significant digit. It must use the number 5 (2 + 3). This will be the third digit from the right of the desired product. In the next digit, taking into account the transfer of one gives the next digit 7 (6 + 1). Continuing to move from right to left, taking into account possible transfers, we get numbers; at figure 6 they are circled in red circles. So, the result is 10428777540. Impressive, isn't it? But that's not all. Is it possible to take into account the transfer automatically, rather than carry out calculations and draw arrows every time, as we did? It turns out that it is possible! This was realized by Henri Zhenai. The dark arrows and triangles on the bars do exactly this transfer. Look at figure 4. For example, the triangle arrow on the block with the number 6, located opposite the number 7 on the base plate, takes into account the transfer of 4 to the place on the left. On the bar with the number 3, located opposite the number 6 in the base table, there are two triangle arrows, since both carry 1 and carry 2 can occur in the digit on the left. As an example of the application of all of the above, consider calculating the product of 7569 and 4 using Zhenaya bars (Fig.7). Moving along the direction of the triangle arrows, we quickly get 30276 as a result.
