KV A NT 2022/No.6

Department of Mathematics

We teach future mathematicians to work with scientific texts, clearly express their thoughts, and provide the opportunity, using our manuals, to advance in the study of various mathematical issues as far beyond the required minimum as they themselves want. We help to deepen and expand the knowledge acquired at school, prepare for the Unified State Exam, Unified State Exam and other exams. For those who already know the pleasure of solving mathematical problems, we hope to bring joy from thinking about new, similar and completely different problems. We consistently teach schoolchildren from 4th to 11th grade. You can start learning from any class.

Introductory work

Next to the serial number of the task, in parentheses it is indicated which class this task is intended for students (meaning the class in which you will study in the next school year). You can, if you wish, additionally solve problems addressed to older classes. You may not be able to solve all the problems in your class; please send solutions to those that you managed to solve. Don’t forget to justify your decisions; a “bare” answer to a problem is not considered a solution. 1. (5 grades). Chipmunks Chip and Dale must store the same amount of nuts for the winter. After Chip brought 120 and Dale brought 147 nuts, Chip had four times as many nuts left as Dale. How many nuts should each of them store? 2. (5 grades) A ​​hunter told a friend that he saw a wolf with a meter-long tail in the forest. He told another friend that a wolf with a two-meter tail was seen in the forest. By passing on the news, ordinary people doubled the length of their tail, and creative people tripled it. As a result, TV reported a wolf with a tail 648 meters long. How many simple and how many creative people “grew” the wolf’s tail? 3. (5 grades) It is known that in January there are four Fridays and four Mondays. What day of the week is January 1st? 4. (6 grades) 35 crows flew into the clearing. Suddenly the crows took off and separated

into two flocks: one flock sat on the branches of an old birch tree, and the other on an alder tree. After some time, 5 crows flew from the birch to the alder, the same number of crows completely flew away from the birch, after which twice as many crows remained on the birch as on the alder. How many crows were there in each of the two flocks initially? 5. (6th grade) The numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., 111 were written on the cards. Vanya took the cards with even numbers, and Tanya took the cards with odd numbers. Which of them had the sum of the numbers on the cards greater and by how much? 6. (6 cells) A square with a side of 5 cells is drawn on checkered paper. Divide it into 5 parts of the same area, drawing segments inside the square only along the grid lines so that the total length of the drawn segments does not exceed 16 cells. 7. (7th grade) Innocent wants to write down 2015 natural numbers in a circle so that for every two adjacent numbers the quotient of dividing the larger by the smaller is a prime number. His friend Makar claims that this is impossible. Who's right? 8. (7 cells) Draw all the diagonals in a regular 2018 triangle. Petya chooses some N diagonals at random. For what is the smallest N among the chosen diagonals, it is guaranteed that there will be two of the same length? 9. (7th grade) The young artist had one can of blue and one can of yellow paint, each of which is enough to paint $38 дм^{2}$ area. Using all this paint, he painted a picture: blue sky, green grass and yellow sun. He got the green color by mixing two parts yellow paint and one part blue. How much area in his painting is shaded with each color if the area of ​​the grass in the painting is $6 дм^{2}$ greater than the area of ​​the sky? 10. (8–10 grades) Two cities A and B are located on the banks of a river at a distance of 10 km from each other. A steamboat can sail from A to B and back in 1 hour. Will it take him more or less time to swim 20 km across the lake? 11. (7–10 grades) a) Is it possible to number the edges of a cube with natural numbers from 1 to 12 so that for each vertex of the cube the sum of the numbers of the edges that converge at it is the same? b) A similar question if you place the numbers 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 along the edges of the cube.
