Note that the angle of the polygon M can be equal to 60q, 90q, 120q, 150q. This means that each external angle M is at least 30q. Since the sum of the exterior angles of a polygon is 360q, M has at most 12 angles, and there can be 12 only if all angles are equal to 150q. If M has less than 12 sides, then not all its angles are equal to 150q. Let's mentally insert several sides of zero length: if there is an angle of 120q, insert a side of zero length into the corresponding vertex, if there is an angle of 90q (60q) - two (three) consecutive sides of zero length. As a result, we get 12 sides, some of which are equal to 0. Let us call the characteristic of a polygon M the set $a_{1}, a_{2}$, ... $, a_{12}$ of the lengths of its sides, listed in the order of traversing counterclockwise from a fixed vertex. We will call the sides $a_{1}, a_{3}$, … $, a_{11}$ odd, and the sides $a_{2}, a_{4}$, … $, a_{12}$ even. Let us call the border of a partition the union of blocks (squares and equilateral triangles) that have at least one common point with the boundary of the polygon M (Fig.19). If the border does not coincide with M, we denote by $M_{1}$ the polygon obtained from M by discarding the border. Consider a border block adjacent to a corner, the side of which lies on the side AB of the polygon M. If it is a square, then all the border blocks adjacent to AB are also squares (the resulting 90q angles can only be tiled with squares; see Fig. 19,a). If this is a triangle, then all the remaining dies adjacent to this side are also triangles (see Fig. 19, b). Thus, either only squares or only triangles adjoin each side of the polygon M. It follows that the sides $M_{1}$ will be parallel to the corresponding sides of M (sides AB and CD on figure 19). If M does not have angles of 60q or 90q, then the lengths of the sides $M_{1}$ will either be equal to the corresponding lengths of the sides of M (in the case of squares) or one less (in the case of triangles). In this case, the length of side $M_{1}$ can become equal to 0. Thus, $M_{1}$ is a convex polygon with no more sides than M. The polygon $M_{1}$ also has the characteristic - a set constructed according to the same rules so that the numbering of the sides in M ​​and $M_{1}$ correspond to each other. Let the lengths of all sides of M be different from zero, i.e. all angles M are equal to 150q. There are two options for placing a square and a triangle adjacent to the 150q corner. When choosing one of the options, the rest of the border is restored uniquely (first, the part of the border adjacent to the sides of the corner is restored, then the partitions of two adjacent corners, etc.). In total, two options for the border are obtained: triangles are located along all odd sides, and squares are located along all even sides, or vice versa (Fig. 20).
