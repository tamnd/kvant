AN OLD COUNTING DEVICE – the so-called “Napeer sticks” – is described in many sources. The device, first described in the book “Rabdology” (“Rabdologi seu Numerationis per Virgulas libri duo”) by the Scottish baron and mathematics enthusiast John Napier (by the way, was the first to propose the concept of a logarithm), published in 1617, made it possible to quickly obtain the product of a multi-digit number by a single-digit number. This or that digit was written, and below were written pairs of digits separated by a slash. Of course, you already understand what kind of numbers are on the sticks. For example, on a stick with the number seven, these are the digits of the product of the number 7 by 2, 3, ..., 9. Other sticks are constructed similarly. The device also included a stick with the numbers 2, 3, …, 9 (Fig. 2). Fig. 2 Let's call it “auxiliary”. How was the result of multiplication determined? Let's look at an example of determining the product of the number 5619 by 5.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.4213/kvant20220603

The last digit of the product – 5 – is equal to the number under the slash on the rightmost stick. The penultimate is the sum of the number above the line on the rightmost stick - 4 - and the number below the line on the penultimate stick - 5, i.e. 9. The remaining numbers were determined similarly (from right to left): 0 + 0 = 0, 3 + 5 = 8, 2 (first digit of the result). The entire product is equal to 28095. Of course, there were cases when, when determining the next digit, the unit was transferred in the mind to the digit adjacent to the left, where it should also be taken into account (check this, for example, for the case of multiplying the number 8365 by 8).
