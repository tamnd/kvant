Neutrinos interact very weakly with matter, but gravity acts on neutrinos. In order for the laboratory and the neutrino to be close again after such a (relatively short) time, equal to 69 minutes, it is necessary that the trajectory of the neutrino and the trajectory of the laboratory in the reference system (FR), in which the center of the Earth is considered to be at rest, and the coordinate axes are directed to distant stars, lie in the same plane. This means that both of these trajectories are in the plane of the Earth's equator. Various hypothetical options are possible. In one of them, the neutrino moved above the surface of the Earth, and in the other it flew in the thickness of the Earth (Fig.1). When flying

There is a well-known problem with the following condition: a planet with the same gravity and the same geometric dimensions as the Earth has no atmosphere; the planet does not rotate; we need to find the time it takes for a stone to fall from a height equal to the radius of this planet if the initial speed of the stone relative to the planet is zero. In this problem, the trajectory of the falling stone is the part of the ellipse that degenerates into a straight line. The first escape velocity on this planet is v = gR = 7.905 km s. The time of one revolution around this planet of a stone thrown horizontally at the first escape velocity is T = 2 S R v = = 84.335 min.
