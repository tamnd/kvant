(See the beginning on the 2nd page of the cover) It is interesting to find all the convex mesh figures from the tans. Let us recall three definitions of a convex polygon (they are equivalent): 1) if the polygon lies in the same half-plane relative to each straight line containing its side; 2) if all interior angles of the polygon are less than the unfolded one; 3) if for any two points inside the polygon, the segment with ends at these points lies entirely inside the polygon. From a complete set of tangram elements, 13 different convex mesh polygons can be folded. Among them are exactly one triangle, six quadrilaterals, two pentagons and four hexagons. Try to explain why there is only one triangle, and there are no polygons with 9 or more sides. Heptagons and octagons also do not exist, but this is more difficult to prove, since you will have to estimate the minimum area of ​​such figures from tans on the grid.
