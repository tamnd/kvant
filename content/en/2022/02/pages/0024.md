FROM THE HISTORY OF SCIENCE

Nobeliada-21

L. BELOPUKHOV

IPO PHYSIOLOGY AND MEDICINE, and in chemistry, and in economics, this year’s awards are well in line with Nobel’s will - “the annual award of those individuals who, during the previous year, have been able to bring the greatest benefit to humanity.” True, for a long time now we have been talking not about the previous year, but about the previous time, sometimes quite significant. The award in physiology and medicine is impressive. A solution has been found to the reaction of human nervous tissue not only to such a phenomenon as pain, but also to much more intimate feelings - heat, cold and touch. Perhaps the solution to such a touch as tenderness and caress is not far off. It is not for nothing that an article about this award in the journal “Science and Life” is called “The Solution to Warm Hugs” by the author. I would like to call this year’s award medical and poetic. I would like to call the work awarded the prize in chemistry unusual, “inverse”. The usual path for the catalytic synthesis of asymmetric molecules is given by nature at the cellular level. And now, for a number of reactions, a path has been found for the catalytic synthesis of such molecules without the participation of a biological cellular mechanism - as biochemists say, “in vitro”, i.e. in glass, in a test tube, and not “in vivo”, i.e. in a living organism. This opens up (and has already opened up) broad opportunities for the synthesis of drugs with targeted action, especially for hereditary diseases. The prize in physics stands somewhat apart. Its overall title is: "For pioneering contributions to our understanding of the complexity of physical problems." In other words, this is a prize for clarifying the understanding of complex

But the 2021 prize attaches importance not to the abstract mathematical side of the concept of “complexity,” but to very specific physical research and mathematical theoretical physical work, brought in some cases to comparison with many years of empirical observations and measurements. As a springboard for research, where the particular complexity of phenomena is manifested, the Nobel Committee chose one of the most pressing scientific problems - the problem of global warming of the earth's climate. This problem is clear to all inhabitants of the Earth; it is discussed in scientific and popular science magazines, in political and economic forums, and simply in human conversations. This topic has become especially relevant in coastal cities and coastal countries (for example, in Holland). After all, rising temperatures on Earth mean partial melting of glaciers and rising sea levels, which threatens flooding of areas located low above the coast. When they began to calculate the possible timing of flooding and the changes in the world's flora and fauna that could occur during this process, different futurologists came up with timings for future cataclysms that differed tenfold. The cause of warming is the absorption of solar heat by so-called greenhouse gases, primarily carbon dioxide. This warming came to be called the greenhouse effect. There has even emerged a split in attitude towards this problem.
