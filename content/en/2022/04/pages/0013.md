Mark Ivanovich Bashmakov

(1937–2022)

On March 31, 2022, a member of the editorial board of the magazine «Kvant» Mark Ivanovich Bashmakov, a versatile scientist, mathematician, teacher, author of more than 50 textbooks on mathematics, mountaineer and art historian-collector, passed away. Mark Ivanovich Bashmakov was born on February 10, 1937 in Leningrad. During the Great Patriotic War, his father went to the front as a volunteer and returned disabled. Five-year-old Mark with his mother and two brothers was evacuated from besieged Leningrad and returned there after the end of the war. He graduated from school with a gold medal and entered the Faculty of Mathematics and Mechanics of Leningrad State University. He studied well in mathematics and mechanics and managed to study not only mathematics - he was the secretary of the Komsomol organization of the entire university. In his Komsomol work, as in life in general, he adhered to the principle of taking on difficult tasks, bringing them to the end and never falling into formalism at the expense of the essence of the matter. Bashmakov completed his graduate studies with the defense of his dissertation “Cohomology of Abelian varieties” at the Department of Algebra and Number Theory. In the 60s of the last century, active scientific cooperation between the USSR and other countries began.

After graduating from graduate school, he was sent as a young scientist to the Sorbonne - the University of Paris. Bashmakov returned from Paris with new ideas on algebraic geometry, which became the subject of his doctoral dissertation and a separate direction that he supported at the Department of Algebra and Number Theory of Mathematics and Mechanics of Leningrad State University. Candidate's and doctoral dissertations in this area were prepared under his leadership. Mark Ivanovich invested most of his life and soul into the organization of the famous physics and mathematics boarding school No. 45 at the university and the organization of educational courses in it. In one year, 1962, it was possible to obtain an order from the ministry, the allocation of premises, the selection of teaching staff, the development of original educational programs, the enrollment of children from rural areas of the North-Western region into the school, and, finally, the opening of a mathematical boarding school in 1963. The then rector of Leningrad University, Alexander Danilovich Alexandrov, a mathematician, physicist, philosopher, mountaineer, and a scientist, greatly contributed to the solution of organizational problems.
