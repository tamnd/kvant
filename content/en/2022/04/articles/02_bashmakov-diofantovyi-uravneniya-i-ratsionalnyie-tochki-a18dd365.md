DIAPHANTINE EQUATIONS AND RATIONAL POINTS

Diophantine equations and rational points

M.BASHMAKOV

THE AREA OF NUMBER THEORY IN WHICH integer solutions of equations of the form P x, y,... 0 are studied, where P x, y,... is a polynomial in several variables with integer coefficients, is called Diophantine analysis, and these equations themselves are called Diophantine. They are named after the famous Greek mathematician Diophantus, who lived in the 3rd century and wrote the first treatise on such equations. Despite the fact that Diophantine equations constitute one of the oldest areas of mathematics, general methods for solving them began to be created only recently. Classical works mainly considered linear and quadratic Diophantine equations. The solution of more complex equations has made great progress in the last 20–25 years due to the widespread use of ideas from other areas of mathematics, primarily geometry and topology, in number theory. In the words of N. Bourbaki, “...classical geometry...has become the universal language of all mathematics, possessing exceptional flexibility and convenience.” Geometry offers not only language, but also its constructions, its intuition. Look how clear the geometric language is. Take, for example, Fermat’s equation $х^{n} у^{n} z^{n}$, divide both its sides by $z^{п}$ and denote x z by u, y z by v. We obtain the equation $u^{п} v^{п} 1$. Each solution x; y; z of the Fermat equation in integers corresponds (for z z 0 ) to the solution of the equation $u^{п} v^{п} 1$ in rational numbers.

If we now consider the graph of this equation on the u plane; v , then we obtain a curve, and the integer solutions of Fermat’s equation correspond to the points of this curve with rational coordinates. The converse is also obvious: each point of the indicated curve with rational coordinates will give (after eliminating the denominator) an integer solution to Fermat’s equation. Thus, the question of solving Fermat’s equation, translated into geometric language, turns into the question of finding points with rational coordinates (we will call them rational) on the curve $u^{п} v^{п} 1$. (Note that when dividing Fermat's equation by $z^{п}$ we "lost" its roots at z 0, but they are easy to study separately.) In this article we will introduce you to two geometric techniques for finding rational points on curves. For simplicity, let's start with the equation $х^{2} у^{2} z^{2}$, whose solutions in natural numbers are called Pythagorean triples. The curve corresponding to equation $х^{2} у^{2} z^{2}$ is circle $u^{2} v^{2} 1$.
