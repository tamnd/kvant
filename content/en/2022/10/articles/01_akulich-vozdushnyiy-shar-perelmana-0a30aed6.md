Perelman's balloon

I. AKULICH

HIS FAMOUS BOOK “Do You Know Physics?” (the first edition of the book was published in 1934, after which the book was reprinted many times) the classic popularizer of natural sciences Ya.I. Perelman included the following problem: “A ladder hangs freely from a balloon, motionless in the air. A man began to climb up it. Where will the ball move: up or down, and by how much?” There he also posted the following solution: “The ball will not be left alone. While a person is climbing the stairs, the balloon will descend. What happens here is the same thing that occurs when you walk along a light boat moored to the shore to get onto land: the boat moves backward under your feet. In the same way, a ladder, pushed down by the feet of a person climbing it, will drag the balloon towards the ground. Moving on to solving this problem from the point of view of the laws of mechanics, we must reason like this. A ball with a ladder and a person on it represent an isolated system, the center of gravity of which cannot be moved by internal forces. It will retain its position unchanged when a person climbs the stairs only when the ball itself goes down, otherwise the center of gravity would rise. As for the magnitude of the movement of the ball, it is the same number of times less than the height of the person’s lifting, as many times as heavy as the ball is heavier than the person.” Here is exactly the case when the well-deserved authority of Yakov Isidorovich played a cruel joke, because this task, together with the above

Where and how much will the boat move? The task is indeed completely similar - here only the medium that creates the buoyant force is not air, but water. But what's the difference? If we solve the problem using the same reasoning as Perelman, then, given the equal masses of the boat and the diver, we can draw an unambiguous conclusion: the diver and the boat will meet at a depth of 5 meters under water. What's it like? But the answer is even more impressive if the diver starts going down the stairs instead of going up. Then by the time he is at a depth of 20 meters, the boat will rise into the air to a height of 10 meters above the surface of the water! I would like to see this - it must be an impressive sight. as an analogue of a boat sliding horizontally on the surface of the water as a person walks across it. Yes, for a person with a boat, their total center of gravity will retain its location, but only because no external forces act in the direction of movement of the person and the boat (and the system is isolated in its projection onto the axis along which the person walks and the boat moves). But for a balloon, the opposite is true - the system is not isolated, since it is in the vertical direction that very significant external forces act on all its elements. This is, firstly, the force of gravity, and secondly, the buoyant force of the air. They determine the movement of the ball. Yes, as a matter of fact, the very presence of a ball with a person in the air in a state of equilibrium is determined primarily by these external forces, and not by any movement of the person along the stairs.
