PHYSICS + BIOLOGY

The watershed that existed for many centuries between these areas of natural science and seemed to mark the boundary between the methods of studying living and inorganic nature, in fact, of course, was overcome “pointwise” more than once. As the number of both the objects of research themselves and the methods for comprehending them increased, physics and biology increasingly began not only to intersect, but, better said, to merge with each other. Completely new scientific directions were born - bioenergetics, molecular biology, radiation genetics, photobiology. This led (mainly in the last century) to revolutionary discoveries and a radical revision of almost all aspects of our lives - from living conditions to medicine, from restructuring our diet to sports. We will try to talk about this in the next issues of Kaleidoscope. But first, let’s take a look at school textbooks (and not only physics!) and look at the “green friends” and “our little brothers” around us.
