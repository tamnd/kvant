What is fresh air

A. KNYAZEV, A. KNYAZEV (JR.)

TALKING ABOUT THE AIR WE BREATHE, we are usually interested in its chemical composition and the content, for example, of dust in it. Don't forget about bacteria and viruses. To improve the composition of air indoors, in some industries, at space stations, etc. chemical and physical filters and masks are used - gas masks, respirators, ventilation. All these measures are effective to varying degrees as individual components in the fight for clean air. However, additional factors remain that influence the physical composition of air. So, it is also important that we live in electromagnetic fields of natural and technological origin. In this article we will look at the physical composition of the air we breathe and the effect of electric fields on it. Currently, this aspect is of particular importance, although research began in the 30s of the last century. Many people know that freshness is clean air that is pleasant and easy to breathe, and that the effect of freshness can be achieved by ventilating the room or placing living plants in it. Have you ever noticed how nice the laundry smells after being dried outside and brought indoors? Some associate this with additives in washing powders, forgetting that this freshness was noted even when there were no washing powders yet. And some believe that this air is saturated and smells of ozone, although it is known that ozone can kill bacteria, but is very dangerous for breathing - just stand near a welding machine. It is especially easy to breathe on the seashore, river, in the field, in the mountains. This effect is in no way achieved either by masks or conditioners. Thus, fresh air is not as simple a phenomenon as it seems at first glance. Let's consider it from a physical point of view and try to find out what is the mechanism for creating fresh air.
