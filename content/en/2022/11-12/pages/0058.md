It shows that the smaller the radius of curvature of the tip and the higher the field strength, the greater the effect of molecule rupture and air ionization. For a specific calculation, it can be shown that the force acting on a soft (deformed in a strong external field) dipole near the tip is inversely proportional to the fifth (!) power of the radius. And then the atoms of the dipole find themselves in inhomogeneous fields comparable to the intraatomic field. In this case, the field energy in this region will be equal in order of magnitude to several electron volts, which is quite sufficient to ionize an atom. Let us add that if the strength of the external field created at ground level is approximately 150 V m in the normal state of the atmosphere, then in thunderstorm weather it reaches values ​​of about 800 V m. Bodies that create a significantly inhomogeneous field with high intensity around themselves can even be small droplets formed during the evaporation of drying laundry. This is the balloelectric effect. First, we hang out the dry laundry and check for a small charge on it. After washing and spinning the laundry, we hang it out and after some time, after the water has drained, we begin observations. Now the surface charge is easily fixed. The experiments were carried out in different years by students of the Lyceum of Applied Sciences and the Physical and Technical Lyceum No. 1 of Saratov, D. Yakovlev (2010) and A. Aleshin (2022).
