Interestingly, the study of snowflakes back in the 17th century led scientists to the brilliant idea that crystals consist of tiny and identical particles with a certain shape. So it would not be a great exaggeration to say that snowflakes laid the foundation for the development of a large branch of physics - crystallography. The basis of an ice crystal is a regular hexagon formed by water molecules. It turns out that in a water molecule the angle between the oxygen-hydrogen bond is 104 degrees, but in the solid state the oxygen atoms form a regular hexagon. More precisely, two regular triangles, slightly offset from each other along an axis passing through their centers.
