PROBLEM BOOK “QUANTA” PROBLEMS BOOK “QUANTA”

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are non-standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After formulating the task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems in mathematics and physics from this issue should be sent to the email addresses: math@kvant.ras.ru and phys@kvant.ras.ru, respectively, or to the postal address: 119296 Moscow, Leninsky Prospekt, 64-A, «Kvant». Send the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication, along with your solution to this problem, to the same addresses. Problem M2722 – M2724 was offered at the XLIV Tournament of Cities. Problem M2726 was offered at the South Russian Mathematical Olympiad “Assara”. The author of problems F2729–F2736 is S. Varlamov.

Problem M2722–M2729, F2729–F2736 M2722. Given an acute non-isosceles triangle. One action allows you to cut one of the existing triangles along the median into two triangles. Can all the triangles turn out to be isosceles after a few steps? E. Bakaev

M2723. It is known that among several banknotes whose denominations are pairwise different natural numbers, there are exactly N counterfeit ones. In one check, the detector determines the sum of the denominations of all real banknotes included in the set we have selected. Prove that after N checks it is possible to find all counterfeit bills if: a) N 2; b) N 3. S. Tokarev

M2724. In an infinite arithmetic progression, where all numbers are natural, there were two numbers with the same sum of digits. Is there necessarily another number with the same sum of digits? A. Shapovalov
