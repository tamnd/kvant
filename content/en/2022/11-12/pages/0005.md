NOBEL PRIZE 2022

Laureates of the Nobel Prize in Physics 2022 (from left to right): Alain Aspe, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger explain why it was given. And along with serious materials, not very intelligible ones appeared, but they had flashy or playful headlines. Here are just a few of them: “Einstein was wrong”, “God plays these games”, “What scientists have proven and why it concerns you”, “God exists - scientific proof”, “Billions of times faster than light”, “Passion at a distance”, “Quantum entanglement and quantum teleportation - hype”, “When the sock becomes right”. Serious materials began to appear long before the award, since 2007. So who were the winners? American physicist John Francis Clauser was born in 1942 in Pasadena. He graduated from the California Institute of Technology, where there are only 200 students in the course and no fewer professors who teach them. There are 31 Nobel laureates associated with this university (now 32). At the age of 29, Klauser became a doctor of science, defending his dissertation at Columbia University. After that, he worked and continues to work in several prestigious scientific laboratories in the United States. French physicist Alain Aspect was born in 1947 in the small town of Agen. He studied at one of the Parisian universities, defended his doctoral dissertation at another and worked in

Parisian scientific institutions. His main research was related to quantum optics - the use of lasers in various physical experiments. Austrian physicist Anton Zeilinger was born in 1945 in the small Austrian town of Ried. He graduated from the University of Vienna, became a Doctor of Science there in 1971, worked at this university all his life and is now its honorary professor. He first studied neutron interferometry, and then quantum optics. From these brief biographical data it is clear that the laureates are almost the same age as they are of advanced age (75, 77 and 80 years old). They began their experiments with entangled photons several decades ago (Klauser in 1972, Aspe in 1981, Zeilinger in 1997). In 2010, all three were awarded the Wolf Prize. This prize in physics is considered second in prestige after the Nobel Prize. In 2004 it was awarded to the Russian physicist Vitaly Ginzburg, and in 2007 to the famous English physicist Stephen Hawking. The significance of the Wolf Prize is especially great in those categories that are not included in the Nobel Prize - mathematics, agriculture, art and culture.
