KVA NT 2022/No. 11-12

If the air temperature is below zero degrees, the freezing point of water, then the water molecules in the cloud will immediately form tiny ice crystals. The size of these crystals is no more than one tenth of a millimeter. True, for this you again need an assistant - a small speck of dust, which will play the role of a seed. It is on a small speck of dust that the first ice crystal forms. Yes, yes, snowflakes start as dust. First, water evaporates from the surface of any body of water. Then water vapor rises into the air, condenses on dust particles already in the cloud and moves down the cloud in air currents that behave rather strangely. And the end result is magical stars that captivate with their beauty. Then the crystal begins its journey across the cloud and turns into a snowflake. If the temperature in the cloud or any part of it is above zero degrees, then our crystal will eventually melt and will subsequently travel in a different capacity - like a drop of water. A snowflake will be able to get out of the cloud only in winter, when the ambient temperature drops below zero. Only then will a snowflake be able to fall to the ground, delighting us with pure white creaky snow. What happens to a snowflake as it moves from the top of the cloud to the ground? The journey begins when, in a cold cloud unable to hold much water vapor, microscopic drops of water are attracted to the smallest specks of dust, freeze, and then this nondescript ice crystal grows into a beautiful star.

Only in books about Santa Claus, all snowflakes are beautiful six-pointed stars. In fact, snowflakes are different. Sometimes these are simple plates, in some cases they are prisms and even needles. The shape of a snowflake is determined by two main conditions: temperature and the degree of saturation of the air with water vapor. Under different conditions, snowflakes of different shapes are obtained. Beautiful snowflakes love high humidity and do not like severe frost. They say that no two snowflakes are alike. But there is something that still unites them. Taking a closer look at different snowflakes, you will notice that their shape is always based on a hexagon. And again, the best illustration is… sugar. If you have enough patience, you can grow quite large crystals of sugar or salt. All salt crystals have the shape of small bricks: rectangular parallelepipeds. If it is possible to grow a large salt crystal, then its shape will be a rectangular parallelepiped, and under certain growth conditions - a cube. Having broken this large cube, for example with a hammer, it is easy to make sure that all the fragments will also have the shape of parallelepipeds. Humanity has long been interested in unusual stones that have the correct shape and cut. The ancient Greeks, by the way, considered rock crystal (quartz) crystals to be water frozen forever. The number of crystals known to man has constantly increased. Various magical properties began to be attributed to crystals. Rich people wanted to own rare and unusual crystals.
