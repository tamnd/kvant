⟦folio 13⟧

NOBEL PRIZE 2022

Quantum computer objects. One of the authors of this idea was the outstanding physicist Richard Feynman. At a conference on the physics of computing at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1981, he gave a presentation on the limitations of conventional computers and the need to develop new algorithmic and programming principles for the transition to completely different physical devices for transmitting and processing information. A quantum property of a micro-object (for example, spin orientation) can have not two values, like a classical information object (zero or one), but a set of values ​​limited by these extreme values. Quantum mechanics allows one to calculate their probabilities. This means that one information cell can contain not one bit of information, but with sufficient probability megabytes and gigabytes. Input and output of information and its processing can be carried out through entangled pairs, i.e. without wasting time, instantly. Such a cell of information is called a “quantum bit,” abbreviated as “qubit” (from quantum bit).

⟦column⟧

Entangled pairs in qubits are achieved in a variety of ways, and all involve processes that require complex and expensive instrumentation. But nevertheless, at the beginning of this century, work began to actively develop in all developed countries on the creation of algorithms and programs for quantum computers, and on the creation of qubits and their combinations. Already five years ago, government spending on work on quantum information reached a billion dollars in the European Union, 400 million in England, 300 million in the USA, and 220 million in China. What are the reasons for this? The power and efficiency of quantum computers is undeniable. Thus, in 2021, the operation of a computing quantum computer consisting of 113 qubits was demonstrated at the National University of Technology of China. In a few minutes, the quantum computer performed calculations that would have taken several billion years (!) to complete on a classical computer. Quantum computers for performing one operation, such as searching, have already appeared on sale (USA, China). The cost is millions of dollars, the size is a huge refrigerator. Huge volumes of processed information are becoming necessary in modern medicine (protein synthesis), in problems of optimizing logistics, in modern meteorology, in astrophysics (processing data from thousands of radio telescopes), etc. But what comes to the fore is the impossibility of outside access to information processed and transmitted by quantum computers, and the possibility of encrypting information in such a way that it is fundamentally impossible to decipher it (a consequence of the probabilistic nature of the information). It is clear how important this is in the modern world in the economic and political spheres.
