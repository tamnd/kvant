Now light a match and heat the spiral until red hot - the light bulb will almost completely go out (Fig. 1).

This means that as the temperature increases, the resistance of the metal conductor increases.

2. Replace the metal conductor with an electrolyte (a solution of salt in water) and check how its resistance varies with temperature.

Cut two small strips from the rigid material and bend one of them so that there is an air gap 1-3 mm thick between the strips. During this interval, add a few drops of salt solution. Select the concentration of the solution such that when the circuit is closed, the light bulb connected in series with the battery and electrolyte burns barely noticeably.

Fig. 3.

Heat the electrolyte (using a hot match) and you will see that the light bulb will burn brighter (Fig. 2).

Consequently, when cutting, the electrolyte resistance decreases.

3. Use the diagram of the previous experiment, only place a tablet of carbolan (activated carbon, it is sold in all pharmacies) between the strips of hard paper - the light does not light.

Press your finger on the top strip so that the coal tablet is compressed - the light will light up (Fig. 3).

Thus, as the resistance of coal increases, it decreases.

...and the trick failed

This conversation took place in a pose.

When everyone got bored with the announced entertainment of a long trip, passenger A. came up with a great idea.

A. Would you like me to show you a trick?
Q. Do you need maps?
A. Oh, no! Just paper and pencil.

So, write some four-digit number.

B. Done!

A. Rearrange its numbers as you wish. Rearranged? Take away less from a large number. Cross out one number in the resulting difference.

B. Crossed out. What's next?
A. Add up the remaining numbers and say the amount.

Q. Just a minute! So... I got 16.

A. You crossed out the two.

V., with the words: “Yes, you are a real telephone!”, showed his piece of paper. It was written on it:
4975; 7594; 7594—4975 = 2619; 6+1+9=16.

Q. What is the secret of your “trick”?

A. This is a simple argument. After you named the sum 16, I took the first number, a multiple of nine and greater than this sum, that is, 18. The difference between the selected number and the sum is the crossed out figure. If your sum turned out to be equal to 19, then I would say eight (8=27-19). If you got 8, then my answer would be one (1=9-8).

“I understand everything!” - V. finished cleaning himself and ran to show a trick in the next compartment.

Q. How much did you receive in total?
C. Exactly 9.

Q. You crossed out the number 9.

Silently, S. showed his recording. Frustrated V. protsel: 4353; 3345; 4353—3345=1008; 1+0+8=9.

Explain why V.’s trick didn’t work.

N. Mikhailenko
