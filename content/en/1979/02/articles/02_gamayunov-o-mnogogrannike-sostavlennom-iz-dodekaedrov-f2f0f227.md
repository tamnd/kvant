This figure shows an intricate but beautiful polyhedron - the union of the heel of dodecahedrons arranged, in a sense, "as symmetrically as possible." The vertices of these 5 dodecahedrons (there are 5×20=100 of them) fall into two types - single (there are 60 of them, three edges of our polyhedron are codent in them, and the vertices themselves are grouped in the form of 12 small pentagons) and double (there are 20=40:2 of them, each of them has 12 edges codensed). Try to find the path of the vertices of one face of one dodecahedron (two double, three single). Find on figure the remaining visible faces of this dodecahedron; find a face of some other dodecahedron. Make sure that any two dodecahedrons have exactly two common vertices and are formed from each other by rotating around the axis connecting these vertices. The model was designed by V. Gamayunov.

In Uzbekistan, the scientific society of students from secondary school No. 110 in Tashkent is well known. The goals and objectives of the society are determined by a special resolution of the pedagogical council and the Komsomol school committee. Scientific and pedagogical leadership is carried out jointly by school and scientists of the Academy of Sciences of Uzbekistan, teachers and students of a number of Tashkent universities. Agreements have been concluded between the school and the schools on the other side.

Every year in September and May, conferences are held at which plans and programs are approved, reports are reviewed, and commemorative certificates “Best Mathematician School”, “Best Physicist School”, and awards from sponsoring organizations are awarded. Between conferences, the leadership of the society is carried out by the Children's Council. The society unites sections of mathematics, physics, chemistry, history, literature, and law. The sections will consist of clubs for students in grades 6-7, problem groups for high school students.

For example, the mathematics section includes clubs “Count, guess, guess” (for 6th grade students), “Entertaining Mathematics” (for seventh and upper grade students), a permanent seminar “Search and Solutions, Research and Conclusions” and a workshop on future research (for students in grades 8-10). Each section and all group problems have scientific advisors. Members of the society study theoretical issues, carry out experimental work in the laboratories of universities and research institutes, classrooms in schools, and produce various aids, equipment, instruments for schools and the national economy.

Junior schools in the subjects of natural sciences and humanities are becoming more and more widespread. These are voluntary, mass associations of students in grades 8-10 of general education schools, created at higher educational institutions and scientific institutions with the aim of deepening knowledge, satisfying and developing the interests of schoolchildren in determining the branch of science, expanding scientific horizons and professional guidance. Teaching is carried out by scientists, research assistants, teachers, graduate students and students on a voluntary basis. Very popular environments for schoolchildren, correspondence, evening, summer school for young mathematicians, physicists, chemists, historians; teachers, doctors. Interesting experience in this work has been accumulated in Belarus, the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, the Gorky Region, the Khabarovsk Territory, and Moscow.

An analysis of the activities of scientific societies of students of all types shows that they actively contribute to improving the quality of knowledge of schoolchildren, expanding their scientific and technical horizons, mastering the skills of independent creative work, and the perception of readiness for a creative choice of a future profession and creative work.

But so far, unfortunately, scientific societies have not been created everywhere. There are no resource, regional, city HOYs in a number of co-resource, some regional centers of the RSFSR, although there is every opportunity for their work. Young scientists and specialists from research institutes are still not sufficiently involved in this work. We are concerned that in a number of associations all the activities of children are aimed at preparing them for entering university. There is no targeted development of a program for HOY yet. Often teachers take a passive position in relation to HOY, do not use it as a good aid in their work to improve the quality of knowledge of scientists, and promote accessible science and technology.

The increase in the efficiency of HOY activities was facilitated by the publication on the pages of relevant journals, such as «Kvant», lists of work topics, assignments for scientists and specialists, institutions and organizations for young researchers.

The task and duty, first of all, of young scientists, teachers, and students is to transform each scientific society of students into a real school of creativity, perception of the future of Soviet science and technology.
