About Newton's laws of motion.
Weight

The laws of mechanical motion of a body were discovered by Newton in the middle of the 17th century. They were set out in his book “Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy,” published in 1687. Without exaggeration, we can say that modern mechanics began with this book.

The basic ideas of mechanics and the laws of motion discovered by Newton will be discussed in this article.

A little about mechanics before Newton

In order to understand the essence of the revolution in the science of the motion of bodies that Newton accomplished, let us first get acquainted in the most general terms with the science of motion that existed before Newton.

The mechanics of Aristotle (384-322 BC) enjoyed the greatest popularity in pre-Dytomov times. We will include it here and tell you about it, although this theory turned out to be false in almost all its parts. But it was she who was replaced by Newtonian mechanics.

In his constructions and conclusions, Aristotle relied on observations and everyday experience. (On this basis, undoubtedly, science is built.) However, these observations were often superficial and were not accompanied by changes. Therefore, the conclusions drawn by Aristotle turned out to be incorrect.

Observations show, for example, that a cart moves on a level road only when it is pulled by a horse. This means that in order for a body to move (have speed), it needs to be influenced by another body. This influence of one body on another is called force. According to Aristotle, force is the cause of motion. In the absence of force, peace and only peace is possible.

On the other hand, observations show that there are movements

going around “without horses”, without strength. So, any heavy body falls by itself, moves down; light bodies (smoke, heated air, etc.) move upward and also by themselves. Observations finally show that planets move “without horses.”

Based on such observations, Aristotle divides all movements into two classes: violent and natural. The cause of violent movements is the influence of other bodies, that is, forces. As for natural movements, the reason is completely different. Aristotle defines it this way: every body is destined by nature to a certain place. Heavy bodies are assigned a place at the bottom, light bodies at the top. Planets and stars are predetermined to rotate eternally around the Earth. So the cause of natural movements is desire. bodies to take the place that is determined for them by nature.

But heavy bodies can also move upward. Like throwing a stone up? According to Aristotle, this is already a violent movement. And the reason for it lies in the fact that nature does not allow the existence of emptiness: the air strives to immediately fill the space vacated by the body during movement, and constantly pushes this body. (This idea of ​​the “fear of the void” persisted in physics until Galileo and Torricelli, who finally put an end to it.)

We see that Aristotelian mechanics was not a unified science of the laws of motion. Different causes were attributed to different movements, and the causes themselves were formulated very vaguely. But the authority of Aristotle was strong that his mechanics existed for almost two millennia, until the time of Galileo and Newton.

First law of mechanics

In Newtonian mechanics, attention is first of all drawn to the fact that motion must be studied in relation to a certain reporting system. In fact, Newton uses a reporting system associated with the Sun.

At that time, it was already known how the planets moved relative to the Sun (the laws of planetary motion were studied by Kepler). Analyzing these movements, as well as the movements of a body on Earth, studied partly by himself, partly by his predecessors, primarily Galileo, Newton came to a Fundamental conclusion: if any body moves relative to the Sun by acceleration, then this acceleration is always caused by the influence of some other body or several bodies on the body. So, acceleration of the planet’s movement. These accelerations are caused by the influence of the Sun. The acceleration of a body falling to the Earth is caused by the influence of the Earth. The acceleration of a body attached to a circle is caused by the influence of a spring, etc. This is the law of motion: the acceleration of any body relative to the Sun is always caused by the influence of some body or bodies on it. According to Aristotle, force is the cause of movement (speed) of a body and, moreover, only violent one. In Newtonian mechanics, force is the reason for the change in motion (change in speed - acceleration) of a body during any movement.

How should a body behave that is not influenced by other bodies? According to Aristotle, such a body should dig, its speed should be zero. According to Newton, such a body in the reporting system associated with the Sun should have zero acceleration. This means that in this system a body that is not influenced by other bodies can either dig around or move in a straight line and uniformly. This is Newton's first law - the law of inertia.

The law of inertia is valid not only when the body is not influenced by any other bodies. Those bodies that are influenced by other bodies are also subordinate to it, but these “other” bodies are not one, but several, and the influence of each of them is compensated by everything else. The word “inertia” means the phenomenon that a body that is not influenced by other bodies (forces) or the influence of which is compensated by other bodies does not change its motion, that is, does not change its speed.

As we said, Newton considers motion in a reporting system associated with the Sun. But if a body moves at a constant speed relative to the Sun, then relative to any other body moving without acceleration relative to the Sun, it also moves at a constant speed (but different). So in the reporting system associated with this “other” body, the law of inertia is also fulfilled. All such systems are called inertial.

Newton's first law is now usually formulated as follows: the existing system also reports that, relative to the components of the incoming moving chalk, their speed remains constant if they are not acted upon by other chalk or the acting chalk is compensated.

What if we connect the coordinate system with a body that itself moves with acceleration relative to the inertial coordinate system?

Relative to such a system, any body that is not influenced by other bodies will move by acceleration. In this system, a body can be at rest, despite the influence of another body on it. All such reporting systems moving relative to the inertial system by acceleration are called non-inertial.

Can we say that the coordinate system associated with the Earth is inertial? Strictly speaking, no, because the Earth moves around the Sun along a curved path (almost in a circle), and therefore, relative to the Sun, it accelerates. In addition, the Earth rotates about its axis. This movement is also accelerated. But the Earth makes only one revolution around the Sun per year, and around its axis per day. Both accelerations are so small that in most cases the Earth can be taken as an inertial reference frame.

The fact of the existence of inertial reporting systems in the world does not seem obvious at all, it goes without saying. The ancient thinker (Aristotle) ​​found it difficult to understand why bodies continue to move even after the influence of other bodies on them has ceased. Why, for example, does the arrow continue its flight after the bow string has stopped pushing it?

Gallia was the first to notice the fallacy of the belief that other bodies must maintain the motion (speed) of a given body. He asked the question: what property of the movement of a body depends on the influence of other bodies on it? No amount of speculative reasoning can answer this question. Here you need to turn directly to nature, that is, to experience.

The answer that nature gave was unexpected and is as follows: there are inertial reporting systems; in them, nothing is required to maintain the movement of a body at a constant speed - it will happen by itself.

When this answer was received, it was the largest discovery ever made. That is why the law of inertia is one of the most important laws of nature.

Interaction of bodies and acceleration. Weight

Another important fact was established by Newton from observations of the movements of bodies. It consists in the fact that when a body influences other bodies and imparts acceleration to it, this other body “responds” to it in the same way, that is, imparts acceleration to it as well. The influence of one body on another is not one-sided - the bodies interact, imparting acceleration to each other. If, for example, two balls collide (collision is an example of interaction), each of them receives acceleration. What are these accelerations? How are they directed? What are their numerical values?

Experience shows that in all cases the accelerations of interacting bodies are opposite in direction.

If, for example, a moving ball collides with the same stationary ball so that the speed of the ball is directed along the line connecting the centers of the balls (Fig. 1), then the speed of the first of them will decrease, and the speed of the second will increase. And this means that the accelerations of the balls are opposite in direction.

As for the absolute values ​​(modules) of accelerations, they can be anything. It depends on how the bodies moved before the interaction (from the initial conditions), on the nature of the interaction, on which bodies interact. But for a given pair of interacting bodies, the acceleration ratio is always the same. They do not depend either on the nature of the interaction or on the initial conditions. This means that it depends on some special property hidden in the interacting bodies themselves. This property, inherent in every body, is expressed by the amount of body mass.

Newton discovered this property and introduced into science the quantities that quantitatively characterize it - mass. What is this property?

In everyday life and in technology, in scientific research, mass is often (but not always) changed in another way - weighing. This method is not related to the law of motion; it is connected with the law of universal gravitation, and it changes not the inertial mass, but the so-called gravitational mass. But surprisingly, these two coincidences coincide in their role in the mechanics of mass. This article is about inertial mass, which is a measure of the inertia of a body. It is precisely because of the essence of inertial mass, because of the essence of the property of inertia, that the interaction of bodies causes the appearance of acceleration (and not speed) in interacting bodies.

In everyday life, and sometimes in books on physics, the word “mass” refers to the amount of substances in a body. But the amount of substances in a body is determined by the number of particles (atoms or molecules) of a given substance in it. And mass determines the inertia of a body and nothing else. But the mass of a body of a given substance is proportional to the number of particles of this substance in the body. Measuring mass is easy, but counting the number of particles is difficult. For this reason, mass is used as a measure of the amount of a substance.

It influenced all of physics, and mechanics was no exception. The relative theory showed that Newton's laws of mechanics are only approximately correct. In particular, formula (1) given by us is not entirely accurate.
