⟦folio 32⟧

Problem
Kwana

M546—M550; F558—F562

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Posted, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than April 1, 1979 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant». In the “To” column, write: “Kwana Problem No. 2–79” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example, “M546, M547” or “F558”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope). Place the conditions of each original problem proposed for publication (or a series of problems) in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope place: “Kwana problem, a new problem in physics” or “... a new problem in mathematics”)). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your first and last name, school number and class in which you study.

M546. From an arbitrary point $M$ of a circle circumscribed about a rectangle, we dropped perpendiculars $MP$ and $MQ$ onto its two opposite sides, and perpendiculars $MR$ and $MT$ onto the extensions of the other two sides. Prove that lines $PR$ and $QT$ are perpendicular to each other, and their intersection point belongs to the diagonal of the rectangle.

Vvede Shafaryan, 10th grade student (Yerevan)

M547. In order for the equation $\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y} = \frac{1}{n}$, where $n$ is a natural number, to have a unique solution in natural numbers $x, y$, it is necessary and sufficient that $n$ be prime. Prove it.

A. Dantselyan

M548. a) There are 4 points on a circle. Through the middle of the chord connecting two of them, a straight line is drawn, perpendicular to the chord connecting the other two points. (Such a line is drawn for each pair of points.) Prove that all six constructed lines pass through one point.

6) There are 5 points on a circle. Through the center of gravity of three of them (the point of intersection of the medians of the triangle with the vertices at these points) a straight line is drawn, perpendicular to the chord connecting the remaining points. Prove that all ten constructed lines pass through one point.

c) Generalize these statements to the case of $n$ points.

A. Lopshitz

M549. Given a natural number $N$. Let's write down all its divisors $d_1, d_2, \dots, d_n$ and for each of them we will find how many divisors it has. Prove that the resulting numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots, a_n$ satisfy the equality

$$(a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_n)^2 = a_1^3 + a_2^3 + \dots + a_n^3.$$
