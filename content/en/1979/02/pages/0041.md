If the wheel is not balanced, then its center of gravity moves along a circle of radius $r$ with a centripetal acceleration $\omega^2 r$ (r is the distance between the center of the wheel and the center of gravity, $\omega$ is the angular speed of rotation of the wheel). Therefore, at the moment when the center of gravity is at the lowest point, the value $|\vec{N}|$ is greater than the value $\frac{1}{4} M|\vec{g}|$ by the amount $m\omega^2 r$ (m is the mass of the wheel). When the center of gravity is at the highest point, the value of $|\vec{N}|$ is less than $\frac{1}{4} M|\vec{g}|$ by the same amount. When the wheel slips relative to the road (skidding always occurs), the friction force between the wheel and the road according to these principles turns out to be variable. Therefore, in some places the tire wears out faster than if the wheel were balanced. And this leads to the need to change tires more often.
