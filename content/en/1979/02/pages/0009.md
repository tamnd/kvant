In practice
1. On a summer afternoon, the beetle decided to fly to the Sun and flew at a speed of $|\vec{v}| = |\vec{c}| - |\vec{u}| \approx |\vec{c}|$ which can be associated with the radius $r$ of this circle: $|\vec{v}| = \omega r$. From here (Fig.4)
$$|SO| = |OL| = r = \frac{|\vec{v}|}{\omega} \approx \frac{|\vec{c}| \Delta h}{|\Delta u|}.$$
From triangle OSF
$$|SF| = \sqrt{|OS|^2 - |OF|^2} = \sqrt{r^2 - (r - h)^2} \approx \sqrt{2rh} \approx \sqrt{2|\vec{c}|h \Delta h}.$$
So, the listener, located at the same height $h$ as the sound source, falls into the “dead zone” at a distance
$$|SS'| = 2|SF| \approx 2\sqrt{2|\vec{c}|h \Delta h}.$$
Reception $\frac{|\Delta u|}{\Delta h} \approx \frac{|\vec{u}|}{h}$. Then finally
$$|SS'| \approx 2h \sqrt{\frac{2|\vec{c}|}{|\vec{u}|}} \approx 3h \sqrt{\frac{|\vec{c}|}{|\vec{u}|}}.$$
For $h=1,5$ m, $|\vec{c}| = 330$ m/s and $|\vec{u}| = 15$ m/s we obtain
$$|SS'| \approx 20$$

quite a reasonable result. An accurate calculation of the shape of the “sound rays” leads to the same result.
