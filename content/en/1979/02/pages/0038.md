**M502. Data for parallel segments $AA_1$, $BB_1$, $CC_1$ that do not lie in the same plane. Let $M$ be the intersection point of planes $ABC_1$, $BCA_1$ and $CAB_1$, and $M_1$ be the intersection point of planes $A_1B_1C$, $B_1C_1A$ and $C_1A_1B$. Let us prove that the segment $MM_1$ is parallel to the intersection line.**

Let $A_0$, $B_0$, $C_0$ be the projections of these segments onto the plane perpendicular to them (Fig. 6). Note that the line of intersection of the planes $ABC_1$ and $CAB_1$ passes through the point $A$ and the point of intersection of the lines $BC_1$ and $B_1C$ (Fig. 7), and its projection passes through the point $A_0$ and divides the segment $B_0C_0$ in relation to $|BB_1|:|CC_1|$. The line of intersection of planes $BCA_1$ and $ABC_1$ passes through the point $B$ and the point of intersection of lines $AC_1$ and $A_1C$ (Fig. 7), so that its projection passes through the point $B_0$ and divides the segment $A_0C_0$ in relation to $|AA_1|:|CC_1|$ (Fig. 6). It remains to note that the facts also hold for the projections of the lines of intersection of pairs of planes $(A_1B_1C$, $C_1A_1B)$ and $(B_1C_1A$, $A_1B_1C)$, so that the points $M$ and $M_1$ of intersection of three to the plane are projected into the same point $M_0$ of the plane $A_0B_0C_0$, perpendicular to $AA_1$. This implies the statement of the problem.
