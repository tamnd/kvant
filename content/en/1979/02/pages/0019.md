In Newtonian mechanics, attention is first of all drawn to the fact that motion must be studied in relation to a certain reporting system. In fact, Newton uses a reporting system associated with the Sun.

At that time, it was already known how the planets moved relative to the Sun (the laws of planetary motion were studied by Kepler). Analyzing these movements, as well as the movements of a body on Earth, studied partly by himself, partly by his predecessors, primarily Galileo, Newton came to a Fundamental conclusion: if any body moves relative to the Sun by acceleration, then this acceleration is always caused by the influence of some other body or several bodies on the body. So, acceleration of the planet’s movement. These accelerations are caused by the influence of the Sun. The acceleration of a body falling to the Earth is caused by the influence of the Earth. The acceleration of a body attached to a circle is caused by the influence of a spring, etc. This is the law of motion: the acceleration of any body relative to the Sun is always caused by the influence of some body or bodies on it. According to Aristotle, force is the cause of movement (speed) of a body and, moreover, only violent one. In Newtonian mechanics, force is the reason for the change in motion (change in speed - acceleration) of a body during any movement.

How should a body behave that is not influenced by other bodies? According to Aristotle, such a body should dig, its speed should be zero. According to Newton, such a body in the reporting system associated with the Sun should have zero acceleration. This means that in this system a body that is not influenced by other bodies can either dig around or move in a straight line and uniformly. This is Newton's first law - the law of inertia.

The law of inertia is valid not only when the body is not influenced by any other bodies. Those bodies that are influenced by other bodies are also subordinate to it, but these “other” bodies are not one, but several, and the influence of each of them is compensated by everything else. The word “inertia” means the phenomenon that a body that is not influenced by other bodies (forces) or the influence of which is compensated by other bodies does not change its motion, that is, does not change its speed.

As we said, Newton considers motion in a reporting system associated with the Sun. But if a body moves at a constant speed relative to the Sun, then relative to any other body moving without acceleration relative to the Sun, it also moves at a constant speed (but different). So in the reporting system associated with this “other” body, the law of inertia is also fulfilled. All such systems are called inertial.

Newton's first law is now usually formulated as follows: the existing system also reports that, relative to the components of the incoming moving chalk, their speed remains constant if they are not acted upon by other chalk or the acting chalk is compensated.

What if we connect the coordinate system with a body that itself moves with acceleration relative to the inertial coordinate system?

Relative to such a system, any body that is not influenced by other bodies will move by acceleration. In this system, a body can be at rest, despite the influence of another body on it. All such reporting systems moving relative to the inertial system by acceleration are called non-inertial.

Can we say that the coordinate system associated with the Earth is inertial? Strictly speaking, no, because the Earth moves around the Sun along a curved path (almost in a circle), and therefore, relative to the Sun, it accelerates. In addition, the Earth rotates about its axis. This movement is also accelerated. But the Earth makes only one revolution around the Sun per year, and around its axis per day. Both accelerations are so small that in most cases the Earth can be taken as an inertial reference frame.
