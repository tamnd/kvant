It remains only to prove that for convex sets inequality (*) can be an equality only for an arithmetic progression. To do this, it is enough to show that if $c_k \geq (c_{k-1} + c_{k+1})/2$, then either all $c_k$, $k = 0, \dots, 2n$ are equal to zero, or all $c_k$, $k = 1, \dots, 2n-1$ are positive (in the latter case, the right inequality in (**) is necessarily strict).

Let's choose the smallest $c_i$; let it not be on the edge: $i \neq 0, i \neq 2n$. then $c_i \leq c_{i-1}$ and $c_i \leq c_{i+1}$, whence $c_i \leq \frac{c_{i-1} + c_{i+1}}{2}$. But by condition $\{c_k\}$ is a “convex” set, which means $c_i = c_{i-1} = c_{i+1}$. Applying the same reasoning to $c_{i-1}$ (or to $c_{i+1}$), we find that $c_{i-2} = c_{i-1} = c_i$ (or $c_i = c_{i+1} = c_{i+2}$), etc. - until we reach the edges; we get $0 = c_1 = c_2 = \dots = c_{2n-1} = 0$. Therefore, if there are numbers $c_k$ other than zero, then the smallest common commensurate is on the edge (and is equal to zero), so for $k = 1, \dots, 2n-1$ we have $c_k > 0$.

F502. The lid of the closed box has a height of $h = 1$ and there is a round hole. How will the illumination of the day change under the hole if there are lines with an optical power of $D = 1$ ditre in the hole? The box sits in the open air, then nums with a uniform film of clouds.

The luminous flux in a single telephone angle is the same in any direction. Let us denote by the luminous flux that flows through a unit angle and falls on a unit area of ​​the lens or bottom under the hole.

In the absence of a lens, rays fall to the bottom, smothering in the telephone angle $\Omega = S_{\text{OTB}}/h^2$ (Fig. 8). Therefore, in this case, the daylight illumination under the hole is equal to

$$E_1 = \sigma \Omega = \sigma S_{\text{OTB}}/h^2$$

The lens will collect in its focal plane at the bottom of the box on an area $s = h^2\omega$ all the rays that fall in the telephone angle $\omega$ (Fig. 9). Such rays carry a luminous flux $\Phi = \sigma S_{\text{OTB}}\omega$, so the illumination of the sky image in this case is equal to

$$E_2 = \Phi/s = \sigma S_{\text{OTB}}/h^2.$$

We see that $E_1 = E_2$, that is, the illumination of the bottom of the box under the hole does not change.
