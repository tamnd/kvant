Thus, in a closed circuit, a continuous direction of movement of electrons, that is, an electric current, arises. The role of the source is currently played by a moving conductor, inside which the electrons move under the influence of the Lorentz force (playing the role of an external force). The potential difference arising at the ends of the conductor ensures the further movement of electrons along the external circuit.

What can be said about the work of the Lorentz force in this case? On the one hand, the Lorentz force is always perpendicular to the speed of the charge, and therefore it cannot do work. On the other hand, as we have just seen, it is thanks to the Lorentz force that a potential difference will arise at the ends of a conductor moving in a magnetic field; it turns out that the Lorentz force does work.

Let's try to understand this contradiction, for which we will consider in more detail the processes occurring inside the vehicle moving in a magnetic field; it turns out that the Lorentz force does work.

Let us introduce the coordinate axes $X$ and $Y$ (Fig. 8): the $X$ axis is directed along the movement of the conductor, and the $Y$ axis is directed upward along the conductor. Let us denote by $\vec{v}_x$ the speed of movement of the electron together with the conductor. As soon as the conductor began to move, Lorentz forces began to act on the electrons. Under their influence, electrons moved inside the conductor along the $Y$ axis at a speed $\vec{v}_y$. Resulting electron speed

$$\vec{u} = \vec{v}_x + \vec{v}_y.$$
