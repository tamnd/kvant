G. Komkin

Why is it bad to shout against the wind?

Why do we hear sound poorly if the wind blows from us to its source?
At first glance, this is clear. A thrown stone falls close against the wind, a bird flies slower... Let's try, however, to check the quantitative assessment.

Let the speed of sound relative to air be $c$, and the speed of wind $u$. Then the speed of the sound signal relative to the ground is $|c| - |u|$ and the time of its propagation over the distance $l$ is $t = \frac{l}{|c| - |u|}$. During this time, the signal travels a distance relative to air

$$l' = |c| t = \frac{l |c|}{|c| - |u|}.$$

The nominal value $l'$ determines the attenuation of sound: being at a distance $l$ from the sound source (against the wind), we hear the sound the same as if there was no wind and we were at a distance $l'$.

Reception $|u| = 15$ m/s (quite strong wind), $|c| = 330$ m/s, $l = 50$ m. Then $l' \approx 52$ m. The wind “pushes” us about 2 m. We, please, would not have noticed this. In reality, the influence of city wind is stronger. Therefore, we must look for another explanation.

Let us pay attention to the fact that the wind speed is not the same at different heights - it increases with increasing height above ground level. Everyone, this should not affect the propagation of sound in any way when the source and receiver are located at the same height. If the receiver (or source) of sound descends, part of the path the sound signal will pass through layers of air moving at a lower speed, and will arrive less attenuated. In fact, if a random (or shouting) person crouches, audibility will only worsen. And yet the point is precisely the dependence of wind speed on height.
