This figure shows an intricate but beautiful polyhedron - the union of the heel of dodecahedrons arranged, in a sense, "as symmetrically as possible." The vertices of these 5 dodecahedrons (there are 5×20=100 of them) fall into two types - single (there are 60 of them, three edges of our polyhedron are codent in them, and the vertices themselves are grouped in the form of 12 small pentagons) and double (there are 20=40:2 of them, each of them has 12 edges codensed). Try to find the path of the vertices of one face of one dodecahedron (two double, three single). Find on figure the remaining visible faces of this dodecahedron; find a face of some other dodecahedron. Make sure that any two dodecahedrons have exactly two common vertices and are formed from each other by rotating around the axis connecting these vertices. The model was designed by V. Gamayunov.
