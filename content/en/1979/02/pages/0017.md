About Newton's laws of motion.
Weight

The laws of mechanical motion of a body were discovered by Newton in the middle of the 17th century. They were set out in his book “Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy,” published in 1687. Without exaggeration, we can say that modern mechanics began with this book.

The basic ideas of mechanics and the laws of motion discovered by Newton will be discussed in this article.

A little about mechanics before Newton

In order to understand the essence of the revolution in the science of the motion of bodies that Newton accomplished, let us first get acquainted in the most general terms with the science of motion that existed before Newton.

The mechanics of Aristotle (384-322 BC) enjoyed the greatest popularity in pre-Dytomov times. We will include it here and tell you about it, although this theory turned out to be false in almost all its parts. But it was she who was replaced by Newtonian mechanics.

In his constructions and conclusions, Aristotle relied on observations and everyday experience. (On this basis, undoubtedly, science is built.) However, these observations were often superficial and were not accompanied by changes. Therefore, the conclusions drawn by Aristotle turned out to be incorrect.

Observations show, for example, that a cart moves on a level road only when it is pulled by a horse. This means that in order for a body to move (have speed), it needs to be influenced by another body. This influence of one body on another is called force. According to Aristotle, force is the cause of motion. In the absence of force, peace and only peace is possible.
