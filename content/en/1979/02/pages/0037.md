M501. Let us select from the sequence of stelenes of the triplets 3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729, 2187, 6561, ... all numbers starting with the number 9; let these numbers (no order) $3f^{(1)}, 3f^{(2)}, 3f^{(3)}, \dots$ (in particular, $f(1)=2$, as the first of the maximal numbers $3^2=9$).

a) Find $f(2)$ and $f(3)$—the numbers of the second and third maximum numbers.

b) Prove that the maximum value of $f(n)$ at $n>1$ satisfies the conditions:

(1) $f(n) - n$ odd;

(2) $\left|f(n) - \frac{n \lg 9}{1 - \lg 9}\right| < 1$,

and is uniquely determined by this number.

Let us first prove the following statement: for any $k>1$ there are two or three $k$-valued pillars of the triple, and if and only if there is a $k$-valued pillar of the triple; then $10^{k-1} \leq 3^m < 3 \cdot 10^{k-1}$. Hence $3 \cdot 10^{k-1} \leq 3^m+1 < 9 \cdot 10^{k-1}$, that is, a $3^{m+1} - k$-digit number that does not start with nine. Further, $3^{m+2} \geq 9 \cdot 10^{k-2}$, that is, a $3^{m+2} - k$-digit number, then it must begin with the number 9. (In any case, $3^{m+3} \geq 27 \cdot 10^{k-1}$ is not a $k$-digit number.)

Let now be the $3f(n) - k$-digit stem of the triple, starting from nine, that is

$$9 \cdot 10^{k-1} < 3f(n) < 10^k$$

(both inequalities are valid, since $3f(n)$ is an odd number). Let us calculate in two ways the number of stems of a triple, less than $3f(n)$. On the one hand, in addition to those $n-1$ stems that begin with the number 9, there are exactly two $k$-digit triplets for each $l=1,2, \dots, k$, that is, there are $2k+(n-1)$ in total. On the other hand, the number of stelenes of a triple, less than $3f(n)$, is equal to $f(n)$: these are the numbers $3^0, 3^1, \dots, 3^{f(n)-1}$. This means $2k+(n-1)=f(n)$, absence

$$k = \frac{f(n) - n+1}{2}$$

Since $k$ is a natural number, it follows that $f(n) - n$ is an odd number. Condition (1) to point c) has been proven.

From (*) and (**) we have

$$9 \cdot 10^{\frac{f(n) - n-1}{2}} < 3^f(n) < 10^{\frac{f(n) - n+1}{2}},$$

or

$$\lg 9 + \frac{f(n) - n-1}{2} < f(n) \lg 3 < \frac{f(n) - n+1}{2}.$$
