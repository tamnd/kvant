Physics
Oral exam assignment
1. A sled slides down a mountain $h=8$ m high along a slope $l=100$ m long. The mass of the sled with a center is $m=90$ kg. What is the average force of resistance to the movement of the sled if at the end of the descent it had a speed of $|\vec{v}|=11$ m/s?
2. The steel sedge of the projectile, falling from a height of $h=500$ m, had a speed of $|\vec{v}|=50$ m/s at the Earth’s surface. By how many degrees will the temperature of the sedge increase when it falls? Assume that the whole body is used to heat the sedge. The specific heat capacity of steel is $c=460$ J (kg K).
3. At what distance from the center of the nucleus is an electron in a hydrogen atom if its orbital speed is $v=2,2 \cdot 10^6$ m/s? What is the direction of the field created by the nucleus at the points of the orbit? The electron charge is $e=1,6 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C, the electron mass is $m=9,1 \cdot 10^{-31}$ kg.
4. An electric light bulb is lowered from the center of the raft into the water to a depth of $h=10$ m. What minimum dimensions (length and width) must the raft have so that not a single ray from a light bulb can cross the surface of the water? The absolute refractive index of water is $n=1,33$.

Leningrad Institute of Precision Mechanics and Optics

Modern instruments designed for research, measurement or control purposes are complex, complex devices. They combine the latest achievements of physics, electronics, optics, computer technology, experience and design principles, as well as technological developments of recent years.

In this regard, there is a need to train instrument engineers of a new type, who must have deep knowledge in the field of physiological science necessary for the design, research, organization and testing of information-based measuring systems. Established in 1930, the Leningrad Institute of Precision Mechanics and Optics (LITMO) is a leading technical instrument-making institution in the field, which produces research engineers, designers and technological instrument-making systems. The faculty has a large computer center based on EC series machines, as well as small-sized computers, in which all students of the institute are trained.

The country's largest optical faculty trains engineers in optical instrumentation: calculation and research of optical systems, design and technological instruments (astrodesic, navigation, photographic instruments, high-speed film equipment, television optical, hydrophotography equipment, etc.).

Graduates of the institute receive quasi-fiction as engineers corresponding to their specialty and work at instrument-making plants and in research institutes of various profiles.

The institute has two evening faculties and a preparatory department. Preparatory courses are organized for all applicants annually from October 1 to August 1.
