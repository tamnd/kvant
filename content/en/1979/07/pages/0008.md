reception With increasing temperature, the speed of thermal movement of atoms, molecules or ions of a substance increases, and this leads to an increase in the magnitude of fluctuations. As the fluctuation value increases, the intensity of molecular scattering increases. At the same time, scattering from crystal defects does not depend on temperature.
Increasing the temperature of the sample makes it possible to isolate molecular scattering in its “pure form.” It was this fact that Landsberg proposed to use.

However, MSU did not have spectral equipment capable of detecting small changes in frequency corresponding to the Mandelstam-Brilluzne effect. Mandelstam and Landsberg tried to get around this difficulty by using a comparative Greek spectrograph.

Surprisingly, the crudeness of the equipment played a positive role, leading to the discovery of a completely new, important phenomenon - Raman scattering of light. With Raman scattering - Raman scattering - a large change in frequency occurred, several orders of magnitude greater than the Mandelstam-Brilluzne effect. The sensitivity of the equipment required to observe the Mandelstam-Brilluzne effect would turn out to be too “delicate” for detecting Raman radiation.

However, the frequencies corresponding to these lines differed from the frequency of the incident light by a much greater amount than expected according to the theory of the Mandelstam-Brilluzne effect.
