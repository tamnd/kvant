Note the following: the sum of the magnitude of all polyhedral angles of these pairs with vertices at the point $A$ is equal to 1; the sum of the values ​​of all trihedral angles at the bases in pairs coincides with the sum of $\varphi$ of all polyhedral angles at the vertices of the polyhedron; the sum of the values ​​of dihedral angles at the base in pairs from our partition coincides with the values ​​$\varphi$ of all dihedral angles of the polyhedron. From here and from formula (4) we immediately obtain that $1 - \varphi + \bar{\varphi} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot n$ or

Note that the requirement for the convexity of the polyhedron is present only for simplicity of presentation. The reader can obtain generalizations in this direction on his own.

The theorema on the generalized sum of angles of a tetrahedron was first proved by de Guze in 1783, further generalizations were published by Brianchon in 1837.

A surprising property of formula (4) is that, unlike the plane case, this formula remains valid in non-Euclidean geometry.
