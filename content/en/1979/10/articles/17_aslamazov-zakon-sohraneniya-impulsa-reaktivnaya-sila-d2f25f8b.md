Law of conservation of momentum. Reactive force

One possible formulation of Newton's second law states that the change in momentum (momentum of motion) of a mechanical system is equal to the momentum of external forces:

$$\Delta \vec{P} = \vec{F}_{\text{внешш}} \Delta t,$$

(1)

where $\vec{P} = \sum m_i \vec{v}_i$ is the total momentum of the system, $\vec{F}_{\text{внешш}} = \sum \vec{F}_{j\text{внешш}}$ is the vector sum of external forces acting on the system.

For a closed (isolated) system $\vec{F}_{\text{внешш}} = 0$ and therefore,

$$\vec{P} = \text{const}$$

(2)

— the total impulse of the system is a constant value. This is the law of conservation of momentum. It allows you to find the final velocities of interacting bodies forming an isolated system, without going into the details of the interaction.

It should be remembered that equation (2) is vector, and the answer may significantly depend on the direction of the initial velocities of the bodies.

Problem 1. Along the main horizontal surface of motion, an underlying mass $M$ with a speed $v$ (Fig. 1). They shoot at it from a gun, and a bullet of mass $m$, traveling at a speed of $\vec{V}$, gets stuck in the shallows. What will be the shallow speed after a bullet hits it in the following case:

a) when the speed of the bullet is directed horizontally, what about the speed of the sandbank, b) when the speed of the bullet is directed vertically downwards?

Let us use the law of conservation of momentum in projection onto the direction of the initial initial motion of the body.

In case a) we get:

$$mV + Mv = (m + M) v_k,$$

where is the final speed of the cart?

$$v_k = \frac{mV + Mv}{m + M}.$$

As you can see, after the bullet hits, the speed of the cart increases.

In case b) the initial impulse of the bullet does not give a projection to the horizontal direction, therefore,

$$Mv = (m + M) v_k.$$

From here

$$v_k = \frac{Mv}{m + M}$$

— the final speed of the truck decreases.

The vector nature of the law of conservation of momentum in a number of cases leads to unexpected results.

Problem 6. On a horizontal surface, a vessel with water, closed with a lekitim movable frame, on which a soft load of mass M (Fig. 6) is placed, will be washed away. At the bottom of the vessel there is a deviation with cross-section s, through which water flows out. What is the steady-state velocity of the vessel if the friction force between the vessel and the surface is proportional to the velocity of the vessel, and the coefficient of proportionality is equal to k. Sectional area of ​​the vessel S.

The required speed (more precisely, its model v) is determined from the condition of equality of the models of the friction force and the reactive force acting on the vessel:

$$F_{tp} = kv = F_p.$$

To find the reactive force, let's go to the reporting system moving with speed v. In this system the vessel is not mobile. To determine the model u of the water outflow velocity, we will use the law of conservation of energy.

When a small amount of water with a mass Δm flows out, the change in the kinetic energy of the water Δmu²/2 is equal to the change in the potential energy of the water Δmgh (h is the height of the water level into the vessel) and the load MgΔh (Δh is the movement of the load equal to the change in the water level):

$$\frac{\Delta mu^2}{2} = \Delta mgh + Mg\Delta h.$$

The change in water level into the vessel Δh is expressed through the mass Δm of flowing water: Δh = Δm/ρS. Substituting this expression into the law of conservation of energy and reducing by Δm, for the speed u of water outflow we obtain

$$u = \sqrt{2gh + 2Mg/ρS}.$$

If the load is heavy enough (that is, its mass is much greater than the mass of water in the vessel: M >>ρSh), the first term under the root can be neglected. In this case, the flow rate of water is almost constant and equal to

$$u = \sqrt{2Mg/ρS}.$$

Now you can find the reactive force acting on the vessel:

$$F_p = u \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} = u \frac{\rho su \Delta t}{\Delta t} = \rho su^2 = \frac{2Mgs}{S},$$

and the speed of the vessel:

$$v = \frac{F_p}{k} = \frac{2Mgs}{kS}.$$

$$Mv = (M - km) v'_k + km (v - u),$$

$$v'_k = v + \frac{kmu}{M - km}.$$
