Your teacher's holiday

The Party and the Soviet government highly value the tireless, fruitful work of teachers and express deep gratitude for their selfless work.

From the greetings of the CPSU Central Committee, the Predium of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR, the Council of Ministers of the USSR to the All-Union Congress of Teachers.

In October, our country solemnly celebrates a traditional holiday - Soviet Teacher's Day. This holiday is a day of flowers and smiles, congratulatory words and best wishes to students by their mentor and teacher. The editorial collection and editors of the magazine «Kvant» sincerely join in the pace of congratulations addressed to the weak artillery intelligentsia, the school has reached the main task of today - the completion of the program of universal compulsory secondary education, the education of a harmonious personality of an active person in the society of socialist development. During 1918–1940, about 1.5 million people graduated from high school, and during 1976–1978, more than 8.9 million people completed secondary education. Think about these numbers! They contain a vivid description of the unprecedented growth of public education in the USSR.

We have already become accustomed to the successes of the Soviet school; its achievements have become natural and logical for us. This is a huge achievement of socialism - the right to education is inseparable from our existence, it is impossible to imagine our children and youth without school, without knowledge. The reality of the capitalist world presents a different picture. According to foreign press estimates, in capital countries about 300 million school-age children do not attend school. And here, for example, is the recognition of the agency press: in the country, out of every four students, one is forced to leave primary school without even finishing the second grade; Of all the students, only half manage to complete primary education. Cause? The difficult financial situation of working families, which does not allow their children to attend school.

The role of a teacher in our life is difficult to overestimate. After all, it is before his eyes, under his direct influence, that the most crucial period of a person’s life passes - the period of emergence, the formation of personality. The birth of every new citizen of the Country of Soviets, a worker and a creator, is to a large extent inseparable from the painstaking, selfless work of a teacher. That is why a teacher, a pedagogue, enjoys the respect of all Soviet people, and his professional holiday has become a national concept.

But the holiday of our teachers is not only a day of congratulations and wishes, no matter how sincere they may be. On this day, each student must especially clearly and clearly answer the main question for himself - are you doing everything to make the work of a teacher more effective, easier, and joyful? The results of a teacher’s work depend not only on his talent, knowledge, experience - they depend on the students, on their desire to learn, on their awareness and understanding of the discipline, on their will and ability to sometimes do not what they want, but what is necessary.

Taking place in May this year. The IV Plenum of the Komsomol Central Committee, in its resolution “On the successful work of the Komsomol in the communist education of students in secondary schools and preparing them for work,” paid special attention to the need to “instill in students the concept that obtaining a universal education is a civic duty and a vital need of every young Soviet person.” This first of all means that the student cannot be a passive party in the process of learning and upbringing. He must actively promote the creation of an atmosphere of creativity and interest in the field of knowledge, responding to cases of indiscipline, striving for a deep internal assimilation of Marxist-Leinist ideology and communist morality.

The editorial board and the editors of "Quant" today address special congratulations to our dear colleagues - a teacher of mathematics and physics. It is to them that millions of schoolchildren owe their knowledge in these subjects. And it is to them that thousands and thousands of schoolchildren owe their love for these sciences and their first successes. To notice the student’s abilities, to awaken his interest in the subject, to guide his further studies, to help transform interest in the subject into a choice of profession - how much work, knowledge, and pedagogical skill all this requires!

Many modest school teachers of mathematics and physics made an invaluable contribution to the development of these sciences - they helped talented youth find recognition and be accepted into greater education. And there is no more pleasant, higher reward for a teacher’s work than the achievements of his student in mathematics or physics.

Not much time will pass and today's high school students - the teacher of "Kvaent" - will graduate from school and enter the broad path of independent life. Most of them will choose a specialty that is somehow related to physics and mathematics. We can confidently say that they turn out to be worthy students of their teachers.
