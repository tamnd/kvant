M531. They did not leave points A and B at the same time, heading towards the other automatically and in magnitude. They met at point C, they could turn around and the second one would head off at point D; here they again turned around and m. d. At what point on the segment AB did their 1978th ​​meeting take place?

For lengths 3, 7, 9 and 21. To advance in solving the problem on a torus, a similar problem on a circle of n cells helps (a similar problem M19 was analyzed in «Kvant», 1970, No. 4).

R. Izmailov

◆ This problem is best solved using motion graphs. The constancy of the speed of motion of a point means that its motion graph forms the same angle with the time axis t (Fig. 4). Let the moments of meetings be t₁, t₂, t₃, ... It is clear that both motion graphs in the sections t₁ < t ≤ t₂ and t₂ ≤ t < t₃ are symmetrical with respect to the straight line t = t₂, so the third meeting will occur at the same point C as the first. In the same way, the straight line t = t₃ is the axis of symmetry of motion graphs for t ≤ t₃ and t ≥ t₃; therefore, the fourth meeting will occur at the same point D as the second, and so on: every odd meeting will occur at point C, every even one, including the 1978th, at point D.

From our solution it is also clear that the numbers t₁, t₂, t₃, ... form an arithmetic progression: meetings occur at equal intervals d = tₙ₁ + tₙ₂, and the whole picture is completely repeated with a period T = 2d.

This is observed - a special case of a useful fact: if the graph of the function y = f(x) is symmetrical with respect to the lines x = a and x = b (that is, f(a + x) = f(a - x) and f(b + x) = f(b - x)), then the function f is periodic with a period of 2|b - a| (in our problem the role of x is played by t).

N. Vasiliev

◆ The diagonal of a convex hexagon is considered equal if there are two vertices of the hexagon on both sides of the straight line on which this diagonal lies.
