Let's use the law of conservation of momentum. It is more convenient to write it in a reporting system moving with the initial speed of the rocket $v$ (since the fuel ejection speed $u$ is specified relative to the rocket). In projection onto the direction of motion of the rocket we get
$$0 = (M - m) v_{1p} - mu,$$
where does the speed of the rocket come from?
$$v_{1p} = \frac{mu}{M - m}.$$
In a stationary reporting system, the speed of the rocket after the release of the first portion of fuel is equal in magnitude to
$$v_1 = v + v_{1p} = v + \frac{mu}{M - m}.$$
We will consider the release of the second portion of fuel in a system moving at a speed $v_1$ (at figure 7 the fuel release is shown in a stationary reporting system). From the law of conservation of momentum we have
$$v_{2p} = \frac{mu}{M - 2m},$$
and in a stationary system
$$v_2 = v_1 + v_{2p} = v + v_{1p} + v_{2p} =$$
$$= v + \frac{mu}{M - m} + \frac{mu}{M - 2m}.$$
It is easy to see that after $k$ emissions the rocket speed will be equal to
$$v_k = v + \frac{mu}{M - m} + \frac{mu}{M - 2m} + \dots + \frac{mu}{M - km} = v + \sum_{l=1}^{k} \frac{mu}{M - lm}.$$
For comparison, let us also find the speed of the rocket $v'_k$ with a one-time release of fuel of mass $km$ with the same speed $u$ relative to the rocket. To do this, we will again use the law of conservation of momentum, but we will write it immediately relative to the motionless reporting system:
$$Mv = (M - km) v'_k + km (v - u),$$
where
$$v'_k = v + \frac{kmu}{M - km}.$$
It is easy to see that $v'_k > v_k$. This result is associated with the assumption that the rate of fuel ejection from a rocket in a stationary reporting system is constant and equal to $v - u$. In reality, as the rocket takes root, the rate of fuel ejection decreases (the ejection speed relative to the rocket is constant). Therefore, the first formula for $v_k$ more accurately describes the real signal.
