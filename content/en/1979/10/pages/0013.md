The shorter the adducted length of the leg for the same length (practically, the thinner the legs are made from bottom to bottom and the faster their thickness decreases from top to bottom), the greater the resonant frequency of the leg’s oscillations and the higher the speed they can develop under the same conditions. On the other hand, the lighter the legs, the less effort is required from the mouse leg to move at the same speed (since the force is proportional to the weight of the leg). That is why all fast animals (antelope, cheetahs, racehorses, greyhounds) have very thin legs and relatively light legs.
