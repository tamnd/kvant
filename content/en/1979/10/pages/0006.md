Generalized sum of angles of a polyhedron

In Euclidean geometry, the theorem on the sum of the angles of a plane polygon occupies an important place, so the question naturally arises about the spatial analysis of this theorem. This article describes such analysis and related concepts.

Measure of a $n$-faceted angle

We will study plane and polyhedral angles as follows. Let $AOB$ be an arbitrary angle on the plane. Let us construct a circle of radius $r > 0$ with a center at point $O$ and take the meter of angle $AOB$ as the ratio of the length of the arc $A'mB'$ located inside the angle $AOB$ to the length of the entire circle (Fig. 1).

With this study of angles, for example, a meter of an angle of one degree is equal to $\frac{1}{360}$, a meter of an angle of l radians is equal to $\frac{1}{2}$, and the well-known theorems on the sum of the angles of a triangle and the sum of the angles of a convex $n$-gon are formulated with correspondence as follows: the magnitude of the sum of the angles of a triangle is equal to $\frac{1}{2}$ and the magnitude of the sum of angles of a convex $n$-gon is equal $\frac{n}{2} - 1$.
