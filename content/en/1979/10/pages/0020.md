Fig. 5. Plasma can be heated to enormous temperatures only if it is of a high degree of purity. Further, ten fractions of a percent of heavy elements (Mo, Cr, Fe) that enter the plasma from the walls of the chamber do not allow the thermonuclear reaction to “ignite”. At the Tokamak-4 installation, research is being carried out on the preliminary cleaning of the chamber walls by heating it to a temperature of 800-1000°C. At the Tokamak-12 installation (see Fig. 6), a special magnetic field configuration has been created, which allows contaminated plasma to be removed from the surface of the plasma filament into a special device - a diverter, where it is deposited on the walls. This protects the deeper plasma elephants from contamination. The trajectories of motion of plasma particles in the divertor are visible on figure, placed on the first page of the cover.

Fig. 6. In order for a thermonuclear reactor to produce a significant amount of energy, it is necessary not only to heat the plasma to a gigantic temperature, but also to maintain such a temperature until a noticeable fraction of atomic nuclei reacts. The lifetime of hot plasma depends on the geometry of the chamber. This dependence is studied at the Tokamak-12 installation, where the plasma cord has an oval shape in its cross-section.
