In order for a rough surface to reflect light specularly, it is necessary that the reduced intensity of the reflected light does not occur due to the interference of waves reflected from different points on the surface. For this, the path difference $\Delta$ of rays reflected at the same angle from different points on the surface must be much less than the wavelength.

Obviously, the beam reflected from the surface and from the top of the roughness has the greatest difference in path (points $A$ and $B$ on figure 13). Let us denote $\alpha$ the angle of incidence and $\beta$ the angle at which reflection from the surface is observed. Drawing a perpendicular $AC$ from point $A$ to the incident rays, we find point $C$ on ray 2, at which the phase of the wave coincides with the phase at point $A$ ($AC$ is the front of the incident wave). Drawing a perpendicular from point $B$ to the reflected rays, we find point $D$, at which the phase of the reflected wave coincides with the phase at point $B$ ($BD$ is the front of the reflected wave). The path difference $\Delta$ of rays 1 and 2 is $|AD| - |CB|$. Carrying out calculations, we find (see Fig. 13):

$$\Delta = a (\sin \beta - \sin \alpha) + h (\cos \beta + \cos \alpha).$$

The reflection will be specular if $\Delta \ll \lambda$. Since the distances $a$ between the depressions and protrusions on the reflecting surface can be completely arbitrary (both small and large), then to satisfy condition $\Delta \ll \lambda$ the first term in (*) must be equal to zero. This means that specular reflection is possible only if $\alpha = \beta$, that is, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

In this case, $\Delta = 2h \cos \alpha$, and the reflection will be specular if $2h \cos \alpha \ll \lambda$, that is, $h \ll \frac{\lambda}{2 \cos \alpha}$. (Note. Since at $\alpha \rightarrow \frac{\pi}{2} \cos \alpha \rightarrow 0$, at large angles of incidence ($\frac{\pi}{2}$), a rough surface with large roughnesses also becomes mirror-like.)

At $\lambda = 5 \cdot 10^{-8}$ m and $\alpha = 45^\circ h \ll 3,6 \cdot 10^{-8}$ m.
