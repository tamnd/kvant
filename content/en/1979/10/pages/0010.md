Running, walking and physics

Can a person tied hand and foot run quickly “untied”? Maybe! Don't believe me? Let's find out...

From the point of view of the theory of oscillations, human running in the coordinate system associated with the runner is a simple dangling of the legs. Thus, when running and walking, they act as pendulums, connected by a hinge to the body and oscillating in the gravity field around their equilibrium position - the vertical position. Hohora is not a “weight on a weightless string”, not a mathematical pendulum that is studied in school: the mass of the leg is not concentrated at one point, but is distributed along the entire leg. Hohora is a physical mint.

The most important characteristic of any pendant is the frequency of its free vibrations. For a mathematical pendulum, as is known, the frequency of free vibrations is equal to $v_0 = \sqrt{g/l}$ and depends only on the length $l$ of the pendulum. In a physical pendulum, in contrast to a mathematical one, the frequency of free oscillations depends not only on its length, but also on the distribution of masses along the pendulum: the more the masses are located towards the axis of oscillation of the pendulum, the higher the frequency of its free oscillations. However, for a physical pendulum, the frequency can also be written as $v_0 = \sqrt{g/L}$, where $L$ is a very important characteristic of the pendulum - its reduced line. This is a mathematical pendant that has the same frequency as a given physical one.
