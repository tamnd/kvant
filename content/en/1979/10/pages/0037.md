The change in internal energy of the system is $\Delta U = \sigma \Delta s + \Delta u$. Here $\sigma \Delta s$ is the change in surface energy due to a change in the surface area of ​​the bubbles by $\Delta s$ (σ is the surface tension of water); $\Delta u$ - change in internal energy of air.

The internal energy of air as a result of the merging of bubbles changes by the following values:

$$\Delta u = \frac{5}{2} \frac{m}{\mu} R \Delta T,$$

where $m$ is the mass of air in the bubbles; $\Delta T$—change in air temperature. (Air consists mainly of diatomic molecules of nitrogen and oxygen, so it can be considered a diatomic gas. The internal energy of one mole of a diatomic gas is $5/2 RT$.)

The pressure $p$ of air in the bubbles in our case remains practically constant. Indeed, the pressure $p$ differs from the pressure in water at the bubble level by values ​​$\frac{2\sigma}{r} (r = r_1 = r_2)$ - in the original bubble and by values ​​$\frac{2\sigma}{\rho}$ - in a new bubble of radius $\rho$ ($\rho > r$). Since fusion occurs at the surface of the water, we can assume that the pressure in the water is equal to atmospheric pressure, that is, equal to $p_0 \approx 10^5$ H/$m^2$. Since $\frac{2\sigma}{r} = \frac{2 \cdot 7,3 \cdot 10^{-2}}{3 \cdot 10^{-3}} \approx 49$ (H/$m^2$) «$p_0$, we can assume that the pressure in the bubbles remains unchanged and equal to $p_0$. Therefore, according to the equation of gas state, $p_0 \Delta V = \frac{m}{\mu} R \Delta T$, hence $\Delta T = p_0 \Delta V \frac{\mu}{mR}$. Thus,

$$A = p_0 \Delta V = c \Delta s + \frac{5}{2} p_0 \Delta V,$$

or $\sigma \Delta s = -\frac{3}{2} p_0 \Delta V$.

Substituting $\Delta V = \frac{4}{3} \pi (\rho^3 - 2r^3)$ and $\Delta s = 4\pi (\rho^2 - 2r^2)$ into this expression, we get:

$$p_0 \rho^3 + 2\sigma \rho^2 - 2p_0 r^3 - 4\sigma r^2 = 0,$$

or, taking into account numerical data,

$$10^5 \rho^3 + 14,6 \cdot 10^{-2} \rho^2 = 5,4 \cdot 10^{-3}.$$

Since $\rho > 3 \cdot 10^{-3}$ m is introduced, the second term on the left side of the equality is small compared to the first and can be neglected. That's why

$$\rho \approx \sqrt[3]{5,4 \cdot 10^{-8}} \approx 3,8 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{ м},$$

that is, $\rho \approx 3,8$ mm.
