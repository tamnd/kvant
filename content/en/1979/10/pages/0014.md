⟦folio 12⟧

The question about the dependence of the resonant part of the leg oscillations on the acceleration of gravity is far from academic in nature: after all, each planet has its own acceleration of gravity.

The second method of solving problems is incomparably superior. If the potential energy stored during each lifting of the leg is not enough to impart the required speed of oscillatory movement to norte, then by tying the legs with an elastic rubber cord or attaching elastic elements to the legs in some other way, we strive to return the leg to a position of equilibrium when the deviation from this position is made, we will thereby increase the potential energy accumulated (due to kinetic) when lifting the leg. As a result, the useless additional load on the muscles associated with the need to overcome the inertia of the legs when walking and running will be reduced. The elastic elements attached to the legs will act as reservoirs of potential energy, replenished and emptied by the movement during each period of oscillation, and, as in all oscillatory systems, they will increase the resonant part of the oscillations. The greater the elasticity of these elements (for example, than just the rubber cord connecting the legs), the higher the resonant part. This means that in order to walk and run faster, you need... to tie your legs (of course, with an elastic connection).

One side of the German fairy tale tells of a man who could run extremely fast. To walk at a normal speed, he tied weights to his legs. Skorod knew what he was doing. After all, by doing so, he increased the reduced length of the leg, and, consequently, decreased their resonant part of the oscillations, and with it the speed of movement. But if he attached elastic elements to his legs together with weights, he would run even faster. However, one might suspect that the secret of the fast beta walker lies precisely in the fact that his legs had a higher resonant part of the oscillations than those of other people.

Any healthy person can become a walker by increasing the resonant part of the oscillations with the help of elastic elements.

Let's imagine such a person taking part in athletics. Before the start, he ties his legs and, in order to somehow explain his strange behavior, he modestly tells the judges that he runs very fast and, in order not to run too far from his rivals, has a common practice of slightly tying his legs. Having taken his place at the start, our athlete takes one leg back with some force, thereby “charging” it with potential energy. As soon as at the moment of start he lifts this leg off the ground, the hora “shoots” forward, like from a slingshot. By the way, this also reduces the acceleration time. Of course, the runner with his legs tied will come to the physicist first. But it will be more difficult for him to stop, and he will run a few extra steps: after all, his legs tied with an elastic band “run on their own.” By the way, if it were consistent to the end, then in order to facilitate beta, elastic elements should also be attached to the arms, which, as already noted, play a certain role when walking and running. This can be done, for example, by tying the arms (higher hands) to the waist with elastic bands. The general choice is this: if a person is “tied” hand and foot, he will run faster.
