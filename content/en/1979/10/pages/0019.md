Fig. 3. At the Tokamak-3 installation, another method of additional heating of hydrogen plasma is being studied - using high-frequency electromagnetic oscillations. As calculations and experiments show, the energy of such oscillations ultimately turns into the kinetic energy of part of the plasma.

Fig. 4. In the windings of electromagnets that create an insulating magnetic field, a lot of energy is lost, which is spent on heating the wires. In the Tokamak-7 installation, superconductor windings are tested, which are cooled to a liquid gel temperature of 4.2 K. In superconducting windings, there is no energy loss due to heating of the wires. Therefore, they will also be used in future fusion power plants. In size, Tokamak-7 is close to Tokamak-10.
At figure only the main part of it is raised - a torid chamber with a superconducting winding.
