Experiments with water in the cold

Why do water pipes often burst and water bottles burst in winter? Why do icicles appear on the roofs of houses with the arrival of spring, and why do ridges sometimes fall to the ground along with the rain in the summer? Why does the water in deep rivers and lakes not freeze until the day?

A lot of similar waters can be formed, because freezing water has many features. You can get acquainted with some of them during experiments with water in the cold.

All the experiments offered to you are very simple; they do not require any special preparation. However, we give some advice. It is better to conduct experiments at a temperature no higher than -5°C. Remember that cylindrical glass or plastic containers with water can burst in the cold, so whenever possible, use finite-shaped containers. In some experiments you will come across a saturated solution of boiled salt. It is better to prepare it in advance by dissolving 30 g of boiled salt in 100 ml of warm water.

Having completed the experiment, he independently identifies the observed phenomena and answers all questions that arise. If you cannot do this, read the answers and explanations in the article.

So, you have to conduct the following experiments:

1. In a cold, unheated room (for example, in a barn, closet, garage) or on a balcony, place a glass plate on a table or other horizontal surface, put a glass, turn it upside down, an overturned saucer and, finally, a large basin. Using a narrow tube, drop a few drops of water onto the plate and fill the indentations in the stem of the glass and saucer with water, pour 2-3 grains of water into the basin.

When the water freezes, look closely at the surface of the ice. You will see that the frozen drops have become sharpened, and cone-shaped tubercles have formed in the middle of the recesses of the glass and saucer (Fig. 1). And in Tauu a historical ice mound grew.

Three experiments on the same topic give different results.

Why?

When water freezes, it expands greatly, which is why a bump forms on the surface of the ice. Water rasters expand very slightly when hardening, their surface remains smooth. Paraffin, when transitioning from a liquid to a solid state, is skimmed, as a result, a vulva appears.

2. For the experiment you will need a thin-walled glass vessel. This can be, for example, a cylinder of an incandescent light bulb without a base (the base can be removed if you carefully cut the glass at its border with the metal with a triangular needle file, while the light bulbs must be turned all the time).

Fill the container with water to its maximum horizontal diameter and allow it to freeze.

You will see a very beautiful picture (Fig. 3). The edge of the formed ice ingot is in the main space, only here and there air bubbles of various shapes and sizes are interspersed into the ice, and in the middle of the ingot there is an opaque clot, reminiscent of a hedgehog curled up in a ball.

First tint the water with green paint (you can buy it at the pharmacy) and carry out the same experiment again.

After the tinted water freezes, the picture will become even redder. Along the edges the ice will remain random and uncolored, while in the middle part the color will thicken greatly, so that the “hedgehog” will appear green.

How can all this be explained?

Obviously the water begins to freeze on its surface and near the walls.

Freezing then gradually spreads toward the center of the water volume, where many directional ice crystals form. This will create an opaque clot of fused crystals, which scatters light in all directions.

As we have already said, when water hardens, its volume increases. There will be voids inside the ice. The smallest air bubbles, invisible to the eye, but always present in the water, merge and form larger ones. They are clearly visible where ice will occur.

Colored water behaves similarly to pure water. When transitioning from a liquid to a solid state, it

3. Take a glass tube with a diameter of 7-15 mm and plug one end of it with a rubber stopper. (You can use a cylinder from the “Ognivo” device, which you have in the school physics room. The screw bottom needs to be removed and the counters closed with a stopper.) Secure the tube in a vertical position with the stopper down (for example, lock it in the leg of a tripod). Fill the resulting vessel to the brim with water, and cover the sides with a circle of thick paper.

When water freezes, the resulting column of ice will rise along with the paper circle (Fig. 4, a).

Do the same experiment with an inverted vessel, that is, with the cork at the top and the paper circle at the bottom. In this case, the column of ice will fall along with the circle (Fig. 4, b).

Now the change from the last experience is somewhat visible. Pour a column of water into the vessel so that its level reaches about 1 cm to the edge. Close the hole with a circle and turn the vessel over (Fig. 4, c). After the water freezes, you will see that the ice column will not fall, but rise, approaching the traffic jam.

What conclusions can be drawn?

When water freezes, it expands, and it expands in all directions. But the “bottom” and walls of the vessel resist movement, so the ice column moves only in one direction - to where the pressure is least.

4. Take two glasses out into the cold. Pour clean water into one, and a saturated solution of boiled cols into the other. For the experiment you will need two icicles. (They can be “grown” artificially. For example, like this: freeze water in a narrow glass or metal tube closed at one end. To remove the formed icicle, slightly heat the tube.)
