XIII All-round
Olympiad for schoolchildren

N. Rozov, M. Smolyansky

Olimpida
in mathematics

From April 11 to 18, the final stage of the XIII All-Union Olympiad in Mathematics for schoolchildren was held in Tbilisi. It enrolls 152 schoolchildren: 39 eighth-graders, 54 ninth-graders and 59 tenth-graders.

Teams of all fraternal resource workers of our Motherland gathered in the capital of Georgia. The team consists of the winners of the Resursican Olympics, zonal Olympics of the RSFSR, Moscow and Leningrad Olympics, as well as guys who were awarded I and II degree diplomas at the previous Olympics (Tashkent, 1978). For the third time, a team of students from vocational schools - winners of the Leningrad Vocational School Olympida - competed in the final stage of the All-Russian Mathematics Olympiad. A separate team of Tbilisi schoolchildren represented the city hosting the Olympics.

From the very first minutes of their stay on Georgian soil, all teams were surrounded by care and attention. Representatives of the party and Soviet organizations of the Olympidad and mathematicians, Komsomol members and schoolchildren of Tbilisi did everything possible to ensure that the mathematics section was successful. And it will not be an exaggeration to say that organizationally the Olympidad in Tbilisi was one of the best.

On April 12, students of the Olympidade in a solemn atmosphere placed flowers at the monument to V.I. Lenin. Then, the official opening of the final stage took place in the Resursikan creator of puners and schoolchildren named after B. Dzneladze. The chairman of the organizing committee, the Minister of Education of the GSSR, Professor O. D. Kinkladze, warmly welcomed the guests of Georgia and cordially congratulated the audience on the start of the event. The Chairman of the Council of Ministers of the GSSR O. E. Cherkezia, the President of the Academy of Sciences of the GSSR Academician E. K. Khoradze, the Secretary of the Central Committee of the Komsomol of Georgia M. P. Tsiklauri and others wished great success in the Olympidade and in further pursuits of their favorite science.

Olimpidad presents a short-term work on preparing assignments for students. For several months, the methodological commission of the central organizing committee of the All-Russian Olympiad for schoolchildren carefully studied the long list of “candidate” problems, compiled according to the proposals of mathematicians from different cities of our country, and meticulously selected the most original and non-standard problems from it.

So that readers of the journal can form a holistic impression of the characteristics of the problem, their conditions are given in full below. All these tasks are included in the Quanta Problem Book - the corresponding number under which the task is placed there is indicated in parentheses after the text of the condition. The Quanta Problem Book will contain solutions to all problems.

As usual, the final stage of the Olympics was held in two rounds. The first round took place on April 13. The Olympidad participants were given three problems each, and it took four hours to solve them.

First round problems

8th grade

1. What is the smallest value that can be the ratio of the areas of two isosceles right triangles, the vertices of one of which lie on three different sides of the other? (M566)

2. The kangaroo jumps along the angle $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$ of the coordinate plane $Oxy$ as follows: from point $(x; y)$ the kangaroo can jump to point $(x+1; y-1)$ or to point $(x-5; y+7)$, and jumping to points where one of the coordinates is negative is not allowed. From what initial points $(x; y)$ can a kangaroo not reach a point located at a distance of more than 1000 from the origin? Draw the set of all such points $(x; y)$ and find its area. (M572)

3. In parliament, each member has no more than three enemies. Prove that parliament can be divided into two chambers so that each parliamentarian in the same chamber with him will have no more than one enemy. (It is considered that if $A$ is the enemy of $B$, then $B$ is the enemy of $A$.) (M580)

1. See problem No. 1 for 8th grade.
2. There are several numbers written in the terradi. It is allowed to add to the numbers already written any number equal to the average of the arthmetic length or several of them, as long as it is different from all those already written. Prove that, starting with two numbers 0 and 1, using such additions you can get:
   a) number 1/5;
   b) any rational number between 0 and 1. (M569)
   c. see task No. 3 for 8th grade.

2. See problem No. 2 for 9th grade.

Second round problems

8th grade

4. On the plane of several pairs $(A; B)$ of these points, vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ are taken, and in such a way that at each point there are as many vectors that begin as there are endings at it. Prove that the sum of all vectors taken is 0. (M576)

5. What is the smallest counting number that needs to be placed on the squares of a chessboard with dimensions
   a) $8 \times 8$ cells;
   b) $n \times n$ cells
   so that on each straight line passing through the center of an arbitrary field and parallel to some side or diagonal of the board, there should be at least one piece? (Chips are placed in the centers of the fields.) (M577)

6. Find $x$ and $y$ from the system of equations
   $$\begin{cases} \frac{x-y}{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}} = a, \\ \frac{y-x}{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}} = b \end{cases}$$
   $(a$ and $b$ are given numbers). (M578)

7. There are several squares whose sum of areas is 4. Prove that such squares can always cover a square of area 1. (M570)

9th grade

4. Prove that for any numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belonging to the interval [0; 1], the inequality holds
$$x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n + 1)^2 \geq 4 (x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n).$$ (M579)

5. The natural numbers $p$ and $q$ are coprime. Segment [0; 1] is divided into $p+q$ identical segments. Prove that each of these segments, except the two extreme ones, contains exactly one of the $p+q-2$ numbers
$$\frac{1}{p}, \frac{2}{p}, \dots, \frac{p-1}{p},$$
$$\frac{1}{q}, \frac{2}{q}, \dots, \frac{q-1}{q}.$$ (M567)

6. Through the point $O$ there are 1979 lines $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_{1979}$ in space, no two of which are mutually perpendicular. An arbitrary point $A_1$, different from $O$, is taken on the line $l_1$. Prove that it is possible to choose points $A_k \in l_k$, $k=2, 3, \dots, 1979$ such that the following 1979 pairs of lines are mutually perpendicular:
$$(A_1 A_3) \perp l_2, (A_2 A_4) \perp l_3, \dots,$$
$$(A_{k-1} A_{k+1}) \perp l_k, \dots, (A_{1977} A_{1979}) \perp l_{1978},$$
$$(A_{1978} A_1) \perp l_{1979}, (A_{1979} A_2) \perp l_1.$$ (M573)

7. A finite sequence $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ of the numbers 0 and 1 must satisfy the following condition: for any number $k$ from 0 to $n-1$ the sum
$$a_1 a_{k+1} + a_2 a_{k+2} + \dots + a_n - k a_n$$ is an odd number.
a) Come up with such a sequence for $n=25$.
b) Prove that such a sequence exists for some $n > 1000$. (M574)

10th grade

4. See task No. 2 6 for 8th grade.
5. See task No. 2 5 for 8th grade.
6. The convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ is cut by its diagonals into four triangles. Prove that if the radii of all four circles inscribed in these triangles are equal to each other, then the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a rhombus. (M568)
7. On a straight line, the points $A_0, A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ are arranged in order so that the lengths of the segments $|A_0 A_1|, |A_1 A_2|, \dots, |A_n-1 A_n|$ do not exceed 1. It is required to mark $k-1$ from the point $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n-1$ in red so that the lengths of the two $k$ parts on which the segment $A_0 A_n$ is divided by red dots differ by no more than 1. Prove that this can always be done
a) at $k=3$;
b) for every natural number $k < n$. (M575)

On April 16, schoolchildren were told the solutions to problems of the second type, and then an individual analysis began - each Olympada participant had the opportunity to discuss their work in detail with the jury members (to file an appeal).
