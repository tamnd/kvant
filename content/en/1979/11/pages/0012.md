⟦folio 10⟧

B. Novozhilov

Thermal explosion

In January 1953, the screw steamer Tyrrhenya with a displacement of four thousand tons exploded and sank in the Red Sea. In the history of maritime shipping, larger disasters are also known - explosions and fires on tankers and ships transporting ammunition. But the Tyrrhenya was, at first glance, carrying a cargo that was harmless from an explosive point of view - ammonium nitrate. If this were the only fact, then the cause of the explosion could be sought among random circumstances. However, the Lloyd's Register of Shipping, which records all major maritime disasters, lists several ten fires and explosions associated with ammonium nitrate. They became more common in the forties and fifties, when the rate of industrial production of this substance increased sharply. The year 1947 turned out to be a record year - during the loading of ships in various ports there were three powerful explosions, which led to a large number of casualties.

Meanwhile, ammonium nitrate (ammonium nitrate, or ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃) has long been used in large quantities in technology and agriculture. Its annual production is ten million tons. It is enough to look at the chemical formula of this compound to tell where it can be used. Ammonium nitrate contains a lot of nitrogen, and it is bound into the two best ions absorbed by plants—ammonium and nitrate. Therefore, ammonium nitrate is one of the most valuable mineral fertilizers. And the presence of a large amount of oxygen provides a second, not always peaceful, use of ammonium nitrate as an oxidizing agent in the production of gunpowder and explosives. Without organic impurities and metals, ammonium nitrate does not explode. It is often used in chemical demonstration experiments. A mixture of NH₄NO₃ with tropyl or aluminum is a strong explosive.

Let's try to figure out why a completely safe mineral fertilizer - ammonium nitrate, which can be stored in large quantities for years in warehouses or in the open air, subsequently became the cause of tragedies at sea.
