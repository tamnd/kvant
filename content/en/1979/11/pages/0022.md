Interaction of rings

Undoubtedly, the most interesting are the experiments on studying the interaction of external wheels. We conducted experiments with rings both in water and in air.

Let a drop of ink from a height of 1-2 cm into a vessel with water, and after seconds let another drop, but from a height of 2-3 cm. Two vortices are formed into the vessel, moving at different speeds: the second is faster than the first ($v_2 > v_1$). When the rings are at the same height, they will begin to interact with each other.

It turns out that three cases are possible. The first case is that the second ring burns the first without touching it (Fig. 9, a). The following happens. Firstly, the flow of water from both rings seems to push the rings away from each other. Secondly, a transfer of ink from the first ring to the second is detected: the water flows of the second ring are more intense, and they carry the ink along with them. Sometimes some of this ink passes through the second ring, which entails the formation of a new small ring. Then the rings begin to be made, then we were unable to notice anything interesting.

The second case is when ring 2, when burned, touches ring 1 (Fig. 9, b). As a result, more intense flows of the second ring destroy the first. As a rule, new small winrys are formed from the ink clot remaining from the first ring.
