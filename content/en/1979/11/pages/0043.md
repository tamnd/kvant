⟦folio 41⟧

Problems can be solved more easily

Those preparing to build universities are asked beforehand:

— Can entrance exams in mathematics offer tasks for which you need to know theorems that are not covered by the school curriculum?
- No, they can’t!

Hearing such an answer, one of my academic classics objected, showing I. Gabovich’s article “Vectors help on the exam” («Kvant», 1979, No. 1):

- Not true. After all, formulas

$$\overrightarrow{BD} = \frac{n}{m+n} \overrightarrow{BA} + \frac{m}{m+n} \overrightarrow{BC},$$

$$\overrightarrow{MN} = \frac{n}{m+n} \overrightarrow{AC} + \frac{m}{m+n} \overrightarrow{BD}$$

They don’t study them at school, but tasks on the application of these formulas are offered at Moscow State University, and at MIPT, and at the Kiev Polytechnic...

Unfortunately, many readers «Kvant» think so and are mistaken.

From the fact that some problems can be solved using some formulas, it does not follow that they cannot be solved without these formulas. For example, consider the tasks from the mentioned article.

Problem 1 (MSU, Faculty of Economics, 1978). In triangle KLM, point A is taken on side KL so that |KA|:|AL|=1:3; on the LM side point B is taken so that |LB|:|BM|=4:1. Let C be the intersection point of lines KB and MA. It is known that

$$S_{KLB} = \frac{4}{5} s, \quad S_{ALM} = \frac{3}{4} s,$$

$$S_{KLC} = 4p, \quad S_{LCB} = 4q,$$

$$S_{KLC} - S_{LCB} = 4p - 4q = 4(p - q) = 4(S_{KLB} - S_{ALM}) = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{20} s = \frac{1}{5} s,$$

$$S_{KLC} + S_{LCB} = S_{KLB} = \frac{4}{5} s.$$

From the last two equalities s=2S_{KLC}=4.

Problem 2 (MIPT, 1977). The base side ABCD of the correct prism ABCD $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ has length 2a, the side edge has length a. We consider segments with ends on the diagonal AD$_1$ of the face and the diagonal DB$_1$ of the prism, parallel to the plane AA$_1B_1B$.

1) One of these segments is drawn through the point M of the diagonal AD$_1$ such that |AM|:|AD$_1$|=2:3. Find its length.

2) Find the shortest length of all segments.

This problem can be solved using the coordinate method.

Solved and e. We will place this prism in a rectangular coordinate system, as shown in figure 2. Through point X of the edge AD, distant from D at a distance x, we draw a plane XTT$_1$X$_1$, parallel to the face ABB$_1$A$_1$. Let R and S be the intersection points of this plane with [AD$_1$] and [DB$_1$]. The abscissas and ordinates of the points R and S can be determined verbally, and the applicates can be determined using the properties of similar triangles ADD$_1$ and AXR, DBB$_1$ and DQS. We have: $R(x; 0; \frac{2a-x}{2})$, $S(x; x; \frac{x}{2})$. So |RS| = $\sqrt{x^2 + (a-x)^2}$.
