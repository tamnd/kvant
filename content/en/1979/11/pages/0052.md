7. There are several squares whose sum of areas is 4. Prove that such squares can always cover a square of area 1. (M570)

9th grade

4. Prove that for any numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belonging to the interval [0; 1], the inequality holds
$$x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n + 1)^2 \geq 4 (x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n).$$ (M579)

5. The natural numbers $p$ and $q$ are coprime. Segment [0; 1] is divided into $p+q$ identical segments. Prove that each of these segments, except the two extreme ones, contains exactly one of the $p+q-2$ numbers
$$\frac{1}{p}, \frac{2}{p}, \dots, \frac{p-1}{p},$$
$$\frac{1}{q}, \frac{2}{q}, \dots, \frac{q-1}{q}.$$ (M567)

6. Through the point $O$ there are 1979 lines $l_1, l_2, \dots, l_{1979}$ in space, no two of which are mutually perpendicular. An arbitrary point $A_1$, different from $O$, is taken on the line $l_1$. Prove that it is possible to choose points $A_k \in l_k$, $k=2, 3, \dots, 1979$ such that the following 1979 pairs of lines are mutually perpendicular:
$$(A_1 A_3) \perp l_2, (A_2 A_4) \perp l_3, \dots,$$
$$(A_{k-1} A_{k+1}) \perp l_k, \dots, (A_{1977} A_{1979}) \perp l_{1978},$$
$$(A_{1978} A_1) \perp l_{1979}, (A_{1979} A_2) \perp l_1.$$ (M573)

7. A finite sequence $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ of the numbers 0 and 1 must satisfy the following condition: for any number $k$ from 0 to $n-1$ the sum
$$a_1 a_{k+1} + a_2 a_{k+2} + \dots + a_n - k a_n$$ is an odd number.
a) Come up with such a sequence for $n=25$.
b) Prove that such a sequence exists for some $n > 1000$. (M574)

7. On a straight line, the points $A_0, A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ are arranged in order so that the lengths of the segments $|A_0 A_1|, |A_1 A_2|, \dots, |A_n-1 A_n|$ do not exceed 1. It is required to mark $k-1$ from the point $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n-1$ in red so that the lengths of the two $k$ parts on which the segment $A_0 A_n$ is divided by red dots differ by no more than 1. Prove that this can always be done
a) at $k=3$;
b) for every natural number $k < n$. (M575)
