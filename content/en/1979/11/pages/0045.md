Note that the sum of the squares of the distances from an arbitrary point on the circle to the vertices of this triangle is equal to $2a^2$.

Solution (Fig. 7). Let $M$ be an arbitrary point on the circle. Then $\overrightarrow{MA} = \overrightarrow{MO} + \overrightarrow{OA}$, $\overrightarrow{MB} = \overrightarrow{MO} + \overrightarrow{OB}$, $\overrightarrow{MC} = \overrightarrow{MO} + \overrightarrow{OC}$. Considering that $|MO| = |OA| = |OB| = |OC| = R$, we get
$|MA|^2 + |MB|^2 + |MC|^2 = 6R^2 + 2\overrightarrow{MO} \cdot (\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC})$.
Since the triangle $ABC$ is regular, $\overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{OB} + \overrightarrow{OC} = 0$ is, and $R = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}$ is.
Therefore, $|MA|^2 + |MB|^2 + |MC|^2 = 6 \cdot \frac{a^2}{3} = 2a^2$.

This solution is not only simpler than that found in I. Gabovich’s statistic, but also more general. It is easy to generalize to the case of a regular $n$-gon, and not necessarily inscribed in a circle; it can also be described, and not only for planimetric problems. For example, the assumption above is valid for the following stereometric problem: around an equilateral triangle, the side of which is equal to $a$, a sphere is described, the center of which coincides with the center of the given triangle; prove that the sum of the squared distances from an arbitrary point on the sphere to the vertices of this triangle is equal to $2a^2$.
