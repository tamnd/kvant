Fig. 6. Let’s write down the moment rule:
$$m_1 g l_1 = m_2 g l_2,$$
(a)
$$m_1 g l_1 = \left(m_2 g - F_A\right) l'_2.$$

(b)
Here $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the masses of metal loads, $F_A$ is the Archimedean force model, $F_A = \rho_B g V_2$ ($V_2$ is the volume of the load $m_2$, $\rho_B$ is the density of water). From (a) and (b) there is
$$m_2 = \rho_B V \frac{l'_2}{l'_2 - l_2}.$$

We define $m_1$ in a similar way:
$$m_1 = \rho_B V_1 \frac{l'_1}{l'_1 - l_1}.$$

Fig. 8. a) Determine the volume $V$ of the sample:
$$V = V_1 + V_2,$$
where $V_1$ and $V_2$ are the volumes of the upper (length $l_1$) and lower (length $l_2$) parts of the sample. Since $\frac{V_1}{V_2} = \frac{l_1}{l_2}$ (the sample is homogeneous) and $V_2 = \Delta V$, we find
$$V = \Delta V \left(\frac{l_1 + l_2}{l_2}\right) = \Delta V \frac{l}{l_2}.$$

6) Let's determine the density of the sample. Let's write down the sailing conditions: $Mg = F_A$, where $M = \rho V$ and $F_A = \rho_B g \Delta V'$. From here the density $\rho$ of the sample rock is found: $\rho = \rho_B \frac{\Delta V'}{V}$.

Fig. 9. Let us write down the equilibrium conditions for the sample in water and in copper sulfate: $F_{A1} = \rho_B \Delta V_1 g$ and $F_{A2} = \rho_B \Delta V_2 g$ ($\rho_B, \rho_K$ is the density of water and the corresponding copper sulfate). Since $F_{A1} = F_{A2} = Mg$, we find $\rho_K = \rho_B \frac{\Delta V_1}{\Delta V_2}$.
