This figure shows the partition of a square into 21 pairwise congruous squares, invented by the Dutch mathematician Duwesin in 1978. The numbers indicate the lengths of the sides of the corresponding squares, if the length of the original square is 112. This construction is a kind of record: it has the smallest number of pairwise congested squares (21). For more information about this, see p. 21.
