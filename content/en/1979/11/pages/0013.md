⟦folio 11⟧

The explosive process is characterized by the very rapid release of a large amount of energy, which is accompanied by a sharp change in the state of the substance. The gases formed during the explosion can perform significant mechanical work during their distribution.

Terms like “fast” and “big” are, of course, relative. With a kilogram of coal, about thirty million joules are released into the air. This heat is enough to heat combustion products to temperatures of many thousands of degrees. This temperature at normal gas density corresponds to a pressure of hundreds of atmospheres. Thus, from an energy point of view, the coal-oxygen system has enough energy to cause an explosion. However, coal does not explode, but burns easily.

The fact is that in this case the second condition of the explosion is not met - the speed of energy release. Indeed, the combustion of coal begins from the surface; the deeper layers begin to burn only after they have warmed up due to the heat released during the reaction in the surface layer. Warming up, as well as the supply of oxygen, occurs so slowly that the combustion products have time to heat up, and there is no significant increase in pressure.

However, there are also substances whose chemical transformations occurred without the supply of an extraction oxidizer. Examples include solid rocket propellants, dynamite, etc.

One of these substances is trinitropoluene - С₇H₅(NO₂)₃. Experts usually call it trope or top. If you burn the fire from the surface, it easily ignites and burns. At the same time, the inside of the substance slowly erupts. And despite the fact that the heat output of this substance is much less than that of conventional fuels (only 4 MJ/kg), tropyl can explode. To do this, you need to increase the temperature throughout the entire volume of the substance. This can be done in different ways, for example, by compression. In this case, the reaction proceeds throughout the entire volume and so quickly that the resulting gaseous products do not have time to expand significantly during the reaction. Occupying a volume approximately equal to the volume of the original charge, and being heated to a high temperature, these gaseous products create a pressure of tens or hundreds of thousands of atmospheres. The subsequent rapid distribution of gases leads to destruction of the environment - an explosion will occur.

In this article we want to introduce the reader to a thermal explosion - a spontaneous explosive process associated with the release of chemical energy.

First of all, we need to recall the basic facts from the study of the rate of chemical reactions.

The first thing that catches your eye when studying a chemical transformation is its low speed. Indeed, in a gas, for example, under normal conditions, each molecule experiences an average of 10¹⁰ centigrade per second. If every collision resulted in a reaction, it would happen instantly. Experience does not confirm this. For example, a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen (explosive gas) at room temperature can be kept perfectly effective, that is, it leads to a reaction.

A chemical act is the transformation of some molecules into others. In this case, one bond between atoms is destroyed and new ones arise. If the new bonds are different from the old ones, then the reaction occurs with the release of heat (an exothermic process). This, however, does not mean that the reaction should be faster. After all, before forming new connections (even empty ones that are stronger), you need to destroy the old ones, that is, expend energy. The strength of any component system (atom, molecule, nucleus) is characterized by bond energy. It is equal to the work that needs to be done to decompose the system into its constituent parts.
