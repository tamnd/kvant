Magic hexagon

Since ancient times, the so-called magic squares have been known. The numbers from 1 to $n^2$, written in the cells of such squares (size $n \times n$), are arranged so that their sums in any horizontal or vertical row, as well as along both diagonals, are the same. One of the magic squares of size $4 \times 4$ is depicted in the upper right corner of the border of A. Durer “Melancholy”. How many such squares do you think there are? It turns out there are 880. And what about magic squares of size $5 \times 5$? There are about 250,000 of them.

A “magic hexagon” is also depicted here: the numbers from 1 to 19 are written in its cells so that in each of the five vertical rows, the heels are inclined “from top left - down to right” and the heels are inclined “from top right - down to left”, the sum of the numbers is the same. How many of these magical hexagons are there? The electronic checking machine, after checking (for forty-two minutes) 196,729 hexagons, found that this hexagon was the only one! (Within accuracy of rotations and symmetry, of course).

This statement was proven by Charles Trigg - see our recently published book “Problems with a twist” (M., “Mir”, 1976).

TO OUR READERS

In each issue of our magazine, in the section “Problem book «Kvant»” tasks in physics and mathematics are published. Every day the editors receive several thousand letters from readers with solutions to these problems. Among the authors of these letters, the editors select schoolchildren who have solved the most problems and come up with the most original solutions. These schoolchildren are awarded a special prize established by the editors of the journal «Kvant», and receive the right to participate in the Republican Optimization in Mathematics and Physics.
Coming up with an interesting new problem is usually more difficult than solving it. More difficult, but more interesting. Try to come up with problems and send them to us.
Schoolchildren who are the authors of the best problems will be awarded special bonuses «Kvant», and the problems themselves will be published in the journal.
Problems must be sent along with solutions. Each task should be presented on a separate sheet.
If the task takes an overlay, the source from where it was taken is indicated.
For problems not accepted for publication, correspondence is not conducted with the authors and the text of the problems is not returned by the author.
We are waiting for your letters.
