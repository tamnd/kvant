In «Kvant», 1979, No. 5, versions of entrance exams in physics were published. Below we have published the 1978 Mathematics Entrance Exam (written exam) options. In addition to the options, solutions and answers, here is a detailed analysis of one of the options, indicating typical mistakes of schoolchildren.

Mathematics

In 1978, MISIS conducted entrance exams in mathematics under the new program for the second year. Instead of the stabilization of applicants’ values ​​that we expected this year, we are forced to note the absence of a noticeable improvement in their preparedness and even a slight decrease in it. This applies, in particular, to sections of the school course devoted to differentiation and integration. The problem is especially bad with geometry: almost no one has the ability to apply vector algebra to solving geometric problems.

In order of increasing difficulty of options, the faculties of MISIS can be arranged approximately as follows: Faculty of Engineering, Faculty of Metallurgy of Ferrous Metals and Alloys, Faculty of Metallurgy of Non-Ferrous and Rare Metals and Alloys, Faculty of Semiconductor Materials and Devices, Faculty of Physics and Chemistry.

Faculty of Semiconductor Materials

1. Find the domain of functions

$$y = \sqrt{\log_{0.5}(3x - 8) - \log_{0.5}(x^2 + 4)}$$

This task consists of solving a system of inequalities. Many of the applicants were unable to correctly obtain this system of inequalities, because Plyugo knowledge of linear and logarithmic functions. The system of inequalities has the form

$$\begin{cases} 3x - 8 > 0 \\ \frac{3x - 8}{x^2 + 4} \leq 1 \end{cases} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} 3x - 8 > 0 \\ x^2 - 3x + 12 \geq 0. \end{cases}$$

Some applicants were unable to solve this system correctly, although the fact that the second inequality is satisfied for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$ immediately yields the answer: $x > \frac{8}{3}$.

2. Solve trionometric equation

$$\frac{1}{1 + \text{ctg}x} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cos \left(x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{1 - \text{ctg}x}{2(1 + \text{ctg}x)}$$

Typical mistakes here are as follows: insignificance of the basic formulas of trionometrics, for example

$$\cos \left(x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \cos x \cdot \cos \frac{\pi}{4} + \sin x \cdot \sin \frac{\pi}{4},$$

$$\text{ctg}x = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x};$$

acquiring built roots.

This equation can be solved, for example, as follows.

$$\sin x \neq 0, 1 + \text{ctg}x \neq 0 \Rightarrow x \neq -\frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi.$$

$k \in \mathbb{Z}$, $x \neq m\pi$, $m \in \mathbb{Z}$.

Let's transform the equation

$$1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left(\cos x \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \sin x \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \left(1 - \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\right) \Rightarrow (1 + \text{ctg}x) \left(\cos x + \sin x + 1\right) = 0.$$

1) $1 + \text{ctg}x = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$

is not included in the scope of definition.

2) $\cos x + \sin x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \sin \left(x + \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \Rightarrow x + \frac{\pi}{4} = (-1)^{k+1} \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi \Rightarrow x = \left[(-1)^{k+1} - 1\right] \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi = \left(2\pi + 1\right) \pi, k = 2n + 1$

$-\frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi, k = 2n$

$x = (2n + 1)\pi$ is out of scope. The answer is $x = -\frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi, n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

3. Find the equation of the common tangent to the curves

$y = x^2 + 4x + 8$ and $y = x^2 + 8x + 4$. Most of the applicants who did not solve this problem were unable to correctly write the equation of a tangent to a curve. Solving the following problem: equation of tangents to the first and second curves

$$\begin{cases} y = f'(x_1)(x - x_1) + f(x_1), \\ y = g'(x_2)(x - x_2) + g(x_2). \end{cases}$$

The condition that the tangent is common is

$$\begin{cases} f'(x_1) = g'(x_2) \\ f(x_1) - f'(x_1) \cdot x_1 = g(x_2) - g'(x_2) \cdot x_2 \end{cases} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} 2x_1 + 4 = 2x_2 + 8 \\ -x_1^2 + 8 = -x_2^2 + 4 \end{cases} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} x_1 = 2 \\ x_2 = 0. \end{cases}$$

We obtain the equation of the common tangent

$y = 8x + 4$.

4. Find the limit of the sequence

$x_n = \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5} + \frac{1}{3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 \cdot 6} + \dots + \frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}$

$= \sum_{k=2}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)}$.

A typical mistake: people arrived at the conclusion that the last term in the sum tends to zero, and on the basis of this they decided that the limit is equal to the first term, or the sum of the first several terms, depending on personal taste. At the same time, they forgot that the number of terms in the sum increases with increasing $n$. You can solve the problem correctly, for example, like this. Let's expand the common term $\frac{1}{k(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)}$ into the sum of simple fractions and add these fractions like this:

$$x_n = \sum_{k=2}^{n} \left[ \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+3} \right) + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{k+2} - \frac{1}{k+1} \right) \right] = \frac{1}{6} \left[ \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{6} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{7} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{8} \right) + \dots + \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+3} \right) \right] + \frac{1}{2} \left[ \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{3} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{4} \right) + \dots + \left( \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{5} \right) \right] + \dots +$$

$$+ \left( \frac{1}{n+2} - \frac{1}{n+1} \right) = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \right) - \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n+2} - \frac{1}{n+3} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{n+2} + \frac{1}{n+3}$$

From here

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \right) - \frac{1}{6} + \lim_{n \to \infty} \left[ \frac{1}{2(n+2)} - \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{1}{n+1} + \frac{1}{n+2} + \frac{1}{n+3} \right) \right] = \frac{1}{6} \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \right) + \frac{1}{4} - 1 + 0 = \frac{1}{72}$$
