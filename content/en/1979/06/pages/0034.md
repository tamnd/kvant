If a person clearly sees objects that are no further than a certain distance, such a vision defect is called myopia. If a person clearly sees objects located no closer than a certain distance, this is far-sightedness. Defects such as myopia and far-sightedness can be more or less successfully compensated with the help of glasses (it is the commen that is reversed, not cured).

What is the role of glasses? Obviously, their lenses should create images of objects being screened at distances convenient for a given eye. This means that one of the requirements for spectacle lenses is that the images of objects are within the clear vision of the eyes. The second requirement is that the resulting images must, of course, be straight. And the direct images created by thin lenses are always minimal. Thus, the lenses of the glasses must create direct minimal images at distances within the limits of accommodation of the eyes.

Let's look at two specific problems.

Task 3. A nearsighted person sees objects located no further than 0.5 m from him. What kind of glasses should this person be prescribed so that he can be loved by the stars?

As we have already said, the spectacle lens must create a minimum image located at the distance from which the eye sees this image, in our case - at a distance of 0.5 m from the eyes.

Since the image of a distant object (and the stars are actually distant from the observer) is in the focal plane of the lens, this person should be prescribed glasses with lenses having a focal length of 0.5 m. In this case, the lenses should be divergent so that the images are minimal.

So, a nearsighted person should be prescribed glasses with diverging lenses of optical power $D = -2$ dioptres.

Problem 4. A farsighted person sharply sees objects located no closer than $d = 1$ m from him. What glasses does he need to recite the gazema while holding it $d_0 = 25$ cm from his eyes?

Let us clearly formulate what is required from a spectacle lens in this case. This lens should create a minimal image of an object located at a distance of $d_0 = 25$ cm (a small gap between the eye and the lens can be claimed), at a distance of $f = d = 1$ m. There is enough data to use the lens formula:

$$D = \frac{1}{d_0} - \frac{1}{f} = 3 \text{ дитр}$$

(the minus sign is in the formula because the image should be minimal).

Thus, a far-sighted person needs glasses with a converging lens with an optical power of $D = +3$ dioptres.
