New books

In this issue we publish annotations on books on mathematics and physics available to schoolchildren, published in the 11th quarter of 1979.

Mathematics
Publishing house "Nuuka"
1. Ignatiev V.I.
In the kingdom of ingenuity. 3rd edition, stereoscopic. Volume 10 l., circulation 300,000 copies, price 40 kopecks.
For a detailed review of this book on borrowed mathematics, see «Kvant», 1978, No. 11, p. 54.
2. Marku Shchev in Cha. I. Rows. 4th edition. Volume 10 l., circulation 40,000 copies, price 10 kopecks.
The purpose of the book is to introduce high school students to the department of mathematics, which Cauchy called algebraic analysis, in an accessible and free form. We are talking about the concept of a series, the basic properties of series (in particular, their convergence), as well as the representation of elementary functions by series (without the help of the Taylor formula).
The book explains how tables of trigonometric and logarithmic functions are compiled and shows that the values ​​of these functions can be obtained by performing algebraic operations on the argument alone. True, the number of these actions is not limited and the more they are performed, the more function values ​​will be obtained. The book also talks about how series allow one to find approximate expressions of trigonometric and logarithmic functions in the form of polynomials, and the error when replacing functions with a polynomial can be made as small as desired.
The book contains a lot of interesting and instructive information from the history of mathematics. The entire presentation is constructed in the form of a connected narrative, the main geometer being the biominal series. The reader who is not afraid of calculations is given the opportunity to follow the paths followed by Newton, Euler, Lagrange, Cauchy, who did a lot to teach mathematicians to handle series as freely and easily as polynomials. At the same time, the reader undertakes that series are not polynomials and that their meaningful use is based on the theory of limits.
3. Marku Shchev in Cha. I. Ivies and lodarphms. 2nd edition. Volume 3 l., circulation 100,000 copies, price 10 kopecks.
The book sets out the geometric theory of logarithms - logarithms appear in it as certain areas. All properties of logarithms, as well as methods for calculating them, are derived from the freedoms of areas. Along the way, the author introduces the reader to the following concepts and properties of integral number, without using the concept of derivative.
The reader is required only to have an initial familiarity with the simplest functions and their graphs, with geometric progression and the concept of limit.
4. Tikhonov A. N., Kostomarov D. P. Stories about applied mathematics. Volume 8 l., circulation 100,000 copies, price 30 kopecks.
The book talks in popular form about applied mathematics, about the use of computers to solve applied problems. The main attention is paid to the following issues: construction of mathematical models of the objects under study, development of numerical algorithms for their research and optimization problems.
The book is intended for readers who want to become familiar with the ideas and methods of modern applied mathematics.
5. Uspensky V. A. Pascal’s Treasure. Edition 2, additional. Volume 2 l., circulation 100,000 copies, price 10 kopecks.
Pascal's triangle (“Algebra and beginnings of analysis 9”, paragraph 7) is not a geometric triangle with three angles and three sides. Pascal's triangle is an infinite number table of a “triangular shape” in which there are units on the sides and any number other than these side units is obtained as the sum of two numbers above it. (This is the form of Pascal’s triangle according to Pascal’s Treatise on the Arithmetic Triangle, published in 1665.) The book talks about triangular and primal numbers, which aroused interest back in Ancient Greece, where mystical properties were attributed to them. Newoton's formula and the formula for the number $C_n^k$ are derived. Shows the use of Pascal's triangle in solving problems of a combinatorial nature.
The presentation does not assume any prior knowledge beyond the scope of the eight-year school curriculum.
