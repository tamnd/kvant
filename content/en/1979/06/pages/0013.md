And the positive ions, once in the grid-aode space, move under the influence of the electric field, accelerating towards the aode. Finally, a current appears in the aode circuit due to secondary charged particles.

So, the appearance of a current in the aodine circuit, as Lenard believed, should indicate the beginning of induction. The induction energy is numerically equal to $eU_n$, where $U_n$ is the value $U_1$ at which the aode current appears.

Although the experiment actually observed the emergence of an aode current at certain values ​​of the accelerating potential, Lenard was still unable to obtain reliable experimental results due to the technical imperfections of his setup. It is important to note that Lenard proceeded from an incorrect understanding of the interaction of electrons with atoms, since he could not yet know about the existence of discrete energy levels in an atom. For this reason, Lenard's experiment did not leave a noticeable mark on physics, although it is undoubtedly interesting from a historical perspective. Lenarda's improved technique was subsequently used in the classical experiments of Frank and Hermtz.

Joint work between Frank and Germany in 1911 at the University of Berlin. During this period, they studied the laws of electric current in gases. Naturally, the external energy of induction of atoms was of interest to them. To determine it, they first completed the Lenarda method, and later made changes to it.

First of all, the design of the lamp was changed. The cathode-grid distance was made significantly larger than the grid-aode distance (Fig. 3).

The pressure of the gas (or steam) guiding the tube was increased to 1 mmHg. Art. Accordingly, electrons escaping from the catath experienced repeated collisions with gas (or vapor) atoms in the cathode-mesh space. Frank and Hermtsy reduced the braking voltage and made it constant (it was selected in the range of 0.5-2 V). Positive current appears in the aode circuit as soon as the accelerating voltage $U_1$ becomes greater than the braking voltage $U_2$.

When starting the experiment, Frank and Hermtz, like Lenard, intended the energy of induction. The red curve on this graph shows the nature of the change in current in the aode circuit when the accelerating voltage changes (current values ​​​​are indicated in units).
