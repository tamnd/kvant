I will give only some properties of developable surfaces that are needed for what follows. Our visual definition does not allow us to prove them, so we will have to consider these properties as experimental facts (take a piece of paper and make sure they are true).

1. Through each point A of the developable surface that does not lie on its boundary, we pass a segment lying on the surface that does not end at A. In other words, at each point, a knitting needle can be attached to the developable surface (a curved but not crumpled sheet of paper) so that it is adjacent to the surface for some extent on both sides of the taken point. Such a segment is called a generator of the surface (let us agree that this name applies only to segments of maximum length that lie entirely on the surface, that is, to segments that are not contained in larger segments with this property).

2. If two different generators pass through a point A that does not lie on the boundary of a surface, and A is not the end of either of them, then a sufficiently small piece of the surface surrounding A is flat. In this case, we will call point A flat.

3. If a point A, which does not lie on the boundary of the surface, is the end of some generator, say a, then the flat points make up the points A is arranged like this. Through point A we pass the only generator that does not end there, say b (Fig. 5). The generatrix is ​​divided into two parts. On my side of the generatrix b, with which the generatrix a is located, a flat piece is adjacent to the generatrix b, on the other side of b, as close to point A, there are non-flat points. In this situation we will call point A semi-flat.

By emphasizing that if a point on a surface is neither boundary nor flat, then a single generatrix that does not end at it passes through it, and the ends of this generatrix lie on the boundary of the surface.

Examples of measures. A sheet of paper, rolled into a tube or into a pound, has no flat (or semi-flat) points. In a tube, the generators form a family of parallel segments; in a pound, they form a family of segments diverging from one point. More complex arrangements of generatrices are possible.
