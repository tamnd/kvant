The point is this. The proof of Theorem 1 leaves unused one important property of our Möbius strip - its lack of self-intersections. The self-intersection of a tape cannot be made of paper, but it can be imagined: like the self-intersection of a line on a plane, it “passes through itself,” and it can be divided into parts, each of which does not have self-intersections.

Let's assume that, speaking of paper Möbius strips, we allowed them to have self-intersections from the very beginning. Then λ takes on a new meaning - the new value of λ will be less than or equal to the previous one. In this case, Theorem 1 will remain true, and not a single word will have to be changed in its proof: the absence of self-intersection is not used in this proof.
