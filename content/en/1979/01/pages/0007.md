Let's return to our main subject: calculating $\lambda$ - the lower bound of the length of paper strips of width 1, from which a non-carrying Möbius strip can be glued together.

3. Theorem 1: $\lambda \geqslant \frac{\pi}{2}$

Proof. Let a Möbius strip be made of a paper strip of length $l$. We wrap a length of paper tape around it. This tape (we neglect the thickness of the paper) will be composed of rectangles of the same length, each of which takes the shape of our Möbius strip. Let us mark rectilinear generators and flat points on the long tape (as in figure 66). The result will be something like figure 7. The picture is periodic: everything repeats with a period equal to 2l. You can say more: when you shift left or right by $l$, the picture changes, but in a strictly defined way; namely, it turns over (that is, it is mirrored in the middle line of the strip). The areas of flat points are quadrilaterals (which can degenerate into triangles) bounded by two segments of opposite edges of the tape and two segments passing along the tape. The parts of the tape that do not fall into these areas are kneaded with generatrices, the ends of which lie on the edges of the tape. (All this follows from properties 1-3 of developable surfaces given in paragraph 2.) Flat areas can also be replaced by generators, so that the entire tape will be covered by a continuous family of generators (Fig. 8). Generators in identical quadrangles can be chosen in the same way, so that the periodicity described above is preserved.

Let's take any generator from our family, say, $[AB]$. If we cut it symmetrically in the middle line of the strip and then move it in any direction (say, to the right) to $l$, we get the segment $CD$, which is also a generator from our family (Fig. 9). Note (this is important) that $|AC| + |BD| = 2l$. When winding our length of tape onto a Möbius strip, the generators $[AB]$ and $[CD]$ will take the same position, with the point $A$ aligned with $D$, and the point $B$ with $C$; in other words, the segments $AB$ and $CD$ will make an angle of $180^\circ$ in space. Between $[AB]$ and $[CD]$ there is a continuous family of generators. When moving from $[AB]$ to $[CD]$, the magnitude of the angle that these generators make in the space $[AB]$ continuously changes from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.
