Introductory test assignment at VZMSH in 1979

1. Let points A, B, C be the vertices of an unequal right triangle. In how many ways can a point D be placed on a plane so that any set of points {A, B, C, D} has: a) a center of symmetry? b) axis of symmetry? c) do the answers to questions a) and 6) change if in the task conditions we replace the words “rectangular” with the words “non-rectangular”?

2. Let us consider all possible nine-digit numbers in which each of the digits 1, 2, 3, ..., 9 appears once. Determine the most common divisor of these numbers.

3. Is it possible to shade 25 squares on checkered paper so that each of them has an odd number of shaded neighbors? (Cells are called neighbors if they have a common side.)

4. Andrey runs faster than Viti, but slower than Zhenya. They simultaneously ran along a circular path from one place in one direction and continued until all three again ran to the same place. During this time, Zhenya overtook Vitya 13 times. How many overtakings were there during this time?

5. 25 numbers were written on one line. The sum of any three adjacent numbers is positive. Can the sum of all 25 numbers be negative?

6. Find all pairs of integers (m; n) for which the equality $m^2+1=2^n$ holds.

7. Let us consider a polygon on the plane, which is the union of three parallelograms: OAHB, OBKC and OCMA. a) Is this polygon necessarily convex? b) How many sides can this polygon have? b) What perimeter can this polygon have if $|OA|+|OB|+|OC|=1$?

8. Two positive integers are guessed. Mathematician A is told their sum, and mathematician B is told the sum of their squares. If A and B combine their knowledge, then they, of course, determine the total number. But they learned these numbers during the following conversation:

B. - I don’t know what numbers these are.
A. — Their sum is more than ten.
B. - Then I know what these numbers are. Find these numbers too.

9. Is it possible to draw a rectangle ABCD and a point M on a plane so that the distances from point M to points A, B, C, D are equal, corresponding to a) 1, 2, 3, 4; b) 1, 4, 8, 7?

10. There are two machines. If you throw 5 kopecks into the first of them, then he will multiply the number given to him by 3. If you throw 2 kopecks into the second of them, then he will add the number 4 to the number given to him. What is the minimum amount of money you need to have in order to use these machines to get 1 number 1979 from a given number?

J. Rabb

Correspondence School of Physics and Technology

The Correspondence Physics and Technology School (3FTSH) at the Moscow Order of the Red Banner of Labor Physics and Technology Institute (MIPT) is enrolling eight-year-old and secondary school students located on the territory of the RSFSR in grades 8, 9 and 10 for the 1979/80 academic year. (In some cases, admission from other allied resources is allowed.) Correspondence education instills independence skills, teaches how to work with additional literature, and express one’s thoughts in a concise manner.

ZFTS provides good additional knowledge in physics and mathematics to its graduates, many of whom become students of the leading challenges of our country.

When applying for admission to the ZFTS, students are expected to live in rural areas and workers' settlements, where our school especially needs help.

School education is free.

In addition to individual students, ZFTS also accepts physics and technology circles, which can be organized on site at the initiative of two teachers - physics and mathematics. The leaders of the circle recruit and enroll students who have successfully completed the introductory task of the ZFTS. The circle is accepted into the ZFTS if the school director informs the ZFTS of the names of the circle leader and the posted list of the circle by class (indicating the final grades for the introductory task).

Students admitted to the ZFTS and heads of physics and technology circles will be regulated to receive tasks in physics and mathematics in accordance with the ZFTS program, as well as the solutions recommended by the ZFTS for these tasks. ZFTSH tasks contain theoretical material and analysis of typical tasks and examples on the topic, as well as 10-14 tasks for independent solution. These are both simple problems and more complex ones (at the level of competitive problems at MIPT). The work of animal education students is checked by the ZFTSH or its branches, and the members of the circles are checked by their leaders.
