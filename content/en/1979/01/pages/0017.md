⟦folio 15⟧

a swarm of smoke arises from the tourist’s fire. But as it rises to the top of the hill, its temperature will decrease. And not only because of the inevitable dispersion of the body in the surrounding air. There is another, more fundamental reason for the cooling of the smoke as it rises.

Tanya. Which one exactly?

Yura. Rising, the smoke expands - in accordance with the decrease in atmospheric pressure - and at the same time performs the work of “corrupting” the surrounding air, which is already familiar to us. This occurs due to the internal energy of smoke. Decreasing it means lowering the temperature.

Tanya. Now everything is clear to me: smoke from the lower fire reaches the top of the hill, significant cooling. This is what distinguishes it from the smoke generated by an overhead fire.

Yura. As you can see, the final result of the combustion of vyzanok at the foot and at the top is not at all the same:
at the same altitude above sea level (just above the top of the hill), the smoke from the upper fire is hotter, in other words, it has more final internal energy. This excess energy is obtained due to the mechanical work that the climber did in delivering his bundle to the top of the hill.

Kolya. Now we have really solved the mystery of the “burnt” energy.

Yura. To be fair, it must be said that the fictitious analogy with cylinders also correctly reflects our “problem of fires,” but in a different version. We just have to assume that combustion does not occur in open fires, but in internal shells made of some non-flammable, flexible, but almost completely inextricable materials. (And light enough so that when filled with hot smoke, the shell can fly into the air and reach the top of the hill without wasting energy.) In this case, the work of lifting the fuel to the top is then rewarded with more heat released during its combustion.

Tanya. In fact, we have figured out three different combustion processes: in the absence of air (on the Moon), inside closed shells and in the earth’s atmosphere. It turned out that the energy imparted to the fuel during its rise to the top has a specific application every time. In the first case, it replenishes the potential energy of combustion products, and in the second, it provides additional heat release during combustion, and in the third, it goes to increase the internal energy of the rising exhaust.

Other cases are possible, please, and therefore new mysteries. But we completed today's task!
