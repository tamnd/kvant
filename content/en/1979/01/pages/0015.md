As you know, in order to increase the temperature of a given portion of gas in a cylinder with a piston, where the pressure is constant, by one degree, more heat is needed than in a sealed state, that is, with a constant volume (Fig. 3). The additional heat is used to perform work: the distribution of gas raises the heavy piston.

Tanya. Yes, but this additional heat, as Yura just proved to us, does not depend on the pressure value. With twice the weight of the piston, the same portion of gas will occupy half the volume; the increase in this volume as a result of heating will also be half as large (Fig. 4). The work will remain the same.

Kolya. You convinced me. But now I - alas! — I stopped understanding why gas does not do any work at all when it is heated in a sealed state. The constancy of its volume is ensured by nothing more than the strength of the walls, that is, the pressure it exerts on the gas. The larger the vessel, the weaker the volume changes, but the more powerful the pressure of the walls. The work of distribution, if it does not depend on pressure, cannot tend to zero with the strengthening of the vessel.

Yura. The counteraction of the walls cannot be delayed with the pressure of the piston, even if it is very difficult.
