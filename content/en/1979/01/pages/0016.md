The molded membrane presses on the gas in the same way as the piston. If the piston rises twice as high, the volume under it doubles, but the pressure remains the same. And when the membrane leaks twice as much (dotted line), it begins to press twice as hard, but the volume will increase slightly. What will an increase in gas temperature, say, doubling, lead to? In the right cylinder - to a doubling of the volume under the piston while maintaining pressure, and in the left - to an increase in the deformation of the membrane, and hence the pressure, almost doubling with a very slight increase in volume. In both cases, the constant ratio $\frac{pV}{T}$ is observed, but the work of expansion in the left vessel is negligible (due to the negligence of the expansion itself), while in the right it is significant and does not depend at all on the weight of the piston.

Kolya. I chimme my objection regarding the equality of work against the forces of atmospheric pressure at the top of the hill. The mysterious “excess of nothing” in the potential energy of the firewood delivered to the summit will have to seek a new explanation.

Tanya. Let's do it again - and as clearly as possible! — let’s formulate the essence of the problem. We will consider two experiments with the same initial conditions: two identical hills lie at the foot of the hill. The end results are the same: the firewood was stored, equal amounts of heat were released, clouds of smoke went into the sky. But in one case the work of lifting the pile of wood was done, but in the other it was not.

Yura. I sensed some vagueness in your reasoning. “The clouds of smoke,” you say, “went into the sky”?

Tanya. I meant: we went up - into those rarefied elephants of the atmosphere, where cold air is no different in density from hot smoke.

Kolya. But this cannot be: the pressure of the smoke is always equal to the pressure of the surrounding air; when rising, the smoke expands, just like the air. At different temperatures they cannot be equal in density at any altitude. In addition, the temperature of the smoke in the process of replacing it upward does not remain unchanged: the smoke cools down, pushing away the surrounding air. So it was not immediately reported to what height the columns of smoke from both fires rose.

Yura. There is no point in bothering yourself with unnecessary complications. It is better to take the smoke as the “final state” not “in the sky”, but directly above the top of the hill.
