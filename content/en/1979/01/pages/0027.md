When the hare is caught, $x_T = y_T = 0$. At the initial time $y_0 = a \cos \alpha$. Thus, the projection onto the axis $Oy$ of the wolf's movement during the time $T$ required to catch the hare is equal to $y_0 = a \cos \alpha$. Therefore, the average value of the projection of the wolf’s speed onto the axis $Oy$ for time $T$

$$\bar{U}'_{\rightarrow Oy} = -y_0 / T = -a (\cos \alpha) / T.$$

Let us now find from (1) and (2) the correspondence between $U'_{\rightarrow OB_1}$ and $U'_{\rightarrow Oy}$. We have:

$$U'_{\rightarrow OB_1} = U'_{\rightarrow Oy} / n + |\vec{V}| / n - n |\vec{V}|.$$

Since the terms $|\vec{V}| / n$ and $n |\vec{V}|$ do not depend on time, the same correspondence is valid for the average values ​​of the projections under consideration, that is

$$\bar{U}'_{\rightarrow OB_1} = \bar{U}'_{\rightarrow Oy} / n + |\vec{V}| / n - n |\vec{V}| = -a (\cos \alpha) / n T + |\vec{V}| / n - n |\vec{V}|.$$

On the other side,

$$\bar{U}'_{\rightarrow OB_1} = -a / T.$$

From (3) and (4) we find the catch exception:

$$T = \frac{a}{|\vec{V}|} \cdot \frac{n}{n^2 - 1} \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha}{n}\right).$$

Let's check this answer for evaluated special cases.

$1^\circ$ $\alpha = 0$: the wolf is annoying the hare. In this case, the relative speed of the wolf’s movement is equal to $|\vec{V}| / (n + 1)$, so it will overtake the hare in time $T_1 = \frac{a}{|\vec{V}| / (n + 1)}$.

The same result is obtained from (5) with the substitution $\alpha = 0$.

$2^\circ$ $\alpha = \pi$: the wolf is beating the hare. In this case, the value of his relative speed is $|\vec{V}| / (n - 1)$ ($n > 1$), and he will finish the hare in time $T_2 = \frac{a}{|\vec{V}| / (n - 1)}$.

Substituting $\alpha = \pi$ into expression (5), we get the same answer.

During the time $T$ the zac covers the distance

$$S_3 = |\vec{V}| T = \frac{an}{n^2 - 1} \left(1 - \frac{\cos \alpha}{n}\right),$$

so the wolf LOB beats the hare at a point on the line $l$, distant from the point $A$ at this distance $S_3$.

1. A straight highway opens into the meadow perpendicular to the clearing of the forest.

A bus leaves the forest at a speed of $\vec{V}$. Does it make sense to walk from every part of the meadow to catch up with the bus if you can move at a speed of $\vec{U} (|\vec{U}| < |\vec{V}|)$?

2. Prove that all four ships $A, B, B$ and $\Gamma$ from problem 5 are on the same straight line at any time.

3. Each vertex of a regular $n$-gon contains a dog. On command, all dogs simultaneously begin to move, each pursuing its nearest clockwise neighbor. How long does it take before the dogs meet and what distance does each of them run if they run at a speed of $|\vec{V}|$?

4. The square garden is surrounded by a fence. At the top $A$ of the fence sits a small dog. At the opposite vertex $B$ there is a hole large enough for hares, but too narrow for a dog. A zatz thief who has sneaked into the garden, noticing the dog, runs to the hole in a straight line at a speed of $|\vec{V}|$; the dog chases him at a speed of $n |\vec{V}|$, $n > 1$, and always runs like a straight hare. Determine (approximately) in which part of the garden the bunny can feel safe.

5. Several dogs surrounded the hare. The speed of dogs does not exceed the speed of a hare. All dogs are visible to the hare. Can a hare escape from an environment, and if so, how, if the dogs, when chasing, run in such a way that the speed of each of them is always directed towards the point where the hare is currently located?
