The complex polyhedron shown on the cover is nothing more than a union of ten identical cubes with a common center. These cubes are arranged in a sense "as symmetrically as possible." In what sense? - Let's explain this for the case of five cubes. Let's take a dodecahedron and fit a cube into it (so that the vertices of the cube lie at the vertices of the dodecahedron); It turns out that this can be done in five different ways; the combination of these five cubes forms a very beautiful polyhedron - try to draw it or build it from thin cardboard. It is simpler, of course, than the ten-cube polyhedron shown on the cover. However, that one can also be built starting from the dodecahedron (or icoahedron), but this is a more tricky matter—try it! More details about a polyhedron made up of five cubes are described on page 57. It also explains how to build a polyhedron made of ten cubes.
