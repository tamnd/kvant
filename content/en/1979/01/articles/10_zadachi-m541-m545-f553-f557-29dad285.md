Problem
quantum

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but their solution does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the Newstein school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time.

Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than March 1, 1979 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35 B. Ordynka 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant». In the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 1–79” and the numbers of the problem whose solutions you are sending, for example “M541, M543” or “F555”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the journal or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (you will receive the results of the decision review in this envelope).

The conditions of each original problem proposed for publication (or a series of problems) are assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your first and last name, school number and class in which you study.

M541. In a group of $N$ people, each person has exactly three friends.
a) Prove that $N$ is even.
b) Can such a company always be divided into $N/2$ pairs so that the people in each pair are friends?

D. Bernishtein

M542. Given a right triangle $A_0A_1A_2$ with legs $|A_0A_2|=a$ and $|A_1A_2|=b$. The ant will climb along the infinite broken line $A_2A_3A_4A_5$ ..., where $A_nA_{n+1}$ is the height of the triangle $A_{n-2}A_{n-1}A_n$.
a) Find the length of its path (from an infinite number of segments).
b) Construct the limit point $L$ to which the ant approaches. At what distances from the sides is it located?

P. Emelyanov

M543*. Let

$$\rho(x, y) = \frac{|x - y|}{\sqrt{1 + x^2} \sqrt{1 + y^2}}.$$

Prove that for any real numbers $a, b, c$ the following inequality holds:

$$\rho(a, c) \leq \rho(a, b) + \rho(b, c).$$

Andrei Paszkiewicz (Warsaw)

M544*. What is the maximum number of vertices of which no diagonal (which lies entirely inside the polygon) can be checked that a non-convex $n$-gon can have? Solve this problem for $n=4, 5, 6, 7$ first.

S. Bychkov

F553. A heavy board of mass $M$ lies on two thin-walled rollers of radii $r$ and $R$ and equal masses $m$. The distance between the centers of the rollers is $l$. With what acceleration does the board begin to move if it is released? The friction between all surfaces is such that there is no slipping.

F554*. A block of mass $m_1$ lies on a board of mass $m_2$, which is on a smooth horizontal surface. The friction coefficient between the block and the board is $\mu$. A force $\vec{F}$ acts on the board, changing with time according to the law $\vec{F} |=bt$, where $b$ is a constant value. Draw graphs of the acceleration of the block and the board versus time $t$.

F555. a) A light rigid rod of length $l$ with a mass $m$ at the top is hinged at the bottom point and held in a vertical position by two horizontal stiffening springs $k$, fastened to its upper end (Fig. 1). The rod cannot move in a direction perpendicular to the springs. At what mass of load will the vertical position of the rod cease to be stable?

b) A steel knitting needle of length $l$ is inserted into the floor so that it stands vertically. If a small horizontal force $\vec{F}$ is applied to its upper end, the upper end of the spoke will deflect by amounts $a \ll l$. Assess how much weight a knitting needle can withstand, disagree, at its upper end. Let us distinguish the formula that the spoke takes under the action of horizontal or vertical forces on it, neglect.

G. Komkin

F556. If you put a wiring loop on an ice block, from which a weight is suspended (Fig. 2), the wiring begins to cut the ice. This is explained by the fact that when the pressure increases, the melting point of ice reaches the melting point, the ice under the wiring begins to melt, and above the wiring it begins to freeze again.

However, if the loop is made not from wiring, but from a nylon thread of the same or even smaller diameter, the ice is practically not cut. Why? Try to check the described experience.

G. Kosurov
