Single-sheet hyperbolide

On the first page of the cover you see a paper model of a single-sheet hyperbolide. This is the name of the surface defined in the space of the equation

$$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} - \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1.$$

The hyperbolide on the cover has the same parameters $a$ and $b$ ($a = b$), and it is a body of revolution with the axis $Oz$ (justify this). The easiest way to make a model of a single-sheet hyperbolide of revolution is as follows. Let's take two wheels of the same diameter with surfaces evenly spaced along the rim. Let's put the wheels on a common axle and insert thin rubber bands into the surface parallel to the axle (see Fig. 1). If we now fasten these rubber bands in the holes and turn the upper wheel by an angle $\alpha$, changing $180^\circ$, then the rubber bands will be located on the surface of a single-sheet hyperbolid. Prove that when rotated through an angle $-\alpha$, the elastic bands will be located along the same surface.

From our rubber model we can see that on the surface of the hyperbolide there are two families of intersecting straight lines. Derive this structure from the surface equation.

The first Moscow TV, designed by the architect Shukhy, was made from such “models” (steel cables were used instead of rubber bands).

A one-sheet hyperbolid of revolution can be obtained by rotating the curve $$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$$ about the axis $Oz$. This curve is a hyperbola. That is why the surface is called a hyperbolide. It is called one-sheet because there is also a two-sheet, obtained by rotating the hyperbola about the $Ox$ axis. The $$\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$$ equation is different from the familiar hyperbola equation $xy=1$. But this should not confuse you - the difference is not fundamental. Why this is really so, you will understand by reading the article on hyperbole in «Kvant» No. 3, 1975. On the first page of the cover both families of crossing lines and a hyperbola are clearly visible. The fourth cover page gives some instructions for gluing together a paper model like the one on the first page.
