Sections of polyhedra

This article examines 10 problems about sections taken from the main study "Geometry 9" and "Geometry 10". The primary goal is to systematize the principles of constructing sections of polyhedra. Here we have selected examples and tasks based on the construction of intersections and parallel design. We will return to the techniques associated with peredicularity in another article.

1. Statement of the problem

When proving theorems and solving stereometric problems, we use drawings, that is, images on a plane of figures located in space.

“In stereometry, we will call the image of a figure (original) any figure similar to a parallel projection of a given figure onto a certain plane” (IX, § 13) *).

Thus, to construct images, it is necessary to take into account the properties of parallel projection. In part (Fig. 1),

1° Dot image point;
2° straight line image of a straight line;
3° parallel lines images by parallel lines;
4° if point M lies on the segment [A; B], then the image of the point M lies on the image of the segment [A; B] and divides the image of the segment in the same segment in which the point M divides the segment [A; B].

*) The reference “IX, § 13” refers to § 13, “Geometry 9”; "X, 73, 84" introduces Exercises 73 and 84 from the Geometry 10 textbook.

2. Auxiliary tasks

Before moving on to the main task, we will consider several auxiliary techniques for determining the intersections of lines and planes.

1. The intersection of two intersecting lines is easy to find: the point at which they intersect on the drawing is the image of their point of intersection in space. But - be careful! This is only true under the assumption that the lines actually intersect. (And straight lines in space “as a rule” intersect!). Don't be in error (Fig. 2).

2. The intersection of the straight line AB and the plane α is not difficult to find if the parallel projections A₁, B₁ of the points A, B onto α (Fig. 3) are known. The construction is clear from the drawing.

3. The intersection of the line AB and the plane α is easy to find in the case when the points of intersection of A₁, B₁ with the plane α are given for all intersecting through the points A, B, respectively (Fig. 4).

4. The intersection of two planes α and (ABC) is found simply if parallel projections A₁, B₁, C₁ of points A, B, C onto α are given. Follow this construction yourself (Fig. 5).

It is possible to indicate many more different situations when it is possible to construct the intersection of two given planes (ABC) and α.

4. Parallel lines and planes

In addition to the previously studied prims, two theorems about parallel planes are often used: (I) the section of two parallel planes by a straight plane is a pair of parallel lines; (II) the intersection of two planes parallel to a line l is a line parallel to l.

5. More difficult tasks

11. Prove that a given segment, not parallel to a given plane, can be projected in parallel onto a segment of any length on a given plane.

12. At what angle on a given plane can a given angle be projected in parallel?

13. Prove that if their images are known for three points of a plane figure that do not lie on the same straight line, then images of all points of this figure can be obtained.

14. Prove that any triangle can serve as an image of a given triangle *).

15. Prove that the image of a quadrilateral can be any quadrilateral whose diagonals are divided by the point of intersection in the same ratios as the original one.

16. Prove that the image of a tetrahedron can be any quadrilateral with drawn diagonals.

17. The exchange cube is placed on a larger cube so that they have a common vertex and their faces are parallel. Construct a section of the resulting figure by a plane defined by three points, from which
- all three lie on three crossing edges less than a cube;
- all three lie inside three different faces larger than the cube;
- one point lies on the upper base of a small cube, and the other two on different edges of a larger cube.

*) Let us recall that the dimensions of the field image do not matter (see point 1), therefore “any triangle” means “any triangle up to similarity”.
