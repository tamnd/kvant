Chase, collision, popping

At exactly noon, the hour and minute hands of the clock coincide. When will they coincide next time? Despite its simplicity, this weight task is impressive. Let's solve it.

Let $v_1$ and $v_2$ be the rotation frequencies of the clock and minute hands. Obviously $v_1 = \frac{1}{12} \mathrm{q}^{-1}, \quad v_2 = 1 \mathrm{q}^{-1}$.

If $t$ is the moment of rotation of the next coincidence of the hands after noon, then $v_2 t = v_1 t + 1$, or by the moment $t$ the minute hand will make one revolution more than the hours. Hence,

$$t = \frac{1}{v_2 - v_1} \approx 1 \text{ ч } 5 \text{ мин } 27 \text{ с}.$$

We see that the value $t$ depends only on the frequency difference $v_2 - v_1$. This suggests that the solution can be obtained by reasoning differently. During the time between two consecutive coincidences, the minute hand makes one revolution relative to the clock. Since the frequency of rotation of the minute hand relative to the clock is $v_{21} = v_2 - v_1$, one can immediately conclude that

$$t = \frac{1}{v_{21}} = \frac{1}{v_2 - v_1}.$$

This task is one of the following types of tasks. In some reference systems, the characteristics of the motion of several bodies are specified. You need to find how the positions of bodies change relative to each other over time. In this math class lesson we will get acquainted with several such problems. All of them will be solved using the same example: choosing a suitable coordinate system.

6. Sad story of a hare

Will we be caught, and if so, what will we eat *)?

Let's move to the reporting system, where the hare is motionless and located at the origin of coordinates $O$. (The hare is probably very scary in this reporting system - after all, he is inactive!). Let the axis $Oy$ be directed along the hare path in the direction of the hare’s movement. The speed of the wolf in this system is $\vec{U}' = \vec{U} - \vec{V}$. Let us prove first of all that the hare will be caught.

Let the point $B_1$ be the position of the wolf at some point in time and let $\vec{OB}_1$ make an angle $\varphi$ (Fig. 6 b) with the axis $Oy$. Projection of speed $\vec{U}'$ onto direction $\vec{OB}_1$

$$U_{\vec{OB}_1} = |\vec{V}|(\sin \varphi - n).$$

By condition $n > 1$; This means that for any $\varphi$ the quantity $U_{\vec{OB}_1}$ is negative, and $|U_{\vec{OB}_1}| \geq |\vec{V}|(n - 1)$. Therefore, the wolf necessarily reaches the hare in a time of no more than $\frac{a}{n - 1}$ (at the initial moment he is at a distance of $a$ from the hare).

$$U_{\vec{Oy}} = -|\vec{V}| + n|\vec{V}| \sin \varphi.$$
