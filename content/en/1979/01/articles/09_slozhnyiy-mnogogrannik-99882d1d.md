Complex polyhedron

The complex polyhedron shown on the second cover page is nothing more than a union of ten cubes with a common center.

Let's first talk about the “relative” of this polyhedron - a polyhedron made up of five cubes. It is built on the basis of the dodecahedron. It turns out that you can fit a cube into a dodecahedron in five different ways. One such cube is shown at figure 1 in green. In the photo you see a model of this polyhedron.

The family of 10 cubes is also based on the dodecahedron. We take a pair of opposite vertices $T$, $T'$ (Fig. 1) and consider three (red) segments $A_1B_1$, $A_2B_2$, $A_3B_3$ (as is easy to see, when turning around the axis $OT$ at angles divisible by $2\pi/3$, they transform into each other). Through these segments, three pairwise perpendicular planes are drawn, intersecting at point $V$ on the axis $OT$ (Fig. 2) and three more planes are considered that are symmetrical to them relative to the center $O$ of the dodecahedron. These planes limit our cube.

Such a cube can be constructed for each pair of opposite vertices of the dodecahedron, and since it has 20 vertices, this will result in 10 cubes. Their combination is depicted on the cover.

Try to “see in full” one of these cubes on the cover. If you add up, it will be easy for you to highlight the rest; You will notice that each cube has two “isolated” vertices (they lie on the axis from which this cube was built), and each of the six remaining vertices has a “neighbor” (the vertex of another cube). These pairs of red "neighbors" are grouped around the decimal stars formed by the union of the intersecting edges of the five cubes.

If you want to build a model of the polyhedron shown on the cover, you can use figure 3: it shows by hatching, with sufficient accuracy, the outward part of each face of each cube. 60 such “templates” need to be made; then they need to be glued together correctly, based on figure on the cover*). But it's not easy! Only a hardworking and skilled modeling enthusiast can glue together such a polyhedron beautifully.

*) This is described in more detail in the book by V. Gamayunov “Projectrography”, M., 1976, Moscow State Pedagogical Institute named after. Lenin.
