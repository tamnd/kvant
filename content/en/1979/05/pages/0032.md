F525. As the current increases, the direction of the arc discharge across the discharge gap decreases, tending to a certain constant value at high currents. The arc was included in series with some ballast resistor. The current-voltage characteristic for a circuit is shown in figure 15. 1) Let's construct the current-voltage characteristic of an arc without a ballast resistor. 2) Using the resulting graph, we determine the maximum resistance of the ballast resistor at which the arc can burn in the direction of the source $U=85 \text{ В}$.

Fig. 16.

1) The voltage $U$ at the system input is equal to the sum of the direction $U_{\text{д}}$ on the arc discharge gap and the direction $U_R$ on the resistor. Since $U_R=1R$, where $I$ is the current in the circuit, then

$$U(I)=U_{\text{д}}(I)+1R.$$

The value $U_{\text{д}}(I)$ kills as $I$ increases and at high current values ​​it tends to some constant value $U_0$. This means that at high currents the dependence $U(I)$ becomes linear -

$$U(I)=1R+U_0,$$

Moreover, the tangent of the angle $\alpha$ of the overhead graph to the current axis is equal to $R$:

$$tg \alpha = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta I} = R.$$

From figure 15 it is clear that for $I>10$ A the graph $U(I)$ is a practical example of a line. By figure is located

$$R = \frac{80-55}{17,5} \approx 1,4 \text{ (Ом)}.$$

To construct the current-voltage characteristic of the arc, it is necessary for each current value $I_i$ to find the value $U_{\text{д}}(I_i)=U_i-I_iR$ (values ​​$U_i$, taken from the graph, $I_iR=1,4 I_i$). The current-voltage characteristic of the arc is shown in figure 16. 2) Let us determine the maximum resistance $R_{\text{max}}$ of the ballast resistor at which the arc can burn in the direction of the source $U=85 \text{ В}$.

On the graph $U_{\text{д}}(I)$ we plot the load caps $U-1R$ ($U=85 \text{ В}$), corresponding to the values ​​$R$. Obviously, the larger the value $R$, the smaller the angle $\alpha$ is between the load line and the current axis. If the load line does not have common points with graph $U_{\text{д}}(I)$, arc burning is impossible. Therefore, it is clear that the maximum resistance $R_{\text{max}}$ of the resistor at which the arc burns corresponds to the load voltage touching the graph $U_{\text{д}}(I)$. Of figure 16 is located,

$$R_{\text{max}} = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta I} \approx \frac{35}{7} = 5 \text{ (Ом)}.$$

(The points of intersection of the load prima with the graph $U_{\text{д}}(I)$ (for example, points $a$ and $b$) determine the possibility of the current value and direction on the arc for a given resistance of the ballast resistor. Point $a$ corresponds to unstable arc burning, and point $b$ to stable.)
