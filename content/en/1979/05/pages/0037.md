Problem 4. Prove that the radius of the circles described by the rounders $AHB, BHC$ and $AHC$,

another $H$ is the point of intersection of the heights of the acute triangle $ABC$, equal to each other.

Solution e. Let us mark one of these triangles (for example, $\Delta BHC$) as a constructive triangle and find the radius of the circle circumscribed around the triangle.

1 way. Through vertex $B$ we draw a line parallel to height $CK$, and through $C$ - a line parallel to height $BM$; let $F$ be the point of intersection of these lines (Fig. 6).

The quadrilateral $BHCF$ is a parallelogram, so $\Delta BFC \cong \Delta BHC$. Consider the quadrilateral $ABFC$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} [FB] \\ [CK] \\ [FC] \\ [BM] \end{pmatrix} \Rightarrow \begin{pmatrix} \widehat{ABF} \\ \widehat{ACF} \\ 90^\circ \end{pmatrix}.$$

Therefore, the points $A, B, F$ and $C$ lie on the same circle, so $R_{\triangle ABC} = R_{\triangle BFC}$ ($R_{\triangle ABC}$ denotes the radius of the circle circumscribed by the rounded $\Delta ABC$).

($\Delta BFC \cong \Delta BHC$) $\Rightarrow (R_{\triangle BFC} = R_{\triangle BHC})$.

Next, $$\begin{pmatrix} R_{\triangle ABC} = R_{\triangle BFC} \\ R_{\triangle BFC} = R_{\triangle BHC} \end{pmatrix} \Rightarrow (R_{\triangle ABC} = R_{\triangle BHC}).$$

It can be proved in a similar way that $R_{\triangle AHB} = R_{\triangle AHC} = R_{\triangle ABC}$.
