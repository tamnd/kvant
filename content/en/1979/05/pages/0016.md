⟦folio 14⟧

The development of nuclear reactions is easy to explain. When a charged particle collides with a nucleus, the probability of its penetration into the nucleus is limited by the Coulomb repulsion force. In the case of a collision of a neutron with a nucleus, restrictions of this type do not exist. The force of interaction between a neutron and a nucleus is initially very small; Only at very short distances does it begin to grow rapidly and has an attractive character... Therefore, even neutrons of very low energy can penetrate the nucleus.”
In 1934, the Italian physicist E. Fermi discovered the artificial radioactivity of atomic nuclei under the influence of neutron irradiation.
Also in 1934, Fermi and his colleagues observed the fission of nuclei of heavy elements under the influence of slow neutrons. (True, Fermi did not give a correct interpretation of the observed phenomenon. Only in 1939 did the German physicists O. Hahn and F. Strassmann make the correct theoretical conclusion: under the influence of slow neutrons, the fission of heavy nuclei with the formation of radioactive waste occurs.)
The discovery of the neutron began the era of nuclear physics, which was marked in the mid-20th century by the development of elementary particle physics and the development of nuclear technology.
In the history of physics, 1932 is usually called the “year of miracles,” in recognition of the abundance of remarkable discoveries that occurred that year. One can agree with this name even if we take into account only the discovery of the neutron.

The discovery of the neutron is a kind of node in the network of directed scientific research
