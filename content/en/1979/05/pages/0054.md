Can schoolchildren use microcalculators in class? Are microcalculators allowed in exams?
V. Ananyev (Volgograd region), etc.

The emergence of small electronic counting equipment is a reality. Microcomputers are becoming more and more widespread in the national economy. Implementing them in schools is a matter of tomorrow.

It is known that students nowadays spend a lot of time practicing various types of computing skills. A microcomputer introduced in schools will free up some of this time. Naturally, there will be a need to determine the range of issues that can be studied further in this regard. First of all, it is important to determine the content of education in the lower grades. Experiments are already underway on the use of microcomputers in schools. In particular, starting next year, several classes in Yerevan will begin testing the possibility of introducing small computers into the educational process, starting from the 1st grade.

It is obvious that in 10 years people will no longer count “in stolnik” and even the slide rule will be a thing of the past. But the mass introduction of microcomputers in schools without a previously thought-out methodology for working with them, or the development of a specific control system, can lead to a loss of “number sense” among students. Freeing them from the need to count orally leads to the fact that students forget the tables of multiplication and addition of numbers and unlearn arbitrary actions; they cannot evaluate the resulting calculation result, at least its order. The everyday life of foreign schools warns us against such a mistake.

However, if a student skillfully and intelligently uses a microcomputer and is able to evaluate the order of the result, then it was technically inappropriate to prohibit him from using the achievements. It is especially useful to use small computers in geometry lessons, when solving, for example, triangles; they can be used in physics and chemistry lessons, when solving problems, during laboratorium and practical work, especially various types of calculation work.

Naturally, if a student used small electronic calculating equipment for calculations throughout the year, then it would not be logical to deprive him of this opportunity during exams.

3. Moiseeva,
Inspector of the Main Directorate of School
Ministry of Education of the USSR
