The center of mass of the system of motion is the same as a particle with mass $M=m_1+m_2$ moves when it is acted upon by forces acting on the system. In our case, only gravity acts on the system. Therefore, the center of mass of the system continues to move along the same parabola along which it moved before the shell exploded.

It is not difficult to find successive positions of the center of mass. From figure it is clear that point a is the vertex of the parabola. This follows from the fact that the previous and subsequent points at which the outside is located ($b$ and $b'$) lie on the same horizontal line. Consequently, after the projectile ruptures, the center of mass of the system, after successive equal intervals of time, appears on the same horizontal lines as points c, d, e, f, etc. Points $c', d', e', f'$, etc., at which the center of mass is located, symmetrical to points c, d, e, f, etc. relative to the vertical line passing through point a.

A helicopter (or model) stays in the air thanks to the reactive force that occurs when the propeller is “thrown” against the air stream. According to Newton's III law, the reactive force acting on the helicopter (on the propeller) is equal in absolute value to the force acting on the jet.

Let us denote $\rho$ the air density, $S$ the area of ​​the jet and $v$ the absolute value of the jet speed. Then, during the time $\Delta t$, the propeller is thrown out of the air $Sv\Delta t$ with a mass $m=\rho Sv\Delta t$, and the air momentum changes by the amounts

$$\Delta p=mv=\rho Sv^2\Delta t.$$

According to Newton’s II law, a force $F$ acts on the vises such that its absolute value is equal to

$$F=\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta t}=\rho Sv^2.$$

The same absolute force acts on the helicopter. In order for the helicopter to remain in the air, $F$ must be equal to the absolute value $Mg$ of the force of gravity acting on the helicopter:

$$\rho Sv^2=Mg.$$

The power $N$ of the propulsion is equal to the energy imparted to the air in 1 second:

$$N=\frac{mv^2}{2\Delta t}=\frac{\rho Sv^3}{2}.$$

Substituting from (*) $v=\sqrt{\frac{Mg}{\rho S}}$, we get

$$N=\frac{1}{2}Mg\sqrt{\frac{Mg}{\rho S}}.$$
