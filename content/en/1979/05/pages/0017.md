After sunset

Is it possible to see an artificial Earth satellite with the naked eye? The answer to this question seems very convenient: of course not. After all, in order for the eye to see a distant object, it is necessary that the angular size f of this object (Fig. 1) be no less than $\varphi_{\min} \approx 1' \approx 10^{-4}$ rad. This means that a satellite located $R \approx 500$ km from the Earth’s surface can be visible to the naked eye if its linear size $d$ is not less than $R \varphi_{\min} \approx 50$ m! So it is impossible to observe the Earth with the naked eye by an artificial satellite. However...
