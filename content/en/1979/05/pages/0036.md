Let's replace the figure

It is well known that the variable change method is widely used in algebra. No less effectively, “replacement” can be applied in geometrics. This technique for solving geometric problems is as follows: the figure in question in the conditions of the problem is replaced by a figure with the same desired value, so that it is easier to find this value.

In the first two problems, solve the problem of the simplest substitution - replacing one segment with another.

Task 1. From point C, taken outside the node AOB, equal to 60°, the variables CD, CM and CN corresponding to the sides OA, OB and to the bisector ON of this node are omitted. Determine |ON| if |CM|=d₁ and |CD|=d₂.

Solution. Let E and P be the intersection points of (CN) with (OB) and (OA) (Fig. 1). The resulting triangle OEP is very stoic. Analysis of figure leads to the idea of ​​​​replacing the segment ON with a congruent segment EF, where (EF)⊥(OP): the length of the segment EF is easy to relate to the given lengths of the segments CD and CM.

We assumed here that d₂>d₁. The case d₁>d₂ is shown in figure 2 and is similar to the case d₂>d₁. Final answer: |ON| = |d₁—d₂|.

Task 2. In an equilateral triangle ABC (|AB|=|BC|), a perpendicular AD is drawn from vertex A to side BC. Semicircles are inscribed in triangle ABD and ACD so that their diameters lie correspondingly on [BD] and [AD]. Find the ratio of the areas of these semicircles if the size of node B is equal to π/4.

Solution. If we take into account that $\frac{S_1}{S_2} = \left( \frac{r_1}{r_2} \right)^2$, then the desired value will be found if we determine the ratio of the radii of semicircles on it; To do this, we replace the radius O₁M (Fig. 3) with a segment congruent to it, which is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with a known acute angle. Since $\widehat{B} = \frac{\pi}{4}$ it follows that |AD|=|BD|, |MB|=|MO₁|=r₁. Considering that |AD|=|AM| and |BA|=|BC|, we get |MB|=|CD| — segment MB can be replaced with segment CD.
