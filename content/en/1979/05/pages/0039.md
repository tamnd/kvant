Problem 7. Prove that the length of the segment connecting the midpoints of the intersecting edges of the metrahedron with a right trihedral angle at the vertex is equal to the radius of the sphere circumscribed about the given metrahedron.

Solution. Complementary tetrahedron $ABCD$ with right plane angles at vertex $A$ to right parallelepiped $ABKCDLMN$ (Fig. 10). A sphere described around a given parallelepiped will also be described around a tetrahedron $ABCD$. From the triangle $AKD$ it follows that $|PQ| = \frac{1}{2}|DK|$. Since the diagonal $DK$ of the parallelepiped is the diameter of the circumscribed sphere, the length of the segment $PQ$ is equal to the radius of this sphere.

Problem 8. The lengths of the edges of a right parallelepiped are 1, 2 and 3. Determine the distance between the crossing diagonals of adjacent lateral faces of this tetrahedron.

Solution. Instead of the parallelepiped $ABCDA_1B_1C_1D_1$, consider the tetrahedron $DB_1C_1C$ (Fig. 11). The volume of this tetrahedron is

$$V = \frac{1}{3} S_{\triangle B_1C_1C} \cdot |DC| = \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot 3 \right) \cdot 2 = 1.$$

Let us now find its volume, use the correspondence $V = \frac{1}{6} \times l_1 \cdot l_2 \cdot d \cdot \sin \varphi^*$, where $l_1$ and $l_2$ are the lengths of the crossing edges of this tetrahedron, $d$ and $\varphi$ are the distance and angle between them. We get

$$V = \frac{1}{6} |B_1C| \cdot |DC_1| \cdot d \cdot \sin \overrightarrow{AB}_1C = \frac{1}{6} \sqrt{50} \cdot d \cdot \sin \varphi.$$

In a trihedral angle $BACB_1$, the cosine of the plane angle $\varphi$ lying opposite the edge $B_1B$ of the right dihedral angle is equal to the product of the cosines of the plane angles bounding this right dihedral angle, i.e.

$$\cos \overrightarrow{AB}_1C = \cos \overrightarrow{AB}_1B \cdot \cos \overrightarrow{BB}_1C;$$

from this equality we get

$$\cos \overrightarrow{AB}_1C = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{50}};$$

$$\sin \overrightarrow{AB}_1C = \sin \varphi = \frac{7}{\sqrt{50}}.$$

Hence,

$$V = \frac{1}{6} \sqrt{50} \cdot d \cdot \frac{7}{\sqrt{50}} = \frac{7}{6} d.$$

From equalities (1) and (2) it follows $1 = \frac{6}{7} d$.

Hence $d = \frac{6}{7}$.
