intervention in the solid state is very much associated with the enormous practical importance of solids. However, the implementation of solid bodies is so complex that even at the current level of development of purely physical science, a purely physical approach based on fundamental laws is not effective in explaining the varied properties of specific materials, and is not able to indicate exactly how to obtain materials with the desired properties.

And modern technology requires more and more new materials - materials that operate at ultra-high and ultra-low temperatures, in strong magnetic and electric fields, in space, etc. The study of materials, the development of new substances and, in particular, metals with SSB properties become possible only with the skillful combination of fundamental knowledge about matter with empirical laws found as a result of generalization of vast experience in the study of materials. It is also necessary to know the methods and technologies for the production of new substances, and to master various experimental methods for their research.

Such a unique combination of knowledge and observations is given to students by the Faculty of Physics and Chemistry of the Moscow Institute of Steel and Alloys. This faculty trains research engineers in a number of specialties related to the study of materials, the development of new methods for their production and research. Students are taught special courses in mathematics, general and theoretical physics, specialized disciplines, and most importantly, even in junior courses, students acquire scientific work in departments, and at the end of their studies they will definitely listen to independent research. This approach allows students to develop creative abilities, the ability to think independently, to connect new ways to solve problems - qualities that are absolutely necessary for research.

In this note it is impossible to talk about all the specializations at the faculty, and we will focus only on those that may be of interest to the readers of Quantum.

Specialists in the study of the physical properties of metals are trained by the Department of Theoretical Physics, which is headed by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. A. Abrikosov. Students are taught theoretical calculations of the properties of real substances and various experimental methods for studying them. In order to study the physical laws for describing the properties of metals, it is necessary to make various approximations, to foresee which method will have an effective influence on the study. It has its own special “kitchen”, the secrets of which are readily shared with students by the department’s teachers. Students specializing in the department use large electronic computers at the institute's computer center in their coursework and diploma work.

Another specialization is “superconducting materials”. Currently known materials become superconducting (do not resist electric current) only at very low temperatures (less than -250°C). It is clear that this limits the possibility of using superconductors at technical temperatures? To work in this direction, one must not only have a good understanding of the physics of superconductivity, but also know the properties of substances, reduce the required compounds and predict their properties. It is precisely this set of knowledge that is given to students studying in this specialization, and students are trained not only within the walls of the institute, but in the largest scientific center - the Institute of Solid State Physics of the USSR Academy of Sciences.

A new specialization, “examination of diamonds and other high-hardness materials,” was created at the department of high-hardness materials, which is headed by Corresponding Member of the USSR Academy of Sciences V. P. Elyutin. Students of this specialization, along with studying the properties of natural diamonds, receive in-depth research into the research of diamonds, bsar nitride and other important materials of modern technology. Artificial diamonds are handed out literally before the eyes of students in laboratories assembled with the most modern equipment.
