We said "observe". But how? Is it possible to see a luminous satellite with the naked eye? Let's see.

There is an energy visibility threshold for the eye: the eye reacts to light radiation if the power of this radiation entering the eye is at least $P_{\min} \approx 3 \cdot 10^{-17}$ W. Let us estimate the radiation power of the satellite entering the eye. More precisely, the power of solar radiation, which, reflected from the satellite, enters the observing eye.

We will call the integrativeness of the radiation energy passing through a unit area per unit time.

In near-Earth space, the integrativeness of solar radiation is equal to $I_0 \approx 1,4 \cdot 10^3$ W/m². If the surface area of ​​the satellite is $s \approx 10$ m², then the power of solar radiation incident on the satellite is

$$P_c = I_0 s \approx 1,4 \cdot 10^4$$ Tue. Part of this energy, approximately 0.1, is reflected from the surface of the satellite *). So we can consider the satellite as a source of radiation with a power of $P_\pi = kP_c \approx 1,4 \cdot 10^3$ W ($k=0,1$ is the reflectance).

The intensity of this radiation at a distance $R$ from the satellite is equal to $I_R = P_\pi/4\pi R^2$ ($4\pi R^2$ is the area of ​​the sphere “surrounding” the satellite; the power of $P_\pi$ radiation from the satellite passes through this area; so $P_\pi/4\pi R^2$ is precisely the radiation power falling per unit area, that is, integrativeness). If the satellite is located at a distance of $R \approx 500$ km from the Earth’s surface, then the intensity of radiation entering the observing eye is $I_R \approx 0,4 \cdot 10^{-9}$ W/m². Assuming that the viewing screen area is approximately 0.3 cm², the radiation entering the viewing eye is found to be $P \approx 10^{-14}$ W.
