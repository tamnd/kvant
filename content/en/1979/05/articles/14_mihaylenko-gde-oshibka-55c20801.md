Task 9. The lengths of the edges SA and BC of the metrahedron SABC are equal to a, the edges SB and AC are equal to b, the edges SC and AB are equal to c. Determine its volume.

Solution. Let the vertices A, B, C of the metrahedron SABC be the midpoints of the edges NK, MK and MN of the metrahedron SMNK (Fig. 12). Then $|KN|=2|BC|=2a$, $|MN|=2|AB|=2c$, $|KM|=2|AC|=2b$. According to the conditions of the problem $|AS|=a$, $|BS|=b$, $|CS|=c$. Therefore, in the triangle KSN, the length of the median AS is equal to half the length of the side KN and, therefore, $\triangle KSN$ is right-angled ($\widehat{KSN}=90^\circ$). We similarly prove that $\widehat{NSM}=\widehat{MSK}=90^\circ$. If the triangle SMNK is placed on a side face, for example, SKM, then the edge SN will be its height and, therefore, $V_{SKNM}=\frac{1}{6}|SK|\times|SN|\cdot|SM|$.

The volume of the required primid is $\frac{1}{4}$ part of the volume of the SKNM primid (the triahedrons SKAB, SANC, SMCB and SABC are equal in size), therefore $V_{SABC}=\frac{1}{24}|SK|\cdot|SN|\cdot|SM|$.

Let's put $|SM|=x$, $|SN|=y$, $|SK|=z$; Then

$$\begin{cases} x^2+y^2=4c^2, \\ x^2+z^2=4b^2, \\ y^2+z^2=4a^2. \end{cases}$$

From here

$$x^2+y^2+z^2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2).$$

Subtracting each of the equations of system (3) from equation (4), we obtain

$$\begin{cases} z^2=2(a^2+b^2-c^2), \\ y^2=2(a^2+c^2-b^2), \\ x^2=2(b^2+c^2-a^2). \end{cases}$$

From here

$$x^2y^2z^2=8(a^2+b^2-c^2)(a^2+c^2-b^2)(b^2+c^2-a^2).$$

The required volume is equal to:

$$V=\frac{1}{12}\sqrt{2(a^2+b^2-c^2)}\times(a^2+c^2-b^2)(b^2+c^2-a^2).$$

Where is the error?

The following logical rules are beyond doubt: if from statement A we follow statement B and from B we follow statement C, then from A we follow C.

Let's apply this rule:

I claim that Socrates is a violent man (A)

I assert that Socrates is a violent man, then I thereby assert that Socrates is a violent man, then I,

Of course, I'm telling the truth. It turns out that if I say that Socrates is a violent man, then I am telling the truth.

Where is the error?

The terms “front focus” and “back focus” help to succinctly formulate the rules for constructing images that are the same for positive and negative lenses.

Let's consider several specific tasks. All of them were offered at the entrance exams to the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology.

Task 1. Knowing the location of the object $A$ and the image $A'$ relative to the main optical axis MN of the lens (Fig. 4), find the position of the lens foci by construction.

The straight line passing through the points $A$ and $A'$, when intersecting the MN axis, gives the center of the lens $O$ (Fig. 5). The perpendicular to the MN axis, restoring from point $O$, determines the position of the lens plane.

The ray $AB$, parallel to the MN axis, having pre-filled in the lens, passes through the points $A'$ and the back focus $F'$ of the lens. If $F'$ lies behind the lens (Fig. 5, a) - the lens is converging, if in front of the lens (Fig. 5, b) - it is diverging. The anterior focus $F$ is located symmetrically relative to the lens.

 Having constructed the ray $AC$ passing through the front focus, we will make sure that after refraction in the lens it hits the point $A'$.

Problem 2. figure b shows a ray emerging from a diverging lens. Construct the path of the beam to the lens.

Let us draw the secondary optical axis parallel to the beam emerging from the lens (Fig. 7). The rays, indicative of a parallel ray after the lens, intersected in its front focal plane before meeting the lens. By connecting points $A$, at which the secondary axis intersects with the front focal plane, and point $B$, at which the output ray intersects with the plane of the lens, we determine the direction of the parallel ray $CB$.

Problem 3. Using a collecting lens, a converging beam of rays is obtained. How to think about rays if a diverging lens (Fig. 8) is placed in the path of the ray?

Let us draw a secondary optical axis parallel to the upper ray of the beam (Fig. 9). Parallel rays after the lens intersect at its rear focal plane (point $A$). Continuing straight line $AB$, we find the path of the upper ray behind the lens. We will symmetrically construct the lower ray of the beam.

Problem 4. A diverging lens with a known arrangement of focal planes was cut along its diameter. The halves were moved vertically to a distance of $h$ by each other (Fig. 10). Construct images of the point $A$ lying on the axis of symmetry of the system.

Each half of the lens gives its own image of the point $A$. First, let's build an image in the upper half of the lens. The main optical axis $M_1N_1$ of the upper half passes through the lenses parallel to the axis of symmetry of the system, $O_1$ is the optical center of the upper part, $AO_1$ is its secondary optical axis (Fig. 11).

Let us draw an arbitrary ray $AB$ incident from point $A$ on the upper part of the lens and a secondary optical axis $CD$ parallel to it. After meeting the lens, the parallel rays intersect at the rear focal plane at point $E$. The intersection point of lines $BE$ and $AO_1$ determines the position of image $A'$.

The lower part of the lens produces a symmetrically positioned image $A''$.

In spherical mirrors, images are constructed according to the same rules as in lenses (Fig. 12):

1) the ray $AO$ passing through the center of the mirror $O$ does not change its direction when reflected from the mirror;
2) the ray $BB_1$ incident on the mirror parallel to the main optical axis, having been reflected, goes through the focus $F$ of the mirror (recall that the focus of a spherical mirror is located after the pole of the mirror $P$ and its center $O$);
3) the ray $DF$ passing through the focus, after being reflected, goes parallel to the main optical axis.
