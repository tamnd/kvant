Physics

In 1978, at the Faculty of Physics of NSU, each version of the written exam in physics consisted of five problems of three types.

The first three problems are computational problems of varying difficulty: from almost standard to relatively complex, requiring ingenuity, deep knowledge, and the ability to understand a non-privibuous or conventional physical situation.

The fourth task is an assessment task. To solve this problem, you need to try to analyze the physical phenomenon, formulate a simple (since you only need to estimate) physical model of this phenomenon, select the dimensional values ​​of the physical parameters and, finally, obtain a numerical result that more or less corresponds to reality. The text of the problem stated that the applicant, having a good understanding of the phenomenon or problem, could himself set the necessary quantities and obtain a numerical answer. The experience of entrance exams shows that a significant proportion of applicants enrolled in the Faculty of Physics cope with the assessment tasks.

The fifth task is a demonstration task, in which you need to explain the physical phenomenon demonstrated in the exam. Here it is important to obtain significant phenomena and highlight the main one among various physical factors.
