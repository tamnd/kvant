What exactly do you think changes this device?

Of course, you agreed that the device you made is a barometer. With the help of such a barometer it is easy to monitor changes in air pressure. If the outside air pressure increases (the pressure inside the can becomes greater), the casing is injected into the inside of the can and the needle rises up the chalet. If the external pressure decreases (and is less than the internal pressure), the shell bends outward and the arrow drops.

A balloon that doesn't deflate

Take a large glass jar and close it tightly with a stopper in which two glass tubes are inserted (Fig. 6). First put rubber hoses on the upper ends of the tubes, and burn the rubber shell of the balloon to the lower end of one of the tubes. (The role of tube $T_1$ can be played by tap $K$, shown in dotted line on figure 6.)

Leave tube hose $T_1$ free and inflate the balloon through tube $T_2$. If you then release $T_2$, the balloon will naturally deflate.

Now do the following. When opening tube $T_1$, inflate the balloon and immediately pinch the hoses of both tubes. If you then open the tube $T_2$, the ball will practically not deflate. Why?

It's pretty easy to explain. As soon as we release the tube $T_2$, the ball begins to deflate under the action of the stretched rubber shell. At the same time, its volume will decrease, and the volume of air in the jar (when closing the tube $T_1$) will increase. As a result, the air pressure in the jar will decrease. At some point it turns out to be less than the air pressure inside the ball. This transfer of pressure causes the stretched shell to heal and the ball will stop deflating. Obviously, the more the balloon was inflated, the less it deflated.
