The new mathematics program requires the student to have solid knowledge of such fundamental concepts of “higher mathematics” as the limit of a function, continuity, and arbitrary integral. Naturally, in school all these concepts are first studied on elementary functions: power, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions.

However, due to lack of time and overload of the school curriculum, there is a significant gap in the logical basis of these concepts - the theory of action numbers is practically completely absent. Meanwhile, the theory of the active number not only underlies the theory of limits, continuity, differentiation and integrability of functions, but also the basis for the construction of the theory of elementary functions, on the basis of which the study of analysis in high school. From textbooks, schoolchildren only learned that there is a certain set of action numbers. These numbers can be added, subtracted, multiplied and divided according to certain rules. It is argued that all elements of this set can be partitioned into two disjoint subsets, each containing only rational or irrational numbers, and that each of these numbers can be represented as an infinite periodic (rational number) or non-periodic (irrational number) decimal fraction. However, we repeat, the structure of the theory of the active number is not presented in the school mathematics course.

The peer-reviewed manual*) has its main goal of promoting an in-depth understanding of those initial chapters of mathematical analysis that were only easy to apply to the mathematical classes of a high school teacher. The main content of the book is to present the theory of action numbers, and then on this basis the theory of limits of a sequence is built, the basic elementary functions are defined and the properties of continuous functions are studied. Undoubtedly, such a presentation is a natural addition to the school course of mathematics and logic forms those intuitive ideas that students have.

Afterwards it consists of an introduction and four chapters. The introduction outlines the basic concepts of the theory of pluralities, gives definitions of correspondence, functions and sequence, and introduces some features of mathematical logic.

The first chapter outlines the theory of limits for a sequence of rational numbers.

The second chapter of the manual outlines the theory of action numbers and the theory of number sequences. IN

*) G. N. Yakovlev. “Numerical sequences and continuous functions” (Moscow, “Prosveshcheniye”, 1978).

The basis of the presentation is the theory of active numbers, developed by G. Kantr and S. Mere. We believe that such a presentation of the theory of active numbers corresponds to the spirit of school mathematics. The author defines the concepts of equality and inequality of action numbers and sets out in detail the arguments for action numbers.

The third chapter discusses the basic elementary functions and gives definitions of functions and proves the basic theories associated with these concepts.

In the fourth chapter, an arbitrary and proving basic theory of differentiating functions is introduced.

Conveniently selected exercises occupy a significant place in the manual. They help readers understand difficult course issues and instill good problem-solving skills. Along with general problems, the manual includes a significant number of problems of increased difficulty. The rate of increase in the difficulty of tasks is quite high. It leads to difficult exercises much faster than is done in the textbook.

Many issues are presented in the book in a fresh and original way. The author skillfully uses symbols and the language of mathematical logic.

Some inadmissibility is caused by the small circulation of the book, but this book is of great interest both for high school teachers and for high school students and will undoubtedly cause great demand. We must hope that the second book I publish will not take long.
