$$W_n = m v_n^2 / 2 = \hbar^2 n^2 / 2 m r^2.$$

The total energy of the electron is the sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy in the nuclear field, which is negative and equal to $U = -Z e^2 / r$. The total energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom is

$$E_n(r) = \hbar^2 n^2 / 2 m r^2 - e^2 / r.$$

In our derivation we assume that the orbital radius $r$ has a fixed value. According to quantum mechanics, the radii of the orbits are “scattered” in the vicinity of the classical orbital sources. As an estimate of the radius, we can take the value $r$, which corresponds to the minimum energy $E(r)$.

To find the minimum $E(r)$, we proceed as follows. Let's rewrite the expression for $E(r)$ as follows:

$$E = A x^2 - B x.$$

We used the following designations: $x = 1 / r$, $A = \hbar^2 n^2 / 2 m$, $B = e^2$. It can be seen that for $x_1 = 0$ and $x_2 = B / A$ the value of $E$ is zero. Inside the integral $]x_1, x_2[$ E is negative. Somewhere inside this integral lies the minimum $E$. For the sake of estimation, we will assume that $E_{\min}$ corresponds to the value $x$ in the middle of the integral, that is, $x = B / 2 A$. Accordingly, $r = 1 / x = 2 A / B$, that is

$$r_n = \frac{\hbar^2 n^2}{m e^2}.$$

(Note that for $n = 1$ this expression
