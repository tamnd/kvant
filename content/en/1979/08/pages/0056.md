Straight and curved

Mathematics is a large and very unique world, surprising with the beauty of conquered and unconquered peaks, openwork places thrown over abysses and connecting one mountainous country of facts and methods to another. However, these horizons that generate imagination are opened only to those who have managed to penetrate the world of mathematics, have learned to see the beauty hidden from the uninitiated, and have mastered the difficult ability to see a wonderful kaleidoscope of figures, numbers and mathematical logic in the world around them. But penetrating the world of mathematical ideas is not easy: in addition to abilities and interest, you also need authenticity, will, desire and the ability to work hard. Only a lot of systematic work and, in particular, problem solving can lead a student to success in mathematics.

But everything is there: interest in mathematics, passion, the desire to solve problems (especially “difficult ones”), perseverance, and hard work. And then the main question will arise: what to read and what problems to solve? After all, if the book you read turns out to be not imbued with the modern “spirit” of mathematics and its applications, or if you solve problems with methods that promote the development of good taste and research and broaden your horizons, then something must be lost in the education and formation of the future mathematician. But if the chosen book is successful, then it turns out to be a kind and experienced teacher and mentor, and this can serve as a guarantee that it will help in cultivating good taste, acquiring an outlook and research skills.

In our opinion, the book by N.B. Vasiliev and V.L. Gutenmacher “Straights and Curves”* is precisely such a kind and experienced mentor for a young mathematician. The book contains an interesting selection of problems, by solving which the reader - perhaps without realizing it - will become familiar with the spirit and ideas of modern mathematics. Connected here (especially in the presented solutions to key problems, which serve as a kind of beacon for the reader) are the languages ​​of set theory and the coordinate method used by various displacements and kinematic messages (which the authors form in the form of “ring theorem”), vector methods for solving the problem and logical connections between the elements of the condition. At the same time, the same problem is often considered from different points of view, solved by different methods, which allows you to compare these methods, acquiring the necessary horizons.

As you can see, this book is not intended for light reading, on the contrary, it teaches hard work,
