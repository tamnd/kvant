How to guess the solution?

Let us give an example of how certain features of a solution are asked for before an apparatus for an exact solution of a problem is built, before the equations on the basis of which the problem will be solved are found. This is backwards and an example of a more complex dimensional analysis than in the case of an oscillator.

One of the most difficult and unresolved problems of theoretical physics is the connection between gravitational and electromagnetic phenomena.

If such a connection exists, then as a result of solving some not yet found equations, a dimensionless number *) will be obtained, giving a correspondence between the gravitational construct γ and quantities characterizing electromagnetic phenomena, such as the speed of light c, the charge of the electron e and its mass m.

If there are quantum phenomena, then the problem may include Planck's h. Knowing the dimensions of the quantities γ, c, e, m, h, it is easy to verify that from these quantities only two independent dimensionless combinations can be made:

$$\alpha = e^2/\hbar c; \quad \xi = \hbar c/\gamma m^2$$

(recall that $\hbar = h/2\pi$).

The first of these combinations characterizes the interaction of the electron with the electromagnetic field (the dimensionless “charge” of the electron) and is called the constructed fine structure. Substituting the numerical values ​​gives $\alpha = 1/137$, $\xi = 5 \cdot 10^{44}$. Is it possible for a number as large as ξ to arise from solving any reasonable equations? Dimensionless numbers that arise as a result of solving physical problems, as we have already said, are of the order of several units or fractions of units.

*) In order for the correspondence between various physical quantities to be independent of the choice of units, it must be written in the form of a dimensionless combination.

Therefore, we can expect that the quantity ξ will enter the problem in such a form that the result will be a number of order 1. So far we have applied common sense. Now a small intuitive logical leap must be made. It is likely that the theory will include a combination

$$\alpha \ln \xi \sim 1.$$

It is clear that knowledge of such a correspondence makes it easier to find a solution. It is in this form that the quantity ξ is included in the currently existing theoretical attempts to solve the problem of the connection between electrins and gravity.

According to quantum mechanics, the energy of an electron in an atom can only take discrete values.

Possible values ​​of the electron energy in hydrogen atom are given by the expression

$$E_n = - \frac{me^4}{\hbar^2} \frac{1}{2n^2}.$$
