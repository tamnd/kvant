The free surface of water in the gravitational field of the Earth is always established in such a way that at any point on the surface the gravitational force is redistributed towards it, that is, directed towards the center of the Earth along the radius. Due to the presence of a cavity, the surface of the ocean is curved - the total force of gravity (from the Earth and from the side of the “cavity”) is directed, generally speaking, not along the radius of the Earth.

In further reasoning, we will use a similar one between the electrostatic field of an exact charge Q and the gravitational field of an exact mass M. (Recall that the gravitational field of a ball of mass M is equivalent to the gravitational field of an exact mass M placed at the center of the ball.)

The force acting on the test charge q from the side of the charge Q and the force acting on the test mass m from the side of the mass M are equal, respectively,

$$F_{эл} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Qq}{r^2}, \quad F_T = \gamma \frac{Mm}{r^2},$$

where r is the distance between Q and q, M and m. These formulas are similar: $F_{эл} \sim 1/r^2$ and $F_T \sim 1/r^2$, $F_{эл} \sim q$, and $F_T \sim m$, that is, the dependence of these forces on distance is the same and the dependence on the magnitude of the test charge and the magnitude of the test mass are the same. Using this analogy, we introduce the potential φ_T of the gravitational field of the exact mass M (equal to the potential energy of a unit mass in the field of mass M). The expression for φ_T is similar to the expression for the potential φ_{el} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Q}{r} electrostatic field of the exact charge Q:

$$\varphi_T = -\gamma \frac{M}{r}.$$

(The minus sign reflects the main difference between F_{el} and F_T: under the influence of gravitational forces, bodies always attract. Since the potential energy for infinitely large r is assumed to be equal to zero, in the case of attractive forces, the potential energy in the gravitational field turns out to be negative.)

The potential energy of a particle of mass m on the surface of the water must be the same. (Since the gravitational force redistributes the surface, the work done by the gravitational force to move a particle along the surface is zero.) Consequently, the surface of the ocean (surface of the Earth) is equivalent to the surface (the analogue is the field equivalent to the surface).

Let us write down the potential of the Earth's gravitational field at point A, located further from the cavity, and at point B, located directly above the cavity (Fig. 12). At point A the cavity found can be neglected and the potential can be considered equal

$$\varphi_A = -\gamma \frac{M}{R},$$

where R is the radius of the Earth, M=4/3πR³ρπ is its mass. To find the potential at point B, we use the principle of superposition. Figure 13 illustrates the superposition of the gravitational fields of the Earth and the cavity. From figure it is clear that the potential at point B consists of the potential of the Earth’s gravitational field (without a cavity) and the gravitational field of a spherical mass with density ρ_B - ρπ, that is, the exact mass μ = 4/3πr³ (ρ_B - ρπ), where r is the radius of the cavity, ρ_B is the density of water. So (see Fig. 13),

$$\varphi_B = -\gamma \frac{M}{R - \delta} + \left( -\gamma \frac{\mu}{r + h - \delta} \right).$$
