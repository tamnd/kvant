⟦folio 38⟧

Problem
Quanta

M576—M580;F588—F592

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are given asterisks. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to the problem from this issue can be sent no later than October 15, 1979. In the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 8–79” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M576, M577 or “F588”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it.

The conditions of each original problem proposed for publication (or a series of problems) are assigned in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope place: “Problem «Kvant», a new problem in physics or “... a new problem in mathematics”).

At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your first name, last name, school number and class in which you are a teacher.

In this issue, the Problem is compiled from problems proposed at the XIII All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren. The number in brackets indicates the class in which the corresponding task was proposed.

M576. There are several points on the given plane. For some pairs (A; B) of these points, vectors $\vec{AB}$ are taken, and in such a way that the same number of vectors begin at each point as end at it. Prove that the sum of all chosen vectors is equal to $\vec{0}$. (8)

V. Proizvolov

M577. What is the smallest number of pieces that need to be placed on the fields of a chessboard of size a) $8 \times 8$ cells,
b) $n \times n$ cells
so that on every straight line passing through the center of an arbitrary field and parallel to any side or diagonal of the board, there is at least one piece? (Chips are placed in the centers of the fields.) (8; 10)

N. Vasiliev

M578. Find $x$ and $y$ from the system of equations
$$\begin{cases} \frac{x-y}{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}} = a, \\ \frac{y-x}{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}} = b \end{cases}$$
(a and b are given numbers). (8; 10)

V. Gutemacher

M579. Prove that for any numbers $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ belonging to the interval [0; 1], the inequality holds
$$\left(x_1+x_2+\dots+x_n+1\right)^2 \geq 4\left(x_1^2+x_2^2+\dots+x_n^2\right).$$
(9)

A. Plotkin, S. Fomin

M580. In parliament, each member has no more than three enemies. Prove that parliament can be divided into two chambers so that each parlamentaria in the same chamber with it will have no more than one enemy. (It is considered that if $A$ is the enemy of $B$, then $B$ is the enemy of $A$.) (8-10)

O. Borodin
