Correspondence school at NSU

Since 1968, Novosibirsk State University has been operating a correspondence school for students in grades 8-10 from Siberia, the Far East, Kazakhstan and Central Asia. Currently, the Correspondence School has three departments: mathematics, physics and chemistry, in which more than 2,000 students study. Studying at a correspondence school deepens students' knowledge, promotes the development of independent logical thinking, and broadens their horizons.

Correspondence school students must complete 5-6 assignments during the school year, the topics of which coincide with the material they have recently covered or are currently studying in the school curriculum. Particular attention is paid to those sections of the program that are not taught deeply enough in secondary school.

Each task begins with a brief selection of theoretical material, sufficient so that after careful study and creative use it is possible to solve all the proposed 15 problems. At the same time, as an example, 1-2 tasks similar to those given in the assignment are considered in detail. The assignments contain two types of problems: several simple problems that must be solved, and 3-4 complex ones that it is highly desirable to solve in whole or in part.

Evening physical school

Evening Physics School (VPS) at the Faculty of Physics of Moscow State University. M.V. Lomonosov announces enrollment of students in grades 8-10 for the 1979/80 school year. Systematic classes at the Higher Physics School contribute to the development of skills in independently solving physical problems and introduce students to the problems of modern physics. Seminar classes are conducted by senior students and graduate students. The most complex issues of the school curriculum are discussed in detail in lectures by teachers and researchers of the Faculty of Physics. Competitive admission to the Evening Physics School is in September. To take part in the competition, you must submit an application to the director of the Higher School of Physics by September 20, fill out a form at the Komsomol Committee of the Faculty of Physics, and also submit 2 cards measuring 3x4 cm.

The application will be accepted from September 2 to September 20 from 16.00 to 18.00 (except for Sunday) at the Komsomol Committee of the Physics Faculty of Moscow State University (Physics Faculty building, room 2-47).

Admission to the school is based on academic results. Ownership requirements do not go beyond the school curriculum.
