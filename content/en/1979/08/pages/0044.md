M525. Prove that for any metrahedron there exist two planes such that the plane projections of the metrahedron onto these planes are at least $\sqrt{2}$.

Fig. 3.

Let us draw parallel planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ (Fig. 3) through two opposite edges $AB$ and $CD$ of the metrahedron $ABCD$. Let $h$ be the distance between them. We will consider only the projections of the metrahedron on planes perpendicular to $\Pi_1$, and we will also find their averages, the ratio of the plane projections of the metrahedron on which is not less than $\sqrt{2}$.

Let $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ be the projection of the metrahedron $ABCD$ onto such a plane $\alpha$. Then $[A_1B_1] \subset \Pi_1 \cap \alpha$, $[C_1D_1] \subset \Pi_2 \cap \alpha$, and therefore $[A_1B_1] \parallel [C_1D_1]$. Thus, the projection $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ of the $ABCD$ metrahedron onto the $\alpha$ plane is a trapezoid with height length $h$ (in some cases degenerating into a triangle). The midline $KL$ of the trapezoid $A_1B_1C_1D_1$ is the projection of the parallelogram $PQRS$, where $P, Q, R, S$ are the midpoints of the edges $AC, AD, BD$ and $BC$ of the metrahedron $ABCD$, respectively (see Fig. 3). (Indeed, all points $K, L, P, Q, R, S$ lie in a plane parallel to the planes $\Pi_1$ and $\Pi_2$ and equidistant from them.) The vertices of the parallelogram $P, Q, R, S$ are projected at the points of intersection of the midline $KL$ with the segments $A_1C_1, A_1D_1, B_1D_1$ and $B_1C_1$, respectively. For the definition, we will assume that $[PQ]$ is the smaller side of the parallelogram, and $[PR]$ is the larger of its diagonals. Obviously $|PR|/|PQ| \geq \sqrt{2}$.

Let us now take the plane $\alpha_1$, parallel to the diagonal $PR$ (and, of course, perpendicular to the plane $\Pi_1$), and the plane $\alpha_2$, perpendicular to $[PS]$ ($\alpha_2 \perp \Pi_1$). The length of the midline of the trapezoid (projection of the metrahedron) in the first case is equal to $|PR|$, and in the second - no more than $|PQ|$. In the first case, the plane projection of the metrahedron is equal to $h \cdot |PR|$, in the second - no more than $h \cdot |PQ|$. The ratio of these planes is no less than $\sqrt{2}$.

For a regular metrahedron, the estimate $\sqrt{2}$ is population: the planes of projections of the regular metrahedron on the planes $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ will be equal to $a^2/2$ and $a^2/2\sqrt{2}$, where $a$ is the length of the metrahedron edge. But this also requires proof.
