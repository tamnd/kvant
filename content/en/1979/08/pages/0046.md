We will present two solutions to point c) of the problem. The first of them uses the method of mathematical induction, and the induction step is carried out from $n = k$ to $n = k + 2$. In this case, it is necessary to check the fulfillment of the corresponding inequalities for two initial values ​​$n: n = 3$ and $n = 4$. This check, in fact, constitutes the content of points $a$) and $6)$ of the task.
a) So, let $n = 3$. From the condition $0 \leq x_i \leq 1$ ($i = = 1, 2, 3$) it follows that $(x_1 - 1)(1 - x_2)(1 - x_3) \leq 0$, that is, $x_1x_2x_3 + x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - x_1x_2 - x_2x_3 - x_3x_1 \leq 1$.
Since $x_1x_2x_3 \geq 0$, we obtain the required inequality $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 - x_1x_2 - x_2x_3 - x_3x_1 \leq 1$.
b) Let $n = 4$. Let us prove that $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 - x_1x_2 - x_2x_3 - x_3x_4 - x_4x_1 \leq 2$ using geometry.
Let's take a square $A_1A_2A_3A_4$ from the side of length 1. On its sides $A_1A_2, A_2A_3, A_3A_4, A_4A_1$ we mark, respectively, the points $B_1, B_2, B_3, B_4$ so that $|A_iB_i| = x_i$ ($i = 1, 2, 3, 4$; - see Fig. 9). Let $S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4$ be the areas of triangles $A_1B_1B_4, A_2B_2B_1, A_3B_3B_2, A_4B_4B_3$. Obviously, $S_1 + S_2 + S_3 + S_4 \leq 1$ (the triangles lie inside a square of area 1 and do not intersect); That's why
$$\frac{x_1(1 - x_4)}{2} + \frac{x_2(1 - x_1)}{2} + \frac{x_3(1 - x_2)}{2} + \frac{x_4(1 - x_1)}{2} \leq 1$$
whence we obtain the desired inequality for $n = 4$.
