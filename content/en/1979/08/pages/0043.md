M522. On the given plane there are several disjoint segments, no two of which lie on the same straight line. We want to check a few more segments connecting the ends of these segments so that all the segments together form one non-linear broken line. Can this always be done?

M523. The piece fits into a node of the chessboard measuring $n \times n$ cells. Each of the two players takes turns transferring it to the adjacent field (which has a common side with the one on which the chip is located). The second one cannot be dispersed onto the field, two chips have already howled. We look after people who have nowhere to go.
a) Prove that if $n$ is even, then the beginning cerru can add a cutout, and if $n$ is odd, then we cut out the second one.
b) Who should we cut out if the chip initially lands not on the node field, but on the one adjacent to it?

Answer: not always. figure 1 shows six segments whose ends cannot be connected in the required way. In fact, each of the six ends of short segments can only be connected to the end of “its own” long segment, and since there should be two free ends, then both ends of one of the short segments must be connected by segments to the ends of the long one. The result will be a closed broken line with four links - a contradiction.
