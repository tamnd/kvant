we reduce system (3) to one algebraic equation of the 8th degree as follows:
1) By replacing the variables $z = ax + by + c$, $t = dx + ey + f$ and selecting suitable numbers $a, b, c, d, e, f$, we reduce the equation $Q(x, y) = 0$ to one of the following forms:
(a) $z + At^2 = B$
(b) $z^2 + At^2 = B$.

Further, the equation $P(x, y) = 0$ can also be rewritten in terms of new unknowns: $R(z, t) = 0$; at the same time (which is very important!) it will remain an equation of the fourth degree (explain!).

2) In case (a), having expressed $z = -At^2 + B$, we substitute this expression for $z$ into the equation $R(z, t) = 0$ and obtain an equation of the 8th degree with respect to $t$.

3) In the case of (6), we take the equation $R(z, t) = 0$ and leave in it a trace of those monomials in which $z$ appears to an even degree, and the rest intersect on the right side and square both sides of the resulting equation. We have obtained an equation of the eighth degree, which contains molecularly even powers $z$. Substituting the expression $z^2 = -At^2 + B$ into it (see (6)), we obtain an equation of the eighth degree with respect to $t$.

Where is the error?

Adding equations (1), (2), we obtain the equation
$$0x + 0y + 0z + 2d = 0.$$ (3)
Hence the numerical equality $d = 0$ is obtained only under the condition that equation (3) has at least one solution. Thus, our “proof” only proves the following: if two planes are symmetrical to the relative origin $O$ and intersect this, then they both pass through the point $O$.
