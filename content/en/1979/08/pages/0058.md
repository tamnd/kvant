Another version of the theorem about an inscribed angle and a ring on a circle:
Theorem on rotating lines.
If two intersecting lines $l_A$ and $l_B$ rotate in the plane of the circumferential points $A$ and $B$ with the same angular velocity $\omega$ (Fig. 10); in this case, of course, the angle between them remains constant), the shape of the point of intersection of these straight lines is a circle.

Explanation. Let's construct a circle $\delta$ passing through three points: $A$, $B$ and another position $M_0$ the point of intersection of lines $l_A$ and $l_B$ (Fig. 11). According to the theorem “about a ring on a circle,” the intersection point of the line $l_A$ and the circle $\delta$ moves along the circle $\delta$ uniformly with an angular velocity $2\omega$. The intersection point $l_B$ with the circle $\delta$ moves in exactly the same way. Since at one point in time these points coincide (position $M_0$), they coincide.

They give it at any other time. This implies the statement of the theorem.

Problem 3. Make your own figure, 纵 sharpening the theorem on rotating lines.

Explanation. Draw a circle and divide it into 12 equal parts. Draw through one of the division points $A$ and the remaining division points are straight lines, as well as the tangent to the circle at the point $A$ (Fig. 12); you will get a bundle of 12 straight lines, dividing the plane into 24 equal angles). By moving the pencil around the circle, you can make sure that when moving from the division point $M$ to the next division point, the straight line $AM$ rotates through the same angle. Select another division point $B$ (say, the fourth from $A$) and construct for it the same bundle of 12 straight lines as for point $A$. For each division point $M$, mark an acute angle between lines $AM$ and $BM$ (all these angles are the same!).

From the theorem on rotating lines it follows that if we continue all 23 constructed lines to the intersection, then all 110 intersection points (not counting $A$ and $B$) lie on the same circle - ten on each.
