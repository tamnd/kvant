In this issue we offer interesting and entertaining tasks that require neither pencil nor paper. These problems can be used in quizzes, math battles and KBNs.

1. Ten coins are arranged as shown in figure 1. What is the smallest number of coins that must be removed so that the centers of any three of the remaining coins are not located at the vertices of an equilateral triangle?

2. Think of four integers whose sum and product is an odd number.

3. The clock shows 8 hours 20 minutes. What is the angle between the arrows?

4. Can the sum of three consecutive natural numbers be a prime number?

5. Let's start counting the fingers on the right hand. The first will be the big one, the second will be the index finger, the third will be the middle one, the fourth will be the ring finger, the fifth will be the minsk finger, the sixth will be the ring finger again, the seventh will be the middle one, the eighth will be the index finger, the ninth will be the big one, the tenth will be the index finger, etc. Which finger will be 1979?

6. Are there triangles whose midpoints of all altitudes lie on the same straight line?

7. Very tired, the physicist went to bed at seven o'clock in the evening, setting the alarm clock for 9 o'clock the next morning. How many hours did he manage to sleep before the alarm went off?

8. 7 candles were burning, three of them were extinguished. How many are left?

9. The sides of the triangle are 17, 35 and 18. What is its area?

10. What is the last digit of the product of all odd numbers from 1 to 99?

11. When Kolya came out of the forest with his dog Zhuchka, his brother Vitya headed from the village into the forest. The bug immediately ran towards the house, but when it met Vitya, it turned and rushed towards Kolya. Having reached Kolya, she turned again and ran to Vita. So she ran until the brothers met. What distance did Zhuchka run if Kolya at a speed of 3 km/hour, Vitya at a speed of

5 km/hour, and the Bug ran at a speed of 8 km/hour? The distance from the forest to the village is 4 km.

12. Two packages fly towards each other at speeds of 15,000 km/h and 21,000 km/h. Their old sites are located at a distance of 1979 km from one another. What will be the distance between the packages one minute before the collision?

13. What number is divisible (without a remainder) by all numbers?

14. The titles of which literary works begin with the numbers 3, 12, 20, 80000?

15. In an equilateral triangle, the base is twice the length of the height. What are its angles?

16. An angle of 1° is examined through a magnifying glass, which gives fourfold magnification. What is the size of each angle?

We also offer several games. In each of them there is a strategy that allows one of the players to win. Try not just to play, but also to find that winning strategy.

Game 1. There are two piles of pebbles: one has 13, and the other has 10 pebbles. Two people take turns taking pebbles: either any number from an arbitrary pile, or equally from both piles. The one who takes the last stones wins.

Game 2. From a pile of 25 pebbles, two take turns taking one, two or three pebbles. The one who has an even number of pebbles at the end wins.

Game 3. There are two piles of candies, 9 in each. Two people play this game: in one move you need to move one candy from one pile to another and eat two candies from one pile. The moves are made one by one. The one who cannot make a move loses.

Game 4. Two players place their pieces on the chessboard: the first one is white, and the second one is black. Then they take turns moving their chips each time to one of the adjacent (horizontal or vertical) fields. The winner is the one who bets his fika on his opponent's fika.

Game 5. There is a piece in the corner of the chessboard - a knight. Two people take turns marking one square at a time on the board with chalk so that the knight can go to any unmarked square without standing in the displaced squares. The one who cannot make the next move loses.
