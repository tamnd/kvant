If a triangle is divided by its middle lines into four triangles and a circle is drawn around each of them, then the figure depicted in this figure will be obtained. The middle circle - circumscribed about the inner triangle - is the Euler circle. Sometimes it is called the circle of nine points: it turns out that it passes not only through the bases of the medians of the main triangle, but also through the bases of its altitudes and the midpoints of the segments connecting the vertices and the orthocenter of the triangle. This circle is described in more detail on p. 36.
