LET'S CALCULATE THE NUMBER $e$

Us. 4 it was proven that the number $e = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^n$ is equal to the sum of the series $1 + \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} + \dots$. However, it is possible to specify a series whose partial sums converge to $e$ most quickly than the partial sums of the series $1 + \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} + \dots$. Below are several problems, solving which, with the help of several calculations, you will learn to accurately calculate many digits of the number $e$.

1. Prove that for $n \geqslant 2$ the following equality holds:

$$1 + 1 + \frac{1}{2!} +$$

$$+ \frac{1}{3!} + \dots + \frac{1}{n!} +$$

$$+ \frac{1}{n \cdot n!} = 3 - \frac{1}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 2!} -$$

$$-\frac{1}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3!} - \dots - \frac{1}{(n-1) \cdot n \cdot n!}$$

Note. Apply the method of mathematical induction.

2. Prove that

$$e = 3 -$$

$$- \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n+1) \cdot (n+2) \cdot (n+2)!}$$

3. Prove that

$$e = \frac{87}{32} -$$

$$- \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n+4) \cdot (n+5) \cdot (n+5)!}$$

4. $e = \frac{87}{32} -$$

$$- \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n+4) \cdot (n+5) \cdot (n+5)!} -$$

$$- R_k$. Rate the remainder $R_k$.

5. Calculate the approximate value of $e$, taking the goal of series (1), and estimate the accuracy of the approximation.

6. Prove that

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(n+4) \cdot (n+5) \cdot (n+5)!} < 0,00047.$$

G. Sorokin
