The oldest technical unit in the country

Moscow Order of Lenin Institute of Engineers of Geodesy, Aerial Photography and Cartoffini in the training of highly qualified specialists for the national economy, a significant contribution to the development of science and in connection with the 200th anniversary of its founding directed by Lenin.

The Moscow Order of Lenin Institute of Geodesy, Aerial Photography and Cartoffini Engineers traces its history back to the Konstantinovsky Surveying School, opened on May 14 (27), 1779. One of the grounds for the creation of such an educational institution was the signing by Catherine 11 in 1765 of the Manifesto on the General International Land of the Russian Empire. In 1766, the “Instruction of the Land Permanent to the General of the Entire Empire of Demarcated Lands” was published. In the specified instructions, in the part concerning filming, it was said: “Removing arbitrary images using the most reliable instrument - an astrolabe with accessories, which, having received, inspect with an attachment in its serviceability and maintain with extreme care so that they do not deteriorate.”

To organize and carry out work on the General International International Research of the Senate, there was a Mezhevaya Expedition as the Department of Education, in Moscow there was a Mezhevaya Office, in the provinces there were Mezhevye Offices; The title of landowners was established.

Naturally, the question arose about training people who mastered the art of measurements on Earth - surveyors (in modern terminology - geodesists).

By the decree of the Governing Senate of April 23, 1779, it was proposed to begin training landowners and students in the land-digging business at the Mekzhevoy Chancellery. In a report to the Senate on the Mekzhevoy expedition on May 16, 1779, the Mekzhevoy Chancellery added: “... the school... was opened today, May 14th..., the Blue Digger School is named Konstantinovsky.”

The co-founder of the school and its director was the first member (director) of the Mekzhevoy Chancellery, Chief Prosecutor of the Senate, Privy Councilor Sergei Rozhnov.

In the first year, 75 people studied at the school.

If you know that the first composition of K3U students more than a year ago consisted of land surveyors, i.e. people who were mature and educated, then the level of training in the survey school at that time must be recognized as the highest in the field of geodesy for the needs of the General Survey.

The first, most significant, form of K3U after its founding was carried out in 1819, when the charter of the school and the new staff were approved. Instead of 100 students, according to the new charter, there were 200. The training period was

The main reason for the next reorganization of the KZU was the shortage of land surveyors, which led to the transformation of the school into the Konstantinovsky Land Survey Institute in 1835.

The famous Russian writer S. T. Aksakov was appointed the first director of the KMI.

When receiving children of land surveyors and officials of the Land Survey Department, preference was given to them.

The duration of training was kept the same - 4 years; Particular attention was paid to the necessity of practical training in geodesy in the field, for which time was allocated in the last two years of study from June 15 to August 1. The following subjects were added: algebra, physics, geodesy, agronomy. A great achievement is the subject “geodesy” as an independent subject, although geodesy was studied quite thoroughly at school. Along with special and physical and mathematical disciplines, more attention was paid to the study of geodesy, always a thorough, leading discipline of the institute. Much attention was also paid to the teaching of higher mathematics, mechanics and other functional sciences. Such outstanding scientists as S. A. Chaplgin and N. N. Luzin were involved in teaching mathematics at the institute. How mathematics was taught at the Land Survey Institute can be judged by the review of the famous military geodesist A.P. Bolotov, who, having beaten geometry classes at the institute, said: “You teach geometry as thoroughly as anywhere else.”
At KMI, exceptionally greater importance was attached to additional training in astronomy and geodesy at the Pulkovo General School, where people were sent to prepare for professorships. The Pulkovo Observatory directly participated in the training of astronomers and geodesists of the Mezhevoy and then the Moscow Geodetic Institute until 1932.

The Konstantinovsky Land Survey Institute has always carried out great scientific work. Constant observations were carried out on astrophysical

Since the 40s, centuries have passed, teachers and students of KMI have participated in many scientific experiments - in the Astrakhan, East Siberian, Altai, expeditions to Spinbergen, in the Russian polar expedition of Toll, as well as in filming many cities in Russia. After graduating from the institute in 1896, K. A. Tsvetkov was sent to participate in the polar expedition of Admiral S. O. Makarov. The director of the institute received a review from Admiral S. O. Makarov: “I sincerely thank international engineer K. A. Tsvetkov for his appointment to Ermak. In an environment completely foreign to him, he soon got used to it and was an energetic collaborator in surveying ice, hydrology and meteorology. I have the best memories of him.”

The students of KMI reacted very sensitively to de-despotism, violence and arbitrariness that took place in the country under the tsarist system: from time to time “riots” broke out at the institute. In 1891, senior students V. Bonch-Bruevich, N. Khavsky and V. Yarmolenko were expelled from the institute for admitting to “providing direct resistance to the administration by action.”

During the period of the first Russian revolution at the Land Survey Institute, the revolutionary mood of students was especially sharply expressed; Because of this, by order of the reactionary Stolpin government, the institute was closed for two academic years.

Geodetic education achieved true calculations after the Great October Socialist Revolution. An outstanding role was played by the Decree on the creation of the Supreme Geodetic Administration, which was signed by Vladimir Ilch Lenin on March 15, 1919.

In 1930, the Faculty of Geodesy was expanded into the Moscow Geodetic Institute with five departments, and the Faculty of Land Management was removed from the institute and transferred to the People's Commissariat of Land. In 1936, the Geodetic Institute was changed to the Moscow Institute of Geodesy, Aerial Photography and Cartography Engineers. Three faculties were created in it: geodetic, cartographic and geodetic instrumentation.

Currently, the institute trains engineers in the following specializations: astronomical geodesy, marine geodesy (since 1974), applied geodesy, space geodesy (since 1968), aerial photogeodesy, map publishing, design and compilation of maps, optical and optical-electronic instrumentation, natural resources research (since 1976).

At MIIGAiK, as before, mathematics remains one of the main subjects and is studied in large quantities.

The educational process in mathematics is constantly being improved. Based on the new programs, work programs are drawn up for all specialties of MIIGAiK, taking into account the requests of special departments. For example, at the Faculty of Geodesy, in addition to the main sections of mathematics, non-doctoral sections of differential geometry and Laplace control are studied; at the Faculty of Optical Mechanics - Fourier transform and operational calculus; at the Faculty of Aerophotogeodetics - Fourier transform and the theory of random functions; at the cartographic faculty they give information about conformal mapping.
