True, the value of $\hbar$ is very small, and for ordinary macrosoptic oscillators this abrupt change in energy is practically not observed. However, there are special macroscopic systems where discontinuity plays a certain role (for example, lasers). The correctness of our expression for the oscillator energy has been verified with great accuracy for many types of oscillators.

There is one more important property of a quantum oscillator, which we also obtained. When the energy is at its minimum, the classical oscillator is at rest in the equilibrium position; Meanwhile, the quantum oscillator oscillates in the direction of the lowest energy state (they are called “zero oscillations”). The kinetic and potential energy of these vibrations is of the order of $\hbar \omega$. In this case, the average value of the oscillator coordinate is zero, and the average value of the squared coordinate is given by the above formula for $q_0^2$. This remarkable property of quantum oscillators has been well tested in experiment and plays an important role in modern physics.

If we consider sound vibrations in a solid body as quantum oscillators, we obtain that at absolute zero temperature the atoms of the solid body are not motionless, but perform zero oscillations. And this fact was confirmed by experiments on light scattering at low temperatures! If we consider electromagnetic waves that can propagate in empty space as a set of oscillators, then we will come to the conclusion that in emptiness, even when there are no particles or quanta in it, zero oscillations of the electromagnetic field should be found. And these fluctuations were also discovered experimentally!

Understanding arises in the process of work

We will introduce an explanation of several incomprehensible words: quantization, wave process associated with a particle, quantum oscillator, etc. And we began to act, not really their concepts. Nevertheless, we have found how the energy of the hydrogen atom depends on the quantum number $n$; learned that a quantum oscillator in the lowest energy state oscillates, and even began to apply the results of oscillator quantization to such an object as oscillations of an electromagnetic field in a vacuum. And as a result of these actions, at least partial understanding arose.

So, understanding arises in the process of work. After all, if we tried to achieve complete understanding before we began our calculation processes, nothing would work. Understanding does not arise suddenly, but as you progress in your work. Instead, the work progresses as understanding deepens.

In order to achieve a deeper understanding, you need to solve problems in this area of ​​physics yourself. Passive study gives only a faint idea of ​​the beauties that are revealed through independent work.

Our Readers' Assignment

1. The inscribed circle of triangle $ABC$ is tangent to its sides $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$ at points $G_a$, $G_b$ and $G_c$ respectively. Excircles touch sides $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$ at points $N_a$, $N_b$ and $N_c$. Let $r$, $r_a$, $r_b$ and $r_c$ be the radii of the inscribed and excircle respectively. Prove the following equalities:

$$\frac{1}{|AG_c| \cdot |G_c B|} + \frac{1}{|BG_a| \cdot |G_a C|} + \frac{1}{|CG_b| \cdot |G_b A|} = \frac{1}{|AN_c| \cdot |N_c B|} + \frac{1}{|BN_a| \cdot |N_a C|} + \frac{1}{|CN_b| \cdot |N_b A|} = \frac{1}{r^2};$$

$$\frac{|BN_a|^2}{r_a r_b} + \frac{|CN_b|^2}{r_b r_c} + \frac{|AN_c|^2}{r_c r_a} = \frac{1}{r^2};$$
