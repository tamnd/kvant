The free surface of water in the gravitational field of the Earth is always established in such a way that at any point on the surface the gravitational force is redistributed towards it, that is, directed towards the center of the Earth along the radius. Due to the presence of a cavity, the surface of the ocean is curved - the total force of gravity (from the Earth and from the side of the “cavity”) is directed, generally speaking, not along the radius of the Earth.

In further reasoning, we will use a similar one between the electrostatic field of an exact charge Q and the gravitational field of an exact mass M. (Recall that the gravitational field of a ball of mass M is equivalent to the gravitational field of an exact mass M placed at the center of the ball.)

The force acting on the test charge q from the side of the charge Q and the force acting on the test mass m from the side of the mass M are equal, respectively,

The reasoning given in the conditions of the problem is not correct: they take into account only the potential energy of the interaction of the balls and do not take into account the energy of the power field of individual balls.

The energy of the electric field of an individual (isolated) ball with charge $q$ is equal to the work that is done when charging the ball. It can be considered as the energy of a spherical capacitor, the outer lining of which is infinitely further away (from the ball). This energy can be calculated using the formula $W = \frac{1}{2} qU$, where $q$ is the charge of the capacitor (charge of the ball), $U$ is the potential difference of its plates. In our case, $U$ is equal to the potential Ф of the ball with respect to infinity (where the potential is pleasant to zero): $U = \varphi = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r}$, where $r$ is the radius of the ball.

Thus, $W = \frac{1}{2} q\varphi = \frac{q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}$. The energy of the system before connecting the wire balls is $W_1 = \frac{q_1^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r} + \frac{q_2^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r} + \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l}$, and after connecting -

$$W_2 = 2 \frac{q^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r} + \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l} = \frac{(q_1 + q_2)^2}{16\pi\varepsilon_0 r} + \frac{(q_1 + q_2)^2}{16\pi\varepsilon_0 l},$$
