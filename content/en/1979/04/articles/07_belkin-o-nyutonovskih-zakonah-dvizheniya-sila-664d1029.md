About Newton's laws of motion. Strength

The word "strength" in conditional speech has many meanings and allows for many interpretations. They talk about the purchasing power of money and the power of habit, about willpower and the power of public opinion, about moral strength and even... about evil spirits. In these examples and in many other silos, “power” does not have a clear and unambiguous explanation. In any case, this word has a completely different meaning in them.

But in mechanics, force is determined in perfect order and unambiguously. As F. Engels said, in mechanics “...they really know what silosa means.” This article will discuss force as a mechanical concept and mechanical quantity.

Interactions and forces

In our first article on the laws of motion («Kvant», 1979, No. 2) it was already mentioned that force is the influence of one body on another. But we have seen that the influence of a body is always mutual - bodies always interact, that is, they influence each other, and impart acceleration to each other. This means that when two bodies interact, a force is applied to each of them. The force applied to the body is the reason for its acceleration. Newton defines force this way: “Applied force is an action performed on a body to change its state of rest or uniform motion.” This is the meaning of the concept of force in mechanics. But for now this is just a concept. We are interested in force as a physical quantity that can be changed and expressed in numbers.

The words “the influence of one body on another” contain something unclear, not

certain. What does influence mean? How does it happen? The body falls down with roots because it is influenced by the Earth. A door loaded with a spring moves with its roots because it is influenced by the spring. A piece of iron moves with its roots under the influence of a magnet. But do these influences influence one another? It is clear that they are completely different in nature. At the time of Newton, nothing was known about the nature of all these influences. But in mechanics, it turns out, you may not even know this. The only important thing is that the effect on the body is that rooting is imparted to the body. This means that influences (forces) that are completely different in nature can be compared with each other if we are able to express them in numbers. And for this it is necessary to establish how the forces are connected with the roots communicated by them.

Newton's second law
This connection was established by Newton from the analysis of experimental data. He formulated it in the form of the second law of motion (Newton's second law).

As Newton himself stated, this law states: “The change in motion is proportional to the applied driving force and has the direction in which this force acts.”

This formulation contains the basic idea of ​​mechanics: force causes a change in motion, and not the motion itself, as they thought before Newton (and as people who have not studied mechanics still think). However, the words “change in movement” are somewhat mysterious: after all, movement is a process, not a quantity; Only quantities can be proportional to each other. It is also unclear how Newton knew that there was just such a law - the law of proportionality between force and change in motion. Of course, this law was established on the basis of experience or experiences. But which ones? Newton does not report this. He simply “tells” bodies to behave one way and not another.

We can now assume that the words “change in motion” are familiar, since rooting represents a rapidly changing speed of movement of a body if its mass is constant *). As for the experience from which the connection between rooting and force can be established, it must consist in the fact that different bodies (different in mass) are supported by the action of the same force; At the same time, it is necessary to change the roots that these bodies receive. The quantity that turns out to be the same for all bodies in these changes will serve as a measure of the force applied to these bodies.

In nature there is one and only force, which does not depend on the body to which it is applied. This is the elastic force of a deformed body, such as a spring. This force is called the elastic force. The peculiarity of the elastic force is that it depends only on how deformed the body is. Thus, the elastic force of a spring depends only on its stretching (or rolling) compared to its original length (this fact was studied in Newton’s time). Therefore, if you keep the tension of the spring constant (fixed), then it acts on any body attached to it with the same force, regardless of what the mass of this body is (of course, it is necessary that the mass of the force of the spring be inconvertibly small compared to the mass of the body attached to it).

So, the experiment that allows the connection between rooting and force can only be carried out using a spring. This experience is described in the textbook “Physics 8” (p. 85). It turns out that a spring, rolled (or stretched) to a fixed length, imparts roots to the bodies attached to it, the directions of which always coincide with the direction of action of the elastic force on the bodies, and the absolute values ​​are different for a body with different masses.

*) In modern books they usually write, as if “correcting” Newton, together “change in motion” - “change in momentum.” In this case, the quantity of motion is understood as the product of a body’s mass and its speed (this quantity is more often called impulse). It is not difficult to understand that this interpretation of Newton’s words does not contradict the one we accept.

But for all bodies the product of the mass $m$ of the body and its acceleration $a$, that is, the value $ma$, remains the same value. It follows that the force $F$ is determined by the equation
$$\vec{F} = ma.$$
Newton's second law is now formulated this way: the force acting on a body is equal to the product of the body's mass and its own acceleration.

It is clear that this formulation closely corresponds to Newton’s, since it follows from it that acceleration (“change of motion” according to Newton) is proportional to force:
$$\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m}.$$
So, force, which was only said to be a measure of the influence of one body on another, has now become a physical quantity that can be expressed in numbers. You can set the unit of measurement for it. As a unit of force, it is natural to take the force that imparts to a body with a mass equal to one (1 kg) an acceleration equal to one (1 m/s²). It is fair that this unit was given the name “newton” (N) in honor of the creator of classical mechanics.

$$\vec{F} = ma - \text{уравнение движения}$$

Newton's second law is the most important law of mechanics, because it is it that allows us to solve any problem about the motion of bodies. The fact is that acceleration $a$, included in the equation of the second law, represents rapid changes (in magnitude and direction) of the speed of a body over time:

$$\vec{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}.$$

⟦folio 13⟧

It must be remembered that behind this vector equation there are three equations for the projections of acceleration on the coordinate axes:

$$a_x = \frac{\Delta v_x}{\Delta t}, \quad a_y = \frac{\Delta v_y}{\Delta t}, \quad a_z = \frac{\Delta v_z}{\Delta t},$$

where $\Delta v_x, \Delta v_y, \Delta v_z$ are changes in velocity projections, $\Delta t$ is the time period over which the changes occurred.

In turn, the projection of velocity is the rapid changes in the coordinates of the body over time:

$$v_x = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}, \quad v_y = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t}, \quad v_z = \frac{\Delta z}{\Delta t}.$$

This means that behind the acceleration values ​​in Newton’s second law, in reality, in a complex form, the change in coordinates with time is “hidden”: rapid changes, rapid changes in coordinates (in the language of higher mathematics, this fancy phrase is expressed more simply: the second derivative of coordinates with respect to time).

Having solved the equation $\vec{F} = ma$, we obtain an expression showing how the coordinates of the body's motion change over time. Therefore, we can find out the position of the body at any moment in time. And this is the main task of mechanics. That's what this science is for. That is why the equation expressing Newton's second law is called the equation of motion.

To create this equation and solve it, you need to know the force acting on the body. In addition, you also need to know the so-called initial conditions - the coordinates of the body and its speed at some initial moment of time. On the contrary, if the law of motion of a body is known from experience, the equation of motion (Newton's second law) allows one to find the force applied to the body. If, for example, the coordinate $x$ changes with time according to the law $x = x_0 + v_x t$, that is, the body moves along the axis $x$ with a constant speed $v_x$, then, according to Newton’s second law, the force acting on the body (along the axis $x$) is zero. If the coordinate $x$ changes with time according to the formula

$$x = x_0 + v_x t + \frac{a_x t^2}{2},$$

The body moves along the $x$ axis with constant acceleration $a_x$. According to the second law, this means that a constant force $\vec{F}_x$ is applied to the body, directed along the axis $x$ and equal in magnitude to $ma_x$.

What does it mean to “know the power”?

Knowing motion means knowing how the coordinates of a body depend on time. This is clear from the examples just given. But what does it mean to “know the power”?

To answer this question, remember that strength is always the result of interaction. If a force acts on a body, then there is some other body from which it “emanates”. And the force depends on the interaction of interacting bodies, on the distance between them. This follows from the fact that bodies infinitely distant from each other do not interact at all. But when moving, the distance between interacting bodies can change. Therefore, the forces of interaction between these bodies can also change. This means that the force applied to a given body depends on the position of the body’s motion relative to the body that acts on it. It may also depend on the speed of movement of the body relative to the body that “creates” this force.

Knowing force means knowing how force depends on the coordinates and speed of a body. And if the type of this dependence is known, then the corresponding expressions need to be substituted together with $\vec{F}$ into the formula $\vec{F} = ma$.

How to recognize this dependence? Here, as always, you need to turn to experience.

For forces studied in mechanics, interactions from coordinates and velocity have been known for a long time.

In 1678, Robert Hooke (1635-1703) experimentally established that the force of a spring is proportional to its elongation. “What is the stretch, is the force” - in such a short phrase Hooke announced the departure

the law covered by them. Teleri this law bears his name. It is expressed by the formula

$$F = -kx.$$

Here $k$ is the proportionality coefficient characteristic of each spring: $x$ is the elongation of the spring, that is, the difference in the length of the deformed and non-deformed spring. If the coordinates of the free end of the spring are compared to the position of this end when the spring is not deformed, then $x$ is the coordinate of the end of the spring. Consequently, formula $F = -kx$ precisely shows how the elastic force depends on the coordinate.

For another important mechanical force - the force of universal gravity - the type of dependence on coordinates was established by Newton himself. This force is determined by the formula

$$F = \gamma \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}.$$

Here $r$ is the distance between the interacting bodies, $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the masses of these bodies, $\gamma$ is the proportionality coefficient. The distance $r$ determines the coordinates of one body relative to another. In terrestrial conditions, the manifestation of the force of universal gravitation is the force of gravity, equal in modulus to $m|\vec{g}|$, where $m$ is the mass of the body, $\vec{g}$ is the acceleration of free fall. The coordinates of the body do not seem to be included in this expression. However, it must be borne in mind that the value of $|\vec{g}|$ depends on the distance to the body from the center of the Earth, that is, on the coordinates relative to this center. So the force of gravity depends on the coordinates. (Only near the Earth’s surface the acceleration $|\vec{g}|$ can be approximately, although with great accuracy, considered constant.)

Some later, already in the 18th century, a third mechanical force was studied - the force of gravity. This is the only mechanical force that depends on the coordinates of the body to which it belongs, and on the relative speed of the body and is directed against the speed.

Two of the three mechanical forces - the elastic force and the gravitational force - are, as it turned out, of an electromagnetic nature. The nature of electromagnetic forces and the laws they obey became known about a hundred years ago. The nature of mechanical forces - the force of universal gravity - was explained only sixty years ago. And the laws of mechanics have been used for three hundred years. This is due to the fact that for mechanics the nature of forces does not exist. It is only important to know how forces depend on coordinates and speed.

The forces add up

So, experience shows that mechanical forces depend on the coordinates and speeds of body movements. And experience shows that they do not depend on anything else. In particular, a force applied to a body does not depend on whether it acts on the body “alone” or together with other forces. The forces applied to the body do not “prevent” each other from imparting acceleration to it. Of course, about a body to which several forces are applied, it cannot be said that it also has several accelerations. He has one acceleration equal to the vector sum of those accelerations that each of the forces would impart to him if it were the only one. We can therefore say that there is only one force acting on the body, but it is the vector sum of all those applied to the body

Newton's third law

We have very little to say about Newton's third law. Not because the law is unimportant and does not deserve server destruction. On the contrary, it contains the most important idea of ​​mechanics. But because we have already discussed this law and we have already used it.

The law of motion, which is listed under the third number in Newton’s book and which retains this traditional number in all books on mechanics from Newton to the present day, states that forces arise during the interaction of bodies, that the forces acting on each of the participants in the interaction are identical in modulo and opposite in direction. We already talked about this in our previous article. It was indicated there that when two bodies interact, each of them receives acceleration; these accelerations are directed in opposite directions and the ratio of the accelerated model is equal to the inverse ratio of the masses of the interacting bodies. All this can be expressed by the formula

$$m_1 a_1 = - m_2 a_2.$$
