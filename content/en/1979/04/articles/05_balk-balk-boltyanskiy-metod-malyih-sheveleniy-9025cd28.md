Small movements method

When using geometric statements for verisimilitude, when searching for counterexamples, a mathematician often argues like this:

— I’ll take a case where the statement is confirmed, and I’ll “move” one point (or a segment, or some other set of points). Is there any way I can get a case where the statement is not confirmed?

Task 4a. Is it possible to arrange two concussive regular metrahedrons in space, so that when they are projected backward onto some - one and the same - plane, the projection of one metrahedron lies entirely (including its boundary) inside the projection of the other metrahedron?

Solution e. Let us arrange the metrahedron $T'$ so that its face $A'B'C'$ lies in the projection plane $\alpha$ (Fig. 4).
