International space crews

March 2, 1977 will go down as a significant date in the history of astronautics. On this day, for the first time on the Soyuz-28 spacecraft, an international crew was launched into space orbit, consisting of the commander of the ship, pilot-cosmonaut of the USSR, Hero of the Soviet Union Alexei Gubarev, and cosmonaut-researcher, citizen of the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic, Vladimir Remek.

The launch of the Soyuz-28 spacecraft opened a new stage in the exploration and exploration of outer space for peaceful purposes, carried out by socialist countries under the Intercosmos cooperation program.
The flight program of the first international crew of cosmonauts was a busy spring - it included the docking of the Soyuz-28 spacecraft with the orbital research complex Salyut-6 - Soyuz-27, piloted by cosmonauts Yu. Romanenko and G. Grechko, and conducting joint research and experiments on board the station.

On June 17, 1978, the Soyuz-30 spacecraft was launched in the Soviet Union, on board of which was the second international crew consisting of the commander of the ship, twice Hero of the Soviet Union, pilot-cosmonaut of the USSR Pyotr Klimuk and cosmonaut-researcher citizen of the People's Republic of Poland Miroslavov Germashevsky.

The field program included docking with the orbital research complex Salyut-6 - Soyuz-29, piloted by cosmonauts V. Kovalenok and A. Ivachenkov, and conducting joint research and experiments on board the station.

On August 26, 1978, the Soyuz-31 spacecraft was launched, piloted by the third international crew consisting of the commander of the ship, twice Hero of the Soviet Union, pilot-cosmonaut of the USSR Valery Bykovsky and cosmonaut-researcher citizen of the GDR Sigmund Jen.

The field program included docking with the orbital research complex Salyut-6 - Soyuz-29 and the continuation, together with Kovalenok and Ivachenkov, of research and experiments begun by previous crews.

The idea of ​​international camps for cosmonauts from socialist countries under the Intercosmos program was envisioned by the Soviet Union back in 1976. In July and September, a meeting of representatives of the People's Republic of Belarus, Hungary, the German Democratic Republic, the People's Republic of Poland, resource specialists from Cuba, the Mongolia, the SSR, the USSR and the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic was held in Moscow, at which the organizations discussed issues related to preparations for international manned space flights.
