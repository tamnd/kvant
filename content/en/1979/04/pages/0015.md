It must be remembered that behind this vector equation there are three equations for the projections of acceleration on the coordinate axes:

$$a_x = \frac{\Delta v_x}{\Delta t}, \quad a_y = \frac{\Delta v_y}{\Delta t}, \quad a_z = \frac{\Delta v_z}{\Delta t},$$

where $\Delta v_x, \Delta v_y, \Delta v_z$ are changes in velocity projections, $\Delta t$ is the time period over which the changes occurred.

In turn, the projection of velocity is the rapid changes in the coordinates of the body over time:

$$v_x = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}, \quad v_y = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t}, \quad v_z = \frac{\Delta z}{\Delta t}.$$

This means that behind the acceleration values ​​in Newton’s second law, in reality, in a complex form, the change in coordinates with time is “hidden”: rapid changes, rapid changes in coordinates (in the language of higher mathematics, this fancy phrase is expressed more simply: the second derivative of coordinates with respect to time).

Having solved the equation $\vec{F} = ma$, we obtain an expression showing how the coordinates of the body's motion change over time. Therefore, we can find out the position of the body at any moment in time. And this is the main task of mechanics. That's what this science is for. That is why the equation expressing Newton's second law is called the equation of motion.

To create this equation and solve it, you need to know the force acting on the body. In addition, you also need to know the so-called initial conditions - the coordinates of the body and its speed at some initial moment of time. On the contrary, if the law of motion of a body is known from experience, the equation of motion (Newton's second law) allows one to find the force applied to the body. If, for example, the coordinate $x$ changes with time according to the law $x = x_0 + v_x t$, that is, the body moves along the axis $x$ with a constant speed $v_x$, then, according to Newton’s second law, the force acting on the body (along the axis $x$) is zero. If the coordinate $x$ changes with time according to the formula

$$x = x_0 + v_x t + \frac{a_x t^2}{2},$$

The body moves along the $x$ axis with constant acceleration $a_x$. According to the second law, this means that a constant force $\vec{F}_x$ is applied to the body, directed along the axis $x$ and equal in magnitude to $ma_x$.

What does it mean to “know the power”?

Knowing motion means knowing how the coordinates of a body depend on time. This is clear from the examples just given. But what does it mean to “know the power”?

To answer this question, remember that strength is always the result of interaction. If a force acts on a body, then there is some other body from which it “emanates”. And the force depends on the interaction of interacting bodies, on the distance between them. This follows from the fact that bodies infinitely distant from each other do not interact at all. But when moving, the distance between interacting bodies can change. Therefore, the forces of interaction between these bodies can also change. This means that the force applied to a given body depends on the position of the body’s motion relative to the body that acts on it. It may also depend on the speed of movement of the body relative to the body that “creates” this force.

Knowing force means knowing how force depends on the coordinates and speed of a body. And if the type of this dependence is known, then the corresponding expressions need to be substituted together with $\vec{F}$ into the formula $\vec{F} = ma$.

How to recognize this dependence? Here, as always, you need to turn to experience.

For forces studied in mechanics, interactions from coordinates and velocity have been known for a long time.
