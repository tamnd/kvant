Task 4a. Is it possible to arrange two concussive regular metrahedrons in space, so that when they are projected backward onto some - one and the same - plane, the projection of one metrahedron lies entirely (including its boundary) inside the projection of the other metrahedron?

Solution e. Let us arrange the metrahedron $T'$ so that its face $A'B'C'$ lies in the projection plane $\alpha$ (Fig. 4). We first position the second metrahedron $T$ so that the projection of its face $ABC$ onto the plane $\alpha$ coincides with the triangle $A'B'C'$. Now, by moving the metrahedron $T$, it will try to ensure that its projection onto the plane $\alpha$ is entirely inside the triangle $A'B'C'$. First of all, use a surface meter $T$ so that inside the triangle $A'B'C'$ there is a projection of the vertex $C$. This can be done, for example, like this: overmeter $T$ around edge $AB$ until the position at which edge $CD$ becomes perpendicular to plane $\alpha$. In this case, the projection of the metrahedron $T$ turns out to be an equilateral triangle $A_1B_1C_1$, and $|A_1B_1| = |A'B'|$ (Fig. 5).

Let us now move the metrahedron $T$ so that the projection of the edge $AB$ is shorter than the segment $A'B'$. To do this, mark the midpoints $F$ and $K$ of the edges $AB$ and $CD$ and the surface meter $T$ at a small angle around the straight line $FK$. After such a movement, the projection of the metrahedron $T$ onto the plane $\alpha$ will turn out to be a trapezoid

$A_2B_2C_2D_2$ (Fig. 6). If the angle of rotation is small enough, then this trapezoid will be located in the triangle $A'B'C'$, with side $A_2B_2$ lying on the boundary of this triangle.

In order for the trapezoid to be entirely inside the triangle $A'B'C'$, it is now sufficient to subject the metrahedron to one more movement, namely, to shift the metrahedron by a small vector, the collinear vector $FK$ (Fig. 7).

We see that the metrahedrons $T$ and $T'$ can be arranged in space in such a way that the projection of the metrahedron $T$ onto the plane $\alpha$ lies entirely inside the projection of the metrahedron $T'$ onto the same plane. Task 4a is solved.

This means that it is possible to drill a hole in the metrahedron $T'$ through which the metrahedron $T$ will pass (the hole in $T'$ should be drilled perpendicular to the plane $A'B'C'$, and so that the projection of the hole onto the plane $A'B'C'$ turns out to be some figure containing inside the trapezoid $A_3B_3C_3D_3$ and lying inside triangle $A'B'C'$.
