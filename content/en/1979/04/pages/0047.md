Problem 3. A bunch of singly charged positive ions Li$^+$(A=6) is emitted by emmeter “E”.
The nonons are accelerated by the electric field $u$, passing through a potential difference $U=3000$ V, and enter a chamber with a transverse magnetic field $|\vec{B}| = 3 \cdot 10^{-2}$ T (Fig. 4). Find the beam deflection values ​​$h$. Chamber length $L=15$ cm, electron charge $e=1,6 \cdot 10^{-19}$ C, promon mass $m_p=1,67 \cdot 10^{-24}$ e.

Having passed through the accelerating potential difference $U$, each one acquires kinetic energy

$$\frac{m|\vec{v}|^2}{2} = eU,$$

where $m$ is the mass of the ion, $\vec{v}$ is its speed. From here

$$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{\frac{2eU}{m}}.$$

At this speed he enters a chamber where only a magnetic field exists. The Lorentz force begins to act on it, which will impart centripetal acceleration to it. Since the Lorentz force at each moment is perpendicular to the speed of the ion, it will move in a circle. The radius of this circle can be found from the equation of motion of the ion

$$\frac{m|\vec{v}|^2}{R} = e|\vec{v}||\vec{B}|.$$

Taking into account the fact that $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{\frac{2eU}{m}}$, we get

$$R = \frac{m|\vec{v}|}{e|\vec{B}|} = \frac{1}{|\vec{B}|} \sqrt{\frac{2mU}{e}}.$$

The amount of beam deflection $h$ is found from simple geometric calculations (see Fig. 4):

$$h = R - \sqrt{R^2 - L^2} \approx 0,02 \text{ м}.$$
