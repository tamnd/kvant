$$\frac{m \left| \vec{v}_1 \right|^2}{2} = -q\varphi_1$$

And

$$\frac{m \left| \vec{v}_2 \right|^2}{2} = -q\varphi_2,$$

where does the particle velocity ratio come from?

$$\frac{\left| \vec{v}_1 \right|}{\left| \vec{v}_2 \right|} = \sqrt{\frac{\varphi_1}{\varphi_2}}.$$

In the direction of the $X$ axis, an electric force acts on the particles, so the projection of the velocity $v_x$ will increase: $v_{x_2} > v_{x_1}$. There are no forces acting on the particles in the direction of the $Y$ axis; therefore, the velocity projection $v_y$ will remain constant:

$$\left| \vec{v}_1 \right| \sin \alpha_1 = \left| \vec{v}_2 \right| \sin \alpha_2,$$

or

$$\frac{\left| \vec{v}_1 \right|}{\left| \vec{v}_2 \right|} = \frac{\sin \alpha_2}{\sin \alpha_1}.$$

Comparing two expressions for relating the models of particle velocities at points 1 and 2, we find the connection between the snus angles $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$:

$$\frac{\sin \alpha_1}{\sin \alpha_2} = \sqrt{\frac{\varphi_2}{\varphi_1}}.$$

Pay attention to this fact: the particle trajectory deviates in the direction of the normal to the equivotential surface if (as in the case we considered) the particle moves in the direction of increasing potential. This makes it possible to understand more complex motions of charges in inhomogeneous electric fields, for example, the principles of electrosystemic focusing. figure 2 shows the so-called electrical system line, composed of two coaxial cylinders; The dotted lines show sections of equivotential surfaces. Point $O$ is the source of the diverging beam of slow electrons, and point $O'$ is the image of the converging beam of fast electrons.

Task 2. We fly an electron into the space between the plates of a flat capacitor, between the plates maintaining a constant difference of the placed $U=60$ V (Fig. 3). Determine the minimum electron speed $\vec{v}_{\min}$ at which it reaches the top plate. The specific charge of an electron is $e/m=1,76 \cdot 10^{11}$ K/kg, and its density is $\alpha=60^\circ$.

When moving in a capacitor, the electron will be subject to a constant electric force directed against the field, that is, from the imprint plate to positive. Therefore, the projection of the electron velocity onto the $Y$ axis will uniformly decrease, and the projection onto the $X$ axis will remain constant. It is not difficult to show that the trajectory of the electron will be a parabola (check it yourself).

The minimum speed of the electron will correspond to the case when the parabola touches the upper plate with a capacitor.
