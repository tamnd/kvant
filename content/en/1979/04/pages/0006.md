Small movements method

When using geometric statements for verisimilitude, when searching for counterexamples, a mathematician often argues like this:

— I’ll take a case where the statement is confirmed, and I’ll “move” one point (or a segment, or some other set of points). Is there any way I can get a case where the statement is not confirmed?

Let's look at a simple example:

Problem 1. From a point P located inside a convex polygon, perpendiculars are lowered to its sides or to their extensions. Let us agree to call the base of the macroperpendicular “straight” if it belongs to the side, and “indirect” otherwise. Is it true that every interior point of any polygon has at least two direct projections?

Solution. Let us first take triangle ABC as the initial convex polygon. If it is acute, then all projections of any of its internal points are spicy. Let ΔABC be typangular.
