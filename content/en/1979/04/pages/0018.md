⟦folio 16⟧

LIVES FOR ALL MOVEMENTS OF ALL BODIES WITHOUT EXCEPTION. FOR MOVEMENTS, FAST AND SLOW, FOR BODIES LARGE AND SMALL.

AND ONLY AT THE BEGINNING OF THE XX CENTURY IT FOUND OUT THAT THIS IS NOT SO. Newtonian mechanics turns out to be incorrect, most likely, when bodies move at speeds close to the speed of light. This was established by A. Einstein's theory of relativity in 1905. This is due to the fact that the interaction between bodies is not transmitted instantly, as previously thought, but at a certain finite speed. This speed is exactly equal to the speed of light. It is clear that when the speed of movement of a body is close to the speed of transmission of interaction, the laws of mechanics become different. But even in the mechanics of the theory of relativity (in relativistic mechanics) we are dealing with familiar concepts and quantities - distances and time, speeds and accelerations, masses and forces. But the correspondences between them are completely different. These new formulas always include the ratio $\frac{v}{c}$ of the speed of the body $v$ to the speed of light $c$. When this ratio is small and can be considered equal to zero, the formulas immediately turn into formulas of Newtonian mechanics. Therefore, the emergence of the theory of relativity did not lead to either the abandonment of this mechanics or its revision.

Secondly, Newtonian mechanics is completely inapplicable to the movement of very small particles from which atoms and molecules are formed. This describes a new theory that appeared in physics fifty years ago. In this theory, even privileged mechanical concepts, such as trajectory, speed, acceleration, force, lose their general “mechanical” meaning. The very concept of movement here differs from that of grafting. But the appearance of this new theory (it is called quantum mechanics) will not affect Newtonian mechanics, which remains unshakable for those movements for which it was created.
