New books

We continue to publish summaries of books on mathematics and physics that are accessible and interesting to readers. In this issue we will talk about books published in the first quarter of 1979.

Mathematics

Publishing house "Nuka"

1. Khinchin A. Ya. Three pearls of number theory. 3rd edition. Volume 4 l., circulation 50,000 copies, price 20 kopecks.

This wonderful book is dedicated to the presentation of three number theories.

The first theorem is named after the Dutch mathematician Van der Waerden and has the following formulation:

Let k and l be arbitrary natural numbers. Then there is a natural number n such that when any segment of a natural series of length n is partitioned in any way into k classes (among which I include empty ones), at least in one of these classes there is an arithmetic progression of length l.

The second chapter examines the Landdu-Shinrelman hypothesis, which asserts the validity of a certain inequality. Proposed in 1931, it attracted the attention of a wide range of researchers in all countries, but proved difficult to define; It was only in 1942 that it was proved by the young American mathematician Mann. A year later, Artin and Sherk published a new proof of it, cities that are lower, although

still perfectly elementary. According to Khinchin, the Artin-Sherk design is “a remarkable combination of subtlety in structure and the utmost elementaryness of the method.”

Finally, the third chapter provides an elementary solution to Waring's problem; proves that for any natural number n there is a natural number k such that any natural number can be represented as a sum of at most k nth powers of some natural numbers. This hypothesis was expressed by Waring back in the 18th century (its special case is Lagrange’s wonderful theorem: what natural number is the sum of no more than is expressed), but only at the beginning of our century (1907) Hilbert proved its validity in its entirety. However, his proof was very cumbersome and not elementary, as, indeed, were the later proofs of Hardy, Leathwood and I.M. Vinogradov. Therefore, mathematicians faced with such an apparently elementary problem as Waring’s problem continued to search for its solution, which would not require concepts and methods that go beyond elementary arithmetic. An elementary solution was obtained by the Soviet mathematician Yu. V. Linnik in 1942 (however, his “elementary” proof is also not very simple). The third chapter of the book is devoted to the presentation of Linnik’s proof.

The author of the book, A. Ya. Khinchin, is a widely known specialist in number theory and a wonderful teacher. His book “The Three Pearls of Number Theory” is certainly one of the pearls of popular mathematical literature. Therefore, I would like all mathematical cooks to work on this book with pencil and paper.

The 2nd edition was published in 1948.

Publishing house "Mir"

2. Dudeney G. Canterbury puzzles. Translation from English Volume 21 l., circulation 100,000 copies, price 1 rub. 40 k.

The author of this book, Henry Ernest Duedeny (1857-1930), is one of the most famous creators of puzzles; Along with Sam Loyd, he is rightfully considered a classic of this genre. It is unlikely that he needs to be introduced as a reader «Kvant»: in 1975, the publishing house “Mir”, in a series of books on general mathematics, published Duedeni’s collection “520 puzzles”, several problems from which were reprinted by our magazine («Kvant», 1975, No. 2).

Canterbury Puzzles is a wonderful collection of puzzles. Actually, the “Canterbury Puzzles” constitute only the first chapter. The story of its writing is as follows. It is known that the classic work of English literature of the 14th century, Jackfrey Chaucer's book “The Canterbury Tales” remained unfinished. Taking advantage of this, Duedeny supplemented it with new, supposedly found, pages: Chaucer’s characters assign each other different tasks. The chapter “Canterbury Puzzles” sets the tone for the entire book: each subsequent chapter (except one) also has its own complex line; Almost every puzzle is framed in the form of a compelling story, with many colorful characters and relaxed dialogues. With its fictionalization, the collection “Canterbury Puzzles” stands out among all Duedeni’s books. In style, perhaps, it can be compared with Lewis Carroll’s book “The Story with Knots” (M., “Mir”, 1973).
