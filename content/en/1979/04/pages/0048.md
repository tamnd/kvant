⟦folio 46⟧

Let us consider the motion of an individual electron whose velocity vector lies in the figure 5 plane and makes an angle $\alpha$ with the vector $\vec{B}$.

Since the electron is only affected by the Lorentz force, the projection of which on the direction of the field is zero, the projection of the electron velocity on this direction will remain constant and equal to $|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha$.

In a plane perpendicular to the vector $\vec{B}$, the electron will move in a circle with a linear speed equal to $|\vec{v}| \sin \alpha$. Let us write down the equation of motion of an electron in a circle:

$$\frac{m(|\vec{v}| \sin \alpha)^2}{R} = e |\vec{v}| |\vec{B}| \sin \alpha.$$

From here we find the radius of the circle $R$ and the period of revolution $T$:

$$R = \frac{m |\vec{v}| \sin \alpha}{e |\vec{B}|},$$

$$T = \frac{2\pi R}{|\vec{v}| \sin \alpha} = \frac{2\pi m}{e |\vec{B}|}.$$

At the same time, the electron moves uniformly across the field with a speed $|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha$.

If after time $\tau = \frac{L}{|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha}$ the electron ends up at point $O'$ (see Fig. 5), this will mean that it has made an integer $n$ full revolutions:

$$\tau = nT,$$

or

$$\frac{L}{|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha} = n \frac{2\pi m}{e |\vec{B}_n|},$$

where $n=1, 2, 3, \ldots$. As a result, we obtain a discrete set of magnetic induction vector model values ​​at which the electron will hit point $O'$:

$$|\vec{B}_n| = \frac{2\pi m |\vec{v}| \cos \alpha}{e L} n.$$

Strictly speaking, the values ​​of the focusing fields depend on the angle $\alpha$. However, at small angles $\cos \alpha \approx 1$ and

$$|\vec{B}_n| \approx \frac{2\pi m |\vec{v}|}{e L} n.$$
