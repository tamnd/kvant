It will gradually lose speed and be bent more and more by the magnetic field (see Fig. 6). At the lowest point of the trajectory, the projection of the velocity of particle $v_z$ becomes equal to zero, and its total velocity will be minimal. The radius of curvature of the trajectory at this point will be the smallest, and the upward acceleration of the particle will be the greatest.

Then the particle begins to move upward along the field $\vec{E}$, its speed will increase, and along with it the radius of curvature of the trajectory will increase. At some moments the particle speed becomes equal to the initial speed $\vec{v}_0$.

As a result, the particle will “drift” along the $X$ axis. The trajectory of its movement is a cyclone. figure 6 shows the particle trajectory for the case when $\left|\vec{v}_0\right| > 2 \left|\vec{E}\right| / \left|\vec{B}\right|$.

(A rigorous calculation shows that motion along a cyclone can be represented as a superposition of uniform motion along the $X$ axis with a speed $\left|\vec{v}\right| = \frac{\left|\vec{E}\right|}{\left|\vec{B}\right|}$ and circular motion in the $XZ$ plane.)
