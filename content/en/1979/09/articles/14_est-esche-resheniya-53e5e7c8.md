There are more solutions!

In the April issue «Kvant» we offered the following task to junior schoolchildren (see p. 97):

Paralelodrami is composed of three equilibrium triangles, among which there are no congestible ones. Find the values ​​of its nodes.

In the fifth issue of the journal, we indicated two solutions to this problem: parallelodra with an acute angle of 72° and parallelodra with an acute angle $\frac{360^\circ}{7}$, while introducing three figure with possible partitions (see p. 63). As reader A. Goldberg from Leningrad rightly pointed out to us, the problem has not two, but five solutions: parallelodramas with acute angles 36°, 72°, $\frac{180^\circ}{7}$, $\frac{360^\circ}{7}$ and $\frac{540^\circ}{7}$. We will apologize to the readers and, without giving a complete solution to the problem (this is a rather boring enumeration of many situations), we place only figure.

⟦folio 37⟧

Summer holidays are over and a new school year has begun. The academic year begins in the “Enrollee's Workshop” - a traditional section of our magazine.

What tasks and questions are offered to applicants for entrance exams in mathematics and physics at various universities? What are the typical shortcomings of applicants’ answers, their typical mistakes? How are the entrance exams conducted? What should you focus on when preparing for these exams? How to most effectively use the remaining time and organization to repeat materials?

The letters from readers received by the editors, their speeches at readers' conferences indicate that such questions are of interest and concern to many people - school students and vocational technical schools, young workers, teachers, lovers of mathematics and physics, and even... a parent, sometimes those who are far from these sciences in their specialty. Answers to all these questions and related materials from the Entrant's Workshop.

First of all, readers will find articles here on individual sections of university entrance exam programs in mathematics and physics. Such articles serve the purpose of providing an in-depth, detailed explanation of the most important and complex topics in school courses in mathematics and physics, and to warn future applicants against the most common mistakes. The presentation of theoretical principles in these articles, as a rule, is used to develop typical tasks taken from the practice of entrance exams. When outlining the topics of articles, the editors study, as they arise, specific proposals and wishes expressed in letters from readers.

Of course, they cannot replace school textbooks, which set out systematic courses in mathematics and physics, to become “Practicum for applicants” - and there is no such need. They are designed for those who have already mastered the content of the textbooks and would like to receive additional explanations and tasks for independent work. In addition to the publications that will appear in the “Applicant's Workshop” in subsequent issues «Kvant», when preparing for entrance exams, it is useful to get acquainted with some of the articles devoted to the magazine. In the process of preparing for the exams, readers will have some questions, why and why?

You should not think that the materials in the “Applicant's Workshop” are intended only for tenth graders. A number of articles in this section are quite accessible to ninth-graders, and they will understand a lot from the remaining articles.

It is quite natural that future applicants are interested in finding out what tasks are offered in the entrance exams. After all, everyone would like to specifically imagine what an exam is, to get acquainted in advance with the level of requirements of examiners in various educational institutions. This is especially important for young people graduating from schools in rural areas, workers’ settlements, and small towns. The “Applicant's Workshop” will systematically contain samples of options for written exams and problems for oral exams in mathematics and physics, which were offered in 1979 in various countries in our country. Although these tasks vary in their complexity, readers will see for themselves the main thing: to solve them, you just need to master school courses well, and no additional knowledge is required. By the way, the published options provide an excellent opportunity to try your hand at solving a “real” exam set of problems in a fixed time (applicants are given 4 hours to complete the work).

It is very important to prepare yourself for exams and psychologically, to educate yourself in higher education, endurance, and self-control. Unfortunately, there are many cases where a well-prepared applicant became lost during an exam, began to get nervous and, as a result, could not solve even simple problems or tell what he knew well.

The editors of the magazine «Kvant» hope that the “Applicant's Workshop” section will become a good advisor to applicants. Probably, in the course of preparing for exams, readers will have some questions, why and why?

The editors will answer your questions - personally or in the “Ask - We Answer” section, and the most interesting suggestions and messages from readers will be published in the “Reader Advise” review.
