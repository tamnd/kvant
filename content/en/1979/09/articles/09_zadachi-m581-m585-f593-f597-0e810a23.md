Tasks

M581 — M585; F593— F597

M581. a) Is there a three-digit number whose cube ends in three sevens?
b) For any set of digits, the last of which is not 0, is there a cube ending with this set of digits?

A. Brotsky

M582. A quadrilateral with mutually perpendicular diagonals is inscribed in a circle with center O. Prove that the distance from point O to each side is equal to half the length of the opposite side.

A. Kelarev

M583. Sets of stones are considered, the mass of each of which is no more than 2 kg, and the total mass of the set is 50 kg. From such a set, several stones are selected, the total mass of which differs from 10 kg by the smallest possible number D for a given set. What is the largest value that the number D can take for all possible sets of stones?

A. Waitrob, A. Pechkovsky

M584. Is it possible to imagine the entire space as a union of lines, each two of which are intersecting (that is, do not lie in the same plane)?

F. Weinstein

M585. N scientists were present at the chemical conference - chemists and alchemists, and there were more chemists than alchemists. It is known that chemists answer the truth to any question, and alchemists sometimes tell the truth, sometimes they tell the truth. A mathematician who happens to be at the conference must determine for each scientist whether he is a chemist or an alchemist. To do this, he can ask any scientist the question: “Who is such and such a chemist or an alchemist?” (In particular: “Who are you?”). Prove that a mathematician can figure out everything that is required

a) for 4N questions; 6) for 2N - 2 questions;

b) * try to come up with a way to establish who is a chemist and who is an alchemist in fewer questions (the authors know of a rather cumbersome way that allows you to do this in [3N/2] questions).

S. Konyashin, P. Blecker

F593. A hoop of radius $r$ rolls down from a height $H$ ($r \ll H$) without slipping along an inclined plane with an angle $\alpha$ at the base and absolutely elastically hits a smooth wall perpendicular to the inclined plane (Fig. 1). To what height does the hoop rise after impact if the sliding friction coefficient is $\mu$?

M. Borodovsky

F594. Imagine what you find in a hot filled bathhouse, and there is frost outside the window. Where will the steam go if you open the window?

A. Varlamov

Fig. 2.

F595. figure 2 shows the dependence of current on voltage for a DC stabilizer - barretor. The barretor is connected in series with a load resistance of $R = 13$ Ohm to a battery with an emf of $\varepsilon = 26$ V. Within what limits can the battery's emf change if the load current should not change by more than 50 mA?

S. Kozel

F596. The time interval between two successive eclipses of Jupter's satellite Io varies throughout the year from a minimum value of 42 hours 28 minutes 21.5 seconds to a maximum value of 42 hours 28 minutes 51.5 seconds. Using these data, determine the speed of light, assuming that the Earth moves around the Sun in a circular orbit with a radius of 150 ml. km. The radius of the city's Jupter orbit is greater than the radius of the Earth's orbit, and the speed of the city's Jupter is less than the speed of the Earth.

V. Belonuchkin

F597. A magnetron is a two-electron electron tube with a cylindrical anomal of radius $r$, along the axis of which there is a thin wire - the cathode. When the cathode is heated, it emits electrons with energies of $E$. The magnetron is placed in a uniform magnetic field parallel to its axis. At what value of magnetic field induction will the current in the anomal circuit become zero?
