A wonderful collection of primary sources on mathematics

Lovers of mathematics received a good opportunity to get acquainted with the basic mathematical theories, ideas, discoveries directly from primary sources and in their historical development: the publishing house "Prosveshcheniye" published the "Anthology on the History of Mathematics" (book 1 - 1976, book 2 - 1977). “The Reader,” compiled by leading Soviet specialists in the history of mathematics, headed by Professor A.P. Yushkevich, covers the development of mathematics from ancient times to the 20th century.
Readers will feel the peculiarity of reasoning during the creation of the corresponding mathematical works. And the peculiarity of these significant ones. I think the language of the original is very difficult. But each passage is accompanied, as a rule, by its retelling in modern language and qualified comments. By the way, the comments contain individual passages into a single whole, complement the missing links and give a general description of the entire content of the relevant documents. Sometimes the comments contain a brief description of other documents, other works of scientists of the same era.
Book 1 consists of three parts.
The first part is devoted to arithmetic and algebra. Passages belonging to the mathematicians of Ancient China, India, Babylon and Egypt are followed by excerpts from the works of mathematicians of these Renaissance (for example, from the works of Cardan on the solution of equations and the introduction of complex numbers) and the works of R. Descartes, I. Newton, R. Descartes.
dekid, N.I. Lobachevsky, K. Gauss, L. Euler, D. Boole.
The second part relates to number theory. The presentation of Euclid's works is followed by the Diophantine equation, Fermat's theorems with proofs due to Euler, elements of Euler's group theory, comparison theory and the theory of complex numbers of Gauss, as well as theorems on number theory by P. L. Chebyshev, B. Riemann, A. Poincaré.
In the third part, containing geometric material, the reader becomes acquainted with the change in volumes in Ancient Egypt, with the geometry of Euclid and with the geometry of the peoples of Central Asia and Europe. Interesting theory of finite sections of Apollo, design issues of Pappus geometry, equations of curves by P. Fermat, R. Descartes, I. Newton and L. Euler. Next, questions of differential and non-Euclidean geometry are presented. The third part of materials on the theory of abstract spaces and topology is being completed.
The 2nd book contains excerpts from works containing elements of mathematical analysis: theorems of Archimedes, Euclid, medieval scientists, scientists of the 17th-18th centuries. In excerpts from original sources, the main directions of development of mathematical analysis in the 19th and 20th centuries are clearly described. This material is closely integrated into the school curriculum for grades 9-10.
In the second part of the 2nd book, the reader becomes acquainted with the basic concepts and theorems of probability theory and their applications in science and practice. At the present stage of school development and with the emergence of the role of mathematics and its applications, becoming familiar with the material in this part is very useful.
Unfortunately, the book's circulation is very small, and its first volume was immediately published by the bibliographic editor.

2. The numbers a and b must satisfy the equation
   $100a + b = k (10a + b)$,
or
   $10a (10 - k) = b (k - 1)$.
It is easy to see that $1 < k \leq 10 (a \neq 10)$.
For $k = 10$ we get $b = 0$; then $a = 1$ and the number needed is 10 (100 = 10·10).
At $k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 8$ there are no solutions.
With $k = 6$ we get the number 18 (108 = 6·18).
At $k = 7$ the number is 15 (105 = 7·15).
At $k = 9$ the number is 45 (405 = 9·45).
Answer: 10, 15, 18, 45.
