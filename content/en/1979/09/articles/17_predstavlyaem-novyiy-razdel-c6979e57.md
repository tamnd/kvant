Introducing a new section

Dear readers! Today in the magazine «Kvant» a new section “The Art of Programming” is opening.

You, of course, know what an important place electronic computers (computers) now occupy in science and other areas of human activity. The ability to operate a computer is necessary for practically everyone - mathematicians and linguists, geologist and librarians, physicists and biologists, engineers and economists, chemists and doctors.

The purpose of the new section is to teach you how to work with a computer, teach you the language of machines, explain what programming is, teach you how to write programs for a computer. By reading the article in this section, you will learn how computers work and where they are used, what an algorithm and algorithmic languages ​​are.

Programming grew out of mathematics and serves it. Therefore, the new section appears precisely on the pages of the magazine «Kvant».

In addition to the article on computer technology and programming, from September to May issue «Kvant» in the section “The Art of Programming” assignments from the Correspondence School of Programming will be published.

⟦folio 45⟧

The correspondence school is organized by the Computing Center of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences (CC SB USSR Academy of Sciences) and the editorial office of the journal «Kvant».
The school accepts students from grades 6 to 10. The full course of the school is designed for 3 years, so students in the 6th, 7th and 8th grades are primarily invited. By doing homework, schoolchildren will learn to write computer programs and master several programming languages.

However, solving a problem by machine is not really just about writing a program. It is very important to run a written program on a computer and “debug” it—to find all the errors and mistakes in the program and remove them. How can the reader be helped in launching and debugging a program if he does not yet have a computer at home or even at his school? For this purpose, the Correspondence School of Programming has its own correspondence computing center, the address of which is indicated below. To this address you can send the programs you have compiled and receive in response not only a letter from the program that read your message, but also (this is the most important thing) interesting and useful.

The best students from our school will be invited to the summer programming school in Akademgorodok near Novosibirsk, where they will already become familiar with modern computers.

Those who complete the full course of the Correspondence School will receive a special certificate.

We also invite ninth and tenth graders to our school: although the first lessons may seem very simple to them, and they will not complete the three-year course, we are sure that they will find a lot of interesting and useful things for themselves here.

In addition to the lessons of the Correspondence School of Programming, our section will have several subsections.

How a computer works will be discussed in the articles in the subsection “Anatomy of Computers.” In one of the upcoming issues, you will read in this subsection about how a machine works as a memo, then about the work of its “brain,” the central process, then about its “sense organs,” input and output devices.

If the “Anatomy of Computers” describes the general principles of the design and operation of computers, then the subsection “Panorama of Computer Technology” is dedicated to the story about specific serial machines and their devices.

Information about the history of the development of science is very important in every scientific discipline, including programming. The “Historical Series” and its “Technical Museum” will tell you about the ways in which the theory and practice of this still young science are developing, who stood at its origins, what the first machines and programs were.

On the pages of the section there is also space reserved for articles by schoolchildren who have written and debugged interesting programs. This place is the “Young Programmer Tribune” subsection.

A good programming lesson can be not only a description of some program technique, but also a detailed analysis of a machine solution to an interaction problem. Stats about typical applied problems faced by programs are placed in the subsection “The computer solves the problem.” In it we will try to explain how the flight path of packages is calculated, how newspaper text is edited on a machine, how the required article or book is searched for at the request of a librarian.

Follow the “Reviews and Bibliography” section at «Kvant». There will now be more frequent messages about textbooks and assignments, as well as new interesting books on programming and the use of computers.

And now we open our section with an article by corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. P. Ershov and teacher of Correspondence Schools of Programming G. A. Zveniggorodsky.
