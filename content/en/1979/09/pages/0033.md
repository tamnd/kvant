Problem 14. Given an equilateral triangle. Find the set of points inside it:

a) allocated from side $AB$ no more than from side $BC$;

b) allocated from side $BC$ no more than from side $AC$;

b) satisfying simultaneous conditions a) and b).

The answer to question a) is shown in color on Fig. 1a), to question b) - on Fig. 1b).

It turns out that problem 14b) is closely related to problem 1. In order to bridge the gap between these problems, we solve the following problem:

Problem 15. Prove that the sum of the distances from any point inside an equilateral triangle $ABC$ to its side is equal to the length $h$ of its altitude.

Solved and e. Take an arbitrary point $M$ inside the triangle $ABC$ and drop from it the redistributions of the lares on the sides $AB, BC$ and $AC$. Let the lengths of the resulting segments be equal to $h_1, h_2$ and $h_3$; these are the distances from point $M$ to the side. It is necessary to prove that $h_1+h_2+h_3=h$. Let's connect point $M$ with vertices $A, B$ and $C$. Obviously, $S_{ABM}+S_{CBM}+S_{CMA}=S_{ABC}$, where $S_{ABM}, S_{CBM}, S_{CMA}, S_{ABC}$ are the areas of triangles $ABM, CBM, CMA, ABC$, respectively. Let us denote the length of the side of the triangle $ABC$ by $a$ and rewrite this equality as follows:

$$\frac{ah_1}{2}+\frac{ah_2}{2}+\frac{ah_3}{2}=\frac{ah}{2}.$$

Hence we prove the equality: $h_1+h_2+h_3=h$.

Addition to problem 15. The statement of this problem is true not only for the interior points of the triangle, but also for the points lying on its sides and at the vertices. (At the same time, we assume that if a point lies on a side of a triangle, then its distance to this side is zero.) Prove this on its own.
