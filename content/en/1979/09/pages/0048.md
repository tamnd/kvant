In March 1980, an Olympiad will be held among students of the Correspondence School of Programming. The winners will be accepted in the summer of 1980 to the Novosibirsk Academgorodok - to the Summer School for Young Programmers.

* *

In addition to the lessons of the Correspondence School of Programming, our section will have several subsections.

How a computer works will be discussed in the articles in the subsection “Anatomy of Computers.” In one of the upcoming issues, you will read in this subsection about how a machine works as a memo, then about the work of its “brain,” the central process, then about its “sense organs,” input and output devices.

If the “Anatomy of Computers” describes the general principles of the design and operation of computers, then the subsection “Panorama of Computer Technology” is dedicated to the story about specific serial machines and their devices.

Information about the history of the development of science is very important in every scientific discipline, including programming. The “Historical Series” and its “Technical Museum” will tell you about the ways in which the theory and practice of this still young science are developing, who stood at its origins, what the first machines and programs were.

On the pages of the section there is also space reserved for articles by schoolchildren who have written and debugged interesting programs. This place is the “Young Programmer Tribune” subsection.

A good programming lesson can be not only a description of some program technique, but also a detailed analysis of a machine solution to an interaction problem. Stats about typical applied problems faced by programs are placed in the subsection “The computer solves the problem.” In it we will try to explain how the flight path of packages is calculated, how newspaper text is edited on a machine, how the required article or book is searched for at the request of a librarian.

Follow the “Reviews and Bibliography” section at «Kvant». There will now be more frequent messages about textbooks and assignments, as well as new interesting books on programming and the use of computers.

And now we open our section with an article by corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Sciences A. P. Ershov and teacher of Correspondence Schools of Programming G. A. Zveniggorodsky.
