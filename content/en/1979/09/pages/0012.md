When is noon?

Before answering this question, let’s ask another: what is noon? It would seem that the word speaks for itself: noon is half the day, that is, the average moment between east and sunset. But is it possible, based on such a definition, to accurately establish the moment of noon, for example, to knock with a hammer and say: “Here it is noon”? Obviously not. Firstly, the moments of sunrise and sunset can only be reliably noticed at sea, where the horizon is perfectly correct. Secondly, sunset will occur several hours later than noon, and therefore the moment when noon occurs can only be determined after the end of the day.

However, there is a phenomenon extremely close to noon that can be observed - this is the cult

mination of the Sun, that is, the moment the Sun passes through the meridian of a given place. So, for moderate people, the Sun rises every day in the eastern part of the horizon, rises above it, culminates, passes through the meridian, then begins to decline and sets in the western part of the horizon.

The culmination of the luminaries is observed using special instruments called passage instruments. Their main element is the critical tube, which can only rotate in height around a horizontal axis directed from east to west. Such a tube always faces the meridiana plane (Fig. 1). For the convenience of the observer, the tube is sometimes made broken, reflecting the light in the horizontal direction through the full axis of rotation, at the end of which there is an eyepiece (Fig.2). Thanks to this, the eye always observes in a constant position and looks in a horizontal direction.
