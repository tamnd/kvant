PLANE WITH CURVES

Our figure shows a family of second-order curves passing through the points $A$ ($0;0$), $B$ ($1;1$), $C$ ($1;2$), $D$ ($-1;2$).

Recall that a second-order curve is the graph of the equation

$$a_1x^2 + a_2xy + a_3y^2 + a_4x + a_5y + a_6 = 0$$

where not all three numbers $a_1, a_2, a_3$ are equal to zero. A complete list of second-order curves: ellipse, hyperbola, parabola, pair of lines (intersecting or parallel), straight line, point, empty set.

It turns out that exactly one second-order curve passes through the points $A, B, C, D$ and any point $E$ ($x_0, y_0$) different from them.
