The geometric figure “Five-pointed star” is very easy to draw if the regular pentagon ABCDE (Fig. 1) has already been constructed. You can, for example, simply continue its sides. For the five-pointed star $A_1B_1C_1D_1E_1$, as for any star polygon, the sides are usually considered to be the segments $[A_1C_1], [B_1D_1]$, etc. (and not $[A_1B_1], [BB_1]$, etc.), and the vertices are only the points $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$, $D_1$, $E_1$. The five-pointed star has five concentric angles:

$$A_1D_1B_1, B_1E_1C_1, \dots$$

Fig. 1.

The original pentagon ABCDE is the intersection of these angles. A five-pointed star from the pentagon ABCDE can, of course, be obtained by connecting its vertices with segments.
