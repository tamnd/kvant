Tasks

M596—M600; F608—F612

We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the school curriculum. More difficult tasks will be marked with an asterisk. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed it to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than February 1, 1980 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, journal editorial office «Kvant». In the “To” column, write: “Problem «Kvant» No. 12–79” and the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example “M596, M597” or “F607”. Solutions to problems from different issues of the magazine or in different subjects (mathematics and physics) are assigned in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solution). The conditions of each original problem proposed for publication (or a series of problems) should be submitted in a separate envelope in duplicate along with your solution to this problem (on the envelope, mark: “Problem «Kvant», new problem in physics” or “... new problem in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your last name, school number and class in which you study.

M596. A five-word recognition with bases $A_1A_2A_3A_4A_5$ and $B_1B_2B_3B_4B_5$ is given. All edges of the bases and all segments $A_iB_j(i, j=1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$ are colored either red or green so that in each triangle with vertices at the vertices of the prism, the sides of which are colored, there are two sides of different colors. Prove that all ten edges of the bases are colored the same.

XXI International Olympiad in Mathematics, 1979

M597. Let's put
$$x_n = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n}.$$
a) Prove the existence of the limit
$$\gamma = \lim_{n \to \infty} (x_n - \ln n).$$
b) Prove that for any $n$, $m \in N$
$$\gamma < x_n + x_m - x_{nm} \subseteq 1.$$
b) Find $\gamma$ to the nearest 0.1.

V. Yasinsky

M598. Given a plane $P$, a point $P$ on this plane and a point $Q$ outside the plane $P$. Find all points $n$ in the plane $P$ for which the ratio $(|QP| + |PR|)/|QR|$ is maximum.

XXI International Olympiad in Mathematics, 1979

M599. a) How many zeros does the number $4^{56} + 6^{54}$ end with?
b) Indicate the most interesting number
$$1978^{1979}1980 + 1980^{1979}1978.$$
P. Gusyatikov

M600. Two quantities travel along two intersecting circles. Each one travels around its own circle at a constant speed. Having simultaneously left one of their intersection points and made one revolution each, the quantities met again at this point. Prove that on the plane in which the circles lie, there is a fixed point such that the distances from which to the magnitude met are the same all the time if they travel:
a) in one direction (counterclockwise);
b) in different directions.

N. Vasiliev, I. Sharien

$^*) a^{bc}$ means $a^{(bc)}$

F608. A spacecraft with a mass of $M=12$ t moves around the Moon in a circular orbit at an altitude of $h=100$ km. To transfer to the application orbit, the jet engine is turned on for a short time. The speed of gases escaping from the rocket soll is $u=10^4$ m/s. The radius of the Moon is $R_\perp=1,7 \cdot 10^3$ km, the acceleration of gravity at the surface of the Moon is $g_\perp=1,7$ m/s$^2$.

1) What amount of fuel must be consumed so that when the braking engine is turned on at point $A$ of the ship's trajectory, it descends to the Moon at point $B$ (Fig. 1)?

2) In the second version of the application, the ship at point $A$ is given an impulse in the direction of the center of the Moon to put the ship into an orbit touching the Moon at point $C$ (Fig. 2). How much fuel should be consumed in this case?

X1 International Physics Olympiad, 1979

F609. The part, made of aluminum, is weighed on an analytical balance using brass weights. During one weighing, the air on the scale is dry, and during the other, it is wet, the water vapor pressure in which is equal to $p_B=15,2$ mm Hg. Art. The external pressure ($p=760$ mm Hg) and temperature ($t=20^\circ\text{C}$) of air in both cases are the same.

At what mass of the part can you notice a difference in the scale readings if their sensitivity is $m_0=0,1$ mg? Density of aluminum $\rho_1=2,7$ g/cm$^3$, brass $\rho_2=8,5$ g/cm$^3$

X1 International Physics Olympiad, 1979

F610. In the Soviet-French experiment on optical location of the Moon, pulsed radiation from a ruby ​​laser at a wavelength of $\lambda=0,69$ µm was directed using a telexcol with a mirror diameter of $D=2,6$ m onto the lunar surface. A reflector was installed on the Moon, which worked like a perfect mirror with a diameter of $d=20$ cm, reflecting light exactly in the opposite direction. The reflected light is captured by the same telexcope and focused on a photodetector.

1) To what accuracy should the optical axis of the telexcol be set in this experiment?

2) Neglecting light losses in the Earth's atmosphere and in the telescope, estimate what fraction of the laser light energy will be recorded by the photodetector after reflection from the Moon.

3) Is it possible to register a reflected light pulse with the naked eye if the threshold sensitivity of the eye is taken to be equal to $n=100$ light quanta, and the energy emitted by the laser during the pulse is equal to $E=1$ J?

4) Evaluate the gain that comes from using a reflector. Assume that the surface of the Moon scatters $\alpha=10\%$ of incident light uniformly into a solid angle of 2π era.
