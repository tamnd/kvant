Maxwell's first scientific work

November 5, 1979 marked 100 years since the death of the great English scientist James Clerk Maxwell. The solution to the nature of Saturn's rings, the theory of color vision, the law of the distribution of gas molecules by speed and, above all, the theory of electromagnetism are now included in the treasury of world science. Maxwell's name, as Max Planck said, “shines on the gates of classical physics.”

For young people, the early works of major scientists are of particular interest. And not because these works are simpler and more understandable than the fundamental discoveries that brought fame to their authors. But because they teach work, creativity, and reveal the beauty of scientific knowledge. Maxwell himself noted that “science captivates us only when, having become interested in the lives of great researchers, we begin to follow the history of the development of their discoveries.”

Maxwell completed his first scientific work when he was fourteen years old. Maxwell's biographers describe this event as follows. After the early death of his mother (she died when James was 8 years old), his son was raised by his father, a deeply educated man, himself fascinated by scientific and technical issues. James developed an interest in technology very early and developed practical skills. To a large extent, this was facilitated by constant promises with my father, reading and, of course, children's games and the first “scientific” games. Until the age of ten, James lived in the village, and this was a happy time for him. Then he reported to Edinburgh, to a school called the academy.

At first, James felt unwell at the academy. He was immediately filled with the absence of his classmates, for whom he was a simple village guy. James arrived at the academy in the middle of the school year; in his dress and behavior he was very different from his peers, he was unsociable, and at first he did not shine in the sciences. He even created a work of “fools.” The boy missed home, his father, and the excitement of life in the village. But then James surprised his classmates. He became interested in geometry and became the first student in mathematics at school. At the age of 14, he was awarded a medal for his brilliant successes.
