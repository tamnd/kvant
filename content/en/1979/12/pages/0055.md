⟦folio 53⟧

The XXI International Mathematical Olympiad, held from June 28 to July 9, 1978 in Great Britain, was one of the most representative Olympiads. Teams from 22 countries took part in it: Australia, Belgium, Bulgaria, Brazil, Great Britain, Hungary, Vietnam, East Germany, Greece, Israel, Cuba, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, Finland, France, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden and Yugoslavia. The Belgian team had an additional member from Luxembourg. A representative of Australia began to act as an observer.

By tradition, the team of each country consisted of 8 schoolchildren and 2 leaders. The exceptions were the teams of Brazil - 5 schoolchildren, Vietnam and Cuba - 4 schoolchildren each. The USSR team included the winners of the All-Union Mathematical Olympiad: tenth-graders Murman Ambroladze (Komarov Physics School, Tbilisi), Aleksandr Dettyarev and Ilya Zakharevich (both from the boarding school at Leningrad State University), Igor Lysenok, Andrey Lyakhovets, Sergei Khlebutin (all from the boarding school at Moscow State University), Mikhail Rudkovsky (boarding school at KSU) and a ninth-grader from Moscow, Alexander Razborov (school No. 2).

The trip included a month-long training camp at the experimental school of the Academy of Pedagogical Sciences of the USSR in the village of Chernogolovka, Moscow region. Since the composition of the team was determined in advance (the team included all 6 schoolchildren who received first premii in 10 grades at the All-Union Olympiad, as well as a schoolchild who received premii in 9 grades, and a schoolchild who received premii in 10 classes), the gathering was only of a training nature, which saved its participants from unnecessary efforts associated with further selection of the team. At the gathering, issues of both theoretical and practical nature were studied, in particular, the tasks of previous Olympiads were broken down. Traditionally, the training camp featured very meaningful cultural and educational work and interesting sports competitions.

Even before the arrival of schoolchildren in the UK, on ​​June 28 in Bristol, the jury of the Olympiad, consisting of scientific leaders of the team, began work on selecting problems. Until July 1, the jury selected 6 problems to improve the number of problems sent by the countries participating in the Olympiad. The tasks, initially compiled in the official languages ​​of the Olympiad: Russian, German, French and English, were then translated into the languages ​​of the countries participating in the Olympiad, so that each student wrote work in their native language.

The schoolchildren, together with their teaching leaders, were approaching London on June 30. The teams were located in the dormitories of Westfieldkled, University of London. In the classrooms of the same collection on July 2 and 3 were held and improved. In each of these two, participants were given 3 tasks.
