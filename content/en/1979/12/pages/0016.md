⟦folio 14⟧

Copernicus, who rejected the idea of ​​the Earth at rest and attributed to it the same circular motion that other planets perform, could not refuse ideal curves and used systems of rolling circles to accurately describe the motion of the planets.

But neither Copernicus nor his predecessors neglected to carefully compare their models with the observed ones. And small discrepancies did not bother them. Only Kepler set himself the task at that time: to describe the motion of the planets accurately, without introducing any discrepancies between what was observed and the theory. Collaboration with the Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe helped him solve this problem, who, observing the position of the planets day after day for almost 25 years, collected material that was unusually complete for that time *).

Kepler wondered why the planets bend their path around the Sun. He sometimes realized that the Sun acts on the planets, despite the fact that the planets are at great distances from it. But Kepler was unable to establish the correct dependence of the force of this action at a distance - he thought that this force was inversely proportional to the distance. Kepler could not even think about deriving the orbital formula - he did not have an equation of motion.
