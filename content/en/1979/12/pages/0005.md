⟦folio 3⟧

To the description of ovals and multifocal oval curves

(with remarks by Professor Forbes)

Mr. Clerk Maxwell insidiously proposes to extend the general theory of the foci of conic sections to more complex curves as follows:

(1) By analogy with the ellipse and hyperbola, for which the sum and difference of the distance from an arbitrary point on the curve to two points called foci are constant, the author comes to the conclusion that the geometric apparatus is to some extent similar to previous ones. Moreover, Maxwell actually proposed a new method, previously unknown to mathematicians.

The significance of this method turned out to be greater than one of the possible solutions to the problem of the ancient Eturoks. Indeed, the geometric constructions of young Maxwell reveal to us the amazing optical properties of ovals and introduce us to the range of interesting problems of geometric optics, on which many remarkable scientists have worked.

Professor Forbes, who patronized young talents, reported on Maxwell's research at a meeting of the Royal Society of Edinburgh on April 6, 1846.
