Laplace vector

Now we can proceed directly to our task. Let's try to find a vector associated with the orbit of the planet, which remains constant when the planet moves and which we can associate with the vector $2c$.

When moving in a circular orbit, the angular velocity is constant and the vectors $v$ and $n$ remain mutually redistributed all the time. When moving along a non-circular orbit (for example, along an ellipse), the angular velocity changes with time and the angle between the vectors $v$ and $n$ does not remain constant.

Let us find by what law the vector $n$ changes. To determine this, we will assume that the orbital motion will occur counterclockwise.

Let us assume that in a short period of time $\Delta t$ the planet has moved along its orbit from point $B$ to point $C$ (Fig. 3) so that its radius vector $r$ has rotated through an angle $\omega \Delta t$ (time $\Delta t$ is so short that the angular velocity $\omega$ can be considered unchanged). Vector $n$ as a result of rotation will turn into a new vector $n + \Delta n$, but the length of this new vector, of course, is equal to one.
