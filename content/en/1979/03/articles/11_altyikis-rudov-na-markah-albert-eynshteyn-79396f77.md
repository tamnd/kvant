F517. A beam of electrons with an energy of $E=1,0$ keV passes through two small capacitors, separated from each other at a distance of $l=20$ cm. Both capacitors are connected in parallel to one generator. By changing the frequency of the generator, mono is added so that the electron beam passes through the system without deflection. Determine the ratio $e/m$ for an electron if two successive frequency values ​​at which this condition is met are equal to 141 MHz and 188 MHz.

The deviations of electrons in capacitors “compensate” each other if during the time $\Delta t=l/v$, while the electrons fly in the gap between the capacitors ($v$ is the absolute value of the electron speed), the phase of the voltage on the capacitors changes to $k\pi$, where $k$ is an integer. Since $\Delta \varphi=\omega \Delta t$ and $\omega=2\pi \gamma$ ($\gamma$ is the generator frequency), the frequency values ​​at which this condition is satisfied are determined by the correspondence

$$\frac{l}{v} = \frac{k\pi}{2\pi \gamma}.$$

If $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are successive values ​​of the generator frequency at which electrons will flow through the system without deviation, then

$$2\pi \gamma_1 l/v=k\pi, \quad 2\pi \gamma_2 l/v=[k+1]\pi.$$

From here is

$$v=2(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)l.$$

So, an electron flying into the system has bookish energy $E=\frac{mv^2}{2}$. The electron acquired this energy $E=1$ keV by passing through a “preliminary” potential difference $u=1$ kV: $E=eu$ (e is the charge of the electron). Thus,

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{4m(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)^2l^2}{2} = eu,$$

where

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{4(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)l^2}{2u} = 1.76 \cdot 10^{11} \text{KJ/kg},$$

I. Slobodetsky
