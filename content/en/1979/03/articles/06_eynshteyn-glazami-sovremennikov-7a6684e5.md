Only the predominance of natural scientific interests over mathematical interests, but also the following timely feeling. I saw that mathematics is divided into many special areas and each of them can occupy the entire short life allotted to us. And I saw myself in the position of Buridan’s donkey, who cannot decide which armful of hay he should take. The point was, very clearly, that my intuition in the field of mathematics was not strong enough to confidently distinguish the basic and important from the rest of scholarship, which could still be dispensed with. In addition, the interest in exploring nature was undoubtedly stronger; It was not yet clear to me as a student that access to deeper fundamental problems in physics requires the most subtle mathematical methods. This became clear to me only gradually, after many years of independent scientific work. Of course, physics was also divided into special areas, and each of them could absorb a short working life without satisfying the thirst for deeper knowledge. The vast amount of insufficiently recognized empirical facts is overwhelming here. But here I soon learned to look for what could lead to depth, and to discard everything else, everything that overloads the mind and distracts from the essential...

Many books and articles have been written about Einstein. He left a deep imprint in the memory of everyone with whom he friends, worked and met. We place here statements by outstanding physicists from different countries: the scientist Niels Bohr, the Swiss Wilhelm Pauli, the Soviet physicist Abram Fedorovich Jufe, the Pole Leopold Infeld, the Germans Arnold Sommerfeld, Max Laue and Max Born, the American Roberta Oppenheimer - which will help our readers better imagine this remarkable man and appreciate his contribution to the development of science. The statement of the first Soviet commissar of education, Anatoly Vasilyevich Lunacharsky, who met with Einstein in 1922, is also cited.

“... Humanity will always be indebted to Einstein for eliminating the limitations of our worldview that were associated with primitive ideas about absolute space and time. He has given us a picture of the world characterized by unity and harmony that surpasses the wildest dreams of yesteryear..."

Niels Bohr

“... As a scientist, Einstein is a figure of the same order as Isaac Newton... Just as during Newton’s time contemporaries could not predict the richness and depth of all the consequences of his influence, so little can we imagine what impact Einstein’s legacy will have on the course of history. Time will tell..."

Max Laue

“... With deep thought and consistency of philosophical thinking, which has not yet been encountered in the minds...”

“...Einstein was... in my impression... a lonely man... He had, of course, countless students, if by this we mean people who, by reading his books or listening to his lectures, learned from him a new view of physics, the nature of our world, learned a new worldview. But he did not create the OLS school. He had few students whom he cared about as his disciples and followers. Two lone scientists always lived in it, which sharply distinguished it from the scientific groups that are popular today. There were no two collaborations in it, under the sign of which the development of some other sciences proceeds. In recent years, Einstein worked together with some collaborators. They were usually called his assistants... Collaborators for Einstein were what he lacked in his youth..."

“...Einstein’s eyes are myopic and distracted. It seems that for a long time and forever more than half of his gaze turned somewhere inside. It seems that a significant part of Einstein’s vision is constantly occupied, along with his thought, with some kind of outline of calculations. The eyes are therefore full of abstract thought and even seem a little sad. Meanwhile, in the dormitory, Einstein is an extremely cheerful person. He loves to joke... he will be swept away by good-natured, completely childish laughter. At the same time, for a moment his eyes become completely childish. His extraordinary simplicity creates a charm that just wants to somehow caress him, shake his hand, pat him on the shoulder - and do this, of course, with great respect. What you get is some kind of feeling of tender participation, recognition of great defenseless simplicity and at the same time a feeling of boundless respect...”
Anatoly Vasilievich Lunacharsky
