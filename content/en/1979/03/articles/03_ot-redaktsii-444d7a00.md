⟦folio 2⟧

March 14 marks the 100th anniversary of the birth of Albert Einstein, who was considered by V.I. Lenin to be the great transformer of the natural name. The name of Einstein is associated with the beginning of a revolution in physics that marked the 20th century. The ideas of the theory of relativity that he created radically changed the views on physical phenomena occurring both in the microcosm and in the entire Universe. The philosophical meaning of these discoveries and their dialectical nature have been fully revealed only in our days.

Einstein contributed to many areas of physics. He was one of the creators of modern quantum concepts about the nature of radiation and laid the foundations of the theory of lasers. His theory of Brownian motion led to the final approval of the molecular theory of the structure of matter.

Alberta Einstein was not an armchair scientist indifferent to the fate of humanity. He was extremely acutely aware of everything that happened around him. Having left Nazi Germany in 1933, he actively opposed the war and for mutual understanding between peoples.

Einstein was one of the first to see the danger of developing nuclear weapons. In his speeches and articles, he sought to do everything in his power to abandon the arms race and stop preaching war. When he died, on a pile next to his large bed they found an unfinished manuscript devoted to the problems of disarmament and world peace.

In this issue we publish materials whose purpose is to introduce Albert Einstein to our readers both as a scientist and as a person.
