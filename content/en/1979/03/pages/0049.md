⟦folio 47⟧

Ohm for closed circuit:

$$I = \frac{\varepsilon}{R + r}$$

The voltage at the source is equal to the voltage at the external resistance:

$$U = I R = \frac{\varepsilon R}{R + r}$$

The power released in the external circuit is equal to the power of heat losses; it can be determined by formula (2):

$$P = I^2 R = \frac{\varepsilon^2 R}{(R + r)^2}$$

The total current power is equal to the power of external forces or the total power of heat losses on the internal and external resistances:

$$P_\pi = \varepsilon I = I^2 (R + r) = \frac{\varepsilon^2}{R + r}$$

For the efficiency we have:

$$\eta = \frac{P}{P_\pi} = \frac{R}{R + r}$$

Correspondence graphics are shown in Figures 2 b–e. Pay attention to the presence of a maximum in the power $P$ released in the external circuit (show yourself that the power $P$ is maximum when the external and internal resistances are equal).

Another example, when not all the work of electrical forces turns into heat, is provided by electric motors.

Problem 4. The electric motor is connected to the post-wash network with a voltage of $U = 220$ V. The resistance of the motor winding is $R = 5$ Ohm. Power of consumed moka $I = 10$ A. Find the mechanical power of the moka and its efficiency.

The total power of the electric current in this case consists of mechanical and thermal powers:

$$UI = P_\pi + I^2 R$$

pose

$$P_\pi = UI - I^2 R = 1700 \text{ Вт}$$

and motor efficiency

$$\eta = \frac{P_\pi}{P} = \frac{UI - I^2 R}{UI} \approx 0,77 = 77\%$$
