In the first case—when charging a battery—the direction of the electric current is opposite to the direction of the current created by the emf (Fig. 1, a), therefore, in formulas (4) and (5) it is necessary to put “plus” signs. This means that the total work done by electrical forces is equal to

$$A_1 = U_1 I_1 t = 100 \text{ Дж},$$

consists of work $\& I_1 t$ against external forces (battery charge).

$Q_1 = A_1 - \& I_1 t = (U_1 - \&) I_1 t = 40 \text{ Дж}$.

$$U_1 = \& + I_1 r, \text{ и } r = \frac{U_1 - \&}{I_1}.$$

In the second case—when charging the battery—the current in the area that interests us coincides in direction with the current created by the emf (Fig. 1, b), therefore the work of electric forces is equal to

$$Q_2 = I_2^2 r t = \frac{I_2^2 (U_1 - \&)}{I_1} t = 1,6 \text{ Дж}.$$
