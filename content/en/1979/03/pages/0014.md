However, when analyzing these equations, Einstein did not take into account all possible solutions. Five years later, Soviet physicist A. Friedman showed that Einstein's equations have other solutions, but these solutions are not statistical - the Universe is expanding. Einstein did not believe Friedman at first and declared that he had made a mistake. At that time, the Soviet physicist Yu. Krutkov was in Berlin, who convinced Einstein that he was wrong, and on May 31, 1923, Einstein published a small note: “... I believe,” he wrote, “Friedman’s results are correct and shedding new light. It turns out that the field equations also allow dynamic (i.e., variable with respect to time) centrally symmetric solutions for the structure of space to violate statistical ones.”
Friedman's solution did not require that the Universe be necessarily closed. Modern data rather indicate that the Universe is expanding infinitely, and the rate of its expansion increases with distance approximately according to the law

$$v = \frac{R}{T},$$

where $T$ is a constant equal to $2 \cdot 10^{10}$ years. This law is named after the astronomer Hubble who discovered it, and the constant $H = 1/T$ is called the Hubble constant. The expansion of the Universe has been confirmed by many observations, and Hubble's law has been tested to the distance that light travels in 10-12 billion years.

So Einstein assured the “conquest” of the laws of the Universe, begun by ATON. Man on Earth has understood the laws that govern the cosmos. “The most incomprehensible thing about nature is that it can be understood,” said Einstein.

XIII. Gravitational waves

Let's talk only very briefly about gravitational waves.

The first person to understand that gravitational interaction travels at the speed of light was Poincaré. The existence of a gravitational wave was a simple consequence of the equation of the theory of relative energy.

The theory did not reject the fact that no signal could be transmitted faster than the speed of light. In 1937 (together with Rosen) Einstein theoretically studied gravitational waves - his work is continued by many theorists today. Gravitational waves have not yet been discovered, but the day is not very far away when a gravitational signal from some great cosmic catastrophe - the birth of a supernova or something else of this kind - will be registered on Earth.

Epilogue

We end with 1937. Einstein did not stop working; Until his death on April 18, 1955, he became more and more immersed in new theories that are impossible to talk about. He wanted to find even more general laws than the laws of gravity - he worked on what he himself called a unified field theory. The last words in his last scientific article were: “... now nothing knows how to find a basis for such a theory.”

Einstein's work in recent years was treated differently. Many believed that success had betrayed him, and everything he did during these years was wrong. Many even believed that the task itself was inaction. Is this true? Can we say that now this significance no longer seems indisputable. The words “unified field theory” appear more and more often on the pages of the journal. Of course, time flows, and the new path is not at all the one that Einstein followed.

But Einstein's deep ideas do not disappear.

“Everything that came from his supremely Great intellect was only clear and red, like well-made art” - these words, written by Einstein in memory of Lorentz, fully apply to himself.
