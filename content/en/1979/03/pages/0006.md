⟦folio 4⟧

Ya. Smorodinsky

Albert Einstein
(1879—1979)

A hundred years ago, in the German city of Ulm, a child was born who was destined to make one of the most remarkable discoveries in the history of the natural world - he was destined to become the father of the theory of relativity: Albert Einstein was born on March 14, 1879.

There was no other theory in the history of science that withstood so much controversy and dispelled such stubborn mistrust as the theory of relativity. Very few of Einstein's contemporaries were able to immediately accept the new teaching - it was so different from everything that nineteenth-century physicists knew. And a lot of time passed before the theory of relativity became the basis of all natural science.

But Einstein's words are based not only on this one discovery. When he was awarded the Nobel Prize in 1922, the theory of relativity was not yet universally accepted, and its application was crowned with the discovery of the laws of the photoelectric effect. It is now difficult for us to understand the indecisiveness of the Nobel Committee, but the work on the photoelectric effect opened a new era of physics. This work proved the existence of a quantum of light - the photon.

Einstein was always interested in the most general questions of physics, he tried to understand how our world works, and tried to find the global laws that govern it. “What infuriates me are the scientists,” he said, “who take a hole, choose the thinnest place in it, and then continue to make a lot of holes where it’s easy to drill.” Einstein himself spent his energy only where it was difficult to drill.

Let's try to talk about his works and various episodes from the life of the great physicist in a series of short, not very interconnected thirteen essays.

1. First works

Einstein wrote his first work in 1901. He sent copies of it to various laboratories, trying to get a job after graduating from the Zurin Polytechnic. These attempts were unsuccessful. (Many years later, those who did not open up to his request received him with the greatest honors.) Only in June 1902, two years after graduating from the polytechnic, Einstein was able to obtain a position as a technical expert at the Swiss Patent Office.

By the beginning of 1905, Einstein already had six published works. Einstein himself subsequently did not rate his first works highly. His first work was, indeed, a rather elementary study of the forces acting between molecules in capillary phenomena. The next four works were devoted to thermal phenomena. In them, Einstein develops a general theory of these phenomena. These works would have been of exceptional importance if... the general theory of thermal phenomena had not been formulated somewhat earlier by Gibbs. Young Einstein was not familiar with the scientific literature and did not know about the work of the great American.

The sixth work was called “Redefining the size of molecules.” It was sent to the editors of the German physics journal “Annalen der Physik” (“Annals of Physics”), in which Einstein’s previous five articles were published, on April 30, 1905. The work was devoted to diffusion in liquids, and in it, in essence, the first ideas of the theory of Brownian motion were laid.
