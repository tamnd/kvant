The quantity $\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$ is now called the Lorentz factor.
If the force is directed along the velocity of the electron (the electron moves in a straight line), then it behaves as if its mass ("longitudinal mass") was $m_0\gamma^3/2$.

If there is arbitrary work done on an electron (for example, if it moves in an electric field), then its energy returns. Einstein showed that the kinetic energy $K$ of an electron is related to its speed ratio

$$K = m_0(\gamma - 1)c^2.$$

The quantity $m_0c^2$ is called rest energy, the quantity

$$W = m_0\gamma c^2 = \frac{m_0c^2}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$

— the total energy of an electron. So the kinetic energy of an electron is equal to the difference between the total energy and the rest energy.

For those who know the elements of differential calculus, we present the choice of formula (2). The total change in energy is obviously equal to work, that is, the product of force and displacement (recall that the electron moves rectilinearly):

$$\Delta W = f\Delta x = ma\Delta x,$$

where $a$ is the acceleration of the electron. Replacing $a$ with the relation $\Delta v/\Delta t$ and remembering that $v = \Delta x/\Delta t$, we get:

$$\Delta W = m\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\Delta x = m\Delta v \cdot v,$$

or, replacing the increment with differential ones,

$$dW/dv = mv.$$

On September 27, 1905, the editor of the same magazine received a very small note from Einstein, “Does the inertia of a body depend on the energy it contains?” If earlier it was said about the change in the mass of a body with increasing speed, then in this note it was proven that any change in the energy of a body changes its mass: “The mass of a body is a measure of the energy contained in it; if the energy changes by values ​​$L$, then the mass changes, accordingly, by values ​​$L/(9 \cdot 10^{16})$, and here the energy changes in joules, and the mass in kilograms.”

This establishes the formula $E = mc^2$, as it is written now (dimension $c$ - m/s).

The new formula was very difficult to test at the time. In his next work, Einstein, proposing a way to change the ratio of longitudinal and transverse masses, noted: “The author is not able to carry out such an experiment at first and will be glad if one of the physicists devotes resources to the proposed method.” Now the formulas of the theory of relative surfaces have been tested at accelerators, in many devices, but at the beginning of the century it was necessary to understand the full depth of the theory in order to believe in them.

VIII. Bose's work

In 1900, Planck announced the patterns of distribution of light intensity in the spectrum of a heated body. To do this, he had to make the assumption that light is emitted and absorbed by quanta. As we have already said, five years later Einstein proved that quanta exist in a free form and their behavior is very similar to the behavior of particles. From this point of view, the formula proposed by Planck can be remembered as a formula that determines how many quanta with a certain energy value are emitted from a heated body. Light from this point of view can be considered as a phoron gas, and the distribution of energy between individual photons of this gas (“how many phorons have a given energy?”) is described by Planck’s formula. But no one, not even Einstein himself, took advantage of this idea at that time.
