Autobogafic notes

In 1949, Albert Einstein wrote Autobogaphic Notes. In them, he spoke about the basic scientific principles that guided his work. Shortly before his death, in 1955, Einstein also wrote a short, spectacled “Autobogathic Sketches” - memories of his student years and how the general theory of relativity was born. We present here excerpts from these works concerning the early period of the life of the great scientist.

...In 1895, at the age of sixteen, I came from Italy to Zorich, after spending a year in Milan with my parents without schools and without a teacher. My goal was to enter the polytechnic, although I did not have a very clear idea of ​​how this could be achieved. I was a wayward but modest young man who acquired my necessary knowledge sporadically, mainly through self-education. I thirsted for deep knowledge, but learning did not seem to me an easy task: I was poorly suited to memorization and had a poor memory. It was with a feeling of well-founded uncertainty that I took the advanced exam for the engineering department. The examination showed me the woeful inadequacy of my preparation, despite the fact that the examiners were lenient and full of sympathy. I understood that my failure was completely justified. The only gratifying thing was that the physicist G. Weber told me that I could listen to his colleagues if I stayed in Zorich. But the rector, Professor Albin Herzog, recommended me to the Kantenal School in Aaru, where, after a year of study, I took the matriculation exam. This school left its mark thanks to its liberal spirit and the humble service of teachers who did not rely on any ostentatious authority; comparison with six years of education in an authoritarian-run German gymnasium convincingly showed me how an education in two freedoms and a sense of personal responsibility is superior to an education that is based on drill, external authority and ambition. True democracy is not empty or an illusion.

That same year in Aaru, I had a question: if it were possible to chase a light wave at the speed of light, would we have a wave field in front of us that does not depend on time? This still seems impossible! This was the first children's thought experiment that related to the special theory of relativity. Discovery is not a matter of logical thinking, even if the final product is associated with a logical form.

1896-1900 — training at the department of teachers of special disciplines at the Swiss Polytechnic. I soon noticed that I was content with the role of a consistent student. In order to be a good student, you need to have ease of perception, a willingness to concentrate your energies on everything that is read in lectures, a love of order in order to write down and then conscientiously process what is taught in lectures. I was fundamentally lacking in all these qualities, as I regrettably discovered... I listened to some of the lectures with great interest. But usually I “walked around” a lot and spent time studying at home the luminaries of theoretical physics... This extensive self-sufficient training was a simple continuation of an earlier introduction.
