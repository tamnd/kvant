An analysis of a similar situation for light and the atom led Einstein to the conclusion that if the probability of absorbing a quantum is proportional to the number of quanta present in a beam of light, then the probability of emitting a quantum consists of two terms. However, one of them describes induced radiation - radiation that is caused by the impact of light on the atom (which “rocks” the atom). This part is proportional to the number of quanta present in the light beam. The second term does not depend on the number of quanta and therefore does not vanish for an atom in vacuum. This term describes the so-called spontaneous, that is, spontaneous, radiation.

If a powerful light flux enters the medium, that is, a large number of quanta, the induced radiation transforms and becomes very intense.

The operating principle of a laser is related to stimulated emission. The effect predicted by Einstein in 1916 has now become the basis for a huge number of devices and installations.
