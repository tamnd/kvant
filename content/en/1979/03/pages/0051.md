⟦folio 49⟧

Using the correspondence, for the source voltage ratio we get:

$$\frac{U_{n2}}{U_{n1}} = \frac{100 + n}{10(1 + n)}$$

Since the transmitted current decreases by 10 times, in order for the voltage to consume the same power, its resistance must be increased by 100 times.

Exercises

1. There is a path of electric lamps designed for a voltage of 110 V. The power of three lamps is 40 W, and the power of two is 60 W. How should they be connected to a 220 V network so that they all burn at full heat?

2. A ring of uniform wiring is included in the target, as shown in figure 5. At what position of the slider B in the ring will the minimum amount of heat be generated? Assume that the voltage at the source is constant.

3. A battery with internal resistance $r=0,5$ Ohm is shorted to an external target. At a current of $I_1=2$ A, a power of $P_1=8$ W is released at the external resistance. What power will be released in the external target at a current $I_2=1$ A? What is the emf of the battery?

4. Why does an electric motor operating when cold heat up less than when it is loaded? In which case is the most heat released in the motor winding?

5. The engine of a locomotive with a mass of $m=50$ t consumes a current of $I_1=100$ A when climbing up a mountain with a slope of $\alpha=0,01$, and a current of $I_2=50$ A when descending from the same mountain. Find the speed of the train in the usual conditions. Line voltage $U=2000$ V; Engine efficiency $\eta=0,7$; friction coefficient $k=0,02$.

6. Explain why, when electrical appliances that consume high power are connected to the network, the brightness of the burning lamps decreases. Calculate how many times the brightness of a light bulb with a nominal power of $P_1=100$ W decreases when an iron with a power of $P_2=1000$ W is turned on in parallel (assume that the brightness of the light bulb is proportional to the amount of heat generated by the power in it). The resistance of the supply wires from the rated voltage source is $U=127$ V to the socket $R=2$ Ohm.
