Einstein submitted this work as a dissertation for the degree of Doctor of Physiology (as the academic degree was then called).

II. Golden year

1905, the year of the first Russian revolution, turned out to be a golden year in the history of physics. This year, four more works by Einstein appeared one after another in the journal Annalen der Physik. During this period, the theory of the photoeffect was created. The second is the theory of Brownian motion. In the third, the basic formulas of mechanics and electrodynamics in the special theory of relativity were obtained. In the fourth article, one of the most significant formulas of the 20th century was obtained - a formula connecting mass and energy.

Each of these works ensured immortality for their author. And these discoveries were made by a man who worked completely alone, did not meet with physicists, and only told close friends about his ideas.

With these works of Einstein, a new century in physics began.

III. Photo effect

In 1900, Planck hypothesized that atoms emit light in separate portions - quanta, the energy of which is proportional to the frequency of light:

$$E = hv.$$

(1)

This proposal about the nature of the study of atoms allows us to explain some patterns in the study of a heated body.

Einstein went further than Planck. One of the questions that he examined was the question of the nomination of gases of light. It was known that light with a long wavelength does not nominate an atom: there is some critical wavelength $\lambda_0$ such that only light with wavelength $\lambda < \lambda_0$ can nominate an atom (for different atoms $\lambda_0$ is different). The mystery of this phenomenon disappears if we propose that light itself consists of portions - quanta, and the energy of these light quanta is associated with the frequency of light by the same correspondence $E = hv$. If we move from frequency v to wavelength $\lambda = c/v$, then correspondence (1) will take the form $E = hc/\lambda$. So light with a wavelength of $\lambda < \lambda_0$ is quanta whose energy is $E < E_0 = hc/\lambda_0$, where $E_0$ is the energy of induction of an atom.

Based on the assumption of the quantum nature of light, Einstein created a theory of the photoelectric effect that explains its basic laws: the existence for each substance of a minimum frequency of light, below which the photoelectric effect does not occur, and the independence of the kinetic energy of electrons emitted under the influence of light from the intensity of the light.

If an electron is located close to the surface of a body and in order to escape beyond its boundaries it needs to do work $P$ (against the forces of attraction to positive charges), then under the influence of light with frequency v (and, accordingly, with the energy of quanta hv) it will fly out with kinetic energy

$$\frac{mv^2}{2} = hv - P.$$

If the energy of light quanta is $E = hv \leq P$, then $v = 0$—the photoelectric effect does not occur. The maximum energy of electrons emitted from a body under the influence of light does not depend on the intensity of the light - it is determined only by the frequency of the incident light.

Einstein came to the Fotoeffect formula while reflecting on the difference between the description of particles and light: “... it may turn out that the theory of light, operating with continuous spatial functions, will lead to a contradiction with experience when it is applied to the phenomena of the emergence and transformation of light,” he wrote at the beginning of the work under the title “On a heuristic point of view concerning the emergence and transformation of light” *), received by the editors of the mentioned journal on March 18 1905
