About friction forces

Many schoolchildren entering universities often have difficulty solving problems in which friction forces operate.

Let us recall the main features of the forces of so-called dry friction - friction between two solid bodies. These forces always arise during direct contact of bodies, are directed along the surface of contact and act on each of the contacting bodies, and they act in such a way as to interfere with the movement of one body relative to another - if these are forces of sliding friction, or so as to interfere with the very occurrence of this movement - if we are talking about forces of friction along the body.

The absolute value of the sliding friction force $\vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}}$ depends on the type of rubbing surfaces and the force $\vec{N}$ of the normal pressure of one body on another:

$$\left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}} \right| = \mu \left| \vec{N} \right|,$$

where $\mu$ is the friction coefficient.

The static friction force $\vec{F}_{\text{тр. п.}}$ is always balanced by all other forces acting on the body along the contact surface. Its absolute value can take any value from zero to some maximum value $\left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. п. max}} \right|$, which is usually considered equal to

$$\left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}} \right| = 0 \leq \left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. п.}} \right| \leq \left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}} \right|.$$

When solving problems, first of all, it is necessary to understand what kind of friction forces—rest or sliding—we are dealing with. Let's consider several specific tasks.

Problem 1. Two bars of mass $M_1 = 7$ kk and $M_2 = 10$ kk lie on a horizontal table, connected by nimyu. Another nim, attached to a block with mass $M_1$, is thrown over a block mounted on the edge of the table, and a load with mass $m = 1$ kk (Fig. 1) is suspended from it. The coefficient of friction between the bars and the table is $\mu = 0,1$. Determine the strength of both sides and the frictional forces acting on each of the bars.

Fig. 1.

First, let's find out whether the bars will move or not. They can begin to move under the influence of the tension force $\vec{T}_1$ of the right thread, and it, in absolute value, cannot exceed the force of gravity of a load with mass $m$:

$$\left| \vec{T}_1 \right| \leq m \left| \vec{g} \right| = 10 \text{ H.}$$

The maximum value of the static friction force for both bars

$$\left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. п. max}} \right| = \mu \left( M_1 + M_2 \right) \left| \vec{g} \right| = 17 \text{ H.}$$

Since $\left| \vec{T}_1 \right| < \left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. п. max}} \right|$, the bars are at rest. It is obvious that a load of mass $m$ is also at rest; hence,

$$\left| \vec{T}_1 \right| = m \left| \vec{g} \right| = 10 \text{ H.}$$

Now consider separately the bars of mass $M_1$ and $M_2$. The right block is subject to the tension forces of the thread $\vec{T}_1$ and $\vec{T}_2$ and the friction force $\vec{F}_{\text{тр. 1}}$ (Fig. 2)*.

These are the rope tension force $\vec{T}$, the gravity force $\vec{mg}$, the support reaction force $\vec{N}$ (equal in absolute value to the normal pressure force) and the sliding force $\vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}}$ (since the block is moving). Since the block moves uniformly and rectilinearly, the sum of the forces acting on it and the sum of the moments of these forces relative to any axis must be equal to zero. Consequently, the sum of the projections of all forces on the $X$ axis is equal to zero:

$$\left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}} \right| - \left| \vec{T} \right| \cos \alpha_0 = 0$$

$$\left| \vec{T} \right| \sin \alpha_0 \cdot \frac{b}{2} - \left| \vec{F}_{\text{тр. ск.}} \right| \frac{a}{2} - \left| \vec{N} \right| \frac{b}{2} = 0.$$

$$tg \alpha_0 = \frac{a}{b} + \frac{1}{\mu},$$

$$\alpha_0 = \arctg \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{1}{\mu} \right).$$
