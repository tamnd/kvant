Below we publish two notes on harmonic oscillations. Harmonic vibrations are studied at the beginning of grade 10 in mathematics lessons (“Algebra and beginnings of analysis 10,” § 16). But they are interesting not only from a mathematical point of view - we often encounter them in a wide variety of processes in nature and technology - it is not for nothing that harmonic oscillations are studied in physics lessons (“Physics 10”, § 5, 6), in particular, in the topic of variable electric current. It is this topic that is dedicated to this. They are also united by mathematical content: both rely significantly on the so-called vector diagrams of harmonic oscillations.

J. Rabb

Did you know that
$$\frac{220 \text{ волт}}{127 \text{ волт}} \approx \sqrt{3}?$$

In an AC network, as you know, the voltage is 220 or 127 volts. It turns out that the ratio of 220 to 127 is exactly $\sqrt{3}$. You can easily verify this by dividing 220 by 127 with a “column” and comparing the resulting result 1.7322... with the value of the number $\sqrt{3}$ taken from the tables: 1.7321...

The school physics room has 380 or 220 volts. If we perform division in this case, we again obtain
$$380:220 \approx 1,73 \approx \sqrt{3}.$$
