Lemma. Let the data $r$ be numbers of numbers $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_r$, not divided by $p$; $0 < r < p$, $p$ - simple. Then from these numbers it is possible to make at least $r + 1$ sums by dividing the various remainders when dividing by $p$ (in this case it is allowed to take the sum of the “empty set of terms”, which is considered equal to zero, the sum “from one term”, from two, ..., from all $r$ terms).

We will give the proof of this lemma below, and now we will show how our problem is solved with its help.

By virtue of the lemma, from the numbers $a_1, \dots, a_{p-1}$ it is possible to form at least $p$ sums that give different remainders when divided by $p$. Since the total of different remainders when divided by $p$ is exactly $p$ pieces: $0, 1, \dots, p - 1$, among these sums there is a sum that gives a remainder $s$ when divided by $p$. Let this sum $a_{i_1} + \dots + a_{i_k}$; then $a_{i_1} + \dots + a_{i_k} \equiv s$. Let us now take all the other numbers that did not include terms in the sum $a_{i_1} + \dots + a_{i_k}$; let these numbers be $a_{j_1}, \dots, a_{j_l}$. Since $a_{1} + \dots + a_{p-1} \equiv 2s$ and $a_{i_1} + \dots + a_{i_k} \equiv s$, we get that $a_{j_1} + \dots + a_{j_l} \equiv s$. Reversing the numbers $a_{j_1}, \dots, a_{j_l}$, we get the number $a_{i_1} + \dots + a_{i_k} - a_{j_1} - \dots - a_{j_l}$, comparable to zero modulo $p$, that is, divisible by $p$.

To complete the solution of the problem, we are left with a lemma. Let's do this by induction.

For $r = 1$ the statement of the lemma is obvious. Let us assume that it is true for $r = k < p - 1$ and false for $r = k + 1$, and we arrive at a contradiction. So, let the sums of $k$ terms $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_k$ give when divided by $p$ $k + 1$ different remainders $0, s_1, \dots, s_k$. Then, since after adding $b_{k+1}$ the number of times and terms of their sums should not increase, all sums $0 + b_{k+1}, s_1 + b_{k+1}, \dots, s_k + b_{k+1}$ modulo $p$ are contained in the set $\{0, s_1, \dots, s_k\}$. In other words, if you add $b_{k+1}$ to any element of this set, you again get an element of the same set. Thus, this set is complete and contains elements $0, b_{k+1}, 2b_{k+1}, \dots, (p - 1)b_{k+1}$. But it is clear that all these elements are distinct (modulo $p$): the difference $ib_{k+1} - jb_{k+1} = (i - j)b_{k+1}$, where $0 < i - j < p$, cannot be divided by $p$, since $p$ is a prime. This means that the set $\{0, s_1, \dots, s_k\}$ contains all $p$ distinct elements, although we assumed that $k + 1 < p$. The lemma is proven.

V. Galperin
