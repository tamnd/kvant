⟦folio 39⟧

V. Kasatkin

Communicative Anya

The reason for this story was the report that Anya Shakhovskaya gave at the traditional “Small Academy of Sciences” gatherings of Crimean schoolchildren this past summer. Anya lives in the village of Nauchny near Bakhchisarai. She is now in eighth grade. Anya is a year younger than the Small Academy - she turned 14 this year.

One summer, when Anya was at a tourist camp, she was appointed responsible for issuing food to tourists.

“Make sure you don’t make a mistake, calculate everything correctly,” the camp commander admonished her, “we don’t have a computer.”

- Don't worry, I'll think of something.

And Anya came up with an idea. In a nearby store she managed to break a scale and several weights.

In the morning, everyone came running to see Anya releasing the kruta to tourists.

Unfortunately, Anya managed to find only 5 weights - weighing 1 g, 3 g, 9 g, 27 g and 81 g. Nevertheless, Anya, apparently, did not experience any difficulties. She deftly placed the griri either on the empty scale or on the bowl with bags of cool. At the same time, she made some incomprehensible, at first glance, notes. Having released 35 g of cool, she wrote down: 1101; after weighing 52 g, the entry appeared: 11011; in the next line she wrote 11101 and said:

— That’s it, I weighed the last 100 grams. The work is finished.

After that, she picked up an abacus of a non-teachable design and, looking at the notes she had made, began to quickly work on them. It was an amazing coming of age.

A few minutes later, Anya finished her calculations and remarked with satisfaction:

- Everything is correct, not a single mistake.
