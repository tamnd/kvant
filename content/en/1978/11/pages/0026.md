(Note also that the coordinate of the projection of the middle of the segment is equal in y-sum to the coordinates of the projections of the ends of the segment.) Since for any three points $a, b, c$ on the line we can choose three points on the plane whose projections are the points $a, b, c$, then from the validity of the statement on the plane it follows that it is true on the line.

It is now easy to give an analytical proof that the number $d = \frac{a + b + c}{3}$ is the general limit of these sequences. To do this, it is obviously enough to show that $|a_n - d| = 2 |a_{n+1} - d|$, $|b_n - d| = 2 |b_{n+1} - d|$, $|c_n - d| = 2 |c_{n+1} - d|$ (with each step the sequences approach the limit twice as much).

Note that for any $n$

$$a_{n+1} + b_{n+1} + c_{n+1} = \frac{b_n + c_n}{2} + \frac{c_n + a_n}{2} + \frac{a_n + b_n}{2} = a_n + b_n + c_n.$$

This shows that $d = \frac{a_n + b_n + c_n}{3}$ for any $n$.

We have:

$$|a_n - d| = \left| a_n - \frac{a_n + b_n + c_n}{3} \right| = \left| \frac{2a_n - b_n - c_n}{3} \right| = \left| \frac{b_n + c_n - 2a_n}{3} \right| = 2 \left| \frac{b_n + c_n}{2} - \frac{a_n + b_n + c_n}{3} \right| = 2 |a_{n+1} - d|.$$

The other two equalities are checked in the same way.

In conclusion, we note the following. The arithmetic mean in numbers, considered as an operation, is non-associative (i.e., generally speaking,

$$\frac{a + b}{2} + c \neq \frac{a + b + c}{2}),$$

and the arithmetic mean of three numbers, generally speaking, is not expressed as a finite combination of the arithmetic mean of these numbers, taken two at a time (prove it!). However, as we have seen, using a transitive transition, the arithmetic mean of three numbers can be expressed through the arithmetic mean of two numbers. Similar limit relations relate the arthmetic $k + 1$ and $k$ numbers for any integer $k \geqslant 2$.
