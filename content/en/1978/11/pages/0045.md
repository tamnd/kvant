Now we need to find out which of the obtained solutions is the coordinates of the point $D_1$. Looking at the drawing, the reader will immediately see that the first solution is correct, and the second is the constructed one. The exam, however, requires a strict justification for choosing the correct solution. Before this justification is given, let us clarify the geometric meaning of the solutions obtained and explain why there are two of them.

The first equation of system (1) is the equation of a circle with center at point $M$ and radius equal to $|MD|$ (Fig. 2). The second equation of system (1) is the equation of a circle with center at point $A_1$ and radius equal to $|AD|$. The second equation intersects the first at two points $-D_1'(7; 7)$ and $D_1''(\frac{101}{17}; \frac{47}{17})$, one of which is the image of the point $D$. (If the point $D$ belongs to the direction [MA], then the second equation will touch the first, and we will obtain one solution to system (1), which will be the desired one.)

It is clear that $\triangle DMD_1'$ and $\triangle DMD_1''$ are equilateral and only one of the angles $\overrightarrow{DMD_1'}$, $\overrightarrow{DMD_1''}$ is equal to $\alpha$ (in our problem $\alpha = \frac{\pi}{2}$). Let's determine which one. Consider $\triangle DMD_1'$. Let's find $|DD_1'|$:

$$|DD_1'| = \sqrt{(7-3)^2 + (7-1)^2} = \sqrt{52}.$$
Let $F$ be the middle of $[DD_1']$, then $|DF| = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{52}.$ FROM $\triangle DMF$:

$$\sin \overrightarrow{DMF} = \frac{|DF|}{|DM|} = \frac{\sqrt{52}}{2\sqrt{26}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},$$

$$\overrightarrow{DMF} = \frac{\pi}{4} \Rightarrow \overrightarrow{DMD_1'} = \frac{\pi}{2}.$$

Blackout. Of the two points obtained when finding the image of a certain point $N(a; b)$, it is natural to choose for verification the one whose coordinates are “simpler” (this simplifies the calculations). If you find out that the point chosen for verification gives the desired solution, then it is the image of the point $N$. If it doesn’t, then, naturally, the image of the point is the second point.
