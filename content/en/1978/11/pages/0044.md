Questions using the coordinate method

The application of the coordinate method to solving geometric problems in «Kvant» has already been written *. New aspects of this important topic have now been touched upon.

Problem 1 (MIEM, 1977). With the new coordinate plane method, we go to a node α with a center at point M, point A (1; 2) to A₁ (6; 5), and B (1; 4) to B₁ (4; 5). Find the image of point C (1; 3). Find an image of another point (at your discretion). Find the values ​​of el α and the coordinates of point M.

Solution. Let us mark points A on the coordinate plane; A₁, B, B₁ and C (Fig. 1). To determine the coordinates (x; y) of point M, we use the fact that rotation is displacement and therefore |MA|²=|MA₁|² and |MB|²=|MB₁|², i.e.

$$\int (x-1)^2 + (y-2)^2 = (x-6)^2 + (y-5)^2$$
$$\int (x-1)^2 + (y-4)^2 = (x-4)^2 + (y-5)^2.$$

Solving this system, we find that x=2 and y=6.

Let $R_M^\alpha(C) = C_1$. Let us determine the coordinates $(x_1; y_1)$ of point C₁. Point C, as is easy to see, is the midpoint of [AB], and therefore its image - point C₁ - is the midpoint of [A₁B₁]. Taking this into account, we find that point C₁ has coordinates (5; 5).
