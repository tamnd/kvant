⟦folio 40⟧

Use only positive integers (and the number 0). It is possible, however, to imagine a system of numbers in which negative integers will also be used as numbers. Since the masses of the weights that were at my disposal were powers of three, I will remember the so-called balanced ternary system.

The basis of the system is the number 3, the symbols used as numbers are: 1, 0 and $\bar{1}$ (read: “one with a line”). The notes I made were numbers in this system. Here's how to understand them:

$$110\bar{1} = 1 \cdot 3^3 + 1 \cdot 3^2 + 0 \cdot 3^1 - 1 \cdot 3^0 = 35,$$
$$1\bar{1}0\bar{1} = 1 \cdot 3^4 - 1 \cdot 3^3 + 0 \cdot 3^2 - 1 \cdot 3^1 +$$
$$+ 1 \cdot 3^0 = 52,$$
$$11\bar{1}01 = 1 \cdot 3^4 + 1 \cdot 3^3 - 1 \cdot 3^2 + 0 \cdot 3^1 +$$
$$+ 1 \cdot 3^0 = 100.$$

If I placed a weight on a bowl with a large scale, then its mass was subtracted, and when recording, I wrote the number “1” in the category corresponding to the mass of the weight. If I need to weigh, for example, 24 g, then I will put a weight of 27 g on a bowl of scales without a load (without grains), and a weight of 3 g on the other bowl and write down: $10\bar{1}0 = 1 \cdot 3^3 + 0 \cdot 3^2 - 1 \cdot 3^1 +$
$$+ 0 \cdot 3^0 = 24.$$

Number entries in this system can also begin with the number “1” - this is how negative numbers are written. Example:

$$\bar{1}1\bar{1} = -1 \cdot 3^2 + 1 \cdot 3^1 - 1 \cdot 3^0 = -7.$$

With numbers written in a balanced ternary number system, it is not difficult to perform the operations of addition and multiplication.

Here's how simple the tables for adding and multiplying numbers look:

**Addition table**

| + | 1 | 0 | $\bar{1}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | $1\bar{1}$ | 1 | 0 |
| 0 | 1 | 0 | $\bar{1}$ |
| $\bar{1}$ | 0 | $\bar{1}$ | $1\bar{1}$ |

To simplify the work, I used an abacus, specially designed for adding numbers in a balanced ternary system. These abacus are depicted on figure 1. The main feature is a vertical partition, to the left of which there are “negative” bones, and to the right - “positive”. At figure 2 the number $11\bar{1}01$ is set aside on the abacus. The second feature is that the black bones are fastened to each other.

Let's learn how to add one to these accounts. Let us need to find the sum 1+1. How this is done can be seen from figure 3, $a = \beta$. At figure 3, $\delta$ the fastened black stones are connected to the already deposited stone; therefore, discarding all three bones from the wire to the right, we must put one negative bone on the same wire, and on the next wire - one “positive” bone (it, as it should be, replaces the discarded stones of the first wire) - see Fig. 3, $\delta$. The result can be checked by looking at the table of adding numbers. Using similar reasoning, you can learn to add negative ones.
