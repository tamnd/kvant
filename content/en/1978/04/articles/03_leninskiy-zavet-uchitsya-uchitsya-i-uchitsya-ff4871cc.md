Lenin's Testament:
“Study, study and study!”

Article 45. Citizens of the USSR have the right to education. This right ensures the freeness of all types of education, the implementation of universal secondary education for youth, the broad development of vocational technical, secondary specialized and higher education based on the connection of learning with life, with production; development of correspondence and evening education; We provide state scholarships and benefits to students; free distribution of school textbooks; the opportunity to study at school in their native language; creating conditions for self-education.

Constitution of the USSR

“Expressing the will of the Soviet people, fulfilling their instructions, the Supreme Soviet of the USSR adopted the new Constitution of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics. The Basic Law of the world's first national social state was approved. A new historical milestone in our movement towards communism is constitutionally enshrined - building the development of the country and benefits for students; free distribution of school textbooks; the opportunity to study at school in their native language; creating conditions for self-education.

A special section of the Constitution - “State and Personality” - establishes the rights, freedoms and obligations of citizens of the USSR. And a separate article in this section secures the right of every Soviet person to education. Moreover, the right to education is not simply declared - the Basic Law specifies what exactly this right guarantees. Millions of Soviet schoolchildren, students of vocational schools, technical students know well from their personal everyday experience how to effectively ensure the right to education, and constantly feel the paternal work of the Communist Party, the Soviet government, and all our people.

The Constitution of the USSR guarantees every Soviet person the right to education. Our state takes on large expenses for the education and upbringing of young people: for example, the annual cost per student in a comprehensive school is 160 rubles (excluding the costs of free issuance of school textbooks), in technical education - 640 rubles, in vzeta - over 1000 rubles. However, while ensuring the right to education, the social state of the people at the same time requires each of its citizens to make maximum use of this opportunity. The Basic Law of the country directly states: “The duty and duty of every citizen of the USSR capable of working is to work conscientiously in his chosen area of ​​socially useful activity, and to observe labor discipline” (Article 60). This provision of the USSR Constitution fully applies to young citizens of our Motherland - schoolchildren. Every

Every Soviet schoolchild must use the present, fight for a high level of knowledge, cultivate in himself a deep, persuasive ideal, hard work, discipline, and prepare for socially useful work.

At all stages of the history of the Soviet state, the parties and the government paid special attention to the issues of education, training and upbringing of the younger generation. The October Revolution took place - and the Soviet government was faced with thousands and thousands of urgent, cardinal problems. The very existence of the young resource workers depended on the fate of many of them. But even in these difficult conditions, V.I. Lenin always found time to direct the process of forming a new Soviet system of educating youth, the formation of a new Soviet school. Already in our first Constitution - the Constitution of the RSFSR of 1918 - the vitally important task was formulated “to provide workers and the poorest peasants with complete, comprehensive and free education.” During the years of intervention and civil war, during the years of economic devastation, unemployment and hunger, Lenin’s concern for children was fully manifested - the country gave them everything possible and all the best.

From the first days of its existence, the Soviet government, under the leadership of V.I. Lenin, energetically began to eradicate the shameful legacy of tsarism - mass illiteracy. And this server problem was solved in an unprecedentedly short time. In 1906, the Russian magazine “Bulletin of Education” wrote that it would take 4,600 (!) years to see illiteracy in Kazakhstan and Central Asia. But Kazakhstan and the resource workers of Central Asia have long become the land of complete literacy. In cities and villages, the Kazakh SCP alone currently employs over 160 thousand people; Before the Great October Revolution there were no conscription in Kazakhstan, but now more than 200 thousand students are studying in 50 high educational institutions. The basis of these achievements is the socialist system of the Land of Soviets; nothing like this is possible in the capitalist world. Here is data from the US Federal Bureau of Education: in the richest capitalist country, which has declared itself a “welfare state,” today there are 23 million adults who cannot even read a railroad schedule. According to experts from the international organization UNESCO, which deals with issues of education, science and culture, up to 800 million people around the globe are now completely illiterate, that is, one adult out of three cannot read or write.

The Soviet school has traversed a glorious path over the 60 years of our state’s existence, and all its successes are a real implementation of Lenin’s immortal covenants. However, one of the replacement social and cultural achievements of the Soviet people during the Ninth Five-Year Plan was the transition to universal secondary education. Now universal compulsory secondary education for young people is enshrined in the Constitution of the USSR. Outlining the prospects for a ten-year, five-year plan for efficiency and quality, the 25th Congress of the CPSU in the “Main Directions for the Development of the National Economy of the USSR for 1976-1980” indicated the need to “further develop the public education system in accordance with the requirements of scientific and technological progress and the task of steadily increasing the cultural, technical and general educational level of the working people.” Fulfilling this instruction of the congress, the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR adopted a number of Resolutions that set out specific ways to improve the process of training and education of students in secondary schools and vocational technical educational institutions. In No. 1 of our magazine for 1977, we already talked about the special attention the government paid to improving the working conditions of rural secondary schools.

The words of V.I. Lenin, spoken by him at the III All-Russian Komsomol Congress, are widely known: “You can become a communist only when you enrich your memory with the knowledge of all the riches that a person has produced.” Vladimir Ilyich formulated this task at the dawn of socialist society. It was relevant then - and it is even more relevant today. After all, what a collective farm machine operator does today in a range of jobs was until recently considered accessible only to a technical person or an engineer. And this places new and new demands on the level and quality of knowledge of any worker - regardless of whether he works in a factory or in the field, in construction or in transport, in a scientific laboratory or in a school. Thus, for every member of a society building communism, secondary education has become an objective necessity.

That is why the Resolution of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR “On further improvement of the process of training and education of students in the vocational technical education system” sets the task of “developing and improving the work of secondary vocational technical schools ... in which the necessary conditions have been created for young people to master modern professions, receive a complete secondary education, and comprehensively prepare for an independent working life.” Soviet professional technical outlook, capable of mastering and improving the new technical school, providing a completed secondary education, opens all the ways for further education both in technical secondary specialized educational institutions and in higher education. Moreover, anyone who graduates with honors from a secondary vocational technical school, after a successful entrance exam, is enrolled in a university without competition.

On the very eve of 1978, the Decree of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR “On further improvement of training, education of students in secondary schools and preparing them for work” was published. In this document, topical issues of labor training, ideological-political and legal education, and professional guidance of students were widely developed. It says, in particular: “In modern conditions, when the country has made a transition to a comprehensively educated, high school graduate over the period of educational areas with deep knowledge of the basics of science and labor skills for work in the national economy, close preparation for mastering a certain profession. The need for a decisive turn in schools to better prepare young people for work in the sphere of material production, to ensure the choice of vocational guidance.”

The decisions of the party and government on education issues pose important tasks for the editorial board and the editors of «Kvant» and require further improvement of the quality of the journal in accordance with current problems of schools. Developing young people's interest in science, strengthening the connection between science and school, promoting physical and mathematical knowledge, comprehensively developing the role and importance of mathematics and physics for practice - all this will undoubtedly contribute to both the deepening of schoolchildren's knowledge and career guidance.

Tomorrow our senior high school students will join the ranks of active builders of communist society. And there is no doubt that they will devote all their strength and knowledge to bringing to life the immortal covenants of the Great Lenin.
