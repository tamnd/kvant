Amazing calculator

Many of you have probably heard about small computers that fit in your pocket and can calculate “anything.”
This article talks about one of these machines: the Elektronka B3-18 microcalculator. The dimensions of this device are 160×90×46 mm, and the weight is only 400 grams, but thanks to the achievements of modern electronics, this “magic box” contains more than 60 thousand elements: transistors, resistors, capacitors...! How did you manage to shrink so many electronic devices into such a small box? It is impossible to talk about the arrangement of Elektronka and its reduction in one article. Therefore, we will limit ourselves to talking about her mathematical abilities.

We had to watch people who first picked up “Electronics” in their hands. Most of them tried to figure out its occurrences on their own, without looking at the instructions. Please, this way of mastering the calculator is the most interesting. But for our great readers this path is closed. Therefore, we invite you to call on your imagination and imagine that you have an “Electronic” in your hands.

The first step, of course, is to press the “on” button (everyone will tell you this). In this case, the number 0 and a comma are displayed in the rightmost digit of the digital display. Let's find the sum 7+3 using a calculator. It is natural to press the keys with the images of numbers and the operation sign. So, press the “7” key (see figure) - the number 7 appears on the digital display, displayed in green. Next, press the “+” key - no changes are observed. Then we press the “3” key: the previous number disappears from the board and the number 3 appears in its place. Finally, we press the “=” key, and 10 appears on the board - the result of solving the problem (note that all numbers - 7, 3, 10 - are displayed on the right side of the board).

It is clear that any argumentative example with single-digit numbers should be solved in exactly the same way. What about multi-digit numbers? Press key 7 twice in a row. “77” lights up on the display. What if we need the number 65? Which key should you press first? Try both options, it’s easy to find out that you first need to press the “6” key, and then “5”. It’s clear how to use the buttons with numbers and numbers

Having solved several problems, we will notice that all the resulting numbers have no more digits, and the leftmost - ninth - digit of the board is intended for the sign of the number: “-” lights up in it if the result is a negative number.

The calculator can calculate the values ​​of expressions with multiple operations. So, to add several numbers, you need to type the first addend, press the “+” key, then type the second addend, press “+” again, etc., and at the very end press “=”. The “,” and “/—/” keys are used to enter decimal fractions and change the sign of a number. If you need to type a decimal fraction, then first type its signs up to the decimal point, then press the “,” key, and then its remaining signs. To make the number sign “minus” first, you need to press the “/—/” key. For example, the table shows how to dial the number - 48.587.

So, the calculator can perform any arithmetic operations: it does it silently and almost instantly. Those who have to work on arithmetic will appreciate the advantages of “Electronics”.
But this is not all that can be considered about the device. It also quickly calculates the values ​​of many functions. From figure it is clear that in addition to the symbol placed indefinitely on the key, a certain designation is written above each key. Basically, these are designations of functions: logarithmic, trigonometric, etc. In order to calculate the values ​​of these functions, you need to use the “F” key.

Let, for example, you want to calculate $\sqrt{20,25}$. To do this, dial the number 20.25; it will be displayed on the display. Then you need to press the “F” and “V^-” keys (“V^-” is the same key as “9”), and then display the result on the display - the number 4.5.

Pay attention to the sequence of key symbols used to solve this problem:

«2» «0» «,» «2» «5» «F» «V^-»

The written sequence of characters is a command to calculate the value of the square root of a given number. Such a command should contain the original number - the argument of the function, then the sign of the function F and then - the designation

Similarly, the calculator calculates the values ​​of the functions $1/x$, $10^x$, $e^x$, logarithmic functions (decimal and natural logarithm) and trigonometric functions.

We'll tell you how to calculate the values ​​of functions $x^y$ using a calculator. Let, for example, we need to calculate $5,1^{3,92}$. First, we dial the number 5.1 (it appears on the scoreboard). After that, press the “F” and “$x^y$” keys. The number 1.629241=ln 5.1 appears on the board (the fact is that the calculator calculates $x^y$ using the formula $x^y = e^y \ln x$). Next, we dial the number 3.92 (it appears on the scoreboard). Finally, by pressing the “=” key, we get the number 593.8486 on the scoreboard, which is the original result: $5,1^{3,92} = 593,8486$.

To appreciate the convenience of solving computational problems using a calculator, just use it in mathematics lessons at school - using tables or slide rules. In this case, it is necessary to logarithm, potentiate and perform other operations. All this takes a lot of time. The calculator, giving a greater gain in time to solve the problem, guarantees the accuracy of the solution and eliminates various technical errors.

To calculate the values ​​of inverse trigonometric functions, the “arc” class is used; and using the “rad/grad” switch, the result can be expressed in degrees or radians. For example, to calculate arctg 1 in degrees, you need to set the switch to the “grad” position and successively press the keys “1”, “F”, “arc”, “tg”. The result will appear on the scoreboard - the number 45 (or perhaps its approximation, for example, without taking into account the person). But in any case, a mathematician cannot complain that the calculator somehow slows down his work. Please, on the contrary - the calculator ends up spending too much time waiting for the next command.

In educational practice, the calculator is a worthy successor to mathematical tables and counting rulers. We hope that the time is not far when these or similar computers will become necessary (and affordable!) devices in mathematics, physics, and chemistry lessons.
