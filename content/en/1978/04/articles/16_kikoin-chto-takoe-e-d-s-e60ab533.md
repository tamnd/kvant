What is emf?

At the very beginning of the 19th century, the Italian scientist Alexandro Volleta created a device capable of maintaining a constant electric current in a closed conductive circuit. He called this device an electromotive apparatus. Later it was called a galvanic cell. Volta believed, and not without reason, that there were some forces acting in this device that caused electric charges to move. Perhaps that is why the main characteristic of the device invented by Volt, and all other similar devices, began to be designated by the term “electromotive force.” That's what they call it today.

However, in reality there is an electromotive force - emf?

To understand this issue, let us briefly recall the basic information about such concepts as electric current and electrical field system.

Electric current and electrical field system

Electric current is an ordered, that is, directed movement of identical electric charges. In metals, the directionally moving charged particles are electrons. These particles are “directed” by the electrosistic field created in the metal. Electrosistic field is a field created by stationary electric charges. The main characteristic of the electrosistic field is the directionality $\vec{E}$.

In an electrosistic field, the work done when moving a charge from one point of the field to another does not depend on the trajectory along which the charge moves between these points. It follows that the work done by the field on a charge as it moves along a closed path is zero.

The work that a field does when moving a single charge between two points is called the electric direction between these points of the field.

From the course of mechanics it is known that the amount of work is associated with another quantity - potential energy. Namely, the work done when moving a body is equal to the change in the potential energy of the body taken with the opposite sign during such movement.

In order to determine the value of the potential energy of a charge at a given point in the field, you need to select the “zero level” - the point at which the potential energy is taken equal to zero. In electrical engineering, the zero level is taken to be any point connected by a conductor to the Earth. In physics, they usually choose any point infinitely distant from the charge creating the field.

Since the work done when moving a charge between two points in an electrostatic field does not depend on the trajectory along which the charge acts, the potential energy of the charge at a given point in the field does not depend on how (by what path) the charge got to a given point. This energy, referred to a unit of positive charge, can serve as a characteristic of a given field point. This is the potential at a given point in the field. Thus, the electrical voltage between two points is the potential difference between those points. (It should be noted that this is only true for an electrostatic field. There are also electric fields for which this is not true.)

A positive electric charge in an electrostatic field moves in the direction of the field strength vector. In this case, the field does positive work, and therefore the potential energy of the charge decreases. This means that a positive charge in an electrostatic field moves from a higher potential to a lower one. On the contrary, the negative charge in the field moves from low to high potential.

Direct electric current and the work of the electrostatic field

Let us now consider a closed circuit through which an electric current flows. We will turn to the most familiar example of current in a circuit composed of metal conductors. In these conductors, the moving charges (current carriers) are free electrins. These are electrins that have been detached from atoms and have therefore become “free”.
Their freedom lies in the fact that under the influence of an electric field they can move in metal. The atoms themselves that have lost electrins are positively charged ions that form the crystal lattice of the metal. As you know, current is the direction of movement of identical charges. These charges are “directed” by the force with which the electrostatic field created in the metal acts on them. Moving in the metal, electrins interact with each other and transfer to them the energy acquired by them in the electric field. (We know this from experience - the metal heats up when a current passes.) The energy acquired by the electrins is equal, very much, to the work done by the field on the electrins. Therefore, this work is not zero. But, as is known, the work of the electrostatic field when a charge moves along a closed circuit is zero. This means that the movement of electrins along a closed circuit cannot occur only under the influence of an electrostatic field. And if there is a current in the circuit, then this means that somewhere in the circuit some other, non-electstatic, field has been created, the work of which on charges along a closed path is not equal to zero. There must be some kind of constant force at work here relative to the electrostatic ones. Their commonality is called side forces. The work of these forces is very indo, and the electrins compensate for the energy that they lose when interacting with them. So in the end, it is these forces that maintain a constant current in the circuit.

The role of outside forces

To understand the role of side forces in a DC circuit, let's first consider a low-voltage circuit, such as at figure 1. Let us assume that somehow, at some point, end A of the circuit accumulates an excess number of electrins, so that it becomes negatively charged. Then at the other end of the chain there is an excess of positive charge. An electrostatic field will exist inside the metal, and free electrins will rush from A to B. In the metal, therefore, there will be an electric current directed from B to A. But this current will not be constant. In a very short time, the excess of electrins in A will disappear, and the excess of positive charge in B will disappear. The field in the metal will disappear, and with it the electric current.

But let us imagine that in the interval between A and B there is a certain

Then, obviously, $A$ will maintain a constant release charge, and $B$ will maintain a constant positive charge. The metal will maintain a constant electric field and a constant electric current.

Note, however, that our fairy must do work against the power field. After all, charges in $A$ and $B$ create such a field not only inside the metal, but also outside it. Power plant forces, of course, are not capable of this. So the fairy who does this acts on the electrons not with an electrical power force, but with some other - external force. The role of a third-party force in an electrical circuit becomes clear: to transfer electrical charges against power plant forces, to separate negative charges from positive ones and “attach” them to the discharge ones. External forces of their work close the circuit and ensure the constancy of the point. There are two sections in the electrical circuit: in one, through the efforts of the fairies, the charges of different signs are separated, and in the other (in the outer part of the circuit) they are connected again. Fey does work by separating charges and creating an electric field. And this latter connects the charges, and inside the metal this work is converted to heating the metal. The activity of fairies fills the activity of Sisyphus, who, according to ancient myth, places a stone on the top of a mountain, which, when reaching the top, rolls down. Just as Sisyphus, lifting a stone, did work against the forces of gravity only so that the stone, having acquired potential energy, could roll down the mountain under the influence of gravity, our fairy, separating different charges, does work against the forces of the power field only in order to acquire potential energy, these charges could, under the influence of power forces, unite again. However, the work of fen cannot be called Sisyphus in the sense that is commonly said. After all, Sisyphus is called “meaningless”, useless work. And fen’s work leads to tangible results - direct current flows through the circuit.

Let's return again to our electrical circuit. Of course, no non-power plant fairies actually exist. But there are special devices in which non-electrical (third-party) forces act, capable of separating negatively charged electrons from positive charges. These devices are called current sources, although in reality they are not immi, as we have just seen. They differ from current sources from other characteristics of the forces in them and the origins of the processes and designs of the devices themselves. They differ in quantity by a value called electromotive force.

What is electromotive force?

Let us consider the non-octoral section of the electrical circuit between points $a$ and $b$ (Fig. 2), in which an electric current exists. Since the current exists, it means there is an electric field. Let there be a voltage $U$ in this area, or, what is the same, a potential difference $\varphi_1 - \varphi_2$. Then the work done when moving the neoctor charge $q$ from $a$ to $b$ is equal to $A = qU$.

Now let’s imagine that in section $ab$ there is also a non-actor current source in which the sides of the force (Fig. 3) act. Now we need to add the work of external forces to the work of the power plant field $qU$.

If, when moving a unit of charge to the side, the forces do work $\mathcal{E}$, then work is done on the charge $q$ when moving it along the area $ab$

$$A = qU + q\mathcal{E}.$$

But if the target is closed (at figure 3 the circuit closure is indicated by a dotted line), then the work $qU$ of the power plant field is zero, and therefore $A = q\mathcal{E}$. From here

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{A}{q}.$$

This is the work of external forces in the source - this is the source - this is actually the source of energy. The side of power at the source is the source (and the only!) difficulties in the power plant circuit. It is they, separating charges of opposite signs and at the same time doing work against the forces of attraction between them, that create a potential difference at the so-called poles of the source, and an electric field in the conductors forming the circuit. Due to work, energy is released in the circuit - the conductors heat up.

As is known, when the target power plant consists of metal conductors, Ohm's law applies. According to this law, the voltage $U$ at the ends of a certain section of the circuit is equal to the product of the current strength in this section and the resistance $R$ of the section:

$$U = IR.$$

Recall that voltage $U$ is the work of the power plant field on a unit of positive charge. If in this area there is a current source with e. d.s. $\mathcal{E}$, then to work $U$ you need to add work $\mathcal{E}$, so

$$I(R+r) = U + \mathcal{E}.$$

We added to the resistance $R$ of the section the resistance $r$ of the current source itself. After all, resistance is a quantity that characterizes the process of interaction of electrons with atoms of a conductor, due to which heat is released in it. But a similar interaction also occurs inside the current source, and therefore some resistance must also be assigned to it.

Formula (*) refers to a section of the chain. For a complete (i.e. closed) chain $U=0$, and the formula takes the form

$$I(R+r) = \mathcal{E}.$$

This is Ohm's law for a complete circuit.

From formula (**) it is clear how the electromotive force should change. This formula can be given the form $IR+Ir = \mathcal{E}$. Since $IR=U$, then $\mathcal{E}=U+Ir$. From here it is immediately clear that at $I=0$ $\mathcal{E}=U$. This means that to determine the E.m.f. A source needs to change the potential difference at its poles when there is no current in it (due to the fact that the circuit is open, or because the current from the source is compensated by the current from another source).

The upper part of the tape goes under the dome. With the help of a motor, the belt is set in motion. In its lower part, the tape passes through a number of points connected to a high-voltage power source, consisting of a high-voltage transformer and a coupling device. The other end of the device is grounded. A high intensity field is created at the tip, and it “plucks” the electrodes from that part of the tape that passes a lot of it. This part of the tape receives a positive charge. It is carried upward by a ribbon, under the dome. Here the tape again passes a lot of the point attached to the dome. Electrodes “drain” from the tip, and therefore from the dome, onto the tape carrying a positive charge. This is equivalent to transferring a positive charge attached to the ribbon to the dome. And since this charge is transferred to the inside surface of the dome, the entire attached charge is transferred to it. (The transfer of charge precisely to the inside surface of the metal cavity is the main idea of ​​the generator device.)

With continuous movement of the belt, there is a continuous transfer of positive charge to the positive charging dome. (The negative charge is transferred to the Earth.) The charge is therefore transferred against electrostatic forces: after all, the positively charged dome repels the positive charge applied to it. And such “counteraction” is ensured by the movement of the charge by both sides. In this case, it is ultimately the static friction force of the belt and pulleys. The work of this force, relative to a unit of charge, is the electromotive force of the generator. A potential difference is established between the dome and the Earth. And if the external part of the circuit is connected to the Earth and the dome, then a constant electric current arises in this closed circuit. The energy due to which work is performed is the energy of the engine power source.

The described generator is used, as a rule, to root charged parts in a vacuum, since a very high potential difference of several million volts can be created between the dome and the Earth. But such a generator cannot create a large current due to the limited speed of the tape, its relatively small width, and other reasons. We give this example because the nature and role of outside forces is especially clear here.

Galvanic cell

Another example is the long and well-extracted galvanic element, which was the first “electromotive device” in history, invented by Alexandro Volta.

The Volta cell consists of a copper and zinc plate immersed in an aqueous solution of sulfuric acid (Fig.5). To obtain the complex processes that occur in this element, we note that solid copper and zinc are composed not of copper and zinc atoms, but of the corresponding copper and zinc isons and electrons: each of the copper and zinc atoms in the metal loses two electrons, which remain in the metal in the form of those very free electrons mentioned earlier. And the atoms become ions with a positive charge.

Zinc and copper ions each carry a double electronic charge ($Zn^{++}, Cu^{++}$). Further, it should be borne in mind that in a solution of sulfuric acid, a significant part of the molecules turns into three ions - two positively charged hydrogen ions ($H^+$) and one on-group $SO_4^-$ with a double negative charge ($SO_4^{--}$):

$$H_2SO_4 \rightarrow H^+ + H^+ + SO_4^{--}$$

Let's see what happens when zinc and copper plates are immersed in such a solution. Let's start with zinc.

In the presence of the acid solution and the siliptic charges it contains, the attraction between zinc ions and electrons decreases, and the zinc ions are torn from the plate and transferred to the solution. The plant ends up with an excess of electrons, so it becomes negatively charged. But as $Zn^{++}$ ions accumulate in the solution, the remaining part of these ions, pressed down by the negative charge of the plate, returns to the growth. Eventually, equilibrium occurs: the number of ions leaving the zinc becomes equal to the number of ions returning to it. But the plant remains negatively charged. The solution near the plate receives a positive charge (due to the positive ions $Zn^{++}$ present in it).

In copper plants, different processes occur. Copper almost does not dissolve in sulfuric acid solution. When present in the zinc plant, zinc ions repel the positive hydrogen ions in the solution, assigning them to the copper plant. Here, these ions, falling on copper, take away electrons from it, becoming neutral hydrogen atoms. the copper, of course, becomes positively charged. Thus, in the Volta element, copper is the positive band, and zinc is the negative band. The potential difference between them is the electromotive force of this element. It is equal to 1.1 V.

If you connect the stripes (electrons) of an element with a wire, then an electric current will arise in the resulting electrical circuit, directed from copper to zinc. This means that the electrons in it move from zinc, where they are abundant, to copper, where there is a shortage of electrons.

What about the element itself? Here, due to the departure of electrons from the zinc, the $Zn^{++}$ ions will be held weaker, and they will begin to move into dissolve (zinc will dissolve), maintaining a negative charge of the plate. And due to the arrival of electrons to the copper plate, it will become more “attractive” to itself of positive hydrogen ions, to which these electrons will go. So the copper plate will also maintain a positive charge. Thus, the end result of these processes in the element will be the departure of electrons from copper and their appearance on zinc: electrons seem to move from copper to zinc.

In a galvanic element, the side sliams are the slims of molecular interaction in metal plates and in solution. Although these slims are electrical in nature, the useful ones cannot be called electrostatic (they are generally called chemical slims). The operation of these slims is electrical in nature! The source of energy here is the energy released during the neutralization of ions in the solution and on the plant. After all, in order to transform an atom or molecule into ions, it is necessary to perform the work of attraction between charges. Therefore, during the processing transformation of ions into neutral atoms, energy is released.
