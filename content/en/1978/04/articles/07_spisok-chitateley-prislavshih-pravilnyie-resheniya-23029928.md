⟦folio 21⟧

$$m_k \left( \left(v_k^{(s)}\right)^2 - \left(v_k^{(s-1)}\right)^2 \right) = m_{k+1} \left( \left(v_{k+1}^{(s-1)}\right)^2 - \left(v_{k+1}^{(s)}\right)^2 \right).$$

Dividing the last equality by the penultimate one, we get the correspondence
$$v_k^{(s)} + v_{k+1}^{(s-1)} = v_{k+1}^{(s-1)} + v_{k+1}^{(s)}.$$

It is valid for an infinite number of collisions, and in it one can go to the limit at $s \to \infty$. For limiting speeds, therefore, we will have: $2w_k = 2w_{k+1}$, whence $w_k = w_{k+1}$. So, all maximum speeds are equal to each other, $w_1 = w_2 = \dots = w_n = w$.

$3^\circ$. Let $v_1^0, \dots, v_n^0$ be the initial velocities; then from the law of conservation of momentum it follows
$$m_1 v_1^0 + \dots + m_n v_n^0 = m_1 v_1^0 + \dots + m_n v_n^0$$
for any $s$. Therefore, passing to the limit at $s \to \infty$, we obtain
$$m_1 v_1 + \dots + m_n v_n = (m_1 + \dots + m_n)w$$
where $w$ is the general limit of all speeds. Hence,
$$w = \frac{m_1 v_1 + \dots + m_n v_n}{m_1 + \dots + m_n},$$
that is, $w$ is the speed of movement of the center of mass of all balls.

Let us move on to the center of mass system, that is, to the coordinate reference system, which moves relative to the original reference system with a speed of $w$. In this system, all limit speeds are 0; therefore, the limiting energy in the center of mass system is equal to 0. Since the center of mass system is also inertial, the law of conservation of energy is valid in it, therefore the initial total energy of the balls in the center of mass system is equal to 0. This can only be when all initial velocities in the center of mass system were equal to 0, that is, when in the original reference frame all initial velocities were equal to $w$; but with the same initial speeds, there can be no collisions between the balls at all - a contradiction.

The reader, who did not consider the transition to the center of mass system in the proof of the coincidence of the initial velocities, can directly show by calculation that the value $\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \left(v_i^s - w\right)^2$ is conserved (and should tend to 0 at $s \to \infty$).

Note that Moscow mathematicians L. Wasserstein and G. Galperin generalized the above reasoning to the case of balls on a plane or in space that are absolutely elastically colliding with each other, and also proved the finiteness of the number of collisions.
