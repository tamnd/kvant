This discovery has some rather unexpected consequences. The fact is that actions cannot form in the depths of stars. Already at a fairly “moderate” temperature of several million degrees, nuclear reactions occur, leading to the destruction of nuclei of activity. Therefore, just like ordinary hydrogen, it must have a “relict” origin. It turns out that the nuclear reactions in which the action was synthesized should have taken place when the temperature of the Universe was several billion degrees, and its age was only about 10 minutes! This is where the “eyes” of radio telecops can look...

A special place in space radio spectroscopy is occupied by the study of the lines of the hydroxyl molecule OH, the wavelengths of which (there are only four) are about 18 cm. The fact is that at this wave, quite unexpectedly, very small sources were discovered, usually located in gaseous nebulae. It turned out that these sources operate on the same principle as lasers—quantum generators of electromagnetic waves in the visible range of the spectrum. For radio waves, the sources are also called *masers*. In addition, very bright sources in the H₂O water vapor lines with a wavelength of 1.35 cm, having the same nature, were discovered in approximately the same places in the sky. Thus, it turned out that nature implements in natural conditions a device that is, in our time, a symbol of the scientific and technological revolution experienced by mankind! What is most curious is that these amazing sources are located in close proximity to the region of the Galaxy, where, according to all data, the still largely mysterious process of the formation of young stars from the interstellar gaseous medium originated. Thus, radio engineering, quite unexpectedly, turned out to be a powerful means of studying this long-standing classical problem.

When listing the outstanding achievements of radio astronomy over the past 10-15 years, one cannot remain silent about the discovery that excited humanity 10 years ago. We are talking about the discovery of *pulsars*. This completely unusual class of radio sources is characterized by a strict periodicity of radio signals. The shortest known period is close to 1/30 of a second, the longest is about 4 seconds. Research has shown that pulsars are very rapidly rotating *neutron stars*, and the period of rotation coincides with the observed periodicity of radio pulses. What is a neutron star? Imagine our Sun rolled down to a size of about 10 km. Since the radius of the Sun is close to 700,000 km, and its average density is 1.4 g/cm³, the average density of a 10-kilometer ball - a neutron star - will be close to 10¹⁵ g/cm³. This is a surprisingly large value, close to the density of the substance of an atomic nucleus.

Neutron stars, as the natural end of the evolution of certain stars, were theoretically predicted over 40 years ago. These stars (with one important exception) cannot be detected in optical rays. Only the methods of advanced radio astronomy have made it possible in our day to discover neutron stars - the dream of two generations of physicists and astronomers around the world. This is one of the greatest discoveries in astronomy and physics of the 20th century, rich in outstanding achievements of science.
