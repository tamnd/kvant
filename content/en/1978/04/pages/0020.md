3°. What remains is a purely geometric problem about the maximum number of reflections of a point from the faces of a dihedral angle of a given value $\alpha$. It follows from conditions (A) and (B) that the segment of the trajectory of the point after reflection from $P$ is symmetrical with respect to the plane $P$ to the continuation of the original segment of the trajectory beyond the point of impact on $P$ (Fig. 6). Consequently, any broken line - the trajectory of a point moving according to the law of elastic reflection between the faces of a dihedral angle - can be “straightened”, that is, by successive symmetries with respect to the faces of the angle, transformed into a straight line, as shown in figure 7, $a$ (in this case, we reflect the dihedral angle itself along with the trajectory). It is more convenient to look at this picture “from the side” - from the side of the common edge of the resulting dihedral angles (in other words, the spatial drawing can be projected onto a plane perpendicular to the edge of the dihedral angle, as in figure 7, 6). If for the first time the point $M$ is contained with the face $P$, then after straightening the continuation of the trajectory will lie on one side of the plane $P$ and can intersect only those half-planes obtained by successive symmetries that lie on the same side of $P$. It is clear that the number of such half-planes is equal to $[\pi/\alpha]$, unless $\alpha \neq \pi/n$, $n \in \mathbb{N}$; in the case where $\alpha = \pi/n$, the last half-plane will lie in the plane $P$, and the straightened trajectory cannot intersect it - in this case, the number of intersection points is no more than $[\pi/\alpha] - 1$.
