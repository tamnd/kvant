The great Norwegian mathematician Niels Henrik Abel once said that “geometry is the art of good reasoning from bad drawings.” Indeed, it is impossible to make an absolutely correct drawing. Moreover, if we are talking about, say, an arbitrary triangle, and you drew it equilateral or rectangular, then this may lead you to a solution that is suitable only for a partial case.

However, if we are talking about a figure with a predetermined property, then you need to build and make the drawing more precise. A clearly executed drawing often suggests an idea for solving a problem.

Here is a drawing for the following problem, intended for the XIX International Mathematical Olympiad:

Equilateral triangles ABK, BCL, CDM, DAN are constructed inside this square ABCD.

Prove that the midpoints of four segments KL, LM, MN and MK, together with the midpoints of eight segments AK, KB, BL, LC, CM, MD, DN and NA, are the vertices of a regular dodecagon.

(One of her decisions can be read in «Kvant» No. 2 for this year)

Try to solve it in your mind while looking at the drawing.

A. Savin
