The most complex problems associated with dark radiation and the meaningful name “ultrafilt catastrophe” received.
If the success of a new idea, the author says, boiled down to only this, it would be unlikely that so much special significance would be attached to it. Planck's quantum hypothesis would also have met the fate of a private concept if it had not explained a whole group of experimental facts and predicted new ones. However, one of the most important was the footage effect, for the interpretation of which Einstein had to introduce the concept of facts. This was followed by numerous other facts in which the corpuscular properties of light and the wave properties of particles were revealed. The problem of dualism, the two-facedness of matter, arose. How to imagine matter that simultaneously has interacting properties: the properties of a particle and the properties of a wave? It turns out that the reason for this apparent incompatibility is that we, in the words of Max Born, “squeeze this (real) world into insufficiently adequate models that have many (but by no means all) features in common with their prototype.” The real world is much more complex than the mechanical models used to visualize it!

Chapter VI “Structure of Matter” gives an idea of ​​the zonal model of the structure of a solid body and, with its help, interprets the properties of metals, dielectrics, and semiconductors. From this point of view, phenomena such as superfluidity, superconductivity, ferromagnetism are also discussed, and the principle of laser design is clearly explained. This is almost the only chapter where the author does not limit himself to purely physical problems, but also touches on non-technical applications.

The next two chapters (“Radioactivity” and “Nuclear Forces and Nuclear Energy”) are devoted to the basics of nuclear physics. It describes the existence of various isotopes, appendices a colorful table of stable and unstable (with different types of decay) nuclei, describes instruments for recording nuclear particles, as well as accelerators

The last chapter, “Elementary Particles,” is the most interesting in the book. And this topic is famous because it is associated with the most important and most difficult area of ​​modern research - particle physics. First, it talks about cosmic rays - a natural source of high-energy particles, and then considers the properties of the so-called elementary particles themselves, which in reality are not elementary. In the final paragraph, entitled “What remains unanswered?”, the author touches on questions borderline for particle physics and cosmology. It turns out that the microcosm and the macrocosm are connecting somewhere, or rather, not just connecting, but are closely interconnected and determine each other. Nature is one!

G. Linder's book is not without shortcomings: it contains inaccurate and sometimes contradictory statements, so the reader should not be very careful. But overall, this is a wonderful gift for anyone who loves physics, especially for high school students. It will undoubtedly bring them usefulness in learning the basics of physics and will provide true natural convenience.

I. Zorich
