Six proofs of the median theorem

You can't ruin your mouth guard with oil.

The theorem about the intersection of the medians of a triangle is not proven in the textbooks “Geometry 6, 7, 8”, but is assumed in the form of a problem. This theorem is then used in the Geometry 9 textbook. Here we present several of its proofs.

Theorem. The medians AA₁, BB₁ and CC₁ of triangle ABC intersect at some point M, and each of them is divided by this point in the ratio 2:1, counting from the vertex:
|AM| : |MA₁| = |BM| : |MB₁| =
= |CM| : |MC₁| = 2. (1)

In all the proofs given below, except for the sixth, we establish only that the median BB₁ passes through the point M, which divides the median AA₁ from the 2:1 ratio.

If, according to the corresponding reasoning, we replace [BB₁] with [CC₁], then we obtain that [CC₁] also passes through M. This will prove that all three medians intersect at some point M, and |AM| : |MA₁| = 2.

Since all medians are equal, we can replace [AA₁] with [BB₁] or [CC₁]; (1) follows from here.
