Squares $MAC$ and $MA_1C$ (Fig. 3).
Their heights, omitted from the vertex $C$, coincide, and the lengths of the sides opposite this vertex are in the ratio 2:1, therefore $S_{AMC} = 2S_{A1MC}$.
Likewise, $S_{AMB} = 2S_{A1MB}$.
But $S_{A1MC} = S_{A1MB}$. Therefore, $S_{AMC} = S_{AMB} = S_{BMC}$.
Thus, triangles $MAB$, $MBC$ and $MCA$ are equal in size. Let $B' = (BM) \cap (AC)$. Let us prove that $|AB'| = |B'C|$. On the one hand, $\frac{|AB'|}{|B'C|} = \frac{S_{AB'M}}{S_{CB'M}}$. On the other hand, $\frac{|AB'|}{|B'C|} = \frac{S_{AB'B}}{S_{CB'B}}$. So, $\frac{|AB'|}{|B'C|} = \frac{S_{AB'B}}{S_{CB'B}}$. Use theorem $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \Rightarrow \frac{c-a}{d-b} = \frac{a}{b}$.
from here we get
$$\frac{|AB'|}{|B'C|} = \frac{S_{AB'B} - S_{AB'M}}{S_{CB'B} - S_{CB'M}} = \frac{S_{ABM}}{S_{CBM}} = 1.$$

Fifth proof (8th grade). Let again $B' = (BM) \cap (AC)$ (Fig. 3). Applying the theorem of sines first to triangles $AB'B$ and $CB'B$, and then to triangles $ABM$ and $A_1BM$, learning that $\sin \overrightarrow{AB'B} = \sin \overrightarrow{CB'B}$, $\sin \overrightarrow{AMB} = \sin \overrightarrow{A_1MB}$, $|BC| = 2|A_1B|$ and $|MA| = 2|MA_1|$, we obtain
$$\frac{|AB'|}{|B'C|} =$$
$$= \frac{|AB| \sin \overrightarrow{ABM}}{\sin \overrightarrow{AB'B}}: \frac{|BC| \sin \overrightarrow{A_1BM}}{\sin \overrightarrow{CB'B}} =$$
$$= \frac{|MA| \sin \overrightarrow{AMB}}{2|MA_1| \sin \overrightarrow{A_1BM}} = 1.$$

So, $|AB'| = |B'C|$.
