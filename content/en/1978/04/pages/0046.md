Then, obviously, $A$ will maintain a constant release charge, and $B$ will maintain a constant positive charge. The metal will maintain a constant electric field and a constant electric current.

Note, however, that our fairy must do work against the power field. After all, charges in $A$ and $B$ create such a field not only inside the metal, but also outside it. And the field outside the metal in the interval between $A$ and $B$ is also directed from $B$ to $A$. The fairy, therefore, has to transfer electrons from the positive charged end $B$, to which the electrons cling, to releasing the charged end $A$, from which they repel. Power plant forces, of course, are not capable of this. So the fairy who does this acts on the electrons not with an electrical power force, but with some other - external force. The role of a third-party force in an electrical circuit becomes clear: to transfer electrical charges against power plant forces, to separate negative charges from positive ones and “attach” them to the discharge ones. External forces of their work close the circuit and ensure the constancy of the point. There are two sections in the electrical circuit: in one, through the efforts of the fairies, the charges of different signs are separated, and in the other (in the outer part of the circuit) they are connected again. Fey does work by separating charges and creating an electric field. And this latter connects the charges, and inside the metal this work is converted to heating the metal. The activity of fairies fills the activity of Sisyphus, who, according to ancient myth, places a stone on the top of a mountain, which, when reaching the top, rolls down. Just as Sisyphus, lifting a stone, did work against the forces of gravity only so that the stone, having acquired potential energy, could roll down the mountain under the influence of gravity, our fairy, separating different charges, does work against the forces of the power field only in order to acquire potential energy, these charges could, under the influence of power forces, unite again. However, the work of fen cannot be called Sisyphus in the sense that is commonly said. After all, Sisyphus is called “meaningless”, useless work. And fen’s work leads to tangible results - direct current flows through the circuit.

Let's return again to our electrical circuit. Of course, no non-power plant fairies actually exist. But there are special devices in which non-electrical (third-party) forces act, capable of separating negatively charged electrons from positive charges. These devices are called current sources, although in reality they are not immi, as we have just seen. They differ from current sources from other characteristics of the forces in them and the origins of the processes and designs of the devices themselves. They differ in quantity by a value called electromotive force.

What is electromotive force?

Let us consider the non-octoral section of the electrical circuit between points $a$ and $b$ (Fig. 2), in which an electric current exists. Since the current exists, it means there is an electric field. Let there be a voltage $U$ in this area, or, what is the same, a potential difference $\varphi_1 - \varphi_2$. Then the work done when moving the neoctor charge $q$ from $a$ to $b$ is equal to $A = qU$.

Now let’s imagine that in section $ab$ there is also a non-actor current source in which the sides of the force (Fig. 3) act. Now we need to add the work of external forces to the work of the power plant field $qU$.
