⟦folio 37⟧

Legitimate question

In planimetry, the following two interaction theorems are proven:

Theorem 1. Equal angles always lie opposite equal nodes of a triangle.

Theorem 2. Equal angles always lie opposite equal sides of a triangle.

Meanwhile, in practical applications of geometry, it makes no sense to talk about strict (exact) equality of sides or angles - there you have to deal with approximate equal geometric quantities. This circumstance prompts a natural question whether the above theorems remain valid after the following modification:

Theorem 1'. Opposite equal nodes of a triangle there are always almost equal sides.

Theorem 2." Opposite the equal sides of a triangle there are always almost equal angles.

However, the “almost” that appears in these two formulations of the words is in our time alien to the language of mathematics *). So, before trying to answer the question posed, one should give the wave a precise meaning to the theorems formulated, freeing them from the vague concept of “almost”. (It is possible that this can be done in different ways, for example, using the concept of a limit or some other way.)

We invite our readers to do this.

Yu. Gaiduk
(Kharkov)

*) It is now used only as a component of weak mathematical terms, for example, “almost everywhere”, “almost pernod”, but in each case in a strictly defined sense.
