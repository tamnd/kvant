XII All-Union Olympiad
SCHOOLCHILDREN

N. Rozov, M. Smolyansky

Olympics
in mathematics

From April 13 to 18, the final round of the XII All-Union Schoolchildren Olympiad in Mathematics took place in Tashkent. 153 schoolchildren took part in it: winners of the Resource Olympiads and children who received diplomas of I and II degrees at the final type of the XI All-Union Olympiad. The final section was also hosted by a team of vocational school students (3 people) - winners of the Olympiad of vocational schools in Leningrad - and a team of the hosts of the Olympiad - schoolchildren from Tashkent (3 people).

In total, 38 eighth-graders, 63 ninth-graders, and 52 tenth-graders improved in the final type.

The grand opening of the final.type took place on April 13 in the assembly hall of the Presidium of the Academy of Sciences of the Uzbek SSR. Chairman of the organizing committee, Deputy Minister of Education of the Uzbek SSR. V.V. Barylnikov congratulated the participants of the final type Olympiad on the beginning of improvement. The chairman of the jury of the Olympiad, academician A. N. Kolmogorov, and the deputy chairman of the jury in

Vice President of the Academy of Sciences of the UzSSR, Chairman of the Supreme Council of the UzSSR Academician S. Kh. Sirazhdinov, Secretary of the Central Committee of the Komsomol of Uzbekistan Sh. N. Makhmudova and other comrades.

Participants of the Olympiad laid flowers at the monument to V.I. Lenin and the grave of the Unknown Soldier. Young mathematicians took part in a traditional communist cleanup day.

The final stage of the Olympiad was held in two types, which took place on April 14 and 15.

8th grade

5. The machine prints pairs of natural numbers on cards. The machines work as follows. The first machine, having read the card $(a; b)$, issues a new card $(a + 1; b + 1)$; the second, having read card $(a; b)$, gives out card $(\frac{a}{2}; \frac{b}{2})$ (it only works when $a$ and $b$ are even); the third machine, using two cards $(a; b)$ and $(b; c)$, issues card $(a; c)$.

Let there initially be one card with a pair of numbers (5; 19). Is it possible to use the machines in any order to get card a) (1; 50)? 6) (1; 100)?

b) Let there initially be one card $(a; b)$, $a < b$, and we want to get card (1; n). At what $n$ can this be done?

6. A $n$-gon of area $S$ is inscribed in a circle of radius $R$. A point is placed on each side of the $n$-gon. Prove that the perimeter of a $n$-gon with vertices at offset points is at least 2$S/R$.

7. The piece stands in the corner of the chessboard with dimensions of $n \times n$ cells. Each of the two players takes turns moving to an adjacent field (having a common structure with the one on which the chip stands). A second time on a field where a chip has already hit is not allowed. The one who has nowhere to go loses.

a) Prove that if $n$ is even, then the one who starts the game can achieve a win, and if $n$ is odd, then the second one wins.

6) Who will win if initially the chip is not on the corner field, but on the one adjacent to it?

8. On a given plane there are disjoint segments, no two of which lie on the same straight line. We want to check several more segments connecting the ends of these segments, so that all the segments together form one non-self-intersecting broken line. Can this always be done?
