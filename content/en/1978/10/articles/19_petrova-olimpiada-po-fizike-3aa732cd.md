The XII All-Union Olympiad for schoolchildren, held this year, was dedicated to the 60th anniversary of the Komsomol.

The final stage of the Physics Olympiad took place in the capital of the Turkmen SSR, Ashgabat. Its participants were 138 students: 39 eighth-graders, 50 ninth-graders and 49 tenth-graders. All of them are winners of district, city, regional and republican Olympiads in 1978 or winners of the previous All-Union Olympiad.

The opening of the final stage of the Olympids took place on April 13 at the city Palace of Pinoye. The Minister of Public Education of the Turkmen SSR B. Palvanova addressed the participants with a dark greeting. O. The eighth classics were given 4 hours to solve problems, and the ninth and tenth classics were given 5 hours.

Immediately after the Olympiad participants completed their work, members of the jury began checking them. The jury consists of scientists from the USSR Academy of Sciences, the USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences, the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences, teachers from the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology, Moscow, Novosibirsk and Turkmen State Universities.

Even before checking the work, the jury members expressed their opinions regarding the comparative difficulty of theoretical problems. The results of the audit showed that the learned jury coincided with the opinion of the participants themselves. Problem 1 turned out to be more difficult for the classicists; Only 8 participants were able to solve it. Problem 4 turned out to be the easiest. For ninth-graders, task 2 was the most difficult; 22 participants solved it correctly. The number of correct solutions was higher for task 4. For the 10th graders, task 4 was the most difficult; Only 10 participants managed it. Problem 3 turned out to be the easiest.

Problems of the theoretical type are included in the Problem Book «Kvant» (Nos. 7, 8 for this year), so that anyone can test themselves and test their strength with the Olympians. Only problem 4 for grade 8 was not included in the Problem Book «Kvant». Here's her solution.

The water will not flow out until the pan learns to float.

Two forces act on the pan: the force of gravity $Mg$, directed vertically downwards, and the force $\vec{F}$ of pressure on the pan from the side of the water, directed vertically upwards. The pressure force in absolute value is equal to

$$\left| \vec{F} \right| = p \left( S_1 - S_2 \right) = \rho \left| \vec{g} \right| h \left( S_1 - S_2 \right),$$

where $p$ is the water pressure at the bottom level of the pan, $h$ is the height of the water level in the tube.

The value $h$ at which the pan begins to float is determined by the condition

$$\rho \left| \vec{g} \right| h \left( S_1 - S_2 \right) = M \left| \vec{g} \right|,$$

where

$$h = \frac{M}{\rho \left( S_1 - S_2 \right)}.$$

Obviously, the problem will have a solution if $h \leq H$.
