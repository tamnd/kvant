Xia on $l$? It turns out that such a $q$ exists if and only if 10 and $l$ are coprime*). (Try to prove this statement yourself; “in one direction” it is very easy to prove, “in the other” - not so much.)

It is easy to generalize our divisibility test to arbitrary number systems.

Let
$$n = \overline{a_1 a_2 \dots a_t}^{(r)},$$
that is
$$n = a_t + ra_{t-1} + r^2 a_{t-2} + \dots + r^{t-1} a_1,$$
and let $q$ be such that $rq - 1$ is divisible by $l$. Let's construct from $n$ the number $n^* = \overline{a_t a_{t-1} \dots a_1}^{(q)}$. Then, for $n$ to be divisible by $l$, it is necessary and sufficient for $l$ to be divisible by $n^*$.

This generalized statement is proven in exactly the same way as the previous one.

Answer to the question: when does the required $q$ exist? — similar: $q$ exists if and only if $r$ and $l$ are coprime.

*) That is, when their greatest common divisor is 1.

A problem that arose from the whim of three girls

The girls were walking with their dads. All six approached a small river and wanted to cross from one bank to the other. They had at their disposal only one boat without a rower, lifting only $\delta$ people. The crossing would, of course, not be difficult to accomplish if the girls had not declared, either out of caprice or playfulness, that none of them agreed to be in a boat, or to be on the shore with one or two other people’s dads without their dad. The girls were small, but not very small, so each of them could drive the boat independently.

Thus, unexpectedly, additional conditions for the crossing arose, but for fun, the travelers decided to try to fulfill them. How did they act?

The entire team safely crossed to the opposite bank of the river and sat down separately. The question arose: is it possible under the same conditions to organize the crossing of four couples? It soon became clear that if the conditions for the girls were preserved, four girls with their dads could be crossed if there was an island in the middle of the river where they could make an intermediate stop and speak out. In this case, at least ten crossings are required for the final crossing.

Find a way to get there.

B. Kordemsky
