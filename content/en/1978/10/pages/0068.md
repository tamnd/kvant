5. The machine prints pairs of natural numbers on cards. The machines work as follows. The first machine, having read the card $(a; b)$, issues a new card $(a + 1; b + 1)$; the second, having read card $(a; b)$, gives out card $(\frac{a}{2}; \frac{b}{2})$ (it only works when $a$ and $b$ are even); the third machine, using two cards $(a; b)$ and $(b; c)$, issues card $(a; c)$.

Let there initially be one card with a pair of numbers (5; 19). Is it possible to use the machines in any order to get card a) (1; 50)? 6) (1; 100)?

b) Let there initially be one card $(a; b)$, $a < b$, and we want to get card (1; n). At what $n$ can this be done?

6. A $n$-gon of area $S$ is inscribed in a circle of radius $R$. A point is placed on each side of the $n$-gon. Prove that the perimeter of a $n$-gon with vertices at offset points is at least 2$S/R$.

7. The piece stands in the corner of the chessboard with dimensions of $n \times n$ cells. Each of the two players takes turns moving to an adjacent field (having a common structure with the one on which the chip stands). A second time on a field where a chip has already hit is not allowed. The one who has nowhere to go loses.

a) Prove that if $n$ is even, then the one who starts the game can achieve a win, and if $n$ is odd, then the second one wins.

6) Who will win if initially the chip is not on the corner field, but on the one adjacent to it?

8. On a given plane there are disjoint segments, no two of which lie on the same straight line. We want to check several more segments connecting the ends of these segments, so that all the segments together form one non-self-intersecting broken line. Can this always be done?
