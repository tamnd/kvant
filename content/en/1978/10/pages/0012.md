Element $CD$ moves with a constant modulus speed $V$ along a circle of radius $R$. Consequently, it has a centripetal acceleration equal to $V^2/R$ and directed to the point $O$ - the center of curvature of the arc $CD^*$).

Let's see what forces impart this acceleration to the element $CD$. From neighboring sections of the tape, it is subject to tension forces $N$, directed tangentially to the arc $CD$ at points $C$ and $D$ (see Fig. 2). Normal pressure forces act on the tape from the walls of the tube. Let a unit length of the tape be acted on by a force of normal pressure from the side of the tube, equal in modulus to $|\vec{f}|$. Then the force acting on the element $CD$ is equal to $|\vec{f}| l$ (since $l$ is small and $l \ll R$, we can assume that this force is exactly $|\vec{f}| l$, that is, the same as if the element $CD$ was rectilinear). According to Newton's P law, the equal force $F$ of all these forces, directed towards the center of curvature $O$, informs the element $CD$

$$2mR\varphi \frac{V^2}{R} = 2 |\vec{N}| \varphi - 2 |\vec{f}| R\varphi.$$

From here we find $|\vec{f}|$:

$$|\vec{f}| = \frac{1}{R} \left( |\vec{N}| - mV^2 \right).$$
