V. Orlov

Experimental problems of the Olympiad in Physics

On April 16, in the laboratories of the Faculty of Physics of the Turkmen State University, an experimental round was held, the final stage of the XII All-Union Olympiad for Schoolchildren in Physics. Each participant was asked to solve two experimental problems. Solutions and analyzes of the implementation of these tasks by Olympiad students are given below.

8th grade

Task 1. Determination of the liquid density ratio.

Equipment: lever-ruler, two weights, two vessels with various liquids, a rod with a coupling and a foot. (An aluminium cube weighing 100 g and a steel weight weighing 100 g were used as weights; colored water and a coli solution were used as liquid.)

Solution. The method for comparing liquid densities is based on the dependence of the absolute values ​​$F_{A1}$ and $F_{A2}$, Archimedean forces on the densities $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ of the liquid in which a load of volume $V$ is immersed:

$$F_{A1} = \rho_1 V g, \quad F_{A2} = \rho_2 V g.$$

It follows that

$$\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2} = \frac{F_{A1}}{F_{A2}}.$$

By balancing the weights on the lever, we establish that the masses of the weights are the same.

Having lowered the alimine cube into the first liquid (Fig. 1, a), we balance the weights on the lever:

$$(mg - F_{A1}) l = mgl_1.$$ (1)

Having replaced the vessel with liquid without changing arm $l$, we again balance the loads (Fig. 1, b):

$$(mg - F_{A2}) l = mgl_2.$$ (2)

From conditions (1) and (2) we find

$$\frac{F_{A1}}{F_{A2}} = \frac{l - l_1}{l - l_2} \Rightarrow \frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2} = \frac{l - l_1}{l - l_2}.$$

The relative measurement error $\rho_1/\rho_2$ is equal to

$$\varepsilon_{\rho_1/\rho_2} = \varepsilon_{l - l_1} + \varepsilon_{l - l_2} = \frac{h_l + h_{l_1}}{l - l_1} \times 100\% + \frac{h_l + h_{l_2}}{l - l_2} \times 100\%,$$ (3)

where $h_l, h_{l_1}$ and $h_{l_2}$ are the absolute errors in measuring the length of the arms.

What does the value of $\varepsilon_{\rho_1/\rho_2}$ depend on? The values ​​of $h_l, h_{l_1}$ and $h_{l_2}$ are determined by the accuracy of the measuring tool (ruler). From expression (3) it is clear that the smaller the difference in lengths $l$ and $l_1, l$ and $l_2$, the greater the relative measurement error. It follows that in order to increase the accuracy of measurements, it is the aluminium cube that should be lowered into vessels with liquids, and not a steel weight, since due to the large volume of the cube, a large Archimedean force will act on it, which, in turn, will lead to a greater difference in the lengths $l, l_1$ and $l_2$. For the same purpose, the shoulder length $l$ should be chosen as long as possible. It did not make sense to produce several wholesale ones with different lever arms, lower both weights into the liquid alternately, and then reduce the result, since this would completely reduce the accuracy of the measurement. Some students, when transferring a load from one liquid to another, measure a shoulder $l$ force $mg + F_A$. This led to a more complex calculation formula and additional measurement errors, increasing the likelihood of calculation errors.
