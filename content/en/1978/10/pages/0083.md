⟦folio 81⟧

Solution. The “black box” contains a convex-convex mirror with radii of curvature $R_1 = R_2 = 21$ cm (Fig. 6).

You can easily verify that there is a mirror in the box by looking into holes 1, 2, 3, first with holes 4, 5, 6 open, and then with them closed, without detecting any changes.

The standard solution is to construct the path of incident and reflected rays from the mirror. To do this, using pins, the directions in which two other holes were visible through one hole were offset. The intersection of the corresponding directions (blue lines on figure 7) gives the position of the three mirror points on one side and the three mirror points on the other. The bissertrices of the angles between the incident and reflected rays give the position of the perpendiculars to the surface of the mirror, the intersection of the perpendiculars is the center of curvature. Measuring the distance from the center of curvature to the surface of the mirror gives the desired value for the radius of the mirror.

The described method of performing the work gives greater accuracy, the smaller the radius of curvature of the mirror surface. In this case, the radius of curvature was quite large, and therefore it was necessary to accurately construct the path of the rays.

Only 13 tenth-graders out of 49 successfully completed this work. The main mistake, which did not allow us to obtain the optical design of the “black box,” was a carelessly made drawing with the construction of the ray path.
