Task 2. Experimentally check the fulfillment of the law of conservation of momentum when the movement of a chain of balls collides with a stationary one installed on a horizontal section of the tray. Explain your results.

Equipment: a tent with a coupling and a foot, an arched tray, a set of two plastic and two steel balls with a given mass, leaves of white and carbon paper, a scale ruler.

Solution and e. The simplest technique for performing the experiment is as follows. Using sheets of white and carbon paper, the place where the ball falls when it rolls down a free tray is recorded and the flight range S of the ball is measured (Fig. 2, a). The initial speed of the ball at the start of free flight is equal to $v=S/t$, where $t$ is the time the ball falls.

Having placed the second ball on the horizontal section of the tray, we measure the flight ranges $S_1$ and $S_2$ of both balls after they collide (Fig. 2, b). The speeds of the balls after the collision are $v_1=S_1/t$ and $v_2=S_2/t$. Since the time of falling of the balls in all experiments is the same, to check the law of conservation of momentum it is enough to check the compliance of the measurements

$$m_1S = m_1S_1 + m_2S_2,$$

where $m_1$ and $m_2$ are the masses of the steel balls.

In order to draw a conclusion about the feasibility or impossibility of the law of conservation of momentum in this experiment, the following are the errors in distance measurements ($S, S_1, S_2$) with the relative deviation of the left and right sides of the expression (*).

Relative error of flight range measurement $\varepsilon_s = (h_s/S) 100\%$ ($h_s$ - absolute error). Therefore, to reduce the value of $\varepsilon_s$, it is necessary to select the experimental conditions so that the value of $S$ is as large as possible. For this purpose, the tray should be extended to the entire height of the frame, and the ball should be released from the top point of the tray.

For plastic balls removed from the tray, the deviation of the left and right expressions (*) turns out to be significantly greater than the measurement errors - the momentum of the balls increased after impact! The reason for the non-fulfillment of the law of conservation of momentum in this case is that the ball rotates when rolling off the tray. When plastic balls of equal mass collide, the moving ball stops at the moment of impact, completely transferring its momentum to the second ball. However, due to the energy of rotational motion accumulated during rolling, the first ball again begins to move along the horizontal section of the tray. The consequence of this right, part (*) turns out to be greater than would be required by the law of conservation of momentum.

For steel balls, the law of conservation of momentum is satisfied within the limits of measurement errors, since the main balls slide off the tray.
