F480. The ship is driven by a water jet, which throws a string of water from the hull at a speed of $u$. Every second a mass of water $m$ is released, which is taken from the river. At what speed of the ship $k$, $n$, $d$. maximum movement? The force of friction and the resistance of water make a claim.

The total work $A$ performed by the engine in a short time $\Delta t$ is equal to the kinetic energy that is imparted to the water ejected from the ship's engine. Since the mass of water released during this time is equal to $\mu \Delta t$, then

$$A = \frac{(\mu \Delta t) u^2}{2}.$$

The useful work done during this time is equal to the change in the kinetic energy of the ship. If the mass of the ship is $M$, then

$$A_{\text{пол}} = \frac{M (v + \Delta v)^2}{2} - \frac{M v^2}{2} = M v \Delta v,$$

where $v$ is the absolute value of the ship’s speed. Neglecting the small square $(\Delta v)^2$, we obtain $A_{\text{пол}} = M v \Delta v$. In order to find $M \Delta v$, you can use the law of conservation of momentum. Since the friction and resistance are small, the impulse of the water-ship system does not change. Hence,

$$M v = M (v + \Delta v) - \mu \Delta t (u - v).$$

From here

$$M \Delta v = \mu \Delta t (u - v).$$

The engine efficiency is equal to

$$\eta = \frac{A_{\text{пол}}}{A} = \frac{2 M v \Delta v}{(\mu \Delta t) u^2} = \frac{2 (u - v) v}{u^2}.$$

This is the expression for the maximum at $v = \frac{u}{2}$.

When a battery of batteries is shorted to resistance $R$, current flows through the circuit

$$I_1 = \frac{n \mathcal{E}}{(n - 1) r + 10 r + R},$$

and power is released at the resistance $R$ of the load

$$W_1 = I_1^2 R = \frac{n^2 \mathcal{E}^2}{[(n - 1) r + 10 r + R]^2} R.$$

After the damaged battery is short-circuited, current will flow through the circuit

$$I_2 = \frac{(n - 1) \mathcal{E}}{(n - 1) r + R},$$

and power will be released at the load

$$W_2 = I_2^2 R = \frac{(n - 1)^2 \mathcal{E}^2}{[(n - 1) r + R]^2} R.$$

Equating $W_1$ and $W_2$, we get

$$\frac{n^2}{[(n - 1) r + 10 r + R]^2} = \frac{(n - 1)^2}{[(n - 1) r + R]^2}.$$

From here

$$R = 9 r (n - 1).$$
