Gear geometry

And what kind of mechanisms do not have gears? And they are in the watches, and in the engine room of the corps, and in the presses...

How does drive gear A transmit motion to driven gear B? Let us turn to figure 1: the “lever A₁” rigidly connected to A is only the “lever B₁” rigidly connected to B and thereby turns B.

Are straight tooth profiles good? It's immediately obvious that it's not very good. First, even if drive gear A rotates evenly, the same cannot be said about driven gear B (why?), and if gear B, in turn, transmits motion to gears C, D, etc., then the unevenness of the transmitted movement can be heard. Secondly, the angle φ between the cylinder F, which drives gear A, and the direction of rotation of gear B is variable. Therefore, only the driven gear is variably stronger than F₁, and the teeth of both gears wear unevenly.

Let's turn to figure on the fourth cover page. It shows a diagram of the operation of an oil pump operating with the help of two somewhat unusual gears (each with only two teeth). The profile of the head of each tooth is very epicycliodic, the profile of the stem is hypocycliodic (these two curves were described, for example, in «Kvant», 1978, No. 4, p. 13.). figure 2 shows how such a profile can be drawn. A circle $\omega$ of radius $R/4$ rolls along a fixed circle (O, R) from the outside. Then point $M \in \omega$ describes the profile of the gear tooth head (part of the epicycliode). Circle $\omega'$, congruent to $\omega$, rolls along (O, R) inside. Point $M' \in \omega'$ describes the heads of two adjacent teeth (often hypocycliodes).

figure 3 shows the position of the two gears in which the tooth head of the lower gear touches the profile of the upper one in the middle of the space between its two teeth. If the top gear is rotated by θ clockwise, and the bottom gear is rotated counterclockwise by the same angle, then they will still have a common point K and, moreover, a common tangent at it. Try to show this and establish the following important property of such a pair of gears: the normal to the profiles of the teeth at the point of their contact passes unchanged through a fixed point P (independent of the position of the point of contact).

Having made a model of such a pair of gears, you will notice its interesting property. In order for the pair to work continuously, the gears must become driving alternately. This means that this pair of gears cannot act as a mechanism that transmits motion. It is not for nothing that they are used to pump oil, and two auxiliary gears of a different type are used to transmit movement from one axis to another (see figure on the cover). Of course, if you make gears with a large number of teeth in a cyclondal profile, you can achieve transmission of motion from the drive gear to the driven gear. But other shortcomings remain. Such gears require, in particular, very precise installation so that the epicyclondal profile is always in contact with the hypocyclondal one. In addition, cyclondal teeth are difficult to cut.
