Below are 7 more challenging problems solved using Catalan numbers. In the first five, I noted how the corresponding double numbers (without the last digit) give a solution to the problem.

1. Two people, $A$ and $B$, are running for office. Each of them receives $n$ votes. In how many ways can $2n$ votes be counted so that $A$ is never ahead of $B$? (1 — vote for $A$, 0 — vote for $B$).

2. Let's put penins below and let*) in a row. On the penins we place face up a deck of $n$ cards, ranked from the top up in order of seniority. At one time, the card can be moved either from penin to below, or from below to daim (other movements are not allowed). Using these two moves, we will move all the cards to give in $2n$ moves. How many different permutations can be obtained on the daim if the initial number of cards is $n$? (1 - moving from penin to below, 0 - moving from penin to daim).

3. The drunkard comes out of the bar door and takes the lead. He is equally likely to take a step forward or backward, and all his steps are equal in length. In how many ways can he return to the door in $2n$ steps? (1 - step forward, 0 - step back.)

This random increase problem can be given another form. The king starts from the 1st row of the chessboard, and, making a move forward or backward vertically, returns to the starting square after $2n$ moves. Let's draw a space-time diagram, plotting time along the horizontal axis. The zigzag line can be thought of as the profile of a mountain range with peaks a whole number of miles high, not exceeding $n$. The curves of the king's moves will depict all mountain ranges of this type.

4. An even number ($2n$) of soldiers, all of different heights, are lined up in two equal ranks - $A$ and $B$. In how many ways can this be done so that from left to right in each line the soldiers are arranged in order of increasing height, and any soldier in line $B$ is taller than the corresponding soldier in line $A$? (Zanuterum of soldiers numbers 1, 2, 3, ..., in ascending height, and zanuterum also from left to right digits in double numbers corresponding to rooted trees with $n$ edges. Then the numbers of ones give the position of the soldiers in the row $A$, and the numbers of zeros in the row $B$. This problem is easily modeled using a deck of cards.)

5. Tickets cost 50 cents and $2n$ customers are in line at the box office. Half of them have 1 dollar, the rest - 50 cents. The cashier starts selling tickets without any money. How many different orders are there in the queue such that the cashier can always give change? (1-50 cents, 0-1 dollar.)

6. Hexaflexagons are fascinating toys that, by folding straight or zigzag strips of paper into hexagons, change their faces when folded (they are described in Chapter 1 of my book*). The correct hexaflexagon determines the type when perebanini goes through various states. The total number of such states for all regular hexaflexagons with $n$ surfaces is the Catalan number. For example, a hexagoscaflexagon (6 surfaces) can be made in three ways. The total number of all states is the Catalan number 42.
