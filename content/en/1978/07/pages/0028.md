Non-recurrent formulas for the $n$th Catalan number have different forms depending on how the terms of the sequence are numbered. The formula takes on its simplest form if the sequence begins like this: 1, 2, 5, ... With such numbering, the $n$th number of Catalan will be

$$\frac{(2n)!}{n!(n+1)!}$$

If the sequence is

starts with 1, 1, 2, 5, ..., then it turns out that all odd Catalan numbers greater than one are in those and only those places whose numbers are a power of two. So, the fourth, eighth, sixteenth, etc. Catalan numbers are odd. This is just one of many extraordinary properties of the sequence.

A word of caution: when solving combinatorial problems, it is easy to confuse the Catalan numbers with the associated sequence 1, 2, 5, 15, 52, 203, 877... (In a note to his bibliography, which also includes a list of references to the above sequence, Gould notes that if the objects being counted are sufficiently complex, then at $n=4$ it is easy to skip the 15th object and assume that you are dealing with a Catalan sequence.) These numbers are called Bell numbers after Eric Bell, who published many articles on this topic. Bell numbers list class partitions of a set of $n$ elements. For example, the number of different rhymes for a stanza of $n$ lines is the Bell number. The four has 15 possible rhymes. If you look at the rhyming rules at the end (consisting of 14 lines).max with your hand, you get 190,899,322 different rhymes (14th Bell number).
