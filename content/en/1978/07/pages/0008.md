It is easy to see that these numbers oblige the killer to a geometric progression, in which each subsequent number is three times smaller than the previous one to the left of it.

Let's return from sports to optics. Take a piece of painted glass. Let's say that it transmits one third of the light incident on it. Let's add a second similar piece. It will transmit one-third of the luminous flux that passed through the first piece, that is, one-ninth of the luminous flux incident on the first piece. Putting another piece, we get one twenty-seventh part and so on.

It is clear that the same result would be obtained simply by doubling the thickness of a piece of glass, twice, etc. As the thickness of the glass increases, the proportion of transmitted light decreases exponentially.

This is the law discovered by Bouguer. In the example of runners, we have already seen how the numbers quickly decrease exponentially.
