Surprises
green glass

What color is green glass?

This question can cause a feeling of natural homelessness. The reader will say with irritation: green glass is called green because it... However, there is no need to rush into condescending explanations. A simple experience will show you that the question of the color of green glass is not at all so simple.

If you have a piece of green glass, carefully break it into several not very small pieces. Then, look through one of them at the filament of the incandescent lamp. As you would expect, the thread will appear green (Fig. 1). Place a second piece of glass on this piece and look at the thread again.

You probably won't notice a change in the color of the thread; it will still be green. But if you put a third one on two pieces of glass and look through all three pieces at the thread, you will see it already uncolored and whitish in color. Through four pieces the thread will appear reddish, and through the heels of the pieces it will appear ruby ​​red!

The result is completely unexpected and very impressive. It turns out that the color of glass depends on thickness, and glass that is green in a thin layer becomes red when the layer is thick enough. Of course, not every green glass has this property, but just the most common two types of green glass.
