M464. On a given plane there are 1000 squares with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. Let $M$ be the set of centers of these squares. Prove that it is possible to mark a part of the squares so that any point in the set $M$ falls into at least one and at most four marked squares.

Let's prove this lemma. We can assume that the common point is the origin $O$. Of the path of the centers of these squares, at least two centers must lie in one of the four planes defined by the coordinate axis (the four are joined by the rays that limit them). Let the centers $O_1$ and $O_2$ of the squares $S_1$ and $S_2$ lie in the first quarter, and $(x_1; y_1)$, $(x_2; y_2)$ be the coordinates of the points $O_1$ and $O_2$. Let's choose the most from the numbers $x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2$;
empty, we will direct, $x_1$. Let us show that then $O_2 \in S_1$. Indeed, it is clear that the point is $(x_1; x_2) \in S_1$. Since $O \in S_1$, it follows that every point $(x; y)$ such that $0 \leq x \leq x_1$, $0 \leq y \leq x_1$ belongs to $S_1$. In particular, $(x_2; y_2) \in S_1$, which is what was required.

Let us now describe the correct “marked” squares. Let $K_1$ be the most of the given 1000 squares (hereinafter, if the most is not several, we take one of them). Let $K_2$ be the largest of those squares of a given family that have centers outside $K_1$; $K_3$ is the most of those squares of a given family that have centers outside $K_1$ and $K_2$, etc. This process ends with the construction of a square $K_n$ such that there are no points of the set $M$ outside $K_1$, ..., $K_n$ (the total number of finite squares). The squares $K_1$, ..., $K_n$ marked in this way satisfy very many of the conditions of the problem: they cover the set $M$. To show that they satisfy the second condition, it is enough to verify that no five of them have a common point. Indeed, according to the lemma, if such a point was found, one of the marked squares would contain the center of the other. It remains to note that this is not the case for the squares we have chosen. Indeed, if $i < j$, then the center of $K_j$ is outside $K_i$ by construction; Moreover, $K_j$ is not greater than $K_i$, therefore, the center $K_i$ is also outside $K_j$.

F478. On a cube lying on a smooth horizontal surface, fly one of the same cubes, and the cubes are struck with their parallel edges. The attack speed of the estimated cube before the collision was directed at an angle of $\alpha$ to the side edges (Fig. 3). How will the cubes move after a collision if the coefficient of friction between the sides of the cups is $\mu$?

Fig. 3.

Let us denote $F$ the absolute value of the elastic forces with which the cubes act on each other when colliding. Then the absolute value of the friction force between the cups is $\mu F$ (Fig. 4).

During time $\Delta t$, the projection of the momentum of each cup onto the axis $Y$ changes in absolute value by $F\Delta t$. Therefore every cup of cups

$$|\Delta (mv_y)| = F\Delta t.$$

The absolute value of the change in the projection of the momentum of each cup onto the $X$ axis is equal to $\mu F\Delta t$:

$$|\Delta (mv_x)| = \mu F\Delta t.$$

From these relations it is clear that for each cube

$$|\Delta (mv_x)| = \mu |\Delta (mv_y)|.$$

This relationship is valid for any time intervals. Therefore, if we denote $v_x$ and $v_y$ the projections of the speed of the cube on the axes $X$ and $Y$ after the collision of the cups, $v_x$ and $v_y$ are the values ​​of its projections before the collision, then

$$\mu m |v'_x - v_y| = m |v'_x - v_x| \Rightarrow \mu' |v'_x - v_y| = |v'_x - v_x|.$$

Let us denote by $u$ and $w$ the speeds of the cups $I$ and $II$ before the collision ($w = 0$), $u'$ and $w'$ the speeds of the cups $I$ and $II$ after the collision.
