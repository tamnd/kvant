To give these values ​​an impression, let’s try to calculate the specific charge of a ball with a radius of 1 cm made of an aluminum alloy with a density of $\rho = 2 \cdot 10^3 \text{kr/m}^3$. Its mass

$$m = \rho V = 4/3 \pi r^3 \cdot \rho \approx \frac{4 \cdot 3,14}{3} (10^{-2})^3 \cdot 2 \times 10^3 \text{kr} \approx 8 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{kr}.$$

What charge can be held on such a ball? It is known that intensive flow of charge from the surface of the body begins when the electric field is directed $E_0 \approx 3 \cdot 10^6 \text{B/m}$. The field voltage of a sphere of radius $r$ with charge $q$ is equal to

$$E = \frac{1}{4 \pi \epsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2}.$$

The maximum charge that such a sphere can “hold” is determined by the condition $E = E_0$. Thus,

$$q_{\text{max}} = 4 \pi \epsilon_0 r^2 E_0 \approx 4 \cdot 3,14 \cdot 8,86 \times 10^{-12} (10^{-2})^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 10^6 \text{KJ} \approx 3 \cdot 10^{-8} \text{KJ}.$$

So, the maximum specific charge of an aluminum ball will be

$$\frac{q_{\text{max}}}{m} \approx \frac{3 \cdot 10^{-8} \text{KJ}}{8 \cdot 10^{-3} \text{kr}} \approx 4 \cdot 10^{-6} \text{KJ/kr}.$$

The specific charge of a proton exceeds the specific charge of an aluminum ball by $10^{13}$ times!

Let's imagine that we would like to obtain a current of 1A using charged aluminum balls. By definition $I = q/t$. The charge of one ball is denoted by $q_1$; then $q = Nq_1$, where $N$ is the number of balls. Current $I = \frac{Nq_1}{t}$, and $N = \frac{It}{q_1} \approx \frac{1A \cdot 1c}{3 \cdot 10^{-8} \text{KJ}} \approx 3 \cdot 10^7$.

The flow of the balls should be such that 30 ml passes through the fixed platform in one second. balls! If we imagine this flow in a pipe, then its cross-section should be

$$S = N \pi r^2 = 3,14 \cdot 30 \cdot 10^6 \times (10^{-2})^2 \text{m}^2 \approx 10^4 \text{m}^2!$$

At the same time, a current of one ampere passes through a wire with a diameter of a fraction of a millimeter, only heating it. This is explained precisely by the fact that microelectrics (electrons) have a large specific charge.

Let us return again to the question of the nature of the current. Current is created by the movement of charged particles. Not charges, but charged particles! And although we write $I = q/t$, by charge $q$ we mean the sum of the charges of particles having a certain mass. Therefore, electric current has mechanical properties. They were first discovered in the experiments of Tolman and Stewart. The solenide, to the ends of which a galvanometer was attached, was set into rapid rotation and then abruptly stopped. The galvanometer recorded the current, and the direction of this current corresponded to the movement of charged particles. In these experiments, the specific charge of particles, the movement of which determined the current, was changed. It turned out that the value found coincided with the value of the specific charge of the electron, found from the deviation in the electric and magnetic fields. Experiments by Tolman and Stewart revealed for the first time the existence of free electrons in metals. Being weakly connected to the crystal lattice, after the solenide stopped, the electrons continued to move by inertia - a short-term current arose.
