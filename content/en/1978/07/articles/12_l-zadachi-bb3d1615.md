5. It is interesting that the bending around the edge of a light wave can be observed without having anything at hand. Look out the window in the evening. You see bright points of glowing lanterns. Squint your eye (the eyelids form a gap) - the light from the lanterns will bend up and down, together with the luminous point you will get two vertical columns of rays (Fig. 3, a). Continuing to rustle, turn your head slightly to the side. The shelve formed over centuries will change its shape - the rays from the lanterns will now diverge at an angle (Fig. 3, b).

6. Pierce the answer in the newspaper with a pencil, cover it with a newspaper on a pole lamp and from a distance of several meters look at the beam of light from the answer through the nylon tape. The tape threads form slits in two mutually perpendicular directions, so the light is also bent in two directions.

7. In the case of randomly arranged small points, similar patterns can be observed.

On a frosty evening, scrape the ice off the window. Carefully apply to a clean area - it will be covered with small, randomly placed ice crystals. Now look through this answer to the street lights. Colored kolyzas will appear around them. Please note that different lamps produce different color rings.

If it's warm outside, this experiment can be performed with a small glass cooled in the refrigerator.

You can test a lot of similar experiments: with carbon paper, with a gramophone record, with negatives on which all sorts of homogeneous and inhomogeneous structures are fortographed.

Try to come up with a similar experience yourself, and you will get even more convenience.

Exercise

On page 29 there is the article “The Three Jugs Problem.” If you have read this article carefully, then try to solve the following problems.

1. There is a vessel with a capacity of 12 liters filled with liquid, and two empty vessels with a capacity of 9 liters and 5 liters.

How to divide liquid into two equal portions?

2. During the robbery, a bottle containing 24 units of balm was stolen from the shopkeeper. While fleeing, they met a glassware seller on the road, from whom they bought three vessels. Having reached a safe place, they wanted to divide it, but it turned out that the purchased vessels together were 13, 11 and 5 units, respectively.

How can they achieve equal division?

3. Let two points $P$ and $P'$ have trilinear coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and $(x', y', z')$ with respect to triangle ABC. Prove that if the coordinates satisfy the equation $xx' = yy' = zz'$, then
$$P'AC = BAP, P'BA = CBP, P'CB = ACP.$$
