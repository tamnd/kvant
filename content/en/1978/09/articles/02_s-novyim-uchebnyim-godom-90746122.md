Happy new school year!

The editors congratulate our young readers on the new 1978/79 academic year!

The past academic year was full of major events that served as a kind of scientific inspiration for the upcoming academic year.

This was the year when the Soviet people and all progressive humanity solemnly celebrated the sixtieth anniversary of the Great October Socialist Revolution, which opened a new era in the history of mankind.

Another important event of the past year is directly related to you, as well as to all citizens of our Motherland - the adoption of the new Constitution of the Soviet Union.

Last year was also the year of the XVIII Congress of the Lenin Komsomol, a youth organization of which the vast majority of «Kvant» readers are members. Our readers especially appreciate the bright speech of the General Secretary of the CPSU Central Committee, Chairman of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR L. I. Brezhnev at the XVIII Congress of the Komsomol. It outlined the tasks, the solution of which the Communist Party would entrust to the Komsomol, expressing confidence that they were within its capabilities.

And at the very end of the school year, the All-Union Congress of Scientists - mentors and friends teaching youth - took place in Moscow. At this congress, problems directly affecting all students were considered. The speeches of the congress participants were imbued with concern for the teaching youth, about how to better and more effectively prepare them for future independent life. Our youth have been given a lot. No state in the world provides boys and girls with such educational opportunities as are available in the Soviet Union.

From the very first days after the October victory, the Soviet state considered organizing public education one of the most important tasks. We must remember that at that time the vast majority of the population of our country was illiterate. Since then, we have come a long way from literacy clubs to universal secondary education. Here, for example, are several figures for only one union republic, taken from the anniversary statistical publication “National Economy of the Uzbek SCP for 60 Years of Soviet Power”: in 9498 secondary schools in the 1976/77 academic year, there were 3,789,900 students who taught 203,600 teachers; 254,300 students were educated in 42 universities.

In our country, all types of education, both in secondary school and in higher education, are free. And the Resolution of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR adopted last year will ensure the free use of textbooks. Most of the pioneer age takes advantage of fertility exercises for free in the summer. And children of preschool age are given the opportunity to receive almost free education in services and kindergartens. For all this, the Soviet state will waste enormous resources.

The Party and the government are constantly concerned about teaching young people and improving the quality of their education. This is evidenced by a number of documents adopted recently, which are of great importance for the development of public education in our country. The middle ones are the Resolutions of the Central Committee of the CPSU and the Council of Ministers of the USSR on the completion of the transition to universal secondary education, on rural schools, on improving the training and education of students in the vocational technical education system, on improving the training, education and preparation for work of students in secondary schools. The Supreme Council of the USSR approved the “Fundamentals of legislation of the Union of SCPs and allied resources on public education.”

All these are questions of one of the fundamental rights of the Soviet person, guaranteed by the Constitution - the right to education. But to whom much is given, much is accomplished. The schoolchild, every vocational school student, must adequately respond to the attention and care of the party and the Soviet government, and make full use of the opportunities presented by the people for learning and obtaining an education.

 The XVIII Komsomol Congress paid special attention to this task. As a result of the decision adopted by the congress, it is said: “The Leninist Komsomol is called upon to make a concrete contribution to the implementation of the decisions of the 25th Party Congress, the resolutions of the CPSU Central Committee and the Soviet

government on further improvement of training, education, preparation for life and work of young people...".

The 25th Congress aimed all workers of our country in the struggle to improve the efficiency and quality of work. This task is of direct relevance to students. After all, the quality of knowledge today means high labor efficiency tomorrow. That is why the 18th Congress of the Komsomol appealed to students to declare war on the “silver troikas”. It is necessary to captivate the children with learning, awaken in them a thirst for knowledge, “guess” and develop their talents. To do this, it is necessary to make maximum use of the opportunity for extracurricular activities, clubs, scientific societies of students, etc.

Our magazine «Kvant» is also intended to help solve this problem. Its main goal is to awaken students' interest in physics and mathematics - the sciences that underlie modern technology. We hope that the interesting materials published on the pages of the magazine, accessible to a wide range of readers, will help improve the level of teaching physics and mathematics in school. The magazine will continue to acquaint its readers with the achievements of physical and mathematical sciences and their application in technical extracurricular activities, clubs, scientific societies of students, etc.

Among the readers of «Kvant» there are many young men and women who are deeply interested in mathematics and physics, have excellent knowledge of these subjects, regularly study in clubs and successfully perform at Olympiads. We would like them to become the first assistants to teachers of physics and mathematics, so that they consider it their duty to study the difficult questions of commodityism, captivating the beauty and joy of scientific knowledge. Olt shows that when explaining a particular issue, you get to its best and deeper points. The help of commodityism in the school is thus mutual. “Not one lagging behind!” - this slogan of production workers should become the slogan of students.

School education in our country is associated with labor education. This policy, pursued by the party and the government, helps students choose their future labor profession. Explaining this policy, L. I. Brezhnev emphasized: “If, for example, earlier we talked about the right to work, now it is an additional right to choose a profession, type of employment and work in accordance with the recognition, abilities, professional training and education of a citizen, and also, which is no less important, taking into account social needs.”

The situation with youth employment in the capitalist world is completely different. Here is a typical story, which was reported by Komsomolskaya Pravda on May 30, 1978:

“...His aptitude for mathematics manifested itself in early childhood. At the age of four he could already count to a thousand. At the age of seven, he was exempted from mathematics lessons at school... At eight, he could give consultations to school graduates... At the age of twelve, he was allowed to take classes at the Faculty of Mathematics of Monchen University, and at thirteen, he made reports at the Pedagogical Institute of his hometown.

All this made us talk about Elmar Eder as a prodigy...

The child prodigy was sent to study at a special mathematics school in England, which he graduated with flying colors at the age of fifteen. Three years later, he received a diploma from the Faculty of Mathematics of the University of Monchen, and two years later he was awarded a doctorate.

The years of study were behind us, and the twenty-year-old mathematician was faced with the question of where to work. Universities, institutes and research centers in which the young mathematician approached him with requests for work gave one answer: there were no available places.

This phrase was repeatedly received in response in Germany by 40 thousand other graduates of higher educational institutions in the country...”

Unfortunately, we still have young men and women who do not want to learn about production and who do not respect working people. They try to distinguish the acquisition of labor skills before leaving school and cover up their lack of interest in work with various excuses. Such students should be reminded of the words of the first secretary of the Komsomol Central Committee, B. N. Pastukhov, who in his report to the XVIII Komsomol Congress noted: “Social useful work is an active comprehension of the world, knowledge of oneself. It does not preclude, but contains a conscious choice of profession, in-depth knowledge. Laureate of the Lenin Prize, academician Rem Viktorovich Khokhlov began working after school as a mechanic, then became a student at Moscow University, and years later - its rector. The outstanding Soviet physicist now always remembered the school of the working collective. Such universities are not forgotten.”

High school students, students of vocational schools, like all Soviet people, experienced legal justice for the outstanding successes that our Motherland has achieved under the leadership of our native Communist Party, clearly realize that the launching pad for all these successes is the socialist social system. We are convinced that the teaching youth of the Country of Soviets will do everything so that the school year - the year of the 60th anniversary of the Komsomol - will be marked by new successes in study and work.
