⟦folio 29⟧

Fig. 8.

transfer of the discriminant parabola.

From (9) it is clear that equations with roots of the form $n + bi$ (n is an integer) correspond on the plane $n$ to ray points $p = -2n$ with vertices on the discriminant parabola (Fig. 8).

Therefore, equations $x^2 + px + q = 0$ having a complex root $a + bi$, satisfying, for example, the conditions:
$$1 < a < 2, \quad 1 < b < 2,$$
on the plane $n$ there correspond points $(p; q)$, the coordinates of which satisfy the system of inequalities:
$$\begin{cases} q > \frac{1}{4} p^2, \\ -4 < p < -2, \\ \frac{1}{4} p^2 + 1 < q < \frac{1}{4} p^2 + 4. \end{cases}$$

Thus, in order to solve a specific equation $x^2 + px + q = 0$, which has $q > \frac{1}{4} p^2$, using the grid nomogram shown in figure 8, you need to look between which rays and between which parabolas on the plane $n$ the point $(p; q)$ lies.

For the interested reader, we propose to consider in the $n$ plane the complex roots for the equations $x^3 + px + q = 0$ and $x^4 - x^2 + px + q = 0$.
