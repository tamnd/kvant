(from letters to a friend)

Hooray! I came up with my own distance!
(first letter)

Mishka, hello! Last year, when I was in the sixth grade, our teacher Pyotr Ivanovich told us that distance can be called not only what is obtained when changing with a ruler, but in general everything that has properties 1-3 from paragraph 4 of “Geometry 6”.

I decided to come up with my own distance. I remembered the “teλεphone model”, with the help of which Pyotr Ivanovich proved to us that the third property of distance cannot be derived from the first two *). I remembered and thought: when I want to call someone, I pick up the phone,

I dialed the number and they were already talking. This means that the “telephone distance” from me to everyone I call is the same, say, 1 minute. The minute it takes to dial a phone number. But, of course, I don’t call myself: when I ask, I answer right away. Therefore, the distance from me to me is zero! Others, of course, in a similar case will serve in the same way as me. So it occurred to me: consider the distance between any two of my planes equal to zero if these planes coincide, and equal to one if they are different (Fig.1).

Is my “distance” a distance? Does it have the right properties?

The first property for my distance is, of course, satisfied: for

The second property, of course, is also satisfied: for distinct points $|AB|=1$ and $|BA|=1$, this means $|AB|=|BA|$; and for coinciding points $|AB|=0$ and $|BA|=0$ - again $|AB|=|BA|$.

I had to tinker a little with the third property - I had to consider several cases.

If all three points $A, B, C$ coincide, then $|AC|=|AB|=|BC|=0$. So, in this case, $|AC|=|AB|+|BC|$, which can also be written as $|AC| \leq |AB|+|BC|$.

Now the path of three points coincides with two. If $A=C$, then $|AC|=0$, and $|AB|=|BC|=1$. Therefore, $|AC| < |AB|+|BC|$, which again can be rewritten as $|AC| \leq |AB|+|BC|$. If $A=B$, then $|AB|=0$, and $|AC|=|BC|=1$. In this case $|AC|=|AB|+|BC|$. (I won’t rewrite equality and strict inequality every time in the form ≤, okay?) Finally, for $B=C$ we have $|BC|=0$, $|AB|=|AC|=1$ and again $|AC|=|AB|+|BC|$.

And when all three points are different, then $|AB|=|BA|=|AC|=1$ and $|AC| < |AB|+|BC|$.

Do you understand what is happening? My “phone distance” turns out to be the same server distance as the one they talk about in school! All (you see, all!) three properties of distance are fulfilled for it. So the distances can be different? The same as in the textbook, and the same as I came up with? Or maybe they could be something else?

In any case, my distance is still worth tinkering with.

Styopa.

(letter two)

Bear! Something is wrong with my segment geometry.

The textbook says that the segment $AB$ is a set consisting of distinct x points $A, B$ and the points lying between them. Okay, in what case is it considered that the point $X$ lies between the points $A$ and $B$? If the points $A, X, B$ and $|AX|+|XB|=|AB|$ are different. But with my distance for different points $A, X$ and $B$ we have $|AX|=|XB|=|AB|=1$. Then $|AX|+|XB| \neq |AB|$, that is, there is no point $X$ for which the equality $|AX|+|XB|=|AB|$ would hold. This means that in my geometry the points lying between points $A$ and $B$ are empty! But then a segment in my geometry is a set consisting of two points: $|AB|=\{A, B\}$.

Well, how do you like it? Probably, these are not yet surprises of my distance.

Styopa.

In “Geometry 6” (item 5) it is said that any point $O$ on a line splits the set of remaining points into two non-empty sets such that the point $O$ lies between any two points belonging to a different set (property 1a).

To understand this property, I made figure (Fig. 2). Here is the straight line $a$. Here is the dot $O$ on it - red. (letter in half)

Bear! Rummaging through old «Kvant», I recently realized that points and lines can be called any objects for which the properties specified in the axioms are satisfied *). Then I realized that distance is any number **) that has properties 1-3 indicated in the textbook.

And in different geometries, distance can be determined in different ways: in school geometry - in one way, in Lobachevsky’s geometry - in another way, and in mine - in a third way. It is only important that three of its properties are satisfied. That's what Pyotr Ivanovich said.

And yesterday I found such a place in Karl Levitin’s book “Geometrical Spread”. The great physicist Einstein explains to his nine-year-old son why he became famous in science: “When a blind beetle crawls along the surface of a ball, it does not replace the fact that the path it has taken is curved, but I was lucky enough to notice this.” Come on, you and I will notice something and come up with something.

R.S. It seems that with distance I am developing a new “craziness”. Wait, I'll write.

Your eternal friend Styopa Moshkin.

Follow up

I’m sure “Stepino’s Distance” seemed very unusual to you. But what to do? Since all three properties (axioms) of distances are satisfied for it, it should be called a distance.

Is it possible to add some kind of axiom and call only that which satisfies the extended list of axioms distance? For example, on a plane for any number there is a pair of points, the distance between which is equal to this number. maybe include this property as a fourth distance property? But then what is considered such on a sphere, globe or sheet of paper will cease to be a distance (explain why).

What is the benefit of fantasies similar to Stepina’s? It is impossible to answer this question in advance and for every case. But for his case, Stepa himself must: one of the properties of the location of a point on a straight line cannot be derived from the properties of the distance.

Another conclusion from Moshkin’s words: when we introduce a certain concept, we start from one or more models (for example, when introducing the concept of distance, we start from a model in which the distance is changed using a scale bar on a plan or sheet of paper). But after we have introduced this concept, we must be prepared for the fact that it may have completely unexpected, unusual models.
