The resulting polyhedron (composed by the lateral surfaces of all eight paramides) already “almost” has no self-intersections: its parts located above and below the plane $ABCD$ are contained (except for the contour $ABCD$) in only two “extra” points (points $a$ and $b$ on figure 8). figure 10 shows this contact at point $a$ (it looks like an external single-point contact of the edges of two two corners).

Let us now eliminate the self-touching of the polyhedron at the point $a$ by making a Conelli notch on the edge $BM'$. Similarly, calculate the points $b$ and make a notch on the edge $DM'$.

The Conelli polyhedron is ready: it is very long and allows continuous bending! Its general appearance is shown in figure 12; it can be made from drawing paper.

A critical reader may have doubts that with the help of the Conelli notch the real can be established in contact at points $a$ and $b$; this, however, will be very useful for those who make a model of the Conelli notch or carefully study figure 11. A meticulous reader will be able to give a strict proof of this fact.

In conclusion, we note that during the bending process the volume of the Conelli polyhedron does not change (it will be able to bend even if it is filled with an incompressible liquid!). Try to prove it yourself.
