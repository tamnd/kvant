This figure shows a family of second-order curves passing through the points (0,0), (1,2), (3,0) and (3,2). A second-order curve is the graph of the equation $ax^2+bxy+cy^2+ex+fy+q=0$ (where the numbers $a, b, c$ are not both equal to zero). Examples of second-order curves: ellipse, hyperbola, parabola, pair of straight lines. (You can read more about second-order curves in «Kvant», 1977, No. 8.) By Pascal’s theorem, in the general case, exactly one second-order curve passes through the path of points. Therefore, a whole family of second-order curves passes through the points, which is shown in figure. A special role here is played by the “red” parabola, the pair of parallel lines $y^2-2y=0$ and the pair of intersecting lines $(2x-3y)(x+y-3)=0$. These lines separate areas filled with different types of curves. For example, the white area is filled with ellipses, the green area is filled with one family of hyperbolas, and the yellow area is filled with another family of hyperbolas.
