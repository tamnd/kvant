Math on a chessboard

In 1975, the Nauka publishing house published a book dedicated simultaneously to chess and mathematics *). The history of the appearance of this book is interesting.

In 1971, in «Kvant» No. 9-12, “Chess and Mathematical Notes” by E. Gik were published. Since among young lovers of mathematics and physics it is difficult to find children who do not know how to play chess, such a publication seemed quite useful to us. Chess, as you know, in itself develops communication, logical thinking, and mental acuity. The more interesting it is to consider issues at the intersection of chess and mathematics. Mathematical and logical problems associated with chess are undeclared, original and generate many amateur and deep ideas. Numerous responses from readers forced us to continue publishing chess and mathematics articles, and E. Gik became, so to speak, our permanent chess and mathematics correspondent. The successful combination of interests (the author is a mathematician, candidate of technical sciences, master of sports in chess and journalist) allowed him to

To write a number of materials on this topic, including in «Kvant» No. 9, 1975, his interview with a copy of the chess world M. Tal “When the leg is the length of the hypotenuse” was published.

These articles formed the basis of this book. The first edition of the book, 10,0000 copies, sold out instantly, and the publishing house released an additional 10,0000 copies. This circumstance alone indicates the great success of the book.

The material in the book under review is very diverse, and it is hardly worth going into detail about its contents. Practical, it touches on all existing points of contact between mathematics and chess. Distances of various problems on the chessboard, routes, placements and strength of the pieces are given by chess-mathematical “records”, and the necessity of playing on the chessboard is described. Here you can find the problems “about the move of a knight” and “about eight queens”, which were studied by mathematicians L. Euler and K. Gauss. The reader learns from the book about the chess “achievements” of computers and the geometric properties of the chessboard, allowing for systems for conducting chess cores, etc. Some of the issues discussed are more mathematical in nature, others will be of interest to both amateur mathematicians and chess lovers. For example, a separate chapter of the book is devoted to the mathematical system of Elo coefficients, which are now widely used to evaluate and predict the results of chess players in tournaments *).

The book “Matematika on the Chessboard” is the result of a lot of research and research, including little knowledge. A number of problems are published for the first time. Many approaches and ideas considered by the author are new and original.
