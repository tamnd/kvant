Channeling parts in crystals

In the second issue of our magazine for this year, an article by A. Kitay-gorodsky “How the distances between atoms in crystals change” was published. In this article I talk about the main methods for studying crystal structures - RENT STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS.

Now we bring to your attention a story about another, very recently appeared, method of studying crystals.

A little history

Anyone who saw a model of a crystal made of balls and thin rods (see the intro to the article), of course, thought about its interesting property. If, slowly rotating, one examines such a model in the light, one can see that in some directions it is almost transparent, in others it is practically opaque. The model turns out to be transparent when holes (or, as they say, channels) open, formed by a number of atoms - balls, or slits between planes, forming balls; opaque - when all the usefulness is completely covered by the balls.

This property of the crystal model back in 1912 (at the dawn of the development of atomic and solid state physics) prompted the German scientist Stark to think about whether the described property of the model was related to real crystals. Stark concluded that he had. In particular, he predicted that if a crystal is clothed with positively charged particles, then the depth of their penetration into the depths of the crystal depends on the angle at which the beam of particles falls on the crystal.
