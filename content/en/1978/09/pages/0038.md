⟦folio 36⟧

Problem
quantum

M521—M525; F533—F537

M521. Let us denote by $a_n$ an integer close to $\sqrt{n}$. Find the amount
$$\frac{1}{a_1} + \frac{1}{a_2} + \frac{1}{a_3} + \dots + \frac{1}{a_{1980}}.$$ (8.9 grades)
G. Gurevich

M522. A plane is given several non-intersecting segments, no two of which lie on the same straight line. We want to check several more segments connecting the ends of these segments so that all the segments together form one non-self-intersecting broken line. Can this always be done? (8 grades)
V. Proizvolov

M523. The piece stands in the corner of the chessboard measuring $n \times n$ cells. Each of the two players takes turns moving it to an adjacent field (which has a common side with the one on which the chip stands). You cannot go to the field a second time where the chip has already been beaten. The one who has nowhere to go loses.
a) Prove that if $n$ is even, then the one who starts the game can achieve a win, and if $n$ is odd, then the second one wins.
b) Who wins if initially the chip is not on the corner square, but on the one adjacent to it? (8 grades)
N. Nechuvtaev

M524. Prove that for no natural number $m$ the number 1978$^m$ -1 is divisible by 1000$^m$ -1. (8 grades).
S. Konyagin

M525. Prove that for any tetrahedron there are two planes such that the ratio of the areas of the tetrahedron’s projections onto these planes is not less than $\sqrt{2}$. (10th grade).
A. Berzins
