5. Notch Conelli

Consider a dihedral angle $QlQ'$, consisting of a straight edge $l$ and half-edges $Q$ and $Q'$ (Fig. 5). We denote its bicsectorial plane by $P$. Let us construct the first Bricard octahedron so that the ring $AB$ lies on the line $l$, the quadrilateral $ACBD$ lies in the plane $P$, and the triangles $AMB, AM'B$ lie in the half-planes $Q$ and $Q'$. After this, we remove the section $AB$ from the edge $l$, and the triangles $AMB$ and $AM'B$ from the faces $Q$ and $Q'$. We will remove the same two triangles from the Bricard octahedron. The figure shown in figure 5 will remain. We will say that a Conelli notch is made on the edge of the dihedral angle.

The polyhedron with an edge, shown in figure 5, allows continuous bending: as $\alpha$ changes, the Bricard octahedron bends and, at the same time, the distance $MM'$ continuously changes, and also (within certain limits) the dihedral angle $QlQ'$ changes.
