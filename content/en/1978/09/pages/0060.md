-2
$$\frac{3\sqrt{1-x}}{2} + \frac{3\sqrt{1-x}}{2} \sqrt{1+x} + \frac{3\sqrt{1+x}}{2}$$

is continuous at the point $x = 0$. But at point $x = 0$ it takes on the value $-\frac{2}{3}$. This means that the limit of this function is $-\frac{2}{3}$. Thus,

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3\sqrt{1-x}-3\sqrt{1+x}}{x} = -\frac{2}{3}.$$

Example 7 (MINCH, 1977).

Embroider $$\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{5-x}-2}{\sqrt{2-x}-1}.$$

Solution. To calculate this limit, it is not enough to free yourself from irrationality only in the numerator or only in the denominator; here you need to multiply both the numerator and the denominator by “conjugate” expressions:

$$\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{5-x}-2}{\sqrt{2-x}-1} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(\sqrt{5-x}-2)}{(\sqrt{2-x}-1)} \times \frac{(\sqrt{5-x}+2)(\sqrt{2-x}+1)}{(\sqrt{2-x}+1)(\sqrt{5-x}+2)} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{((5-x)-4)(\sqrt{2-x}+1)}{((2-x)-1)(\sqrt{5-x}+2)} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(1-x)(\sqrt{2-x}+1)}{(1-x)(\sqrt{5-x}+2)} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{2-x}+1}{\sqrt{5-x}+2}.$$
