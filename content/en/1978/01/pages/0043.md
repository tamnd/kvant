⟦folio 41⟧

“Well, now we can draw a conclusion,” said the teacher. — What does it mean to “prove the third property of distances using the first two properties”?

“This means proving that if properties 1 and 2 are satisfied, then property 3 will also be satisfied,” Styopa answered immediately for everyone.

— And in considering the model, properties 1 and 2 are satisfied, but property 3 is not satisfied. This means that it is impossible to prove property 3 using properties 1 and 2! Property 3 is independent of properties 1 and 2.

4. New model

You are probably eager to find out whether Styopa and his friends have proven the second property of distances using the first one? And does such proof exist?

After receiving the “lesson,” Styopa Moshkin’s “mathematical society” began to act more cautiously. Its members realized that doing mathematics involves not only proving proofs, but also finding out: do they exist? First, the guys decided to try to build a model in which the first property of distances would be fulfilled, but the second would not. Luck would have revealed that there is no proof of the second property based on the first. And they were lucky. This is the “telephone” model they built: $|AB| = 2, |BA| = 1, |AC| = |CA| = |BC| = |CB| = 1$. Obviously, the first property of distances is satisfied for all points in such a model. However, for the point

$A$ and $B$ the second property is not satisfied: $|AB| \neq |BA|$. Consequently, the second property of distances is independent of the first property.
