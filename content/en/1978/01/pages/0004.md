Hans Christin Oersted]

When the forty-three-year-old Copenhagen professor Hans Christin Oersted sent out his “pamphlet” to his colleagues - four pages in Latin - and scientists from France, Switzerland, England and Russia were able to familiarize themselves with it, in addition to scientific problems, they also included universal human problems. How to identify the author of these pages, how to evaluate his work? Who did this man seem to them: a hardworking scientist, a dreamer, a romantic, a lucky man? And anyway, who was he? Physicist, chemist, pharmacist, philosopher or posture?

To answer these difficult questions, which are certainly interesting for us, let’s go back two centuries and imagine the distant Danish island of Langgeland and a town on it called Rüdkobing, and the family of a poor pharmacist into which Hans Christin was born.
