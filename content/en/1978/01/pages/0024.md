Evariste Galois (1811-1832), an outstanding French mathematician, recently entered the history of science *). He did not have time to write many works. In the Russian edition, his writings and rough sketches occupy only 120 pages in a small book. However, he rightfully considers one of the creators of modern algebra, a number of understandable ones bearing his name: Galois theory, Galois group, Galois field, Galois extension...

E. Galois. was interested in the following question: what necessary and sufficiently conditional conditions must the coefficients of an algebraic equation of the nth degree satisfy in order for it to be solved in radials? Before the fatal death that cut short his life, E. Galois, within one night, outlined in a letter to a friend his results, which gave the answer

"I don't have time"
A three-part film about the great French mathematician is coming to the silver screens in Italy. The film calls it: “I don’t have time.” Its creator, screenwriter Ansano Gianarelli (the script was written by him together with the famous publicist and playwright Edoardo Sanguineti), reveals the short and vivid dramatic life of Galois against the backdrop of the most important events of the Elohi: the last years of Napoleon’s life, the Bourbon restoration, the revolution of 1830, the appearance of the proletariat on the historical arena.

Critics especially note the performance of the main role by the young actor Marro Garriba. A great contribution to the creation of the television film was made by a scientific consultant, a well-known mathematician in the country, a teacher and editor of the magazine Riforma della Scuola, and publicist Lucno Lombard Radice. He performs here in a completely new capacity for him... as an actor. Radice first played the role of a mathematics professor - the only one who was able to appreciate the genius of Galois, who worked in an atmosphere of complete misunderstanding and suspicion on the part of representatives of official, academic science.
