M436. Given 20 numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{10}, b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{10}$. Prove that a set of 10 numbers (optional different) $a_1 + b_1, a_1 + b_2, \dots, a_{10} + b_{10}$ can be divided into 10 subsets, 10 numbers in each, so that the sum of the numbers in each subset is the same digit). It is easy to see that in each column (in each row) there are representatives of all subsets, so that the indices $i$ and $j$ of the numbers $a_i + b_j$ included in each of the subsets take all values ​​from 1 to 10 (exactly once). Therefore, the sum of the numbers in each of the subsets is the same: $a_1 + a_2 + \dots + a_{10} + b_1 + b_2 + \dots + b_{10}$.

Let $P_m(x)$ be a polynomial in $x$ of degree $m$. Then an arbitrary expression $x^k(x+1)^p P_m(x)$ has the form
$$x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} P_{m+1}(x),$$ where $P_{m+1}(x)$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree $m+1$ ($k$ and $p$ are any real numbers). Really,

$$(x^k(x+1)^p P_m(x))' = kx^{k-1}(x+1)^p P_m(x) +$$
$$+ px^k(x+1)^{p-1} P_m(x) + x^k(x+1)^p P'_m(x) =$$
$$= x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} [k(x+1)P_m(x) + pxP_m(x) +$$
$$+ x(x+1)P'_m(x)] = x^{k-1}(x+1)^{p-1} P_{m+1}(x),$$

where $P_{m+1}(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $m+1$, written in square brackets (recall that the degree of a polynomial $P'_m(x)$ is equal to $m-1$).

The action of friction forces leads to the fact that a certain distribution of velocities of different parts of the flow is established in the river bed. Thin layers of liquid adjacent to the banks (and the bottom of the river) are motionless, and as you approach the middle of the river (and the surface of the water), the speed of the water continuously increases (Fig. 8).

If a log is placed in a river at an angle to the current, then the part of the log that is close to the shore will move at a lower speed than the part of the log that is close to the middle of the river. This causes the log to begin to rotate. And it will turn until the log close to the middle of the river is in front, and the log itself is oriented along the current.
