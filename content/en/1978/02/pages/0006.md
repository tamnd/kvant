The phenomenon in question is far from simple. But we don't need to go into details. Therefore, all problems will be addressed on the subject. The first simplification - we will limit ourselves to studying the projection of the crystal structure, the second - we will consider a rectangular coal lattice, the third - we will assume that there is one diatomic molecule per cell.

Let us first deal with the description of the “let” of the lattice from which the atoms are “removed”. One node left. Father and son Bragg showed that the diffraction of RENTHEN RAYS can be considered as a kind of selective (that is, occurring only at certain discrete angle values) reflection of rays from systems of nodal planes into which the grating can be divided.

It is clear that the spatial lattice of a crystal can be divided into families of nodal planes in a variety of ways. In the picture of the projection of a lattice “freed” from atoms, it is easy to show traces of nodal planes perpendicular to the drawing plane (Fig. 4). We have depicted only five family planes. However, what is effective for the selective reflection that exists as will be described below are the integral details.

Which system of planes is characterized by the indices $h$ and $k$. Their meaning is illustrated by the example of a family with indices $h=10$ and $k=3$ (see Fig. 4). In order not to clutter the drawing, we constructed six planes close to the node $O$ and drew another one, designated $L$. The plane $L$ is close to the node $O$, cutting off an integer number of periods $a$ and $b$ along both lattice axes. These integers are equal to 3 on one axis and 10 on the other. Passing through cell $O'$, plane $L$ cuts off $1/10$ fraction of period $a$ and $1/3$ of period $b$. The meaning of the indices $h$ and $k$ becomes very interesting. We leave it to you to compose a phrase of the type that mathematicians love: “The indices $h$ and $k$ are called...”.

The plane system is also characterized by the mechanism of distance $d$. Plane $L$ is close to node $O'$, therefore $|O'B| = d$. The segments $|O'A|$ and $|O'C|$ can be written as $b/k$ and $a/h$. Since

$$\sin^2 O'AB + \cos^2 BO'C = 1,$$

we get

$$\frac{1}{d^2} = \frac{h^2}{a^2} + \frac{k^2}{b^2}.$$
