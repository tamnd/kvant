Tasks
XL Moscow Mathematical Olympiad for SCHOOLCHILDREN

From February 27 to March 27, 1977, the traditional Moscow Mathematical Olympiad was held. The first round was held only for tenth-graders; the rest of the classes (7-9-e) immediately participated in the second, final round, along with tenth-graders who successfully passed the first round. Below we publish the tasks proposed for the Olympiad.

First round (tenth grade)

1. The sequence of natural numbers $\{x_n\}$ is constructed according to the following rule: $x_1=2, x_{n+1}=[1,5 x_n]$ ([ ] is the integer part of the number).
a) Prove that there are infinitely many odd numbers in the sequence $\{x_n\}$.
b) Prove that there are infinitely many even numbers in the sequence $\{x_n\}$.
(S. Konyaets)

2. There are $n$ cardboard squares and $n$ plastic squares on the table. No two cardboard squares have common points, including border points. The same applies to plastic squares. It turned out that the set of vertices of cardboard squares coincides with the set of vertices of plastic squares. Does every cardboard square necessarily match some plastic one?
(B. Prasolov)

3. a) Twelve thin solid rods of length 1 were fastened together to form the frame of a cube. Is it possible to make a hole in the plane with an area smaller than 1/100, without breaking the plane into pieces, so that this frame could be pulled through it?
b) The same question for the frame of a tetrahedron with an edge of length 1.
(B. Prasolov)

4. Each point on the number axis whose coordinate is an integer is shown in either red or blue. Prove that there is a color with the following property: for every natural number $k$ there are infinitely many points of this color whose coordinates are divisible by $k$. (S. Konyaets)

Final round

5. (7-8 cells) At each vertex of a convex $k$-gon there is a hunter armed with a laser gun. All hunters simultaneously shot at the hare, sitting at its point $O$ inside this $k$-gon. At the moment of the shot, the hare disappears, and all the hunters die. Prove that there is no other point other than $O$ that has the specified property.
(S. Eliseev)

6. (7-8 grades). Cube $3\times 3\times 3$ is made up of 14 white and 13 black cups on side 1. A column is three cubes standing side by side along the same direction: width, length or height. Could it be that there is an odd number of white cups in each column a)? 6) odd number of black cups?
(B. Avilov)

7. (7-8 grades). Prove that it is possible to find any number of triples of natural numbers $a, b, c$ for which the equality $a^{15} + b^{15} = c^{16}$ holds.
(D. Bernishtein)

8. (7th grade) There are 1977 nails sticking out in the dock. Two people play and take turns. The move consists of the player connecting two nails with a wire. If the move results in a closed target, then the one who made this move is considered to have won. Who wins in the ruling game: first or second? (It is not allowed to connect two nails of already connected nails with a wire.)
(N. Aeakhanov)

9. (7-8 cells) Find the smallest $n$ such that any convex 100-gon can be obtained as the intersection of $n$ triangles. Prove that for smaller $n$ this can be done not with any convex 100-gon.
(C. Lando)

10. (9 cells) There are $n$ segments in space, no three of which are parallel to the same plane. For any two segments, the straight line connecting their midpoints is perpendicular to the segments. At what maximum $n$ is this possible?
(G. Galperin)

11. (9-10 grades) See problem M479.*
12. (8-10 grades) See problem M478.

13. (9 cells) There is a convex polygon in space, all of whose vertices are at integer points. There are no other whole points inside, on the faces or on the edges. (An integer is a point whose three coordinates are all integers.) Prove that the number of vertices of a polygon does not exceed eight.
(S. Mironov)

14. (9-10 grades) See problem M477.

15. (10 cells) Is it possible to arrange infinitely many circles on a plane so that any straight line intersects no more than two circles?
(N. Konstantinov)

16. (10th grade) The sequence of natural numbers $\{x_n\}$ is constructed according to the following rule: $x_1=2, \dots, x_n=[1,5 x_n-1]$ ([.] is the integer part of the number). Prove that the sequence $y_n=(-1)^{x_n}$ is non-periodic.
(S. Konyaets)

G. Galperin
