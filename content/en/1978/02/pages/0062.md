The problem can be solved in another way - using vectors. In the plane $ABC$ through the point $C$ we draw a line $m$ perpendicular to $CD$. Let us introduce a rectangular coordinate system: for the origin of the coordinates of the straight line the point $C$, for the abscissa axis of the straight line the point $CD$, for the ordinate axis - the straight line $m$, for the applicate axis - the straight line $CS$ (see Fig. 3; the positive directions of the axes are indicated on it).

Let $P$ and $Q$ be points, respectively, on lines $SE$ and $CD$ such that $PQ \perp SE$ and $PQ \perp CD$. Then the required distance is $|PQ|$. Let us denote by $\alpha$ and $\beta$ the numbers that determine the position of the point $P$ and $Q$: $\overrightarrow{SP} = \alpha \overrightarrow{SE}$, $\overrightarrow{CQ} = \beta \overrightarrow{CD}$.

$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{PS} + \overrightarrow{SC} + \overrightarrow{CQ}.$$

Let's find the coordinates of the vectors $\overrightarrow{PS}$, $\overrightarrow{SC}$ and $\overrightarrow{CQ}$ in the selected coordinate system. $\overrightarrow{PS} = -\overrightarrow{SP} = (-\alpha) \overrightarrow{SE}$. Since $|CE| = |EB|$ and $|BD| = |DA|$, $\overrightarrow{SE} = \left( \frac{|CD|}{2}; \frac{|AB|}{4} \right)$; $-|SC|$. $|CD| = \sqrt{|AC|^2 - |AD|^2} = 2\sqrt{6}$. So, $\overrightarrow{SE} = (\sqrt{6}; \sqrt{2}; -2)$.

Therefore, $\overrightarrow{PS} = (-\sqrt{6}\alpha; -\sqrt{2}\alpha; 2\alpha)$. Obviously $\overrightarrow{SC} = (0; 0; -2)$. Since $\overrightarrow{CD} = (2\sqrt{6}; 0; 0)$, $\overrightarrow{CQ} = (2\sqrt{6}\beta; 0; 0)$.

So,

$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = (-\sqrt{6}\alpha + 2\sqrt{6}\beta; -\sqrt{2}\alpha; 2\alpha - 2).$$

We will find the unknowns $\alpha$ and $\beta$ from the conditions $\overrightarrow{PQ} \cdot \overrightarrow{SE} = 0$ and $\overrightarrow{PQ} \cdot \overrightarrow{CD} = 0$.

$$\begin{cases} (-\sqrt{6}\alpha + 2\sqrt{6}\beta) \sqrt{6} + (-\sqrt{2}\alpha) \sqrt{2} + (2\alpha - 2)(-2) = 0 \\ (-\sqrt{6}\alpha + 2\sqrt{6}\beta) 2\sqrt{6} + (-\sqrt{2}\alpha) \cdot 0 + (2\alpha - 2) \cdot 0 = 0. \end{cases}$$

From the second equation of the system $-\sqrt{6}\alpha + 2\sqrt{6}\beta = 0$ (this could have been predicted: since $\overrightarrow{PQ} \perp \overrightarrow{CD}$, the first coordinate of the vector $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is 0). Then from the first equation $\alpha = \frac{2}{3}$. So, $\beta = \frac{1}{3}$. So,

$$\overrightarrow{PQ} = \left( 0; -\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}; -\frac{2}{3} \right).$$

So, $|PQ| = \sqrt{0^2 + \left(-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\right)^2 + \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)^2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$.

From $\overrightarrow{SE} = (\sqrt{6}; \sqrt{2}; -2)$ we get $|\overrightarrow{SE}| = 2\sqrt{3}$.
