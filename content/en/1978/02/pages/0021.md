Test 1. If the frame is installed at an angle of 45° to the vertical, then the point $A$ (Fig. 2) is visible in the sight (along two hairs), coinciding with the 52nd division of the millimeter scale, that is, $a = 52$ mm. The height $h$ of the frame's rotation axis is also 52 mm. Consequently, experience confirms that in a homogeneous medium light propagates rectilinearly.

Test 2. Pour water into the civet to the level of the axis of the frame. Terrier, with the same position of the frame as in the first experiment, not the point $A$ will be visible through the sight, but the point $B$, located at a distance $b < a$ (see Fig. 2). This means that a ray of light having a direction $BO$ in water, crossing the water-air boundary, is refracted and travels in the air in a direction $OC$. In other words, the angle $\alpha$ that the incident ray makes from normal to the interface is not the same as the angle $\beta$ of the refracted ray.

Test 3. Set the aiming frame strictly vertical. At the same time, through the sight under water you can see the beginning of the scale - its zero division. This means that if the angle of incidence is $\alpha = 0$, then the angle of refraction is $\beta = 0$. Or, in other words, if a ray falls perpendicular to the boundary of two media, then it is not refracted. This is an important special case of the law of refraction, which should not be forgotten.

Test 4. Let's determine the refractive index of water relative to air. To do this, we install the frame at a certain angle, for example, at an angle of 30°. This will also be the angle of refraction $\beta$ for a ray of light passing from water to air. The 21st scale division will be visible through the scope, that is, $b = 21$ mm (see Fig. 2).

Let's find the sine of the angle of incidence $\alpha$:

$$\sin \alpha = \frac{b}{\sqrt{b^2 + h^2}} = 0,37.$$

Sine of the angle of refraction

$$\sin \beta = \sin 30^\circ = 0,5.$$

According to the law of light refraction

$$\frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \beta} = \frac{n_2}{n_1},$$

where $n_2/n_1$ is the refractive index of air (the second medium) relative to water (the first medium). Hence the refractive index of water relative to air

$$n = \frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{\sin \beta}{\sin \alpha} = 1,33.$$

Using the proposed device, it is possible to determine the limiting angle of total internal reflection for water. This time the aiming frame is set at an angle close to 90°. To prevent the scale from being visible at the bottom of the vessel, it is better to increase the dimensions of the device by 2-3 times.

You can also conduct experiments not with pure water, but with solutions of sugar or salt and study the dependence of the refractive index on the concentration of the solution.
