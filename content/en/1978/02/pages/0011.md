But the reader undoubtedly noticed that the calculation in question is not the one we need. The task is to find the atomic structure from experimental data, and not to calculate the diffraction pattern based on information about the structure. The first task is much more difficult than the reverse one. Integivity is proportional to the square of the amplitude of the resulting wave, which is the sum of trigonometric functions. It is not only technically difficult, but also simply impossible to determine the arguments of cosines, knowing only the square of their sums. Now, if experiment gave values ​​for scattering amplitudes, then the situation would be quite simple.

At first glance, the situation seems hopeless. For a long time, researchers used the so-called trial and error method. This means: we came up with a structure and saw whether it fits with experience. But you can’t go on like this.

Ways have been invented to get around this difficulty. Electronic computers played a decisive role in this. I would like to give the reader an idea of ​​how this difficulty can be overcome, but our conversation about RENT gene-structural analysis has dragged on. 《Little tricks》 that researchers resort to are, firstly, the structure of heavy atoms (then, to a first approximation, we can assume that the crystal consists of just these atoms) and, secondly, a very elegant theory that shows that there are connections between different amplitude structures.

Yes, it would be nice to tell the reader about this, but for «Kvant» the conversation would be too long. Therefore, there is nothing left to do but advise the interested reader to turn to specialized literature.

The success of a method is judged by its results. The fact that the structures of more than 15 thousand crystals have been determined so far, including several ten structures of proteins, the molecules of which consist of many thousands of atoms, speaks for itself. Determining the structure of complex molecules lays the foundation for biological chemistry and biological physics. These sciences are now in a rapid period of development. They are expected to discover the secrets of life, and moreover, of death. X-ray diffraction analysis, despite its respectable age of sixty-five years, remains at the forefront of science.
