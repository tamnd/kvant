To describe our set of points (denote it by $F$), we need several statements about rotations. We will formulate them as necessary in the form of lemmas, and the proofs of all lemmas are given at the end of the article. It will be very useful for the reader to prove these lemmas on their own.

1. First of all, we will show that through each point of our set there pass two straight lines belonging to it.

Let $m$ and $n$ be the data of the crossing line and the point $A$ is equidistant from them. Let us denote by $A_1$ and $A_2$ the orthogonal projections of this point onto the lines $m$ and $n$, respectively (Fig. 3). Let $l$ be a perpendicular to the plane $A_1AA_2$ passing through the point $A$. Let's rotate the space around the $l$ axis so that the point $A_1$ goes to the point $A_2$. The straight line $m$ will then transform into the straight line $m_1$, passing through the point $A_2$ and perpendicular to the straight line $AA_2$. Then you can make two rotations with the axis $AA_2$ such that straight line $m_1$ turns into straight line $n$: $R_{AA_2}$ and $R'_{AA_2}$. As a result of the composition of turns $R_l$ and $R_{AA_2}$ ($R_l$ and $R'_{AA_2}$), straight line $m$ will turn into straight line $n$. It turns out that the following lemma is true: the composition of two rotations about intersecting axes is a rotation about the axis passing through the point of their intersection. Therefore, there is a line $u$ passing through the point $A$ such that $R_{AA_2} \circ R_l = R_u$. Similarly, there is a line $v$ passing through $A$ such that $R'_{AA_2} \circ R_l = R_v$.
