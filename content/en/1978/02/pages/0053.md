⟦folio 51⟧

The XIX International Mathematical Olympiad, held in July 1977 in the capital of Yugoslavia, Belgrade, was a record for the number of participants - for participation in modern close teams from 21 countries: Australia, Algeria, England, Belgium, Bulgaria, Hungary, East Germany, Italy, Cuba, Mongolia, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, USSR, USA, Finland, France, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Sweden and Yugoslavia.

The Soviet Union team included: Amiran Ambroladze from Tbilisi, Andrey Auzin from Rigga, Yaroslav Vinnishin from the boarding school at KSU, Muscovite Viktor Galperin, Alexander Kodryan from the city of Rybnitsa in the Moldavian SSR, Grigory Rybnikov from Moscow, Dmitry Flaas from the boarding school at NSU and Andrey Churbanov from the boarding school at Moscow State University. The team was accompanied by V. Orlov (USSR Academy of Pedagogical Sciences) and the author of these lines (team leader).

All schoolchildren - participants in the modern day - were accommodated in the town of Pinoje on the outskirts of Belgrade, located in a shady park, which was especially pleasant: the weather was hot.

The grand opening of the Olympics took place in July 1977 in the town of Pinoyer. Both rounds of the Olympiad were held there on June 6 and 7; Each round offered three tasks.

On July 10, a symposum of Olympiad participants was held. The Soviet team made presentations:
Ambroladze Amiran - “On the representation of natural numbers as the difference of two primes”,
Auzin Andrey - “Optimization of the work of two porters”,
Galperin Victor - “Commuting polynomials”,
Flaas Dmitry - “On a one-room problem in group theory.”

On the same day, students, who received special recognition for their original solution to the problem, told the participants of the Olympiad their solutions. The Soviet team was represented by Viktor Galperin, who received a special award for solving the third problem. The jury coordinators also noted the original solutions to the 6th problem by Grigory Rybnikov and Andrey Churbanov and the 2nd problem by Viktor Galperin. The solutions to the problems given by our participants will be published in a special collection published in Serbo-Croatian and English.

On July 11, all participants of the Olympiad took an excursion along the Danube to one of the largest hydroelectric power stations in Europe, built jointly by Yugoslavia and Romania. It is interesting to note that the hydrofoils on which the excursion was made, and all 12 hydraulic turbines installed at the hydroelectric power station, are branded “Made in the USSR”.

On July 2, a symposium of scientific leaders of the team called “Youth and Mathematical Olympiads” took place, at which the Soviet team A. Savin spoke.
