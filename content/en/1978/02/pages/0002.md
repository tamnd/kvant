“... I have the pleasure to inform you of some striking results, many obtained... The main of these results... is the creation of a device that in its actions... is similar to a Leyden jar... but which, however, operates continuously, that is, its charge is restored by itself after each discharge; in a word, this device creates an incalculable charge...” This is what Alexandro Volta wrote to the chairman of the Royal Society (British Academy of Sciences) about the device he invented, which he himself called an “electromotive apparatus.” Afterwards the French began to call it the “galvanic column” or “voltaic column”.

In Milan (Italy) in the Museum of the History of Science and Technology, Voltaic pillars are stored, made by Alexandro Volta himself. (You can see them in the photo.)

In the history of the invention of this device, Luigi Galvani and his significant “galvan experiments” played a major role. You can read about this story in the article “The Frog Dispute” in this issue of the magazine.
