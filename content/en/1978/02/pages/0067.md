Who's changing?

In this game, two players: a “riddler” and a “guesser” exchange questions and answers. True, they do it silently. The game starts like this. The riddler (he is not in the picture - he has moved away) selects four chips from six colors and puts them into a niche so that the riddler does not see them. The guesser then begins to ask questions, placing a set of four tiles in the direction of the riddler's hidden tiles. The riddler gives answers as follows: if, having accurately directed his chips, there is a chip of the same color, he places a black “bulk” on the side field; if, having directed his chips, there was a chip of a different color, but among the other three chips placed by the guess, there is a chip of the same color - a white “bulk”. (We recommend that the riddler first report in his head how many bald and black “bullets” he should put, and then immediately place them next to the riddler’s “question” in any order.) The riddler’s goal is to guess the riddled set using the possibility of fewer questions.

Since guessing is more interesting than answering, the games on the second half of the board switch roles: on it the guesser makes a guess, the riddler guesses. In a “two-sided” game, the winner is the one who guessed the ground chips in fewer questions.

The board can be easily made from cardboard or polystyrene foam, and colored chips can be made from matches (painted in six different colors). Games can be played without a board - with sets of four numbers selected from the set {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} (see p. 35).
