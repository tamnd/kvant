Composition of two axial symmetries

The reason for the student’s note is problem 56 from among the tasks for repetition in the course of grades VI-VIII of the textbook “Geometry 8”. Prove that the composition of two symmetries whose axes intersect at an angle α is a rotation, where the center of rotation is the point of intersection of the axes, and the angle of rotation is 2α.

What do we mean by angle α here? If α is the value of the angle between the axes of symmetry, then α takes a value from 0° to 90°. At the same time, the angle of rotation is characterized by a directional value and 2 α can take a value from -180° to 180°*). Perhaps we were “lucky” and the angle α between the symmetry axes corresponds to a rotation angle of 2α in the range from 0° to 180°? Not really. We will clarify this later, but for now we will solve the problem.

The intersection of straight lines l and m defines two directed angles - the angle from straight line l to m, equal to α, and the angle from straight line m to l, equal to (-α).

With this approach to the magnitude of the angle between straight lines, the composition of two symmetries and rotation around the point of intersection of their axes are removed. Our problem can now be formulated more precisely as follows:

Let the angle from straight line l to m be equal to α; model composition of symmetries S_m° S_l is a rotation around the intersection point of these O axes by an angle of 2α.

Let us illustrate the same approach by proving the well-known theorem about the inscribed angle.

Theorem. The magnitude of the inscribed angle is equal to half the angular magnitude of the duen *) on which it rests.

Proof. Angle ABC is inscribed in a circle with center O. Let us draw lines l and m through O, perpendicular to its sides. These lines will pass through the midpoints of chords AB and BC, respectively.

Let ABC = α, where 0°<α≤90°. In this case, the angle from straight line l to m can be equal to α (Fig. 3) or (–α) (Fig. 4). The composition of symmetries S_m° S_l will transfer point A to point C, since with S_l A goes into B, and with S_m-B into C. On the other hand, this composition can be replaced by a rotation of R_0^2α or R_0^{-2α} around the center O by an angle of 2α or (-2α), moreover, C = R_0^2α (A) or C = R_0^{-2α}(A). In both cases, the angular magnitude of the duna AnC, on which the inscribed angle ABC rests, is equal to 2α (since point A goes to C, passing through the duna AnC).
