What does it mean - “set the focus”?

Everyone had to sharpen the image, that is, add a sharp image. In many cases, the meaning of this action is simple and clear. For example, when photographing for sharpness, it means that the image created by the camera object must be placed precisely on the emulsion layer of the film. In the cinema, the public's demand for sharper images on the screen comes down to the requirement to combine the image of the film frame with the plane of the screen.

The meaning of focusing is somewhat obscured if a screen is not required during observation, for example, if the object is viewed with the naked eye. Let's do a little experiment. Look out the window. Distant objects are visible quite sharply. Then look at objects located at a distance of several meters. They are also clearly visible. Finally, as you read this page, you see a sharp image of the text. All this happens because the eye “automatically” focuses. This procedure is controlled by the accommodated muscle of the eye, which correspondingly deforms the lens of the eye. As a result, the image of the object falls exactly on the retina of the eye (so to speak, on the screen) - the image turns out sharp (Fig. 1).

$$2r = l_0 \text{tg} \alpha \approx l_0 \alpha \approx 75 \cdot 10^{-4} \text{ см} = 0,075 \text{ мм.}$$

Let points A and C (see Fig. 3) in the screen plane be represented by scattering circles of the same radius r. In this case, the geometric depth of field (the distance between the planes in which sharp images of points A and C are obtained) is equal to

$$l_1 + l_2 \approx \frac{2rL}{D} \approx \frac{Ll_0 \alpha}{D},$$

where L is the distance from the line to the screen, D is the diameter of the line. This clearly shows that the depth of field is really related to the width of the light beams.

Since different points are represented not by points but by scattering circles, it is important that these circles do not overlap each other. If two adjacent circles of scattering do not overlap, they are perceived by the eye with resolution.

In this case, the optical system is said to resolve these two points. If the circles overlap each other, the image results in one bright spot, that is, the points represented by the system are not resolved. As the size of the lens decreases, the size of the scattering circles decreases and, consequently, the resolution ability increases. If the diameter of the lens is large, then the resolution becomes low and instead of a clear image, a blurred spot is obtained. Thus, limiting the beam of light coming from an object is fundamental.
