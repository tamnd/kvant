Mysterious diffraction pattern

One evening I was riding on a bus. It was about twenty degrees below zero outside. While there were few passengers, the windows of the bus were clean. But then people filled the cabin, and after a while the window glass was covered with a thin layer of ice. The ice was so thin that the evening city was clearly visible through it.

The personal lanterns came out very nicely. Each lantern was edged with a colored star, such as that shown at figure. The delicate colors of its rays, from violet in the center to red at the ends, shimmered beautifully on the matte surface of the glass. Surely it was a diffraction pattern! How did it come about?

It is very difficult to explain the origin of this picture only by appearance, without additional research. Apparently, one explanation could be like this.

Ice crystals shining on the glass form a diffraction grating. This is what creates the diffraction pattern. The white image of the lantern in the center is the zero maximum, and along the rays of the star there are first-order maxima for all colors (first-order spectrum). The hexagonal shape of the picture is most likely associated with a similar shape of ice crystals (snowflakes deposited on glass).

Based on the observation results, one can even estimate the lattice period. Let us write down the condition for the main maxima for the lattice:

$$d \sin \varphi = k \lambda.$$

Here $d$ is the grating period, $\varphi$ is the angle that determines the direction to the maximum of the $k$ order, $\lambda$ is the wavelength. When considering a distance of about two meters, the radius of the star for red is approximately two centimeters. This means that the first maximum ($k=1$) for red light ($\lambda_{\text{кр}} = 7 \times 10^{-7} \text{ м}$) is visible at an angle φ also, which

$$\sin \varphi \approx \frac{2 \text{ cm}}{200 \text{ cm}} = 0.01.$$

Then

$$d = \frac{\lambda_{\text{кр}}}{\sin \varphi_{\text{кр}}} \approx 0.07 \text{ mm}.$$

How do you account for the appearance of this red picture?

V. Bulam
