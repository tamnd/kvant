In the economic life of a country, especially one as large as the USSR, complex processes take place. In order to better understand them and solve practical problems, attempts are made to find more or less adequate mathematical schemes for these processes. Such schemes are called mathematical models.

Let us give an example of a complex economic process and a corresponding model - the mechanical balance model (in the case of a simplified form). Let's consider a country whose economy produces a large range of different products: steel, gas, potatoes, cinemas, suits, Quark magazine, etc.

The economy in the country under consideration is planned. This means that you need to understand in advance what and how much should be produced, say, over the coming year. Production planning naturally begins with a certain number of products that should go to so-called “final consumption” during the year. Final consumption is considered to be those products (called the final product) that bring immediate usefulness to a page or group of citizens. For example, a certain amount of gas *), potatoes, suits, copies of Quark magazine, cinemas **) is used for final consumption each year.

Let us know how much of each product should go to final consumption (this is a separate difficult task, we will not discuss it here). Then you need to subtract how many products need to be produced in a given volume.

First, for simplicity, let’s consider a non-operating economy in which only gas is produced and consumed ***). Let's try to compose for her

As a result, the economic situation is naturally much more complicated. Obviously, in order to have a given final product, it is necessary to produce not only it, but also a number of other products. In order to produce gas, you only need to have gas; you need, for example, to have a drilling rig. In turn, to produce a drilling rig, machine tools, steel, etc. are required. Much is also needed to produce machine tools, steel, etc. Some of these products may be partially used for final consumption, and some not at all; for example, the drilling rig does not bring an immediate attempt at the product. To study the model of such a multi-product economy, the means of school mathematics are no longer sufficient, but such problems can still be solved (using linear algebra methods). The cumbersome calculations required are carried out on a computer.

The synthesis of economics and mathematics creates a new direction in science. The goal of this direction is to use mathematical methods and computer technology to better understand existing economic phenomena, learn to better see their principles, more accurately predict consequences, and improve methods of planning and managing the national economy.

Economics and mathematics have already achieved considerable success, but solutions to the most interesting, most difficult, and most important problems are still ahead. Participation in their solution is the task of students and graduates of our department.
