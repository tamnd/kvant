⟦folio 16⟧

M. Gravovsky

Wax ball floating

Make a ball out of wax and place it in water. Since the density of wax is less than the density of water, the ball will float on the surface of the water. Now squeeze out a small piece of metal inside the ball so that the average density of the resulting ball is slightly higher than the density of water. If the ball is suddenly lowered or thrown into the water, it will sink. If you place it very carefully on the surface of the water, it can stay there and not sink to the bottom.

This peculiar swimming can be explained as follows. Water does not wet the wax, so the free surface of the water becomes distorted (Fig. 1), and dreams appear that act on the ball from the side of the distorted surface of the water. These dreams are caused by surface tension and are directed in our case vertically upward (we can assume that water does not wet the wax at all). Together with the Archimedes force, they balance the force of gravity of the ball, preventing it from sinking to the bottom.

Several other explanations can be given. The curvature of the water surface causes an additional volume of water displaced by the ball - the Archimedes force returns and balances the force of gravity of the ball.

This experience allows for an interesting variation. Place a metal-weighted wax ball onto the bottom of a larger glass vessel filled with water. Then load a glass into the vessel, turn the house upside down, and cover the ball with the glass so that it floats on the surface of the water inside the glass (Fig. 2). Now slowly lift the glass - the floating ball rises with it. (This way you can lift the ball from the bottom of the container without touching the ball itself.) When the ball rises to the level of the water in the container, carefully remove the glass. To do this, slowly lift the edge of the glass on one side, being careful not to cause bubbles and not to stir the surface of the layer of water in the vessel.

In conclusion, a few instructions for conducting the experiment. The recommended diameter of the wax ball is 2-3 cm, and the diameter of the glass is approximately twice as large. To ensure stability of the ball's floating, it is better to place a piece of metal not in the center of the ball, but to move it to the side. Before the experiment, the ball must be dried.

Instead of a ball, you can use a piece of paraffin candle with a diameter of approximately 2 cm and a length of 4-5 cm. To make the candle heavier, you can stick several iron nails into it or attach a piece of copper wire. Then the experiment is carried out in the same way as with a ball.
