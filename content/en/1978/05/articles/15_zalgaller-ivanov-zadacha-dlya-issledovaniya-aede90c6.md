Finding a function
In some problems, it is asked to find not even a point on a graph, but a sum function. This is a typical task.
Example 7 (MEIS, 1977).
Find the equation of the parabola $y = x^2 + bx + c$ tangent to the line $y = x$ at the point $M$ (1; 1).
It is useful to reformulate the problem as follows: the equation of the tangent to the graph of the function $f(x) = x^2 + bx + c$ at the point $M$ (1; 1) has the form $y = x$; find $b$ and $c$. This formulation more clearly contains two conditions:
1) point $M$ (1; 1) lies on the graph of function $f(x) = x^2 + bx + c$;
2) $f'(1) = 1$ (since for the straight line $y = x$ there will be $k = 1$).
From here, taking into account the formula $f'(x) = 2x + b$, we obtain the system
$$\begin{cases} 1 + b + c = 1, \\ 2 + b = 1, \end{cases}$$
from which $b = -1$, $c = 1$. (Having solved a similar problem, it is useful to check that the equation of the tangent to the graph of the function $f(x) = x^2 - x + 1$ at the point $M$ (1; 1), lying on the graph of this function, has the form $y = x$)
