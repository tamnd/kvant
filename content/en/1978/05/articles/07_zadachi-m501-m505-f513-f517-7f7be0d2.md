We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. More difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. M501. Let us choose from the sequence of powers of three:
3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729, 2187, 6561, ...
All numbers starting with 9; let these numbers be (in order) $3^{f(1)}, 3^{f(2)}, 3^{f(3)}, ...$ (in particular, $f(1)=2$, since the first of such numbers is $3^2=9$).
a) Find $f(2)$ and $f(3)$ - the numbers of the second and third triple of the number.
b) Prove that there are infinitely many such numbers.
b) * Prove that $f(n)$ with $n>1$ satisfies the conditions: (1) $f(n) - n$ is odd,
(2) $\left| f(n) - \frac{n-1\lg 9}{1-\lg 9} \right| < 1$,
and is determined by this condition.
E. Turkevich

M502. Given are three parallel segments $AA_1, BB_1, CC_1$ that do not lie in the same plane. Let $M$ be the intersection point of planes $ABC_1, BCA_1$ and $CAB_1$, and $M_1$ be the intersection point of planes $A_1B_1C, B_1C_1A$ and $C_1A_1B$. Prove that the segment $MM_1$ is parallel to three vertices.

Z. Skopets

and find out for which sets it turns into equality.

M505*. a) Let $n$ materials of points of equal mass be placed on a straight line. Let us consider an arbitrary segment of length $2r$ containing at least one of these points, and find the center of gravity $O_1$ of all points falling within it. Let's consider a segment of length $2r$ with a middle $O_1$ and find the center of gravity $O_2$ of all points that fall into this segment. Then we find the center of gravity $O_3$ of all points falling in a segment of length $2r$ with a midpoint $O_2$, etc. Prove that, starting from a certain number, all points of the sequence $O_1, O_2, O_3, \dots$ coincide.

b) Let $n$ material points be placed on the plane. Consider an arbitrary circle of radius $r$ containing at least one of these points; let us denote by $O_1$ the center of gravity of the points falling within it and construct the sequence $O_1, O_2, O_3, \dots$, where $O_{n+1}$ is the center of gravity of the points falling within a circle of radius $r$ with center $O_n$. Is it true that, starting from a certain number, all points in this sequence coincide?

P. Bleser, G. Galperin, M. Kelbert

F513. On a clear sunny day, when the Sun is high above the horizon, get on a smooth screen, for example, on a white wall, the shadow of the flat edge of a piece of cardboard. If you now bring your finger to the sheet of cardboard as shown in figure 1, then the shadow of a second finger will stick out on the screen towards the shadow of the finger from the shadow cast by the sheet of cardboard. Carry out such an experiment and explain its result.

G. Solovyanok

F514. Rubber-cord fabric is made of strong elastic threads (cord) filled with soft elastic rubber. The cords form an angle $2\alpha$ with each other (Fig. 2). A circular cylinder is made from such fabric, to the ends of which metal covers are attached (Fig. 3). Air is pumped inside the cylinder. In experiments, it was noticed that when air is pumped, the cylinder takes one third of the shape shown in figure 4. The shape depends on the magnitude of the angle $2\alpha$ between the threads. What angle value does each of the shapes correspond to?
