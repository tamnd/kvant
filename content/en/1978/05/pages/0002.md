This figure depicts a torus—the surface that fills the bubble—and two circles. The surface is covered with a network of “parallels” and “meridians”. Both selected circles lie, as it may not seem to the countries at first glance, in the same secant plane (which coincides with the plane of the drawing).

These circles are interesting not only in how they are obtained: they are “lines of constant course” on the torus, that is, they intersect all its meridians at the same angle. Such lines are called rhoxodromes; You can read more about this mathematical-geographical term on p. 17. It is also proved there that when considering a plane section of a tora, the real result is two circles, and not just closed lines similar to them.

Take a look at the drawing - how many parts of our circles does the torus divide into?
