V. Vasilov, I. Melnikov

Tangent

“I don’t know who the world sees me as, but to myself I see myself as a boy who wanders on the seashore and from time to time finds a stone that is smoother, or a shell that is more beautiful than usual, while the vast true ocean lies in front of me, much unknown.”

Isaac Newton

In Geometry 7, a tangent to a circle is defined as a straight line that has only one common point with the curve. This definition is of a private nature and does not reveal the essence of the matter. If we tried to apply it to the parabola $y=x^2$, then at the origin both coordinate axes would fit this definition; Meanwhile, it is intuitively clear that in fact only the abscissa axis should be called tangent.

We now give the definition of a tangent to the graph of a function. the coordinates of the tangent point $M_0=(x_0; y_0)$ are considered given: $x=x_0$, $y=y_0=f(x_0)$. Let's consider, in addition to the point $M_0$, another point $M=(x; y)$ on our curve and draw a line $(M_0M)$ - a secant. When point $M$ moves along the curve, the secant will naturally change its position. If the point $M$ tends to the point $M_0$, it can be so that the secant will tend to take some position independent of how the point $M$ approaches the point $M_0$ (Fig. 1). This limiting position of the secant (if, of course, it exists) is the tangent. So, the tangent to the curve $y=f(x)$ at the point $M_0=(x_0; f(x_0))$ is called the limit position *) secant $(M_0M)$, when the point $M$ tends along the curve to $M_0$.

Let us emphasize again: our definition of a tangent contains the assumption that the limiting position of a secant exists; not every function $y=f(x)$ has a tangent at any point on its graph. figure 2, a, b show graphs of functions that do not have a tangent at the offset points, since if the limiting position of the secant already exists, it should not depend on whether the point $M$ is “on the left” or “right” tends to the point $M_0$. Note that our definition of a tangent does not require that the tangent and the curve have only one common point.

*) The limiting position of the secant (its equation will be $y-f(x_0)=\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}(x-x_0)$) should be understood as the straight line $y-f(x_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0)}{h}(x-x_0)$.
