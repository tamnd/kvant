⟦folio 27⟧

Kepler can no longer remain in Linz. He takes his family, loads books, manuscripts and simple household utensils onto the ship, and showers up the Danube, and goes to Ulm, where he agreed to publish the “Rudolfin Tables”. Severe frosts begin, the river is covered with ice. Kepler is forced to leave his family in Regensburg, and he himself gets to Ulm on horseback. Kepler lives in Ulm for about a year, of which nine months are spent printing the Tables.
Finally, the “Rudolfin Tables” saw the light of day. Kepler names the family in Regensburg and goes to Prague, where Emperor Ferdinand was at that time.
The Emperor meets Kepler, accepts a copy of the “Tables” from him as a gift and passes it on to the catalogue, promising all sorts of benefits for this. And again Kepler opens up - he cannot make a deal with his property.
The emperor's favorite, the commander Albrext Wallenstein, is involved in the fate of Keplar. He has known Keplar for a long time, having referred to him twice as an astrologist. Wallenstein tells Kepler to enter his service. Ucheny agrees, and in the summer of 1628 he and his family move to the Duchy of Sagan, the domain of Wallenstein.
In August 1630, Wallenstein received his resignation, and Kepler was again left without a patron and... without children. In the autumn of the same year, he went to Regensburg to collect German books, where the emperor was then located, in order to receive at least part of the money owed to him. Soon after his arrival in Regensburg, Kepler became seriously ill with fever. Here he died on November 15, 1630 at the age of 59.

Keplar's misadventures continued after his death. As a result, the cemetery where he was buried was completely destroyed, and not even a trace remained of Keplar’s ​​grave. His leadership legacy passed from hand to hand, was lost, wasted, until, finally, in 1774, most of the Keplar archive was acquired by the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences *).

The name of Keplar is associated with the basic laws of celestial mechanics. In his honor, one of the lunar craters and minor planet No. 1134 are named. At the beginning of the 19th century, the residents of Regensburg built a monument to Kepler, and on the 300th anniversary of his birth, a monumental monument was erected in Kepler’s homeland in Weil der Stadt. A museum named after him has been opened in these same cities.
