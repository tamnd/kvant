We have been running this section from issue to issue since the founding of the magazine. The problems published in it are not standard, but solving them does not require knowledge beyond the scope of the current school curriculum. The most difficult tasks are marked with an asterisk. After creating a task, we usually indicate who proposed this task to us. Of course, not all of these problems are published for the first time. Solutions to problems from this issue can be sent no later than April 1, 1978 to the address: 113035, Moscow, M-35, B. Ordynka, 21/16, editorial office of the magazine «Kvant», “Problem «Kvant». After the address on the envelope, write the numbers of the problems whose solutions you are sending, for example: “M486, M487” or “F498”. Solutions to problems in each of the subjects (mathematics and physics), as well as new problems, should be sent in separate envelopes. Please also send problems from different issues of the magazine in different envelopes. Include an envelope with your address written on it (in this envelope you will receive the result of checking the solution). Send the conditions of the original problems proposed for publication in two copies along with your solutions to these problems (on the envelope put: “Problem «Kvant», new problems in physics” or “...new problems in mathematics”). At the beginning of each letter, please indicate your actual school number and the class in which you are a teacher.

M506*. Prove that for positive $a, b, c$ and $d$ the following inequality holds:
$$a^4 + b^4 + c^4 + d^4 + 2 abcd \geq a^2 b^2 + a^2 c^2 + a^2 d^2 + b^2 c^2 + b^2 d^2 + c^2 d^2.$$
E. Turkewitz

M507. Published last year in the Russian translation of “Selected Problems” from the journal American Mathematical Monthly (M., “Mir”, 1977), two problems by the remarkable mathematician P. Erdos (Nos. 138 and 139) were given without solutions. The editors of «Kvant» received a number of letters containing solutions to these problems. Before introducing the solutions to the readers of the magazine, we suggest that you think about these difficult problems on your own; for this purpose we include them in Problem «Kvant».
Let $a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_n < 2n$ be a finite sequence of natural numbers ($n \geq 6$).
a) Prove that
$$\min_{i,j} |a_i, a_j| \leq 6 \left( \left[ \frac{n}{2} \right] + 1 \right),$$
where $|a_i, a_j|$ means the least common multiple of the numbers $a_i$ and $a_j$, and the minimum is taken over all parameters of the numbers $a_i, a_j$.
b) Prove that
$$\max_{i,j} (a_i, a_j) > \frac{38n}{147} - c,$$
where $c$ is independent of $n$ and $(a_i, a_j)$ denotes the greatest common divisor of $a_i$ and $a_j$.
The estimates in problems a) and b) cannot be improved (that is, the coefficient 6 can be replaced by a smaller one, and $\frac{38}{147} - \text{бóльшим})$.

M508. The circle is tangent to three positive ones with diameters $AB, BC$ and $AC$ ($C \in |AB|$, Fig. 1). Prove that the radius of the circle is half as large from its center to the straight line $AB$.

M509. Solve the following equations in natural numbers:
a) $2^x + 1 = 3^y$;
b) $z^x + 1 = (z + 1)^2$;
c) $z^x + 1 = (z + 1)^y$.

D. Fleishman

M510. In the book “Hungarian Mathematical Olympiads” the following problem is given (No. 148): “Prove that for all positive $\alpha < \pi$ the inequality holds
$$\sin \alpha + \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\alpha + \frac{1}{3} \sin 3\alpha > 0.$$
Prove the following generalization of this inequality: for all $0 < \alpha < \pi$ and natural $n$
$$\sin \alpha + \frac{1}{2} \sin 2\alpha + \frac{1}{3} \sin 3\alpha + \dots$$
$$\dots + \frac{1}{n} \sin n\alpha > 0.$$
I. Birder, R. Ushakov

F519. A weight $A$ is suspended from a mug $BC$ using a thread $AB$ (Fig. 3). What should be the amplitude of the oscillations of the load for these oscillations to be harmonic? Load mass $m = 0,1$ kg, spring stiffness $k = 1600$ N/m.

F520. In a flow calorimeter (Fig. 4), the gas to be tested is passed through a pipeline and heated using a spiral. The gas enters the calorimeter at a temperature of $t_1 = 20^\circ\text{C}$. With a heater power of $W_1 = 1$ kW and a gas flow rate of $m_1 = 540$ kg/h, the gas temperature behind the heater turned out to be the same as with a heater power of $W_2 = 2$ kW and a gas flow rate of $m_2 = 720$ kg/h. The gas pressure in the pipe is the same everywhere. Find the temperature of the gas $t_2$ if its specific heat at constant volume is $c_V = 21$ J/(mol K) and the molar mass of the gas is $M = 29$ kg/kmol.
