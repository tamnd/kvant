Symmetry in physics problems

With understandable symmetrical matter - in nature, technology, art, science. Let us recall the symmetry characteristic of the apple and the maple leaf, the symmetry of the forms of automation and the airplane, the symmetry of the classical construction of a poem or musical phrase, the symmetry of ornaments, the symmetry in the atomic structure of molecules and crystals. In particular, symmetry is quite common in physics problems.

What is symmetry? Many find it difficult to answer this question, although manifestations of symmetry in various specific cases are perceived by almost everyone. In everyday practice, understandable symmetry is usually compared with something balanced, harmonious, and having a good ratio of proportions. In the specific application of this concept, they speak, for example, of symmetry about an axis, symmetry when reflected in a mirror, symmetry of a square, regular hexagon, etc.

When solving problems, we often use symmetry without stating this fact, as something taken for granted. Thus, it seems very clear that the center of a gravity-homogeneous rectangular plate is located at the point of intersection of its diagonals, that the field outside a charged metal ball can be considered as the field of an exact charge located in the center of the ball, that mathematically the magnet makes oscillations symmetrical with respect to the direction of the gravity force.

In solutions to problems, you can find phrases like “from the reported symmetry it is clear that...” or “based on the symmetry one can conclude...”. Here are some specific examples.

Problem 1. Four identical powerful charges q are placed at the vertices of a square. What charge Q of the opposite sign must be placed in the center of the square so that the system of charges is in equilibrium?

Equilibrium means that the sum of the forces applied to each charge is zero. In this case, it is enough to consider the equilibrium of one of the five charges. He asks which one? FROM the reported symmetry it is clear that it makes no sense to consider the charge Q, since it will be in equilibrium regardless of its value.
