V. Litsevsky

Johannes Kepler

It is difficult to imagine a fate more difficult than that which befell Johann Keplar. Hunger and poverty, religious persecution and immigrant wanderings, illness and death of loved ones followed him all his life. Almost every day was filled with Japanese livelihoods, and it is unclear when he managed to read everything that had been written by scientists before him, receive outstanding achievements in criticism, optics, mathematics, astronomy and discover his famous laws of planetary motion! After Keplar's death, there remained one worn-out dress, two shirts, several copper coins, 12,694 guilders of unpaid salary, 57 calculation tables, 27 printed scientific papers and a huge leadership legacy. What kind of realness, dedication, hard work, and desire to know the laws of nature must have been possessed in order to complete such a colossal amount of work and make such a significant contribution to world science in those incredibly difficult conditions in which Kepler lived and worked!

Johannes Kepler was born on December 27, 1571 in the small German town of Weil der Stadt, which then had only a few hundred inhabitants. His father, Heinrich Kepler, was a man without specific occupations, a wanderer (according to Johannes Kepler), who completely disappeared from home in 1589.
