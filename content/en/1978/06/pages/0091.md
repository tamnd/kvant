The assumption $E(f) = \left[ \sqrt{2}; \sqrt{10} \right]$ naturally arises (see, however, the remark after the solution). Let's prove it. To prove the equality of two sets $A = B$, it is enough to prove two “inclusions”: $A \subset B$ and $B \subset A$. The inclusion of $E(f) \subset \left[ \sqrt{2}; \sqrt{10} \right]$ follows from (1). To prove the “inclusion” $\left[ \sqrt{2}; \sqrt{10} \right] \subset E(f)$, we show that for any $a \in \left[ \sqrt{2}; \sqrt{10} \right]$ the equation $\sqrt{x-1} + 2\sqrt{3-x} = a$ (2) has a solution. If we denote $\sqrt{x-1}$ by $u$ and $\sqrt{3-x}$ by $v$, then
$$\begin{cases} u^2 + v^2 = 2, \\ u + 2v = a. \end{cases} \tag{3}$$
The exact meaning of this phrase follows: if $x_0$ is a solution to equation (2) and $\sqrt{x_0-1} = u_0$, $\sqrt{3-x_0} = v_0$, then the pair $(u_0; v_0)$ is a solution to system (3); if, conversely, the pair $(u_0; v_0)$ is a solution to system (3) and $u_0 \geq 0$, $v_0 \geq 0$, then $x_0 = u_0^2 + 1$ is a solution to equation (2). Only the second of these statements is important to us now. Solving system (3) using the substitution method, we find that for $|a| \leq \sqrt{10}$ the pair
$$u_0 = \frac{a + 2\sqrt{10-a^2}}{5},$$
$$v_0 = \frac{2a - \sqrt{10-a^2}}{5}$$

is its solution (we do not need the second solution of the system here). For $\sqrt{2} \leq a \leq \sqrt{10}$ we have $u_0 \geq 0$, $v_0 \geq 0$. Consequently, for such $a$ equation (2) has a solution.

Comment. Generally speaking, from min $f(x) = c$ and max $f(x) = d$, where $|a; b| = [a; b]$ it does not follow that $E(f) = [c; d]$ (build an example!). For this conclusion to be true, it is sufficient that the function $f$ be continuous on the interval $[a; b]$. However, neither the concept “the function $f$ is continuous on the interval” nor the theorem “if $D(f) = [a; b]$, min $f(x) = c$, max $f(x) = d$ and the function $f$ is continuous on the interval $[a; b]$, then $E(f) = [c; d]$ is not in school textbooks.
