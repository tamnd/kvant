What happens in a helium-neon laser?

The helium-neon laser is becoming a propagation device, widely used in a wide variety of fields of technology and science. Such lasers have appeared in many schools, and with their help the properties of coherent light are studied.

The heart of each laser is its own so-called active medium, which absorbs the light beam passing through it. In a helium-neon laser, such a medium is plasma, which is formed when an electric current passes through a mixture of helium and neon in a gas-discharge tube.

Typically, when passing through a medium, a light beam experiences attenuation - part of the energy of the beam is absorbed by the medium. However, it is possible to create an environment in which, when light passes through someone, it will not be weakened, but will be compressed. The possibility of creating such an environment was first indicated in 1939. Of course, in the process of the success of light, the law of conservation of energy is not violated. The light beam draws additional energy from the internal energy of the atoms of the medium.
