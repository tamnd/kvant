Future creator of space technology

What boy doesn't dream of becoming an astronaut! Over the years, the passion for the romantic profession is complemented by an interest in the scientific and technical foundations of astronautics, in the designs of rockets and spaceships, and the best, surest way to gain access to this field is modeling. At one time, the automodel sport brought many famous aircraft and aircraft engine creators to research institutes and design bureaus. And now the design of model rockets and spaceships is the primary basis for training future engineers, future builders of such rocket planes that will fly to distant worlds. The book by utility engineer P. Elshtein, the Russian translation of which was published by the publishing house "Mir"*, is addressed to young designers of model rockets.

Of course, in order to build model rockets, you need to have some kind of scientific and practical basis. Therefore, the author prematurely teaches the reader the basics of the mechanics of structural motion and the laws of aerodynamics. The book describes simple experiments that clearly demonstrate some of the processes occurring during the movement of rockets and allow the research to be verified. These experiments do not require

some special equipment. A plastic shampoo bottle can float through the water like a torpedo; with the help of two vacuum cleaners it is not difficult to build a wind tunnel to test the model; In a plastic ball, you can create conditions for new ismobility.

Along the way, the book talks about the main structural elements of the rocket, the conditions for its stability, and provides examples of calculations and experimental assessment of the most important parameters of the rocket. Having mastered these basic principles, you can participate in the construction of design model rockets. At first, these are not even models, but mock-ups - cardboard and wooden, similar to a rocket except in their external outlines. Gradually it can become more perfect, large-scale, reproducing at least the external - all the elements of real rockets.

The next stage is the production of model parts, which, strictly speaking, cannot be called rockets, or they lack the main thing - a jet engine, and are launched by a rubber rolling rod like a slingshot. But it is on these models that you can practice the design elements - choosing the right size matches, installing stabilizers, mastering the processes of launch, flight and return.

Having gone through these preliminary stages, the modeler can consider himself adequately prepared to begin building and testing models equipped with engines.

The main element of the starter kit is an electric ignition system, which allows for remote starting of the model. The chapter “Rocket Electrical Engineering” provides diagrams of such systems.

Unlike real rockets, which, as a rule, are intended for uniform use (a space rocket, having completed its role, dissipates in the atmosphere, a military rocket explodes), it is advisable to use models repeatedly, and for this they must first be returned by a device. Models descend using parachutes, and their deployment is ensured by an automatic system. The author gives several examples of automatic systems with a description of the main components and parts.

The final chapter of the book describes the most convenient models of rockets and rocket planes, models created in different countries and distinguished by the originality of their design, the perfection of their equipment, and their flight qualities.

One important detail to note is that throughout the book the author emphasizes the need for strict adherence to safety precautions. “The issue of technical security cannot be mistrusted - it’s crazy. Elshtein. “This is not only a moral duty towards oneself and others. The culprit of accidents will be treated with reasonable responsibility. A number of articles of the Criminal Code provide for punishment both for unintentionally causing damage to trams and materials, and for committing frivolous actions that pose a threat to the health and life of people.”

Anyone who strives to work creatively in the fascinating field of rocket modeling needs solid knowledge of physics, mathematics, and technology; it is necessary to constantly monitor published materials introducing the achievements of “big rocket and space technology.” The author gives advice and recommendations on how to select these materials, how to accumulate and systematize knowledge. There is no doubt that P. Elshtain’s book will become a real book for future creators of space technology.

I. Zorits
