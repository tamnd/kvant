Construction of the tract

A long time ago, from the city of Moutonville, along the shore of a large lake, there was an old road. Near the tract itself, at equal distances from each other, were the villages of Albzhika, Bondi, Vilancourt, Garbusier and, finally, Diopre, beyond which the forest begins to pass through (see figure on the third page of the cover).

The route has fallen into disrepair. It had to be leveled and paved with rubble, of course, at the expense of the residents of these five villages. All five village chiefs gather in Moutonville to negotiate the purchase of stone and find workers.

“The construction of the road requires 600 Lundors,” summed up the headman of the village, Garbusier. “Immediately after the harvest, our village will contribute its 120 Lundors.

“If Garbusier pays one hundred and twenty, then we give sixty,” said the elder Bondi. “After all, the section of the road to our village is exactly half as long as to Garbusier.”

“In that case, we must introduce only 30 Lundors,” said the village chief Albzhika.

In the end, the elders agreed that the required 600 lundors would be paid, with the inhabitants of each village participating in the payment of that part of the tract that they use when they take up their goods at Moutonville (the distance from Moutonville to Albzhik is the same as between neighboring villages).

How much did the residents of each of the five villages have to pay?
