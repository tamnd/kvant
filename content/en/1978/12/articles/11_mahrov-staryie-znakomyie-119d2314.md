V. Makhrov

Old
familiar

Five well-known heroes gathered in a small square in the City of Toys: Winnie the Pooh, Piglet, old woman Shapoklyak, Crocodile Gena and the Hedgehog. Yes, yes, the same Hedgehog who lost a bundle with a jar of jam in the fog.

Yesterday they agreed to visit Cheburashka, who was vacationing with his grandmother in the village. There are two roads of equal length leading from Toy Town to the village: through city X and through city Y. The road through city X passes through the forest, and the Hedgehog suggested taking it. It was summer and no one objected. Stretched out in a chain, the friends hit the road. You probably already agreed that they took with them a pot of honey, a balloon, a bundle with a jar of jam, a slingshot and, of course, an accordion. True, it was a little different from the countries that each traveler was holding in his hands an object that was completely different from his own. But they were all great friends and did not pay attention to this.

Winnie the Pooh walked ahead, Piglet almost ran behind him, and behind him, not lagging behind, was the old woman Shapoklyak. Without losing sight of Gena the Crocodile, the Hedgehog was the last to run.

“By the way,” Piglet suddenly said, “there is a bridge on the other road (past town Y) across the river, which we will now have to ford.”

“Then why didn’t we go that way?” - Old woman Shapoklyak grumbled dissatisfiedly.

“Don’t quarrel,” said Gen the Crocodile in a deep voice. - Better help me solve a problem related to our journey.

It is known that the road from X to Y through Toy Town is shorter than through the village of Chebrashka. In addition, it is known that city Y is closer to a village than to Toy Town. You need to find out how many sections of different lengths there are between towns and villages, and arrange these sections in ascending order of their length.

“I know how to solve this problem,” said Winnie the Pooh, drawing the YY loop route in the sand (see figure). — Let’s denote the middle of the circular route YY with the letter C, the letter I with the Toy City, and the village with the letter D. It is obvious that city X is located in area SI.

“I don’t really care about this at all,” the statue Shapoklyak muttered again.

Ignoring her, Winnie the Pooh continued:

— The letters a, b, c and d denote the lengths of the sections, respectively, between X and D, X and I, Y and D, Y and I. According to the conditions of the problem, c < d. It is easy to show that the SI and DU sections are identical in length. From the fact that X lies on the SI region, it follows that b < c. We get the following system:

$$\begin{cases} a + b = c + d, \\ d + b < c + a, \\ c < d, \\ b < c. \end{cases}$$

“You don’t have to tell me any more,” interrupted Winnie the Pooh and Crocodile Gena. - I can already complete this task.

- Why don’t you solve the problem? - Piglet asked the Hedgehog.

- Because I came up with another problem; let's offer it to Cheburashka. Listen to her terms. We will tell Cheburashka who followed whom, but we will not tell us who carried out what things. However, we will tell him that Gena did not carry the accordion, and if I, the Hedgehog, run to a new place and stand between those of you who are carrying a pot of honey and a bundle of jam, then next to the one who carries the jam will be the one who carries the accordion, and the one who carries the balloon will be exactly in the middle. Cheburashka will have to figure out what object each of us was carrying.

You have read a comic story about Winnie the Pooh, Piglet and their friends. Can you explain the status of Shapoklyak, why city X lies on the SI site, and help Cheburashka solve the Hedgehog problem? Have you guessed how Crocodile Gena should “complete” his task, and what is the answer?
