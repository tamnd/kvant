XX International Mathematical Olympiad for Schoolchildren

The XX Olympiad took place in July 1978 in Bucharest. Teams from 17 countries took part in it. As usual, each country's team consisted of 8 schoolchildren. Participants were offered 6 tasks - 3 in each of the two days of the North. A certain number of points were awarded for solving each of the zhorny problems. The maximum possible sum of points for solving all problems for an individual participant was 40, and, therefore, for the team as a whole - 320 points. In the unofficial summer, the Romanian team was ahead (237 points). Next come the teams of the USA (225 points), Great Britain (201 points), Vietnam (200 points), Czechoslovakia (195 points). (The USSR team did not participate in this Olympiad.) Four problems of the XX Olympiad are placed in Problem Book «Kvant» of this issue. The conditions for all problems and solutions to problems not included in the Problem are given below.

First day

1. Let $m$ and $n$ be natural numbers such that $n > m \geqslant 1$. In decimal notation, the group of the last three digits of 1978$^m$ is the same as the group of the last three digits of 1978$^n$. Find $m$ and $n$ such that the sum of $m+n$ is the smallest.

2. Let $P$ be a given point inside a given sphere and $A, B, C$ be arbitrary three points of this sphere such that the segments $PA, PB, PC$ are mutually perpendicular. Let $Q$ be the vertex of the parallelepiped defined by the segments $PA, PB$ and $PC$ diagonally opposite to $P$. Determine the geometric location of points $Q$.

3. The set of all natural numbers is the union of two disjoint subsets $\{f(1), f(2), \dots, f(n), \dots\}$, $\{g(1), g(2), \dots, g(n), \dots\}$, where $f(1) < f(2) < \dots < f(n) < \dots$, $g(1) < g(2) < \dots < g(n) < \dots$ and $g(n) = f(f(n)) + 1$ for all $n \geqslant 1$. Define $f(240)$.

Second day

4. The circle touches internally the circle circumscribed around the right-femoral triangle $ABC$, as well as the equal sides $AB, AC$ of this triangle at points $P, Q$, corresponding. Prove that the midpoint of the segment $PQ$ is the center of the circle circumscribed by the triangle $ABC$.

5. Let $\{a_k\} (k=1, 2, \dots, n)$ be a sequence of distinct natural numbers. Prove that for every natural number $n$ the inequality

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{k^2} \geqslant \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k}.$$

6. The international society consists of representatives from 6 different countries. The list of members of the society consists of 1978 names, marked with the numbers 1, 2, ..., 1978.

Prove that there is at least one member of the society whose number is equal to the sum of the numbers of two members from his country or double the number of some member from his country.

Solutions

Problem 1. From the condition it follows that $1978^n - 1978^m = 1978^m (1978^n - m - 1) = 1000 a$, where $a$ is some natural number. From this it is clear that $m \geqslant 3: 1978^m$ must be divisible by 8. It remains to find $n-m$ for which $A = 1978^n - m - 1$ is divisible by 5$^3$. It is easy to check that $A$ is divisible by 5 only when $n-m=4k$. Since we are interested in the remainder when dividing the number $A$ by 125, we can replace 1978 by 103, and 103$^4$ by 6. Then $6^k - 1 = (1 + 5)^k - 1 = k \cdot 5 + \frac{k(k-1)}{2} - 5^2 + \dots$, where the omitted terms are divisible by 125. Therefore, $2k + 5(k-1)k = k(5k-3)$ is divisible by 25, whence $k$ is divisible by 25. Therefore $n-m=100k$, and $n+m=100k+2m$. Answer: 106.

Problem 5. It is easy to check that from $x_1 > x_2$, $y_1 > y_2$ it follows $x_1y_1 + x_2y_2 > x_1y_2 + x_2y_1$. Hence, for $k_1 < k_2$ and $a_{k_1} > a_{k_2}$ we get

$$\frac{a_{k_1}}{k_1^2} + \frac{a_{k_2}}{k_2^2} \geqslant \frac{a_{k_2}}{k_1^2} + \frac{a_{k_1}}{k_2^2}.$$

Let's rearrange all $a_k$ with $k=1, \dots, n$ in order of their age: $a_{k_1} < a_{k_2} < \dots < a_{k_n}$. Then by (*)

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{k^2} \geqslant \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{i^2}.$$

Considering that $a_{k_i} \geqslant i$ we obtain an exception inequality.
