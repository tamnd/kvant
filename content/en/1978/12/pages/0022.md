To obtain the general form of the function $V(t)$, let us turn to expression (*). This expression is the sum of six terms, each of which is the product of three components, each of which, in turn, is a sum of the form (****). After opening the brackets in each product, we get the sum of forty terms of the form

$$\lambda p_1(t) p_2(t) p_3(t), \quad (****)$$

where $\lambda$ is a number and $p_i(t)$ is either $\sin \omega_i(t)$ or $\cos \omega_i(t)$, $i = 1, 2, 3$.

Fortunately, we do not need to write down these 48 terms explicitly, since the values ​​of the coefficients $\lambda$ turn out to be unimportant for us (just as the values ​​of the coefficients in the example from the previous paragraph were not important).

After bringing similar terms, we find that $V(t)$ is the sum of several terms of the form (****). To show that the integral of each term (****) in the expression $V(t)$ is equal to zero.

Let's transform the product $p_1(t) p_2(t) p_3(t)$ into a sum. Various cases are possible, depending on how many times - one, two or three - the snus is included in this work. For example

$$(\sin \omega_1 t \sin \omega_2 t) \sin \omega_3 t =$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left( \cos \left( \omega_1 - \omega_2 \right) t - \cos \left( \omega_1 + \omega_2 \right) t \right) \sin \omega_3 t =$$

$$= \frac{1}{4} \left[ \sin \left( \omega_1 - \omega_2 + \omega_3 \right) t + \sin \left( \omega_1 - \omega_2 - \omega_3 \right) t - \sin \left( \omega_1 + \omega_2 + \omega_3 \right) t - \sin \left( \omega_1 + \omega_2 - \omega_3 \right) t \right].$$

Having similarly transformed the remaining products, we obtain that $V(t)$ is the sum of terms of the form $\mu \sin vt$ or $\mu \cos vt$, where $v = \pm \omega_1 \pm \omega_2 \pm \omega_3$ with all possible combinations of signs. For integer values ​​of $v$ other than zero, we obtain

$$\int_0^{2\pi} \sin vt dt = \left( -\frac{\cos vt}{v} \right) \int_0^{2\pi} = 0;$$

$$\int_0^{2\pi} \cos vt dt = \left( \frac{\sin vt}{v} \right) \int_0^{2\pi} = 0.$$

Of course, the calculations just made can be carried out only at $v \neq 0$. However, for any combination of signs, the sum $v = \pm 2 \pm 3 \pm 4$ is non-zero. That's why

$$\int_0^{2\pi} V(t) dt = 0.$$

Consequently, there is a value $t_0 \in [0; 2\pi]$ such that $V(t_0) = 0$, i.e., there will definitely be a moment of flattening.

6. Problem about satellites-suns

We reduce, but unfortunately not in an elementary way, the following theorem about a planet and three satellites:

Theorem. Let $\omega_3$ be the most catching speed among the catching speeds $\omega_i$, $i = 1, 2, 3$.

1) If $\omega_3 \neq \omega_1 + \omega_2$, then for any initial locations of orbits and satellites on them there will always be a moment of flattening.

2) If $\omega_3 = \omega_1 + \omega_2$, then for some initial arrangements of orbits and satellites on them there is always a moment of flattening.
