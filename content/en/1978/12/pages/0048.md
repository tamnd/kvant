To do this, we mentally free the rod from its fastening, for example, in the lower section $A$, and replace the action of fastening with an external force $\vec{R}_A$. Let us denote by $\Delta l_1$ the elongation of the rod (displacement of the section $A$)

under the action of force $\vec{F}$, and through $\Delta l_2$ - elongation of the rod (again, movement of the section $A$) under the action of force $\vec{R}_A$. In reality, the rod does not lengthen (section $A$ does not move). Consequently, the equation for the compatibility of deformations (displacements) can be written in the form: $\Delta l_1 + \Delta l_2 = 0$.

We find the values ​​of $\Delta l_1$ and $\Delta l_2$ from Hooke’s law (for more details, see Problem 2), taking into account that $N_1 = -F = |\vec{F}|$ and $N_2 = -R_A$:

$$\Delta l_1 = \frac{|\vec{F}|b}{ES} \quad \text{и} \quad \Delta l_2 = -\frac{R_A(a+b)}{ES}.$$

Considering that $a+b=l$, we get:

$$\frac{|\vec{F}|b}{ES} - \frac{R_A l}{ES} = 0,$$

where

$$R_A = |\vec{F}| \frac{b}{l}.$$

From the equilibrium condition of the rod we find $R_B$:

$$R_B = |\vec{F}| - R_A = |\vec{F}| \frac{a}{l}$$

The projections $R_A$ and $R_B$ are positive; This means that the actual directions of the reaction forces coincide with those accepted at figure 6, $a$.
