On the other hand, in many problems the most sensible thing to do is to draw up equations. And here a common “tactical” mistake is often encountered: the construction is often taken without any thought as an unknown obligatory thing that is sought in the problem, although this is far from always reasonable.

Let us consider, as an example, a problem—one of the most disliked type constructions—on “pools”:

Three rubles are connected to the set. Through the first ruble the complovan can be filled 5 hours faster than through the second, and through the first and second together - three times faster than through the third. How long will it take to fill the layout when all rubbish are working simultaneously, if the throughput of the first rubbish is 80% higher than that of the third?

Let us denote by x, y, z the throughput capacities of each of the tribes, measured, for example, in m³/h. In order to determine the time of filling the pit, we need to know its volume. Let us denote this volume by V. To harmonize the units of measurement, we assume, of course, that the volume is also measured in m³. Then rubles can fill the pit, respectively, for $\frac{V}{x}$, $\frac{V}{y}$ and $\frac{V}{z}$ and we get the correspondence:

$$\frac{V}{x} = \frac{V}{y} - 5; \quad 3 \frac{V}{x+y} = \frac{V}{z}, \quad x = 1, 8z.$$

We got 3 equations with 4 unknowns. We will solve the resulting system of equations, not paying attention to the fact that the volume V is unknown to us. Expressing x and y in terms of z (from the 3rd and 2nd equations), and then substituting the resulting x and y values ​​into the first equation, we get the equation

$$\frac{V}{1,8z} = \frac{V}{1,2z} - 5,$$

or $\frac{V}{z} = 18$.

Well, how can you find out the filling time now? It is clear that in 1 hour, three rubles together, $(x+y+z)$ m³ arrives and, therefore, it will take

$$\frac{V}{x+y+z} = \frac{V}{4z} = \frac{9}{2}(ч).$$

Significant emphasis on property equations of equations and inequalities. In this case, the main emphasis is not on testing calculation and transformation techniques or mastering cunning solution techniques, but on the conscious application of known properties of functions, which should be included in the base of values ​​as a mandatory minimum.

Thus, to solve the equation $|34x^2 - 7x - 22| = -2$ it is not at all necessary to find out (in accordance with the definition of the module) for which x the expression $34x^2 - 7x - 22$ is positive or negative, although this is where, as a rule, well-trained applicants begin solving the solution for entrance exams. From the well-known property of the modulus it immediately follows that this equation has no solutions - after all, the model of a number is a non-negative number!

When solving the inequality $|\sqrt{x-2} - 1| > -1$ if the inequality is not true, the part is declared that it holds for any x. In fact, it holds for any x included in its domain of definition, i.e. for $x \geq 2$. Equation and inequalities

$$\sqrt{x-2} + 4 = 0, \quad \sqrt{x-3} + \sqrt{2-x} \leq 2,$$

$$3 \sin^2 x + \sin^3 x < 100,$$

$$4 \cos^2 x + \cos^3 x = 5$$

can be solved immediately if you know well and correctly use the properties of the functions included in them.

The first equation has no solutions, since the argumentative square root, by its definition, cannot be a negative number. The first equation has no solutions, but for a different reason - its domain of definition does not contain a single value of the variable: in fact, the first term makes sense at $x \geq 3$, and the second - at $x \leq 2$, and these two conditions are not simultaneously satisfied. To solve the second inequality, it is enough to say that sine cannot be greater than 1, so the left-hand side cannot be greater than 4. Therefore, this inequality holds for any x. As for the last equation, it can be solved by similar reasoning, but the end of the perfect is different: the fact is that $4 \cos^2 x \leq 4, \cos^3 x \leq 1$, and therefore the right side can be equal to 5 only for $x = 1$. Therefore, this equation is equivalent to the equation $\cos x = 1$, i.e., it is satisfied at $x = 2\pi k$, where $k$ is any integer.

It is also necessary to know the simplest formulas and transformations, logarithms, properties of exponential functions, and formulas for adding trigonometric functions.
