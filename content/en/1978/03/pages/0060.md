In conclusion, we invite you to figure out the following “paradoxes” for yourself.

1. Car $A$ moves with speed $v$ relative to the Earth. After the speed of the machine doubles, its kinetic energy increases by amounts

$$\Delta K_1 = \frac{m(2v)^2}{2} - \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{3}{2}mv^2.$$

From the point of view of an observer located in the automaton $B$, which moves with the same speed $v$ and in the same direction as the automaton $A$, the change in the kinetic energy of the automaton $A$ will be equal to

$$\Delta K_2 = \frac{mv^2}{2} - 0 = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

For an observer located in the automaton $B$, which moves with the same absolute speed as the automaton $A$, but in the opposite direction, the change in the kinetic energy of the automaton $A$ will be equal to

$$\Delta K_3 = \frac{m(3v)^2}{2} - \frac{m(2v)^2}{2} = \frac{5}{2}mv^2.$$

The result was strange at first glance: the amount of spent fuel in automatic $A$ is unchanged, but the change in kinetic energy automatic $A$ is different in different reporting systems. Is there a contradiction here with the law of conservation of energy?

2. A bullet of mass $m$, moving with speed $v$, hits a sand platform moving with speed $u$ in the same direction and gets stuck there. Let us find the number of tellots released in this case.

In the reporting system associated with the Earth,

$$Q_1 = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{mu^2}{2} = \frac{m}{2}(v^2 - u^2).$$

In the reporting system associated with the platform,

$$Q_2 = \frac{m}{2}(v - u)^2.$$

It turned out to be a paradox: perhaps a bullet will melt in one reporting system, but not in another?
