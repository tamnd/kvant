This interaction changes the Earth's energy, and in different reporting systems this change will not only differ in magnitude, but also its sign will be different - in some systems the Earth's energy increases, in others it decreases.

In the reporting system, in which the Earth will initially end, part of the fuel energy is spent on increasing the kinetic energy automatically, and part - on increasing the Earth's energy $\Delta K_3'$. The increase in Earth's energy in this reporting system is equal to

$$\Delta K_3' = \frac{mv^2}{2} \cdot \frac{m}{M}.$$

When taking into account the correspondence $m \ll M$, this increase can be neglected and it can be assumed that all the energy $W$ released during fuel combustion is spent on increasing the kinetic energy automatically:

$$W = \frac{3}{2} mv^2 + \Delta K_3' \approx \frac{3}{2} mv^2.$$

In the reporting system associated with automatic $B$, the Earth has an initial velocity of $\vec{v}$. As automatic $A$ accelerates, the Earth's speed increases. The increase in kinetic energy associated with this change is equal to

$$\Delta K_3'' = \frac{M}{2} \left(v + \frac{m}{M} v\right)^2 - \frac{Mv^2}{2} = mv^2 + \frac{mv^2}{2} \cdot \frac{m}{M}.$$

Taking into account the correspondence $m \ll M$ we have: $\Delta K_3'' \approx mv^2$.

Thus, in the “car $B$” report system, part of the energy of the fuel burned in the automatic $A$ is spent on increasing the automatic kinetic energy:

$$\Delta K_2 = \frac{mv^2}{2},$$ and the other part of this energy is spent on increasing the energy of the Earth: $\Delta K_3'' = mv^2$. So

$$W = \Delta K_2 + \Delta K_3'' = \frac{mv^2}{2} + mv^2 = \frac{3}{2} mv^2.$$

In the reporting system associated with automatic $B$, the change in kinetic energy automatic $A$ is real equal to $\frac{5}{2} mv^2$. Part of this energy is received by the automatic $A$ due to the fuel, and the other part is imparted to it by the Earth, which in this system decreases its speed when accelerating the automatic $A$, and therefore “loses” part of its kinetic energy. This loss is equal to

$$\Delta K_3'' = \frac{M}{2} \left(v - \frac{mv}{M}\right)^2 - \frac{Mv^2}{2} = -mv^2 + \frac{mv^2}{2} \cdot \frac{m}{M} \approx -mv^2.$$

Thus, in this case, the paradox is symbolized when taking into account the change in the energy of the “larger” body - the Earth:

$$Q = \Delta K_3 + \Delta K_3'' = \frac{5}{2} mv^2 - mv^2 = \frac{3}{2} mv^2.$$

2. The changes in the kinetic energy of the bullet in the selected reporting systems are actually different:

$$\Delta K_1 = -\frac{m}{2} \left(v^2 - u^2\right),$$

$$\Delta K_2 = -\frac{m}{2} \left(v - u\right)^2.$$

However, part of this energy is spent on increasing the energy of the platform $\Delta K_{\pi\pi}$. In a reporting system in which the platform is stationary, the change in the energy of the platform can be neglected, since $M \gg m_\pi$. In this case, the amount of heat released is equal to

$$Q = -\Delta K_2 = \frac{m}{2} \left(v - u\right)^2.$$

In the Earth report system, the platform moved with a speed of $u$, therefore, the change in the energy of the platform cannot be neglected.

Indeed, the change in platform energy in this reporting system is equal to

$$\Delta K_{\pi\pi} = \frac{M \left(u + \Delta u\right)^2}{2} - \frac{Mu^2}{2} = M \left| \vec{u} \right| \left| \Delta \vec{u} \right|,$$

where $\Delta u$ is the change in platform speed after interaction with the bullet. According to the law of conservation of momentum

$$m \left( \left| \vec{v} \right| - \left| \vec{u} \right| \right) = M \left| \Delta \vec{u} \right|,$$

that is

$$\Delta \vec{u} = \frac{m \left( \left| \vec{v} \right| - \left| \vec{u} \right| \right)}{M}.$$

Hence,

$$\Delta K_{\pi\pi} = M \left| \vec{u} \right| \frac{m \left( \left| \vec{v} \right| - \left| \vec{u} \right| \right)}{M} = m \left| \vec{u} \right| \left( \left| \vec{v} \right| - \left| \vec{u} \right| \right).$$
