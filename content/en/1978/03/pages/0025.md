In full agreement with common sense and the results of experiments with real crystals, the BLN model indicates that the volume of one vacancy is slightly less than the volume per occupied position. Indeed, after the bubble bursts, its formerly neighboring one will move into the formed void and reduce it. It is almost impossible to see this with the naked eye, but if you project film or photographic film onto a screen and carefully measure the distances between the bubbles, you can be convinced that, compared to the occupied position, the vacancy is a little noticeable. For physicists, this evidence of the BLN model is not just qualitative or spatial, it also has quantitative value.

Very often, in a crystal, due to its prehistory, there appears to be a built-up inclusion, a deformation of the crystal. When solving many problems in crystal physics, it is very important to know how the atoms surrounding the inclusion mix. It turns out that the presence of a foreign inclusion is felt not only by direct connections, but also by atoms located at a considerable distance from the inclusion. The BLN model clearly illustrates this (see Fig. 7).

Most crystalline solids are polycrystalline. This means that they consist of many randomly oriented crystals, separated by boundaries. It is almost obvious that many properties of polycrystals (such as mechanical strength or resistance to electrical current) must depend on the structure of the boundaries. The BLN model also turns out to be useful in this matter: crystal physics has suggested how the structure of the boundary changes depending on the relative orientation of the adjacent crystals, the presence of impurities located at the boundary, and much more. In polycrystals, a process of enlargement of some areas (grains) may occur at the expense of others, as a result of which the average grain size increases. This process of crystallization is extracted and it occurs for an obvious reason: the larger the grain size, the smaller the total surface of the boundaries, and therefore the smaller the excess energy that is associated with the boundaries. The energy of a polycrystal during recrystallization decreases, therefore, this process can occur spontaneously (since it approaches a state of stable equilibrium, in which the energy reserve is minimal).
