L. Sofman

Sums of length and minimum energy

Mathematical problems are often suggested by nature, or rather by our interpretation of nature, by the physical sciences. The solution to a mathematical problem can also be suggested by nature; physics provides us with clues which, if left to ourselves, we would have very little chance of securing for ourselves.

D. Sing a

In this note we will look at several famous geometric problems. We will show how they are solved using a simple mechanical principle - the principle of minimum potential energy, according to which any mechanical system in a stable equilibrium position has a minimum potential energy.

Problem 1. On the plane there are given straight lines $l$ and points $A$ and $B$ lying on one side of $l$. Find the maximum point $X \in l$ so that the length of the broken line $AXB$ is minimal.

Obviously this problem is solved using symmetry: if $B'$ is a point symmetrical to $B$ with respect to the straight line $l$, then the required point is $X = l \cap [AB']$ (Fig. 1). Thus, the segments $AX$ and $BX$ form equal angles with $l$: $\alpha = \beta$.

Let us now consider such a mechanical problem. The weightless pierce $X$ can slide without friction along the horizontal rod $l$. Attached to $X$ are two threads thrown over blocks rotating around axes fixed in a vertical plane at points $A$ and $B$ (Fig. 2).

Loads of equal masses are attached to the free ends of the threads $C_1$ and $C_2$. (The threads are weightless and non-stretchable, the blocks rotate without friction, the sizes of the blocks and the ring $X$ are so small that they can be considered as material points.) It is required to find the position of the ring $X$ on the rod $l$, at which the entire mechanical system is in equilibrium.

Let us show that this mechanical problem is in some sense equivalent to our geometric problem. Indeed, in the equilibrium position, the potential energy of a mechanical system is minimal; therefore, in the equilibrium position, the loads should be placed together as low as possible, i.e., the sum of their distances from a given horizontal level (ground surface) should be minimal (the loads are the same!).
