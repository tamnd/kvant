Interaction between atoms in a crystal

One, perhaps the most important, characteristic of this interaction directly follows from the simplest fact: the distance between two neighboring atoms as a result of temperature has a certain value in the wave. (We are talking, of course, about the distance between the positions around which atoms perform thermal vibrations. The amplitude of these vibrations is significantly less than the distance between the atoms.)

The definition of distance means that if we try to increase it, the atoms, resisting this, will hide towards each other, and if we try to reduce it, they will respond. So, only from the fact of the presence of a certain distance between atoms it follows that the interaction between them has the features of both concealment and deflection at the same time.

At certain distances between the atoms (we called it a certain one), the forces of contraction and detachment turn out to be equal in absolute value. The atoms in the lattice are located at this distance.

It would be nice to come up with a modeling technique that would convey the competition between the forces of attraction and repulsion. In other words, it would “revive” the interaction between atoms in the crystal. This is exactly what the authors of the BLN model did! The element builders in this model are not clay or wooden balls, but... small soap bubbles.
