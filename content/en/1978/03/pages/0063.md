⟦folio 61⟧

Why and how the empty space disappears

This is the name of another popular science book by Professor Ya. E. Geguzin *), a well-known reader «Kvant» as the author of “Drops” (see «Kvant», 1974, No. 8) and “Essays on diffusion in crystals” (see «Kvant», 1975, No. 7). Like the first books, it is written vividly, excitingly, and vividly. At first glance, it may seem that the topic is too narrow and special: the book is devoted to the history of the development of physical ideas of the modern theory of sintering, that is, the process in which solid metal or ceramic materials are created from powders. However, in reality the content of the book is much broader. Essentially, this is a book about scientific philosophy, about the struggle with ideas, about how theory drives practice, and practice shows the path of theory, how the logic of living science guides the thoughts of a scientist.

How is the powder sintered? How does a porous body turn into a solid body? Where does “empty go”? How are particles specialized among themselves? To answer these questions, it is necessary to consider - this is what the author of the book does - how a solid body is built, what are the defects in its atomic structure, what role do pores play in a crystal and how they move, how grains move on the surface of a solid body, etc.

Along the way, the author talks about many experiments, findings of experimenters,

The book contains almost no formulas, no mathematical conclusions, but there are clear qualitative ways to derive formulas, accurate estimates of orders of magnitude and basic propositions.

The range of issues considered is very wide. Here is the atomic structure of crystals with their structural defects, and the physics of the diffusion process, and the kinetics of the movement of defects in a crystal, and the physics of technological processes and operations, and a detailed description of a number of experiments. It is enough to cite the headings of some sections - “How did it start?”, “Does the sought-after formula exist?”, “The price of the formula”, “The merging of theory”, “On technical and creative”, “How much technology does a physicist need?”, “A look into the future” - to see how lively and exciting the author is in his narrative. Especially the fact that he doesn’t put an end to it. does not complete, does not necessarily strive to show something completed, but to recognize it at the end as a question mark - a look into the future, into what remains to be done.

The book reads like a fascinating story and is difficult to put down. At the same time, the author of the book never gets carried away with excessive popularization. The presentation of the Veda is strict, accurate, at the most modern level of science. The author himself defines his book as a book about scientific research, “about the compelling prior processes of research, ... about the history of scientific results that sometimes looked like a discovery, but turned out to be a mistake, about articles that, when they appeared, were recognized as weak, but turned out to be viable, about conferences, meetings and discussions at round and non-round tables, during which the truth, which is hidden by average experimental books, electronic microscopic photographs, clear formulas and not very clear comments on them.”

The author completely misses this idea. Starting with a simple question of how powders are pressed to make metal machine parts or refractory materials, the author drew on a wide range of physical phenomena in crystals and crystalline powders and spoke about them in a lively, vivid and fascinating manner based on a commercial and historical picture of the development of science in this area.
