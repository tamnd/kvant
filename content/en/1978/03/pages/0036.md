Second solution. Let's project a sphere from the center $P$ onto the plane $\alpha$, parallel to the equator $O$. then a pair of symmetric $n$-gons on the sphere, not direct to $O$, will be mapped onto a $n$-gon in the $\alpha$ plane, and a pair of $n$-gons, direct to $O$ - onto a $(n-2)$-angle; Using the result of problem a) - the uniqueness of the division of the plane by four straight lines and the first table - we conclude that any five large circles divide the sphere into two pentagons (their projection is a 3-angle), $2(1+4)=10$ quadrangles (of which eight are straight lines to a given great circle $O$, and these four pairs are projected onto 2-angles), and $2(2+3)=10$ triangles (six of them are straight to the great circle $O$).

Those who are familiar with the concept of the projective plane (see, for example, «Kvant», 1974, No. 3) will notice, of course, that the “projection” we are talking about is more naturally explained as a mapping of a sphere onto the projective plane (in this case, the mappings of two diametrically opposite points of the sphere are fused into one, the images of all great circles are straight lines, including the images of the circle $O$-infinitely remote line).

Figure 6, illustrating the second solution, needs to be calculated. Since the “line at infinity” is difficult to draw, we drew a point parallel at figure 6 very close to the great circle - the equator $O$; its image during projection will be a circle of very large radius in the plane $\alpha$, containing within itself the points of intersection of four straight lines; these straight lines divide the “very large circle” that appears on the plane into 11 regions: a “pentagon”, five “triangles” and five “quadrangles”; returning from the plane to the sphere, we get the answer after doubling. In conclusion, we note that in all three tasks, not only the types of partitions (the number of sides), but also their relative positions are determined uniquely.

We invite the reader «Kvant» as a research task to find out what kind of divisions of a sphere there are six large circles or a plane - a fifth by straight lines (there is no longer the only one). The most difficult thing here, apparently, is to prove that all possible partitions have been found.
