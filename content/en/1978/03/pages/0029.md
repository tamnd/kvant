On the other hand, in the equilibrium position, the sum of the forces acting at the point $X$ is zero. In $X$ there are three tension forces of threads $\vec{T}_1, \vec{T}_2$ and $\vec{T}_3$, equal in absolute value (equal loads are equally tensioned by each thread): $|\vec{T}_1| = |\vec{T}_2| = |\vec{T}_3|$, and $\vec{T}_1 + \vec{T}_2 + \vec{T}_3 = 0$. From these two conditions we obtain that these forces must be equally inclined to each other, so that in the equilibrium position of our mechanical system the angle between any two of the three threads with a common end $X$ must be equal to $120^\circ$. Therefore, the answer in problem 3 (provided that in triangle $ABC$ all angles are less than $120^\circ$!) follows: point $X$ is a point from which all sides of triangle $ABC$ are visible at an angle of $120^\circ$.

(In the case when the points $A, B$ and $C$ form a triangle in which one of the angles is $\geq 120^\circ$, the point $X$ coincides with one of the points $A, B$ or $C$ (the vertex of the “large” angle). Think over this answer and set it to explain.)

Problem 4. On different banks of the river there is a path of settlements $A_1, A_2, A_3, B_1, B_2$ ($A_1, A_2, A_3$ on one bank, $B_1, B_2$ on the other; Fig. 5). It is required to build a bridge $CD$ so that the sum of the lengths of roads $A_1C, A_2C, A_3C, B_1D, B_2D$ from populated areas to the place is minimal. (The banks of the river are parallel, the bridge is perpendicular to the banks.)

To solve this problem, consider the following mechanical system. A rigid rod $CD$ can slide without friction along two parallel rails, remaining perendicular to them all the time (see Fig. 5). Threads are attached to the ends $C$ and $D$ of the rod, throw them over the blocks, rotating around the axes fixed at points $A_1, A_2, A_3, B_1$ and $B_2$.
