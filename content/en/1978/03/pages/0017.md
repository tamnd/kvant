The probability of such a distribution is equal to $\frac{1}{16} \cdot 6$, i.e. $37^{1/2} \%$, while the probability of the initial state (which was at the moment when the shutter was already raised, but the molecules had not yet left part I) is only $\frac{1}{16}$, i.e. $6^{1/4} \%$.

If we have only $N$ molecules in our system, then the total number of possible combinations will be $2^N$; the number of those in which part $I$ contains $x$ molecules, and part $II$ contains the remaining $N - x$, is equal, as elementary algebra shows, to the number $\frac{N!}{x!(N - x)!} \cdot 1$. Therefore, the probability of a gas state in which part $I$ contains $x$ molecules is $\frac{N!}{x!(N - x)!} \cdot \frac{1}{2^N}$. The probability of the initial state is only $\frac{N!}{x!(N - x)!} \cdot 2^N$, the greater the closer $x$ is to $\frac{N}{2}$. Therefore, after the valve is raised, the gas will go into the most probable state - in a state in which its molecules are distributed equally between both parts of the vessel. However, if we wait long enough, eventually a combination should appear that corresponds to the accumulation of all $N$ molecules in part $I$; after this we could lower the damper again and declare that the reversal of the “irreversible” process of gas distribution into the void has occurred.
