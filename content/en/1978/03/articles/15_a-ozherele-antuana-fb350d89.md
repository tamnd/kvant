Antoine's necklace

This is the name of the intricate decoration shown on the first page of the cover. But this is not the creation of an ovelier artist: it was created in the early 20s by the French mathematician L. Antoine. (Somewhat later, but independently, a similar example was obtained by the Soviet mathematician P. S. Urson, immediately before his tragic death at the age of 26.)

What does figure represent? A chain of six points of the first rank $T^{(1)}_1, T^{(1)}_2, \dots, T^{(1)}_6$. In each of them there is again a whole of (smaller) tori; in total - 36 tori of the second rank, forming a more intricate whole $T^{(2)}_1, T^{(2)}_2, \dots, T^{(2)}_{36}$. Using the same scheme, third-rank tors $T^{(3)}_1, T^{(3)}_2, \dots, T^{(3)}_{n}$ are constructed.

Of course, Antoine did not come up with this set for fun or even for beauty; set A has completely unexpected properties; these properties are studied in one of the most significant branches of modern mathematics - topology.
