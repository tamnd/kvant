Viviana

The curve, as defined in the caption to the four pages of the cover, is sometimes called “Vivian’s curve,” sometimes “Vivian’s windows,” sometimes briefly “Vivian,” and sometimes simply “oblique eight” for its shape. This spatial curve was first pointed out by the illyan mathematician Vinchenko Viviana (1622-1703), who was fortunate enough to be a student of G. Galileo and a friend of E. Torricelli. In 1692, Viviana sent letters to a number of mathematicians in which he suggested that they make (speculatively, of course) two windows in the “dome” (hemisphere) so that the surface of the hollowed cupola was equal to the square that could be built on the diameter of the cupola. It turned out that Viviana, if a cupal cut is made along it, satisfies the conditions of this problem. The middle-class scientists whose attention was attracted by Vivian's problem were I. Bernouli, G. W. Leibniz and J. P. Roberval.

Leibniz, using integral calculus, completed Vivian's problem the fastest—in one day. Vivian himself solved his problem without the help of integrals.

Vivianne can construct a compass on the surface of a cylinder; it is only necessary that the length of the diameter of the cylinder, i.e., the radius of the sphere (Fig. 1), expand. To prove the equivalence of the two definitions of Vivianna, indicated in the caption to the four pages of the cover, consider figure 2, on which $O$ is the center of the sphere, $M$ is the current point of Vivianna, $A$ is the point at which Vivianna intersects itself, $N$ is the projection of point $M$ onto the base of the cylinder. Now from the equalities $|OM| = |OA| = r$ it follows that $\frac{r \cos \alpha}{\cos \beta} = r$, i.e. $\alpha = \beta$.

Let us now consider the central projections of Vivianna from different points of the sphere on different planes, passing through the center. If the projection center is taken to be the point $B$, then the projection of Vivianna onto the plane $Oxy$ will be construction («Kvant», 1977, No. 2, p. 43). If we take the point $A$ as the projection center, then the projection of Vivianna onto the plane $Oyz$ will be constructed as a hyperbola (this curve is usually used to demonstrate the inverse proportionality). If, finally, we leave the same projection plane, and choose as the center of projection the point $A'$ of the sphere, diametrically projected to the point $A$, then the image of Vivianne will lie in Bernoulli («Kvant», 1977, No. 1, p. 25).
