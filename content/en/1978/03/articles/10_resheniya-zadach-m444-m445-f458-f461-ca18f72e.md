How will the time required for cutting on this spiral with water to boil change if one of the spirals burns out?

Let $v_0$ be the speed of the river current, $v_1$ be the speed of the boy relative to the current. Then the speed of the boy relative to the shore is

$$v = v_0 + v_1.$$

The boy's velocity vector relative to the current can have different directions, but its end must necessarily lie on a circle of radius $|v_1|$ (Fig. 13). From figure it is clear that the vector $v$ makes a minimum angle with the coastline in the case when it is directed tangent to the circle - the geometric locus of the ends of possible vectors $v_1$. In this case, the boy will obviously be carried away by the current to the minimum possible distance. From figure we find that the boy must swim at an angle to the shoreline equal to

The resistance of a tile with three parallel-connected spirals is $R_1 = R/3 = 40$ ohms. After one spiral burns out, the resistance of the tile becomes $R_2 = R/2 = 60$ Ohm.

Until the spiral burns out, current $I_1 = \frac{U}{R_1 + r}$ flows through the circuit (U is the network voltage). After burnout - $I_2 = \frac{U}{R_2 + r}$.

The amount of heat $Q$ required to cut a kettle of water to boiling is, in the first case, released in the tile during the time

$$t_1 = \frac{Q}{I_1^2 R_1} = \frac{Q (R_1 + r)^2}{U^2 R_1},$$

in the second case - in time

$$t_2 = \frac{Q}{I_2^2 R_2} = \frac{Q (R_2 + r)^2}{U^2 R_2}.$$

Let's find the ratio of time $t_1$ and $t_2$:

$$\frac{t_1}{t_2} = \frac{(R_1 + r)^2 R_2}{(R_2 + r)^2 R_1} = \frac{243}{242}.$$

Hence $t_2 = \frac{242}{243} t_1$.

F461. Each square meter of the surface of a body, cut to a temperature of $T$, emits energy $W = 5,67 \times 10^{-8} T^4$ W per unit time. At what distance $R$ from the Sun do iron filings turn into drops if the flux density of solar radiation (energy passing through a unit area per unit time) on the Earth's volume is $E_0 = 1400 \text{ Bm}/\text{m}^2$? The melting point of iron is approximately $T_0 = 1535 \text{ K}$, the distance from the Earth to the Sun is $R_0 = 1,5 \cdot 10^{11} \text{ m}$.

Fig. 15.

Let $U_C$ be the voltage across the capacitor. Since, according to the conditions, the change in the charge of the capacitor during time $\tau_1$ (and therefore during time $\tau_2$) is small, we can assume that after a larger period of time after the start of the switch, the charge $U_C$ does not change with time. Then, when the capacitor is charged, current $I_1 = \frac{U - U_C}{R}$ flows through the circuit, and during time $\tau_1$ charge $q_1 = I_1 \tau_1 = \frac{U - U_C}{R} \tau_1$ passes through. When the capacitor is charged, current $I_2 = \frac{U_C}{r}$ flows through the circuit, so that in time $\tau_2$ charge $q_2 = I_2 \tau_2 = \frac{U_C}{r} \tau_2$ passes through. $q_1$ and $q_2$ are the change in the charge of the capacitor during its charge and discharge. In the set mode $q_1 = q_2$, i.e.

$$\frac{U - U_C}{R} \tau_1 = \frac{U_C}{r} \tau_2.$$

From here we find $U_C$:

$$U_C = U \frac{r \tau_1}{r \tau_1 + R \tau_2}.$$

The energy consumed from the source during time $\tau_1$ is equal to $U I_1 \tau_1$. Therefore, during time $\tau_1 + \tau_2$ the average power consumed from the source is equal to

$$P_{cp} = \frac{U I_1 \tau_1}{\tau_1 + \tau_2} = \frac{U^2 \left(1 - \frac{r \tau_1}{r \tau_1 + R \tau_2}\right) \tau_1}{R(\tau_1 + \tau_2)} = \frac{1}{180} \text{ Вт.}$$

I. Slobodetsky

A grain of iron is heated to a temperature $T$ at which the energy emitted by it becomes equal to the energy proportional to the surface area of ​​the grain. For the sake of estimation, we will assume that the sawdust is a ball, the average radius of which is equal to $r$. Then the energy emitted per unit time by each grain is heated to a temperature of $T_0$, equal to $W_{изл} = 4 \pi r^2 W$.

The energy received from the Sun per unit time is proportional to the area of ​​the largest cross-section of the grain, that is, $\pi r^2$, and is equal to

$$W_{погл} = \pi r^2 E,$$

where $E$ is the solar radiation flux density at that distance $R$ from the Sun at which the grain is located. Since constant energy will spread per unit solid angle from the Sun, then (Fig. 15) $ES_1 = E_0 S_2$ and

$$E = E_0 \frac{S_2}{S_1} = E_0 \left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^2.$$

Hence,

$$W_{погл} = \pi r^2 E_0 \left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^2.$$

Equating $W_{изл}$ and $W_{погл}$, we get

$$4 \pi r^2 W = \pi r^2 E_0 \left(\frac{R_0}{R}\right)^2.$$

From this equation we find

$$R = \frac{R_0}{2} \sqrt{\frac{E_0}{W}} \approx 5 \cdot 10^6 \text{ км.}$$

A. Smasenko
