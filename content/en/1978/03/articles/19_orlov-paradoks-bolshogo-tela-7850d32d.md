The big body paradox

When solving problems in which the interaction of a body with significantly different masses is considered, the change in the energy of a body with a larger mass is usually neglected. And this neglect often turns out to be correct. However, it is necessary to clearly understand when such an assumption is possible and when this cannot be done.

To clarify this issue, consider a number of problems formulated in the form of paradoxes.

Problem 1. A stone of mass $m$ falls to the Earth from a height $h$. 1) Let us write down the law of conservation of energy, considering the movement of the stone in the reporting system associated with the center of mass of the stone-Earth system. By the time the stone falls to the Earth, all its potential energy $mgh$ has transferred into kinetic energy $\frac{mv^2}{2}$:

$$mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2},$$

where $v$ is the speed of the stone before hitting the Earth.

2) Let us write down the law of conservation of energy, considering the movement in the reporting system associated with an elevator that moves down at a constant speed

In this article, we will call a “large” body a body whose mass is many times greater than the masses of other bodies interacting with it.

height $v$ relative to the Earth. In this system, the Earth has kinetic energy $\frac{Mv^2}{2}$ (M is the mass of the Earth), the stone at the initial moment had kinetic energy $\frac{mv^2}{2}$ and potential energy $mgh$. By the time the stone falls to the Earth, both its kinetic energy and its potential energy are equal to zero, so for all systems we can write:

$$\frac{Mv^2}{2} + \frac{mv^2}{2} + mgh = \frac{Mv^2}{2} \Rightarrow \frac{mv^2}{2} + mgh = 0.$$

It turns out that the law of conservation of energy is not fulfilled in the “elevator” system, but is fulfilled in the reporting system, relative to which the elevator moves at a constant speed. Paradox?!

Problem 2. A bullet of mass $m$, flying at a speed $v$, hits the mountainside and gets stuck in it.

Applying the law of conservation of energy in a reporting system in which the Earth is stationary, we find the change in bullet energy:

$$\Delta K_1 = 0 - \frac{mv^2}{2} = -\frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

We will consider the movement of a bullet in a reporting system associated with a machine gun, which moves at a speed $u$ in the same direction as the bullet. Then the change in kinetic energy of the bullet is equal to

$$\Delta K_2 = \frac{mu^2}{2} - \frac{m(|\vec{v} - \vec{u}|)^2}{2} = -\frac{mv^2}{2} + m|\vec{v}||\vec{u}|.$$

3) In the reference frame associated with a machine gun, which moves with a speed $\vec{u}$ in the direction opposite to the direction of movement of the bullet,

$$\Delta K_3 = \frac{mu^2}{2} - \frac{m(|\vec{v} + \vec{u}|)^2}{2} = -\frac{mv^2}{2} - m|\vec{v}||\vec{u}|.$$

Where does some of the bullet's kinetic energy go? When a bullet suddenly decelerates, heat is generated; in other words, the energy of the bullet turns into heat: $|\Delta K| = Q$. And we can change the amount of heat released (a bullet, for example, hits the calorimeter exactly). However, from our reasoning it follows that if we make the same changes three times, we should get different results: $Q_1 \neq Q_2 \neq Q_3$. Paradox?!

One can think of many more similar problems in which “paradoxes” arise only because the systems of bodies under consideration are not closed, and in all reasoning this is not taken into account. In Problem 2 above, the system does not include such “large” bodies as the Earth. And in task 1, point 2), although it is included in the Earth system, the change in its energy is considered equal to zero. In paragraph 1) Earth energy does not appear at all, however, any reader will probably say that everything is written here correctly.

What's the matter? Why do we get completely correct results when choosing some reference systems, but paradoxes when choosing others? Which reference system should be chosen when solving the problem, so that only more mass can be neglected, thereby simplifying the solution of the problem? To answer these questions, let’s return to the “paradox” problems and carry out all the reasoning “absolutely strictly.”

Problem 1.1) In the reference system associated with the center of mass of the stone-Earth system of bodies, at the initial moment the Earth will end and the entire energy of the system is equal to the potential energy of the stone. By the time the stone falls to the Earth, the energy of the system is equal to $\frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{Mu^2}{2}$, where $\vec{v}$ is the speed that the stone has traveled under the influence of the Earth’s gravitational force, and $\vec{u}$ is the speed that the Earth has traveled under the influence of the stone’s gravitational force. Let's find the speed $\vec{u}$, use the law of conservation of momentum:

$$m|\vec{v}| - M|\vec{u}| = 0,$$

where

$$|\vec{u}| = \frac{m}{M}|\vec{v}|.$$

Now let’s write down the law of conservation of energy of the system:

$$mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{M|\vec{v}|^2}{2} = \frac{mv^2}{2}\left(1 + \frac{m}{M}\right).$$

This entry is “absolutely strict.” However, it is clear that in all implementations the problem (about the fall of bodies on Earth) $m \ll M$ and the value $\frac{m}{M}$ is nothing small compared to 1, so it can be neglected. Then in the reference system associated with the center of mass of the stone-Earth body system, the law of conservation of energy will be written as follows:

$$mgh = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

2) In the “elevator” reference frame, the naal energy of the stone-Earth system is equal to $mgh + \frac{mv^2}{2} + \frac{Mu^2}{2}$, at the moment the stone falls, the energy of the system is equal to $\frac{Mu^2}{2}$, where $\vec{u}$ is the speed.

We previously believed that the energy of the Earth does not change and at the moment the stone falls is equal to $\frac{M v^2}{2}$, that is, we neglected the value $\frac{M u^2}{2} - \frac{M v^2}{2} = mv^2 + \frac{m}{M} \cdot \frac{m v^2}{2}$, which is greater than the kinetic energy of the stone. Obviously, this was our mistake, which led to the paradox. “Strictly” the law of conservation of energy of a system should be written as follows:

$$m g h + \frac{m v^2}{2} + \frac{M v^2}{2} = \frac{M v^2}{2} + mv^2 + \frac{m}{M} \cdot \frac{m v^2}{2}.$$

Using the “large” body condition, i.e., taking into account that $m \ll M$, we can neglect the quantities $\frac{m}{M} \cdot \frac{m v^2}{2}$, and then from the law of conservation of energy we find

$$m g h = \frac{m v^2}{2}!$$

So, in the reference system associated with the center of mass of the stone - Earth system, in which the “big” body - the Earth - was initially abandoned, the change in the energy of the “big” body can be neglected. In the “elevator” system, the “large” body Earth has an initial speed of $v$, the change in its energy is comparable to the change in the energy of a stone, and the change cannot be neglected.

Let us now turn to problem 2.

Problem 2. The change in the kinetic energy of the bullet in the considered cut-off systems is different:

$$\Delta K_1 = -\frac{mv^2}{2},$$
$$\Delta K_2 = -\frac{mv^2}{2} + m |\vec{v}| |\vec{u}|,$$
$$\Delta K_3 = -\frac{mv^2}{2} - m |\vec{v}| |\vec{u}|.$$

According to the theorem on kinetic energy - $A = \Delta K$ - the work of the external force, the force acting on the bullet from the side of the mountain, is also different. In other words, the consequence of relative movement in various systems is cut off by the relative work of the external force, and, consequently, the change in kinetic energy of the non-closed system of bodies is also relative.

The paradox we came to in this problem is caused by a recording error

$$Q = -\Delta K.$$

This expression is valid only for closed systems of bodies, since for such systems the change in the kinetic energy of all bodies of the system is inverant (i.e., unchanged) relative to any inertial cutoff systems.

So in problem 2, the amount of released body is $Q = |\Delta K_\pi| + |\Delta K_3|$, where $\Delta K_\pi$ and $\Delta K_3$ are the changes in the kinetic energies of the bullet and the Earth. However, the answer obtained in point 1) is correct, this is the amount of heat that is released, no matter in which system we solve the problem:

$$Q = |\Delta K_1| = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

Let's prove this statement. To do this, let us strictly carry out all our previous reasoning:

1) In the cut-off system in which the Earth (mountain) initially ends, the energy of the system is equal to the kinetic energy of the bullet $\frac{mv^2}{2}$. When a bullet hits a mountain, the Earth acquires a certain speed $\vec{u}$, which we can find from the law of conservation of momentum: $M |\vec{u}| = m |\vec{v}|$, hence $|\vec{u}| = \frac{m}{M} |\vec{v}|$. Consequently, the Earth acquires kinetic energy $\Delta K'_3 = \frac{Mu^2}{2}$, and the law of conservation of energy all systems must be written in the form

$$|\Delta K_\pi| = \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{m}{M} \cdot \frac{mv^2}{2} + Q,$$

or

$$Q = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{m}{M} \cdot \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

Using the conditions of the “larger” body - $m \ll M$, we get

$$Q = \frac{mv^2}{2} = |\Delta K_1|!$$

2) In the cut-off system associated with the machine gun, which moves at a speed $\vec{u}$ in the same direction as the bullet, the kinetic energy of the Earth changes by values ​​$\Delta K'_3$. This change is caused by a decrease in its speed from a value of $|\vec{u}|$ to a value of $|\vec{u} - \Delta \vec{u}|$.

The value $\Delta u$ can be determined by applying the law of conservation of momentum for the bullet-Earth system of bodies in the inertial cut-off system associated with their center of mass (change in the velocity of the inverant relative of the inertial cut-off systems!):

$$M |\vec{u}| = m |\vec{v}|, \quad \Delta u = \frac{m}{M} |\vec{v}|.$$

Given this value $\Delta u$ the change in the Earth's kinetic energy

So, the expression $Q = \Delta K$, which is valid only for closed systems of bodies (i.e. when $\Delta K$ is a change in the energy of the entire system), turns out to be correct for an open system of bodies, when this system is considered in the reporting system in which the “large” body will initially end.

In conclusion, we invite you to figure out the following “paradoxes” for yourself.

1. Car $A$ moves with speed $v$ relative to the Earth. After the speed of the machine doubles, its kinetic energy increases by amounts

$$\Delta K_1 = \frac{m(2v)^2}{2} - \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{3}{2}mv^2.$$

From the point of view of an observer located in the automaton $B$, which moves with the same speed $v$ and in the same direction as the automaton $A$, the change in the kinetic energy of the automaton $A$ will be equal to

$$\Delta K_2 = \frac{mv^2}{2} - 0 = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

For an observer located in the automaton $B$, which moves with the same absolute speed as the automaton $A$, but in the opposite direction, the change in the kinetic energy of the automaton $A$ will be equal to

$$\Delta K_3 = \frac{m(3v)^2}{2} - \frac{m(2v)^2}{2} = \frac{5}{2}mv^2.$$

The result was strange at first glance: the amount of spent fuel in automatic $A$ is unchanged, but the change in kinetic energy automatic $A$ is different in different reporting systems. Is there a contradiction here with the law of conservation of energy?

2. A bullet of mass $m$, moving with speed $v$, hits a sand platform moving with speed $u$ in the same direction and gets stuck there. Let us find the number of tellots released in this case.

In the reporting system associated with the Earth,

$$Q_1 = \frac{mv^2}{2} - \frac{mu^2}{2} = \frac{m}{2}(v^2 - u^2).$$

In the reporting system associated with the platform,

$$Q_2 = \frac{m}{2}(v - u)^2.$$

It turned out to be a paradox: perhaps a bullet will melt in one reporting system, but not in another?

3. From a cannon installed in fact, a shot is fired in the direction of flight easily at a speed of $u$.

1) Let the projectile, relative to the reporting system, moving at a speed of $u$, acquire a speed of $v$. Kinetic energy $\frac{mv^2}{2}$ is imparted to the projectile due to the energy $\Pi$ of the aged powder gases:

$$\Pi = \frac{mv^2}{2}.$$

2) Relative to the Earth-related reporting frame, the projectile speed is $v + u$, and therefore

$$\Pi = \frac{m(v + u)^2}{2} - \frac{mu^2}{2}.$$

Since the value $\Pi$ is invariant with respect to the reporting system, then from the above considered it follows that

$$v^2 + u^2 = (v + u)^2.$$

Find the flaw in the reasoning.
