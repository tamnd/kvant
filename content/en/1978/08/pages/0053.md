Getting started

figure 1 shows the cutting of the most common “blank” for playing stomaton - a rectangular ABCD with sides corresponding 1:2. Instead of such a rectangle, sometimes they take a square or even an arbitrary parallelogram. The method of dividing the original figure remains the same in all cases.

Let ABCD be an arbitrary parallelogram, F and E the midpoints of AD and BC. Drawing segment EF, divide the original parallelogram into parallelograms ABEF and FECD. Let's draw the diagonals AC, BF and FC. Let L and R be the points of intersection of the diagonal AC with the diagonal BF and the segment EF. Through the middle G of the segment BE and draw a straight line parallel to side AB of the original parallelogram to the intersection with the diagonal BF at point H and the segment GK of the straight line GA (K is the point of intersection of the straight line GA and the diagonal BF). Finally, by connecting vertex B with the midpoint M of segment AL with a straight segment, we will divide the parallelogram ABEF into 7 parts.

Let N and O be the midpoints of the segments CD and FC. Let's draw the segments ON and OE. Let Q be the point of intersection of segment OE with diagonal AC, and P be the point of intersection of line BO with side CD of the original parallelogram. Let's draw the segment OP. The FECD parallelogram also turns out to be divided into seven parts.

Thus, the original parallelogram ABCD is divided into 14 parts.

When composing new figures, parts of the original figure can be digested with the “face” side down. You just need to make sure that the composition of the figure contains a complete set of parts of the stomaton - all 14 of its parts. In compliance with this indispensable rule, the figures shown in Figures 2-4 were compiled. Having acquired the secrets of the “dental brain” (having figured out how these figures are arranged), you will learn to compose your own compositions.

A little history

Among geometric puzzles, stomaton stands out not only for its venerable age, but also for its invention.

The game "stomaton" was known even before our era. We also find mention of it in the works of some Primal authors of the 4th-6th centuries. Archimedes was considered the creator of stomaton, but for a long time no documentary evidence could be found.
