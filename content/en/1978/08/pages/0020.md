3. Tymeladze

Ferma is leading the investigation.

The stifling heat prevented the corner chamber of the Toulouse Parliament from meeting in full to serve the cause. All thirteen members of the chamber looked with curiosity at the accused Pierre Criomont, a selfish insolent man who had recently returned from Paris. The defendant did not admit his guilt even under torture, but this did not change anything. According to Councilor de Broval, on the day of St. George, at ten o’clock in the morning, the wealthy peasant Jean Morel from the village of Amber, on his way to church, heard the cry of the miller Jacques Criomont. Another voice, reminiscent of his son Pierre's, shouted curses. When Morel ran up to the unfortunate man, a dagger belonging to Pierre was already sticking out of his chest.

That is why all the members of the chamber have gathered today: although times are bad - the kingdom is restless, the apostate Huguenots have only recently lowered their heads, and a plague is already approaching in the north - but still it is not every day that you get to see the father. There is something to look at: a hooked nose, shoulders almost higher than the head, and the voice is almost inaudible. It is a pity that the royal decree prohibited the public administration of justice - it would be useful for the mob to look at this wicked head when the cane separates it from the puny body. And de Broval continued:

“The son had previously complained about his father’s stinginess and was clearly not averse to profiting from the inheritance.
