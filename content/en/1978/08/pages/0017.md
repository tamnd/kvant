Let us now return to the optical system, composed not of two lines, but of an infinite sequence of interconnected collecting and scattering lines. Individual rays of light (trajectories of accelerated particles) travel different paths in the optical system (in the system of magnetic lines), depending on the initial conditions - on the distance to the optical axis, on the angle of inclination to the axis. It is very difficult and of little use to follow these individual rays. It is more important to consider the “envelope of the beam” - the line separating the area filled with light from the unfilled one (the border of the particle beam). It is clear that the type of beam envelope depends on the initial conditions. However, as it passes through the optical system, each beam becomes consonant over time - the shape of its envelope between any successive collecting

Magnetic forming systems, consisting of a sequence of forming and defoxing magnets, are called hard-foxing. In such systems, constraint (2) is adjusted, the values ​​$n_z$ and $n_r$ can simultaneously apply large values, the amplitude of particle vibrations decreases and there is no need for a wide vacuum chamber and large magnets. All large modern accelerators are based on the principle of rigid forming.
