Let us denote by $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{-v}$ the velocities of charges moving away from each other, and by $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{-u}$ the velocities of charges approaching each other. Since the thread is inextensible, the following equality holds for the velocity projections onto the thread:

$$|\vec{v}| \cos \alpha = |\vec{u}| \sin \alpha, \text{ или } |\vec{u}| = |\vec{v}| \text{ ctg} \alpha.$$

For small displacements of charges from the equilibrium position, the angle is $\alpha \approx 45^\circ$, cot $\alpha \approx 1$, therefore $|\vec{u}| \approx |\vec{v}|$. So the kinetic energy of the system is

$$2 \frac{mv^2}{2} + 2 \frac{mu^2}{2} \approx 2 mv^2.$$

The potential energy of the system is equal to the work that must be expended to create the system, that is, to transfer charges $q$ from infinity to given positions.

Let charges 1 and 3 be displaced from the equilibrium position by a small distance $x$ (Fig. 10). In this case, charges 2 and 4 are displaced by a distance $y$ also, which

$$\left( \frac{l}{\sqrt{2}} + x \right)^2 + \left( \frac{l}{\sqrt{2}} - y \right)^2 = l^2$$

(since the length of the thread $l$ does not change).

When transferring the first charge, the equation is not perfect, since there is no electric field; when moving the second charge in the field of the first, the following happens:

$$A_1 = \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l}.$$

To introduce a third charge against the field created by the first two charges, you need to perform a reverse

$$A_2 = \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l} + \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (\sqrt{2} l + 2x)};$$

and when the fourth charge is introduced, a revolution occurs

$$A_3 = 2 \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 l} + \frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (\sqrt{2} l - 2y)}.$$

Thus, the potential energy of the system is equal to

$$A_1 + A_2 + A_3 \approx \frac{q^2}{\pi\varepsilon_0 l} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \right) + \frac{3q^2 x^2}{2\sqrt{2} \pi\varepsilon_0 l^3}$$

(here we used the connection between $x$ and $y$ given by formula (3)).

The total energy of the system consists of kinetic and potential energy and does not change over time:

$$2mv^2 + \frac{q^2}{\pi\varepsilon_0 l} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \right) + \frac{3q^2 x^2}{2\sqrt{2} \pi\varepsilon_0 l^3} = \text{const.}$$

Moving the constant terms to the right side of the equality, we get

$$2mv^2 + \frac{3q^2 x^2}{2\sqrt{2} \pi\varepsilon_0 l^3} = \text{const.}$$
