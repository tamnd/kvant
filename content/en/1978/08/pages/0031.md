At present, at least several dozen proofs of Morlel's theorem are already known. However, interest in it does not fade, and new evidence, generalizations and variants of this elegant proposal appear all the time.

Below we give two elementary proofs of Morlel's theorem.

The proof of the first (geometric; it coincides, in essence, with the Naranyengar-Child proof). Let us denote the angles of triangle $ABC$ by $3\alpha$, $3\beta$ and $3\gamma$ (Fig. 5). Since $3\alpha + 3\beta + 3\gamma = 180^\circ$, then $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 60^\circ$ and $\alpha + \beta = 60^\circ - \gamma$. Let $U$ be the intersection point of the trisketris $AY$ and $BX$. Since $AZ$ and $BZ$ are bisectors of triangle $AUB$, then $ZU$ is also a bisector of this triangle. Because

$$\widehat{AUZ} = Z\widehat{UB} = \frac{1}{2} \widehat{AUB} =$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} (180^\circ - 2\alpha - 2\beta) = 90^\circ -$$

$$- (\alpha + \beta) = 90^\circ - (60^\circ - \gamma) = 30^\circ + \gamma.$$
