M466. Average 1977 coins 50 counterfeit. Each counterfeit coin differs from the real one by 1 g (in one direction or the other). There are bowl scales with an arrow showing the difference in mass between one bowl and another. In one weighing, you need to find out about one selected coin whether it is counterfeit or tuned. How to do this?

M467. Points D and E divide the sides AC and AB of the correct predecessor ABC in the relations |AD|:|DC| = |BE|:|EA| = 1:2. Receivers BD and CE intersect at point O. Prove that AOC is straight.

Let there be 2n+1 coins, of which 2k are counterfeit (k ≤ n), and each counterfeit coin differs from the real one by 1 g (in one direction or the other). Let the mass of a real coin be a grams. Let's set aside one selected coin, and from the remaining 2n coins, put n coins on the first pan of the scales and n coins on the second. Note that the mass of gry on each scale differs from na (the mass of n real coins) by an integer number of the same parity as the number of counterfeit coins on this scale.

If the set aside coin is in tune, then the total number of counterfeit coins on the cups is 2k (even), which means that the number of them on both cups has the same parity, therefore, the difference in the masses of one and the other cup will be even. If the chosen coin is counterfeit, then the number of counterfeit coins lying on the scales is odd - (2k-1); then the reading of the scales is odd.

So, we see that the chosen coin is in tune or counterfeit depending on whether the scale reading is even or odd.
