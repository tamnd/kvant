On July 13, all participants and organizers of the Olympiad moved to the capital of Czechoslovakia - Prague. On July 15, in the ancient building of one of the first universities in Europe, Charles University, the ceremonial closing of the Olympiad and the presentation of prizes and certificates took place.

1st prize

7 Olympiad participants received: P. Glaser (GDR), P. Dinev (Bulgaria), J. Matena (Czechoslovakia), P. Meiel (GDR), F. Pardo (France), J. Svoboda (Czechoslovakia), P. Sharinov (SSR).

2nd prize

16 Olympiad participants received awards; Among them are Soviet schoolchildren: A. Ganopolsky, V. Reshetov, K. Tretyachenko and V. Shukin.

III prize

16 participants also received.

Another 12 participants were loaded with certificates.

Special prizes were also awarded at the Olympics. Thus, A. Ganopolsky received a prize for the best solution to the second theoretical problem, and P. Sharinov - as the youngest participant in the Olympiad.

It is pleasant to note that the organizers of the Olympiad did everything to ensure that the Olympiad passed the maximum limit. From the first day of work, relations of mutual trust and goodwill were established between the members of the International Commission and the Organizing Committee, which, without a doubt, helped to correctly assess the solution to the problem and sum up the results of the Olympiad.

Meeting with each other, the team leaders discussed various issues related to holding the Olympics in their countries. This exchange of views is very important; it helps to borrow the best experience and use it in your work.

No less important are the first professional meetings of the Olympiad participants - future engineers and physicists. During such meetings, schoolchildren exchanged souvenirs and served various tasks.

Both the Olympiad participants and team leaders left Prague with a feeling of gratitude to the organizers of the Olympiad, who managed to make the Olympiad a showcase of scientific creativity among schoolchildren.

Without a doubt, the International School Olympids are an important example of cooperation between different countries in the field of public education, helping to develop methods for developing the creative activity of schoolchildren.
