⟦folio 32⟧

Fig. 8.

At the yarn

1. On figure 8, along with the vertices X, Y and Z of the Morley triangle, the intersection points U, V and W (AY) and (BX) are also depicted (this point also has on figure 5 - it played a large role in the 1st proof of Morley’s theorem); AZ and CX; CY and BZ. Prove that the segments [XW], [YV] and [ZU] intersect at one point Q.

2. Prove that the point Q in Problem 1 is the center of a regular Morley triangle XYZ.

3. Prove that

a) the segments [AX], [BY] and [CZ] (where XYZ is the “Morley triangle” for ΔABC) intersect at one point R;

b) straight lines AW, BV and CU (Fig. 8) intersect at one point S;

c) points R, S and point Q of problem 1 lie on the same line.

4. Let $\bar{X}, \bar{Y}, \bar{Z}$ be points inside (arbitrary) $\triangle ABC$ such that $\hat{B}\hat{A}\hat{Z} = \hat{C}\hat{A}\hat{Y}$ ($= \alpha$), $\hat{B}\hat{X} = \hat{A}\hat{B}Z$ ($= \beta$), $\hat{B}\hat{C}\hat{X} = \hat{A}\hat{C}\hat{Y}$ ($= \gamma$). Prove that the lines AX, BY, CZ intersect at one point R (see problem 3a).

5. The additional following is an "indirect" proof of Morley's theorem for triangle ABC with angles $\hat{A} = 3\alpha$, $\hat{B} = 3\beta$ and $\hat{C} = 3\gamma$. Let XYZ be an arbitrary equilateral triangle. On its sides we will construct two $\triangle XYZ$ equilateral triangles XYU, YZW and ZXV (sp. Fig. 8) with angles at the bases [XY], [YZ] and [ZX], respectively, equal to 60°-γ, 60°-α and 60°-β. Let us further denote the point of intersection of the lines UY and VZ by A; UX and WZ through B and VX and WY through C. Prove that the angles $\triangle ABC$ are equal to 3α, 3β and 3γ and that the lines AY and AZ; BZ and BX; CX and CY are trisecgris of this triangle. We called this proof “indirect”, since in it the original $\triangle ABC$ is constructed from its Morley triangle XYZ.
