Let line ADB at figure 2 represent the fastest path. At point D this path crosses the Sot River. Let's draw a circle with a center at point D of a single radius - it intersects the path ADB at points C and E. Let CDE be the shortest path between C and E (if this were not so, then by choosing the best path in the section, CDE could be improved and weighed from A to B).

The shortest path is completely determined by the angles α and β (Fig. 2). Let's find a correspondence between these angles.

Let Cryomon travel the path $|CD|=1$ with the speed $u$, and the path $|DE|=1$ with the speed $v<u$. Thus, he covered the weight path CDE in time $z=\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}$.

Let us now shift the intersection point D of the path ADB with Soth by quantities ε - let us denote the new point by O. According to the geometry theorem known to Euclid,

$$|CO|=\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}-2b\varepsilon,$$
$$|OE|=\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}+2a\varepsilon,$$

where for brevity we denote $|FD|=b$, $|DH|=a$ (Fig. 2). Dividing both of these distances by the corresponding speeds, we obtain the travel time along the path COE:

$$\bar{z}=\frac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}-2b\varepsilon}{u}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}+2a\varepsilon}{v}.$$

If the values ​​of ε are taken to be very small, then the values ​​of z and $\bar{z}$ will hardly differ. Therefore, we equate both times z and $\bar{z}$:

$$\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v}=\frac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}-2b\varepsilon}{u}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}+2a\varepsilon}{v}.$$

To get rid of irrationality, let’s square both sides of the best equation twice—it’s not easy, but we’re talking about human life!

$$\frac{4}{u^2v^2}(1+\varepsilon^2-2b\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon^2+2a\varepsilon)=$$
$$=4\left[\frac{1}{uv}+\varepsilon\left(\frac{b}{u^2}-\frac{a}{v^2}\right)\right]^2+$$

Not much left, gentlemen. Since we generally know that for the fastest path the sines of angles α and β are proportional to the speed of movement u and v, we can put sin α = ku, sin β = kv, where k is the proportionality coefficient. Knowing the sines of the angles, it is easy to find their tangents:

$$tg\alpha = \frac{ku}{\sqrt{1-k^2u^2}},$$
$$tg\beta = \frac{kv}{\sqrt{1-k^2v^2}},$$

and with their help it is quite easy to determine the lengths of the segments PD and DQ:

$$|PD| = |AP|tg\alpha, |DQ| = |QB|tg\beta.$$
Remember that |AP| = |QB| = 1 league, u = 4 leagues/hour and v = 2.5 leagues/hour, we get:

$$|PD| = \frac{4k}{\sqrt{1-16k^2}},$$
$$|DQ| = \frac{2,5k}{\sqrt{1-6,25k^2}}.$$

The sum of these quantities is equal to 3 leagues (see Fig. 1); that is

$$\frac{4k}{\sqrt{1-16k^2}} + \frac{2,5k}{\sqrt{1-6,25k^2}} = 3.$$

Don't ask me, gentlemen, how I managed to find the appropriate root of this equation - it would lead us astray. Its approximation value is k ≈ 0.23. Then |PD| = 2.36, |DQ| = 0.71 (residual 0.07 when substituting into the equation is allowed to be neglected),

$$|AD| = \sqrt{1+(2,36)^2} = 2,56,$$
$$|DB| = \sqrt{1+(0,71)^2} = 1,22.$$

This means that the fastest route will lead from Amber to Bene in $\frac{2,56}{4} + \frac{1,22}{2,5} = 1,13$ hours. Previously, Kryomon could not be added, and therefore could not perform the performance.
