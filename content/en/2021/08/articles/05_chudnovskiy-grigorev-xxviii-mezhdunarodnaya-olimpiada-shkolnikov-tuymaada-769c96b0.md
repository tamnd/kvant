XXVIII International Olympiad for schoolchildren "Tuymaada"

Physics

In July of this year, the XXVIII International Olympiad “Tuymaada” in physics, mathematics, computer science and chemistry was held in Yakutsk. A feature of this year’s Olympiad was the full-time-distance form (without the usual experimental round): schoolchildren from Yakutsk participated in person, and from other regions and countries - remotely. Participants in the Physics Olympiad competed, as usual, in two leagues: senior and junior (the jury distributes participants into leagues depending on their age and class). According to the program of the Physics Olympiad, participants can be offered problems on any topics contained in the in-depth school curriculum (in the junior league - with the exception of topics related to graduation and pre-graduation classes). Authors of original problems (both theoretical and experimental) can send them to the methodological commission (achudn@mail.ru) - the best problems will be included in the final set and will be published in educational journals. The article was prepared on the basis of the methodological manual: A.V. Chudnovsky, R.E. Avanesyan, A.B. Akimov, A.I. Bychkov, S.D. Varlamov, M.M. Ermilov, S.I. Koshoridze, S.P. Kryukov. Senior League

Problem 1. Jumping cat 1) The cat wants to jump from one shelf to another, and then back. The minimum initial speeds $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ of the cat are known at which it can make these jumps. Find the height difference module H and the distance R between the shelves. 2) A cat of mass m jumps on the shelves $A_{1}, A_{2},..., A_{n}$ in the indicated order $( A_{1} o A_{2} o$ … $o A_{n} ),$ stopping after each jump. The height difference between the last and first shelves is ' H, and the length of the broken line $A_{1} A_{2}$ ... $A_{n}$ is L. What is the minimum energy required by the cat for the entire series of jumps? Consider that when jumping, the cat’s efficiency is 100%, and when landing it does not expend any energy. Consider the cat and all the shelves as material points, do not take into account air resistance, the free fall acceleration g is known. M. Ermilov

Problem 8. Mirror Earth At the initial moment of time, the ISS (international space station), flying in a circular orbit around the Earth at an altitude of H 400 km above its surface, crossed the segment connecting the centers of the Earth and the Sun, and directly under the ISS, the astronauts could observe through a clear sky a complete calm on a large area of ​​the Pacific Ocean. After what time T will the image of the Sun in the ocean approach the ISS at a speed of u 0.4 km s? At what distance L from the ISS will the image be at this moment in time? Consider the Earth to be a ball with radius R 6400 km, the acceleration of free fall on the Earth's surface is g 10 m $с^{2}$. S.Varlamov
