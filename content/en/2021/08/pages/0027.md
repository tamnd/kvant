SCHOOL AT KVANT

"Don't forget the watch!"

S. PARNOVSKY

This is what poet Andrei Voznesensky, singer Maxim Leonidov and the Litsedei clownery theater say in the old film How to Become a Star. How bad will it be if you forget? Let's ask the scientists. Now our lives are simply permeated with information about where exactly we are in the flow of time. By turning on a smartphone or computer, we immediately find out the exact time, and every minute the watch changes its readings, synchronized with precise time signals, with data from navigation satellites, with standards... In short, with a particularly accurate watch. Japanese scientists compared the progress of similar superchronometers at the foot and at the top of the Tokyo TV tower and once again confirmed Albert Einstein's prediction that the speed of time flow for these clocks is $различаются.^{1}$ At the top, time flows a little faster. But there was a time when no one counted minutes, let alone nanoseconds. There was nothing and, generally speaking, there was nothing special. Let us remember the classic: “When d’Artagnan approached the vacant lot located near the monastery, noon struck. Athos waited for him for only five minutes - therefore, d’Artagnan was impeccably accurate and the strictest judge in the laws of dueling would have no reason to reproach him.” And who is no longer satisfied with “impeccable accuracy” with
