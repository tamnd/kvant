Mysterious scale, or a little investigation

S. DVORYANINOV

TELL ME, HAVE YOU LOOKED INTO THE ATTICT OF YOUR HOUSE FOR A LONG TIME? But the attic is the place where the adventures of many literary heroes begin. Let us recall the lines from A. Gaidar’s story “Timur and His Team”: “Zhenya walked around the barn... She dragged a rotten ladder to the window and, climbing up it, jumped onto the floor of the attic. Very strange! This attic was inhabited. On the wall hung coils of rope, a lantern, two crossed signal flags and a map of the village, all covered with incomprehensible signs... Near the holey mossy roof stuck out a large wheel that looked like a steering wheel. There was a homemade telephone hanging above the wheel.” The boy Leshka, the main character of S. Alekseev’s book “The Giant’s Son,” ends up in the attic at the very beginning of the story: “When Leshka pushed the attic door, it did not budge. The door was locked." Sometimes you don’t want to throw away an old thing - what if it comes in handy again? And it turns out to be in the attic or in the far corner of the pantry or shed. And even the owner then forgets where and what is located. And the attic turns into a museum of antiques. So the author recently came across an old thing (Fig.1). It is clear that this is a steelyard - a spring scale. According to Wikipedia, this word is borrowed from Turkic languages, where vesne means “scales”. The Turkic vesne received the sound “bezel” under the influence of folk etymology: in the popular consciousness it was interpreted as steelyard – “without change”
